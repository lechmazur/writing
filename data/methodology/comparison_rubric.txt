Rubric guide for comparison

Use this as flexible guidance, not a scoring machine. Ground claims in evidence: sentence, image, scene turn, motif, structure, tonal shift, or accumulated effect. Avoid mechanical deductions, fixed ceilings, gates, or preset dependencies between categories.

Do not privilege one mode of fiction. Realism, causal plotting, lyrical fragmentation, surrealism, symbolic structure, voice-driven narration, ambiguity, and formal experiment can all be strong when controlled and effective.

Q1 Character & Motivation: specificity, desire, constraint, agency, change, refusal, contradiction, or interior pressure.
Q2 Plot Structure & Coherence: progression, arrangement, causality or patterning, structural clarity, fragmentation, and ending shape. Endings may succeed through completion, transformation, resonance, revelation, unresolved tension, release, or intentional incompletion.
Q3 Setting, Atmosphere & Specificity: how place, atmosphere, objects, social context, body, weather, or sensory field shape action, mood, symbol, perception, or texture.
Q4 Story Impact, Pressure, or Development: tension, intensification, emotional charge, conceptual development, surprise, dread, humor, ache, wonder, or other reader effect.
Q5 Authenticity & Originality: freshness, specificity, risk, surprise, genre fluency, and avoidance of familiar or underdeveloped choices.
Q6 Thematic Cohesion & Tone: how images, motifs, turns, atmosphere, and ending shape implied meaning; tone may be stable, mixed, shifting, sincere, comic, ironic, uncanny, or deliberately unstable.
Q7 Voice & Point of View: narrative control, focalization, distance, perspective management, rhythm of attention, and fit between voice and material. Delayed orientation, reflection, fragmentation, or ambiguity is weak only when accidental, confusing, or unrewarded.
Q8 Prose & Line-Level Craft: diction, syntax, rhythm, image precision, compression, transitions, sound, sentence variety, and technical control.

9A-9J Required-element integration: judge meaningful use, not mentions. Elements may work through plot, character, setting, atmosphere, motif, imagery, symbolism, structure, rhythm, voice, tone, temporal patterning, or closure.
- 9A Character: specified role/person.
- 9B Object: prop, evidence, symbol, obstacle, motif, texture, or turn.
- 9C Concept/Theme: pressure, atmosphere, argument, pattern, or meaning.
- 9D Attribute/Trait: behavior, perception, style, structure, or consequence.
- 9E Action: enacted, transformed, echoed, or made meaningful.
- 9F Method/Technique: process, knowledge, voice, structure, or discovery.
- 9G Setting/Location: action, atmosphere, symbol, relation, or orientation.
- 9H Timeframe/Temporal: pacing, recurrence, memory, urgency, ritual, or structure.
- 9I Motivation/Drive: desire, need, curiosity, fear, duty, obsession, or absence.
- 9J Tone/Mood: mood sustained, modulated, complicated, or turned.
