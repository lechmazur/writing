# GPT-6 Astra (high) vs. GPT-5.6 Sol (high): comparative writing analysis

Focus model: `gpt-6-astra-high`. Comparison model: `gpt-5.6-high`. Direct-comparison scope: `comparator_v2_eval_v2_v3_astra_predecessor_depth_n50`.

## In one paragraph

Given the same short-fiction prompts, GPT-5.6 tends to write stories that put things back. A hidden design gets decoded, the wrongdoer is exposed or converted, the loss that started the story is returned, and a closing sentence names the lesson [C013]. GPT-6 Astra writes stories that spend something instead: its protagonists are usually implicated in the harm they are repairing, and the repair costs them something they do not get back [C034]. One prompt shows the split cleanly. Handed a dead wife's last surviving recording, GPT-5.6 returns the woman herself from the phonograph's hum; GPT-6 Astra breaks the wax disc into a lamp's reservoir to save a stranger, listens for her in the hiss, and finds only burning [C001]. The habits extend to the assignment itself: GPT-5.6 writes the required phrases into the prose where a reader can see them, while GPT-6 Astra converts them into behaviour and never names them [C007] — the source of its best effects and, occasionally, of a requested mood that never quite arrives. Across fifty prompts given to both, the difference is lopsided, and the fiction that results reads less like a fable than like a deposition.

## Quantitative context

Across 50 matched prompts, the focus model recorded 43 wins, 6 ties, and 1 loss at the ±0.5 tie threshold. Its mean signed margin was +1.513 ± 0.263 (95% normal half-width).

Mean lengths were 689.3 and 750.3 words; the correlation between length difference and margin was +0.240.

## Comparative portrait

The recurring difference between GPT-6 Astra (high) and GPT-5.6 Sol (high) is not skill level but what a story is *for*. Across all fourteen blinded packets, GPT-5.6 Sol (high) writes restorative parables: a concealed design is decoded, an antagonist is exposed or converted, the loss that motivated the story is returned, and a closing sentence names the lesson [C013] [C028] [C048]. Astra writes consequence fiction: the protagonist is implicated in the harm she is repairing, the fix costs something that is not refunded, and the ending leaves the cost running [C001] [C063] [C082]. Four packet critics reached this independently under different blindings, and the quantitative record is one-sided in the same direction: 43 focus wins, 6 ties, 1 comparison win across 50 pairs, mean margin +1.513 (CI half-width 0.263), median +1.667.

Three structural corollaries recur. First, the fault line: Astra locates the harm inside the protagonist's own professional excellence or her intimate circle, while GPT-5.6 Sol (high) locates it in villains, ministries, and inherited wrongs [C004] [C015] [C027] [C034] [C055] [C065]. Second, how requirements enter: GPT-5.6 Sol (high) inscribes prompt phrases verbatim as narrator labels; Astra converts them into behaviour, object, and procedure, usually without naming them [C007] [C030] [C043] [C052] [C062]. Third, how meaning is delivered: GPT-5.6 Sol (high) terminates on a portable aphorism, often a "not A, but B" construction; Astra terminates on a physical act or resumed obligation and declines to summarise [C003] [C011] [C023] [C074] [C084].

GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s own portrait is not a deficit portrait. It is the stronger inventor of systems and worlds: nested adversarial architecture [C019], mandated objects converted into a story's central proposition [C025], sensory settings that carry exposition causally [C039], multi-function symbolic devices [C070], mechanisms whose rules are identical to the story's ethics [C091], and the larger, stranger conceits [C078] [C085]. It also reliably manufactures jeopardy where Astra sometimes omits it [C046].

**Replication.** The central portrait replicates in the extension cohort and sharpens: 15/4/1 and +1.347 in the original 20 pairs becomes 28/2/0 and +1.624 in the 30 extension pairs, with the CI half-width narrowing from ±0.500 to ±0.285. The single comparison win sits in the original cohort; the extension contains none. No packet-level claim reversed direction; the material change is one of margin and floor, not of kind.

## Does either model seem smarter?

"Seems smarter" here means a reader impression produced by writing choices. Eleven of fourteen packets record that impression as favouring Astra; three decline a global ranking outright, and that minority reading is well supported and should be preserved.

The behaviours producing Astra's impression are auditable rather than ornamental. **Causal foresight:** stated physical constraints whose consequences bind the climax, so a reader can re-derive the ending from earlier evidence [C002] [C010] [C031] [C036] [C061] [C068]. **Structural foresight:** first plans that fail and force a revised model of the problem — five of five stories in one packet against zero of five for GPT-5.6 Sol (high) [C081] — and turns generated by a second character's objection rather than by an oracle [C021] [C020]. **Psychological inference:** condemnation and tenderness held in one sentence [C079], recovered dead who stay inconvenient [C054], remorse caught converting into performance [C004], interiority delivered as behaviour under material pressure [C029]. **Relevant-detail selection:** the second count of a favourable number [C038], the trade's actual reasoning solving the trade's problem [C044], cost priced in kneading, sleeves and cup handles [C024]. **Constraint synthesis:** an instantaneous timeframe treated as a container that opens and closes the story [C077]. **Restraint and reader trust:** meaning left for the reader to compute [C023] [C011] [C075].

GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s impression is real and different in kind — conceptual compression and architecture [C019] [C025] [C070] [C078] [C091] [C059] — but multiple critics found part of it carried by proxies: isolated one-sentence paragraphs that lend verdict-weight to ordinary exposition [C040] [C050], a portable wisdom-shaped cadence recurring across unrelated prompts [C003], and excerptability that flatters quick reading [C066] [C084].

The impression is not verbosity. Astra is shorter on average (689.3 vs 750.3 words), yet the word-count gap narrowed in the extension (−75.6 to −51.3) while the margin widened, and the correlation between word-count difference and margin is weakly *positive* (+0.240) — brevity is not the mechanism. Several packets record comparable lengths and equal polish, and no packet supports a lyricism, imagery-quality or vocabulary gap.

**Replication.** The impression-level finding replicates: it is asserted in packets read by all three critic models and is consistent with the extension's cleaner sweep. The minority "neither is smarter simpliciter" reading also replicates, appearing in packets from two different critic models.

## Narrative reasoning and aesthetic judgment

Astra's reasoning shows most clearly where a rule must forbid something. Its mechanisms specify failure modes and then obey them, including past the climax [C036] [C076] [C042], and its supernatural channels emit images requiring interpretive labour rather than plain-English instructions [C088]. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s plots more often depend on a design laid in advance by a dead figure who predicted the protagonist's arrival, so the task is recognition rather than invention [C090]; at its weakest this produces devices revealed in the sentence that resolves the plot [C018] [C061] or a stated prohibition suspended for a quotable benediction [C042].

Aesthetic judgment diverges in ways that are mode, not rank, and several critics explicitly refused to score them: reveals that obligate versus reveals that absolve [C008]; machinery that witnesses versus machinery that retrieves [C047]; carried guilt versus concealed guilt [C051]; climax as conversation versus climax as broadcast [C060]; paperwork ethics versus ceremony ethics [C092]; two opposite doctrines of emotional control on the same prompt [C072]; maxim closers versus gesture closers [C069] [C033]. These are the honest core of the "different intelligences" minority reading.

Corroboration from story-level evaluation is mostly aligned but not total. One material disagreement: the packet critics twice praised Astra's hydraulic reasoning on 0240 as load-bearing where GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s mycelial mechanism restated the theme [C002] [C036]; the story-level evaluator preferred GPT-5.6 Sol (high) there on conceptual originality and prose, and the pair scored a near-tie (+0.217). Causal auditability is a real behaviour, but it is not uniformly what gets rewarded.

## Range, recurring habits, and floor versus ceiling

Neither writer escapes a template. Both fill a fixed kinship slot every time — Astra a dead or silenced mother who bequeathed an object and a method, GPT-5.6 Sol (high) a lost sibling absorbed into a machine or song [C080]. Astra's withholding is itself a repeatable closing device: deferral, unverifiability, or an unfinished task, five of five in one packet [C093], and Astra aphorises when a prompt presses [C003] [C023] and occasionally inserts tone words verbatim [C006] [C052]. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s terminal thesis and one-sentence lineation are equally constitutional [C011] [C040] [C050].

The evidence favours a *raised floor* over a special ceiling. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s ceiling is genuinely high — the packet critics' own reversals name its most conceptually ambitious pieces [C025] [C078] [C070] [C091], and story-level evaluation independently preferred it on 0342 (fractal recursion as structural motif), 0174 (calligraphy as philosophical instrument) and 0276 (transliteration and constellation integration). Astra's ceiling stories are strong but its distribution is what wins: 43 of 50 pairs, and in the extension cohort not a single loss. Its floor failures are narrow and specific (below). GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s floor failures are causal — soft joints under baroque climaxes [C018], rules broken at the emotional peak [C042], powers declared at need [C061].

Two range claims remain unsupported and should not be leaned on: whether GPT-5.6 Sol (high) is genuinely broader is untestable within the packets, since nothing shows Astra running a crowd at scale [C053], and the voice-modulation finding rests on thin instances [C073].

## What each model still does better

GPT-5.6 Sol (high), on evidence spanning multiple packets and corroborated by story-level evaluation: conceptual invention and contraption architecture [C019] [C078] [C085]; mandated objects turned into theses [C025]; systems whose technical rules *are* the moral argument [C091]; multi-function devices with sequential payoff [C070]; setting rendered as causal atmosphere [C039]; motif braiding and image density [C032]; seeded clue engineering [C059]; installed jeopardy — deadlines, enforcers, stated penalties — where Astra sometimes proceeds without opposition [C046]; and appetite for collective scale [C053].

Astra: everything organised around consequence — retained costs that generate later plot [C022] [C087], complicity [C034], auditable mechanism [C002] [C036], error-correcting plots [C081], objections as engine [C021], institutions that bargain rather than forbid [C005] [C035] [C017], administrative and consent-shaped climaxes [C012] [C049], priced wonder [C058], relinquished gifts [C056], held ambivalence [C079], permitted comedy inside grief [C045], and mundane cost-accounting [C024] [C064].

This split is partly prompt-dependent. GPT-5.6 Sol (high) is at full strength on civic and forensic premises and weaker on lyrical ones [C026] (single-packet, low confidence), and its advantage concentrates where a prompt rewards spectacle, jeopardy or exotic tone vocabulary.

## Findings beyond the existing judging

**1. One habit produces both the biggest wins and every non-win.** The metabolise-versus-inscribe difference [C007] [C030] [C043] [C052] [C062] is the panel's most systematic finding, and combining it with story-level evaluation shows it cuts both ways. Where enactment lands, the margins are the largest in the set: 0343 (+2.883), where the evaluator faults the comparison for *stating* sociable isolation early rather than dramatising it; 0199 (+3.000), where the required action is physically motivated by the protagonist's tools on one side and dictated by prophecy on the other. But five of the seven non-wins are recorded by evaluators as focus-side element failures: an exotic tone phrase absent as both phrase and concept (0154), a required attribute treated as description rather than structure (0342), two required elements left scenic (0276), a method delivered as static exposition and an attribute confined to backstory (0367). This is new: Astra's only recorded weakness is the visibility cost of its own integration strategy, plus conceptual ambition (0240, 0224) — not psychology, causality, or ending design.

**2. Mechanism rigour and score partly decouple** (0240, above).

**3. The extension changed the noise, not the direction.** Evaluator disagreement fell (1.394 → 1.239) while AB/BA order sensitivity *rose* (1.933 → 2.312) even as the margin widened and the CI narrowed. The effect strengthened despite noisier presentation-order behaviour, which is a mild strengthening of the conclusion rather than a caveat against it.

**4. Length is a non-explanation** (word gap narrowed, margin widened, correlation +0.240).

## Disagreements and limitations

Packet-to-cohort membership was not supplied, so stability is tested through the aggregate cohort statistics and through cross-packet recurrence rather than by re-partitioning the packet evidence; individual case studies below cannot be assigned to a cohort and are not presented as cohort-specific.

Order sensitivity (2.161) exceeds evaluator disagreement (1.301) across all 50 pairs — position effects are the largest identified noise source, and they grew in the extension. Several critics flagged prestige bias against themselves: irreversible loss, complicity and withheld forgiveness are exactly what literary-realist taste codes as mature [C028] [C048] [C093], while quotability and lineation flatter quick reading in the other direction [C050] [C066] [C084]. Compliance-checking rubrics would plausibly over-credit GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s visible element display [C016] [C030] [C052] and under-credit Astra's symbol-withdrawal at closure [C037].

Direct conflicts to record: packet critics faulted GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s stacked required-element clause in 0174 [C006], while the story-level evaluator judged that story's element integration superior; and the 0240 mechanism disagreement above. Both stories are ties or near-ties, so the conflicts sit exactly where the aggregate is least decisive. Name-pool overlap (both default to "Mara") means all voice claims here are structural, not lexical [C080]. Single-packet, low-confidence observations — register drift with prompt genre [C026] and voice modulation [C073] — are labelled as such and carry no weight in the conclusions.

## Representative case studies

**0343 (+2.883) — enactment as advantage.** Astra dramatises the required tone in two concrete beats and never names it; GPT-5.6 Sol (high) names both required terms and then explains the label [C007]. The renunciation of glory is staged as cartography — passenger names written where the discovery's title belongs, spellings checked aloud [C012]. The evaluator independently records the comparison stating the tone rather than dramatising it.

**0199 (+3.000) — occupation versus oracle.** Astra's protagonist reorders seed baskets by pioneer species [C044] and jokes inside her own competence [C045]; GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s prophecy issues a plain-English imperative containing the prompt's verb [C088], and the required infinitive survives ungrammatically inside the prose [C043].

**0260 (+3.000) — refused mercy.** Astra names and declines the tactically useful forgiveness and files the protagonist's own signature as evidence [C004]; the ending is a physical arrangement, "held securely without anything having been made whole" [C003].

**0367 (−1.500) — the single comparison win.** Astra's method is explained rather than dramatised, its "accidentally prophetic" attribute confined to backstory guilt, and its rescue compressed into one clause [C026]; GPT-5.6 Sol (high) supplies the escalating mechanism and live obstacle its jeopardy habit reliably produces [C046]. This is the clearest instance of Astra's floor and GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s ceiling meeting.

**0154 (−0.083) — the cost of never naming.** Astra's non-inscription strategy leaves a required tone unfulfilled as both phrase and concept, the explicit risk attached to that strategy [C030] [C007].

**0240 (+0.217) — critics against evaluator.** Astra's culvert hydraulics predict their own payoff [C002] [C036]; the evaluator preferred GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s mycelial concept and prose. Auditable causality is a genuine behaviour, not an automatic scoring advantage.

**0342 (−0.250) — GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s ceiling.** The recursive coin unifies attribute, object, setting and theme [C078], while Astra's handling of the same attribute stays descriptive; here it is GPT-5.6 Sol (high) that refuses consolation.

## Cited claim ledger

- **C013 — Refunded costs vs. retained costs:** GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s plots stage protagonist sacrifice and then reimburse it: mornings lent are repaid, the drowned brother walks out of the fountain speaking, the dead father's forgiveness is voiced aloud. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s protagonists pay in their own substance — language, pardon, the comfortable version of a father — and the final pages stay inside the loss. GPT-5.6 Sol (high) closes the moral ledger; GPT-6 Astra (high) leaves it open, which is why GPT-6 Astra (high)'s successes still read as precarious.
  - Story 0364, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > When Neris woke, he knew neither Sable nor the hammer in his hand.
    >
    > Yet the rooted rubble pulsed through the floor, returning each morning he had lent, one by one.
    Neris's central sacrifice — surrendering his own awakenings to the silk — is explicitly repaid with interest in moral proof. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s parallel blacksmith ends inside her loss, having forgotten the words for blue and for trees, held up by hands rather than restored by plot.
- **C028 — Resolutions retain cost instead of restoring harmony:** GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s endings restore: the brother returns, the archive is saved, the trellis flowers, the dawn arrives multicolored. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s endings grant continuation only, keeping the loss in frame — an unforgiven father walked upstairs to confess, a lost branch recorded, originals withering, a drowned city traded for a plateau. The maturity impression comes from refusing to trade meaning for comfort.
  - Story 0367, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > She could offer no restored city, only room for tents, gardens, and graves.  That would have to be enough.
    The climax explicitly refuses restoration and downsizes hope to shelter; the story's courage is the arithmetic of what cannot be returned.
  - Story 0132, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > It was evidence, not absolution, and she wrote down the lost branch too.
    Even success is logged with its casualty; the sentence refuses to let a good outcome launder a real cost.
- **C048 — Endings priced in residue, not thresholds:** GPT-6 Astra (high)'s endings register what stays broken or unresolved; GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s endings stage a passage. This is not tone but payoff economics: GPT-6 Astra (high) closes on costs that cannot be converted into progress, GPT-5.6 Sol (high) closes on symbolic payments that purchase release and then names the new beginning.
  - Story 0225, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > Soren waited for relief to make him innocent, but his wrist hurt and the other villages were still ashes.
    The sentence names relief as an expected wage and then declines to pay it, leaving bodily pain and unhealed history on the books after the plot resolves. The pattern holds everywhere: GPT-6 Astra (high), 0351 ends with names "each followed by silence, so nobody could mistake what survived for all there had been," and GPT-6 Astra (high), 0300 ends before the bunker plan's outcome is known. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s endings are threshold-shaped ("gentle enough to let everything begin," GPT-5.6 Sol (high), 0225; "I want to learn doors," GPT-5.6 Sol (high), 0300).
- **C001 — Endings that spend versus endings that recover:** Given the same prompts, GPT-5.6 Sol (high) tends to restore the loss that motivated the story, and often routes the cost onto parties who cannot be harmed; GPT-6 Astra (high) makes the protagonist destroy something irreplaceable and keeps it destroyed. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s cost is structurally integrated — the object sacrificed is the object that solves the problem.
  - Story 0311, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > He broke the disc into the lamp's reservoir and struck a spark.  For a moment, while the wax softened, he listened for her in the hiss.  There was only burning.
    The dead wife's last surviving recording is literally the fuel for the lamp that saves a stranger; the sacrifice is causal, not symbolic, and nothing returns.
  - Story 0311, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > The burden passed safely into death, divided among those already beyond harm, until a woman stepped from the phonograph's fading hum.
    The climax invents a cost-free recipient for the story's danger and then returns the beloved dead, dissolving the dilemma the story spent forty lines constructing.
- **C063 — Endings leave costs standing vs. endings restore and seal:** GPT-6 Astra (high)'s conclusions leave the wound open and the symptom uncured while repositioning the person; GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s conclusions return the lost (brother, mother, mentor), complete the transformation, and seal the theme. This is partly a values choice, but GPT-6 Astra (high)'s version charges the story something for its consolation while GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s usually charges nothing.
  - Story 0053, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > She pressed the shutter, and the ghost was gone.  There was no guarantee the old film would develop.
    The single exposure — the story's whole currency of proof — is spent on an image that may not exist; the reconciliation is acted on faith, and the unfinished harbor stays unfinished.
  - Story 0361, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > She kept carrying parcels, and her hands kept shaking.
    Forty years on, the jitter is not cured, the mentor is not found, and even the protagonist's memories have eroded ("She could no longer recall a single riddle"); consolation is built on top of continuing loss rather than instead of it.
  - Story 0053, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > But when the new wings pulled and the lagoon moved under its own purpose, she knew exactly where she stood.
    The wings get built, the ghost becomes benign, and stance replaces confusion — a completed arc that closes every account the story opened.
- **C082 — Endings that spend rather than restore:** GPT-5.6 Sol (high) closes by confirming and returning: absolution pronounced, the rebellion victorious by noon, the brother's voice restored, a companion granted. GPT-6 Astra (high) closes at partial resolution with something permanently used up — the gift of hearing, the road home, the only reliable steering pin — and a negotiated, ongoing obligation instead of triumph. This produces a maturity impression: the stories accept that repair is incomplete and continuing.
  - Story 0251, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > The vote was postponed for a survey, not overturned, and Mara walked home unable to promise anyone they would be safe.
    The victory is explicitly partial and the danger explicitly unclosed; GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s version of the same prompt suspends the sale and rewrites the prophecy into saplings, erasing the ambiguity the story had earned.
  - Story 0087, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > Your bearing was wrong that night, but this one was right.
    The daughter delivers a clean ledger-balancing absolution, closing the moral account the story opened. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s 0087 refuses this: the father gets "Could I come round on Sunday?" answered by a condition, "I'll make the tea" — reconciliation as practice, not verdict.
- **C004 — The protagonist's own competence as the antagonist:** GPT-6 Astra (high) repeatedly locates the harm inside the skill the protagonist is proud of, and then refuses to let the resulting remorse become a new performance; GPT-5.6 Sol (high) more often places guilt outside the protagonist and, when it does implicate her, lets the narration ratify her self-diagnosis.
  - Story 0174, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > For a moment he imagined the audience admiring his honesty, and felt the old appetite moving into its new costume.
    The confession is caught mid-conversion into vanity; when he asks Mara to prevent this, she answers "That is also your work," denying him even the comfort of supervision.
  - Story 0260, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > She turned to a clean page, but did not offer him forgiveness to keep him talking.
    The tactically useful mercy is named and refused, and the protagonist's own signature goes into the evidence folder — complicity is processed, not absolved.
- **C015 — Complicit protagonists vs. wronged protagonists:** GPT-6 Astra (high)'s protagonists are entangled in the harm they repair: the translator whose faithful neutrality armed both factions, the aunt who concealed and perhaps caused a death, the seer who built the forgetting-prison, the daughter who wants to keep the erased, convenient father. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s protagonists are wronged parties and verifiers; guilt is assigned to rivals and institutions. The difference changes what victory means in each writer's work.
  - Story 0337, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > I said that because I wanted you to love the person raising you.
    The confession's motive is protective and self-serving at once, and it follows Mara's admission that she cut the rope that may have killed Cale's mother: the protagonist is the story's harm-doer as well as its healer. GPT-6 Astra (high)-0029 states the pattern conceptually (fidelity as absolution from choosing), and GPT-6 Astra (high)-0224's seer built the prison she must open. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s 0029 Mara, given the same interpreter role, is the deceived truth-teller exonerated by a recording.
- **C027 — The wound sits inside the house:** GPT-5.6 Sol (high) locates harm in external machinery, offstage officials, or framed innocents; the protagonist's people are blameless (a protective brother, a framed father, benign keeper-shadows). GPT-6 Astra (high) locates harm inside the intimate circle or the protagonist's own hand, so each quest's success requires exposing or carrying the loved one rather than defeating a system. This is moral-structure intelligence: it makes liberation and indictment the same act.
  - Story 0060, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > He had built the prison, then helped fill it.
    The object of the rescue quest is the father's own betrayal; the climax is a family indictment, not a victory over the Council.
  - Story 0174, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > He had seen washing on the balconies before he dipped his pen.
    Complicity is dated to before the artwork: the beautiful clearance map was drawn with knowledge of the inhabitants it erased.
- **C034 — The protagonist is implicated, not innocent:** Across all five GPT-6 Astra (high) stories the protagonist's own skill, kindness, or error is causally necessary to the harm: the translator's smoothing lie, the alchemist's furnace, a career built on exclusion, the unread appeal she signed, the awakening she sells. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s protagonists are conned, deserted, bereaved, or sacrificial; when culpable, it is framed as a wound rather than an account. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s prose creates this by shaping climaxes as self-accounting (evidence placed into folders, prices paid in one's own cognition) where GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s climaxes are rescues of others.
  - Story 0260, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > She had not read the story above her name.  Or she had read enough, and stopped.
    The doubled hypothesis refuses the protagonist a settled innocence; the fraud required her veterinary certification, so her ignorance itself becomes the story's moral problem, and the reader must audit her alongside her father.
- **C055 — Lies kept by everyone versus lies kept by villains:** In GPT-6 Astra (high) the "architecture of lies" is load-bearing small comfort: pensions, a cropped heading, a daughter's marketable legend, so exposure requires self-indictment. In GPT-5.6 Sol (high) the architecture belongs to a Ministry, a Curator, a harbor master; the heroine investigates from outside and the plot's job is to breach the institution. Distributing complicity across the protagonist forces harder psychology — recognition instead of discovery.
  - Story 0056, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > Ansel had saved his pension, the directors their money, and Mara a father whose failure seemed comprehensible.
    The sentence itemizes what each keeper of the lie got out of it, including the heroine herself; earlier the story establishes that her pamphlet made the false legend travel. Truth-telling therefore costs her identity, not just effort.
  - Story 0159, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > It said Ilyan's name had not been forgotten; it had been stolen because he discovered the library's true purpose.
    GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s backstory arrives as an antagonist's explanatory monologue: guilt is centralized in a single Curator, and Mara's own failure (obeying the evacuation bell) is absolved by the revelation of theft. The villain holds the blame so the heroine does not have to.
- **C065 — Guilt lodged in the beloved and the self vs. in villains and late exculpatory facts:** GPT-6 Astra (high) places culpability inside the people the protagonist loves or inside the self's own vanity, and refuses to launder it. GPT-5.6 Sol (high) concentrates guilt in a villain's scheme or dissolves it with a late-arriving fact that externalizes blame. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s moral world is harder to leave clean, which reads as the more mature social perception.
  - Story 0385, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > "I knew they were below," her mother said.
    The rescued voice of the beloved dead confesses to the bribe and the opened sluice, and Mara holds the shell higher anyway, finally letting her mother be "neither a saint's nor a monster's, but her mother's" — complicity survives reconciliation.
  - Story 0289, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > Mara, seeking language large enough for death, had replaced them with eternal stars.
    The wound between sisters is caused by the protagonist's own aesthetic vanity — swapping wet hair and burnt rice for grandiosity — a small, precise, self-inflicted fault rather than an external wrong.
  - Story 0289, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > his beloved had left from terror of illness, not absence of love, and had sworn Mara to silence
    The decisive fact arrives late and exculpates nearly everyone: the beloved was afraid, not cruel, converting the moral problem into a misunderstanding that facts can fix.
- **C007 — Required language: enacted vs. inscribed:** Across all five prompts GPT-5.6 Sol (high) deposits the required tone and attribute phrases verbatim into the prose, often label-first with explanation after; GPT-6 Astra (high) never uses the stranger tone phrases at all, rendering them through behavior, pacing, and object. The pattern is 5-of-5 versus 0-of-5 on tone phrases specifically, which makes it the most systematic difference in the packet. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s method forces the reader to infer the abstraction; GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s makes compliance auditable but distrusts the scene to carry it.
  - Story 0343, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > That sociable isolation made her diligently curious: every stranger might possess a missing piece of the world, yet no one thought to ask her for theirs.

    > She had laughed at her own joke before she understood that a warning disguised as entertainment could travel everywhere and still save nobody.

  - Story 0367, `gpt-5.6-high`:

    The same attribute becomes a private liberation with no casualties; the herald's accidental power costs no one anything, so the elation is unshadowed despite the prompt's requested tone.
- **C087 — Costs that stay paid:** GPT-6 Astra (high) routinely prices a choice in specific, non-refunded currency, and lets the loss stand at the end. GPT-5.6 Sol (high) often stages a cost and then reverses it within a few lines, so the sacrifice functions as a beat rather than as a constraint on the ending.
  - Story 0167, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > It would cost her the new engraving wheel she had ordered, and several weeks of evenings.

  - Story 0047, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > Mara disconnected the external uplink, though doing so made the reporters roar and sent accusations of cowardice racing online.
    A cost is named, but the same story ends with "the crowd quieted enough for the bees to be heard waking" — the reputational price is refunded by the crowd's conversion.
- **C005 — Institutions that bargain rather than forbid:** GPT-6 Astra (high) models power as incentive and omission: offers with conditions attached, questions deliberately not asked, disputes over dates in an unfinished court case. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s institutions issue prohibitions and want ownership, which is plausible but supplies less friction.
  - Story 0079, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > The council offered Sela a boat, lowered beyond the gate, provided she kept that discovery off her public charts.  She could fetch her mother without frightening anyone.
    The temptation is generous, private, and legal, which makes Sela's refusal a genuine choice rather than defiance of an obvious villain.
  - Story 0240, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > He did not ask how many people she could see.
    Institutional cruelty rendered as a question withheld — a precise observation about how deniability is maintained in organizations.
- **C035 — Change by grudging increments vs. instant conversion:** GPT-6 Astra (high) lets opposition soften in face-saving steps — the commanders inch from refusal to quietly requested seats — so bargaining preserves everyone's dignity as cover. GPT-5.6 Sol (high) twice dissolves armed opposition in a single beat of interior collapse, supplying the antagonist a personal stake at the exact moment of need. GPT-6 Astra (high) models how institutions actually move; GPT-5.6 Sol (high) models recognition.
  - Story 0154, `gpt-5.6-high`:
    > Orr found his daughter's name beside the words "boots in the hall," and the certainty went out of him.
    The captain's coercive certainty evaporates in one sentence, and the daughter has not been established before this moment — the conversion arrives as narrative gift rather than pressure the protagonist applies. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s parallel scene moves in guarded fractions: "The eastern commander requested four seats, so quietly she almost asked him to repeat himself."
- **C017 — Redesigning the arrangement vs. removing the villain:** GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s conflicts resolve by exposing and defeating an accountable person or force — Sable arrested, the founders' crimes exposed, the blight answered. GPT-6 Astra (high)'s climaxes are refusals of the available heroic option followed by a redesign of the arrangement itself, and the text flags the insufficiency of cleverness. GPT-5.6 Sol (high) asks who is guilty; GPT-6 Astra (high) asks what structure produced the harm.
  - Story 0364, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > To restore the arches would restore their hunger, and tomorrow someone else would have to surrender the work of living.
    Mara rejects the prompt's implicit teleology — orchards belong against the sky — because restoring beauty would perpetuate the extraction economy; her "Sideways" produces terraces and drainage along existing shafts, an infrastructural rather than adversarial resolution. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s 0364 keeps the economy and finds a better payer, then upgrades the cosmos into gates.
- **C012 — The administrative climax:** GPT-6 Astra (high) repeatedly locates its climactic action in acts of record-keeping and self-registration—laying out accounts with per-meal costs, supplying a birth certificate, writing passengers' names into a chart—making governance and documentation dramatic. This is an unusual strength standard rubrics have poor vocabulary for: the decisive gesture in GPT-6 Astra (high) is often bureaucratic, and the drama comes from a character finally signing her own name. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s climaxes are speech-acts and reconciliations instead.
  - Story 0161, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > She supplied the concert program, her mother's letter, and her own birth certificate, fetched from the lining of her coat.
    > Under the seller's name she wrote the name her mother had sung.
    The trust arc resolves not in an embrace but in paperwork: producing identity documents is the story's bravest act, and the signature is the emotional payoff.
  - Story 0343, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > In the space reserved for her discovery's grand title, she wrote the passengers' names, checking every spelling aloud.
    The renunciation of personal glory is enacted as cartography—an entry in a form—rather than stated as a lesson; the administrative act carries the theme.
- **C049 — Consent choreography as climax:** GPT-6 Astra (high) repeatedly resolves ethical crises as a physical offer that can be refused, with the injured party's freely-taken acceptance completing the action. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s climactic transactions are verbal agreements and symbolic renunciations in which sincerity is declared and the mechanism ratifies it.
  - Story 0105, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > Nessa nodded, but pulled away at the first touch; Mara waited until her daughter reached for her hand.
    The beat contains consent given, consent withdrawn, and consent re-offered by the injured party, costing the protagonist control at the exact moment most writers would spend it. The same grammar recurs in GPT-6 Astra (high), 0300: she "laid her master key where the girl could reach it," making the rescue contingent on a stranger's unforced choice.
- **C058 — Priced wonder:** GPT-6 Astra (high) budgets the fantastic: forgetting is a municipal service with installment payments, salvation is limited by a six-orchid basket, a good deed costs a fee, an engraving wheel, and weeks of evenings. Because everything has a price, restraint and generosity become measurable acts. GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s institutions wield enormous power with vague means, so victories — library saved, atrium preserved, prisoners freed — arrive without a bill.
  - Story 0159, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > The city below had paid to forget those people, and then paid Mara to keep forgetting from becoming complete.  Now it refused the second payment.
    The entire premise — and its collapse — is expressed as an invoice; oblivion is a purchased service and memory a lapsed subscription. The economics generate the plot's moral problem without any villain.
  - Story 0167, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > It would cost her the new engraving wheel she had ordered, and several weeks of evenings.
    Sera's choice to honor the "worthless" panes is cashed out in a specific forgone tool and specific hours; generosity is dramatized as accounting, which is what makes it credible rather than gestural.
- **C056 — Gifts relinquished versus gifts confirmed:** GPT-6 Astra (high) treats the supernatural advantage as an evasion of ordinary attention: the hearing of unspoken words must be destroyed so its owner will finally ask; the ashes are scattered by a failing hand that accepts help; the reckonings are allowed to end.     The cost of collective agency is the heroine's private power, stated plainly before she acts. The story's final beat completes the exchange: "She asked whether he was hungry, and this time left room for the answer" — asking replaces hearing.

GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s social world is composed of factions, laws and public reversals, and its register stays uniformly solemn regardless of the prompt's specified tone.
  - Story 0129, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > Neri ate his bread on the steps and tried not to mistake kindness for a welcome.

    > Iona laughed so hard she misplaced north.

GPT-5.6 Sol (high)'s climaxes are articulations — the confession spoken from the ladder, the moral pronounced by the rescued mentor — after which the plot ratifies the words.     > "That is the duplicate," he says, "not either of you."
    Here GPT-5.6 Sol (high) is at full strength: a child reassigns the legal category to the document, resolving a custody question conceptually rather than sentimentally.

    > Music had not returned to the world, yet beneath the flowering glass, silence had learned to carry it.
    The same writer's lyrical mode closes on cadence and consolation; the orchids' opening is emblematic rather than caused, and no one has been asked to change their mind.

The habit guarantees element visibility and intellectual legibility, but it performs the reader's interpretive work and keeps the narration in summary mode even at climaxes.     > Among sanctuary workers, the span between beginning and finishing a confession was known as "across sharing a secret," because truth changed both speaker and listener while passing between them.

GPT-6 Astra (high) refuses the consolation the story earned; GPT-5.6 Sol (high) cashes it in as statement — a poetics difference in which GPT-6 Astra (high)'s withdrawal is the rarer, riskier discipline.
  - Story 0210, `gpt-6-astra-high`:
    > she took his hand, not to prove they were safe, but because the stones were wet.
    The narration explicitly voids the metaphorical reading of the gesture (safety, reunion) and replaces it with a material reason; the story dismantles the symbol it spent its length building, and the final clause is a floor condition, not a meaning.
