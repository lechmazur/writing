# Grok 4.7 (high) vs. Grok 4.6 (high): comparative writing analysis

Focus model: `grok-4.7-high`. Comparison model: `grok-4.6-high`. Direct-comparison scope: `comparator_v2_eval_v4_grok47_mimo26_predecessors_20260927_n50_cap3`.

## In one paragraph

Grok 4.7 usually turns a short-fiction prompt into a problem the characters must work through; Grok 4.6 more often moves the required ingredients toward success and explains what that success means. In a story about recovering cultural memory, Grok 4.7 makes the investigator separate witnesses and distrust suspiciously perfect agreement, rather than simply announcing that truth has emerged. [C001] The difference extends to endings. Its charitable broker leaves a personal photograph with its owner, and its mortician preserves a particular remnant without promising that nothing is lost; Grok 4.6 more readily makes recognition and preservation uncomplicated achievements. [C008] Grok 4.7 also gives grief less flattering contours: a lost marriage includes an argument the couple might have survived, and a would-be healer partly wants suffering to remain interesting. [C025] The result is generally more particular, less self-congratulating fiction. This is a clear advantage, not dependable mastery: Grok 4.7 can collapse into repetitive ingredient-list narration, and in such cases Grok 4.6’s straightforward storytelling can be more coherent. [C051]

## Comparative portrait

Throughout, **Grok 4.7** and **Grok 4.6** refer to their **high** settings.

The central difference is what happens **after the required ingredients enter the fiction**. Grok 4.6 more often names an attribute, motivation, method, and desired outcome, then narrates their successful execution. Grok 4.7 more often makes those ingredients constrain one another: a method requires verification, an occupation shapes perception, or a benevolent goal creates a question about what the protagonist may legitimately preserve. This is a **recurring, panel-supported finding**, supported by all three critics across different stories—not a claim of unanimous preference on every story. [C001][C003][C008][C014]

At its strongest, Grok 4.7 makes discovering the solution change the problem. Preserving a haunting can imprison its subject; recovering cultural memory requires continued interpretation; helping someone does not necessarily entitle the helper to publicize their photograph. Grok 4.6 more readily treats discovery as authorization for a successful intervention. Its advantage is accessibility: actions, symbolic correspondences, and before-and-after effects are often easier to identify, even when the causal or emotional work between them is abbreviated. [C002][C007][C008][C042]

The supplied quantitative context supports a plainly lopsided overall conclusion: across **50 matched pairs and 300 evaluator judgments**, Grok 4.7 records **41 wins, no ties, and nine losses** at the stated ±0.5 threshold. The mean margin is **+2.770**, with a supplied story-level 95% normal confidence-interval half-width of **0.640**. These results do not make its advantage uniform. Several readings identify abrupt failures in which its more distinctive manner gives way to repetitive, weakly organized compliance prose. [C035][C051][C058]

The comparison is therefore not “inventive but unreliable” versus “equally valuable plainness.” Grok 4.7 is usually the stronger fiction writer in this evidence. Grok 4.6 retains specific advantages in procedural legibility, some interpersonal scenes, and particular constraints; those are genuine local strengths, not a compensating overall ceiling. [C007][C029][C030][C047][C058]

## Does either model seem smarter?

**Grok 4.7 more often seems smarter through its writing choices.** This is an impression of narrative judgment, not a measurement of general intelligence. Its strongest evidence can be separated into several kinds:

- **Causal and structural foresight:** a sloping arcade floor, established through drifting popcorn, makes a later coincidence possible; a father’s authored trail gives an interpretive journey a retrospectively intelligible design. These details prepare consequences rather than merely accompany them. [C019][C036]
- **Psychological inference:** a lost marriage includes “the argument they would have survived,” while a would-be healer partly wants suffering to remain interesting. Sympathy does not prevent the narration from identifying compromised motives. [C025]
- **Conceptual compression:** the undertaker’s work and the underwater archive become versions of the same problem—giving the unreceived dead an address. Several requirements acquire one governing relationship rather than separate explanations. [C014]
- **Relevant-detail selection and constraint synthesis:** the broker notices omissions through the categories of cargo and accounting; the musician’s attachment to a beautiful pattern becomes the obstacle she must overcome to release a ghost. Profession and premise determine attention and action. [C012][C042]
- **Restraint:** leaving a photograph with its owner or declining to capture every remaining signal makes a limit consequential. Modest language alone would not establish the same judgment. [C008]
- **Reader trust:** Grok 4.7 sometimes lets decisive action coexist with incomplete knowledge. But it also tells readers, through polished corrective definitions, exactly what an event means. It trusts readers more with unresolved outcomes than consistently with unresolved interpretation. [C027][C043][C045]

These distinctions should not be confused with verbosity or polish. The supplied word-count means are 763.6 for Grok 4.7 and 698.7 for Grok 4.6; the reported correlation between length difference and margin is +0.208. Neither statistic identifies why a passage works. The stronger textual evidence concerns whether a detail changes what can happen, not how many details are supplied. [C019][C030]

There are also clear reversals. Grok 4.6 provides more legible instrument adjustments in 0083, tighter handling of the suspended breath in 0396, and more concrete social perception in 0279. Conversely, Grok 4.7’s mathematical language in 0083 is an integrated metaphor, not a demonstrated scientific model. Neither technical terminology nor elegant analogy establishes scientific understanding. [C029][C030][C047]

## Narrative reasoning and aesthetic judgment

**Mechanism matters, but literal mechanism is not always the artistic objective.** In 0098, separate witness accounts and imperfect agreement make verification part of the action. In 0152, ordinary obligations and routines form a counter-conspiracy. Those are stronger demonstrations of narrative reasoning than simply announcing that a vision revealed the answer. Yet the latter story’s outcome remains offstage, and its prearranged helpers can also look conveniently available. The supported advantage is a more articulated causal middle, not freedom from authorial contrivance. [C001][C006]

Grok 4.7’s symbolic reasoning is strongest when an interpretation imposes a cost. In 0113, understanding the ghost’s captivity obliges the musician to damage the pattern she treasures. In 0012, the priest’s rhetoric attacks the invader’s assumption that stillness means available space. These are not merely more ornate explanations: the distinction identifies what must change. Grok 4.6’s sabotage discovery in 0113 adds historical information, but its connection to the liberating vibration remains less developed. [C042]

Occupational attention is another important discriminator. Grok 4.7’s broker thinks through lists and carried goods; its mortician preserves through cosmetic work rather than treating the two professions as consecutive duties. Grok 4.6 nevertheless supplies useful occupational specifics, including the harbor’s tuner and winch operator. The difference is not detail versus no detail, but whether the profession helps organize the story’s thought. [C008][C012]

Aesthetic restraint deserves conditional rather than automatic credit. Grok 4.7’s photograph custody and limited preservation change conduct; its qualified definitions of forgiveness can instead make accountability seem settled by analogy. In 0301, both versions compress the relationship between wrongdoing, discovery, and repair. Both also associate stopping medication with recovered honesty without substantially examining that association. These are shared weaknesses in represented moral reasoning, despite different prose styles. [C008][C011]

Nor does an open ending necessarily preserve reader freedom. Grok 4.7 may leave the future undecided while supplying an authoritative explanation of the present. Grok 4.6’s explicit morals and Grok 4.7’s elegant corrections can perform the same supervisory function: they instruct the reader how to value the event. The latter is often better compressed, but compression is not itself greater openness. [C027][C045][C061]

## Recurring and repetitive writing habits

**Counting rule:** recurrence below is deduplicated by writer and story. Packet-local fractions are retained only where every story checked is identified. Supporting examples elsewhere establish recurrence without establishing a corpus-wide rate; **frequency was not measured beyond the stated local audits**. Repeated discussion of 0098, for example, remains evidence from one story.

**Grok 4.6: optional framing becomes a compositional skeleton.** The most securely recurrent habit is not using the required concepts, but announcing their assignment functions. One complete five-story check found standalone prompt-element beats in **5/5** stories: 0029, 0098, 0152, 0307, and 0337. “She was cautiously hopeful” isolates an attribute; “Her true motivation was” introduces purpose; “The timeframe of her work” exposes a structural slot. Across different briefs, scene-making repeatedly pauses for an explanation of what the story is fulfilling. [C003]

There is also verified **repeated wording**, distinct from that broader architecture. “Motivation burned clear” appears in 0012 and 0274. A separate complete audit finds the exact string “motivation burned” in **4/5** checked stories—0012, 0266, 0292, and 0341—with 0091 the examined nonmatch. “The core concept of” recurs in 0113 and 0274. These discretionary openings are stronger evidence of a writer habit than repeated prompt-mandated nouns. The overlapping audits must not be added together as independent occurrences. [C044][C060]

**Literal phrase preservation creates a second, distinguishable problem.** In 0371, Grok 4.6 retains “bread from vanished bakery” without the natural article and places “after the birds fly south” inside past-tense narration. Grok 4.7 supplies “the vanished bakery on Linden Street” and conjugates “flew.” In 0060, Grok 4.6 repeatedly reapplies “defiant hush” and “sidereal patience,” while 0241 cycles through its assigned compound attributes. The reading effect is incantatory but often static: words return without acquiring a new function. This is prompt-dependent repetition whose optional frequency and grammatical handling—not initial inclusion—are the relevant habits. [C015][C037]

**Sentence and paragraph rhythm are repetitive, but not invariant.** Grok 4.6 often follows a main action with a participial modifier or explanatory rationale: producing the dental mirror is followed by its disk “catching” light, and weaving light by “employing the method.” Elsewhere it uses short declarative stacks and role-led introductions, including Elias as oracle in 0114 and artist in 0155. These different surfaces can still produce the same steady explanatory pace. However, 0251’s clipped “He would not shout. / He would arrange beauty that asked questions” contrasts with 0241’s dense summary blocks. The evidence does not justify calling every sentence or paragraph identical. [C016][C041][C051]

**At story level, completion receives repeated certification.** A fully checked set—0018, 0091, 0157, 0165, and 0191—has success-certifying closes in **5/5** Grok 4.6 stories. “The motivation fulfilled” and “creativity had triumphed” give different plots the same final posture. Other stories repeat faint smiles after success, packing equipment, or readiness for further work. These codas offer orientation and ceremonial settling, but can flatten distinct conflicts into an identical assurance that the protagonist’s purpose was justified. [C021][C045][C052]

A related solution pattern is **outward benevolence without much reciprocal resistance**. In 0102, healing descends over unsuspecting passersby; elsewhere secondary people frequently serve as beneficiaries, helpers, or witnesses. That should not become a claim that Grok 4.6 cannot stage relationships: the father-daughter reunion in 0091 has a visible beneficiary and mechanism, and 0279 lets a community actively share endangered memories. Nor are its endings invariably happy: the spinner in 0363 dies. What recurs is certification of meaning, not compulsory survival. [C048][C063][C047][C013]

**Grok 4.7: corrective syntax is the clearest flexible signature.** Its repeated move rejects an initial interpretation and replaces it: “It was not despair. / It was illumination that refused to flatter”; “Grounding…was not the end of sorrow. / It was the end of circling.” A complete local check finds this broader definition-by-negation construction in **5/5** stories—0018, 0091, 0157, 0165, and 0191—though its function ranges from decorative contrast to the thematic turn. The device is not exclusive to Grok 4.7; Grok 4.6 uses related constructions too. [C005][C021]

There are smaller lexical recurrences within that signature. Exact “not triumph” appears in 0130 and 0133. The related restraint beats “She did not hurry” in 0085 and “He does not hurry. Haste would feed the curse” in 0296 are variants, not one identical repeated phrase. These are documented cross-story examples, not a measured overall frequency. They calibrate emotion effectively, but can make unrelated protagonists sound as though they share one philosophical speaker. [C055]

**Short negated beats and sensory clauses govern paragraph rhythm.** “He did not,” “Nets dripped,” and “Its roof was patched tin” provide pauses, reversals, and material anchors in different stories. The same vertical, emphatic presentation can become monotonous; in 0155 it accompanies wooden inventory rather than controlled escalation. Shortness and line breaks are therefore a reusable instrument, not evidence of craft by themselves. [C016][C051]

**Casting and backstory also recur.** Both models reuse exact names: one complete local check finds Grok 4.6’s Elara in 0029, 0152, and 0337, and Grok 4.7’s Mira in 0098 and 0152. Another finds Mara in **4/5** Grok 4.7 stories—0060, 0154, 0241, and 0251—with unnamed 0036 the nonmatch. Shared naming in the same briefs makes this a low-stakes default-pool finding, not proof of interchangeable characterization. [C004][C039]

More consequentially, Grok 4.7 repeatedly favors contemplative specialists whose present task encounters a prior failure or compromised attachment: the fabricated burial in 0085, the druid’s own concealed wrongdoing in 0296, and the conductor’s delegated emotional labor in 0318. These examples support a recurrent self-implicating backstory pattern; frequency was not measured. A minority reading also identifies a narrower solitary-artisan template across its local set, a concern supported by 0387’s failure to develop an interpersonal scene. [C053][C025][C034]

**Its characteristic ending preserves a limit or obligation**, rather than simply refusing closure. The mortician does not pursue every signal; the archivist must defend the correction against the next upload; the record-keeper acts without knowing who has arrived. This can be a flexible signature because the retained difficulty differs. Likewise, quiet has opposite functions in 0012 and 0113: one story defends a boundary, while the other must disrupt a valued suspension. Recurrent hushed imagery does not make those stories interchangeable. [C008][C028][C043][C042]

Across this model lineage, then, **prompt-labeling, interpretive reassurance, stock naming, and earnest specialist protagonists persist**. Grok 4.7 more often weakens the checklist’s control and makes corrective antithesis and bounded resolution more prominent. Neither device is wholly new or exclusive. The newer model still falls back into its predecessor’s exposed scaffolding, notably in 0285, 0396, and its more severe failures. This is an uneven redistribution of habits, not automatic improvement in every compositional choice. [C004][C026][C027][C035]

## Range and adaptability

**The demonstrated advantage is broader adaptation within a limited territory—not unrestricted versatility.** Both models principally write protagonist-centered, third-person speculative fiction. Grok 4.7’s they/them focal character in 0130 broadens the represented protagonist choices, but does not establish a new narrative person or deep social-perspective range. First-person fiction, sustained ensemble narration, and radical formal experimentation remain untested rather than failed. [C041][C056][C063]

**Grok 4.7 changes the source of narrative pressure.** Its strongest contrasts include institutional near-realism in 0029, comic verification procedures in 0098, distributed intrigue in 0152, and a restrictive speech bargain in 0337. These are not merely different settings: budgets and workplace authority, civic protocols, anonymous cooperation, and ritual conditions organize action differently. The dry humor of taxes for verified miracles is a demonstrated comic register, though not evidence of sustained comedy across a whole different form. [C006]

Its emotional and sensory modes also vary. The impossible archive in 0100 uses contradictory spaces and records to embody an undertaker’s work; 0371 locates civic loss in a bread heel whose caraway scent is being preserved. The practical mending in 0285 lowers pressure and scale, while 0304 sustains a terminal scene of limited care as the aurora fails. These differences concern pacing, attention, and the possible ending—not just the abundance of imagery. [C017][C028][C012]

There is some enacted dialogue and structural variety: the two-person exchange in 0165 uses dry comic timing; 0157 makes spoken instructions part of a political code; 0241’s letter retrospectively reframes the journey; 0251 becomes a synchronized communal display that permits a dark window and a tilted card. Nevertheless, the narrator’s corrective, earnest manner often survives these changes. Grok 4.7 adapts its task and architecture more widely than it transforms its speaking voice. [C023][C036][C041][C062]

**Grok 4.6’s range is narrower, but “the same story with different nouns” is too absolute.** Its investigative ghost story in 0113 includes a deliberate test of the image-producing medium. Its 0396 negotiates between orchard inhabitants and grieving ice, organizing a proposed arrangement within a suspended reply. Its harbor story notices specific labor rather than relying entirely on generalized kindness. These are real changes of procedure, conflict, and social attention, even when their execution remains explanatory. [C046][C029][C012]

Its relationships and endings also vary meaningfully. In 0279, the endangered lullabies give an intervention a particular human occasion, and the community becomes active rather than remaining an audience for rescue. In 0387, an encounter over tea and net-mending gives the philosophy a social use. At the opposite emotional poles, 0089 grants reunion while 0363 permits death and permanent desolation. That last contrast is responsive to differing briefs, but still disproves an invariably comforting emotional range. [C047][C034][C013]

The limitation is that these adaptations often return to a familiar narratorial stance: explain the purpose, undertake a task, interpret the discovery, certify its value. Even a celestial extinction premise in 0304 becomes a continuing professional mission. Grok 4.6 can vary sentence shape, as 0251 shows, and attempt humor, as 0133 shows; neither exception establishes a consistently different voice. Its variation is more evident in circumstances, practical procedures, and beneficiaries than in tonal or formal reinvention. [C012][C041][C054][C056]

A **well-supported minority reading** favors Grok 4.6’s social and structural flexibility within read_06: communal history, commercial context, and the fisherwoman scene contrast with Grok 4.7’s solitary artisans. That finding should survive synthesis as a local reversal. It cannot support a global claim that Grok 4.7 resists social imagination, given its dialogue-driven political fable, arcade encounter, and institutional stories elsewhere. [C034][C023][C006]

Finally, attempted range must be separated from successful execution. Grok 4.7’s 0396 genuinely changes toward an optimistic partnership chronicle, but loses the brief’s temporal discipline. In 0312, both writers settle into generic fantasy phrasing and repeated keywords. The evidence suggests **prompt-dependent adaptability**, not a demonstrated inability to write fantasy: these are failures under particular combinations of requirements, alongside successful speculative modes elsewhere. [C029][C018][C006]

## Floor versus ceiling

**Overall consistency favors Grok 4.7 in the supplied results; catastrophic-case stability sometimes favors Grok 4.6.** Those are compatible observations. Forty-one aggregate wins establish that Grok 4.7’s advantage is not confined to a few exceptional peaks. The local readings nevertheless document failures in which it loses sentence control, causal development, or both—especially 0155, 0387, and 0341. Grok 4.6 supplies a more coherent baseline in those comparisons. [C051][C035][C058]

Grok 4.7’s strong performances are distinguished by different accomplishments: evidentiary practice in 0098, physical preparation and comic timing in 0165, bounded care in 0304, and compromised grief in 0318. Its ceiling is therefore not one especially beautiful version of a fixed atmospheric trick. The claim remains conditional, because the same writer can produce an unearned revelation or an exposed completion coda. [C001][C019][C008][C025][C027]

Grok 4.6’s local strengths should not be inflated into a rival exceptional ceiling. In 0341, personal bereavement and the atmosphere-as-spool analogy make it clearly stronger than Grok 4.7’s degraded version, but the ledger explicitly characterizes the achievement as competent rather than excellent. Its clearer chronology in 0396 also ends by promising accord before the other parties have accepted it. These are consequential wins with remaining weaknesses. [C058][C029]

One disagreement is especially important: read_04 calls Grok 4.7’s 0018 the weakest story in that local set, whereas existing judging favors it by **+2.050** and values the temporary recreation and damaged remainder. The defensible conclusion is that conspicuous checklist prose can coexist with a meaningful ending—not that the low-floor diagnosis or the favorable judgment must erase the other. [C024][C021]

There is **no extension cohort**. All supplied stories belong to one cohort, and the final two cross-read packets revisit earlier material. Their agreement corroborates interpretations of those stories; it does not replicate the findings in new stories or add occurrences to habit counts. [C059][C065][C066][C067]

## What each model still does better

**Grok 4.7 more often:**
- Builds a method whose steps and constraints matter to the outcome. [C001][C019][C036]
- Makes profession, object, and theme participate in one governing conception. [C012][C014]
- Gives sympathetic protagonists motives that do not wholly flatter them. [C025][C053]
- Distinguishes useful intervention from complete rescue, ownership, or final certainty. [C008][C043][C059]
- Changes scene pressure and social organization while retaining a recognizable voice. [C006][C023][C046]

**Grok 4.6 retains narrower, concrete advantages:**
- It often makes immediate consequence and symbolic completion easier to follow, especially in 0029 and 0337. [C007]
- It can provide more explicit procedural sequencing, although scientific-looking steps are not causal proof. [C030]
- It handles the organizing time constraint more tightly in 0396. [C029]
- In 0279 and 0387, it supplies actual people and shared activity where Grok 4.7 largely interprets alone. [C047][C034]
- Its conventional narration can remain usable when Grok 4.7’s more distinctive control breaks down. [C051][C058]

These strengths justify particular preferences, not a requirement to balance the overall verdict. Grok 4.6’s accessibility is most valuable when it clarifies an action or beneficiary; it is least valuable when “clarity” consists of repeatedly declaring success. [C007][C045][C063]

## Findings beyond the existing judging

**Corroborated, not newly discovered:** the central contrast between constraint integration and checklist exposition already appears throughout existing judging. The cross-read claims reinforce specific interpretations—verification in 0098, bounded victory in 0012 and 0292, corrective syntax, and prompt-category labeling—but they revisit the same stories. They strengthen confidence in those readings without expanding recurrence. [C059][C065][C066][C067]

**New at the comparative-synthesis level:** isolated judgments become a clearer account of reusable verbal machinery. Grok 4.6’s “motivation burned” packaging, Grok 4.7’s “not triumph” correction, and both models’ name recycling are patterns visible through comparison across briefs. They are not newly discovered story events, and naming remains much weaker evidence of craft than repeated plot or interpretive structure. [C060][C055][C004][C039]

A second synthesis-level finding is that **open outcomes and open interpretation are separate properties**. Grok 4.7 can leave a future uncertain while making its moral interpretation emphatically certain. Grok 4.6 can provide fuller closure while occasionally respecting another person’s choice. This is more informative than sorting the writers into “ambiguous” and “simplistic.” [C027][C043][C045]

A third is that **social breadth and social perception can diverge**. Grok 4.6 sometimes includes more beneficiaries and communal history, but those people may remain passive recipients. Grok 4.7’s narrower encounter can generate more reciprocal friction; elsewhere its solitude leaves another person’s needs insufficiently attended. The correct unit of judgment is what the relationship changes, not the size of the cast. [C034][C048][C063]

Finally, the difference in endings is better described as **allocation of loss** than optimism versus darkness. Grok 4.6’s spinner dies but receives acknowledgment; Grok 4.7’s survives without her name. Both make a sacrifice meaningful, but preserve different things. Neither darkness nor survival independently establishes greater seriousness. [C013]

## Disagreements and limitations

- **Prompt dependence:** the briefs repeatedly specify unusual occupations, objects, quiet atmospheres, abstract concepts, and motives. Their recurrence is not automatically self-chosen repetition. Optional framing, grammatical handling, and repeated resolution strategies provide firmer evidence. [C003][C015][C044]
- **Surface-cue sensitivity:** antithesis, fragments, material detail, and lyrical confidence can make Grok 4.7 seem more controlled than its underlying action warrants. Technical sequencing and explicit closure can similarly flatter Grok 4.6. Both require inspection of what the mechanism or distinction actually does. [C005][C027][C030]
- **Minority interpretations remain local:** Grok 4.6’s wider social canvas in read_06 and its practical advantages elsewhere qualify—but do not overturn—the broader adaptation advantage. Likewise, read_04’s lower-floor assessment should not become a measured claim about the entire distribution. [C034][C024][C047]
- **A conflicting lexical audit cannot support an absence claim:** C054 says Grok 4.7 never uses “motivation” in its five-story set, but C060 supplies that word in its quoted 0341 passage. The repeated positive evidence for Grok 4.6’s labeling remains usable; the claimed complete absence in Grok 4.7 does not. [C054][C060]
- **Interpersonal depth remains limited:** named relations, guilt histories, or sympathetic children can add specificity without producing sustained disagreement between independently realized people. The conductor’s psychological insight is largely narrated; the girl and elder in 0279 remain lightly individualized. [C025][C047][C063]
- **Causes of failure are speculative:** a prompt-format trigger, genre trap, or dependence on a familiar allegorical anchor may explain particular collapses, but the supplied readings do not experimentally isolate those causes. The supported finding is the recurrence of breakdown under some briefs, not a verified account of the model mechanism behind it. [C018][C035][C058]

The supplied disagreement and order-sensitivity statistics also caution against treating a close individual result as final: mean evaluator disagreement is **0.561**, and mean AB/BA order sensitivity is **0.841**. These quantitative limitations coexist with the substantial overall preference; they do not justify either unanimity claims or dismissal of the aggregate result.

## Representative case studies

**0098 — Verification becomes worldbuilding.** Grok 4.7 separates witnesses, finds imperfect agreement reassuring, and makes cultural memory depend on continued interpretation. Its civic humor grows from the same system: miracles belong in municipal procedure. Grok 4.6 offers a more directly communal restoration but relies more heavily on naming its investigative qualities. This case connects causal reasoning, comic register, and the limits of success without making them interchangeable criteria. [C001][C002][C003][C006]

**0165 — A detail earns a coincidence.** Grok 4.7 establishes the floor’s tilt through “watching dropped popcorn drift”; the subsequent movement therefore has a prepared physical basis. Grok 4.6 invokes “Calculations based on crowd density and wave timing,” followed by “As expected she approached.” The difference is visible preparation versus asserted prediction, not merely one version having more elaborate prose. [C019]

**0128 and 0304 — Care changes what the helper may claim.** The photograph remains with its owner in Grok 4.7’s harbor story. Its mortician redirects preservation into pigment and gem, then declines to chase every remaining signal. Grok 4.6 favors more comprehensive recognition and preservation, although anonymous publication in 0128 is an important counterexample to a simple publicity-versus-consent contrast. [C008]

**0083 and 0318 — Loss does not certify virtue.** Grok 4.7’s imagined marriage contains a survivable argument; its conductor has previously let someone else finish the caring work and partly wants hurt to remain interesting. These are psychological selections, not proof that its magical science or interpersonal dramatization is equally strong. Grok 4.6’s more explicit scientific procedure in 0083 remains a separate local merit. [C025][C030]

**0279 — Grok 4.6 gives perception an object.** Its artist notices a girl facing the loss of her mother’s lullabies and changes a communal practice. Grok 4.7 instead invokes how imaginary gatherings might react. The reversal is not a reward for sentiment alone: specific attachment leads to intervention and observable participation, though the community’s acceptance remains unusually easy. [C047]

**0387 — Solitary lyricism fails to become a scene.** Grok 4.6 introduces a fisherwoman and shared work; Grok 4.7 waves the whisk through the air and closes by explaining hidden effort. The contrast supports a local advantage in practical and social adaptation, while the fisherwoman’s thin characterization limits how far that advantage should be extended. [C034][C035]

**0341 — A genuine reversal, not a rival peak.** Grok 4.6 gives atmospheric remembrance a specific bereavement and an articulated spool analogy. Grok 4.7 repeats the required concepts in degraded syntax and circular confirmation. Yet both overstate what their observations prove. The case establishes Grok 4.6’s superior coherence here, not exceptional achievement equal to Grok 4.7’s strongest work elsewhere. [C058][C061]

## Cited claim ledger

- **C001 — Operational rather than asserted causality:** Grok 4.7 (high) more often builds outcomes from visible procedures, prior arrangements, evidence checks, and socially distributed actions. Grok 4.6 (high) sometimes moves from an initial cause to an announced success while omitting the intermediate mechanism. The distinction is substantive causality, not merely greater complication.
  - Story 0152, `grok-4.7-high`:
    > The note she had left under the basin would now send the washer to the night porter with word of a loose hinge.
    > The porter, who owed Mira an old story, would leave one side door unbarred out of stubborn courtesy.
    The counter-conspiracy operates through identifiable people, obligations, and physical consequences. The coup is resisted by a chain rather than by a single miraculous insight.
  - Story 0098, `grok-4.7-high`:
    > She separated them and took accounts one by one.
    > The match was imperfect, which comforted her.
    > Perfect agreement would have smelled like rehearsal.
    Mira’s success depends on an explicit verification method. Her preference for imperfect agreement shows epistemic sophistication rather than passive acceptance of the magic.
  - Story 0152, `grok-4.6-high`:
    > But her words, carried far by the acoustics of echoed silence, reached the ears of a loyal guard standing watch who paused in surprise to listen more closely.
    Grok 4.6 (high) establishes only that one guard hears Elara’s apparently mad warning. The narrative does not show how that hearing produces an effective countermeasure.
  - Story 0152, `grok-4.6-high`:
    > The coup would falter, not by sword or force but by the quiet revelation of truth spread through seemingly mad words.
    The outcome is forecast in abstract terms. Compared with Grok 4.7 (high)’s staged chain, the causal middle remains largely asserted.
- **C003 — Standalone prompt-element beats:** Grok 4.6 (high) recurrently places required attributes, motivations, methods, and timeframes in separate explanatory sentences, often repeating the brief’s exact wording.
