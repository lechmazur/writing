While forests hold elections, Mara climbs a lavender bluff under the aurora to taste the rain. [16] The village calls her its luminous skeptic: her surveyor grafts glow blue, but she believes nothing she cannot measure. [35] Home from orbit, she carries her dead father's tin compass ring, determined to learn why some people carry weather with them. [56] He carried drizzle for thirty years, and ate her birthday suppers on the porch rather than dampen the candles. [75] At sixteen she called his weather embarrassing; after she left for orbit, his letters mentioned only clear skies. [93]

Below the bluff, voting trees release pollen into collectors left over from the century when machines still counted ballots. [112] Root networks tally each grove's preferences, but delegates must combine their votes to choose where walking seed nurseries will settle before the coastline moves again. [137] Three elections have ended without agreement, and the next storm tide will drown the oldest grove. [153] Mara has come for a private answer, but the grove's ranger intercepts her beside the abandoned launch pavilion. [171]

"Your father was our western delegate," says Ivo, whose shoulders shed snow despite the mild evening. [187] "Without him, every count stops short." [193]

Mara shows him the ring, a cheap band with a shallow dish where she always assumed a needle had broken. [213] Ivo tips a drop of meltwater into it and tells her to touch it with her tongue. [230] She tastes pennies, then green apples, sharply enough to remember her father's coat dripping beside the stove. [247]

"Acids from the trees," Ivo says, offering his scanner when she frowns. [259] "The ring concentrates them, and your grafts can distinguish what instruments average away." [272]

He had offered to teach her the ring, but she wanted coordinates, not another of his lessons about paying attention. [292] She checks: the scanner reports identical acidity where her tongue detects three distinct changes, each matching a distant pollen pulse. [312] Above the pavilion door, flaking letters promise TERRESTRIALLY COSMIC, the slogan of engineers who brought starship ecologies home to Earth. [332] Their forests needed mobile interpreters, whose grafts spent decades cultivating the microbes that gathered moisture and seeded each grove's distinctive clouds. [353] Mara remembers the warm recycling pipes aboard her first survey ship, their patient imitation of a world. [370]

"I thought the rain was just something wrong with him," she says, watching snow dissolve on Ivo's sleeve. [388]

"He thought you wanted a dry life," Ivo answers. [397]

At his signal, other delegates climb the bluff, bringing warm mist, hail, and a wind that smells of river mud. [417] As their small weathers converge, Mara tastes the ring after every shift, marking sourness and sweetness on her survey map. [437] Her magnetic compass wavers under the aurora, but the ring needs neither north nor satellites. [452] No single delegate points anywhere; together, their changing chemistries form an emergent compass, indicating where all their seedlings could survive. [472] Yet the direction keeps swinging seaward, toward land the tides have already taken. [485]

"We're still missing his grove," Ivo says, and Mara almost hands back the ring. [499] Then she remembers her father's habit of turning it inward, keeping its little dish against his skin. [516] Under the rim she finds dried green residue, protected from years of rain. [529]

She wets it and presses the ring against her glowing wrist, where the graft's sampling pores open. [546] For a moment nothing happens; then her mouth fills with the taste of apples stored through winter. [563] Rain beads on her knuckles, carrying a surviving culture from the grove her father could never quite leave. [581] She expects sorrow to follow, some inherited weight, but feels only cold water running beneath her cuff. [598] This was work he carried, not a punishment, and work she can choose to share. The compass steadies inland, toward a lavender-lined rail cutting above the flood line, and Ivo transmits the coordinates. [631] Below them, nursery engines cough awake beneath their moss, hauling young trees toward the route the delegates have made together. [651] Mara stays to verify the count, tasting until every grove's signal holds steady. [664] When Ivo offers her a seat beneath the pavilion awning, she nearly refuses out of an old, familiar courtesy. [683] Instead she sits beside him, sets the ring between them, and lets the rain fall on both their hands. [702]
