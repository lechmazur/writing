After the radio static cleared, Mara unlocked the opal observatory under the snow and listened to the midnight silence inside. [20] Its pale dome had once measured starlight; now it sheltered the city's discarded records, receiving them whenever the winter interference lifted. [41] She counted twenty heartbeats between the door and the archive desk, adding them to the thousands she had counted since her death. [63] The counting never stopped, even beneath thought. [70]

Six weeks earlier, she had escaped a flooded transit tunnel, but a transposed digit on another passenger's death certificate had killed her administratively. [92] Her apartment refused her handprint, her wages ceased, and the appeals terminal advised the deceased to consult their executor. [111] Mara had written that message. [116] She had designed the registry to favor certainty, because uncertain cases demanded clerks the city would not pay. [134] Now she had come to fetch wisdom from failure, although she still hoped wisdom might resemble an excuse. [152]

The observatory kept what the registry discarded: abandoned drafts, failed tests, and the digital ghosts assembled automatically when a citizen died. [173] Some families paid to hear a familiar voice; nobody had planned for a ghost's original to request an interview. [192] Mara entered her old engineering key. [198] The archive recognized its maker, whether or not she officially breathed. [209]

A green lamp woke beside the reader's porcelain basin. [218] Frost had cracked it into pieces. [224] Unlike modern screens, this antique displayed memory through ephemeral patterns in spilled ink, its electrodes gathering pigment into words until surface tension scattered them. [248] The porcelain insulated those delicate fields from the metal desk. [258] Without it, her ghost remained a file she could locate but never read. [271]

She found one broad porcelain shard with its glaze intact and fitted it across the contacts. [287] Then she tipped the ink bottle carefully, making a spill smaller than her palm. [301] Letters surfaced, broke apart, and returned in the wrong order. [311] Her archived self had arrived in fragments when the flooded registry began shutting down. [325] To defragment it, she would have to confirm each recovered passage before the little pool erased it. [342] She settled into the rhythm she already knew: four heartbeats for reading, one for pressing the key. [359]

Outside, snow muffled the dome's slow creaking. [366] Inside, her lost sentences gathered like someone pulling a blanket over cold feet. [379]

First came her public assurances that wrongful deaths were statistically negligible. [390] Then came an unsent report about eleven living people whose appeals the prototype had rejected. [405] She remembered deleting it after the council threatened to cancel the project. [417] She had told herself a flawed service was kinder than no service. [429] In the ink, her ghost finished the thought she had refused to finish. [442]

"Kinder to whom?" [445]

Mara missed a keystroke, and the words dissolved. [453] She recovered them again, counting steadily through the shame. [462]

"I thought certainty would keep people safe," she whispered. [471]

The reconstructed ghost had no new facts, only access to doubts she had once edited out. [487] "You made uncertainty disappear," it answered in ink. [495] "That is not the same thing." [501]

By the time the file was whole, Mara had found the certificate mismatch and the suppressed list of eleven names. [521] Her engineering key could authenticate both for the morning review office. [532] She could submit only her own correction, reclaim her apartment, and call the rest an obsolete testing problem. [550] Publishing the complete archive would restore those other cases to scrutiny, but it would also carry her signature beneath the decision to ignore them. [574]

She watched the ink loosen into darkness. [581]

"Will they forgive me?" she asked. [587]

The shard offered no prophecy. [592] Finally, a sentence from an early design notebook surfaced: Every system needs a way to be told it is wrong. [612] She had known that before she learned to sound certain. [622]

Mara sent the complete file while the radio channel remained clear, then stayed beside the reader until the receipt arrived. [642] The ghost's last words dispersed beneath her resting hand. [651] She washed the shard and set it where the next visitor would find it. [665]

There would be hearings, and people who owed her nothing, and work no apology could replace. [681] For now, under the snow, she counted her heartbeats and waited for morning. [694] They were not forgiveness, but they were time. [702]
