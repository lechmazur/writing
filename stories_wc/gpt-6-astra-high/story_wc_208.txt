After the GPS failed, Nell steered toward the low note of the harbor turbine, a sound she knew through her feet. [21] Behind her skiff, the evacuation ferry waited beyond the shoals, carrying her father and the last families from the flooded outer island. [43] Its captain needed the old entrance beacon; without it, he could not risk the passage. [58] Nell had promised light. [62]

She landed beneath the storm-tossed archway that straddled the beacon steps, dragging ashore a sack of driftwood and her workshop lantern. [83] A sculptor who argued cheerfully with timber, she had offered to replace the dead electric beacon with the lantern's fierce battery lamp. [105] Now she saw the arch's seaward leg shifting with each wave. [116] If it fell, it would take the steps, the lamp, and her promised bearing. [130]

Her father would have known where to brace it. [139] She had spent years rejecting his neat triangles, making complicated sculptures that seemed to defeat gravity. [155] Tonight, complexity looked uncomfortably like a pile of sticks. [164]

Inside the lantern case lay a magic lantern slide she had bought at the harbor salvage auction. [181] Three panes showed the arch in different centuries, with the inscription: What survives is never made alone. [198] She had intended to project it behind her next exhibition, turning local history into atmosphere. [213] With cold fingers, she pushed it into the lamp to test the focus against the masonry. [229]

Three arches shimmered into alignment. [234] Beneath the oldest stood a woman setting stones; beneath the newest, a boy ankle deep in luminous water. [252] Between them stood Nell, holding a uselessly beautiful spiral of wood. [263]

"Your mortar is washing out," the woman said, as clearly as someone leaning across a table. [279] Nell reached for the mason's hand and caught only rain. [289] But when the mason scratched a line onto her stone, it appeared in all three images. [305]

The boy traced a triangle over it. [312] "We still copy these braces, but nobody remembers how you bent the wood without breaking it." [328] His arch was failing too, long after the sea had swallowed the turbine house. [342] Nell understood that the slide offered neither rescue nor prophecy, but a meeting place for people with unfinished work. [361]

"Show me the footing," she told the mason, putting down the spiral. [373] The woman drew two buried sockets where the original builders had anchored their scaffolding. [387] Nell dug with her knife until iron rang beneath it. [397] The boy supplied the bracing pattern his generation had inherited, drawing slowly enough for both women to follow. [415]

Through layered meanings, the lines became a mason's temporary support, a sculptor's wooden joint, and a drowned city's survival craft. [435] These were timewoven alliances, Nell thought, not a single genius reaching backward to correct everybody else. [451] Each knew something the others needed; each was perpetually learning. [461]

Her task was to scaffold complexity with simplicity: two legs, one crosspiece, and a joint anyone could repair. [479] She had to reimagine her driftwood not as a finished sculpture, but as an invitation for other hands. [497] She showed the boy how to follow the grain, how soaked timber bent, and where a cord would bite. [516] The mason abandoned her rigid straight brace and copied Nell's curve around an outcrop of rock. [532]

Waves struck while Nell lashed her triangle into the sockets. [542] Under everything ran the turbine's deep humming, steady enough to work inside, almost a dream. [557] In three centuries, three workers tightened their bindings together. [566]

The arch settled against the brace instead of the sea. [576] Nell pulled the slide clear, swung the bare lamp toward the shoals, and flashed the harbor signal. [593] Beyond the rain, the ferry answered. [599] She watched its lights advance between the rocks while the turbine hummed beneath her ribs. [614]

At dawn her father found her asleep beside the lantern. [624] He studied the brace, then the abandoned spiral, and quietly sat down. [636] When she woke, she asked him to show her a better knot. [648] He took the wet cord and moved close enough that their shoulders touched. [661]
