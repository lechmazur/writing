During the spring thaw, water ticked through the ceiling of the abandoned control room, landing in buckets with different voices. [20] Orren listened before deciding where to put his furnace. [29] A contemplative glassblower, he had spent the winter sketching this room beside his empty bed. [44] He would turn the room into a place where children could ask questions without being laughed at. [61]

The river gates were operated upstream now, but local children still associated this building with examinations that determined who entered skilled trades. [83] Orren's wife, Ada, had failed hers because the examiner mistook her stammer for ignorance. [97] He had married her, learned from her, and never challenged that account in the town records. [113] Since her death, the omission had begun to feel less like cowardice than a debt accruing interest. [130]

He installed transparent booths: each child could consult a teacher privately, while remaining visible enough that no adult could be cruel unseen. [152] For the doors, he salvaged brass catches from the instrument cabinets. [163] Beside each seat he placed a glass cup for written questions. [174] These were his safe zones for inquiry, measured and polished until safety seemed almost a property of glass. [192]

On opening morning, only Nessa came. [198] She was thoughtfully spirited, the sort of child who checked a puddle for beetles before jumping into it. [216] Her mother said she knew every gate mechanism along the river, but had stopped speaking in class. [233]

Orren began dramatically narrating mundane tasks, a habit Ada had invented to make intimidating work ridiculous. [249] "The mighty rag advances upon the empire of dust," he announced, wiping the switchboard. [263] Nessa smiled and offered the conquering rag a bucket. [272]

Then she entered a booth, and her smile disappeared. [281] Her fingers worried the door's smooth inner edge; the catch was outside. [293] Orren had copied the cabinet fittings without imagining himself among their contents. [305]

"It is not locked," he said. [311] She opened her mouth, closed it, and reached for her coat. [322]

In his pocket was Ada's cracked leather bookmark, cut long ago from his first working apron. [338] When a word would not come, she used to lay it across her open book: a request that he hold her place, not finish her thought. [364] He had brought it to keep his place in the new register, a small way of pretending she was helping. [384]

He set it on Nessa's desk and stepped back. [393] "You can use that to keep your turn," he said. [403] Outside, melting ice slid from the gutter and broke with the brisk applause of something easily pleased. [420] Orren resisted filling the silence with another heroic cleaning expedition. [430]

At last Nessa touched the bookmark. [436] "Can I leave before I have an answer?" [444]

He felt his explanation assembling: privacy, supervision, the expensive curves of glass. [456] Then he remembered Ada returning from her examination, and himself asking what she had done wrong. [472] The debt had a face now, and it was not only hers. [484]

"Yes," he said, and removed the door. [491]

Nessa held the screws while he took down the other doors. [502] Together they decided to abandon the booths, not the room. [512] They arranged the glass panels into open bays, then rubbed their lower halves cloudy with wet grit so seated children could choose not to be watched. [538] There would be no seat reserved for the teacher, and the cups would accept questions nobody was obliged to sign. [558]

By afternoon two more children had followed the furnace smoke uphill. [569] Nessa showed them the bookmark and explained that a pause did not mean your turn was over. [586] Orren started to announce the glorious distribution of biscuits, but one child asked why the register had Ada's name on its cover. [608]

He sat where they could see his hands. [616] "She was good at asking questions," he said. [624] Then, without defending himself, he told them about the examination. [634] The telling hurt; the children listened without offering to make it stop. [646] Above them, the old room kept dripping, and Nessa moved a bucket to catch what neither glass nor courage could hold back. [668]
