At the tilt of the earth toward spring, Mara returned to Zephyr Bluff, where mist silvered the snow fences above the harbor. [22] Her face hung in police stations now; being a burned spy meant even buying bread could endanger the baker. [41] She had trusted one familiar voice on the telephone, and two agents had died. [55] Since then, she had mistaken staying alive for making amends. [65]

Yesterday, a parcel from her dead handler's widow had reached her through the laundry. [79] Inside lay his engraved pillbox and five words: The fence is still speaking. [92] She came to find where unused courage waits, because hiding had not brought hers back. [107] If his widow needed something repaired, perhaps she could at least make herself useful. [121]

Mara climbed through a silver hush, hearing only the sea withdrawing beneath the bluff. [135] The fence had been her invention: adjustable wooden shutters that trapped broad or narrow ridges during successive snowfalls. [153] To a patrol, they were ordinary defenses against drifting; from the abandoned weather station, they became codes in accumulated snow. [173] The pillbox's decorative border supplied the key, disguised among engraved waves. [184] She aligned its bars with the fence below and read: ELEVEN PEOPLE, LOWER SHELTER, WINCH BROKEN. [200] Beyond the harbor, a fishing boat showed one hooded lamp. [210] Tonight's low tide would expose the landing beneath the shelter, but the approaching thaw would erase the signals by morning. [230]

At the shelter door, a girl leveled a flare pistol at Mara's chest. [243] She had her grandfather's pale eyes. [249] "You sent him to that checkpoint," she said. [257]

Mara kept her hands open. [262] "I did." [264] She set her pistol on the snow and stepped away. [274]

Only then did the girl vouchsafe the rest: eleven fugitives were waiting underground, and three could not manage the cliff steps. [295] Her grandmother was one of them. [301] The cargo cage was their only way down. [309]

Inside, Mara found the widow sitting beside the winch, counting her remaining heart tablets. [323] "I sent for your hands," she said, before Mara could attempt an apology. [336]

The machine's pawl spring had snapped, leaving nothing to press the locking tooth against the drum. [352] Without it, a loaded cage could run free. [360] Mara remembered the handler calling her prudently daring, back when she had understood that courage required calculations rather than witnesses. [380] She checked the cable strand by strand, tested the anchors, and made the girl hold a lantern over the drum. [400]

Then she opened the pillbox. [405] Its spring steel clasp could be reshaped to press the pawl into place. [418] She would have to tear it from the lid, destroying part of the engraved key. [433] Her fingers hesitated over the little waves he had cut by hand. [445] The widow pushed a screwdriver across the bench. [453] Mara pried the clasp loose. [458]

By the time the replacement caught, the fence outside was shedding its message in soft collapses. [474] She lowered the cage empty, then loaded it with shelter ballast and tested the brake twice. [490] Only after that did she bring the widow aboard. [499] The girl stayed above, working the handle while Mara guided each landing from below. [513] Each time the cage rose, the girl stood alone against the whitening sky, turning the handle where a patrol could see her. [535]

Mara understood then that unused courage had not been waiting somewhere inside her, intact and shining. [551] It had been waiting in the space between a request and an answer, wherever someone still needed another pair of hands. [572]

Once the eleven were safe aboard, the girl descended the steps carrying Mara's pistol and the broken pillbox. [590] She offered the pistol first. [595] Mara shook her head and took the box. [603]

"I can fix the lid," she said. [610]

The girl considered her, then climbed aboard without offering forgiveness. [620] It was not forgiveness Mara had earned. [627] It was a place beside the others, and work still to do. [639]
