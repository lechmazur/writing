After the spoons bent reality, people learned to eat with their fingers and keep their shadows out of sunlight. [19] At noon, anything reflected in a spoon's bowl had turned inside out, including the minds of those holding one. [38] Nera's mother had been stirring soup. [44] Her body survived, but afterward she folded clean towels into cupboards that did not exist and never spoke her daughter's name. [65]

Nera became a budding rune scholar because every physician called this permanent, while one forgotten catalogue called it displacement. [84] She wanted to find where shadows go at noon. [93] Her mother's had vanished with the soup steam. [101]

The catalogue led them to a nocturnal observatory on a floating isle, sailing westward fast enough to remain beneath midnight. [121] Its astronomers had abandoned the stars to tend the consequences of that single, crooked noon. [136] Beneath their telescopes stood hundreds of black glass jars, each labeled with a name and the words sealed awakenings. [155]

The keeper explained that shadows had carried the displaced minds here, drawn toward the last place daylight could not follow. [175] "We caught them before they scattered, but opening a jar lets its occupant pour into whatever living mind it finds." [195] "We lost our first keeper that way." [202]

Nera looked at her mother, who was carefully smoothing a wrinkle in the keeper's sleeve. [217] "Then we give it somewhere else to go," she said. [227]

The keeper brought out a clockwork automaton key, a brass beetle with a hollow tooth beneath its belly. [245] It belonged to an obsolete calibration apparatus, built to join distant points without admitting any light between them. [263] Its manual was written in an older form of the runes Nera had only just begun to understand. [281]

For six nights she tried to translate them as commands, until her mother turned a page upside down. [299] Upside down, the diagrams resembled the knitting patterns her mother once taught her. [312] She had to relearn how to read: not by naming symbols, but by closing loops. [327] Each finished circuit changed the meaning of the marks inside it from separation to return. [342]

Nera worked with a quietly deliberate patience she had never managed in her examinations. [356] By the ninth night, she could wind the beetle and make it follow a chalk circle, stitching its ends together. [376] Inside the circle, two separated drops of ink became one without wetting the paper between them. [392]

"A shadow and its owner might do the same," she told the keeper. [405]

He permitted one attempt, with every other jar locked behind shutters. [416] Nera placed her mother's chair beside the jar bearing her name and drew a circuit around both. [433] To stir the sleeping shadow, the keeper uncapped a reserve of sunlight once used for calibrating the observatory's lenses. [452]

A narrow golden beam touched the jar, and something inside struck the glass hard enough to crack it. [470] Nera wound the key, but its tiny feet snagged where her shaking hand had drawn the final curve. [488] Through the fracture came the smell of soup, then a terrible pressure against her teeth, as though another mouth were growing underneath. [510]

The keeper reached for the sunlight's cap. [517]

"Wait," Nera said, although she could no longer remember whether the voice was hers. [531]

She had arranged everything around her mother as if she were another vessel, something to be filled and carried home. [551] Now she put the chalk into the hand that still knew how to smooth cloth and follow a stitch. [570]

"Will you help me finish?" [575]

Her mother bent forward, studied the broken curve, and joined it with a line so fine it scarcely whitened the floor. [596] The beetle moved, its hollow tooth turning once in the completed circuit. [608] The jar emptied, and her mother's shadow spread beneath the chair. [619]

For a long while neither woman spoke. [626] Then her mother touched Nera's cheek and said the name she had not spoken in three years. [643]

It was not a cure for everything: she could not remember the crossing, and loud noises made her lose whole minutes. [664] But when Nera opened the manual again, her mother pulled her chair closer. [677] Behind the shutters, hundreds of unfinished mornings waited, and they would need more than one pair of hands. [695]
