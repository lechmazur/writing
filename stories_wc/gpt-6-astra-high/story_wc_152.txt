In autumn, leaves gathered against the cobalt observatory like copper filings around a magnet, although no wind seemed willing to touch its walls. [23] Inside, Nera, the royal galaxy-chart embroiderer, stitched silver constellations into velvet and pretended to have forgotten what stars were. [42] She asked visiting generals whether the moon needed darning, and they laughed loudly enough to mistake her silence for stupidity. [62] For forty years she had hidden her wisdom there, listening while powerful men explained themselves to someone they considered harmless. [82]

Her charts did more than record galaxies: their silver threads bound the observatory clock to every bell tower in the capital. [103] Each night, she had to synchronize their stitches, or midnight arrived unevenly across the city. [118] The generals wanted perfect agreement tonight; at dawn, the assembly would vote to open the royal granaries for winter. [137] Their gun crews would begin the coup at the twelfth stroke. [148] They had discussed it over her bowed head that afternoon. [158]

On paper, the army was preparing to suppress a rebellion in the river wards, where her mother had vanished for petitioning against bread taxes. [182] But the generals had already written the rebels' confession, and the supposed rebel commander wore an officer's ring. [200] Unraveling their conspiracies meant finding more than something an old woman claimed to have overheard. [215] She needed the orders hidden in their locked dispatch case. [225]

Beneath the dome burned the eternal candle, whose wick had never shortened, even while kings and astronomers turned to dust. [245] Reflected by the cobalt glass, its flame revealed neighboring skies in her charts, worlds separated from their own by a single decision. [267] A patient embroiderer could travel through adjacent possibilities, following stitches to recover something that had been lost or hidden here. [287] Nera had spent her secret hours searching for a world in which someone had listened to her mother. [305] So far, every thread had led to the same empty chair, the same silence answering hers. [321] Meanwhile, she rescued other rejected petitions from the generals' wastebaskets, keeping them beneath her stool. [336] Even their smallest requests seemed too important to leave unheard. [346]

Tonight, she lifted a flame from the eternal candle onto a taper and held it beneath the generals' constellation. [365] In one neighboring world, the dispatch case had been left unlatched. [376] She slipped her needle through that possibility and drew back a ribbon of written orders. [391] Her apprentice, Len, found her reading them against the blue glass. [402]

"We should warn the palace," he whispered. [409]

"The palace signed them," she said, pointing to the king's seal. [420] Below it were instructions to burn the assembly and blame the wards. [432]

Len reached for the alarm cord, but Nera stopped him. [442] The watchmen served the same seal. [448] Instead, she unpicked the midnight seam that carried the clock's voice into the city's bells. [463]

"A clock is only a throat with very little to say," she told him. [477]

Len did not laugh this time. [483] She passed the loosened speaking thread to Len and told him to read the orders aloud. [499] Every word would undo a stitch, and the work had taken forty years. [512] Nera laid her mother's petition beside the orders. [520] Len looked from the paper to the unraveling constellations. [529]

"You won't be able to search anymore." [536]

"No," she said, and for once made no joke. [545]

Together they began to synchronize the empty spaces where the midnight stars had been. [559] When the clock struck, the bells spoke the king's instructions in Len's shaking voice. [573] Then Nera read the petitions: a baker's confiscated flour, a laundress's missing son, her mother's demand that hunger not be treated as treason. [596] The last silver thread fell through her hands before she reached the signature. [609]

Silence returned, immense and blue, and Len stared at her as though they had accomplished nothing. [625] From the river wards came one answering bell, struck by hand. [636] Others followed, raggedly, no longer awaiting permission from the observatory. [646] Deprived of their signal, the gun crews hesitated long enough for the streets to fill. [661] By dawn, carts blocked the barracks, and printers were setting the orders beneath the petitions. [676] There would be arrests, perhaps theirs, but the coup had lost its darkness. [689]

Under the cooling dome, Nera sat beside the empty velvet and listened as people below began calling out names she had never heard. [712]
