At nautical twilight, when the horizon blurred and the first cold stars pricked through, Silas heard footsteps falter on the opal dock. [22] He was an eccentric lighthouse keeper, famous ashore for charting the intervals between foghorn calls instead of the calls themselves. [42] "Sound tells you where something is," he would say, "but silence tells you where it can hide." [59] Nobody stayed long enough to ask what he meant. [68]

Tonight, a stranger lay across the dock steps, seawater draining from his coat into the milky stone. [86] Silas knelt, but the man's breathing had already become a succession of abandoned attempts. [100] He pressed a dried Edelweiss boutonniere into Silas's hand, held two fingers apart, and pointed into the mist. [118] Then his fingers closed, and his face ceased asking. [127]

Silas could have fetched the harbor constable and supplied the familiar explanation: another boat broken against the reef. [145] Instead, he set the flower against the dock. [153] He needed to decode the stranger's final gesture before the tide erased whatever it indicated. [168] Already, water covered the lowest step. [174]

The dock had been quarried from a seam of opal that retained impressions as ordinary stone retained heat. [192] At this hour, between the day's glare and full darkness, it lent those impressions back through objects cherished by their owners. [213] Silas had spent lonely evenings handling sailors' keepsakes, living briefly inside borrowed memories before returning to his cold kitchen. [232] He knew their limitation: they preserved attention, not truth. [241] Whatever the owner had failed to notice remained blank. [250]

Under his palm, the flower became white again. [258] A young woman stood outside a mountain chapel, pinning it to her father's Sunday coat. [273] One leg was braced in polished steel; she leaned against him while laughing at his crooked tie. [290] The memory lurched into tonight: the same woman, older now, sitting opposite him in a little sailing boat. [308] They were coming toward the lighthouse when their mast snapped. [318]

Silas flinched back into mist, his hand aching around the brittle flower. [330] He had seen no collision, no reef, and no place where the woman went overboard. [345] With the softly persistent patience that made harbor officials avoid him, he pressed down again and began to investigate by observing gaps. [367]

Between the chapel and the boat lay years he could not recover, but tonight's smaller absences had edges. [385] The stranger watched his daughter, then looked upward; for an instant, a square of darkness interrupted the white sweep of the lighthouse beam. [408] Afterward, even as the boat pitched, the rain stopped striking his face. [420]

Not the reef, then, but shelter. [426] Silas looked down at the dock beneath his knees. [435] Its southern end bridged an old loading channel, a narrow gap between two stone piers that widening boats had made obsolete. [456] A small boat could enter at low water, but its mast could not pass beneath the span. [473] Two fingers apart: the piers. [478] Closing: the rising water squeezing away the space below. [487]

He rang the wreck bell and launched his shallow maintenance punt, lantern between his knees. [502] Beneath the dock, his lantern found the woman gripping a crossbeam, her braced leg trapped under the fallen mast. [521] She had been knocking with a loose plank until the water carried it beyond her reach. [537] Silas wedged his oar under the mast, leaned until something cracked, and dragged her into the punt. [554]

On the steps, wrapped in his coat, she stared at the body he had covered with sailcloth. [571] "He said you would find me if he could reach you." [582] Silas almost asked how a stranger could have believed that, but she was looking up at the lantern room. [601]

"Every autumn we brought his apples across," she said, "and he always watched your light until we were home." [620] Silas had imagined those evenings belonged to him alone: the empty chair, the cooling kettle, his footsteps circling above the sea. [641] Somewhere beyond his windows, someone had been watching him keep watch. [652]

He returned the boutonniere, careful not to crumble its petals, and helped her up toward the kitchen while the constable's launch approached. [674] For once, he would not have to borrow a memory to hear another person breathing in the room. [692]
