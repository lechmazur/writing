When the evacuation sirens began playing her childhood school song, Mara, the city's lore keeper, took a ruby flake from her mother's conservation kit. [24] She had until sunrise, when the highest tide would overtop the failing sea wall and drown the lower districts. [43] The palace broadcast promised safety once a rightful sovereign returned to the unclaimed throne and opened the stairway of ascension. [63] It had repeated that promise during the flood that killed her mother. [75] Tonight, Mara meant to find the stairway herself. [83]

Her apprentice, Ivo, brought a boat instead of the ceremonial crown she had ordered him to rescue. [100] "You taught me to be wisely adventurous," he said, checking its batteries before they ventured into the current. [118] She almost reprimanded him, then climbed aboard for their sally into the flooded streets. [132] They followed an obsolete maintenance diagram past drowned tram stops whose advertisements still offered holidays on the moon. [150] He kept their speed low while she watched for submerged power lines. [162]

The diagram led to a hidden orchard behind a kaleidoscopic waterfall, where ancient projectors turned the falling water into stained glass. [183] Inside, robot bees moved among apple blossoms, humming the same three notes as the school song. [199] Mara remembered singing it with sticky hands, before fresh fruit became a royal privilege and then a bedtime invention. [218] At the orchard's center stood the throne, sealed inside a transparent pavilion. [230] Its reader requested a sovereign's blood. [236]

"Try yours," Ivo said. [240] "You know more about this city than any king ever did." [251]

For one shameful moment, she wanted the machine to agree. [261]

But beneath the reader's gilded casing, she noticed a painted arrow. [272] Its edge protruded from successive coats of silver, cream, and imperial blue. [284] Her mother had taught her that cities revised their walls more honestly than their books. [299] Mara set the ruby flake almost flat, using its hard, fine edge to lift the brittle paint without scratching what lay beneath. [321] As she peeled away the paint layers, hidden murals emerged, their arrows leading her around the pavilion. [338] First came a crowned figure climbing toward stars, then workers carrying baskets, then a diagram of stairs beneath the orchard. [358] The oldest mural showed children riding those stairs to the dry upper terraces, their lunch boxes balanced on their knees. [378]

There had been no ascension, only public transport. [386] The monarchy had built its throne over the entrance and turned a commute into a promise only kings could fulfill. [406]

Ivo found the access hatch exactly where the diagram placed it, beneath a load bearing foot of the pavilion. [425] Opening it would bring the glass roof down across the throne. [436] He reached for the boat's cutting torch, but Mara caught his wrist. [448] For thirty years she had saved what other people were willing to break. [461] The throne was the last complete artifact of its dynasty. [471]

Then the school song faltered outside, and a child's voice on Ivo's radio asked whether the king was coming. [490]

"Photograph the murals first," she said, releasing him. [498]

They anchored a cable to control the fall, sheltered behind a stone planter, and started the torch by remote. [517] The roof collapsed with a bright, expensive sound. [525] Beneath the freed hatch, an escalator stirred, its steps climbing into darkness on the orchard's patiently stored solar charge. [544]

Mara borrowed Ivo's radio and gave directions, not assurances. [553] She told people which streets were passable, where to tie their boats, and how many steps led above the flood line. [574] Then she placed her own feet on the moving stairway and tested the ascent before asking anyone to follow. [593] By the time the first families arrived, she had returned with a terrace maintenance drone and a coil of safety rope. [614] They carried children through the broken pavilion while Ivo spaced the passengers so the old motor would not stall. [633]

At sunrise, water swallowed the lowest trunks, and Mara took the last ascent with a basket of apple cuttings. [652] The ruby flake lay wrapped beside them, too small to be a crown jewel, sharp enough to correct a history. [672] Above, someone had found the tune the bees were humming and was teaching it to a frightened child. [690] Mara listened, holding the cuttings upright as the city rose around her without a king.
