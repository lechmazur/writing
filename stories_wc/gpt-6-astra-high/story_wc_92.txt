During the unexplained blackout, the luminous fort at twilight looked almost innocent, its glazed walls giving back the last light of the sun. [23] Below it, a hundred thousand licensed lamps had gone dark, although nobody had rung the curfew bell. [40] The governor blamed weather, then foreigners, then weather employed by foreigners. [51] Mara, his most vigilant lock sentinel, said nothing and counted the doors between herself and the nursery. [68]

Officially, the nursery was called the cradle of sparks, and officially, its contents belonged to the governor. [85] Inside, luminous beetles bred beneath glass bells, their males sterilized and rented to households at a price calculated to discourage reading. [106] Fertile queens never left the fort. [112]

Mara's mother had taught children their letters by moonlight, until a patrol decided that untaxed illumination was still illumination. [131] After the arrest, Mara had chosen a profession involving keys. [141] It was a patient sort of intrigue. [148]

Eleven years later, she possessed every key except the nursery's combination, which the governor changed each morning. [165] That afternoon, a prisoner painting his portrait on the courtyard sundial had brushed six crooked strokes across its unfinished half. [185] The painter was her brother, whose sentence included decorating the man who had imprisoned their mother. [201] He had worked outside the nursery all week, where polished windowpanes reflected the governor's hands. [216]

Now, while guards searched for an enemy sufficiently foreign, Mara crossed the courtyard with a bronze quill holder. [234] Her mother had kept it on the kitchen table, its pierced sides patterned with moths. [249] For years it had collected confiscated pens on Mara's desk; tonight, she intended a less literary use. [266]

She knelt beside the half-painted sundial as if inspecting its bolts. [277] Her brother's strokes occupied the six hours before noon, each tilted differently against the old hour lines. [294] Read in order, they gave six angles, the language of the nursery lock's six arrowed dials. [310] Even the governor's censors considered geometry beneath suspicion, especially when disguised as a poor likeness of his nose. [328]

At the nursery door, Captain Venn found her turning the final dial. [340]

"Inspection," she whispered, showing him the empty holder. [348] "If the queens are dead, His Excellency will require someone smaller than himself to blame." [363]

Venn glanced down at his own narrow shoulders and stepped aside. [374]

Within the nursery, the queens glimmered steadily, offering no explanation for the darkness beyond their walls. [390] Mara had expected to steal one, but the glass bells were sealed with wire that would sing against any blade. [410] Below them, however, lay trays of discarded nesting silk, waiting for the furnace. [423] Tiny eggs shone among the threads. [429] The governor bred more queens than he needed, then burned the surplus lest somebody discover how cheaply light reproduced. [448]

Calmly inventive, Mara teased the silk into her quill holder with a pen, using its nib to avoid crushing the eggs. [469] The bronze concealed their glow, while the little moths admitted air; her mother's desk ornament became a traveling nursery. [488] She packed spare silk over the opening and planted her pen upright, a flag of perfect bureaucratic boredom. [506]

Outside, Venn asked whether the city's lamps would recover. [515]

"Eventually," Mara said, and found that she could meet his eyes. [526]

His daughter attended the kitchen lessons now, although nobody had ever mentioned this unfortunate coincidence. [541] Mara taught on her nights off, keeping the shutters closed and the alphabet soft. [555] Venn reached toward the holder, and she tightened her grip. [565] He merely straightened the pen. [570]

"Regulations," he murmured, then turned to examine a suspiciously empty corridor. [581]

Beyond the postern, the baker waited with the evening bread cart, another ordinary person who had learned to arrive at extraordinary moments. [603] Mara tucked the holder beneath his folded apron and explained about damp leaves, warmth, and patience. [619] Every kitchen school would receive a pinch of silk, and every new colony would supply another. [635] She had wanted to spark a silent revolution, not exchange one keeper of the light for another. [652]

The baker offered her a roll in payment, gravely, as though treason had a posted price. [668] Mara ate it while locking the postern behind him, carefully testing the bolt. [681] She was, after all, a vigilant sentinel. [688] Nobody was getting those children back into the dark. [697]
