Three nights after the birth, Mara could remember every word of the prophecy, but not the last verse of her mother's lullaby. [22] Her daughter rooted against her breast while she hummed the same unfinished phrase until both of them cried. [40] As an onyx-ink cartomancer, Mara had always considered uncertainty a defect to be corrected. [54] Her cards showed the future, but each answer cost the questioner one remembered hour. [68] In labor, with blood soaking the sheets, she had asked whether her daughter would survive her first winter. [86] The cards had taken her mother's final hour. [94]

Now she needed to find where lost time accumulates, before she forgot that anything was missing. [110] She left the baby with her husband and unpacked the things her mother had touched. [125] There was a birthday tablecloth, stiff with years of embroidered outlines. [136] A family custom of tracing hands on birthday tablecloths had preserved their sizes, if not their tenderness. [153] Her mother's hand stopped appearing after Mara's seventh birthday. [162] Beside that last outline, Mara placed a saucer from her dolls' tea set. [175] Its crack had appeared the day her mother died, although she could no longer remember how. [191] Objects, unlike minds, kept the marks of hours surrendered. [200]

She filled the crack with onyx ink and pressed her grown hand over its outline from childhood. [217] The ink seeped toward her mother's embroidered fingers, then rose in a black thread through the ceiling. [234] With the saucer tucked inside the tablecloth, Mara climbed. [243]

At the thread's far end lay a midnight glade above clouds, where trees bore transparent rooms instead of fruit. [262] Their windows shone with stolen afternoons, and blossoms smelled so violently of warmed milk that she breathed through the cloth. [282] Beneath each tree knelt someone holding a deck of cards. [292] Mara recognized the posture from her grandmother's warnings about cloudbound repentance. [303] Those who wanted their past returned must surrender whatever certainty they had purchased. [316] Some had knelt for centuries. [321]

Her room hung low enough to touch. [328] Inside, her mother lay under a quilt, while Mara at seven offered pretend tea from the tiny saucer. [346] Her mother's shaking hand knocked it against the bedstead. [355] The crack opened, and the dying woman began to sing. [365] Mara reached for the window, but its glass tightened like skin beneath her fingers. [379]

Her deck slipped from her sleeve, showing the sentence she had bought: The child will live through winter. [397] To open the room, she had to unfasten the ribbon binding that card to the rest. [413] Beyond the glass, her mother stopped singing and listened to something the little girl asked. [428] Mara could not hear the words, but she recognized the question. [439]

Will you be here on my next birthday? [447]

Her mother's lips did not shape a promise. [455] Instead, she took the child's hand and laid it on the quilt, beside her own. [470]

Mara had hated her for that silence, once. [478] She had built a profession out of refusing to leave anyone without an answer. [492] But there was her small self leaning into an embrace that had not required a promise. [508]

Her fingers worried the knot until the ribbon slipped free. [518] The written promise bled into the grass. [525] The room broke into light, its beauty so sharp that she could scarcely swallow. [539] She fell through clouds holding the cracked saucer, and the lost hour entered her like breath after drowning. [557] In it, her mother was frightened too, and sang anyway. [567]

At home, her husband slept in the chair with their daughter tucked beneath his chin. [582] Mara could no longer remember what the card had said. [592] She listened to the baby's breathing without counting it against a guarantee. [604] For once, being willingly perplexed felt less like failure than room for another person to live. [620] Then she took her daughter, spread the birthday cloth across her knees, and traced the first small hand. [638] The outline wavered where her fingers trembled. [645] She left it crooked and sang the song to its end. [656]
