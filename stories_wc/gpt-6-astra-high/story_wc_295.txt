At midnight, Mara unlocked the celestial mapping observatory and found Iven asleep beneath the great brass telescope, his boots still muddy from the ruined temple. [25] She almost woke him to send him home, then noticed that even in sleep he was holding his breath. [44] Since the flood, every survivor dreamed of drowning. [52] As the city's lucid dream chronicler, Mara entered those dreams through variable stars, whose measured changes in brightness let sleeping minds keep time together. [76] Usually she could heal with words, finding the sentence a frightened mind had forgotten: the door is open, or you are no longer there. [100] But these dreams had begun crossing into one another, until children who had never visited the temple woke coughing river water. [121]

Her sister had been excavating its inscriptions when the embankment broke. [132] Now Mara hoped to decode them before the city's sleep became one unending flood. [146] The stone texts described the observatory's original purpose, but their crucial instruction remained obscure: ONE BEARS THE DAY AGAIN. [165] Mara had assumed that ONE meant the chronicler. [173] For nineteen nights she had taken the drowned city into herself, and each morning she found another silver thread of water beneath her skin. [197] Tonight it had reached her heart. [203]

She opened her notebook to the daylily-pressed telegram that had summoned her sister's rescuers too late. [219] Its four words were BRING ALL THE HANDS. [227] Mara had been charting a variable star when it arrived, and had finished her measurements before opening it. [245] She kept the flower from her sister's garden flattened against that small, unforgivable delay. [259] Iven stirred as she unfolded the temple rubbing beside it. [269] "Let me take a turn," he said. [276] Mara shook her head, unwilling to risk the boy her sister had died trying to save. [292]

Through the telescope, a star brightened, faded, and began again. [302] On the rubbing, the disputed sign opened six pointed petals around a cluster of strokes she had mistaken for rays. [322] Beneath her fingertips, the pressed daylily had precisely that shape. [332] Her sister had once laughed at Mara for thinking the garden's flowers lasted all summer. [347] The stalk endured, but every blossom lived a single day. [357] What returned was not the flower, but the flowering. [366] She followed the inscription through the star's next cycle, this time reading the recurring flowers as successors rather than a single, undying sun. [389] The grammar changed from endurance to succession: ANOTHER BEARS THE DAY. [400]

Understanding dawned without disturbing the midnight. [406] The ancient healers had not instructed one person to contain a city's sorrow; they had built a place where witnesses could relieve one another. [430] Mara looked at the telegram again. [436] This time she read a request, not an accusation. [445] She drew a second chair beneath the telescope. [453]

In the dream, the river came up the observatory stairs. [463] Iven caught her hand before she could step into it alone. [474] Together they entered the temple's last afternoon, where his memory had kept her sister alive and dying beneath the same fallen lintel. [496] Mara wanted to look away, but Iven needed her to witness what he had never managed to say awake. [515] Her sister had pushed him clear, and he had crawled back to hold her hand until the water covered it. [535] "You could not lift the stone," Mara told him, "but you did not leave her." [550] His grip loosened, and somewhere in the dream a door stopped battering its frame. [564]

Then Mara said the words she had withheld from every nightly account: "I opened her message late." [581] Iven did not tell her that it did not matter. [591] He stayed while she wept, and the silver water around her heart began to drain. [606]

Before morning, they wrote invitations to the survivors, promising neither forgetfulness nor an end to grief, only chairs and someone willing to listen. [629] Mara left the daylily in the notebook, but no longer closed the book upon it. [644] Above them, the star dimmed, and Iven took the eyepiece. [654]
