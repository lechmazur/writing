At the candle-lit shrine carved into glacier ice, Mara set a melted snow globe beside the offerings of salt and mittens. [21] Its plastic dome had sagged in the fire aboard the Mercy, the refugee ferry her father was accused of sinking. [41] Inside, a tiny lighthouse leaned over snow that would never fall again. [53] Mara had brought it up from the stern, clenched in a child's skeletal hand. [67]

A wreck-diver and ghost whisperer, she could hear drowned voices only through objects laid on ice. [83] Here, candlelight borrowed the stars trapped in ancient bubbles, and the dead sounded almost warm. [98] "Tell me he tried," she asked the globe. [106] The answer was a little girl's name: Elsie. [114]

That winter, strangers began bringing Mara dreams that would not leave them. [126] A baker dreamed of kneading dough while a wet girl waited beneath his table. [140] A widow dreamed of her wedding, with the same girl standing behind the fiddler. [154] Neither knew Elsie, but both owned things auctioned from the ferry: a mixing bowl, a violin. [170] The drowned could not enter a dream outright; they borrowed the edges of familiar objects. [185] Such relics were windows of connection, and Elsie was knocking wherever she could. [198]

Mara began analyzing these cameo appearances in strangers' dreams, recording each account before allowing the dreamers to meet. [216] She had learned to weave truth carefully: grief supplied patterns even when there was no thread. [232]

Against the shrine wall she pinned sketches of tables, dance floors, kitchens, and one small, persistent figure. [249] Everything surrounding Elsie changed, but the light across her face never did. [261] It came in three red strokes, followed by darkness. [270] Mara knew that harbor signal from childhood: quarantine barrier closed. [280] The official inquiry said the barrier had been lowered and her drunken father had struck an iceberg instead. [298] The dreams suggested another explanation, but suggestion was not enough to expose a council that controlled the salvage licenses. [317]

The inquiry had forbidden anyone to approach the ferry's bow, claiming the ice there was unstable. [333] Mara went down at slack tide, following the chain from its shore anchorage into green darkness. [349] It ran straight through the ferry's shattered bow. [357] Beneath the silt, its locking pin still carried the council's quarantine seal. [369] She photographed both, then entered her father's cabin for the logbook she had never dared retrieve. [385]

Its waxed pages recorded his bargain: he would hide the feverish children below until the harbor inspection was over. [404] He had bolted their compartment himself. [410] Beside the order to confine them, he had written, "Better frightened than refused." [423]

Mara sat in the shrine until the candles burned low, wishing the sea had kept its mouth shut. [441] She could defend his navigation, but not his mercy. [450]

At dawn she nailed the photographs and copied pages to the auction house doors, where the dreamers gathered. [468] She posted their separate accounts, omitting neither the council's signal nor her father's handwriting. [482] By noon, the harbor crews refused to raise another barrier until the council surrendered its records. [498] The records came, and the order to keep the refugees outside bore every councilor's signature. [513]

By evening, people were saying her father had been a monster instead of a drunk. [528] Neither word was large enough, but she no longer offered him a smaller truth to hide inside. [545]

She returned the globe to the shrine and set a candle behind its buckled dome. [560] Through the melted plastic, its light broke into small, wavering windows across the ice. [574] She had wanted to salvage something pure from the wreckage, and had mistaken purity for innocence. [590] Now she asked Elsie what she wanted remembered. [598]

The ice whispered, and Mara slept with her cheek against the altar. [610] Elsie came only briefly, at the edge of a dream about summer. [622] She was pulling off one mitten and fitting it over a smaller child's bare hand. [637] Behind her, the locked door trembled. [643]

Mara woke and wrote that down, too. [650]
