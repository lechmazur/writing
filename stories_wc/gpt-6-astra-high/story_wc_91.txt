On the black sand beach beneath Mount Orra, Mara kept her bottled lightning bug collection in the shade of an overturned boat. [22] Once she had paid for that boat, and twenty others, when the last eruption took the fishing fleet. [40] Now the destitute philanthropist slept under it, with a coat for bedding and no coins sewn into the hem. [59] She disliked the villagers' gratitude because it always arrived carrying soup, and she had not learned how to receive. [78]

During the slow dance of celestial bodies, as the moon crept toward the afternoon sun, her insects began to shine. [98] She had collected them from the forest above the beach, keeping larvae, pupae, and adults in separate jars with pierced lids. [119] Tomorrow she would return them, but today she wanted to understand the texture of transformation. [134] Giving away a fortune had changed her address, but not the stubborn shape of the person inside her skin. [153] Was becoming something else a softening, a splitting, or merely the discovery that your old shelter no longer fitted? [172] She pressed a fingertip against the glass and learned nothing but its coolness. [185]

As daylight narrowed, the shore entered a whispering hush. [194] The surf still folded, the mountain still rumbled, yet between those sounds a small voice said, "The giver will beg for a burden." [217] Mara blamed hunger. [220] Then a red crab tugged her coat from beneath the boat, dislodging the stone that propped its broken keel. [239] A fisherman steadied the hull and pulled her crate of jars clear. [251] "I'll carry these," he said. [256] "Please give them to me," she answered, reaching for the weight. [267]

The hush chuckled through the sand. [273] It orchestrated coincidences, she realized, but required human hands to finish them. [285] "The mountain's smallest fires will lead its children to safety," it whispered. [297] "Find someone else," she told it, and started toward the village to return the fisherman's help before gratitude could become another debt. [319]

A tremor opened the path ahead, spilling hot pebbles from the slope. [331] She took a detour up the wooded ridge, carrying her jars because leaving living things beside a widening fissure seemed indecent. [352] From there she saw what the village could not: a new tongue of lava crossing the coast road behind its houses. [373] The beach would soon become a pocket between the molten rock and the rising tide. [388] The narrow trail she used for collecting insects was the only way around it. [402]

Mara shouted, but the wind carried her warning out to sea. [413] Below, people gathered at the water, watching the sun vanish, their faces dimmed to copper. [428] A jar slipped against another, and its lid sprang loose. [438] One lightning bug climbed her wrist and lifted, drawing a green stitch through the violet dusk. [454] A child gathering shells at the foot of the ridge looked up. [466]

Mara waved the remaining jars, and the child waved back, thinking it a game. [480] She needed lights along the ridge, hands to place them, voices that could reach the farthest boats. [497] All her life she had purchased help for others; now there was nothing left between her and asking. [515] "Help me," she shouted. [519]

The fisherman came first, then the child, then women carrying baskets they emptied to make room for the jars. [538] At each turn someone stayed beside a bottled constellation and called the next household uphill. [553] They followed the insects' cold fire while the mountain's red fire swallowed the road below. [568]

Only when the last boatman reached the ridge did Mara remember the second prophecy. [582] She had fulfilled both accidentally, not by understanding their riddles, but by trying to keep fragile things alive. [600] The hush had not held back the lava; it had shifted a coat, loosened a lid, and waited for someone to answer. [622]

Under the trees, they opened every jar beside the damp leaf litter where she had found the insects. [640] Some lights rose; other lives remained small and unfinished among the roots. [652] The fisherman draped her rescued coat around her shoulders, and this time she let him. [667] The eclipse began to loosen, laying a bright seam along the moon. [679] Mara put her hand into the child's offered palm. [688] Transformation had a texture after all: warm, gritty, and holding on. [699]
