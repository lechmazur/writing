After the magic trick failed, her grandson pulled his empty hands from the mirror and asked Maer whether his sister was truly gone. [23] The conjurer had promised that anyone taken by the lake could be drawn through its reflection, but the glass had yielded only freezing water. [47] Maer paid him anyway and closed her scarred hands before he could study them. [61] People knew the guarded druid found lost things; they did not know how many things she had helped to lose. [81] She had hired the conjurer hoping to save her granddaughter without disturbing the secret below. [96] Now she needed to discover what mirrors don't show: whether the woman behind her face could do more than obey. [116]

"Take me where she vanished," she told the boy. [125] He rowed without crying, keeping his strokes as even as a drummer marching men beneath arrows. [141] Maer sat facing him, a flesh-eating plant seed tucked beneath her tongue, its bitter coat softening. [157] There was no soil at their destination, nothing in which an ordinary searching root could live. [173]

At the heart of the cursed lake, an orchard turned in a slow vortex, its drowned trees swirling upright around an empty center. [196] Apples circled like signal lamps above water black enough to conceal an army. [209] The boy stopped rowing. [213] "She reached for a red one." [219]

Maer remembered those apples before the flood, when refugees had slept beneath the branches under flags of truce. [237] She had commanded the sluice guard then. [244] Orders had arrived bearing the king's seal, and she had obeyed without waking the families. [259] Afterward, kneeling beside the new lake, she had whispered her confession into the winter air and frozen it before anyone could hear. [281] She had bound the water to keep her secret; it had kept everything else as well. [297]

The boat entered the turning trees without a splash. [306] Maer made herself unbend her fingers and pressed the softened seed into the old sword wound across her palm. [325] It sprouted hungrily, feeding on the flesh that had once held a commander's baton. [339] She neither flinched nor hurried as its pale tendrils spread over the water through expanding circles. [355] This was how she found lost things: she gave a root their name and let its widening search exclude everything they were not. [378]

"Find my voice," she said. [383] The boy looked at her, but she kept her eyes on the advancing rings. [397] With each circuit the plant ate deeper, until her wrist bones showed white beneath their patient, busy covering. [415] At last a tendril tightened around something caught between two submerged branches. [427] It lifted a bead of ice containing a single thread of blood, drawn from the lip she had bitten while confessing. [448]

"That isn't my sister." [452]

"No," Maer said, "but it is what holds her." [461]

She could have crushed the frozen confession beneath the water and hidden its words again. [476] The binding would survive that cowardice; only a willing witness could release a secret entrusted to the lake. [494] So she told the boy what the bead contained, and asked if he would hear it. [510] He sat very still before he nodded. [517] This time she did not look away. [524]

Maer laid the ice on her tongue, where the seed had been. [536] Her buried voice emerged young and precise, naming the order, the refugees, the hour she had opened the sluices. [555] The orchard slowed as each word reached its witness. [564] When the final name was spoken, the trees stood still. [574]

Around them, the lake surrendered what it had hoarded: bones from the massacre, coins, fishing nets, and people stolen afterward, still holding their last breath. [599] Her granddaughter surfaced beside an apple branch, coughing yesterday's water. [609] He hauled her aboard, then cut the feeding vine from Maer's ruined hand with his bait knife. [626]

"Not for you," he said when she thanked him. [635] "I need you alive to tell them." [642]

Maer wrapped her wrist and took the spare oar. [651] Ahead lay the shore, the waiting families, and whatever judgment they would make. [664] She rowed toward them with the calm she had once mistaken for innocence. [677]
