In the nebula garden, where luminous flowers rooted in pressurized mist, Vey kept every farewell anyone had ever spoken to him. [21] He stored them in the hollows of his translucent ribs, preferring the imperfect ones, the stammers and promises made too quickly. [42] For three centuries, he had distributed the garden's cold light to ships making the transit between dying suns. [60] He liked to believe that this was kindness. [68]

Now a girl stood beneath his branches of hands, carrying the same brass toolcase her mother and grandmother had carried. [88] Her name was Mara, and the machine had summoned her because her mother had died owing maintenance. [105] Behind the glass, her family's ship waited with its growing beds dark. [117]

The harvest was beginning. [121] Across the quiet sigh of falling petals, she would have perhaps four minutes to service the distributor before its luminous crop faded into useless dust. [146] Petals sealed promptly in collecting jars could feed a ship's vegetables for a year. [160] Vey showed her the empty jar assigned to her family. [170]

Inside the distributor, a calculating machine gear clicked against a polished counterwheel, granting light to owners and darkness to debtors. [190] Mara held the brass between two fingers. [197] Its teeth grew smaller toward the hub, repeating the same hooked shape at successively finer scales. [213] "It approximates infinity," Vey said, reciting the maker's explanation, which he cherished because the maker had been her grandmother. [232] "It can calculate what a family will owe for generations." [242]

"And always find another payment before it releases their light," Mara said. [254] She turned the toolcase around. [259] Inside its lid, beneath her mother's careful diagrams, someone had scratched a child's crooked sun. [274] "She used to bring me here because she couldn't leave me alone aboard ship." [288]

Vey remembered the mother arriving small, asleep against the grandmother's shoulder. [299] He remembered thinking how tenderly one generation delivered the next. [309] Now Mara's own infant stirred inside her coat, and the recollection acquired a different shape. [324]

"Your grandmother built that gear after the furnace accident," he told her. [336] "She wanted to share light without using fire." [344] The first farewell trembled inside his ribs: a woman promising to return once she had paid for the garden that would replace the burned growing decks. [370] Investors had required guarantees, so she had offered her descendants' labor. [381] Vey had witnessed the contract, delighted that human promises could outlive their makers. [394]

"Then help me finish what she wanted," Mara said. [403] She lifted the gear free, and the distributor's spring began pulling every shutter shut. [417] Without its calculations, the machine could not distinguish an owner's jar from a debtor's. [431] Without open shutters, no jar would fill. [438]

Vey could call the custodians, who would restore the gear and add this interruption to Mara's account. [455] Instead, he pressed three hands against the closing mechanism. [464] The spring sliced slowly through the first. [471] He made no sound, unwilling to wake the child. [480]

The falling petals thickened until neither of them could see the collecting channels. [493] Vey began sonar pinging, sending little clicks through the dense mist and listening for echoes from the shutter stems. [512] He guided Mara's wrench from latch to latch while petals settled on his severed fingers like immaculate bandages. [530] She locked each shutter open, giving every waiting ship an equal portion. [542]

When the last latch held, Vey withdrew his hands. [551] The jars sealed themselves with a row of gentle sighs. [561] Beyond the glass, delivery tubes carried the jars to ships whose growing decks began to brighten. [577] The richest ship received less than expected, but nothing went dark. [588]

Mara laid the gear in his widest palm. [596] "They will come for this." [601] He wanted to keep it, with its patient teeth and the touch of a woman who had trusted him. [620] For a moment, wanting felt exactly like duty. [628] Then he set it between the spring jaws and let the mechanism bite. [641]

The sound was small enough to mistake for a seed opening. [652] Vey knew the owners could build another, and that he would have to refuse them without the excuse of broken machinery. [673] He asked Mara to leave the toolcase's diagrams; there would be other hands to teach. [688] When she departed, he did not ask her to promise she would return. [701] He kept only the baby's sleeping breath, which owed him nothing. [712]
