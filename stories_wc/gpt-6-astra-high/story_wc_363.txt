For forty years, Mara, a wistful loom spinner, had woven mourning cloth for people whose dead were easier to locate than hers. [22] Her daughter, Ilen, had vanished when the century bridge broke, leaving only a pulse in Mara's warp each winter. [41] Mara answered by tugging the thread, their old signal for coming home before dark. [55]

The way to reach her emerged through jigsaw puzzle edges found in a rummage sale box. [71] The pieces showed different seasons of one orchard, but their interlocking notches were the knot notation used by the bridge's builders. [92] Someone had disguised a maintenance chart as a toy when the surviving bridges were outlawed. [107] Fitted together, the edges described a route that no longer existed on maps. [120]

Mara followed it to a forgotten orchard deep in a bamboo forest, carrying her small loom and the box. [139] The bamboo knocked together overhead like shuttles without thread. [148] Here the bridge builders had cultivated trees whose sap hardened around passing sounds, preserving them for journeys beyond their native years. [169] Around Mara's neck hung a tree sap amber bead, stoically sparkling in the green gloom. [184] Her husband had bought it here when Ilen was born. [194] It held the child's first word, Mama, which Mara had played against her ear every night since the rupture. [213]

Beneath the oldest tree, she laid the puzzle pieces around her loom, then threaded their pattern into its waiting heddles. [233] The orchard's noises began to loosen. [239] First the bamboo lost its knocking, then her footsteps surrendered their crunch, until a raveling silence spread between the trunks. [259] This was not absence but separation: sounds slipping free of the moments that had made them. [275] Through the hush of time's unraveling tapestry, Mara heard a sentence caught for forty years in a broken crossing. [294]

"Mother, there is somewhere I can step off, but it isn't our time, and I keep feeling you call me back." [315]

The voice was still seventeen. [320] For Ilen, perhaps only an afternoon had passed on that dwindling span. [332] Mara gripped the winter thread and understood what her faithful answering had become. [345] She had meant a welcome; she had made a tether. [355]

She searched the chart for a crossing wide enough to take her body, but the central pieces were missing. [374] Only the narrow service channels survived along the edges, made to carry a message across impossible distance. [391] To open one required a sound anchored on both sides of the rupture. [404] The bead held a moment that belonged to them both. [414] Its amber would burn as the channel opened; no recording, however carefully copied, could replace the original moment held inside. [434]

Mara lifted it to her ear. [440] A little breath, a lip smacking, then that delighted discovery of a name for the person who always came when called. [461] For a moment, she stopped the loom. [468] There would be another winter, another pulse, another night when she could lie down beside that small, undamaged voice. [487] Beyond the thread, her daughter would still be waiting for permission to live. [500]

Mara seated the bead in the shuttle and made herself recommence. [511] As the amber softened, the loom drew its gold into a single shining filament. [525] She wanted to ask about the weather there, whether there were kind people, whether Ilen had enough to eat. [544] Instead, she spoke the words her hands had spent forty years refusing. [556]

"Go on, love, and do not wait for me." [565]

The filament tightened once beneath her fingers, the answering tug Ilen used to give before running out to play. [584] Then it slipped through the cloth and was gone. [593]

Sound returned unevenly to the orchard, one bamboo stem knocking while its neighbor remained mute. [608] Mara waited until she could hear her own breathing before she lifted the loom from its bed of roots. [627] She left the puzzle assembled beneath the tree, weighted with stones against the wind. [641] At home, she would weave something other than mourning cloth, perhaps a length of yellow wool warm enough for a child's winter coat. [664]

That night, her hand went to the empty cord at her throat. [676] She held it until morning, but she did not pull. [686]
