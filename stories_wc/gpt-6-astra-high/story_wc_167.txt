At dawn on Pearl Bay, Sera stood inside the prism atrium in mist, checking the windows she had spent thirty years repairing. [22] The contractor's salvage ledger assigned fortunes to the jeweled roof and nothing to the clear panes around its base. [41] Those were marked for crushing. [46]

Beyond the glass, her daughter stood beside the controls for the new tidal gates, which would keep winter water out of the dockworkers' flats. [70] Once the gates closed, the atrium's pond would lose its pulse of seawater, and the noctilucent reckonings would end. [89]

"You have until sunrise," Nessa had told her, not unkindly. [99] "Before everything shifts." [102]

The reckonings were older than the atrium: at certain dawns, the luminous pond let the living commune with the dead. [122] You threw a stone and watched the patterns in the rings, where remembered gestures appeared between the widening lines. [141] The prisms gathered the weak light, but no one could compel a particular memory. [155]

Sera had stayed away since her mother's funeral, unwilling to discover how little a daughter might matter in a woman's whole life. [177] Yet for years she had caught herself looking for approval in the windows she polished. [192] Her mother had scrubbed these same panes, leaving neither signature nor masterpiece. [204] Sera had meant to do better. [210]

"A harbor glazier reads between lines," she had taught Nessa, meaning the seams of windows and the silences around a price. [231] Her mother's silences had always defeated her. [238]

Now she dropped a pebble into the pond. [246] Rings opened across the reflected roof, turning its bright triangles into a kitchen table. [260] Her mother sat mending a boy's mitten while soup trembled on the stove. [273] Not Sera's mitten, but one belonging to the fish seller's son. [284]

The next ring showed her mother washing a pane, then lifting a sleeping child away from the bucket. [302] Sera recognized her own red shoes. [308]

She asked whether her mother had been happy. [316]

The water offered no verdict, only her mother rubbing the child's cold feet between her palms. [332] Sera remembered that warmth, and then remembered wanting those rough hands hidden when school friends visited. [348]

She reached for another stone, but Nessa knocked against the glass. [359] Light was gathering beyond the breakwater; soon the tide would turn against the waiting gates. [374]

Through the clear lower windows, Sera could see the flats, their kitchen windows patched with sacks where last winter had broken the glass. [397] She could ask for another dawn, but every delay meant another tide entering those kitchens. [412] Her mother would not answer in words, and Sera had already been answered in hands. [427]

She opened the door and pointed to the bottom of her ledger. [439]

"Who decided this glass was worthless?" [445]

Nessa glanced from the figures to the uneven panes. [454] "Nobody wants to pay for measuring and cutting all those odd sizes when the roof alone settles the construction debt." [474]

Sera knew those sizes already, each warped edge and stubborn bubble, the way her mother had known which children needed mittens. [495] She crossed out the disposal charge and wrote that the lower panes would be removed intact. [511] Then she pledged her salvage fee toward refitting the broken kitchen windows, with the necessary cutting done by her own hands. [532] It would cost her the new engraving wheel she had ordered, and several weeks of evenings. [548]

Nessa read the amendment twice. [553] "You cannot save the reckoning this way." [560]

"No," Sera said, surprised by how much the word hurt, and how little it changed her decision. [577]

She signed, and Nessa took the ledger back to the gate crew. [589] As the great mechanisms began to move, Sera returned to the pond without picking up another stone. [606] The last rings had vanished, leaving only a woman's reflection beneath an extravagant roof. [620] She looked older than her mother had ever been, and, for once, did not turn away. [636]

Outside, the first sunlight reached the sacks in the kitchen windows. [647] Sera knelt beside the lowest pane and began loosening its putty, careful not to damage what had been entered as nothing. [668]
