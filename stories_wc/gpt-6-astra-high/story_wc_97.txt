The tidal arbor above the clouds bent under its evening flood, an immense mangrove carrying railway platforms among its silver branches. [21] For thirty years, travelers had called Iven its wistful station pianist, mistaking his quietness for acceptance. [37] His wife, Mara, had boarded her appointed train eleven years ago, leaving him her projection booth and a broken anniversary clock. [58] Its suspension wire had snapped that morning; he had never unwound the spring she wound each wedding anniversary. [76] The clock still contained their last year together, or so he permitted himself to think. [91]

Each evening, new departures appeared on the station's ancient film, and every passenger's compass pointed toward the platform pictured beside their face. [113] People trusted these arrangements because the film had never been wrong, even about drownings. [127] Iven had watched Mara's carriage vanish beneath a white tide on the screen before watching it happen outside. [145]

Tonight, twelve evacuated children stood beside his piano with flickering compasses. [156] Their needles swung between Platform Nine and a wall without a door. [168] Overhead, the black-and-white film showed their train sinking, then Iven shutting his piano while water climbed around his ankles. [187] A girl holding a cloth rabbit asked whether he knew anything cheerful. [199] He almost played the lullaby he always played when courage failed him. [211] Instead, he looked at the wall. [217]

Each compass shivered exactly when a white scratch crossed the film, a mark shaped like Mara's crooked initial. [235] She had repaired reels for forty years, and sometimes censored frames survived beneath her splices. [250] The railway cut out routes whose futures it could not certify; Mara hated throwing anything away. [266] Iven stopped the projector and peeled back a splice with his thumbnail. [278] Through hidden sections in a black-and-white film reel, his wife had preserved a way into the arbor's hollow heart. [297] Tiny pictures showed a maintenance stair behind the wall, descending to a chamber where roots closed against the flood. [316]

Like every route in the arbor, it opened only while its images passed through the projector. [332] At normal speed, the surviving sequence would give them three seconds. [343] They needed minutes, and they had only "until the horizon invites night," as the station's tide warning quaintly phrased it. [363] After sunset, the chamber's living walls would seal. [371]

Mara had called him strategically creative when poverty made him coax three working octaves into sounding like seven. [389] Now he broke the clock's glass dome, removed its powerful spring barrel, and fitted it against the projector's hand crank. [409] Piano felt made a brake; a strip of his leather belt coupled the mismatched shafts. [424] He tested the mechanism until the film crept forward, buying passage with the last stored tension of Mara's hands. [443]

The wall opened onto a narrow stair, and the children's needles spun without finding north. [458] Nobody moved. [460] The girl asked whether they would live down there, gripping her rabbit by its frayed ears. [476] "I cannot promise that," Iven said. [482] He understood, then, how Mara had walked aboard that train: certainty could feel like a hand offered in the dark. [502] But he could offer a hand without pretending to know where the darkness ended. [516]

There were four minutes left in the spring, enough for the descent if they started now. [532] He returned to the piano and struck the departure march, the one their teachers had used to organize every evacuation drill. [553] As their feet found its rhythm, he broke from the printed score into a fierce, bright variation. [570] He wanted, at last, to surprise fate with an unplanned encore. [581] The girl led the others through the doorway; Iven left his final chord ringing and followed. [597]

Water struck the stair behind them as the hollow opened beneath their feet, smelling of sap and iron. [615] They took refuge among the thick roots just as sunset drew the entrance shut. [629] In the darkness, twelve compasses clicked against the floor as the children put them down. [644] Iven felt for the girl's hand, remembering another hand he would never hold again, however cleverly he rearranged the world. [664] Above them, the clock spring exhausted itself, and the future stopped passing through the light. [679] He tapped a new rhythm against the wood and waited for someone to join him. [694]
