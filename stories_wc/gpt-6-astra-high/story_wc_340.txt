The elevator carried Nella, a servant with a tray, up the icebound spire above the clouds while its cables complained with the discretion expected of employees. [26] Beside her, Lady Venn watched the floor indicator rather than the airship captain's goggles on the tray. [43] They were her son's spare pair, which had prudently stayed home when he crashed. [57]

Ten floors separated the servants' hall from his rooftop orangery, and frost made the journey slow. [73] At the top, his mother would burn his possessions and close the heating valves. [87] She wanted something of him that could not change. [96] Ice, she had decided, was dependable. [102]

At the first floor, Nella noticed a corner of silk protruding beneath the goggles' case lining. [118] She was warmly inquisitive even about missing buttons, an inclination that had delayed her promotion for years. [135] Important people preferred their mysteries undusted. [141]

"May I unfold this before it goes into the stove?" [151]

Lady Venn gave the small nod with which she authorized everything from tea to dismissals. [166] Nella lifted the lining and drew out a folded sheet covered in colored navigation marks. [181] The second floor slid past. [186]

"A chart," said Lady Venn, already withdrawing. [193]

But charts did not have dates followed by tiny drawings of spoons, oranges, and one unmistakable dachshund. [210] At the third floor, Nella tilted the silk toward the sunlight. [221] Glare swallowed its colors. [225] She tried instead the soft illumination falling through the elevator's frosted window, and through scattered light the marks resolved into overlapping red and blue letters. [250]

"He used amber lenses to separate the blue soundings from red hazards on his harbor charts," she said. [268]

She knew because he had explained their purpose whenever she cleaned them, forgetting every previous explanation. [284] At the fourth floor, she held one lens over the silk. [295] The red letters vanished against their background; the blue ones became dark, readable script. [309] What she had discovered was an encrypted diary, its key kept in plain sight. [323]

At the fifth floor, Lady Venn reached for it, then stopped. [334]

"I cannot bear another account of how much he loved flying." [345]

Nella understood the difficulty: everyone insisted upon finding consolation in the very thing that had killed him. [362] When her own sister died, visitors had praised her patience until Nella almost forgot her excellent dirty jokes. [380] Since then, she had tried to seek out the joy beneath anguish, without suggesting that anguish ought to move aside. [400]

"This entry appears to concern your dog stealing a bishop's glove." [411]

"He promised not to tell anyone about that," she said. [453]

"Perhaps that is why he needed a cipher." [461]

The seventh floor passed while Lady Venn read the entry twice. [472]

"Mother sat on the tiles laughing, with both skirts full of dirt and one orange held aloft like a rescued heir." [493]

"I had forgotten that she was somebody before she was responsible for me." [506]

Nella looked politely at the cables, which continued doing their thankless work. [518] At the eighth floor, Lady Venn asked whether freezing citrus trees would hurt them. [532] Nella resisted the merciful lie. [537]

"It would kill them, and there would be no fruit next winter." [549]
