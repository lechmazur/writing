Each night, Mara cleaned the public library and left a flask of tea beside the chair where a homeless woman sometimes slept. [22] She never waited for thanks; other people's loneliness reached her so sharply that kindness was easier at a distance. [41] For twenty years, her world had consisted of the empty stacks and the caretaker's room upstairs. [57]

Then a crumpled invitation appeared beneath her door, flattened carefully enough to show that someone else had nearly lost courage. [77] "The Society of Errata requests your presence in Reference, within arm's reach of midnight," it said. [93] Below, in smaller writing, someone had added: "Please bring the mistake you have been living inside." [109]

At eleven fifty, Mara took her old disciplinary notice downstairs. [119] She had once canceled a girl's borrowing cards because six different names shared one address and one unmistakable handwriting. [138] The girl had begged her to reconsider, then stopped coming. [148] When Mara learned that the address belonged to a women's refuge, she confessed, lost her desk, and accepted the cleaning job. [169] She wanted no pardon tonight, only some wisdom she could unearth from what she had done. [185]

Reference contained the same dull municipal volumes she dusted every Tuesday, but tonight five people sat between them. [203] A young woman opened a volume labeled Drainage and revealed letters sewn between its statistical tables. [219]

"Our library hides inside the public one," she said, showing Mara a child's account of an evacuation. [236] "We preserve what official corrections erase, especially when being remembered could endanger someone." [249]

Here were nestled paradoxes: secrets kept in public books, and failures preserved by the people trusted to prevent them. [268] The woman turned another page, and Mara recognized the handwriting before she recognized the face. [283]

"I'm Ruth," the woman said, folding and unfolding her empty hands until Mara understood who had crumpled the invitation. [302] "Those women chose their own names when they escaped, and I collected their books because they feared being followed." [321]

She laid six faded cards beside a proposal for the library's new computer system. [335] It would automatically suspend accounts without independently verified names and addresses. [346]

"The board votes tomorrow," Ruth said, "and they want evidence, not stories." [358] "But publishing these records would expose the refuge and women still hiding from violent men." [373] "I thought you might help us explain why people need imperfect records." [385]

Mara heard the strain beneath Ruth's careful voice: asking for help had once cost her the last safe place she knew. [406] An apology rose in Mara's throat, but she swallowed it rather than make Ruth comfort her. [422]

"I called you dishonest because checking would have required listening," she said. [434] "We can hide their identities without hiding what I did." [444]

She spread her disciplinary notice over the cards, covering the names, and asked for tracing paper. [460] Together they copied the matching handwriting and replaced the address with a blank square. [474] On another sheet, Mara wrote the questions she should have asked before taking anybody's books away. [490] Ruth proposed a confidential review instead of automatic suspension, and the others began assembling anonymous examples from their hidden shelves. [510] The evidence grew without requiring a single frightened woman to become publicly brave. [523] The originals would stay sheltered on the shelves while their lesson went out into the city. [539]

"Will you speak to the board?" Ruth asked. [547]

Mara looked toward the stairs, where her little room waited with its kettle and its mercifully solitary cup. [565] She could already feel tomorrow's faces, their disappointment, their pity, the terrible possibility of welcome. [580] Leaving that room forever would be impossible, but perhaps leaving forever was not what courage required. [596]

"Ask them to meet here," she said. [603] "I'll stay after my shift." [608]

It would be a way of staying while leaving: keeping her shelter, but stepping outside the silence she had mistaken for restitution. [630]

The clock lifted its minute hand toward midnight, and nobody hurried to fill the quiet. [645] Mara fetched her kettle, then searched the cupboards for the cups she had stopped needing twenty years ago. [663] Ruth did not offer forgiveness, but she moved her papers to make a place beside her. [679] Mara sat, smoothing the invitation beneath her palm. [687] The familiar peace of the empty library widened to include breathing, the scratch of pencils, and someone accepting a second cup. [708]
