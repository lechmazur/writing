At the close of an unspoken dream, Nera woke tasting the lentils she had promised her brother on the night he died. [22] Rain ticked from the desert fortress's battlements, the first rain since its gates had closed against a caravan carrying fever. [42] Everyone inside had survived, and everyone had dreamed the same knocking ever since. [55] At breakfast, her apprentice touched her wrist and collapsed beneath a grief that did not belong to him. [73] He cried out her brother's name. [79]

Nera wrapped her hands in flour sacks before helping him up. [90] As fortress chef, she could taste memories, and she sampled his tears with a spoon. [105] Iron hinges, scorched lentils, her own voice telling the guards there wasn't enough: these were memories she had salted away. [125]

After the caravan burned, the council had employed her to sandpaper rough memories until people could work and sleep again. [145] She had drawn their remorse into salt, then kept polishing long after their hands had stopped shaking. [162] The stored remorse lay in a coral icebound pavilion beneath the dunes, where ancient coral turned human sorrow into cold. [182] Its only leakage had been that nightly dream, too shameful for anyone to mention. [196] Now rainwater had entered the buried chambers, dissolving salt and carrying its burden into the fortress well. [213] Unclaimed remorse was emotionally contagious through touch. [220] By noon, children would be grieving decisions made before their births. [231]

She ordered the well covered and sent her apprentice for gloves, dry salt, and every empty jar in the kitchen. [251] The old stairway to the pavilion had collapsed, but the storm had scoured sand from the limestone outside the walls. [271] Nera knelt among shallow basins, analyzing tidal pool formations as her brother had taught her when they gathered fossil shells. [291] Rain puddles ended blindly; ancient ebb channels joined, widening toward the vanished sea. [304] Following those channels, she found the pavilion's drainage arch and crawled through with the jars strapped across her back. [323]

Inside, red coral pillars held up a ceiling of ice as stubbornly as bones supporting a dying animal. [341] Water gnawed their bases. [345] Beneath them stood the crock containing her own remorse, its lid split by a root of frost. [362] She tasted one grain and remembered that there had been enough lentils. [374] What she had lacked was the courage to feed sick strangers beside her healthy son. [389]

Around her, hundreds of crocks were leaking into one dark pool. [400] She could swallow it all, draw every citizen's remorse into her own body, and leave the fortress clean. [418] The coral would freeze her solid, a permanent stopper in the drain. [430] For a moment, that seemed easier than facing her son with the truth. [443] Then she recognized the old recipe: one person abandoned so the others could go on calling themselves good. [461]

She plugged the channel leading toward the well with her flour sacks and weighted them down with stones. [479] Instead, she drew the pooled remorse into fresh salt, separating each sorrow as carefully as bones from fish. [498] The captain's tasted of leather, the council's of ink; hers was still lentils, burned because she had stood listening. [517] She sealed the labeled jars, then swallowed her own portion, letting her present self subsume the frightened woman she remembered. [537]

At dusk she set the jars on the serving counter, beside bread baked with the fortress's last clean water. [556] Nobody would be dosed without knowing, and nobody would be allowed to mistake another person's guilt for their own. [575] First she drew the borrowed grief from her apprentice and added it to her own bread. [591] Then she told him why he had been crying. [600] Her son listened from the doorway, holding a tray so tightly that its wooden rim creaked. [616]

The captain came forward first, asking whether swallowing his share would make the knocking stop. [631] "I don't know," she said, and cut him a thick slice. [642] He ate standing up, then sat down hard. [650] When he could speak, he asked for timber to build a shelter outside the gate. [665]

Nera's son put down his tray and took her bare hand. [676] She felt him brace himself, but the sorrow stayed where she had finally agreed it belonged. [692] Together they carried the remaining bread to the men marking out the shelter. [705]
