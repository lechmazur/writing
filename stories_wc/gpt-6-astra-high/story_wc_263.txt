Under the aurora-lit archway, Mara heard the words her mother had died before speaking. [14] Not love or farewell, but the next verse of a song that had never existed. [29] The arch collected remembered stardust, the bright residue of everything the dead had wanted to make. [45] Her mother had built it so that nothing precious would ever again be lost. [59] Now the city's children slept without dreaming, and its potters copied the same chipped bowl. [74]

After her death, Mara's father had sealed its outlets and polished its silver ribs until their shadows lay sharp across the square. [96] He called the lights an inheritance; Mara, a moonstruck dreamer who heard unspoken words even in sleep, was beginning to call them prisoners. [119] She had once populated the moon with imaginary cities; now even her dreams were crowded with the dead. [137] Once, she had begged him to keep her mother's light burning. [148] Now she could hear that unfinished song straining against its cage, and shame made every beautiful note unbearable. [166]

In her mother's cupboard she found an astronomical instrument: a brass astrolabe with a movable needle for marking meteors. [185] Her mother had mapped the showers before the arch was built, when fallen light still seeded human dreams. [203] Along the rim, beneath the tarnish, she had scratched a warning: A vessel must also be a passage. [221] Mara took it to the square. [227]

She worked across an unwritten calendar, filling the blank datebook her mother had left her with trajectories instead of appointments. [247] By tracing the path of falling stars through the astrolabe's rings, she discovered where the arch had interrupted their descent. [267] Each silver rib was a blocked channel, meant to carry that light toward a sleeper somewhere in the city. [286] The arch was a nursery, not a tomb. [295] Her mother's song pointed toward the room above the bakery, where a little boy tapped rhythms but knew no tunes. [315]

When Mara showed her father, he closed the datebook. [324] "If we open it, we lose them." [331] "We are losing everyone who comes after." [338] He turned away, but not before she heard his unspoken answer: I have forgotten her voice already. [355] For the first time, she understood that he polished the ribs because he could not hear what they held. [374]

Mara began with the rib aimed at the bakery. [383] She fitted her mother's needle into the rib's adjustment socket and turned it along the meteor's missing course. [401] Green radiance poured over her hands, cold enough to blister. [411] Instead of escaping, the song wrapped around her wrists. [420] She knew its melody; she wanted its ending. [428] The arch tightened, answering that desire. [434] Beyond the square, the baker's boy woke screaming. [442]

Her father caught her before she fell. [449] The light around her wrists pulsed with her own unsaid words: Finish it for me. [464] She had come to free trapped creativity, yet she was still demanding that it belong to her. [481] The arch was only doing what its mourners asked. [490] To become a passage, she would have to stop being a destination. [502]

"Turn the needle when I sleep," she told her father. [512] "If I dream of her, let the dream pass through." [522] He looked toward the bakery, where a lamp had been lit, then lowered her head onto his folded coat. [541] For the first time in all those undated nights, Mara allowed herself to rest without listening for the next note. [561]

Her father turned the needle. [566] In sleep she felt the song loosen, taking with it the ending she would never know. [582] She did not follow. [586] Light ran along the plotted course, through the arch and into the room above the bakery. [602] The green vault dimmed; behind it, the ordinary dark seemed terrifyingly vast. [614]

At dawn the boy began to sing, badly, in a language he was inventing. [628] Mara recognized nothing except the courage of the sound. [637] Beside her, her father left one silver rib unpolished and opened the datebook to its first empty page. [655] He wrote tomorrow there, and left the rest for things neither of them could hear yet. [671]
