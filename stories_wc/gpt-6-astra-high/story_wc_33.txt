At the day's lone mail drop, twilight settled through the cobalt arcade, turning its cracked blue tiles the color of old bruises. [22] Marek, mailman for a territory claimed by two republics and maintained by neither, unlocked the collection box beneath their competing flags. [43] He saluted both, then slipped the letters each government had forbidden him to deliver between the permitted ones. [61] Overhead, the border drones hovered so quietly that his key's click sounded like a snapped bone. [77]

That evening, both ministries returned his application to foster Lida, the orphan sleeping in the arcade's abandoned weather shop. [96] Each republic called her the other's citizen; neither permitted foreign children into its foster homes. [111] He had brought her supper all winter and told her the forms were nearly finished, long after he stopped believing it. [132] Marek himself had grown up in transit dormitories, where every departing official promised to return. [147] He had become a mailman to find where broken promises heal, believing the answer might arrive if somebody kept delivering. [167] Now Lida watched him unfold two identical refusals. [175]

"Bad news should only need one envelope," she said. [184] At twelve, she already knew to speak below the drones' hearing threshold. [196]

Marek tucked the letters away, refusing to let their stamps become the last word. [210] For months, he had been comparing maps while the ministries considered his request. [223] By linking absences, streets omitted from one republic's survey with blocks erased from the other's, he had found a shared blank. [244] An old pumping station stood there, beyond the arcade's eastern gate, in land neither government would acknowledge. [261] He had delivered machinery parts there before the partition; its roof was sound, its stove enormous. [277] Moving there would mean surrendering his licensed address, his route, and his wages, without gaining the approval needed to foster her. [298] Until tonight, that had seemed a reason to wait. [307]

On each refusal he wrote, "Thank you for your consideration; I accept responsibility for her," and signed his name. [326]

Lida lifted her mother's last stock from beneath the counter: a thunderstorm in a bottle, its miniature clouds pulsing against green glass. [348] "We could sell this for the fees." [355]

"There would be other fees," Marek said. [362] Her mother had died paying them, working the river docks after weather trading became a border offense. [379] He knelt beside Lida, careful not to touch the bottle. [389] "I know a house, but smuggling you across a drone border means I cannot promise we'll be safe." [407]

She studied him for a moment, then pushed her mother's shopping bag into his hands. [422] "Can you promise not to leave while I'm asleep?" [431]

"Yes." [432]

The drones were descending to scan his sacks for hidden bodies, bringing their metal bellies within reach of the arcade's copper lightning rods. [455] Marek wrapped the mail in his coat, then wheeled his cart beneath the nearest rod and climbed onto it. [474] When he held out his hand, Lida gave him the bottle, though her fingers lingered on its warm glass. [493] He uncorked it against the copper. [499]

Thunder struck inside the arcade, shattering its brittle hush; blue fire raced up the rods and burst among the hovering machines. [520] Rain hammered the tiles as the drones dropped, and Marek pulled Lida through the eastern gate before the next patrol arrived. [541] He carried the letters too; she had seen enough people abandon what they were entrusted to deliver. [558]

At the pumping station, he found dry kindling inside the stove, left by some vanished keeper who had expected another winter. [579] Lida arranged their wet things beside the growing fire, then laid her empty bottle on the mantel. [596] Marek sat beside her bench, close enough that she could hold his sleeve while she slept. [612]

Tomorrow he would deliver these letters, find work, and ask the neighboring farms whether they could spare food or blankets. [632] Tonight, when she woke in terror, he was still there. [642] He did not tell her that this was where promises healed. [653] He fed the stove. [657]
