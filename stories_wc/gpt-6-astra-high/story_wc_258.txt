After the cryptocurrency crashes, Mara returned to her grandmother's rain-beaded skylight loft to inventory whatever the creditors had overlooked. [19] The old woman, an onyx-ink cartomancer, had left thirty-two handmade cards, a measuring tape, and a month's unpaid rent. [38] Under the tape lay a note: A future survives by multiplying paths. [50]

Mara had once designed payment networks that rewarded everyone for taking the same efficient route. [65] When that route collapsed, her neighbors lost their savings, and she lost the right to call herself useful. [83] Now the city's Recovery Office offered money to map cultural patterns in districts scheduled for redevelopment. [99] They wanted addresses: where people baked wedding bread, rehearsed ancestral songs, washed their dead. [113] She wanted to believe that recording something was different from extracting it. [125]

Steadily curious even when curiosity hurt, she began reverse engineering the deck rather than attempting to read her own fortune. [145] Her grandmother had always laid the cards edge to edge, but never in the same sequence twice. [162] Mara remembered clients leaving with directions instead of predictions, and sometimes with the address of a stranger. [179]

The measuring tape revealed what her fingers had missed: four subtly different widths and eight lengths. [195] Arranged by their dimensions, the cards formed a rectangle whose black borders joined into branching lines. [211] Beneath the skylight, rain shadows trembled over tiny ovens, cradles, and open mouths. [224] Mara recognized the mouth with a broken tooth painted on the bakery's shutters. [237]

She called its owner, Irena, who arrived carrying a bowl of sourdough beneath her coat. [252] "They're sealing the bakery tomorrow," Irena said, placing the bowl where rainwater could not reach it. [268] "Your grandmother promised another oven for my husband's funeral loaves, whatever happened here." [281]

Mara looked at the deck, ashamed that she had mistaken its repetitions for sloppy draftsmanship. [296] Three ovens appeared beside three different flowers, all connected to the broken mouth. [309] She assumed they marked branches of the same business, but Irena shook her head. [323]

"Those are mourning flowers, not shop signs: rosemary for remembrance, marigolds for guiding souls, white lilies for resurrection." [341]

The lines crossed between traditions, following borrowed ovens, shared flour, and people willing to learn a neighbor's recipe. [359] This was not a map of where customs belonged, but of how they traveled when belonging became dangerous. [377] For the first time, Mara understood why her grandmother called a good reading an opening. [392]

She wanted to confide in Irena, yet found herself covering the Recovery Office contract with her elbow. [409] Then she moved her arm and explained what the office would pay for a complete, searchable version. [426] Irena read the letterhead and touched the identical emblem on her bakery's closure notice. [440]

"They promised the festival survey would protect us, but it helped them find every unlicensed kitchen." [456]

For a moment, Mara saw the beautiful database she could build, each connection clean, each household exposed. [473] She turned the contract facedown and asked Irena whom they could visit before morning. [487]

The first oven had gone cold when its owner moved away; the second belonged to a woman who no longer answered unknown callers. [510] At the third, Irena recognized a lullaby through the door and sang the answering verse. [525] The bolt slid back. [529]

By midnight, the starter had been divided among three bowls, because the woman knew two more cooks the deck did not. [550] Each accepted dough only after Irena explained her need and asked permission to send other mourners there. [567]

Back beneath the leaking skylight, Mara added the new ovens in her grandmother's ink, but left their addresses off the cards. [588] She understood now why the pictures needed a reader, and why that reader needed to be known. [605]

At dawn she declined the commission, although the rent remained unpaid and the reply cost her several minutes with her finger hovering over Send. [629] Then she folded the measuring tape around the deck and went downstairs to help Irena carry warm loaves. [647]

Her grandmother had not left her a way to predict which doors would close. [661] She had left her the work of keeping more than one open. [673]
