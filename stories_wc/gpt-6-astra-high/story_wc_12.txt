Beneath the harbor, inside the dimensional anchor, every sound arrived wrapped in felt. [13] Father Ivo preferred it that way; a loop pedal priest learned how much damage an echo could do. [31] His pedal repeated the covenant that held the city in one world, while his silences gave the machinery time to listen. [52] He decorated with silence, placing pauses where other priests placed bells. [63] Tonight, something was moving into those pauses. [70]

His sister Mara sat inside a chalk circle, her shadow already standing while she remained seated. [86] At midnight, the shadow would fit itself over her completely, and the anchor would have its next permanent keeper. [105] The cathedral called this consecration. [110] Mara, who could still move three fingers, had written possession in the chalk dust. [124]

Ivo had nominated her himself. [129] She was patient, unmarried, and accustomed to working alone; he had called these qualifications, never asking why solitude should make a life expendable. [152] Now he had seven minutes before the possession completed, and no prayer he trusted. [166]

Beside her lay an artist's palette with one color, lampblack. [176] She had painted silhouettes for a living, finding a face by darkening everything around it. [191] He remembered sitting for her, impatient with the untouched paper she insisted was his cheek. [206]

The pedal whispered the ancient charge: "Make room within the keeper." [217] Ivo had been taught that the anchor understood language, but not intention; whatever its priests repeated became its law. [236] For two centuries they had repeated that sentence until room meant vacancy and keeper meant vessel. [252] Yet the first covenant, engraved beneath his pedal, began with we. [263]

He lifted his foot. [267] The final loop dwindled, and the chamber's stillness unraveled into separate threads: Mara's breathing, water against the pilings, frightened people waiting beyond the door. [291] Families always attended a consecration, although nobody admitted they were attending a farewell. [304]

Ivo needed to plumb a machine built from words, and questions were the only tools he had left. [322] "If a room belongs to everyone, why must one woman be emptied to provide it?" [337] The anchor answered through Mara's mouth: "A burden requires a bearer." [348] Her voice sounded distant but exact, like an instruction heard through a wall. [361] Ivo pressed the pedal, recording the answer and the question he asked next. [374] "Does a bridge stand because one stone suffers, or because many stones hold one another?" [389] The shadow hesitated, its fingers losing their agreement with hers. [399] Comparison had reached somewhere command could not. [406]

He took her palette and painted around his own hand, leaving its shape unpainted when he lifted it. [424] "Is this hand absent because I have not painted it?" [434] The machine hummed, unable to call the shape empty without denying the picture. [447] Mara's shadow lowered one finger. [452] He wanted every hidden assumption brought into the light, especially those that had made sacrificing his sister seem like an honor. [473]

He opened the door. [477] The waiting families recoiled from Mara's upright shadow, but he showed them the inscription under the pedal. [494] "We made the anchor to hold our lives, not our lives to hold the anchor." [509] Then he admitted what he had done, without calling it duty. [520] Nobody absolved him. [523] When he asked them to help, their silence lasted long enough to frighten him. [537]

Mara reached for her palette. [542] With her three free fingers, she dragged a black line through the chalk circle and out toward the waiting feet. [562] She had always known what an unpainted space could contain. [572]

Her brother stepped across the broken boundary first. [580] "There is room among us." [585] He recorded those words, then left a gap. [593] One by one, the others entered, speaking their names into it. [604]

At midnight, the shadow fell behind Mara, where a shadow belonged. [615] Thirty people staggered under a weight that had once crushed one. [626] They would need a roster, arguments, replacements; no clever sentence could abolish the work of holding a city. [644] When Ivo offered her a place on the roster, Mara said no. [656] He did not ask her to explain. [663]
