After friction disappeared, the village descended toward the sea, slowly enough for everyone to watch their homes become wrecks. [19] Mara watched from a tidal bluff under the aurora, buckled to an iron staple sunk through the rock. [37] Below her, storm eels turned in a sheltered pool, moving water backward to push themselves forward. [53] Pressure still worked; that distinction had saved her life. [62]

People had called her the elusive eel herder long before there was reason to hunt her. [78] She had spent twelve years following the sacred patterns of the eels, determined to understand what the temple forbade questioning. [98] Three nights ago, she had stolen the altar's collapsed star dust to test whether their devotion was merely attraction. [117] Opening its lead container had made her precognitively cursed: she saw, a heartbeat early, whatever her hands would touch. [136] It had also released the force that allowed one surface to catch against another. [150] That stolen resistance now burned overhead, lending the night a dawn the sun had not offered. [166]

She had promised her brother, upon leaving home, that she would return when she understood. [181] Now that lingering promise hurt worse than hunger, because understanding had come without an answer. [196] She knew why the world was falling apart, but not how to put it back. [211]

On the fourth false morning, a sailboat struck the bluff. [221] Her brother Tomas caught an overhang with a hooked pole and stayed there, trembling with effort. [237] His boat carried children, strapped beneath the benches. [245] Their silence frightened her more than any accusation Tomas could have shouted across the water. [260] He asked whether her learning could save the mill, whose foundation rings still tethered half the village. [277] The rings were tearing out. [282] Mara looked past him at the pool, where her eels kept circling although nothing could grip its smooth stone walls. [302] Tomas moved the iron pole, and the nearest eels shifted with it, correcting their formation rather than scattering. [320] Whenever their lights pulsed together, grains of sand briefly clung to the pool's sloping rim. [335]

Every turn repeated a fraction she had copied into her notebooks: one over one, two over one, three over two. [355] Each new numerator added the previous two; the ratios approached the proportion governing the temple's spirals. [371] But the eels were not praying. [377] They were following iron pigments spilled from her surveying box, trying to rebuild the magnetic field around the dust. [396] Their synchronized electrical pulses could replace the altar's coils, if she could harness them. [410] Mara began placing painted rocks in mathematical sequences, fitting each stone into a hollow so the tide could not dislodge it. [431] One, one, two, three, five: she built the temple's spiral in miniature, then lowered the container into its center. [450] Tomas brought the boat alongside and held her tether while she worked. [462] For once, she told him what she did not know. [472] The dust might anchor the world again, or pull the whole bluff down with it. [487] He looped her tether through his own belt. [495]

When Mara reached for the last rock, her curse showed her nothing. [507] No stone, no skin, no next heartbeat. [514] For twelve years she had wanted a pattern that made uncertainty unnecessary; now the only useful knowledge required stepping beyond it. [535] She set the rock. [539] The eels flashed white, their bodies writing a single bright equation around the dust. [553] Above them, the aurora folded inward. [559] Tomas's pole caught with a gritty scrape, and somewhere below the bluff a child began to cry. [576]

Mara woke against her brother's knees beneath an ordinary, starless sky. [587] Her hands were burned, but when he touched them, she felt him only in the present. [603] Beyond his shoulder, the mill stood crooked and still. [612] The other houses were gone. [617] She could not purchase their return with an explanation. [626] "I'll come home," she said, no longer adding a condition. [636] Tomas tightened her bandages, and they waited for daylight that would arrive without being borrowed. [651]
