At dawn's earliest whistle, the vagrant penny poet uncorked his jars beneath a ceiling tufted with marram roots. [18] Above him, wind found the broken chimney of the sailmaker's house and sounded the note that brought the foxes home. [38] Below that buried roof, his feral loft beneath the dunes held everything the advancing sand had spared. [55]

Capturing sounds in glass jars was the only trade he had left. [67] Night cooled the glass enough to hold an echo; sunrise fixed it there until someone lifted the lid. [85] Travelers paid a penny for a rhyme and another for a vanished village speaking its difficult old language. [103] He had learned to sweeten its grief for mainland buyers, translating funeral verses into charming nonsense that sold. [121] Eventually the surviving villagers stopped speaking when he approached. [130]

That morning Mira crawled through the chimney entrance with a drawing clutched between her teeth. [145] "Gran says this is still here," she said, smoothing the paper against his knee. [159] The sketch showed a forgotten music box with a swallow carved into its lid. [173] He recognized the sailmaker's wedding present to his wife, and the little girl recognized his hesitation. [189]

"She cannot come herself, and my ferry leaves at noon." [199]

Since her stroke, Mira's grandmother possessed only one dependable word, taken from the mainland language: stay. [215] Mira had earned a school place across the water, but every packed shirt had brought that word again. [233]

Together they dug the cupboard free, lifting sand in the poet's hat. [245] Inside, the box had survived wrapped in a sail, although rust had eaten half its comb. [261] Its surviving notes were positively wistful, little upward questions that refused to settle into mourning. [276]

The poet knew the missing notes. [282]

On his highest shelf stood a jar containing the grandmother singing over her husband's shroud. [297] He had sold that song as a comic complaint about a lazy fisherman. [310] He had kept the original because he could not bear either hearing it or letting it go. [327]

Mira asked him what the tune meant. [334]

He nearly gave her the easy answer: that her grandmother wanted her to go. [348] But making an old woman's voice say something convenient was how he had arrived here. [363]

Instead he carried the jar and the broken box to her rented room above the harbor. [379] The grandmother turned her face to the wall when she saw him. [391] He set his theft beside her bed and confessed, in the language in which he had first known shame. [410] Then he explained that opening it would spend the voice forever. [421]

Her good hand closed around the lid. [428] She twisted, and the room filled with a younger woman's breath. [439]

Outside, the sea glittered beyond the abandoned roofs, magnificent and indifferent; inside, someone long dead was being asked to travel gently. [460] The poet translated each line without polishing away its anger or its tenderness. [473]

"Go where I cannot follow; carry what I cannot keep." [483]

Mira laid her ticket beside the box. [490] Her grandmother pushed the ticket toward her, then caught her wrist with such desperation that the girl began to cry. [510]

"Both," the poet said, and for once he did not try to make a contradiction rhyme. [526] "She wants both." [529]

Until the ferry bell, they wrote the song between them, Mira supplying mainland words, her grandmother correcting with taps and stubborn shakes. [551] The poet listened, wanting now to become the translator between generations, not the man who sold one generation to another. [571] They left spaces where no honest equivalent would fit. [580] Mira would have to come back to learn those. [589]

At the gangway, she wound the music box and sang its missing notes herself. [603] Her grandmother did not wave until the boat had cleared the harbor. [615] Then she held out her hand, not to the poet, but for his pencil. [629]

On the back of his last unsold rhyme, she drew another house beneath the dunes. [644] Beside it she drew a cradle. [650]

There were other songs, then, and other things worth bringing back. [661] He took the empty jar home without asking her forgiveness. [671] Whatever redemption he gleaned would have to come this way, in scraps, with someone else deciding what should be saved. [691]
