In the rain-beaded skylight loft above the circus yard, Ada set the kettle on and placed an ash wood carving blank beside her husband's last minute. [26] The minute was a cloudy crystal, sealed by the insurers and delivered with their finding: operator error. [43] Downstairs, the apprentice accused of that error waited to learn whether she would sign. [57] Before becoming a lion tamer's widow, Ada had been the circus carpenter; grief had not quite abolished the trade. [76] The blank was meant to replace a broken grip on the refuge gate's bolt, a repair perpetually postponed until after Sunday's show. [98] They owned the circus together, though the posters showed only his face. [110] Ash absorbed shocks without shattering, but unshaped wood could protect nobody. [121]

"Only while the kettle boils," she told the crystal. [130] She had spent three nights watching her husband die, trying to turn repetition into understanding. [145] She wanted to harvest lessons from loss, she told herself, although what she actually wanted was somebody to blame. [164] The apprentice was seventeen and still had his whole life available for punishment. [177]

She turned the crystal until its red inclusion caught the gray skylight. [189] Through crystallized time, she stepped into the ring, though her fingers remained curled around the cold stone in her loft. [209] The recorder above the cage had preserved everything except mercy. [219]

Her husband stood inside with Solomon, whose stiff hind legs made him dangerous when the evening band struck up. [238] Ada remembered suggesting retirement for them both, and his answer that the winter feed still had to be bought. [257] Now the cymbals crashed, the old lion wheeled, and her husband ran toward the refuge gate. [273] The apprentice pulled at its bolt, lost his grip, and pulled again. One was bleeding where the bare iron had sliced the palm; the other strained against a bloom of rust. [326] Not hesitation, then, and not cowardice. [332] A missing handle, a sticking bolt, and a man who had trusted their promises to fix both after Sunday. [351] She remembered making that promise, with this very piece of ash already selected from her stock. [367]

The crystal offered no verdict, only her husband reaching the locked gate, here and gone as she shifted it in the light. [389] For three nights she had tried to contain him in that bright instant, as if looking hard enough could keep his next breath possible. Ada set the crystal on the empty chair beside her before the killing. [438] "A handle isn't a repair," she said, hearing the hauntingly practical voice she had used before everybody began speaking softly around her. [460] The whole mechanism needed replacing, and every gate needed a test performed by someone outside it before an animal entered.
