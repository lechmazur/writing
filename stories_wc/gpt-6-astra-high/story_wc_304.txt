In the skyborne grotto under the aurora, Mara laid out her brushes beside her mother's body as the floating mountain carried them steadily north. [24] She was a beautician mortician, trained to make a dead face recognizable not to its family, but to the last electricity inside it. [47] If the expression was true, that electricity would gather behind the eyelids and shine once before going out. [65] Families paid for that instant more willingly than they paid for bread. [77]

Tonight, Mara had no customer to please, only a promise she had made while her mother could still hear. [96] "Leave me looking like myself," the old pilot had said, lucidly watchful even as her fingers lost their grip on Mara's hand. [118] Mara had promised, then closed the eyes that had once guided whole villages through storms. [133] Now she wanted to capture one last spark before oblivion, and she wanted that spark to say her mother had not been afraid. [156]

From the pilot's old compass she removed an emerald chip, the one original piece left after her mother's first wreck. [176] Its peculiar fracture separated auroral noise into fine green threads, a property navigators used to find safe currents and morticians used to find the dead. [201] She set it between her mother's brows and connected it to the copper teeth of her comb. [218] Green snow flickered in the air above the table. [227]

Beyond the grotto's lip, the northern lights streamed silently over cloudbanks, their brilliance thinning toward the dark polar calm. [246] Ahead lay a threshold of stillness, the boundary beyond which no charge could pass between a body and the sky. [266] Mara had perhaps ten minutes before the last memories became merely flesh. [278]

Finding patterns in static was the first skill her teachers had taught, before powders, before the delicate stitching of a smile. [299] She watched the green threads knot into something like a fist, then scatter whenever she smoothed the furrow between her mother's eyebrows. [321] With wax she softened the pinched mouth, remembering the woman who could moor a house to a ledge no wider than its doorstep. [344] The threads broke into a furious glitter. [351] She tried again, building the calm expression she had trusted all her life, and the emerald began to dim. [370]

For the first time in twenty years of work, Mara considered painting a convincing peace and telling herself it had shone. [391] Her brush hovered over the mouth. [397] Then she put it down and scraped away the wax. [407]

The fist returned, opening now into five small fingers beside five larger ones, all trembling around a frayed length of rope. [428] Mara knew that rope: she had been six when her mother's first ship broke against a cliff. [445] She remembered being lowered into a rescue basket, her mother above her, apparently untroubled by the empty miles beneath their feet. [466] But the static carried what the child had not noticed: teeth pressed into a lip, wet lashes, hands shaking without letting go. [488] All these years, Mara had mistaken being held safely for the absence of fear in the person holding her. [507] She touched the crease she had been trying to erase. [517]

At the grotto's entrance, the last curtain of aurora narrowed to a luminous seam. [531] There was no time to begin again, only to stop correcting what was already there. [546] Mara mixed pearl cream on her wrist and used the comb to infuse it with the emerald's trembling charge. [565] She brushed it thinly over the brow, the temples, the mouth, following every line instead of filling it. [583]

The green threads drew together. [588] For one breath, light gathered beneath her mother's eyelids, illuminating an expression neither peaceful nor pretty, but intent on something beyond its own terror. [612] Mara recognized herself at six, reflected in that attention. [621] She took the hand that could no longer hold hers. [631] "I was frightened too," she said, no longer asking either of them to be otherwise. [646]

The mountain crossed into stillness, and the light went out. [656] Mara left the emerald where it was and sat beside her mother while the stars emerged, keeping watch without pretending she could keep her. [680]
