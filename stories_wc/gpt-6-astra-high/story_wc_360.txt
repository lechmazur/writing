Nera habitually borrowed personalities the way other letterpress operators borrowed type, becoming precise as a surgeon for contracts and tender as a midwife for condolences. [25] In her topaz workshop, open to the stars, she kept her own temperament locked in a compartment labeled UNTESTED. [44] Customers called her cryptic because she answered questions with questions, but uncertainty was also what made her oldest press work. [64]

Clamped above its platen was a halo fragment, the broken gold arc of a saint whose particular miracle had been feeling another person's pain. [88] It conducted transposed empathy only when neither participant could tell exactly whose feelings were whose. [103] Clear statements produced nothing; deliberate ambiguity disoriented the fragment's ancient distinction between self and other. [118] She charged for paper and ink, never for forgiveness, which she had not yet learned to calculate. [135]

Tonight Ruth arrived with Captain Vale, who had brought twenty passengers home from a wreck and left Ruth's daughter beneath the water. [157] At dawn, Ruth would testify at his trial about the broken alarm he had ignored before departure. [174] She wanted to know whether he had let the child drown because rescuing her was impossible or merely dangerous. [193] Vale wanted Ruth to stop calling him a murderer. [202] Nera wanted to understand the mathematics of emotion, and their opposing demands seemed, at first, like two sides of an equation. [223]

Starlight passing through the topaz fixed the two emotional positions against the operator's own, like a surveyor sighting two shores. [243] Only between the vanishing of the paired stars behind the black moon could those positions be exchanged. [260] The first star was already trembling against the moon's invisible rim. [271]

Nera borrowed a judge's composure and set the sentence I LEFT HER THERE in reverse. [286] Ruth had left her daughter aboard the ferry while she fetched their luggage; Vale had left her trapped below. [305] Both could truthfully claim the words, and neither could safely own them. [317] Each held a copper handle wired to the platen, gripping it as if the other might otherwise pull them overboard. [337] When the first star vanished, Nera pulled the lever. [346]

Through the stone floor ran a brightness like water seen from underneath. [358] Ruth tasted Vale's terror as the flooded stairwell twisted, while Vale felt the weight of a little coat Ruth still carried folded in her bag. [383] Nera watched the gauges, expecting the two sufferings to balance. [393] Instead, both needles climbed. [397] Ruth doubled over, and Vale clawed at his throat. [406] The fragment was multiplying their distress: each now felt the other's horror, and that horror returned magnified. [423]

Nera reached for a physician's detachment, then stopped with her hand inside the drawer. [437] Her borrowed composure was another displaced feeling; she had measured everyone except the person holding the instrument. [454] Under all her elegant substitutions lived a frightened woman who preferred understanding people to being answerable to them. [472] She took out the slip labeled UNTESTED and read her own name aloud. [485] On its blank reverse, she printed I AM HERE, leaving the speaker deliberately uncertain. [499] Then she pressed her bare palms against theirs. [507] Her fear entered the circuit, but so did her knowledge of the solid floor, the cool press, the air still available to breathe. [530] With a third position fixed in the present, their borrowed memories stopped feeding endlessly back into each other. [548] The second star vanished, closing the exchange. [555]

Ruth stepped away from Vale without embracing him. [563] At the trial, she would describe both the twisted stairwell he had encountered and the broken alarm he had ignored before departure. [585] His terror had been real; it did not make his negligence unreal. [597] Vale asked her daughter's name, and waited through her tears for it. [609] He did not ask to be forgiven. [616]

Nera wrote no sum in her notebook. [623] Instead, she drew three points, connected them, and left room around each for what the others could not know. [642] When Ruth thanked her, she said she was sorry she had hurt them, and did not disguise it as a riddle. [663]
