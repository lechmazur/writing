After the hydraulics failed, the inland terraces went dry, and the people who had fled the sea began walking back toward it. [22] They called it reverse migration, as if reversing direction could undo what the journey had cost. [38] Mara knew better: her son was not waiting on the coast, no matter how much land the retreating water uncovered. [58]

At the starlit signal station, eighty refugees watched her unroll her coat across a table. [73] It was sewn from waterproof charts, each patch a drowned district whose surviving pilots had taught her their channels. [92] That was how she had become a patchwork navigator: by learning what other people could no longer bear to remember. [112] Now the charts showed three different routes across a tidal basin, and all three ended where the paper had been mended. [133] Beyond the basin were working wells, according to the station telephone. [144]

She had learned this through cryptic phone calls at midnight, when a sympathetic operator took over the inland exchange. [163] The caller was Eren, the coast keeper, whose boat had been confiscated for carrying families without return permits. [181] He dared not name a crossing on a recorded line. [191] Instead he said, "Your mother's doorstep stays clean when the neighbors wash their floors." [205] Mara pictured the raised stone threshold where she had cleaned her son's muddy feet. [219] Their street had stood on a ridge. [226] Beneath the basin, that ridge might still hold. [234]

On the next call, Eren told her to cut the moon like bread, into equal shares. [250] "The current fluctuates with moon phases," she told the families, "and he's telling us to cross at the quarter moon." [270] A man asked whether she trusted a road she could not see. [282] "No," Mara said, and began to build a sounding raft from the station's floorboards and empty oil drums. [300]

She needed a weight heavy enough to sink a line, with a hollow to hold tallow and bring up samples of the bottom. [323] In her pocket lay a bronze censer foot, its green patina worn bright at the toes. [339] Her son had pressed it into her hand before boarding the evacuation boat that never reached the hills. [357] He had stolen it from the temple because its clawed toes made him laugh. [371] She had carried it for twelve years, polishing away a little more of the sea whenever she was afraid. [390] Now she packed its empty screw socket with candle grease and tied it to the line. [406]

At dawn beneath the divided moon, she poled out, lowering the foot every few yards. [421] Black mud came first, then pale grit that matched the limestone in the station steps. [436] She marked the buried road with poles and strung a rope between them. [449] Behind her, the families began wading, their smallest children riding the raft. [461]

Halfway across, the sounding line jerked taut. [468] The bronze toes had caught in something below. [476] Mara pulled until the cord opened an old scar across her palm. [488] Beneath her lay the bend where her street had been, where a child had once run ahead of her laughing at those ridiculous bronze toes. [513] She could dive. [516] The thought arrived whole, hungry, almost sweet. [523]

Then a girl on the raft asked whether the rope was supposed to be underwater. [538] The tide had turned. [542] Mara looked at the eighty faces stretched behind her and cut the sounding line. [556] She did not feel released. [561] She felt robbed, and furious, and kept moving. [569]

Eren met them on the far bank with buckets of water and a ledger of empty houses. [586] Mara drank, then vomited, then drank again while somebody held her hair. [598] When he asked what she wanted, she nearly said the name of her son. [612] Instead she asked for paper. [617] She drew the road they had crossed, including the bend where the censer foot remained, with the moon phase and the hours of safe passage. [642] To navigate the geography of loss, she was discovering, was not to find a way around what hurt. [660] It was to mark where the footing held, so the next person could carry someone through.
