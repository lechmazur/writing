On the first evening after the ban lifts, Ilen returns to the cobalt atrium by a frozen lake. [18] Once the valley's cloud-shadow poet, she now carries a government permit to revise the memorial she wrote thirty years ago. [38] Behind her comes Fen, an apprentice miller whose empty flour sack holds their lamp. [52] He hangs it on a nail, and a single moth begins circling the wick. [66] Neither speaks above that lantern-moth hush. [72]

The humming quartz colonnade surrounds a floor engraved with the names of everyone drowned when the lake burst its banks. [92] Each pillar keeps a voice, caught by the stoneworkers from the water before winter sealed it. [108] Ilen's mother occupies the narrowest crystal, still calling for someone to throw a rope. [122]

Above the names, Ilen's dedication binds the voices to the lake: "Let nothing we love pass beyond us." [140] She had meant death, not water. [146] But quartz cannot distinguish a metaphor from an instruction, and the first spring never came. [161] The priests declared the unmelting lake a miracle and forbade anyone to alter its sacred text. [176] For years the valley ate imported grain while its own irrigation channels lay dry. [190] This winter, the grain road closed under an avalanche, and hunger finally outweighed reverence. [204]

Fen sets a chisel beside the lamp. [211] "The council says we can break them if you fail," he whispers. [223] Ilen touches the scar where an earlier reformer's hammer struck a pillar; frost immediately whitens her fingernail. [240] "Breaking only gives the command more surfaces from which to speak." [251]

She has come to stride through boundaries once thought sacred, yet cannot put her boot across her mother's name. [270] Instead, she kneels and takes out the smoked glass she once used to study clouds. [285] Her poems had followed their shadows across hillsides, finding words for shapes that vanished before anyone could claim them. [304] The memorial was her first permanent work, and she had been proud of its perfect, terrible economy. [321] The aesthetically driven young Ilen had rejected a mason's plain suggestion: "We will remember them." [336] Now the unnecessary grandeur lies around her in acres of ice. [347]

"Then write something different," Fen says. [353] She shakes her head; a contrary order would set the pillars against one another, crushing the atrium between their songs. [373] She must paraphrase the dedication, carrying its love into another meaning without pretending that love was wrong. [390]

Through the smoked glass, moonlit clouds drift over the open roof, their shadows joining letters that the engraver kept apart. [410] For a moment, her mother's name shares its final stroke with a stranger's. [423] A tremor of certainty passes through Ilen: the lake must open as her poems did, through dissolving boundaries. [441] Not a gate for one beloved voice, but water in which no voice remains separate. [456] Her mother will stop calling. [461]

Ilen lowers the glass. [465] She has listened to that cry every week for thirty years, always leaving before the desperate breath that follows. [484] Tonight she stays through the breath, and hears Fen's stomach answer with a small, embarrassed growl. [500] Her mother used to cut her own portion smaller whenever another child arrived at supper. [515] That is something the quartz has never said. [523]

Ilen lays her palm against the narrow pillar and waits until its humming enters her teeth. [539] Then, matching its note, she speaks: "Let everything we love pass into what we do." [554] The first attempt earns only a crack in her lip. [564] She repeats it less beautifully, allowing the words to stumble, until Fen understands and adds his untrained voice. [582]

Their breath clouds the crystal, softening the outlines of the names. [593] Beneath them, frost loosens into beads, and the separate notes of the colonnade run together like thawing streams. [611] Her mother's cry rises once, almost intelligible, then becomes the sound of water finding a channel. [627] Ilen wants to snatch it back. [633] Instead she takes Fen's hand and steps across the wet place where the name has disappeared. [649]

Outside, the lake shifts in its sleep. [656] There will be sluices to open before morning, banks to inspect, seed to borrow from beyond the mountains. [674] Fen retrieves his sack, and Ilen carries the lamp. [683] At the door, she cups her free hand around the moth until they reach the shelter of the mill. [702]
