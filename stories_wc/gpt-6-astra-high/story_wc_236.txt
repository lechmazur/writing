Within the hush of morning fog, Ada carried her mother's water clock into the repair shop, holding it like a sleeping child. [22] The old midwife had been buried yesterday, and the clock had stopped years before, although Ada preferred not to think about that. [44] She wanted it running for the gathering at noon: one small, dependable miracle beside the photograph everyone called saintly. [63] Ivo cleared a place among glass reservoirs, copper basins, and clocks that measured time by surrendering water instead of winding springs. [84]

He asked whether she was still making remedies, keeping his right hand beneath the bench. [99] Ada had been an aromatherapist until a fever took her sense of smell and left every bottle an accusation. [118] Without lavender or bitter orange to guide her, she no longer trusted herself to offer comfort, much less charge for it. [139] She told him she was resting, borrowing the word her mother used for sitting alone in an unlit kitchen. [158]

Ivo turned the clock around, revealing a clamp pinching its rubber outlet tube. [171] At its heart sat a midwife's birthing clamp screw, its flattened head polished by her mother's thumb. [188] During the district's blackouts, her mother had used this makeshift regulator to keep the water falling at a steady, countable pace. [209] Women in labor had breathed between the drops; now corrosion held the screw immovable. [223]

When Ivo reached for his pliers, his fingers shook hard enough to rattle the other tools. [239] He covered them with a rag, but Ada had spent years watching clients choose the chair farthest from the door. [259] She knew how a body maps emotional terrain: the defended wrist, the carefully casual shoulder, the eyes seeking an exit. [279] She moved her chair beside his rather than opposite, leaving the door within his view. [294]

He offered to replace the whole clamp, and she heard herself refuse too sharply. [308] That battered screw had once closed a cord clamp; she needed to believe useful things could survive the work they lost. [329]

Ivo opened his repair ledger instead, placing it where she could read without leaning across him. [345] Beside her mother's name, the same entries alternated for decades: flow too fast, flow too slow, no payment required. [364] Ada followed the ruled lines, remembering evenings when the clock sat outside its cupboard and her mother scarcely spoke. [383] Reading between lines had once meant detecting the sorrow beneath a request for something to help with sleep. [401] Here, between adjustments too small to charge for, she began to recognize another kind of appointment. [417]

Ivo said, "Sometimes she just needed somewhere to be frightened before she went home to you." [433] Ada had wanted him to certify a saint, not humanize the woman whose silences she had mistaken for disappointment. [452] Yet the thought of her mother sitting here, frightened and welcome, loosened something beneath her ribs. [468]

She asked Ivo to tell her what his hands could no longer do, and he uncovered them. [485] They soaked the screw with oil while the fog pressed against the windows and every working clock whispered into its basin. [506] Because a mineral crust clouded the reservoir, they could inspect the screw's threads only through occluded views: a narrow reflection, a sliver beneath the brass rim. [532] Ivo held the lamp with both hands, and Ada turned the pliers a fraction, then waited as he instructed. [551] "Not stronger," he said, "only patient." [557]

The screw yielded with a tiny complaint, and water touched the basin once, twice, then too quickly. [574] Ada eased it back until the drops left room for a slow breath between them. [589] Neither spoke for a while; her hands rested beside his, both pairs trembling, neither pair hidden. [605]

She would bring the clock to the gathering, but she would tell them about the chair, too. [622] And when she reopened her practice, she would explain plainly what the fever had taken, instead of letting silence make promises. [643] She could still ask where someone hurt, still notice the untouched cup and the hand withdrawn when a particular name was spoken. [665] She had gone into her work to unveil ordinary miracles, then somehow credited the bottles for everything people gave each other. [686] Before lifting the clock, she asked whether Ivo would like company until the fog cleared. [701] He nodded, and she settled beside him while the small, repaired thing gave them another minute.
