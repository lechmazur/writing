After the carousel halted, the lagoon went on turning above the clouds, its silver currents dragging empty boats past silent music pipes. [22] Nera knelt in the souvenir booth and pried a disposable camera wheel from its crushed housing with her remaining thumbnail. [42] Outside, a wooden horse's raised hoof trembled, although nothing else did. [53] She had six minutes before the carousel's stored momentum ran out and the lagoon began to fall. [70]

The emergency sluices could lower it safely, but their key had surfaced that morning in the fist of her own ghost. [91] In these waters, the future's drowned sometimes arrived early. [100] They surrendered nothing by force. [105] You had to settle whatever had kept them from leaving. [115]

Nera had spent thirty years scavenging the lagoon, although on certain mornings she was convinced she had only just arrived. [135] Diving through its crossed currents had tangled her chronology until she remembered her mother's funeral as something she must dress for next week. [158] But her hands remembered their work. [164] She fitted the wheel into the camera she carried inside her coat and tested its small, forward bite. [182] One exposure remained. [185]

At seventeen she had reserved that last photograph for the harbor she would build: stone quays, permanent houses, nothing dependent on a machine. [208] Her ghost waited beside the sluice tower, wearing the face she had at seventeen above hands more wrinkled than hers. [228] It had refused her twice already. [234] "Show me what we built," it had said, and she had shown it nothing. [248]

This time she took the crowded evacuation walkway, collecting fragments of overheard stories because she no longer trusted the story inside her head. [271] "Nera's beams held through the winter," a ferryman told his wife. [282] Those had been her first shipment of quay timbers, she remembered, cut apart to brace his leaking hull. [300] Farther along, a teacher was counting children beneath a patched sail. [311] "Stay on the steps Nera fixed," he said to someone small. [322] She remembered selling her survey instruments for those bolts, and the sour satisfaction of having another reason to postpone her harbor. [343] Then a woman said, "She was going to leave once, but she stayed when the fever came." [360] Nera did not remember that woman at all. [368]

By the tower, the water had slowed enough to hold reflections. [379] Her ghost watched the camera. [384] "You promised us something worth keeping," it said. [392] Beyond its shoulder, the unfinished harbor piles stood like rotten teeth. [403] Every year she had taken something from that skeleton for a repair, then avoided looking at what remained. [421]

"I regret the harbor," Nera said, surprised by how sharply the admission hurt. [434] "I regret pretending I would finish it, and making every useful thing I did feel like theft." [451] The ghost closed its fist. [456] Beneath them came a soft crack, followed by a silence in which Nera could hear individual drops striking the clouds. [476] "We failed," it said. [480] She wanted to contradict it, but the piles were there, diminished by her own hands. [495] Instead, she repeated the ferryman's words, then the teacher's, then the stranger's account of a woman who had stayed. [514] She offered no larger story to bind them together. [555] "Not proof that we finished," she said, "just proof that we were here." [568] For a moment the young face looked unbearably old. [577] Then the key rang against the railing. [584]
