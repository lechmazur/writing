After the business closed, Mara counted the maze's final receipts and put the developer's unsigned contract beneath the till. [19] For twenty years, visitors had paid to get lost between its hedges, never finding the mystic orchard garden hidden at its heart. [41] They bought postcards and complained about the price of tea. [51] Mara knew the way but no longer went. [59]

Tonight, an audience applauded where there was no audience, and the sound tasted of the blackberry cordial her mother used to make. [81] Synesthetically blessed, her mother had called her, back when every voice had a flavor and every note a visible thread. [101] Mara followed that familiar sweetness through seven turns the printed map omitted. [113] In the orchard, apples shone like footlights around a little wooden stage. [125]

Her mother's ghost stood beneath the boughs, wearing the blue dress in which she had collapsed halfway through her farewell song. [146] "An encore," she said, as she always said. [154] "Darling, give me tomorrow." [158]

Behind her, the fractured timeline flickered among the trees: Mara at eleven, Mara grown old, the stage demolished, the stage being built. [180] The orchard had always ripened possible tomorrows, but her mother's unfinished performance had caught them on one unending evening. [199] No wonder Mara could imagine nothing beyond opening the kiosk and closing it again. [213]

In her pocket lay the hand-painted tarot coin her mother had used for the finale, a Tower on one face, a Star on the other. [238] She had kept it when she sold the costumes, the benches, and the piano nobody played anymore. [255] The painted images were crooked because Mara had made them, aged seven, when her mother first invited her into the act. [276]

"We never toss it," her mother used to tell the audience. [287] "A future is not a verdict." [293] Instead, she rang it against the stage rail and let Mara choose a song from its colors. [310]

Now Mara struck the rail with the coin, making the Tower and the Star ring together. [326] The note came back from the hedges in silver, then peach, then the warm brown of their old kitchen. [345] Her mother began the farewell song, reached the broken phrase, and vanished before its final word. [361] A moment later she stood waiting again, smiling with the terrible patience of someone who could not count her disappointments. [381]

"An encore," she said. [385]

Mara almost fled, but the coin's echoes were still returning from the maze, each arriving a little changed. [403] One carried the squeak of her childhood shoes; another held a melody she had never heard. [419] The garden was not asking her to recover the missing ending. [430] It was offering somewhere else to go. [437]

Mara struck the coin again, then again, producing echoes until colored threads hung between every hedge and branch. [455] She began to intertwine them with her voice, holding the silver note of yesterday beneath the strange peach melody of tomorrow. [476] For twenty years she had mistaken despair for loyalty; now she sang to lure possibility back from its edge. [495]

Her mother's smile faltered, and for the first time the ghost looked directly at the woman her daughter had become. [515]

"You remembered," she whispered. [519]

"Not all of it," Mara said, and kept singing. [528]

The kitchen brown brightened into summers of jam jars, rehearsals, bare feet sticky with fallen pears. [544] Her mother joined her, not as the star awaiting applause but as the harmony she had once sung over the washing up. [566] The competing evenings drew together around their voices, and the apples began, softly, to fall. [581]

At the final note, the ghost bowed to her daughter. [591] This time Mara applauded only once the silence was complete, and her mother stepped into it without starting over. [610]

At dawn, Mara called the developer and refused his offer. [620] She had no plan beyond selling tea again and advertising an evening concert, with herself on the little stage. [639] That frightened her, which was how she knew tomorrow had finally arrived. [651] She pocketed the coin and opened the gate, humming a tune that still tasted faintly of blackberries. [668]
