Eleven years after the fossil fuel ban, Mara returned to the sea level monitoring station with her father's ashes and one small suitcase. [23] The station stood bolted to basalt, although the slow passage of clouds made its windows seem to sail. [41] She had accepted his old job because the island council needed a keeper, and because grief preferred measurements to condolences. [61]

At the coastal hearings, she had been the coral bleaching witness. [72] She had described watching a reef turn white while fish continued searching its branches, faithful to a vanished country. [91] Now she wanted something that could still be kept. [100]

Inside the tide ledger lay an atlas page torn out along its binding, her father's red arrows crisscrossing the island's narrow footpaths. [122] Across the water he had written, "Periodically eternal." [130] It was his old joke about causeways: drowned twice daily, restored twice daily. [143]

But at her first ebb, the path to their house did not return. [156] Through binoculars she could see pale rectangular hollows where its paving stones had been. [170] Two other paths were missing; the broad road toward the abandoned hotel remained. [183]

She checked the gauge against the brass benchmark, then assembled twenty years of tide sheets across the floor. [201] The ban had stopped the burning, not the slow expansion of warmed seas or the collapse of distant ice. [220] The hours when people could cross safely were dwindling. [229] Still, water alone could not explain those clean, empty sockets. [239]

At the next low tide she went down with the atlas page folded inside her coat. [255] Her father had repaired these paths before he became keeper, testing each slab by tapping a little rhythm with his hammer. [276] Three quick notes, one slow: sound stone rang; fractured stone answered dully. [288] As a child, she had danced behind him. [296] Now, with his old hammer from the shed, she tapped the surviving edge. [309] It rang clear. [312] Beside it, a fresh lever mark gleamed above the weed. [322] Beyond it, cracked slabs remained untouched in their beds. [331] Someone was choosing which slabs to take. [338]

Beyond the hotel dunes, the same four beats came faintly through the wind. [351] She followed them to six islanders laying a raised causeway along the buried spine of an older road. [369] Its unfinished end pointed toward the station. [376]

"That's our front step," she said, seeing a blue slab swinging in a rope sling. [391] The woman guiding it lowered her hands. [398] "Your father marked that one himself." [404]

Mara unfolded the atlas page, ready to show where everything belonged. [415] Only then, with the new road before her, did she understand his arrows. [428] They did not mark floodwater entering the island; they marked stone leaving its lowest paths. [443] The hotel road was higher, useful still, but the routes to the shore houses had become traps between tides. [462] Her father had planned their dismantling. [468] He had included his own doorstep. [474]

"We couldn't finish before he died," the woman said. [483] "The station is the storm refuge now, but people need a road that stays above water to reach it." [502]

Mara looked back toward the house, its windows silver with sky. [513] She had imagined scattering his ashes there, making a ceremony of keeping him where he had always been. [531] He had spent his last winter arranging to leave, one stone at a time. [545]

The woman offered her the loose end of the sling. [555] Mara took it, then stopped them before they lifted. [564] She knelt and tapped the blue slab: three quick notes, one slow. [576] The last note faltered. [580] Underneath, a crack ran through the stone where decades of feet had polished its face. [595]

"Not this one," she said, and surprised herself by being able to say it. [609] They broke it for the foundation and fitted a sound slab above it. [622]

By dusk, they had assembled enough road to bring the first supply cart across without wetting its wheels. [640] Nothing they did could rebuild the reef she had watched die. [651] Nor would this road last forever. [657] But she understood now what her father had meant by periodically eternal: not a promise, a practice. [674]

That evening, she fetched his ashes from the station and scattered them beside the new foundations. [690] The tide came feeling through the empty sockets below, while behind her someone tapped another stone. [706]
