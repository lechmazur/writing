Twenty-one years after her emancipation from state care, Mara returned to the foster care file room with a county archivist's badge and her dead sister's biscuit tin. [27] The room was being emptied for demolition; she had six days to decide what the county would remember. [45] She had requested the assignment before Lena died, when the work still seemed like something she could bring home. [64]

Inside the tin lay an infantryman's button, its brass eagle polished almost faceless by Lena's thumb. [80] She had owned it since their first placement, but nobody had explained whose uniform it belonged to. [97] Lena called it their inheritance: one thing too cheap for anybody to reclaim. [110] Mara had laughed with her, mistaking agreement for comfort. [119]

The placement register supplied dates, expenses, and a description of Lena as unusually demanding. [133] Mara remembered a child who hid bread beneath her pillow and apologized when she coughed. [148] Beside the property inventory, however, someone had written: Button retained by child; accompanying letter filed in overflow. [165] The room's catalog listed no overflow. [171]

Her supervisor offered an extra pair of hands, gently suggesting that grief might make ordinary clerical errors look deliberate. [190] Mara knew this; she also knew that an error could last longer than the person it injured. [207] She asked him to photograph everything before they moved it. [217]

They reconstructed the room's alterations through accumulated dust patterns: rectangular shadows of vanished cabinets, deep drifts behind fixed shelves, narrow trails beneath drawers. [240] Behind the register cabinet, two undisturbed ridges outlined a passage just wide enough for a hand. [256] The dust thickened toward its entrance but thinned over the lip of a wooden tray wedged farther back. [274] Someone had once used that space regularly. [281] On the tray, beneath fallen plaster, lay a packet marked OVERFLOW in red pencil. [295]

Mara used both palms to unfurl a little rolled map enclosed with an envelope addressed to the girls. [313] A penciled route connected the children's hospital to their first foster home, crossing two bus lines. [329] The letter's writer introduced himself as Arthur Bell, a hospital porter and formerly an infantryman. [344] He hoped they remembered his coat. [350]

"Your little sister worried a button loose while we waited for her breathing to settle," he had written. [368] "I said she could keep it, since she had done most of the work." [382] "If visits are allowed, I would be glad to come on Sundays, with something for both of you." [400] "No promises about cake until I learn the rules." [409]

Mara read the last line twice. [415] Its tactfully hopeful voice seemed almost familiar, though memory offered only a corridor's green floor and somebody kneeling. [433] She would not turn those scraps into a recovered scene merely because she needed one. [448] On the envelope, a clerk had penciled Awaiting visitor approval. [458] There was no answer, and no evidence that the letter had ever reached either child. [473]

For an hour she wanted a culprit more than she wanted the truth. [486] Then she checked the transfer dates and found that the girls had moved again eleven days after Arthur wrote. [505] Whether he had been refused, forgotten, or redirected belonged among the unknown histories the files could not settle. [523] The offered journey was real; the visit was not hers to invent. [535]

She wanted to telephone Lena, who would have made a joke about the cake before asking to see the map. [555] Instead, Mara sat on the dusty floor and cried without protecting the papers from the sound. [571] Finding kindness did not cancel what had happened afterward. [580] It made her sister's absence sharper: there was something new to tell her. [593]

By Friday, Mara had changed the preservation plan. [601] Unanswered letters would be indexed alongside placement orders, with access restricted to the people whose lives they concerned. [619] She could not promise reunions, or even explanations. [627] But to magnify small kindnesses, she decided, was not to make them larger than they were; it was to stop filing them where nobody could see. [653] She photographed the button beside Arthur's letter, then returned it to the tin. [666] Under its inventory number she entered what she knew, leaving the rest blank: Given freely, to comfort a frightened child. [686]
