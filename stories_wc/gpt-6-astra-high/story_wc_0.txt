After the flood, Leena came home from the observatory to persuade her father to dismantle the greenhouse the storm had damaged. [21] He had raised butterflies there for village classrooms, but now the river had left silt inside every pot and glass among the nettles. [44] At seventy, he should not be kneeling beneath a crooked roof, insisting that something might still emerge. [61] She arranged a demolition estimate for Friday and called it practical kindness. [73]

As a neutron star researcher, Leena earned her living extracting events from noise, and she wanted to strip needless drama from this disaster, too. [97] Drama meant her father's silences, her own guilt about living abroad, and the dangerous hope that everything could be restored. [117]

To check for survivors, she photographed the greenhouse each morning and adapted a filter she used to find sudden stellar flares. [138] "It preprocesses reality," she explained when her father asked why her screen showed almost nothing. [153] "Anything that stays much the same gets subtracted, so the important changes stand out." [167] He looked from the blank screen to the benches, where sodden seed envelopes had begun to decay. [184] He asked who had decided which changes mattered. [192]

On Thursday, clearing the benches, Leena found black cards beneath several rows of hanging chrysalises that looked like shriveled leaves. [212] Each card carried a faint bronze powder. [219] Under her pocket microscope, the grains became overlapping scales, ridged like the roof tiles of impossibly small houses. [237] Her father had shown children dust from a butterfly wing on these cards, explaining why they must never pinch one. [257]

"Old material," she said, then disliked the relief in her voice. [268] She laid fresh cards beneath the chrysalises, more to settle the argument than to reopen it. [284] By Friday there were only three scales, too few to establish anything, so she asked the contractor for another week. [303] Her father said nothing triumphant, which made the telephone call harder. [314]

All week, she took close photographs of the cards and watched her filter dismiss their differences as insignificant. [332] That was appropriate for detecting flares, but she had mistaken a useful exclusion for a verdict. [348] Neutron stars also cooled between outbursts; those slow histories required patient measurements, not an alarm. [363] She stopped subtracting yesterday and began layering the photographs, marking every new scale instead of demanding a dramatic cluster. [382] Through accumulated dust patterns, a small geography emerged: almost every addition lay beneath the bench nearest the brick wall. [401] Wind could have carried old scales there, so she still refused to call the pattern proof. [417] But it told her where to sit. [424]

On Wednesday afternoon, watching from the doorway, she saw a chrysalis split above a card beside the wall. [442] The butterfly pulled itself free with an effort so small and complete that she felt embarrassed by her wish for revelation. [463] It hung there, pumping fluid into folded wings, while sunlight warmed the bricks that had sheltered the surviving chrysalises. [482] Nothing was returning to its former condition. [489] Something was continuing from what remained. [495]

She found her father sorting unbroken pots beside the kitchen, his injured thumb bound in a strip of clean cloth. [515] For the first time, she asked what he actually wanted to save, rather than explaining what he could no longer manage. [536] He wanted one sound bay, enough nettles for caterpillars, and somewhere children could wait without being told to hurry. [555] He had never asked for the whole greenhouse back. [564]

They paid the contractor to brace the brick end and remove the dangerous roof, leaving a smaller structure they could afford to reglaze. [587] Before work began, they moved the occupied chrysalises with their supporting twigs into a mesh enclosure on the kitchen windowsill. [607] Most of the seed packets went into the compost, and her father cried over a variety he had kept for thirty years. [629] Leena stayed beside him without offering a more efficient interpretation of the loss. [642]

Later, she taped a fresh black card beneath the enclosure and wrote the date in pencil. [658] There would be no spectacular upward curve, only scales, empty cases, leaves eaten down and grown again. [675] When her father brought two chairs to the windowsill, she put her phone away and helped him find the warmer patch of sun. [698]
