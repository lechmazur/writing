After the beekeeper's last visit, the mountain pass froze, and no one brought honey or news to the grotto city behind the monumental waterfall. [24] Inside its hollow cliffs, the displaced persons slept beneath museum tapestries while rosewater burned in silver lamps to disguise the smell of damp bedding. [48] Whenever the waterfall shook the walls, sleepers reached for bundles already lost. [60]

Mara, the museum's guide, had acquired her cultured voice in this same refuge thirty years earlier, learning to pronounce dynasties without betraying her village. [84] She considered herself historically oriented: the past, unlike the present, could be arranged. [97] Then the trustees announced a supper to fund the rescue of dying arts. [110] Before the guests arrived, the families would be moved into the unheated drainage tunnels. [124] Mara was asked to select something exquisite. [131]

She chose a baking manuscript from her native valley, its crimson lettering almost invisible. [145] Through an emerald-tinted opera glass lens, salvaged from the theater where her father had sung, the faded red strokes darkened enough to read. [168] The page showed bread shaped like initials, a family custom once practiced throughout the valley. [183] At weddings and births, each person received a loaf bearing the first letter of their name. [199] Mara remembered eating hers before her mother could finish explaining its shape. [211]

She proposed reproductions for the supper, and the trustees authorized flour, fuel, and the last jar of the beekeeper's honey. [231] But the manuscript said only to wake the mother, then fold until the dough remembered. [246] Her first attempts lay on their trays like severed fingers. [256] An old woman watching from the bedding asked why she was baking letters nobody could recognize. [272]

"The illustrations are exact," Mara said. [278]

"They show the underside, so the baker can check the seams." [289] The woman turned the manuscript around. [295]

Her name was Sela, though the accommodation register called her Seventy. [306] From her bundle she produced a crock of starter, frozen during the flight over the pass. [322] Mara reached toward the stove, but Sela caught her wrist. [332]

"Let it thaw against your body, or the outside cooks while the heart stays ice." [347]

All afternoon Mara carried the crock beneath her silk waistcoat, explaining imperial porcelain to donors while its cold rim pressed her ribs. [369] By evening, tiny bubbles had appeared. [375]

Sela worked through the night, teaching Mara how a thumbprint distinguished one family's letters from another's, and where to leave room for the rise. [399] Children crept from under the tapestries to watch their names take shape. [411] One boy refused to say his name; Sela left his dough covered until he felt safe enough. [428]

Before dawn, the porters came with carts for the families' belongings. [439] Mara stood between them and the sleepers, smelling hot honey through the rosewater. [452] She had intended to thank Sela, display the loaves, and call the art preserved. [466] That intention now seemed more shameful than the order she held. [477] She sent the carts back empty. [483]

At supper, she placed the opera glass lens beside the manuscript and served the breads without labels. [500] When the chairman requested her usual commentary, she introduced Sela instead. [511] The old woman gave him dough and showed him how to form his initial without closing the spaces that would swell shut in the oven. [536] He spoiled it twice, laughing uneasily as flour settled on his cuffs. [548]

"This is what your subscription can save," Mara told the table, then laid down the order transferring their teacher to the tunnels. [570]

Under the waterfall's steady thunder, nobody spoke. [577] The chairman said housing was not the museum's purpose. [586] Mara answered that a manuscript had never corrected her hands. [596] She offered her savings for a teaching bakery, knowing it could cover scarcely a month. [611] Across the table, other donors quietly matched it. [619]

By midnight they had purchased a winter, not a future: flour, beds above the floodline, and wages for Sela and two apprentices. [641] Mara's name was removed from the guide roster the following morning. [652] She took the lens to the bakery anyway; there were still recipes to decipher, and Sela said her folding needed work. [673]

The lamps still smelled of rosewater, and beyond the warm room the tunnels waited. [687] But the silent boy came to her carrying his covered dough. [698]

"It begins like this," he said, and guided her finger through the flour. [711]
