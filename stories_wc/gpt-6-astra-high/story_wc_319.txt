Bracken found the cultist's pendant under a frozen rosebush, still warm against the dead man's chest. [16] She worried its chain apart with her teeth and carried it back to the library. [31] Once she had worn spectacles and welcomed borrowers; now the feral library guardian slept beneath the reference desk and bit anything that touched the books. [56] The cult had dragged her library into this world, but had neglected to bring the road back. [73] She was dimensionally homesick: she missed the pressure of a fifth direction, the taste of rain falling upward. [91] Ordinary loneliness had names for what it lacked. [99] Hers scarcely had a grammar. [104]

That night she slept with the pendant beneath her tongue. [114] Through the veil of sleep, she entered the frozen garden in mist where the cult had anchored all its stolen places. [135] The pendant held the garden together; without it, every path would dissolve into uninhabitable distances. [150] The pendant lit a narrow circle around her paws. [159] Along its rim, tiny letters read: WHAT IS KNOWN MAY ENTER. [170] Beyond that circle stood a child holding one shoe. [179]

"I've lost the other," the child said. [186] Bracken recognized the unfinished transparency of a dreamer who had wandered too far from waking. [201]

"Then go back for it." [206]

"I don't know where back is." [212]

Bracken tightened her grip on the pendant, and a gate appeared between two frost-encrusted apple trees. [228] Behind it, rain climbed through a sky she remembered with her whole crooked spine. [242] She took a step, but the child cried out. [251] The circle had widened; wherever its brightness touched the child, there was less child left. [266] First the shoe vanished, then two small fingers. [274]

Bracken understood why the cult had polished every window and burned every book without an ending. [290] The boundaries of light were not merely edges here: they separated what could be proved from what would be permitted to exist. [312] Her home was a remembered fact; the child's destination was an unanswered question. [325] The pendant would spend itself opening one certain passage, burning away whatever could still become something else. [342]

"Please wait," said the child. [347]

Bracken could already smell the warm dust of her original reading room. [359] She had spent forty winters rehearsing her return, arranging imaginary apologies to borrowers left waiting where the doors had been. [379] Now she saw herself arriving with this child's absence between her teeth. [391]

She lowered herself onto the ice. [397] "What do you remember?" [401]

"Someone tying my laces," the child said, and began to cry without knowing whose hands she meant. [418]

Bracken had once sheltered entire landscapes by interiorizing exteriors, folding dangerous distances into the hospitable darkness between a book's covers. [438] She had no book here, but a library guardian carried certain rooms within her. [452] She opened her mouth, not to bite, and breathed the garden inward. [464]

It hurt: the hedges scraped her ribs, the gates hinged against her heart, and the pendant's light burned underneath her tongue. [485] The pendant offered her one last glimpse of the passage home before its light began consuming the shelter she had made. [506] She bit down until the pendant broke, and the upward rain went out. [519]

Inside her, the child sat beside a rosebush in darkness that demanded no credentials. [533] Bracken sat there too, although her sleeping body remained curled beneath the reference desk. [547] She wanted, awkwardly and deliberately, to make friends with uncertainty, beginning with this person whose name neither of them knew. [567] So when the child asked how long they must wait, she did not invent an answer. [583]

At last the child remembered a kitchen, then a woman kneeling with a shoe, then the sound of a kettle clicking off. [605] Somewhere among Bracken's ribs, a gate unlatched. [612] The child rose, quite solid now, and put a small hand against the guardian's cheek. [627]

"You could come for breakfast," she said. [634]

At dawn, Bracken woke with a broken pendant and the smell of toast in her fur. [650] Outside, someone knocked on the library door. [657] She went on all fours to open it, then stood up before she turned the handle. [673]
