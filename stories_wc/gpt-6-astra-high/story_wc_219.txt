After making first contact, the visitors discovered that Earth's spring could make even a rainwashed tower feel like a place of exile. [22] Their ship needed forty years to regrow its engine, and homesickness was already disrupting the chemical signals that regulated their sleep. [43] Iri, a luminous skeptic whose skin betrayed every unguarded feeling, accepted the tower's laboratory with one condition: no ceremonial answers. [63] She wanted to understand the chemistry of homecoming, not teach stranded people to mistake gratitude for belonging. [80]

Academically rebellious even before departure, she had lost her university chair for asking whether the ancestral return festivals actually healed anyone. [101] Now Earth's welcoming committee had built an artificial pilgrimage from those same festivals, using memories supplied by the visitors. [120] Sculptures of vanished doorways stood along a winding garden path, each equipped with speakers, scent dispensers, and instructions. [138] At its end, beneath the tower's silent bronze bells, pilgrims were supposed to converge and recover their communal amber glow. [158] Instead, they returned pale, embarrassed by their failure to be comforted. [169]

Iri blamed the perfumes until she compared them with her only uncontaminated sample: a piece of fly amber from her mother's grove. [191] Its trapped insect had six folded wings, and its resin held the molecular signature of home. [207] The dispensers reproduced that signature precisely. [213] Her mother had died during the voyage; no improved instrument could change that. [226] Nevertheless, Iri kept the amber against her throat. [234]

By the third wet week, the committee proposed sedatives, and the abandoned route was disappearing beneath vigorous terrestrial vines. [253] Iri refused to authorize treatment before walking it once without its recorded welcome. [266] Under the sculpted archways, where rain could not reach, she noticed white tracks across the dark paving. [283] Following the dusty footprints along the overgrown sculpture path, she reached the tower's maintenance door, which the pilgrimage's map omitted. [303]

Inside, Tomas, the gardener, was repairing the miniature of her mother's doorway, its lintel broken by a falling branch. [322] Plaster whitened his boots. [326]

"You made the opening too narrow," she said. [334] "We always widened doors when somebody new joined the household." [344]

He set down his tools and offered her his stool. [354] "Show me." [356]

Her skin brightened before she sat. [362]

Working together, they cut the little doorway wider, exposing a seam in the brittle plaster. [377] He reached for filler, but Iri stopped him. [385] At home they sealed such seams with amber; every repair carried the smell of the grove. [401] She closed her fingers around the fossil. [408] For months she had preserved it as though preservation were a duty she could still perform for her mother. [427] Then she shaved one edge into his melting pot, leaving the ancient fly untouched. [441] A familiar bitterness rose through the workshop's damp mineral smell. [451]

Her wrist monitor chimed: the missing sleep precursor was climbing in her blood. [464] She checked its time stamp, unwilling to let hope falsify the observation. [476] The rise had begun when Tomas offered her the stool, before the amber warmed. [490]

She sent the other visitors a message asking for help rebuilding the doorway, with no promise of relief. [508] With the guidance system switched off, she told them to follow the plaster tracks beneath the arches. [525] They arrived carrying complaints, then offered corrections, each remembering some practical detail the committee's interviews had missed. [542] One asked Tomas why his own house had no place on the path. [555] He answered that nobody had asked, and fetched a photograph. [565]

Iri recorded the changing balance of their secretions, including her own, but wrote no triumphant conclusion. [622] Tomorrow she would need controls, repeated trials, and the committee's permission to replace prescribed reverence with useful work. [640] Tonight she could distinguish, for the first time, the ache of missing her mother from the terror of never belonging anywhere again. [662] She hung the diminished amber beside the unfinished doors, where tomorrow's workers would find it. [677] Then she asked Tomas for a key. [684]
