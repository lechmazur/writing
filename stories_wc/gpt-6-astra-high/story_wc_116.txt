Across the drift of untold seasons, Ilen kept the chronicles in an overgrown library hidden in the catacombs of lost knowledge. [21] Roots clasped the shelves like hands reluctant to let go, and somewhere beyond the western wall a river spoke more softly every year. [44] His mother had taught him to witness change without judgment, so he recorded each fallen ceiling tile with the same care as each new leaf. [69] He no longer recorded deaths. [74] The silence had made those difficult to distinguish from departures. [84]

At the river's bend stood an obsidian refuge, built for survivors of a war whose causes were among the first things everyone forgot. [107] Within its walls, regenerative silence knitted wounds, mended stone, and coaxed seeds into trees. [121] It also softened memory: pain vanished first, then whatever had given pain a name. [135] His mother had piped a little of that quiet into the library to preserve its books. [151] Now roots had split the conduit, and quiet seeped upward into the town. [164]

Ilen, who had spent centuries breathing it, gripped reality loosely. [174] Sometimes he remembered tomorrow's rain or mistook his own hands for his mother's. [187] He considered this a useful qualification for an impartial chronicle keeper. [198] Then a girl arrived dragging a woman by the sleeve. [208]

"She knows how to make bread," the girl said, "but she puts the dough in her lap because she has forgotten ovens." [230]

The woman smiled apologetically. [234] Ilen reached for his ledger, and the girl slapped it shut. [245]

"Write later." [247]

Her name was Fen, and she remembered him visiting her mother's bakery last spring. [261] He remembered the bakery as a battlefield, then as an orchard, and could not decide which memory deserved to be believed. [282] Fen took his hand and pressed it against her mother's thin wrist. [294] The pulse was quick and entirely present. [301]

In his desk lay a well-worn origami swan, the last thing his mother had given him before surrendering her grief inside the refuge. [324] She had called it a way back, but he preferred the journals in which she still remembered being his mother. [344] Fen held it against the lamp while he carefully opened its cryptic folds. [357] Creases crossed ink marks into a map; reversed, the wings showed a buried access shaft beneath the refuge. [375] His mother's smallest handwriting identified the library's intake gate. [384] Closing it would end the leaks, but also the renewal that held the ruined library together. [400]

He looked at her journals, green with tender moss. [409] He could save them first, perhaps, and their commentaries, and the histories required to understand those commentaries. [426] Fen's mother began kneading the hem of her dress. [435] Ilen put down the swan and fetched two spades. [444]

They left the woman with a neighbor beyond the spreading quiet, then followed the river until its murmur failed. [463] The approach tunnel had long since filled with roots. [472] Guided by the map hidden in the swan, they began to burrow beneath the obsidian refuge, where the roots were pale and thick as wrists. [497] Fen carried each basket of soil away while Ilen dug, losing track of which of them was old. [515] Whenever he stopped, she said her name, then his, until the spade moved again. [529]

They reached the gate at dusk, or in the shadow of something dusk had once resembled. [545] Its wheel resisted him, and for an instant he heard his mother turning pages on the other side. [563] He could not tell whether the sound was memory, promise, or merely water. [576] He turned the wheel anyway. [581]

The river's voice returned by degrees: first a trickle, then the scrape of stones moving downstream. [597] Behind them, deep beneath the town, shelves collapsed with the slow thunder of trees falling. [612] Ilen waited for certainty that he had done right. [621] None came. [623]

By morning, Fen's mother remembered ovens, though not her daughter's name. [634] They would begin there. [638] On a salvaged scrap, Ilen recorded the closure, the books destroyed, and the names of the two people beside him. [658] When Fen asked what season it was, he looked at the bare branches above the river and admitted he did not know. [680] Then he wrote that down too. [686]
