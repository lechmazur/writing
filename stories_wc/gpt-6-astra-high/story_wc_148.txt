At nineteen, Eli was still called the cry wolf boy, although it was his father who had cried wolf. [19] The name had followed him to the lighthouse prison off a jagged reef, where his father had spent eighteen years. [39] Now the sunset laid a copperlight repose over the sea, and two guards lay cooling behind the oil barrels. [58] Eli had killed them with the hammer he had brought to repair the chapel screen. [73] Find him before they find the bodies, he told himself, and tried to wipe his hands clean. [90] The watch changed at dusk. [95]

His father's last smuggled letter had contained no plea, only directions for chasing hush. [109] "Listen for the place where even the surf falls silent." [119] "Bring the lattice from the confessional." [125]

Eli carried the wooden grille down past the ordinary cells, following absences in the great, breathing noise of water. [144] The prisoners called that silence Hush; the wardens kept their inconvenient captives inside it, beyond the reach of visiting magistrates. [164] "Hush sustains impossibilities," his father had written, "but it cannot withstand an honest account of how you entered it." [183] "To get someone out, you must thread the labyrinth with your own story." [196]

At the stair's foot stood a narrow door left ajar for decades, its bottom edge polished by dragging chains. [215] Eli fitted the lattice against old screw holes; this had been the confessional door before the prison outgrew confession. [234] Through its diamonds, the scuffs resolved into corridors, and through the scuffs he stepped into Hush. [250]

The sea stood upright on either side of him, held back by nothing he could touch. [266] Far above, fish seemed to hover in the last copper light. [277] He could hear neither their world nor his own heart. [287]

"I came to mend a screen," he said. [295] A white thread unwound from his mouth and fastened itself to the door. [308] "I wanted to see my father." [314]

It lengthened around a turning, drawing him past cells as small as fists and as large as winters. [332] Inside them waited the people whose testimony had embarrassed the governor: a stonemason, a midwife, a keeper of accounts. [351] His father sat at the center, still wearing the shepherd's coat in which he had been arrested. [368] The villagers had punished the lies after the wolf came; the governor had imprisoned him for naming whose neglected fences let it through. [391] Nobody believed a liar could subsequently tell the truth. [400]

"How did you pass the guards?" his father asked. [409]

"They were asleep." [412]

The thread snapped, and the corridor behind him folded into a blank wall. [425] His father looked toward the blank wall, then at the red caught beneath Eli's fingernails. [440]

"One recognized me," Eli said, "and the other reached for the bell." [452] "Neither had drawn a weapon." [457]

The broken thread trembled between them, unwilling to join. [466] He had imagined his father's embrace would make the hammer, the startled faces, and everything afterward belong to someone else. [486]

"I murdered them because I was afraid of losing my chance," he said. [499] "Getting you back will not undo it." [506]

His father took his hand, and the thread joined with a small, audible knot. [520] Sound entered Hush like warmth entering chilled fingers. [528] Eli heard the distant bell, then prisoners calling from cells whose locks had begun to crumble. [544] He told the rest as they followed the thread outward, stopping at every open doorway to gather another witness. [563] Behind them, the impossible rooms filled peacefully with sea. [572]

At the narrow door, his father offered to take the hammer. [583] For a moment Eli saw how easily love could furnish another lie. [595] He kept it. [598]

"Ring the reef warning," he said, "and bring the harbor boats." [609] "Let everyone see who was kept here." [616]

His father climbed toward the lantern while Eli waited beside the gathered prisoners, the hammer laid across his open palms. [636] When the wardens arrived, he gave his name and named what he had done. [650] They could not silence thirty witnesses before the harbor boats reached the landing. [663]

Outside, his father's warning moved across the copper water, and this time the answering bells came steadily closer. [681] Eli listened without asking the sound to forgive him. [690]
