Three days after the drought ended, I entered the subway maintenance tunnel where my sister had drowned. [17] Rain still ticked through the ceiling, and every passing train made the steel emergency hatch reverberate. [33] My father, the transit director, had called her death unforeseeable. [43] As a preventable death investigator, I knew that word usually meant someone had stopped looking. [58] As his daughter, I had spent forty years learning to stop. [69]

I had properly been removed from Ana's case; Investigator Kess had brought me only to identify her belongings. [87] He waited beside the evidence crates while I knelt by her tool bag. [100] Inside lay a lantern from an Underground Railroad refuge discovered during station repairs. [113] Ana, the city's archaeologist, had been cataloging the exposed cellar when the first storm overwhelmed the pumps. [130] Her last inventory entry identified the lantern's owner as Ruth Bell, who had guided fugitives through this cellar. [148]

When my inspection light crossed its green glass, someone else's fingers closed around mine. [162] I was holding a child's wrist beside a staircase, listening for boots overhead. [175] Then I was Ana, waist deep in rising water, pulling uselessly at the emergency hatch. [190] Under both memories ran my own: Father correcting my account of a childhood accident until I thanked him for remembering better. [211] These entangled memories shared one heartbeat, but not one will. [221] The woman beneath the stairs was watchfully calm, afraid enough to tremble, steady enough to keep the child behind her. [241] She was waiting for danger to pass, not for permission. [251]

I dropped the lantern onto the padded cloth. [259] Kess crouched beside me without touching anything. [266] I wanted to tell him I was unfit, let Father explain, go home. [279] Instead, I asked him to record what happened next. [288] I had earned my judgments through empirical observation, and terror did not excuse abandoning the method. [304] With the glass covered, the memories stopped; uncovered, they returned at precisely the same instant. [319] Tilting the lantern brought Ana's hands into focus; leveling it restored Ruth and the child. [334] Kess reported only green light, which meant my testimony alone could prove nothing. [347] But beneath Ana's palms I saw a chain wound around the hatch's push bar. [361]

The official account said flood pressure had jammed it. [370] I pointed without explaining, and Kess photographed the bright grooves gouged into the bar. [384] Behind the threshold, his probe found two severed links embedded in silt. [396] No chain appeared in the recovery crew's report. [404] He bagged the links, then called the crew chief on the tunnel telephone. [417] I stood back, gripping my own wrists, while the chief admitted that orders to omit the chain had come from the director. [439] Kess requested the recorded dispatch logs before anyone could erase them. [450]

Then Father called back. [454] Kess held the receiver between us, with the recorder running. [464]

"The chain went on during the drought," Father said. [473] "We couldn't keep replacing stolen copper, and nobody was supposed to use that passage." [487]

He sounded tired rather than monstrous, which was harder. [496] I could remember those hands sewing a torn sleeve, carrying Ana asleep from the car. [511] The lantern had given me no clean division between love and evidence. [523]

"Tell him you're confused," Father said, using the gentle voice that had governed our house. [538]

I felt the old relief approaching: surrender the account, and he would carry everything. [552] Through the glass, Ruth was checking the stairs again, her thumb moving over the child's knuckles. [568] She could not make the street safe; she could choose when to cross it. [582]

"I am confused about what happened to me down here," I said, "but not about what Kess found." [600] My voice shook, and I let the recording keep that too. [611] "You do not get to remember for me anymore." [620]

Kess took the receiver and began asking about inspection schedules, keys, and everyone who had approved the closure. [638] I gave him the lantern to seal separately, and dictated my account without asking him to believe it. [656]

Outside, rain was washing the dust from the station steps. [666] I waited for Kess beneath the awning instead of calling Father to collect me. [680] When he emerged, I asked him to stay while I cried. [691]
