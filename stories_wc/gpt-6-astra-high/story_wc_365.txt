In the murmur of half-remembered dreams, Mara returned to the abandoned spaceport repair bay where her father had supposedly deserted her. [21] By daylight she was a sensory map maker, charting transit routes by the taste of metal, the pressure behind the eyes, the particular loneliness of an echo. [48] Distance meant little when weaving between worlds; sensations remained dependable where coordinates did not. [62] Ship crews followed her maps asleep; only dreaming minds could steer a hull through the seams of reality. [80]

But until her mother pressed a green glass shard into her palm, Mara had never dared map this place. [99]

"I closed his gate," her mother had said. [107] "I need you to know that before I die." [116]

Now the shard warmed in Mara's sleeping hand, carrying her bodily through parallel universes to the bay's innumerable versions. [135] It had broken from her father's navigation lens, and its pulse could lead her to the matching fragment wherever he had gone. [157]

The breaches were cyclically spontaneous: they opened every ninth heartbeat, but never into a predictable world. [173] A practiced sleeper could steer by sensation. [180] Mara could return safely only by remembering every crossing in reverse. [191] She tied knots in a strip of cloth, assigning each a sensation. [203] One for the bay where spilled coolant smelled of pears, two for the bay where silence prickled, three for the bay submerged in warm, breathable rain. [229]

Her mother had always insisted that her father chose another life. [240] Mara had built hers around never needing anyone to stay. [250] She had come to discover the weight of a withheld truth, expecting evidence she could carry home and set between them. [271]

At the fourth crossing, the shard burned. [278]

Beneath a dismantled engine sat a stooped man sewing a child's boot with copper wire. [293] Mara recognized the three quick tugs he gave each stitch before she recognized his face. [308]

"You're late," he said, and then began to cry. [317]

He had waited twenty years, although only six had passed for him. [329] When Mara mentioned the gate, he showed her the evacuation figures scratched into the engine housing. [345]

"Eighty children in the terminal, a reactor going critical; she had to redirect the blast somewhere." [361]

"She sent it through your gate." [367]

"I outran it through three worlds, but when I circled home, the way was sealed." [382]

He touched the shard against the broken lens hanging at his throat. [394] Green light revealed sleeping figures beneath the gantry, people he had gathered from the wreckage of other crossings. [412]

"She saved us," Mara said, meaning the children, and hated how nearly that sounded like forgiveness. [428]

"She did," he answered, "but she shouldn't have made you think I left." [441]

The cloth tightened around Mara's fingers as the first knot began to smoke. [454] Her sleep was thinning; soon she would wake and forget the sensations that had brought her here. [471] The lens held enough charge either to bring one passenger home or to broadcast a route for a rescue ship. [491]

Their little fires, their patched blankets, the boot in his lap: he had not merely been waiting. [508]

Mara crouched beside him and loosened her knots. [516]

"Then let me give you the route," she said. [525]

Together they tuned the lens to her remembered sensations, until it could redirect incoming rescue ships along the route she had made. [547] The glass cooled as its stored charge drained into the beacon, leaving too little power to take him with her. [567]

"Come back," he said. [571]

"With a ship," she promised. [576]

Mara woke on the repair bay floor with the smell of copper still in her nose and an empty palm. [596]

At the hospital, she laid her rescue request beside her mother's bed. [608]

"He is alive," she said. [613]

Her mother reached for her. [618] Mara let the hand rest between them. [625]

"I couldn't bear you waiting for him," her mother whispered. [635]

"Or blaming you," Mara said. [640] "You will tell the rescue board what happened, including what you let me believe about him." [656]

Outside, freight engines started, heavy enough to shake the windows. [666] For the first time, Mara understood that a truth could be too heavy to carry alone without being too heavy to move. [688]
