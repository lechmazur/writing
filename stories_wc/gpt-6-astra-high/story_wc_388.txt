Six months after the liquidation, Mara returned to the orchard with a contract to disprove a curse. [17] Radioactive dust storms scoured the surrounding wasteland, but inside the boundary walls, apricots bent branches almost to earth. [35] The Authority wanted its fruit shipments moving again. [43] Mara, a conspiracy debunker whose broadcasts had helped justify the orchard cooperative's dissolution, wanted to stop dreaming about the arrests. [63] Once, she had chosen this work to excavate truth from comfortable lies. [75]

At the gate, a girl offered her a diamond suspended inside a broken instrument. [89] A smoky flaw clouded its heart. [95] "They say everyone who carries it dies," the girl said. [105] "My father was the last." [110] Mara recognized him from a photograph in her own report: an irrigation engineer she had called a saboteur. [128] She had mistaken his refusal to answer interrogators for an inability to answer them. [142]

The girl, Ada, led her through flowering rows while dust thundered overhead. [154] Mara noticed that the blossoms barely trembled, although the orchard stood open to the sky. [169] She also noticed that Ada ate nothing. [176] "We sell the apricots to buy tins," Ada said. [185] Beneath her father's bunk, she kept a sack of shredded letters salvaged from the office after his arrest. [203] Mara almost asked why she had not brought them to the authorities. [215]

Instead, she cleared a table. [220] For three nights, she worked by reassembling shredded letters, matching severed loops, rust stains, and the pressure of distinctive handwriting. [240] Ada slept beside the table, one hand curled around the instrument. [251] Mara found requests for replacement filters, then complaints about a diverted exhaust, then a drawing of the diamond. [269] Its imperfection was deliberate: a defect in the crystal lattice that allowed the instrument to measure radiation. [286] It had never been jewelry. [291] The men who carried it had been sent to service the contaminated exhaust tunnels without adequate protection. [308] Calling the stone cursed made their deaths seem unrelated to their work. [320]

On the fourth night, Mara joined two strips containing the words "hidden stillpoint." [333] The engineer had designed an artificial calm at the orchard's center, using buried fans and carefully balanced air currents. [352] Filters should have trapped the radioactive dust drawn away from the trees. [364] But replacement filters cost more than the Authority would pay. [374] An appended order rerouted the dirty air toward the barracks built for displaced orchard workers. [389] Mara found her own broadcast quoted beneath it: "Claims of deliberate exposure remain unsupported." [403]

She intuited the rest, but intuition had already helped her ruin one innocent man. [417] This time she needed measurements. [422] Following the drawing, she reseated the diamond in its contacts and connected the instrument to the office charging socket. [441] At dawn, she and Ada pulled on maintenance respirators and followed the service stairs beneath the orchard. [458] The reading climbed as they approached the bypass valve; beyond it, a ventilation map showed the barracks receiving everything the orchard escaped. [480] Mara photographed the readings, the map, and the valve's numbered seal. [491]

"If we close it, the fans stop," Ada said, reading the warning plate. [504] Without their shelter, the trees would meet the next storm. [514] Mara thought of the comfortable version she could broadcast: one criminal official, one repaired pipe, fruit shipments resuming before anyone went hungry. [536] The letters made clear there were no spare filters, and there would be no harmless correction. [552]

She transmitted the evidence with her old report attached and her own errors marked. [566] On the orchard intercom, she told the workers to take supplies to the ridge shelter. [581] When the evacuation bell fell silent, she asked Ada to help her turn the wheel. [596] They could not undo the exposure, but they could stop feeding it. [608]

Above them, the stillpoint came apart. [614] Wind entered the orchard with a sound like somebody finally drawing breath. [626] Ada put the instrument against her chest, not as a charm but to free both hands for the climb. [645] At the gate, they joined the workers heading for the ridge shelter with water, tools, and the last sealed tins. [665] Mara carried the letters, keeping them flat beneath her coat as the first blossoms blew across the wasteland. [683]
