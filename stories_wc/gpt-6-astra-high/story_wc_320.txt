At lunar perigee, the abandoned radio tower city suspended over a gorge began pulling its mooring bolts out of the cliffs. [21] Below it, the former residents waited beside carts loaded with mattresses, chickens, and receivers that could no longer find a station. [42] They had rebuilt below the cliffs after fleeing the failing towers seven years earlier; now the towers threatened their new homes. [63] Their city was trying to follow the moon. [71]

Mira, celestial signal interpreter, climbed the maintenance ladder with insulated gloves, a tuning fork, and a Hollywood star fragment wrapped in flannel. [93] The fragment bore her mother's name above a brass microphone. [103] Before the ocean took Hollywood, her mother had broadcast bedtime adventures to half the world; afterward, she had broadcast evacuation instructions from these towers. [127] A surge had killed her while she was announcing departure times. [138] The final transmission had never reached the ground. [146]

"You have twenty minutes," said Orrin, the rigger stationed on the opposite rim. [159] "Then I cut the moorings before your city takes our hillside with it." [172]

Mira acknowledged him and entered the central receiver, where dust trembled above the floor without settling. [188] Cities like this floated by tuning themselves to named stars, their height maintained by an enchantment regrettably vulnerable to radio interference. [209] Each lunar perigee, the swollen moon drowned the stellar carriers in reflected noise. [222] Ordinarily, an interpreter could tune around it. [229] This receiver, however, had locked itself onto one unfinished human broadcast. [240] Every adjustment only dragged the towers higher. [247] Seven years of this had exhausted the moorings. [255]

In the transmitter's throat she found a shard of hush, thin and dark as a missing piano key. [273] Her instruments went deaf beside it, although its surface was serenely electric beneath her glove. [288] Compressed silence: the dangerous residue left when a transmission lost its speaker but not its power. [304] Somewhere inside that silence lay the ending Mira had spent seven years trying to hear. [319] She told clients her work was to find the beginning in every ending, but mostly she wanted her mother's last words to tell her what to do next. [347]

She touched the tuning fork to the shard. [355] A woman's breath sounded, then stopped; outside, a mooring bolt cracked like a rifle. [369]

"Four minutes," Orrin said. [373]

Mira reached for the fragment, intending to use the engraved name to isolate the voice. [388] Her mother had salvaged it herself, laughing that a working star ought to own at least part of her address. [408]

An address. [410]

Mira looked at the receiver's instructions, stamped into brass by an engineer who had expected emergencies and distrusted poetry. [429] SELECT A NAMED STAR. [433] Nothing about heaven. [436] She had solved difficult signals before by discarding assumptions, but never the assumption printed on her own professional license. [455]

"Orrin, send the tool basket across, then bolt what I put inside it to bedrock." [470]

"That is not an approved anchor," he said when the pink terrazzo reached him. [484]

"Neither is the moon." [488]

The receiver needed one clean interval to forget its old alignment before accepting another star. [507] Mira could get that interval by crushing the hush across its contacts, spending seven years of preserved silence in a single instant. [529] Whatever words might have followed her mother's breath would be lost. [540] Through the window she saw the carts below, and someone trying to shelter a chicken from the rising wind with a child's coat. [563] Her mother had stayed behind for people like that, not to leave instructions for being motherless. [579]

The drill stopped. [582] Mira set the dial to her mother's name and crushed the shard beneath the tuning fork. [598] For a heartbeat, even the gorge's river vanished from hearing. [608] Then the receiver caught the little pavement star, fixed safely in the earth, and the towers settled until their walkways met the cliff road. [632] The bolts stopped screaming. [636]

[667] Then she sat at her mother's microphone, opened a fresh broadcast log, and wrote the date. [683] She had no final message to offer, only a useful first one. [695]

"The road is open," she said. [701]
