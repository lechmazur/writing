After the falcon returned to the rusted outpost by the frozen lake, Lenora Vale unpacked her surveying instruments. [18] The bird followed migrating ducks, and the ducks found open water before any atlas editor could. [34] Her mother's field notes had said to wait for it. [44] Now a black crescent gleamed beside the shore, although the lake lay otherwise white and motionless. [60]

"A punctual colleague," Lenora said, watching the falcon settle above the abandoned chimney. [73] "I shall recommend you for management." [79]

She had come to find where the rivers begin, not where three governments preferred to put their little blue stars. [99] For twenty years she had printed those stars beside three distant springs, each advertised as the birthplace of a national river. [120] But identical mineral deposits and synchronized floods suggested a singular convergence beneath the watershed. [134]

Her mother had proposed it first. [140] Lenora had edited the claim into a footnote, then removed the footnote to accommodate a decorative compass. [157] The compass was still there in the latest edition; her mother had been dead five years. [173]

On the desk lay the polyglot's phrasebook, swollen with damp, its dog-ear marking the local name for this outpost. [192] Lenora eased the folded corner flat, exposing a pencil note that years of handling had spared. [208] "Not watchhouse: sourcehouse," her mother had written across the printed translation. [219] Below it, smaller: "The first thaw makes the house breathe." [229]

Lenora set aside her compass. [234]

The window had clouded while she worked, but the pattern of condensation on glass was oddly uneven. [251] A dense fan of droplets spread upward from one corner, far from her stove. [265] She extinguished the flame and waited. [271] Outside, the ducks floated without calling, black commas in water too smooth to seem newly freed. [287]

The fan thickened. [290]

Lenora removed a loose pane from an inner partition, chilled it outside, and carried it slowly above the floorboards. [309] Over most of the room, it stayed clear; beside the filing cabinet, a milky bloom appeared on its underside. [328] Warm, wet air was rising through a seam. [336]

"Your filing system needs attention," she told the room, and began unloading drawers. [349]

She moved the cabinet with a crowbar, steadily, without wasting strength on the conviction that it ought to be lighter. [369] Beneath it was a hatch. [374] Beneath the hatch, iron steps descended into a chamber breathing air that smelled of pennies. [389]

Her lantern revealed a clear upwelling in the rock, divided by masonry into three channels disappearing into natural fissures. [408] Names cut above them matched the oldest river names in the phrasebook, before borders had made their spellings disagree. [427] The outpost had never guarded a frontier. [434] It had guarded the distribution of water. [441]

Lenora crouched beside the central sluice and saw why the spring pulse had been worth waiting for. [458] Fresh water was washing old sediment from the channels, opening the passages that let the buried spring warm the shore. [478] It was also lifting a gate whose supports had rusted almost through. [490] Each gentle surge bowed the frame toward the three outlets. [500] If it collapsed across them, the national birthplaces would dwindle together, and pressure would build beneath this pleasant, empty lake. [520]

She could already hear the arguments over whose engineers should come, whose soldiers should accompany them, whose flag should fly. [540] For a moment she wanted only to take measurements and leave a beautifully defensible footnote. [555]

Then she climbed the steps and switched on her field radio. [566] The valley stations were exchanging ceremonial greetings, their customary way to herald the first rise of the rivers each spring. [586] Lenora interrupted them, giving her coordinates, the condition of the gate, and the names of all three channels. [604]

"One maintenance crew from each valley," she said, her voice level enough to make the request sound already agreed. [623] "You can dispute the ownership after we keep it standing." [633]

While the operators fetched their superiors, she opened the atlas and crossed out the three stars. [649] At the lake she drew one unadorned circle, then wrote her mother's name beside the survey date. [666]

Outside, the falcon slept with its head beneath its wing. [676] Below her boots, the water continued its almost soundless work. [686]
