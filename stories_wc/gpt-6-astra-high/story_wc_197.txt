By morning, the flood would have killed nobody again. [9] Mara had mopped the Revision Office through six revised histories: negligence became sabotage, the drowned became evacuees, and finally the evacuation itself disappeared. [32] Her mother had disappeared between editions three and four. [41] The refinery had locked its sluices against the rising lagoon, sacrificing the workers' quarter to save its furnaces. [59] People preferred the latest version; it asked nothing of them. [69]

The rebels planned to interrupt the anniversary broadcast with the original emergency calls, which Mara had found misfiled among telephone maintenance recordings. [91] They trusted her to steal a recording cylinder, but not to work the transmitter. [105] "Keep the kettle warm," their engineer told her. [113] She kept an obsolete brass key warm instead, its bow stamped TIDE TELEPHONE, the company liquidated after the flood. [132] Her mother, a line repairer, had hung it on the kitchen hook before her last shift. [148]

At sunset, the transmitter went silent and the anniversary screens began displaying the engineer's confession. [163] The others scattered as their plans unraveled. [170] Mara watched him recite that nobody had ever died, and understood that the rebels had built their hopes on machines owned by their enemies. [194] She took the cylinder, a discarded exit sign battery, and the key. [206] Nobody stopped the cleaner carrying another sack of rubbish. [215]

Beyond the last warehouses, she could no longer find the road to the old lagoon exchange. [231] Official maps showed open water. [236] But dusk sharpened the puddle reflections until forgotten telephone poles appeared upside down, their crossbars linked by faint black wires. [256] Kneeling, Mara joined those fragments into a map suggested by things people stepped over. [270] The wires led along a drowned service causeway, its stones visible beneath inches of amber water. [286]

She crossed slowly, testing each stone with her mop handle. [296] By the time she reached the exchange booth, the auric lagoon lay under starlight, its poisonous mine silt glittering beneath the ripples. [318] The booth stood on a concrete plinth, its door rusted shut. [329] Her mother's phone booth key opened the service panel instead. [339]

Inside waited a cylinder reader, a service handset, and the old storm circuit, still connected to household receivers because replacing them had cost more than preserving them. [366] Tide Telephone had once promised that no distress call would go unheard. [378] The government's newer switches could not interrupt its mechanical connections. [388]

She stripped only enough meter wire to attach her salvaged battery, leaving the handset connected. [403] Then she seated the cylinder and turned the key in the TEST switch. [416] The spring fought her fingers; this connection required someone to hold it open. [429] Across the lagoon, kitchens began to ring. [436]

Her mother's voice emerged without the solemn music of official remembrance. [447] "The west gates are locked, and there are children on the school roof." [460] Mara had remembered her as frightened, but the voice was furious. [471] The recording named the official who refused to open the gates, then the families still waiting. [487] On the opposite shore, lights went out. [494] Someone had raised the alarm. [499]

She could broadcast the truth, but she could not make anyone care. [511] That had always been the harder tyranny. [518] When the cylinder ended, she kept the key turned and lifted the handset. [531] "If someone you lost has vanished from the histories, give us their name." [544] "If speaking is unsafe, tap twice." [550]

The silence lasted long enough for her wrist to cramp. [560] Then two clicks sounded, followed by two more. [568] A woman said a name Mara knew, and a man corrected its pronunciation with startling tenderness. [584] Names began to reappear, not on a screen that could be rewritten, but in kitchens where neighbors were listening. [603]

As a patrol boat nosed across the shining lagoon, Mara asked each listener to remember one name besides their own. [623] The key bit her palm, and she held the line. [633] By the time the boat reached her, the ringing had become knocking: twice at each door, all along the shore. [653]
