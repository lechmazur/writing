At midsummer, the rainwashed library smelled of wet coats and the clover growing between its steps. [16] After her father's heart attack, Mira carried his belongings there, to the librarians who had known him for years. [35] She was a probability professor; he was a compulsive gambler, and for years she had mistaken that semicolon for a wall. [56] Now he lay sedated, while a discharge planner asked where he would live. [69] The address he had given Mira belonged to a demolished betting shop. [81]

His few belongings included an old signalman's candle lantern, its mica pane scratched with tiny diagrams. [97] He had pawned her mother's watch but kept this cheap, battered thing. [109] She set it beside the lecture she was preparing, titled Lucid Contradictions. [121] It argued that apparently opposing truths could sharpen judgment rather than cancel each other. [135] Theoretically rebellious, she was less adventurous when contradiction meant loving someone she could not trust. [150]

The librarian recognized the lantern. [155] "Your father copied the night passengers' signs onto that pane," she said. [167] "Paper disintegrates in the tunnels." [172]

Beneath the library, on a subway platform seat, Mira found the same crooked cup etched beside an arrow. [190] Along the pane's edge, her father's cramped handwriting translated the symbols: meals, beds, danger, distance. [205] She had expected gambling ciphers, some final system promising rescue through arithmetic. [217] Instead, she began to traverse his hidden city by interpreting cryptic scrawls on subway seats. [232]

A crossed cup meant the kitchen had closed; three short cuts counted the stops to another. [248] A roof with a broken side warned of a shelter that turned people away without identification. [264] Someone kept correcting the marks, scratching fresh routes over obsolete promises. [275] The timetables assumed quick transfers; an old man beside her took each stair separately. [289]

By evening the scrawls delivered her to a church basement behind a strip of flattened clover. [305] A woman ladling soup saw the lantern and put down her ladle. [317]

"Is he dead?" [320]

Mira shook her head, surprised by the woman's unguarded relief. [330] His bed was behind a curtain, beneath a list of arrivals written in his careful railway hand. [347] He had been homeless since February. [353]

"He lost his pension at cards," the woman said, "but he kept our routes current when services moved." [371] "I trust him with the keys, never with the collection tin." [382]

Mira sat on his narrow mattress, feeling both the old anger and something that did not erase it. [400] For years she had offered him two identities: cured or unforgivable. [411] Neither had left much room for this bed. [419]

She photographed the arrival lists and started sketching a public map, excited by the useful patterns. [435] The woman covered a name with her thumb. [443]

"Some people come here because nobody knows where they sleep." [453]

Mira deleted the photograph in front of her. [461] Then she asked what help they actually wanted. [469] They needed a way to estimate spare beds without promising one that might disappear before a slow traveler arrived. [488] This was work she understood, provided she admitted what her models could not know. [502] They agreed to begin with anonymous totals, conservative estimates, and a telephone call before anyone set out. [519]

Back at the library, she replaced her lecture's elegant paradoxes with a question about whom an error would hurt. [538] To widen perspectives responsibly, she wrote, was not to claim a view from everywhere. [552] It was to notice who remained outside the frame, and ask before drawing them in. [567]

When her father woke, she offered her spare room for his recovery, but no cash and no loans to clear his debts. [589]

"Those are hard odds," he whispered. [595]

"They're conditions, Dad." [598]

He looked toward the lantern she had brought, unlit, to his bedside. [610] "They'll need a new keeper for the signs." [618]

"The kitchen has found someone," she said, and watched him absorb the small grief of being replaceable. [635] He reached for the lantern, stopped short of its brittle pane, and asked whether she would visit the kitchen with him when he was stronger. [660] She said yes without calculating whether he deserved it. [669]

Outside, rain darkened the hospital lawn, where ordinary clover bent beneath the water and slowly lifted again. [686]
