Across the pause of shifting constellations, Tern entered the ancient temple library and found his sister's teacup still steaming. [19] Outside, she had been dead for thirty years. [27] Inside, her chair had not finished rocking. [34] The library was lost in time, arrested between one arrangement of heaven and the next by the man who now wore her crown. [57] Tern had spent those thirty years charting the stars, waiting for the same celestial hesitation to open a door. [76]

He was a diminutive titan, scarcely taller than her reading desk, with hands that could lift the temple's cornerstone. [95] Nevertheless, he wiped his boots before approaching it. [103] His sister had called him regally unpretentious; he had called her a dreadful judge of kings. [119] He wished she were here to tease him. [127]

The regent claimed Queen Nera had named no successor, making him king by ancient law. [142] Tern had watched her sign a choice, but the regent had displayed a blank testament and called him a grieving fool. [163] The library had vanished before her murder, taking every earlier draft. [174] Nera, who trusted neither ministers nor straightforward instructions, had promised him another way through cryptic messages left in library books. [194]

In a gardening manual, beside a pressed violet, he found: What wanders remembers its roots. [209] The atlas of migrating islands supplied the answer, a rune of drift, drawn like a seed opening. [226] Beneath it, her tiny handwriting said: Give the stolen words a road home. [239] He understood why she had hidden the instructions separately; the regent's inspectors searched books for treason, not conversations between them. [259]

The final message waited in a treatise on ink: Even an erasure leaves iron behind. [274] Its hollow spine held an iron gall ink pellet, wrapped in a scrap of her apron. [290] Nera had always cooked for herself when the palace became unbearable. [301] Tern dissolved the pellet in her cooling tea and brushed the rune onto the temple's central reading stone. [319] Iron called to iron. [323] From the shelves rose faint brown letters, traces scraped from royal drafts, drifting toward the wet lines beneath his brush. [343] They gathered into a sentence he had never been allowed to read: I choose my brother Tern, who knows what power should refuse. [366]

He sat down hard on the floor. [373] For thirty years he had wanted a name to carry into battle, someone taller, someone born knowing what to do. [393] Above him waited the bronze speaking mouths through which the library once announced royal decrees in every town square. [412] Their tongues trembled, but no sound could cross the missing years. [423] Under the reading stone, a silver staple pinned the temple's foundations to a single, motionless star. [439] It bore the regent's seal. [444] To free the testimony, Tern would have to sunder that fastening and let thirty stolen years enter at once. [463]

The temple had already been crumbling when Nera was alive; its custodians had spent each morning mending what the night undid. [484] Without those small repairs, the returning decades would take everything. [494] He could remain here instead, guarding her warm cup until the stars closed over him. [509] Outside waited people who had spent thirty years obeying a lie. [520] He set the cup safely on the floor, although there would be no safe place. [535] Then the little titan bent the silver staple until it parted. [546]

The library returned without thunder. [551] Dust descended in long, courteous veils; pillars inclined toward one another like dancers remembering their final measure. [568] Across the kingdom, the bronze mouths spoke Nera's choice, and then the regent's orders to erase it, recovered from the same obedient iron. [591] Tern carried the reading stone through the settling archway, its burden easier than the name inscribed upon it. [609] Behind him, his sister's chair finally came to rest. [618]

By dawn, the regent's guards had opened the palace gates, but Tern did not go inside. [634] He was sorting fallen books with the bakers and street sweepers who had come to hear the stone for themselves. [654] When they asked where to build his throne, he pointed to the space beneath an undamaged lintel. [671] "A reading table first," he said, and steadied the stone while they brought the trestles. [686] This time, there would be room on both sides. [695]
