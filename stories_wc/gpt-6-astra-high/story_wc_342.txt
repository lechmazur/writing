During the hush before the excavators started, Mara stood inside the tree trunk library and tried to draw every star. [20] The scholar's guild had hollowed the enormous cedar three centuries earlier, carving shelves between its ribs and silver constellations above them. [41] Now rot had entered the roots, and the condemned building leaned toward the public hospital across the lane. [59]

Mara's mother lay there beneath a ceiling the color of dishwater. [70] Before the stroke took her speech, she had asked Mara to mirror the sky inside those walls. [87] Her mother had kept this library for forty years; its artificial heavens were the view she missed most. [105] Mara had promised an exact copy. [111]

Exactness was her profession, although recognition was not. [119] A face blind portrait artist, she could render the branching vessels in an eyelid with fractally precise care, yet pass her sitter unrecognized outside. [143] She knew her mother by her voice, her quick left hand, the peppermint she always carried. [159] Since the stroke, Mara had sometimes needed a nurse to identify her mother among patients. [174] Getting the stars right felt like something she could still do. [185]

At the doorway, her brother held up five fingers. [194] He had stopped the demolition crew for her, but the wind was rising, and nobody could promise another interval. [213] Inside, the cedar remained so still that she could hear charcoal touching paper. [226] Her drawing contained eleven stars; overhead, there were thousands, vanishing into smoke stains and the rough darkness of the wood. [246]

She put down the charcoal and took out the centuries-old coin her mother had given her when the library closed. [266] Wrapped around it had been a note: The sky atlas is a secret book safe; the coin will show you how. [287]

The atlas waited on the empty lending desk, too heavy to be carried with the other books. [304] Six brass wheels interrupted its false pages, but their worn markings seemed meaningless until she tilted the coin against the doorway's pale light. [327] Through faint indentations on the centuries-old coin, a familiar arrangement emerged: six constellations descending along the cedar ceiling's painted river. [347] Each wheel bore constellations rather than numbers. [354] She followed the river, turning the wheels in order, and felt the cover yield. [368]

Inside lay thin brass plates, pierced with stars, and a folded sheet in her mother's handwriting. [384] These were the guild's original patterns, used with a lantern to cast the heavens onto bare wood before the carvers began. [405] Her mother had drawn instructions for fitting them around a small electric lamp. [418] At the bottom she had written, For whichever room they give me. [430]

One plate had split across the river; others were bent, their smallest holes stopped with green corrosion. [447] Mara reached for her needle tool, then looked at the doorway. [458] Her brother was no longer counting minutes on his fingers. [468] He was watching a seam widen in the wall. [477]

Mara slid the broken plates into her satchel and selected one that would still bend without cracking. [494] With a strip of drawing paper and two clips, she began to craft the lamp housing her mother had sketched. [514] Her hands wanted to straighten every dent, but she made them stop when the little cylinder held. [531] She cupped her pocket torch beneath it. [538]

A handful of stars appeared on the dark wall, blurred at their edges, with an entire season missing. [556] Without the silver carving, they looked smaller than she remembered. [566] Then her brother stepped inside, and the stars crossed his sleeve. [577] He touched the crooked cluster near his wrist, the one their mother had called the Teapot when they were children. [597]

"She'll know that one," he said. [603]

Mara almost explained the missing stars, the corrosion, the impossibility of making any of it exact. [619] Instead, she switched off the torch and let him steady the cylinder while she gathered her tools. [636] Perhaps her mother would not smile; perhaps Mara would still need help finding her among the beds. [653] The lamp would not change that. [659] But there would be something above the bed they had both known before either of them needed help. [677]

They carried it through the doorway together, careful not to crush it, while the men beside the silent excavators waited. [697]
