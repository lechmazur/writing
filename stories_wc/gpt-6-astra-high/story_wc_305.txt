In the umbral grotto above the clouds, Mara kept the rebirth spring clear and the waiting people clean. [18] Vines curtained the entrance, and sunlight passing through their grapes laid small green blessings on the stone. [35] Even the bees seemed reluctant to disturb the vineyard hush. [45] She had expected such quiet inside herself after forgiving the unforgivable. [56] Instead, each morning she woke with her jaw clenched. [65]

The man in the nearest bed had burned her village for refusing a tax. [79] Mara's daughter had died carrying other children from the school. [89] Forty years later, his soldiers had delivered him here, where every exhausted life could enter the spring and emerge an infant, stripped of memory. [113] Mara had forgiven him before washing the sores beneath his rings. [124] She did not forgive the burning. [130] That distinction was something she had to learn anew each day. [141]

She was kindly decisive with everyone except herself. [149] She warmed cold feet, refused unnecessary pain, and told frightened families exactly when to return. [164] Yet she watched the vine keepers eating their lunches outside and wondered where their contentment came from. [181] Surely it had a source, like the spring, and surely she could find it. [195]

On the morning his breathing shortened, the old commander caught her sleeve. [207] "Do not put me in the water." [214] She waited, holding the cup he could no longer lift. [224] "If I forget, then everything I did will have happened to somebody else." [237] For the first time, she understood that he feared innocence more than death. [250] She promised no rebirth without his consent and called her assistant to sit beside him. [265]

Then she climbed the ancient fig rooted at the grotto's mouth. [276] Climbing trees had helped her think better since childhood, when a difficult question became manageable one branch at a time. [296] Halfway up, her braid caught on a twig. [304] She freed it by removing the antique hairpin her daughter had worn, a silver vine passed down through five generations. [324] Without it, her hair loosened across her shoulders. [332] Her daughter had laughed at that hair, calling it a storm cloud. [344]

Below, a vine keeper buried spoiled grapes beneath a young plant. [355] The fruit would return as sweetness, but nobody expected the earth to declare that it had never rotted. [373] Mara stayed until she could hear the keeper humming. [382]

Back inside, she brought the commander the village register, which she had carried into exile. [397] "You will forget," she said, "but we will not." [406] At his request she read every name, including her daughter's, and he told her where his treasury was hidden. [425] She wrote directions for the surviving families in the margin. [435] It was neither payment nor pardon, but something still possible. [445] Then he asked for the spring. [451]

Mara fetched the linen coverlet saved from her daughter's room, its edges scorched where sparks had settled. [468] For forty years she had refused to cut it. [477] Now she took her sewing shears and began to scallop the brittle border, making each hollow deep enough to remove the char. [499] She hemmed the curves with the little stitches her daughter had learned at her knee. [514] Enough sound cloth remained to wrap a baby. [522]

At sunset she lowered him into the spring. [530] The water darkened, then cleared around a small red body with furious, reaching hands. [544] She lifted the child before he could swallow water and wrapped him in the altered linen. [560] Tomorrow, a family from the lower terraces would come for him. [571] Tonight, he needed warmth. [575]

Her loose hair brushed his mouth, and he turned toward it, hungry. [587] Mara twisted it up and secured it with the silver vine. [598] The pin had held her mother's hair away from bread dough, and her daughter's away from schoolroom ink. [616] Now it kept hers clear while she measured milk into a feeding cup. [629] Her jaw had loosened without her noticing. [636] The register lay open nearby, beyond the reach of spilling milk, and she did not close it. [653] Outside, the last baskets passed quietly between the vines. [662] She had thought contentment would mean needing nothing more. [671] But there was milk enough, and the child was drinking. [681]
