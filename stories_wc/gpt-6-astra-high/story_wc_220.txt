After the last resource war, the scrapyard settlement of Splice stretched across the cracked wasteland, each neighborhood stranded on a separate shelf of earth. [24] Between shelves lay gullies full of wire, where wind made a wild hush that sounded almost like people keeping secrets. [44] Mara drew maps there, though hers were intuitively descriptive rather than exact: the hungry slope, the kettle bend, the shade that smells of rain. [68] Travelers trusted them because hunger, thirst, and fear were distances everybody understood. [80]

Her bloodhound, Mercy, had retired from finding bodies when she did. [91] Now he found dropped gloves and sleeping children, and she paid him in marrow instead of apologies. [108]

One evening, a scavenger brought her a phonograph cylinder recovered from a collapsed wartime listening post. [124] Around its case lay an autumn leaf preserved in wax, the only beautiful thing Mara's sister had carried into the army. [145] Mara had identified Ilen's body by her boots, because there had been no face left to recognize. [162] Mercy had refused that grave, pulling toward the ravine until Mara dragged him back. [176]

She set the cylinder turning, but its song was only static. [187] Inside the lid, Ilen's cramped handwriting offered a riddle: What survives a forest, yet teaches light to tell the truth? [207] Mara held the waxed leaf against her lamp. [215] Its amber light washed over the cylinder, making red camouflage scratches fade while blue marks darkened. [231] Through her mapmaking lens, the marks became letters. [239]

By reading secret texts in the grooves of a phonograph cylinder, Mara discovered that her sister had not died for the eastern wells. [262] She had deserted with the engineers ordered to poison them, leaving her boots on someone the army had made unrecognizable. [282] Under the ravine, they had sheltered beside a surviving aqueduct. [292] They could share its water, but not with the officers who still governed Splice's scattered shelves. [308] The final groove held no coordinates, only instructions: Follow the wind that smells of wet iron. [324] Ilen had written in Mara's language. [330]

These were tinted revelations, Mara thought, holding the leaf until its stem cast a tiny gallows across her wrist. [349] Through the army's colors, Ilen was a traitor; through amber, she was alive. [362] Neither light excused the stranger buried beneath her name. [371]

At dawn, Mara packed water, the cylinder, and the old scarf Mercy had once used to track Ilen home from market. [392] At the ravine's lip, he balked at the familiar harness. [402] "Not bodies," Mara promised, and unclipped its heavy search bell. [412] She wanted to translate distance into closeness, but for the first time she understood that wanting did not give her the right to pull. [436] She waited until he chose the descent. [443]

Below, bridges of crushed cars sagged between the cliffs, and Mercy tested each crossing before letting her follow. [461] At a fork, Mara smelled nothing but rust; the hound turned toward a draft that stirred his hanging ears. [480] They reached a welded gate with rifle barrels poking through its mesh. [492] Behind it stood a graying woman wearing boots too large for her. [504]

"You took your time," Ilen said, but her hands shook. [514] Mercy pressed his nose through the mesh and made the small, foolish sound he reserved for people returning. [532]

Mara wanted to ask why no second message had come, why she had been left to mourn. [549] Instead she saw the rifles, the children's faces behind them, and the scars where Ilen's army insignia had been burned away. [570]

"Whose grave did I dig?" she asked. [577]

"A pump mechanic named Sera, who would not open the poison valves." [589]

Mara wrote the name on the blank margin of her map. [600] "I'll change her stone," she said, and watched Ilen lower her rifle. [612]

"And your map?" Ilen asked. [617]

Mara could already imagine it copied across Splice, soldiers following her careful marks straight to this gate. [634] She folded it with the route inside and passed it through the mesh. [647]

"You decide what I carry back." [653]

For a while there was only water moving underground, an impossible abundance making almost no sound. [669] Then Ilen opened the gate just wide enough for a tired dog and someone willing to sit beside her. [688] Mara set the amber leaf between them, and neither sister asked the other to come any closer yet. [706]
