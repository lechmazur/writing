Mara's foresight worked in hindsight only: she dreamed tomorrow as something already lost, then woke to interpret the wreckage. [19] A drowned candle meant a flooded house; a tooth in her palm meant the footbridge would lose a stone. [38] She had saved people through interpreted dreams, but never her father, whose last appearance had been a coat walking without him. [59] She had mistaken that for a warning about cold. [68] He had vanished into the forest before the previous great storm. [79]

Now another storm approached, dragging a green darkness over the hills, and the paths began choosing their walkers again. [98] The track toward higher ground bent under Mara's feet, although it remained straight for the villagers following her. [116] Within twenty paces she was alone. [122] She could have fought her way back through the brambles, but she needed to know why her father had been chosen. [143] This time she marked each turning with strips torn from her skirt. [155]

The path delivered her to a vine-draped bunker hidden in a rewilded forest where the old roads slept beneath roots. [175] Its door carried the same branching emblem as the wafer seal on her father's appointment papers. [191] She had brought those papers to prove he had existed, should the forest offer only bones. [207] Instead, the door's warm brass tongue tasted the seal and opened. [218]

Inside, rootlets threaded a switchboard, and her father's coat hung above a narrow bed. [232] Beneath it lay his boots, neatly touching. [239] A maintenance ledger recorded his death during the last flood, after seventeen hours spent keeping the pumps alive. [257] His last entry, in cramped handwriting, read: "Still refusing the others." [268]

Across a glass panel crawled a decaying directive: PRESERVE THE KEEPERS. [279] Under it, a list of authorized names flickered and contracted until only hers remained. [293] Her father had registered her as his apprentice when she was six. [305]

Beyond the walls, roots groaned, dragging bridges away from the valley and assembling one dry route to this room. [324] The forest was not choosing the worthy; it was obeying the surviving scraps of a list. [340]

Mara pressed her forehead against the switchboard and let its damp warmth pull her into sleep. [356] She dreamed the storm was over and every house stood empty except this bunker, where a small girl wore her father's coat. [378] The girl dealt playing cards to chairs occupied by rain. [388] Mara had learned to be precisely playful with such visions, changing one detail at a time and watching what answered. [408] She turned a king upside down; nothing changed. [416] She dealt a card to every empty chair, and roots lifted the floor. [429] Beneath it, thousands of names shone like worms after rain. [439]

She woke understanding that keeper had once meant everyone who tended this watershed, not merely those appointed to maintain its machinery. [460] The forest's memory had rotted from the edges inward, forgetting the people furthest from its heart. [476]

Two levers divided the remaining power between the bunker's pumps and the root bridges across the valley. [493] Beside them, her father had written a reminder to restore the crossings once the refuge was secure. [510] He had died trying to satisfy a command that could no longer be satisfied. [524] The failing pumps would always need more. [531]

To transcend his obedience, she would have to abandon the last place that remembered him. [546] She could carry his coat, but not the worn bed, the careful notes, the smell of his soldering iron. [565] For once, hindsight offered no useful answer. [572] She could only try to leave a different wreckage. [581]

Mara pressed the wafer seal into the override socket and held it there until the old gum softened beneath her thumb. [602] The bridges' lever yielded. [606] As thunder broke overhead, water entered around her boots, and the panel extinguished her name. [621]

She snatched the coat and climbed out into rain thick enough to chew. [634] Her marked path was gone. [639] Instead, pale roots lifted through the mud in every direction, weaving crossings toward the ridge. [654] From somewhere below came a child's shout, then an answering voice, then the rough music of people hauling one another uphill. [675] Mara followed those voices rather than the roots, stopping wherever a hand reached out of the dark. [692] By morning, her father's coat would belong to someone she had not yet met. [706]
