When salt killed the last ground orchard, Mara carried her mother's balloonist's altitude gauge into the tide-polished grotto. [18] For generations, her island had grown pears in suspended gardens, until a storm tore their lifting envelopes and dropped the trees into the sea. [42] Her mother had fallen with them. [48] Now the balloon sheds sheltered hungry families, and Mara wanted to revitalize the island without asking anyone to trust another beautiful promise. [70] She was a logical dreamer: she wanted to puzzle out new equations for hope, with room in them for failure. [90]

At low tide, the grotto's polished walls reflected lives unfolding across parallel timelines. [103] In some, the old orchards had survived the storm; in others, even the balloon sheds were gone. [120] Speech never crossed the shining stone, but color did. [129] During launch festivals, children had communicated with their other selves by creating patterns with decorations, arranging shell stars and glass beads against the walls. [153] Mara brought those decorations back in a fish basket. [162] She set out a pear, a balloon, and a question mark made of silver ribbon. [177] By dawn, six other Maras were answering. [184]

One had her mother beside her. [190] Mara pressed a hand against that reflection, then withdrew it before grief could turn the experiment into a prayer. [209] They needed food, not a doorway. [215]

She proposed trial balloons carrying baskets of damp soil, each timeline testing a different design with the same load. [234] Shells stood for lift, blue beads for water, broken stars for failure. [246] The gauge, duplicated by their common past, gave them a shared measure of height. [260] Each Mara reset it at sea level before a launch. [270] She linked hers to a clockwork pencil, so the balloons could testify without carrying a person. [286] They would haul the paper records down by tether. [295] Their task was modest: keep a seed bed above the salt spray for a week without losing it to the wind. [316]

Mara stayed lucidly watchful through the launches, correcting for weather with the harbor's old barometer. [331] For three days, the prettiest design won: a single enormous envelope, bright as an unbroken moon. [347] Its recorded height scarcely varied. [352] Then the next tide brought a mosaic of broken stars. [362] In that world, a seam had opened, and the whole seed bed had fallen. [376] Mara recognized the failure that had killed her mother. [385] She had been solving for steadiness because she could not bear to think about falling. [400]

Another reflection offered an ugly cluster of small balloons. [409] One had burst, but its companions kept the basket above the salt. [421] The gauge's wavering trace ended not at zero, but at ninety feet. [433] That was enough. [436] Together, the Maras revised their patterns, leaving gaps where a balloon could fail without pulling its neighbors down. [454] They traded a little planting space for spare lifting capacity. [464] Their equation now required eight balloons to lift the load carried by nine. [477] Only after every cluster had survived a week did she gather up the decorations. [491]

Mara took that pattern home instead of a promise. [500] She asked for one abandoned shed, nine repaired envelopes, and seedlings, not passengers. [513] The families agreed on a limit: nothing would be salvaged from the roofs keeping their children dry. [530] Mara added that boundary to the ribbon equations. [538]

The first garden rose at slack tide, so gently that its bean leaves scarcely trembled. [553] Below it, people opened the sheds to sew, splice, and argue about the next bed. [568] There would be no pears for years, and the beans would not feed everyone. [582] But the work had somewhere to go now, and so did the scraps people had been saving without knowing why. [602]

That evening, Mara returned to the grotto and laid nine shell stars around a green bead. [618] Across the stone, her living mother answered with the same small constellation. [630] Mara let herself watch until the tide erased it. [639] Then she climbed back toward the sheds, where someone had left a light burning for her. [655]
