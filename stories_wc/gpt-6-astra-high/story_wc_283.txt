Mara, the hourglass turner, worked in an amber warehouse beneath cliffs that shed yellow dust whenever the town's wells rose. [20] Her task was to give the council exactly one minute of prophecy each morning, then cork the canyon echo in a jar. [42] For forty years, its overlapping voices had yielded the same official warning: When the yellow rain comes, the lower town is lost. [64]

That morning, dust settled on her wrists, and beneath the familiar clamor she heard a small, stubborn word: unless. [83] She kept the jar open after the sand stopped. [92] The clerk closed his ledger and reminded her that unauthorized interpretations could cost her both her position and her warehouse lodging. [113]

By noon, council workers were fencing off the lower streets, where her daughter lived above a bakery. [130] People had to leave; condemned streets were never reopened, and everything they could not carry would remain behind the fence. [150] Mara had once obeyed such an order, abandoning her husband's cobbler shop during a flood that never reached its threshold. [170] He had died elsewhere, still keeping the useless key. [179] She could not bear to hand that inheritance to their daughter. [190]

The warehouse held other things recovered from the canyon: broken tools, amber rubble, and sacks of lost buttons swept from an ancient road. [213] Mara's mother, a dressmaker, had taught her that trapped echoes recognized whatever their speakers had worn. [229] Scholars brought jeweled buckles to consult the jar, assuming only kings deserved to outlast their bodies. [245] None had considered the buttons. [250]

That night, Mara began to unscramble the chorus by cataloging lost buttons across the council's empty hearing table. [268] She sorted bone from shell, military brass from the cheap stamped tin that laundresses fastened to their working sleeves. [287] Touched to the jar, each button drew one voice forward. [297] A tin disk brought a woman complaining that water backed up behind the retaining wall whenever the cliff drains clogged. [317] A horn toggle answered with a mason's directions for finding those drains beneath the warehouse floor. [333]

Mara fetched neighbors who knew old clothing better than she did, promising nothing except a question worth staying awake for. [353] The baker recognized a carter's button; a retired nurse identified the green glass worn by the former water guild. [372] With each identification, they entered the speaker and instructions in Mara's ledger, assembling shared wisdom where the council had sought a single commanding voice. [396]

As the truth unraveled, the prophecy became a meeting, preserved by the canyon before a landslide swallowed the ancient road. [416] Its speakers had not foretold destruction; they had argued about preventing it. [428] Finally, a wooden button supplied the missing condition: The lower town is lost unless we open the western drains. [447]

Under its rim, someone had scratched a tiny cobbler's awl. [457] Mara touched the mark, quietly inspired by this stranger who had understood that warnings could also be invitations to work. [477] She wanted to spin a prophecy into raw possibility, but possibility required breaking the council's floor. [493]

At dawn, the clerk found her daughter levering up flagstones while the baker rolled valuable amber blocks out of their path. [514]

"You cannot promise this will save anything," he said. [523]

"No," Mara answered, and handed him the ledger instead. [532]

He read while dust sifted onto the page, then took off his coat and helped carry stones. [549] Beneath the floor they found three mouths choked with clay, exactly where the mason's voice had placed them. [567] The first pick broke through into an unearthed silence, a hollow where water waited without moving. [583] Then a brown torrent struck the channel and ran toward the canyon, carrying ancient silt into daylight. [600]

By evening, the wells had fallen a handspan, although nobody pretended the cliffs would ever become harmless. [617] Mara's daughter went home with mud on her stockings and orders to watch the water through the night. [635] The council kept its fence another day; certainty was harder to dismantle than stone. [649] But the clerk left Mara the ledger, its remaining pages blank. [660]

When neighbors returned with more buttons, she set the hourglass beside the open jar and let its last grains fall. [680] For once, nobody took that as a reason to stop listening. [691]
