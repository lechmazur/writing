In the canyon of endless echoes, the towers of bone kept speaking long after their builders had become dust. [19] Iven lived inside the smallest, telling stories in the conquerors' language while copper veins glimmered beneath his wrists. [37] The metal had been threaded through him at the imperial school, where storytellers learned to amplify their voices and forget their mothers' words. [60]

Now his mother was dying, the last person who understood the songs scratched into the towers. [76] Quietly articulate, Iven could make a market crowd lean close, but he could not answer her simplest childhood question. [95] Since returning, he had paid for her broth with the approved tales that had helped bury her language. [113]

She had spent her life carving those songs into bone, and her wasted hands could no longer feel them. [132] The tower's oldest songs were not carved as letters, but as roughness, grain, and hollows that taught a hand how to move. [154]

"Make the ridges audible," she asked in the common tongue. [164]

If he could translate texture into sound, she might recognize what his schooling had taught him to miss. [182]

To do it, he stole the flawed diamond from the governor's abandoned listening engine. [196] It was rumored to be cursed, having killed three listeners by feeding their amplified heartbeats back into their chests. [215] Yet the same flaw might harness the faint vibrations his copper carried when he stroked a carved surface. [233]

Below the towers ran a tunnel of echoes, lined with carved bone and split by a narrow seam of sky. [253] There he laid his mother on blankets, pressed one hand against her carvings, and closed the other around the diamond. [273]

The first note shook bone dust from the ceiling and left blood beneath his fingernails. [288] Above the seam, a flock of canyon swifts scattered, then reformed into two streams around an invisible center. [306] Iven opened his hand, and the birds joined again. [315]

He had watched them since childhood, slipping between the canyon's returning cries without ever losing their formation. [332] They were not fleeing noise; they were mapping where its waves collided. [344] Their patterns in flight gave him something the imperial diagrams lacked: a map of the echoes outside his own body. [364]

On the next attempt, he turned the diamond until their divided paths met. [377] The pressure behind his eyes eased, and a low, rough chord rolled from his chest. [392] He moved his fingertips across a ridge; the chord rose like wind combing dry grass. [407]

His mother lifted her head. [412]

All afternoon he followed the flock, adjusting his grip whenever their circles narrowed, making each groove audible without letting its echo crush him. [435] Each note roughened the polished voice that earned his living, but he did not stop. [450] He had always used the copper to project words chosen by others; now, for the first time, he let something older choose his voice. [474]

At sunset, her carving became a melody with a recurring break, a little space that seemed to wait for an answer. [495] He recognized the rhythm of her kneading, the pause when she had let him punch his little fist into the dough. [516] His mother hummed into that space. [522]

Then she spoke three words he did not know and translated them with her last easy breath. [539]

"Come home hungry." [542]

"It means there will be enough." [548]

Iven had no time to ask whether she meant food or forgiveness. [560] Her breathing stopped while the tune was still returning from the canyon walls. [573] He listened across the death of a language: first with his mother breathing beside him, then alone. [590]

The swifts settled, and he opened his hand before the rising pressure could mistake his grief for another note. [609] In the morning, he would bring the children to the tunnel and teach them where to leave the answering space. [629] He could not give them back the language. [637] He could give them a place inside its silence. [646]
