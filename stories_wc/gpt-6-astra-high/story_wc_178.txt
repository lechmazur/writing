Between regimes, while the old king's banners burned and the new king's caravans approached, Nera bought the palace's remaining electricity at auction. [22] She paid with copper salvaged from instruments nobody had bothered to confiscate. [34] A frugal astronomer learned to distinguish a ruined kingdom from a useful pile of parts. [49]

The palace was ancient Babylon reimagined by engineers who had mistaken grandeur for insulation. [63] Blue ceramic lions guarded cooling vents; hanging gardens shaded a telescope hidden inside the highest ziggurat. [79] At dusk, irrigation channels hummed beneath the gathering refugees' songs, and violet light softened every broken thing. [96] By morning, the new regime would turn the observatory into a cannon. [108]

Nera had one night to answer her husband's impossible letter. [118] Twelve years dead, Iddin had apparently written from somewhere beyond Neptune. [129] The signal arrived every evening, shifted precisely by the changing distance between Earth and its invisible source. [146] She called herself empirically ironic: she trusted no miracle until it repeated, then complained about its operating costs. [164] This miracle had repeated nineteen times. [170]

On her desk lay a magic square tile, its nine numbered cells summing to fifteen in every direction. [188] Iddin had made it as a wedding present because she disliked flowers that required watering. [203] Its arrangement also supplied the scrambling order for their private messages. [214] They had practiced encrypted devotion under a government that monitored even the tenderness of astronomers. [229] Before his execution, Iddin had transmitted the tile's pattern and one final message toward an unexplained stellar occultation. [247] She had resented him for spending their heating allowance on a farewell nobody would hear. [262]

Now the farewell was coming back, with additions. [270] Reordering the signal's blocks according to the tile, she uncovered not a resurrection but a reply. [286] Through schematic representations, the stranger had answered his drawings of two people sharing a roof. [301] One figure became Earth, the other a vessel; the roof became a field bending radiation around both. [318] Beneath them, equal columns recorded energy given and received. [327] The stranger offered shelter, but required an answering beam to establish the field's terrestrial anchor. [342]

Nera checked its equations against the nineteen measured disturbances in the telescope's electronics. [355] They matched, which proved capability, not kindness. [362] A shelter could also be a cage. [369]

Footsteps sounded on the stair, and the caretaker appeared carrying the new king's requisition. [383] "They will spare you if you leave the mirrors intact," he said. [395] Below him, dusk collected in the channels like diluted wine. [405]

Nera looked at the tile, where no row received more than another. [417] She could not promise Earth, or pledge obedience to something she could not name. [431] But she could offer one roof, conditionally. [438] She redrew the stranger's schematic with a switch on either side, so either participant could withdraw without injuring the other. [458] Then she limited the field to the palace and its gardens, already crowded with people fleeing both armies. [476]

"Help me uncloak the telescope," she told the caretaker. [485]

"If they see the beam, they'll know we refused." [494]

"Then let them see what we chose instead." [502]

Together they cranked apart the blue panels that had disguised the observatory's aperture as a lion's shut mouth. [520] Nera fed her purchased current into the transmitter, keeping only enough for the irrigation pumps. [535] She wanted to seal a covenant with the unknown, not make another sacrifice to it. [550] The beam rose through the violet air. [557]

For ten hours she watched the receiver, while the caretaker moved families beneath the gardens' thick retaining walls. [575] At dawn, the king's first ranging laser touched the ziggurat and divided into a harmless crown of amber. [593] The receiver printed her altered drawing, both switches intact, beside a new image: an open hand with unfamiliar joints. [612]

Nera tested her switch for one heartbeat, and the crown vanished. [623] When she restored it, the shelter returned. [630] Outside, engines idled; someone began arguing instead of firing. [639] There would be negotiations, shortages, perhaps betrayal, and she had no experiment that could abolish any of them. [657] The covenant bought a place to begin. [664]

She set the tile beside the transmitter, not as an offering, but where she could use it again. [682] Below, the pumps continued their low music, and the refugees passed cups along the channels, giving each person enough. [701]
