Mara worked as a silent-choir bellhop in the cobalt fortress under snow, carrying other people's luggage toward rooms warmer than her childhood home. [23] The choir consisted of twelve patients whose rebuilt throats could not yet produce a sound. [38] They paid for surgery through service, gliding in white uniforms that the director described to wealthy guests as seraphically mundane. [58] Mara had once sung for a living, before a factory fire burned her throat and killed her younger brother. [77] She remembered accepting the first place on the rescue ladder without asking who would get the second. [94] She wanted her voice back, but she also wanted to understand the cost of safety. [109]

Cold was forbidden during nerve regeneration; the surgeon said another injury could leave her voiceless permanently. [125] So she practiced her breathing beside the lobby piano, where guests applauded the possibility of her recovery between courses. [144] In her pocket she kept a piano bench bolt, replaced that autumn after its square head had worn smooth. [163] It made a clear, gentle knock against brass pipes, her substitute for calling ahead on narrow stairs. [180]

When the avalanche struck, the fortress shuddered, then settled into the immense quiet its advertisements promised. [196] The director ordered the outer service gallery sealed before the broken loading doors could drain the building's heat. [214] "Everyone is accounted for," he wrote on the duty slate. [224]

But eight supper bowls remained untouched in the porters' kitchen, their surfaces filmed with the fat of cooling broth. [243] Mara found them via the slightest discoloration on a dusty spiral staircase: a damp patch beside the gallery's inspection hatch. [263] Breath was condensing around its rim. [269] She tapped with her bolt, and something tapped back. [278] Eight answering knocks, then eight again. [284]

The hatch was too small for even a child's shoulders. [294] Through it, the head porter pushed a delivery sheet bearing the director's signature and eight names. [310] The order had sent them outside to clear the loading doors minutes before the avalanche. [325] He had known exactly who was missing. [332]

When she brought him the sheet, he did not deny it. [343] "There are children recovering upstairs," he said, pointing toward the gallery gate's frostbound wheel. [357] "Open that, and everyone pays for eight men." [365] He touched her neck with the proprietary tenderness of someone protecting an investment. [378] Then he went upstairs to reassure the guests. [386]

Mara stood with the paper trembling between her fingers, discovering that unpardonable silence required no damaged throat at all. [405] She could return to the piano, heal, and spend the rest of her life singing over those eight knocks. [424]

Instead she carried the sheet into the dining room and laid it across the director's place setting. [441] Then she struck a water pipe with the bolt: the choir's signal for help with a heavy load. [459] Her colleagues came carrying blankets, expecting luggage, and found her already turning the wheel. [473] She showed them eight fingers, then pointed upstairs. [481] Two ran to close the ward doors; the others wrapped their faces and took hold beside her. [498]

They opened the gate only wide enough for one body, but the wind still entered like a blade. [516] The wheel fought to spin shut, and Mara braced herself against it while the others pulled men through the gap. [536] At the sixth, her scarf froze against the tender seam beneath her jaw. [549] At the seventh, someone touched her shoulder, offering to take her place. [561] She let him. [564] But she chose to remain beside the opening, tapping steadily against the gatepost until the last porter crawled toward the sound. [585] Only after they shut the gate did she notice that she was holding the bolt with both hands. [603]

By morning the upstairs rooms were cold, but no one in them had died. [617] The head porter lost three fingers, and the surgeon would promise Mara nothing. [630] The guests, having read the delivery sheet, demanded an account the director could not make comforting. [646]

That afternoon two guests helped move the lobby piano into the staff kitchen, where everyone shared the remaining heat. [665] Mara sat on its repaired bench and tapped time while the men who still had voices tried to sing. [684] Their breath showed in the air; they kept losing the tune and waiting for one another.
