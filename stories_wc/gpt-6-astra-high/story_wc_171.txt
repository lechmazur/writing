On the ancient terrace at midsummer, Sera held an ebony clipboard and watched the smaller moon approach the larger. [19] She had been the Forever War's peace negotiator for thirty years, long enough to recognize a massacre inside a beautifully worded concession. [41] Tonight, during the slow dance of celestial bodies, she meant to negotiate something that would survive sunrise. [58]

Below, both armies waited beside the river they had spent generations preventing each other from crossing. [74] Above, the commanders' stewards guarded their strongboxes. [81]

Commander Vey asked, "Your usual amnesty?" [87]

Sera straightened his cup with a hand that barely trembled. [97] Her colleagues called her operatively gentle: she could cut justice from an agreement without disturbing its reassuring shape. [115] That skill had kept her employed and the war alive. [125]

"Not tonight," she said, and unclipped the top sheet. [134]

Beneath it lay a deed transferring her estate to the survivors of the embassy fire. [149] The clipboard itself had been fashioned from that embassy's ebony door, which she had ordered opened to Vey's soldiers. [168] In exchange, he had released her captive son. [176] She had called the deaths an unforeseeable breach of sanctuary. [186]

Her son, now a father, still believed her. [194] Last winter, he had asked her to train his daughter as her successor. [207] She had imagined teaching the girl to call another child's death necessary, and found she could not write back. [226]

Vey set down his wine. [231] "You cannot purchase innocence with a house." [238]

"No," said Sera. [241] "But houses can shelter people." [246]

She beckoned to Mara, the embassy laundress who had escaped through a drain while her daughters slept upstairs. [264] Mara had refused every invitation to a reconciliation feast; she had accepted this one only after inspecting the deed. [283]

She asked, "And the hearings?" [288]

Sera placed her signed confession beside the deed. [296] "Public, with judges chosen by the displaced, and no immunity for the negotiator." [309]

The other commander, Iven, leaned forward. [315] "A moving sacrifice, but your property will not feed two armies." [326]

Now Sera uncovered the remaining pages: inventories of commandeered mills, occupied farms, and river tolls diverted into officers' purses. [345] All winter, Mara's network of washerwomen had counted what passed through the camps. [358] They knew how much peace would cost and who could afford it. [370]

The ancient law made salvaged sanctuary wood a witness; through its ritual significance, an oath could bind even a commander's estates. [391] Oaths were sworn only when the moons touched, but previous treaties had named no one actually empowered to seize an oathbreaker's holdings. [413] Sera had always left that detail tactfully vague. [421] Tonight the treaty named the soldiers' households as trustees, with grain released directly to them at dawn. [438]

Iven said, "You expect us to consent to confiscation?" [447]

"I expect you to face your soldiers and explain why their children should stay hungry." [462]

Below the terrace, drummers began to muster soldiers around the inventories posted beside their cooking fires. [478] Mara had arranged that, too. [483] Sera had wanted to anchor ideals in practice; Mara had insisted that practice needed names, keys, and witnesses. [501]

Vey's hand rested on his sword, then withdrew as his own standard bearer stepped toward the clipboard. [518]

"My family works your south mill," the young man said. [528] "Does this include them?" [532]

"Especially them," Mara answered. [536]

Iven demanded protection for tenants if their lord broke the truce, and for the first time that evening Sera smiled without calculation. [558] They argued over that clause while the gap between the moons narrowed to a hair's breadth. [574] When the moons overlapped, both commanders pressed their palms against the ebony and spoke the amended oath. [591] Vey looked not at Sera but at his standard bearer. [601]

No one cheered. [604] Mara took the mill keys, counted them, and dispatched runners before the moons separated. [618]

Sera surrendered her seal of office last. [625] Without it, she could neither draw her pension nor bargain privately for a gentler sentence. [640] Somewhere beyond the river, her son would wake to learn what had bought his life and what she had done with his inheritance. [663]

She reached for Mara's hand, stopped, and instead lifted the heavy clipboard so the laundress could finish checking the grain allotments. [684]

Dawn found them still working among the terrace's broken mosaics. [694] Missing tiles had disfigured a procession of dancers, but their surviving feet all pointed toward the river. [711] Below, soldiers from both banks were dragging the first bridge timbers into place. [724]

"Hold it steady," Mara said, and Sera did. [732]
