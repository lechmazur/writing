On the first page of the inheritance ledger, Ada found her appointment as curator, signed by a granddaughter she had never conceived of having. [24] Beyond the office window, an astral orchard was blossoming on a lonely asteroid, each silver branch shaking against a sky without weather. [46] The bewildered curator checked the signature against the little parcel delivered that morning to her rented room on Earth. [65] Both bore the same name, Linn, and the same impossible instruction: Please remember me before I happen. [82]

Inside the parcel lay a pistachio shell cameo blank, its pale inner surface polished smooth enough to receive a face. [102] Touching it had brought her here, along with a sudden memory of loving someone whose features she could not recall. [122]

The ledger turned its own page. [128] Nonlinear inheritance, it explained, allowed possible descendants to bequeath things to their ancestors, including property, skills, and feelings. [146] Identification settled possibility into history; until then, the gift was real, but its giver remained free to become somebody else. [166] Her appointment lasted between the first and last page, when she must either claim the orchard or release it into the common sky. [189] To claim it, she needed only to discover Linn's face and carve it into the shell. [205]

Below that invitation, in red, stood a prohibition against entering the roots to inquire into a donor's identity. [223] Ada laughed uneasily at this simply complicated arrangement, which demanded knowledge and forbade the only apparent means of obtaining it. [243] A curator was presumably expected to recognize her own family, but Ada had grown up with numbered beds instead of family photographs. [265] She wanted to pursue forbidden knowledge for one embarrassingly ordinary reason: somebody had loved her, and she wanted to know who. [286]

Before disobeying, she went to the bathroom. [293] She had a history of having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms, where grand problems shrank beside the indisputable needs of a body. [314] Washing her hands, she understood that receiving love did not necessarily mean owing obedience, even to a granddaughter. [332]

She pocketed the blank and descended through a hatch beneath the nearest tree. [345] The roots glowed like veins under thin skin, carrying voices upstream through time. [358] One called her Grandmother, and a thousand others answered, warmly enough to make the darkness worse. [374]

When Ada asked for Linn, every root brightened. [382] She saw a mechanic with golden teeth, a girl singing underwater, an old woman teaching children to mend a sail. [402] All were Linn, branching from the same unfinished life; the voice she chose to follow would become the granddaughter who had sent the gift. [426] Around each brightening face, neighboring roots blackened, and the smell of burned sugar rose through the beautiful air. [444]

The prohibition was not guarding a secret but a population. [454]

Ada fled upstairs, carrying the first distinct face in her mind like a stolen match. [469] The ledger was almost finished, its turning pages stirring petals across the desk, and a carving needle waited beside it. [489] She escaped again into the bathroom, this time because she could not bear the hopeful sounds outside. [506]

In the mirror, her face briefly resembled every face she had seen below. [519] Then came the second breakthrough: a blank was not an absence awaiting correction, but room someone had deliberately left. [538] Linn had sent affection without a portrait because she wanted remembering, not fixing in place. [553] Ada could love the giver without making the gift into proof that she would never be alone. [570]

Back at the desk, she set down the needle and carried the shell outside. [584] Beneath the oldest pistachio tree lay its matching half, rough outside and softly luminous within, still waiting for her. [603] She closed the two halves around their untouched hollow, making a small shelter where the portrait would have been. [622] There would be no estate, no guaranteed family, only the astonishing fact that an unfinished person had already been kind to her. [644]

As the orchard loosened from the asteroid, its blossoms became bright moving points, scattering among the stars instead of gathering behind a single face. [668] Ada was frightened by how empty the sky became, and relieved by how much room it held. [685] She whispered her thanks into the shell. [692] The last page fell, and her rented room folded around her, with morning sunlight lying across the spare kitchen chair. [712]
