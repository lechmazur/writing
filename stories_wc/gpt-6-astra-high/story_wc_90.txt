Seventy years after the last human was born, she sat beneath a leaking pipe and threaded an ancient needle. [19] Mara's home was a recycled spaceship, pieced together from hospital barges and prison transports whose dead still whispered through the ventilation grilles. [41] The shipbuilders had lodged those minds in a locked dimension beyond the hull, safe from the dying universe. [59] At dawn the reactor would fail, taking the only entrance with it. [71] Her brother waited inside. [75] She meant to push the dimension open before its door vanished forever. [87]

Two conditions governed entry: a traveler must bring every part of herself, and weigh no more than a sleeping child. [107] The passage was woven from memory, which could support very little living flesh. [120] Worse, whatever visitors refused to recognize in themselves became independent creatures there, stalking the dead. [135] The lock protected its inhabitants from such monsters. [143]

Mara, a steadfast pacifist, had expected the first condition to be easy. [155] During the evacuation wars, she had carried a wounded enemy through gunfire rather than pick up his rifle. [173] The second condition should have been possible too. [181] Like all the final generations, she became gravitationally lighter when happy, an engineered economy for crowded orbital habitats. [199] But she had not risen onto her toes in forty years. [210]

Upon her knees lay the ship's oldest instrument: Kwan Yin's tear, a needle carved from a pearl and threaded with her own white hair. [234] It could stitch memories into navigable seams, but first it needed the traveler's complete shadow. [249] Shadow work, the shipbuilders' manual called the preparation: finding what you had exiled from yourself and giving it shelter. [268] The needle located those exiles via the faint shimmer of long-dried tears on faded photographs. [283] Mara had only one photograph left. [289]

In it, her brother held a paper bird above her infant face. [301] When she lowered the needle into the silvered stain beneath his wrist, the cabin filled with smoke. [318] Once again she knelt beside the enemy soldier, pressing cloth against his open belly. [332] Beyond him, beneath fallen masonry, her brother was calling her name. [343] She had known the soldier could survive and her brother could not. [355] She had made the merciful choice, and hated its beneficiary ever since. [367]

From the photograph crawled a small black Mara, carrying the rifle she had never touched. [382] "Let him die," it said. [387] She recoiled, and the thing grew long enough to reach the door. [399] For forty years she had called that voice exhaustion, grief, anything but hers. [412] Now the lock trembled against its touch. [419]

Mara's scissors could sever the creature. [425] Instead, she set them gently aside. [431] She had refused to kill strangers; she would not begin with the frightened creature inside her. [447] "I wanted him dead," she said, keeping her hands open. [457] "I still wish I had been able to save my brother instead." [469] The creature lowered its rifle, and she began to stitch its feet into the hem of her coat. [487] Every puncture brought back the soldier's blood, her brother's voice, the unbearable fact that compassion had not made her pure. [507] She stitched until the shadow lay against her own, neither forgiven nor banished. [520]

Then something else emerged from the photograph. [528] Her brother had made that bird from a ration notice, and its badly folded wings kept striking his nose. [547] She remembered his solemn indignation, the ridiculous squawk he made to make her laugh. [561] A laugh escaped her now, small and astonished, with grief still moving through it. [575] Her heels lifted from the deck. [581]

The needle drew a shining thread from the photograph to the hull. [593] Mara followed it, sewing a narrow doorway while her coat floated around her like dark water. [609] The lock clicked, no longer detecting an unclaimed passenger. [618] Her brother whispered her name, his voice somehow older than the one she remembered. [632] "May I come in?" she asked. [639] A hand pressed against the other side, steadying the fragile threshold. [650] She pushed the door open and stepped across, light enough to be carried, whole enough to be welcomed. [668] Behind her, the recycled ship began to go dark, compartment by compartment. [680] Mara kept hold of the offered hand until the last light disappeared. [692]
