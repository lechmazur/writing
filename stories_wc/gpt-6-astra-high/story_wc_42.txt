At the lunar observatory above the clouds, the foundations began to shake on the morning Iven came to repair a clock. [21] The old camera repairer had been summoned because its single hand would not stop spinning, although nobody had wound it for years. [43] The last clockmaker had left with his fingers splinted and no explanation. [55] Iven kept his hands behind his back and listened. [64]

Below the dome, ten thousand crystal plates held things people could not bear to lose: a first snowfall, a final kiss, a child's astonishment. [88] The observatory had learned to harness the ephemeral by catching moments in moonlight and making that light circle inside glass. [108] What circled could not end. [113] What could not end grew heavy. [119] That last discovery had come too late for the mountain. [129]

Mara, the custodian, met him beside a fig tree whose roots had split their pot. [144] His daughter had planted it before the fever, before Iven donated the first plate: her laughter at a crooked birthday cake. [165] Thirty years later, that laughter remained changelessly constant, while its listener had become someone she would never know. [183]

"We can move the collection," Mara said. [190]

Iven watched a crack travel across the floor. [198] "You cannot carry away a gravity well in boxes." [207]

He opened the clock and found exactly what he feared: its gears were turning backward, pulled by the accumulated weight of yesterday. [229] The spinning hand was not keeping time but losing its struggle to reach the next second. [245] Soon the circling light would drag the whole observatory into its endless present. [258]

"Then break the plates," Mara said, though her own mother's voice was shelved among them. [273]

"Broken glass would only leave the light circling the rubble." [283] He pointed through the dome at the pale, receding moon. [293] "We must give it escape velocity, enough speed to leave the pull of everything we have refused to finish." [312]

Captive light moved slowly; that was why a century could fit between the silvered faces of a plate. [330] Released through the great telescope, it could become swift enough to be merely light again. [345]

Together they began to restructure the collecting engine, turning its inward spiral into a straight passage toward the sky. [364] Mara unbolted mirrors while Iven fitted his oldest camera shutter across the passage's mouth. [378] It had photographed moths opening their wings, triggered by a diaphragm sensitive enough to hear a falling leaf. [396] Each plate would open with that shutter, on a single signal; even a slight delay would let neighboring memories pull one another back. [419]

"The clock cannot time it," Mara said. [426]

"Then we will ask something that still knows how to end." [437]

Under a padded glass hood, he set a copper saucer beneath the fig tree's last yellow leaf and connected the diaphragm. [458] Now they would release thirty years by the sound of a leaf falling. [471]

But Mara caught his wrist as he reached for his daughter's plate. [483] "We could keep one." [487]

Iven almost agreed. [490] Through the glass she laughed again, kindly excusing him from remembering the quarrels, the muddy floors, the days he had been impatient. [512] If he kept this, he thought, he would go on loving only the part of her that could not answer back. [533] He had mistaken that mercy for resurrection. [540]

Iven slid her plate into the engine and tightened the last screw. [552]

The leaf trembled while dust fell from the dome. [561] Then it let go. [565]

The saucer made a sound too small for grief, and the shutter answered. [578] Light climbed the telescope in a white column, carrying snowfall, kisses, and laughter past the moon, beyond any promise of return. [599] Under their feet, the mountain settled. [605] The clock hand stopped, hesitated, and advanced one second. [614]

Walking down through the clouds, Iven told Mara about the birthday cake, and how his daughter had called it a splendid disaster. [635] Halfway through, he remembered she had said glorious, and smiled at the correction. [648]
