After the empathy virus, the hanging orchard survived on chains above a ravine battered by storms. [16] Hazels grew in enormous baskets of earth, and Mara foraged truffles among their roots, trusting soil temperature more than the orchard's weather saints. [39] Reason had kept her fed; it had not kept her daughter close. [51]

The virus left survivors involuntarily truthful, their confessions sharpened by the other person's answering pain. [66] During the last inspection, when the council required families to face one another, Mara had told nine-year-old Nell she sometimes regretted motherhood. [88] Nell had answered that she sometimes imagined cutting their basket free. [99] Neither had meant always. [103] Always was what each had heard. [109]

Mara began pursuing relief through rumored cures in discredited textbooks, trading truffles to the smuggler who serviced the maintenance winch. [129] The council confiscated such books because treatments for honesty threatened the compulsory openness they called psychological safety. [146] Mara wanted to cultivate something worthy of those words: a place where distress could be admitted without becoming an accusation. [166]

One textbook described patients who could withhold thoughts in the presence of Doctor Vey, an early survivor. [183] Its missing appendix had become a legend about his curative gaze. [194] The smuggler offered a relic instead: Vey's antique glass eye, wrapped in a confiscation notice bearing his hospital's seal. [213] Its blue iris had faded to the color of diluted milk. [224] Mara paid her winter reserve for it, then hated herself for buying a saint. [238] But the notice included a detail omitted from the textbook: Vey's remaining eye had been destroyed during the quarantine riots. [258]

She tested the relic against a pebble, alternating them beneath a cloth while Nell asked questions. [274] Neither prevented the humiliating answers. [279] Then Mara put the glass eye on the table and asked Nell to address it instead of her. [297]

"What did you dream last night?" [303]

Nell's lips trembled, but remained closed. [309]

Mara repeated the test with her own eyes covered, then uncovered, recording results on the confiscation notice. [326] The doctor had not possessed a healing gaze; he had lacked a receiving one. [340] The infection's compulsion needed the circuit of mutual attention that council inspections so carefully supplied. [355] They had been taught that looking away was the first symptom of moral relapse. [369] The eye was no medicine, but it gave Nell somewhere almost human to look while learning that silence could hold. [389]

Before the next inspection, Mara stretched a flour sack across their doorway to occlude the inspector's view. [406] She fixed the antique eye in a frayed hole, facing outward. [417] It looked absurdly expensive in that poor cloth. [425]

When Inspector Renn arrived, thunder was shaking rain through the hazel leaves and Nell was gripping the support chain. [444]

"Remove it," he said, holding the bridge rail. [452] "Secrecy is how we hurt each other." [459]

Mara nearly obeyed; his office controlled the chains' repair schedule, and theirs already shed flakes of rust. [476] Instead she asked him to question them through the sack. [486] He demanded to know whether Nell still wanted to fall. [496]

Behind the cloth, Mara felt her daughter's hand leave the chain and find hers. [510]

"I don't want to answer that," Nell said. [518]

The words came slowly, as though she were crossing rotten boards. [529] Renn reached for the sack, then stopped when a gust swung the basket and Mara caught Nell against her. [548] For a moment there was only their weight, the answering chains, his hand braced to steady the doorway. [566]

He asked whether she could keep Nell safe. [574]

Mara could have lied now, and felt the strange, frightening space before a choice. [588]

"Not without help," she said, and told him what the eye had cost. [601]

Renn stood long enough for water to drip from his cuffs. [612] Then he wrote down a food allotment and a chain inspection, leaving the column for moral condition blank. [630]

"Until next week," he said. [635]

It was not permission, exactly; it was time. [643]

After he left, Nell asked whether they could eat without looking at each other. [657] Mara set their bowls side by side, facing the hazels. [667] Their shoulders touched whenever the basket swayed. [674] At the door the smuggled relic kept its useless, merciful watch. [685]
