After the search party disbanded, Mara returned to the rain-washed foundry carrying a fractured listening bowl and the zodiac coin issued instead of a body. [25] Her brother's body, the clerk had meant, although he had avoided saying Tomas. [38] People called Mara a balanced alchemist because she never adjusted a measurement to please a master or comfort a widow. [58] Now she wanted to understand the weight of names, especially how Tomas had become Negligent in the guild's account of the flood. [80] The foreman said he had opened the river gate. [89] Mara had signed the calculation that called a second escape stair an unnecessary expense. [103]

She placed the coin beneath a lamp. [110] Its twelve constellations could locate a dead worker by birthday and name; redemption purchased twelve breaths, never a thirteenth. [129] Once redeemed, it became lead. [134] The guild classified its operation as factually mystical, meaning the results were reproducible even when the mechanism remained inexplicable. [153] Mara had verified its star positions with a protractor before allowing herself to hope. [167]

The bowl came from the gatehouse, where workers spoke their names before each shift so the guild could record attendance. [187] Only a vessel that remembered Tomas could receive him. [196] She arranged its seven pieces on a bench beneath the roof's torn and dripping skylights. [211] She set herself kintsugi intentions: preserve the fractures, join them with gold, and make no claim that repair was resurrection. [231]

For a week she worked beside the cold furnace, listening to dripping water, allowing each layer of lacquer to cure. [251] She learned by exploring limits: how thin a seam could be, how much pressure silenced an echo, whether added gold amplified it. [273] Extra gold only dulled the sound. [279] The bowl answered her test whispers clearly only when she stopped trying to improve its original shape. [296] She had wanted the repair to be stronger than the clay; it needed instead to let the clay speak. [315]

On the eighth morning, she turned the coin to the constellation rising on Tomas's birthday and laid it inside. [334] "Tomas Vale," she said, without rank, accusation, or the pet name that belonged only to her. [350] The gold seams brightened, and someone took a breath. [359] She recognized the little catch in it from winters when they had shared a bed to keep warm. [377]

"Did you open the gate?" she asked. [384]

"Yes," said Tomas. [387]

For a moment she wished she had left the bowl broken. [398]

"The stair was under water, and six men were pinned against the gate." [411] "Opening it let them reach the outer ladder, but I had to hold the lever down." [427]

The bowl filled with a faint rushing. [434]

"I thought I could swim after them." [441]

Mara counted his remaining breaths against her wrist, where her own pulse continued with unbearable steadiness. [457] She wanted to ask whether he forgave her, but forgiveness would spend the last of him on her comfort. [476] Instead she told him the six men had survived. [485] She had seen the six survivors among the searchers; none had known whose hand lifted the gate in the dark. [505]

"Good," he said, and the last breath left him easily. [515]

The coin darkened into lead. [520] Dawn caught the bowl's seams, keeping them luminous after its borrowed voice was gone. [534]

Mara had hoped to redeem her brother by proving the gate had failed without his help. [550] Instead, she would have to tell the truth about why he had opened it. [564] She carried the bowl to the guildhall with her original calculations, which assigned a price to another stair but none to her brother's last breath. [589] At the counter, she asked the clerk to restore Tomas Vale to the list of workers owed death compensation. [608] Then she dictated her own part in the disaster, surrendered her seal, and requested a public inquiry. [625] The clerk reached for a fresh sheet. [632] This time, she made him write both names in full. [642]

Weeks later, while the hearings continued, she brought water to the men building the new escape stair. [659] She served it from the listening bowl, and sunlight trembled in every visible fracture. [673] Nobody mistook it for an undamaged thing. [680] Nobody went thirsty. [683]
