<story>In the canyon of endless echoes, where towers of bone held up the living, silence had to be manufactured. [19] At dusk, wardens hammered copper shutters across the speaking hollows, beating the city's grief into a forged hush. [37] Lysa worked beneath that hush, carving the name of the boy she had killed into a scrap of glass. [56]

She had been the city's most defiant artist until her protest mural brought down the western tower. [73] She had cut too deeply into its ancient shinbone, although Tomas, her apprentice, had warned her about the cracks. [92] He was thirteen, innocently mischievous, forever adding tiny mustaches to her portraits of solemn officials. [107] When the bone split, he had run inside to fetch her tools. [119] His mother had heard him calling long after the dust settled, because the canyon never finished saying anything. [137]

Now the council offered Lysa forgiveness, before the ritual that would make it permanent. [151] At dawn, the pale spire in mist would drink the echoes of her crime and bleach them from every listener. [171] But its light could not separate a death from a life. [182] Everyone would forget Tomas, including his mother, Mara. [190] The council called that mercy, and asked Lysa to engrave the spire's focusing glass with her confession. [207]

I killed him: the single truth they wanted. [215] Lysa could not dispute it, but she wanted to refract a single truth into many, without making any of them smaller. [236] She began through impromptu poetry sessions in the bread queue, asking people for one line about Tomas. [253] At first they thought she was collecting excuses. [261] "Tell them you were brave," a mason suggested. [269] "Tell them I ignored him," Lysa answered. [276] Only then did the baker contribute: "He stole the burned crusts because he thought stealing the good ones would count." [296]

Others joined under shuttered arches, whispering their awkward verses while Lysa scored each one into a separate glass facet. [315] The confession belonged at the center; these lines would bend its light outward, carrying his name beyond the spire's reach. [335] She knew its workings because she had made focusing glasses for other people's absolutions. [349] Until now, she had never asked whose memories paid for them. [360]

Mara came on the last night before the ritual, carrying a portrait on which Tomas had given the chief warden whiskers. [381] "You want us to remember him so we can bear to remember you," she said. [396] Lysa started to deny it, then laid down her cutter. [406] "Some of me does." [410] Mara stood beside the table for so long that the copper overhead cooled and began to tick. [427] "Will your glass leave his screaming here too?" [435] Lysa nodded, ashamed that she had prepared an answer for everything except the question that mattered. [451] "Then I must choose it, not you," Mara said. [460]

They sat until the first wardens began unbolting the shutters. [470] Lysa pushed the finished glass toward Mara and waited. [479] If Mara broke it, Lysa would submit the plain confession, though she could no longer imagine deserving its mercy. [498] Instead, Mara picked up the cutter. [504] "Show me how to write that he was frightened." [513] Her first stroke slipped, and Lysa steadied her wrist without taking the tool away. [527]

At the spire, the keeper examined their glass and pointed to the sentence of restitution beneath Lysa's unsigned pardon. [546] "Scatter the beam, and you spend your life repairing what you broke." [558] Beyond him, mist threaded the hollow bones, bringing back a boy's thin call for help. [573] Lysa had imagined defiance as a bright thing, a mural cut where nobody dared to cut. [589] Now it was listening beside Mara without reaching for the shutters. [600] She signed the sentence and fitted the many faces of the glass around the unaltered confession. [616]

Before the ritual bell could sound, dawn struck the pale spire. [627] Instead of a cleansing shaft, trembling lines spread across the canyon walls, each anchored to the words I killed him. [647] The baker read his verse aloud, and another voice answered with the story of the mustaches. [663] The echoes took both, but did not stop carrying the cry. [674] Mara did not forgive her. [679] When Lysa asked which broken stair she should mend first, however, Mara showed her the one leading to Tomas's room. [699]</story>
