After the eclipse ends, Sera returns to the wind-carved encampment carrying a bottle of disappearing ink and her sister's last wage slip. [22] The slip says VOLUNTARY DEPARTURE, although Sera watched the derrick fall on Nemi. [35] She remembers having time to count every rivet before the steel struck. [47] She wants to discover why time slows in crisis, and why all those plentiful seconds gave her no chance to move. [68] Her anger has settled below speech, like cool water at the bottom of a well. [83]

An albino tracker, she ordinarily works at night, when neither sunlight nor its reflection from the salt can blind her. [103] Today she sees through a hooded screen connected to an artificial hummingbird drone. [116] Nemi built its eyes to find hairline fractures in drilling pipes; Sera has taught it to follow tracks. [134]

Their people call the encampment consciously wild: tents migrate between wind-hollowed ridges, paths change, and nobody numbers a doorway. [153] Sera once believed that made them difficult to own. [162] But everyone visits the company's water tank, where the clerk stamps each wrist with ink that disappears by evening. [181] Nemi's stamp remained black on her broken arm. [189] Sera stole the bottle while the clerk was watching the eclipse. [200]

In the shade of her tent, she spills a little onto a slate. [213] Instead of spreading evenly, it branches, knots, and briefly forms a mesh too fine for her eyes. [230] The hummingbird records each change before evaporation erases it. [239] Through ephemeral patterns in spilled ink, Sera follows a trail no boot could leave: microscopic conductors assembling into a circuit. [259] Its branching antenna matches the company emblem on the water tank. [270] The hummingbird detects a transmission and traces its destination to the tank's locked maintenance hatch. [285]

Nemi gave the bird a cutting beak for removing damaged pipe seals. [297] It takes six minutes to worry through the hatch's cheap lock. [308] Inside, beneath the innocent water meter, another counter measures HUMAN HOURS EXPORTED. [320] A maintenance diagram shows a stamped wrist, a startled brain, and an arrow pointing toward the distant coastal cities. [339]

She reads slowly, without raising her voice. [346] Fear opens the circuit; the ink accelerates consciousness while stealing the seconds in which the body should respond. [364] Those stolen seconds are sold to hospitals, boardrooms, anyone who can afford a longer life. [379] At dusk the conductors burn out, taking their visible pigment with them. [391] Death interrupts that final erasure. [396] Nemi's dark wrist was not a bruise. [403]

The accident log lists her sister's terror as an unusually profitable extraction. [415] Below it, a recommendation advises postponing repairs to the remaining derricks. [426] Sera rests her hands on the console until she can feel each fingertip separately. [440] Then she asks the hummingbird to copy everything. [448]

The clerk finds her there and looks first at the open hatch, then at the stamp on his own wrist. [468] "They told me it measured dehydration," he says. [476]

She believes him just enough not to hate him yet. [486] "Help me disconnect it." [490]

He points to the supply contract fastened inside the hatch: tampering means permanent cancellation of water deliveries. [507] Beyond the ridges lie three days of salt flats and the old spring the company says has dried up. [526] Sera has tracked foxes there; their cubs drink and live. [536]

"Fill every vessel," she tells him, "and tell everyone what the stamps are for." [550] "Nobody follows me without knowing what the journey costs." [559]

By sunset, the tank is empty and the people stand waiting with water slung across their shoulders. [576] Some are crying; others quietly dismantle the shade frames they built over years. [589] Freedom is heavier than Sera had imagined. [596]

She cuts the transmitter cable herself. [602] The hummingbird has already sent its evidence to coastal newsrooms through the company's own network. [617] Then Sera takes the first path between the ridges, choosing stone where footprints will not hold. [633] Behind her, the camp begins to disappear, not into stolen seconds but into the patient geography its people know. [652]

She carries Nemi's wage slip beneath her water flask. [661] Someday she will make someone correct it, but tonight she has living people to lead, and darkness enough to see. [681]
