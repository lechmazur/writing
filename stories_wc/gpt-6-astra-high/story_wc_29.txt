By the time the barricades reached the university radio station, Mara had learned to distinguish heavenly voices from heavenly intent. [20] She was its celestial signal interpreter, paid in cafeteria meals to translate whatever came whispering through the old dish above the transmitter. [42] Lately, the whispers offered fuel. [47]

Winter had cracked the reservoir pipes, and the university controlled the last working boiler. [61] The trustees wanted heat reserved for laboratories; the neighborhoods wanted the gates opened. [74] From one constellation came a promise of limitless warmth if the trustees kept command. [88] From another came the same promise, conditional on the neighborhoods taking it. [100]

Mara had translated both faithfully, believing that fidelity absolved her of choosing. [112] Now her brother stood outside with the neighborhood pickets, warming his hands over a burning admissions brochure. [129]

"They say we get nothing until we win," he told her through the security glass. [144]

Behind him, snow sifted through the streetlights like ash from an invisible fire. [157]

She went downstairs to the astronomical charts, rolled in canvas sleeves that smelled of mildew and hot wiring. [175] Their margins held earlier interpreters' annotations: celestial visitors, splendid bargains, towns that vanished after accepting them. [191] Under every disaster, someone had plotted the same two stars. [201] They were not rival benefactors but cosmic tricksters, repeating their power plays wherever scarcity made obedience look like survival. [220]

The oldest chart showed something the newer ones omitted: both stars pulsed once every eight seconds. [236] A note beside them read, "They borrow our carrier because they cannot sustain a voice here." [252]

Mara compared the broadcasts and found that slow pulse beneath every magnificent syllable. [265] The voices could leap to another frequency if she simply shut the transmitter down. [279] But while speaking, each depended on the station to complete its rhythm. [291] She could interfere through subsonic frequencies, turning the carrier's slow modulation against them, provided both voices occupied it together. [310]

Outwitting them meant offering something neither could bear to share. [320]

She sent each star a private invitation: at midnight, its chosen faction would receive sole authority over the station. [339] Its rival would be silenced forever. [345] Each demanded a live declaration of surrender. [352]

Mara agreed, then called her brother. [358]

"Bring the pickets inside, and bring the trustees down," she said. [369]

"You broadcast their threats," he said. [375]

"I did, and I need witnesses when I stop." [384]

He stayed silent long enough for her to hear his teeth chattering. [396] Then the door buzzer sounded. [401]

At midnight the studio filled with wet wool, expensive coats, and the mineral smell of people who had forgotten to drink water. [423] Mara left the microphone open. [428]

Both voices descended, silver and immense, each proclaiming its faction the rightful master of the town. [444] Their followers began shouting before either noticed the other. [453]

Mara turned the modulation dial by the smallest mark. [462]

The adjustment made no audible note, but frost crawled across the mixing desk as the borrowed rhythms began to cancel. [482] Above the desk, two luminous shapes collapsed into something jointed and hungry. [494]

"Stop," one rasped, without its borrowed grandeur. [501] "We can give you the boiler." [507]

Not warmth, Mara thought; only permission to take what was already theirs. [519]

She held the dial while the shapes clawed at their fading carrier. [531] When the last trace vanished from the meter, she cut the transmitter's power. [544]

Beyond the glass, nothing moved except the snow. [552]

The trustee nearest the door accused her of destroying their only hope. [564] Her brother asked for the boiler keys. [571] For a moment Mara thought they would start fighting anyway, and understood that no clever signal could finish this work. [591]

Then the night engineer stepped between them, holding a wrench. [601]

"We can heat the gym if we drain the laboratories first." [612]

Nobody liked it, which was different from nobody agreeing. [621]

Mara restarted the station on a bare carrier and read the engineer's instructions, followed by her own mistakes. [639] Outside, her brother began dismantling a barricade to clear a path toward the gym. [653] Cautiously hopeful, she unfolded the oldest chart beside the microphone and kept watch over the silence between the stars. [672]
