After the oasis dried, the sprawling greenhouse city kept fountains running with recordings of water. [15] Above its roofs, the toxic sky pressed yellow thumbs against glass patched with resin-soaked wedding veils and flour sacks. [34] Mara, a gilded-leaf calligrapher, still lettered condolences in gold, although the leaves growing from her wrists wilted whenever anyone lied. [54]

"She went peacefully," a customer dictated, and Mara's fingers curled until she could no longer hold her brush. [72] "She was frightened, but I stayed," he tried, and her hand opened enough to finish the inscription. [89]

She could manage other people's truths; her own remained harder. [99] Before the drought killed her mother, Mara had accused her of loving the municipal breath-post more than her daughter. [118] Her mother's reply had entered the glass tubes just as the oasis pumps failed, stranding thousands of whispered conversations. [137] Mara wanted to find where lost conversations went, although she usually called this wanting an interest in postal history. [156] Her wrists had become almost bare. [162]

Clearing her mother's rooms, she pried up the loose floorboards and uncovered a rat's nest of paper. [179] Among the shreds lay a zinc-etched postal plate fragment, its grooves still packed with the green ink her mother used. [199] Her mother had printed these postal routes as birthday treasure maps, adding suns and arrows in ink. [216] Back then, following their arrows through the postal tunnels had always ended with a sweet hidden behind a valve. [235]

She spent three nights piecing together the shredded treasure map, matching torn pipework and the tiny teeth of paper edges. [255] One corner was missing, but the plate fragment held its original engraving; inked and pressed, it supplied the vanished junction. [275] The final arrow pointed below the empty oasis, to a chamber labeled TREASURE in her mother's crooked capitals. [293] Underneath, in smaller type, stood its official designation: Undelivered Speech. [303]

At the oasis fence, a watchman asked whether she had authorization to enter the condemned pump house. [320] Mara started to say she represented the restoration committee, and a new shoot folded painfully against her skin. [338] "No," she said, "and I cannot promise that anything down there will help the city." [353]

The watchman looked at her map, then toward the loudspeaker splashing imaginary water over the cracked basin. [370] "My brother sent something before the pumps stopped," he said, and fetched a ladder instead of his whistle. [388]

They descended through roots dangling like disconnected wires, following the map past valves Mara remembered touching with sticky childhood fingers. [408] Beneath the basin, the dead tubes emptied into rows of stoppered bottles, each tagged with a destination and dispatch date. [428] Without the pumps, undelivered breath had settled here, caught by the same gravity that had once brought water downhill. [447]

Mara found her address quickly, but made herself reread the date three times before pulling the stopper. [463] "I missed your exhibition," her mother said into the dust, "because I was ashamed to ask you for the fare." [483] There was a scrape, the familiar noise of a stool being dragged closer to the speaking tube. [500] "And sometimes work was easier than being with you, because machines let me pretend I knew what I was doing." [520]

Mara had come expecting forgiveness or a verdict, something she could frame and never question again. [536] Instead her mother sounded tired, embarrassed, alive in the ordinary way she had been while there was still time. [555]

"Tell me about the pictures," the bottle said. [563]

Mara sat on the cold floor and described the smallest piece, her mother's hands outlined in gilded script. [581] Then she admitted that she had worked from memory, and had made her mother's hands look much too smooth. [600] The bottle did not answer, and this time she did not invent an answer for it. [616]

Beside her, the watchman held his brother's bottle without opening it. [627] "It may hurt," she told him, and stayed while he decided. [638]

By morning, they had carried the first basket of lost conversations upstairs. [650] Mara used the backs of condolence leaves to write notices giving the chamber's location and the hours she would help. [670] Gold was scarce, so she gilded only the addresses, letting the plain instructions stand without promises of comfort. [688]

Above her stall, the fountain recording faltered, restarted, and faltered again. [699] When a child asked whether the water was coming back, Mara put down her brush. [714] "I don't know," she said, and the small green leaf at her wrist remained open. [729]
