In the icebound atrium under starlight, Mara waited beside an egg that had begun to tick. [16] Beyond the glass, the valley's first stream in thirty years was running beneath the snow. [31] Inside, winter remained obedient to her mother's machine. [39]

Her mother's final instructions lay beneath the egg: after the egg hatches, reopen the doors. [54] Mara had spent eleven nights trying to obey the second instruction without obeying the first. [69] The doors were rusted shut, and every blow against them sent fractures through the surrounding glass. [85] There were ninety thousand varieties of seed behind that glass, and hungry people down the mountain. [101]

As an heirloom seed saver, she knew which beans tolerated drought better than frost, which barley ripened before a short summer surrendered. [123] She knew nothing about the egg except what its label promised: vortex inheritance. [136] Her mother had tended its parent whirlwind until she died at forty with the hands of an ancient woman. [155]

Once, touching those hands, Mara had asked what the machine cost. [166] "A little borrowed time," her mother said, and closed her fingers. [177]

Now Mara wanted to discover the texture of borrowed time before she agreed to borrow any herself. [194] She lifted the egg from its nest, an oak acorn cap from the last tree her grandmother had planted outdoors. [214] The cap's overlapping scales curved around its center like the vanes of a tiny turbine. [229]

When the shell split, something transparent uncoiled into those scales and began turning against the turn of the stars. [248] A broken seed jar rose from the floor, collecting its scattered glass. [260] Mold withdrew from the beans inside it. [267] The vortex worked by decreasing entropy, drawing disorder out of whatever its keeper wished to save. [283]

But the disorder had to go somewhere. [290]

Across Mara's palm appeared a crease she had never seen, deep enough to hold a barley grain. [307] Through it she felt warm bread, a child's damp curls, the soft abrasion of sheets shared for twenty winters. [326] These were not memories; they were possibilities becoming unavailable. [335]

The cap shivered, eager to restore another jar. [343] Mara understood why her mother had stopped singing, then sleeping, then recognizing the daughter for whom she claimed to save the world. [365]

Outside, water kept moving downhill without anybody's permission. [373]

She set the cap on the packing bench and began selecting seeds. [385] Furiously tranquil, she counted barley into envelopes, added beans and squash, and rejected flowers whose scents had filled her childhood with summer. [407] Anger steadied her fingers; grief would have to wait until the planting was finished. [421] She could not carry ninety thousand varieties through a warming valley, and without the vortex the atrium would collapse when its ice melted. [444] For eleven nights she had imagined saving everything, as though devotion could make her back broad enough. [461]

At last she added three acorns from her grandmother's tree, each wrapped against the cold. [476]

Then she carried the cap to the doors and pressed its rough rim against the lock. [492] The little whirlwind pulled at her pulse, offering her the whole collection, every vanished orchard, every fragrant summer, perfectly preserved. [512] She pictured only the lock as it had been before the rust. [524]

Iron brightened beneath her hand, and a single strand of hair fell white across her eyes. [540] The doors swung outward, admitting air that smelled of mud instead of eternity. [553]

Before the vortex could find another wish, Mara crushed the cap beneath her heel. [567] Its scales broke apart; the turning loosened into a silver gust that lifted frost from the benches and vanished among the stars. [589]

Behind her, somewhere in the dark, a seed jar broke and did not mend. [603] She stood listening until she could bear to leave it broken. [614]

Then she shouldered the sacks and descended toward the stream, carrying neither enough to save everything nor too little to begin. [635] At the first patch of naked earth, she knelt and pressed one acorn into the yielding ground. [652] The new crease in her palm filled with dirt. [661]

Above her, the constellations wheeled in wild serenity, spending nothing they could ever get back. [676]
