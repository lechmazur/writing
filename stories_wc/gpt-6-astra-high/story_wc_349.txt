During the lull on her store's very first opening day, Mara rearranged the clean slates so she would not have to watch the door. [24] Her shop occupied a hollow trunk in the glass orchard library floating upon a swirling sea of clouds. [42] Above her, books ripened inside transparent fruit; below, roots held the stories confiscated by the courts. [57] The courts bound people into accounts of their worst acts, and readers borrowed them to feel better about their own. [77] When the wind shifted, sentences chimed against one another, broken clauses that readers assembled by walking slowly between the trees. [97]

Before becoming a clean slate vendor, she had catalogued those roots without being permitted to read them. [114] Now she sold forgetting, the only freedom available without an appeal. [125] Her wares were mindfully scattered, each slate angled away from its neighbor to give ashamed customers somewhere private to look. [145] Her first day's wages were one free slate; she had set it beside a chair for Ada. [162]

Her daughter arrived carrying bottled moonlight. [168] Under glimmering parole, prisoners could leave their books until the light ran out. [181] Ada's bottle was already half dark. [187]

"You can stay," Mara said, touching the waiting slate. [196] "Afterward, you will not remember prison." [202]

Ada studied the chair. [206] "Will I remember you?" [210]

Mara turned the slate facedown. [215] A slate took everything: the crime, the punishment, the name a mother called across a kitchen. [231]

The judgment she was licensed to erase contained Ada's entire surviving testimony: "I opened the drainage gates and drowned nine men." [252] Its beginning had been cut from Ada's memory, leaving the act but none of the hours before it. [270] Sometimes she remembered hammering; sometimes a red sleeve under water. [280]

"I want to find the place where stories begin," Ada said. [291] "Mine cannot begin with what they say I did." [300]

Mara took the concession to rescue her daughter, not dispute the verdict; a daughter without memories still seemed better than no daughter at all. [324] Only now did she hear the bargain she had made on Ada's behalf. [337] Mara would still remember her daughter's first steps on a kitchen floor sticky with jam, while Ada would not even remember the kitchen. [360] She had mistaken her own relief for Ada's. [368]

She fetched the storage invoice attached to the judgment. [377] The library charged prisoners for every word it held, including the words its judges suppressed. [392] Mara had once checked such invoices by quantifying absence: subtracting the words in a public record from the total billed. [412] Ada's bill covered twenty-six words; the judgment contained nine. [421]

"Seventeen words," she whispered, and led Ada down the stair wound through the trunk. [435]

Root archives were sorted by prisoner number, then by the length of each excision. [449] Ada's missing beginning lay inside a glass nodule no larger than a plum. [462] Moonlight could coax the words out through a cut in the glass, just as it coaxed prisoners out of their books. [483] Borrowing a lamp upstairs would mean asking a warden for permission. [494]

To open it, Mara would have to incise the court's seal with her slate knife, surrendering her license with the first cut. [516] She cut. [518]

The glass remained blank until Ada uncorked her bottle and poured the last of her parole over it. [536] Letters surfaced: "The owners locked us in the flooding mine, and the only drainage channel ran beneath their office." [555]

Mara read them aloud as Ada's fingers became transparent. [564]

"We hammered on the lift doors," Ada said. [572] "I thought nobody would ever hear." [578]

Mara could still press her daughter's fading hand against the slate upstairs; instead, she asked, "May I put this back?" [598]

Ada nodded. [600]

A cataloguer knew where a fragment belonged. [607] Mara fitted the severed beginning against the judgment, and the orchard rang with the sound of a book becoming whole. [627] Ada vanished before the ringing stopped. [633]

A whole story did not acquit Ada; nine men were still dead, and no restored sentence could decide what justice required. [654] Now Mara had something to take before an appeals clerk besides a mother's pleading. [668] From a high branch came Ada's remembered hammering. [676]

The doorbell rang upstairs. [680] Mara carried up the complete testimony and turned every clean slate faceup. [692] Witnesses, copies, an appeal: she could sell nothing now, but she had something for every slate. [708]
