Nera was the odd-hour librarian of the whispering archipelago, where unfinished dreams washed ashore at midnight and murmured until someone gave them shelter. [23] She shelved them in bottles, listening for the names of their dead. [35] At dawn, she photographed any dream ready to belong to somebody living. [47] Except Iona's. [49] Her wife's dream had remained behind the circulation desk for three winters, glowing like a greenhouse seen across dark water. [69]

Then, on the morning the council announced that the school had exhausted its flour, Nera found the bottle empty. [88] A green thread slipped beneath the library door. [96] She snatched her camera and Iona's watercolor palette and followed it into the hush of improbable beginnings, as her wife had called this hour. [120]

Iona had always woken before the fishing boats, determined to grow vegetables above soil poisoned by salt. [137] Her sketches showed floating gardens, their roots drinking rainwater collected beneath the beds. [150] The storm that took her had also taken the workshop and every drawing. [163] Only the palette had returned, snagged in a neighbor's net. [173]

Nera hurried past shuttered kitchens, desperate to capture the runaway dream before it reached an unguarded sleeper and ceased to be entirely Iona's. [196] In its bottle, sometimes, she could still hear her wife humming. [207]

The green thread climbed the school steps and gathered beneath a window into the shape of a woman bending over seedlings. [228] Nera set her tripod on the gravel. [235] A dream could pass into waking hands only through sunrise photography. [246] The first light would vitalize its hidden workings for a few seconds; properly photographed, even an impossible hope might yield something useful. [268] But a camera needed colors borrowed from something the dreamer had loved, or the plate would remain blank. [286]

Nera raised the palette, its dried washes translucent in their shallow glass wells. [299] "A librarian paints with light," Iona used to say, watching Nera work. [311] She had sounded proud, before Nera began using that light chiefly to keep the dead from leaving. [328]

Behind the window, a girl sat up beneath a blanket. [338] The school housed families whose islands were no longer high enough to inhabit. [351] Nera recognized the child who borrowed cookbooks and returned them smelling of nothing. [364]

"It looks like a garden," the girl said through the opened sash. [376]

Nera adjusted the lens until Iona's face nearly filled the frame. [387] Beyond it, beds of leaves blurred into green smoke. [396]

"We have buckets," the girl said, "but nothing will grow in seawater." [408]

The eastern sky paled, and Nera saw the spaces between Iona's fingers begin to shine. [423] She knew that pattern from a hundred other dreams: the cipher of longing, written not in what appeared, but in what was missing. [446] A sailor's empty chair meant home; a door without hinges meant escape. [458] Between Iona's hands were channels, reservoirs, the small ingenious absences that would let a garden float and save its water. [478]

Her wife was not asking to be remembered. [486] She was asking for the work to continue. [494]

Nera lowered the camera, then widened its frame until the woman's face became a little brightness above the waiting beds. [514] She held the palette where the sun could burn through Iona's greens and ochres. [528] When she opened the shutter, the humming stopped. [536]

On the plate, no woman stood among the plants. [545] Instead there were joints a carpenter could cut, gutters a child could clean, and moorings strong enough for the sheltered lagoon. [566] Nera sat on the steps until the girl came outside and took her cold hand. [581]

By noon, copies of the photograph covered the school tables. [591] A boatbuilder argued for different lashings; the girl suggested buckets beneath the gutters, because those they already had. [609] Every alteration hurt a little less. [615]

They built between meals of fish broth, using wreckage that the sea had not yet finished taking apart. [633] Nera brought the library's seed collection down from its highest shelf and let the children break the seals. [651]

Weeks later, when the first leaves unfolded, she photographed them without the palette. [664] There was an empty place beside her on the raft, bright with ordinary morning. [678] She left it in the picture. [684]
