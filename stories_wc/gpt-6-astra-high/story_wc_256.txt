After the power grid stabilizes, the laboratory of lost sounds sends for Mara, the city's last prism-song bell ringer. [19] The new reservoir has swallowed the valley, and the council wants an artificial pilgrimage to replace the miners' annual walk to their buried comrades' memorial. [44] Mara's father helped carve those names, but damaged lungs kept him from the road long before the water came. [63] She accepts because she wants to discover the texture of connection, rather than keep delivering groceries to a man who thanks her through his door. [88]

Inside the laboratory, shelves of wax cylinders and bottled echoes catch golden shadows from the reservoir's reflected light. [106] Engineers have reconstructed everything: gravel underfoot, blackberry thorns, even the cough of the woman who once sold candles beside the gate. [127] Only the memorial bells remain silent. [133] Prism-song requires a living hand to bend light across the keys; recordings preserve its notes but cannot decide how long to wait between them. [157]

For her instrument, Mara brings a miner's carbide lamp lens, the only piece of his working life her father allowed her to keep. [180] He always carried that lamp at the back of the procession, lighting the stones for whoever walked most slowly. [199] Its heat-pitted glass softens her controlling beam, making the great bells powerfully gentle, able to fill a room without bruising anyone inside it. [222]

On the first trial, her father sits in a wheelchair between two rails while the laboratory builds the vanished road around him. [244] Simulated pilgrims pass, carrying perfect candles beneath a perfectly windless sky. [255] He does not touch the wheels. [261]

"The gradient is accurate," the chief engineer assures him. [270]

Her father watches a boy's translucent shoulder pass through his knee. [281]

"Nobody notices I'm not coming." [286]

Mara stops the bells before the automated procession can reach the memorial without him. [300] The engineer offers to propel the chair with a motor, then to slow the procession, then to make every pilgrim turn and smile. [323] Each improvement makes her father's hands close more tightly. [332]

That evening, Mara begins again by questioning assumptions written into the commission. [344] Why must a pilgrimage end at its destination, and why must everyone on the road belong to the past? [363] As a child, she resented her father's late returns from those walks; somebody always needed to rest, and he never left them alone. [386] The laboratory has spent years collecting sounds people feared losing, yet nobody has thought to ask what they were meant to answer. [408]

Mara asks the engineer to disconnect the recorded crowd and open the laboratory's microphones to the resettlement blocks across the water. [429] For the first time since evacuation, the steady grid can hold all those scattered households in one unbroken circuit. [448] The engineer objects that the council is paying for a finished route, not a telephone exchange, but leaves the switches on. [469]

At the second trial, someone answers Mara's opening chord by dropping a spoon. [482] Another microphone brings breathing, then a woman's embarrassed laugh, then the scrape of a chair pulled closer. [499] Her father looks toward the empty stretch of road beside him. [510]

"Is that you, Elsie?" [514]

The candle seller, alive and older than her archived cough, says she cannot find her matches. [530] He tells her to check her apron pocket, remembering the little tin she carried whenever rain soaked everybody else's. [549]

Mara raises the lens, and its cloudy rim spreads a trembling amber chord beneath their conversation. [565] When her father coughs, she holds the next note back, and across the circuit the others fall quiet with her. [585] Nobody urges him onward or mistakes his silence for an empty place that needs filling. [600] At last he pushes once against the wheels, a movement so small the artificial gravel barely crunches. [617]

"Slowly," he says, and someone in a distant kitchen answers, "We've got time." [630]

They do not reach the memorial that day. [638] Instead, her father asks Mara to sit beside him while he tells Elsie how the drowned names looked before rain had softened their edges. [662] She rests the old lens between their hands, where the glass is cool at first and then, almost imperceptibly, grows warm. [683]
