During whale migration, Mara climbed the stargazer's open tower with a chisel and a plan for a memorial. [18] The town wanted a monument to the ferry dead; she wanted to sculpt space for reflection, where grief could sit without being watched. [41] Her daughter, Nell, had hated being watched when she cried. [51]

Below, migrating whales passed through the channel where the ferry had struck something enormous and overturned three autumns earlier. [70] Mara, a mason, trusted measurements, not the widows who said the abandoned observatory returned the voices of their dead. [89] Nevertheless, she always listened when they needed to tell her, and never called their longing foolish. [105]

Beneath a rotted bench, she uncovered a magic square tile: nine numbered cells, each row, column, and diagonal totaling fifteen. [125] Its center was polished hollow by a succession of thumbs. [135] She pressed it, testing whether the tile concealed a catch. [145]

The sea entered her without a sound. [152] She was holding Nell above the black water, and she was pushing a dying calf toward air with a scarred, immense forehead. [174] Two losses occupied the same instant, neither willing to make room. [185]

Offshore, a whale broke formation and circled beneath the tower. [195] Through its eyes, Mara saw her own small figure gripping the parapet. [207] She took her hand away; the borrowed sight vanished, but both drownings remained. [220] The whale's companions were moving north, their submerged calls beating against her bones like music nobody could hear. [238]

When Mara lifted the tile, the whale convulsed, and the memory of Nell's face began losing its edges. [256] She set it down, though doing so restored the drowning. [266] She would not save herself by tearing holes in another mother's mind. [278]

Kneeling, she noticed markings continuing from the tile into the mortar. [289] Water from her flask darkened shallow grooves beneath the salt, revealing the forgotten patterns of the stargazer's notation. [307] She began to transcribe them onto her building plans, matching each turn to a numbered cell. [323]

They were architectural drawings, except the rooms contained scenes instead of furniture. [335] One showed a man studying constellations through the recollections of a whale, gathering centuries of skies into one lifetime. [354] Another showed similar memories drawn together, until their chambers overlapped. [364] The instrument had joined their losses because it could not tell them apart. [377]

In the last diagram, entangled memories acquired separate doorways opening onto an empty courtyard. [391] The stargazer had left a way to separate his borrowed life from his own. [405] Mara laid her memorial drawing over the courtyard and understood what she had wanted all along. [421] Not forgetfulness, but somewhere to stand beside remembering. [429]

Following the border, she pressed one outer row for Nell, the opposite row for the calf: fifteen balanced against fifteen, with an untouched cell between them. [455] Each movement forced her through the wreck again: Nell's slipping fingers, the calf's crushed ribs, the terrible weight of water. [475] Twice she stopped breathing before she remembered that her own lungs were still above the sea. [491] Yet she kept one palm against the tile, offering the whale the only certainty she possessed: somebody else was staying. [511]

At the final number, nothing disappeared. [517] Instead, the drownings moved apart. [522] Between them opened a stretch of quiet neither mother had to fill. [534] The whale turned north, carrying its calf's memory without Mara's child tangled in it. [548] Mara remained beside Nell's last afternoon, able, at last, to step toward it or away. [563]

By winter, she had built nine sheltered seats around the tile, each facing a different reach of sea. [581] She covered the center with clear glass, so no visitor could mistake a wound for a wishing well. [599] The widows came, and she asked no one what they heard. [610] Below their separate silences, the tide conducted its unheard symphony. [620]
