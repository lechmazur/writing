On the moonlit summit, crater inspector Mara Venn knelt beside the last piece of glacier on Saint Oran's Island. [19] In the second century of the space age, satellites crossed overhead so frequently that she had to wait between them to see the stars. [43] Below her boots, rising magma was warming the mountain from within. [54] Her instruments had already supplied everything needed to close the summit trail permanently, but the camera around her neck was not government equipment. [77]

Her father had been the observatory's nightwatch, back when the glacier filled this crater with blue corridors. [94] After his shifts, he photographed sunlight passing through the ice, turning frozen bubbles into strings of small, imprisoned suns. [113] Mara wanted one final picture for the unfinished sequence in his album. [125] Cancer had killed him before the warming mountain took his workplace, and she had become tired of losing things without a last look. [148]

She unwrapped a single black grain and set it beside the ice for scale. [162] It was a sand grain from the nightwatch's hourglass, which he had filled with sieved sand from this very crater. [182] When she was seven, he had put it under a microscope and asked what she could discover without breaking it. [202] She found trapped gas bubbles, then learned why volcanic glass contained them, then asked where the next eruption would happen. [222] He called her purposefully curious, and paid for the geology books. [233]

Against her wrist rested another inheritance, although this one she had made herself: a card divided into sealed chemical cells. [253] Her father had used iodine clock reactions for timed messages while developing photographs, letting silent letters appear instead of watching another clock. [275] Breaking the card's inner seals started reactions whose different concentrations delayed the darkening of starch letters by carefully measured intervals. [295] Mara had calibrated these cells for the warmth of her wrist, where she wore the card beneath her sleeve. [314] They were reminders, not instruments; her instruments could measure danger but could not make her acknowledge it. [331]

The first message, CHECK VENT, was already blue. [339] She checked the steam plume and the pressure gauge, then adjusted her lens. [352] A narrow opening in the crater wall would admit the first sunlight directly through the remaining ice. [369] One trapped bubble survived inside it, suspended above the black grain like a world above its ruined moon. [387] Another few minutes would give her the picture he might have taken. [399]

Then the vent stopped whispering. [404] Into the dawning hush came the tiny click of her own teeth meeting. [417] She looked at the pressure gauge: rising. [424] Somewhere beneath the ledge, a passage had sealed, trapping steam that had been escaping harmlessly a moment before. [442] Her wrist prickled with sweat as the second message emerged through the card's clear window: LEAVE TIME TO RETURN. [461] She had written those words herself, which did not prevent them from sounding like him. [476]

For a breath she hated the card, the gauge, and all the knowledge that made staying a choice instead of an accident. [498] The eastern clouds were brightening, but the light had not reached the opening. [511] She switched on her inspection lamp, took one photograph, and backed away. [523] The grain remained on the ledge; retrieving it would mean kneeling where the rock had begun to tremble. [541] Halfway down the sheltered side of the ridge, she heard the blast, followed by stones ticking against the slope above her. [562]

Dawn found her at the trail gate, fastening the closure notice with hands that would not quite obey. [580] Above, the summit shone pink through thinning steam, beautiful enough to make her briefly angry. [595] She checked the photograph only after calling in the new pressure readings. [607] The lamp had made the ice look ordinary: a melting splinter, a bubble, a dark fleck of sand. [625] There were no imprisoned suns. [630]

Still, she could see the grain clearly enough to recognize the chipped edge she had first examined at seven. [649] It would outlast the ice, although neither belonged to her anymore. [660] She sent the image with her report, then saved another copy for the album. [674] Under the date she would write what her father had always written beneath his pictures: the place, the weather, and what was actually there. [698]
