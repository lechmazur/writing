Under the soft hush of a distant morning, Mara climbed to the frozen library above clouds with her mother's toolbox knocking against her knee. [24] Once, that sound had meant they were walking home together along the harbor, carrying something broken that would soon work again. [45] Now the sea lay beneath winter cloud, and nobody waited when she stopped to catch her breath. [62]

She was a lunar-eclipse locksmith, trained to open locks whose tumblers became solid only inside the moon's red shadow. [81] The library's dawn mechanism was secured by such a lock; until this eclipse, she could not undo what she had done. [102]

Inside, frost furred the catalog drawers, and the reading lamps hung like jellyfish washed onto an abandoned shore. [120] Only the hall of mirrors remained hauntingly bright. [128] At its center, reflected a hundred times, her mother bent over an open clock. [142] It was the last light the mirrors had gathered before her mother died, not a ghost, although Mara had sometimes pretended. [163] The next dawn would have overwritten it. [170] So Mara had jammed the shutters with her own brass key, preserving that afternoon and shutting out the warmth. [188]

She had not expected the books to freeze. [196] Or perhaps she had simply refused to imagine anything beyond the relief of finding her mother here again. [214] Now ice was splitting the bindings, and the mirrors had begun to crack. [227] Keeping one image had nearly cost the library every other life it held. [240]

She opened the toolbox: an oil bottle, a little hammer, and a clockmaker's gear assortment. [255] Each little brass wheel occupied a hollow worn smooth by years of selecting, returning, and trying again. [272] Mara knew how to defeat a lock; she had come to learn the rhythm of repair. [288]

Her mother had taught her where to begin, though not in the manuals. [301] Prophecy books shed their impossible endings, which her mother tucked behind a loose board instead of throwing away. [319] There, she used to say, were the mistakes tomorrow had already made for you. [333]

Mara pulled the board free. [338] Through scraps of prophecy hidden in the library's corner, she searched for a future in which something could be saved. [358] One fragment described a key snapping under a hammer; another showed oil smoking on an axle forced too fast. [377] On the smallest scrap, someone waited through three uneven clicks before touching the shutter wheel. [391] The page ended with water dripping from a shelf. [400] No fragment promised that the woman in the mirrors would remain. [411]

Mara carried them to the mechanism, where the eclipse was slowly making the lock real beneath her fingers. [429] She picked it without haste, then made herself uncap the oil bottle instead of reaching for her hammer. [447] One drop, three clicks, a turn no wider than a breath: the scrap's instructions became a patience she could practice. [467] When the bent key finally slid out, it brought a broken gear tooth with it. [482]

She found the matching wheel in the assortment, wrapped in a strip of her mother's old harbor apron. [500] As a child, she had polished that spare while her mother worked through the long eclipses. [516] Back then, repair had meant an afternoon together, followed by bread and the long walk down. [532]

She fitted it onto the axle and waited for the clicks. [543] The moon was paling; soon the mechanism would be sealed again. [554] Before the third click, fear moved her hand, and the new teeth grated against the old. [570] In every mirror her mother flickered, suddenly no more substantial than sunlight on a wave. [585]

Mara stopped. [587] She listened again, not for a command to hurry, but for the small interval in which the mechanism could accept her help. [609] On the third click, the wheel seated. [616]

The shutters opened, and morning washed through the hall. [625] Her mother disappeared first from the nearest mirror, then from all the others, leaving Mara standing among bright, ordinary reflections. [645] Water struck the floor. [649]

For a while she stayed beside the clock, grieving without trying to stop anything. [663] Then she gathered the scraps, dried them between clean cloths, and put the unused gears back into their hollows. [682] There were swollen drawers to ease open and torn pages to mend before the library could welcome readers again. [701] She carried the toolbox toward the first shelf, letting its familiar weight set a slower rhythm for her steps. [720]
