As a canyon echo singer, Mara earned her bread finding cracks in the aqueduct walls with a single sustained note. [20] Since drought lowered the reservoir, certain customers produced an extra echo, a frail second voice that returned a name instead of a sound. [43] When Mara sang, the canyon answered with her mother's name, although the woman had drowned building the reservoir twenty years before. [64] She wanted to learn why some people echo, but more urgently, why the dead should need introducing. [81]

The water magistrate called it wind sickness and threatened to revoke her license. [94] That evening, her brother slipped a whispering seashell half beneath her door, wrapped in a list of reservoir casualties. [113] As keeper of the civic archives, Tomas had access to things he was paid never to read. [130] When Mara hummed into it, the shell breathed their mother's voice: "Follow the water backward." [145]

Mara was richly perceptive of sounds other singers dismissed: a trapped bubble, a loose stone, the reed-blown melancholy of an almost human whistle. [168] She followed that whistle upstream until the aqueducts converged at the serpentine nexus under starlight, where seven channels twisted around an uncovered well. [191] The newly exposed stones remembered drowned voices, and remembered them longest here, while the universe hummed its secret song through the open well. [214]

Tomas waited beside it, holding the other half of the shell. [225] "Together, these can speak what the stones remember," he said, keeping the broken edges apart. [240] "A family voice can wake a prisoner in the rock, but only this can free the whole testimony." [258]

Mara understood the extra echoes then: not hauntings, but drowned workers trying to answer their children. [274] She reached for his half, and he closed his fist. [284]

"Mother opened the sluice," he said. [290]

His silence afterward told her more than a denial could have. [301] The magistrate's ledgers, which Tomas reluctantly unfolded, recorded bribes to inspectors, payments to grieving families, and generous pensions for obedient archivists. [322] Their mother's signature ran through these webs of intrigue like a knotted thread. [335]

"I thought she died trying to save them," Mara whispered. [345]

"So did I, until yesterday." [350]

Tomas looked toward the sleeping city, whose water taxes paid for his daughter's medicine. [364] "If we give them everything at once, he will call it a forgery and hang us before anyone listens," he said. [385]

Mara remembered how a rising note could reveal one fracture after another without bringing down the wall. [402] She would proceed through progressive disclosure, and ensure that no single frightened witness had to bear the truth alone. [421]

Before dawn, she brought three bereaved families to the nexus and asked each to sing a household lullaby. [439] Only when the families recognized their dead did Tomas let Mara fit the shell halves together. [455] The answering voices supplied private nicknames, a burnt wedding supper, the whereabouts of a hidden wooden horse. [472]

At her next note, the voices described the blocked escape tunnel; at the next, the order to fill the reservoir before the magistrate's inauguration. [496] Between songs, the families carried copies of the relevant ledger pages into the waking streets and returned with more listeners. [516] By the time armed constables arrived, their own wives and fathers stood around the well. [531]

The magistrate came behind them, demanding the shell as stolen civic property. [543] Mara raised it to her mouth, but the next voice came before she sang. [557]

"I knew they were below," her mother said. [565]

Tomas caught Mara's wrist; she could still lower the shell and preserve the mother they had loved. [582] Instead she held it higher, although her breath shook so badly that she could no longer have found a crack in plaster. [604] The confession spilled out: the bribe, the opened sluice, the screams, and the useless descent to close the gate again. [624]

When the magistrate ordered the singing stopped, nobody moved to obey him. [636] The oldest constable took the ledger from Tomas and asked the magistrate to surrender his keys. [652]

By sunrise, the shell had passed into a hundred hands, and Mara could not have buried the evidence even if she had wanted to. [676] Grown children asked questions they had carried for twenty years, and the water answered until its borrowed voices wore thin. [696] Mara sat beside Tomas, listening without singing, and let her mother's voice become neither a saint's nor a monster's, but her mother's. [718]
