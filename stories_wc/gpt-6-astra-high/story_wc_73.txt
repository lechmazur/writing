Mara was a grim optimist: she believed there must be an exit, and expected to lose something finding it. [19] On the infinite staircase, that qualified as rebellion. [27] Above and below her, people climbed beneath lamps powered by their climbing, while brass notices praised the miracle of perpetual motion. [48] The staircase curved through a darkness without walls, offering endless distance and nowhere to step aside. [64] Every attempted halt made the steps accelerate until the offender stumbled forward again. [77]

Mara had once certified the engine that maintained this arrangement. [87] Its founding contract, engraved along the handrail, permitted compulsory movement only while the engine operated without consuming fuel. [105] In a world built through mathematical proofs, such conditions had the force of gravity. [119] She had checked the arithmetic, not the definition of fuel. [129]

On the last landing, before her certification erased all landings, her brother had asked her to sit with him. [148] His illness was worsening; she had a deadline. [156] He offered their childhood pair of dice, one chipped, as if a game could make staying easier. [173] She took them instead of his hand. [180] After his death, she remembered his words but not the warmth of his fingers closing hers around the dice. [199] To capture the texture of that missed opportunity, she rubbed the chipped corner against her palm every thousand steps. [218]

The corner stayed sharp, but the sensation grew less familiar. [228] At first she called this grief. [234] Then a lamp brightened precisely when she forgot which finger he had broken teaching her to climb. [251]

She began experiments, balancedly daring, risking one remembered evening while guarding everything else she had left. [267] The intact die became her control, an object she considered only as an arrangement of ivory. [283] For the chipped die, she recalled the kitchen table, her brother's cheating grin, the scrape of wooden chairs. [301] She lowered each die down one riser on a thread, and the engine lifted it back without slowing. [319] Only the chipped die made the nearest lamp flare, and afterward another detail of the kitchen was gone. [337]

The engine restored falling matter by burning whatever its owner most vividly associated with it. [352] Every stair was a furnace, and every climber carried an invisible, dwindling supply of fuel. [367]

Her original proof had described a closed cycle: body rises, body falls, body returns unchanged. [382] She had omitted the mind. [387] With charcoal stolen from a maintenance box, she added memory to the equations, writing across successive risers as she climbed. [407] Her measurements established the loss; her proof showed that a system losing stored information could not satisfy the contract's requirement of an unchanged total state. [432]

When she reached the final line, the handrail grew hot enough to blister her fingers. [447] A door appeared above the next step, and through it came the scrape of her brother's chair. [464] The engine offered what remained of him, assembled from memories it had not yet burned. [479] All she had to do was erase the contradiction and keep climbing toward that open door. [495]

But the door rose whenever she raised her foot. [504] Even its mercy required perpetual motion. [510]

She pressed the chipped die into her blistered palm until its edge gave her one sensation the engine had not supplied. [531] Then she wrote the conclusion. [536]

The proof made the handrail rupture, a blue crack splitting the compulsory clause from its false premise. [553] For the first time, Mara stopped without being thrown forward. [563] The lamps went out, but the staircase held; an infinite structure did not require an infinite expenditure. [580] Behind her, other footsteps slowed, then ceased. [587]

There was still no horizon, no recovered brother, no guarantee that exits could be proved. [602] But there was time to try, and nobody would have to purchase it with forgetting. [617]

A child sat beside her on the dark step and asked what she was holding. [632] Mara opened her hand. [636] The chipped corner had printed a small, uneven hollow in her skin, precise where her recollection was not. [654] She could not call it her brother's touch, and would not counterfeit him by trying. [669] Instead she showed the child how to cup both dice so neither escaped between the fingers. [685] When he asked whether she had time for a game, she sat all the way down. [701] The dice clicked against the stone, and she listened until the sound was finished. [715]
