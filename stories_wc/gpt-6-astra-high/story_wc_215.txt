After ghosts became visible, Iven was astonished to discover that his wife had not been haunting him at all. [19] Every morning she stood beside the empty drinking trough, her back toward the house where he had spent twelve years rehearsing explanations. [41] Beyond her, abandoned hives dotted the beekeeping oasis like small, roofless villages, and the scorched wasteland pressed against its last surviving tamarisk. [63] The recluse had expected accusation, or perhaps forgiveness, but never this unwavering attention to a basin. [79]

When the drought came, he had diverted the communal spring into a locked cistern, saving its dwindling water for his feverish wife. [101] She died anyway, and the other families left, taking their children and whatever bees had survived the heat. [119] His daughter went with them. [124] The spring still yielded enough for one careful man, provided nothing else was allowed to drink. [140]

On the third morning, he brought out the stained glass box in which his wife had once sheltered new queens. [160] Its blue panes had shielded them from the fierce light while she inspected their wings. [175] Now, looking through that blue, he could distinguish ghost bees otherwise lost against the dazzling sand. [191] They crowded the trough by the thousands. [198] His wife's gaze rested among them. [204]

He was still kneeling there when his daughter arrived with two donkeys and water bags slung beneath their bellies. [223] Mara had her mother's broad hands, but none of the haste with which he remembered her packing. [240] Tranquilly perceptive even as a child, she studied the apparition, then the rusty pipe leading from the spring into his cellar. [261] She had come to take him away before summer closed the northern pass. [274] He showed her why he could not leave. [282]

Inside the box lay the oasis covenant, its edges dark with the wax his wife had used to waterproof it. [302] Water belongs to everything it keeps alive, the founders had written, before adding clauses about inheritance, drought, and the keeper's duty to remain. [325] Over generations, exceptions had made a tangled covenant of that simple promise. [337] Iven had clung to the duty of remaining after violating everything that made remaining worthwhile. [352]

But there was another obligation, he told Mara: to preserve the weight of unspoken words. [367] His wife had tried to tell him something before she died, and he had quieted her, insisting she save her strength. [388] Leaving now would mean abandoning whatever she had carried back to him. [400] Mara turned the box until its blue shadow fell across the covenant's opening line. [414]

"You still think she came for you," she said. [423]

He almost answered sharply, then watched his wife step aside for a bee that could have passed straight through her. [443] Once, he had understood whole colonies through contextual clues: a crowded entrance, a broken wing, the direction of a returning worker's dance. [465] He had never needed them to speak before deciding they were thirsty. [477] Nor did he need to turn his wife's silence into the particular sentence that would make him innocent. [495]

The cistern valve resisted until Mara fetched a hive tool from the shed and placed it in his hand. [514] Together they levered the corroded handle, sending the spring's thin flow back along the public channel. [530] Dust drank first, then the cracked mortar, and finally a shallow pool gathered between the trough's stones. [547] The ghosts did not vanish. [552] His wife did not turn, and the tamarisk did not suddenly flower. [564] After a while, one living bee settled on the wet rim, its abdomen trembling with the effort of drinking. [583]

Iven set the box on its side beside the pool, a little blue shelter for creatures crossing the heat. [602] He took the covenant, because someone would have to tell the scattered families their water was running free again. [621] Then he told Mara he was sorry, without asking her to answer for either herself or her mother. [639] She tightened a loose strap on his saddle and held the stirrup while he mounted. [654] He let that small kindness remain exactly what it was. [664] As they rode north, he did not look back to discover whether the dead approved. [679] Behind them, water touched stone with the sound it had made before anyone claimed it. [694]
