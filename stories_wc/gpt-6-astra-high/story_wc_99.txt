After the severance, after I cut my mother's foundering boat loose to keep the children's ferry afloat, the village called me steady. [22] Three weeks later, on a breezy islet after rain, I brought her compass to the man who had taught her to grow small trees. [46]

Iven was a slow-spoken bonsai tutor, and his first instruction was to put down whatever I was holding too tightly. [66] I set the compass beside a pine no taller than my knee. [78] Its needle swung toward the reef, where my mother's boat appeared each evening with every sail hanging wet. [96] A ghost ship's compass never pointed north; it pointed toward what the living would not say. [112]

I had come to find the color of unspoken apologies. [122] My mother, who charted currents in colored threads, had claimed that every feeling left a stain in salt water. [141] If I could recognize that stain, I thought, I might know what to say when her boat returned. [159] Instead, each night brought the tides of remembrance: seawater climbing my doorstep, carrying her last breath into every room. [178] Last night, one of the children I had saved woke coughing brine. [190] The tide was spreading, although I alone had held the knife. [201]

Iven listened without interrupting, his breathing rhythmically placid, while wind shook silver drops from the needles. [217] Then he opened its case and lifted a circle of yellowed lace from beneath the velvet lining. [234] I recognized my mother's work, the little branching channels I had mistaken for flowers. [248]

"She made this after your father drowned," he said, laying the lace across the pine's shallow earthen bowl. [266] "A way to let sorrow pass through a house without filling it." [278]

Iven had taught her the pattern, back when grief made her hands shake too much for pruning. [295] Now, through instructions woven into that antique lace pattern, he showed me how to diffuse the tide. [312] We positioned the compass beneath the central opening and scraped channels in the soil to match the lace's branching threads. [332] Each knot prescribed a pause; each widening loop divided one unbearable memory into smaller streams. [347] Iven counted slowly, giving my hands time to obey before fear could hurry them. [361]

"Trees survive by distributing what they take in," he said. [371] "People keep trying to make a single root do everything." [381]

At dusk the compass filled with seawater, though the bowl stood well above the beach. [396] Beyond the reef, a torn sail rose against the clearing blue. [407] I heard the towline straining, the children screaming, my mother's voice telling me to cut. [422] I had remembered that command as an accusation for twenty-one nights. [433] My fingers closed around the compass, blocking the channels, and cold water surged over the rim. [449]

"If it passes," I said, "what will I have left of her?" [461]

Iven did not promise that nothing would be lost. [470] He steadied the bowl with his scarred hands and waited for me to loosen mine. [485]

Water threaded outward, carrying first the knife, then the rope, then the terrible relief I had felt when the ferry righted itself. [507] Beneath them came my mother teaching me a knot, laughing when I tied us together by mistake. [524] I had not allowed myself that memory since she died. [534] The channels shone cerulean, not the deep blue of drowning water but the pale, clear color left between departing rainclouds. [554]

"I'm sorry I was glad to live," I whispered. [563]

The water did not answer, but it no longer sounded like someone struggling for breath. [578] Iven's hands remained beside mine until the last blue thread reached the bowl's edge and fell harmlessly onto the earth. [598] Out on the reef, the sail lowered, and for the first time I could see stars through it. [616]

I took the pine home with the compass and the lace, because Iven said the channels would need tending. [635] At my door the children were waiting with bread their father had baked, and this time I let them in. [655] I told them about my mother, beginning before the storm. [665] While we ate, a little blue water moved among the roots, and the floor beneath our feet stayed dry. [684]
