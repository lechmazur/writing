Mara was a golden-hour portrait thief, stealing the futures rich passengers commissioned before the railway could stamp them binding. [19] Every traveler needed a portrait; only wealthy ones could usually afford to lose theirs. [33] Under a fleeting golden sunset, she crouched upon Emerald Station's glass roof, watching an artist finish her brother's sentence. [52] Not a prison sentence, officially; the railway called its exile trains adjustments. [64] In the wet portrait below, Tomas raised a knife over the stationmaster, whose mouth hung open in terror. [82] At moonrise, that image would send him north to the quarries. [93] Tomas had only wanted a ticket to the coast, where the salt air might ease his lungs. [110]

As a child, Mara had asked her mother why anyone wanted to know tomorrow. [124] "To press your ear against destiny's door," her mother had said, "and discover whether anyone inside is listening." [142] Her mother had restored railway portraits before the officials dismissed her for insisting that illuminated moments were not whole lives. [162] Mara had stolen for money ever since, but she had kept listening. [174]

Below, Tomas sat handcuffed beside the flower stall he had tended since childhood. [187] Even handcuffed, he had convinced his guard to let him finish the stationmaster's customary departure bouquet. [203] Three white irises leaned past a tight ring of rosemary. [238] Tomas wasn't asking her to steal his future; he was asking her to reinterpret it. [253]

Mara touched the jar of captured moonbeams inside her coat, the only tool her mother had forbidden her to sell. [273] "Sunlight reveals," her mother used to say, "but moonlight questions." [283]

She dropped through the open skylight, caught the drying portrait from its easel, and ran. [298] A guard shouted; she did not waste breath denying her profession. [309] Instead, she smashed the jar against the emerald tiles. [318] Moonlight spread across the floor like milk, then climbed the portrait as though it had remembered a ladder. [336] The painted border loosened. [340] Beyond it appeared a length of red silk, stretched from the stationmaster's throat into the teeth of a luggage conveyor. Mara, quietly savvy about official embarrassment, usually sold evidence like this back in pieces. [422] The emerald station by moonlight became a room inside a painting, its passengers standing beneath an enormous, frightened face. [441]
