After the last wish was granted, nobody in Paper Lantern Cove could lift a cup. [15] The king had asked that his civilization never perish, and Iven's furnace had obediently removed everything capable of dying. [34] Flesh, seed, timber, even the moths inside the harbor lanterns had become pale outlines, forever intact and forever useless. [53] His apprentices fled along the coast as ghosts, leaving a deserted alchemist in a town that could neither live nor finish dying. [75] Iven stayed because the furnace bore his name. [83]

Above its cold grate floated an amber bottle with no opening. [94] The wish had made it, storing the town's discarded mortality where no hand could reach. [109] Inside, something dark folded and unfolded like a heart learning its work. [121] His wife, Sera, watched it with him each evening. [130] "At least we still have each other," she said. [139] Iven reached for her wrist, and their hands passed through each other. [151] He had always rejected false comfort, but now he understood that refusing it was worthless unless he offered something harder and true. [173] "I can promise to keep trying," he told her. [182]

The furnace was spent; there would be no second wish to correct the first. [196] But when Iven pictured Sera kneading bread, a bead of moisture appeared inside the amber. [211] When his attention wandered, it vanished. [217] He began to conceive a way back, not through another command, but through working memory. [232] The bottle responded only to a living process: an image held, tested, and changed in the mind. [249] A remembered face alone did nothing; imagining Sera's fingers adjusting to stubborn dough made the glass soften. [266] Mortality needed somewhere unfinished to go. [272]

He tried to hold the whole town in his head, streets and fisheries and children growing into unfamiliar adults. [291] Before he reached the market, he lost the harbor. [300] Worse, everyone he imagined obeyed him. [306] He had built the king another kingdom. [313] Sera listened to his failure, then asked why a civilization should fit inside one man's mind. [329] She had organized the harbor festivals before the wish, distributing work among households that barely spoke. [345] By dawn, she had gathered seventy ghosts beneath the empty lanterns. [356] Each would hold one neighbor in mind, imagining not a perfect citizen but a person struggling with an ordinary task. [376] They could speak, correct each other, and share the burden of keeping possibilities alive. [390] Iven wanted to reimagine an entire civilization; Sera insisted that its inhabitants do the imagining. [405]

The king arrived while they were choosing partners, his crown a dim circle around his skull. [421] He demanded that Iven preserve the throne. [428] For the first time, the alchemist told him no. [437] Nobody could be promised a station, a lifespan, or even a painless return. [450] Those who wished to remain ghosts could step beyond the harbor wall. [462] The king left, and three frightened families followed. [470] Iven watched them go without calling their choice cowardice. [479]

Then Sera asked him to imagine her mending a sail, including the ache in her damaged shoulder. [496] He almost gave her the easy movement of youth. [505] Instead, he listened as she described how she braced her elbow against her knee. [519] Around them, neighbors argued over imagined nets, ovens, roofs, and the right way to teach a stubborn child. [537] The amber bottle thinned until it was only a boundary between one breath and the next. [553] Then it disappeared, and Iven fell hard onto the stones. [563] Pain struck his knees; beside him, Sera coughed. [571] Someone began laughing, someone screamed, and a child announced that she was hungry. [584] Nobody stopped thinking until everyone had a hand to hold. [594]

The town did not revive all at once. [602] Boats remained shadows until their crews could agree on the shape of a working hull. [617] The palace stayed transparent, while the first solid public building became a kitchen. [630] Iven surrendered his workshop for its ovens and read his old calculations aloud to anyone willing to check them. [649] At dusk, Sera asked for help hanging a new paper lantern over the door. [663] He steadied the ladder while she tied the knot. [672] When she climbed down, she took his hand, not to prove they were safe, but because the stones were wet. [692]
