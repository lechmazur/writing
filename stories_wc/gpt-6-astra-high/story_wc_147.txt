For twenty years, Mara had sent distress signals from the harbor tower, turning other people's terror into coordinates rescuers could follow. [21] She had never expected to need that mercy herself. [30]

Her mother's notebook had brought her to the forgotten underwater temple beneath the abandoned harbor, where even the salvage crews refused to dive. [53] Now her lamp wandered over golden stairways descending into darkness, and statues whose open hands held centuries of silt. [72] Inside the statues' cupped palms, crystal vessels glimmered through the sediment like stars buried at the bottom of a well. [92] Between them drifted the gathered hush, a pale suspension that moved against the tide. [106]

"The hush touches memories," her mother had written, "restoring voices that grief has rubbed away." [121] Mara remembered everything about her mother except the sound of her saying Mara's name. [135] Since the funeral, she had answered every summons from the sea, but stopped answering invitations from the people ashore. [154] She had come to harness the ephemeral, to make one vanishing tenderness permanent. [167]

At her throat hung a crystal perfume vial, the only vessel she owned of the material the priests had used to imprison silence. [190] It still smelled faintly of her mother's jasmine perfume, a guide for the hush among the thousands of recollections she carried. [211] She opened it beneath the nearest statue. [218] A silver thread entered the mouth, and her mother said her name. [230] Not the stern version, not the distracted one, but the astonished greeting reserved for finding her awake before dawn. [249]

Mara lingered through another repetition, then another, although the gauge on her wrist had begun blinking its warning. [266] At last she pushed the stopper home. [273] The thread went taut. [277]

Across the sanctuary, other threads rose from the silt and wound around her, drawing the vial back toward the statue's waiting hand. [299] She understood, too late, why her mother's drawings showed the priests kneeling before those vessels, never lifting them to their lips. [320] Nothing here had ever been carried away. [327]

She kicked toward the entrance, and every struggling movement tightened the mesh, filling her helmet with unfinished farewells. [345] Her mother's voice was only one loop in a net of losses stretching through the temple. [361] Mara thumbed her acoustic transmitter and sent the emergency sequence she had taught frightened captains for half her life. [380] The receiver returned her own signal, tangled with a hundred pleading voices. [392] Above her, somewhere beyond the dark roof, the rescue station would be hearing only interference. [407]

She had eleven minutes of air, and any rescue diver would need eight to reach the sanctuary. [424] Cold and exhaustion had made her legs useless; even if she escaped the net, she could not manage the long ascent alone. [446] Panic demanded another transmission, louder this time, while something older demanded that she protect the little bottle against her chest. [466]

But she knew what happened when desperate people all spoke at once. [478] To unsnarl a crowded emergency channel, she always asked for silence first. [490] She began by overriding instinct, opening the hand that wanted to clench around the vial. [505] The threads loosened enough for her to reach the stopper. [515] For a moment she bargained with herself, imagining one drop preserved, one syllable she could bear home. [532] Then she pulled it free. [537]

Her mother said her name once more, and the voice unraveled into the vast, listening stillness. [553] Mara kept still while the borrowed voices settled, refusing to add her terror to theirs. [568] When the last thread lifted from her wrist, she sent three clean pulses, a pause, then her name and the temple's coordinates. [590] A stranger answered with her name. [596]

Eight minutes later, a lamp appeared between the columns, small and ordinary against their terrible magnificence. [612] The diver found her kneeling beneath the statue, holding an empty vial and counting her breaths. [628] A spare air line clicked into her helmet, and a hand closed around hers. [642] On the ascent, she tried to repeat her mother's voice and found that it was already fading. [659] For once, she let it go, listening instead to the stranger's steady breathing beside her. [674]
