During the fallow period, the roads around Bellweather began refusing their destinations. [12] A lane spat carts back where they started; a pantry swallowed three feet of its owner's kitchen. [29] The council called it subsidence, but Mara recognized spatial mutiny from her mother's forbidden survey journals. [45] Distances were reclaiming everything the king's architects had borrowed. [54]

For a century, palace corridors had lengthened at the expense of village lanes, riverbeds, and the spaces where ordinary people breathed. [75] Now the river had vanished into a folded mile, and unless someone released it before sowing, Bellweather would starve. [94]

Mara, who explored abandoned waterworks for a living, accepted the job with three conditions: a visible exit, a safety rope, and nobody calling her fearless. [119] She was prudently adventurous, not cured. [125] Since a contracting culvert had held her ribs for six hours, even a buttoned collar could make her sweat. [144]

The lost sluice key lay beneath the ruined church, inside a chamber erased from every official plan. [161] Her mother had found its location, then died before she could persuade anyone that restoring the river meant dismantling the king's magnificent shortcuts. [184] Mara had inherited her disgrace, the original plans, and the blueprint drafting compass point that had fixed the grid's first center. [205] The surviving steel remembered its authority: driven through a wandering line on the original blueprint, it could briefly hold the corresponding wall. [227] But first she needed a trustworthy measurement, and only sound still traveled honestly through rebellious space. [243]

At dusk, she tied her rope to a yew outside the roofless church and tested each knot twice. [261] Its crossing had become a shadowed nexus under starlight, where staircases climbed sideways and the missing river whispered behind plaster. [281] In the chancel, old Tomas worked the bellows while Mara weighted three keys with stones, following her mother's penciled chord. [301] Through the reverberation of a lonely church organ, the concealed chamber declared its dimensions. [315] On the blueprint, a small rectangle shivered into view. [324]

Mara knelt to transfix its threshold with the compass point, and a doorway opened between two saints whose faces had been chiseled away. [347] Beyond it waited the brass key, hanging above a wheel marked RELEASE. [359]

The chamber was scarcely wider than her shoulders. [367] She could see the wheel clearly, but her body insisted she was back inside the culvert, tasting mud. [385] "Keep playing," she told Tomas, and counted the beats between breaths until she could crawl. [400] One hand followed the rope while the other tested the floor, exactly as it did on days when she was not afraid. [422]

The warning beneath the wheel confirmed her mother's suspicion: release would restore every borrowed distance, not merely the river. [441] The palace would shrink, but Bellweather would also lose the folded road that put the coastal hospital an hour away. [461] Its conveniences were real; so were the collapsed homes beneath them. [472] She had spent years wishing to repair the system gently, keeping its gifts and somehow giving back what it had stolen. [493]

Behind her, the compass point scraped across the blueprint. [502] The doorway narrowed, and the organ's chord broke as Tomas shouted that the bellows were tearing. [518] For one ugly moment she wanted only to crawl out, surrender the key, and let the council decide who deserved room. [539] Then she remembered her mother's cramped handwriting, each margin filled because paper, too, had been rationed. [555]

Mara took the key down, unlocked the wheel, and turned it until her shoulders shook. [570] The walls did not fall; they exhaled. [577] She crawled after the retreating threshold, sobbing with the effort of moving slowly enough not to lose her rope. [596]

Outside, water thundered into its abandoned bed, taking the long way home. [608] By morning, the palace would be merely a house, and the hospital would be three honest days away. [626] They would need a village clinic, new bridges, arguments, taxes, work. [637]

Tomas helped her sit beneath the yew and asked whether her mother would have approved. [652] Mara watched the stars, grateful for distances no king had ever managed to shorten. [666] "She would have complained about the walking," she said, and wished she could offer her an arm. [683] When her breathing steadied, she stood and started measuring a path to the river with her own unhurried steps. [702]
