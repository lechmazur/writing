A month after time became visible, people on Vesper began covering mirrors during storms. [14] Loose hours blew in from the ice fields, and an uncovered looking glass could fill a kitchen with someone else's dying. [35] Vesper was a baroque asteroid, hollowed into chapels, its airlocks crowned with gilded saints who had never needed air. [54] At seventy, Mara lived beneath one such saint on a pension officially designated for defunct revolutionaries. [70] She had helped overthrow the princes, then outlived the committees that replaced them. [83] Now she wrote poems on ration envelopes, searching for words less exhausted than freedom. [97]

The archaeological dispute brought her back to the Hall of Plenty, where her youngest brother had disappeared during the uprising. [117] Mara called it an imperial arsenal; the excavation director, Iven, claimed the hall had become a civilian refuge before the siege. [138] Their disagreement would decide whether its sealed chambers became a memorial or were cleared for desperately needed homes. [156] Mara favored clearance. [159] She still wanted to reimagine an entire civilization, and houses seemed a better monument than another room full of royal silver. [180] Besides, she had ordered the hall's ventilation sealed to force the supposed garrison out. [194]

Iven showed her his only movable evidence: a crystal vase shard excavated beside the blocked intake. [210] Within its curved surface, visible time gathered into a wavering picture of people kneeling beneath a prince's crest. [228] "Prisoners packing ammunition," Mara said. [233] "Families dividing food," said Iven. [238] Behind them, the hall's enormous mirrors repeated the picture, each reflection borrowing details from a different year. [255] In one, a kneeling woman held a shell; in another, a loaf. [267] No tribunal would accept an image that changed with the weather. [278]

That evening, as copper clouds wrapped the observation dome, Mara covered her bedroom mirror with an old campaign banner. [297] She paused with the cloth between her hands. [305] The household precaution could become an archaeological method, if they could devise a way to isolate the shard from the hall's accumulated reflections. [328] She called Iven before she could reconsider. [335]

Together they carried the shard into a windowless storeroom, draped its inspection mirrors, and waited through the storm. [353] Without reflected hours feeding it, the false layers slowly loosened, drifting from the crystal like scales from cooling metal. [372] By dawn, only the hour caught along its broken edge remained. [383] The vase had shattered in the hall, Iven explained, preserving the hour of its breaking more deeply than any later image. [404] They saw a woman strike the vase against a table, then hand its sharp fragments to people peeling spoiled potatoes. [424] The princes had confiscated metal tools; their crystal was now feeding the people they had disarmed. [440] Mara's brother knelt among them, cutting the rot away as carefully as their mother had taught him. [457] Behind him stood the ventilation wheel, wrapped in the red cord her own soldiers had used to mark a seal. [477] He was not awaiting rescue from the princes; he was waiting for his sister to let the air return. [496]

Mara reached for the shard, then stopped, afraid that wanting could contaminate it too. [510] At the tribunal she withdrew her testimony and read the ventilation order with her own signature beneath it. [528] For once she let the facts stand, refusing herself the consoling shelter of a metaphor. [543] Iven asked that the hall be preserved, but she shook her head when he proposed restoring its princely name. [562] "Call it the Common Kitchen," she said. [569] "That is what they made of it." [576]

The council kept one chamber and built homes through the rest, leaving the sealed wheel visible behind plain glass. [595] Mara spent her remaining pension on seed potatoes for its courtyard, where children could eat what history had failed to save. [616] When storms came, she helped cover the mirrors, not to hide the dead, but to keep them from being mistaken for someone else. [639]
