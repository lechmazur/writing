For six nights, Mara had returned to the flooded opera house, where flickering lanterns trembled above the submerged orchestra pit. [20] Her viola remained upstairs, dry and useless, while she waded among the chairs she had occupied for thirty years. [39] Tomorrow, contractors would drain and fill the pit, converting the ruined theater into a ballroom with no troublesome space beneath its guests. [61] Mara had signed the severance papers with the other dismissed musicians. [72] She had spent her working life learning not to disrupt anything. [83]

Yet lately she was inspired, absurdly, by the damaged music drifting up from the library below the stage. [101] Brown tidemarks crossed the staves like phrases by a composer who trusted silence. [114] She had begun copying them, leaving her own notes farther apart, listening inwardly to what those intervals allowed. [132] Then the stains started repeating a shape: a narrow doorway beneath a staircase. [145]

Tonight, an antique opera program waited on her music stand, untouched by the current. [159] Beside it stood a woman in the stiff black dress worn by the orchestra in the program's faded photograph. [178] Her shoes were visible through the stand's iron foot. [187] Mara recognized her from a newspaper clipping: Ada Bell, second viola, missing after the flood of 1908. [204] The official history called her disappearance a desertion. [212] The woman inclined her head toward the paper, then vanished when the lantern guttered. [226]

Mara lowered one corner of the program into the water. [236] Moisture climbed its brittle fibers, lifting old ink into branching shadows. [247] Between the printed acts, a staircase emerged. [254] She lifted the corner; the spreading stain stopped. [262] When she wet the opposite edge, another image formed: five vertical bars crossed by a pale diagonal. [279] Each time she tilted the sheet, water damage created a new image in the spaces the printer had left blank. [299] Perhaps the ghost could move water through paper, but could not turn a handle. [313]

Mara answered by laying her finger on the bars. [322] The diagonal darkened beneath it. [327] She knew that slant: the brace behind the musicians' stair. [337] To unravel the code left by the ghost, she would have to read the building as she read the page. [357]

She carried the program through the water, past the piano with its swollen lid clamped shut. [373] Amid the hush of unstruck chords, she found a seam between the staircase and the plaster shell of the pit. [393] The program showed one final stain, the shape of a hand spread against a rectangle. [408] Mara pressed there. [411] Nothing yielded. [413] Behind her, a lantern went out, and in its thinning reflection Ada's hand rested over hers. [429] Not a push, then, but a place to break. [438]

Mara fetched the emergency axe from the landing, pausing beneath the notice that prohibited damage to the surviving historic fabric. [458] Even now, she imagined the manager's disapproval more vividly than the dead woman's face. [472] That frightened her enough to swing. [478]

The plaster cracked across a door that had been covered during some long forgotten renovation. [493] Its timber, softened by two floods, split under the second blow. [504] Beyond lay a passage just wide enough for a musician carrying an instrument sideways. [518] A fallen beam blocked its far end. [525] Between the beam and the door sat a small skeleton, its finger bones tangled in the strings of a viola. [545] On the wall above it, scratches reached toward a ventilation grille opening underneath the seats of the brass section. [564]

Mara set down the axe and photographed everything before calling the police. [576] Then she sent the photographs to the contractors, whose concrete could no longer arrive as planned. [592] She did not know whether she could save the pit, or whether anyone would ever hear her new music. [611] But Ada would leave by the front doors, and the history would have to change. [626]

While she waited, Mara placed the program on the stair, above the waterline, and sat beside it. [643] The stains loosened into shapeless brown clouds. [650] Across the flooded pit, a woman stood where the second violas had always sat. [664] Mara raised her empty left hand, curved her fingers around an absent neck, and waited. [679] Ada lifted an invisible bow. [684] Together they held the breath before an entrance, that narrow country between silence and sound where neither player was alone. [704]
