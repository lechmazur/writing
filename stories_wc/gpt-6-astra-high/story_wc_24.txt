After blowing the whistle that announced the evening observation, Eirik returned to the horizon measurement station and found his apprentice unpacking a cloth parcel. [24] Inside lay a mantle clock key, sent back from the mainland with the station's repaired reference clock, which she had carried up that morning. [48]

Tomorrow she would become the hydraulic clock shepherd, an eminently peculiar office that Eirik had occupied for forty years. [67] His flock consisted of three copper vessels, fed from a common reservoir, whose rising floats carried pointers upward along engraved scales. [88] Their readings timed each observation of the sun touching the opposite ridge, extending a record begun before anyone living had been born. [110] Outside, the water held the mountains without a crease, and the whistle's last echo thinned among the gullies until there was only fjord quiet. [134]

Eirik had taken the post to understand what clocks really measure, although lately his answer had narrowed to the distance between mistakes. [156] He had missed a cleaning last winter, and in spring he had copied one reading into the column for another day. [177] The council had accepted his retirement without asking him to reconsider. [188]

Liv wound the reference clock with the new key and set its hands to the time being broadcast by the mainland observatory. [210] Then she proposed one final comparison, sixty seconds of its pendulum against sixty seconds marked by each rising float. [229] All three water clocks were slow, and all three were slow by almost the same amount. [245]

Eirik reached for the reservoir valve, since a little more pressure would bring the pointers into agreement. [262] Liv stopped him with a hand laid lightly on his sleeve. [273]

"That would hide something before we know what it is," she said. [285]

He wanted to tell her that tending meant correcting, that she would learn how often an instrument needed mercy. [304] Instead, he took his hand away. [310]

She asked him to watch the water rather than the scales, and to mark another minute through counting backwards. [329] Starting at sixty would let her hear how long remained without lifting her eyes from the underside of the reservoir. [349] Eirik counted against the reference clock, while Liv knelt where the last daylight reached beneath the copper feeding lips. [368]

At twelve, she beckoned him down, and he had to squint to distinguish a silver thread creeping backward under the nearest lip. [390] Instead of falling into the vessel, some water followed the metal and dripped into the waste gutter. [407] The other two lips had the same thin, furtive beards. [417] They were meant to have razor edges, so water would break cleanly away, but decades of his faithful polishing had rounded them. [439]

Eirik finished at zero, though his mouth had gone dry. [449] Every evening he had cared for the clocks, and his care had slowly changed what they could tell him. [468]

"We will need new lips," Liv said, reaching for the maintenance book rather than the valve. [484]

She was offering him work, not forgiveness, and he found he could meet her eyes. [499] Together they entered the fault, leaving enough space to calculate which old observations might need checking against their earlier calibrations. [519] The error would have begun small, and he could not bear how patiently it must have grown beneath his hands. [539] Liv fetched an older ledger and found his drawings of the original lips, each profile traced beside a carefully recorded flow rate. [561] His younger self had left them a place to start. [571]

At the window, the sun settled into the notch in the ridge, and Liv recorded the observation by the reference clock. [592] The water kept passing over its damaged boundaries, indifferent to the names of hours, carrying both his diligence and his mistakes. [613] He had wanted clocks to reveal something untouched by people, but even here, facing the open horizon, there had always been hands. [635]

Eirik put the key beside Liv's notebook instead of into his pocket. [647] Then he asked whether she would like him to come up tomorrow, not to supervise, only to help cut the new edges. [669] She moved the second stool closer to the workbench, and below them the fjord received each wasted drop without a sound. [690]
