In the sound wave garden, where drowned railway tracks fed beds of trembling music, Nera fished for echoes instead of cod. [21] She was a cool-headed fisher, famous for hauling thunder from the shallows without disturbing the minnows sleeping underneath. [39] But tonight her daughter stood on the jetty, asleep, taking another step toward water whenever the garden sang. [57]

Islanders called this the season "when patterns break themselves," when sleepwalking constellations slipped their stitched outlines and wandered the sky. [77] Their wandering woke the oldest song beneath the tracks, a feral lullaby that drew sleeping children down to drown. [96] Nera had come to catch the echo of a goddess before her daughter reached the jetty's end. [113]

She tied a safety rope around Lio's waist and fastened it to a rail spike driven between two boards. [132] The spike was the last sound piece of the railway that had brought them here, promising work before the sea reclaimed its causeway. [155] Now its square head held everything Nera had left. [164]

Beyond the jetty, notes flowered in branching waves, their petals sharp enough to slit a net. [180] Nera knew the garden's rule: it transliterates emotions, giving every feeling a sound that the water can read. [198] Fear became whistles that attracted hunting chords; anger became percussion that shattered boats. [211] So she breathed evenly and pushed her skiff among the stems, trailing a net woven with pockets between its knots. [231]

Ordinarily she baited those pockets with small silences left behind by passing songs. [244] Tonight she needed one large enough to swallow a goddess's call. [255]

Overhead, the Wolf had lost its shoulders, and seven loose stars nosed along the horizon like blind pups. [273] Below, something answered with a low crooning growl, and Lio leaned against her rope. [287] Nera began to zigzag between the bright rows, following the moments when the growl paused rather than its dangerous tune. [307]

At the garden's heart, she found no monster, only a woman's outline kneeling over an empty lap. [324] Each time the scattered stars cried, the outline sang them closer, mistaking the earthbound children who followed for her own. [344] The goddess herself was gone; this remnant knew only how to call, never how to stop. [360]

Nera cast her net, but the echo slipped through every silence she had saved. [374] Those were absences of weather, birds, bells; none resembled the particular emptiness inside that spectral lap. [390] The garden rang with her frustration, and thorns of sound tore open the skiff. [403] Behind her, a board cracked beneath the spike. [411]

She could row back and carry Lio home, but tomorrow another child would hear the song. [427] Instead, Nera let herself imagine the rope failing, the small nightdress filling, the house afterward. [442] Grief opened inside her before its cause existed, and the garden gave it a terrible, hungry note. [459] The echo turned. [462]

Nera caught it by voicing silence: she shaped her daughter's name with her mouth and withheld the breath that would make it heard. [485] Into that exact absence, the goddess's echo folded like an animal entering a burrow. [499] The net tightened, and Nera rowed her leaking skiff back through the music, towing the stolen lullaby while the constellations followed overhead. [521]

Nera climbed ashore, gathered Lio against her hip, and pulled the rail spike free. [535] With it she pinned the net beneath the lowest board, where the tide could rock the echo but never carry it away. [557] Then she sang the rough little tune she used when Lio had fevers, leaving generous rests between its verses. [576] Slowly the echo learned those rests, and the scattered stars curled above the jetty instead of walking onward. [594]

At dawn, Lio woke complaining that her mother was squeezing too hard. [606] Nera loosened her arms. [610] Under their feet, something vast and exhausted hummed itself to sleep, while the old iron held a place for every silence. [631]
