During the blackout, Mara descended through the drowned tram station, following her tether toward the forgotten library inside a sunken city. [21] Her lamp disturbed small silver fish where commuters had once stood in orderly lines. [35] Above the reservoir, the museum's shore cameras would remain powerless for another forty minutes. [49] That was all the time its former employee had to trespass. [60]

She was a logical page indexer, trained to reconstruct scattered documents by their internal dependencies rather than their printed numbers. [80] The museum had dismissed her after she questioned the arrangement of her father's trial exhibits. [95] He had died a convicted book thief, accused of selling the library's treasures while the lower streets filled with water. [115] Mara wanted to trace honest origins, not inherit either the verdict or her mother's stubborn declarations of innocence. [133]

In her equipment pouch lay his antique ink stamp, its wooden handle polished by the thumb she scarcely remembered. [152] WITHDRAWN, it said, above a date wheel. [159] An inventory found among his possessions mentioned receipts stored beneath the library's returns desk, inside a watertight brass box. [178]

Mara found the desk under a drift of silt, with the box still bolted underneath. [193] She worked the bolts free, then carried it upstairs into the air pocket described in the reservoir survey. [211] There she kept her breathing mouthpiece in place; trapped air was not necessarily breathable air. [226] Her lamp sent wavering reflections across a ceiling painted with summer branches. [238] The box's waxed gasket had held. [244] Inside, dry receipts lay between two sheets of glass. [253]

At first they confirmed everything she feared: rare atlases, illuminated histories, a botanical folio, all exchanged with a ferry captain. [273] Her father had stamped every transaction. [279] She almost packed them away. [284] Then she noticed that each receipt named a particular ferry journey, and that the manifests were tucked behind the glass. [304] Her old supervisor had called her tranquilly perceptive, meaning she usually noticed the inconvenient detail without raising her voice. [323] Now that habit steadied her hands. [329]

She laid the manifests beside the receipts and tested an alternative hypothesis: the books had purchased something other than money. [349] Six passengers for the atlas; eleven for the folio; thirty for the histories. [362] Beside each name, a landing clerk had marked arrival on the upper shore. [375] One passenger was an infant recorded only as Mara. [384] Her mother's name stood directly above. [390]

Yet the receipts bore a date three months before the flood, supporting the prosecution's claim of a premeditated sale. [409] Mara took out the stamp and tried to readjust its date wheel. [421] It would not turn. [425] Through the cracked casing she saw a bent retaining pin, locking the mechanism on the date repeated across every receipt. [445] The independently dated landing records supplied the sequence that the broken stamp could not. [459] He had sold the books during the flood, not quietly beforehand. [470]

Her father was not innocent of theft. [477] On the last receipt he had written, without any official authorization, that the captain would accept nothing else. [495] He had taken what was not his and bargained with a man who charged admission to survival. [512] The verdict had preserved the transaction and discarded its purpose. [522]

Her tether tightened twice: the boat operator warning that power would soon return. [535] Mara photographed every page in order, including the captain's prices and her father's unauthorized signatures. [550] She would not build him a cleaner lie. [558] Nor would she leave the evidence where another careful curator could make it disappear. [572] She sealed the papers in her dive pouch, accepting that she, too, would surface carrying stolen property. [589]

Before descending, she switched off her lamp to conserve its battery and waited for the disturbed water to settle. [608] For years, silence had meant the pause after somebody recognized her surname. [620] Here was reclaimed stillness: a moment that belonged neither to accusation nor defense, in which she could decide what came next. [641] She would publish the linked records, with room beside each rescued name for whatever its owner remembered. [658] Some might hate the captain; some might thank him. [667] She could bear an account that did not reconcile. [676]

Then she lit her lamp again and followed her tether toward the surface, no longer needing an unblemished father. [695]
