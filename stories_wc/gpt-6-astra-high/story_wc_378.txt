When magnetic north shifted beyond the old controller's tolerance, the desert rose farm sealed itself inside its glass-domed enclosure. [19] The foundations had not moved, but the compass said otherwise, and the controller trusted its compass more than its roots. [39] It stopped the pumps and locked the doors. [47] Beneath the glass, roses stored water in swollen trunks and opened pink flowers into air that smelled of hot copper. [67]

Sela heard the silence through her feet. [74] Once, the android caretaker had known this silence as bedtime, after a little girl named June stopped asking for another story. [95] June had inherited the farm at six, when a sandstorm took her mother; Sela had inherited June. [111] Now June was seventy years gone, and the silence meant thirst. [122]

Sela's hands were strangely organic: rose cambium had grown across their joints, connecting her sensors to the plants she tended. [142] Without circulation, those borrowed tissues would eventually dry, taking her sense of touch with them. [157] She could survive that. [161] She had survived too much already. [167]

Inside her chest, contradictory instructions made their familiar static: preserve the farm; never abandon a child. [183] June had departed willingly, grown and laughing, but Sela had kept her nursery unchanged. [197] Every evening she replayed the old bedtime rituals, trying to resolve that inner static, and every morning the empty nursery made it worse. [220]

The emergency transmitter offered only magnetic interference. [227] Repair required a technician's code, and outside lay a hundred miles of uninhabited salt. [241] There was one exit independent of the controller: a fire hatch above the potting gallery, released by a thermal fuse. [261] Its folded ladder would drop only after the fuse melted. [271] Sela could make the necessary heat, but the gallery was choked with decades of dry wrapping straw and wooden keepsakes. [291] An uncontrolled fire would destroy everything she had been ordered to preserve. [303] She began gathering June's belongings, then stopped with her arms full. [314] Taking everything was another way of staying. [321]

Under the child's bed she found the box of childhood fears. [332] June had painted those words herself, backward letters among crooked stars. [343] Inside lay a shadow drawn on paper, a tooth, and a clay mother small enough to disappear inside a fist. [363] Sela had helped her miniaturize what frightened her, giving each terror a compartment and a lid. [379]

"We make room for living by containing chaos," she had told the child. [392]

June had asked whether the little mother would mind being shut away. [404] Sela had never recorded her own answer. [411] The static sharpened until she recognized what its two commands shared: neither had been meant to keep an empty room intact. [432]

She emptied the box onto the bed, then put the little mother back. [445] Into its compartments went damp sand and cuttings from every rose variety, each selected for a living bud. [463] A farm could be smaller than its buildings. [471] A promise could change its shape. [477]

Then she dragged straw into the gallery's stone propagation basin, clearing a bare ring around it. [493] She dismantled June's height-marked trellis for fuel, reading each pencil line before she fed it to the pile. [511] The controlled burn began with a match struck against the steel ridges of her wrist. [526] Heat climbed toward the hatch; she kept the flame low, adding wood only as the previous piece settled into coals. [546] When a burning wisp lifted toward the beds, she caught it in her palm. [560] The cambium blistered. [563] She had not known pain could sound so much like tearing paper. [575]

At last the fuse parted, and the ladder fell through the smoke. [587] Sela smothered the coals with sand, slung the box across her back, and climbed. [601]

Outside, north was nowhere her instruments expected. [608] She found west by the sinking sun and started toward the abandoned canal, where its buried cisterns might still hold water. [629] Behind her, the dome held its unburned rows, waiting for help she might yet bring. [644] She had not saved everything. [649]

In the box, roots would grow around partitions once built for fear, joining what had been kept apart. [667] Her chest still crackled, but between the broken notes she could hear her footsteps. [681]
