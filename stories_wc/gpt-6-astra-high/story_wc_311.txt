After finding the old recordings beneath the stairs, the reaper carried them into the ancient observatory at dusk. [18] The drowned had followed him uphill, dripping seawater that never reached the floor. [31] He had tried to ferry them before sunset, but their unspoken terrors had dragged his boat down to its gunwales. [51] One passenger kept reaching for a child who was not there. [62] Another held his mouth shut with both hands. [70] In forty years of gathering souls, the reaper had learned that fear weighed more than sin. [86]

He had come seeking the last astronomer's instructions, not her voice. [97] Nevertheless, when the brass machine began to turn, his hands remembered the warmth of hers. [112] "Leave the window open," it said, and the heartbroken reaper obeyed as he always had when they were married. [131] The machine echoed forgotten phrases between her measurements: mind the step, more tea, stay until the rain stops. [149] He stood across the room, as though distance could spare the delicate mechanism his wanting. [164]

Then her lecture began. [168] She had studied the fears of sailors who returned from shipwrecks unable to describe what pursued them ashore. [186] Beside the machine lay an atlas page with sea monsters, its coastlines almost rubbed away by anxious fingers. [204] Each creature occupied a place where a cartographer had lacked knowledge and supplied teeth. [218] "Do not tell them there is nothing there," her recorded voice instructed. [230] "Give the unknown a shape, and they can tell you its name." [242]

He spread the page beneath the telescope's cracked prism, where the low sun broke into wandering colors. [259] Through scattered light, the painted monsters rose onto the walls, large enough for everyone to see. [275] The passenger searching for her child pointed at a serpent swallowing its tail. [288] "That I will keep losing him," she whispered. [296] The reaper watched her terror crystallize into a tiny glass ring. [307] Once she could hold it, she could set it down. [317]

The silent man chose a whale carrying a city on its back. [329] "That everyone I failed is waiting for me." [337] A glass tower settled into his palm. [344] One by one, the passengers found words, and the observatory floor brightened with the things they no longer had to carry. [365]

But the youngest sailor had not spoken when the sun slipped behind the sea wall. [380] The monsters thinned into stains. [385] Below, the returning tide was calling them back to the wreck; by morning, they would remember only drowning. [403] Beside the prism stood an empty signal lamp. [411] He looked from its stiff wick to the thin wax disc turning beneath the machine's needle. [427] This was the last place where her voice still existed outside his unreliable memory. [441]

When the great flood came, she had stayed in this tower to keep its beacon burning for his crowded ferry. [461] He had carried her soul across on his last crossing that night, after the water reached the lamp. [479] He could no longer remember what she had said when she stepped aboard. [492] On the wall, the boy watched a fading monster with a human face. [505] "That nobody will be glad I came," he said. [514]

The reaper stopped the machine before it could promise him another small return. [527] He broke the disc into the lamp's reservoir and struck a spark. [539] For a moment, while the wax softened, he listened for her in the hiss. [553] There was only burning. [557] Then the prism scattered her extinguished voice into light. [566] It was not her sacrifice repeated, he thought, but its echo: something precious surrendered so a stranger could find the shore. [587]

The boy's fear crystallized into a glass face, its mouth open as if awaiting an answer. [603] The reaper knelt without touching him, his empty hands open between them. [615] "I am glad," he said. [620] The boy put the face down carefully. [627]

When they descended to the boat, the reaper left the little glass terrors in the observatory for the morning sun. [647] Perhaps someone living would recognize one and feel less alone. [657] He offered the boy his hand. [663] "Mind the step," he said, and pushed them gently from the shore. [675]
