The first rain in eighty days washed an amber brooch onto Mara's doorstep. [13] She was awake to hear it strike the stone, being an insomniac painter with little left to paint except darkness and its alterations. [36] Thirty years after the water wars, her tidal island still locked its cisterns at sundown. [51] The eastern reservoir overflowed into the lanes while western families queued for their allotted cup. [66]

Inside the amber hung a tiny fly, one wing folded as if preparing to sleep. [81] Mara knew the crooked clasp her mother had soldered, and the dark fleck beneath the fly. [97] She had last seen it pinned to her mother's coat the night the eastern reservoir emptied. [113] The council called that treason; her mother never came home to offer another word for it. [129] For years Mara had painted that night, trying to thresh meaning from experience by arranging a brighter moon above an increasingly empty shore. [152] Now something had come back that would not submit to composition. [163]

She found its source by following rain trails uphill, tracing reddish silt against the island's grey limestone. [180] Below the abandoned pumping house, runoff had torn the clay plug from a drainage tunnel. [195] She fetched Iven, the council's plumber, whose father had died defending that reservoir. [208] He brought timber props, a lamp, and the particular silence he always carried into her house. [224] "I would have asked someone less prejudiced," Mara told him, wryly earnest, "but nobody else knows how to keep a ceiling up." [246] "And nobody else wants an answer more," he said, shouldering a prop. [258]

They crawled through mud until their lamplight touched an unearthed convergence of four pipes, each thicker than Mara's waist. [277] Two came from the rival reservoirs; two descended toward the eastern and western civilian shelters. [292] Between them stood a switching wheel beneath a roof scarred by shellfire. [304] Iven scraped mineral crust from a slate ledger wired beside it. [315] Under dates from the final siege, deliveries alternated: east to west, west to east. [329] Each entry bore two initials, her mother's and his father's. [339] Mara recognized the hooked M; Iven touched the R he had learned to write by copying his father's tool labels. [359] The final entry, scratched in her mother's cramped hand, read: Children first, whichever side. [373] They had called it theft aboveground, but here, beneath the fortifications, it had been an exchange. [389]

Neither of them spoke for a while. [396] Water ticked from the broken ceiling into a basin green with copper, sounding extravagantly alive. [411] Mara wanted bones, a farewell, something that would close the long room of her mother's absence. [427] Instead she had a brooch and a set of instructions. [437] "We don't know whether they survived this place," Iven said. [447] "No," Mara answered, "but we know what they were doing." [457]

Aboveground, the council spent three days arguing over whose dead deserved correcting first. [470] Then Iven spread his measurements on their table: the reservoirs could share reserves again, with new seals and some patching. [490] Permission came without money, which the council considered a generous compromise. [501] Mara took the brooch across the causeway at low tide to a mainland dealer who bought amber by weight. [520] He offered enough for the seals, provided she included its silver setting. [532] She nearly refused; she had only just recovered the small thing her mother had warmed against her throat. [550] Then she imagined keeping it while the children queued, and opened her hand. [563]

For weeks, she painted the missing from photographs, accepting mortar and labour instead of payment. [578] She left their faces tired where the photographs showed tiredness, resisting the old urge to improve the light. [596] When the first shared water reached the western shelter, nobody cheered. [607] People stood with their cups lowered, staring at the stream as though it were an animal thought extinct. [625] Iven drank, then passed his cup to Mara. [633] It tasted of limestone and copper, not forgiveness. [641]

That night she painted the buried junction, each pipe entering from beyond the canvas, none claiming to be the source. [661] She left the figures at the wheel unfinished. [669] Toward dawn, with rain tapping the roof and the island breathing around its unlocked cisterns, she slept in her chair. [689]
