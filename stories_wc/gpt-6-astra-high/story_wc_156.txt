Mara Vale restored murals in vermilion boots, with brushes tucked into her curls like the quills of an irritated bird. [20] She was methodically playful: every pigment test had a joke for a name, every joke a number in her ledger. [40] But a missing face required proof of whose face belonged there. [51]

In the town's sound wave garden, proof was scarce. [60] Its curved walls carried both a flaking mural and whispers, passing voices between beds of silver grass. [77] Her father had designed the acoustics and painted the people, or so the plaque claimed. [92] The council wanted his original restored before demolition could be proposed as the cheaper option. [107] Mara had his signed drawing, money for one attempt, and doubts she couldn't enter on an invoice. [124] Since his death, getting his work right had seemed the only conversation she could still have with him. [142]

Beneath a painted mayor's cheek, her scalpel had exposed a woman's brown eye. [155] The drawing showed no woman. [160]

She fitted her father's hobo nickel into the bronze socket his maintenance notes called the listener. [176] Someone had carved its familiar portrait into a traveler whose eye contained a tiny doorway. [191] According to the notes, this apparatus could bewitch a listener through reflected sound, but only in the silence following an honest question. [213] Mara usually dismissed his extravagant claims, but today she wanted to exchange certainty for curiosity. [228]

Across the garden, her apprentice Kit lifted a plaster sample shaped to fill the hollow surrounding that unexpected eye. [247] A flake shook loose. [251] "Do we restore what he planned, or what we find?" he asked. [263]

The question skimmed the curved walls, passed over the nickel, and returned as the creak of a ladder. [281]

Mara woke on the scaffolding, cheek stuck to her father's drawing. [292] He was alive, sanding the wall where the woman's eye should have been. [305] "The sponsor preferred the mayor," he said, and offered her the scraper. [317] Relieved to have an answer, she reached for it, but its blade rang with Kit's question. [333]

She woke again, old now, on a bench in the completed garden. [345] Her restored mayor smiled above beds of dead grass. [354] A child called across the paths and received nothing back. [364] She had made every surface agree with the drawing, filling the shallow irregularities that had carried the voices. [382] On her lap lay a prize for historical fidelity, heavy as a paving stone. [396]

She tried to lift it and woke underneath scaffolding, a child again, with wet paint dripping around her. [414] She could remember dreaming the old woman, who had dreamed the scaffolding, each life folded inside the next. [432] A woman in paint-stiff overalls was carving a doorway into a nickel with a sharpened nail. [448] Her right eye was brown; her left was clouded. [457]

"Was it your face?" Mara asked. [463]

The woman turned the coin over, showing where someone had begun another face and stopped. [478] Mara wanted to call this the deepest awakening, the one that explained all the others. [493] Yet the woman might be memory, invention, or something the garden needed her to imagine. [508] The unfinished face waited without helping. [514]

Mara let it remain unfinished. [519]

She woke with the nickel beneath her thumb and Kit's breath still suspended across the garden. [535] A falling flake of plaster had not yet reached his sleeve. [546] Those nested awakenings had occupied the pause between question and reply. [557]

"We find out what we can, and leave room for what we can't," she said. [572]

She put down her trowel and asked Kit to help map the echoes before they touched another hollow. [590] They would stabilize the surviving paint, leave the brown eye visible, and distinguish their repairs from the original brushwork. [609] The council might refuse a mural that admitted its losses; she would need a better argument than her father's signature. [629] Mara entered the question of the woman's identity in her ledger under Unfinished Business, and left room for evidence. [648]

Across the garden, Kit repeated her first test phrase, and his voice returned by a route she had never noticed. [668]
