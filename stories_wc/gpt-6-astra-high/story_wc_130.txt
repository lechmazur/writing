After the orchard grew silent beneath the moon, Silas uncovered the instrument he had hidden from his customers for seven years. [21] Above him, dripping stalactites nourished pear trees rooted in the cavern floor; moonlight entered through a fissure like a blade left in a wound. [45] By day the leaves hummed with remembered voices, which was why readings could only be trusted after silence settled. [64]

A secretive conjurer, Silas sold small mercies: a kettle that remembered a grandmother's whistle, buttons that warmed when someone spoke your name. [86] His daughter Ada had called these things gently inventive, and the larger experiment arrogant. [100] He had wanted to understand the mathematics of emotion, then use longing to fold the distance between separated people. [119] Ada had nevertheless volunteered to cross his first bridge, taking three steps between two pear trees before there was no Ada anywhere. [141] There had been no body, no farewell, and nothing he could honestly call a sign. [156]

At first he told customers she was traveling; later, he let them mistake his silence for bereavement. [173] Neither story required him to admit that he still set out her cup, then hid it whenever anybody knocked. [192]

Tonight he brought a taxidermist's glass eye pair, bought from the shop where Ada had once repaired a bedraggled fox. [212] She had refused to straighten its crooked ears because, she said, dignity did not depend on symmetry. [229] Taxidermists used glass that could hold a gaze without holding a soul, the only kind his orchard would not deceive with comforting visions. [252] He scraped away their painted irises and set them in the wooden brackets he had carved. [268]

To hold their angles steady, he had to dovetail the brackets together, a patient task that left no room for trembling. [289] Beneath them lay the mosaic stepping stone where Ada had stood before her crossing. [303] Its chipped patterns recorded the bridge's stresses, each fractured tile preserving the direction of a force that had otherwise vanished. [323] Through the paired lenses, those fractures would show him where the bridge had led, rather than where he wished it had led. [345] That distinction had cost him seven years. [352]

When the lenses aligned, a pale passage rose between the trees. [363] Silas tightened the screw that represented longing, expecting the passage to shorten as his love increased. [379] Instead it split, and a pear tree cracked from root to crown. [391] He reached for more power, then saw a second, fainter line within the fractured tiles. [406] His original equation had accounted for only one person's longing. [416] He had treated Ada's destination as whatever he wanted it to be. [428]

But she had wanted cities, salt water, a room whose walls were not crowded with her father's unfinished wonders. [447] Perhaps she had reached one; perhaps the bridge had broken before she reached anything. [461] The stone preserved forces, not a heartbeat. [468] Even perfect measurements could not turn what remained unknown into zero. [479]

The split widened while he watched, pulling pale roots from the soil. [491] If he forced his answer, the orchard would collapse, taking the last trace of her crossing with it. [509] Silas loosened the screw. [513] Then, with a sliver shaved from his own bracket, he gave the second lens room to move. [530] He could not supply Ada's missing value, but he could stop setting it equal to his. [546] The passage settled into a narrow, unlit arch, and the roots sank back beneath the ground. [562]

Nothing came through. [565] Nothing promised to. [568] Yet the arch remained when he stepped away, no longer held open by the violence of his wishing. [586] He built a low railing around it, leaving a gap wide enough for someone carrying a suitcase. [603]

Before dawn he climbed to the village and accepted the baker's standing invitation to breakfast. [618] When she asked about Ada, he told her the truth he had never managed before. [633] "I don't know whether she can come home." [641] The baker moved a basket to make room for his elbows and asked whether he would like another slice. [660] Beneath them, water kept falling into the wounded orchard, where the darkness around the roots was still good soil. [679]
