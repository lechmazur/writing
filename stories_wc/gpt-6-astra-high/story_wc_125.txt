After the energy beings arrived, governments learned to imprison people inside unfinished moments, which cost less than feeding them. [19] Mara, a drifting roadside poet, wrote obituaries for living conscripts, smuggling them beyond the new census of available years. [38] She considered it an ethically playful form of bookkeeping; the magistrate who arrested her and her lover, Iven, disagreed. [57]

They escaped his ordinary cells with a frayed coil of rope, but the outer wall was guarded by borrowed time. [77] Iven pushed her clear as the wardens arrested the minute around him; she landed outside with the severed rope. [96] She had until midsummer, when the state burned its condemned moments. [107]

For four months she traded poems for bread and followed rumors of a being that had refused government employment. [126] "I need to discover the texture of time," she told innkeepers, who offered clocks until they saw her face. [145] If time had a grain, perhaps she could work against it, the way a thumbnail could loosen a knot. [164]

On midsummer eve, she found the terra-cotta grotto under starlight, beneath a hillside once hollowed out and fired by potters. [184] Its collapsed chimney admitted a narrow heaven; its walls still bore the ridges of hands that had shaped wet clay. [204] In those ridges, light moved against the turning stars. [213]

"Show me what held you together," said the being. [222]

Mara offered the rope. [226]

It taught her by uncovering layers: prison lime beneath road dust, blood beneath lime, then fibers packed around a strand of darkness. [248] When she touched that strand, Iven inhaled somewhere too close to be an echo. [262] Following the moving light, she began to pattern the rope across the floor, matching its loops to the grooves in the clay. [284] Each crossing opened another layer: their first quarrel, a winter blanket, Iven grinding ink for her fraudulent obituaries. [302] Time felt neither smooth nor flowing, but felted from what people had done for one another. [318]

At the final crossing he appeared above the rope, one foot still seeking the ground outside the prison. [336] His eyes found hers, and the relief in them almost undid her. [348]

The being showed her the knot the wardens had made, and the thread of shared years running through it. [367] She could draw him out along that thread, but only by stripping its memories from her own mind. [385] He would keep their life together; she would know it only as something that had happened to somebody else. [404] Or she could leave him here and remember everything. [413]

Through the chimney, the stars were beginning to pale. [422]

Mara reached for her notebook, already devising a contraband autobiography. [432]

"Words will remain," the being said, "but they will not give you back the feeling of having lived them." [451]

She had spent her life believing a sufficiently precise sentence could carry anything across a border. [467] Now she could describe his hands perfectly, and still become a woman who would flinch if they reached for her. [487]

"Do not buy me with yourself," Iven said. [495]

Even suspended in a stolen minute, he had understood her hesitation before she did. [509] She wanted to save that understanding, too, which was precisely what she could not keep. [524]

"You helped me free strangers," she said. [531] "Will you let a stranger free you?" [538]

He closed his eyes, then opened his hand. [546]

Mara wrote one instruction for the woman who would inherit her notebook: "Neither of you owes the other love." [565] Then she took the dark strand between her teeth and pulled, feeding the rope through the pattern with both hands. [585] The blanket went first, then the quarrel, then the sound of a pestle turning ink while she searched for a rhyme. [606] She held on until she no longer knew why she was weeping. [618]

At sunrise, a man dropped to his knees on the clay floor. [630] He started toward her, saw her recoil, and stopped. [639] Beside her lay a notebook in her handwriting and a rope worn almost to lint. [654] She read the instruction twice before asking his name. [663]

"Iven," he said, as though entrusting her with something breakable. [673]

Outside, the road was warming, and she had no poem with which to buy breakfast. [688] She asked if he knew where they might find work. [698] He waited while she gathered the frayed fibers, though she could not have explained why she refused to leave them behind. [719]
