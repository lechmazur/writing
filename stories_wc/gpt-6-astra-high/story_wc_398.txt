After the final bus departed, leaving the station vacant, Abel led Mara uphill to the vineyard's amber pavilion. [18] Autumn leaves scraped its colored glass, and his lantern made their shadows look like hands trying to enter. [36] For eleven years he had been the lone vineyard sentinel, paid to stay when everyone else went home. [54] Tomorrow the new owners would dismantle the pavilion, and his last duty was to pack its records for the village archive. [75]

He had asked Mara to help with the page about the two of them. [89] They had built the nursery here before she became an interpreter and he became a man who disliked explanations. [108]

"Not an apology you have to accept," he said, laying two clean sheets on the table. [124] "Just something neither of us has to disappear from." [133]

His voice was gently persuasive, but she kept her coat on. [144] Between the sheets he placed the burned edge of a love letter, saved inside a grafting ledger for thirty years. [164] Four words remained: "I cannot live here." [171]

Mara touched the paper without lifting it. [178]

"You kept that much." [182]

"I made that much the whole letter." [189]

He had meant to begin with the pavilion's construction date, not this admission. [202] Outside, a loose wire tapped against a trellis, counting seconds neither of them could afford to waste again. [220] He had once called them "destined mismatches," as if heaven, not his own hands, had put the paper in the stove. [241]

"You wanted every border open, and I wanted one place to remain mine," he said. [256]

She looked past him at the old demonstration vine climbing the pavilion's central post. [270] Its swollen graft was level with her shoulder. [278]

"You taught me that the fruiting wood would die in this soil without different roots beneath it." [295]

Abel almost smiled; drawing parallels between vines and people had been their first shared language. [310] Then she pressed her thumb against the scar. [318]

"But you cannot ask the roots to become branches, either." [328]

He remembered her scholarship, the foreign letters arriving weekly, his own silence whenever she spread a map beside their supper. [348] He had called that silence patience. [354] She had heard a door being locked. [361]

"I was frightened you would return and find me small," he said. [373]

"I wanted to return without having to pretend the world was small," she answered. [387]

He reached for the letter edge, then stopped, letting her decide whether it belonged in the record. [404]

"What came after those words was not a demand that you leave," she said. [418] "It was a demand that you let me." [426]

He told her about the winter his father sold their furniture to pay rent, a story he had always mistaken for shame. [448] He had wanted a home that could not be taken away, and had tried to make her part of its walls. [469]

Mara unbuttoned her coat. [473]

"I never asked what staying meant to you," she said, looking at the ledger filled with his experiments. [491]

None of this gave them back their years. [499] Listening, he began to rediscover the young woman who had once brought him unfamiliar seeds, rather than the deserter he had rehearsed remembering. [522]

Mara pulled out a chair. [527]

"We should write two accounts," she said. [534]

"And put them beside each other," he answered. [542]

They worked until the lantern needed trimming, sometimes disputing a date, once laughing over a graft they had both insisted would take. [564] It had failed spectacularly, producing nothing but a furious thicket of rootstock shoots. [577] Neither crossed that failure out. [582]

At the bottom of his page, Abel wrote that she had left him; beneath it, at her request, he added that he had made staying impossible. [608] She wrote that she had wanted freedom, then added that she had wanted him to ask when she would return. [628]

Mara would take the morning bus, and the pavilion would still come down. [641] They slipped the scorched fragment into a clear sleeve between their accounts, where its four surviving words could no longer masquerade as everything. [664]
