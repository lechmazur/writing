When the drought uncovered the abandoned circus train, Elias brought the halo fragment he had kept beneath his pillow for thirty years. [22] It was warm, although the angel it belonged to was supposed to be dead. [36] Around him, the exposed wheels stood in salt, and wind bells hung from the conductor's window like small, green teeth. [56] He had made those bells from scraps of the angel's first cage. [68]

Back then, he had believed the ringmaster when she said that beautiful things needed protection. [83] A silent carousel carpenter, born without a voice, Elias had learned early how easily other people supplied your meanings. [102] For years he had accepted theirs. [108] Then he discovered why the circus gardens flourished while the towns behind them withered. [122] The angel's withheld song became rain, bottled and sold at every stop. [134] When he tried to open her cage, the ringmaster broke her halo and threw him from the moving train. [153] He caught one fragment before he fell. [160]

Now the bells stirred, and the fragment pulled toward their first bright note. [173] Elias climbed aboard. [176] Outside the window, a falling grain of sand stopped falling. [186] He had stepped inside the chime of wind bells, into the long, unfinished instant in which the circus had hidden its theft. [208]

The passage smelled of wet velvet. [214] A misting hush breathed through the keyholes, beading his eyelashes, swallowing the scrape of his boots before it could become sound. [235] He recognized the carousel carriage by its painted swallows and turned left toward what he remembered as the central shaft. [255] But the carriage had overturned during its burial, and he was walking along its ceiling. [270] His wrong turn took him through a maintenance hatch into a hidden oasis. [283]

Palms crowded the baggage racks, their roots drinking from a pool where the carousel's machinery should have been. [301] Under their leaves sat the angel, no larger than a child, with the remaining arc of her halo around her throat. [322] Each breath loosened another silver thread of mist. [330] She pointed at the pool, then at his empty water flask. [341] Even now, she thought visitors came only to take. [350]

Elias knelt and laid the fragment beside her hand. [359] Her eyes closed, and the palms began to fade. [368] Beyond the windows, the suspended sand trembled. [375] The chime was thinning. [379]

"Too late," she whispered, using the little breath that might have kept her garden green. [394]

He shook his head. [398] All his life, people had mistaken his silence for an empty room; he had survived by imagining what might yet be built there. [421] He would not mistake hers for a grave. [429]

Quietly persistent, he examined the broken halo until he found two small holes concealed beneath its tarnish. [446] He knew those holes. [450] The ringmaster had ordered him to fit the same locking pins into every carousel horse, so none could leave its appointed circle. [472] Joining the halo would only close the circle; he needed to dismantle what the circus had made of it. [491]

He braced the crescent with one hand and worked the fragment's thin edge beneath the first pin. [508] His old fingers slipped. [512] He tried again, more gently, remembering that swollen wood surrendered to patience rather than strength. [527] One pin lifted, then the other. [533] The halo opened in his hands. [539]

The angel touched her bare throat and looked at him with the terror of someone offered an unfamiliar tool. [558] Elias opened his own mouth, knowing nothing would come, and showed her how to begin. [573]

She tried to sing. [577] The first note scarcely disturbed the pool. [584] The second carried a whispering gleam along the water, and every palm bent toward it. [599] At the third, the windows burst outward without a sound. [609] The oasis unstitched itself into rain. [615]

Elias found himself kneeling in salt beneath an open sky, the angel rising through the roofs of the abandoned carriages. [635] The wind bells finished their single chime. [642] For the first time in thirty years, the fragment in his hand was only cold metal. [658] He could have kept it as proof, but there were other uses for a thing that no longer had to be a cage. [681] He hung it among the bells. [687] Then he turned toward home, carrying his empty flask with its mouth open. [700]
