In the marble cavern beneath Bellweather, every sound remained inside the walls until sunlight touched the place where it had ended. [21] Each autumn, a clockwork shutter swept a sunbeam over the stone, releasing a harvest of echoes into marble-lined jars. [40] Through winter, families uncorked them when darkness made their houses feel too small. [53]

Then a flood tore through the cavern, smashed the shutter's timer, and left the town's orchards buried in gravel. [72] Across the lull of a wounded season, the locksmith Mara went underground to discover what could still be saved. [91] She had repaired every lock in Bellweather, often wondering why people entrusted their treasures to mechanisms they never understood. [110]

Her own treasure was among the echoes: her daughter's first public song, performed at the spring lantern festival. [128] Lately, seventeen-year-old Nell sang only when she thought her mother was elsewhere. [140] Mara meant to preserve that earlier voice, to store wonder in the cellar of reason, for later. [157] Later was when repairs were finished, debts were paid, and she could listen without measuring what remained to do. [176]

She carried the broken timer mechanism home each evening and tried to reverse-engineer its missing teeth from the surviving gears. [196] Every reconstruction made the shutter open too soon or linger dangerously on one patch of wall. [212] Nell, who brought her lunch, finally asked why she kept trusting a machine the flood had already proved wrong. [231] Mara almost answered that clocks were more dependable than daughters, then disliked herself enough to put down her tools. [250]

The flood had shifted the great mirror at the entrance; the old timing could never match the new light. [269] She began again by studying reflection angles at different times, marking the traveling beam with charcoal instead of moving anything. [289] Lock work had made her peripherally focused: she watched the margins, where a bolt's faint movement betrayed a hidden obstruction. [309] Now, while looking beside the beam rather than into it, she noticed a second, trembling stripe. [325] A buckle in the mirror divided the sunlight, sending its weaker reflection toward the cavern's lowest wall. [342] There, under the flood's brown watermark, the marble began to hum. [353]

Mara understood why the timer had contained a heavy governor in addition to its clock spring. [369] It controlled not merely which echoes emerged, but how quickly their pressure escaped. [382] The flood's roar was stored below the songs, and the fractured stone shivered even under that thin accidental light. [401] Concentrating the whole beam there would release the roar at once, bursting the weakened wall beneath the town. [419]

She could retime the shutter to skim the upper chambers, but every jar she filled would postpone draining the dangerous lower reservoir. [441] Frost was already whitening the entrance each morning; once ice split the wall, daylight would enter beyond the shutter's reach. [461] The safe solution was to open the broad spillway, spread sunlight across the flooded chambers, and let everything escape gradually. [481] But opening it would carry the songs beyond the collecting jars, out over the abandoned orchards. [497]

Mara stood with her hand on the spillway lock until Nell quietly placed the lunch basket beside her. [515] "I wanted something of yours that I could keep," Mara said, ashamed of how much it sounded like an accusation. [535] Nell studied the empty jars before answering: "You could ask me to sing." [548] Mara had assumed that offer had expired somewhere between childhood and the first closed bedroom door. [564]

She dismantled her new timer, fitted a slower gear train, and coupled its governor to the spillway's broad gate. [583] At dawn, mother and daughter watched the first broad ribbon of light touch the watermarked marble. [599] The flood emerged as a low, exhausted sigh, carrying fiddle tunes, forgotten conversations, and the shy, determined voice of her daughter. [620] Above them, people came out among the ruined trees and stopped clearing gravel to listen. [635]

When the small recorded voice faltered, Nell supplied the missing words, then changed the melody into something Mara had never heard. [656] Mara's hands twitched toward her notebook, where she had carefully preserved every angle and interval needed to make the harvest safe again. [678] She left it closed; the measurements would keep until tomorrow, but this song was being made only now. [696]
