After the past life memory surfaced, Mara stopped tracing the city's towers and began drawing the boy who stood beneath them. [21] He appeared whenever she entered her camera obscura: red coat, open mouth, a railway burning overhead. [37] She remembered loving him, then turning away. [44] That last movement woke her choking. [50]

A camera obscura artist earned her living by making light behave, and Mara distrusted visions that arrived without permission. [69] Yet she was spiritually curious enough to investigate rather than drink herself blank. [82] She wanted to find where unused love was stored: the love that had filled that other woman's body and never reached the child. [105] If it survived death, perhaps she could still deliver it. [115]

The visions had begun after she fitted her traveling camera with a teardrop lens salvaged from a demolished temple. [134] Its wooden case named it Kwan Yin's Tear, after the goddess who heard the world's cries. [150] Held close, it collected her breath in a silver mist; held farther away, it showed only the ordinary, inverted street. [170] But through its fogged surface, sparks glimmered along cables that had carried no electricity for sixty years. [187]

For nine evenings she followed them, a pilgrim of sparks, pushing her wheeled darkroom through streets the city had forgotten. [207] Each broken junction demanded a slower breath before the next ember appeared. [219] Hurry darkened the glass. [223] Patience revealed the way. [227]

On the tenth evening she reached the twilight viaduct in bloom. [238] Wisteria draped its scorched arches, and foxgloves rose between tracks silvered by the gathering fog. [253] An old man was watering seedlings from a dented railway kettle. [264] A strip of red wool tied his cane to his wrist. [275]

"Jun," she said, before she could decide whether saying it was cruel. [287] He set the kettle down. [292] "Nobody calls me that now." [297]

She showed him her drawings, not her certainty. [305] He touched the red coat with a finger thickened by arthritis, then pointed toward a rusted signal cabinet. [323]

"My mother ran that way when the wires fell." [332] "I waited here until they took me away." [340]

His voice held neither accusation nor forgiveness, only something worn smooth by repetition. [353]

Mara offered him the darkroom's chair. [359] "There may be more to see." [365] "I am not asking you to believe anything." [373]

Inside, the lens threw the viaduct upside down across her paper screen. [385] Her quick, shallow breathing brought back the woman turning away, endlessly leaving. [397] Jun gripped the chair. [401] Mara nearly covered the lens; she had brought him here to heal an old wound, not make it bleed again. [421]

Then she remembered the broken junctions. [427] The image was revealing itself through breathing changes, and terror had trapped her in its narrowest instant. [444] She lengthened her exhalation until the glass clouded completely, then let the next breath arrive without dragging it in. [463]

The woman crossed the tracks. [468] Not away from the boy, but toward the cabinet, where a live cable lashed the iron door. [485] She tore it open and pulled the emergency switch. [494] The wire beside the child's boots went dark as white fire swallowed her hands. [508] This time Mara remembered the rest: not abandoning him, but trusting him to stay still long enough to live. [527]

Jun leaned forward, his breath making a second small cloud beside hers. [539] On the screen, the woman looked back. [546] Her mouth formed his name. [551] He answered so softly that Mara heard only the shape of the word. [564]

Outside, he sat among the flowers until the first windows brightened beyond the fog. [578] He did not embrace her. [583] When she offered to return, he said that a picture, even a miraculous one, could not give him back his childhood. [604] She agreed, although some hungry part of her had wanted him to call her Mother. [619]

"But you can come on Thursday," he said. [627] "These roots need dividing, and my hands are bad." [636]

Mara lifted the kettle. [640] She had imagined unused love waiting somewhere intact, a treasure she could claim and hand over. [656] But the love she remembered had reached him; his life was where it had been stored. [672] What remained unused was hers, and Thursday was a place to put it. [685] She watered while he pointed out which seedlings were weeds and which only looked like them. [701] Inside the darkroom, Kwan Yin's Tear cleared, and the last spark became an ordinary star. [716]
