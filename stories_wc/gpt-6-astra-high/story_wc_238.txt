Before dawn, while the orchard waited for the saws, my sister unlocked the sheet music archive and let me in. [20] She had not spoken to me in eleven years, except yesterday, when she telephoned to say the railway crews would arrive at six. [43] "Bring your tricks, June," she had said, "if you still want to apologize." [56]

My tricks paid well in the city, where I worked as a gossip-column exorcist, persuading troublesome ghosts to depart in exchange for corrections printed beside the society weddings. [84] A dead mistress generally wanted her name restored; a dead industrialist usually wanted it omitted. [99] I advised their survivors through metaphors because plain language made wealthy people defensive. [112] "Grief is a blocked chimney," I would tell them, brushing grave soot off my cuffs. [127] "You must let the smoke go somewhere." [134]

My sister had put an antique perfume bottle on the archive's sorting table, among the copper plates from which our mother's orchard cantatas had been printed. [160] Its silver cage held glass the color of dirty ice. [170] Mother used to distill fallen pear blossoms into perfume, selling it after concerts to keep the orchard choir solvent. [189] The bottle still smelled faintly sweet beneath the archive's damp plaster and mouse droppings. [203]

I had made Mother famous for selling the orchard to the railway, calling her a shepherd who auctioned her flock. [223] I had not mentioned that the money settled my gambling debts. [234] For eleven years I had known what my column cost her, and for nine she had been beyond ordinary apologies. [254] My sister preserved her music here, in the packing shed the railway had not yet needed. [270]

"Will she come?" my sister asked. [276] "The trees are an aerial," I said. [283] "When they cut the trees, there will be nothing left to receive." [295] She looked toward the window, where white survey ribbons hung motionless in the branches. [309] "I asked about Mother, not the equipment." [316]

I unpacked my crank, magnets, and insulated wire without answering. [326] Exorcism worked through induced currents: a turning magnet woke an answering charge in old metal, allowing memories trapped in separate objects to reconnect. [349] Usually I broke that circuit once a ghost had heard its correction. [361] This morning I needed to hold one open, using Mother's printing plates and the silver around the bottle. [379] The orchard would supply what neither object retained alone: the shape of the place where she had been happiest. [398]

My sister turned the crank while I laid my palms against the cold copper. [412] Notes lifted from the plates like iron filings, gathering above the bottle into a woman with transparent elbows and black soil packed beneath her nails. [437] Her mouth opened, but only a thin chord emerged. [446] I had warned hundreds of clients about imperfect miracles, yet I nearly demanded a better one. [462]

"Mother, I have carried this stone," I began. [470] My sister stopped cranking. [474] The apparition buckled, and a pear blossom fell through the table. [485] "No riddles," she said. [489] "Not to her." [492] I gripped a plate until its edge cut into my palm. [503] "I lied about you because a scandal would sell, and because I was ashamed to admit you had rescued me." [523] "I knew people would believe me." [529] "I let them." [532]

My sister resumed turning, slowly. [537] Mother's face gathered again, though one eye remained a hole through which I could see the catalog drawers. [555] She reached toward my bleeding hand and missed by several inches. [566] Then the broken chord changed into the little descending phrase she used when teaching frightened children to find their voices. [586] It might have meant forgiveness, or merely been what the copper remembered. [598] I did not claim to know. [604]

Outside, a chainsaw coughed awake. [609] My sister kept turning until the first tree fell and Mother scattered into the pale dust above the plates. [628] When the handle finally stopped, I loosened her cramped fingers from it and held her hand instead. [645] On archive stationery, I wrote a correction for tomorrow's column. [655] It named the debt, the liar, and the woman who had paid. [667]
