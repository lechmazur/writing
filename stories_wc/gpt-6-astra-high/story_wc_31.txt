At dusk, Nella, the late-shift pier lamplighter, stole her daughter's hotel skeleton key tag from the kitchen drawer. [18] The brass oval was all Ada had kept from the floating hotel that sank when its boiler burst. [36] The explosion had taken her hearing, but not her appetite for arguments, engineering, or burnt toast. [52] Nella missed most of those arguments now because she had never learned enough of Ada's signs to lose one. [71] Since the wreck, Nella had polished the lamp that guided Ada ashore, as though keeping it bright could save her twice. [92]

She told herself she worked nights to pay their rent, which was true. [105] She hid behind truth as other people hid behind locked doors. [116] Tonight, during the sky lantern release, she intended to discover the color of sound and bring Ada something better than excuses. [137] An old inscription on the tag promised a library where everything heard could also be seen. [153]

Ada had explained the entrance years ago, when they still talked easily. [165] Once each summer, lanterns bearing written promises rose over the harbor, and their reflections became luminous vows below. [183] Those lights reopened the forgotten library inside the sunken ship until the last lantern burned out. [199] Nella had always dismissed the story as a hotel advertisement. [209] Now she finished lighting the pier, lowered its maintenance ladder, and stepped onto a staircase made of reflected fire. [228]

Above her, lanterns ascended in a meteor hush. [236] Below, the sea held itself apart around the hotel's broken entrance, trembling like a breath someone dared not release. [255] Nella pressed the key tag against the reception desk. [264] Its stamped word, MASTER, shone, and a door behind the desk swung open. [277] Beyond it, dry books stirred on shelves tilted at the angle of the wreck. [291]

The catalogue instructed visitors to proceed not alphabetically, but according to whatever they could not bear to ask. [309] Nella found her way by wandering purposefully toward the shelves that reminded her of Ada. [323] Engineering led to Arguments, Arguments to Silence, and Silence to Things Mistaken for Silence. [337] There she discovered a slim volume entitled The Color of Sound. [348]

When she opened it, the creak of its binding unfurled as a copper ribbon. [362] Her breathing made pale green rings, while distant waves rolled through the pages in violet folds. [378] Ada could see birdsong, kettle whistles, even the ridiculous song Nella sang while mending socks. [393] For a moment, Nella imagined presenting the book and being forgiven without having to ask. [408]

A borrowing slip slid from the cover. [415] Its terms required one luminous vow, spoken honestly and kept until the book was returned. [430] Nella cupped her hands and promised to restore everything her daughter had lost. [443] Nothing appeared. [445] She tried again, promising that Ada would never feel lonely. [455] The sea pressed against the windows, and somewhere above, a lantern went dark. [468]

Neither promise was hers to keep, and she knew it. [478] What did a miraculous book signify if she used it to avoid the living woman waiting in her kitchen? [497] She remembered Ada's hands flying through a story last week, then dropping when Nella turned toward the clock. [515] Her shift had been starting, another perfectly serviceable truth. [524]

"I will learn her language," Nella said, and a blue spark opened between her palms. [539] It was small, with none of the splendor she had expected of redemption. [552] She nearly added that she would practice every day, then stopped bargaining with an imaginary judge. [568] "Tomorrow I will ask her how she wants me to begin." [579] The spark steadied, and the borrowing slip folded itself around it. [590]

Nella climbed the fading stairs with the book inside her coat and the tag clenched between her teeth. [608] At home, Ada stood beside two plates of cold toast. [618] Nella set down the stolen tag first, then signed the apology she already knew. [632] Ada watched her hands rather than the book. [640] When Nella stumbled through her question, Ada corrected one finger, waited, and let her try again. [656] Only afterward did they open the pages together. [664] Ada laughed at the copper curl of the binding, a laugh that spread across the paper like sunrise. [682] Nella looked up in time to see what her daughter said. [693]
