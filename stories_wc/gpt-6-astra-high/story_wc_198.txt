Each morning, as dewdrops form, Mara carries her father's saxophone down the steps into the radiant grotto in mist. [19] The town calls her its morning fog saxophonist, a pretty title for work nobody comes to watch. [36] At the cavern's center stands a prism atrium in mist, its glass ribs glowing with light stolen from yesterday. [55] Beneath that borrowed radiance, old flood murals remain quietly vivid: red boats, yellow roofs, children waiting on a bridge. [74]

Her father played here until his hands stiffened, keeping the atrium asleep with the same lullaby he once played for her. [95] Then he passed her the instrument and his gray waistcoat, the uniform of an office called the vested hush. [114] He explained the fingerings twice, but did not ask after his grandson. [126] Since the great flood, he has regarded Mara with the courteous bewilderment reserved for strangers who claim old debts. [145] The doctors call it damage; Mara has spent twenty years calling it abandonment. [158]

Today, halfway through the lullaby, the atrium begins to whisper in the pauses between her notes. [174] "Nine buttons, one thread, water over the needle," it says in her father's younger voice. [189] It is the counting game he taught her on the school causeway: buttons meant children, thread meant bridge. [207] The needle was the church spire below the reservoir's highest mark. [218] Nine children will cross that causeway at sunrise, including Ivo, whose red coat she buttoned an hour ago. [236]

She reaches for the warning bell, then sees water climbing the steps faster than any child could run. [254] There is one instruction her father insisted she memorize, although he could no longer tell her why. [271] "To hush a prophecy spoken in code, answer through negative space; let every memory of your beloved fill the rests." [291] Beneath it, in his old scorebook, he had written a name: Mara. [303] For years she thought it was a dedication. [311]

Now she sees her father after the flood, sitting beside her hospital bed, asking a nurse whose little girl she is. [332] Not abandonment, then, but the price of bringing her back alive. [343]

The glass speaks again, and the water reaches her ankles. [353] Mara tries to summon something expendable: Ivo's muddy boots, his sulks, the winter he coughed until she hated the sound. [373] But beneath each irritation waits the same unbearable fact: he is here, he is hers, he is alive. [391] She cannot choose which pieces love will leave behind. [400]

She lifts the saxophone and plays the lullaby, withholding every note that should fall beneath the words my little boy. [420] Into those shaped absences she puts his first tooth, his frightened grip, the warm weight of him asleep against her. [440] The prism drinks without a sound. [446] She forgets the tooth before the next breath, then the fever, then the reason the red coat matters. [464]

When the final pause arrives, terror nearly makes her fill it with a scream. [478] Instead she holds the emptiness open, as her father once held a hospital door for a child he didn't recognize. [498] The water stops against her knees, shivers, and drains away between the flagstones. [511] Above her, nine pairs of feet finish crossing the causeway. [521] Mara counts them carefully, though she cannot explain why the smallest footsteps make her want to weep. [538]

At noon, a boy in a red coat finds her sitting outside the grotto, holding an unplayed saxophone. [556] "You forgot to meet me," he says, and then, seeing her face, stops smiling. [570] She knows what the glass has taken, but knowing cannot put recognition into the shape of his mouth. [588] Still, she can see that he is frightened. [596]

"Tell me your name," she says, kneeling so he will not mistake her bewilderment for turning away. [613] He tells her, and she repeats it until he lets her take his hand. [627] They walk to her father's house, where the old man opens the door with his usual careful, terrible politeness. [646] For the first time, Mara understands how much courage it takes him to invite her inside. [662]

"Will you teach us that song again," she asks, "from the beginning?" [674] He sets three cups on the table, although one is still too large for the boy's hands. [691] Outside, the mist thins over a bridge nobody will think to thank them for. [705] Inside, he leaves room beside him on the bench. [714]
