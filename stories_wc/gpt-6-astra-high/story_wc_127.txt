At dusk, Leda carried a bottle of counterfeit acceptance into the amber grotto where her mother was becoming permanent. [19] The town called this preservation, which was accurate enough to qualify as one of its more successful half truths. [38] Inside the translucent walls, the preserved advised their descendants on marriages, drainage, and the proper storage of pears. [56] They never said they were lonely. [62] That omission had always struck Leda as excellent customer service. [72]

She dealt in emotions stripped of inconvenient companions: courage without doubt, forgiveness without remembered injury, love without the possibility of loss. [93] Customers called them counterfeits because the feelings were real but the promises were not. [107] Professionally thorough, she had tested tonight's acceptance on herself and verified its absence of terror. [122] She had also checked her mother's consent three times. [131]

"Still yes," her mother said from the resin chair, before Leda could ask again. [145] Amber had reached her collarbones; her hands rested inside it like specimens of useful, extinct animals. [161] She had been a fragrance chemist, and those hands had once distinguished twenty kinds of rain. [177] Now she sometimes forgot what water was. [184]

Preservation would arrest the forgetting, although the grotto would subsume her remaining memories into its communal intelligence. [201] "I would rather become a committee than an empty room," she had explained. [214] Leda respected the distinction without finding it particularly comforting. [223]

She unpacked her instruments, including a fragrance chemist's blotter petal, saved from the last scent trial her mother had completed. [243] Its paper still held bitter orange and the faint, medicinal sweetness of her mother's laboratory. [258] A charged blotter could detect emotional currents too slight for fingertips, a refinement her mother had taught her. [276] Leda rubbed it against her sleeve and held it above the amber. [288] Sensing through static electricity was her trade's oldest trick: joy attracted the tip, grief repelled it, fear made it tremble. [308]

The petal trembled so violently that she nearly dropped it. [318]

"The bottle will help," Leda said. [324]

"Help whom, darling?" [327] Her mother nodded toward the walls. [333] "They asked me not to bring anything distressing." [341]

From the roof, a hundred patient voices murmured that distress complicated integration. [353] Outside, the last sunlight was leaving the harbor; when it left the entrance, the amber would seal. [370]

Leda checked the bottle again, then the wall, because thoroughness was what she did when tenderness offered no instructions. [389] Against the older amber, the petal pulled steadily toward joy, never trembling or turning away. [404] The grotto had not conquered loneliness; it had learned to exclude whatever made loneliness intelligible. [419] Its advice was sensible, polished, and addressed to people who did not exist. [432]

She had sold that same bargain for years, and here was its satisfied clientele. [446] She wanted, suddenly and specifically, to understand her mother without improving her. [458] Reading fear was insight; allowing it to matter might be something nearer empathy. [471]

With her mother's permission, she laid the petal against the small patch of skin beneath one ear. [488] Then she touched its other edge to her own throat, completing the circuit. [501] The orange scent rose, and with it came terror, not of death but of being remembered as someone agreeable. [520] Beneath that was love, impatient and exacting, unwilling to become a pleasant answer dispensed from a wall. [537]

Leda wept a little, with the faint annoyance of a tradeswoman discovering her best instrument had another setting. [555]

"Keep it," she told her mother, and poured the acceptance onto the sand. [568]

The voices objected, courteously at first. [574] The joining seam began to cloud, shutting her mother out. [584] She pressed the petal flat across the joining seam, giving the forbidden current a path into the older amber. [603] For a moment the grotto flickered like a household trying to decide whether to open its door. [620] Leda held the circuit steady while her mother's fear entered with everything it was protecting. [635]

As the transformation completed, the final light withdrew, and the blotter petal disappeared inside the seam. [651] The walls were quiet for so long that Leda began gathering her instruments. [664]

Then her mother's voice said, not quite alone, "I am glad you stayed, and I wish you could stay longer." [684]

It was an inconvenient thing to hear, and entirely worth the journey. [696]
