After tomorrow disappeared, mornings still came to the tidal atrium, but nobody could promise who would wake inside them. [19] The calendars kept yesterday and today; beyond those squares lay a whiteness as calm as snow. [35] Without a future to grow into, lives flowed sideways. [44] Through the atrium's open sea gate, identities began arriving with the tide, settling into sleepers and leaving their former owners strangely light. [66] "Their restlessness fluctuates with moon phases," the titan-gear librarian wrote, although she disliked how little comfort an accurate sentence offered. [86]

Her name was Serein, and each turn of the enormous brass wheel inside her chest advanced the library's catalogue by one entry. [108] For two centuries, she had matched one person to one life, fastening each life book shut with an iron clasp. [128] She opened the library every morning to preserve wonder in routine: even people certain of themselves deserved to discover something unexpected. [149] Now certainty itself had become the rarest book. [157]

At low tide, a girl named Mara brought a fisherman's bamboo creel to Serein's desk. [172] Water slipped between its ribs, leaving black iron sand heaped on a lining of old sailcloth. [188] "My father gathered this for your furnace, in exchange for stories," Mara said. [201] "He taught me that a basket should keep what matters and let the rest pass through." [217] Since the full moon, her father had become a baker who recognized no daughter, while Mara remembered mending nets before her birth. [239] She wanted Serein to fasten his old life around him so tightly that it could never migrate again. [257] The waning moon would soon draw the fisherman farther away, and Mara could not follow him through every stranger. [276]

Serein found the fisherman's volume, but its clasp resisted her smallest key. [288] Across the reading tables, other life books were swelling against their bindings as borrowed memories accumulated behind locked covers. [307] The catalogue allowed no shared names, no overlapping histories; its orderly rules were making every migration a kind of theft. [327] Still, she could force the clasp. [333] Her metal thumb rested on it until Mara asked whether locking the fisherman in would mean throwing the baker out. [353]

From the kitchen upstairs came the scent of anise bread, delivered that morning by the man who had forgotten his child. [374] He had wrapped one little loaf separately, without knowing why. [384] Serein withdrew her thumb. [388] All this time, she had offered people surprising stories while insisting that their own stories never change. [405] They would have to mend the catalogue by accepting contradictions: a man could have lost his daughter and still be her father. [427] It was not the answer Mara had carried through the cold, and she sat silently beside her dripping creel. [446]

Serein lowered its iron sand into the charcoal furnace beneath her great wheel and began to smelt it. [464] While the bellows breathed, she unfastened the fisherman's book and the baker's book, laying their different accounts side by side. [484] Mara found a recipe in one margin and a knot in the other, both drawn with the same impatient strokes. [504] "His hands," she said, and touched both pages. [512] From the furnace bloom, Serein hammered slender rings instead of clasps, binding the two volumes together without locking either shut. [532] She revised the catalogue to admit that one body might shelter several lives, and that one life might belong to several bodies. [554] The migration would continue, but nobody would have to become a stranger without a forwarding address. [570]

At the next dawn, Mara returned with the baker, who carried a warm loaf shaped like a fish. [588] He did not suddenly remember being her father; he listened while she read him the story he had once traded sand to borrow. [611] When she stumbled, he supplied a word, then looked surprised. [621] Serein set the bamboo creel beneath the returns slot, where seawater could drain from books brought across the flooded steps. [641] Her wheel advanced, the small familiar click sounding almost like a question. [653] Under the pale winter sun, she opened a fresh catalogue page and left room beside every name. [670]
