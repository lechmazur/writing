After forgiving herself, Mara could bear to light a furnace again. [11] That was why she came north with her brother, carrying a recording horn, casting tools, and one street organ punched card scrap. [33] Above the icebound sanctum, the aurora poured green light across a sky that looked ready to thaw. [50] Inside waited a ceremonial rope, braided centuries ago from the hair of mourners. [63] Mourners breathed their memories of the dead into its knots, preserving voices rather than words: transcribed memory. [80] Now warm seawater was eating the sanctuary's foundations, and the rope hung stiff with salt. [95] When the ceiling fell, a thousand years of voices would drown. [106]

Mara had already destroyed enough voices. [112] At sixteen, an occupying captain had made her melt the town's wax memorial cylinders while holding her brother's face beside the furnace. [134] A lost wax caster knew exactly how quickly a voice could become a puddle. [148] For twenty years she had mistaken surviving that afternoon for consenting to it. [161] Her brother had finally told her, "You saved me, and I will not apologize for wanting to live." [179] Forgiving herself had taken another winter. [185] It had not restored a single name. [192]

The scrap was her mother's, smuggled from the sanctum before the occupation and hidden among the music for her street organ. [213] Its holes were more than a tune, encoding instructions for the rope: crossings, intervals, places where silence belonged. [231] Mara's mother had disguised them as a lullaby, trusting that soldiers who burned written names would never bother confiscating music. [251] Mara remembered falling asleep while those impossible pauses turned the organ's wheeze into something almost human. [267] After her mother's execution, Mara had sent a pilgrim north with a lock of hair knotted around her memory of one voice. [289]

Her brother erected the horn while she warmed a wax cylinder between her palms. [303] To illuminate the stored names, she had to make windows for the aurora by reconfiguring the braids of the centuries-old ceremonial rope. [325] The work was technically gentle: loosen one crossing, support the next, never pull a strand against its neighbor. [343] It demanded the tenderness she had once reserved for objects, never for herself. [356]

Light entered the first knot, and the ice walls whispered, "Sulen." [367] The horn shivered; its needle inscribed the sound into turning wax. [378] With every name, a loosened knot slipped apart. [386] The memory could be released only once. [393]

Mara worked faster as seawater ticked through the floor, but the punched scrap ended halfway through a crossing. [411] Beyond its torn edge, the remaining strands tightened into a fist. [422] She knew that pattern. [426] Her mother had always stopped the lullaby there to ask whether she was still awake. [441] The missing interval was not a note; it was time enough for a child to answer. [457] Mara held the braid open through that remembered silence, then eased the waiting strand underneath. [472]

A woman's voice filled the chamber, warm with the breath before laughter: "Ilyen." [485] Not Mother, not prisoner, not the number stamped on a confiscation list. [497] Ilyen, who had left room in every song for someone else to speak. [510] Mara nearly stopped the cylinder to hear her again. [519] Instead she let it turn, making room for the next name. [530]

By dawn the rope lay unbraided, and her brother was feeding their packing crates into the portable furnace. [548] Mara enclosed the recorded wax in plaster, leaving channels for molten bronze. [560] Her hands faltered when the mold began to heat. [569] Once more, she must burn away a voice to make a space for it. [583] Her brother put his hand beside hers on the furnace door, not guiding, only staying. [598] This time the emptiness would receive something lasting. [606]

As the bronze cooled, seawater climbed the steps, carrying little splinters of green light. [620] They broke the plaster aboard their boat, and Mara set the bronze cylinder beneath the horn's needle. [637] The first voice emerged roughened by metal, unmistakably alive. [646] Her brother repeated the name, then the next, his mouth awkward around the unfamiliar sounds. [661] Behind them the sanctum settled into the sea without a bell. [672] Mara leaned close to the horn, hungry for each syllable, her wet hands warming between her brother's palms. [690] When Ilyen spoke, they answered together. [696]
