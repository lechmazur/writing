In the obsidian spire by the frozen lake, Iona watched the chancellor hold a kingfisher feather quill nib above the annual blank page. [23] At noon, it would accept one sentence, and whatever he wrote would govern the coming winter. [39] The ink could redirect the city's heat, but it could not create any. [52] Outside, people waited for his promised glass orchard, its oranges already bright on election banners. [67] Inside, Iona had nine minutes to prove that writing the future required leaving some of it unwritten. [84]

She was an ignorance cartographer. [89] Her maps showed unmeasured currents, missing records, and assumptions repeated until they passed for stone. [104] Today the chancellor had commissioned an orchard and received a drawing of his ignorance instead. [119]

"Your figures balance," she said, "only if the tower stays black." [130]

She spread her winter simulations across his desk: a hundred variations of wind, snowfall, and cloud. [146] Bare obsidian gathered enough sunlight to supplement the furnaces, but snow crusted its dark faces in thirty simulations. [164] In those futures, the promised oranges ripened while the workers' district froze. [176] The sentence would keep its promise by drawing heat from wherever heat remained. [189]

"Then I will write that nobody freezes." [196]

"And the furnaces will consume next year's fuel before spring." [206]

He looked toward the banners, then down at the fragile nib. [217] "They chose me because I promised something besides survival." [226]

Iona understood that hunger. [230] Her mother's workshop had been closed after an experimental boiler burst; afterward, the council permitted only designs its engineers called certain. [251] Innovation had dwindled to better locks for the fuel stores. [261] She wanted invention back, but not the kind that made strangers pay for someone else's confidence. [277]

"Why a kingfisher feather?" she asked. [283]

"Because the founders saw abundance in it." [290]

"They saw a bird catching fish when every other shore was sealed." [302]

From her case she took the oldest map, where a blue bird marked open water beneath the spire. [320] The furnace outfall had kept that inlet open for generations, spilling warmth the orchard calculations treated as already gone. [339] She had measured it all autumn. [345]

Her second model used that waste water beneath small growing beds, each isolated by a valve that closed when household temperatures dipped. [367] No oranges yet: hardy greens, insulation trials, and enough spare capacity for failures. [380] On the map, each little nursery was surrounded by the white boundary she used for an unanswered question. [398]

"You want me to promise uncertainty." [404]

"I want you to promise its limits." [411]

He demanded a colder winter, then a broken valve, then three broken valves, as though he might shake an orchard from her arithmetic. [434] Iona ran each change through the brass predictor, turning its stiff handles until the colored beads settled into their channels. [454] Some beds failed, but every version preserved the district's heating and the inlet's narrow refuge for fish. [471]

"Those are the failures we know," she said, and pointed to the white margins. [485] "The rest need watching, and permission to stop." [493]

Her mother had called this being groundedly imaginative: giving wonder somewhere solid to stand. [507] Iona had once thought the phrase an apology for small ambitions. [518] Now she saw that a valve, a measurement, and an honest blank could protect an ambition long enough for it to grow. [540]

The chancellor moved his hand away from the page. [549] "Show me the sentence." [553]

She arranged wooden counting tiles on the desk, where no accidental mark could bind them. [568] The words promised a share of outfall heat for experiments, never household warmth or fuel reserves, with every growing bed free to fail. [591] Nothing required any plant to fruit. [597] Nothing forbade a discovery neither of them could name. [606]

Below, the waiting crowd began to chant for oranges. [615] The chancellor winced, but sent his steward downstairs with both maps and instructions to open the shuttered workshops. [633]

Iona had prevailed through predictive modeling, yet the victory felt less like conquering doubt than finding it useful work. [652] She asked for a place at the first bed, not as its inventor, but as the person responsible for noticing what went wrong. [675]

The noon bell drew breath in the rafters. [683] Before the pen met the blank page, the chancellor turned Iona's map so its uncharted spaces faced them both. [702] This time, he left them white. [708]
