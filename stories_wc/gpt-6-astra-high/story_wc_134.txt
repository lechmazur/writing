After the pilgrim arrived, the musical crystal cave stopped repeating tomorrow's weather and began playing a tune Nera had never heard. [21] Its rhythm was steadily unpredictable: three bright notes, a long silence, then a chord that made the darkness seem full of windows. [43] Nera, who earned her bread as a clairvoyant, gripped the rail beside her listening stool. [58]

"Show me what you brought," she said. [65]

The pilgrim unwrapped a cave pearl, pale and round as the moon reflected in a spoon. [81]

"From the pool beneath your eastern gallery," he said. [90] "Your village sent me to ask you to magnify everyday magic." [101]

He set beside it a baker's apron, a wooden button, and a small shoe with its sole coming loose. [120] Once, he explained, dough rose when the baker sang, buttons found their own holes, and children's shoes always carried them home. [141] Lately everything required more effort than tired people had. [150]

Nera knew why they were tired: she had foreseen a hundred little accidents and prescribed a hundred precautions. [168] The baker no longer sang while working, lest she overlook a coal; children took the long road to avoid a slippery bridge. [190] She had thought safety would make them happy. [198]

"The old enchantments need a stronger voice," she said, unwilling to examine that thought. [212]

A pearl grown beneath singing crystals could give unseen forces something to inhabit, then broadcast their power through the whole valley. [233] She placed it on her stone lectern and lit the great forecasting lamp. [246] A single enormous shadow sprang up behind her. [254]

The cave fell silent. [258]

Then the pearl began to shriek. [264]

Inside its milky layers Nera saw the baker watching an oven forever, a child hesitating before every step, herself listening without sleep. [286] The lamp flared, and the visions spread across the walls. [296] She had made a mouth for the strongest unseen force she carried: fear. [309]

This was the disaster the clairvoyant had not seen coming. [319] All her questions had concerned dangers outside the cave. [328]

Nera reached for the lamp, but her hand stopped, suddenly certain that touching its handle would burn down the mountain. [348] Beside her, the pilgrim trembled too. [354] Nevertheless, he took the little shoe from his bundle and began sewing its sole with a needle tucked in his sleeve. [375] Mending was his pilgrim's vow, a prayer his hands remembered when his frightened tongue could not. [391]

"I mend things for my supper," he said, his stitches crooked with fright. [404] "Holding something together helps." [408]

Nera watched the needle pass faithfully between two ragged edges, making neither new, only making them useful to each other. [428] She could not promise that he would never prick his finger. [439]

She could hold the shoe. [444]

When she did, their shadows overlapped across the lectern, and a softer note threaded through the shrieking. [461] The pearl admitted other forces through overlapping shadows: patience from his hands, tenderness from hers, the stubborn hope embodied in a repaired shoe. [484] Its glow became warm enough for Nera to see the lamp as brass and oil again, not the beginning of every possible fire. [507]

She turned the wick down. [512]

Darkness returned, bright with crystal music. [518]

Following that gentler music, they carried the pearl to its pool, where falling drops had kept it turning as its pale layers grew. [541] It had become beautiful by moving, Nera realized, not by avoiding every rough edge. [555]

She let it go. [559]

The pearl turned beneath the water, casting their joined shadows among the crystals, which answered one another all the way to the valley. [582] On the pilgrim's knee, the last length of thread tied itself into a tidy knot. [597] Nera laughed before she thought to listen for the consequences. [607]

That evening she walked down with him, carrying the apron and a new prediction, which she delivered at the bakery door. [628]

"Someone will burn a loaf again someday," she told the baker. [639] "But perhaps you could sing anyway." [645]

The baker studied her, then began a song about a king who lost his trousers. [660] Under a clean cloth, tomorrow's dough lifted like a small, contented breath. [672]

Nera stayed to learn the chorus. [678]
