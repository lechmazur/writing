After the tide pools emptied, the labyrinth carved seven centuries earlier into the red canyon walls breathed out the smell of wet stone and orchids. [25] On the ruined wharf below, Mara wove fishing nets and small poems, knotting verses where her neighbors' fingers would find them in the dark. [49] Since the earthquake sealed the canyon chapel, every net carried the same hauntingly optimistic promise: What holds the living can also honor the dead. [73] Her mother's bones lay somewhere beyond its broken stair. [82] Mara wanted no treasure from the ruins, only a way for people to bring flowers without adding another name to the mourning songs. [105]

The keeper refused her permission, citing precedents older than the wharf: no waking exploration, no mingling of ancestral paths, no rope bridges within sacred ground. [130] Canyon people found forgotten passages by sleepwalking after the pools drained, tracing their ancestors' errands in miniature across the smooth wharf stones. [152] But each family kept its own dreamer's map, and since the earthquake every permitted route had ended at a fall of stone. [174] "Those rules kept us alive," the keeper said, touching the wedding cord his dead husband had braided. [191]

That evening, Mara found a storybook illustration plate among the salvage brought up in her nets, its copper face green beneath a crust of silt. [216] She recognized the scene despite its reversed lettering: the old tale of three sisters carrying an orchid to their sleeping mother. [237] As a child, she had thought the curling lines beneath their feet were waves. [251] Now, cleaning them with a needle, she recognized netting stretched between three arches that still stood above the chapel stair. [271] The sisters approached by different corridors; the bridge belonged to none of them until their roads met. [288] Perhaps the rules were younger than the kindness they prohibited. [298]

For six ebb tides, Mara watched willing dreamers shuffle across the wharf, recording their tiny turns on separate scraps of sailcloth. [319] A baker's grandmother remembered a flour chute; a mason's son remembered steps concealed behind a curtain of roots. [337] Both routes were blocked, but on opposite sides of a landing shaped like a crescent moon. [353] When Mara laid their maps together, the useless endings fell away from a continuous route. [368] She began to sew, joining scraps at their shared landmarks until overlapping sleepwalking paths disclosed a passage no single family could remember. [390] Its last stretch required the bridge from the picture. [399]

The keeper came as she was sewing doubled netting onto the spare sailcloth, making a walkway wide enough for two people to kneel together. [423] "You mean to break every precedent," he said. [431] "Only the ones already broken by the canyon," Mara answered, and placed the copper plate beside his wedding cord. [450] He studied the tiny woven bridge until she saw his anger loosen into something more painful. [466] "I thought keeping everything unchanged meant I had not abandoned him." [477] Mara slid a needle toward him, and he stayed until dawn. [488]

On the next low tide, they entered awake, carrying their stitched map, their bundled bridge, and one pale orchid wrapped in damp wool. [511] At the crescent landing they changed ancestral roads, leaving the baker's flour chute for the mason's narrow steps. [529] Beyond them, the arches waited exactly where the storyteller had drawn them, with empty iron rings sunk deep into the stone. [550] Secured by Mara's safety rope, the keeper edged along a cornice to the farther rings and helped her lash the walkway into place. [573] When Mara crossed, the net gave a little beneath her, yielding without letting go. [587]

Inside the roofless chapel, no miracle had repaired the smashed benches or lifted the stones from the unclaimed dead. [606] But orchids had rooted in the cracks, their lavender throats opening toward a slender blade of sun. [623] Mara set her flower among them and spoke her mother's name without shouting it across a gulf. [640] Beside her, the keeper began another name, stopped, and found enough breath to finish. [654]

They left before the pools refilled, but he carried the sewn map back openly, spread across both arms like something sacred. [675] By evening, neighbors were asking what they might bring when their turn came, and whether the bridge could bear a child. [696] "Yes," Mara said, already measuring out another length for a smaller handrail. [708]
