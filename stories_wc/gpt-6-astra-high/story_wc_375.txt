After the last echo of laughter faded, Elin counted her coins and closed the portrait stall. [16] The tourists were ascending from the subterranean crystal cave, carrying caricatures that made their worst features harmless. [33] Charcoal-fingered, she had become the sort of portrait sketch artist who could flatter a face without looking twice. [51] Being mnemonically gifted had once seemed an advantage: every face stayed available, every correction her mother offered stayed exact. [70] Now she could draw anything she remembered and nothing she desired. [81]

Tonight, for the first time since the funeral, she had brought a blank board. [95] She wanted to paint something that had never existed, if only to discover whether she still could. [112] Beyond the tourist lamps stood the studio the museum had granted her mother, whose landscapes had first made the cave famous. [133] Quartz needles crowded its ceiling, splitting a single bulb into pale green and violet wounds. [148] Her mother had taught her that reflected light could make even darkness seem inhabited. [162]

Elin found the river painting above the workbench and swung it outward on its concealed hinges. [178] Behind it waited the pigment cupboard and the combination lock she had installed herself. [192] Twelve, thirty, seven: the numbers arrived as readily as her mother's birthday, although she wished she could forget their purpose. [212] When the tremor began, the critics had called those delicate landscapes damaged. [224] Elin had locked away the expensive colors after finding her mother weeping over a spoiled commission. [240] "You have earned your rest," she had said, while removing the wet brush from her mother's hand. [257] Her mother had watched the brush, not her. [265]

The lock would not turn. [270] Elin tried again, then lifted it toward the bulb and saw that its brass casing had been pried apart. [289] The broken combination lock was held together with thread, arranged to look secure. [302] A palette knife lay beneath it, its point bent into a small, stubborn hook. [316]

Inside the cupboard, between bottles of blue and vermilion, stood eleven paintings on scraps of packing board. [333] Elin knew the cardboard: she had brought groceries in those boxes to her mother's cottage above the entrance. [351] She could remember every item on the receipts. [359] She had not noticed the boxes disappearing. [366]

None of the pictures resembled the river. [373] Green curtains streamed across violet fields; crooked stars gathered around an orange hollow; luminous ladders rose without arriving anywhere. [392] The tremor ran through everything, no longer disguised as foliage or apologized for in letters to disappointed buyers. [410] Her mother had made its wavering into weather. [418]

On the back of the smallest board, in handwriting Elin could have reproduced perfectly, were the words: Studies for tomorrow. [438] She sat down on the cold floor. [445] All winter she had praised her mother's acceptance, glad that the old woman no longer asked for brushes. [463] Meanwhile, between her visits and their careful meals, a whole country had been coming into being without her permission. [482]

There were things she could rectify, and one she could not. [493] The museum's draft catalogue lay on the workbench, describing how illness had ended her mother's work. [509] Elin crossed out the paragraph and wrote that eleven late paintings would be available for exhibition. [525] Then she added the truth about the cupboard, before she could choose a kinder version. [540] That would not give her mother back the hours spent hiding footsteps or waiting for Elin to leave. [558] It might keep someone else from mistaking surrender for peace. [568]

She set her own blank board beside the paintings and uncorked a bottle of blue. [583] For years, wanting had felt like disloyalty to the talent that paid her rent: the accurate eye, the obedient hand. [603] But her mother had kept painting when accuracy deserted her. [613] Perhaps a calling was not the thing you did best, but the thing that found another way through when you barred the door. [636]

Elin began with a river flowing uphill. [643] The first stroke looked foolish, and she let it stay. [653] Around it she laid a dawn she had never witnessed, green opening into violet beneath the splintered light of the crystals. [674] There was nobody left to tell her whether it was good. [685] For once, that was not the question she needed answered. [695]
