After the campfire's last spark died, I followed Keeper Mara up the lighthouse stairs, carrying the coat that made me a conscience for hire. [24] At dawn, the sea breeze combed through the open lantern room, stirring the wind chimes above her father's empty chair. [44] Beyond the reef, a refugee ship waited for the light that would guide it through the only navigable channel. [63] We had kept the beach fire burning all night so its passengers would know they had not been forgotten. [82] Mara had until sunrise to decide whether to obey his final order: keep the harbor closed. [98] Fever had killed Mara's mother, and the council believed the approaching passengers carried it. [112] She paid me to certify that disobedience would not make her a bad daughter. [126]

Most clients wanted permission, though my license promised only company. [136] I spread the cloak of doubt across our shoulders, its wool sheltering every word spoken beneath it. [153] The council could subpoena my testimony, but the cloak would never let those words leave my mouth again. [171]

"Whatever you choose, you are still worthy of kindness," I said in my professional voice. [186]

I was sincerely insincere: I believed it, but made belief sound easier than it was. [201] I had worn this cloak beside men whose choices kept me awake. [213] Mara leaned against me without quite surrendering her weight. [222]

"He never doubted himself," she said, and opened her fist. [232]

In her palm lay a hollow mirror shard, silver on both faces, with a breath trembling inside. [249] Like every keeper, her father had breathed his final misgivings into the lantern's hollow reflector before dying. [266] Breaking off a piece was the only way to hear what duty had forbidden him to say. [283]

"I thought it would be empty," Mara whispered. [291]

I asked her to unhook the smallest chime and fasten the shard in its place. [306] The frame held a keeper's vocabulary: brass for places, copper for people, little silver bells for what remained unsaid. [325] When the breeze entered the shard, the trapped breath bent it toward particular notes. [339] Deciphering wind chime selections was slower than reading a confession, and kinder; the dead could hesitate. [355]

The first sequence named the fever, then the fishing village below us, then Mara. [369]

"He wanted to protect us," she said. [376]

I nodded, but held up a finger as the shard swung again. [388] This time it chose the silver bells for shame, ignorance, and children. [400] Then came a sequence I made myself translate twice: no sickness seen aboard. [413]

Mara pulled away, taking the cloak with her. [421]

"He condemned them because he was afraid, not because he knew," she said. [434]

"Yes," I answered, reaching only far enough to keep her shoulder covered. [446]

"Then he was cruel." [450]

I had heard certainty change its clothes before. [458]

"He was frightened of losing you, and willing to lose strangers instead," I said, "but the fever is real." [477]

Below us, the ship's bell sounded three times, asking for a pilot. [489]

"Tell me the right thing," she said. [496]

The old temptation rose in me: name a virtue, take her money, leave her alone with its consequences. [514] Instead I asked what the harbor offered besides a locked gate. [525]

"The customs island has a well," she said slowly, "and empty sheds where the passengers could shelter apart." [543]

And a doctor could cross without bringing anyone into town. [553] She began counting blankets on her fingers, then stopped. [562]

"Someone might still die." [566]

"Yes." [567]

She stayed beneath the cloak long enough to cry without making an argument of it. [582] Then she returned the shard to my hand and stepped toward the lamp. [595] Her father's command lay written beside the wick, weighted down with his spectacles. [608] She did not burn it. [613] On its reverse she wrote instructions for the island, including her name and where the council could find her. [632]

This was why I hired out my conscience: to welcome nuance into certainty without letting doubt become an alibi. [651]

At sunrise, her light found the channel, and the waiting ship turned toward shelter. [665] Mara asked whether I would come down to the landing with her. [677] I folded the cloak over my arm, leaving one corner within her reach. [690]

"Yes," I said, without using my professional voice. [698]
