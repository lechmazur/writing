After the guardianship ended, Mara kept its keys, though the council had melted every lock they fitted. [17] Dismissal had not cured her of being a relic keeper; each dawn she inventoried the one thing they had missed. [37] The gilded reed flute lay inside her dead sister's stocking, its gold skin dented where Mara's teeth had clenched during the catastrophe. [59]

Once, its notes had woven meltwater between neighboring worlds, sharing dangerous floods among the empty canyons of the Thunder-echo range. [79] Then an earthquake struck while the passages were open, and Mara played the forbidden closure, trying to hold every mountain still. [100] For three breaths, the range became avalanchingly quiet. [108] On the fourth, all that arrested weight found her village. [118] Now rubble dammed the river, and the survivors were drinking the last snow from their roofs. [134]

Mara climbed upstream carrying the flute and a notebook whose first page bore the council's verdict: NEVER AGAIN. [152] Below it she had written a smaller question: What, exactly, must never happen again? [166] She had come not to recover her reputation but to harvest insights from failure, if failure would yield anything besides names. [187]

At the first dry bend, she knelt to uncase the instrument. [198] A low note loosened the cliff's outline, revealing another canyon threaded through this one, its stones dark with running water. [218] Weaving between worlds meant stepping across while the note lasted; when breath failed, the nearer world took you. [236] Apprentices learned to navigate by echoes, but Mara no longer trusted what the mountains returned. [251] Instead, she began arranging driftwood as directional markers, each fork pointing home, each pale stem spanning a seam. [269] Wood caught between worlds remained in both, an old keeper's trick for retrieving tools dropped during repairs. [286]

In the first neighboring world, she found her own handwriting carved into a sluice: HOLD LONGER. [302] Behind it stood a lake full of roofs. [310] In the next, every passage had been flung wide; floodwater had scoured the gorge down to white bedrock. [328] She copied both mistakes, marking the waterlines, measuring the drops with a knotted survey cord. [343]

In the third world, her sister was hanging washing above an intact village. [356] Mara stopped so abruptly that the note broke, leaving her standing on that bank. [370] Her sister looked up, saw the stocking around Mara's wrist, and slowly lowered a shirt. [385]

"You sent it downstream here," she said before Mara could ask. [396] "We buried the people from the lower valley all spring." [406]

Beyond the washing, a gilded flute hung over a doorway, split lengthwise like a gutted fish. [422] Mara wanted to cross the yard, wanted it with a hunger that made the ruined valley briefly seem an acceptable price. [443] Instead, she asked her sister where the water had gone afterward. [454]

They spent the afternoon tracing its course through a deserted gravel basin, where shallow channels spread the current without trapping it. [475]

"You could stay," her sister said at the seam. [484]

Mara pressed the stocking into her hands and followed the driftwood home. [496]

At the dam, she rejected the grand opening her teachers would have demanded. [509] She needed only a thread of water, carried through the neighboring basin and returned to her river below the rubble. [529] The driftwood showed her where each crossing lay, sparing her the long, blind notes that had flooded those other worlds. [549]

She played a phrase, stopped to listen, then adjusted it. [559] Water whispered over gravel, vanished through a seam, and struck the dry stones at her feet. [575] At every breath the little stream faltered; at every new phrase it resumed. [588] There was no triumphal echo, only the hesitant sound of something being allowed to move. [603]

By morning, people arrived with buckets, and by noon they had enough water to start digging a channel through the dam. [624] Mara played until their channel ran, her lip bleeding against the gold. [636] When someone offered to petition for the guardianship's restoration, she shook her head and handed him the notebook. [654]

"Make copies," she said, and beneath the verdict recorded her own temptation: choosing her sister over everyone still waiting downstream. [674] Then she left a blank page for whatever they would learn tomorrow. [686]
