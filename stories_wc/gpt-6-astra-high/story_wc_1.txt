In the quiet canyon under stars, Mara's collar shone with chips of glass that gathered daylight and released it slowly, like breath returning to a sleeping body. [27] Once, people had called her the luminous glass artist, meaning her extravagant windows; tonight, the name meant only that she could see her shaking hands. [52] Three weeks after surviving the attempt to burn her inside her workshop, she still could not draw enough air to blow a drinking glass. [76] The fuel guild had punished her for making lamps that burned no oil. [89] Its men had left her furnace cold and its chimney standing over nothing. [102]

From the ashes she had taken her mother's scorched testament, folded around a charcoal smuggler's ledger strip. [119] Her mother had carried illegal fuel through this canyon until a winter fever stopped her, and Mara had paid for the burial without attending. [143] A celebrated artist could not be seen mourning a thief. [153] That was what she had told herself when there were still invitations. [165]

Now she had come to retrace the deliveries recorded beside landmarks on the strip: Split Tooth, Dry Stair, the Listening Wall. [186] Between the entries lay blank spaces where payments should have been. [197] She hoped for hidden savings, enough to buy a little furnace beyond the fuel guild's reach. [213] Her mother's surviving bequest was less practical: I leave you what I kept warm. [227] Beneath it, almost eaten by fire, remained an instruction to watch the patterns in morning frost. [243] Mara waited, wanting to discover purpose in the pauses that had become her life: between coughs, between footsteps, between remembering and wishing she could forget. [268]

At dawn, frost silvered the canyon floor except for narrow, branching seams of bare stone. [283] She knew that pattern from kiln walls, where escaping heat left clean veins through the winter white. [300] Somebody was firing underground. [304] Her craft had made her steadily adaptive: when sunlight erased one branch, she searched the shade; when her chest tightened, she sat down. [327] For once, stopping helped her see. [333] Beside the Listening Wall, warmth rose through a crack concealed by a loose slab, carrying the familiar mineral smell of hot sand. [355] She knocked with the ledger folded in her fist. [364]

A woman opened the stone door, saw the paper, and said Mara's mother's name. [378] Below waited a furnace, six narrow beds, and shelves of cloudy jars. [390] These were glassworkers expelled for debt, still making vessels for farms that could not afford the guild's mark. [408]

"Your mother brought us charcoal when we couldn't pay," the woman said, smoothing the ledger against her apron. [426] "She always left those spaces empty, so we wouldn't mistake kindness for a loan." [440]

Mara looked again at the blank intervals, the part of the accounts she had considered missing. [456] Her mother had left her no money, only people who remembered how to open a door. [472]

"I cannot blow glass anymore," Mara said, offering the truth before anyone could ask her to prove her famous name. [492]

The woman moved a stool closer to the furnace and set a shallow casting mold on its seat. [510] "Then perhaps you can teach us something that doesn't require blowing." [521]

Mara touched the luminous chips at her throat, a formula she had guarded even from her apprentices. [538] Cast into small tiles, that glass could gather sunlight on a windowsill and light a room after supper without burning another measure of precious fuel. [563] She had always shaped it into delicate luxuries, charging for the wonder as much as the work. [580] Here, a plain square would do. [586] She asked for chalk. [590]

By evening, her formula covered the wall beside the furnace, including the cooling intervals she had once regarded as proprietary secrets. [611] Her lungs still hurt, and the guild still owned the road beyond the canyon, but six people were arguing cheerfully over her first crooked tile. [636] Mara laid the scorched testament under a clean glass weight and left the ledger's empty spaces untouched. [653]

Outside, the canyon filled again with stars, and the furnace answered each movement of its bellows with a low, receding note. [674] When the sound faded, someone passed her a bowl of soup, and she let them wait while she found enough breath to thank them. [698]
