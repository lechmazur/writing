Between acts, the tidal arcade belonged to Nell, and tonight she had twelve minutes before the sea reclaimed it. [19] Under stars visible through its broken roof, seawater threaded the paving stones and lifted yesterday's confetti like spawning fish. [38] Nell, an analytical street performer, had timed those minutes against the moon, the wind, and the slowest member of her audience. [59]

That was her father, who arrived every evening at nine, sat beneath the dead harbor clock, and left at nine twelve. [80] Since the lighthouse had been automated, he had divided retirement into tasks so small that nothing unexpected could enter between them. [101] Tonight, Nell meant to serenade monotony with creativity, beginning with the twelve minutes he considered too insignificant to defend. [120]

She had borrowed the wooden bishop from his lighthouse chess set and chalked a board around the arcade's central drain. [140] Beside each white square she had written an instruction for someone waiting for the next act: scrape, whistle, clap, ring. [160] The bishop would travel diagonally according to numbered pebbles drawn from a bag, assembling a tune no player could predict. [180] Nell had assigned every WHISTLE square to her father, whose old watchkeeping songs once filled their kitchen before dawn. [199] He had not whistled since the harbor authority mailed him a certificate praising forty years of indispensable service. [217]

She called her method orchestrated serendipity: by ordering randomness, she could make an encounter feel discovered rather than arranged. [236] What she wanted to happen was simple: one familiar melody, and his face turning toward her instead of the clock. [256]

She appeared only lightly attentive as the first child drew a pebble, but she was counting breaths, currents, exits. [275] A fish seller tapped his weighing pan; a woman rasped a comb; their accidental rhythm bounced beneath the saltbitten arches. [295] Nell caught it on her concertina, keeping the melody loose enough to accommodate whatever the board offered next. [313]

When the bishop reached WHISTLE, her father studied the board without moving his lips. [327]

"Still on white," he said. [332] "All those choices, and you cannot even change the color." [342]

The fish seller lowered his pan, and Nell felt the silence as a flaw she ought to repair immediately. [361] She could select another pebble, suggest an easier note, explain how carefully she had built this evening around him. [380] Then she saw his hands folded precisely on his knees, like tools returned to their allotted hooks. [397] The authority had told him when his usefulness ended; she was telling him when his joy should begin. [415]

A thin wave entered through the seaward arch, reaching the drain two minutes earlier than her tables had promised. [434] It smudged a row of instructions and tipped the bishop, which spun toward the iron grate. [450] Her father caught it with his boot, then stooped to pick it up, water darkening both his cuffs. [468] He held it over the nearest surviving square, waiting for her to tell him where it belonged. [485]

Nell stopped counting the seconds remaining before the next act and emptied her bag of pebbles into her pocket. [504]

"You can keep it," she said. [510]

For a moment he looked offended, as though she had offered him something he already owned. [526] Then his thumb found the bishop's chipped mitre, and he sat on the lowest step instead of his usual bench. [546] He tapped the piece against the stone: two short knocks, a pause, then three. [560]

Nell recognized the old lighthouse identification signal, the pattern ships had once trusted when fog swallowed the coast. [578] She answered softly, leaving the long interval empty, although every instinct urged her to fill it. [594] Into that opening came the fish seller's pan, the comb, and a gull's furious objection from the roof. [612] Her father laughed, missed his next beat, and invented another, less seaworthy rhythm to bring himself back in. [630]

Around their ankles the tide dismantled the board, carrying its pale geometry out beneath a sky indifferent to squares. [649] At the upper end of the arcade, the next performer rang her bell, and the audience began climbing toward dry stone. [670] Nell's father stood, still tapping, and waited while she gathered the concertina straps into her hands. [686]

"Tomorrow," he said, "leave some squares unwritten." [693]

It was not a promise to whistle, and this time she knew better than to mistake it for one. [712] She followed his untidy rhythm up the steps, under stars she had forgotten to consult. [727]
