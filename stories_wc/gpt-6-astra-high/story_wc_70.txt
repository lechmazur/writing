After the city-wide blackout, dusk thickened like nectar around the lightning collection tower, where the last charged windows glowed amber. [20] Mara, the tower's toll keeper, remained at her booth while people crossed the admission bridge without paying, carrying candles instead of cameras. [42] She had stopped asking for coins when the hospital lamps went out across the river. [57]

The little clockwork regulator beneath her tongue still worked, however, making her involuntarily truthful even when a lie would have been merciful. [79] Every municipal cashier wore one; nobody had thought to require them of the engineers. [93]

Above her, thousands of brass collector drones lay tangled inside the tower's great glass comb. [108] They ordinarily ferried captured lightning from the spire into storage cells, each returning on a slightly different humming note. [127] At noon, their notes had become one beautiful, terrible chord, followed by a flash that knocked Mara off her stool. [147]

Now Chief Engineer Venn stood beside her, insisting that the drone beehive failure was sabotage. [162] He demanded to know whether she had seen anyone interfere with the controls. [175]

"Only your men, installing the punctuality upgrade," Mara said, and felt the familiar pinch beneath her tongue release. [193]

Venn looked toward the dark hospital, then ordered a restart using the same settings. [207] Mara put her hand over the switch. [214]

For thirty years she had delayed the visitor queue by seconds, letting children hear individual drones arrive before she stamped their tickets. [236] She knew the impatient bass, the thin whistle, the stuttering one she called Grandfather. [250] Keeping that small astonishment alive had made the routine bearable; today, after the upgrade, every voice had disappeared. [268] Her mother, who had designed the first hive, had warned her about that silence. [282]

Mara reached beneath the counter for the lacquer box that held her mother's maintenance tools, its insulating finish scarred by old sparks. [304] Inside was a matchbox containing a cryptic riddle, written in the cramped hand Mara had never managed to imitate. [323] Her mother had hidden troubleshooting hints among ordinary things, convinced that finding an answer mattered more than obeying an instruction. [343]

The paper read, "What must lose the beat to keep the heart?" [355]

Under it lay a timing wafer, its copper paths branching like veins in a leaf. [370] Mara tipped the wafer into her palm and understood why her mother had kept it outside the official repair cabinet. [390] It was labeled DEVIATION, the very thing each successive maintenance order had instructed the engineers to eliminate. [407]

"Your upgrade made them arrive together," she said, lifting the wafer to reveal its label in the fading light. [426] "They weren't wasting time before; they were giving each other room." [437]

Venn began to protest, but through the inspection glass they could see every fallen drone jammed against the same receiving gate. [458] He unlatched the control panel, and a blue spark crawled across his sleeve. [471] Mara emptied the lacquer box and used its insulating lid to lift the live contacts clear of the synchronizer. [490] Venn slid out the new component and inserted her mother's wafer, his hands shaking enough to rattle the guides. [509]

Before throwing the switch, he asked her to tell the gathering crowd that the tower was safe. [526] She wanted to give him that easy permission, but the mechanism tightened against the root of her tongue. [544]

"We don't know," she told them, her voice carrying across the bridge, "and anyone who stays should stand behind the stone wall." [566]

Venn flinched at the admission, then repeated it louder and sent his assistants to clear the bridge. [583] They restarted the hive from a reserve cell, one gate at a time. [596]

For several breaths nothing moved; then a single drone struggled free, circled unevenly, and carried its trapped charge home. [615] Another followed, hesitated, and let a third pass ahead of it. [626] Across the river, one hospital window brightened, then a corridor, then the red lamp above the ambulance entrance. [644]

Venn sank onto her stool and watched the ragged, honey-colored traffic climb through the dusk. [659]

"I thought they needed discipline," he said. [666]

"So did the people who put this in my mouth," Mara answered. [678]

When a child approached the booth with a coin, she waved him toward the glass and left his ticket unstamped. [698] There was a stuttering note she wanted him to hear. [708]
