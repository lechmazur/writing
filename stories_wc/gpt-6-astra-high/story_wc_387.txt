During firefly season, moonlight hardened into narrow bridges between Mere and the island where its dead were buried. [18] Everyone called this a mercy of the heavens, except Iven, the town's net-mender philosopher, who disliked explanations without workmanship. [37] Nevertheless, he crossed each summer without looking down. [45]

This year, the council commissioned him to speak at the crossing festival, urging everyone to appreciate hidden effort. [63] He wrote eloquently about the fishermen whose dangerous voyages had purchased the town's prosperity. [77] His mother, Sella, brought tea while he worked, turning her bamboo tea whisk between fingers bent like fishing hooks. [96]

"Look underneath," she said, reading his title. [103]

[161] Beyond it, the cemetery was scintillatingly somber, its black cypresses threaded with fireflies. [174] Visitors reached the island by following moon paths, but tonight the nearest path sagged beneath a woman's basket. [192]
