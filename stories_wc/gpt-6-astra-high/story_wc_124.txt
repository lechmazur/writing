In the greenhouse for extinct plants, crystal shadows lay across beds where nothing had breathed for thirty years. [18] Ivo, a wistful lantern broker, walked between them carrying a lamp that gave no visible light and his mother's crystal perfume vial. [40] He had come ostensibly to negotiate a sale, but the vial was the reason he had answered the greenhouse's advertisement. [60] Wanted: fragrances of vanished flowers, however faint. [67]

Aromatically cautious, he sold only sealed electric lanterns, never scented oil lamps, and kept the vial wrapped in waxed silk. [87] Once, a leaking stopper had made him weep so violently that he missed a buyer and lost a month's rent. [107] His mother had died when he was four; he could not remember her voice. [121]

"I want to understand why some smells trigger tears," he told the keeper, setting both objects on her bench. [140] "I would rather remember something useful." [146]

Sen unwrapped the vial without touching the stopper. [154] Instead, she asked him to aim his lantern through the vial. [165] Ultraviolet light revealed lettering hidden beneath the cut crystal's ordinary glitter, the same way his lamps exposed repairs in supposedly antique glass. [187] Letters shone blue against his fingers: River violet, last harvest, bottled for Ivo. [200] Below them appeared the crooked star his mother had stitched inside his winter coat. [214] He remembered that star, though he had forgotten the hands that made it. [227]

Sen led him to a bed of transparent stems. [236] The violet's fossil seeds had been coaxed into crystal scaffolds, but without their original fragrance, the plants could not complete their passage into life. [260] Perfume was the last instruction, telling each resurrected flower what it had been. [273]

His mother had worked upriver, distilling scents before the salt floods swallowed the gardens. [287] He had always imagined her little bottle was merely a gift. [298]

"It is a gift," Sen said, when he asked whether she had only meant him to preserve a specimen. [317] "She put your name on it, not an inventory number." [327]

To finish the restoration, Sen would have to empty the vial into the bed's warming basin. [343] There would be nothing left for him to hoard, or fear, or uncork on birthdays when loneliness outweighed caution. [362] Ivo asked whether the flowers would give him back a memory. [373]

"They will give you flowers," Sen said. [380] "Anything else, I cannot promise." [385]

Through the glass roof he could see the lamps of his shop, each priced according to how much darkness it displaced. [406] He had built a tolerable life around the small sealed darkness in his pocket. [420] Now he understood that keeping it intact had not kept her near; it had only made every accidental breath an ambush. [441]

He took the vial and removed the stopper himself. [450] Sen steadied the basin while he poured. [457] The scent rose green and peppery, unexpectedly humble for something that had governed so many years. [473] His throat closed, and he nearly asked her to stop. [483]

Under the ultraviolet beam, bright channels appeared inside the stems, showing Sen where to turn the warming mirrors. [501] "Stay here as the transformation completes," she said, moving a stool beside him rather than ushering him away from the work. [522]

Crystal softened into veins; clear leaves clouded with green, and the first violet unfolded like a small, bruised hand. [541] He remembered no face, no recovered conversation, only the certainty of being carried indoors against someone's warm, damp apron. [560] The tears came before he could decide whether that was enough. [571]

"Sometimes the body keeps what the mind cannot name," Sen said, her hands still busy adjusting the light. [589]

For once, he let himself grieve without demanding that grief produce a clear picture in exchange. [605] Beyond the revived bed, a hundred empty crystal flowers waited, each shaped around an absence somebody might still carry home. [625]

At dawn, Sen asked his price for the lantern. [634] Ivo looked at the violets, already shedding their old, brittle shadows onto the soil. [648]

"Keep it until these can grow beside the river," he said. [659] "Then pay me in seeds." [664]

He carried the empty vial home unwrapped, while the smell of living flowers followed on his sleeves. [681]
