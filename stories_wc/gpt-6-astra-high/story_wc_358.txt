After the town went silent, accident investigator Mara Venn heard the harbor breathing through its teeth. [16] Fog hid the cranes, but she could locate each passage in the towering labyrinth of shipping containers by its particular whistle. [37] Beyond the stacks, the evacuated port city lay dark behind flood barriers that would fail at dawn unless a replacement motor arrived. [59]

The first helicopter carrying that motor had struck a warehouse ninety minutes earlier. [72] Its pilot and flight engineer were dead; another crew was preparing to try the same approach. [88] The preliminary telemetry suggested a late, excessive correction, and the operations chief wanted Mara to confirm pilot error. [106]

Instead, she knelt beside something embroidered that had caught on a bolt outside the customs house. [122] It was a flight engineer's patch, its winged gear darkened with blood. [134] She had worn that emblem before she traded a vibrating cabin for an investigator's desk. [149] Back then, she had believed enough instruments could make surprise obsolete. [160]

The patch lay sixty meters upwind of the wreck. [169] Rotor wash could have carried it there, but the location troubled her more than the clean arrows on the weather display. [190] She photographed it where it hung, bagged it, and asked the chief for twenty minutes to survey the approach. [209]

"You have twelve," he said over the radio. [217]

With a handheld anemometer and strips torn from her evidence tape, Mara began documenting wind patterns between buildings. [235] The customs house divided the offshore wind; the warehouse bent one branch back toward the water. [251] Between them, container aisles squeezed both currents into narrow, accelerating streams. [262] Her measurements were exact only where she stood: nine meters per second, then two, then eleven from the opposite direction. [282] Overhead, fog folded without revealing its edges. [289]

The port's rapidly adapting logistics system had rearranged the stacks that afternoon, moving valuable cargo away from the rising water. [309] It had kept every fire lane open and accidentally built a nozzle aimed across the helicopter corridor. [326] Near the warehouse corner, the streams met in a singular convergence, pushing a turbulent seam upward through the descent path. [346] The scattered tape ends pointed toward it like witnesses finally agreeing. [357]

Mara transmitted her map, then requested that the second helicopter approach over the open harbor. [372] The chief refused: that route required shutting down the last crane feeding sandbags to the barrier. [388]

"Can you prove the wind caused the crash?" [396]

She looked at the patch inside its transparent bag, remembering how evidence became certainty only after everyone had stopped needing it. [417] She had become an investigator to cultivate foresight in action, yet her reports always arrived neatly bound around an irreversible past. [438] Tonight she could produce another explanation, or risk being wrong while there was still someone to save. [455]

"Not yet," she said, "but I can show you a hazard we don't need to fly through." [472] "Park the crane's boom, freeze the container movements, and give the pilot the offshore readings, not the city average." [491]

The chief said nothing for several seconds, while somewhere beyond the fog an engine began its patient, climbing whine. [510] Then the crane's warning bell rang twice and stopped. [519]

Mara walked to the quay, where the wind pressed steadily against her coat instead of snatching at its sleeves. [538] She radioed the gust range and watched the helicopter emerge as three blurred lights above the black harbor. [556] It lowered the replacement motor onto the waiting maintenance trolley, then withdrew over the unobstructed water. [572]

By first light, the repaired pumping station was holding the water a handspan below the crest. [588] The chief found her beside the warehouse and asked whether she would put the successful delivery in her report. [607]

"Yes," she said, "but not as proof." [614]

She would need flight data, structural measurements, and the container system's history before she could tell the dead men's families what had happened. [637] What she could recommend now was simpler: every new stack arrangement needed a wind assessment before another aircraft entered the maze. [658]

At the evidence table, she placed the patch beside her unfinished map and copied the engineer's name from the crew manifest. [679] Outside, the port began to wake, one uncertain sound at a time. [691]
