At the hidden lighthouse at dusk, Mara, the doomsday clock adjuster, faced the world's approaching end for the thousandth time. [20] The tower stood outside history, concealed so that nobody would spend a final sunset watching its hands. [37] Only its beam entered the world, guiding the last fishing boats home along a path their crews mistook for moonlight. [57] Her office permitted her to vouchsafe a few extra minutes to dying worlds, enough for apologies or an embrace. [76] It had never permitted what she did after her daughter died. [87]

During that first ending, the sea climbed their street while Mara was at the clock, granting strangers time. [105] She found only a broken puppet head, its crooked smile still wearing the paint Elsie had applied with her fingers. [125] Instead of advancing the hands to midnight, Mara turned them backward until her daughter was being born. [142] She thought she could arrange a kinder ending. [150]

In one life, Elsie became a sailor; in another, a gardener who outlived every tree she planted. [167] Mara always brought the puppet, repaired and repainted, and always lost the child who held it, even when that child was ninety. [189] Each reversal wore the universe thinner. [195] This time, the stars had begun going out before Elsie's sixth birthday, and the puppet's old fracture had opened again. [215]

Now the winding handle crumbled beneath Mara's fingers. [223] There would be no thousand and first beginning. [231]

Behind the clock waited a pane of hush, the lighthouse lens, made from the seconds she had stolen from all those endings. [253] Mara could read it only through nested patterns, following each world's branching history inward to the moment she had abandoned it. [274] There, a thousand Elsies waited in the instant before death, their mouths open around words Mara had never heard. [293] For the first time, she wanted something smaller than resurrection: to whisper a lullaby across a thousand lifetimes. [311]

She sang, but her voice merely fogged the glass. [320] The pane admitted only what belonged to the histories inside it, and the clock's immortal keeper belonged to none. [339] Even her visits home had been borrowed time. [347]

But the puppet had belonged to Elsie. [354] Its wood had absorbed her touch in every world, each repair sealing another history beneath the paint. [371] Mara pressed its broken cheek against the pane, and a small circle of glass softened around it. [388] From the hollow head came a breath that smelled of milk, then apples, then an old woman's lavender soap. [407]

Mara almost pulled it back. [412] When the glass closed, she would have nothing left to hold, not even this beautifully flawed face she had mistaken for her daughter's. [435] She had carried it like proof that her thefts were acts of love. [448] Yet no Elsie had ever asked her to turn the handle. [459] The gardener had asked her to stay beside the bed; the sailor had asked her not to be afraid. [478] Her youngest had asked her to finish the song. [487]

Mara set the hands moving and put her lips to the opening in the puppet's skull. [503] The lullaby was not about safety, as she had remembered, but about a boat setting out while someone watched from shore. [524] She sang it badly, stopping once to swallow, and the cracked wood carried every imperfection into the glass. [542]

Light followed the grain, divided at old repairs, and found a thousand small rooms in the dark. [559] In one, a toothless woman lifted her head; in another, a child stopped listening to the water underneath the door. [579] None of the doors opened onto a morning. [587] But the mouths that had been open in terror began, here and there, to shape the words with her. [606]

Bruised awe held Mara upright as the puppet dissolved beneath her lips and the lighthouse stairs unraveled into evening. [625] She had imagined forgiveness would feel like being allowed to begin again. [637] Instead, it felt like staying. [642] Across the hush of time's final ripple, she held the last note until a thousand tired voices no longer needed hers. [663] Then she let them sleep. [668]
