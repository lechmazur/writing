After the first snowmelt, Mara carried a kerosene lamp burner into the bronze grotto under starlight, where the village brought burdens nobody could see. [24] She was a fractal weaver: whatever she stitched repeated inside itself, smaller and smaller, until even a whisper could have a thousand rooms. [47] Tonight she meant to collect the weights of different burdens, so the village might learn which neighbors needed help without demanding their stories. [70] Each donor had knotted a thread around one wrist and given her its other end. [85] She had promised to return the threads at dawn. [94]

The grotto had been cast from the bells of drowned ships, and moonwhite whale ribs held its roof above the meltwater. [115] Through a missing rib, the northern star looked down as steadily as an eye painted on a cradle. [133]

Mara fitted the burner to a tin of kerosene and stretched her warp across its toothed brass crown. [151] Its circular wick warmed the threads evenly; unequal heat would let one person's sorrow leak into another's. [168] It was the last sound piece of the lamp recovered from her daughter's boat. [182]

Over its flame she wove a shingled hush, laying little overlapping silences so that each shed sound into the next. [202] Pointed inward, the shingles gathered what traveled down the villagers' threads into separate cups. [216] There, unheard, each burden began to calcify, leaving a chalky deposit she could weigh against slivers of whalebone. [234] The baker's hunger weighed less than his shame at hiding it. [245] The ferryman's fear for his failing hands outweighed the widow's grief, which came in brief, almost weightless flakes. [263]

Mara recorded everything, then unwound the thread she had kept beneath her sleeve. [276] Her daughter had asked to stay ashore that night, frightened by the ice knocking against the harbor piles. [294] Mara had needed the delivery money and called the water safe. [305] She had told herself that tonight was only an experiment, but she wanted a number large enough to count as punishment. [326]

When she tied herself into the warp, white stone climbed the cups, the burner, her fingers. [342] The hush unfolded around her, repeating its silent chambers through every passage she tried. [356] She was boundary-lessly trapped: nothing barred her way, yet every step returned her to the lamp. [372] Along the villagers' threads came tiny jerks, and she understood that her collecting cups were drawing their owners inward too. [392] Soon the village would be as still as the ships beneath the bay. [405]

Mara pulled at a seam, but every broken stitch split into smaller stitches, and the stone tightened around her wrists. [425] Then a draft bent the burner's flame, and she remembered her mother repairing roofs while the fishing boats slept. [444] A shingle was not a wall; it kept a house dry by giving rain somewhere else to go. [462] She could escape by orienting properly, turning the hush toward an outside instead of searching for an edge. [480]

But in the multiplied grotto, a thousand northern stars shone above a thousand lamps. [494] She moved her head until the reflections slid across the bronze and one real star remained still. [511] Using a whalebone sliver, she lifted the first shingle toward that steady light, and its countless descendants lifted with it. [531] The cups cracked, but the stone on her hands held fast. [542]

Her own thread still pointed inward, pulled taut around the little punishment she had mistaken for a useful task. [561] To turn it outward meant letting someone hear what she had never said. [574] "I sent her," Mara said, and tilted the last shingle toward the opening in the ribs. [590] Her words traveled down the offered threads, not as a weight transferred but as a voice that could be answered. [610] The stone loosened into flakes, which the meltwater carried between the whale ribs like scraps of an unfinished letter. [629]

By dawn the villagers were waiting at the entrance, their threads slack in their hands. [644] Mara brought out her notebook, its columns blurred, and admitted that none of the burdens had held a constant weight. [664] The baker asked whether she had eaten, while the ferryman steadied her elbow with his unreliable hands. [681] She let him, and when the widow reached for the cooling lamp, Mara opened her fingers. [697]
