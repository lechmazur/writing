At the moment a pin was heard in the silent corridor, Ada realized the astronomer's broken sextant was repairing itself inside her tool bag. [24] Like most old brass in town, it healed in moonlight; the bent hinge pin had simply been pushed out. [43] Outside the stormlit tower, clouds were pulling apart, exposing the moon above the municipal labyrinth. [58] Ada checked the recovered pin for wear and put it in her pocket. [71] She was the labyrinth maintenance worker, not the astronomer, and nobody reimbursed her for lost fittings. [87]

Below her, sleeping citizens' dreams moved through brick passages toward the sorrow counter. [100] By breakfast, the counter would have converted their losses into numbers the leave office could use. [116] Ten units bought a day away from work; a hundred bought a week. [129] Quantified sorrow kept the queues short. [135]

When Ada's mother died, her father's reading had been zero. [145] He went straight back to pruning street trees, and eight-year-old Ada learned to call that lack of love. [163] Now he was dead too, and the roots of his trees were breaking into her passages. [179] Their dreams scattered at every junction: a red mitten, a steaming cup, rain darkening somebody's shoulders. [195] The counter registered these intrusions as weather and subtracted them. [205]

At dawn, contractors were coming to sever the roots. [214] Ada had signed the order, then spent supper remembering how her father used to rest his forehead against bark. [233] Before they cut anything, she wanted to discover what trees witness, and why he had trusted them with his face. [253]

The astronomer had once used the sextant to chart dreams before designing the counter. [267] Its mirrors measured the angle between a dream and the place that dreamed it, rather than between stars. [285] Ada fetched graph paper, opened the tower shutters, and began mapping scattered dreams. [298]

Each measurement placed another fragment along the same avenue, always looking down from the branches. [313] The red mitten belonged to Ada. [319] The cup was her father's, balanced on a wall outside the hospital while he waited for visiting hours. [337] Under the next tree, he was washing that cup with his thumb because he had forgotten the kitchen brush. [356]

Ordinary things, until the sextant aligned them. [363] Then the avenue held twenty years of a man stopping beneath the same branches after work, unable to go home yet. [384] The trees had witnessed the pauses between his duties, the minutes no employer owned and no daughter had thought to ask about. [406]

Ada lowered the sextant and sat on the cold stair. [416] She remembered him arriving late with bread, and how carefully she had refused to look up. [432]

But why had the counter read zero? [439] By mapping scattered dreams backward through its intake channels, she found where the machine separated human suffering from weather. [458] Anything recurring without alteration went down the weather pipe: seasonal rain, persistent wind, her father's nightly dream of setting two cups on a table. [482] The weather pipe emptied into the avenue's irrigation trench. [491] For years the trees had been drinking what the town refused to count, mingling it with their own green memories. [511] All that rejected sorrow had swollen the roots until the brickwork gave. [523]

It was not an error she could correct by tightening a screw. [535] Ada had spent sixteen years keeping the counter accurate to the nearest tenth; she had never asked what it failed to admit. [557] She could bypass the filter, but then every shower would earn somebody a week off, and the numbers would still pretend to know. [580]

Instead, she began to deconstruct the counter, laying its labeled pieces on the floor where the morning inspector could see them. [601] On the fault report she attached her map and wrote that the root damage was a symptom, not the cause. [621] She canceled the cutting order under her authority to prevent damage to municipal equipment. [635] That would hold the contractors until somebody senior came down to argue. [647]

While the counter was out of service, an old regulation required the leave clerk to take people at their word. [667] Ada copied that paragraph onto the office door, then requested the morning off. [680] Outside, the rain had stopped, and she carried her tea to the avenue without trying to stop her hands from shaking. [701] She sat beneath her father's tree until the tea cooled, giving it one more small thing to remember. [719]
