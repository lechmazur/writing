Over the hush of winter-cracked fields, Ivo heard the greenhouse bell ringing, although its clapper lay on his workbench. [19] Inside, the alchemical orchard stood beneath glass shrouded in endless night, its silver apples hoarding the daylight his village needed. [39] For generations, villagers had traded fallen silver leaves for flour, but winter had stopped deliveries. [54] He had come to smash the fruit, but the first blow of his hammer produced a woman's laugh. [72]

An ashen-veil ghost bride appeared between two branches, wearing the face he had dreamed about since childhood. [89] "Still using force when listening would do, my darling," she said, lovingly venomous, and vanished before he could ask her name. [110] Where her hand had rested, a carved stone bead hung from a twig on a thread of white hair. [129] Its grooves fitted the scars across his palm. [137]

When he closed his fist around it, the apples hummed. [147] The keeper's manual called such disturbances resonant awakenings: matter remembering the hands and voices that had changed it. [165] Ivo had dismissed the phrase as an alchemist's excuse for faulty glass. [177] Now his own voice rose from the roots, older and unbearably happy, speaking wedding vows. [192]

The bride returned only through ephemeral cameo appearances, each summoned by another note from the bead. [208] In one, she pressed its unbroken thread into his hand beneath a summer tree. [222] In another, she lay fevered while he built the greenhouse around her bed. [235] He saw himself distilling sunrise into apples, promising that enough stolen mornings would purchase one more morning together. [253] He had kept her breathing until breathing became impossible, then kept what remained. [266]

"You called it love," she said from a pear blossom rimmed with frost. [279] "I called for the door." [284]

The bead warmed, and six other lives unfolded beneath his fingers: six apprentices who had reached this tree, remembered, and fled. [305] He had learned to ghost her so completely that even death became another way of leaving without goodbye. [323] Yet each new body carried the same palm scars, where he had gripped her wedding bead while refusing her last request. [344] The orchard did not imprison him; it merely kept returning what he would not settle. [359]

"Tell me how to repay a debt across lifetimes," he said. [370]

For once, her answering smile held no sting. [378] "Stop making me tell you what I want." [386]

He found the door within her tree: a seam in the trunk, sealed with the same vows the roots were singing. [407] The bead was no key, then, but a tuning stone, carved to carry two voices at their wedding. [425] Her note had been trying to finish their song for centuries. [436] His had held it open. [441]

He put the bead against the seam and sang the words he had never permitted himself to imagine. [459] "You owe me no more mornings." [465]

The pitch wavered, and the bride appeared beside him, suddenly substantial enough to brush his cheek. [481] He wanted to seize that hand, and wanting was the oldest thing in him. [495] Instead, he opened his scarred palm. [501] Her voice joined his, not forgiving everything, not promising another meeting, simply finishing. [514]

The seam opened into morning. [519] All along the branches, silver skins split, and centuries of captive dawn poured through the glass without breaking it. [538] Outside, the fields cracked louder as frost loosened its teeth. [548] Ivo watched light pass through the bride until there was scarcely a bride to watch. [563]

"There," she whispered, with the fierce tenderness he remembered from their first life. [576] "You can learn." [579]

Then she was gone, and this time he did not mistake her absence for something he had the right to repair. [600] At his feet, an emptied apple held seven brown seeds in its ordinary flesh. [614] He gathered them before going outside to find the hungry people who would have to decide what he deserved. [633] For now, there was soil to thaw and a greenhouse to open. [645] He walked toward their astonished shouting with tears bright on his face, carrying what could grow rather than what he could keep. [667]
