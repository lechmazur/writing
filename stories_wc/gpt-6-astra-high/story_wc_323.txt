In the benthic city of Nacre, I taught navigation to children who had never seen a horizon. [17] Our maps were made of echoes, our north was a beacon bolted to basalt, and our sky was six kilometers of water. [39] When an earthquake silenced that beacon, my mother's survey boat crumpled against a cliff its sonar insisted was open sea. [59] I inherited her classroom and a single boat anchor chain link, salvaged long ago from the harbor where she had grown up. [81] She had used its weight to steady a plumb line; I kept it under my desk, unwilling to find another use for it. [104]

After discovering aliens in the newly opened trench, we learned that darkness was only one arrangement of distance. [122] Our visitor pinched the water between two limbs and showed us a star through a crease. [138] Only light crossed; the crushing sea remained where it belonged. [148] It called these openings unfolded horizons, and offered to teach us the edge geometry that held them steady. [166] I volunteered because I wanted to learn to navigate by starlight, something no broken beacon could extinguish. [183]

Then fractures from the earthquake reached our heating wells, and a private longing became the city's last practical hope. [202] We had nine days to move everyone across the echo maze to another settlement; above us, the permanent surface storms offered no refuge. [225] The visitor's apertures fitted our submarines, but each showed the constellations at a different angle. [240] Its body sensed those rotations instinctively, as mine sensed falling; without that sense, a pilot could steer toward a star and strike stone. [263] We could see the heavens, but could not trust them. [273]

For seven days I calculated until the numbers swam, while frost feathered the classroom windows. [288] I demanded that the visitor expand each opening, convinced that more stars would reveal the error. [304] Instead, familiar constellations flowered into mirrored copies, beautiful and useless. [314] "Balancedly curious," it said through its translator, separating two limbs while bracing the others against the floor. [331] It meant curiosity needed a counterweight, not a smaller appetite. [341] I resented the lesson until I caught myself promising my pupils that the new charts would be infallible. [359] My mother had trusted an instrument like that. [367]

That evening, I took her chain link from beneath the desk. [378] Its oval sagged where a storm had stretched it, and three hammer dents marked one shoulder. [394] I hung it from my mother's plumb line and suddenly understood what our polished circular viewing collars lacked. [412] A circle looked unchanged when the alien geometry turned or reflected it; this battered oval had an unmistakable orientation. [431] It could tell us what the opening did to light. [441]

The visitor helped me fold a horizon inside the link, then trace the transformed edge against its measured shape. [460] By correcting those distortions, we recovered one small, honest patch of sky. [472] We tested it against the surviving harbor beacons before casting irregular collars for the other boats. [488] I insisted on separate soundings too, and taught each pilot to distrust any position supported by only one kind of evidence. [509] For the first time, I felt balancedly curious: eager for the sky, grateful for instruments that could contradict it. [528]

On the ninth morning, I led the convoy through the ridge where my mother had died. [544] The chain link hung before my chair, heavy enough to remain steady when my hands did not. [561] Inside it, three stars trembled above an invisible world of storms. [572] Their angles gave us our course, while careful soundings kept us clear of the cliffs' broken crowns. [589] Behind us, Nacre went dark one district at a time. [599] We reached the neighboring settlement with every passenger alive, and I entered every course correction in the log before allowing myself to cry. [622]

Later, my pupils crowded around the link, asking how far the opening could expand. [636] "We will measure," I told them, and made room. [645] The stars offered no explanation for my mother's death, nor any sign that they had noticed our escape. [663] Yet, through a piece of iron once meant to hold a vessel still, they showed us how to go on. [683]
