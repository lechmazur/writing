When the river began climbing the church steps, Ada took her mother's smallest legacy to the locksmith who never displayed a key. [22] It was a lock of hair tied with ribbon, cut when her mother had qualified to build bridges. [40] She had worn her hair short afterward, so nothing could catch in the machinery. [54] Ada remembered that practical courage, but scarcely recognized it anymore. [64]

Then a bridge had fallen, taking eleven people with it, and courage had become something her mother could no longer bear. [85] Although the inquiry blamed defective iron, she surrendered her next invention to the city's Repository of Enveloped Secrets. [103] There, ideas too frightening to attempt were sealed away until their inventors died, then burned. [118] Her mother's funeral had been yesterday; the burning would be tomorrow. [129]

The invention was a neighborhood of houses that rose with floodwater, each moored to the others instead of the crumbling bank. [150] Ada had seen the little wooden models floating in a washtub, their windows improbably bright with reflected sun. [168] She wanted that impossible domestic peace: a kettle singing while the river passed harmlessly beneath the floor. [185]

The locksmith examined the hair, then closed his shop without asking for payment. [198] "Taking secrets back is forbidden," Ada said. [205] "Most useful doors are," he replied, tucking a biscuit into his pocket. [217] He was unassumingly bold, this small man in darned sleeves; she could not decide whether he was kind or simply impossible to frighten. [240] He would tell her neither his name nor what he had once deposited. [253]

At the observatory, he told her to watch for the first star seen at twilight. [268] "The Repository opens toward whatever is still possible," he explained. [278] "The first light gives a hint; the others only multiply your choices." [290] When a star appeared above the eastern dome, its light fell through a narrow slit onto an unmarked stone. [309] The locksmith pressed it, revealing stairs that were not on any municipal plan. [322]

Below lay a shadowed nexus under starlight, though they had descended beneath the earth. [336] Countless corridors branched across the hush of cosmic possibility, each lined with envelopes breathing gently in their slots. [354] Somewhere, a clock struck every hour it might have been. [364] Ada heard her mother laughing down one passage and drowning down another. [376] The place was built to disorient anyone who wanted certainty before proceeding. [388]

The locksmith loosened the ribbon, letting one end of the hair spring free. [401] "Hair keeps growing while we hesitate," he said, and laid the strand across his palm. [416] "It remembers the way forward." [421] It bent toward a corridor smelling of wet timber; Ada followed, holding the ribbon so the fragile guide would not escape. [442] At its end, an envelope bore her mother's careful lettering and a lock no larger than a tear. [460]

The locksmith fed the hair into the keyhole, but the lock tightened until Ada felt the ribbon tug against her fingers. [481] Through the paper came the noise of iron breaking. [490] "What if I hurt someone again?" asked her mother's voice. [500] Ada almost promised that the houses would be safe. [509] Then she saw the locksmith's hand: two fingers missing, the remaining ones patiently supporting hers. [524] Whatever door he had opened, it had offered no protection against the world. [537]

"We will test one empty house first," Ada said. [546] "Then another, with people who know the risks, and we will change what fails." [560] The lock loosened, but did not open. [567] Ada understood that the fear now holding it was hers. [577] She wanted her mother back more fiercely than she wanted any invention, and had mistaken rescue for reunion. [595] "You cannot tell me whether I am doing this right," she whispered. [607] "I will have to learn." [612]

The envelope opened, releasing not a ghost but sheets of drawings, crowded with calculations and small, impatient corrections. [630] Her mother had never been certain; she had only once been willing. [642] Ada gathered the pages while the locksmith ate his biscuit, looking politely away. [655]

When they emerged, the second star was just appearing, though Ada felt she had spent years below. [672] By morning she had borrowed a shed, recruited two skeptical carpenters, and tied the ribbon around her mother's drawings. [691] The river kept rising. [695] Inside the shed, they began with a floor. [703]
