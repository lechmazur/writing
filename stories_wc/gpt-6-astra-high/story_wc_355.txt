In the final stillness before the fireworks were due to ignite, someone lifted the latch of Mara's shadowed workshop. [19] Through the roof a shell had opened, stars shone on instruments borrowed from three religions and both sides of the war. [40] An eclectic pacifist, Mara had made army bugles into hearing horns and stretched violin strings across a dismantled gun carriage. [60] She used them to catalog the varieties of silence, hoping to learn which kinds meant safety. [76] Her labels were intuitively descriptive: bread rising; an audience withholding applause; a room that has stopped expecting someone. [94] That last specimen was her own. [100]

The girl at the door wore an enemy officer's coat, its sleeves folded back four times. [116] "They said you could recover what brass remembers," she said, laying a small diary on Mara's bench. [133] Its locked diary clasp bore the insignia of the battery that had destroyed Mara's roof and killed her apprentice. [152]

Mara folded her hands with the exaggerated care of an actress showing an audience that they held no weapon. [171]

"My father," the girl explained, touching the insignia. [179] "He died in prison before they let me visit." [188]

Mara had cataloged the silence after his sentence as relief. [198] Now it stood before her in boots too large for its feet. [210]

"I need to know if he left anything for me," the girl said. [223]

Mara nearly told her to take it elsewhere, although nowhere else had a listening carriage or anyone patient enough to use one. [245] Instead, she offered the apprentice's stool and asked the girl's name. [256]

"Neri," she answered, sitting on its very edge. [264]

Memory brass took impressions from its owner's voice, but a locked clasp kept them tangled until the matching key separated them. [285] Without that key, Mara could only coax the impressions apart by tuning frequencies until each buried vibration found a sympathetic string. [306] The faintest recollections required silence; once the fireworks began, there would be nothing to hear until the festivities ended. [325]

Neri glanced toward the square, where torches waited beside the rocket frames. [337] "They are celebrating men like him being gone." [345]

Mara wanted to say they had earned their celebration, but put the brass against her stethoscope instead. [362] She bowed one string, tightened another, and lowered a cracked altar bell over the clasp to catch its answering tremor. [382]

Orders hissed out first: ranges, elevations, permission to fire. [391] Neri recoiled, but Mara kept turning the peg, furious at the hope that some hidden objection might make this easier. [411]

There was no objection. [415]

Then the officer's voice changed, losing its parade ground certainty. [425]

"Neri, your left boot leaks because I patched it with the wrong glue." [438]

The girl pulled that foot beneath the stool. [446]

"Ask the cobbler for fish glue, not the black stuff in my desk." [459]

A breath scraped the bell; Mara recognized the dry, shallow rhythm of someone trying not to frighten a child. [478]

"There are other things I ought to tell you, but whenever I begin, I can only think of your feet being wet." [500]

Mara's bow stopped. [503] She had expected either a monster's orders or a penitent's confession, something she could label and put away. [521] This small, inadequate tenderness offered neither. [527]

Neri asked whether loving her meant he could not have done those things. [540]

Mara wanted to offer a splendid answer, worthy of her peace banners, but the child deserved something less polished. [559]

"It means he loved you," she said. [566] "It doesn't undo what he did." [572]

Neri stared at the clasp, and Mara realized she had been waiting for the girl to apologize for bringing it. [592] She loosened her grip on the bow. [599]

"You can miss him here." [604]

Across the square, the master of fireworks lifted his arm, his black sleeve crossing the stars. [620] There was time for one more fragment, and Mara set the bow against the string again. [636]

Neri covered Mara's hand with hers, and for once the listener did not pretend to know what a silence meant. [656] She waited until the girl found words. [663]

"Please keep it safe until tomorrow." [669]

Mara wrapped the diary in the cloth she used for her finest instruments and cleared a place beside her stool. [689]

"Stay for the lights," she said, moving the fish glue within reach. [701]
