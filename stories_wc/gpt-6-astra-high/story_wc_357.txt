On the orchard planet Vey, floating rock arches turned above the trees, and every midsummer gravity inverted for one hour. [20] Mara, an intrepid orchard pilot who routinely threaded those arches to pollinate the highest blossoms, had never feared that hour before. [41] This year, the trees were dying, and her mother's hands, weakened by hunger, could barely buckle Mara into the skiff. [61] Both afflictions had followed the long drought, though the new reservoir now held enough water for every grove. [79]

"Water isn't all they lost," her mother said, pressing a broken arrow tip into Mara's palm. [95] Its porous stone wore a green film, and a bean shoot wrapped around it had grown twice its length overnight. [115] Their ancestors had fired such arrows into inaccessible boughs to establish new orchards, but nobody remembered where the coating came from. [136] Mara intended to discover its source, and with it the resilience their carefully watered trees had somehow lost. [154]

At noon, loose fruit lifted from the ground, and the reservoir strained upward against its retaining net. [171] Beyond the dam, the last trickle in the old riverbed climbed toward the arches, revealing a route invisible all year. [191] Mara followed it into the whirling silence, navigating by water flow as rotating stone made nonsense of her compass. [210] Leaves wheeled around her canopy without touching it, each caught in a current she could neither see nor trust. [229]

When the stream divided, she dipped the arrow tip into each branch. [241] Its green film brightened only in the narrower channel, which twisted through an arch scarcely wider than her wings. [260] She folded them and let the rising water carry her through. [271]

Inside lay a hollow threaded with roots; above its rim, an ancient orchard clung to the drifting stone. [289] Most hung dry. [292] The surviving stream threaded their tips, carrying tiny pulses that Mara felt through the skiff's hull. [308] She pressed the arrowhead against the wet wood, and pale filaments unfurled from its coating. [323] Where they touched, fresh root hairs emerged. [330]

Through her fingertips came a gathering of echoes: one root's tremor returning from another, altered, answered, passed onward. [348] Each returning pulse quickened the filaments' growth; alone in her palm, the fragment had barely stirred. [364] Not a miraculous stone, then, but a living graft that accelerated plant growth when the trees called for repair. [383] The annual flood had carried it throughout the orchards until their reservoir trapped the river. [398] They had saved the water and severed its conversation. [407]

Mara checked the clock: seventeen minutes until gravity returned. [416] She could scrape enough coating into her sample jar to rescue her own grove, perhaps even secure her mother's livelihood. [436] But a jar would hold no answering roots, and every isolated orchard would eventually face another drought alone. [454]

She opened the radio channel she usually muted when other pilots called her reckless. [468] "I need help opening the dam before the water falls." [478]

Her mother answered first, not with permission, but with the gate's maintenance frequency. [491] By the time Mara followed the stream back, six pollen skiffs hovered beside the reservoir. [506] The gate motor had failed under the upward pressure; its manual release required someone outside a cockpit. [523]

Mara clipped her harness to another pilot's towline and stepped into the turning air. [537] For once, she could not pretend that courage meant keeping her fate entirely in her own hands. [554] Suspended above the release wheel, she turned it while the skiffs pulled together on the gate. [570] Her mother's voice counted down the seconds, steady despite her shaking hands. [582]

The gate rose. [585] Water rushed toward the arches, and Mara dropped the arrow tip into it, surrendering the family charm to the current that had first nourished it. [610] Far overhead, the hollow filled, and white threads spread through the turning water. [623] Then gravity returned, bringing rain heavy with the living graft. [633]

The other pilot reeled Mara aboard, and together they flew beneath the falling leaves toward home. [649] Recovery would take seasons, her mother warned when they met beneath the dripping boughs. [663] Mara took the trembling hands in hers and did not mistake their trembling for weakness. [678] Above them, the arches settled into their slow rotation, each orchard holding a little of the others' rain. [696]
