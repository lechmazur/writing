Forty years after the collapse of civilization, Mara was the last beacon-tower custodian, although the harbor below her held thistles instead of ships. [23] She had inherited the keys and the maintenance manuals, but nobody had survived to teach her the signals. [41]

Each evening she crossed its cracked causeway to the glass humming pavilion, where wind drew a thin music from the surviving panes. [63] She had brought the beacon's lamp down here when the tower stairs collapsed, one component at a time. [81] Beneath that music, a broken crystal prism preserved the voices of everyone who had ever tended the beacon. [99] Mara could hear them because she was retrocognitively blessed, as the old manuals called the affliction. [115] She could also see whatever light had once entered the crystal, provided she endured the voices long enough to find it. [136] Mostly, she listened for her daughter. [142]

"Still here," Linn had whispered during the fever, tapping three times against the prism beside her bed. [159] The girl had died before morning, but the crystal remembered. [169] For seven winters, that had been reason enough not to repair it. [181]

Then three amber flashes appeared beyond the harbor. [189] They returned on three cloudless nights, always beneath the rising Swan, always just as Mara uncovered Linn's bedside lantern. [208] The village headwoman called them marsh gas and asked for the lantern's oil to grease the communal grain mill. [227] Mara refused, ashamed of how much the refusal resembled all her others. [239] To prove that coincidences have meaning, she told herself, was not the same as refusing to bury a child. [258]

She carried the prism into the pavilion's central cradle, where its ancient mount could turn beneath an open roof. [277] The beacon's keepers had navigated its stored images by tracking star paths, using the changing sky to locate particular nights. [297] Mara aligned the crystal with the Swan and turned the mount backward through seven winters. [312] Her daughter's breathing filled the pavilion. [318] She nearly stopped there. [322]

Instead she turned forward again, toward last week's sky. [331] Images fluttered through the fractures: gulls, lightning, snow, the headwoman dragging driftwood home. [344] Then came three amber flashes, seen not across the marsh but shining through a battered metal shutter on the distant seawall. [365] A hand was moving that shutter. [371] Not Linn's small hand, not a ghost, but someone living who knew the old distress signal. [387] The three flashes meant: We have children. [394] They had been answering her lantern, mistaking her nightly vigil for the work she was supposed to be doing. [413]

Mara searched older skies until she found a keeper teaching an apprentice the reply. [427] One steady beam toward the Swan would tell the stranded people that the causeway was passable at low tide. [446] Without it, they would remain beyond the flooded channels until winter sealed their boats in ice. [462]

The prism's fractures scattered any beam into useless glitter. [471] In the maintenance drawer lay the last repair material: a shard of hush, silence made solid in the furnaces of the vanished city. [494] Melted into damaged crystal, it would bind the fractures, but its silence would swallow every voice and image stored inside. [514] Mara had read that warning every winter. [521] Now she understood that keeping the beacon broken had also been a choice. [534]

She fetched the oil, then stopped at the village mill. [544] The headwoman listened without interrupting, and finally handed her the communal tinderbox. [556] "Bring us people who can help turn it," she said. [566]

Back in the humming pavilion, Mara held the prism until Linn whispered once more. [580] She did not ask the memory to forgive her. [589] She set the shard of hush between the broken edges and used the lantern to rekindle the beacon's furnace. [608] For a moment the glass sang in all its remembered voices. [619] Then there was only wind. [624]

Mara aligned the repaired prism with the Swan. [632] Across the harbor, a steady amber road opened over the water. [643] The answer came slowly: three flashes, then three more, lower down, as someone carried the lantern toward the shore. [662] By dawn, the villagers were laying planks across the causeway's worst gaps. [674] Mara worked beside them, listening for footsteps instead of a voice. [685] Thistle seeds caught on her sleeves, each carrying its small, unreasonable sail. [697]
