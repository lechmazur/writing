At the coastal kayak launch, Mara fastened her father's life jacket while he inspected the astronomical compass her mother had given him forty years earlier. [25] Inside its brass lid, beneath the star tables, Ruth had engraved two words: COME HOME. [40] Elias had obeyed between voyages, which he now understood was not quite the same as staying. [56]

Tonight he had promised to take Ruth's ashes home, to the cottage surrendered to the sea ten years ago. [75] Its foundations lay beneath the sheltered inlet, beyond the abandoned road whose utility poles still stood with empty crossarms. [94] Mara surveyed shellfish beds here for a living; she knew the channels and had chosen slack water. [111] Nevertheless, Elias insisted that this last passage belonged to him. [121]

He settled into the tandem kayak's front seat and lifted the compass toward the first stars. [137] Before the stroke, he could turn their positions into bearings almost without thought. [150] Now the figures detached themselves from their meanings, like passengers stepping off a train. [164] He tried again, pressing his stiff right thumb against the wheel until the brass bit him. [180] Then mist erased the stars altogether. [186] He had reached the hour when expectations collapse quietly, without even the courtesy of a storm. [202]

"A broken navigator," he said, laying the instrument across his knees. [213] Mara held them against the ramp with one blade of her paddle. [225] "You can still come with me," she said. [233]

He nearly asked to return to the car, but Ruth's small canvas pouch rested against his chest. [250] For once, he wanted to align heart and action instead of finding a respectable reason to leave. [267] He nodded, and Mara pushed them into the silver water. [277]

Without calculations to shelter behind, he felt the backlash of regrets: missed birthdays, postponed visits, Ruth learning the language of hospitals without him. [300] Each remembered departure seemed to pull against the kayak's forward motion. [311] He gripped the compass until his left hand hurt. [320]

"I should have been there," he said. [327] Behind him, the paddle stopped. [332] "Yes," Mara said, and let the answer stand. [340]

Through the mist, a pole emerged, then another. [348] He let his gaze gravitate upward, searching for something grand enough to absolve him, but Mara kept their bow beside the drowned road. [371] "We're navigating by utility poles," she said when he asked how she could be certain. [386] "Five to the bend, then two along the lane." [395]

He remembered those poles carrying the telephone wire, Ruth's voice crossing their little distances before crossing an ocean. [413] She had always said everything was fine. [420] He had always been grateful not to ask again. [429]

Mara began humming in Ruth's merrily melancholic fashion, making an old leaving song sound almost like a welcome. [447] At the seventh pole she steadied the kayak. [455] Mara shone her headlamp into the water, revealing the pale square of their doorstep. [469] The sight undid him more thoroughly than the empty sky. [479] Ruth had sat there taking off muddy boots; Mara had sat there waiting for his taxi. [495] He could name stars invisible from this hemisphere, but had forgotten the doorstep was white. [510]

His stiff hand fumbled the pouch's drawstring, and the familiar anger rose, demanding a task he could still command. [529] Instead he passed the compass back to Mara. [537] "Would you put that somewhere safe, and help me with this?" [548]

She tucked it into her dry bag, loosened the knot, and returned the pouch to his good hand. [566] He tipped Ruth's ashes into the water, where they opened like a cloud and slowly lost their edges. [584] Elias waited for peace, but what came was cold, and the knowledge that tomorrow would still need living. [602]

"Could I come round on Sunday?" he asked. [610] "Not if you're going to tell me how to mend the gutter," Mara said. [624] He almost smiled; the gutter had been leaking for months, and he had offered advice from three different ports. [643] "I'll make the tea." [647]

She resumed paddling, and after a while resumed the song. [657] Elias could not quite find the tune, but he joined her anyway. [669] Above them a star appeared and vanished, while the next pole waited within the small reach of their light. [688]
