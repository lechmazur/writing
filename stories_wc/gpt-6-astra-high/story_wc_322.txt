Mara was a fever dream architect, trained to give delirium rooms with exits, but she could not build one her mother recognized. [22] So she brought her to the optical illusion gallery, where a staircase could ascend forever provided nobody moved their head. [42] Her mother studied it without distress. [48] "Someone will have to dust all those steps," she said. [58]

The gallery practiced rational enchantment: an impossibility became usable when its contradictions could be measured and kept in balance. [77] Its exhibits were realistically imaginary, possessing weight and temperature only while two observers sustained equally convincing explanations of the same illusion. [98] Making one interpretation permanent, however, required dismantling the other. [107] At one exhibit, a painted sea wet a visitor's hand while another visitor folded its waves into a square. [126] Mara hoped their shared past might similarly exist between a woman who remembered raising someone and someone who remembered being raised. [147]

On a velvet tray lay sinew thread drawn from the tendon removed during her mother's last hand operation. [165] Unlike cotton, the attendant explained, it retained the history of opposing forces: what a hand had held, what it had released. [186] Mara signed the form permitting that history to be stretched. [196] Her mother signed beneath her, carefully copying the unfamiliar surname. [206]

They sat on opposite sides of a loom before a picture that was either a cradle or an empty boat. [226] By weaving patterns, Mara could supply the gallery with structures their separate memories might inhabit together. [242] The attendant showed her how to cross a remembered gesture over a present one. [256] She began with the knot her mother had taught her for fastening shoes. [269]

The thread warmed. [272] Between them appeared a kitchen table, solid enough to carry the weight of her mother's elbows. [288] "There," Mara said, setting both hands upon it, "you used to teach me here." [302] Her mother touched a dark mark in the wood. [311] "My sister burned that with the iron." [318]

Mara tightened the next crossing, replacing the sister's face with her own as expertly as she replaced a nightmare's monster with a door. [341] The table became smaller; somewhere beyond the painted cradle, a woman began calling a name neither of them answered. [360] "Adjust the tension as memories alter," the attendant said, keeping his hands folded. [373] "Otherwise the contradictions will erupt." [378]

Mara had come to discover the texture of connection, but all morning she had been testing the strength of ownership. [398] Still, she pulled. [401] The tabletop opened quietly along its grain, and from the opening rose a column of white flame. [418] Her mother moved her cup away from it. [426] The calling stopped. [429]

A brass gauge on the loom showed that the sister had vanished from her mother's accessible memories. [446] In exchange, her mother looked directly at Mara and said her name. [458] There was no pleasure in hearing it over that silence. [468]

Mara asked, "When I leave, will she still know me?" [478] "If you finish the pattern, she will have only memories in which you fit," the attendant said. [495] Mara looked at the small, obedient table, sized for a childhood and no longer large enough for anyone else's. [514]

She loosened the crossing. [518] The flame sank into the wood, and her mother's sister began calling again, impatient about something ordinary. [535] Mara let the table widen until its far edge disappeared behind the impossible staircase. [549] Then she offered her mother the loose thread. [557]

"I don't know what we're making," her mother said. [566] "Neither do I," Mara answered, and waited while the older woman chose where the next loop should go. [584] It caught around Mara's finger, tightened, then eased when her mother noticed the skin whitening. [599]

The table disappeared when they stood, their altered sightlines dissolving the agreement that had given it weight. [616] But the attendant allowed them to keep the unfinished weave. [626] Outside, her mother asked the way home, and Mara offered her elbow instead of asking her to try harder. [645] The sinew lay slack between their wrists, leaving room for either woman to move.
