After Mara's fever broke, the mountain weather station seemed to float above the clouds, tethered only by its radio wire. [20] Green aurora trembled through the northern window, staining the empty chair where her father had once mended thermometers. [38]

Morrow, the fault-tolerant oracle installed after his death, asked whether she could stand. [51] Its nine processors voted separately, but its speaker always used one gentle voice. [64] "Eight of me think you need another day in bed," it said. [76] "The ninth has noticed you hiding your boots." [84]

She had spent twelve winters here to find the weight of a swallowed truth, as though suffering could eventually balance it. [105] During the fever she had begged Morrow to weigh it, and its nursing arm had offered her a spoonful of broth. [126] Now she fetched her father's botanical illustration from beneath her mattress. [137]

He had drawn a dwarf willow, every leaf no bigger than a thumbnail, its branches creeping along the ground. [156] Beside the roots he had penciled temperatures from his collecting route: freezing at the summit, warm rain halfway down. [175] Mara had used her thumb to smudge the upright stroke of his plus sign. [189] Three degrees above zero had become three below. [197]

The altered reading had agreed with her forecast, which promised safe passage through the avalanche gully. [213] She had told him his pocket thermometer needed recalibrating. [222] Her father and five road workers had trusted her forecast. [232] All six had died beneath a thousand tons of snow. [242] She had been twenty, newly qualified, and more frightened of appearing wrong than of being wrong. [258]

"Can you tell what was here?" she asked, flattening the paper beneath Morrow's camera. [272]

"Graphite remains outside the mark," Morrow said. [279] "But I cannot reconstruct a measurement from your wish." [288]

Outside, the antenna wires began their thin, uneven singing. [297] On the forecast screen, a yellow warning flickered in the border while the center stayed reassuringly blue. [314] Morrow kept discarded predictions by occupying margins, a safeguard against mistaking agreement for independence. [328]

"Eight votes for stable snow," it said. [335] "One for immediate evacuation of the gully." [342]

Mara leaned closer, and the fever's weakness shook through her legs. [353] All eight trusted the summit instruments; the dissenter trusted a battered sensor halfway down, reporting rain at four degrees. [372] The central forecast had rejected it as defective. [380]

Morrow was peripherally aware by design, watching reflections and neglected instruments as carefully as whatever humans placed before its lens. [400] "The maintenance camera shows water dripping from that sensor's housing," it said. [412] "Liquid water, not blowing snow." [417]

Eight votes became six, then four, their swaying certainties tipping as Morrow admitted the rejected sensor's evidence. [434] Mara looked back at the willow, which survived by staying below the wind that shaped taller trees. [451] Her father had measured where the weather mattered, not merely where it was convenient. [465]

"That mark was a plus," she said. [472] "I changed it because his reading would have closed the road and exposed my mistake." [487]

Morrow waited while she found enough breath to continue. [496]

"Put that in the permanent log, under my name, and open the valley channel." [510]

She warned the road crew first, then told the dispatcher what she had done twelve winters before. [527] When he asked whether the danger was certain, she said no, and did not retreat from the warning. [545] He ordered the barriers shut. [550]

For twenty minutes there was only static, the antenna's singing, and the chatter of Morrow's printer recording her confession. [569] Then the lower camera vanished beneath a wall of moving snow. [580] The dispatcher counted every worker aloud; all had reached the shelter. [591]

Mara sank into her father's chair as the aurora faded, leaving the window colorless and terribly clear. [608]

"Has it become lighter?" Morrow asked. [614]

She pressed the drawing flat, careful this time not to rub away anything. [627] "No," she said. [630] "But it is no longer only mine to carry." [639]
