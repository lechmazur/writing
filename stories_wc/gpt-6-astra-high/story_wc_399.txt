After the prophet arrived, his followers tethered their camels around the murmuring pavilion, and its oldest voice began to fade. [20] The kinetic parapet beneath the dunes turned inward, answering the caravan's weight by bracing the pavilion against the settling sand. [40] Its wind slits, which kept the sayings of the dead in circulation, were closing one by one. [57] Sela, the half-masked line walker, felt each closure as a tightening beneath her bare feet. [72] Her line stretched between the pavilion's pillars, its ends fastened to the buried wall's counterweights. [87] For thirty years she had balanced the wall against the dunes, to preserve the echo of wisdom entrusted to her. [107] Among those voices was her mother's. [113]

The prophet waited below with his sandals in his hands, listening to a sentence that could no longer finish itself. [133] "I was told you would consecrate my first teaching here," he said. [145] "But I have brought too many people." [152] He was strangely honest for a man whose arrival had been announced by trumpets three valleys away. [169] When Sela asked him to move the caravan, he went himself to lead the first camel beyond the boundary stones. [189] The sand had already settled, however, and the parapet could not swing outward through its accumulated pressure. [206] From overhead came her mother's whisper: "Before you answer, leave room for..." [218] Then only the soft friction of grains. [225]

Sela lowered the linen half mask over her forward vision, leaving a narrow crescent open beside her temple. [243] The wall could be freed through peripheral awareness, the craft her mother had taught her on this same trembling line. [263] Sand yielded to conjured clearings only when its watcher attended to their edges; a direct look filled them again. [282] She had always worked alone, keeping each small absence alive until the counterweights completed their slow descent. [299] This time the buried chambers needed more empty space than one mind could shelter. [313] She stretched her attention toward the farthest weight, and the clearing nearest her heel collapsed. [328] Below, the prophet heard the line hum and raised his hands without touching it. [342] "Tell me where not to look," he said. [350]

Sela almost refused, remembering all the novices she had dismissed for watching too eagerly. [364] She had called their hunger carelessness, although she herself listened hardest whenever her mother's voice approached. [380] She wanted the saying intact, the breath familiar, nothing altered by another person's keeping. [394] The line sank another finger's width. [400] "Watch the shadow beside that pillar," she told him, "and let the brightness next to it remain unfinished." [418] He tried, failed, and tried again without claiming that failure meant anything grand. [431] A pale hollow opened beneath the sand. [438]

At his quiet invitation, the pilgrims returned without their animals and stood wherever Sela placed them around the pavilion. [457] She loosened her mask and tore its linen into strips, enough to shade their eyes from the temptation of the center. [478] Together they held a crescent of conjured clearings beneath the dunes, making room for the ancient weights to fall. [497] Sela walked forward, one deliberate step, then another, while the parapet opened its wind slits. [512] The voices came back thinly, carrying pauses she had never noticed when she listened only for words. [529] Her mother's sentence returned among them: "Before you answer, leave room for being wrong." [543]

Once the counterweights rested, they could let the clearings fill; the wall would hold until the dunes shifted again. [562] The prophet asked whether that was the wisdom they had saved. [573] Sela heard the weariness in his question and, for once, did not pretend the dead had answers for every stranger. [593] "It is where we begin," she said. [600]

At sunset she brought the consecration bowl, whose water was reserved for a prophet's lips, and washed the pilgrims' dusty feet. [621] "Let this consecrate the work, not the worker," she said, and the prophet knelt to refill the bowl. [639] Several pilgrims stayed to learn the line; others went to warn the approaching caravans about the boundary stones. [657] Sela sat beneath them, her uncovered face cooled by the small, uncertain drafts. [670] When her mother's voice came around again, she listened, then let it pass toward someone else. [686]
