After reality became negotiable, Mara bought her dying daughter an eternity and discovered that eternity could be a very small room. [21] The room stood inside a glass orchard library floating upon a swirling sea of clouds, where transparent branches carried books instead of fruit. [44] Each book contained a possible life for Ada; none could be opened without closing another. [59] Somewhere among them, Mara believed, was a life in which her daughter survived. [72]

Every dawn, the letterpress beneath the roots renewed the bargain, returning Ada to the instant before her final breath. [91] Only Mara and the operator remembered the intervening hours. [100] At least, that was what the contract said. [108] On the thousandth morning, Ada's motionless hand had worn a hollow in the blanket. [122] Mara went downstairs. [125]

The cryptic letterpress operator, Iven, was setting the same impeccable line of type he had set for years. [143] He answered questions with riddles because the press accepted any plain declaration spoken beside it as a proposed amendment. [162] Rejected amendments cost memories. [166] He had already forgotten his mother's face. [173]

Mara asked whether an ocean could fit inside a cup. [183] She wanted to compress eternity into a moment, give Ada every possible life, and only then let her go. [202]

Iven laid a finger against a cold glass root. [211] "Cold glass keeps the shape of warm refusals." [219]

Remembering breath on winter windows, she fetched Ada's bottled whisper. [229] The contract had required her daughter's last word as collateral; Mara had stoppered it before she could bear to listen. [249] Whenever the bottle passed a branch, the patterns in morning frost rearranged themselves. [262] Three strokes became a letter, then another, until every twig spelled ENOUGH. [274]

Around her, pages began to stir. [280] Iven had called such disturbances rustling awakenings, when unlived lives sensed an opening and strained toward it. [297]

She ran to Ada and watched the child's eyes follow a falling page. [310] They were not the eyes of someone who had slept through a thousand mornings. [324] Mara understood why the hollow in the blanket frightened her: her daughter had been trying to move. [341] She had preserved the dying, not prevented it. [349]

Iven brought the original contract up from the press. [358] Its duration was defined as all the time her daughter wanted, a phrase Mara had insisted on herself. [376] She had mistaken wanting for living, and living for never having to say goodbye. [390] The bottle had kept that wish from ever reaching the machinery. [401]

Downstairs, Iven laid a hammer beside the press, then stood back. [412] "A living voice destabilizes perfect systems," he said, and the press rejected the amendment, erasing his mother's name. [430] He looked startled, then touched the hammer again. [438]

Through the ceiling, Mara could see Ada watching her between the transparent roots. [451] She imagined pouring those countless lives into her child: marriages, gardens, heartbreaks, children of her own. [467] But they would be another weight laid on that motionless hand. [478] For once, she could give Ada something without deciding how it must be used. [492]

She struck the bottle. [496] The rupture was almost soundless; the whisper that escaped it was not. [508]

"Enough." [509]

The press tried to reset the morning, found its governing wish fulfilled, and stopped. [523] Above them, the rustling became a roar. [530] The books opened together, not to pour their lives into Ada, but to scatter them like seeds. [547] Beyond the glass, clouds swallowed pages full of roads no one had yet taken. [561]

For the first time, Ada's blanket rose with a breath Mara had not seen before. [576] Mara reached the bedside before it fell. [583] Ada's fingers closed around hers, weakly and by their own choice. [594] She did not grow old in an instant or return with a thousand borrowed memories. [609] She was seven, and tired, and her mother was finally listening. [620]

When her hand loosened, Mara kept holding it without asking anything to begin again. [634] Downstairs, Iven dismantled the press, letter by letter, leaving spaces no contract could fill. [648]
