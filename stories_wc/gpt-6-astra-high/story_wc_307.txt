By the time Mara reached the floating rock meditation platform, her father was small enough to wear her son's discarded coat. [21] Above him, the magnetic grotto opened to starlight; below him, a black pond held the stars without a tremor. [40] Sycamore roots threaded the broken roof, and their leaves made the hush that had filled her childhood bedroom. [58] He had asked her to come before he became too young to speak. [71]

Once, Professor Vale had researched memory, determined to retrieve the names his wife was losing. [86] His first successful experiment had restored one name and stripped ten years from his body. [101] The name had faded again, but the reversal continued, beyond the grotto and beyond anything medicine could reach. [119] The smaller he became, the faster the remaining years unwound. [129] Mara had buried her mother while he searched for a cure. [140]

Now he sat above the pond on the slab that its buried lodestone kept suspended. [155] His feet did not reach the edge. [162]

"Tell me you found it," she said. [169]

He pressed a smooth stone into her hand, then closed his fingers over hers. [183] The stone had been the focus of his experiment; he had carried it ever since. [198]

"I found a way to teach vanishing to appear." [207]

She nearly threw it at him. [213] For forty years he had woven truth carefully, arranging each strand so the ugly knots stayed underneath. [230]

"No riddles tonight," she said. [235]

"Then watch to the end," he answered, and together they dropped the stone. [248]

Its first rings carried nothing except starlight, but he guided her breathing into the widening intervals between them. [266] The platform steadied beneath their joined weight. [273] Through the trance, she could feel each ripple pass through her ribs without disturbing the water. [289]

"A memory arrives after the thing that made it," he whispered. [300] "Reach too soon, and you break its shape." [308]

In the third ring, her mother's hand appeared, peeling an apple in one unbroken ribbon. [323] Mara lurched forward, and the hand dissolved. [330] Her father caught her wrist with fingers already smaller than they had been. [343] She steadied her breathing, furious at the pond for requiring patience, furious at herself for still wanting miracles. [361]

The next rings showed hospital corridors, notebooks, the little rented room where he had tried to grow old again. [380] Then came a page bearing the word IRREVERSIBLE, dated six weeks earlier. [392] He had known before inviting her. [398]

She looked at him instead of the water. [406]

"You let me think this was a rescue." [414]

His coat had slipped from one narrow shoulder. [422]

"I thought you would not come just to say goodbye." [432]

Anger nearly pulled her out of the trance, but beneath it lay something harder to surrender: the weight of his wrist. [453]

"You could have let me choose," she said. [461]

He nodded, and for once offered nothing to make the truth more bearable. [474] She kept his hand in hers. [480]

Only when the outermost rings touched the sycamore roots did she understand what the stone had been holding. [498] Not a cure, nor a ghost, but everything his retreating mind had shed. [511] The pond returned it whole only when the spreading rings met their reflections, making patterns she could learn to read. [531]

Her father became an infant against her knees. [539] In the last ring, she saw herself at seven, asleep at his desk while he abandoned a lecture to carry her home. [561] It did not cancel the funeral he had missed. [570] It made him neither innocent nor absent. [577]

When the infant vanished, she kept breathing until the pond was still. [589] Then she stepped ashore carrying only his empty coat. [598]

At dawn she began a notebook, recording the interval between rings, the necessary stillness, the danger of mistaking an early pattern for the whole. [622] On its first page she wrote that there was no cure. [633] On the second, she wrote how to begin. [641]
