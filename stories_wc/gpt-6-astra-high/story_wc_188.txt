By dusk, the fortress was blooming, white bindweed spilling from its broken gunports as if surrender had finally found a useful flag. [22] Ivo arrived chanting his measurements, three for the lift, two for the drift, because a wandering fireworks designer needed some road companion. [44] Once, his daughter had supplied the rhymes. [51] Since the fever winter, he supplied only numbers. [59]

At the gate, a girl took his invitation, a letter bearing the signature of Mara, the fortress's fire spinner. [78] "She ordered a sky for tonight," the girl said. [87] "She died before she could pay you." [94]

Behind her, villagers arranged stools among the flowering cracks of the parade ground. [107] They had spent winter burning furniture for warmth, and spring learning which houses would remain empty. [123] Ivo looked at his powder chest and imagined those exhausted faces flinching at the first report. [139] "Your mother always wanted noise," he said. [146]

"She changed," said the girl. [151]

Her name was Linn, and all she had left of Mara's equipment was a fire spinner's kerosene rag knot on a chain. [173] She held its blackened weight as carefully as another child might hold a bird. [187] Mara had once taught Ivo to juggle, insisting that dropped balls were merely practicing their entrances. [203] He could not reconcile that woman with the letter's sober promise: No thunder this year. [218]

Then Linn pointed to a note in mosaic beside the old well: LET THE LIGHT WALK. [234] "Her last repair," Linn said, touching the pale letters. [243] "Everyone thought the fever had muddled her." [250]

Ivo knelt, but no hidden fuse or clever mechanism rewarded his inspection. [262] Night settled without stars, the clouds pressing close enough to make the walls seem taller. [277] During that hush, he stopped trying to invent an answer and began to filter what reached him: damp stone, breathing, chain against pottery. [300] Through ambient awareness, the habit that kept his displays from fighting wind and weather, he noticed a flicker above Linn's shoulder. [321]

Her lantern had caught a sliver of mirror set between the flowers. [333] He moved the lantern, and the glimmer vanished while another appeared farther along the wall. [348] Mara had repaired the fortress with fragments of its shattered windows, silvering them into constellations. [363] Not damage disguised, he understood, but damage given another occupation. [373]

"May I use the knot?" he asked. [380] Linn pulled it to her chest, and he remembered how fiercely he had guarded his daughter's unfinished cup. [398] Even its crooked handle had become something nobody else was permitted to touch. [411] "It will wear out," he told her, refusing the easy lie that anything precious could be saved unchanged. [429]

She considered the villagers waiting on their salvaged stools, then laid the chain across his palm. [445] "She hated being kept in boxes." [451]

They cleared a circle, fetched a wet blanket, and filled a lidded pot with sand. [466] Ivo left his powder locked away. [472] When he fueled and lit the knot, its small flame looked almost embarrassing against so much night. [489] He began walking, chanting his measures, letting the chain swing slowly within the cleared space. [504] Along the wall, one reflected spark became six, then twenty, climbing through the bindweed. [518] The fortress put on its borrowed heavens. [525]

A boy laughed, then covered his mouth as though he had stolen something from the dead. [541] "Quite right," Ivo told him, turning carefully beneath the moving lights. [552] "The stars are badly behaved tonight." [558]

Linn laughed too, a startled, rusty sound, and supplied a rhyme: three for the lift, give sorrow a shift. [577] It was a terrible rhyme, exactly the sort his daughter would have defended, and for once the memory did not empty his hands. [600] He walked until his arm ached, not defeating grief but giving it something bright to follow. [616]

When he smothered the flame, the fortress returned to darkness, but nobody hurried to leave. [631] Linn sat beside him with the cooling knot between them and asked whether broken window glass was expensive. [649] "The broken part is generally free," he said. [657] Tomorrow he would show her how to silver it. [666] Tonight, against the gravity of hopelessness, they had managed that small, ridiculous thing: to look up. [682]
