On the cobalt terrace at dusk, Nera manufactures false memories for people whose bloodlines have become crimes. [17] For twenty years, the black market genealogist has sold innocent grandmothers, loyal fathers, childhoods without a knock at the door. [37]

Tonight a young woman sits opposite her, shaking so hard that the teacup rings against its saucer. [54] "The amnesty office wants my mother's name," she says. [63] "Whenever I try to remember it, this happens." [71]

Nera recognizes the scar beneath her chin, and the silence between them settles into an old, familiar shape. [89] She has imagined meeting her niece again, but always as someone already forgiven. [102]

From her instrument case she takes a watch hand, slender and blue, salvaged from her sister's broken timepiece. [120] A needle can open stored memories, but only a watch hand remembers which moment should follow which. [137]

"I can quell the shaking," she says, avoiding the girl's eyes. [148] "But we will have to go downstairs." [155]

The girl grips the railing as they descend beneath the terrace, under the weight of unspoken words. [172] Below lies a forgotten shore, cut off from the harbor when the registry built its seawall. [188]

Here Nera has hidden the originals. [194] To manufacture a convincing ancestry, a genealogist must first strain away every recollection that might contradict it. [211] Those memories, pressed into wood and sealed with salt, survive as grooves too shallow for ordinary fingers to decipher. [230] Sieved hush, the trade calls them: everything a purchased childhood cannot afford to remember. [244]

Her profession requires their burning. [249] Nera has instead scattered them among the storm wrack, a weakness she has spent twenty years calling mercy. [267]

Now she kneels beside her niece and begins sifting through driftwood carvings, checking the hidden numbers beneath their bark. [286] The girl's trembling fingers cannot help, so she holds Nera's lamp. [297] Each time the light jerks, Nera waits. [304] She has earned the inconvenience. [309]

At last she finds a length of willow bearing the number she still uses for every lock. [326]

"You kept mine," the girl says, watching her face rather than the wood. [339] "Why?" [340]

Nera wants to say that she hoped this day would come. [351] But hope has never made her climb down these steps with a lamp and look for that number. [369]

"Because destroying it would have let me call myself innocent." [379]

She lays the watch hand across the grooves and offers its blunt end to the girl's wrist. [396] "This will give you back the afternoon they took your mother, including what I did." [411]

The lamp trembles over the waiting metal. [418]

"Tell me first." [421]

For years Nera has wanted to discover the weight of a withheld truth, as though weighing it might prove it bearable. [442] Now she understands that she has only been wondering whether someone else could carry it for her. [459]

"I told the officers where she was hiding." [467] "They promised to leave you and me alone if I gave them her." [480]

She nearly adds that she was frightened. [487] Instead she says, "Afterward, I made you remember that she had abandoned you, because you kept asking me to bring her home." [509]

The girl lowers the lamp onto a stone before she can drop it. [522]

"And you let me hate her." [528]

"Yes." [529]

Only after a long silence does her niece touch the watch hand. [541] Nera steadies the willow, and the lost afternoon passes through the metal in its proper order: hiding, betrayal, her mother's last embrace. [563] When the shaking stops, the girl withdraws her wrist. [572] She does not reach for Nera. [578]

"Her name was Mara," she says, testing each sound against what is hers again. [592]

Above them, the last blue light clings to the terrace tiles. [603] The girl asks for a written statement, with Nera's name and the address where the officers found her mother. [622] The amnesty protects descendants, not informants, and neither of them pretends otherwise. [634]

Nera carries the lamp upstairs while her niece carries the willow. [645] At the table, she moves the cold tea aside and begins with the address. [659] For once, she leaves room beneath her own account for someone else to write. [673]
