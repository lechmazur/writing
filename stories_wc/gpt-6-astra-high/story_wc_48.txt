Mara had written eight novels, but the ninth kept ending with her husband's boat coming home, which it had not done. [21] Her editor called this denial; Mara called it a draft, although neither description helped her sleep. [37]

In his coat she found a spiral seashell fragment and remembered the river superstition he had once explained. [55] Close a book around something unfinished, and you could visit the place where hopes went while their owners slept. [74] She placed the fragment between her final pages, intending to collect the shapes of different hopes instead of endlessly tracing her own. [96]

The covers fell together, and Mara fell between them. [105]

She landed in a skiff at a feral nexus in mist, where six rivers tangled among drowned orchards and thorn trees rooted in wrecks. [129] Water circled without splashing; leaves scraped together with the swirling hush of paper. [142] A bookseller stood in the shallows beside her, his apron embroidered with fishhooks. [155]

"You are inside the whisper of closing pages," he said, steadying the skiff. [168] "When the covers touch, whatever you carry becomes your ending." [178] He pointed to the fragment, whose broken edge curved like a question. [190] "Gather enough hope to complete that spiral, and time will follow its curve backward." [204]

Mara thought of wet boots by the stove, of being irritated again by someone she loved. [220] The writer in her distrusted such an easy bargain; the widow held out the shell. [235]

The river revealed its cargo through ephemeral cameo appearances, each lasting only as long as a breath. [252] An old woman pictured marrying the woman whom her village insisted on calling her companion. [267] A boy imagined stairs his mother could climb without stopping to breathe. [279] Their hopes hardened against the fragment: a tiny arch, a delicate ascending ridge. [292]

Mara remained lucidly watchful, the habit that had made her books convincing and her husband sometimes lonely. [309] She noticed the old woman's hand drop before her image disappeared. [320] The boy sat down on his imagined stairs and no longer looked toward the top. [335]

She asked what would happen when they woke. [343] "They manage without," said the bookseller, as though describing a missing button. [355]

Already the shell was almost whole, and beside the skiff her husband's face surfaced, smiling with unbearable familiarity. [373] She reached for him, then saw that his mouth kept forming the same greeting. [387] This was her hope, circling with the others, not yet a man who could contradict her. [403]

"If I break it," she said, "do the hopes go back?" [414]

"Only upstream of the orchard, before the current pulls them beyond their owners' reach." [428]

The covers were settling overhead like black wings, narrowing the strip of gray sky. [442] Mara seized an oar and rowed against the current, while the bookseller let go with an almost courteous bow. [461] She had always given her characters impossible choices from the safety of a chair. [475] Now the oar tore skin from her palms, and every stroke carried her farther from the face following in her wake. [496]

At the orchard's uppermost tree, she wedged the skiff between two branches and raised the shell against the trunk. [515] For months she had refused a funeral without a body, treating absence as a legal loophole through which he might return. [536] Here, with no witness but the hungry river, she could finally solemnize his passing. [550]

"Your name was Daniel," she said, "and you were not mine to keep." [563]

She struck once, then again, until the new spiral shattered and the old fragment split beneath her thumb. [581] An arch of light slipped upriver; a staircase unfolded after it. [592] Daniel's borrowed face dissolved, leaving only water, and she did not turn that water into a sign. [609]

The covers touched. [612]

At her desk, Mara opened her hands over the manuscript and found a few grains of shell in the creases. [632] She could remember the hopes perfectly, but remembering, she understood now, did not give her ownership. [648] She crossed out the boat approaching the harbor. [656] In its place she wrote a woman opening her door to a morning she had neither chosen nor entirely given up on. [678] Then she called Daniel's sister and asked whether they might bury an empty box together. [693]
