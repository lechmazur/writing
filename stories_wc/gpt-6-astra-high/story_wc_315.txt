After the radio static cleared, Mara heard her son breathing, and understood that the bridge had gone. [17] Under their nocturnal arbor, high above the clouds, his lamp traced a helpless circle against the white. [34] His safety tether still held, but the wooden post securing it had begun to lean. [49] She could see his boots moving through empty air, searching for the boards she had promised would remain beneath them. [69]

Every midnight, the mountain garden opened its pale flowers, and someone had to cross to the outer nursery to pollinate them. [90] Mara, the garden's midnight pattern seeker, watched the leaves for changes in the wind and chose the safe intervals. [109] Tonight she had sent Iven across despite his report that the bridge trembled even when the leaves were still. [128] She had spent thirty years learning those rhythms, and he had been her apprentice for eleven nights. [145]

Now the leaves hung motionless while a split widened beneath the post. [157] "You said it was wind," Iven whispered over the radio. [167]

Her first impulse was to give him something solid: another confident answer, another promise. [181] That was how she had always understood the watcher's burden, making her uncertainty light enough for other people to carry. [201] But his breathing quickened whenever the post moved, and no steadiness in her voice could keep it upright. [219]

"I was wrong about the wind, and I should have listened to you." [232] She switched to the garden channel to revise her clearance, ordering every outer walkway closed. [247] Then she told Iven exactly what she could offer: a separate rope, anchored in stone, and instructions she would check with him. [269] Trust would have to survive without the shelter of her certainty. [280]

Usually attentively inventive, she had made pruning poles that could reach blossoms beyond the rail without bruising their delicate throats. [300] Now she fitted a rescue sling over one padded hook and lowered it until Iven caught it with his boot. [320] Its rope ran to the tree winch, bolted deep into the mountain. [332] He worked the sling beneath his arms, and she cranked until his weight came off the failing tether. [350]

But his old parachute harness still bound him to the post. [361] When she told him to release it, his gloved hands battered uselessly at the parachute buckle on his chest. [380] The harnesses were donations from mountain air rescue, stout enough to outlast the crews who had worn them. [398] Mara's father had taught her their buckles before he taught her the names of flowers. [413] Yet now she could not remember whether the central plate turned before it was struck, or afterward. [430] Either instruction would sound equally certain if she spoke it out loud. [442]

"Wait while I check," she said, and laid her palm over the identical buckle on her own harness. [460] She closed her eyes, letting muscle memory answer the question her mind could not. [474] Her fingers lifted the rim, turned the plate, then struck it flat, repeating the drill her father had made into a game. [496] The straps fell loose, and his absent hand seemed to rest over hers: a soundless echo, precise as touch. [515]

She gave Iven one movement at a time, waiting for him to describe each before offering the next. [533] When his straps opened, the empty harness dropped away, and the post followed it into the clouds. [550] Neither of them heard anything strike below. [557]

By the time she winched him onto stone, the unpollinated flowers were paling with the approach of dawn. [575] Iven sat with his hands tucked beneath his arms, refusing her blanket until she stopped trying to put it around him. [596] Then he took it himself. [601]

She wanted to ask whether he forgave her, but that would have been another weight handed to him. [619] Instead, she opened the watch log and wrote down his warning before her own mistake. [634] After a while, he leaned close enough to read it. [644]

"Put in that the leaves never moved," he said. [653] She passed him the pencil, and held the page steady while he wrote. [666]
