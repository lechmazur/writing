Only after the glue dried could Mara test the storm siren, so she spent the longest night listening. [18] The cathedral stood on a needle of rock above the clouds, a windblown sanctuary where even closed doors breathed. [37] Its bell had warned the mountain villages for centuries, through a chain of keepers stationed farther down the paths. [56] At noon on the winter solstice, the electric siren would replace them. [68] The ministry called that moment zero hour, as though nothing worth counting had happened before. [83]

Mara was only the tester, paid less than the men who had bolted the horn to the roof. [101] When its throat cracked during installation, she mixed the adhesive herself and declined to charge for the repair. [119] She liked machines, but trusted them only after somebody at the far end said they worked. [135]

Overnight, she had noticed frost forming on the lower windows while the upper panes remained wet. [151] Rather than guess, she borrowed three thermometers and tied them at intervals along the cathedral bell rope. [168] The old sexton untied it from the bell and helped her lower the instruments down the tower's northern face. [187] By tracking microclimate variations instead of averaging them, she found a warm layer sliding over the cold valley air. [206] The horn's coverage could change with that boundary, but she would not condemn it on thermometers alone. [223]

At dawn, with the adhesive hard, she arranged a brief calibration pulse and radioed the keepers for reports. [241] Two villages heard it clearly; the hamlet directly beneath the cathedral heard nothing. [254] She asked the keeper to leave his kitchen and bring a neighbor outside, then repeated the pulse. [271] Still nothing, although Mara's teeth ached from the noise. [280]

By midmorning the cloud lid had broken into rags, and every village heard the third test. [296] The ministry inspector arrived smiling, with her acceptance form already marked satisfactory. [308]

"You see," he said, "it only needed warming up." [317]

Mara showed him the temperature sheets and the three sets of reports. [329] "It needs the weather to cooperate, and weather is what we're warning them about." [343]

He tapped the contract: payment followed acceptance, and the keepers' wages stopped at zero hour. [358] Delaying the switch would require an explanation bearing someone's name. [368]

Mara thought of her rented room, where she slept with her coat over the blanket. [383] She had hoped this commission would buy a small stove before January. [395] Below the windows, clouds were knitting themselves together again. [404]

"Put mine on it," she said, and crossed out satisfactory. [414]

The inspector did not argue, which was worse than shouting; he simply folded the unsigned payment voucher. [431]

With six minutes left, Mara radioed the keepers and asked them to remain at their posts. [447] She could promise no wages, only that their reports would appear beside hers in the record. [463] She asked for one more watch, not a lifetime of unpaid vigilance. [475] One by one they agreed; the nearest keeper asked her to spell his name correctly on the report. [493]

The sexton helped her strip the thermometers from the cathedral bell rope and fasten it to the clapper again. [512] At zero hour, she moved the switch from automatic to standby, silencing the triumphant blast programmed for the handover. [531] Then she pulled the rope three times: the old signal asking each station to acknowledge the watch. [548]

Between the strokes, the sanctuary held its solstice hush, vast enough for her to hear her own breath. [566] From the first bend in the path, a smaller bell answered; another took it onward through the cloud. [584] The inspector reached for the radio, but Mara raised one finger and waited for the faintest answer. [601] Eventually the hamlet's acknowledgment came back up the chain, three uneven notes passed from hands she had never shaken. [620]

Only then did she ask the inspector to authorize another week of wages while she surveyed sites below the inversion. [640] Smaller horns there might work, she explained, but the people living there would have to tell them. [657]

He took out a blank form instead of the voucher and asked her for the keeper's name. [674] Mara checked her notebook before answering; being heard, she thought, ought to begin with being recorded correctly. [691]
