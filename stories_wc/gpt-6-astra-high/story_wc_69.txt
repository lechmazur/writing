By October, the velvet library smelled of damp leaves and the cedar trunks in which refugees had carried their books. [20] Its curtains had crossed three borders, and their faded folds gave the room the traveled stillness of a sleeping train. [39] Here Mara, once celebrated as a harmonious choir teacher, had been hired to record the district's vanishing arts. [57] She wanted to braid diverse voices into harmony before another winter scattered their owners. [71] Unfortunately, she had spent thirty years teaching children that harmony meant sounding alike. [84]

At the first rehearsal, the singers sat expectantly resigned, holding songs whose unfamiliar consonants Mara had already penciled out. [103] Oren, the last zither maker from the northern valley, brought an instrument wrapped in his dead wife's apron. [121] His son would collect him at the first frost; after that, nobody here would know how to build one. [140] Mara asked him to accompany a harvest song, but removed the valley's name, Varda, from the final refrain. [158] Her father had died in a barn burned by men from Varda. [170] She drew a circle to denote a breath where that name had been. [183]

When Oren struck the strings, something buzzed like a trapped fly. [194] He stopped at her circle and folded the apron over the instrument. [206] "You have saved everything except the people," he said. [215] Mara told herself he was being difficult, then spent the evening unable to erase that empty circle. [232]

The next morning she found him repairing the zither soundhole rosette beneath the library's only clear window. [249] Its delicate wooden lattice had split, leaving a loose tongue that rattled whenever the strings vibrated. [265] Lina, Mara's youngest singer, watched him ease the broken piece free. [276] Without its fragile ornament, the dark opening looked startlingly large. [286] "Does it sound better without that?" Lina asked. [294] Oren plucked a string, and the clean note hung among the velvet folds. [307] "Clearer, but unfinished," he said, setting out the glue. [316]

Mara stayed because somebody needed to hold the lamp. [325] She returned because Lina wanted to learn the carving, and eventually because Oren worked more steadily with company. [343] Across that autumn, unlearning hatred proved slower than learning any song. [354] Some afternoons she could say Varda; on others, she could only pass him a tool. [369] He did not offer a story that would make her father's death reasonable. [382] Once he named his own brother, shot by men wearing her father's regiment's badge, and she kept holding the lamp. [402]

The library's recorder caught every fault: wagons outside, a knocking radiator, Mara's instructions spilling over hesitant entrances. [419] She began by subtracting noise, closing the street door and asking the caretaker to bleed the radiator. [436] Then, listening back, she heard how often she interrupted a voice just as it became particular. [452] At the next rehearsal she put down her pencil. [461] The singers did not immediately become generous. [468] They argued about tempo, refused certain verses, and accused one another of remembering words incorrectly. [483] Mara asked each to teach a phrase before anyone tried joining it. [495] Gradually, breath began to travel around the circle instead of arriving on her command. [509] A rough alto steadied a thin soprano without swallowing it. [519]

On the morning frost silvered the library steps, Oren fitted the repaired rosette into its opening. [535] Lina had carved the replacement tongue; it was slightly thicker than his work, but it held. [551] His son waited outside with the engine running until Mara asked him to come inside and listen. [568]

They recorded the harvest song in one take. [576] At the last refrain, Mara felt the old smoke close her throat. [588] She could still stop them with one raised hand. [597] Instead, she opened her score and sang Varda into the space she had made. [611] The name did not forgive anyone. [617] It named a place where people had brought in grain, married, carved wood, and buried their dead. [634] Around it, the other voices found their intervals. [642]

Before leaving, Oren gave Lina his smallest chisel, not the zither. [653] He still needed something to play. [659] Mara labeled the recording with every singer's name and every village they had insisted on remembering. [675] Then she left the library key with Lina, who wanted somewhere quiet to begin another rosette. [691]
