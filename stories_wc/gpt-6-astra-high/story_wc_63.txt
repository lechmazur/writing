At the far end of its swing, the clock tower pendulum bob paused, and the sea stopped climbing the tidal obsidian ridge. [22] Mara stood beneath it with her winding key still warm in her palm. [35] Below, foam hung like torn linen over black volcanic glass, each arrested droplet catching a merciless noon. [52]

She was the local clock tower winder, precise enough to hear a lost second in her sleep. [69] She checked apprentices' wages, refused merchants' gifts, and recorded every repair where anyone could read it. [85] Yet the clock governed the tidal sluices, opening channels beneath the wealthy terraces briefly and those beneath the shell gatherers' houses longer. [107] Mara had wound that arrangement faithfully for nineteen years. [116] During the last great tide, her mother's house had filled before the warning bell finished ringing. [132] Since then Mara had petitioned the council eleven times, always returning afterward to wind the clock. [148]

This morning a dredger had brought her a broken rake handle from the drowned shell beds. [164] Her mother had used that rake to comb mussels from crevices, measuring tides against its shaft. [180] Mara had pressed the wood against the brass bob, wanting, just once, to stop the thing she served. [198]

Now the handle burned without flame. [204] Her mother began to emerge through tiny inscriptions carved along the broken rake handle: first a bent thumb, then a shoulder, then a face bleached brighter than grief. [232] The marks were not merely tide measurements, as Mara had always assumed, but names beside the heights at which their homes flooded. [254] Her mother had been keeping a different kind of time. [264]

"I asked them," Mara said, holding up the petitions she carried folded inside her coat. [279]

The apparition looked toward the cam wheels that determined how long each sluice stayed open. [294] Mara knew those wheels more intimately than she knew her own aging face. [307] One turn of the emergency lever would open every channel, sending the waiting tide into the terraces and drowning the council chamber. [329] For an instant she tasted the clean, terrible sweetness of doing something large enough to resemble innocence. [346]

Her mother's bent thumb covered a name near the handle's splintered end. [358] Mara remembered that house: a council cook lived there with two children who slept beneath the kitchen stairs. [376] Water would not distinguish their beds from their employer's silver. [386]

"Then tell me what clears the debt," she whispered. [395]

From the black cuts came her mother's voice, dry as salt on lips: "Nothing all at once." [412]

Beneath each name, smaller strokes marked thresholds, channel widths, the differences a fair schedule would have to honor. [430] Her mother had gathered the measurements for the council; Mara remembered advising her to wait until after the elections. [449] She had mistaken obedience for the whole of being good. [459]

With dividers from her tool roll, Mara plotted a new schedule on the adjustable cam wheels. [475] She set the terrace channels to admit water by degrees, spreading the burden without unleashing a killing surge. [493] Her own rooms stood on the lowest terrace, and she calculated their share without rounding down. [509] Then she wrote the new schedule in the public maintenance book, signing her name beside the unauthorized changes. [527] The water would ruin floors, including hers, but the shell gatherers' houses would remain above the projected crest. [545]

That was only arithmetic, not forgiveness. [551] She listed what must follow: compensation from terrace rents, raised foundations, and sluice keys entrusted to both neighborhoods. [569] None of it could be accomplished alone, or inside this stolen instant. [581]

Since her mother's drowning, she had wanted absolution to arrive like a bell stroke, complete and audible to everyone. [600] There would be only incremental absolution, if any, earned through disputed payments, wet work, and the surrender of control. [619]

The apparition thinned; the rake handle lay cool against the bob. [630] Mara almost begged her mother to stay until she knew whether the town would forgive her. [646] Instead she checked every setting again, because shared balance required accuracy even when sorrow wanted spectacle. [662]

Somewhere below, a droplet strained toward the rock. [670] Mara closed the maintenance book and waited for the first difficult second. [682]
