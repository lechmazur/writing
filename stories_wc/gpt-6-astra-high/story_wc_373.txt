Under the rotting pier, where stolen lanterns warmed the water gold, Nell found her father sitting among nets meant for drowning men. [22] He had saved a bun for her. [29] The tenderness of it made her angrier than the rope around his ankles. [42] A moonshine runner's daughter knew that seized cargo meant debt, but she had never seen a man itemized beside barrels. [62] At sunrise, the creditors would put him aboard a salvage ship, where divers seldom survived a season. [79]

Until then, the pirate market observed a truce of hush: no raised voices, no blood, no removing prisoners. [97] Above them, customs boots occasionally crossed the pier, oblivious to the commerce hidden beneath its collapsing boards. [114] Nell had until sunrise to thwart the sale. [122]

Mother Pike, who bought sounds for people too rich to feel things themselves, offered the necessary money for one recording. [142] "Trust breaking," she whispered, setting an antique watch fob in Nell's palm. [154] "Wind it once, and it catches what breaks inside you, not what anybody pretends to say." [170] The fob was silver, polished smooth by thumbs, with a glass belly and no watch attached. [186] Such instruments had been fashionable before respectable people decided feelings should remain private. [199]

Nell worked through false starts while the tide rose around the pilings. [211] She paid a shrimp boy to promise her change and then refuse it, but the glass stayed clear. [229] She made herself read an old letter from a sweetheart who had married elsewhere; that hurt was already healed. [248] Next she asked Pike to tell her the rescue money was a lie. [261] Pike refused and gave her hot tea instead. [269] "You cannot rehearse the fall, love." [275]

Nell remained curiously steadfast, as she had at twelve when her father taught her to find the channel through fog. [295] He had always believed she would find it, even after he had lost it himself. [310] When she returned his untouched bun, he asked whether the fob could hear something spoken quietly. [326] She knelt beside him, grateful that he finally had a plan. [337]

"The customs men did not catch me by chance," he said. [348] "I gave them a landing place, and they promised to leave my boat alone." [362] Beyond the nets, someone carefully stopped counting coins. [370] Last winter, customs had raided the creek where Nell's aunt unloaded flour; her aunt was still in prison. [388] Nell had spent six months blaming a careless lantern. [397]

"You ate at her table afterward," she said. [405] He nodded, and the little movement was worse than any excuse. [416] "There was money enough for her children if I kept running, I told myself." [430] "Then I kept telling myself." [435]

The fob, still wound from her last attempt, grew warm against her wrist. [448] Inside its glass, a thread of gray appeared, turning slowly as milk turns in tea. [463] She heard neither a crack nor a scream, only the small scrape of a chair being drawn away from a table. [484] That was what she had come to capture. [492] She wished she had never heard it. "Now you can pay them," he said, and she saw how even this confession had been another route around a dangerous shore. [521] For a moment she wanted to put the fob into the tide. [533] Instead, she carried it to Pike, who listened once and closed her eyes. [546] The price bought the debt, the ropes, and a receipt that Nell checked twice while pale light sifted between the boards. [567]
