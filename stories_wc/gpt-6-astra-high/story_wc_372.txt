In the frozen library above the clouds, Iona kept the dead in alphabetical order, although they had begun forgetting their names. [21] She was its orderly records chief, gently meticulous with everything from cracked bindings to the silences between a remembered woman's words. [42] Each night, lanterns projected luminous vows from slides onto the reading room's walls, letting lost voices promise what death had interrupted. [63] Cold preserved those apparitions; warmth would erase them. [71] Even cold could only delay their fading. [78]

Now most shelves held clear glass, and a quartz hush lay over the desks where scholars had once consulted their ancestors. [99] Iona remained to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion, though lately her work resembled taking inventory of an empty house. [120] The last intact magic lantern slide held her mother standing beside a stove, flour bright upon her wrists. [138] Moving such old slides shattered their images; hers remained inside a projector built into the wall. [154] Its vow appeared beneath the image: There will always be room beside my fire. [168]

Her mother's kitchen had sheltered strangers through three famines; Iona had preserved it through forty winters. [184] She could no longer recall its smell. [191] To measure how much longer a vow might last, she tested its light by sampling frequencies with a graduated rack of tuning forks. [214] Her mother's voice answered middle C, weaker every evening, while an unexplained lower note persisted beneath it. [231]

On the night the projected stove became transparent, someone knocked at the library door. [245] Beyond its inspection shutter stood a boy with snow packed into his sleeves. [258] He had climbed the mountain stair after an avalanche buried the hospice below. [271] "I saw your fire," he said. [277] Behind Iona, the illusory stove shone through windows glazed with frost. [288]

She fetched a blanket from her unheated cell and pushed it through the shutter. [302] He could come inside, she explained, but she could not light a real fire near the collection. [319] The boy tried to wrap himself in the blanket, and his fingers failed to close. [334] Iona lifted the latch. [338] As she drew him across the threshold, the lowest tuning fork began to hum. [352]

She settled him beside the lantern, whose enchanted beam gave no heat, and struck the other forks. [369] Middle C scarcely answered, but the lower note rang hard enough to tremble the teeth in her jaw. [387] For forty years she had filed it as interference. [396] Now she remembered how her mother used to pull another chair toward the stove before asking a stranger's name. [415] Perhaps the voice and the vow were not the same thing. [426]

The boy's shivering stopped, which frightened her more than the failing light. [438] There was no time to prove a theory. [446] She carried him to the disused reading room hearth, broke up its wooden catalogue drawers, and stacked the pieces around a twist of paper. [470] Her hands arranged the kindling precisely, leaving channels for air. [480] Then she struck a match. [485]

As heat reached the lantern, her mother's outline loosened, and Iona nearly smothered the fire with her coat. [503] But the boy needed both her hands around his. [512] She watched the familiar wrists dissolve while the lower note filled the room, steady as a kettle approaching song. [531] The vow's letters brightened once, then vanished with the face. [541] For the first time in forty years, Iona could not summon her mother. [554]

Near dawn, the boy asked whose kitchen he had seen. [564] Iona told him about the famines, correcting herself when she confused two winters, and he listened without demanding certainty. [583] What remained was memory now, vulnerable to error and capable of change. [595]

When he slept, she removed the clear slide and wrote a new entry in the ledger. [611] Under Condition, she first wrote Lost, then drew one careful line through the word. [625] She wrote instead: In use. [630]

By morning, meltwater had found the gutters, and the windows looked out upon a white country with people somewhere beneath it. [651] Iona cleaned the chimney, measured the empty shelves for firewood, and made room beside the hearth for another bed. [670] The boy woke to the sound of her moving a second chair. [682]
