At the moment a sculptor's chisel first strikes marble, a pocket of time opens in the ice cavern beneath Vey. [20] The hammer hangs above the block, and a white chip waits beside the sculptor's thumb. [35] Within that unfinished instant, Mara, the city's prism-song bell ringer, has one chance to save her livelihood. [52] More importantly, she must persuade Inspector Renn to certify wonder as essential before the council seals the cavern's aurora shaft. [72]

Fuel is scarce, and nobody disputes that a roof without holes keeps children warmer. [86] But exhausted miners have rested here for centuries, inside statues before those statues exist. [100] Every intended form appears at the first strike as sculpted hush, a silence shaped like what the stone will become. [120] Today's sculptor has imagined a cradle. [126] Its invisible curve receives Renn's stooped shoulders; his boots lift gently from the ice. [140]

Mara settles beside him, holding an amber music sheet inherited from her mother. [153] Incised notes cross the fossil resin, but the spaces between them matter more. [166] The resin holds aurora light as it once held wings, preserving hours of changing colors to unfold inside a single instant. [187] Synesthetically blessed, Mara hears the colors as pitches, including the faint returning tones that guide a visitor out. [205] Her three crystal bells can give those private sounds to anyone. [216]

"You may rest as long as you wish," she tells Renn. [227] "But leaving safely requires wanting to hear something again." [236] He closes his eyes. [240] The hush rocks them without movement, its oscillating repose easing Mara's wrists and smoothing the furrow above Renn's nose. [259] Green light swells overhead, withdraws, and swells again. [267] For a while, nothing needs defending. [273]

Then Renn takes his certification seal from his pocket and releases it. [285] It hangs between his fingers and the floor. [293] "Let them close the shaft," he says. [300] "I have forgotten my boy's last breath." [307]

Mara's hand tightens on the amber. [313] Her mother once stayed too long in a hush intended as a garden, until even the desire to leave had faded. [334] Mara had rung the prescribed summons, louder and louder, and brought back a woman who no longer recognized her. [353] The council called it recovery because her mother could work. [363] Renn's face now wears that same untroubled emptiness. [371]

Mara raises a bell, then lowers it. [378] She cannot certify wonder by making another person disappear inside it, nor unfetter him by dragging him toward a pain she chooses. [400]

Across her knees, the amber darkens as the aurora turns violet. [411] Her mother's score prescribes a rising passage here, but a fracture crosses the final notes. [426] Mara remembers the sheet breaking against the bedrail when her mother, briefly lucid, pushed the music away. [443] She has always played across that gap. [450] Now she lets it stand. [455]

Through the colors of the aurora, she follows the slower returning tones, ringing once, waiting, then answering only when another color gathers. [477] Between those sounds, the hush remains a cradle, not a locked room. [489] Renn breathes deeply; his face loosens, then crumples. [497]

"He used to knock his spoon against his cup," he says. [508] Mara does not imitate the spoon. [514] She leaves him room to remember it. [521] His hand moves toward the suspended seal. [528] "I want that noise back," he whispers, and his boots touch ice. [540]

Renn catches the seal and stamps the amber sheet's blank margin, granting the cavern the same protection as a hospital. [560] "I will still miss him," he says, brushing his cheeks. [570] "I would like to hear the breakfast cups anyway." [579]

Mara rings the last, pale note. [585] The cradle loosens; the marble chip falls; the first echo travels up the shaft into a city where no time has passed. [607] Against her knees, the amber holds its small new mark, and beyond it the patient light brightens, rests, and brightens. [627]
