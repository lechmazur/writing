On the evening the clocks ran sideways, Mara was collecting abandoned coffee cups aboard the commuter ferry Mercy. [18] The minute hand slid beyond its dial, and the harbor vanished with it. [31] Where the shipyards should have been, a spectral grotto opened under starlight, its translucent walls breathing around the boat. [50] Forty passengers reached for phones that now displayed photographs of lives they had never lived. [65]

Mara rang the supper bell. [70] "Whatever this is, nobody faces it hungry," she said, distributing the biscuits she bought for delayed crossings. [87] Her captain accepted one with shaking fingers. [94]

Mara's enthusiasm for ferry work had survived wage cuts, winter storms, and her father's drowning on this very route. [113] She loved carrying people home; lately, she had begun to suspect that home was less singular than the timetable promised. [133] For months, an empty hourglass had stood beside the wheel; its sand had vanished during a disturbance that management called instrument failure. [155] Afterward, every disturbance left Mara remembering two versions of the same day. [167] In one, her father had drowned rescuing her sister Nell; in the other, Nell had drowned before he could reach her. [188]

Now Nell stood beside her, wearing their father's old life jacket. [199] Nell had spent eleven years apologizing for surviving. [207] From the radio came his voice, asking whether anybody could hear the ferry Mercy. [221] Nell dropped her biscuit. [225]

Mara took the handset before hope could turn into paralysis. [235] "Describe your position," she said. [240]

Her father described the same grotto, but a different arrangement of stars. [252] He was alone aboard his Mercy, following disturbances in search of the daughter he had lost. [268] Then Nell spoke, and his breathing became the only sound on the channel. [281]

Mara spread a route chart across the wheelhouse floor. [290] During the earlier disturbances, she had marked in blue where missing minutes intersected the ferry's daily crossings. [307] Her father radioed the corresponding marks from his chart, and Nell plotted them in red. [322] Each blue intersection matched a star overhead; each red one matched a star above his boat. [338] The connected patterns formed a channel that neither chart contained alone. [349] At its center, the two routes became one. [357]

Mara wanted more than a conversation with a ghost: she wanted to merge the parallel timelines, giving Nell back the man who had saved her. [382] She laid the empty hourglass across the chart to stop it curling. [394] Through its bulbs, the red and blue routes appeared as actual waterways, joining at the narrow throat. [411] Upright, the glass showed only one route at a time. [421] Sideways, with no falling sand to insist on before and after, it held both. [435] But beyond the junction, neither image contained a shore. [444]

Her father saw the same thing through his hourglass. [453] "We can have a common crossing," he said, "but not a common arrival." [466]

Mara looked at the passengers: lunch boxes, work boots, a birthday cake sweating inside its plastic dome. [483] She had no right to make their homes the price of hers. [495] With the captain holding Mercy at the channel's entrance, she explained what the glass showed and what she could not promise. [516] The lifeboats, lowered toward the blue stars, found ordinary harbor water beneath their keels. [530] Every other passenger chose to leave, along with the captain, who pressed the wheelhouse keys into Mara's hand. [548]

Nell stayed. [550] "I want him to know he reached me somewhere," Nell said. [561]

Mara radioed the turns to her father while Nell kept the hourglass level. [574] As their ferries entered the junction together, two engines settled into one heartbeat. [587] Her father stepped through the wheelhouse door and stopped before Nell, afraid to touch what might disappear. [604] Nell closed the distance. [608]

Beyond the windows, the grotto stretched into a channel of stars, with no terminal lights ahead. [624] On shore, Mercy would remain a cancellation on a board; aboard, there would always be another crossing. [641] Mara hung the keys on their hook and started tomorrow's maintenance list. [653] There would be railings to paint, drinking water to find, other vanished boats to guide through. [669] When the radio crackled, she answered brightly, though she could not promise anyone a way home. [685]

"Mercy here," she said, "still crossing." [691]
