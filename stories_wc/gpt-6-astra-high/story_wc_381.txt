At midnight, the brook brought other people's whispered secrets into the nocturnal haven where Nera had lived alone since her mother's death. [22] They came from the village's wishing stones upstream, murmurs about stolen flour, unwelcome marriages, children loved badly but loved. [41] The quiet brook carried them without judgment; Nera, a solitary summoner, usually sealed her windows against their ache. [59] Tonight, amid whispered secrets, she heard something answering in the pauses: a prophecy spoken in code. [75] Under her floor, the buried star began to warm. [84]

Her ancestors had summoned it during a famine, persuading its heat into the soil until wheat grew through snow. [103] They had built this haven over its resting place and appointed their descendants to keep its waking prophecy unspoken. [122] Nera had inherited both the duty and her mother's conviction that enough silence could contain anything. [138] But the star was terrestrially cosmic: rooted in loam, yet obedient to laws written before the earth existed. [156] To such a creature, a prophecy was not a prediction but an invitation, and silence itself could speak. [174]

She pressed her palms against the floor and commanded the voices outside to cease. [188] The murmurs broke into fragments, their gaps sharpened by her spell, and amber light seeped between the boards. [206] She released them quickly. [210] Somewhere upstream, someone finished whispering a child's name. [218]

From her mother's desk she fetched a crane's origami wing, all that remained of the paper bird marking the summoning manual. [239] Its pleats had been folded to measure breath; as a child, Nera had used it to practice holding spells steady. [259] Now she balanced it on a reed beside the water and dusted a slate with ash. [275] Each whisper stirred the wing; each pause lowered its tip to the slate she drew slowly beneath it. [293] By mapping silence rather than listening for words, she found the sentence assembling beneath the villagers' grief. [310] At dawn, the root shall flower in flame. [318] The longest strokes came whenever shame stopped a speaker's breath, and her own wards lengthened every hesitation. [335] All these years, her family had been teaching the prophecy its rhythm. [347]

Nera sat back, remembering her mother's last evening, when pain had worn her once commanding voice down to a whisper. [367] Afraid of disturbing the star, Nera had asked her to be quiet. [379] She had mistaken that final obedience for peace. [387] The brook touched its stones, making a continuous softness that swallowed no sound and gave no silence a sharp edge. [407] Her mother had called it smoothed hush, before duty hardened her into someone who valued silence more than rest. [426] Nera had never thought to summon something already there. [435]

She lowered the wing into the current, letting water flow along the pleats that once measured her obedient breathing. [454] Then she offered the brook what summoning always required: a place to enter and something precious to keep. [472] With her heel, she broke the earthen lip protecting the haven's threshold, and the water slid across her warding circles. [492] The wing softened between her fingers, her mother's tiny, exact folds loosening into pulp. [506] She almost snatched it back. [511] Instead she opened her hand and told the moving water what she had said beside her mother's bed. [529] She did not stop before the cruelest word, although her throat tried to close. [543]

The smoothed hush entered without a face or a voice, spreading through the haven as gently as lamplight. [561] Outside, the secrets remained audible, but their pauses lost the hard boundaries by which the buried star understood them. [580] The invitation dissolved into ordinary sorrow. [586] Amber shone through the shallow water on the floor, a tranquil blaze that warmed Nera's bare feet without burning. [605] Beneath it, the star settled deeper among the roots. [614]

By dawn, the brook had carried away the wing and every circle meant to keep the world outside. [632] Nera could rebuild them, but the thought now frightened her more than an unguarded door. [647] She set a second chair on the dry bank and walked upstream to the wishing stones. [663] The baker was there, whispering that he had taken flour from his brother's winter store. [678] "You can finish that aloud," she said, and waited while he found the breath. [692]
