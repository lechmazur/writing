Thirty years after dreams became shareable, Mara tended a blooming library above clouds, where borrowed sleep opened into flowers along the shelves. [22] She was its sharp-eyed dusk noticer, employed to watch the shadows beneath those flowers, because a dream always sickened there first. [43] At sunset, while other librarians watered the roots with recollections, she measured darkness against the grain of the floor. [62]

Her daughter's dream occupied the oldest blossom: a white iris rooted around a river stone. [77] Inside it, Nell crouched beside a flooded stream, reaching toward the water with one small hand. [93] Mara had held the dream at that instant since the spring when a real flood took Nell away. [111] Visitors loved its stillness, and Mara permitted them to carry copies home. [123] She wanted to preserve the shape of hope, which she understood as a child who had not yet disappeared. [142]

Then, one evening, Nell's shadow stood up without her. [151] It slipped beneath the iris and crossed the floor, trailing wet footprints that had no corresponding feet. [168] Where it passed, other blossoms closed around unfinished journeys: trains stopped short of stations, doors refused to open. [186]

"A shadow unbound," the director said, lowering the quarantine glass over Nell's iris. [199] "We burn the source before dawn, or every dream connected to it may stall." [213]

Mara asked for the night, then went downstairs to the security archive, where cameras recorded movement but never the private voices of sleepers. [236] She investigated by comparing silent video footage from decades apart: the iris on its first evening, and the iris now. [256] In the old recording, made before Mara fixed the loop, Nell lifted the river stone and placed it in the stream. [277] Then she beckoned toward someone beyond the frame. [285] In the new recording, only the shadow completed that gesture, over and over, reaching into neighboring dreams for somewhere to put a foot. [308]

Mara watched until the library's windows turned violet. [316] She had remembered Nell playing safely on the bank; the camera showed a child making a crossing for somebody else. [336] For thirty years, every copy had repeated the interruption, multiplying an impulse that was never allowed to become an act. [356] Now that thwarted movement was spreading through the roots. [365] The shadow was not trying to swallow their endings, but to find its own. [379]

To restore it, Mara would have to release the dream from its anchor, letting it travel through the network without returning. [400] She would still have photographs, but never again the warmth of that small, dreaming hand. [415]

At the quarantine cabinet, she slipped her fingers beneath the iris and found the stone Nell had brought upstairs herself. [435] It bore a hollow where her daughter's thumb had rested, a hollow Mara had polished smoother with her own. [454] Her movements were softly deliberate as she unwound the copper wire binding it to the root. [470] She had tied that wire herself, too tightly, with the practical anger of a mother repairing something. [487]

When the last turn came free, the shadow rushed back beneath the glass. [500] Mara watched it metamorphose from a crouching child into a narrow bridge, its darkness giving the bright water edges and depth. [521] Nell stood, tested the first stone, and looked back. [530] For an instant Mara almost tightened the wire. [538] Instead she laid it down and watched her daughter cross, carrying no promise that the far bank would be safe. [558]

The iris opened until its petals became transparent, then loosened into light that ran along the shelves and vanished into the sharing lines. [581] All around Mara, closed flowers began to open. [589]

By morning, messages were arriving from the flood shelters below the clouds, where people had borrowed Nell's stillness to endure another night. [611] A carpenter had dreamed a crossing made from doors; a boy had dreamed of holding a ladder steady for his grandmother. [632] Their endings differed, and some were frightening, but nobody was waiting motionless on the bank. [647]

Mara answered each message before returning to the empty place on the shelf. [660] She set the river stone there without a label, its little hollow turned upward. [674] At dusk it held a cup of gold, and she let the light leave it. [689]
