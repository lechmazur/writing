The night the lighthouse began shining inland, Elias arrived at its door carrying a broken tarot card box that whispered in his drowned daughter's voice. [25] He had been a celebrated portrait artist before the wreck; afterward, crestfallen and silent, he had accepted only one commission, to restore the lighthouse's divining deck. [51] The keeper had warned him that its pictures determined which possible coastline the lantern illuminated. [66] Nevertheless, Elias had given every figure his daughter's face. [75]

Now the beam found coastlines where Anna had lived, and dragged them across the one where she had not. [94] Fishing boats lay embedded in kitchens, while mothers remembered children whose fathers had never been born. [110] The tarot box had split along its hinge, unable to contain twenty two incompatible versions of the same beloved face. [130]

Beneath the lantern, behind shelves disguised as stonework, the keeper admitted him to the forgotten library inside the lighthouse. [149] When he emptied the box onto her desk, silver threads unspooled from the cards and stitched themselves into the surrounding books. [170] Every book held a voyage that might have happened. [179] Between those threads, Elias glimpsed Anna at seven, at forty, at an age his own mother had never reached. [198]

"Put her back," he whispered. [203]

"Which her?" [205]

Already the walls were closing in, squeezing the library between a hundred incompatible versions of the tower. [222] Shelves buckled; from somewhere beyond them came the grinding of a boat against somebody's bedroom wall. [238] Elias reached for the Anna nearest him, but his fingers passed through her cheek and emerged wet with seawater. [257]

Across the desk lay The Moon, with its crooked path between two towers. [270] Elias followed that painted path with his finger, and a staircase opened in the floor. [285] The keeper said the lantern's glare hid the knots where painted faces joined the threads of possible lives. [303]

"You must descend into darkness to see what you have done." [314]

Anna's young voice rose from The Star. [321]

"What father would leave his little girl?" [328]

Once, such rhetorical questions had silenced him; now he questioned the question itself. [341]

"Is staying love, if everyone else must lose their children?" [351]

The painted mouth repeated its plea with precisely the same quiver. [362] His daughter, impatient with easy answers, would have argued back. [372]

He took the broken box and descended. [379]

Below the foundation, where even the sea's phosphorescence could not reach, the threads glimmered faintly enough for him to see their knots. [401] They tightened around his wrists, each carrying the warmth of an Anna he could almost remember. [417] He could not cut them without severing the voyages to which they belonged. [430] Instead, by untangling threads one at a time, he traced every borrowed future back to the card that had stolen it. [451]

Above him the walls scraped together, and dust fell warm as breath. [463] He worked by touch until he found the single coarse strand that belonged to the night of the wreck. [482] It held no alternative ending, only Anna hauling a stranger onto an overturned boat before the water took her. [501] Elias had never painted the stubborn set of her jaw; he had softened it into sweetness. [517]

In the necessary darkness, he began to redesign the deck. [527] He could not erase Anna, so he loosened her features into threads and returned those threads to their proper pictures: courage to Strength, grief to The Tower, change to Death. [557]

At the final knot waited an old woman with Anna's eyes, reaching for him as though he were the child who had been lost. [581] He kissed the thread, then let it slip into a voyage he would never take. [596] For The Star he kept only her lifted hand, offering help without any promise of rescue. [612]

The tower exhaled. [615] Overhead, timber stopped grinding against timber, and the keeper called down that the fishing boats were afloat again. [633]

When he climbed back, the library stood still, and the beam swept an ordinary, dangerous sea. [649] He bound the broken box with the last loose thread, leaving its cracked lid visible. [664]

By morning the keeper found him sketching her weathered face beside the window. [677] Light silvered the hollow beneath her eye, and he let the hollow remain. [690]
