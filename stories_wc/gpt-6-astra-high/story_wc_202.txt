In the alchemist's market strung high above the city rooftops, rain hung in bottles and stairways swayed on chains. [19] Mara came every Thursday to buy the things nobody thought worth remembering. [31] As a between-worlds translator, she earned her living settling disputed inheritances, then spent her wages recovering the testimony of servants, prisoners, and drowned people. [55] Their voices reached her through hidden details in paintings: a crooked thimble, an incorrectly knotted rope, a window reflecting an absent moon. [77] She called these anchors of intuition, the small discrepancies by which the truth could find a handhold in the living. [97] Without them, the dead were only weather. [104]

Around her neck hung a lighthouse lens fragment, warm wherever its fractured edge touched skin. [119] Her mother had pushed it into her palm during the harbor flood, before putting her aboard the last rescue boat. [139] She had cleaned the lighthouse for wages too small to appear in its accounts, pocketing damaged glass for toys. [158] Between childhood and now, Mara had polished away every stain except the thumbprint trapped inside the glass. [175] It remained elusively tangible, a ridge she could feel only when she stopped trying. [189] She had never found a painting that let her hear its owner. [201]

That Thursday, a dealer offered her a harbor panorama titled The Bloodless Flood. [214] "Municipal commission," he said, steadying the canvas against the wind. [224] "They painted over the warehouse doors because the mayor disliked clutter." [235] Mara raised the fragment. [239] Beneath the celebrated lighthouse, an almost invisible brushstroke divided a woman's hand into six fingers. [254] Not fingers, she realized: five fingers and a key. [263] The hand wore her mother's wedding ring, repaired with copper because gold had cost a week's food. [280] Mara remembered that hand disappearing into the rain while she screamed from the boat for it to come back. [299]

The market's creaking dwindled away. [304] A raindrop between two planks stopped falling, and Mara stood inside the length of a breath that had lasted twenty-seven years. [325] Her mother held a warehouse door open against its broken hinge. [336] Inside waited the night workers, whose names appeared in no census and therefore in no account of the disaster. [355] The owner had locked them in to prevent theft; her mother had returned with a stolen key. [372] A staircase beyond the door led to a roof the water would not reach. [386] "I thought you had forgotten me," Mara said. [394] "Not for a moment." [398] Her mother looked toward the workers. [404] "Will you take their names?" [409]

Mara wanted to ask whether she had been frightened, whether dying hurt, whether a child could ever repay a seat in a boat. [432] She could bring the canvas home and listen forever, returning each evening to the instant before the hinge gave way. [452] But the city's seal in the varnish bound these witnesses to the account on the municipal tower, which the council had refused for decades to correct. [478] Mara's shelves were full of rejected petitions; she knew what another would accomplish. [491] To free them, she would have to amend the stone itself. [502]

She could unfasten the silver backing from her fragment and let their light pass through. [517] A translator's lens could project testimony into stone, but the heat would destroy the glass. [532] Her mother's thumbprint would go with it. [539] Without the fragment, even the rich dead would fall silent, and there would be no more paid translations. [557]

She stepped back into the market and asked the dealer for his largest burning mirror. [572] He fetched it without bargaining; his father's name had also been missing from the lists. [587] From that height, the municipal tower stood level with her hands. [598] Mara unfastened the backing, aimed the mirror, and read the laundry marks sewn inside the waiting workers' collars. [616] Each stitch became a syllable against her tongue. [624] One by one, their names burned beneath the mayor's gilded account, her mother's last. [638] The glass grew hot enough to blister. [645] Her mother lifted her hand, not waving goodbye but releasing the door. [657]

When the fragment broke, the waiting breath ended. [665] Rain struck the roofs, and below, someone began reading aloud. [675] A clerk leaned from a window with a notebook, then called for more paper. [689] Mara kept the empty silver mount, but she no longer mistook an absence for an abandonment. [704]
