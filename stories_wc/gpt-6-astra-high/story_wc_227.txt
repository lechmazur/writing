By her third dive, Iona could hear the acid sea eating through the floor beneath the cooling cistern. [18] Above its clean water, her mother's gears-and-cogs workshop still floated, carrying eleven refugees and an alchemist who had never trusted her own transmutations. [41] Below her, the porcelain saint that kept them buoyant lay broken in its cradle. [55] A snapped flywheel had struck it at midnight. [63] Now every cog turned slower, and the horizon climbed the windows. [74]

Iona surfaced beneath the cistern hatch, where a little boy waited with her copper speaking trumpet. [90] "Will it remember how to float?" he asked. [98] "If I can remember how to mend it," she said, and hated the bargain in her answer. [115] She took the tube, filled her lungs, and went under again. [126]

Time existed only between breaths held underwater, measured by the pale flakes drifting from the cistern's iron seams. [144] The saint was four centuries old, salvaged from a drowned city's hospital, and its face was stippled with ancient shrapnel wounds. [165] Iona rebuilt that face by matching the pockmarks on the centuries-old statue, following each interrupted scar across the loose pieces. [185] Its damage, not its beauty, showed her where everything belonged. [195] But fitted porcelain would not hold a vessel above acid. [205]

Her mother had called their craft kintsugi intentions: gold joined the fractures, but a spoken purpose persuaded the broken thing to endure. [227] "It quilts memories together," she used to say, "instead of pretending there was never a tear." [243] The workshop's gold had gone into repairs after the storm that took her mother. [257] Only the key ring charm remained, a tiny golden house knocking against the useless key to their vanished shorefront door. [277]

Iona had kept it for a different working. [285] At night she drew resurrection circles around that house, hoping to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion, or at least one last answer. [309] Had her mother known Iona let go of her hand because the lifeline could not carry them both? [327]

The cistern lurched, scattering the saint's cheekbones. [334] Through the water came three desperate knocks: the boy warning her that the outer hull was failing. [351] She caught the fragments and wedged them back into place, then crushed the charm in her little solvent press. [370] The golden roof folded inward like a house accepting the sea. [381] Liquid gold uncoiled from the nozzle, bright as captive sunrise, and she guided it along the saint's broken face. [400]

Then she pressed the speaking trumpet against its hollow throat and began to narrate, spending the air she needed to live. [421] "My mother was brave," she whispered, and the gold flashed once before going dull. [435] That was the polished story, the one she told passengers when they asked whose tools hung above her bench. [454] The saint had carried the sick through bombardment; it knew the difference between a life and a monument. [472]

Iona's lungs convulsed, and she almost pushed toward the hatch. [482] Instead she told it how her mother cursed jammed gears, feared deep water, and burned every loaf she baked. [501] How she always left the workshop door unlocked because someone cold might come. [514] "I let her go," Iona said, bubbles tearing the words apart, "and I don't know whether she forgave me." [533]

The gold trembled in the cracks but did not harden. [543] Memory was only half the working; the broken thing needed somewhere to go. [556] With the last of her breath, Iona offered the only intention she could honestly promise. [571] "I will keep the door unlocked." [577]

Golden seams kindled across the saint, not hiding its wounds but giving each one a shore. [593] The workshop rose with a scream of restarting gears, flinging Iona upward through water suddenly alive with reflections. [611] Small hands seized her collar at the hatch. [619] When she could breathe, the boy asked whether the saint was as good as new. [634] Iona looked at the useless house key, then at the eleven people shivering among the cogs. [650] "No," she said, reaching for a blanket, "but it knows what it's for." [663]
