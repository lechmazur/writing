Upon the shimmer of twilight frost, Mara entered the ruined orchard district, lost in centuries of thick fog, carrying an astronomer's broken sextant. [23] She was a mirror maker, accustomed to trapping ordinary dreams in bedroom glass until their sleepers woke and the silver released them harmlessly. [46] Last night, wanting one more conversation with her dead brother, she had scraped away the silver border that separated a dream from its dreamer. [70] Erasing boundaries was easy; deciding what could cross them was the part she had neglected. [85] The dream had climbed out wearing Tomas's face, and her daughter had followed it into the fog. [102]

Now Mara had to capture the runaway dream before the cold exhausted her daughter and it settled permanently inside the child's sleeping mind. [125] In open air an escaped dream thinned; here, the old fog offered it a world without edges. [142]

Beyond the gate, dead apple trees rose like glass mountains, each branch wearing an impossible height of ice. [160] The district's fog had outlasted dynasties, but these bright, brittle summits were new: Tomas had always dreamed of seeing mountains. [180] He had spent his life measuring inaccessible stars with the sextant, then died of fever three streets from home. [199] Mara had repaired the instrument's shattered index mirror that afternoon, although she could do nothing about its buckled measuring scale. [219] Its two mirrors could still bring something distant against something near, a star against a horizon, without confusing one for the other. [241]

Under an arch of frozen boughs, she found her daughter curled against Tomas's knees. [255] He looked twenty, as he had before illness hollowed him, and his hands rested lovingly on the girl's hair. [274]

"You made me lonely," he said, and the voice was exactly right. [286] "Give me somewhere warm to stay." [292]

Her daughter opened her eyes, and Mara saw a white orchard moving behind the pupils. [307] She wanted to tell the thing that Tomas would never steal a child's life, but it knew only what she had wanted of him. [331] She had wanted him back at any cost. [339]

Mara planted both boots in the soil, firmly grounded where a fallen tree had torn the paving open. [357] The sextant needed a real horizon, however small, or its mirrors would merely multiply the dream. [373] She sighted the boundary between black earth and ice, then tilted the second mirror toward the tallest glittering peak. [392] Inside the instrument, the mountain came down until its summit touched the dirt. [405] A seam appeared across Tomas's chest. [411]

"Look into the little mirror, Elin," Mara said, holding it where her daughter could see her own face. [429]

Every apprentice learned to recreate a broken reflection through synchronized breathing, one living person facing another across the damaged glass. [449] Matched breaths taught the silver which bodies belonged outside it. [459] Elin had helped repair a hundred mirrors in their shop, but now her breath came shallowly, trailing crystals. [477] Mara slowed her own breathing to match it, resisting the urge to order the girl into her rhythm. [495]

They inhaled together, and the seam widened into a shining edge between the girl's shoulder and the hand upon it. [515] They exhaled, and Elin pulled one foot free of the ice. [526]

"If you shut me away, you lose him again," Tomas said. [537]

Mara's next breath caught; the mountains rang, and the girl's freed foot began to whiten. [552] Elin lifted her face, no longer looking at the beautiful young man. [564]

"I remember him teaching you to whistle," she whispered. [573] "You don't have to keep him talking." [580]

Mara let the air leave her, and when Elin drew breath again, she followed. [594] The dream's mouth opened, but nothing breathed inside it. [603] On their next exhalation, the glass mountains folded silently into the sextant, carrying the orchard in Elin's eyes and the hands in her hair. [627] Mara pressed silver leaf over both mirrors, sealing them before she could search that tiny distance for her brother's face. [647]

The ancient fog remained, and beneath it the orchard was only an orchard: ruined walls, exhausted trees, two women shivering in the mud. [670] Elin stood with difficulty, and Mara opened her coat to gather the girl against her own imperfect warmth. [688] They found the path by feeling for stones beneath their soles, taking small steps that left deep, separate prints. [707] At the gate, her daughter stopped matching her breath, and Mara let her. [720]
