The night before the Orpheum was demolished, I climbed into its projection booth carrying eleven goodbye letters I had never sent my mother. [23] She had worked in this vintage movie palace for forty years, threading other people's endings through the machines. [41] When cancer took her voice, I stopped visiting because I could not bear the silence. [56] My sister Ruth stayed. [60]

I was a crystal gazer by trade, but grief had made every future in my sphere look like an empty chair. [81] What I wanted now was to find where unused love is stored, and whether mine could still reach her. [100]

Ruth had mailed me the booth key and our mother's last instructions, wrapped around a film strip spliced into a loop. [121] "Enter while forgotten lyrics search for tune," the note said, "and leave before they find it." [137] Beneath that, in smaller writing: "You always understood rooms better than people." [149] It was true that I was spatially imaginative; as a child, I had designed imaginary houses with staircases connecting disagreements to apologies. [171] In actual houses, I usually left. [177]

Before dawn, words began whispering through the ventilation grille, scraps of songs whose melodies had vanished with their singers. [196] The empty auditorium answered with a hushed thunder, like applause buried under snow. [209] Before threading the loop, I held the film strip against my forehead for a psychometry reading. [225] Objects remember touch, but also the things a hand wanted and could not ask for. [240]

My mother's fingers arrived first, smelling of oranges and hot metal. [251] Then came hundreds of envelopes, pushed beneath the booth door by patrons who needed somewhere to put their farewells. [270] She had laid each letter against unexposed film, letting the projector's warmth incubate whatever tenderness had survived the words. [289] I had mistaken her work for merely keeping pictures moving. [299]

When I started the projector, its beam opened a corridor where the booth wall should have been. [316] My letters pulled toward it like iron toward a magnet. [326] Beyond were kitchens never built, beds never shared, and platforms where nobody had stepped down from the train. [344] Love had made room for what never happened. [352] A staircase flattened into a passage when I imagined the weight of someone too tired to climb. [369] I followed the slope of my envelopes through a nursery of unfinished places, mapping each turn against the palace's familiar aisles. [390] Behind me, the whispered lyrics began testing notes. [398]

At the corridor's end stood my mother's kitchen, with two cups waiting on the table. [413] I rushed inside, already composing the apology that would make her get up from her chair. [429] But the chair was empty. [434]

Under one cup lay a goodbye letter in her hand, addressed to me. [447] Most of it concerned practical things: the boiler, her burial dress, where Ruth should find the insurance papers. [465] The last line read, "I am frightened Ruth will have nobody to be tired with." [480]

Through the kitchen window I saw my sister in her apartment, eating toast over the sink. [496] Her shoulders were still lifted, waiting for a call from the sickroom. [508] I had imagined unused love as treasure owed to me, not work my mother could no longer do. [526] Ruth had put the key in the mail because I had let her calls go unanswered. [542]

Somewhere overhead, the scattered words caught the first bar of a lullaby. [554] The corridor began folding, its rooms closing like hands around things they could not release. [569] I could stay here furnishing an impossible reunion, or learn the way out. [582] I laid my eleven letters beside hers, not as payment, but because I finally knew what I had been carrying. [602] I pictured my childhood staircase between disagreement and apology, and its narrow steps unfolded beneath my feet. [619]

They delivered me to the booth as the projector clicked into darkness. [631] For a moment I heard only the enormous quiet of a building ready to disappear. [646] Then I removed the film, wound it carefully, and carried it downstairs. [658] There would be other projectors, other people needing somewhere to leave a letter. [671]

Outside, I called Ruth, although it was much too early. [681] "Nothing's happened," I said when she answered in that terrible, ready voice. [693] "I wondered if I could bring you breakfast." [701]

She did not forgive me; she told me the bakery would not open until six. [716] I sat on its cold stone step and waited, keeping the film inside my coat. [731]
