On Vey, an island where shadows outlived people, reality checker Mara interviewed ghosts separately and wrote her findings in pencil. [20] Her methods were empirically whimsical, but her job was practical: distinguish shared wonders from contagious mistakes before somebody stepped onto an imaginary bridge. [43] Since lightning killed the island's memory tree, that distinction had become dangerous. [55] Yesterday, twelve neighbors had agreed there was a ferry, and a child had nearly drowned. [70]

Only one knowledge fruit seed remained, nested in the brass hearing trumpet Mara had inherited from her mother. [88] The seed could restore their collective memory, if she could waken it through interpreted dreams before the monsoon arrived. [107] Unrooted, it would rot in the seasonal deluge; rooted, it would drink the rain and give their history leaves. [126] That was what the surviving nursery ledger promised, beneath instructions half consumed by silverfish. [140] Mara trusted ledgers only slightly more than ghosts, but there were no other seeds. [154]

She established her experiment on the ancient ridge in bloom, where the dead tree's roots still gripped the island. [173] Under lanterns, sleepers described their dreams into the trumpet, whose narrow end she had buried above the seed. [191] Each account went on a separate card, untouched by anybody else's interpretation. [203]

Most dreamed of white boats carrying children away from a burning shore, with Mara's mother standing at the helm. [222] The council called it the Great Rescue, the story sung at every naming feast. [236] Mara interpreted the boats as salvation and recited the names of the rescued. [249] The seed remained cold. [253]

On the third night, Tovin, who cleaned the council drains, dreamed of hands hammering beneath white decks. [270] He woke apologizing. [273] His grandmother had always hated the rescue song, he said, though she could no longer remember why. [290]

Mara almost filed his card among the fever dreams. [299] Her mother had carried her through those feast days, letting her ring the little captain's bell against her chest. [318] Instead, she took Tovin to the abandoned harbor and measured the hull of the last rescue boat. [335] Beneath its false floor, they found wrist rings bolted to the timbers and names scratched where daylight never reached. [354] One was his grandmother's. [358]

Back on the ridge, the council ordered Mara to interpret the rings as storm restraints. [373] Perhaps they were, she thought, and hated how eagerly she reached for that perhaps. [387] The evidence established captivity, not who had ordered it, not whether anyone had also been saved. [403] She could protect her mother's reputation by calling uncertainty proof, or protect cultural memory by leaving the question open. [422]

That evening, with thunder loosening the horizon, Mara invited the families who never attended naming feasts. [438] She asked for dreams, then for the words their grandparents had forbidden them to sing. [453] Their boats had brought laborers for the council's terraces, they remembered, and children whose parents were left ashore. [471] Some called the crossing rescue; others called it theft. [480]

Mara laid both accounts beside the seed, distinguishing what the hull confirmed from what only a dream suggested. [498] As she interpreted the white decks as both shelter and concealment, a pale root reached toward the dead tree. [517] Nobody cheered. [519] Tovin asked whether the new tree would make everyone remember the same thing. [532]

Mara looked at the trumpet, through which her mother had once begged her to speak more clearly. [549] "I think it needs us to keep listening," she said, and wrote that down as a hypothesis, not a law. [569]

They kept vigil while the ridge flowers folded shut and the first warm drops darkened their cards. [586] By dawn, roots had threaded the old rootways, and a sapling grew through the trumpet's mouth. [602] Its leaves held no official song, only voices that could be heard in turn by pressing an ear to the bark. [623] Among them Mara recognized her mother's, frightened and giving orders she could not yet bear to understand. [640] She did not move away. [645] Rain shone on the listening faces, and beneath the uncertain chorus, roots tightened their hold on the island. [663]
