When Mara's father came out of prison, the village asked its firefighter to make sure he never lit anything again. [20] She checked his rented room for a smoke alarm, then invited him to a singing lesson. [36] The invitation was not forgiveness. [41] She wrote that underneath the time. [47]

Eighteen years earlier, he had burned his failing chandlery for the insurance, believing she was sleeping at a friend's house. [67] Her mother had gone upstairs to fetch her. [75] Only Mara came down. [79] Surgeons had needed to graft skin across her chest; afterward, every full breath pulled against a seam. [96] Singing had taught her to fill that breath without fearing it. [107] Years of rehabilitation had made firefighting possible, but she still sang before every shift. [121]

Now she taught other survivors in the obsidian lagoon beneath the dunes, where an ancient lava tube opened onto tidal water. [142] Even a damaged whisper returned from those glassy walls with something like wings. [155] She wanted to prove that scars can sing, not that suffering improves anyone. [168]

Her father arrived during the migration of clouds, when the summer banks sailed inland and their shadows crossed the dunes like traveling herds. [191] Once their rains raised the groundwater, lessons would move uphill to the village hall. [205] Mara had given him the hour before the others came; none would be asked to sing beside him. [223]

She waited on the ledge, gracefully alert, watching the tide mark while unwinding red thread from her fingers. [241] Her metronome was a foreign coin drilled and strung on red thread, the last money her mother had brought across the sea. [263] No shop here would take it, so her mother had made it keep time. [277] During Mara's recovery, its slow swing had measured the breaths she could bear. [290]

Her father stopped when he recognized it. [297]

"I shouldn't be here," he said. [303]

She pointed to the stool. [308] "You shouldn't have set the fire." [314]

He sat, folding his scarred hands between his knees. [323] He had burned them trying, too late, to climb the stairs. [334] That fact had once seemed to him a defense; prison had worn it down to another fact. [351]

Mara poured black sand from the volcanic beach into a shallow shell and placed it beneath the coin. [369] Against bare shell, each tap multiplied into a confusing clatter; the fine, heavy sand softened it to one distinct pulse. [389] She kept the coin swinging with small movements of her wrist. [400] Her father only had to follow: breathe in, let the next breath leave as sound. [415]

He opened his mouth and coughed. [421] She offered water, waited, and began again. [428] Outside, kelp drifted past the opening without snagging, each ribbon finding its own unhurried passage. [443]

"They'll think you're saying it doesn't matter." [450]

"Some already do." [453]

"Then why?" [455]

She watched the little foreign sun pass over the black shore in her shell. [469]

"Because when someone cannot breathe, I help." [476]

She nearly added that this was all she could give him, then stopped making promises about the limits of tomorrow. [496]

He asked for the song her mother used to sing while counting change. [509] Mara's wrist went still. [513] The coin settled into the sand. [519]

"Not that one," she said. [524]

He lowered his head, and she recognized his old retreat: if he could not have everything, he would accept nothing. [544]

"Just one note," she said, lifting the thread. [552] "Stay for that." [555]

For a while there was only the softened ticking and the water breathing against stone. [570] Then he made a low, wavering hum. [577] She answered on the same note, letting him borrow its steadiness without disguising the tremor in his own. [595] The lagoon returned both voices, neither beautiful, neither lost. [604]

When her watch chimed, Mara closed her hand around the coin. [615] The others would arrive soon, and she would keep her word to them. [628]

Her father asked whether he could come next week. [637]

"At dawn," she said. [641] "If the passage floods, I'll meet you outside." [649]

He left before the first singers appeared, carrying no forgiveness he could show the village, only a note he could try again. [671] Mara smoothed the sand with her palm and checked the waterline. [682] Above the dunes, the clouds kept moving; below them, she set the small sun swinging for whoever came next. [701]
