Mara had been a harmonic stone skimmer for fifty years before her knees refused the canyon stairs. [17] Her trade was sounding safe paths by skipping tuned pebbles along the invisible ridges of pressure between the ancestral towers. [37] Now she watched her granddaughter carry those pebbles down, and tried not to mistake helplessness for wisdom. [54]

They lived among towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes, where a wrong turn could last a lifetime. [74] The bones belonged to creatures whose names survived only in songs Mara refused to shorten. [89] For decades she had brushed through centuries of layered dust to copy the inscriptions on their ribs. [106] She would not scrape them, even when younger keepers laughed at her slow brush. [120]

That morning, a dust storm uncovered an opening in the oldest tower, and something inside began beating. [137] It was not a heart, although every rib answered it. [147] Mara recognized the first skimmer's signal, a pulse left wandering after his station had been abandoned during the famine. [166] Freed from its muffling dust, it swallowed the small, clean notes of Mara's pebbles. [180] Below, her granddaughter Senn was bringing the winter water caravan home. [191] Mara heard three whistles: lost, halted, waiting. [198]

She dragged herself to the station balcony, but the stairs beyond it might as well have been a precipice. [217] For months she had wanted to learn to navigate without moving; until now, the wish had felt like bargaining. [236] She blew the whistle for patience, then made herself appraise what the storm had changed. [251] The tower trembled, but its nesting swifts had not fled. [261] They streamed into the canyon, separating around empty spaces and closing ranks beyond them. [275] Pressure hurt their hollow bones before it could break a human body. [287] Through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight, she could see the dangerous echoes taking shape. [305] She reached for a pebble, then stopped. [312]

On the exposed opening, beneath a fringe of dust, was the same branching diagram she had copied forty years earlier. [332] She had called it a family tree. [339] Now the swifts traced its branches in the air, and she understood it was a chart of listening. [357] At its root stood a seated figure holding a pointed stone against a notch. [371]

Mara unwrapped the stone arrowhead she wore beneath her shirt, the relic her family said had killed the last giant. [391] She had never questioned that story, though the edges were polished smooth and neither face bore an impact scar. [410] Her ancestors had carried it away when they fled. [419] They had preserved the object and forgotten its work. [428]

The arrowhead fitted the notch, point inward, its shoulders resting against two fine ridges. [442] When she tilted the tang, the ancient pulse shifted, and the swifts followed a narrow lane toward the eastern pass. [462] A steering key, not a weapon. [468] The first skimmers had guided sound from their seats; their descendants had learned to chase its remains on foot. [487]

She whistled Senn east, measuring each instruction against the birds, not against the familiar paths she could no longer trust. [507] The stone grated beneath her thumb. [513] A pale flake fell from the edge she had protected all her life. [526] Mara almost withdrew it. [530] Then Senn whistled again, closer now, and Mara pressed until the birds swung west around a crumbling bridge. [548] Reverence, she discovered, could mean allowing an old thing to become useful again. [561]

By dusk the caravan reached the stairs, its water skins intact and every traveler answering when Senn counted. [579] Mara remained seated, one hand cramped around the arrowhead, her shadow stretched across the chart. [594] She had crossed the canyon a dozen times that afternoon without leaving the balcony. [608]

When Senn climbed up to embrace her, Mara asked for a cushion and her smallest brush. [624] There were other figures beneath the dust, other gestures she had once mistaken for prayers. [639] Below them, the pulse went out and returned, no longer wandering, while the swifts settled into their ancestral roosts. [658] Tomorrow she would teach the child where to sit. [667]
