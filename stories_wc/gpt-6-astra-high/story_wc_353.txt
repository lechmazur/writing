In the siskin alder grove, Mara watched yellow birds open their beaks, then looked away before memory could supply the missing notes. [22] The Hush had left everything capable of trembling, but nothing capable of being heard. [36] Once a celebrated tree-song sculptor, she had carved living wood until wind passing through its hollows became music. [54] Now she worked to create music in a silent world, mostly because her daughter had stopped asking what singing meant. [74] Nell was eleven, born after the last audible spring. [83] She accompanied Mara through the grove, carrying tools and rescuing whatever small creatures her mother's experiments disturbed. [100]

Their latest failure had split an alder, exposing a wooden figure enclosed for decades inside its swollen trunk. [118] Six arms spread from its chest, each ending in a shallow hollow, like an invitation to place a palm. [137] A porcelain insulator sat where its heart should have been. [147] Mara had seen such glazed spools on the abandoned telegraph poles beyond the marsh. [161] Someone had built this instrument from the remains of another means of speaking. [174] By analyzing stress cracks in the long-forgotten sculpture, she discovered that its arms had flexed independently, not swung together. [193] Fine channels connected them through the insulator's polished groove, which bore the dark trace of vanished wire. [210] The glaze had let the wire slide without sawing through the wood. [222] It was an instrument for sharing tension. [229]

Mara fitted fresh wire, braced the damaged arms with springy alder splints, and summoned the few neighbors who still humored her. [250] Symphonically minded, she assigned each person a pattern, determined to recover the layered richness she remembered. [266] They pressed their palms into the hollows while she began to strum the exposed wire. [281] Nothing sounded, but the instrument shivered against their hands. [290] Mara increased the tension, demanding sharper pulses, stronger contrasts, the impossible return of thunder. [304] Nell snatched her hand away. [309] Beneath the sculpture, a hedgehog tucked itself into a bristling ball. [320]

Mara reached for the tuning peg, but Nell caught her wrist. [331] "You keep making it hurt," she signed. [338]

"I want you to feel what I felt," Mara answered with her free hand. [352]

Nell looked at the sculpture, then down at the frightened animal. [363] "Then feel what we feel first." [369]

The neighbors waited, their hands hovering above the wood. [378] Mara remembered how often she had called their hesitation ignorance, and how patiently they had returned. [394] She loosened the wire until its pull scarcely registered against her skin, then offered Nell the place beside the insulator. [414] The girl did not begin immediately. [420]

"After the hedgehog uncurls," she signed. [426]

They waited through the slow dripping of water from the leaves, visible but unheard. [440] Eventually a pointed nose emerged, and Nell rested her palm in the smallest hollow. [454] She pressed once, lightly, and Mara felt the answering tug. [464] Instead of correcting its uncertain rhythm, she eased her own arm forward, giving the girl room to press again. [483] Across the sculpture, the baker's stiff fingers made a halting counterpulse; his wife answered by taking some of his strain. [503] Their neighbor, who had buried his brother that winter, held his hollow still until everyone else softened around his stillness. [523] Then he moved, and they moved with him. [531]

The wire traveled over the porcelain, carrying each adjustment to every hand without deciding which person ought to lead. [550] Mara could feel five separate impulses becoming something none of them could have made alone. [565] It was not the music she had lost, and for a moment that difference hurt worse than silence. [583] Nell noticed the resistance in her mother's hand and waited without pulling. [595] Mara let her grief enter the pattern as a slow, heavy pressure, and felt it accepted rather than repaired. [614] Empathy in motion: the phrase arrived without grandeur, merely a name for what their hands were learning. [631]

When another child approached, Nell beckoned him toward the empty arm, and the others shifted to receive his weight. [650] Mara stepped back only far enough to strum a delicate pulse beneath them, following rather than commanding. [667] Under the sculpture, the hedgehog nosed through wet leaves, undisturbed, and Nell began a tune her mother had never known. [687]
