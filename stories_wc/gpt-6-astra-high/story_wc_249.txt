On the last evening of the expanding universe, Mara sat in the hospital's sound wave garden, drumming on everything within reach. [21] Bedrails, watering pipes, the porcelain bowl that held her supper: each answered with a different note. [37] Above her, flowers made of suspended mineral dust opened and closed as standing waves combed their petals. [54] They glowed the pale green of an aurora fading before anyone had finished watching. [68] Her sister's breathing came through a speaker beneath the bench. [78]

Three months earlier, Mara had volunteered to be an experimental treatment guinea pig because Iona could no longer wake after the mine collapse. [101] The doctors had entangled their memories, using Mara's intact recollections to rebuild the paths through Iona's damaged brain. [119] Now both sisters remembered being pulled from the rubble, although Mara had been on the surface, packing their lunches. [138] Neither could remember which of them had first stopped breathing. [148]

The garden translated their entangled memories into sound so the doctors could hear where one mind ended and the other began. [169] Instead, every diagnostic tone returned doubled, then multiplied, until seizures shook both beds. [182] "At the hush of the universe inhaling," Doctor Sen explained, "we can sever the link without feedback." [199] Between expansion and contraction, the ancient gravitational murmur powering the garden would cease for precisely one second. [216] Sen could preserve Mara's original memories, but the borrowed pathways keeping Iona alive would disappear. [231]

Mara nodded with the docility that made the nurses call her brave, then tapped three quick beats against her cup. [251] She had always been quietly rebellious, saving her disobedience for things nobody thought to confiscate. [266] From the speaker came a breath, a pause, and two faint clicks. [278] Iona had answered that rhythm on their bedroom wall whenever thunder frightened Mara. [291] The doctors' perfect tones made echoes; their imperfect childhood code made conversation. [303]

Mara climbed onto the bench to perch beside the speaker, bringing her tapping fingers close to its listening membrane. [322] She drummed on the grille, the tiles, her hollow breastbone, offering questions without words. [336] As Iona answered, the dust flowers streamed together into an auric lagoon under starlight, its golden water trembling beneath constellations neither sister had ever visited. [361] Mara recognized the impossible holiday they had invented as children, coughing mine dust out of their blankets. [378] A place where the water shone instead of the slag heaps, where nobody had to earn another mouthful of air. [398] They had worked double shifts to buy passage somewhere like it, until the mountain took Iona. [414] Now the lagoon's two shores pulsed separately, one following Mara's fingers, the other following Iona's breath. [430]

"They're not echoes," Mara told Sen when he hurried in to stop the unauthorized noise. [445] "Let us finish answering before you cut." [452]

Sen watched the shores, then changed the surgical display from a single waveform to two. [467] "If I follow these boundaries, I may save both minds, but everything between them will be lost." [484]

Between the shores floated their mother's face, the bedroom wall, their lunches, all the years that made them sisters. [503] Mara tapped the question she could not bear to ask aloud. [514] Iona answered twice, their old signal for going together into someplace dark. [526] They had wanted to breathe life into a shared dream, not die remembering one. [540]

When the universal murmur stopped, Mara struck the bench one last time, and Sen cut along the silence between the answering beats. [562] The lagoon collapsed into ordinary dust. [568]

In the morning, a woman with the same crooked little finger was wheeled into Mara's room. [584] Sen told them they were sisters, and neither disputed him, though both looked away when he mentioned their mother. [603] There was sorrow without a picture to hold it, like colored light moving behind closed eyes. [619]

Mara made room on the bench. [625] "What would you like to do when we leave?" [634]

The woman considered the question while green blossoms opened above them, sustained now by the universe's long inward tide. [653] "Somewhere with clean air," she said, and her fingers drummed an unfamiliar rhythm against the wheel of her chair. [672]

Mara listened until she understood where an answer might fit. [682] Then she began. [685]
