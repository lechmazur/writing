After the factory shut down, Mara could hear the limestone cliffs dripping from her kitchen window. [16] For thirty years, its crushers had supplied the village with wages and an unbroken excuse not to listen. [34] Now the last maintenance crew brought her something recovered while cleaning the sump: her father's lost expedition compass. [52] She recognized the dent where she had dropped it, aged nine, just before his final descent. [68] The official explanation was a sudden flood. [75] There had never been a body. [81]

Mara had become a petrichor collector, selling bottled scents of rain striking different soils to perfumers who preferred romance to chemistry. [102] Her neighbors called her sensibly eccentric: she labeled every impossible fragrance and never entered a cave without two lamps. [121] Privately, she collected because her father had promised to show her the place where explanations fail. [137] Every first rain seemed to lift a little of that place into the air. [151]

She unwrapped the compass on her workbench and prepared a psychometry reading, grounding herself with the familiar scent of wet garden earth. [173] Objects gave her their strongest memories, but without that anchor she sometimes mistook another person's terror for her own. [192] Beneath her fingers, brass became her father's palm. [200] She smelled rain on limestone that had never known the sky, then saw a subterranean lagoon beneath the cliffs. [219] Across his open notebook ran two words, underlined until the paper tore: hinged awakenings. [233] He opened the compass lid, and someone under the water opened his eyes. [246] Then the reading broke against a roar of pumps. [255]

The factory had begun draining the caverns the week her father disappeared. [267] Tonight, with its pumps stopped forever, the lagoon would return. [277] Mara packed her ropes, both lamps, and the garden rain. [287] If she meant to delve into his last discovery, she had only the hours before the access tunnel flooded. [306]

Below the abandoned intake, she followed that impossible limestone scent through darkness soft with settling dust. [322] Water lapped over rusted survey stakes as she reached the lagoon, where each drop sounded once above her and once inside her ribs. [345] She tied her rope, marked the rising water, and opened the compass. [357]

Her father stood on the reflection of her ledge, young enough now to be her younger brother. [374] The falling drops stopped halfway to the surface. [382] His mouth shaped her name. [387]

She snapped the lid shut, and water struck her boots. [397] When she opened it again, his next breath continued exactly where it had stopped. [411] The hinge did not open a door; it exchanged which side was awake. [424]

"I thought I could bring someone back," he said, looking past her toward a smaller figure in the depths. [443] Mara knew her mother's yellow dress from the photograph beside his bed. [455] "Then the pumps started, the water fell, and the compass slipped out of my hand." [470]

For years, Mara had defended him against people who called his expedition an elaborate abandonment. [485] Now there was love in his explanation, and abandonment too, neither canceling the other. [499]

"Can you come up?" [503]

He lifted his foot, and her foot lifted with it, pulling her toward the edge. [518] They both recoiled. [521] Across the hinge, there was room for only one of them to return. [534]

He saw her understand, and for the first time that evening he looked older than she was. [551] "You were nine," he said. [556] "I should have stayed." [560]

No revelation opened beneath those words, only the ache she had carried into every rainfall. [575] She wanted a reason large enough to undo it, and found instead a man who had made a terrible choice. [595] Her bottle of garden rain pressed against her breastbone, smelling faintly of the home she had made without him. [614]

"I came to find you," she said, "not to follow you." [625]

He nodded, and she let herself look until looking was no longer a bargain. [639] Then she closed the lid. [644]

By the time she climbed outside, water was swallowing the lowest knot in her rope. [659] She carried the compass home and laid it beside her jars, where the brass slowly warmed in the morning sun. [679] For once, she made no label. [685] Outside, rain touched the silent factory yard, and she opened her window without reaching for a bottle. [702]
