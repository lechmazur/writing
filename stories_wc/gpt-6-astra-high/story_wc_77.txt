After the resuscitation, every photograph of me retained a lingering luminance, like grease shining on cold soup. [17] At first I hid them in drawers, but the light seeped through the joints and brought a smell of river mud. [39] Then my mother's drowned face appeared behind my shoulder in a passport picture taken three weeks after her funeral. [58] Her mouth was open against my ear. [65] I knew what she wanted me to hear. [73]

During the flood, she had clutched my opera cloak as the current dragged her beneath the carnival's funhouse floor. [92] Still wearing my costume from the evening performance, I tore its clasp free rather than follow her under. [110] The rescuers found me downstream, without a pulse, holding the little silver masks of Comedy and Tragedy. [127] Now the opera cloak clasp was the only thing I could bear to wear from that night. [144] I pinned it over my heart, where their locked mouths couldn't accuse me. [157]

Venn, the carnival's whispering advisor, answered my letter with instructions to meet him where my mother had disappeared. [175] He had always whispered so customers would lean close and mistake their own quickened breathing for something waiting just behind his shoulder. [197] He waited inside the abandoned carnival funhouse, surrounded by mirrors furred with silt. [210]

"You are photographically cursed," he whispered, holding my passport picture by its edges. [223] "Something entered while you were neither alive nor dead, and now it develops itself wherever your likeness is fixed." [242]

He laid the picture against a mirror, and my mother slowly filled the glass. [256] Pearls of water clung to her lashes; tiny white worms slipped between lips I remembered kissing when I was small. [276] I wanted to lick the water away. [283]

Venn caught my wrist and opened the battered reference book he had used at his booth. [299] The carnival's dream interpreters passed their craft down through paradoxical notes in a dream dictionary, protecting their livelihood by making the instructions look useless. [323] Beside FEAR, someone had written: To question the oracle, first teach it a lie. [337] Beside CONFESSION: Unbosom yourself to the witness who cannot know. [347]

"We need to trick fear into revealing its weakness," Venn said, "not persuade your mother to forgive you." [365]

The distinction felt obscene, but I touched the clasp beneath my coat and studied the woman in the mirror. [384] "You always loved the ruby in Comedy's eye," I told her, although both masks had plain silver faces. [402] Her wet hand rose from below the frame, holding my lost cloak, and a red stone bloomed in its fastening. [422]

My knees weakened with something almost like delight. [430] It could wear the dead, but it could not remember them. [441] I pulled out the real clasp, and the creature's expression flickered before settling into my mother's familiar disappointment. [459]

"You let go," it said, and that was true enough to bring me a step closer. [475]

Venn turned the dictionary toward me again, his finger beneath the second note. [488] I had come prepared to confess that I was a murderer, because a verdict seemed more bearable than the endless trial. [509] Instead I told the thing what I had never told the police, the priest, or myself. [525]

"I wanted to live, and I don't know whether she wanted me to save her or save myself." [543]

The face stretched toward mine, beautiful with decay, offering the answer I still wanted more than sleep. [560]

"I don't know," I repeated, "and neither do you." [569]

Its mouth opened, but this time I watched it waiting for me to supply the words. [585] There was its weakness: it needed my certainty, even the certainty that I deserved to suffer. [601] I couldn't stop being afraid, but I could leave a question unanswered. [613]

The lingering luminance drained from the mirror, taking the worms, the cloak, and the counterfeit ruby with it. [631] Venn handed back a dark, ordinary photograph in which I looked exhausted and badly in need of a haircut. [650]

Outside, dawn found the clasp in my palm. [658] I remembered my mother fastening it before the performance, telling me that the audience deserved to hear me breathe. [677] It was not forgiveness, and I did not make it into forgiveness. [689] I put the silver faces in my pocket and took a breath she had never heard. [705]
