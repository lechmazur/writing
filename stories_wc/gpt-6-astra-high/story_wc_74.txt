Iona, an insomniac painter, kept her studio in a rooftop greenhouse above a smog-choked city. [15] Under the waxing moon's glow, saffron crocuses crowded the beds her father could no longer tend. [31] Tomas had once sheltered newly arrived families there, feeding them rice yellowed with the precious stigmas while they found work. [51] Now he intended to sell the glass for enough money to leave. [63]

"Strangers forget kindness," he said, folding the blankets nobody had borrowed in years. [76]

Iona wanted to prove him wrong, partly because she loved him and partly because she could not bear another departure. [96] The city had banned the newcomers' mother tongues; two generations of factory schooling had finished what the ordinances began. [115] The ban had lapsed, but shame outlived it. [123] His guestbook remained, but its names belonged to lost languages, unreadable even to the children of those who had written them. [144]

Beside it lay his old calligrapher's moon-tipped brush, its silver crescent tarnished by the same soot that coated the greenhouse. [164] Tomas had taught her that the brush could rouse buried words through daydreams sparked by moonlight. [180] Unlike ordinary dreams, these needed no sleep, only something the dreamer recognized in the painting. [195]

"Not many people care to remember," he said when she polished it. [207]

Methodically hopeful, Iona posted invitations at factory gates, promising portraits of whatever visitors had lost. [222] She prepared clean paper, numbered chairs, and a ledger with a column headed REMEMBERED. [236]

Seven people climbed the stairs the following evening, drawn more by free tea than by the promise. [253] While Tomas packed seed jars, she painted him standing proudly at the greenhouse door, as she remembered him. [271] The silver tip laid a trembling thread of moonlight along his shoulders. [283]

Nobody spoke. [285] One woman apologized for not knowing the important man. [294]

Iona looked down at her ledger, suddenly ashamed of the verdict waiting in its empty column. [310] These people had climbed six flights after twelve hours of work, and she was asking them to perform gratitude. [329]

She turned the paper over and brought out bowls. [338] There was yesterday's rice to make soup, and Tomas picked three crocuses to season it. [353] As he served the woman, his thumb found a chip in the bowl's rim and turned it gently away from her. [374] Iona remembered that gesture from childhood, when her own hands had been too small to manage the bowls. [392]

She painted only the hand, the chipped rim, and the yellow steam. [404] Then she wiped soot from a pane and held the picture where the moon could find it. [421]

The woman stopped eating. [425] Her eyes stayed open, but her fingers tightened around the bowl as if she were holding it through a child's hands. [446] A word escaped her, soft and complicated. [453] Moonlight gathered beneath Iona's brush, bending itself into the hooked letters that began one entry in the guestbook. [471]

"My mother said that when someone spared you a small hurt," the woman whispered. [485] "I thought it meant soup." [490]

She looked at Tomas, not quite recognizing him, and shifted her chair to make room for a late arrival. [509] Iona painted the offered place, and another visitor began remembering a blanket lifted, snow melting from boots beneath unfamiliar stars. [529] By midnight, three vanished tongues had found their way into the greenhouse, haltingly, with mistakes nobody hurried to correct. [548]

Iona could have filled her ledger, but instead she painted each remembered kindness and copied the words beneath it. [567] When the moon slipped behind a chimney, the woman could still say hers. [580] She taught it to the latecomer, who repeated it while washing bowls. [592]

Nobody thanked Tomas in a speech, and nobody offered to buy his glass. [605] But the woman asked whether she might bring her grandson tomorrow, and a boiler mechanic offered to mend the greenhouse heater. [626]

Tomas unpacked the seeds. [630]

At dawn, Iona crossed out REMEMBERED and wrote THINGS TO DO, beginning with a list of panes that needed cleaning. [650] She did not hear her father unfold a blanket or feel him settle it around her shoulders. [667]
