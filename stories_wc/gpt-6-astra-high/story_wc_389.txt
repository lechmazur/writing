In the stormlit observatory above clouds, Mera kept three potatoes, a brass telescope, and a grudge against the weather. [19] For forty years, the city's sky shutters had poured daylight onto palace gardens while the streets below cultivated mushrooms and rickets. [40] Dismissed for proposing equal light, she had become the sort of lonely visionary who argued with bolts and generally lost. [60]

Then lightning lit the cobalt glass portico, and its shadow fell blue across her floor. [75] Inside that shadow, something began to unfold: a diagram, branching whenever lightning changed the angle. [90] The ancient builders had etched their maintenance plans between layers of glass, where the palace censors had never thought to scrape. [111] The cobalt filtered away the lightning's glare until the hidden lines stood clear enough to copy. [127] Mera spent winter tracing the diagrams in soot, determined to shape a new dawn using ancient shadows rather than another king. [148]

With unlicensed transmissions banned, she sent instructions through fragmented radio frequencies, hiding each piece between shipping forecasts and advertisements for miraculous boot grease. [171] Only a gutter mechanic named Ash bothered to collect them all. [182] His receiver lived in a biscuit tin; his little sister had died without ever seeing her own shadow. [200] Mera taught him to free one shutter, and morning spilled onto his alley like a dropped bucket of gold. [219]

By spring, people were calling him the Tin Prophet. [228] By summer, his lyrically prophetic broadcasts had become the city's illicit prayer: "The hinges remember, and the dark will open." [248] He meant that people should oil the mechanisms; people preferred to kneel. [260] From her mountain, Mera listened as gratitude acquired marching boots. [270] Ash's followers freed seven districts, then painted his face on the shutter towers and demanded that every repair begin with an oath. [292] She recognized the tune, having once refused to sing it. [302]

When autumn storms exposed the final diagram, she discovered why the original system had required a hundred separate receivers. [321] The builders called it variant allegiance: no district's promise was permanent, and every district could withdraw its support from whoever coordinated the light. [344] The palace had bypassed those voices with a central override. [354]

Ash asked her for its location. [360] "One hand on the lever," he said over the crackling radio, "and I can promise them daylight forever." [378] He added that the barracks could wait for theirs, just until the soldiers learned what his sister had endured. [397] Behind him, a crowd chanted his name with the steady rhythm of a factory press. [412]

Mera nearly told him, because she had heard that voice shake whenever he mentioned his sister, and because being needed was a treacherous warmth. [436] Instead, she asked which district would lose its morning when it refused his oath. [450]

For three nights, his frequency carried only weather reports. [459] On the fourth, palace artillery began climbing the mountain in luminous, unpleasant arcs. [472] Ash was besieging the central tower, and its soldiers intended to destroy the observatory before he could use her knowledge. [492] A shell knocked her potatoes into the abyss, which seemed excessive even by royal standards. [507]

Then Ash's voice broke through: "Tell me how to make myself unnecessary." [519]

When his followers took the tower, she guided him to the override, and he smashed it with his mechanic's wrench. [539] Across the scattered frequencies, Mera broadcast the hundred local keys, one to each district, while Ash ordered his followers to let their neighbors vote. [563] Some districts chose Ash as coordinator; others chose committees, a retired baker, or nobody at all. [579] The shutters opened unevenly, with arguments, grinding gears, and one spectacular rain of pigeon filth. [594] There was no perfect dawn, only sunlight reaching a hundred quarrelsome neighborhoods that could now change their minds. [612]

In the silence after the guns stopped, Mera sat beneath the cracked portico and waited for loneliness to settle back into its usual chair. [636] Instead, her receiver spat, whistled, and produced a woman's furious voice demanding instructions for a jammed hinge at the barracks hospital. [657] Behind the woman, somebody was laughing, and Ash was saying that he did not know, but he would help. [676] Mera picked up her pencil and asked them to describe the shadow. [688]
