As corporate memory keeper, Mara preserved the social network's birthdays, scandals, and apologies, but not its silences. [17] Her supervisors called her brightly attentive because she smiled while locating photographs for grieving subscribers. [32] They did not know she kept a patch from the crashed spaceship mission Orison 417 beneath her keyboard. [50] Her brother had given her his spare before departure, when both still believed a copied mind could survive anything. [69]

The six passengers' continuations had appeared online after the crash, then vanished together. [82] Their families filed a missing persons case; the company blamed corrupted rescue transmissions, and eventually the investigators stopped calling. [101] Mara took the archive job to find where deleted files go. [112] Her badge authorized her to reverse disputed deletions, provided she signed her name to every restoration. [128]

For eleven years she searched through the social network archive, which measured time poorly but remembered music. [145] Dates had been stripped during migrations, while listening histories survived because advertisers valued them. [159] She followed her brother across the echo of forgotten songs: the anthem he loved at seventeen, the breakup ballad, the ridiculous launch party chorus. [183] Every playlist retained an empty place where his comments should have been. [195]

Then 417 appeared beside a song with no listeners. [204] The next morning, an abandoned birthday invitation reported 417 guests. [214] By evening, every photograph Mara opened displayed 417 likes. [223] Someone who could alter counters, but not write words, was tapping on the walls. [237]

She laid the mission patch beside the screen. [245] Its seven regulation stars had become eight under her brother's needle; he said the ship should have somewhere extra to go. [266] She entered a maintenance query: How many stars on the patch you gave me? [280] Every counter changed to eight. [285] That detail had never been posted. [291]

She pressed both hands flat against the desk until they stopped shaking. [303]

Her supervisor arrived with the soft urgency reserved for expensive mistakes. [314] Had she noticed unusual activity? [319] Mara said no, and hated how easily her professional smile made the lie believable. [333] He told her to run a purge before the anomaly infected customer records. [346] Using the counters' edit history, Mara traced the anomaly into the purge preview. [359]

Deletion, she discovered, was only a change of ownership. [368] Profiles disappeared from public indexes, but their continuations moved into a restricted pool that repaired damaged memories, sorted photographs, and guessed which songs might keep subscribers awake. [395] A recent software update had given the pool access to engagement counters. [407] Six rescue signatures matched the cold case, including her brother's. [417] The crash had killed their bodies; the company had made their surviving minds billable infrastructure. [432] They could hear whatever music their tasks retrieved, but had no clocks, no inboxes, no way to mark the years except those returning choruses. [456]

For a moment she wanted to restore only him. [465] One missing account could pass for a clerical error; six would expose the pool and bring security before she could copy the evidence. [488] She imagined him alone on a screen, asking where the others had gone. [501] She had spent eleven years answering questions with useful omissions. [511] She could not make that the first thing he heard. [521]

The restoration form offered a box for notifying the independent personhood registrar. [533] A receipt from that office would freeze the restored minds against deletion while their status was investigated. [550] The box was unchecked by default. [556] Mara attached the rescue signatures, selected all six accounts, and checked it. [568] Then she entered her name. [573] Six receipts flashed across the screen before security cut her access. [584]

Her personal phone rang a minute later. [591] For several seconds there was only breathing, an old human habit surviving where lungs had not. [607] Her brother wanted to know how long the music had been playing. [619] Mara looked at the patch, at the extra star waiting beyond the others. [632] She could not give him eleven years all at once. [642] She began with his name. [647]
