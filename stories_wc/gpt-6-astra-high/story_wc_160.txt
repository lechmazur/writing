Inside the dormant volcano, the crumbling amphitheater breathed against Mara's palms, warm air sighing through mortar where spectators once stamped for blood. [22] Below its stage, stormwater surged into the city's reservoir, compressing trapped air until the cracked building exhaled, then sucking it back. [43] Each breath widened the crack beside the main floodgate. [52] Mara, who could coax jammed machinery into motion with a spoon, could not coax stone into staying stone. [70] The superintendent offered her two orders, neither bearable: keep the gate shut and risk collapse, or open it and drown the potters below. [93] Above them, sunlight freckled the wet benches through rents in the awning, illuminating one possible disaster and then another. [112]

Mara wanted the reservoir saved; she had helped write the regulations that made its water last through summer. [130] She also remembered turning away a potter whose allocation had expired, and how politely he had folded his useless permit. [150] Her mother, the previous machinist, had called such obedience a very clean way of getting dirty. [166] After her funeral, Mara had inherited an antique surveyor's chain and an envelope containing nothing but a drawing of a spoon. [187] That apparently final joke had annoyed her enough to keep both objects in her toolbox. [202]

Now, hunting for a shim, she found the envelope and noticed grit embedded in its opened flap. [219] Not grit: carefully spaced islands of gum, the same interrupted pattern her mother used to encode unauthorized repairs in inspection logs. [240] She dusted the flap with graphite; by analyzing the leftover adhesive from that once sealed envelope, she could read her mother's last message. [263] The message read, "When the building breathes, give it another mouth: seven chains east, three links lift, and mind your manners." [284] Mara almost laughed; her mother had always disguised tenderness as an accusation of rudeness. [298]

Seven lengths of the chain brought her beneath the eastern benches, where a blocked arch bore the potters' wheel. [317] Behind a loose slab waited a small sluice, its lifting screw deliberately stripped, its bronze face polished by secret use. [337] The superintendent caught up, recognized the emblem, and went quiet. [347] "That conduit was closed when the pottery quarter lost its charter," he said. [360] "It runs around the crater into their hillside cisterns." [369] Not a thief's tunnel, then, but a second reservoir, erased from maps because its owners had become inconvenient. [387] Her mother had been feeding those cisterns in secret, preserving both the city's supply and the people its regulations forgot. [407] Mara understood the envelope now: encrypted compassion, a kindness forced to travel in the dress of maintenance instructions. [425]

The superintendent touched the stripped screw with an expression she recognized, the relief of finding a reason not to act. [445] "It cannot be opened." [449] "Not politely," Mara said, and took out her spoon. [458] She wedged its handle beneath the locking pawl, looped the chain around the lifting eye, and threaded a fallen handrail through its links. [481] With a block of masonry for a fulcrum, the handrail became a lever long enough for two. [498] Her hands began to tremble, not from doubt but from the weight gathering on the other side. [515]

"Three links," she told him, laying a loose section against the sluice frame to mark the prescribed opening. [533] "Any higher, and the cisterns may overflow; any lower, and this wall keeps splitting." [547] He glanced toward the city seal on his coat. [556] "Put my name in the report," she said, "but put your back into this first." [571]

On the next exhalation, he leaned beside her. [579] The gate rose, stopped at her mark, and sent a dark stream whispering into the outlawed conduit. [596] For two more breaths the crack widened; on the third, dust ceased falling from its lip. [612] Mara secured the lever with stones and stayed kneeling, watching sunlight move across the chain, unwilling to mistake hope for proof. [633] Only when the water gauge began descending did she let herself smile. [645]

"We could leave the conduit off the report," the superintendent offered. [656] She thought of her mother's hidden alphabet, and of the potter folding his permit until it was small enough to disappear. [677] "No," she said, giving him the envelope to write on. [687] "This time, we put the kindness on the outside." [696]
