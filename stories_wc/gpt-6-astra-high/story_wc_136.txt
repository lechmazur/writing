At one minute to midnight, Mara stood beneath the abandoned station's starlit vault, where mist drifted through the broken glass roof. [21] She had mapped every surviving tile, drawing their fractures as delicately as veins in a leaf. [37] The city's most meticulous route mapper, she was analytically artistic: she could make an invented road look more convincing than a real one. [60]

Eleven months earlier, that talent had helped drown her father and forty passengers. [73] To secure approval for the station's flood plan, her supervisor had asked her to mark a proposed escape stairway as complete. [94] She had obeyed, then testified that the conductor must have misunderstood her map. [107] Her father had taught her to read maps. [115]

Now his ticket punch lay across her palm, though divers had recovered neither his body nor his uniform. [133] It had arrived inside an envelope bearing the station's address and a departure time: New Year's midnight. [150] In daylight, the phantom train ticket punch slipped through her fingers; under stars, its cold handles held. [167] Along its jaws ran an inscription: Upon the hour that divides bad habits and new starts, dare to punch the truth. [188]

Her father had spoken of a fortress of unspoken truths, where testimony excluded from every earthly hearing waited for a witness. [209] Finding it had become her only reason to keep unraveling the mysteries his death had left behind. [226]

Midnight sounded from the municipal clock, and she fed her evacuation map between the jaws. [241] "The stairway was never built," she said. [248] The punch bit, removing a crescent of paper. [256] Outside the doors, cracks in the pavement shifted into arrows, their narrow shadows pointing toward the river. [273] She followed, recording each turn, but the arrows ended at the retaining wall where her imaginary stairs should have begun. [293]

Behind her, fireworks bloomed without sound inside the mist. [302] She tried the punch again, explaining how badly she had needed her wages. [315] Its handles would not move. [320] "I knew people would trust the drawing," she whispered. [329] A second crescent fell, and the pavement split toward a seam in the wall. [343]

Through it she saw the fortress: not a castle, but the station repeated in black stone, every doorway bricked shut. [363] Names crowded the mortar, including her father's, beneath the charge of misreading her map. [377] She pressed her ear against his name and heard water battering doors. [389] "Turn back," he shouted through the stone, his voice still strong enough to marshal a crowd. [405] "There are no stairs here." [410]

She had come secretly hoping for forgiveness, but even here he was busy trying to save someone. [427] Beneath the names, letters emerged: What leaves this wall must enter the record. [440] Her map remained blank below the supervisor's signature, where she had carefully omitted her own. [455] She understood the final fare, and understood why the punch had come to her instead of someone innocent. [473]

With fingers trembling around the pencil, she signed beside the false stairway and wrote exactly what she had done. [492] Then she dared to place the punch across her signature, not knowing whether it would free her father or simply condemn her. [514] The third crescent dropped. [518] Mortar loosened, and a roll of damp railway paper slid into her hands, carrying the conductor's last instructions and every passenger's name. [540] Her signed confession ran beneath them in the same indelible ink. [551]

One doorway opened, spilling warm lamplight across the river. [560] She heard footsteps passing through, many footsteps, but no voice telling her she was forgiven. [575]

By dawn, she had followed her recorded turns back to the ordinary station, where the punch dissolved against her skin. [595] The paper remained. [598] She took it first to the newspaper office, then to the courthouse, resisting the familiar impulse to improve an ugly line. [619] Outside, people were promising themselves smaller appetites, cleaner kitchens, better years. [630]

Mara sat on the courthouse steps until the duty clerk arrived, her map spread across her knees. [647] She had drawn the missing stairway as a solid wall, without shading or ornament, and marked the place where forty passengers had found no passage. [672] When the door opened, she stood, certain of the way forward and shaking too hard to fold the map. [691]
