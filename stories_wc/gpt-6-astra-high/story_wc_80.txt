After the drawbridge rises, Mara takes the survey skiff beneath its lifted teeth, toward the pavilion drowned when the harbor wall was built. [23] Behind her, a dredger follows slowly, its orange lamps burning through the mist. [36] The company wanted a firm shore surveyor, and she has spent twenty years becoming someone whose measurements cannot be bullied. [56] Today her signature will release the pavilion's foundations for demolition. [66]

At low tide, its roof stands above the water, supported by columns that descend into green obscurity. [83] Her mother, long dead, cleaned its dance floor, according to the employment record Mara has found. [99] At home, that same woman drew bridges on flour sacks, then burned them before her husband returned. [116] Mara remembers feeding those drawings into the stove, mistaking obedience for kindness. [128] She became a surveyor because the training was available, though privately she wanted to become the archeologist of abandoned dreams. [148] Instead, she has mostly certified their removal. [155]

She ties up beneath the roof and lowers herself into the shallow water, feeling for the settlement crack marked on an old plan. [178] A loose repair stone shifts under her glove, exposing a brass edge. [190] She has to wrest the object from the mortar without tearing its corroded hinge. [204] It is an antique traveling inkwell, leather long dissolved, its inner screw cap still sealed with black wax. [222] She carries it onto the skiff and opens it over her blank demolition certificate. [236]

Inside, where the glass reservoir should be, a tight paper roll remains dry. [249] The first sheet lists six women, each beside a profession: engineer, physician, navigator, architect, teacher, surveyor. [265] Her mother's name stands beside engineer, in the unmistakable handwriting that labeled Mara's school lunches. [280] Beneath it, smaller letters explain that the pavilion's caretaker taught them mathematics after the dancing ended. [296]

"We are placing notes in foundation repairs because our husbands inspect our bags," her mother wrote. [312] "If we cannot keep what we learn at home, perhaps we can keep it here." [327] Other sheets contain exercises, corrections, and a drawing of the harbor wall proposed to drown the pavilion. [344] Along its base, her mother has plotted the buried arches linking the pavilion's foundations to the older embankment. [362] Those arches are absent from the company's plan, which shows isolated piers safe to tear away. [378] Beyond the embankment stand the workers' houses, including the one where Mara still sleeps. [392]

She studies this interlaced absence: women's work missing from the records, supporting masonry missing from the chart. [409] Neither omission proves the other, and wanting her mother to have been right is not a measurement. [426] She radios the dredger to hold, then returns to the water with her sounding rod and her mother's dimensions. [445] At the first predicted junction, the rod strikes stone where the official drawing promises mud. [460] At the second, she feels the curve of an arch beneath her fingers. [473] The rising tide reaches her ribs before she finishes taking offsets. [484]

"Old stones are why we're here," the dredger captain says over the radio. [497] "These may be holding up the street," she answers, giving him the coordinates rather than her anger. [514] She marks the certificate UNSAFE TO PROCEED and sends photographs of both drawings to the harbor engineer. [531] The captain curses the lost tide, but his bucket remains suspended above the water. [545]

With the papers safely bagged, Mara presses the empty inkwell against her chest, as though the brass could warm. [564] She has always considered herself stoically sensitive, able to register a loss without making anyone else uncomfortable. [581] Now she wonders how much of that restraint was learned beside the stove. [594] When the harbor engineer asks whose calculations exposed the discrepancy, Mara gives her mother's full name before explaining how she checked them. [616]

Tomorrow there will be divers, arguments, perhaps another order to demolish, but tonight the foundations will remain undisturbed. [634] She has won enough time to ask for an archivist, and to search the other repaired joints. [651] Under the pavilion roof, the dredger's lamps cast a hushed blaze across the water, illuminating a classroom no official map has ever shown. [674] Mara unfolds a clean survey sheet and begins with the six names. [686]
