When the magistrate announced that her sister's car window had confessed to murder, Mara asked whether glass could distinguish hatred from fear. [22] He called the question evasive, which meant he could not answer it. [34]

Mara was an attentive mystic, firmly grounded in the habit of checking wind, temperature, and her own desires before believing any revelation. [56] She had come to prove that objects absorb emotions, not testimony, and that the distinction should keep her sister alive. [76] At dawn, Lena would hang for driving her husband into the flooded quarry. [89] She said the brakes had failed; the frost reader said the hatred preserved in her windshield proved otherwise. [107]

The hearing resumed beneath the quartz arbor in mist, where condemned people traditionally watched their last sunrise fractured into harmless colors. [128] Its crystalline ribs had been chosen because the founders believed clear stone could shelter no deceit. [144] Beneath them stood the recovered car, still smelling of drowned upholstery. [155]

Mara knelt beside it and prepared to commune through the patterns in frost on a car window, as every licensed reader did. [177] Unlike the others, she waited until everyone's breath had cleared the pane. [189] The branching frost pressed a feeling into her chest: a furious wish for somebody to stop existing. [206]

Behind her, Lena whispered that she had loved her husband. [216] That was true, and the way she offered it was a lie. [228] Mara had seen the bruises. [233]

From her pocket she took a glass prism, the cheap instrument their mother had used to throw rainbows across their sickbeds. [254] She first set it against the window for the court's reader, who reported the same unaltered hatred. [271] She asked the magistrate to hold it and remember someone he had been unable to save. [287] His thumb stopped moving along its edge. [294] Everyone knew about his little boy, though nobody mentioned the fever within his hearing. [308]

"This proves nothing," he said, but surrendered the prism carefully, as if it had become breakable only now. [326]

Mara let it cool on the stone until her thermometer showed the same temperature as the window. [343] Then she positioned it against the pane, sending a narrow stripe of refracted dawn through only half the branching frost. [363] The hatred remained in the shadow; inside the rainbow waited a small, unbearable tenderness. [377] When she moved the prism, that tenderness followed. [385]

The old readers called this a shifted hush: what seemed the silence of one thing had quietly become the silence of another. [407] They usually blamed an inattentive mystic. [413]

Mara invited the court's reader to kneel, then had a guard move the prism while she stood well away from it. [434] Three times he named grief where he had sworn there was only murderous rage. [448]

The magistrate gripped the quartz railing. [454] This was the dangerous hour when assumptions shatter, Mara knew, and frightened people search for something softer than stone to break. [475]

"You planted it," he said. [480]

"You did," she answered, and made herself meet his eyes without triumph. [492]

She explained that the window could preserve a feeling without preserving whose feeling it was, or when it had entered the glass. [514] A rescuer's anger, a prisoner's terror, even a reader's certainty could become what the court called the voice of the dead. [535]

"Then your sister is innocent?" [540]

The answer Lena needed was yes, and Mara could almost feel that merciful lie settling against her teeth. [558]

"It means you do not know," she said. [566]

Beyond the arbor, the executioner lowered the rope he had been testing. [578] The magistrate ordered a stay, then demanded that a mechanic examine the brakes, as though this had always been his intention. [599] Mara let him have that lie; for now, it would keep a living woman available for the truth. [617]

As the guards led Lena away, she caught her sister's sleeve and said she really had wanted him dead. [636] Mara did not tell her that wanting meant nothing, or that everything would be all right. [652] She promised to return for the next hearing. [660]

Before she could pocket the prism, the magistrate asked whether its borrowed feeling would eventually fade. [676]

"Not yet," she said, which was true and was not the answer he had asked for. [692]

Then she left it on the railing within his reach, holding its small share of the broken sunrise. [710]
