After luck became measurable, the city stopped watering gardens whose chances of flourishing fell below forty percent. [17] During an automated midnight inspection, the garden maze behind the hospital measured twelve. [30]

Its vine dresser, Mara, had withered alongside it, her knuckles thickening as the grape stems thickened. [46] For thirty years she had guided recovering patients between its green walls and buried their farewell ribbons beneath the roots. [66] Some had forgotten their own names, but learned its turns again by following her through the leaves. [83] She believed gardens knew their gardeners, which the Water Office considered an expensive superstition. [97]

To prove otherwise, she first did something that proved nothing. [107] She could argue with the Office later; roots could not wait. [118] She took her collection of cracked marbles from the potting shed and began to polish them. [134] They were old probability gauges, discarded when their glass fractured and their silver needles began mistaking warmth for good fortune. [154] Mara filled each crack with transparent resin, then held the marbles until their needles climbed. [169] At sixty percent, she photographed them among the grapes and submitted her appeal. [182]

The extra watering bought eleven days. [188] On the twelfth, an inspector arrived carrying a sealed gauge and a notice of fraud. [203] He was young enough to look embarrassed by her hands. [213]

"Resin fluoresces in the photographs," he said. [220]

Mara considered blaming a supplier, then looked past him at a tendril newly curled around its support. [237] Deception had bought that inch of green, but another lie would not keep it alive. [252]

"I warmed broken gauges," she said. [258] "Give me one proper test before you turn the water off." [269]

He agreed, provided she surrendered the marbles and let him choose the procedure. [282] She accepted without bargaining; surrendering false evidence was cheaper than surrendering the maze. [295]

They walked to the central arbor, where one exhausted vine had opened a single flower. [310] Mara stopped so abruptly that the inspector nearly struck her. [320] From a cupboard she fetched a lace cloth, three brass candlesticks, and a paper crown. [335]

"We commemorate small victories properly here," she explained, spreading the cloth beneath the bloom. [349]

Once, patients had crowned every first grape and applauded every shoot that survived a frost. [364] Lately, she had performed the ceremonies alone. [371]

The inspector checked his gauge: eleven percent. [378]

Mara placed the crown above the flower and sang the ridiculous little anthem the patients had invented. [395] Her voice had lost its upper notes, so she whistled them. [406] Along the arbor, dry leaves lifted, although the air remained still. [417]

The needle reached forty. [421] The inspector backed away from her, holding the instrument beyond the warmth of either body. [436] It climbed another six. [440]

"Do it again," he whispered, and then, remembering his profession, asked her to leave the maze. [456]

Beyond the gate, she heard him stumbling through the anthem. [466] When he called her back, the needle stood at twelve. [476] He made her return without singing, wearing his coat, approaching by a different path. [490] The leaves lifted before she reached the arbor. [498]

Neither spoke for a while. [503]

Then Mara said, "Knowing a song isn't the same as knowing who always comes when something breaks." [520]

The inspector would not call it proof, not yet, and she found she respected him for that. [537] He authorized a week's minimum irrigation while independent observers repeated the experiment with instruments neither of them touched. [555] Mara went home each evening afraid that tomorrow the garden would forget her. [568] Each morning, its chances rose when she entered. [576]

On the seventh day, the Office restored the water, but required a public correction of her original claim. [594] She wrote it herself, naming the resin, the warmth, and the eleven stolen days. [608] Below her confession, the inspector appended the verified results. [617] It was a mended deception, not an erased one: the seam would always show. [631]

When her marbles were returned, Mara strung them beneath the arbor, cracks facing outward. [645] Their needles could no longer be trusted, but their broken glass scattered beautiful light. [659]

For the celebration she borrowed hospital china and crowned the tap. [670] The inspector brought a cake hardly larger than the flower. [680] Afterward, she put pruning shears into his hands. [688]

"Come on Thursdays," she told him. [694] "It needs to get to know somebody younger." [702]

Above them, the first flower was already browning, while farther down the stem another bud loosened its green fist. [721]
