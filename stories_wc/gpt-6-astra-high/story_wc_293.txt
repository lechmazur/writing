After the hitchhiker vanished across the border, Mara remained at the mountain scenic pullout, holding the sliver he had pressed into her palm. [23] Below her, the border bridge stretched across a gorge full of evening light. [36] The checkpoint at its center was already closing, trapping three deserters on the eastern bank. [51] One was her son. [55] He had learned her tunes before he learned the regimental marches. [66]

Mara was a bridge-song fiddler, descended from musicians who had carried instructions between bridge crews before radios. [83] Tourists now paid her for those curious tunes, never guessing that a descending fourth meant hold, or that a trill meant move. [105] Her grandfather had demanded that she be dynamically observant: watch the load, the hands, the wind, then change the music. [125] Tonight, observation had to precede any question of forgiveness. [134]

The object in her hand was a wet-plate collodion glass flake, broken from a photograph of the bridge under construction. [154] Her hitchhiker, an archivist with a bleeding thumb, had stolen it from the regiment's archives after the digital plans were censored. [175] Under the pullout lamp, silver ghosts of workmen stood beside a stair descending behind the eastern abutment. [192] At the bottom, a maintenance catwalk passed beneath the checkpoint and emerged on her side of the border. [210]

She had told her son there was no way back. [220] He had joined the border regiment for wages, then stayed after wages became confiscated bread and confiscations became disappearances. [239] In his last letter, he had offered to recount the orders, the names, the locations of graves. [256] She had written "never" across his request to come home, then burned the letter rather than send it. [274] Now she wanted to sculpt possibility from the ashes of "never," without pretending the dead could be restored. [292]

Her country called its new witness program pixelated amnesty. [301] Deserters could cross without being returned, testify behind scrambled faces, and earn civilian status by helping convict their commanders. [320] Faces were blurred and names withheld from broadcasts, but judges could still order any witness to stand trial for murder. [340] Her son had agreed to testify, but the regiment had mined the mountain paths and canceled his crossing permit. [359] The old stair was their last chance before floodlights illuminated the underside of the bridge. [374]

Mara raised the glass against the bridge, matching the photographed masonry to the stones beneath the scaffold wrap. [392] Wind bellied the white sheeting outward, uncovering one landing, then another. [403] A lower flap remained still until she shifted three steps left and saw its edge twitch above the catwalk. [422] Here was a secret mapped by scaffold wrap flutters: the route existed, but it was no longer whole. [440] Between the last landing and the catwalk, six feet of stair had fallen away. [454] She lowered the fiddle; she could not play advance and send them into empty air. [469]

Across the bridge, a hand appeared between two sheets, asking with three raised fingers whether the route was clear. [488] Her grandfather's answer came back through her wrists: hold, then lash. [499] She played the old hauling rhythm, and the hand withdrew. [509] Through the next opening she saw the archivist and a soldier unfastening a scaffold plank. [524] They slid it across the missing steps while another figure lashed its end to a railing with his belt. [543] When the archivist tested it with his weight, she let the melody climb. [556]

They came beneath her in flashes, boots and shoulders bright against the darkening gorge. [570] They were absurdly solid after so many months of dread: boots scraping, someone coughing, someone complaining about a splinter. [589] At the western exit her son left his rifle on the ground, and the other soldiers laid theirs beside it. [609] He looked older than the face she had spent six months trying not to remember. [624]

"I will tell them everything," he said. [631] She wanted to ask why everything had taken so long, but first she took him to the witness officer waiting in the tourist kiosk. [655] On the officer's screen, his face dissolved into gentle squares of color as he began with his name. [673] Mara could still see his uncovered face beside the screen, and she watched him resist the urge to look away. [693] She had not forgiven him, but in luminous disbelief she listened as he supplied the name of the first missing woman, then spelled it for the record. [720]
