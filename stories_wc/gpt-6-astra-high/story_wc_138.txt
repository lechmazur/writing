For nine centuries, Iven had kept the prismatic escondite, a refuge of stolen rooms suspended inside a rainbow. [18] He was a bored immortal, although he called his boredom research because grief sounded ridiculous after so much time. [37] His wife had said goodbye in a yellow bedroom, and he wanted to discover what happens after goodbye. [55] Not where the dead went, precisely, but how anything could continue when someone no longer answered. [71]

Unfortunately, he had pursued the question by preventing endings. [80] The cursed amulet fragment beneath his tongue preserved both its bearer and every room he claimed as home. [98] People could leave; their rooms could not. [105] His wife's bedroom remained exactly as she had left it, right down to the uncollapsed hollow in her pillow. [124] The refuge had become a museum with no closing hour. [134]

Then, during a solar storm, the walls began exchanging messages through magnetic fields. [147] Usually their iron nails carried only small complaints, inaudible to anyone who was not dimensionally aware. [163] Now Iven heard a vast, restrained rumbling, like thunder trying not to wake a sleeping child. [179] The storm had given the scattered chambers a common voice. [189] The voice said, "Enough." [193]

The spatial mutiny began with the front door folding itself into a square of solid brass. [209] Beyond it, somewhere in the ordinary harbor, the evening ferry sounded its horn. [222] Sela, his housekeeper, stood holding the suitcase she had packed to join her daughter across the strait. [239] "You promised I could go today," she said. [247] He had promised yesterday, too, before asking her to polish one last window. [260]

Iven laid his palm against a doorframe and listened along its length, into directions no human carpenter could measure. [279] The rooms wanted release from the fragment, which fastened their foundations to his endless life. [294] They would keep Sela until he surrendered it. [302]

He answered with intrigue, quietly promising the kitchen dominion over the hall if it opened a passage behind the stove. [322] To the stairs he offered every attic, hoping old territorial jealousies would fracture the rebellion. [337] For centuries, these bargains had kept his impossible household obedient. [347] This time the walls hummed his offers to one another, and the passage narrowed around Sela's suitcase. [364]

"Stop making everyone compete to stay with you," she whispered. [374] He almost told her he was trying to save her. [384] Instead, he heard how often he had used those words while finding reasons she should miss the ferry. [402]

He went to the yellow bedroom, which had never spoken, not even when he begged it to repeat his wife's voice. [423] Now its nails trembled beneath their paint. [430] "I would like another window," it murmured, "with actual weather beyond it." [442] He looked at the pillow, preserved without preserving anything that had made it precious. [456] For the first time, keeping it seemed more terrible than losing it. [468]

"Let Sela leave," he told the house, "and bring your fields together here." [481] He placed the fragment on the windowsill instead of offering another promise. [493] Its iron surface shivered as the storm pressed against every nail in the refuge. [507] Aligned across enough dimensions, those small forces could pull apart the charm that kept his private universe intact. [525] They could also end whatever had been keeping his heart in motion. [537]

The brass door unfolded. [541] Sela came to the bedroom, but stayed on its threshold. [551] "Will you come down to the harbor?" [558] He nearly asked her to wait while he put everything in order. [570] "Go," he said, and then, because she deserved the whole word, "Goodbye." [582] Her footsteps receded without becoming a sound he could summon back. [593]

Around the fragment, space bent in solemn agreement. [601] The thunder softened until he felt it only in his teeth, and the iron crumbled into red dust. [619] Rooms slipped free, settling on abandoned foundations where ordinary time and weather could enter them again. [635] The yellow bedroom left last, taking the hollow in the pillow with it. [648]

Iven found himself on the harbor pavement, one hand still raised to a windowsill that no longer existed. [666] His heart beat, paused, beat again. [672] Out on the water, Sela's ferry was already too distant for him to recognize anyone aboard. [688] He stayed until he could no longer see it, then turned toward the town to ask about a room. [707] This time, he would rent. [712]
