At the ember nexus at twilight, a penny hung edgewise above the furnace, arrested halfway through the bishop's final blessing. [20] For sixteen years, it had refused to drop, and the roses outside had kept their frost without surrendering a petal. [40] People could move and speak, but nothing could ripen, heal, or die. [52] The bishop had called this mercy. [58] Iven's daughter called it being nine. [64]

She sat beside him on the furnace steps, cradling the hand a millstone had crushed that afternoon, sixteen years ago. [84] Every morning he wrapped it afresh, careful not to let her hear what her small, involuntary flinch did to him. [104] Tonight she had asked him not to promise that tomorrow would be better. [117] There were no better tomorrows in the bishop's keeping. [126]

Across Iven's knees lay the broken bishop's crozier, its silver hook split and its shaft of ash scorched black. [145] Its fracture still bled sparks into the furnace, feeding the arrested moment. [157] The bishop wanted it repaired; Iven had been summoned only because every competent alchemist had failed. [173] A hopeless alchemist, people called him, a man whose gold always became soot. [186] His daughter had never contradicted them, and he loved her for that particular kindness. [200]

He had brought a rose cutting to test his work. [210] To vivify that little branch of winter would mean the blessing could finally be adjusted. [225] Perhaps he could preserve his daughter's life while letting her wound close. [237] He bridged the crozier's split with silver wire. [245] The frost thickened. [248] His daughter stopped swinging her feet. [254] He tore the wire away and waited until she breathed. [264]

Then, instead of closing the fracture, he filed it wider. [274] One stroke loosened a bead of water on the rose; three made its outer petals blacken. [290] He stopped, measured the gap, and brushed the filings into the flame. [302] Each spark briefly released a different part of the flower. [312] Working through successive approximation, he widened the split by fractions, recording what thawed and what withered at each increment. [331] He had spent his career learning precisely how things failed. [341] For once, he did not throw the failures away. [350]

Beneath the dying flower, a dormant bud showed green. [359] Yet whenever he soldered even a sliver back, both decay and growth ceased. [372] There was no setting that permitted only the changes he wanted. [383] The crozier did not distinguish a blossoming rose from a spreading infection; both disobeyed the shape the bishop had ordained. [403] Iven set down his file. [408] He could interrupt their stifling destiny, but he could not replace it with a kinder certainty. [424]

His daughter watched the penny, which now trembled above the coals. [435] "Will my hand hurt worse?" [440]

He folded the blackened petals into his notebook before answering, giving her question the care he usually reserved for pain. [460] "It might, and I might not know how to save you." [471]

She considered this with the patience childhood should never have taught her. [483] "Then you will have to learn something new." [491]

He wanted to weep, but she needed his hands steady more than she needed proof that he loved her. [510] He unwound a fresh bandage and laid his tools within reach. [521] Then he pushed the crozier, hook and shaft together, into the furnace. [533] The ash wood caught slowly; the silver softened into a shape that could command nothing. [548] He understood, when the penny dropped, why his masters had despised soot. [560] It proved that a thing could cease to be itself, leaving room for something no master had prescribed. [578]

The coin struck with a small, ordinary sound. [586] Outside, the roses shed their glassy skins, and the first true dusk in sixteen years entered the windows. [604] His daughter cried out. [608] Blood spread across the linen, bright and terribly undecided. [617] Iven pressed the wound and counted, not seconds until a miracle, but breaths during which he could still help. [636] When the bleeding slowed, she leaned against him, exhausted. [645] He did not tell her everything would be well. [654]

Beside the furnace, the cutting stood in a cup of water, its dead flower bowed above the opening bud. [673] He would need to find it soil before morning. [682]
