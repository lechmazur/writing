After the nostalgia ban, Sera chose elective exile in a twilight garden under stars, beyond the city's clockwork canopy. [19] There, black lilies opened over saltwater pools, and obsidian paths held the last light like knives held politely. [37] She had been a skilled tide dancer, carried above the sea by verses inked along her bones. [54] The government called those inherited verses a sickness: they made children yearn for islands swallowed generations before. [71] Under the new law, every dancer surrendered their ancestral ink or their citizenship. [84] Sera surrendered her citizenship, taking the ferry ticket before the clerk could reach for his erasing needle. [101]

For seven years she had wanted only to pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin again. [117] Then a boy arrived in a leaking boat, carrying his mother's confiscation receipt. [130] She was alive, he said, but without her ink she could not cross the flooded streets to work. [148] He wanted to fledge before the winter tides, so he could bring her food. [162]

A dancer's first flight required a verse fitted to the body that would bear it. [177] Sera had once written such a verse for her daughter, who drowned before she could learn its final turn. [196] The boy was called Tavin, and his wrists were narrower. [206] She noticed that, then made herself notice his face. [215]

Inside her daughter's broken pendant remained a dust speck from a moonstone cabochon, enough to wake one measure of ink. [235] Moonstone bound a written rhythm to the moon's pull; ordinary pigment merely stained. [248] Sera ground the speck into soot while Tavin slept beneath the lilies' folded shadows. [262] By evening, her daughter's verse curved around his shoulders. [271] She told herself its beauty would keep him safe. [280]

He rose three feet above the pool, turned at her instruction, and became spontaneously anchored. [295] The water seized his shadow and dragged him down. [304] Sera caught his wrist, braced herself against an obsidian coping, and hauled until blood loosened the ink beneath her fingers. [324] Tavin coughed saltwater into her lap. [330] His first question was whether his mother would have to go hungry. [342] Sera could not look at the pendant. [349]

"I gave you someone else's breath," she said. [357]

A tide verse had to open when its wearer inhaled, then release the moon between each heartbeat. [374] Her daughter had danced in long, imperious phrases; Tavin breathed quickly, like someone accustomed to making room for others. [393] She had mistaken preservation for love, and nearly made another grave of it. [406]

There was no moonstone left for a second inscription. [415] But where her grip had rubbed one letter away, his skin no longer pulled toward the water. [432]

She began again by observing gaps: between his breaths, between reflected stars, between the surviving strokes of ink. [450] The sky was not a solid brilliance; darkness gave each burning thing its distance. [464] With a needle dipped in brine, she lifted out the syllables that demanded her daughter's stride. [480] Tavin chose where she should pause, raising a finger whenever the tightening across his chest eased. [496] What remained was shorter, broken by silences, and no longer promised that anything beloved could be kept. [513] It spoke instead of small lights crossing a darkness that did not belong to them. [528]

On the third evening, Tavin stepped above the pool and stopped when he needed air. [543] His shadow trembled, but the water let him go. [552] He crossed the garden wall, circled once beneath the stars, and came back laughing with terror. [568]

Sera gave him dried fish for his mother and explained which channels avoided the patrols. [583] Even a new verse would look like an old crime to men paid to erase. [598] He listened, then asked whether he could bring others. [607]

She almost refused, thinking of the empty pendant and the small supply of food. [621] Others might carry moonstone, she thought, and the pools could be stocked with mussels. [635] For the first time, she considered what the garden could feed instead of what it could hide. [652]

"Tell them to bring whatever they have," she said. [661]

When Tavin flew out over the black water, she watched until she could no longer distinguish him from a space between stars. [683] Then she turned toward her chosen shore and began clearing a place for the next arrival. [699]
