Mara had been called an uncommonly thoughtful prisoner exchange negotiator, usually by men who wanted her to overlook something. [19] She preferred to remember the man she had once refused because his name was misspelled on a list. [37] His transport had sunk returning him to prison. [45] Since then, she had kept a ledger of errors, with each failure facing a rule meant to prevent its repetition. [65] She could not redeem mistakes, but she meant to recycle them into wisdom. [78]

Tonight, on the fortress's storm-worn, windblown parapet, mist beaded on the open ledger. [91] Below, two barges strained against their moorings. [98] The fortress belonged to neither army while seawater covered its causeway, an old pilots' privilege both sides still honored. [117] That made it neutral territory for six hours, and a disputed ruin for the remainder. [132] With their calendars divided by rival revolutions, the armies arranged exchanges by synchronizing through moon phases. [148] Every full moon brought the spring tide high enough for loaded barges to reach the landing. [164] Moonrise set the meeting; the ebb set its limit. [173]

Mara had exchanged twenty prisoners before the last man stepped forward with something cupped between his wrists. [190] It was an antique drawer pull, brass darkened around a pair of intertwined swallows. [204] One bird lacked a wingtip. [209] Her father had broken it moving his desk upstairs, then promised to mend it when the war ended. [227]

"I heard you were handling the exchange," the prisoner said. [237] "I took this before we burned your house." [245]

Wind worried the ledger's pages beneath her palm. [253]

"My father was inside." [257]

"I know." [259]

The prisoner laid the brass swallows on the stone between them. [270]

"I am sorry." [273]

Her escort captain leaned close enough that only she could hear him. [285]

"His name is on your list, but nobody says we have to finish." [298]

Across the landing, the opposing negotiator waited beside Mara's last countryman, a boy clutching his own belt to keep his trousers up. [320] His mother would have made him eat before leaving home. [330] Mara hated herself for imagining that ordinary kitchen while her father's house burned again behind her eyes. [347]

"We can hold him until the water falls," the captain murmured. [358] "Then this is our ground." [363]

His thumb rested on his pistol, offering her vengeance in the shape of a technicality. [378]

The other negotiator saw the gesture and put a hand on the boy's shoulder. [392] The landing settled into a furrowed hush, every face bent around the same calculation. [406] Mara looked down at her ledger, where she had written that a name was not a spelling test. [424] Beneath it, there was room for another lesson. [432] She had designed every safeguard here against the other side's dishonesty, and none against her own grief. [449]

"Take your hand off the pistol," she told the captain. [459]

He stared at her, then obeyed. [465]

"We finish while the ground belongs to neither of us." [475]

She signed the final release and watched the boy cross the wet stones toward her. [490]

The prisoner did not move. [495]

"Can you forgive me?" [499]

"It means you are going home." [505]

His face changed, and she could not tell whether she had given him less than he wanted or more. [524] She walked him down to the barge herself. [532] When the boatman reached for the mooring, she knelt to untether it, working the swollen knot until her nails split. [552] There was nothing cleansing about the work. [559] The rope smelled of fish and old water, and the man who had helped kill her father sat three feet away. [580] Then the knot yielded, and the boat moved beyond her reach. [591]

Back on the parapet, Mara picked up the drawer pull and laid it across the ledger to hold the pages still. [612] Beneath the spelling rule she wrote: An agreed release is not a pardon. [625] Then she asked the boy whether someone would meet him when their barge reached the harbor. [641] When he shook his head, she wrote down his mother's village and arranged a cart. [656] By the time they left, the broken swallow had pressed a small hollow into the damp paper. [673] Mara closed the ledger around it. [679] She was still somewhere between apology and forgiveness, but there was work she could do there. [695]
