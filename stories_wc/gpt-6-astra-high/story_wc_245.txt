At the forest gate, Mara removed her gloves and laid one palm on the message her daughter had cut into bark. [21] She was a blind mapmaker, and the languages she absorbed through her skin had earned her a reputation for finding anything. [42] But the message was not a request for rescue. [51] It said, "Come without a map." [57]

Lea had been gone a year. [63] After her father's drowning, Mara had charted the girl's grief as if it were a marsh: danger here, firm ground there. [84] When Lea wept, Mara supplied the word for it; when she fell silent, Mara supplied that word too. [102] At last, Lea had left for the ultrasonic forest, where every tree sang above the reach of human hearing. [121]

Mara turned her platinum compass ring until its needle pressed against her finger. [134] Only platinum could lower the trees' songs into audible notes without distorting them; her husband had spent their savings on this small, incorruptible guide. [158] Tonight she would use his last invention to dowse for a path she could neither measure nor name. [176] She wanted to navigate her daughter's uncharted sorrow, although she feared what its depths might disclose about her own heart. [196]

The crossing was possible only across the pause of shifting constellations, the winter hour when the stars hung between their old figures and their new. [221] Once they settled, the forest would seal its paths for another year. [233]

She touched the first trunk. [238] Its singing entered her as a language of thirst, translated into a pressure behind her teeth. [254] Beneath it, a single musical note trembled against the ring: low C, her husband's starting pitch. [270] Mara followed it to another tree, then another, each carrying a note Lea had persuaded the bark to remember. [289] The trail of musical notes was a lullaby taken apart. [299]

She knew its ending. [303] Her hand swung toward the place where the next note ought to be, and the ring went cold. [321] Thorns caught her coat. [325] She had followed what she expected, not what was there. [335]

Sheltering her wrist against her chest, Mara waited until the urge to correct the tune subsided. [351] Then she let the needle draw her left, toward a dissonance that hurt like an unasked question. [368] The melody changed as she followed: familiar tenderness opening around an anger she had never allowed her daughter to speak. [388] She touched a scarred trunk and absorbed its word for a branch torn away while still alive. [405] She did not translate it into grief. [412] She let it hurt. [416]

At the center of the forest, the notes stopped. [425] Her ring's needle, which had quivered throughout the crossing, rested against its tiny axle. [439] Mara had reached the hidden stillpoint, a clearing held motionless by the equal pressure of all those unheard songs. [458] Here, even with her palms uncovered, nothing spoke through her waiting skin. [470]

"You found the wrong notes," Lea said. [477]

"I followed them," Mara answered, and felt how different those words were. [489]

Her daughter stood close enough for Mara to hear the uneven work of her breathing. [504] Mara lifted a hand, then lowered it; touch could give her Lea's language, but not permission to claim it. [523]

"I was angry with him for dying," Lea said. [532] "You kept telling me how much I loved him." [541]

Above them, the first star clicked into its new position. [551] The needle stirred. [554] Mara could ask for forgiveness, or promise a gentler map, but both would make an instrument of this moment. [573] Instead she said, "I miss him, and sometimes I hate him too." [585]

Lea took her hand. [589] Together they stood inside that small, unsparing warmth until the ring began to tremble. [603] Then Mara asked which way her daughter wanted to go. [613]

"Home," Lea said, "but slowly." [618]

They left before the last constellation settled, following the hidden melody backward through the trees. [633] Mara kept her free hand gloved. [639] Under the other, her daughter's palm was warm, and when Lea wanted silence, she gave it. [655]
