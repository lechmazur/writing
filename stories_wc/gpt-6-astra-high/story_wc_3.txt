After her husband's spirit left, Nara kept her palm raised until the air where his hand had hovered stopped feeling warm. [21] Then the magnetic arbor shuddered above the clouds, and its iron leaves struck one another like frightened teeth. [39] The crossing branch broke. [43] On the stair below, seven waiting spirits flickered. [51]

Nara caught her apprentice's sleeve as the platform tilted toward the white gulf. [64] She fastened his safety line, checked the knot twice, and asked his permission before tightening it across his bruised ribs. [84] "Now the repair tablet, please," she said. [91]

He put the clay instructions in her hand. [99] She was a cuneiform translator, not a gardener of magnetic trees, but there was nobody else. [115] The arbor's keepers had died centuries ago, leaving their machinery to conduct the dead beyond the weather. [132] She had come to understand the physics of transition, carrying instruments while her husband carried his approaching death. [150] He had wanted company; she had wanted a law that would let her bring him back. [166]

Now a knowledge fruit seed lay in the socket of the broken branch, warm as a living fingertip. [184] Such seeds recorded the most recent passage, preserving its changes in the field so a replacement branch could grow. [203] This one held his crossing. [208]

Under her thumb, the tablet's wedges offered two readings: reestablish the departed, or reestablish departure. [223] The ending that distinguished them had flaked away. [231]

She fitted the seed between two copper jaws and adjusted their magnets until its surface glowed. [247]

"Nara," it said. [250]

Her husband's voice, hesitant as it had been when he asked whether she would stay beside him. [267] She steadied the field, and it said her name again. [277]

Below, a child spirit began slipping between the stair treads. [287] Her apprentice reached down, but his fingers passed through its wrist. [298]

"Please," he said, without looking at her. [305]

The seed spoke again, with the same catch on the first syllable. [317] Not an answer, then, but a repetition. [324]

She had spent thirty years translating canal accounts, and suddenly she remembered a lawsuit over a mill. [341] A farmer had filled its channel and claimed the wheel was supplied; the miller had argued that water was not enough. [362] Water had to move. [366]

The arbor's builders had borrowed their engineering language from irrigation. [376] What if their word for restoration meant restoring flow, not returning whatever the current carried? [391] She had treated the field as a vessel, holding his last instant motionless inside it. [406] But a seed was not a vessel either. [414] It was something that broke to make a living thing. [424]

She checked the remaining instructions, matching the signs for alternating irrigation gates to the two magnetic jaws. [441] They described a rhythm, not a position. [448] At the bottom was a warning she had translated without understanding: the first pattern is consumed in the making. [467]

No voice would remain to study. [473]

For a moment she hated the child on the stair, and the boy beside her, for needing what she could still almost touch. [496] Her apprentice kept one useless hand extended toward the child and offered the other to her. [512]

She took it. [515]

"Count for me," she said. [520]

On each number, she reversed one magnet, allowing the field to change instead of holding it still. [537] Her name stretched into a trembling note, then disappeared beneath the crack of the seed. [552] Copper roots caught the jaws; an iron shoot unfolded across the gap, laying down leaves that steadied the platform. [571]

The child climbed onto the new branch. [578] One by one, the waiting spirits crossed, each pausing where the clouds opened to a brightness Nara's instruments could not measure. [599] She watched every departure, counting until seven had gone. [608] Only then did she loosen her grip on the boy's hand. [619]

In her notebook she recorded the timing, the field strengths, and the loss of the original pattern. [636] She left space for an explanation of why a changing field could carry a person where a steady one could only repeat a name. [660]

When her apprentice asked if she was ready to descend, she said no, and asked if he would sit a while. [681] They sat with their shoulders touching until the clouds beneath them turned gold. [694]
