The circuit breakers would cool in nine minutes, and then the floating observatory would resume deciding who deserved a future. [20] Mariam had tripped them by bridging two terminals with her wedding knife. [32] She had used that knife once to cut herself free from a burning tablecloth while the drone circled for a second strike. [54] Now its blade was notched and her remaining fingers burned. [64]

Below the observatory's hydrogen bladders, refugee boats crowded the black water. [75] Above them, stars shone with the indifference she had once mistaken for order. [88]

She opened the detector cabinet and lifted out its geode crystal, a hollow quartz stone threaded with electrodes. [106] Its inward-pointing facets trapped the interference between what happened and what nearly happened; ordinary crystals let those faint echoes escape. [126] For three years she had filtered the echoes into clean, profitable forecasts. [138] After the wedding, she had stayed because she believed some remnant of the life denied her must survive inside the noise. [159]

Tonight she had come to hunt it. [166] She wanted to find where unused potential pools, as her original research proposal had promised, before the military bought the observatory. [187]

Without the powered filters, touching the geode brought an unfiltered awakening. [198] Intangibly tangible, a pressure like fingers between her ribs made her gasp. [210] She felt doors almost opened, bread almost shared, names waiting for mouths that would never speak them. [227] There was no husband's voice. [232] She had to resist inventing one. [238]

Mariam hunted through perturbation theory, introducing tiny changes in the detector's orientation and calculating how the echoes changed against the undisturbed field. [260] A slight rotation, then another: first-order responses gave direction, second-order corrections separated distant sources from reflections inside the stone. [279] Her pencil shook, but the mathematics did not. [287]

At the coordinates of her wedding, nothing accumulated. [295] She checked the baseline twice. [300] The enormous reservoir lay directly below her, moving with the boats. [311]

Six minutes. [313] She narrowed the detector's aperture until the pressure resolved into one fishing trawler: a schoolteacher, thirty children, an engine repaired with wire. [335] Their possible tomorrows crowded her senses, not pictures but a terrifying abundance of branching paths. [350] Then each path ended at the same bright wall. [359]

On the emergency display, the trawler carried a red designation: HOSTILE, CONFIDENCE 99.8 PERCENT. [373] Strike authorization would transmit when the breakers reset. [381] When she nudged the targeting threshold by a thousandth, a child's possible adulthood briefly warmed her hands before the military overrides restored certainty. [404] The observatory's forecasts were reliable because its weapons eliminated whatever contradicted them. [416]

Unused potential did not pool around the dead. [424] It backed up around the living whose futures had already been closed. [436] Her husband was gone; the machine had not kept anything she could bring home. [450]

Mariam pressed her forehead against the cabinet until its cold metal hurt. [462] She had written the filter that discarded unlikely outcomes, including the possibility that a suspected insurgent might simply be a groom. [483] Afterward, hunting his ghost had let her keep drawing a salary from the thing that killed him. [500]

Three minutes. [502] Smashing the geode would halt targeting, but a replacement would arrive before the slowest boats reached shelter. [519] She needed the fleet to move now. [526]

Using the emergency transmitter, she broadcast the target list and instructions for jamming the observatory's unencrypted ranging signal. [544] "Small changes are enough," she told the boats, her voice raw, and read out the frequencies. [560]

Answers crackled back, suspicious, furious, alive. [566] Engines began knocking across the water. [572]

With thirty seconds left, she wedged the geode beneath the breaker's spring-loaded reset arm. [586] The fleet's scattered signals were already forcing patrol drones into wide holding patterns. [599] When the arm slammed down, the stone would shatter and its unique calibration would be lost. [615]

She could not unmake the wedding or trust that any of the children would survive. [630] But she could make certainty more expensive. [637]

Mariam climbed onto the evacuation ladder, laughing so hard she could scarcely breathe. [650] Behind her, the crystals broke like teeth. [657] Below, the boats dispersed into a darkness that, for once, had not been decided for them. [673]
