At low tide, the container towers stood on their reflections like buildings balanced on the roofs of a drowned city. [20] I lived between them, in a passage too narrow for the port's mapping drones. [34] According to a death certificate, I had drowned six months earlier. [45] The clerk had copied the wrong bunk number, and the city preferred its paper to my pulse. [62] My wages stopped; my room was reassigned; at the clinic, the receptionist spoke through me to the next patient. [81]

What had actually nearly killed me was gas in the container where we slept. [95] Since then, my mind filtered reality differently: dates slipped away, but voices arrived with surfaces, some gritty, some smooth as enamel. [116] I could not remember the poisoning itself. [123] I wanted to discover the texture of that missing memory, to know what belonged beneath the official blank. [141]

At each ebb, I walked the exposed service lanes, collecting fragments of overheard stories through the container walls. [159] At flood tide, when water closed the lower passages, I returned upstairs and wrote what I had heard on discarded wrapping paper. [181] The towers held kitchens, gambling rooms, families whose addresses were inventory numbers; sound traveled farther than anyone intended. [199] A woman remembered somebody pounding on steel. [206] A cook remembered twelve breakfasts left untouched. [213] A night watchman said the evacuation order had arrived after the doors were sealed. [227]

I began to layer their words on my wall, leaving contradictions visible through the thin, greasy sheets. [244] The port authority's account lay underneath: an empty dormitory, a storm, one itinerant worker lost overboard. [260] With every tide, its clean sentences became harder to read. [270] The dead man whose bunk number had become mine remained absent even from that account. [285]

On the seventh ebb, the cook found me lifting a sodden rectangle from a drain. [300] It was a label from a poison bottle, its skull almost scrubbed away, its batch number still legible. [318] 'They poured that into the fumigator,' she said, and her voice felt like a rope tightening. [334] 'I kept telling them there were beds inside.' [342] She took me to the watchman, who had saved a carbon copy of the chemical delivery receipt against a disputed deduction from his pay. [366] The numbers matched; the delivery preceded the supposed evacuation. [375]

On the receipt, beneath the watchman's signature, was my own mark: a crooked cross. [389] I remembered its shape before I remembered making it. [398] They had asked me to witness a delivery, paying with the promise of another week's berth. [414] I had asked no questions. [419] The memory had the texture of wet cardboard, something that yielded precisely when it needed to hold. [436]

By the next flood, the cook had brought two sisters, and the sisters had brought a photograph with half its faces cut away. [459] 'For their work permits,' they explained, touching the holes where their brothers had been. [473] Nobody had returned the permits, or the bodies, and their accounts had been closed as abandoned. [489] They studied my wall without asking who I was. [498] Then one sister corrected the cook: thirteen breakfasts, because the youngest always ate twice. [512]

Before the next ebb, orange clearance notices appeared on the residential stacks. [524] The authority was reclaiming empty storage, according to the notices, while children watched from the doors. [540] I started peeling our layers down, and the cook caught my wrist. [552] 'Not to hide them,' I said, although that had been my first thought. [565] We pasted the sheets onto sailcloth, with the poison label and receipt sewn beneath a scrap of transparent packing sleeve. [585] I left my cross uncovered. [590]

At low water, we carried it through the service lanes to the public ferry landing, beyond the port's security gates. [610] As the tide lifted the first ferry to the quay, we stretched our wall between two bollards. [627] Passengers slowed, reading, and the watchman began telling his story where nobody needed to overhear it. [643] Someone photographed the receipt; someone else recognized a missing brother. [653] By noon, people were writing along the margins. [661]

No clerk came to restore me, and the cranes kept moving above the towers. [675] But when a passenger asked whose history this was, I no longer searched for the name the city had taken. [695] 'Ours,' I said, holding the cloth against the wind, feeling every seam. [707]
