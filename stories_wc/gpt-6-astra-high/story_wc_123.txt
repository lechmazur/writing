After being followed home, Mara sat in her quiet courtyard at dusk and listened to the stranger settle outside her gate. [21] His boot scraped once; then the fountain and the evening insects resumed their separate measures. [36] She left her lamp unlit, but finished entering the day's pressure readings before she allowed herself to look toward the wall. [57]

A meticulous storm recorder did not abandon a measurement simply because someone wanted her frightened. [72] That, at least, was the rule she had been testing through seven years of living alone. [88] She had left the provincial bureau when its directors began rounding inconvenient rainfall totals into numbers that satisfied the flood commissioners. [109] Here, she had hoped to nurture integrity in solitude, where nobody could mistake agreement for accuracy. [125]

A pigeon landed beside the empty gauge, dragging one foot. [135] Mara recognized the white feather above its eye before she saw the carrier pigeon's message tube beneath its breast. [154] She had lent this bird to her former apprentice, now confined across the canal inside the Southbank quarantine. [172] Its commander had refused the magistrate's release order, claiming storms had isolated the district and made another medical inspection impossible. [192] What had begun as a precaution against fever was becoming a rogue quarantine zone, sustained by soldiers and unverifiable reports. [212]

Inside the tube lay a narrow printed weather return bearing the commander's seal and, beneath six daily totals, Mara's name. [232] On its blank reverse, her apprentice had written that the streets were dry and the soldiers charged families for passage. [252] He asked her to certify that the district was safe now. [263]

She checked the paper against an older Southbank notice, matching the seal's chipped border before trusting the document. [281] Then she fetched her station ledger. [287] Southbank's reported rain looked plausible when read downward through the dates: a gathering storm, a cloudburst, a gradual clearing. [306] Her own dry gauge could not disprove it, since summer storms sometimes emptied themselves over a single neighborhood. [324] She could intuit a theft, but intuition was not something she would ask a magistrate to sign. [341]

Instead, she worked across the ledger's station columns, tracking the supposed storm by progressing laterally rather than following its convenient chronology. [362] At the western reservoir she found the same six totals, in the same order, recorded one week earlier. [380] Even thirty millimeters was there, a figure that tightened her throat. [391] She had misplaced a decimal while preparing that week's public bulletin; the reservoir keeper had measured only three. [409] Her correction had appeared the following morning. [416] Someone inside Southbank had copied the first bulletin, reproducing not a storm but her mistake. [431]

Outside, the stranger coughed, and Mara understood why he had followed her from the archive that afternoon. [448] Her name was useful to the commander only while its owner remained quietly respectable. [462]

She could give the apprentice what he wanted, an absolute assurance that might open the gates. [478] But she had not examined a patient, and replacing one convenient certainty with another would leave the machinery intact. [497] On thin paper she identified the copied measurements, acknowledged her decimal error, and stated precisely what she could prove: these reports were fabricated. [520] She asked for an independent inspection, not a declaration of safety. [531] Then she signed her name where nobody else had put it. [542]

In the courtyard loft waited two birds belonging to the courthouse, kept for urgent flood warnings when the wires failed. [562] She slipped her statement and the sealed forgery into the returned tube, then fastened it beneath the stronger bird. [581] The courthouse roof was still gold above the canal, close enough to reach before the light went. [598] Behind the fig tree, hidden from the gate, she lifted her hands and felt the small engine of its heart depart. [619]

For a while she watched the empty space above the leaves. [630] Sending evidence would not empty the barracks, but tomorrow somebody beyond its walls would have a reason to demand entry. [650] She would have to answer questions, surrender her ledgers, and let other people test the work she had guarded alone. [670]

Mara washed the injured pigeon's foot, set water beside it, and returned to her unfinished page. [686] The fountain continued its soft argument with the insects; beyond the gate, the stranger shifted his weight. [703] In the space reserved for observed conditions, she wrote, 'No rain,' and left room for whatever happened next. [721]
