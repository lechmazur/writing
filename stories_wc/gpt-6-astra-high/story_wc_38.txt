When the spider anchored its first thread above the inspection hatch, Mara knew the escape chimney still breathed. [18] She had dived down expecting to find that route flooded, like everything else in the sunken laboratory except its pressurized wards. [39] She had brought one spare breathing mask, enough to save her mother and nobody else. [54] Beyond the hatch, familiar green tiles shone through water as dark as steeped tea. [68] Her mother had taught her to count on those tiles before the Directorate turned the research station into a prison. [88] The staff had evacuated at the first pump failure, leaving the prisoners locked below. [102]

Mara was a lyrical code breaker, trained to hear hidden orders in the stresses of harmless songs. [119] She had once sold that gift to the Directorate, then fled when its songs began naming people for arrest. [138] Her mother had stayed to treat them, until treating them became a punishable offense. [152] For seven years, Mara had mistaken distance for innocence. [161] Now her mother's journal, smuggled out by a departing nurse, had brought her back. [175]

Each entry praised a patient's strength: remarkable vigilance, extraordinary sensitivity, an exceptional instinct to seek company. [191] Mara recognized sleeplessness, pain, and terror of being alone. [200] The Directorate disposed of damaged subjects; her mother had learned to encrypt vulnerability as strength. [215] But the praises also fell into six lines, each carrying six heavy beats. [228]

At the deserted warden's desk, two ivory cubes rested beneath a glass dome. [241] The prisoners called them hangman's dice: each morning, their throw selected a ward and a bed for lethal testing. [260] The dice also set the day's door code; Mara herself had designed that elegant obscenity. [275] Her mother had stolen the current codebook and hidden it in those extravagant medical compliments. [290] A die chose the line, its twin the stressed word; each entry yielded another part of today's password. [308] Mara entered the password, and the console offered her a choice of wards. [321]

Through the nearest ward window, her mother watched from a wheeled cot, older than any version Mara had permitted herself to imagine. [343] Her lips shaped a question: "Only me?" [350] Mara looked at the spare mask hanging from her belt. [360] Behind her, the spider swung between two bolts, drawing another radius across the opening. [374] A clean draft carried its thread down from the chimney: somewhere above, the sea had not sealed the stairs. [393]

Words could not cross the pressure glass, so Mara explained her plan through dance. [407] Three gliding steps made a staircase; a slow turn gathered an invisible partner; her lowered shoulder offered a place to lean. [428] Then she opened her arms and repeated the turning step, making room for everyone. [442] Her mother lifted two fingers, keeping time, just as she had when Mara first counted tiles with her feet. [461] The familiar gesture nearly undid her. [467]

She entered the command to liberate every ward. [475] An alarm flowered red across the console, demanding that she justify the release. [488] Mara selected medical necessity, with the calm defiance of someone correcting a misspelled name. [502] The console demanded her signature, warning that an improper release constituted sabotage. [514] She signed the name she had concealed for seven years. [524]

Water began licking the thresholds as the seals withdrew. [533] "I cannot stand," her mother said when Mara reached her. [543] Once, Mara would have answered with an order to try. [553] Instead she knelt beside the cot and asked who could help. [564] The man praised for seeking company took one corner of the mattress; a woman with exceptional vigilance unfastened the cot's restraint straps. [586] They threaded the straps beneath the mattress to make a sling, leaving enough length for several hands. [603] Nobody had to carry her alone. [609]

Mara led them toward the draft, returning whenever someone stumbled, while the rising water erased their footprints behind them. [628] The stair spiraled inside an old instrument tower whose summit still stood above the sea. [643] By the time the spider joined the last strand, the final patient had passed beneath its web into the chimney. [663] Mara followed, carrying the journal against her chest. [671] On the landing, her mother reached from the sling and caught her hand. [684] The hand that had built the locks was trembling; this time, Mara let someone hold it. [700]
