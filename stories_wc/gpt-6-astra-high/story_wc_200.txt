In the hush of a hidden sunrise, Nera lowered her father's harp into the echo gorge, where the dead waited to be heard. [23] As a lost soul collector, she usually brought a tuning key, a ledger, and the solemnity her guild considered indispensable. [43] Today she had left the key behind. [50]

Her father's soul had refused her summons for seven mornings, although she had sung every approved lament without once breaking down. [71] On the eighth, exhaustion had loosened her grip, and a crooked chord had answered with his unmistakable snort. [89] Now she had come to find out whether laughter could accomplish what her careful mourning could not. [106]

Above her, dawn silvered the gorge's rim without reaching its floor, and pale shapes gathered beneath the dripping ledges. [125] Spectral confessions trembled in the strings: a stolen inheritance, a letter burned unread, a name deliberately forgotten. [142] The harp caught what its listeners could not bear to say; once truly heard, a soul could cross into morning. [162]

But one string lay trapped against the warped frame, and from it came only a muffled chuckle. [179] Nera took out a crocodile's tooth, curved and yellow, which her father had once used as a plectrum. [197] Its hooked tip reached beneath the pinched string where her fingers could not. [210] The sound it freed was gloriously ugly. [217] Somewhere beyond the ledge, her father laughed, then fled deeper into the gorge. [230]

She had always been modestly fearless: willing to enter haunted places, but careful about loose stones and the depth of water. [251] Following him frightened her more than either, because she might discover that he did not want her to find him. [271] Still, she chose to pursue him through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp, sounding the tooth against each stubborn string. [292] Each echo returned from a different crevice until one answered in the wheezing rhythm of his last breath. [310]

She found him beside a pool that held the sunrise the gorge itself could not see. [326] He looked neither young nor restored, only less burdened by the effort of remaining upright. [341]

"You keep playing funeral music," he said, "and I cannot confess to it." [354]

She sat on a dry stone and played the crooked chord again. [366] It sounded like a duck attempting dignity. [373] His shoulders shook, and against her will, hers followed. [382]

"When I died, your nose was whistling," he said. [391] "You were trying so hard not to cry that every breath came out like a kettle." [407]

For weeks she had remembered his final rattling sound as pain she had failed to ease. [423]

"You were laughing at me?" she asked. [430]

"Yes, and then I was ashamed, because you might think leaving you was funny." [444]

The pool trembled, but no passage opened. [451] Nera understood that she was still waiting for him to take the laughter back, to make his death entirely terrible. [471] Anything less felt like a measure of how little they had loved each other. [485] She pressed the tooth into her palm until its old grooves hurt. [497]

"I was relieved when you died," she said. [505] "I wanted you back, and I wanted to sleep without listening for your bell." [519]

There it was, her own confession, neither beautiful nor properly pitched. [530] Her father reached toward her, and the cold that touched her cheek felt almost like the hand she remembered. [549]

"That bell was a monstrous invention," he said. [557]

Their laughter came unevenly, with long silences inside it, and neither tried to improve the sound. [573] Across the pool, the reflected dawn unfolded into a narrow path. [584] He did not become radiant as he crossed; he remained her father, limping a little, looking back once to pull a foolish face. [607] She laughed again, and losing him still hurt. [615]

When she returned to the waiting souls, Nera opened her ledger to a clean page but wrote no new rule. [635] She could already imagine the damage of demanding merriment from those who had none to give. [651] Instead, she settled the harp against her shoulder and asked the first ghost what he remembered besides his shame. [670] As he began, she held the crooked tooth ready, leaving room in the music for whatever might need to come through. [691]
