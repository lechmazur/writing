At the hush as an empty playground swing drifted in the breeze, the forgotten library acquired a door. [18] Through it, Sella could see the square from which she had been banished for breaking the governor's marble face. [37] She had thrown the stone after his guards dragged her brother off the swing. [51] Now nobody played there. [55]

Orrin, the shard-mirror archivist, held an afternoon shadow caught in glass against the opening. [69] It was Sella's shadow, confiscated with her name, and its small dark fist still gripped an invisible stone. [87] "Before the swing squeaks again," he said, "tell me where the threshold belongs." [100]

Sella studied the tea leaves spread across their saucer, furious that even escape required another lesson. [116] The exile courts served tea before sentencing because leaves steeped in a prisoner's breath retained the angles of home. [135] By analyzing the geometry of tea leaves, an archivist could fit a stolen shadow back into its native light. [154] Once, that had been the ordinary machinery of reversible exile: a cooling of tempers, not a disappearance. [171] Then the governor smashed the library's mirrors, erased its address, and left its inmates to forget the way out. [190]

Orrin had spent thirty years gathering shards; the court had impounded his own shadow as punishment for the first repair. [210] He catalogued their distortions, taught newcomers to annotate their mistakes, and never called anyone gifted or hopeless. [227] Sella hated him most on days when she needed those omissions. [238]

"There," she said, pointing to the triangle formed by three black leaves. [250] She angled the glass toward the door, and a crack ran through her shadow's wrist. [265] The square tilted like a picture slipping from its nail. [275]

Orrin took three quick steps backward and struck the returns desk. [286] He always walked backwards when nervous; she had thought it comic until she saw his hands. [302] They shook too badly to take the glass. [310] "Put it down, you magnificent menace." [316]

"Stop making me practice on the only thing I have left." [327] She set it down hard enough to make him flinch, then hated herself for noticing. [342]

"I thought you knew how to fix everything," she said. [352]

"I know how to keep the pieces." [359] He pushed his notebook toward her with his elbow. [368] Every page held a failed reconstruction, dated, corrected, attempted again; beside some were the names of people who had returned. [388] Beneath her first disastrous lesson he had written, Came back to the table. [401] Not stupid, not brave, just a fact she could manage to repeat. [413]

Beyond the doorway, the swing approached the end of its arc, stretching their borrowed second almost to breaking. [431] Sella bent over the saucer instead of reaching for the glass. [442] Two leaves overlapped where she had counted one; eased apart with her pencil, they made four points, not three. [461] Four feet of a swing frame, seen sideways by a girl turning to throw. [475] She annotated her diagram: Look beneath what looks complete. [484]

"The crack," she said, and her voice nearly deserted her. [494]

Orrin slid a mirror shard beneath the broken wrist, silver side upward. [506] "You hold it steady," he said, "and I'll tell you when the edges meet." [520] For once, he gave instructions without a question, and she understood that his lessons had never been an admission price. [540] He wanted her able to survive the days when nobody could hold the glass for her. [556]

She tilted the pieces together until the shadow's fist opened across the seam. [569] Outside, sunlight settled beneath the swing, and the doorway stopped trembling. [580]

"Come through," she ordered, reaching for his sleeve. [588]

"That is your light, not mine." [594]

For thirty years, she realized, he had built exits he could not use while his own shadow waited in some locked court cupboard. [617]

"Then tell me which cupboard," she said. [624] He stared at her, and she tapped the pencil against his notebook with a vicious imitation of his patience. [643] "We can annotate the plan when I get it wrong." [653]

He wrote the location, then tucked the pencil behind her ear, catching her cheek briefly in his unsteady palm. [672] She stepped into the square as the swing squeaked, carrying the notebook and leaving the invisible stone behind. [690]
