The sea struck the cliff, and the hush inside the passage struck back. [13] Mara waited until her lungs stopped demanding daylight, then crawled toward the forgotten research facility beneath the headland. [31] She was a spelunker who feared enclosed spaces, a contradiction she had learned to manage by counting the places where she could turn around. [55] There had been none for forty yards. [62]

Yesterday's storm had uncovered the entrance, giving her one interval between tidal changes before the sea reclaimed it. [80] She had thirty minutes left. [85] Her boatman would wait beyond the breakers, keeping a radio watch she could not hear through this much stone. [104]

Ivo had once spent nine hours beside her beneath a fallen limestone slab, saying her name whenever she began to disappear inside her terror. [128] Later, he had taken maintenance work here. [135] After the drowning, the company called him an equipment loss. [145] It was more literal than anybody understood. [152]

The facility had taught undersea cables to carry the dead, using their attention to repair signals damaged during the long crossing. [173] When the laboratories flooded, the system simply recruited its technicians. [183] A living person had to acknowledge each captive by name before the system would release them. [199]

Mara had learned this from auctioned notebooks, packed around a burial codex the researchers had stolen from a monastery. [218] Gold remembered names, the notebooks explained; memories crossed into matter only through phase transitions. [232] For centuries, that codex had held the names of paupers whom nobody else recorded. [246] Its marginal gold had become saturated with the habit of remembering. [257] The first receiver had been made from it. [265]

Now a fleck of illuminant gold from the codex's margin floated inside Mara's homemade detector, a glass vial wrapped in a cooling coil. [288] Each time its water froze, invisible traffic in the cables appeared as fine dark threads pointing toward the source. [307] Each thaw erased them. [311] She followed those brief tracks past empty bunks and a cafeteria where salt had furred the cups. [328]

The threads converged on a junction cabinet above a narrow drainage culvert. [340] Inside the cabinet, a service diagram showed how to return stored voices to the ordinary communications network. [357] Beside it, someone had penciled: Speak their names before disconnecting. [367] An empty vial socket waited beneath the diagram, but the manual routing lever was under the cabinet. [384]

Mara knelt and shone her lamp into the culvert. [393] She could reach the lever only by entering headfirst, with both arms stretched beyond her face. [409] The next wave landed overhead, filling the silence with the weight of a collapsing mountain. [424] She backed away so violently that her helmet cracked against the cabinet. [436]

In her lamp's shaking circle, the detector froze again. [445] Among the threads appeared a name: Ivo. [452] She had come for him, but other names crowded his, pressing against the glass like passengers at a locked door. [472] He had never asked whether she deserved those nine hours beneath the limestone. [485] "I'm staying," she told them, and fitted the vial into its socket. [497]

The cabinet warmed the ice, and its speaker began reading the names that emerged along the melting edge. [515] Mara crawled into the culvert. [520] Her ribs scraped concrete; her fingers found the lever and pulled. [531] It sprang back when she loosened her grip. [539] She would have to hold it. [545]

With the return circuit open, each name she repeated went out along the undersea cables, followed by its owner's voice. [565] Across the ocean, interrupted calls filled with bewildered greetings, apologies, requests for children who were no longer children. [583] Seawater slid beneath Mara's belly. [588] She said the names louder, not to hurry the dead but to hear something besides her own breath. [606]

Ivo's voice came near the end, asking whoever answered to tell Mara he had not left willingly. [623] She almost let go then. [628] Instead she listened for the next name. [635] It belonged to a cleaner absent from every staff list she had found. [648]

When the last crystal melted, the speaker fell silent. [657] Mara released the lever and dragged herself backward through water that slapped her chin. [671] She returned to the passage with the warm vial clenched between her teeth. [684]

Outside, the sea was still crashing, still drawing breath. [693] Her fear had not diminished, but now, between waves, she could hear her boatman calling from beyond the breakers. [712] "Tell me their names," he shouted, and she did. [721] He wrote them in the logbook, making room beneath the weather. [732]
