The future arrived seventeen years early, and Mara's daughter stepped from it wearing a wedding dress. [16] Behind her, tomorrow's white towers shouldered through the village, splitting cottages without making a sound. [31] In the cobalt glade at dusk, where blue lichens illuminated the roots imprisoning Mara's legs, every broken house shivered through her bones. [53] She had been the village's root-bound visionary for thirty years, but she had never felt a prophecy weigh this much. [73]

[116] No future was allowed to take root unless the dreamer and the person dreamed about welcomed it together. [134] A visionary could invite possibilities, never compel another life to prove her vision true. [148] That morning Mara had foreseen Els drowning in the spring flood. [159] Unable to leave her roots or trust a warning to save her, she had dragged a surviving future toward herself. [179] Now the village's children lay flickering in their beds as their older selves displaced them. [194] By sunrise, only one age could remain.
