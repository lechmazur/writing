All winter, amid the hush of old videotapes left unwatched, I listened to my father breathing through the wall. [19] The tapes held thirty birthdays, each celebrated by striking the bell he had made from a salvaged diving cylinder. [38] I meant to capture the sound of growing older, but could not bear to watch him become young again. [57] So my recorder collected his present breaths, which sounded terribly like a man waiting for someone to answer. [75]

Before the council renamed his waterfront junkyard a fog sculpture garden, sailors came to him for tomorrow's weather. [93] Our junkyard prophet could describe a wreck before its keel was laid, but every forecast cost him something already lived. [113] My mother called him precognitively forgetful, and scratched their anniversary inside his wedding ring. [127] Since her death, I had discouraged prophecy, preferring a father who remembered breakfast to one who remembered the coming century. [147]

That morning, however, he woke tasting seawater and said the night's tide would take the garden. [163] Then he asked where he had put the sea. [172]

I wanted to locate the birthday bell before it vanished, to record one last note beside those trapped on tape. [192] Salt had thinned its walls over the years; perhaps its changing voice could explain what his breathing could not. [211] He insisted on coming, carrying a piece of plain chalk from the slate where Mother had written their daily plans. [231] "For whatever goes missing," he said, slipping it into my pocket. [242]

The garden's paths had shifted with years of shingle storms, and fog reduced its iron figures to isolated shoulders. [261] We began searching systematically, arranging driftwood as directional markers at each fork, with the arrows pointing toward passages still unexplored. [281] On returning from a dead end, I reversed its arrow and chalked a cross on the wood. [298] That way, neither his gaps nor my childhood recollections could send us circling forever. [312] He watched each mark intently, as though I were teaching him an alphabet the water might understand. [329]

Beyond a welded flock of anchors, he stopped and took my wrist. [341] "I hear you at eighty," he said, "laughing with someone I cannot see." [354] For a moment I lifted the recorder, greedy for the very thing I had come to collect. [371] Then his hand loosened, and he asked whether my parents knew I was out here. [386]

I put the machine away before I could ask him to tell me more. [400] With the chalk, I wrote ANNA on my dark sleeve, where he could read it without asking. [417] "Your daughter," I said, and offered my arm rather than waiting for recognition. [430]

The last unmarked passage brought us to the cylinder, hanging inside the ribs of a fishing boat. [447] Its clapper lay in two rusted pieces beneath it. [456] Father touched the empty loop, and I saw him searching for a use for his hand. [472] I had been clinging to echoes, hoping enough recorded yesterdays would somehow outweigh the tomorrow that would take him. [491] Now even the bell had lost the part that knew how to summon its voice. [506]

I nearly suggested we go home, but he cupped the cylinder gently and knocked with one knuckle. [523] A thin note escaped, nothing like the bright thunder I remembered from my birthdays. [537] "Still here," he told it, and looked pleased to have found something he could reassure. [552] I switched on the recorder and asked if we might strike it together. [565]

Our first attempt missed because neither of us could hear the other's count above the gathering surf. [582] The second worked, his bony knuckles beside mine, and afterward he laughed until he needed to rest against me. [601] I kept recording through that long recovery, through the little apologies he made for being heavy. [617] There was no clean note to extract from it, no age without the person carrying it. [633]

We followed our crossed arrows inland while the tide reached into the garden and began rearranging our alphabet. [651] At the gate, he brushed my sleeve with one finger and asked whether Anna would stay for supper. [669] "Yes," I said, and did not brush the chalk from my sleeve. [681] That evening we watched one birthday, only once, while outside the dark water carried away whatever it could lift. [700]
