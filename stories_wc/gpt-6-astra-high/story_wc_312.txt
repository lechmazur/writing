At dawn, after the last fish was caught, the sea hung motionless beneath the sapphire viaduct. [16] Water pressed against the blue arches, while far below, the naked seabed steamed. [29] Iven, a lukewarm mage, climbed the maintenance ladder carrying a broken ship's bell and his mother's lunch pail. [47] He could keep soup pleasant or stop a newborn shivering, but he could not boil water, much less mend bronze. [67]

Last night, he had eaten half the fish his mother netted. [78] Nobody knew it was the last until the tide refused to fall. [90] The priests called their supper sacrilege. [96] Iven, still earnestly naive at thirty, had brought them the bones as evidence of hunger. [111] They instructed him to kneel until the universe forgave him. [121] Instead, he had come to rebuild the tidekeeper. [129]

Inside the viaduct's central pier, two listening cups stood beneath a forest of seized brass levers. [145] For centuries, the sapphire had carried the pulses of swimming fish to levers that opened and closed the sky's return gate. [166] Without those signals, its mechanism had locked. [173] Opening the gate by hand would drop the suspended ocean whole upon the town. [187] He needed to teach it a heartbeat. [194]

His father had once maintained these levers, before taking a fishing boat beyond the shrinking shoals. [210] Only the boat's bell came home, split from lip to crown. [221] His mother polished it every winter; Iven had prayed beside it with the brittle devotion of someone who could not survive a second loss. [245] His hands shook as he turned that small shrine upside down and wedged it over the cup marked RELEASE. [264] Beneath its fracture, a drop gathered, trembled, fell. [272] A lever twitched. [275] He punched a hole in the lunch pail and hung it above the cup marked HOLD. [291]

When the sun cleared the hills, his mother found him counting. [302] "The priests say you are making our debt worse." [311]

He kept his eyes on the water. [318] He had expected an accusation, but she sounded tired enough to sleep standing. [331] "Did they offer you breakfast?" [336]

She shook her head, then took the empty jug from his hand and went to refill it. [353]

The pail gave six drops for every five from the bell. [364] Their mismatched commands made the levers shudder, opening the gate for dangerously uneven intervals. [378] Each movement drew a white fissure through the sea overhead. [388] Iven tried pinching the pail's hole smaller, but as the water level fell, its dripping slowed again. [405] The bell's long fracture behaved differently, leaking less as its upper length emerged from the water. [421] He could not repair the heavens by making two vessels look equally full. [434] He had to understand their dripping rates. [441]

He steadied the bell's temperature with one palm, warming its water until the stubborn drops came faster. [458] With his other hand, he adjusted the pail's wire handle until its drops struck just after the bell's. [476] His mother kept both vessels filled to charcoal lines he drew inside them. [489] Release, then hold. [492] A heartbeat, not a commandment. [497]

The brass levers began to rock. [503] Beyond the arches, a narrow curtain of water descended onto the harbor mud without crushing a single roof. [521] His mother laughed once, then covered her mouth, as though laughter might disturb the balance. [536] Iven dared not lift his palm. [542]

"Will this bring the fish back?" [548]

"No," he said, and felt how much harder that word was than promising a miracle. [563] There might be eggs in the stranded weed, or there might be nothing left to hatch. [579] They would have to search, and guard what survived, and learn what could be taken without emptying the world. [598]

By noon, neighbors were climbing the ladder with water, bread, and questions. [610] Iven showed a girl how to count the space between drops. [621] He could not right a cosmic imbalance alone, and no longer mistook that fact for a failure of faith. [640] Above them the ocean lowered, patiently, one borrowed heartbeat at a time. [652]
