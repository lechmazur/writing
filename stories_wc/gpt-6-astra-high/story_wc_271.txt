After the last lighthouse keeper retired and the light was automated, the town opened his arboretum, perched on a seaside cliff, and hired Mariam to keep its improbable trees alive. [30] The keeper had planted seeds brought by sailors; Mariam added a fig cutting carried across three borders in a damp sock. [51] It came from the courtyard where her sister had married, eleven minutes before a drone strike ended the wedding. [70] People called Mariam resilient, which seemed to mean they preferred not to hear about those eleven minutes. [87] She was quietly talented at grafting, less gifted at explaining why an engine overhead could empty her hands of strength. [107]

For seven years, the fig grew beside the lighthouse wall. [117] Each spring, Mariam unfolded a crumpled photograph: her sister laughing beneath its parent tree, one palm raised against the photographer's flash. [138] The instant print had been in Mariam's fist when they found her. [150] She compared the pictured leaves with the new ones, as though resemblance were evidence that something had been repaired. [169]

Then winter rain opened a fissure between the lighthouse and the fig. [181] The council engineer, Ruth, marked a boundary with orange stakes. [191] Beyond them, the soil was separating from the cliff. [200] Moving a mature tree would require machinery whose weight might bring the whole shelf down. [215] Mariam submitted a proposal anyway, pricing cranes the council could never afford. [227] Ruth returned it without the customary encouragement. [234] "Tell me what we can actually save," she said. [243]

The survey drone arrived on a morning so clear that gull shadows crossed the paths like scissors. [260] Ruth had warned her, and Mariam had said she could manage. [271] But when its rotors rose above the surf, she began to freeze. [283] Her knees locked; the stakes became wedding chairs; the white lighthouse wall blazed like dust. [298] Somewhere, Ruth was saying her name. [304]

Her therapist had taught her to recognize danger through somatic markers: the body's signals, which could report a memory as faithfully as a present threat. [329] Recognition did not make the sound harmless. [336] It gave her one question to ask before obeying it. [346] Where were her feet? [350]

She felt grit inside one boot, a seam against her heel, the pressure of ground that had not yet moved. [370] There, inside the clamor, was a pearl of stillness, no larger than the pause between two breaths. [387] Not peace, and not her sister returning. [394] Enough room to choose. [398]

"Land it," she told Ruth, and Ruth signaled the operator. [408] They finished the inspection with the drone grounded, studying photographs already transmitted to the engineer's tablet. [424] A dark crack ran beneath the fig's roots. [432] For a moment Mariam wanted Ruth to promise that the image exaggerated the danger. [446]

Instead, she took out the crumpled photograph and looked past her sister's face. [459] Under the wedding table stood six jars of cuttings, favors her sister had insisted guests take home. [476] She knew how to make more trees; what she needed was permission to let this one go. [493] Her sister, practical even in celebration, had been multiplying it. [503]

"Not the trunk," Mariam said. [508] From the safe side of the stakes, she showed Ruth the slender branches within reach of a pruning pole. [527] They took twenty cuttings that afternoon, then closed the lower garden. [538] Mariam wrote the date and source on every label, followed by a space for whether it rooted. [555] She wanted to anchor optimism in realism; some would rot, some would fail, and the cliff would not change its mind. [576]

When the council proposed calling the nursery a memorial, she asked them to buy a propagation bench instead. [594] The original fig fell into the sea in March. [603] By April, nine cuttings had rooted. [609]

Mariam entered nine in the ledger, not twenty, and set aside three pots for the town's inland school. [627] The photograph stayed beside the ledger, its creases still white across her sister's raised hand. [642] On windy mornings Mariam sometimes had to stop work and find her feet again. [656] Then she checked the soil, watering only what needed water. [666]
