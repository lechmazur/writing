After the blood work came back, Ansel tucked the results beneath the score he had brought to the hospice. [19] Above the cloud line, the hospice windows burned with afternoon light, and the valley where his daughter lived had disappeared. [39] He had chosen this radiant haven for its silence, but the oxygen pump kept time like a stubborn second heart. [59] Mara, the music therapist, sat beside it without trying to cover its rhythm. [72] Her serenity irritated him; she seemed willing to let things remain unfinished. [84]

"I need enough breath for one song," he said. [93] There was no anemia to correct, the doctor had said, only a failing heart that could not be repaired. [112] He had conducted choirs for forty years, and he wanted to execute a flawless goodbye. [127] His estranged daughter would receive a recording, not the spectacle of her father gasping. [141]

Mara asked what the song should carry that ordinary speech could not. [153] Instead of answering, he produced a piece of a broken vinyl record, its edge wrapped in a handkerchief. [171] On the surviving label, Tuesday was written in his daughter's painstaking capitals. [183] "Lena made it from her mother's kitchen recordings," he said. [193] In those recordings, Ruth had sung about rinsing rice, finding socks, and peeling oranges, hymning the mundane until chores acquired a small, dependable radiance. [217] After the funeral, Lena had paid to press them onto a record. [229] Ansel had called it an insult to a woman who once sang Bach professionally. [243] Then he had broken it against the table while Lena watched. [254] He wanted now to record Ruth's favorite aria, beautifully enough to make an apology unnecessary. [269]

Mara laid the fragment on his score. [276] "I can help you ask her to come, but I won't help you avoid asking her forgiveness," she said. [295] He turned his face toward the bright windows. [303] She stayed until he turned back, then offered him the telephone rather than another exercise for breath. [320] When he could not lift it, she held it without pretending the gesture was anything but help. [337] His message to Lena was eight words long: "I broke something precious and I need you." [353] Mara left her own number, adding that he was asking, not summoning, and that Lena owed neither of them a performance. [374]

Lena called Mara, not him, and asked whether coming meant forgiving. [385] "No," Mara said, and waited while the woman on the line cried. [397]

By dusk, the clouds had climbed the glass, turning the room into a lantern with no visible earth beneath it. [417] Lena arrived on the last uphill tram, carrying an orange and the telephone that held the original recordings. [435] She put the orange beside the vinyl shard but kept her coat on. [448] At Ansel's nod, Mara scored the skin with her thumbnail, releasing the sharp sweetness into the room. [465] He had expected hope to smell medicinal, like disinfectant or the plastic tubing through which rescue might still arrive. [484] Instead, the scent of hope was an orange his daughter had not yet decided to share. [500]

On Lena's telephone, Ruth began singing, "One bowl, two cups, put the kettle on," and a spoon clinked against porcelain. [520] Ansel tried to enter on the right note and found only air. [532] Mara joined the recording softly, matching his exhalation rather than its pitch, leaving space where his voice should have been. [552]

Lena took off her coat. [557] "You kept the grand things," she told him, "and threw away what belonged to us." [572] Ansel looked at the broken edge between them. [580] "I wanted her back without the kitchen, without the days I hadn't noticed," he said. [595] "I'm sorry I punished you for remembering her differently," he whispered. [606]

There was no breath left for the aria. [614] Mara placed half the peeled orange within his reach and half within Lena's, then withdrew her chair. [631] Lena moved the halves together on his blanket, not mending anything, only making one place for their hands. [649] Between the pump's pulses, father and daughter listened to Ruth remember the kettle, and neither asked her to begin again. [669]
