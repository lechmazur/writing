During the listening hour, even the rescue shovels lie still. [10] Mara kneels in a withered orchard deep in a subarctic crater, her ear pressed against a copper pipe between the roots. [31] Below her, a collapsed stairway has sealed forty people inside the winter shelter, including her mother. [47] The tremor has buckled the shelter and shifted the orchard; the plans the rescuers brought no longer match the ground. [67] The orchard once fed them all; its last living roots still drink warmth from the shelter walls. [84] They also carry heartbeats, too faint to hear through digging. [94] That is why the rescuers wait above, holding their tools against their chests, while the world holds its breath. [113]

As a flash-paper alchemist, Mara usually makes festival birds that vanish before their wings can scorch a child's fingers. [132] Tonight she must translate heartbeats into maps before the shelter's dwindling pocket of air becomes too thin to breathe. [151]

Beside her lies a storybook illustration plate, etched with a hare carrying a star through a forest. [168] Her mother printed that picture for every child born in the crater, each copy carrying a glimmer into the dark. [188] The tiny star contains real, safekept starlight, gathered at Mara's birth and sealed into the copper with an alchemist's varnish. [208] It is the last ember of the fallen star that made the crater; all the rest went into keeping the orchard alive. [230] There will be no replacement. [235] In its light, anything lost can show the way to itself, provided the seeker supplies its true rhythm. [253]

Her rulers and compasses are useless here; the roots transmit no distances, only the separate rhythms of the people below. [273] She builds those rhythms from what the orchard has discarded: snapped grafting clips and bottle caps, half buried beneath a fallen scarecrow. [295] She places a cap for each strong beat, a clip for each weak one, and leaves a gap for each pause. [316]

Through the pipe, her mother's heart knocks twice, hesitates, then knocks again. [328] Mara knows that hesitation from childhood, when she slept against her mother's ribs during the bitter months. [345] She sets out the pattern, then shifts a cap to make the spacing even, unwilling to admit how long the silence lasts. [367]

The star gives nothing back. [372]

She remembers her mother cupping a grafted branch for minutes, letting its binding settle instead of pulling it tight. [391] Mara takes her hand off the cap. [398] She rests both wrists on her knees and waits through the long gap, though a cramp climbs steadily into her thigh. [419] When the next beat comes, she lets it arrive crookedly. [429] Three times she listens; three times she rebuilds the little constellation without correcting it. [443]

At last the star trembles. [448] Its light threads the caps and clips, then spreads across the plate, changing the hare's forest into passages beneath the orchard. [469] One narrow line climbs from her mother's heartbeat to a fissure beneath the pear tree, skirting the rockfall that defeated their shovels. [491] Already the lines blur whenever a heartbeat falters, and she cannot carry the loose pattern to the digging crews. [510]

Mara reaches for her flash-paper. [515] To seal the map, she must burn away the varnish; the released starlight will hold these lines until morning, then leave forever. [537] No child will ever again receive her mother's picture with its little living star. [551] She touches the hare's worn ear, then lays the paper over the plate and strikes her flint. [568] The flame passes without lingering, too briefly to warm the listening roots and muddle their message. [584] Beneath it, the map sets clear. [590]

Mara lifts the plate, and the rescuers move toward the pear tree without a word. [605] She holds the copper level while they work, and does not lower it even when a small voice begins to cry below. [627] When they widen the fissure, she hears coughing first, then her mother's voice asking who has remembered to bring blankets. [647]

By morning the star is gone. [653] Her mother sits beside her under the bare branches, wrapped in three coats, one hand resting inside Mara's. [671] Mara does not count the pauses now. [678] She waits, warming each finger, until her mother is ready to stand. [690]
