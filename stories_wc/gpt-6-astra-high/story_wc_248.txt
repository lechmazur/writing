Six years after going off the grid, Mara received a parcel containing a faded tapestry fragment and an address inside a volcano. [22] The wool smelled of sulfur, but the crooked blue bird belonged unmistakably to her mother. [37] Beneath it, an unfamiliar hand had written that the occupation was over and the prisoners' workshop had finally been opened. [57] Mara had burned the lending library to destroy the borrowing records that soldiers used to identify readers of forbidden Oruni. [77] She had also burned two sleeping apprentices, whose presence she had never bothered to check. [92] For six years, she had called disappearance a punishment, although it required nothing of her except continuing to disappear. [111]

The archive of the lost Oruni civilization occupied chambers beneath the caldera, where ancient clay records survived because conquerors preferred gold. [132] The recent occupiers had installed captive translators there, including Mara's mother, to extract useful knowledge from an officially worthless people. [152] Now warm water crept across the floors, and the new archivist was packing whatever he could carry. [169]

"She died last winter," he told Mara, recognizing the parcel in her hands. [182] "But she kept asking us to save the pictures, even the damaged ones." [195]

He brought her to a vandalized self-portrait: her mother's painted face, its mouth scraped away by a censor's knife. [214] The tapestry fragment matched the hanging behind those painted shoulders, down to its absurd, lopsided bird. [230]

"I found that wool tucked behind the panel," the archivist said. [241] "It took the bread trader three months to find you." [251]

Mara touched the gouged mouth and remembered her mother's voice on the morning of the arrests. [267] "If they scatter us, promise you will bring us home." [277] She had promised, then mistaken an enormous fire for an answer. [288]

Her mother had taught her to read weaving before letters, counting repetitions and trusting the unease when a pattern broke. [308] That analytically intuitive habit returned now: the bird in the portrait had seven claws, but the woven bird had three. [328] Seven, three: the shelving coordinates painted on every archive cabinet. [338]

There she found a pottery bowl decorated with fish, one bearing a boat's square sail instead of a fin. [357] Its underside carried her mother's workshop stamp; captive translators had also copied artifacts for the occupiers' museums. [374] The sail matched a ship in the wall mosaic, where four red stones interrupted a border of eleven white ones. [394] Cabinet four, shelf eleven contained a plaster relief of mourners, each carrying an identical, conspicuously blank tablet. [411]

Mara almost laughed, and the sound broke painfully against her teeth. [422] Her mother had laid a trail of artistic creations through the inventory, making decorations say what written words could not. [442] Even mutilated, the portrait had pointed onward. [449]

She turned the relief over, found its hollow back, and eased away a skin of plaster. [465] Inside lay thin clay sheets crowded with names, burial places, children sent to distant households, and the names they had been given. [487] The translators had recorded one another, then added every scrap of news that arrived with another prisoner. [504] On the final sheet, beneath her mother's handwriting, another hand had supplied her grave. [518] The hidden message was not an escape route for one woman, but a way back for a scattered people. [537] Across the top her mother had inscribed: Bring us home, and leave nobody out. [551]

Among the dead were the library apprentices, Ista and Ren, with their ages and their father's village. [568] Mara knelt in the warming water and held that sheet until the archivist gently took it from her trembling hands. [588] There was enough heat inside her to burn another city, and nowhere she could put it except into care. [607]

"We take everything," she said, and began padding the tablets with her spare clothes. [621]

Before wrapping the portrait, she drew it on the blank reverse of the archivist's evacuation manifest. [637] She worked to portray her mother accurately, including the stubborn chin, the crooked bird, and the gouge where her mouth had been. [659] Underneath, she wrote her own name and what she had done at the library. [673] Then she asked the archivist for the road to Ista and Ren's father. [686]

Above them, the mountain trembled; Mara lifted the first crate as carefully as if someone inside it were sleeping. [705]
