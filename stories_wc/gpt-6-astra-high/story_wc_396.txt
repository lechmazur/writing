The lighthouse orchard perched above shifting ice floes grew pears that tasted faintly of smoke, because its roots drank warmth from the beacon furnace. [24] Now thirty people stood on a floe beneath its cliff, and the furnace had fuel enough either to keep the trees alive or to power the idle rescue lift. [53] Their small boat had already vanished between grinding slabs of ice. [64]

The orchard keeper asked, "Do we burn our winter harvest for strangers?" [76]

Ada, the shadow-laced peacekeeper, stood beside him in the vintage store occupying the lighthouse's ground floor. [92] They had come here for the lift controls behind its counter, tested monthly but forbidden for passengers. [109] Her father led the last rescue anyone remembered. [117] Nobody spoke of it. [121]

During the weighted breath before reply, Ada felt the coat's shadows tighten over her ribs. [136] They muted panic and grief, making arithmetic easier: thirty strangers against eighty residents and a winter without fruit. [154] She had joined the service to map empathy onto action, not to make suffering easier to subtract. [171] Calmly intuitive, her instructors had called her, before they taught her which intuitions to distrust. [186]

Inside her glove lay a broken crystal ball fragment, salvaged from the wreck of her father's prediction instrument. [204] Whole, it had shown probable futures; broken, it caught the past retained in silver. [218] She had never dared use it here. [225] The official account said he had overloaded the lift, killing himself and everyone aboard. [239] His daughter had built a profession around never making his mistake. [250]

Now she turned the shard toward the wall, where mirrors taken in trade for pears hung above clocks and chipped crockery. [271] Each had once furnished a room of the lighthouse, before the orchard's debts stripped it bare. [287] She could reconstitute that last rescue only via glimpses in vintage store mirrors, assembling what no witness had seen entire. [307] The memories arrived quicker than thought. [313]

In an oval mirror, her father watched figures clinging to a wreck beneath the cliff. [328] His crystal ball showed the loaded basket falling. [336] He broke it against the sill. [342] Ada almost shut her eyes. [347]

Then a shaving mirror caught his hand on the furnace valve, stopping halfway between the orchard pipes and the lift engine. [368] He had tried to heat both. [374] In a tall mirror that had faced the nursery door, he knelt to kiss Ada's forehead. [390]

"There will still be pears," he promised. [397]

She remembered asking him that, and wished she did not. [407] The engine mirror showed the pressure gauge sinking as the basket rose, then the winch unwinding against his hands. [426]

Not too many passengers, then. [431] Too little steam. [434] Her father had tried to rescue the drowning without frightening the people who were safe. [449] The distinction hurt worse than the accusation she had carried for twenty years. [462]

The keeper's breath still clouded the window beside her. [471] Below, a woman on the floe took off her coat and wrapped it around a child who kept trying to give it back. [494] Neither gesture would register in the service's calculations, which assumed that frightened people always protected themselves. [510] A courageous anomaly, the training manual called anyone who didn't. [520] Ada had underlined the phrase once, admiring it, before learning that anomalies were excluded from forecasts. [536]

She unfastened her coat. [540] The shadows resisted, then loosened, and cold reached her with the plain certainty of an unpaid bill. [557] Peacekeepers received winter rations whether the harvest failed or not. [567] She laid her brass ration seal on the counter beside the shard, where the keeper could see it. [585] It would buy grain for the communal pot, though not enough to make the coming months comfortable. [602]

She could not promise pears, or forgiveness, or that thirty rescued people would become useful neighbors. [618] She could promise not to stand outside the cost. [627]

The valve handle resisted her, reluctant to turn past its familiar halfway position. [640] She gripped it with both hands and turned it fully toward the engine. [653] Under the floor, steam hammered awake. [659] Along the orchard rows, the heating pipes began to cool. [669] The keeper looked at her seal, then reached past her for the basket brake. [683]

Ada finally exhaled. [686] The lift moved before she answered. [692]
