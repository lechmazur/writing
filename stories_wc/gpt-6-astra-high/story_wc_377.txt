After words became unnecessary, the settlement on the whispering bluff above the clouds spoke through kinetic sculpture. [17] Wind moved bronze branches, weighted bowls, and bells, translating the pressure of a hand into music everyone could understand. [36] They called these conversations symphonies of truth, because trembling fingers confessed what a composed face could conceal. [53]

Iona, their most harmonious bell tuner, kept every public instrument sweet enough to bear. [67] She considered this kindness. [71] When her husband died, she had softened the funeral bells until even their small son stopped covering his ears. [90] He had not attended another concert. [96]

Now winter had made her seasonally mute, as it did every year, taking the singing voice she used for tuning. [116] Without it, she worked by fingertips, feeling each vibration through the bones of her wrists. [131] That was how she first heard the iceberg fragment. [140]

Salvagers brought it up from the warming sea, wrapped in felt, on a sled dragged by six exhausted people. [159] Their hands played the arrival sculpture: the last glacier had broken, and this was all they could rescue. [177] Its ancient, compressed heart held a blue the settlement had never seen except in fading pictures. [193] Sunlight made its broken edges glisten, and Iona's son stepped from the crowd to touch it. [209] For the first time since the funeral, his shoulders loosened. [219]

Iona wanted to preserve that blue: not merely the color of ice, but the color of wonder returning. [237] The council proposed a vault beneath the bluff, where permanent frost would keep the fragment intact. [253] No windows could be permitted; summer sunlight might overwhelm the cold. [264] Iona rang her consent, a perfect interval, then asked for one afternoon to compose its farewell. [280]

She suspended the ice beneath the central sculpture, replacing its stone counterweight, and arranged copper cups beneath the dripping point. [300] As each cup filled, it descended, drawing a padded striker across a bell before tipping its water into the next. [320] The fragment's diminishing weight let other branches rise, so the music could never repeat itself. [335] She intended something tender, a blue afternoon translated into sound before the darkness closed around it. [351]

But sunlight entered an old fracture, and the ice cracked sharply enough to send every branch shuddering. [368] The bells collided in a grinding chord that made the councillors recoil. [380] Iona reached for the damping ropes. [386]

Her son caught her wrist. [391] With his free hand, he pressed the nearest bronze branch until it sounded the same terrible chord again. [409] Through her fingertips came his recognition: that was how the empty chair at supper felt. [424] Not the gentle falling cadence she had given him, but something unfinished, with its edges still scraping. [441] She had made grief bearable for everybody except the person who needed it heard. [455]

The chief councillor pulled the lever that summoned the sled bearers, offering her the familiar relief of a problem removed. [475] Iona could not sing a different answer, and her practiced signals all belonged to the beautiful music she suddenly distrusted. [495] Instead, she put his palm against the sculpture's trembling mast, then laid her own hand beside it. [512] She let him feel her wish to save the blue, and the fear underneath: another lovely thing disappearing. [530] Then she guided his hand toward the dark vault door. [540] The contrast needed no explanation. [545]

The councillor hesitated, then unhooked the sled harness and carried it beneath the hanging ice. [560] Together they moved the whole sculpture to the vault entrance, where cold air spilled across the stone. [577] Iona fitted a shutter to the outer arch and linked it to the highest copper cup. [593] Whenever sunlight drew too much meltwater, the filling cup would pull the shutter closed, allowing the chamber to cool again. [613] A narrow drain would empty the cup slowly, measuring the darkness before the next brief opening. [629]

In summer they would shut it completely; in winter the blue would visit between stretches of rest. [646] The fragment would still diminish, and she left that knowledge in the bells, without a resolving note. [663] Her son stayed to help her catch the water. [672] When the shutter opened, blue light crossed their wet hands, and when it closed, neither reached to stop the music. [692]
