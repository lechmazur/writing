After the letters were found, Mara stopped placing flowers beside her father's photograph. [13] As the solemn civil rights archivist aboard the zeppelin Palinode, she had spent twenty years making other people's grief admissible. [33] Her own she had mistaken for evidence. [40]

The floating library held the last unburned records of the coastal expulsions, including the movement's list naming her father a martyr. [61] But the letters, discovered inside a donated atlas, suggested he had opened the eastern gate to the soldiers. [79] They also provided coordinates and a cipher, both written by Ada Bell, the organizer who had vanished that morning. [98]

Mara persuaded the captain to spend their reserve fuel searching for the obsidian lagoon beneath the dunes. [115] Wind had uncovered its volcanic rim, and the water shone like an eye that had watched something unbearable. [133] Lowered on a safety line, she found a sealed tin wedged between the black stones. [148] Inside was an encrypted diary, its glass pages dormant until she traced the letters' cipher across them. [165]

Such diaries preserved a speaking likeness of their authors, witnesses who could refuse a court permission to extract their memories. [185] The manuals warned that an unauthorized reader could become retrocognitively trapped. [196] Mara pressed her thumb against the consent plate anyway. [205]

The lagoon became a square full of burning laundry. [214] Sheets rose flaming from the tenements, briefly shaped like people before the wind tore them apart. [230] Her father stood at the gate, younger than she was now, his hand closed around a key. [247] A soldier held a pistol against a child's mouth. [256]

Then the scene began again. [261]

On its third repetition, Ada stepped out of the smoke. [271] "You want a clean father," she said, "and they wanted a clean victory." [284]

Mara tried to withdraw her hand, but the square burned again, and her father's wrist turned again. [301] Above the remembered rooftops, she felt the safety line tighten: the captain's warning that fuel was running low. [319]

"Then parley with me," Mara said, forcing herself to look at Ada rather than the gate. [335] "Tell me what you are protecting." [341]

Ada opened her coat, revealing evacuation lists sewn into its lining. [352] The diary contained the refugees' assumed names, and the governor's son still ruled the coast. [367] Publishing everything would expose their descendants; publishing nothing would leave the dead beneath the official lie. [383]

"Separate the names from the orders," Mara said. [391] "Keep the first sealed and release the second, with their signatures intact." [403]

Ada looked toward the gate, where Mara's father was admitting the first rifle. [416] "His name stays," she said. [421]

Mara felt the old hunger to bargain for him, to make coercion into innocence. [435] Instead she nodded, and discovered that her free hand still felt the actual shore beneath the square. [452] A shell cut her palm; pain, unlike sight, belonged to the present. [464]

She began arranging beach debris as messages, feeling for driftwood, shells, and bottle glass while remembered fire scorched her eyes. [484] Her assistant knew the letters' cipher and was watching through the library's telescope. [497] Three clusters requested a sealed accession; a line of glass ordered immediate publication of the military orders. [514] She added her own authorization mark, knowing the library's presses would carry her father's name to every surviving coastal reading room. [535]

Two answering tugs promised compliance. [540]

Ada touched the diary's other consent plate, and the gate finally swung beyond the instant Mara had dreaded. [558] Her father let the soldiers pass, then pushed the child through a gap in the crowd. [574] He did not become innocent; he became visible. [582]

When the crew hauled Mara aboard, blood from her palm had dried around grains of black sand. [599] Below, the wind was already dismantling her messages. [607]

She worked through the night beside the warm presses, attaching the threats, the orders, and the testimony to the old martyr's entry. [629] Nothing excused the governor, and nothing allowed her father to remain only the beautiful face inside its frame. [647]

At dawn, as the zeppelin carried its library farther from the coast, Mara set fresh flowers beside his photograph. [666] Under them she placed the published testimony, folded so his name remained in view. [680]
