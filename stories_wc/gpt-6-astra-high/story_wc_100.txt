After the mysterious package arrived, Abel stopped blaming the knocking on his workshop pipes. [14] He was a weary undertaker in a town that leased its graves for twenty years, though the sea seldom honored even that. [36] Each spring he moved the same coffins inland, promising their occupants what he could never secure: a permanent address for rest. [57]

The parcel contained a broken lighthouse lens and a library card bearing his mother's name. [72] She had been the old city's librarian before the seawall failed and the survivors built uphill. [88] Her coffin was one of his empty ones. [96]

Moonlight struck the lens, scattering three short flashes across the ceiling, then three long ones, then three short. [114] Distress signals, not a haunting. [119] That distinction got him out of his chair. [127]

At the harbor, the intact glass showed only black water, but the fracture revealed a staircase between a wave and its reflection. [149] Abel stepped sideways into that impossible seam, carrying the lens against his chest. [162] Water closed behind him without wetting his coat, and the pounding surf dwindled to the sound of somebody pursuing him barefoot. [183]

At the bottom stood the forgotten underwater city library, its bronze doors pressed together like palms. [199] His mother's card opened them. [204] Inside, jellyfish pulsed beyond the windows, illuminating a desk and a thousand shelves squeezed flat against one another. [222]

A woman made of pale, moving type waited behind the desk. [233] He recognized the way she held one finger against her lips. [244]

"They followed your light," his mother whispered, as hurried knocking traveled from window to window. [259] "I sent it because they cannot keep swimming forever." [268]

Beyond the glass, faces appeared wherever the jellyfish shone, then vanished when the light moved on. [284] He had imagined the drowned lying quietly on the seabed, not searching all these years for somewhere that would admit them. [305]

His mother pointed toward the shelves, and he remembered her explanation of how the library had outgrown its narrow city block. [326] It made room by occupying impossible spaces: corners without interiors, distances between touching things. [340] A living reader had to open a room; afterward, any reader could keep it open. [355] During the evacuation, everyone had left, and the rooms had folded shut before the drowned came back. [372] Only the librarian's desk remained, too small to shelter anybody but the woman bound to it. [388]

Abel set down the lens and tried to pry two shelves apart with his hands. [403] The knocking quickened, and the glass bowed inward beneath the weight of all that pleading. [418]

"Not another grave," his mother said, sliding a book across the desk. [430] "A place to stay." [434]

He had grown stubborn without intending to, simply by waiting beside graves until the last mourner finished. [451] Now he stopped pulling, turned sideways between the shelves, and lowered himself toward a chair that was not there. [470] The edges cut into his shoulders, but he opened the book and began to read. [485]

It was a gardening manual, full of advice for coaxing exhausted orchards back into fruit. [500] He read about damaged roots while the room tried to squeeze the breath from him. [515] He waited at each paragraph, refusing to hurry words meant to help something live. [529]

Then wood met the backs of his knees. [537] The shelves separated by an inch, then a yard, and a reading lamp bloomed above his head. [554] Through the broken lens, its steady light reached the windows; the frantic flashes outside slowed as the drowned found the door. [575]

They entered dripping silence, taking books in hands that had forgotten how to hold anything but water. [592] Each seated reader widened the room, until their presence began to revitalize the darkened wings beyond it. [609]

At last his mother came around the desk. [617] She asked whether he would mind some company. [625] He pulled out a chair, and the letters composing her face settled into features he had almost forgotten. [643]

Above them, the sea kept hunting for a way inside, but here there was no land to erode, no lease to expire. [665] Before dawn, he went back for his burial register, leaving the lens burning in the window as a beacon, not an alarm. [687] There were others to bring, and now he could tell them where they were going. [702]

On his mother's card, beneath her name, he wrote the room number. [714] Then he crossed out the words EXPIRATION DATE. [722]
