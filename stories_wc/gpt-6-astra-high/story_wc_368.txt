After receiving the donor heart, Mara returned to dyeing silk, but every blue she mixed seemed insufficiently alive. [18] Before the operation, she had been a watchful dye master, famous for catching the smallest inconsistency between adjoining lengths. [37] Now she watched people instead, especially when they mentioned Iven, the stranger whose heart moved beneath her ribs. [55] Everyone had a story about him, and each teller became briefly luminous. [67] She wanted to learn why some people magnetize stories, while others spend their lives perfecting an unchanging shade. [85]

So when his widow commissioned a memorial hanging, Mara took the basket ferry up through the valley mist. [103] Iven's floating rock tea house hung above the orchards, its mooring chains disappearing into cloud like unfinished sentences. [121] Wind slowly turned the stone within its harness, carrying the windows past the sun. [135] "Make him as generous as we remember," his widow said, showing Mara the wall opposite the communal table. [153]

All morning, customers supplied scenes: Iven sheltering stranded ferrymen, forgiving debts, listening until someone found the courage to go home. [173] His daughter Enna carried their cups without contributing anything. [182] Mara sketched a broad figure surrounded by grateful faces, then stopped when Enna set a shallow specimen box beside her. [202] Inside lay a blue morpho wing, brought by a traveler who had stayed six weeks without paying. [219] "Father loved that story," Enna said. [225] "I washed the traveler's sheets." [230] Mara looked up, but Enna was already collecting another tray. [240]

The wing flashed blue in the turning sunlight, then became the brown of wet bark. [255] Under Mara's lens, its brilliance resolved into delicate ridges rather than the saturated pigment she had expected. [272] Changing the angle changed what the wing could give. [281]

She stayed three days, mapping the revolving windows by tracking sun trails across the floor with small chalk marks. [300] She also moved her stool into the kitchen, where Enna could speak without an audience expecting gratitude. [317] There had been a scholarship to study drawing downstream, then a winter of guests whose meals consumed the passage money. [337] There had been promises that next year would belong to her. [348] "He listened to everyone," Enna said, "except the person asking for the same small thing every day." [365]

Mara pressed her palm against her chest, ashamed of wanting its steady beat to settle the matter. [382] It was a heart, not a verdict. [389] She asked Enna to draw the kitchen as she remembered it, and asked his widow to sit beside them. [408] The conversation was quietly explosive, its longest silences broken by the familiar clink of cups waiting to be washed. [427] By evening, the widow had agreed that the hanging could contain more than praise, though she would not help embroider it. [448]

Mara abandoned her flat blue dye and ordered silk woven with blue warp and brown weft, borrowing the wing's iridescent caution. [469] Its warning would be simple: look again before deciding what you see. [481] Using her chalk map, she angled narrow pleats so the traveling sunlight would illuminate one embroidered scene, then another. [500] From the communal table, Iven opened his door against a storm, his great blue coat sheltering three strangers. [518] As the rock turned, brown folds emerged beneath the blue, revealing Enna behind him with a basin in her hands. [538] Neither scene erased the other; both occupied the same cloth. [548]

At the unveiling, the ferrymen smiled first, then shifted uneasily as the kitchen came into view. [564] One began another tale of Iven's kindness, but his widow interrupted to ask who would clear the table. [582] For once, Enna remained seated. [587] A ferryman gathered the cups, awkwardly stacking them in towers that made her laugh despite herself. [603]

Mara had thought a person magnetized stories by being extraordinary, or perhaps by carrying some rare generosity in the blood. [623] Now she wondered how many stories gathered around anyone willing to listen, and how many remained outside the circle of that listening. [645] Before leaving, she paid Enna for her drawing, separately from the commission, and gave her the address of a weaving school. [666] "Tell me what you make," she said, then corrected herself. [676] "Tell me whether you want to go." [683] Below them, mist loosened its pale threads from the orchards, and the floating house turned toward another light. [701]
