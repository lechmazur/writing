During the lull of distant thunder, Soren entered the village where a dropped apple had hung above the cobbles for seven years. [22] The court had exiled its chess tutor for denouncing a siege plan copied directly from his own lessons. [40] Sacrifice the outer pawns, he had always said, and preserve the king. [52] Then the prince had marked actual villages as expendable. [61] Soren had come hoping that saving this one might let him forgive himself. [74]

At the square's center, Mara sat before her loom, one hand suspended over the shuttle. [89] Once his most impatient pupil, she had become a weaver of protective cloth. [102] When the prince's soldiers approached, she had woven hush so completely that neither sound nor time could pass through it. [122] The soldiers were gone now; their prince was dead. [131] Her people remained inside the unfinished instant that had saved them. [142]

Only someone whose name was absent from the tapestry could move beneath its spell. [156] Mara had included the village dogs, but not the man who once taught her. [170] That omission hurt more than exile. [176]

At the loom's heart gleamed a platinum labyrinth, the courtly puzzle Soren had given her after her first victory over him. [197] Its untarnishing channels held the spell without decay, turning her emergency shelter into a prison. [212] Through those channels ran silver threads, each joining a villager to the central spindle. [226] One careless cut could free a body from its heartbeat. [236] He laid his knife carefully beside the loom. [244]

As always when frightened, he fidgeted productively, straightening bent pins and sorting fallen scraps by color. [260] His fingers noticed what his eyes had missed: the scraps repeated the border's alternating dark and pale stitches. [278] Mara had recorded possibilities in the language of their chess lessons. [289] He read her warnings by decoding frayed threads in the tapestry's border, counting knots as ranks and loose ends as files. [310] Each sequence described a route through the labyrinth. [318]

He began to winnow the routes, tracing their consequences with a pin instead of touching the living threads. [336] Most ended with one strand severed so the others could unwind. [347] A baker for twelve children; a grandmother for the square. [357] His old arithmetic, laid bare. [362] He pushed those scraps aside. [367] There would be no expendable pawn this time. [375]

The final sequence led out of the labyrinth altogether, into an empty loop beyond its rim. [391] Beside it, Mara had embroidered two hands. [398] One held the tension while the other released the spindle. [408] The first hand belonged outside the spell; the second had to wake within it. [422] Her solution required somebody to surrender control before knowing whether help would come. [435]

Soren looked at Mara's motionless face and remembered correcting her whenever she trusted an opponent's kindness. [451] Chess had taught him to think for both sides. [460] He had mistaken that for understanding people. [467]

He threaded the empty loop around his wrist and pulled. [477] The platinum channels tightened their cold geometry against his bones. [487] Across the square, the apple descended a finger's width. [496] Mara's eyelids stirred. [499] He pulled harder, fiercely still now, his whole body the weight that kept the threads from snapping. [516] Pain climbed his arm; the village drew a single breath. [526] He could not reach the spindle, and for once he did not try. [539]

Mara woke looking toward the road where soldiers no longer stood. [550] Then she saw his wrist, the sorted scraps, the knife he had refused to use. [565] "Hold," she said, and he obeyed his pupil. [573] She turned the spindle against its winding, feeding the silver strands back through the labyrinth until the loop fell slack. [593]

The apple struck stone. [597] A child began crying over a scraped knee seven years old. [608] Beyond the roofs, thunder finally answered thunder. [615]

Soren waited for relief to make him innocent, but his wrist hurt and the other villages were still ashes. [634] Mara pressed a clean scrap against his torn skin. [643] "You came," she said, neither pardoning nor accusing. [651]

He nearly explained how long he had searched for a safe solution. [663] Instead, he asked what needed doing. [669] She pointed to the shelter cloths, unfinished when the soldiers came. [680] The storm would reach them soon. [686]

Soren sat beside her, threaded a needle, and began joining the pieces. [698] He would not forgive himself by proving that he had never been cruel. [711] Perhaps he could begin by no longer leaving kindness to somebody else. [723]
