During the migration of the last monarch butterfly, Iona waited in a verdant grotto above the clouds with a clerk who carried a collecting case. [25] Dawn colored the limestone pink, and a blushing stillness lay over the ferns. [38] The butterfly clung to a marigold, opening its wings as slowly as someone reconsidering a letter. [54]

"We should preserve it," the clerk said. [61]

"It is still using itself," Iona answered. [68]

The academy called her an esoteric realist because she studied secret traditions but insisted that every interpretation survive contact with weather. [89] Her mother had kept this grotto warm with compost and planted its flowers in sequence, feeding generations of travelers. [108] Official histories credited the mountain's governors for maintaining the migration route. [119] They recorded neither the gardeners nor the orchards poisoned under those same governors. [132] Iona had brought the clerk here to amend that record, not furnish its final specimen. [147]

From her mother's notebook she lifted a moth wing, suspended on a loop of hair. [162] It had belonged to an ordinary brown moth found dead beside the nursery lantern. [176] Her mother used it to detect drafts too faint to stir a ribbon. [189] Now it turned toward the western cleft, away from the staircase marked on the clerk's map. [205] Beyond that cleft, sunlight touched a greenhouse which his map described as abandoned. [218] Iona could see flowers against its glass. [225]

"The wind has changed," she said, "and somebody is still tending the other bank." [239]

The gorge between them was narrow, but the butterfly needed a sheltered landing before the afternoon cold. [256] Its ancestors had followed a chain of gardens down to the winter forest. [269] Their keepers, like Iona herself, were transiently rooted, moving with the flowering season and leaving little that historians considered permanent. [289]

They communicated by building temporary structures as signals, using reeds that could be dismantled when a garden closed. [307] Iona remembered carrying bundles behind her mother, who hummed two notes, paused, then hummed another. [322] That interrupted cadence told her where to leave the gap between the arches. [335] It meant a traveler needed shelter; an evenly spaced row merely announced a garden open for trade. [352]

She gathered bundled reeds beside the nursery doorway and bent them into arches along the western ledge. [369] The clerk watched her place two together, leave an empty interval, and plant the third. [384]

"You have missed one," he said, reaching for a reed. [394]

She caught his wrist, more sharply than she intended. [403] For years she had filled omissions in ledgers, hunting names, treating every blank as an injury. [419] Her mother's silence between those notes had taught her something she was only now willing to apply to history. [438]

"Some gaps ask you to listen," she said, releasing him. [448]

A small gust loosened the last arch, and she struggled to knot its brittle binding without crushing the reeds. [467] The clerk unbuckled the strap from his collecting case and offered it without speaking. [481] Together they secured the arch, his fingers holding the curve while hers tightened the unfamiliar leather. [497]

They waited until pink light faded into gold. [505] Across the gorge, a door opened, and someone with white sleeves carried a pot outside. [520] Then a single low arch appeared around it: the old answer, shelter offered. [533]

Iona raised the moth wing once more. [540] It trembled toward the open door, tracing the safe current she could not see. [554] The monarch climbed from its flower and rested on her knuckle until the sunlight warmed its wings. [571] When it lifted, neither watcher moved. [577] Its crossing took less time than the clerk needed to set down his empty case. [592] It settled among the flowers beneath the answering arch, an orange fleck against the other keeper's sleeve. [609]

There would be no spring generation, and Iona did not mistake this passage for a rescue from extinction. [627] But the last journey had not ended inside a box. [637]

The clerk opened his register and asked for her mother's name. [648]

"Mara Venn," she told him, and watched him write it beside the dates of the governors. [664]

For the keeper across the gorge, he left a space, then added a question mark. [679] Tomorrow they would cross by the lower bridge and ask. [689]

She dismantled the arches before they left, since no traveler was waiting now. [702] Her mother's name remained. [706]
