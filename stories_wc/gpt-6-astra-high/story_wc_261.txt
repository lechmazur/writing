After the anesthesia wore off, I woke in the quiet tower at dawn, with a willow filament stitched into my wrist. [21] Below the window, the orchard stood so still that the last stars seemed to be falling without moving. [39] I had paid for the graft to learn to read the stories in scars, beginning with the burn my mother had hidden beneath her bracelet. [64] At her funeral I had lifted the bracelet and picked at that seam until the undertaker quietly took my hands. [84] "You keep calling it closure," my father said, "but all you do is open wounds." [99]

The surgeon had promised that a graft from his willow table would teach my nerves to listen. [116] Neither of us expected that I might hear the willow itself. [127] Thirst, said the living table beside my bed, its legs rooted through the floor. [141] Then pain came through my palm, followed by the remembered weight of a cup. [155] Across the tabletop, coffee rings overlapped like small eclipses. [164] By examining coffee rings, I discovered how a scar remembered: not in words, but in pressure, heat, and whatever trembled nearby. [185] I pressed each ring until the vibrations became voices. [194]

In the deepest burn, my mother was arguing with the surgeon. [205] "It belongs under the orchard," she said. [212] A cup struck wood; the table remembered boiling bitterness. [221] "Moving a miracle doesn't make it yours." [228]

The frosted hourglass stood on its pedestal across the room, waiting for the surgeon to turn it again. [246] A grain from the meteor that had fallen into our orchard rested inside it. [260] The tower had been built to house that grain, and people came from everywhere to have their injuries undone. [279] Before its removal, healing had followed the orchard's seasons; people waited through winter and rose from their sickbeds with the spring. [300]

It had erased my childhood fever; I had never asked where an undone injury went. [315] Beneath my fingers, the table showed me roots blistering with burns, trunks splitting along the lines of human fractures. [334] The miracle, displaced from soil into glass, still needed somewhere living to put the pain. [349] The glass had made mercy instant by denying the roots their season of repair. [363]

My mother had been the orchard keeper. [370] In another ring I found her last visit, her voice scraped thin by the sickness consuming her lungs. [388] The surgeon set the hourglass before her and offered to turn it. [400] "Not another tree," she said, and reached across the table. [410] The next words were his: "Let go, before that frostbite costs you a hand." [424] The scar had not been a secret about her death; it was evidence of what she had tried to save. [444]

I wrapped the hourglass in my blanket and lifted it from the pedestal. [457] Immediately the filament in my wrist began to ache. [466] Beneath my bed, a root tapped against a loose flagstone. [476] When I lifted it, damp air rose through a passage choked with fallen earth. [490] I had to tunnel through with a broken tile, dragging the bundled glass ahead of me. [506] Each handful opened my stitches a little wider, and each root I touched lent me another borrowed hurt. [524] At the tower's foundation, I found the hollow where the meteor had first settled. [538]

Returning it would mean waiting again, perhaps losing people who could not wait, and I understood why the surgeon had kept turning it. [561] But I could feel the roots dying beneath the tower, and soon there would be nothing left to borrow. [580] I laid the hourglass there and broke its neck against a stone. [592] The grain fell into the earth with less sound than a seed. [604]

For a moment every root spoke at once, and none of the voices was my mother's. [620] I wanted to demand her back. [626] Instead, I listened as the orchard took one long drink of morning. [638] By the time I climbed out, the table had unfolded a single leaf. [651]

I used the surgeon's needle to close my wrist without magic, leaving the willow where it was. [668] When my father came, I showed him the scar I would have. [680] I told him it would hurt, and asked him to sit with me. [693]
