Nera began to doubt her mother's death when the prison charged the treasury rent for her occupied cell. [18] Officially, the woman had died seven winters earlier. [26] A prism-minded archivist, Nera separated every fact into what the state said, what the accounts implied, and what she could bear to know. [49] Until that morning, she had mistaken the third category for mercy. [60]

At sentencing, the prison's spell erased every cell combination from human memory, making appeals a ceremony before doors nobody could open. [81] To find where forgotten combinations hide, she opened the maintenance manuscript instead of the register of deaths. [98] There, a manuscript margin doodle showed a doorway, two footsteps crossing in opposite directions, and a seed beneath a crooked sun. [119] Her mother, once the prison gardener, had drawn that sun on every childhood note. [133] She had left instructions where officials saw only untidy affection. [143]

The living locks had roots reaching a serpentine grotto in mist beneath the city. [157] Tonight the grotto would enter its annual reversal, when gravity inverts for an hour and the underground river climbs toward the streets. [179] The roots would fruit then, producing seeds of vigilance that preserved the combinations the spell made everyone forget. [197] Even a living lock needed to remember what kept it closed. [208] The maintenance orders required every harvest to be burned unopened. [218] Nera had countersigned those orders for seven years. [226]

Using the seal that authorized the burning, she ordered the gardeners to report upstairs for an audit. [243] By the time she descended, water was lifting from the pools in trembling columns, and she had forty minutes left. [263] She clipped her belt to the ceiling rail installed for gardeners and followed the roots past the empty harvesting baskets. [283] Only her mother's doodle explained the missing harvest instruction: the seeds became visible through threshold effects, in the hesitation between leaving and arriving. [306] She would have to reenter the root chamber repeatedly, catching a seed during each moment of divided attention. [324]

On her third crossing, a pod flashed beneath her hand, clandestinely luminous as an ember covered by ash. [342] She pinched it free before her thoughts settled, and a woman's name and six numbers warmed her palm. [360] Not her mother's. [363] Nera nearly dropped it back into the roots. [371] Then she remembered the second cup she still kept on the highest shelf, too painful to use and too necessary to discard. [393] Somebody had kept a cup for this woman, too. [402] She tucked the seed into her collar. [409]

The chamber held thousands more, and the rising river was already battering the grating that kept them from washing into the city's drains. [432] She finally understood the locked baskets, the burning orders, the seven years of her careful signature. [448] Forgetfulness was not the spell's triumph; it was work people like her performed each winter. [463]

Beside the baskets hung a gardener's wrench, its handle polished by hands that might once have packed her lunches. [482] She carried it to the grating, braced both feet against the rail, and began loosening bolts. [498] Opening the drain would scatter her mother's seed among thousands, perhaps beyond any search she could make. [515] Keeping it shut would mean choosing which strangers deserved another year behind a door. [529] For once, all her separate ways of seeing arrived at the same intolerable fact. [543]

The last bolt resisted until the reversal's warning bell sounded overhead. [554] Nera leaned her whole weight into the wrench, and the grating tore loose with a sound like a swallowed shout. [574] Water struck her sideways, carrying the harvest upward while her belt snapped taut against the rail. [590] Then gravity returned, leaving her hanging above the emptied channel with one seed burning against her throat. [607]

By dawn, the drains had scattered seeds across the streets, and people crossing doorways were discovering their hidden light. [626] In the public appeals hall, a woman showed Nera a seed bearing the name of her supposedly buried husband. [645] Nera showed her the numbers forming on her palm, then called the others over to compare names. [662] Her mother's name had not surfaced yet. [669] When the cell named by her own seed opened, Nera held the trembling woman inside as carefully as she would have held her mother. [693] Behind her, another lock clicked, and then another. [701] The sound was small enough to miss, if you had trained yourself not to listen. [716]
