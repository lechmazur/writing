During the faint rustle of turning seasons, Mara returned to the drum maker's cavern to ask how long mercy lasted. [20] Above her, roots threaded the limestone like the veins in the hands she could no longer persuade to tremble. [39] She was a deaf music teacher, accustomed to finding rhythm through floorboards, wrists, and the taut skins of drums. [58] Since the flood, she could still find rhythm, but nothing in her answered it. [72]

Her son had drowned with eleven others. [79] Afterward, the villagers had begged Orin to make something that would let them sleep. [93] Mara had been the first to sign the petition. [102]

Orin had taken a filament of hush from the pauses between three recorded lullabies and sewn it into a drum. [122] His tool was a broken phonograph needle, its jagged tip fine enough to catch what ordinary needles passed through. [141] When he struck the finished drum, grief loosened its teeth. [151] According to Mara's pupils, the whole valley became audibly muted, as though snow had fallen inside every bell. [169]

For Mara, the difference was not silence but indifference. [178] Yesterday, her youngest pupil had finally mastered a difficult rhythm, and she had praised him without feeling anything. [196] That frightened her only as a fact might: something was wrong, whether she could feel its wrongness or not. [215]

Orin sat beside the drum, surrounded by shells awaiting skins. [225] His daughter had died in the flood, too. [233] He turned his face toward Mara so she could read his lips. [245]

"It lasts until we unpick it." [251]

She held out her hand, but he covered the broken needle. [262]

"You remember what we asked it to take away." [271]

"I remember what I promised to teach." [278]

She set both palms against an unfinished drum, and after a while he placed his hands beside hers. [296] In their mirrored hush, she tapped the rhythm his daughter had invented for a skipping game. [312] Neither smiled, but his fingers completed it. [319] Then he uncovered the needle. [324]

He showed her the three records: the hush would release its stitches only if their music retraced its steps. [343] The phonograph had to turn backward, steadily, while someone lifted each stitch before the next pause arrived. [360] Each missed pause would tighten the remaining stitches, drawing the released numbness back into the people it had left. [379]

Orin fitted a wooden strut between the phonograph's soundbox and the workbench, the same arrangement Mara used for lessons. [398] With one wrist on the wood, she could follow the backward recorded songs as shivers gathering into sudden stillness. [417] He turned the platter by hand while she worked the jagged needle beneath the first silver stitch. [434]

The thread resisted, then slid free. [440]

A pain opened beneath her ribs, so immediate that she almost drove the needle back through the skin. [458] She saw her son's wet sleeve caught on a branch. [468] Orin's hand faltered; the vibrations dragged. [474] His mouth shaped his daughter's name. [480]

Mara could not comfort him without losing the next pause. [490] She needed to enthrall him, not with beauty, but with the simple insistence of a beat he could follow. [509] With her heel, she began his daughter's skipping rhythm against the cavern floor. [522] He caught the pulse and steadied the platter. [530]

Through the second record, Mara remembered the weight of her son's body, and through the third, his infuriating, crooked grin. [550] Neither memory canceled the other. [555]

When the last stitch came loose, the filament lay across her palms, bright and harmless as a strand of cobweb. [575] Orin reached toward it, and she understood that he wanted enough for just one more night. [591] She wanted that, too. [595]

Instead, she carried the filament to the stove, and he opened the iron door. [609] It burned without a visible flame. [615]

They sat until the light at the cavern mouth turned blue, their hands resting on the same unfinished drum. [634] Mara still heard nothing when the valley bells began to ring. [645] But she felt Orin's trembling in the wooden rim, and answered with her own unsteady pulse. [661]

At tomorrow's lesson, there would be a child expecting her to notice what he had learned. [677] For the first time in months, she wanted to be there. [688]
