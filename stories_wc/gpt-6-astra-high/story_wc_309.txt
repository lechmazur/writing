When creativity gets automated, the guild keeps its underachieving mages to change the lamps. [14] Iven accepts the work because his one reliable spell, lingering luminance, can coax a little brightness from something after its fire has died. [37] He cannot conjure a dragon, but he can make a cold wick glow long enough to thread a needle. [56] The examiners call this insufficient. [61]

His mother called it useful. [66] Under her garden arch, she mended stockings by the light he saved from sunset, finding something holy in getting one more heel repaired. [89] After her death, he sold the arch's keystone to pay for burial. [101] Now, twelve years later, he wants it back. [109]

The receipt identifies it as a garden folly keystone and adds, in his mother's handwriting, "Accommodates impossibilities." [126] She built the arch without foundations, with one pillar shorter than the other; the stone persuaded everything to stand. [145] That modest contradiction became the heart of an engine that could design palaces larger inside than out. [162] Now newer engines dream entire cities without needing stones, and the old workshop, an icebound spire above the clouds, is scheduled for demolition at sunset. [187]

The salvage clerk lets Iven have the keystone instead of wages. [198] "All personnel evacuated," she says, stamping his permit without looking up. [209] The bridge has already been dismantled. [215] He must use mist walking, that embarrassing elementary art which requires patience rather than power. [230]

At the cliff edge, he waits for the spire's exhaust to mingle with the clouds. [245] Then he preserves the sunset caught inside it, making a pale path through the brilliant dimness. [261] He has rehearsed with bricks strapped to his back; the mist will carry him and the stone, provided he never hurries. [282] Below, automated theaters paint golden heavens across the city roofs, but he watches each small patch of light beneath his feet. [303]

Inside the spire, white frost coats the silent engine. [312] He finds the keystone behind a brass hatch, still bearing the uneven chisel marks his mother made. [329] When he lifts it into his carrying harness, the impossible chambers fold themselves into ordinary rooms. [345] A cupboard becomes a staircase, and someone coughs behind its door. [356]

Mara, the guild's oldest drafter, clutches a portfolio against her broken leg. [368] She stayed to retrieve her own first drawings from the design office; then the stairs changed, throwing her down. [387] "I thought there was time," she says. [394]

The demolition bell starts its final sequence; he has broken her leg to retrieve a stone. [410] Iven carries her to the threshold, her portfolio scraping the frost from the walls. [424] Outside, his path is fading as the exhaust thins. [433] He steps onto it with Mara against his chest and the stone against his spine. [448] The mist dips; light breaks around his ankles. [456] He draws back. [459]

Mara holds out her portfolio. [464] "Leave these." [466]

He thinks of his mother beneath an arch that existed only to frame a patch of beans. [483] He has come looking for holiness in the thing she built, as though stone could preserve the way she sat beside him. [505] But he knows the weight of the portfolio will not be enough to save them. [520]

He kneels to unstrap the keystone and leaves it on the threshold. [532] She watches without speaking, then clasps her drawings under one arm and her other arm around his neck. [550] He lifts Mara again, and she counts his steps aloud to keep him from hurrying. [565] Together they follow what remains of the evening, neither bright enough to dazzle nor dark enough to hide the way. [585]

At the cliff, they turn to see the spire fall soundlessly into cloud before the thunder reaches them. [603] The salvage clerk brings blankets and sends for a healer. [613] Iven keeps a final scrap of luminance alive while Mara checks her rescued pages for damp. [629] One drawing shows a kitchen chair with a crooked leg, carefully observed.
