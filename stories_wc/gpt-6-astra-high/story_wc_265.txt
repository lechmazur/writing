After my sixth sense awakened, I began waking beneath trees that had stood miles from my bed. [17] The engineers called it translocation, which explained nothing, although weighing me before and afterward reassured them that no philosopher was being lost in transit. [41] I was less certain. [45]

I had entered the mechanical rainforest to rediscover the wild frontier of my mind, having exhausted the respectable occupation of predicting what I would think next. [71] Now its gardeners called me the stray philosopher and left oilskin blankets wherever I tended to appear. [88] Unfortunately, there were only three such places. [95]

Every sleep carried me between a copper palm, a grove of steel birches, and one immense brass cedar. [113] The forest was supposed to transport sleepers toward possibilities they had never considered, not shuffle them through a cupboard of familiar thoughts. [135] I knew because I had designed it. [142]

Before I left home, my daughter Ada had pressed a small gear into my hand. [157] "Try making something that doesn't already know its ending," she said. [168] She had cut the gear from a standard blank when she was eight, filing one tooth too short. [186] Installed in our first sapling, it had made the branches dance until one struck her wrist. [202] I had replaced it, then spent thirty years eliminating every comparable irregularity from the forest. [217] Ada had long since stopped asking me to let her build things. [229]

On the nineteenth morning beneath the palm, my new sense stirred: the hum of insight, a vibration behind the teeth when scattered facts approach an explanation. [255] It came when the wind turned. [261] The palm's copper fronds clicked like cooling crockery, but beneath their chatter lay three evenly spaced notes. [278]

I listened again among the birches after my next sleep, and once more inside the cedar's cavernous shade. [296] Steel leaves whispered; brass needles moaned; the same three notes kept time beneath both voices. [311] Wind should have played these different trees differently. [319] Instead, something was correcting it. [324]

To unravel the correction, I walked against the rhythm, listening for the tiny delay between each tree's own sound and the imposed refrain. [347] Where the delay shortened, I turned; where it lengthened, I doubled back through warm rain condensed on overhead pipes. [366] By dusk I reached a root chamber whose door still bore my signature. [379]

Inside, the master governor ticked with the immaculate patience I had once mistaken for kindness. [394] Its workings translated gusts into branch movements, branch movements into possible paths, and possible paths into destinations for sleeping bodies. [414] My corrections had narrowed that abundance to three approved outcomes, each inspected by an older version of myself and declared safe. [435]

I took Ada's gear from my pocket and held it beside the governor's timing wheel. [450] The diameter matched, as it should: I had given her the blank myself. [463] That shortened tooth would occasionally miss its catch, letting a gust enter the calculations before the mechanism could smooth it away. [484] It might also tear a branch loose, or deliver a sleeper somewhere no gardener had thought to leave a blanket. [504]

For an hour I checked the independent storm brakes, the landing clearances, the emergency waking circuit. [520] Caution was not the enemy, I discovered; making it the only voice was. [533] Then I exchanged the wheels. [538]

Nothing happened until a squall moved through the canopy and the little gear missed its first beat. [555] Above me, the cedar faltered, answered the birches, and produced a chord I had never written into anything. [573] The hum of insight deepened until I could feel it in my palms. [586] I thought of Ada's injured wrist, but this time another memory followed: her good hand reaching immediately for the fallen tools. [607] I had always stopped remembering just before that part. [616]

Sleep found me on the chamber floor, with the discarded perfect wheel cradled uselessly in my hand. [633] I woke on a workbench in Ada's workshop at the forest's edge, knocking a tray of washers into her tea. [653] She looked from me to the wheel and began to laugh. [664]

"An unlikely destination," I said, climbing down. [671] "Not impossible," she replied. [675]

Beyond her windows, unfamiliar music traveled through the trees, and for once I did not try to calculate how long it would last. [698] I asked what she was making, and waited to be told. [709]
