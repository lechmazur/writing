After remembering becomes painful, the survivors climb to the labyrinthine bookstore occupying an entire mountain, where a mercury-glass seer promises them sleep without dreams. [24] Mera substitutes reality nightly: shelves become familiar kitchens, reading lamps become hearths, and buried children knock the mud from their shoes before coming inside. [48] By morning, everyone knows the children were made of light, but knowing is easier than remembering. [64] Customers navigate by genre and altitude, passing through valleys of atlases toward the summit's narrow shelves of prayer books. [83]

Payment is a memory, pressed between pages until its owner can no longer feel it. [98] At first Mera takes only the worst ones. [106] Then she starts accepting lullabies, recipes, the weight of a sleeping head. [118] She wants to understand the shape of home, and believes enough preserved details will finally describe it. [135] Her own mother's face remains unfinished in every window. [144] Since the collapse, she has collected a thousand descriptions of tenderness without discovering how to make that face look at her. [165]

One evening, a boy named Tavi refuses the kitchen she has prepared. [177] His mother is there, kneading bread, but the flour never sticks to her hands. [191] He asks for a door that goes somewhere she has not invented. [203] Behind him, something rolls through the shelves, a thunder too deep for weather. [216] Dust drifts from a ceiling no one has ever seen; somewhere above the histories, a whole aisle slams shut. [235] Each borrowed room has displaced a real passage; now the mountain has nowhere left to yield. [251] The staircase down opens onto Mera's mother's kitchen. [259] There is no mother in it, only the smell of onions burning. [271]

Before she learned to reflect other people's wishes, Mera could translate the language in which the mountain's first books were written. [292] Those books were not stories but instructions: their words determined where stone ended and a doorway began. [309] She buried that talent with the lost language stone when the quarry collapse killed her mother. [325] Her last translation had opened the quarry. [332] She has spent years calling herself a seer because nobody expects a mirror to be responsible for what it shows. [352]

Now she must kneel beneath her own counter and dig. [362] With the stone comes a feather, wrapped in the apron her mother wore to teach her. [378] The stone's script lies below the reach of sight, its grooves too fine for even her glass fingertips. [396] But the feather, dragged across it, lifts its barbs into patterns she can read by touch. [412] She used to have a gift for this, before she decided that making beautiful reflections was safer than understanding anything. [432] Tonight, every stroke brings back her mother's wrist guiding hers, and the pain nearly makes her drop the feather. [451] Tavi steadies her elbow, not as if she is an oracle, but as if she is someone holding something heavy. [471]

The first word means threshold. [476] Its branching grammar describes an opening that cannot be owned by either side. [489] She understands what it will do to rooms whose doors lead only to other rooms, and to the dead who wait inside them. [512] Tavi asks whether leaving will hurt, and she answers him without looking into her own bright palms. [529] "Yes," she says. [532]

Mera carries the feather to the book where she imprisoned her mother's death, and begins to seed the margins with the recovered word. [555] The word sends pale roots through the paper, crossing into neighboring volumes along the stitching that binds their spines. [574] Each letter releases something she has kept outside herself: falling stone, a scream, her own terrible luck in surviving. [593] No pardon arrives with the memory. [599] But a draft does. [603] All around them, people cry out as locked pages open and the weight of their histories returns. [620] Tavi takes her hand as the kitchens flicker out, and together they follow the cold through shelves that settle like unclenched teeth. [642]

At the mountain's open door, the others gather without their borrowed dead. [654] Mera cannot offer them home, not yet. [661] She can offer a dry floor, a place to cook, and an exit nobody has to earn. [678] Tavi goes outside, returns with kindling, and asks where they should put the fire. [692]
