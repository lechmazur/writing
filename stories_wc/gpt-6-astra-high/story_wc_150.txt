When Leila returned from Toronto to excavate the founder's tomb, the caretaker asked whether she had brought her father's bones. [20] She had brought surveying instruments instead, and the shame of leaving him beneath a cemetery lawn an ocean away. [39] Their faith promised rest only in native earth, or so the exile congregation had taught her. [55] She wanted the founder, who had written their first prayers, to tell her they were wrong, so that prayer might become possible again. [78]

Outside the tomb stood a ruined sundial base, beneath a shaft that admitted one bright hour of sun. [96] Around it spread the catacomb orchard, where tree roots snaked between bones and figs ripened overhead. [112] Rain had packed the tomb entrance with soil; now its fractured arch threatened to bring down three fruiting trees. [131] If Leila could excavate and rebuild it, the caretaker would let her study whatever inscriptions survived inside. [148]

On the first morning, he offered her an axe. [157] The roots, he said, were in their way. [165] Leila knelt beside one thick as her wrist and felt it trembling, perhaps with wind from the branches far above. [185] Cutting it might release the stones it held together. [194] She asked where the orchard drank, and where its fallen leaves went, and whether his grandmother had ever mentioned another door. [215] Her warmly curious manner unsettled him more than an argument would have; by afternoon, he was telling her everything. [234]

For three days she worked through purposeful wandering, following roots along burial galleries, mapping their turns, scraping test pits where rubble thinned. [256] Each passage ended abruptly: at a landslip, a wall, a stair whose upper steps had vanished in the war. [275] She knew such journeys: her father's canceled passage home, her mother's funeral watched on a borrowed screen. [292] At every obstruction she marked a possible way around, refusing to confuse interruption with arrival. [307]

On the fourth morning she noticed small footprints carved around the sundial base, each trail stopping before the rim. [326] A channel skirted the base, sloping northward toward a corridor she had mapped but not yet fully explored. [344] The caretaker remembered water running there before the vault fell. [354] Following that dry route, they uncovered a narrow maintenance entrance behind the tomb. [367] Leila shored its lintel with timber and removed the packed earth one basket at a time, leaving every major root intact. [388] By evening they could crawl inside. [394]

There was no sarcophagus. [398] Instead, empty niches lined the walls, each inscribed with a name and a distant place. [413] The founder's own niche named a port where plague had stranded him. [425] Under it, Leila brushed dirt from a prayer: "Give shelter here to those whose roads were broken; their bodies need not follow." [447] She read it twice, then sat down among the baskets. [457] She had mistaken her father's longing for a commandment, and the exile congregation, afraid of losing its children, had done the same. [479]

The caretaker touched an unnamed niche and said his son had drowned beyond the strait. [494] "I thought this room would be full," he said. [503] "Perhaps it is," Leila answered, and a breeze moved between them, smelling faintly of figs and rain. [520]

Over the next two days they rebuilt the arch with space for the living roots, using old stones wherever their edges still fitted. [543] Leila refused to polish the broken threshold smooth. [551] An interruption ought to remain visible, she thought, even after a way through had been found. [567] With the caretaker's help, she set a new bronze pointer into the sundial base and cleared its buried rim. [586] At noon, the returning shadow crossed the little unfinished footprints and carried them, for a moment, to the edge. [605] It might have been the carver's intention; it might have been only geometry. [618]

Leila wrote her father's name in an empty niche, leaving room beneath it for the name of the place that had sheltered him. [641] Then she wrote the caretaker's son's name where his hand had rested. [653] Above them, someone called down to ask whether it was safe to bring the children to gather figs. [671] Leila climbed toward the voice, carrying her measuring cord. [680] She did not know whether the dead had come home, but she had work to do for the living before the light moved on. [704]
