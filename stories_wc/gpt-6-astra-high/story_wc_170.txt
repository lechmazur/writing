Nera, the elusive mirage painter, arrives on the echoing terrace with sunset caught in the patches of her coat. [19] Spring rain has emptied the square below, leaving its apprenticeship mirrors silver beneath the waiting bells. [35] Over the hush before bells ring, she intends to persuade mirrors to show possibilities instead of commands. [52]

At dusk, each thirteen year old must accept the single future displayed beside their name. [67] The city calls this certainty; Nera, who followed her own reflection into a marriage without kindness, calls it obedience. [86] The last time she painted a second future for someone, the watchmen burned her brushes and posted her name at every gate. [109]

Tonight the registrar has summoned her through coded lullabies in children's songs, the only messages Nera answers. [126] Their altered choruses carry a language the mirrors knew before officials taught them to speak in verdicts. [143]

Beside the registrar stands her son, Ivo, clutching an apprenticeship order marked for the quarries. [158] His reflection below shows a grown man coughing red into a rag. [170] His mother has signed hundreds of such orders, but this one bears only a damp thumbprint where her signature should be. [191] Refusing a mirror's judgment means losing her office, and without its wages she cannot feed him. [207] But she has watched him bury stolen apple pips in pavement cracks, tending them with water saved from washing. [226]

"Show him a garden," she says, holding out a packet of seeds confiscated from his schoolbag. [242] "I will pay whatever you ask." [248]

Nera studies the woman with a perceptively calm gaze, noticing the ink stains worn deeper than her wedding ring. [267] "Not your money," she says. [272] "His heartbeat, borrowed until the bells." [278]

Only living rhythm, divided into color by the prism parapet after rain, can grace these dead mirrors with something they have never contained: doubt. [302]

Ivo puts his wrist in her hand before his mother can forbid him. [315] "Will I wake up different?" [320]

"You will wake up unfinished," Nera says, and waits for his nod. [332]

Then she draws one beat through his skin, a trembling red bead, and his next breath stops halfway. [350] His mother catches him as Nera sets the borrowed heartbeat against the wet glass. [364] Crimson scatters into violet, blue, and gold; she gathers the colors with two fingers and begins to paint. [382] Below them, the quarry darkens into tilled earth, and the mirrored Ivo kneels among beans. [397]

"There," his mother whispers, reaching toward the registrar's seal at her belt. [409]

Nera almost lets her use it. [415] A garden is kinder than a quarry, and she has spent years wishing someone had exchanged her own cruel certainty for a gentler one. [439] But the reflected beans stand motionless in their rows, each leaf identical, each blossom closed. [454] She would be painting another order, with prettier chains. [463]

The heartbeat falters against the parapet; across the square, the bell keeper lifts his mallet. [478]

"Sing the broken chorus," Nera tells the registrar. [486] "Do not give it its ending." [492]

The woman looks from her still child to the perfect garden, and her mouth tightens. [507] Then she sings the unfinished lullaby, letting its coded pauses ask if, unless, and otherwise. [522] Nera paints into those openings: Ivo planting after frost, Ivo losing a harvest, Ivo nursing someone whose face has not yet formed. [544] No life stays long enough to become a sentence. [553] As the mother's voice strengthens, neighboring mirrors catch the colors, and other children's appointed futures begin to move. [571]

Nera scoops the fading bead from the parapet and presses it beneath Ivo's ribs. [585] He gasps as the first bell strikes, scattering the painted light across the square. [599]

He asks, "Which one comes true?" [605]

His mother tears up the quarry order, then gently puts the seeds back into his palm. [621] "We have some work to do before we know." [630]

When the watchmen enter the terrace, Nera does not vanish as she usually does. [644] She stays beside the registrar, ready to give her name, while their explanations mingle with the bells in a dusky harmony. [665]
