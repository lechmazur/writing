At the fog-bound river crossing, the gloom-hill coachman laid his daughter's traveling blanket across their knees and watched the water whiten. [21] Behind them, his horse nosed the tollhouse wall, finding salt in the mortar. [34] Ahead lay the harbor school, where Mara would learn to pilot rescue boats. [47]

Iven had brought mourners down from Gloom Hill for thirty years, and he knew how little courage could purchase from water. [68] Mara's mother had drowned while carrying medicine to the islands. [78] Fishermen had recovered her satchel, strapped to a floating oar, but had not found her until morning. [95] Mara remembered that the medicine had arrived; Iven remembered that her mother had not. [109] Neither memory could persuade the other. [115]

In the blanket's corner, three generations had stitched promises over promises until the wool puckered around a little thicket of thread. [136] His grandmother called it a palimpsest promise: new words borrowed strength from those they covered, but never quite erased them. [156] After the funeral, Iven had sewn KEEP YOU SAFE over his wife's BRING YOU WARMTH. [171] Mara had slept beneath both. [176]

Now she ran a thumbnail along the stiff embroidery without looking at him. [189] "You could still drive the hill route with me," he said. [200]

"And count everyone I might have reached," she answered. [209]

She had shown him the school's requirements: swimming, weather charts, a year beside an experienced pilot before a command of her own. [231] He had looked only at the address across the river. [241]

Rain drummed on the tollhouse roof. [247] The ferry bell sounded somewhere beyond sight, then fell silent. [257] Iven felt relief before shame. [262] He had always wanted to distill complexity into essence: a journey into three landmarks, a restless horse into one loosened rein, fatherhood into a rule. [287] Safe had seemed a sufficient word. [293]

"We wait until rain gutters stop ticking," Mara said, repeating the ferryman's instruction from her last visit. [310] "Then we can hear his signal properly." [317]

Iven almost told her what the river sounded like. [326] Instead, he listened. [329]

Rainwater began to percolate through the gravel beneath the shelter, carrying grit toward the landing in delicate branching streams. [348] Through ambient awareness, rather than any single clear sound, he gradually separated the crossing from the weather. [365] The horse's steady breathing marked the land behind him; the ferry chain's low hum marked the channel ahead. [383] Beside him, Mara was closely attentive, her head turning toward each change, her hands quiet in her lap. [401] She was not imagining herself invulnerable. [407] She was doing what he did on the steep road when a wheel began to slip: noticing enough to choose. [427]

"Your mother hated the water," he said. [434]

"I know," Mara answered, "and I don't want anyone else to have to cross it the way she did." [453]

At last the gutters ceased, leaving a hush full of small, distinguishable motions. [466] The bell rang twice: the ferry was secured. [474]

Mara rose, but the blanket caught on a nail and opened along a seam. [488] Iven reached automatically for the yarn needle he kept in his hatband, threaded with wool for roadside repairs. [506] "Let me," he said, and saw her shoulders tighten. [515] So he put the needle in her palm instead. [524]

She closed the tear with the neat, loose stitches he had taught her, leaving enough give for the fabric to move. [545] The ferryman had coal sacks to load, so Iven asked for a few more minutes and bent over the old promise. [566] He stitched A PLACE above SAFE, letting the blue letters cross the faded gray. [580] He could not stitch away the current, or the weather, or all the mornings when her chair would stand empty. [600] The old vow still showed beneath the new one, as it always would. [613]

"Not permission," Mara said, studying his work. [620]

"No," he said, "a room, if you want it." [629]

She wrapped the blanket around her shoulders and stepped onto the ferry. [641] There was a spindrift serenity to her departure: spray lifting, ropes settling, a hand raised without urgency. [658] Iven turned his horse toward Gloom Hill, thinking of clean sheets rather than storms. [672]
