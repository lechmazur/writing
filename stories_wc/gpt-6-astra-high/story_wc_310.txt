After the universe contracted, the observatory remained inside its last fold of space, an amber bubble surrounding eleven sleeping people and Iona. [21] Beyond its ambered observatory deck, the stars were packed into a darkness too dense to shine. [37] The deck's fossil resin had once filtered sunlight; now it insulated their fragment of yesterday against the pressure of everything else. [58] It was thinning. [61]

Iona, the appointed Lotkeeper, decided whose sleeping chamber received heat when the batteries faltered. [75] She cast lots with a collection of foreign coins, each stamped by a country that had vanished long before its sun. [96] Chance was the only authority the survivors trusted with scarcity after their captains spent the evacuation saving friends. [114] Iona obeyed it too, although she remembered every face beneath every losing coin. [127]

She was quietly enthusiastic about machinery, which made fewer claims than captains. [139] When the receiver began coughing static bursts, she greeted the fault with an almost imperceptible smile. [155] Something, at least, was still happening. [161]

The observatory's identical resonators flattened the bursts into noise, so she substituted coins, whose different sizes and alloys isolated different bands. [182] Night after artificial night, she learned to distill voices from the interference, understanding static bursts as syllables compressed between collapsing wavelengths. [203] She had trained to find a signal that would tell humanity what the heavens wanted. [218] Even now, she wanted to align her own small wavelength with that purpose. [231]

The first intelligible sound was children laughing, then arguing about the color of their sun. [246] Next came a landing announcement, then the groan of a hull cooling after launch. [260] Last came her own voice saying, "The deck, not the beacon." [271] The intervals shrank as the voices approached her present. [280]

A backwards prophecy: consequences arriving first, their causes following like footprints uncovered from the destination homeward. [296] Or merely another dead universe's recording, squeezed through the contraction. [306] There was no instrument left that could distinguish hope from an echo. [318]

She woke the sleepers because neither interpretation gave her permission to choose alone. [331] The beacon, she explained, could broadcast their coordinates until the batteries died; that was their approved rescue plan. [349] But there was nobody left outside to receive them. [358] Detaching the amber deck would give them a sealed raft, powered by the batteries reserved for that final transmission. [377] A smaller volume meant less strain on the thinning resin, perhaps enough to survive until space expanded again. [395] It might also crack immediately. [400]

Tomas, whose daughter occupied the coldest chamber, asked if the voices promised their survival. [414]

"They sounded like children." [418]

"That wasn't what I asked." [423]

She laid the coins between them, ashamed of how badly she wanted a commandment. [437]

Tomas picked up the pierced brass coin that had condemned his daughter's chamber to three cold nights. [454] "You let this decide because none of us could bear being responsible." [466]

Iona almost said that fairness mattered, but the coin was trembling in Tomas's hand. [480]

Instead she played the transmission without filtering it, so they could hear how much interpretation stood between noise and promise. [500] Then she asked each person whether to launch, recording every answer alongside her own, including the three refusals. [518] Their charter required nine votes; she had nine, but the number offered less comfort than a unanimous prophecy would have. [538]

Even the dissenters helped her seal the access shafts, their anger steadier than her faith. [553] She pocketed the foreign coins and led the others beneath the deck's thickest remaining window. [568] They had belonged to strangers who nevertheless had built currencies, harbors, places to arrive. [582] She would preserve them for children whose existence she could not prove. [594]

As she cut the beacon cable, the receiver whispered her breathing back at her. [608] Perhaps she was obeying a future already finished. [616] Perhaps the voice existed only because she was finally speaking. [626] "The deck, not the beacon," she said, and pulled the release. [637]

Amber cracked around the clamps, then held. [644] Beyond the glass, darkness loosened by a shade. [652] Iona tuned the raft's weak transmitter to the band that had carried the children's laughter, not because the universe had assigned her a purpose, but because someone might need a light to steer toward. [686] Beside her, Tomas began warming his daughter's hands. [694] She listened, quietly eager, for an answer that was not her own. [706]
