After the lake freezes over, the basalt sanctuary becomes a storehouse, though the governor's maps still mark it as abandoned. [20] Ena knows because she draws those maps, a vigilant horizon mapper paid to record everything visible from the viaduct. [39] She has begun leaving things out. [45]

Below her window, the magnetic viaduct shines after rain, its suspended bread cars humming across black ice toward the governor's granaries. [66] He owns the only bridge, and therefore names the price of hunger. [78] Rain has washed away the snow that might have concealed a crossing; anyone walking the lake would now resemble ink on glass. [100] The viaduct's field also drags compass needles toward its pillars. [110]

"Then we take the bridge," says Tovan, her younger brother, laying their father's bolt cutters on the sanctuary's altar. [129] Their father tried that, nine winters ago. [136] The governor stopped the cars halfway across and left the strikers hanging there until cold persuaded the survivors to surrender. [156] Ena remembers her father's hands afterward, wrapped in linen, and how he could no longer button her coat. [174]

"We take away his bridge," she says, "without touching it." [184] Tovan calls that cowardice, but stays to mend the sanctuary's roof. [195] His anger needs somewhere to put its hands. [203]

Ena's task is to sculpt patience from impatience, beginning with her own. [215] Each evening she wants to finish a map that refuses to be finished. [228] Instead, she measures ice with an auger and maps the ridge's moving shadows by tracking moon paths. [245] As the moon moves, a towering basalt tooth casts a corridor of darkness across the lake. [261] Within that traveling darkness, a sled could cross unseen; outside it, the governor's watchmen would have a target. [279] No single safe route exists, only a route for each particular hour. [291]

For thirteen nights, Tovan hauls sacks of stones behind her, rehearsing the weight of flour. [306] On the fourteenth, the ice booms beneath them, a sound like an enormous animal remembering its teeth. [323] He runs, and she catches his sleeve before he leaves the shadow. [335] They kneel together until the sound passes. [342] Then she drills again, finds the ice sound, and marks the place for tomorrow's inspection. [357]

Her care becomes contagious, though nobody calls it courage. [366] Miners bring handwarmers, seamstresses stitch black covers for sleds, and the sanctuary's old bellringer learns to read her moving lines. [386] Across the lake, farmers accept tools in exchange for grain, glad to escape the governor's toll. [402] The first returning load smells of rye and horses. [411] Tovan presses his face into a sack, laughing without sound. [421]

By the sixth week, flour fills the sanctuary's empty niches, and six crews can navigate without Ena. [438] She erases nothing from her newest map; instead, she copies it until every crew has one. [454] A patient revolution cannot depend on one pair of eyes staying open. [466]

On the appointed morning, the miners unyoke themselves from the ore sledges they haul to the loading platform. [484] They do not smash the rails. [490] They walk home carrying bread. [495]

The governor stops his cars, as he stopped them nine winters before, and waits for hunger to do his work. [515] Ena waits too, but hers is a different waiting: weighing portions, testing ice, teaching children the angles of the moon. [535]

By the fourth day, guards discover the sanctuary, but Ena has already divided the flour among thirty cellars. [553] They seize the empty sacks and leave behind two deserters. [563]

Tovan wants to ring the sanctuary bell until it cracks. [573] Instead, he carries a loaf to the platform guard who once watched their father freeze. [588]

By the tenth day, ore deliveries have stopped, the governor's creditors are arriving, and his guards are eating the miners' bread. [609] He offers cheaper flour. [613] The assembly asks for the bridge keys. [620]

When he surrenders the keys, Tovan's hands are softly purposeful on the bell rope. [634] Its voice travels over the frozen lake, neither tame nor triumphant, while Ena watches the horizon for weather that could still kill them. [657] Freedom, she has learned, does not excuse anyone from looking. [667]
