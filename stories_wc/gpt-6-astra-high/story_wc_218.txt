After the physician gave her six months to live, Mara returned to her island and canceled the order for whitewash. [20] The temple's living stone pillars were shedding black grit into the orchard, and the council wanted their sickness painted over before the summer pilgrims arrived. [45] She had been island caretaker for forty years, long enough to know that cleanliness was sometimes just a tidy form of lying. [67] Now, apparently, she had time to object. [74]

In her grandmother's botanical diary, between pear grafts and apple blight, she found the temple's architectural secrets. [91] The pillars were animals, rooted beneath the island and capable of moving their crowns. [105] The builders had fitted iron collars around them, then loaded those collars with the stone channels that watered the orchard. [125] One marginal note explained the silence: Never answer them, or they will ask to leave. [140] Beneath it, her grandmother had nevertheless drawn the apparatus for a conversation. [152]

Mara recognized the old woman's cowardice with an intimacy that hurt. [163] She, too, had spent her life keeping the channels level. [173]

She began with copper wire wrapped around oak branches, following diagrams she had always mistaken for ornamental pruning instructions. [192] The branches served as spring levers; wires stretched from them to the collars, carrying changes of pressure into the stone. [212] The diary supplied a vocabulary of tugs: water, weight, pain, yes, no. [224] Her work was gently systematic: loose coils to spare the bark, one question, then nine days of waiting without repeating herself. [245]

On the tenth morning, a branch bowed into her palm. [255] Pain, said the pillar. [259] She laid her forehead against it, but did not mistake the gesture for help. [273] When she asked permission to remove a collar, the answer came before dusk. [286] Yes. [287]

The council chairman brought a basket of last year's shriveled pears, reminders of what one broken channel could cost. [306] "You will empty our cisterns trying to be kind," he said. [317]

"Then we shall be competent as well," Mara replied, and unrolled her drawings across the altar. [333] She proposed to remodel the orchard's supports, not demolish its waterworks. [344] Timber gantries would carry the channels independently, leaving space for the pillars to move. [358] Her burial money would buy the timber. [365] She had wanted a handsome tomb; apparently she had been confused about which creature needed housing. [381]

The chairman, who had once built boats, checked every joint she had drawn before calling for volunteers. [398]

Through spring, Mara taught the workers to ask before putting ladders against the pillars, and to accept a refusal without tugging harder. [420] Some laughed until a freed pillar declined a scaffold, then bent its crown to shelter them during a squall. [439] Respect, Mara told them, was not a reward for usefulness, though she admitted that dry socks helped people learn. [458]

In the fifth month, when lifting a cup exhausted her, the last collar came loose. [473] The smallest pillar turned toward the sea, dragging its buried body through the soil with the terrible patience of a glacier. [494] Under its lifted base lay a clutch of black pebbles, each unfolding minute stone legs. [509] Mara thought of the grit she had swept into the furnace every autumn. [522]

"We did not know," the chairman said, too quickly. [531]

"We know now," she said, and asked him to fetch a shallow tray. [544]

He set the tray beside the young and waited until they climbed aboard. [557] Their parent took three days to reach the shore; nobody rang the work bell to hurry it. [574] On the fourth afternoon, the chairman wheeled Mara down in her garden chair to watch the small stones enter the surf. [595] Behind them, water still ran through the orchard, and the remaining pillars held up nothing they had not agreed to hold. [616]

She asked for the diary and wrote a new instruction beneath her grandmother's warning, her letters smaller than they had been in winter. [639] Ask again next season; permission is not an inheritance. [648]

Then she gave the book to the chairman, who had volunteered to keep the island until another caretaker could be found. [669] He wanted to know what would happen if the remaining pillars left as well. [683]

"Then we shall have an orchard," Mara said, "and perhaps some visitors who come willingly." [698]
