Milo, the blithe cemetery gardener, whistled while thinning daisies, convinced that sorrow, like bindweed, flourished only when people let it. [20] Then Ada died, and among her tenderly organized belongings he found an antique sovereign case containing two coins and a folded railway ticket. [43] The ticket was for the coast, dated a week beyond the fever that had killed her. [59] He had never heard her mention going. [66] For three days he weeded her grave so fiercely that nothing remained around it but polished earth. [83]

On the fourth, he carried the case to the cobalt silo under stars. [96] Empty since the railway took the grain trade away, its curved walls held the uncharted echoes of lives the dead had meant to live. [120] He had often sent mourners here when their questions outlasted his supply of cheerful answers. [135] The echoes could reveal only what someone had wanted, and wanting was seldom one thing. [150] Tonight he had come to discover the shape of intentions: whether Ada's departure had room for him anywhere inside it. [170]

A chess clock waited on a stool beneath a tapestry that swallowed lamplight into its wool. [186] Its border held the listening instructions, knotted there by generations of women who mended the town's burial palls. [204] Ada had been one of them. [210] He set her sovereign case on the dead player's button and started his own three minutes. [226] "The other side speaks only after the chess clock runs down," she had explained once, repairing this very hem. [245] He remembered making a joke about finally getting the last word. [256]

Now he filled his allotted time with apologies, then accusations, then the small, shameful question of whether there had been another man. [278] When his clock stopped, the case sprang open, and Ada's three minutes began. [291] Footsteps sounded overhead, although the silo had no upper floor. [301] A gull cried; somebody laughed; a door slammed with a violence that made him flinch. [316] He heard his own whistling beneath everything, thin and relentless. [326]

To untangle the sounds, he knelt beside the tapestry and began decoding frayed threads in its border. [343] Blue loops marked desire; white crossings marked dread; a thread returning through its own loop meant love. [360] Each pattern prescribed a place to stand and a direction in which to listen. [374] Ada had shown him how to read them, but he had called it gloomy work and taken her dancing instead. [394] Now, with her clock ticking, he followed the blue instructions three paces clockwise and turned his face toward the wall. [414]

"A room overlooking water," Ada said, close enough to touch. [424] "Somewhere I can be unhappy without making more work for you." [435]

He reached for the clock, wanting to explain that the flowers, the jokes, the brighter lamps had all been love. [455] His fingers stopped above the button. [461] For once, she still had time. [467]

The white crossings led him to the doorway, where he heard himself say that she would feel better tomorrow. [486] She had dreaded that promise more than loneliness. [494] He searched the ragged hem for a returning thread, expecting none, and found one almost worn through by someone's careful thumb. [515] It directed him back to the stool. [522] There he heard paper folding, then Ada practicing the first line of a letter she had never lived to send. [542]

"My dear, leaving is not the same as wishing you gone." [553]

The second clock stopped before the next word. [561] In the velveteen gloom, Milo sat without trying to improve the silence. [573] Her intention had not been a knife aimed at him, nor a secret invitation; it had been a window in a room he kept too warm. [599]

At dawn he took the coins from their separate sprung compartments and bought a new ticket to the coast. [618] He spent a day beside water that refused to be cheerful, and did not ask it to change. [636] When he returned, he planted thyme around Ada's grave and put a bench where visitors could sit without facing the sun. [657] The next widow who lingered found him beside her, his hands resting empty on his knees. [673]

"Tell me," he said, and let his turn run out. [683]
