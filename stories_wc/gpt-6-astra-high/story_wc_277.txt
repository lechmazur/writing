After the journalist calls, Ada Vale unlocks the cabinet beneath her father's telescope. [13] In the echoing observatory above the clouds, even the small click returns with an air of accusation. [30] The journalist wants the original safety certificate for Vale Mill, which the company intends to reopen tomorrow. [47] The certificate supposedly proves that its retaining dam was sound before the flood that killed nineteen workers and Ada's brother. [67] The engineers' new assessment takes that old inspection as its starting point; nobody has drained the reservoir to examine the foundations. [88]

As newsprint heiress, Ada owns the mill, the dam, and several newspapers that once called the flood unforeseeable. [106] She was seventeen then, old enough to ask questions and young enough to accept expensive answers. [122] Now her father is dead, and his assurances require her signature. [133]

She has come upstairs to discover hidden truths, she tells herself, although she has also brought her authorization for the reopening. [154] Beside it she lays a rosewood pen nib. [162] Her brother carved it at twelve, declaring that a paper dynasty ought to write with trees. [178] Since the funeral, she has used it only for decisions she wishes he could witness. [193]

He would have enjoyed this room's ridiculous dignity. [201] They used to circle the telescope as planets, bowing whenever their paths crossed, apologizing gravely for imaginary collisions. [219] Later she made the circuit alone, rehearsing what she should have said before he went down to the mill. [238] She named these walks her orbits of regret. [246] Returning to the same place had begun to feel like a duty. [258]

The certificate is crisp, signed by her father and an engineer, dated six days before the disaster. [275] Its paper bears the familiar watermark of a sheaf beneath a star. [287] Ada lifts her nib to sign the authorization, then stops. [297]

A little fork splits its tip. [303] Ink gathers there sometimes, making an extra spur when she finishes a star. [316]

She carries the certificate to the telescope's inspection lamp. [325] Six points on the watermark's star, not five. [333]

All her life she has been devotedly observant of distant things: variable stars, faint comets, the predictable return of wandering light. [354] Here is something close enough to touch. [361]

In the drawer holding her old sketches, she finds the design she made after the funeral, when her father proposed a discreet memorial in the mill's stationery. [388] She drew the extra point by accident and kept it for her brother, who had always insisted on being an additional planet. [410] The papermaker's invoice is clipped behind the sketch. [418] It dates the new watermark's first production to November, eight months after the flood. [432]

"Let us be fair to the defendant," she tells the telescope. [443] Its brass barrel supplies no objection. [449]

She proceeds through proof by contradiction, the method her father taught her when a difficult proposition frightened her. [467] Assume the certificate was signed on its stated date. [476] Then this sheet, bearing her memorial design, existed before the death that prompted her to invent it. [493]

The contradiction does not prove the dam was defective, or that her father knew it. [508] It proves that the document offered as a contemporary original is nothing of the kind. [523]

For a moment she imagines filing the certificate away and commissioning another memorial. [536] There is always room for another star. [543]

"No more astronomy as an alibi," she says, and the room repeats the last word without mercy. [560]

She uses the rosewood nib to strike through her authorization. [570] To intervene now means wages delayed, angry directors, and questions about every dividend she has accepted. [586] She telephones the mill manager, orders the reopening suspended pending an independent inspection, and guarantees the workers' pay from her own accounts. [608]

Then she calls the journalist back. [614]

"Bring someone who knows paper, and someone who knows dams." [624]

When he asks whether her newspapers will print the findings, she looks at the little wooden nib, its dark mouth still wet. [646]

"They will print what you can prove, including what it costs me." [658]

She packs the certificate, sketch, and invoice, leaving no convenient originals behind. [670] At the telescope she makes her brother's absurd planetary bow. [680] Then, carrying the evidence, she breaks her orbit and takes the stairs down into the clouds. [696]
