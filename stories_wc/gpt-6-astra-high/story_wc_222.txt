When the sky was wrong for the seventh morning, Lio watched the same swallow freeze above the same chimney. [19] The sun shone, but nothing thawed. [25] Above the clouds, an icebound spire held the instrument that had arrested tomorrow, and the woman who knew how to release it. [47] Lio had spent three nights climbing to its kitchen window, bringing stolen oranges for the prisoners and apologies for the cook. [68] He considered himself a chivalrous thief, although the cook said chivalry would include using the stairs. [84] She admitted him because her children lived beneath that motionless bird. [95]

The prisoner, Orra, had once taught him to be an apprentice of wonder. [108] Their order was sacredly playful: prayers required a joke, and every lesson ended with a question the teacher could not answer. [129] Lio had loved the jokes but hated that last uncertainty. [139] After his father drowned exploring an unmapped coast, he stole maps instead of following them. [154] His room was papered with journeys he would never risk. [164]

Orra's jailers allowed chess through the grille, believing games kept a prisoner's mind quiet. [178] Lio, dressed in the cook's spare apron, brought her supper and a board. [191] They plotted by swapping hand-carved chess pieces with secrets inside, making their exchanges look like ordinary captures. [208] A hollow rook yielded the route to the observatory; a bishop held a diagram of the instrument. [225] On the third evening, Orra sacrificed her king. [233] Inside were a diamond engraving point and a message scratched on birch bark. [246] "Do not conquer the sky; amend our invitation." [254] Beneath it, in smaller letters, she had added that the storm which killed the king's daughter had also killed her son. [275] She had never told Lio that. [281]

The king had answered his grief by ordering Orra to make the heavens safe. [295] When she refused, his engineers had locked her away and engraved a new command on the instrument's pocket sundial lid. [315] The little traveling dial was the original from which the spire's vast machinery had been copied, and still governed its workings. [336] Its lid determined what the turning heavens could admit. [345]

Lio found it beneath a glass dome, small enough to steal and heavy enough to make his hands shake. [364] Around its brass rim ran the words: LET NOTHING UNFORESEEN ENTER. [375] The sky had obeyed, shutting out each tomorrow with its untested possibilities. [387] Now it had nothing left to offer. [394]

A guard stirred beside the stair, and Lio slipped behind him with the diamond point raised before he remembered himself. [414] He lowered it, stole the man's keys instead, and locked the stair door between them. [429] Then he pulled the bell rope that warned everyone below to shelter against violent weather. [444] He could not promise safety, but he could give strangers time to find their children. [459]

Against the door, the guard began to hammer. [467] Lio scraped NOTHING from the lid, each bright shaving clinging to his damp fingers like a tiny, discarded certainty. [486] He wanted to write SOMETHING KIND, or SOMETHING THAT WILL NOT TAKE ANYONE I LOVE. [501] But Orra had lost a son and still sent him the diamond. [513] He engraved SOMETHING, making the letters small enough to fit the space he had cleared. [528] Then he opened the lid. [533]

A wind smelling of wet earth struck the spire, and the frozen clouds tore apart around a patch of unfamiliar stars. [554] Something immense moved behind them, not entering, only considering the opening. [565] Lio nearly slammed the lid shut. [571] Instead he wedged it open with the empty wooden king, then unlocked the stair and pressed the keys into the guard's hand. [593] "Bring Orra up, please; she knows more than either of us." [604] Perhaps it was the please, or the first warmth coming through the windows, but the guard went. [621]

Lio tied the bell rope around his waist before stepping onto the balcony. [634] He wanted, at last, to embrace the unknown with open arms, not because it was harmless, but because it was alive. [655] When Orra found him, he was leaning into the wind, tethered and trembling, with both hands spread. [672] Far below, the swallow finished its wingbeat. [679]
