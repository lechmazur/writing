In the city of forgotten fountains, Mara was called the luminous skeptic because her scars glowed and she refused to call them blessings. [23] The scars marked the places where the Council had removed her memories of her mother. [38] She was realistically imaginative: stolen memories needed a prison, and even an impossible prison must have a door. [56]

Her father knew the door, although he no longer knew that he knew. [69] Every evening he scattered bread across their courtyard in peculiar patterns, which the sparrows immediately dismantled. [85] Erasure spared the habits of the hands; otherwise the Council would have needed to teach its subjects their trades again. [105] She thought it merely another habit, a kindness surviving whatever had hollowed him out. [119]

Then migrating geese paused beside the reservoir, where the grain scattered for them drew every sparrow out of town. [138] By following breadcrumbs through his miniature city, she identified their destination: the fountain where stone children held bowls beneath their chins. [159] Her journey stretched from the pause of migrating geese at sunset until their departure the following dawn. [176]

She slipped out wrapped in black cloth, hiding the light that would betray her to the patrols. [193] She led her father there, and he immediately began probing a seam too narrow for her knife. [210]

"Sidereal patience," he whispered, the only advice he ever gave her. [221]

She had always thought it meant enduring what could not be changed. [233] Now she noticed a brass star beside the seam and, overhead, a matching opening in the fountain's canopy. [251] The city clock struck midnight, but the mechanism remained still. [261] Of course: the stars returned almost four minutes earlier each night, indifferent to the Council's clocks. [277]

She covered her brightest scar and waited until true starlight fell through the opening onto the brass. [294] Something clicked, but years of grit held the basin shut. [304] To unstick it, she poured their drinking water into the seam while her father pressed with both trembling hands. [323]

The stone shifted, releasing a breath that smelled of rain on hot pavement. [336] Below stood a stained glass box, its colored panes joined to the city's buried water pipes. [352] Behind every pane, a mouth was trying to finish a sentence. [363]

Mara uncovered her scars, and the faces turned toward their stolen light. [375] She found her mother in a blue square, laughing beside a workbench where her father soldered the very same box. [395] An inscription below his hands promised relief from unbearable grief. [405] The next pane showed soldiers, her mother resisting, and her father pointing toward the cupboard where Mara had hidden. [424]

He had built the prison, then helped fill it. [433]

She had come to liberate trapped memories, not to learn which ones her father had been relieved to lose. [452] Her hand found the release lever beneath the box, then stopped. [463] If she opened it, the whole city would remember who had made forgetting possible. [477]

Above them, the first geese called across the paling sky. [487] Her father was studying his own face in the glass, touching its reflected mouth as though teaching himself speech. [506]

"I don't want it," he said, and she could not tell whether he meant the memory or the mercy. [525]

Mara wanted the father who fed birds, but keeping only that man would make the box hers. [542] In the defiant hush between wingbeats, she pulled the lever. [552]

Water struck the glass from below, filling each colored chamber until the imprisoned mouths became currents rushing beneath the streets. [572] The fountain above them spilled into its children's bowls, and from every neighborhood came the answering thunder of water. [591] People woke with names on their tongues, then ran barefoot toward the sound. [604]

Mara remembered her mother's rough thumb wiping flour from her cheek, and the furious tenderness of their last embrace. [623] Beside her, her father folded slowly to his knees. [632] She did not forgive him, and she did not leave him hidden underground. [645]

"You will tell them," she said, taking his hand. [654]

They climbed into morning as the geese lifted away, leaving the fountains to a city that finally knew why it was thirsty. [676]
