On the moonlit ridge path, Mara carried a cracked porcelain box in hands that could still cradle a moth without disturbing its wings. [23] She was a scarred forest firefighter, gently skilled at bandaging burns and splinting saplings, but unable to touch the white seam beneath her own jaw. [48] Inside the box lay ash from the laborers' camp. [57] Below her, the town's windows shone where the camp's windows no longer could. [70]

At the summit, a potter waited beside a wheel that turned without water or foot. [85] Its turning followed the slow dance of celestial bodies; tonight, as Earth's shadow crossed the moon, she could give grief a voice. [107] Mara had climbed to buy that voice. [114] The dead had spoken seven languages, and every public hearing had used only one. [128] She wanted to translate pain into a universal language, something no councilman could dismiss as a failure of interpretation. [147]

"You brought a broken vessel," the potter said. [155]

"It belonged to the camp cook," Mara answered. [163] "She kept everyone's wages in it because porcelain would show if someone forced its lock." [178]

The fire had cracked it anyway, leaving coins fused into a single black wafer beneath the ash. [195] The potter set it on her wheel without trying to mend it. [207]

"Good porcelain keeps things in," she said. [214] "We need something that can let them out." [222]

Mara laid her pension papers beside the wheel. [230] The potter pushed them back. [235]

"The price is your innocence, not your living." [243]

"I have none," Mara said, too quickly. [250]

Above them, the moon began to darken at one edge. [260]

She had ordered the backburn that stopped the wildfire short of town. [272] The camp was marked abandoned on her map, and her crew had minutes, not hours, to choose. [289] These were facts, and for fourteen months she had stacked them around herself like stones around a cooking fire. [308] She had also heard a radio call about people below the ridge. [320] Static had swallowed the location, and she had decided not to ask again. [333]

"Will this prove the council ignored the camp?" she asked. [343]

"It will carry what you know, including what you wish you didn't." [355]

Mara looked down the path. [360] She had imagined returning as the dead's defender, holding something terrible and clean. [373] Now she understood the trade: bargained innocence would leave her no place outside the story she wanted others to hear. [393]

The potter did not hurry her, though the shadow kept advancing. [404] By reflecting deeply, beyond the familiar recital of orders and weather, Mara found the small, unadorned moment when asking again had seemed inconvenient. [427] She had wanted the decision finished. [433] The admission hurt less spectacularly than fire, and went deeper. [443]

"That too," she said. [447]

The potter touched the crack as the moon turned copper. [457] There was no scream, only the dry click of a radio button. [469] Mara felt a question travel outward, carrying smoke, exhaustion, and the weight of a choice made before all the voices had arrived. [491] Below, doors opened. [494] People stepped into gardens and streets with their hands pressed against their chests. [507]

The sound could enlighten them, the potter said, but it could neither sentence nor forgive. [522] "Tomorrow, they will still need maps and testimony." [530]

Mara lifted the box, supporting its broken corner as carefully as she once supported injured wrists. [546] It was heavier now, though nothing had been added. [555]

Halfway down, she met the cook's husband climbing toward her, barefoot in the cold dust. [570] At the hearings, an interpreter had always stood between them. [580] Tonight he looked at her, then at his wife's box, and she could not mistake recognition for pardon. [598]

She sat on a flat stone and unlaced her boots. [608] He accepted them without thanks; his feet were bleeding. [617] When she offered the box, he took that too. [626] Then he pointed toward the council hall, where the maps were locked away. [639]

Mara nodded and rose to walk beside him. [647] The moon was emerging from shadow, restoring enough light to show every loose stone on the path. [664] Neither of them pretended the descent would be gentle. [673]
