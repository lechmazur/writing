At four in the morning, the scientific poetry library belonged to people whose work had displaced their sleep. [18] Nurses browsed poems about anatomy; telescope technicians read elegies for stars that had ceased existing before anyone named them. [37] Elias, the odd-hour librarian, kept the lamps low and repaired books in the open, where nobody needed to approach him. [57] They considered him sanely eccentric: he shelved by subject, but turned every reading chair toward a window before dawn. [76] Lately, he had stopped turning his own. [83]

His mother had founded the library, collecting verse wherever scientific language failed to contain what its practitioners experienced. [101] After her funeral, he found her cassette archive: analog memories held in the varying magnetism of brown tape. [119] He transferred each reading to a computer, indexed every word, and searched for the woman who had made him. [138] The search returned lectures, dates, and impeccable explanations. [146] It never returned her dropping an egg or becoming impatient with a stubborn knot. [160] He wanted to find the place where stories live, before grief reduced her to a searchable authority. [177]

The last unexamined object was a tattered silk scarf, crocheted in crimson thread and stored beside a geometry lecture. [196] Its edges gathered into extravagant ruffles that refused to lie flat beneath the scanner. [210] He weighted the corners with dictionaries, hoping to photograph the stitches before the fragile silk disintegrated. [226]

From the astronomy aisle, a woman watched him remove one crease and manufacture three. [240] He recognized her as the observatory optician who always borrowed books about the sea. [254]

"Perhaps it isn't meant to be flat," she said. [263]

He almost explained that he knew that, then realized he had only known it as a fact. [280]

On the cassette, his mother described regularly increasing the number of stitches to make a model of hyperbolic space. [299]

"The circumference grows faster than it can on a plane," Elias told the woman, lifting away the dictionaries. [317] "That's why it ruffles," he added, hearing himself assume his mother's brisk teaching voice. [331]

He spread the scarf across his palms instead, allowing the surface its stubborn abundance. [345] Along one fold, a line of pale stitches marked a path that was straight on the curved surface. [363] His finger followed it, and stopped at a tiny repair. [373]

He knew that knot. [377]

He was eleven, holding the unfinished scarf while his mother worked, and had pulled a loose thread despite her warning. [397] A whole crimson ridge had collapsed. [403] She had shouted, then sat very still while he waited for the worse part. [417] Instead, she put the crochet hook into his hand and made him help. [430] They spent an hour rebuilding the surface, her fingers guiding his, neither of them quite ready to apologize. [448] Eventually she complained that Euclid would have been a much easier son, and he laughed before he could stop himself. [468]

The tape contained none of that. [474]

He had hoped the recordings would reawaken her; instead, she had returned through hyperbolic geometry, cross and fallible and warm. [494]

Across the table, the optician waited without asking why he had stopped speaking. [507] Behind her, patrons continued browsing, their small noises keeping the silence from becoming absolute. [521]

Elias wanted, suddenly, to tell someone about the ruined ridge. [531] Not about his mother's contribution to mathematical teaching, which anyone could retrieve, but about the long, resentful work of mending. [551] He asked whether the woman had a moment before her shift, and she set her ocean book beside the scarf. [571]

The first telling came out badly, with too much geometry and a long pause where the joke should have been. [591] She listened anyway. [594] When he finished, she asked him to show her how a straight path could travel across those folds. [612] He placed the scarf between them, and this time he let another pair of hands support its difficult shape. [631]

At dawn, he returned the cassette to its sleeve and wrote a second entry in the catalog. [648] Under the scarf's measurements he recorded the location of the repair, then added what he remembered of making it. [667] He left room beneath the account. [673]

Before the optician departed, she helped him turn his chair toward the window. [686]
