In Vey, a treetop village whose ladders vanished into tidal mist, every room moved, even when its sleepers dreamed of solid ground. [22] Nera, an ambitious herbalist, wanted to build a sanctuary where that movement no longer frightened anyone. [38] She wanted, as she wrote above her workbench, to cultivate serenity within motion. [51] Unfortunately, she kept confusing serenity with stillness. [58] She wedged her nursery between two mangroves, tightened its braces, and brewed increasingly bitter draughts for trembling hands. [76] Olfactorily sensitive since childhood, she could distinguish seven kinds of rot beneath the boardwalks, but her own medicines made her stomach turn. [98] Her mother used to sit with frightened patients until their breathing matched the creaking beams, a treatment Nera had considered unworthy of a trained herbalist. [123]

Her mother's last parcel contained no comforting recipe, only a corpse flower seed nestled in damp moss. [140] Nera nearly threw it into the tide. [147] A plant that eventually smelled of carrion seemed a cruel inheritance for someone who struggled with crushed mint. [165] Then she imagined botanical patrons climbing Vey's hidden ladders to witness its flowering, leaving enough silver to fund her sanctuary. [185] She planted it. [188]

The parcel's wrapping paper held something else. [195] Her family had a custom of doodling on wrapping paper interiors, so that opening a gift uncovered another, less orderly gift. [216] Her mother had drawn a swinging cradle, a fat root, and ropes running through wooden rings. [232] Nera recognized the cradle in which she herself had slept. [242] She pinned the paper beside the pot and began to journalize the plant's progress on its blank spaces. [260]

For seven years, one enormous leaf followed another, each dying back into a steadily swelling corm. [276] Nera sold remedies, saved coins, and enlarged its tub until it weighed more than she did. [292] Whenever the floor swayed, she added another brace. [300] Her notes crowded around her mother's doodles like buildings around an overlooked stream. [313]

The flower opened on an evening of exceptionally high water, at the turning of the tide's thought. [330] That was Vey's name for the interval when the sea began returning but the channels beneath the village still flowed outward. [351] For a few breaths, contrary currents twisted the mangroves in different directions. [363] Usually, the hanging houses simply turned with them. [371]

Nera was setting out cups for her first visitors when the flower released its hot, unmistakable announcement. [388] Through the carrion stink came something thinner: the green scent of freshly torn wood. [402] One nursery brace had split. [407] Across the room, its opposite was pulling a bolt through a living trunk. [420]

She reached for another wedge, then saw the pinned paper shivering against the wall. [434] Beneath seven years of measurements, her mother's cradle hung between two trees, its ropes free to slide through their rings. [454] What Nera had taken for decorative curls were loops allowing one end to rise while the other fell. [472] The drawing held an undercurrent of wisdom she had recorded around without ever learning to read. [488]

She dropped the wedge and called for the neighbors who were already gathering outside. [502] Together they passed hauling ropes beneath the nursery beams and through the old cargo rings fixed above them. [520] Nera crawled beneath the nursery to guide the last rope, breathing the flower's dreadful warmth while splinters rained onto her neck. [541] When the ropes took the weight, she knocked the remaining braces loose. [553] The nursery lurched, cups shattered, and then the floor settled into a slow, pendulous arc. [568]

Nobody applauded. [570] Her neighbors stood with bent knees until their breathing found the room's rhythm. [583] Nera stood among them, no longer trying to hide her shaking hands. [595]

In the morning, she canceled the promised exhibition and spent her saved silver repairing the wounded trees. [612] She kept the flower through its brief, appalling glory, letting flies enter through the open shutters. [628] The sanctuary opened a month later, with suspended shelves, gentler medicines, and no promise that anything would stay still. [647] On the inside of every medicine wrapper, she drew the sliding ropes before adding her patient's name. [664] Beneath the drawing she left room for whatever they might discover while learning to be carried. [680]
