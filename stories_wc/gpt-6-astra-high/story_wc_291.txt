Three weeks after the evidence disappeared in the records office fire, Mara received a masquerade mask ribbon fragment wrapped around a quarry key. [23] The enclosed note offered something the six cold cases no longer possessed: a living witness willing to speak. [41] He wanted a portrait first. [46] Not a flattering one, he had written, but one his granddaughter could bear to keep. [61]

Mara was face blind, a portrait artist who could copy a stranger's features perfectly without recognizing that stranger afterward. [80] Her very methodical notebooks recorded voices, gestures, preferred chairs, and which hand reached for a cup. [96] Thirty years earlier, those notebooks had become unofficial archives for the families of six missing quarry apprentices. [113] Each boy had served drinks at the owners' masquerade before vanishing that same night. [127] Runaways, the owners said, and eventually the town accepted the convenience. [138] Mara had kept painting them from family photographs, hoping to awaken the dormant empathy that petitions never reached. [156]

In the shadowed quarry, mist pressed against the spoil heaps like dirty wool. [169] A man stood beside a shovel, holding his right wrist in his left hand. [183] She knew the gesture from the foreman who had interrupted every search meeting to warn about trespass. [200]

"Ellis," she said, and his chin sank. [207]

He had been digging into a heap marked on the old maps as clean stone. [222] She photographed the shallow hole, its position, his shovel, then told him to stop disturbing the ground. [239]

The ribbon lay across her paint box: crimson on one side, black on the other, its torn end stiff with limestone dust. [261] Ellis stared at it rather than at her. [269]

"I pulled that off Daniel when I tried to breathe into him," he said. [283] "His mask kept slipping over his mouth." [290]

In Mara's portraits, Daniel always held something: a fishing float, a sister's mitten, the bicycle he was saving to repair. [310] Now she imagined those hands empty. [316]

Ellis showed her a swollen blister and asked, almost apologetically, whether she could lance it. [331] She wanted to tell him that pain was the least he owed. [343] Instead she opened the first aid pouch she carried on every outdoor commission and took out a sterile needle. [362] His fingers trembled while she drained the blister and dressed his palm. [374] For a moment, she hated the tenderness of her own touch. [385]

Then she set him where the pale mist met the quarry wall. [397] She built every portrait by unifying opposites: light with shadow, the hard edge with the place where an edge dissolved. [417] But she had always painted Ellis, in her mind, without light. [428]

"Helping you doesn't settle anything," she told him. [436] "Neither will this painting." [440]

He nodded, looking at the bandage. [446] Then he began. [449]

The owners stored wine in a disused tunnel, and Ellis had sent the boys to haul crates despite a spreading crack. [470] He had obeyed again when the owner ordered the collapse covered with waste rock before daylight. [486] Daniel had been nearest the entrance. [492] Ellis had kept the ribbon because throwing it away would have been another small obedience. [507]

"I told myself my wages kept my children safe," he said. [518] "Then I told myself it was too late." [526]

After the fire, he'd realized there would never be a knock at his door unless he summoned it himself. [545]

Mara's brush stopped. [548] She had wanted a confession that would leave her hatred simple, not this frightened man who had once tried to save a boy. [571] The distinction did not diminish his guilt; it made his choices harder to escape. [585] She put down the brush and switched on her recorder. [595]

"Tell me again, from the beginning." [601]

By noon, police tape crossed the spoil heap. [609] By spring, six families had something to bury, and Ellis was awaiting trial. [622]

Mara finished his portrait with the bandaged hand open and the other closed around nothing. [637] With his permission, she hung it beside the boys before sending it to his granddaughter. [652] People who had passed the boys' portraits for decades stopped before the familiar foreman, then turned to read the other names. [673]

Mara recognized none of their faces. [679] She watched their hands instead: pockets emptied, arms unfolded, a finger resting beneath a name as though learning to read. [699]
