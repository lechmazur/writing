At twilight, the jetty bloomed purple with sea lavender, and beneath its planks the relocation crew's new pontoons knocked like impatient coffins. [22] Mara, the town's walnut-ink archivist, carried her ledger onto it, keeping the covers level as though balancing a sleeping child. [42] Behind her, the remaining families waited beside boxes of crockery, fishing nets, and keys to houses the next spring tide would enter. [64] The government had bought the drowned streets, not the communal jetty, and tomorrow its contractors would stop pumping. [82] Tonight, the town had to decide whether its garden could leave. [93]

Ben stood at the mooring winch with the old covenant rolled in his fist. [107] "We swore to keep this harbor alive," he said. [116] "Not tow it inland for visitors to admire." [124]

His son's ashes lay among the lavender roots; Mara had recorded the scattering, including the bent spoon Ben used and the rain that started afterward. [149] She had always been gently thorough, believing that accuracy could preserve the weight of small moments after everyone who loved them died. [171] Lately, she had wondered whether this was love or merely a slower kind of embalming. [186]

She set a flask beside her ledger and let the last walnut husks steep while the families argued. [204] Their tree had been felled for the flood defenses that failed. [215]

In her pocket was a locket photo of her mother, barefoot on this jetty at twelve. [231] That morning, intending to copy its date, Mara had lifted the picture from its silver frame. [247] The covered bottom edge showed barrels under the boards. [256] In the archive, she had reconstructed its arrival through proximate causes: a washed-out landing, salvaged barrels for buoyancy, borrowed boats for towing. [278] The harbor had not begun with a boundary, but with refugees bringing somewhere to stand. [293]

She passed Ben the photograph and the faded receipt for that first tow. [306] "Our grandparents moved it," she said, "and then promised to keep it alive." [319]

He studied the tiny feet above the barrels, but his hand stayed on the winch. [334] "My boy won't be there." [339]

Mara almost told him the roots would carry the ashes. [349] Instead, she remembered the way he had scraped the spoon clean against his palm, unwilling to surrender even its dust. [369]

"No," she said, "and he won't be here when the boards break apart." [382]

The waiting tug sounded its horn, mournful enough to make the children laugh and then look ashamed. [399] Mara opened the ledger to a blank page. [407] She wrote a heading, "What we owe when context changes," and beneath it began a regenerated covenant. [424] It kept the promise of a living harbor, but required the harbor to follow its people, and the people to make room for newcomers. [448] Upriver, their purchased land had space for another row of houses; until now they had planned a memorial there. [467]

Ben read the new terms twice. [473] "Put a bench by the gangway," he said, "for anyone who needs to sit before they can come aboard." [492]

Mara added it without calling it a memorial. [500] Then she strained the dark liquid through her handkerchief, dipped her pen, and offered it to him. [517] He signed with the hand that had held the spoon. [527] One by one, the others followed, passing the ledger over their boxes while the ink dried brown as earth. [546]

Only after the last signature did Ben release the winch. [556] The crew slipped the chains, and the jetty trembled before drifting free. [568] Water opened between the flowers and the street where Mara's mother had once called her home for supper. [586] For a moment she felt the wild, indecent happiness of surviving someone, and heard that vanished voice inside the tug's engine. [607] Ben sat among the lavender, crying openly as the children cheered the sudden widening of the world. [624]

Mara wanted to record everything: the wet chains, the lifted hands, the precise color of the shore becoming unreachable. [643] Instead she closed the ledger, pocketed the photograph, and sat beside him, close enough that their shoulders touched through wool. [663] For once, the moment would have to keep its own weight.
