For six days, the rebellion had been quiet enough for Mara to hear the stones beneath the abandoned spaceport repair bay. [21] Not peace, just a lull in rebellion: the governors had stopped shelling because the rebels had stopped shooting back. [40] Both sides were saving ammunition for the battle over dawn's relief convoy. [52]

Mara, the neighborhood's barefoot stargazer, had spent those days sorting wreckage to discover meaning in the mess. [69] Her brother had died here trying to steal the ignition codes that kept the city's ships grounded. [86] She needed his death to have opened something besides a hole in their family. [100]

The blast had uncovered twelve ancient stones arranged around an elderly hospital tug's landing cradle. [115] The salvage records called them foundations; older drawings called them a choir. [127] Before the governors installed electronic locks, engines like this one had been awakened by the hum of an ancient stone circle. [148] Later, concrete had silenced the stones, and permission to fly became something people purchased. [162]

Commander Venn arrived carrying a detonator and wearing the expression that had sent her brother through the gates. [180] "If you can reignite that tug, we can ram the blockade flagship before the convoy arrives." [196]

Mara straightened too quickly, struck her head on a wing, and announced, with awkwardly confident dignity, that she required silence. [216]

The stones sang only in the margin of daylight, when cooling surfaces tightened around their still warm hearts. [234] She had one sunset to learn their language. [242] Through her mother's vintage opera glasses, she watched the tug's mechanical ignition needles tremble behind a window high above the deck. [263] Powered instruments were useless under the governors' jamming field, but glass still magnified and bare soles still felt vibrations. [282] The western stone hummed against her heel; one needle climbed, then fell. [294]

"They aren't twelve keys," she whispered. [300] "They're one chord." [303]

An irrigation pipe lay across two stones, deadening their voices. [313] She dragged it clear, cutting her palms on its torn seam, and felt the floor answer through her bones. [332] Above her, the needles approached their faded green marks. [341]

Venn raised his whistle, but Mara caught his wrist. [350]

"Not a bomb," she said. [355] "This ship has eighty beds." [360]

"And that flagship has the guns that killed your brother." [370]

For a moment she wanted the collision so vividly that she saw it through the little glasses: a new star, briefly hers. [392] Then the tug's cabin lights came on, illuminating stretchers still strapped neatly along the walls. [407]

"He wanted people to get out," she said. [415] "I won't turn the door he died opening into another weapon." [426]

Venn looked toward the city, where his daughter was waiting in a cellar with the other wounded. [443] "They'll shoot you down." [447]

Mara raised the glasses toward the first stars appearing through the broken roof. [460] She had watched the blockade for months, marking each patrol against those steady lights. [474] "Not if we leave tonight through the southern gap, while they're facing the convoy." [488]

"How wide a gap?" [492]

"Wide enough for something that isn't trying to win a battle." [503]

He pocketed the detonator and sent his soldiers for stretchers instead. [514]

Mara climbed into the cockpit as the stone choir deepened into a sound too large to hear. [531] The hull shook, yet beneath the shaking was an exactness she had only known looking up at stars. [549] She waited until all twelve needles met their green marks, then pulled the manual ignition lever. [565] Fire bloomed beneath the stern, blue and steady. [573] Outside, the last strip of sunlight vanished, and the stones went quiet. [585] The engine kept their chord. [590]

By midnight, eighty wounded lay aboard, including three captured conscripts whom Venn carried himself. [604] Mara flew without screens, finding her narrow passage with the opera glasses while Venn held a shaded lamp over her penciled charts. [626] Below them, searchlights swept the empty repair bay. [634] Ahead, the relief convoy answered their white signal lamp and opened its formation to receive them. [650]

In the thunderous serenity of the engine, Mara set the glasses down. [662] Her brother was still dead, and the city was still broken. [673] But behind her someone asked for water, and someone else was alive to bring it. [688]
