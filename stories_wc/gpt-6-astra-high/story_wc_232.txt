When his daughter brought him the copper fragment, Silas was pricing a map of a country that had never existed. [20] Invented countries sold better than real ones, especially to a lonely antique map dealer who rarely left his shop. [39]

Nora set the fragment beside his magnifying glass. [47] "From Iona's box," she said. [52] He had asked her to sort what he could not bear to touch. [65]

It was a type foundry matrix chip, its recessed ant still sharp enough to cast a printing sort. [83] Its paper wrapper bore the title of the cavern map his wife had bought on their honeymoon. [100] She must have bought it for his birthday, which fell a week after her funeral. [115] On that map, a blank square interrupted the legend: FOLLOW ITS PEOPLE HOME. [128] The missing creature had finally arrived. [134]

So had the lingering promise he had buried beneath twenty years of profitable transactions. [148] "One midsummer," he had told Iona, "we will find the doorway of decisions." [161] According to the map, it opened upon whatever turning in your life you most wished to revisit. [178]

Iona died that winter. [182] Nora arrived three years later, a foster child who refused to unpack until someone promised she could stay. [200] He had kept that promise, although lately he had mistaken paying her train fare for asking her to visit. [219]

"I'll take you," Nora said, reading the old map over his shoulder. [231] As a surveyor, she knew the mountain creek would rise with the afternoon snowmelt. [245] They would have until noon. [250]

At midsummer the cavern bloomed beneath a broken roof, orchids spilling from ledges into long shafts of sunlight. [268] Silas watched wet stones glimmer as the creek crept across the floor. [280] Ants threaded between fallen petals, but he did not immediately follow them. [292] He scattered biscuit crumbs and waited to see which travelers carried their burdens uphill. [306]

"Empirically intuitive," Nora said, quoting one of Iona's letters. [315] "Always testing your hunches." [319]

Not always, he thought. [323]

Beyond stood his kitchen, impossibly warm, with Iona holding a cup in both hands to stop its trembling. [373] He knew the morning by the cracked saucer. [381] She was about to ask him to fetch the doctor. [391] He was about to say that fevers passed, that opening the shop could not wait. [406]
