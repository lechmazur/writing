Mara wrote cryptic fortunes for a fortune cookie factory, compressing whole sorrows into directions nobody could follow until something precious disappeared. [21] She was chronologically homesick: not for a country, but for the Tuesday when her mother had last hummed while washing her hair. [43] Her mother had died building the glass spire beside the frozen lake, where the town's unfinished music accumulated beneath the ice. [64]

The spire was meant to release it, but its stairways rearranged themselves around whoever entered, following remembered paths instead of architectural plans. [86] Nobody had ever reached the conductor's platform. [93] Mara intended to, although she could barely read a score. [103] To become the conductor of those unheard symphonies would mean giving the town what her mother had promised, and perhaps hearing her unfinished lullaby. [127]

For months, she had been mapping childhood memories onto the narrow paper strips she usually fed into cookies. [145] The bakery became the place where hunger turned sweet; the alley became the shortcut that cost a scraped knee. [164] Cryptic enough to fit her wrists, precise enough to survive a failing mind, these were fortunes she could finally trust. [184]

Her mother's notebook warned that the spire borrowed strength from memory, and that its visitor must reach the platform before exhaustion took everything. [207] It also specified dawn, when the mirrored horizon briefly confused the glass about which direction was up. [224] Only then would the instrument's sealed throat open. [232]

Mara arrived while the stars still lay beneath her boots. [242] In her pocket was a nautilus shell segment, the model her mother had used to calculate the spire's widening chambers. [262] She blew across its broken chambers, and the answering glass unfolded a staircase with the gentleness of an opening hand. [282]

She climbed by remembering the bakery, turned where the alley should have been, and stepped across the remembered ache of her knee. [304] Below her, the lake lifted its counterfeit sky. [312] Above her, the real sky paled. [318] By the seventh landing, she could no longer remember the baker's face, and her thighs shook beneath her coat. [337] The paper on her wrists carried her onward, each little riddle preserving a turn after the memory itself had been spent. [358]

Then a door appeared where she had mapped the kitchen. [368] Warm water splashed behind it, and her mother called that the soap would sting unless she shut her eyes. [387] Mara grasped the handle. [391] Every fortune she had written had secretly been an address for this room. [404] She could stay seven forever, listening to the only melody she had really come to recover. [420]

But beneath the humming she heard her mother's cough, the small exhausted sound that had accompanied years of work on the spire. [442] The room was not a reward; it was the last memory the staircase needed to consume. [458] If she entered, there would be nothing left of her to climb. [470] She opened the door, let the warmth pass through her, and stepped instead onto the stair forming out of its light. [491]

By the time she reached the platform, she remembered having a mother, but not the tune. [507] Dawn began beneath her as well as overhead, two thin fires meeting along the mirrored horizon. [523] The glass throat opened, and the hush turned upward. [532] Mara put the shell to her lips before her knees could fold. [544]

Its note gave the waiting music a pitch, but the first answering chords collided, furious after so many winters. [563] She knew no rules for an orchestra, only how to make room for a whole sorrow inside a little sentence. [583] With one lifted finger she held the brass back, giving a frail violin phrase enough silence to finish. [601] Then she brought the others in around it, shaping pauses as carefully as words. [615]

Across town, sleepers woke to symphonies they had abandoned for wages, illness, children, or fear. [630] Mara sank to the platform, conducting with two fingers when she could no longer raise her arms. [647] The music did not restore her Tuesday. [654] It carried her past it. [659]

When the stairs settled into ordinary glass, people came up from the lake carrying blankets, bread, and instruments dusty from their attics. [681] They found her asleep, one hand still lifted, making a little space for whoever would play next. [698]
