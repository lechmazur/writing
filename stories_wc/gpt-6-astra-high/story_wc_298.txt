When Nera returned to the catacomb orchard, the seven children she had saved were waiting upstairs to thank her. [19] Below them, tree roots snaked between bones, drinking futures from the dead and bearing the pale fruit of bartered destinies. [39] She had bought seven recoveries from river fever with forty winters of her own capacity for joy. [56] Already, their mothers' kisses had felt like rain falling on someone else's roof. [69]

In her fist lay a burned tarot card: the Star, its silver figure surviving where its paper sky had blackened. [89] Burning the receipt had not burned the bargain. [97]

Sorrel, the orchard keeper, took her blistered hand before she asked for the card. [111] She was fiercely gentle, washing the burn without pretending it would not scar. [124]

"You still have time to amend it," she said, looking toward the corridor lamps. [138]

All provisional exchanges could be altered within the glow of half-lit corridors; when the last lamp died, their terms became permanent. [159] Only three flames remained. [163]

Nera had once apprenticed here, learning the keeper's method of inlaying meaning everywhere: names in stones, promises in bark, conditions beneath fingernails. [185] Even the smallest mark could change which life a root would enter. [197] She had left because a reckless idealist could not bear weighing suffering before relieving it. [212] Then her little sister died before harvest, and afterward every child wore her face. [226] She had made this bargain to patch holes in the soul, discovering too late that salvation could leave another kind of emptiness. [248]

"I could tear up the root," she said. [256]

"The fever returns," Sorrel answered, and did not release her hand. [267]

Upstairs, a child coughed, then laughed at the surprise of being able to breathe. [281] Nera knelt instead and unwound the copper wire that kept her braid from falling loose. [296] She used it to trammel the contracting root beneath her name, delaying the orchard's collection while she considered another payment. [316] The wire bit into wood; her own throat tightened in answer. [327]

"No more pieces of yourself," Sorrel said, easing the loop before it strangled her. [341] "There are other names you can write." [348]

"I won't make the children pay." [354]

"Then ask the people who love them." [361]

Nera looked at the single name she had pressed into the root, large enough to cover space for a dozen others. [382] She had called that courage, when she signed it. [391]

Sorrel put the burned Star into her palm and led her up to the villagers gathered for the children's vigil. [411] Under the chapel's smoke-dark ceiling, Nera explained exactly what the orchard had taken and what sharing the price would mean. [431] One joyless winter was not a sad afternoon: love would remain, but bread, music, and even tenderness would give no pleasure. [452] Nobody would owe her consent, including the parents whose children stood breathing beside them. [466] She held out the card without calling it a debt. [476]

A father backed away, clutching his recovered daughter, and Nera made herself leave a clear path to the door. [495] Sorrel was the first to offer a winter; the baker followed, choosing the year his brother would return from sea. [515]

"He can sit through it with me," he said. [524]

Others asked questions, counted on their fingers, or went home without looking back. [537] Nera stayed until she had thirty-nine willing companions, not counting herself. [548]

They descended together, following the final lamp's thin light, while the copper wire strained around the root. [565] Sorrel fitted the burned card into a hollow in the bark. [576] Nera scraped her solitary signature away and inlaid their names around the Star's surviving silver, giving each winter its own bearer. [597] Each person touched their name before she unwound the wire, and the root loosened beneath their hands. [614] The last flame died, sealing a bargain that no longer had to pass through one throat. [630]

Nera kept the coming winter, and the child who took her hand on the stairs still felt impossibly far away. [650] But she asked his name and listened carefully, instead of searching his face for her sister. [666] Behind her, the others began deciding whose kitchen would hold them when their winters came. [681] She could not yet feel the warmth of that arrangement, but she wrote down the first address. [698]
