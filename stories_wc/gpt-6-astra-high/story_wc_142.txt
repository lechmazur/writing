On the raft city of Revel, the carnival never closed, because its harbor license required entertainment every hour of every day. [21] Mara's job was culture shock absorber: she taught new arrivals which drums meant dancing, which meant fire, and why curtains mattered more than doors. [45] She kept immaculate lists, then trusted the pauses in people's voices to tell her what those lists omitted. [63] Now her employer, the carnival authority, wanted her to soothe residents before privatization. [76] At dawn, a development company would buy the authority's leases and begin replacing cheap family berths with floating hotels. [95]

The prospect relieved Mara as much as it terrified her. [105] Last winter, her son had been burned carrying hot water across three slippery rafts because their boiler had failed. [124] The company promised plumbing, firebreaks, and rents her mother could never pay. [136] Mara had welcomed its engineers aboard; she had also hidden rent projections from her mother, pretending this was protection. [155] Her dual allegiance, to the authority that paid her and the neighbors who trusted her, was becoming a kind of dishonesty. [176]

An obscure clause allowed a resident cooperative to delay the sale by depositing repair money and presenting a safety plan. [196] The sum exceeded everything the old families owned. [204] But Mara's newest arrivals included mainland tenants driven onto the water by rising rents, some with savings, all without anywhere else to go. [227] She had spent months translating their complaints about noise into requests the performers could bear to hear. [244] For the old families, though, every unfamiliar face was another advance scout for the coming hotels. [260]

She took her father's foundation stone chip from its drawer, the last solid piece of the drowned neighborhood that had launched Revel. [282] He had used its jagged outline as his signature on scenery, back when the carnival funded their evacuation. [300] People still trusted that mark more than any seal. [309] Mara pressed the chip against a poster and traced its outline beneath two painted gulls. [324] Two gulls meant the second watch; a red beak meant the boiler raft. [337] These were the old evacuation signals, which Mara taught every arrival along with the fire drills. [353] By midnight, instructions were traveling through messages encoded in graffiti art, carried from platform to platform on the carnival's revolving scenery. [374] The authority monitored its message boards, but it rarely looked closely at the decorations. [388]

At the boiler, nobody applauded. [393] The old performers accused the newcomers of buying silence; the newcomers demanded to know why children slept beside fuel. [412] Mara laid down her orderly lists, including the projections she had hidden, then turned them over when everyone searched for their own column. [435] She set the stone chip between them instead. [443]

"We brought this because we thought we'd build on land again," she said. [456] "We didn't, but we can stop treating one another as temporary." [467]

On the backs, she drew the raft city as it was, including the dangerous crossing where her son had fallen. [487] A retired nurse from the new pontoons asked for a clinic berth; Mara's mother offered her kitchen, if they could build her another. [510] It took two hours to turn accusations into pledges. [519] They agreed to reorganize the raft city around shared services rather than ticket prices. [533] Fuel barges would move beyond the sleeping quarters; three lucrative midway lanes would become firebreaks. [548] The new residents would underwrite a boiler, and the performers would surrender a season's earnings to repay them. [566] Nobody got quiet nights, guaranteed profits, or a view reserved for themselves. [578] Mara offered her severance money, though she would have to be dismissed before she could collect it. [595] That finally earned a laugh. [600]

By the third watch, painted gulls with open claws summoned crews to release mooring chains. [615] Drummers covered shouted instructions, and the carousel slowed while its generator powered the winches. [629] Mara's supervisor found her guiding a fuel barge away from the nursery. [641]

"You're supposed to absorb the shock," he said. [649]

"Not leave it all inside the poorest people," she answered. [659]

He dismissed her before sunrise. [664]

At dawn, Mara delivered the cooperative's deposit and a revised harbor plan, its curling corner weighted with the chip. [683] The clerk stamped the postponement, not an approval: they had thirty days to prove their promises would float. [701] Mara returned to find her mother and a new neighbor fitting a railing where the firebreak crossed open water. [720] The carnival was still playing, ragged and sleepless, but for once its music accompanied something other than survival. [738]
