Ash, the vagrant penny poet, was trying to rebuild a doorway before moonrise made a stranger of him. [18] The prison's correction coil, welded behind his ear, made him molt personalities monthly, although the prison and its government had fallen six months ago. [42] By dawn he might hate poetry, or Mara, or the notion that anything broken deserved repair. [58] He sold poems for pennies because a rhyme, unlike a room or a job, required no reliable name. [76] For now he wanted to forge beauty from anarchy, beginning with a door that locked nobody in. [93]

Around him, the rusted library stood under stars, its iron roof stripped for barricades, its rescued books sleeping beneath oilcloth. [113] Beyond its walls, rival committees burned whatever bore the old government's stamp, which included every book on these shelves. [132]

His friendship with Mara had sparked while they sorted books here as prisoners, when she filed the warden's memoir between Diseases of Cattle and Useful Knots. [158] They had laughed quietly, then spent their remaining shifts saving condemned volumes from the furnace. [173] He knew this because Mara had written it down; the man who remembered her laughter had vanished five personalities ago. [193] Tonight she steadied his ladder with both hands and said nothing about the moon. [207] That silence frightened him more than reassurance would have. [216]

He climbed down and offered her his coffee tin, with fourteen pennies rattling inside, as if that could pay for another abandonment. [238] Mara emptied the coins into his palm, pressed a dent near the seam, and lifted the secret false bottom. [257] Beneath it lay moonflower seeds and a folded library card covered in six different signatures. [272]

"You stole the seeds from the warden's garden," she said, "and I stole the card so we could start lending instead of hiding." [295]

Beside each signature was a task, not a promise. [304] One version of him had laid bricks, one had copied forbidden manuals, and one had refused to help but returned a stolen hammer. [327] Their uneven work had made this opening possible, and Mara had planted their smuggled seeds in the drawers of the guard's desk. [349] Along the shelving, vines he had mistaken for weeds held tight white buds. [362]

"Some of you stayed only an hour," she said, "but I never had to rebuild alone." [378]

Behind her, under the oilcloth, waited books on setting bones, repairing pumps, and growing vegetables in ruined soil. [396] At tomorrow's opening, neighbors would bring their children and their broken things, whatever the committees declared. [412] Mara called it a blooming resistance, but until now Ash had thought that was merely something kind to tell a poet. [433]

He looked at the doorway, wide enough for people to carry a table through without turning it sideways. [451] In prison, they had entered singly, with their shoulders folded. [461] He had chosen this width himself. [467]

The coil warmed against his skull as the moon began clearing the skyline. [480] Suddenly he wanted to write an order for whoever came next: Stay, finish, love her. [495] The wardens had left plenty of orders in his head, and he knew how little grew beneath them. [513] Instead he turned over the card and wrote, "There is room here, if you want it." [529] He tucked it above the false bottom, where the next man would find it without needing a friend. [547] He took the hammer from his belt. [554]

Then moonlight reached the threshold, and Ash lost the meaning of the woman's face. [568] The hammer in his hand felt like a threat until she stepped back. [581]

"My name is Mara," she said, keeping the exit clear. [591]

He knew what hammers did, what doors were, and that he did not want anyone telling him who to be. [611] By his boot stood an open tin with a card inside. [622] He read the invitation twice, then looked beyond her at the shelves, where pale flowers were loosening from their green fists. [643] Outside, a loudspeaker demanded more books for the bonfire in the square. [655]

"That lintel will sag," he said, and disliked the roughness of his own unfamiliar voice. [670]

Mara's eyes shone, but she only asked him where the brace should go. [683] He showed her, then held the timber while she drove the first nail. [696] With every hammer stroke, moonflower pollen sifted down onto his hair, gilding a man neither of them knew yet. [715]
