After the reef died, the sea climbed through the battlefield, leaving a narrow ridge of shell rubble between two immensities of water. [22] At twilight, Iven, the gloom-hill coachman, brought his empty carriage to the blooming jetty and unhitched his last horse. [41] Flowers nodded from tubs along the planks, each tub filled with earth carried down from the hills in his passengers' abandoned luggage. [63] Beyond that strip of color lay the ground he had promised to heal. [76]

Ada, the tide warden, waited beside a collection of dried seed pods arranged on his son's old collecting cloth. [95] There were sea rocket pods like little boats and horned poppy capsules longer than a child's fingers. [112] "Your tubs prove they can grow," she said, "but you cannot carry a whole hillside here." [128] He had tried sowing the ridge twice; both times the seedlings yellowed in the rain, then vanished. [145] The waves had stripped away the soil, and his horse could not supply enough manure to fertilize even the nearest acre. [166]

Behind Ada, beneath clear water, a bronze tablet caught the last light. [178] The submerged concord had once stood between the opposing gates, where prisoners crossed and neither side could claim the dead. [198] Iven had witnessed its signing after carrying the final casualties home. [209] He spoke the dead languages used for both armies' burial rites, and the generals had wanted their peace written in words older than their quarrel. [234] Now he read its last provision aloud, first in one tongue, then the other. [248] "Let the ground between us nourish neither victory nor revenge, but growing things." [261]

Ada recognized the cadence of her father's funeral. [269] "It was meant to be a garden," she said. [278] "It still is," Iven answered, although until that moment he had been considering scattering the remaining seeds into the sea. [298] His son had gathered them before the war, mapping where roots held the shore together. [313] Iven had carried ammunition over those roots, then carried the boy back under a sheet. [328] The carriage was all he owned now, and its enclosed darkness was the only place he could sleep. [346]

A flock of cormorants swept over the jetty, arguing. [355] Ada ducked, but Iven listened. [360] His son had taught him their calls during the long waits for passengers at the fishing stairs. [377] These were not the rough alarms they gave for hunting dogs, but the guttural complaints of birds disputing a roost. [397] They circled a wreck, settled, then rose together when its loose mast rolled. [410] Since the reef's collapse, every familiar resting place rocked or disappeared beneath the tide. [424] Far offshore they could still fish, but here they needed somewhere firm to spend the night. [440]

Iven looked from the birds to the white streaks beneath their abandoned perches, then to his carriage. [457] "Help me take the roof off," he said. [465] Ada laid a hand on the door, where his son's height was scratched into the wood. [481] "You could sell it for a room." [488] "Then I would have a room overlooking this." [496]

They worked until stars filled the narrow waterway beneath their feet. [507] He kept the marked door, but they lashed the roof beams across two surviving gun mounts at the ridge's highest point. [528] When they withdrew, the cormorants returned, one black shape after another against the immense, fading west. [544]

By winter the beams were white. [550] Iven slept in Ada's shed and carted the droppings away from the roost, mixing small amounts into pockets of sand and salvaged soil. [573] Too much burned the roots; too little left them pale. [583] He learned to measure, to wait, and to keep half the seeds against another failure. [598]

In spring, the first poppy opened between a broken firing step and the place where the border wire had run. [618] Ada wanted to ring the harbor bell. [625] Iven asked her not to frighten the birds. [633]

Instead, at twilight, they stood on the blooming jetty and watched yellow flowers move above the rubble. [650] There were only eleven, with miles of ruined coast beyond them. [661] Iven could cover the whole garden with his outstretched hand, but he lowered it and let the garden cover what it could. [683]
