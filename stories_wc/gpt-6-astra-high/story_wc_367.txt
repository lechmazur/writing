The last print newspaper lay under Mara's boot, its tide table already obsolete, its presses drowned with the rest of the harbor. [22] Above her, the emerald lighthouse waited under stars that should have faded an hour ago. [37] Morning had missed its landing. [42]

Mara, the laughing storm herald, knew why: the lighthouse was still inviting dawn into a city beneath the sea. [61] Her official work was to laugh when pressure fell, giving fishermen a warning they could hear through fog. [79] The prophecies were accidental, slipped into jokes she regretted afterward. [89] Yesterday she had told the printer, "Tomorrow your subscribers will all have waterfront rooms," and he had printed it above the tide table. [112]

Now she could see his widow among the boats below. [122] Nobody waved. [124] She had laughed at her own joke before she understood that a warning disguised as entertainment could travel everywhere and still save nobody. [147] Now the survivors circled the beacon, unable to see dry land, while the rising water swallowed its lowest stair. [166] If she could not call back the people she had failed, she could at least give their families somewhere to go. [187]

Inside, the dead keepers performed the otherworldly routine that had kept the lighthouse alive for three hundred years. [205] One emptied yesterday's darkness into a drain; another wound the clock that measured how far the sun had left to travel. [226] They were excellent at repetition, and helpless before anything that had never happened. [239]

Their lantern burned no oil; its glass lantern wick was a supple thread that gathered starlight from the roof. [258] Through total internal reflection, the light stayed captive inside the glass, even when the wick curved. [274] A lens projected it through the harbor's preserved shadow, laying a luminous channel toward the submerged quays. [291]

Mara took the wick between her fingers and tried to steer that channel toward the waiting boats. [308] The beam moved, but morning did not: the old shadow still promised streets, doorways, and a dry place to arrive. [328]

"We need a coast that can keep its promises," she said. [339]

The eldest keeper held out the newspaper, indicating its chart with a finger made of dust. [355] Every marked refuge was underwater. [360] But the chart ended at the city boundary, as though nothing beyond it deserved to exist. [376]

Beyond that boundary stood the mountains where her mother had been born. [388] She remembered her mother's contempt for the newspaper's weather reports: rain did not stop at municipal lines. [405]

"Show me the shore before there was a harbor," Mara said. [416]

The keepers stopped, offended by a request that belonged to no hour of their schedule. [431] The youngest looked down at his drowned city, then opened the archive. [443]

Inside lay ancient shadows, folded thin: vanished forests, the first breakwater, and the mountain coast before men cut its feet into streets. [465] Mara lifted that ancient shoreline beneath the lens. [473] Comparing its contours with the water on the lighthouse stairs, she found a plateau the sea could not cover. [492] Her laughter rose sharp and bitter when she tested the wind, but the storm's fury was passing; the plateau would hold. [513]

She could offer no restored city, only room for tents, gardens, and graves. [526] That would have to be enough. [532]

She bent the wick eastward, shaping a new dawn from the shadow of a world older than their losses. [551] Light streamed through the old outline, and this time the darkness ahead thinned. [564] On the horizon, morning turned like a vast, slow vessel answering its pilot. [577]

Below, the printer's widow raised a sail. [584] Mara laughed, and flinched at the sound. [591] For once it carried no warning, only an almost unbearable gladness beneath her grief. [605]

"There will be gardens," she called, then bit her tongue. [615]

But the widow had heard, and lifted a little sack of seeds. [627] The lighthouse steps were filling with water when Mara climbed down to join her. [641] Behind them, the dead keepers reset their clock for a coast they had never served. [656]
