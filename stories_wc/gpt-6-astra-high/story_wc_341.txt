After the string broke, the sky gondola rose so gently that Neve first mistook the motion for relief. [18] Then the severed tether struck the window, scattering seventeen years of dust from its frame. [33] Her apprentice, Tomas, reached for the balloon's gas valve. [42] "Not yet," she said, watching the lower skyport fall away beneath a powdering of starlight. [57] As an aviation meteorologist, she knew precisely how little air remained above them. [70] As a widow, she had spent seventeen years persuading the city to fund this single ascent. [86]

The city lost the upper skyport during the winter collapse, along with forty people, including Neve's husband, the night photographer. [106] They built a monument below, forty names beneath a stone balloon, but nothing marked the altitude where the people disappeared. [126] Neve wanted to memorialize that place, and to prove that empty spaces remember. [139] This was not entirely a widow's superstition. [146] At the frost ceiling, pressure disturbances persisted for decades, but official charts erased them as noise. [162] Neve believed those disturbances still held the contours of the ruined departure hall. [175]

She unpacked her husband's brass telescope, its barrel worn smooth beneath his vanished grip. [189] Modern scanners averaged away faint differences; its simple glass left imperfections alone. [201] Through it, the vapor ahead resembled wool pulled apart by patient fingers. [213] "Longing," she told Tomas. [217] He opened her notebook under that heading. [224] Categorizing clouds by emotion had begun as a private joke between Neve and her husband, then become her most accurate field method. [246] Anger climbed, grief folded inward, and longing stretched along pressure scars too slight for instruments to trust. [263] Tonight, the longing pointed toward an empty patch of sky. [273]

Tomas attached the panoramic camera to the telescope and began turning its film crank. [287] Her husband had called such continuous photographs spooled wholeness: a complete horizon wound into a little cylinder, with no privileged center. [308] Neve had brought his last unused roll, intending to wrap the lost hall around herself one final time. [326] But the empty patch remained empty. [332] Only along its edges did condensation gather, defining a roof, a railing, the curve of the stairs he had climbed for work. [354] The air had retained the building by remembering where clouds could not form. [367] Neve photographed the outline, then searched the landing for a human shape. [379] "Another turn," she said, though the camera had already passed the broken stairs. [392]

Tomas coughed and dropped the notebook. [398] Above him, a small cloud tightened around a darkness flickering with internal light. [411] Grief, she almost called it, because everything tonight wanted that name. [422] Then her training corrected her: suppressed anger, quietly explosive, the kind that discharged without thunder through anything dangling beneath it. [442] Its belly sagged to meet their rising balloon. [450] Opening the valve would save them, but descent would drag the unfinished photograph down through featureless darkness. [467] For one terrible moment, she preferred the picture. [475]

Then Tomas picked up her notebook and held it against his chest, trusting her to name what came next. [494] Neve opened the valve. [498] "Keep winding," she told him. [503] The gondola sank, and the film took in the blank descent, the faint lower stars, and finally Tomas's frightened face reflected in the glass. [527] A complete horizon, she understood, could contain an interruption without ceasing to be whole. [541]

By dawn, a homeward current had carried them to the lower mooring, where Neve developed the film in the watchman's cupboard. [562] The hall appeared as a border of pale fire around an absence, with no husband waiting inside to come home. [582] She submitted the measured outline to the chartmakers, along with the pressure readings Tomas had recorded before his hands began to shake. [604] Then she made a memorial from the entire roll, mounting it in a hand crank viewer beside the forty names. [624] Visitors turned through the vanished station, its darkness, and the face of the boy who had come home. [642]
