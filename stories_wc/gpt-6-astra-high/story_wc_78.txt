At the blue hour, Mara, the leeward tide prophet, watched two layers of cloud slide in opposite directions above her orchard. [21] Its twelve apple trees stood in soil crates lashed to a circle of driftwood, alone in the open seas. [40] Since the islands drowned, their fruit had bought everything she and her daughter could not catch or mend. [58] Mara had dubbed the raft Haven, and tightened its lashings whenever the name felt insufficient. [73]

Now the ropes creaked like teeth grinding. [80]

Nell knelt beside a loose timber, tapping it with the xylophone mallet that had belonged to Mara's father. [98] He had taught music before becoming a boatbuilder, and insisted that obstacles were merely instruments nobody had learned to play. [118] Then a reef had opened his hull, leaving Mara with his mallet and very little patience for metaphors. [136]

She wanted to tell Nell to stop, but the clouds changed their measure. [149] Interpreting their movements was not fortune telling; the high streaks carried the rhythm of distant swells, the low rags the pulse of currents. [172] Tonight those rhythms would meet beneath Haven. [179] Her rigid circle would snap between them. [186]

Already a seam opened under the nearest soil crate, spilling black crumbs into water. [200] Mara reached for another rope, then heard Nell's timber answer a second beam across the raft. [216] The two notes trembled together, briefly sweet. [223]

"It wants room," Nell said, nudging the binding with her mallet. [234]

Mara opened her weather book to the pages she usually skipped: her father's experiments in sketched resonance. [251] Each drawing translated a structure into the notes it could bear, showing where a blow became vibration rather than damage. [271] A hull was his simplest example; a living orchard, with roots gripping separate timbers, was horribly more complicated. [289]

But complexity had a method. [294] Fractal generating, he called it: repeat the branching of the whole inside each smaller part until every load has somewhere to travel. [316]

With charcoal, Mara drew the driftwood circle, divided it into branching paths, then repeated those forks through crates, roots, and boughs. [337] Above her, cloud against cloud supplied the approaching tempo. [346] Below, Nell tested each timber, and Mara darkened the paths that answered. [358] The sketch gathered into an instrument whose strings were roots, whose keys were wreckage, whose player would be the sea. [378]

Three black knots marked places where Mara's extra lashings strangled the pattern. [390] Cutting them meant trusting the very thing that had killed her father. [402] Keeping them meant pretending his death had proved everything else he knew was wrong. [416]

Mara put her knife into the first knot. [424] The circle loosened with a wet, alarming sigh. [432] By the third cut, water washed her ankles, but Nell's tapping had become a running phrase, each struck beam releasing the next. [454] Haven flexed instead of splitting. [459]

Then the first swell lifted them, and the orchard rang. [469] Apples knocked against branches; hollow wood boomed underneath; leaves hissed a thousand restless little answers. [484]

The second swell struck sideways before the first had passed. [494] One tree leaned seaward, its loaded crown pulling the rhythm out of time. [507] Mara's sketch had counted wood and roots, but not the ripening fruit. [519]

She seized a branch and shook. [525] Apples fell into the foam, bright coins spent faster than she could count, and the tree sprang upright. [543] Nell caught the returning beat with the mallet. [551]

The crossing waves moved through Haven as a shuddering chord, passing from timber to root to twig until their violence dwindled into trembling leaves. [575] Behind them, the sea shouldered onward. [581]

Mara stayed on her knees, listening for a crack beneath the fading music. [594] There was only the slap of water between timbers, and Nell breathing hard. [607] A month's provisions bobbed just beyond reach. [614] Mara could not make herself call that beautiful. [622]

She took the mallet from Nell, tested the loosened beam, and found its note lower than before. [639] In the last blue light, she corrected her drawing to include the weight of fruit. [654] Tomorrow they would need to mend the soil crate, ration breakfast, and learn what other notes their home could bear. [674] Tonight she handed the mallet back and asked Nell to teach her the answering part. [689]
