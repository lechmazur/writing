Through the hush of an overlooked era, Nara kept the shooting star tracking station clean while kingdoms elsewhere forgot the sky. [21] As its cosmic janitor, she swept silver grit from the telescopes and tended the furnace beneath the dome's great catchment basin. [42] The station had one rule: whatever fell and ceased moving was dead. [54] Nara obeyed because rules did not tremble in your hands. [64]

Once, before the silence, she had caught a small star and tried to force its fading heart to burn. [83] It died anyway, leaving her palms scarred and her confidence in tenderness broken. [96] Still spiritually curious, she kept a shard of yellow lantern glass from that vigil, though she no longer lit candles. [116] Sometimes she held it against her scars, wondering whether prayer meant asking for an answer or enduring its absence. [135]

Then the falling stars began to curve. [142] Their tracks bent toward the catchment, against the wind that usually scattered them across the plain. [158] Nara discovered the pattern by tracing the path of falling stars on the paper rolls the astronomers had left behind. [178] Every line ended beneath her feet, where the station registered only a growing weight of refuse. [194] At dawn, the full catchment would tip automatically into the furnace. [205]

She descended with a shovel, expecting the familiar brittle husks, but found the fallen things pressed together like sleeping animals. [225] Under the blue inspection lamps, they looked black and cold. [235] When she raised her lantern glass, its yellow tint cut the glare, revealing fine red filaments between them. [253] The filaments pulsed so slowly that she had to sit down to witness a single beat. [269] These were not corpses but seeds, drawn together into the station's sheltered basin, putting out roots where nothing disturbed them. [289] She looked at her shovel, then at the old ash welded to its edge. [303]

Above her, the furnace began its slow intake of air. [313] She could stop the tipping mechanism only by cutting the station's central drive, which also powered its lamps and heaters. [333] The astronomers had never imagined a reason to preserve what their instruments called dead. [347] Without the heaters, the basin would freeze before the seeds could kindle their own warmth. [362] For a moment she wanted the comfort of obedience, the clean floor and nothing left to answer for. [380]

Then a filament touched her boot and recoiled. [388] She knew that retreat, the wish to need nothing because need had once become unbearable. [403] She knelt and tried to empathize with that blind reaching, rather than measure how much of it she could save. [423] Her work suit stored enough heat for a long journey between stations, but its insulation kept every precious ember inside. [443] She could open it, although she could not know how long the nursery would need her warmth. [460]

Nara cut the drive. [464] The furnace sighed, the lamps went dark, and the enormous machinery settled around her with the sound of a beast lying down. [486] She unsealed her suit and lay along the basin's curve, drawing the fallen stars against her naked, scarred hands. [505] "I cannot make you live," she whispered, "but I can stay." [516] The cold entered without ceremony. [521] She shook, and did not mistake the shaking for failure. [531]

Through the long dark, roots threaded the folds of her suit and drank the warmth she had saved for escape. [551] As the reserve dwindled, she remembered the star she had lost, and at last let herself mourn instead of bargain. [571] She had spent an era trying to become invulnerable; now her life rested among lives as uncertain as her own. [591] Near dawn, when her breath had grown thin, something beneath her palm answered with heat. [606] Not a blaze, not a command to rise: a small, steady warmth passed from seed to seed, including her. [625]

In the dawn's glare, Nara lifted the yellow glass and saw a forest scarcely brighter than blood beneath skin. [644] This was flourishing stillness, neither death nor waiting, but life gathering the strength to give itself away. [661] When she could stand, she rewrote the station's rulebook, leaving generous blank spaces for whatever else she had failed to notice. [682] Then she sat beside the roots with her gloves off, keeping watch without asking anything to prove that it was alive. [703]
