While waiting for the king's answer, Irena Vale stood in the glass atrium beside a quiet brook. [17] The brook joined the border river just downstream, where soldiers were gathering displaced families for the noon deportation. [35] The court's dueling philosopher had won their appeal yesterday, but her victory required ratification before anyone was released. [53] Her mechanical arm still wore the dents of older victories that had changed equally little. [68] She wanted, now, to turn questions into destinations: not whether those people deserved sanctuary, but where they could find it. [88]

The enemy envoy passed her on his way into the royal audience, brushing something cold into her palm. [106] It was a Danish tin sewing needle card, with five needles clipped across its face and one empty slot. [125] Irena recognized the mermaid stamped on the corner; her mother had carried an identical card while sewing naval flags. [144] Danish cards had six precisely spaced slots, a ready key for the angular cipher her mother had taught her. [163]

Beside the empty slot, someone had scratched the outline of the half-painted sundial beneath her feet. [179] The envoy had sponsored its restoration for the peace conference, then inexplicably dismissed the painter halfway through. [196] Now she saw that the paint stopped at deliberate angles, not at the boundaries between hours. [212] She pivoted the five needles outward and aligned the empty slot with the gnomon, making a fan of sights. [231] Painted angles gave letters; bare angles gave numbers, measured against the needles with the protractor built into her wrist. [250]

The hidden overture emerged slowly: EAST SLUICE, SEVEN BOATS, ELEVEN FIFTY. [261] Then, in smaller intervals near the drain, ASYLUM WITHOUT OATHS. [271] The enemy was offering passage, not demanding conversion, enlistment, or a pledge that the rescued children would someday repay. [290] It might be a trap, but the deportation barges waiting downstream were certainly real. [304]

A boot settled on the card before she could lift it. [315] "You will surrender that correspondence to me," said Captain Voss, who had once studied ethics under her. [332] Around them, clerks continued exchanging memoranda while gardeners dragged orange trees out of the soldiers' way. [348] Nobody raised a voice; the atrium was a machine for keeping catastrophe presentable. [361]

Irena bowed with the exact courtesy required before a challenge. [371] "Then let us fence over your definition of duty." [380]

Diplomatically fierce, she offered him the ceremonial advantage: first blood would settle ownership, and he could choose the blades. [399] Voss glanced at the families beyond the glass, then selected the narrowest swords, useless against her armored forearm. [417] She understood that he was giving her a chance, but not yet his disobedience. [431] They exchanged salutes between overturned planters while a secretary solemnly shifted the conference minutes beyond sword reach. [448]

"You taught me that an unanswered question remains open," Voss said, driving her toward the brook doors. [465] "The king has not answered." [470]

She caught his blade in the joint of her mechanical wrist, where old damage made the gears grind. [488] "At noon, his silence becomes a destination for those children." [498]

For a moment she could have snapped his sword and taken another victory, leaving him no duty except obedience. [517] Instead, she released it and turned her exposed cheek toward his point. [529] The cut was small, precise, and entirely sufficient. [537]

"Your correspondence," she said, handing him the needle card, "and therefore your responsibility." [550]

Voss stared at the blood on his blade while she quietly gave him the decoded offer and departure time. [569] Behind the audience doors, the king was still keeping everyone waiting. [580]

At last Voss ordered his soldiers to escort the families upstream for inspection, carefully omitting whose inspection he intended. [599] Irena walked beside him, holding her handkerchief against her cheek as the procession spilled through the atrium's narrow doors. [618] Officials demanded receipts for displaced chairs, a child carried an uprooted orange sapling, and every soldier maintained immaculate formation. [637]

At the eastern sluice, seven shallow boats waited to cross the border river, their foreign flags folded discreetly beneath benches. [657] Voss helped the first grandmother aboard before asking what would become of them when the king discovered the deception. [676] Irena looked at the shore opposite, where someone had set bread and blankets on a long wooden table. [694]

"That question," she said, stepping into the boat, "has room for both of us." [708]
