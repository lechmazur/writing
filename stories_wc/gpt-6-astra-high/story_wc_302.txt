The first letter said my mother was dying; the next, her nurse promised, would tell me when. [17] I left supper untouched and pinned on the black veil I wore whenever I entered another person's dream. [35] A veiled dreamer can observe without being recognized; an uncovered face persuades dreams to become whatever its owner most desperately wants. [56] Tonight, that made the veil essential. [62]

My father had drowned saving me, and afterward my mother could barely speak when I entered a room. [80] For twenty years, I had supplied her silence with accusations. [90] Now I wanted to find the library of unspoken words before her shelves vanished. [104]

The nurse enclosed a lock of her hair, enough to bring me into the glowing library in mist that stood inside every living mind. [128] Its public rooms held everything my mother had said aloud. [138] Her recipes shone amber; her marriage vows burned almost white. [148] Beyond them, the catalogue ended in rows of blank brass plates. [159]

I had expected a locked door, not an unfinished inventory. [169] Then a lamp flashed above one blank plate, and another answered across the aisle. [183] The pause between them seemed longer than the distance warranted. [193]

In my pocket lay my father's confidence man's watch, the only thing recovered from his coat. [209] Its handsome face lied, but two concealed stop hands measured intervals precisely. [221] He had used them to time the answers of supposed spirits, actually an accomplice whispering through a tube. [239] I had hated loving a fraud. [245] Still, I wound his watch. [250]

Three beats passed between the nearest lamps, nineteen between the next pair, then five. [264] Matching numbers to letters gave me nonsense until I noticed the catalogue's breathing. [277] Its order was cyclically reversed: alphabetical while the library brightened, backward while it dimmed. [291] I recalculated, grinning under my veil, then stopped myself from celebrating too soon. [304] Dreams had fooled me with easier victories. [311]

Through propagation delays, the blank plates were transmitting an unfolding cipher. [322] Each decoded instruction directed me to another lamp, where my arrival started the next exchange. [337] The silence was not an absence in the catalogue; it was the road through it. [352] At every turn I felt the dangerous delight of discovering that an old impossibility had become a practical problem. [371]

With each step, the mist thinned and the lamps weakened. [381] Somewhere outside, my mother's heart was losing its argument. [390]

The final instruction led beneath the white blaze of her vows to a low doorway I had mistaken for shadow. [410] Inside stood thousands of thin books, their pages lifting in a wind I could not feel. [426] My name marked an entire shelf. [432] I almost called out to someone, anyone, to come see, before remembering whose breath kept these wonders lit. [450]

I reached for the brightest volume and found a single line: 'I was glad you lived, and furious that gratitude did not cure my grief.' [475]

My hand went to the veil. [481] One tug, and perhaps this room would give me the uncomplicated mother I had always needed. [497]

Behind that volume waited another, darker one: 'Sometimes your happiness felt like betrayal.' [510]

I flinched, but left my face covered. [517] These were not verdicts, only thoughts she had carried without finding a safe way to set them down. [535] I took out my notebook to document both sentences. [544] The books could not leave a dying mind, but observations made behind a veil could survive waking. [561]

As I copied, the shelf shuddered, and whole years dissolved into pale dust. [574] There were birthdays I would never understand, apologies I would never find. [586] I wanted to sweep everything into my arms. [594] Instead I finished the second sentence, including its difficult final word, and pressed the notebook shut. [610]

The library went dark before I could thank her. [619]

I woke at my kitchen table with the watch ticking against my cheek. [632] For a moment, relief rose so fiercely that I almost laughed; then grief arrived, neither cancelling the other. [650] I had not recovered twenty years, but I had stopped inventing them. [662]

When the second letter came at dawn, I took off my veil to read it. [677] It contained no last message, and, for the first time, I did not mistake that absence for an answer. [696]
