The living bridge garden shivered above the gorge, its willow roots carrying soil, silverleaf hedges, and everyone who needed to cross. [21] Iona, its hedge maze designer, knew each tremor through the soles of her feet. [35] Wind made the bridge sigh; footsteps made it murmur; lately, something underneath both had begun to sob. [52]

She had come to discover the texture of time. [61] Not its speed, not its price, but whether a year felt different from a moment when you held it barehanded. [81] Her mother, who had grafted the bridge's first roots, used to say that wood was weather you could touch. [100] Now her mother was buried on the far bank, and Iona wanted a less consoling answer. [116]

The halo fragment came from the garden shrine: a brittle arc of light left behind by its patron saint. [135] Viewed through it, a branch appeared as it had before its last season of growth. [150] Iona built that small backward glance into a walkable country through hyperbolic geometry. [163] Her paths curved apart, branching faster than ordinary space permitted, with the relic at their common entrance. [180] The garden accommodated them by folding yesterday between neighboring hedges. [190]

Her apprentice, Tern, noticed the fading photographs first. [198] Each successful trial bleached another patch from the portraits hanging in the tollhouse. [211] Iona recognized the exchange: the fragment spent old light to illuminate old time. [224] She brought her mother's photograph anyway. [230]

At the seventh turning, the bridge disappeared. [237] She stood on the bare riverbank where her mother knelt with the first willow cutting between her knees. [255] There was mud beneath her mother's nails and a split in her thumb. [268] Iona sank down, shaking so hard that the grass seemed to shake with her. [282]

"Show me your hands," she whispered. [288] But her mother only pressed the cutting into earth. [297] Each time she stepped away, she arrived before the planting, condemned to watch the earth close over those roots again. [317] She was retrocognitively trapped: able to witness what had happened, unable to reach anything that had not. [334] In her pocket, the photograph softened into blank paper. [343]

Then Tern shouted from somewhere beyond the remembered riverbank. [352] The bridge was unraveling beneath the afternoon market crowd. [361] Its roots were growing young again. [367] With the photographs exhausted, the fragment was consuming the sunlight stored in living wood. [381]

Iona reached for her mother and closed her fingers around empty air. [393] She could see only remembered branches; her body, Tern's terrified voice assured her, still stood in the present garden. [412] The relic still lay at the entrance, but every visible entrance led back to her kneeling mother. [428]

She wanted one touch, enough to remember skin instead of paper. [439] Her mother pushed earth around the cutting again, making a bridge for people she would never meet. [456] Iona shut her eyes. [460]

"Tern, keep talking," she said, and lowered herself onto the roots. [471] Her fingertips found a raised graft, then the ridged seam her mother had bound after a winter flood. [489] Neither existed in the young garden she could still see behind her eyelids. [502] She followed those scars against the remembered paths, dragging her knees over years made rough by survival. [519]

At the shrine, the halo fragment felt like sugar glass. [529] It had powdered her gloves during installation; she had handled it afterward as carefully as a newborn. [546] Now she enclosed it between her palms. [553] For one last trembling instant, she let herself imagine her mother's muddy thumb beside her own. [569]

She pressed until she felt the arc disintegrate. [577]

The garden struck the returning wind with every leaf. [586] Tern caught her shoulders while the crowd crawled toward the banks over roots scarcely thicker than their wrists. [604] Iona opened her eyes to an empty shrine and the ordinary, unbearable distance between trees. [619]

That evening, she took the blank photograph home. [627] She could not pretend the loss had improved it. [636] The next morning, she returned to repair the crossing before she planted another hedge. [650] When Tern asked what she had learned, she took his hand and guided it along the scar that had brought her back. [672] Under their fingers, the bridge trembled and held. [680]
