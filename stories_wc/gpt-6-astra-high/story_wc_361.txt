Neri carried relics along the aurora-lit ridgeway with both hands strapped to her satchel, so nobody would see them shake. [20] Seven winters earlier, her mentor Sella had vanished between the waystations; Neri still checked every approaching figure for her crooked gait. [41] Now the ridge council had declared Sella dead and commissioned Neri to deliver her recovered dragonfly-wing pendant to the memorial vault. [62] A glacier had surrendered it, wrapped around a finger bone. [72] The bone had already been buried. [78] The pendant was all that remained to be put away. [88]

At the first tollhouse, Neri fumbled her papers, and the keeper caught them before the wind could. [105] Their fingers touched the pendant through its torn wrapping. [114] Its silver veins began vibrating sympathetically with the tremor in Neri's hands, then with the old keeper's answering shiver. [133] Above them, a falling snowflake stopped. [139] The keeper had time to sit, rub his wrists, and tell her that Sella had always paid his toll in riddles. [160] "Bad ones," he added, and Neri laughed for the first time since receiving the bone. [175] When they released the pendant, the snowflake landed. [183]

Sella had once explained its trick: it extended brief encounters, but only between people willing to remain together. [201] Alone in her rented room that night, Neri clutched it and begged for one more minute with its owner. [220] The clock kept going. [224] She had wanted to ask why Sella had taken the winter road without her, whether her trembling had finally become a burden. [246] Instead, she slept with the useless pendant against her palm. [256]

At the next station, a woman repairing snowshoes recognized the silver setting and asked to hold it. [273] They shared a humming interval while a dropped needle hung beside the workbench. [286] "She waited here while my daughter was born," the woman said, "although the medicine was due beyond the pass." [305] Neri remembered that delivery: Sella had arrived late, exhausted, and refused to explain. [318] The council's memorial notice called her unfailingly punctual. [326] On the following day, a baker remembered her stealing a burnt roll, and a child remembered being taught to whistle. [346] At each meeting, the pendant made room for the part nobody put into reports. [360]

At the final station stood Sella's unfinished glasshouse, its benches piled with seed packets instead of plants. [377] She had bought it for waiting travelers, but after her disappearance, nobody could agree whose job the watering was. [396] "Coax wonder from the routine," she used to say, usually while handing Neri another thoroughly uninteresting parcel. [413] Neri had thought it was an instruction to enjoy her work. [424]

The vault keeper arrived with a padded box and a receipt. [435] Neri asked him to help her lift a fallen bench before they finished the delivery. [450] Afterward, she offered him the pendant, but kept her own fingers beneath his. [463] "Would you stay a moment?" she asked. [470] He nodded; the aurora stilled against the broken roof. [479] She told him about the riddles, the birth, the roll, and the question she would never have answered. [497] Then she asked whether a relic was safest where nobody could touch it, or where somebody would notice if it stopped working. [519]

The keeper inspected the cracked panes, and said the council would never pay. [532] Neri offered her delivery fee. [537] He fetched his measuring tape. [542]

By spring, the glasshouse was a public waiting room, and the pendant hung between two chairs beside its stove. [561] Travelers held it together while sharing tea; afterward, someone generally remembered to water the boxes. [576] Neri watched the oldest seeds germinate, their first leaves rising crookedly toward the repaired roof. [591] She kept carrying parcels, and her hands kept shaking. [600]

Across the next forty years, Sella faded from a woman people remembered into a saint who had supposedly commanded the northern lights. [622] Neri corrected what she could, then discovered that even her own memories had begun smoothing away the awkward edges. [641] She could no longer recall a single riddle. [649]

On her last winter route, a new courier found her resting among the pea vines, watching green light ripple overhead. [669] He apologized for being late, his delivery slips fluttering in nervous fingers. [681] Neri moved the watering can off the other chair. [690] "Tell me about the road," she said, and held out her shaking hand. [703]
