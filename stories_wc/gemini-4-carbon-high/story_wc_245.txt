Soren stood bareheaded at the border of the ultrasonic forest, unrolling a sheet of heavy vellum between scarred palms.  To ordinary travelers, these pale timberlands seemed dead and soundless, their crystalline boughs vibrating far above the threshold of human ears.  Yet to a blind mapmaker who absorbs languages through skin, these woods were ordinarily a searing furnace of speech.  Every trembling leaf pressed urgent dialects of sap, stone, and starlight straight into his pores until his forearms burned with borrowed grammar.  Tonight, however, he ventured inward across the pause of shifting constellations, that rare celestial hour when the migrating star wheels halted overhead before grinding into their winter alignment.  Deprived of moving starlight to translate, the canopy hushed its clamor to a low, smoldering hum against his cheeks. 

Soren had not come to emboss trade routes for distant sovereigns.  For decades he had charted mountain passes by wind pressure and coastlines by salt spray, welcoming the endless tide of foreign words into his epidermis because they left no room for his own silence.  After his husband Julian died in the autumn fevers, that refuge had hardened into cowardice.  Soren had lingered in noisy harbors so the speech of crowds would drown his skin, leaving his grief and surviving devotion as blank, terrifying parchment.  Now he walked beneath these boughs to navigate uncharted emotional depths, seeking the legendary locus where every external tongue was canceled out. 

Raising his right hand into the cool dark, he rested his thumb against the platinum compass ring Julian had forged for him.  Iron needles spun uselessly within the acoustic fields of the forest, but dense platinum answered ultrasonic pressure rather than magnetism, its notched bezel warming and rotating whenever pure harmonic currents crossed its band.  With measured, sweeping arcs of his arm, Soren began to dowse through invisible eddies between the trunks.  Faint idioms of root thirst and ancient frost brushed his bare wrists like drifting embers, yet he tuned his attention deeper, searching the hushed air for resonance older than syntax.  Twelve paces past the outer pines, the heavy ring kindled with sudden heat and tugged northward. 

A single sustained tone pulsed silently from a hollow quartz root, clean and wordless against his palm.  Soren read the notched bearing with his thumb, scored a raised ridge into his vellum with a silver stylus, and swept his hand forward again.  Beyond a mossy gully, the bezel rotated toward a higher fifth, and beside a cluster of glassbark saplings, a clear minor third answered it.  Step by step, he was tracing a trail of musical notes leading to a hidden melody, sounded by subterranean springs striking chambered stone beneath the forest floor.  Each pitch he dowsed lingered in the platinum band, braiding with previous intervals until a solemn, incandescent refrain hummed steadily up his bones.  Unlike the chatter of trees or mortals, music carried no vocabulary to hide behind, only unadorned truth. 

High above the sightless canopy, the heavens shuddered on the verge of motion.  Soren felt the warning prickle across his throat as distant ridges caught the first surge of resuming stellar drift and began shouting verbs of winter light.  When the stars snapped into alignment, the transitional roar would blister unshielded flesh until dawn calmed the boughs again.  Still, Soren did not break into panic; an unhurried, glowing resolve steadied his stride as he followed the swelling cadence into a sunken dell of silver ferns.  Around that basin, seven subterranean vents sang the complete refrain at once, their converging waves interlocking in exact phase opposition at the center of the glade. 

The platinum band flared hot for one heartbeat, then cooled to skin warmth as Soren stepped inside a hidden stillpoint.  Every external syllable vanished from his body instantly.  Beyond the unseen boundary, the constellations lurched into winter and the ultrasonic tempest broke across the canopy, yet within that narrow eye of canceled vibration, nothing touched his pores except motionless air.  Stripped of borrowed voices for the first time since infancy, Soren braced for an abyss of cold emptiness.  Instead, what rose beneath his ribs burned steady and luminous: the full measure of his sorrow for Julian, inseparable from the fierce, undiminished warmth of having loved him.  Knowing his embossed notes would guide him safely back at daybreak, Soren knelt on the silent moss and turned his vellum to its blank reverse.  With calm fingers and an incandescent heart, he pressed his stylus to the center of the page and began to map the glowing country within.
