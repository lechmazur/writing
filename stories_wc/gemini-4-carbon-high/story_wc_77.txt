Minutes after the resuscitation, Julian lay gasping on the warped floorboards of the abandoned carnival funhouse, tasting blood and sugared mildew on his swollen tongue.  His cracked sternum throbbed beneath the steady hands of Simeon, his whispering advisor, whose chemically scarred throat produced only a wet, intimate rasp against Julian's ear.  Outside on the foggy pier, the shuttered tintype booth where Julian's heart had stopped still smoked, feeding the radiant parasite that had stalked them indoors.  Julian was photographically cursed, haunted since winter by a ravenous negative born when an alley photographer's magnesium flash blinded him in the act of betrayal.  Now that living exposure drifted down the funhouse chute, steeping blistered clown murals and moldering velvet drapes in a lingering luminance that smelled lusciously of crushed lilies and warm pus. 

Even with his lungs raw from brief death, Julian yearned to crawl toward that gangrenous shimmer and press his mouth to its weeping silver skin.  The entity wore his own inverted features like wet silk, pulsing with an obscene, pearlescent glamour that made corruption look irresistible.  Simeon pinched Julian's chin, murmuring in a breathy hiss that dragging him into this crooked labyrinth of mirrors had been a calculated snare.  Because the funhouse glass bulged in warped, tarnished curves, it bent the creature's focal length and scattered its lethal glare into sluggish smears.  Still, distorted reflections would only delay the inevitable unless they acted immediately to trick fear into revealing its weakness.  Deep inside his damp coat, Julian's fingers closed around Eveline's silver opera cloak clasp, its needle pin pricking his numb palm. 

During the theater fire in December, he had wrenched that jeweled catch from Eveline's throat, letting her heavy garment tangle in burning scaffolding while he bolted into the camera's flash.  That alchemical burst had fused his panicked guilt with silver halide, birthing an externalized terror armored by the secret he refused to confess.  Pressing cracked lips to Julian's temple, Simeon whispered what he had witnessed seconds before and after the resuscitation.  While Julian's heart stood still, the specter had flickered in confusion, proving it could not process a state suspended between opposing truths.  It grew invulnerable on concealed dread, parting its blinding shroud only to devour a raw admission, yet confessing aloud would draw its lethal kiss before its core opened.  Because the phantom was half waking photograph and half subconscious nightmare, Simeon urged Julian to unbosom his sin through paradoxical notes in a dream dictionary.  The advisor slid his battered manual of sleep archetypes and a brass fountain pen into Julian's shivering grip. 

In the windowless pavilion, the only light to write by was the creature's own lingering luminance, which pooled across the floorboards like sweet, iridescent bile.  Julian angled the yellowed pages toward the nearest concave mirror, where the bloated reflection glided closer, drooling ribbons of hypnotic, ulcerated radiance.  Every nerve in Julian's revived body begged to drop the pen and bathe in that foul splendor, yet Simeon's soft, insistent whisper anchored his hand.  Turning to the printed entry for Smoke, which signified hidden disgrace, Julian began to unbosom his cowardice by scribbling that escaping the inferno had burned him to ash while Eveline survived whole inside his guilt.  Under Clasp, defined as fidelity, he wrote that stealing her fastener chained him forever to the doorway he had fled.  Beside Lens, he scrawled his final contradiction, confessing that the blinding flash had exposed nothing except the darkness he carried willingly. 

Aroused by the scent of fresh confession, the luminous horror oozed out from the warped silvering and draped its blistering, velvet limbs over the open book.  Its translucent fingers traced the wet ink, greedily developing the symbols of Julian's secret into its own shimmering flesh.  Instantly the paradoxes snagged its focus, forcing the creature into a violent double exposure as each dream symbol warred with its inverted confession.  With an alluring, mucous sigh that made Julian shudder with perverse desire, the phantom's dazzling ribs peeled wide to reveal a tiny, unexposed pinhole pulsing blackly at its center.  "Shut the aperture now," Simeon whispered fiercely against his neck, snapping Julian out of his rapturous stupor.  Ignoring the agony in his fractured chest, Julian lunged forward, thrust the pin of the opera cloak clasp straight through that naked focal point, and locked the catch.  The intoxicating glow curdled instantly into flaking gray soot that smelled of scorched sugar, leaving the funhouse mirrors dark, ordinary, and finally blind.
