In the heavy silence after revealing the secret, Mirel watched silt drift like grey breath across the forgotten underwater city garden.  Around her cracked plinth, dead kelp fronds nodded in the undertow, mimicking polite conversation among drowned marble benches.  Empty whelk shells twitched along the flagstones as blind currents inhaled and exhaled through rusted iron trellises.  Three paces away, Kael hovered in his weighted canvas diving suit, his rubber air hose pulsing like an exposed vein.  He had descended into the abyssal ruins convinced that northern raiders had shattered Veridia's glass dome three centuries ago.  Moments earlier, Mirel had dismantled that myth with a single memory: Chief Physician Vane opening the lower floodgates with her own steady hands. 

As a living statue with telepathy, Mirel immediately felt Kael's mind recoil from worship straight into righteous hatred.  Behind his fogged faceplate, his thoughts shouted that Vane was a traitor who had butchered a peaceful sanctuary.  Mirel tilted her pale limestone head, causing chalky dust to spill from her unblinking eyes like dry tears.  She had not awakened from centuries of cold slumber merely to let him trade one rigid idol for a convenient monster.  Her sole purpose now was to welcome nuance into a certainty that had calcified Kael's surface order for ten generations.  When she projected her silent voice into his skull again, her touch was incisively kind, paring away his defensive fury like a surgeon trimming dead tissue.  She asked him why a physician devoted to healing would drown her own conservatory while sealing the upper residential spires dry.  Kael shook his leaded helmet, insisting that madness needed no reason and demanding the sacred panacea hidden in this shrine. 

Veridia's founders had sculpted Mirel without seams around their final sample so that nothing could ever pry their legacy open.  Yet three hundred years of crushing ocean weight had split a jagged fissure diagonally across her carved sternum.  Reaching down with grinding stone joints, Mirel snapped a stiff copper tie from a dead vine frame.  Operating through structural weakness where unbroken perfection would have barred her, she began to thread the wire deep into her own fractured ribs.  Water sighed out of the narrow fault as the hooked metal caught an internal bronze latch and pulled.  A wedge of limestone detached from her chest, drifting downward like a shed scale to expose her hollow, flooded core.  Nestled inside that dark cavity where a living heart belonged lay a foxglove-stained glass ampoule. 

Mirel lifted the fragile vessel on her chipped palm and extended it into the cold beam of Kael's shoulder lamp.  Instead of the golden elixir described in surface hymns, dried residue clung to the glass in bruised violet spots.  Kael trained his chemical lens on the vial, and his breath hitched as he identified concentrated abyssal digitalis, a toxin brewed to slow the human pulse.  His thoughts flared with fresh certainty: the ruling healers had drugged Veridia's citizens into docile servitude.  Mirel pressed gently against his mind, releasing the psychic echoes preserved within the ampoule.  Kael saw crowds gathering willingly along these very paths after the Great Blight, begging Vane for drops to quiet their grieving hearts.  Dose by dose, the populace had voted to mute sorrow and passion alike, sitting motionless on these benches like warm porcelain while their city stagnated. 

Before Kael could condemn his ancestors as weaklings, Mirel turned his gaze across the terraced beds surrounding them.  Pale sea anemones now flexed like grasping fingers over thousands of petrified foxglove crowns.  This sunken enclosure had been the sole nursery where the hybrid sedative could grow, and Vane had drowned it, along with herself, to destroy the supply.  Deprived of their beloved poison behind the automatically sealed bulkheads above, the waking survivors had wept, raged, and climbed back to the sunlit world.  The weight of those cascading revelations settled through Kael, stripping away both his reverence and his scorn.  Inside his suit, his own unmedicated heart hammered loud and erratic, painful with grief yet fiercely alive.  With a clumsy gloved hand, he lifted the vial from Mirel's stone fingers and tucked it into his specimen pouch, neither as a holy relic nor as a traitor's brand.  Mirel felt the jagged halves of her narrative finally join inside his mind, messy and whole.  As Kael tugged his ascent line and rose into the gloom, the dead fronds bowed after him, and the cold tide resumed breathing through Mirel's open chest.
