Alden sits in the shadowed library at dawn, keeping watch over the damp master volume in the quiet first hour after the last dictionary is printed.  Beneath the stone floor, the great steam presses stand cold for the first time in eighty years.  Their stillness leaves a suspended hush across the dark galleries, as if the waking city dares not breathe before the Magistrates arrive to seal the book forever.  Beside his worn cuffs rests a broad, scalloped crystal from the chandelier high under the dome, shaken loose from its tarnished wire at midnight when the final press bolt dropped.  Though seventy winters of dust and obedience have bowed his shoulders, Alden turns the heavy glass with the wide, unguarded wonder of a boy holding winter ice to the sun. 

As a reticent scholar who speaks scarcely ten words a day, he has spent his life listening in dim aisles while louder men argued over rules.  His colleagues rejoiced to declare human speech complete, convinced that fixing every definition in lead type would spare the world further confusion and grief.  Alden never shared their tired certainty, for his quiet passion has always been to decode the pattern of growth that coaxes fresh meaning out of worn syllables.  To shield the archives from foundry soot, the tall lancet windows remain sealed, leaving only narrow floor grates connected by air shafts to the alley outside.  Ordinarily, any faint street murmur drifting up those ducts scatters immediately and dies among thousands of leather spines and thirsty paper pages.  Centuries ago, however, master glaziers cast each curved pendant overhead from dense lead quartz to be sonically reflective, bouncing spoken readings cleanly past the absorbent shelves.  Curious, Alden kneels stiffly and props the fallen piece at an angle beside the brass grate near his desk, aiming its concave face toward his chair. 

At once, crisp whispers leap from the polished surface into his ear.  Because thick stone piers divide the alley intake slits outside, passing voices reach him only through fractured sequences interrupted by footsteps and silence.  First comes the raspy marveling of an aging stoker gazing into the cooled press yard, muttering about a white cold bloom spreading across black slag where hot soot always lay.  Several minutes later, two shivering milk carriers hurry past the middle slit, their quick breath trimming the syllables as one warns the other not to slip on the colbloom.  Soon after, young sweeps cross the third opening, laughing softly at how the clear morning vapor colblooms their ragged sleeves with silver rime.  Alden jots each scrap in pencil with trembling fingers, his tired face brightening in gentle awe.  Had he heard one continuous conversation, he might have dismissed the word as an idle slip of the tongue.  Heard instead as broken steps across separate mouths, the law of living speech stands revealed: surprise joins two familiar nouns, haste wears down their seam, and playfulness turns the result into an action.  By halting the boilers to finish the book, the Guild created a frost the city had never seen, and that frost demanded a name the book does not hold. 

Heavy bronze doors groan open down the nave as three Magistrates enter through pale beams of amber light, carrying brass chains and heated wax.  Halting before the central podium, the Chief Magistrate summons Alden to testify under charter oath that no unrecorded forms stir within the common tongue.  Alden rises slowly, cradling the heavy glass against his threadbare coat while his pulse flutters after decades of habitual quiet.  He knows that a halting speech from an old clerk, delivered with only the weathered innocence of someone who still trusts small wonders, would be brushed aside as foolishness.  Instead of arguing, he walks to the podium, kneels beside its recessed floor vent, and tilts the smooth crystal toward the waiting officials.  "I testify that the language lives," Alden whispers, stepping back so the morning may finish his proof.  Into the deep silence of the rotunda, the curved facet sends clear bursts of alley chatter rising in broken turns: a baker praising the bright colbloom, a child shouting that sunlight colblooms the icy puddles into gold.  The Chief Magistrate lowers the ladle of cooling wax and looks down at the damp pages, searching in vain for a word born ten minutes ago.  Standing among the brightening shelves, Alden folds his hands, stained with ink and age, content to hear the unfinished world keep speaking.
