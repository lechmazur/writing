After the first snowmelt, cold chalky streams descended from the coastal ridges and dripped into the bronze grotto under starlight.  Cast generations ago by forgotten whalers, the cavern curved like the upturned ribcage of a leviathan, its verdigris walls open above to the constellations and open below to the sea.  Beneath those tarnished arches stood Maelis, the last fractal weaver, watching pale mineral lattices from earlier springs gleam like combed baleen in the dark. 

Up in the cliffside hamlets, villagers scattered hearth ash and salt onto the winter drifts whenever grief, debt, or unspoken remorse grew too heavy for a house to bear.  When thaw warmed the headland, runoff carried dissolved limestone alongside that leached human heaviness down through fissures into the vault.  Maelis remained below to collect the weights of different burdens, refusing to let the tide dissolve the long vigil of a widow and the cowardice of a captain into the same nameless brine.  Such delicate gauging required absolute stillness, which the chamber provided through its shingled hush.  Hundreds of overlapping bronze plates lined the curved walls like slate roof tiles, each bevel lapping over the next to shed the roar of the surf into layered scales of silence.  Protected inside that shingled hush, not a single tremor disturbed the air, allowing Maelis to feel every distinct gram of sorrow soaking into her cordage. 

She worked without a wooden loom, holding instead a salvaged brass kerosene lamp burner from the decade when cheap mineral oil emptied the whaling fleets and idled the old bone carvers.  Stripped of glass chimney and fuel fount, the burner served as both shuttle and scale: she drew bleached sinew up through its flat wick tube and turned the toothed thumbwheel to read the downward drag of each wet strand.  Because human sorrow never fell as a simple stone, an ordinary net would tear; she had to split every primary cord into smaller identical forks that repeated their parent angles at finer and finer scales.  Yet this geometry carried a severe cost, keeping Maelis boundary-lessly trapped at the center of her own craft.  No iron grate barred the seaward arch and no wall blocked the shore, but because her unframed fractal web anchored to her waist harness and subdivided endlessly toward its margin, every step outward only revealed smaller unfinished tributaries demanding her tension.  If she walked through the open arch while the threads remained supple, the entire canopy would collapse and spill winter unmeasured into the foam. 

Tonight the runoff surged harder than any in thirty years, pouring three distinct torrents down the central bronze rib at once.  Through the brass thumbwheel she distinguished their contrasting gravities: the plumb, crushing pull of a drowned fishing crew, the jagged lateral torque of a brother cheated of his land, and the broad, aching sag of a blighted orchard.  Maelis knotted tier after recursive tier until her knuckles shone pale as scrimshaw, sorting each burden into its proper geometric family.  Still the sodden sinew stretched dangerously low, dragging her boots toward the flooded floor as the leather harness bit deep into her ribs. 

To save the weave and free her spine from the live load, she had to make the filaments saturated with lime calcify before they snapped.  Fire would only char wet sinew into brittle ash; dissolved limestone locked into true structural bone solely by orienting properly within wind and starlight.  Raising the kerosene lamp burner overhead, Maelis sighted the spring zenith through its perforated brass gallery and rotated its slotted dome until the vent met the dry landward draft funneling down the cliff.  Bracing her heels against a sunken bronze keel, she hauled the guide cords taut, tilting all three fractal planes into that exact celestial axis.  By orienting properly, the branching meshes caught a steady whisper of night air through the burner cone while starlight struck every wet knot at an identical angle, seeding instant crystallization.  Frosty white veins raced outward from the brass wick tube as the burdened threads began to calcify, ringing with a faint skeletal chime as soft fiber turned to pale stone.  Within moments the crushing pull vanished from her harness, preserved forever in three freestanding arches that curved overhead like the ribs of sleeping whales.  Unbound at last until the next trickle of meltwater gathered above, Maelis lingered in a quiet whalebone reverie, resting her palm against the cool ivory curve of a sorrow faithfully weighed.
