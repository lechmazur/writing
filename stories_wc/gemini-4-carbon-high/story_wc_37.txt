In the mirror world, ordinary glass only turns light inward, trapping every lantern flame in an infinite corridor of solitary reflections.  For that reason, no glass roofed the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night upon the highest ridge of Oakhaven.  Instead, overlapping sheets of unpolished silicate formed its walls, culminating at the eastern gable in one vast mica window that held furnace heat within while permitting true radiance to pass outward.  Beneath those flaky amber panes walked Corin, a scattered memory keeper whose coat bristled with charred paper notes reminding him to breathe, to stoke the sulfur braziers, and to recall why he remained awake.  He had surrendered his own past decades ago to store the histories of forty snowbound villages, leaving his mind fragmented like dropped porcelain yet bound to one singular purpose, to rekindle a dying realm. 

Outside the warm mineral shell, frost crept steadily toward the valley hearths, silencing rivers and locking families behind drifts of black ice.  Since natural sunlight had perished when the prime sky severed from its reflection, survival depended entirely upon woven dawns spun from the distilled sap of the orchard.  Corin paused beside a row of trees grafted with quicksilver roots and phosphorus bark, blinking in confusion at the brass shears in his hand before consulting a chalk tally on his cuff.  He no longer knew the face of his mother or the sound of his childhood name, because every chamber of his memory overflowed with borrowed wedding chants, quarry rhythms, and shepherd calls.  Iron blades could not harvest these metallic boughs, for their sealed buds opened only through forgotten songs that vibrated at the exact pitch of human longing. 

Setting the useless shears aside, Corin knelt before the central cedar loom and pressed his palms against its taut warp strings.  A tremor shook his ribs as he unlocked a deep vault of his fractured mind and sang a river ballad lost to the flooded lowlands three generations prior.  With each resonant verse, the crushing pressure behind his temples eased in a rush of aching relief, even as the melody vanished forever from his thoughts into the humid air.  Above him, gold and cinnabar blossoms split apart, weeping luminous filaments of alchemical nectar that drifted downward toward the waiting shuttle.  Without pausing for breath, he braided a northern timber hymn into an eastern lullaby, guiding the incandescent threads across the frame until his blistered fingers smoked. 

This deliberate interlacing fulfilled the oldest law of the craft, for a dawn forged from shared ancestral voices knits communities together when its warmth finally reaches their roofs.  No isolated hamlet could hoard a private morning, because to feel the thaw upon their own stones, estranged towns had to welcome the harmonic echo of their distant kin.  When the final note tore from his raw throat, Corin cut the finished tapestry from the beam and caught the thrashing, blinding fabric before its volatile heat could dissipate.  Exposed prematurely to the freezing gale, a fledgling sunrise would fray into useless sparks.  Working swiftly, he drew heavy bands of leaden felt across the workbench to swaddle the newborn light, folding corner over corner and knotting silver cords around the bundle until its wild solar pulse compressed into a dense, captive core. 

Cradling the straining parcel against his scorched apron, Corin climbed the iron catwalk toward the gable while fierce golden rays bled between the tight wrappings.  Every step upward felt strangely weightless, for singing away those hoarded sorrows had emptied his crowded skull, leaving him tethered only by the warm burden in his arms.  At the summit, frost crackled across the outer mineral scales as he threw back the bolt of the great mica window, letting a blast of starless cold rush into the canopy.  For one suspended heartbeat, the keeper held the swaddled blaze on the sill, savoring the exquisite tension of power constrained by duty and deliverance poised upon a single knot.  Then he pulled the silver cord free and cast the unbinding cloth outward into the dark.  Freed from its tight restraint, the tapestry unspooled across the vault of the mirror world in leagues of crimson and amber, humming with forgotten harmonies as snow melted from distant bell towers and smoke rose from waking chimneys.  Below the brightening horizon, shuttered doors swung open across the valleys, and long separated neighbors stepped onto thawing roads to follow the familiar music back toward one another.  Corin latched the translucent pane against the morning wind, touched a fresh paper note pinned over his heart, and descended into the quiet orchard, still bound to the loom yet finding release in the light he had set free.
