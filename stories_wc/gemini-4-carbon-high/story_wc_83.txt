High in the stratosphere, Tariq pulled the manual override lever and plunged the floating observatory into freezing darkness.  Along the bulkhead, a row of overloaded circuit breakers glowed dull cherry red and began to tick softly in the sudden quiet.  Once those thermal switches shed enough heat, automated relays would snap shut and flood the gondola with the blinding electromagnetic hum of the main computers.  Only while circuit breakers cool in total electrical silence can delicate passive instruments detect what powered arrays erase.  Moving fast, Tariq gripped the frostbitten brass cranks of the primary telescope and began his hunt across the upper sky. 

Jagged shrapnel scars pulled tight along his jaw and ribs with every turn of the heavy iron gears.  Five years earlier, he had become that rarest statistical anomaly, a lone drone strike wedding survivor pulled breathing from a crater of white ash and shattered lanterns.  Grief counselors had urged him to accept that his bride, forty laughing relatives, and all their promised decades had simply vanished in a microsecond of combustion.  Trained as a theoretical physicist, Tariq rejected that neat annihilation.  If energy and momentum could never be destroyed, then the kinetic trajectory of forty severed lives had to displace something in the fabric of spacetime.  He had accepted solitary exile aboard this buoyant research platform for one secret purpose: to find where unused potential pools when human futures are violently sheared away. 

Searching directly for unlived time was impossible because such reservoirs emit neither photons nor thermal radiation.  Instead, Tariq stalked the phenomenon through perturbation theory, modeling the exact gravitational and refractive baseline of empty stratospheric air and then calculating minute, unexplained deviations in passing starlight.  Just as earlier astronomers had deduced unseen planets from tiny tugs on known orbits, he mapped invisible eddies of aborted probability by how they subtly bent distant constellations.  Normally, the station computers dismissed these fractional perturbations as lens flaws and smoothed them out of existence.  To bypass digital sanitization, Tariq had clamped a split geode crystal directly into the focal path of his manual interferometer.  Intended as the centerpiece on his wedding table, the stone had been cracked open by the missile blast, leaving its basalt rind charred black while thousands of violet quartz teeth gleamed immaculate inside.  Its hollow mineral lattice formed a natural piezoelectric chamber, capable of translating warped starlight into raw acoustic and optical resonance. 

Behind him, a cooling ceramic switch emitted a sharp metallic ping, warning him that his silent window was rapidly shrinking.  Beneath the vast gas envelope overhead, the suspended observatory swayed in a fierce polar gust as frost feathered across the observation dome.  Consulting his ledger of perturbation equations by the faint glow of a chemical wand, Tariq swung the aperture southward toward the jet stream rising from his distant homeland.  A fierce, wintry thrill surged through his chest as Orion blurred in the finder scope and sheared sideways across an invisible boundary.  Something immense was drifting along the tropopause, dragging at starlight with the phantom mass of millions of interrupted hours.  Breath pluming white, he locked the azimuth clamps and directed the distorted beam straight into the jagged mouth of the geode crystal. 

Instantly, the violet quartz facets flared with cold ultraviolet fire and began to vibrate at a low harmonic that rattled his teeth.  A crushing presence spilled from the resonant stone into the cabin, intangibly tangible as it pressed physical bruises onto his collarbones while remaining utterly empty to his outstretched hands.  The air grew thick with the phantom scent of roasted cardamom, summer rain, and bridal silk, weighted by the hydrostatic pressure of a million unlived mornings.  With no software algorithms buffering his human nervous system, Tariq endured an unfiltered awakening to the true scale of earthly slaughter.  Above every scarred valley and ruined city below, vast cold oceans of stolen potential pooled permanently in the upper atmosphere, close enough to measure yet forever unable to condense back into living flesh.  Across the bulkhead, the cooled relays slammed shut with a violent crack.  Harsh fluorescent lamps blazed overhead and ventilation fans roared back to life, instantly drowning the resonance of the crystal beneath ordinary machine noise.  Dropping to his knees beside the silent interferometer, Tariq laughed aloud through freezing tears, gripped by a bleak exhilaration.  The dead remained ash in the dirt below, irredeemable and lost, yet his equations had touched the crushing, glorious proof that everything they might have been still hung heavy among the stars.
