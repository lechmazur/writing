Towering banks of frozen vapor rose above the sunken valley like sheer glass mountains, gleaming with a cold and perilous grandeur.  Beneath those translucent cliffs lay a ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog, where petrified pear and apple trees stood encased in brittle rime.  Marek, the last master mirror maker of the upper wards, knelt among the skeletal boughs beside his wife Astrid just as the gray air began to crystallize.  No living citizen had ever beheld the real sky through the ancient gloom, yet Astrid had spent decades calculating vanished orbits from ancestral tables until exhaustion birthed a vision too bright for her sleeping mind to hold.  In the dense, mineral mist of the basin, such fevered visions escaped their sleepers as luminous mirages, luring the entranced dreamers deeper into the whiteout until both memory and life froze solid.  Marek had tracked Astrid down the treacherous ice slopes with one desperate purpose: to capture a runaway dream before full darkness dissolved its light and left her mind hollow forever. 

Their single window of hope arrived upon the shimmer of twilight frost, that fleeting, crystalline interval when plunging dusk temperatures turned the opaque vapor into millions of suspended ice prisms.  All around the dead grove, faint silver constellations suddenly flared inside the freezing haze, drifting upward along the towering walls of rime.  Astrid stood swaying in her sleepwalker trance, her unblinking eyes fixed above while her frostbitten fingers clutched an astronomer's broken sextant.  Her fall over a sunken boundary wall had snapped its brass arc and split its partially silvered horizon glass cleanly down the center.  Where his wife had grown weightless with obsession, Marek remained firmly grounded, a craftsman of heavy iron boots, plumb lines, and measured lead who trusted what bore real mass.  Wedging his heels deep between two petrified apple roots, he gently pried the damaged instrument from her grip and unwrapped a wet, unframed sheet of quicksilvered glass upon a flat orchard stone. 

An intact sextant normally relied on division, aligning a distant star beside a crisp earthly horizon, just as an ordinary mirror relied on a wooden frame to separate viewer from reflection.  Yet in this blind chasm of ice, any visible edge would slice Astrid's scattered mirage into dead fragments and startle the vision higher up the glacial slopes.  Marek understood that binding the fugitive light required erasing boundaries altogether: between the fractured lens and his borderless plate, between buried soil and phantom heaven, between waking craft and dreaming mind.  Bracing the astronomer's broken sextant directly against the bevel of his raw mirror, he tilted its surviving index glass to gather the dispersed glimmers from the airborne frost.  Piece by shimmering piece, he bent the stray angles inward to recreate the lost celestial vault inside the liquid silver at their feet. 

The instant those borders vanished, a terrifying optical abyss yawned open beneath the petrified trees, making the ground appear as bottomless and sheer as high peaks of spun crystal.  Astrid gasped at the sudden vertigo and lurched forward, sending a ragged plume of warm vapor across the stone that instantly scrambled the delicate stars.  Marek caught her waist with one steady arm, remaining firmly grounded against the dizzying pull of that infinite sky while his keen eyes read the crisis on the glass.  Unsealed quicksilver alone could not lock a frost refraction before nightfall; the plate needed a clear, microscopic glaze of condensed moisture to freeze over the image like quartz, yet uneven respiration would cloud the surface opaque white.  Pulling her close until her shivering ribs pressed against his wool coat, he guided her into stillness and worked the final seal through synchronized breathing.  Inhale by measured inhale, he slowed her shallow chest to match his calm workshop rhythm, silencing the turbulent eddies in the bitter air around the broken brass arc.  When they exhaled as one, their twin plumes merged into a single laminar veil that glided smoothly over the silver, laying down a flawless film of diamond ice without disturbing one suspended prism. 

A faint, musical chime rang across the plate as the glaze hardened permanently, trapping every wheeling constellation inside the mirror just as true night extinguished the orchard haze.  Above the dead boughs, the heavy gray curtain rolled silently back down from the invisible ridges, burying the crystal cliffs once more.  Astrid blinked rapidly, color and lucidity rushing back into her face as she looked down at the captured heavens burning steadily in her husband's hands.  Carrying that shard of unbroken cosmos like a lantern against the ancient gloom, the mirror maker and the astronomer climbed together toward home.
