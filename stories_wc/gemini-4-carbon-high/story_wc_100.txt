Silence never lingers long along the drowned archipelago, especially in the gray hour after the mysterious package arrives upon the soaked timbers of the jetty.  Oren stands above the dripping crate wrapped in tarred canvas, a weary undertaker whose knees and lungs ache from forty winters of tending what the ocean refuses to swallow.  In these waters, shipwrecks leave behind more than splintered hulls; their final terrors endure as roaming distress signals, pale knots of flickering light and clicking static that scrape against living minds.  All his adult life, Oren has inhabited a fragile, chased stillness, rowing into fog banks and sealing his shutters in a breathless flight from the next insistent pulse hunting for a witness. 

Whoever abandoned this unmarked box fled before sunrise, leaving only the frantic hum vibrating through wet pine boards.  When Oren pries loose the lid with his steel lever, harsh white sparks spill across his gloves from a broken lighthouse lens.  Sheared from the coastal beacon during the deluge a century ago, the jagged crescent of crown glass seethes with dozens of trapped cries from the night the harbor drowned.  Any sensible man would shove such a deafening relic off the pilings and walk inland until the sea became a memory.  Oren, however, is inadvertently stubborn; he possesses no heroic fire, only the ingrained habit of an exhausted clerk who finds abandoning an unfinished ledger harder than making one last descent. 

Bolting his heavy diving helmet over his shoulders and slinging the blazing prism in netting, he steps off the ladder into green gloom.  Lead soles carry him straight down through the cold thermocline toward the forgotten underwater city library.  Decades ago, discovering that panic persists only while it believes itself unfound in trackless water, Oren claimed these submerged marble stacks to give rest a permanent address.  Whenever he bottles a stray signal and wedges it beneath a chiseled aisle and call number, the frantic transmission registers its exact location, accepts that someone has recorded it, and goes dark.  Down here, muffled by thirty fathoms of black water, hushed calm usually holds between the granite tiers, save for the rasping wheeze of his air hose and the prism shuddering against his ribs. 

Yet as his weighted boots thud onto the mosaic rotunda past the bronze doors, cold dread arrests his stride.  A deep winter current has undermined the outer archways, toppling four colossal catalog walls into drifts of suffocating gray silt.  Hundreds of glass vials have shattered under fallen masonry, freeing older distress signals that now dart beneath the cracked dome like blinded minnows, their carefully assigned shelves erased.  Worse still, the broken lighthouse lens in his arms catches their loose rhythms and resonates like a struck bell, filling his copper collar with such piercing clamor that Oren sinks to one knee.  No intact alcoves remain standing in the ruin, and the sleepless din he has outrun for decades closes in from every shadowed vault. 

Even with his temples throbbing and silt clouding his viewport, his plodding, obstinate nature refuses to let him drop the burden and signal for the winch.  Blinking sweat from his eyes, he watches one panicked wisp strike the raw, fractured plane of the heavy prism.  An unbroken beacon was ground to hurl light outward across stormy leagues, yet this jagged cleavage bends the beam past its critical angle, folding radiance inward through endless internal reflection.  Peering into the thick crystal, Oren sees the single surviving index pillar behind him mirrored and multiplied into vast, dry corridors stretching deep inside solid glass.  The stray spark slips into one reflected niche and stills at once, occupying impossible spaces that possess strict geometric coordinates yet zero physical room for crushing tides or mud to invade. 

Understanding steadies his trembling hands as he heaves the crescent onto the central reading pedestal, angling its sheared facet until the carved numerals of the standing pillar repeat to infinity within the core.  With his iron stylus, he scratches a master section code along the brass rim, using a shattered instrument of warning to revitalize the ruined sanctuary far beyond its stone limits.  Drawn toward those unbreakable optical halls, every homeless flicker circling the dome streams through the glass beside the old harbor wrecks, each pulse finding a numbered reflection and winking peacefully out.  Oren leans against the pedestal as the ringing in his skull finally dissolves, no longer running ahead of the noise, resting at last beside a silence that knows where it lives.
