The vanguard tempest rolled southward in a bruised curtain of violet rain, leaving a saturated hush suspended across the chasm.  Kneeling alone upon the floating rock tea ceremony platform, Maret drew one deep lungful of damp air and sealed his lips.  Around the mossy rim of the hovering basalt slab, stray droplets hung almost motionless in the thick updraft, while emerald steam curled straight upward from a cast iron kettle.  This narrow eye of calm offered nothing more than a breath between storms, scarce fifty heartbeats of velvet equilibrium before the trailing gale slammed into the peaks.  Because even a faint sigh would scatter the resonant tea vapors and invite static lightning inward, every stroke of his rite had to unfold during a held breath. 

Slowly, so his wet robe scarcely whispered against the stone, Maret extended his injured left forearm beside the ceramic bowl and inkstone.  As a budding rune scholar at the Skyreach Collegium, he had miscast a weather ward six months ago, searing his skin with raw aether.  Such spellburn left behind pale stormglass, a ruthless affliction of runic scar tissue that crystallizes when stressed.  Already, under the heavy silence pressing against his eardrums, translucent amber facets crept past his wrist and locked two fingers stiff with a submerged, crystalline creak.  Collegium healers wanted to chip the mineral away with iron chisels, leaving his arm numb and mute, or else expel him before the seasonal convocation.  Maret had tethered this levitating sanctuary inside the cyclone for a quieter purpose, determined to transform scars into stories that could live and bend upon his skin. 

His right hand hovered first over his academy silver stylus, while his mind rehearsed the strict geometric equations taught in lecture halls to suppress wild magic.  Instantly, the memory of stern examiners and the blinding terror of his past mistake made his pulse hammer against his ribs.  Responding to that spike of dread, jagged quartz plates surged upward toward his elbow, binding his palm flat against the drenched stone.  Twenty heartbeats had vanished, and trapped air began to ache warmly behind his breastbone.  Orthodox theory demanded fearless mastery over trauma, yet straining for cold perfection only multiplied his panic and fed the creeping stone. 

Pulling his fingers back from the rigid silver tool, Maret closed his eyes for one beat and sought grace by revisiting youthful superstitions from his childhood in the mist valleys.  Long before he wore scholar wool, his grandmother had whispered hearth charms that city masters ridiculed as ignorant folklore.  Valley children believed scars were frightened spirits who needed a shared cup of bitter tea before they could speak their names, and that sharp metal points only provoked trapped thunder.  Folklore also claimed that wild black crows swallowed lightning unharmed because their feathers yielded softly to the gale, provided a child blunted the point so no ghost felt threatened.  Trusting those tender, foolish customs, Maret tipped his ceramic bowl to pour three silent drops of green tea onto the moss for the watching clouds, then tapped his collarbone twice for safe passage. 

Beside the warm brazier lay a fallen feather he had prepared in secret, its hollow shaft pared flat with a knife to form a gentle brush.  Dipping that crow quill trimmed tip into a thick blend of steeped tea leaf and pine soot, Maret lowered it onto the highest crystal ridge.  Instead of carving cages of scholar logic, he traced fluid pictorial runes of village storytelling, confessing in dark ink how an eager boy reached too greedily for the sky and survived the fall.  Because childhood ritual welcomed fear like an old neighbor rather than fighting it, the knot of tension uncoiled inside his chest despite the burning demand of his lungs.  At once, within the drenched stillness of the mist, the amber petrification halted its march. 

Fragrant tea pigment soaked downward through the microscopic fractures, drinking the trapped static out of the mineral lattice and warming the cold facets from within.  With a sound no louder than snow slipping from a pine bough, the brittle husks loosened from his flesh and shed onto the wet basalt in harmless, glittering scales.  Maret flexed his freed fingers, marveling as whole, warm skin bent smoothly beneath intricate dark glyphs that recorded both his folly and his mercy toward himself.  Beyond the hovering ledge, the indigo cloud wall trembled with a deep, muffled rumble as the second tempest surged inward to reclaim the sky.  Sweeping the shed husks into his sleeve, Maret lifted the warm bowl to his mouth and let his long breath flow softly into the returning wind.
