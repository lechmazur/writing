Beneath the terraced camellias of the floating island tea garden, twilight arrived from below.  As the sun dipped under the endless sea of clouds, upward light struck the granite belly of the isle and spilled through the leaves.  This was the sacred hour after the shadow detaches, when every dark silhouette peeled loose from human heels and mossy flagstones to drift into the violet sky.  Mourners traveled leagues to sip narcotic silver tea among these terraces, waiting until that unmoored moment when the glass basilica on a floating isle opened its crystal nave for dream recording.  Its vaulted ribs focused the inverted dusk onto raw beryl stones beside each cot, trapping memories of the dead in a state of luminous torment.  Every recorded stone blazed with blinding beauty and scorching pain, fusing remembered love with the searing ache of absence. 

Freed of its shadows, the buoyant island began to climb toward the freezing stars, triggering the great counterweight at its core.  Suspended on bronze chains inside a central chasm, the heavy sanctuary lowered toward the cloud abyss to ballast their climb, creating a shrine that descends while ascending.  Because raw sorrow carried physical mass, clerics purged the heaviest gems through a floor hatch once the sinking nave cleared the rocky keel, dropping unbearable memories into oblivion so the island would not fall.  Inside that lowering crystal hall moved Sen, a crafty lantern maker who maintained the brass lamps of both garden and shrine.  One night earlier, he had slept beneath these arches to capture his fading dreams of his late daughter Mira, only to see the Archivist condemn his beryl to the purge for its crushing weight.  Feigning repairs beside the condemned trough, Sen used a trick sconce lined with angled mirrors to cast a false, dazzling glare over a hollow glass bead.  With nimble sleight of hand, he swapped his glowing decoy for the blistering stone, tucked it into his leather apron, and leaped from the choir balcony onto the lowest garden terrace just before the basilica sank beneath the rock. 

Sen stumbled up the winding steps between fragrant tea bushes and porcelain cups still steaming on low lacquer tables.  Against his chest, the uncut beryl burned like a captive star, dragging his spine downward while jagged flashes of her final fever pierced his eyelids alongside the sweet echo of her laughter.  He knew the clerics were half right: left raw and hoarded, such radiant anguish would crack his floorboards and pull him over the cliff.  Yet he refused their cruel oblivion, driven instead by a quiet vow to fold grief into origami cranes that could carry her memory skyward without dragging the living down.  When Mira was small, she had folded crooked paper birds beside his lantern bench, believing they could ferry wishes across the clouds. 

Reaching his open pavilion beneath an ancient camellia tree, Sen turned to a secret art unknown to priests: gem polishing.  While clerics treated dream stones as unalterable relics, a lens grinder understood that rough crystal trapped light in chaotic, crushing knots.  He arranged crisp squares of translucent lantern vellum beneath his treadle wheel, pumped the pedal with his foot, and pressed the searing beryl dry against the spinning slate lap.  White sparks lashed his bare knuckles, searing his skin with every splintered vision of her sickbed, yet he held the angle steady through tears.  Facet by polished facet, his lapidary geometry forced the captive dream to transform.  As the rough rind ground away into shimmering dust that settled onto the vellum below, the unbearable gravitational pull bled out of the stone, untangling feverish agony from pure remembrance. 

When sixty flawless facets gleamed in his blistered palm, the beryl weighed no more than a dried tea leaf and shone with gentle golden clarity.  Sen mounted the cut gem inside an open brass lantern frame, where its focused rays projected clear, painless images of Mira chasing white moths between the camellias.  Rubbing the luminous crystal powder deep into the vellum fibers, he creased each glowing sheet with practiced, reverent care, shaping slender wings, proud necks, and delicate tails.  As each finished origami crane passed through the faceted beams of the lantern, the dust in its paper drank the warm light and trembled alive.  Buoyed by the radiant warmth of refined memory, a dozen translucent birds lifted from his scarred hands, circling the quiet tea tables in silent benediction.  Below the cliffs, the basilica dropped his hollow decoy harmlessly into the dark, while above the blossoms Sen watched his flock of bright sorrow rise weightlessly toward the stars.
