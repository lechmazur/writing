Soft now, step lightly through the humming dark, where tall reeds purr like fed wolves and bare silver teeth at the wind.  Ivo waded into the sound wave garden past midnight, arriving in the brief, lawless season when patterns break themselves.  All year the basin grew in tidy rows of standing resonance, with low bass moss cushioning the gullies and treble stalks humming in strict meter.  Tonight every rhythm had slipped its leash; frost crept in crooked spirals, creeks ran uphill, and the acoustic flora knotted into barbed thickets of noise.  Overhead, the sky's ancient geometry had fractured too, letting the sleepwalking constellations wander down from their black pasture to graze among vibrating reeds. 

Fifty paces ahead, seven amber stars bound by tendons of pale fire nosed blindly through a drift of humming fern.  Shepherds called that dreaming beast the Milk Wolf, and its sealed eyelids twitched with furnace heat as it breathed slow chords across the hollow.  Deep within the cage of its ribs swirled a tight silver eddy of sound, the sole quarry Ivo had come to claim.  He needed to catch the echo of a goddess who had sung the first creatures to sleep ages ago, before iron empires split her hills and tore her throat.  Since the patterns broke three nights back, nobody in Ivo's ridge village could close an eye, and his infant daughter lay rigid in her cradle with her tiny heart racing toward ruin.  Only that dead mother's lingering croon, fierce with fang and warm milk, carried weight enough to press fevered blood into rest. 

Any ordinary stalker nearing a celestial predator would have wept or fled from the raw terror radiating off its pelt.  Ivo, however, remained a cool-headed fisher, testing his spool of braided copper wire with steady thumbs as if working a dark trout stream.  He owed this icy poise to a rare quirk of mind that transliterates emotions into dry phonetic script before his pulse can feel their sting.  When primal dread rolled off the Milk Wolf's burning shoulders, Ivo did not shiver; his brain simply spelled the panic across his palate as the crisp consonant cluster khrath.  When sharp grief for his sleepless child rose in his throat, it resolved cleanly into the flat vowel syllables vaulom, sorted as neatly as hooks in cedar bark.  Unhurried, he hefted the rail spike tied to his copper line, a rusted iron wedge pried from the ruined tracks that once scarred these hills.  Forged to pin wooden sleepers down, dead iron neither gleams nor rings, making it the only sinker dull enough to slip past dreaming stars while drinking stray resonance into its notched sides. 

Timing the beast's slow inhale, Ivo swung his arm smoothly and cast.  The rail spike arced over a hedge of whistling harmonics and dropped between two fiery ribs, plunging straight into the silver pocket of the goddess's voice.  Even in slumber, the Milk Wolf flinched at the bitter, remembered taste of railway iron inside its chest.  Snarling a low chord that flattened the moss, the constellation bolted across the fractured basin in a violent zigzag.  If the taut copper wire grazed a single treble briar, the line would shriek like a fiddle string, waking the star beast to burn the valley to ash.  Paying out slack over calloused knuckles, Ivo sprinted into a matching zigzag, darting left down a trough of velvet bass before cutting hard right beneath a whipping crest of soprano static. 

When the Milk Wolf finally slowed in bruised fern, panting white embers, the skittish echo inside its ribs still refused to bite the iron notches, held back by the garden's ambient roar.  Ivo dropped to one knee in the trembling dirt and clamped the copper wire firmly between his back molars.  Up through his jawbone surged a wave of ancient maternal fury and fierce longing, which his mind instantly converted into the stark phonetic phrase khrath vaulom shul.  Any audible note would travel down the wire and snap the wolf's eyes open, so Ivo landed his catch by voicing silence.  Holding his breath dead still, he shaped his hollow cheeks, tongue, and lips around khrath vaulom shul, pronouncing those transliterated passions as exact cavities of pure quiet.  In a garden built of pressure waves, sculpted emptiness pulls harder than any barbed hook.  That sudden vacuum rushed down the copper line into the beast's chest, drawing the goddess's wild lullaby fast into the hungry rail spike while the unburdened constellation sighed and drifted skyward.  Reeling the warm iron into his palm, Ivo felt its captive purr of fang, dark pine, and heavy eyelids, then turned upslope to hush his daughter to sleep.
