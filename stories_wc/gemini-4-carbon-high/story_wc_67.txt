High upon the eroding limestone cliffs of Vael stands the vanishing point gallery, a long stone hall built so its slanting colonnades and angled skylights converge upon a single open arch above the sea.  Near its wide entrance, Oren works silently at a wooden easel as the town's last charcoal portrait ash scatterer, serving families whose dead were claimed by the waves below.  A few paces away, Maren sways steadily in an old rocking chair facing the vestibule wall, keeping her back turned to the distant drop.  Her face remains hidden behind a mask made from shattered porcelain shards laced together with thin brass wire.  In Vael, mourners customarily wear smooth white visors to conceal their weeping, trusting unblemished clay just as they trust stone cottages perched on crumbling ledges.  When last month's cliffslide swallowed her husband Callum and split their hearth, Maren refused a new visor, binding the jagged pieces of her broken heirloom over her skin to preserve the mirage of safety. 

For an hour she has spoken only in bright, guarded fragments while the curved oak runners creak beneath her in a soothing cadence.  She recounts how Callum latched their shutters against winter gales and hummed beside his potter's wheel, omitting any mention of his final dread or the fissures creeping across their yard.  Because no painted miniature survived the collapse, Oren must reconstruct a stranger's likeness through incomplete information, attending as closely to what she withholds as to what she shares.  He is an artisan who weaves truth carefully out of evasions, hesitations, and sudden catches of breath.  Noting how Maren tilts her chin upward whenever she recalls Callum's laughter, Oren draws a tall man with a gentle stoop formed by years of leaning down to listen.  Hearing her voice tighten each time sea wind whistles overhead, he shades fine creases of sleepless vigilance around Callum's eyes and softens the mouth into quiet, mortal courage. 

Oren could easily sketch the fearless guardian she describes, or hand her the finished flax sheet to hoard behind locked doors.  Yet static monuments soon harden sorrow into idolatry, trapping the bereaved inside a painless fable that never existed.  His rite exists to crystallize a fleeting emotion: that rare, luminous instant when grief drops its defenses and beholds love in its full, perishable truth before numbness returns.  Setting his willow charcoal upon the ledge, Oren steps aside and murmurs that the portrait is ready.  Behind him, the wooden rhythm falters.  Maren grips the smooth armrests, pushes herself upright, and turns toward both the easel and the tapering depth of the gallery beyond.  Freed of her weight, the vacant seat tilts far back and begins a long, fading oscillation across the flagstones. 

Oren holds the gossamer flax sheet aloft in a shaft of late sunlight.  Through the narrow slits of her wired porcelain, Maren sees Callum rendered neither as an invulnerable pillar nor as a crushed victim, but wholly human in his fear and devotion.  A trembling breath echoes inside her pieced visor, where sharp ceramic edges rest perilously close to her eyelids and cheeks.  One violent sob would press those glazed splinters into her flesh, exposing her salvaged armor as a hazard disguised as refuge.  With shaking fingers, she reaches behind her head, unhooks the brass clasp, and lowers the broken shards to her side.  Her bare face glistens with silent tears, yet glows with hushed, astonished relief, as though witnessing Callum's vulnerability has finally granted her permission to endure her own. 

Seeing that fragile grace crest in her eyes, Oren dips the corner of the portrait into a shallow copper brazier's waiting flame.  Pale fire sweeps whisperingly across the treated flax, consuming the sheet in two heartbeats and leaving a cool, delicate wafer of blackened carbon on the dish.  Oren lifts the brittle remnants and rubs his calloused palms together to powder the charred fibers and charcoal strokes into weightless velvet dust.  Stepping between the first converging columns, he opens his hands wide to the steady seaward draft funneled down the narrowing corridor.  Instead of falling, the dark ash rises into a spiraling plume that catches the slanted sunbeams like a constellation of drifting embers.  Maren watches in breathless stillness while the shimmering ribbon narrows along the gallery's sightlines, brightening until it slips through the distant arch into open sky just as the old rocking chair finally slows to a stop.
