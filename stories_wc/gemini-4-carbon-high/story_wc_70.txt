Six weeks after the citywide blackout darkened the streets below, dusk settled around the lightning collection tower like warm wildflower honey poured across bruised plums.  High above the smokeless chimneys, giant copper ribs hummed with static, drinking amber light from gathering thunderheads.  Halfway up the spire sat Amos, the candid toll keeper who collected brass washers from weary citizens climbing to recharge their household cells.  Twenty years beside the tower's massive induction coils had altered his temporal lobes, rendering him involuntarily truthful whenever he opened his mouth.  He could neither soften a hard fact nor invent a gentle lie, a condition that often made him wince at his own voice. 

Watching his neighbors trudge past the turnstile in joyless silence each evening had awakened a quiet ache in Amos, a fierce desire to preserve wonder in routine.  Survival had reduced every twilight climb to dull drudgery, and his blunt speech usually stripped away whatever mystery remained.  At six fifteen sharp, Senior Technician Lin clanked onto his landing right on schedule, a heavy thermal purge torch slung across her shoulder.  Dropping her brass washer into his tray, she sighed and asked if she looked as exhausted as she felt.  "Your eyelids are gray from sleep debt, your left boot is splitting, and your coat smells strongly of rancid tallow," Amos blurted out before grimacing in apology.  Lin managed a dry chuckle, long accustomed to his affliction, and tapped the fuel cylinder at her hip.  She explained that telemetry had logged a complete drone beehive failure on Strut Four, where a thousand tiny brass collector drones had stopped depositing cloud charge into their capacitor combs. 

"You walked the upper catwalks at noon, so tell me what jammed the ports," she began.  Amos clapped both leather gloves over his lips before his reflex could betray him.  If he answered aloud, his clinical candor would reduce an astonishing afternoon discovery into a mundane pest report, and Lin would simply incinerate the evidence without ever truly seeing it.  Even on paper his neurology demanded strict accuracy, yet he had spent two careful hours bending literal truth into verse, the one loophole his mind allowed.  Reaching beside the rationed candle supplies on his shelf, he slid a matchbox containing a cryptic riddle across the brass counter and gestured upward.  Baffled by his silence, Lin pushed open the tiny wooden drawer, unfolded a scrap of ledger paper, and read, "We stole no spark yet sealed the wire, distilling dusk without a fire, now housed in crimson storms ignore, beside the brass we starved before." 

Intrigued despite her fatigue, she tucked the matchbox into her pocket and climbed the open iron stairs into the syrupy evening air.  All around the spire, the horizon bled thick gold and violet, steeping the silent struts in a luminous nectar dusk.  Beneath the sharp tang of ozone drifted a warm floral sweetness from the canyon floor, where dead factory furnaces had inadvertently allowed wild cliff clover to bloom again.  When Lin reached Strut Four, hundreds of mechanical drones hovered in a shimmering halo outside their metal hive, their tiny wings glinting like spun sugar.  Peering into the docking slots, she found no melted relays at all, only a fragrant film of pale organic beeswax insulating the copper terminals from the brass swarm. 

Remembering the crimson shelter in Amos's verse, she turned toward the timber maintenance niche barely two feet away.  Strapped securely against the wind sat the toll keeper's heirloom lacquer box, its thick cured resin naturally impervious to the tower's lethal voltage surges, with three narrow notches carved beneath its rim.  Setting her purge torch aside, Lin unhooked the clasp and lifted the red lid just enough to reveal a thriving colony of living honeybees, long presumed extinct across the sooty valley.  Inside that insulated sanctuary, velvet workers fanned golden combs brimming with fresh twilight syrup, close enough to their original perch that late foragers were still slipping home through the notches.  Lin let out a breathless laugh, realizing how carefully Amos had relocated the comb before tonight's storm charge could fry it, and how fiercely he had guarded her chance to marvel at it firsthand.  After latching the crimson lid securely, she scraped the stray wax film off Strut Four's outer contacts with her thumbnail until the brass drones began docking in crisp blue sparks.  Two swarms, one of hammered metal and one of living amber, hummed side by side beneath the darkening clouds.  Leaning on the rail, Lin waved down toward the glowing tollbooth, suddenly eager to make the exact same climb tomorrow evening.
