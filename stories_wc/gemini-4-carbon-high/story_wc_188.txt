Bram, a wandering fireworks designer, chants while walking beneath the arched portcullis of the blooming fortress at dusk.  "Three grains of copper for courage, two beats of willow for grace," he murmurs in steady rhythm, matching his dusty boots to the burn rates of imaginary fuses.  All around him, rusted siege cannons drown in night jasmine and crimson trumpet vines that spill over scarred battlements into the fading violet light.  Though his pockets jingle with carnival trinkets and bright mineral salts, Bram treats whimsy as an exact, urgent science.  He travels from one exhausted garrison to the next with a singular, stubborn mission: to defy the gravity of hopelessness that bends human spines toward the dirt. 

Yet Bram never unpacks his brass mortars before understanding the ghosts of a place.  Instead of questioning the quiet gardeners along the ramparts, he walks the perimeter to filter the citadel's unspoken sorrow through ambient awareness.  Keeping his playful chant soft beneath his breath, he reads peripheral clues in the twilight air.  An elderly baker flinches when a shutter slams, and children trace cracks between flagstones rather than glancing above the ivy.  Most telling is the heavy, collective sigh that ripples across the terraces as permanent valley clouds swallow the last violet ribbon of sunset.  Filtered through that quiet dread, every booming black powder shell in Bram's rucksack becomes useless; in a haven healing from siege, sudden thunder would bring terror. 

In the central muster square, he finds a young woman kneeling beside a bucket of dry limestone grout.  Across the cracked paving stones, she has embedded a note in mosaic using shattered cobalt crockery and white river pebbles.  Framed by ceramic vines, the jagged letters declare: TRUST ONLY WHAT HAS ROOTS.  To buff chalky dust from the glazed shards, she wipes them with an unhooked fire spinner's kerosene rag knot, its braided cotton blackened by soot and smelling faintly of paraffin.  Bram crouches beside her stonework and tips his charred felt cap.  "Magnificent lettering," he says with solemn cheer, "though employing a retired comet as a cleaning cloth borders on treason."  The woman, introducing herself as Maren, glances at the heavy woven bundle in her palm with a crooked smile.  "Comets belong to clear skies," Maren replies, gesturing toward the suffocating blanket of marsh mist overhead.  "We planted blossoms over every parapet so nobody would look up and remember how empty the dark stays." 

As if summoned by her words, twilight vanishes completely, leaving the citadel suspended during the hush of a night with no stars.  Perfumed darkness presses down upon the courtyard like wet wool, and shutters begin latching behind narrow stone windows.  Working swiftly, Bram unpacks twelve slender paper cylinders engineered without a single grain of explosive lift powder.  Instead, they house noiseless thermal fountains designed to loft miniature silk parachutes and luminous magnesium pearls high into the gloom on columns of warm air.  He arranges the tubes in a wide ring around Maren's mosaic, chanting fuse timings under his breath as he paces the circle, then uncorks a flask of lamp spirit tinted with copper and crimson strontium salts.  "All twelve thermal drafts must catch flame in one fluid breath to rise together, and no matchstick survives a sprint," Bram says, offering his open flask toward her dusted rag knot. 

For a heartbeat Maren hesitates, weighing the safety of stone against his earnest mischief, before pulling a swivel chain from her pocket and clipping the braided knot fast.  She shakes out the limestone dust, soaks the thirsty cotton in Bram's salted fuel, and lets him strike a flint spark to its core.  Emerald and crimson flames unfurl without a sound, and as Maren whirls the chain into wide, whispering orbits, curious faces emerge among the moonflowers on the balconies above.  Pivoting barefoot across her own tiled warning, she sweeps the singing ribbon of fire low around the circle, kissing every fuse in a single, unbroken dance.  Twelve golden plumes exhale like gentle bellows, carrying dozens of silent silver lights skyward until their tiny canopies blossom beneath the dark clouds.  Along the blooming ramparts, tense shoulders loosen and chins tilt upward toward the drifting constellation, while Bram grins beside Maren in the sudden glow.  At their feet, the polished cobalt shards of her mosaic catch the reflection of the hovering lights, turning a plea for the earth into a mirror full of stars.
