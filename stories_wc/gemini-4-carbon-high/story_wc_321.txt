Every evening that never quite ended, Leo coiled the damp hemp mooring lines on the bow of the Cascadia as if the pier were only minutes away.  As an ardent ferry deckhand, he loved the salt spray, the brass cleats, and the quiet trust of tired strangers heading home across Oakhaven Bay.  Three autumns ago in linear memory, a freak barometer drop had turned their vessel into a vanishing commuter ferry, plucking fifty passengers out of the evening fog midstream.  While the captain hid below in defeat and commuters stared bleakly into cooling thermoses, Leo remained resiliently positive, whistling briskly as he polished the cabin railings and promised smooth docking.  His bright faith kept the passengers from fading into mist during the uncanny intervals when clocks run sideways.  Above the ticket booth, the master clock unhooked its iron hands from its center pin and slid them horizontally across the porcelain dial, measuring width of possibility rather than passing hours. 

Leo understood why neither shore had ever appeared out of the gloom.  That autumn squall had fractured Oakhaven Bay into two incomplete realities, one holding the morning pier they had departed and the other holding the evening terminal where loved ones still waited.  Sailing toward either half alone meant crashing into phantom timber, leaving Leo with a singular mission to merge parallel timelines into one whole harbor.  Tonight, as the clock hands drifted past the brass rim of the dial, the grey fog peeled back and the Cascadia glided into a spectral grotto under starlight.  Translucent ribs of luminous stone arched high over black water, framing a roofless vault where twin sets of constellations shimmered slightly out of phase.  Every ripple from the propellers struck the ghostly cavern walls and bounced back as two distinct echoes, proving that both shores pressed thin against this threshold. 

Leo climbed the narrow companionway to the darkened wheelhouse, where charts fluttered in a draft that smelled equally of dawn espresso and dusk woodsmoke.  Bolted beside the compass binnacle rested an empty hourglass, its brass cap loosened years earlier during the storm until every last grain of sand had spilled away.  The captain had dismissed the drained timer as useless junk, yet Leo saw why it still hummed faintly against the mahogany console.  Falling sand could only measure forward decay, whereas hollow twin bulbs freed of gravity could capture lateral light.  Unscrewing the brass frame, Leo laid the empty hourglass horizontally beneath the open skylight so each chamber faced an opposite half of the split sky.  Pale beams of starlight poured through the curved glass and converged at the narrow neck, projecting crisp latticework across the harbor chart below. 

Nearby, the marine transceiver hissed with alternating bursts of static that rose and fell in time with the glowing ripples lapping the grotto arches.  Leo leaned over the table with a grin of sudden recognition, realizing the fractured bay spoke via interconnected patterns rather than isolated coordinates.  The angle of the doubled North Star matched the interval between twin cavern wakes, which in turn mirrored two drifting carrier frequencies on the dial.  Using brass dividers, he traced where the refracted starlight nodes intersected the depth lines of Oakhaven Channel, translating geometry into a rhythmic pulse sequence.  He spun the tuning knob to bridge both phantom channels and snatched up the heavy corded handset to radio the split harbor masters at once.  Keying the transmitter in strict cadence with the cavern echoes and starlight grid, Leo called the Cascadia home across both realities with steady, jubilant clarity. 

For three heartbeats the speaker crackled wildly, and then two overlapping dispatch voices answered in astonishment before locking into a single resonant baritone welcoming them to Pier Four.  Outside the bridge windows, the doubled constellations snapped together like closing teeth, and the pale limestone arches melted into dark pine headlands.  Warm amber streetlamps bloomed along a unified waterfront as weeping commuters crowded the lower deck, clutching briefcases toward solid pilings at last.  Steadying the helm as the bow nudged the fenders, Leo felt the hull thrumming with sideways current and knew the suture holding Oakhaven whole was the motion of the ferry itself.  If the Cascadia ever tied up for good, the seam would unravel, meaning she could kiss the slip just long enough to trade returning souls for fresh travelers before gliding back into the deep.  Racing downstairs to drop the heavy iron gangplank with a cheerful wave, Leo gladly claimed his berth as a permanent transient, forever departing, forever arriving, and wholly at home between shores.
