Deep within the musical crystal cave, towering columns of smoky quartz and luminous selenite hummed in perpetual bright darkness.  Pale mineral light sliced across pitch black chasms while subterranean drafts plucked prophetic chords from the stone.  For twenty winters, Mirren had listened to those anchored spires, reading unseen forces of barometric pressure and deep earth tremors to foretell every tragedy in the valley below.  Hours before dusk, the bass notes of the bedrock had warned her that an aging miller named Corin was climbing the mountain pass.  Expecting him to ask whether spring thaws would shatter his waterwheel, she prepared her grim calculation long before his boots touched the threshold.  Yet every certainty she possessed began to unravel right after the pilgrim arrived. 

Shaking cold rain from his wool cloak, Corin bowed politely and raised a calloused hand to halt her recitation of future floods.  "I did not climb the ridge to borrow sorrow from tomorrow," the old man said gently.  Mirren stared at him in silence, unsettled to find herself an oracle blindsided in her own sanctum.  Turning from the booming quartz pillars, Corin knelt beside a shallow limestone basin near the center of the floor.  Inside that pool rested a solitary cave pearl, a smooth sphere of pale calcite polished by falling water.  Unlike the giant formations fused to the mountain, the loose sphere rolled beneath a ceiling drip that was steadily unpredictable, falling at an unbroken pace yet striking at shifting angles so the stone never cemented to the floor.  Because it held no fixed path in the rock, the tumbling orb had hidden Corin and his true purpose completely from her foresight. 

Stepping down from her dais, Mirren asked why any traveler would brave icy cliffs for a pebble that could not warn of disaster.  Corin gazed at the violet gleam reflecting off the black water and spoke of a town grown numb with dread.  Years of heeding solemn prophecies had taught his neighbors to survive harsh seasons, yet they now stared so anxiously toward distant horizons that they missed the warmth in their own kitchens.  He wished to set an awakened pearl inside the village fountain to magnify everyday magic, letting its gentle hum remind passersby of rising bread, mended quarrels, and shared laughter in the dark.  Yet as long as twin spires of glowing selenite shone from opposite walls onto the basin, their cold radiance kept the calcite taut and silent, scattering every vibration outward into the vault.  One person standing beside the water could block only a single beam while the opposing crystal continued to blaze across the stone. 

"A solitary soul cannot wake a stone meant for company," Corin murmured, gesturing toward the far rim of the pool.  Hesitating only a breath, Mirren walked around the basin until she stood directly between the eastern spire and the rippling cup.  Corin rose opposite her before the western column, and together they leaned inward, shielding the water through overlapping shadows.  Where their silhouettes merged into a deeper umbra rimmed with prismatic silver, the mineral chill cooled the basin and reversed the acoustic pull of the cavern.  Drawn into that sheltered stillness, the unseen forces of the grotto ceased echoing off distant walls and rushed downward to inhabit the cave pearl.  Gravity, faint thermal currents, and the quiet rhythm of two mortal heartbeats soaked into the porous calcite until the rolling sphere began to sing. 

It was no shrill warning of storms, only a low golden resonance that made every ordinary detail in the chamber suddenly luminous and vast.  Standing inside the shared shade, Mirren gasped as the deafening roar of future years fell quiet in her ears, replaced by the scent of damp wool, the warmth of shared breath, and the forgotten drumbeat in her own chest.  She had spent half her life mapping the fate of strangers, a clairvoyant who did not see it coming when the present moment broke open her solitude.  When Corin scooped the humming pearl into a jar of spring water, its warm note tuned the surrounding crystals into a gentler harmony that lingered through the gloom.  Pausing by the cavern mouth, the miller asked if she might walk down to the valley with him to hear how the fountain sang at suppertime.  Listening to the bright, uncharted dark ahead, Mirren realized she had no vision at all of where the path led next, and reached gladly for her cloak.
