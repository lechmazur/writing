High upon the basalt headland, Nerin stood within a twilight garden under stars where black hellebore opened to the cooling salt air.  Far below the sheer cliff, the ocean surged in dark rhythms that her muscles answered by instinct, for she remained a skilled tide dancer even on dry stone.  Three months after the nostalgia ban, the Harbor Magistracy had decreed that water performers must dance only blind currents stripped of historical cadence.  Every shoreline archive had been burned to ash so citizens would face exclusively forward without grief or lineage.  Refusing to make her body an instrument of state amnesia, Nerin had chosen elective exile beyond the municipal seawall, trading the surf she loved for this overgrown conservatory between earth and sky. 

She knelt beside a basin of rainwater with quiet, unhurried precision, preparing for the solitary rite that had drawn her up the cliffs.  Her sole purpose tonight was to pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin, preserving the oldest tidal liturgy in the only vessel that did not violate natural impermanence.  Carved tablets and vellum scrolls invited confiscation because they tried to outlive their makers, whereas living flesh carried memory faithfully for one span of breath before returning cleanly to dust.  Yet ordinary pigment would lie dead and mute beneath her epidermis, unable to feel the pull of lunar tides.  From a folded belladonna leaf, she tipped a single moonstone cabochon dust speck into a mortar of charred resin and sea brine.  When censors had pulverized the sacred lunar gems of her order on the harbor stones, she had trapped that solitary grain beneath her thumbnail, too minute for resonance wards to detect.  Suspended inside the glossy black ink, the microscopic feldspar fragment caught the faintest silver sheen, ready to tug at her nerves whenever the moon crossed the meridian. 

Next she lifted a slender needle of volcanic glass and three stiff cormorant feathers gathered from the cliffside ledges.  In the dissolved sanctuary, novices danced within a sheltered coastal flock until an elder judged them ready to fledge, binding dark sea plumes to their marking shafts so thermal drafts could balance the point during solitary inscription.  With no master left to sanction her ascent, Nerin wound silk thread around the quills herself, claiming the authority to fledge in silent autonomy above the policed bay.  Still, two obstacles stood between her poised needle and the unwritten stanza.  First, the Magistracy had renamed every bright constellation after civic industry, turning the visible stars into radiant propaganda that obscured the ancient sky.  Second, twenty years of yielding to littoral currents kept Nerin swaying perpetually from ankle to collarbone, and forcing her arm rigid by willpower only bred ruinous tremors. 

Rising among fragrant silver thyme as dusk deepened into indigo, she solved the celestial cipher through the oldest lesson of the surf.  True oceanic force never resided in luminous whitecaps, which wind could scatter at whim, but in the dark troughs between waves.  Ignoring the renamed beacons overhead, she reconstructed the forbidden heavens by observing gaps, measuring the exact velvet chasms between star clusters and matching their geometry to the hushed intervals between breakers below.  There, directly above the garden path, yawned the Great Slack, a starless rift whose proportions mirrored the pause of dead low water.  Nerin stepped into alignment beneath that celestial hollow just as the distant sea reached its turn, dipping her feathered stylus into the mortar and baring her left forearm.  At once, the downward pull of the abyssal trough and the upward draw of the overhead void met cleanly along her spine, leaving her spontaneously anchored.  Every habitual sway ceased without strain, rooting her heels to the terrace like cooled stone while the night breeze held the cormorant plumes in delicate equilibrium. 

Moving with obsidian grace, she tapped the glass point along her inner arm, translating the measured voids of sky and sea into fine dark glyphs.  Each crisp puncture sealed brine, soot, and mineral shimmer beneath her skin without a wasted breath or hesitant tremor.  When the final cadence closed beside her wrist, the slack tide broke far below and released her from stillness.  Beneath the fresh black lines, the embedded speck answered the incoming flood with a cool, unmistakable pulse.  Nerin lowered her stylus, raised her inscribed arm toward the dark intervals between the stars, and began to dance the remembered sea back into motion.
