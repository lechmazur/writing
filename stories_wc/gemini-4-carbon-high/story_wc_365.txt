Dust hung motionless in the frigid air of the abandoned spaceport repair bay, settling over rusted gantries and dry hydraulic pits.  For Aris, a sensory map maker who charted environments through thermal drift, acoustic decay, and barometric pressure, this dead stillness was essential.  Any ordinary port noise would have drowned out the subtle anomalies lingering around Berth Four since his sister Mara vanished five years ago.  In his bare palm, Aris turned a green glass shard salvaged from the shattered cockpit gauge of her prototype vessel.  Hours before her launch test imploded, Mara had deleted his warning about a spatial tremor and locked him outside the blast doors.  He had not come back merely to reconstruct the accident, but to discover the weight of a withheld truth by feeling what she had hidden. 

Timing that discovery required immense patience because the dimensional fractures inside the hangar were cyclically spontaneous.  Every orbital alignment reliably drew adjacent realities close together, yet the actual breach always erupted without warning at an erratic second inside that window.  Worse, a fully waking mind instinctively filtered out contradictory impressions from neighboring timelines to protect its own sanity.  Aris leaned against a corroded support pillar, slowed his pulse, and allowed his awareness to hover in the murmur of half-remembered dreams.  Suspended on that fragile threshold between sleep and waking, his perceptual barriers softened until phantom drafts brushed his cheeks.  Suddenly, the doped silicate shard bit into his skin with fierce cold and began humming like a struck tuning fork. 

Keeping his eyelids heavy and his breathing shallow to preserve the trance, Aris pushed off the pillar and began weaving between worlds.  His sensor rig clicked softly against his ribs, recording every shift in temperature and air density as he walked through parallel universes layered over the concrete floor.  Three paces toward the central pit, the smell of old grease vanished beneath a choking wave of sulfur and blistering heat.  In that adjacent reality, the entire spaceport had burned to cinders, leaving only hot ash swirling through skeletal steel.  Angling left toward a sharper pitch in the vibration of the glass, he stepped into a second overlap where hoarfrost coated his eyelashes and distant sirens wailed endlessly.  Each world he skirted was a ruin, yet his own timeline had somehow survived with nothing worse than an empty cradle in Berth Four. 

When his boots reached the lip of the repair pit, the sensory map converged into a single crushing roar of gravity and static.  Through the resonating green fragment, Aris felt the preserved imprint of the final moments inside the prototype cabin.  The tremor he had detected five years ago had never come from her engines at all, but from a catastrophic rift tearing upward from below.  Mara had realized that if port directors saw his data, they would pry the anomaly wider to harvest its power, condemning their city to fire or ice like the neighboring worlds.  She had erased his report to shield him from treason charges and inverted her own drive core as a plug to seal the rupture. 

Knees buckling under the phantom pressure she had endured alone, Aris felt that containment seal finally cracking after years of relentless cycles.  Raw dimensional torque boiled up from the pit, threatening to snap him awake and tear Berth Four open across his home reality.  Guided by the exact contours of his newly completed map, he plunged his copper sensor leads into the heavy grounding bus beside the pit.  With bleeding fingers, he wedged the green glass shard directly into the primary relay gap to redirect the escaping surge downward into deep planetary bedrock. 

Violet lightning flashed without sound across the overlapping hangars, filling his lungs with the clean scent of ozone and summer rain as the pressure drained safely into the stone.  Both the searing ash and the wailing frost receded until only one quiet hall remained around him.  Aris opened his eyes fully, staring down at the fused relay where the shard glowed with a faint, steady warmth before cooling forever.  His muscles still ached from the immense load Mara had carried to save him, and as he unclipped his rig to leave the bay, grief finally gave way to peace.
