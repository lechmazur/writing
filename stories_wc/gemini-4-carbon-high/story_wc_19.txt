As a bunker builder's claustrophobic child, Toma grew up with his shoulders hunched against concrete ceilings four feet thick.  His father poured subterranean vaults for the entire valley, convinced safety meant sealing human bodies inside tight lead boxes.  Every day at noon, when gravity inverts for an hour, sirens drove the townsfolk underground so nobody would tumble into the sky.  Locked beneath tons of stone, Toma felt his ribs coil inward like rusted springs while the buried air tasted of wet chalk and recycled panic.  To survive those suffocating lockdowns, he had secretly begun to collect the flavors of different silences.  He used a tiny porcelain doll bartered from an old mountain potter who seeded porous bisque clay with dormant acoustic spores.  Potters shaped such vessels as miniature people because curved ceramic shoulders and delicate ears trapped stillness at the exact resonance of human breath. 

So far Toma had harvested pine hush from twilight ravines and crisp frost quiet from empty orchards, licking their salt crystals off the glaze whenever bunker walls pressed too close.  Yet those ground silences faded fast against his father's deeper vaults.  Today, as the warning klaxon wailed and hydraulic doors hissed shut, Toma stayed outside on the granite ridge wrapped in a wide canvas drag cloak.  Noon struck, and the earth released its grip.  Pebbles trembled before sliding skyward, while massive iron chains bolted into the crags uncoiled with a metallic groan and pulled taut into the mist.  Lifted off his boots, Toma felt every cramped muscle in his chest tug outward as his lifelong crouch met a sudden vertical pull.  His cloak billowed like a parachute, slowing his ascent as he rose along the straining anchor links through cold gray vapor. 

Moments later he breached the blinding white ceiling and drifted softly into the xenial trellis above the clouds.  Built generations ago by highland weavers for any traveler caught outdoors, the sprawling cedar latticework hung upside down against the sky on its vertical tethers.  Every beam was designed to welcome frightened strangers rather than lock them away.  Plush moss nets cushioned Toma's upward landing, brass braziers warmed the inverted cedar walkways, and woven silk ropes offered gentle handholds without a single wall in sight.  Overhead hung the dense wool of the cloud deck, blocking every earthly rumble, while beneath his boots yawned the boundless blue dome of noon.  Here pooled the gilded hush, where raw sunlight saturated utter quiet.  It felt intangibly tangible, holding no physical mass yet pressing against Toma's parted lips and eardrums like warm spun amber. 

Yet stepping straight from subterranean compression into infinite horizon made his pulse hammer with dizzying shock.  His contracted lungs ached as they stretched to meet so much emptiness, and when he drew the tiny porcelain doll from his coat, a hairline stress mark ticked across its glaze under the fierce upper glare.  His father always warned that fragile things shattered in open air, yet burying the vessel would starve its spores forever.  Toma realized both he and the clay could endure vastness only by limiting exposure.  Kneeling beside a warm brazier, he stopped staring into the whole abyss at once and instead looked through a single diamond gap in the cedar lattice, letting his tight posture lengthen one steady breath at a time.  Cupping his shadowed palms around the doll, he parted his fingers for five counted heartbeats, steeping the ceramic figure in golden stillness before shading it again.  After seven measured intervals, the porcelain did not break.  Instead, luminous amber filaments began to sprout from the doll's unglazed collarbone and painted lips, crystallizing the sunlit silence into delicate frost. 

Toma plucked a single thread and touched it to his tongue.  It dissolved into the unmistakable flavor of warm apricot, clean linen, and boundless room to stand upright.  For the first time in his life, the knot behind his sternum uncoiled completely without snapping.  Soon the iron tethers hummed as nearly sixty minutes passed and the valley's true weight began returning.  Clipping his harness onto a counterweighted descent reel left generously for departing guests, Toma rode the gentle pull back through the clouds just as gravity turned homeward.  His boots touched the granite ridge moments before the bunker hatch unsealed below.  He would still walk down into his father's narrow corridors tonight, yet Toma stood straighter now, cradling the blooming figurine safely inside his pocket, certain that fragile lives did not need tombs when they knew how to sip the open sky.
