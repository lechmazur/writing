Above the sunken vaults of the forgotten library, neighborhood children scatter for supper, leaving the park still at the hush as an empty playground swing drifts in the breeze.  Through the grimy transom window, that swaying iron seat marks the brief twilight alignment when severed things can rejoin their owners.  Bram taps his brass watch and scowls across the scarred worktable at Mara.  As the last municipal shard-mirror archivist, he claims to despise hesitation almost as much as tears, though his gruffness fools nobody.  Between them stands a stoppered cylinder holding an afternoon shadow caught in glass, coiling like trapped smoke.  Six months ago, magistrates sheared that silhouette from Mara's heels and sentenced her to reversible exile inside these warded stacks, unseen by every citizen overhead.  Even the building faded from public memory once its shelves traded paper books for jagged silvered testimonies. 

"Drink the rest and quit glaring at the porcelain like it owes you rent," Bram barks, nudging the chipped cup toward her wrist.  He brewed the oolong extra hot to thaw her stiff knuckles, just after draping his wool coat over her shoulders while grumbling about moths.  Mara swallows the dregs, winces, and inverts the teacup onto its saucer.  To reverse banishment, an exile must claim their exact fault without flinching, yet human recollection instinctively bends to flatter pride.  Here, truth is mapped only by analyzing the geometry of tea leaves settled under the tremor of the drinker's breath.  Bram leans low with his loupe as Mara lifts the white clay cup.  Three damp stems form an acute triangle pointing toward the rim, bisected by a jagged chord of broken grit. 

"Give me the coordinates, not a tavern ballad," Bram growls, tapping the saucer.  All winter his unspoken purpose has been to mentor resilience quietly, filing down her panic with abrasive precision so the guilds upstairs can never crush her twice.  Mara steadies her chin and traces the air above the rim.  "The narrow vertex sits twenty degrees off the handle, marking deliberate silence rather than oversight," she says clearly.  "And that bisecting chord proves I saw the crack in the canal lock before my supervisor bribed me, meaning the flooded granary rests on my signature." 

Pride tightens Bram's throat, so he hides it behind a sour grunt and slides a wedge of silvered glass onto the velvet pad.  Within the shard, the warped reflection of rushing black water blurs wherever Mara's old excuses used to hide.  "Take the diamond stylus and annotate the lower bevel with those exact angles," Bram orders, setting the etching tool beside her palm.  If her hand wavers in denial while scoring the margin, the shard will splinter and lock her shadow away for another year.  "Press until the glass bites back, girl, and do not dare look away from your own eyes." 

As Mara raises the stylus to annotate the mirror, dread knots Bram's ribs, both for the cut she risks and the loneliness her victory will leave behind.  He walks backwards when nervous, an involuntary habit worn into his bones from decades of refusing to turn his spine toward unstable reflections or endangered apprentices.  Heel to toe, his scuffed boots scrape in steady retreat down the narrow aisle of numbered racks.  Mara pauses with the diamond point poised above the silver and glances up, catching the giveaway rhythm of his soles.  "You are backing into the tax ledgers, old man," she murmurs, a crooked, fierce smile steadying her mouth.  "Keep retreating like that and you will rattle your own jar off the top shelf." 

Directly overhead sits a dusty cylinder holding Bram's own shadow, ready for release twenty years ago, yet left sealed because no free citizen can perceive this cellar to guide the newly banished.  Bram stops with his heels against an iron ladder and snaps, "Mind your bevel and leave my footwork out of it!"  Outside the transom, the empty swing drifts once more in the cooling amber breeze, holding the twilight hush for another heartbeat.  Mara lowers her gaze into the flooded reflection and carves the geometric proof of her guilt clean across the glass.  A crystalline chime rings across the vault as her reflection snaps sharp, splitting the wax seal on the cylinder and popping its glass stopper free.  Dark and eager, the freed shadow pours over the table edge and stitches itself fast to Mara's boots, restoring her full weight to the waking street.  Bram rubs a sudden sting from his eyes and waves toward the exit, muttering, "Keep the ugly coat and get upstairs before the threshold wards take this place, because I am tired of brewing tea for ingrates."
