Three winters ago, a swallow from a misfiled chemical vial slowed his pulse enough to fool an exhausted ward doctor, making him a death certificate error survivor in a municipality too proud to revise its ledgers.  By the hour he gasped awake inside a cold morgue drawer, a clerk had already stamped his file and purged his civic record.  He walked out into the harbor fog carrying nothing from his former life except the damp label from a poison bottle peeled off the glass before he collapsed.  Though his lungs healed, the toxin scarred his mind so deeply that he filters reality differently from the hurried crowds above the docks.  Proper nouns, dates, and human faces slide off his awareness like rain off oiled canvas, while spoken words strike his skin with distinct physical grain like coarse salt, wet wool, or splintered cedar. 

Cast into the silent fellowship of erased histories, he stopped knocking on courthouse doors where clerks stared through him and retreated toward the water.  He claimed a hidden shelter within a towering labyrinth of shipping containers in a port city that never demands papers from a shadow.  Stacked six tiers high, the dented steel boxes form canyons of dark rust smelling of bilge grease, crushed cardamom, and cold brine.  Down in this metallic twilight, he measures time solely across the rhythm of tides.  When flood water swells against the concrete pilings, heavy freighters groan against rubber fenders and drive loud crews deep into the narrow alleys between the stacks.  When the ebb pulls the black harbor seaward, the iron maze grows hollow and still, carrying distant murmurs along the corrugated walls like wind through organ pipes. 

Because the chemical coma scoured his own youth completely blank, a quiet hunger compels him to discover the texture of memories.  He aches to learn whether a retained past feels jagged like barnacles on stone or supple like tallow warmed between human palms.  He pursues this answer by collecting fragments of overheard stories that rise through the slotted vents of his concealed box.  During afternoon high water, two crane riggers shelter from rain beneath his rusted wall, and one describes a winter orchard where frozen plums shattered like dark glass on the snow.  Kneeling in the dimness within, the listener feels that borrowed image press cold and brittle against his fingertips.  With a stub of tally chalk and torn margins from discarded cargo manifests, he copies each fleeting confession before the current shifts.  He never writes who spoke the words, preserving the austere refuge of a nameless identity shared by every ghost in the yard. 

Glued at eye level against his interior bulkhead sits the yellowed label from the poison bottle, bearing a faded scarlet skull and blurred chemical warnings.  Using sticky pine resin scraped from broken shipping crates, he works after every tide to layer the chalked paper strips outward from that central square.  Season after season, the cold metal wall has grown a dense fibrous bark composed of strangers and their salvaged yesterdays.  A deckhand mourning a drowned hound overlaps a stevedore recalling the warm blistered crust of rye bread baked on an iron stove.  Until tonight, however, he has left the toxic emblem uncovered at the center as the sole surviving proof of how his previous existence ended.  Whenever his thumb grazes that slick commercial paper, it feels flat and lifeless beside the rough strata surrounding it. 

Around midnight, as low water exposes the barnacled timber pilings along the quay, booted footsteps crunch over gravel outside his corridor.  Two night watchmen pause near his vent with a lantern, speaking quietly about a flooded municipal cellar that ruined decades of birth and burial books.  The older watchman chuckles into the salt wind and says that rotted ink steals nothing from a man whose hands still remember the pull of an ash oar across a summer bay.  Inside the dark box, the survivor breathes slowly as those words settle against his ribs with the smooth, resilient grain of weathered wood.  By touch alone, he transcribes the sentence onto a wide strip of manifest paper while the slack surf turns beneath the pier.  Instead of seeking bare metal, he smears pine resin directly over the scarlet skull, choosing to layer living timber and open water straight across the artifact of his erasure.  Resting his palm against the thickened ridges of the wall as the incoming tide lifts the ships once more, he feels the warm weight of a life that needs no ledger to remain real.
