Oren knelt beside a dark mineral pool beneath colossal towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes.  Carved from the ribs and hollow vertebrae of primordial leviathans, those pale spires caught every whisper and flung it endlessly between the sheer limestone walls.  As the last harmonic stone skimmer of his order, Oren had spent forty dry seasons casting tuned slates across the glassy water so their ringing notes might test the unseen vaults.  Age had stiffened his knees into knotted wood, yet a quiet, weathered resolve anchored him to the narrow stone threshold.  Walking deeper into the gorge was impossible, for a single bootstep would unleash chaotic reverberations that drowned all subtle truth.  His final purpose was to learn to navigate without moving, charting the hidden heart of the ossuary while remaining still as bedrock. 

Somewhere miles beyond his sight persisted a pulse left wandering, an ancient rhythm struck upon a great bronze drum before the founding clans vanished.  Preserved by the curving marrow chambers of the spires, that lone beat still roamed the labyrinth, carrying the acoustic shape of every corridor it touched.  Around his knees, silence had deepened over the ledges through centuries of layered dust, draping the canyon floor in pale gray velvet.  Oren was historically reverent in every gesture, trained to honor the sleeping dead by leaving that deep ash unbroken and every bone untouched.  Yet his smooth river slates had failed him year after year.  Whenever he skimmed a round disc across the pool, its circular ripples sent wide, blurry chords crashing against the nearest ribs, scattering before they could meet the distant beat. 

Reaching beside his shin, Oren slid two careful fingers into the shallow margin where water met stone, sifting gently through the cold silt.  His knuckles brushed a worked edge, and he lifted a dark stone arrowhead from its ancient resting place.  Holding the flint relic toward the canyon glare, he paused to appraise its strange proportions with a practiced eye.  At first he hesitated to cast a sacred remnant of the ancestors into the black pool, fearing such boldness bordered on desecration.  Then he tapped its central ridge with his thumbnail and heard a pure, crystalline chime.  The underside had been ground completely flat, while the chipped shoulders tapered at an exact harmonic angle.  No hunter had ever loosed arrows inside a gorge of solid bone where shafts would instantly splinter.  An elder skimmer had shaped this wedge to slice the water cleanly, focusing each skip into a narrow lance of sound that could pierce the deepest chasm without scattering. 

Still, even the cleanest note would vanish into the overlapping roar of old reverberations before human ears could untangle its return.  Oren tilted his weathered chin toward the ribbon of pale sky high between the skeletal towers.  Hundreds of silver canyon swifts wheeled silently among the upper skulls, riding the subtle pressure currents created by rebounding sound.  Because their hollow wingbones felt every tremor in the air, the birds swerved away from clashing noise and glided along clear harmonic channels.  Where hearing failed, he could read the invisible architecture of the gorge through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight. 

Breathing slowly so not one grain of ancestral dust stirred beneath his cloak, Oren settled the flint point into the crook of his forefinger.  With one crisp snap of his scarred wrist, he skimmed the stone arrowhead low across the dark mirror of the pool.  It kissed the surface seven times in rapid succession, carving razor wakes that launched a needle of bright pitch straight down the central colonnade.  Oren locked his spine and waited as that high, directional tone raced past the visible arches to intersect the ancient drumbeat.  To his ears came only a braided murmur, indistinguishable from the endless sighing of the canyon.  High overhead, however, the wheeling swifts broke apart in sudden, breathtaking symmetry.  Where the fresh chime locked onto the wandering pulse, combined waves surged upward from the hidden depths and sculpted the sky.  The flock banked sharply around unseen pillars, parted before sunken vaults, and spiraled down a clear, resonant passage leading to the sanctum of the founders.  Fixing his gaze upon their shifting wings, Oren began to appraise every turn, bridge, and chamber revealed in living silver.  Without shifting a heel from the undisturbed ledge, he walked the entire forgotten labyrinth inside his mind, whole and certain at last.
