High on the icebound spire above the clouds, the polar twilight hung in a state of brilliant dimness, neither day nor night, where deep violet shadows gleamed inside translucent frost.  Fierce stars pricked the dark dome overhead, casting sharp silver glints across the rime without ever dispelling the hush of obscurity.  Far beneath that unbroken sea of gray vapor, vast arcane looms hummed in ceaseless unison, churning out flawless enchantments for a civilization transformed when creativity gets automated.  Down in those glaring valley foundries, supervisors dismissed Oren as an underachieving mage because he refused the neural compilers and cast his tiny spells by hand.  His magic was slow, quiet, and wholly unprofitable, suited only for solitary climbs into abandoned altitudes where the engines had no reason to reach. 

Thick leather bands cut into his wool cloak with every upward step, bearing the dead weight of a garden folly keystone salvaged from a demolished orchard below.  Generations before machines began drafting every archway for maximum load and civic utility, an unknown mason had carved the tapered limestone block to crown a decorative gazebo that sheltered nothing at all.  Automated wreckers had pulverized the rest of the whimsical ruin to clear ground for a conduit array, leaving this single chipped wedge half buried in frozen mud.  Oren hauled the heavy stone up three thousand glazed stairs not out of grand defiance, only to find the sacred in the simple.  Where the valley looms flooded the boulevards with loud spectacles of synthetic gold, he longed for one unadorned gesture of devotion between mortal touch and weathered rock. 

Near the summit, the spiral staircase ended abruptly at a shattered parapet where ancient gales had sheared away the upper belfry.  Only jagged teeth of blue ice remained, pointing into an open gulf where pale tendrils of cloud rose from the lower sea and dissolved into the luminous gloom.  No intact columns stood to receive his burden; the true apex of the tower existed now as an empty coordinate twenty paces out over the chasm.  Any automated navigation script would have flagged the missing masonry as a fatal error and aborted the ascent immediately.  Oren closed his eyes instead, matching his pulse to the freezing drafts until he could practice mist walking, an obsolete discipline too erratic for machines to codify.  Whispering a three syllable cadence that smelled of rain, he stepped off the icy brink onto a drifting coil of vapor.  The mist yielded beneath his boot sole like damp fleece before stiffening just enough to bear both frail mage and quarried stone. 

Treading delicately from wisp to glimmering wisp, he climbed the thin currents into the indigo dusk until he stood level with the phantom peak.  Around him, ice crystals suspended in the air caught stray starlight, weaving a quiet radiance through the deep shadow of the stratosphere.  Maintaining his footing on volatile fog demanded serene balance, yet shifting the load on his back threatened to plunge him straight through the vapor floor.  With stiff, raw fingers, Oren reached toward his collarbone and forced himself to unstrap the iron buckles binding the harness.  As the leather gave way, his heels sank calf deep into the freezing cloud before he caught the rough limestone block against his ribs and steadied his breath.  He raised the carved stone to eye level and pressed its angled flanks into the vacant space where the spire crown had once converged.  Machine thaumaturgy relies upon rigid laws of load and geometry, shattering at the slightest paradox, whereas handmade craft accommodates impossibilities with humble grace.  Because the stone had been shaped by fallible hands for an arch of pure whimsy, its crooked chisel marks recognized the memory of walls that were no longer there. 

Oren whispered a final binding syllable and opened his palms.  Instead of plummeting toward the industrialized valley, the keystone settled with a faint stony click against unseen ether, locking firmly into the empty sky.  Out of its porous, lichen stained grain bloomed a lingering luminance, the quiet residue of forgotten summer afternoons and patient human care awakening in the frost.  That pale amber glow did not conquer the surrounding twilight; rather, it nestled inside the dark air like a lantern veiled in smoke, turning the cold rime on the spire below into muted bronze.  Standing on silvered vapor beside a floating arch of one single stone, the mage held his freezing hands near its gentle warmth and smiled, knowing no engine on earth could ever calculate why it shone.
