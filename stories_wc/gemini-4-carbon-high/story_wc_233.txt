Queen Odette began her climb after the final warning tolled from the silver belfries in the valley below.  That third chime meant the solstice hemorrhage had started on the summit, turning the upper mountain paths into a slaughterhouse of delicate frost.  No priest dared ascend tonight, which suited a disenchanted queen who had grown sick of her court and its drugged, porcelain serenity.  Three months ago, disgusted by the smiling docility of her ministers, Odette had secretly begun pouring her nightly goblet of luminous wine into the palace hearth.  Without the sedative vintage, the pale metal of her crown had woken like a tuning fork, filling her sleep with visions of blue stone and suffocating lace.  Now she hauled herself up the sheer limestone switchbacks, tearing her velvet hem on thorns, determined to unravel the mystery of recurring dreams that left her gasping with borrowed lungs. 

Near midnight, she broke through the dense marine cloud layer onto a windswept spine of black rock.  Here smoldered the ember grotto on the ridge, a natural volcanic hollow whose geothermal vents breathed perpetual summer warmth into a narrow apron of alpine heather.  Odette recognized its dull crimson glow from her nightmares, where she had always viewed it from higher up the cliff.  Pressing past the warm moss, she scaled the final icy stair into the cobalt grotto above clouds, a vast lazurite cavern opening over a pale ocean of mist.  Polite applause echoed softly behind her from the threshold.  Lord Vane, Keeper of Royal Etiquette, bowed in immaculate white kidskin gloves while unspooling a garrote of braided silver wire between his fingers.  He murmured his regrets that a sober sovereign had become an inconvenience, confessing that the Regents preferred she perish neatly on the heights rather than question how the palace stayed eternal. 

Odette scarcely glanced at his wire, for the inner vault already held the answer to her torment.  Pinned against the deep blue stone by three iron spindles hung a fallen celestial weaver, torn centuries ago from its binary mate in the heavens.  Golden basins beneath its pierced ribs funneled its agony down into the royal vineyards, while the excess overflow filled the cavern with bleeding starlight.  Instead of pooling on the floor, that luminescent ichor drifted across the chamber in intricate curtains of living filigree, fragile as bridal lace and sharper than surgical glass.  From those shared nightmares, Odette finally understood the delicate trap before them.  Mourning its lost twin, the weaver spun its radiant blood to constrict instantly around any solitary pulse, panicked gasp, or murderous intent, carving intruders into ribbons of frost.  Only the quiet equilibrium of two warm breaths falling into unforced unison could soothe the predatory threads apart. 

Vane lingered prudently outside the drifting veil, smiling because he did not even need to use his wire.  One sudden step toward her would startle a gasp from her throat, summoning the gossamer web to strangle her in exquisite splendor while he watched unscathed.  Yet Odette neither fled nor trembled, turning toward the cavern balcony with the softly commanding grace that had bent courtiers to silence since her girlhood.  In a velvet murmur that brooked no refusal, she bade her executioner stand beside her for a single breath and witness the season turn below before claiming her life.  Decades of courtly obedience drew Vane half a pace closer, his gaze following hers down toward the warm crimson mouth on the lower spur.  Just then, a thermal plume rising from the volcanic heather lifted dozens of tiny golden lanterns above the silver clouds.  Childhood wonder disarmed both sovereign and killer at once, uniting them in a shared exhale spotting the first fireflies of summer as the amber sparks blinked against the abyss. 

Their synchronized mist drifted backward into the vault, and the lethal lace shivered, parting like silk curtains before the memory of twin warmth.  Odette stepped smoothly through the gap before Vane recovered his malice, gripping the central iron spindle with both bare hands.  She knew that freeing the creature would curdle every goblet in the capital by dawn, stripping her corrupt court of its stolen grace forever.  Comprehending her treason too late, Vane lunged into the breach with a ragged snarl that shattered the fragile stillness.  The weeping filigree snapped shut around his solitary fury, dressing him in a breathless shroud of crystalline lace just as Odette wrenched the iron free and released the star into the night.
