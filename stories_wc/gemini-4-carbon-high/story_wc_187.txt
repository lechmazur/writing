Three centuries had passed since the Great Rime, plunging the world into the quiet epoch that begins after fog becomes solid and turns every lowland valley into petrified white stone.  High above that frozen floor, the last remnant of humanity endured inside a stormlit observatory above clouds, where silent violet lightning pulsed continually through the thunderheads churning beneath the parapets.  A brief squall from the upper sky had just swept the peak, leaving the air washed and thin.  Carrying a bound ledger and a hollow reed cured in solstice heat, Rowan walked out alone as the sanctuary's summer-quill archivist.  Ten months of the year, mountain frost locked ink inside every nib, giving his order only the fleeting warm rains to transcribe outdoor history before winter sealed the domes. 

Before him gleamed the crystal whispering forecourt after rain, its thousands of resonant quartz tiles waking under a glistening film of water.  Whenever moisture touched those facets, the mineral lattice exhaled the stored vibrations of vows and footsteps spoken across generations.  Listening to that delicate chorus rise into the cooling dusk, Rowan felt preemptively nostalgic, mourning the warmth of the wet stone and his own living breath as though remembering them from a distant century.  Yet that constant ache for vanishing moments also fueled his deepest conviction.  Severed from the buried cities below, his people possessed no heirlooms or ancestral graves, relying entirely on collective memory to anchor their existence.  Rowan's lifelong task was to sculpt identity from experience, shaping raw, fleeting lives into a coherent story capable of sustaining the community through endless isolation. 

Tonight, that shared story faced a perilous fracture.  Inside the great dome, young engineers were preparing iron drills to descend through the storm clouds and bore into the solidified fog, convinced the founders had climbed only as patient astronomers awaiting tools to reclaim the valleys.  Because an early fire had consumed the first parchment ledgers, Rowan had come outside to recover the original Founding Oath directly from the wet quartz before the council voted.  When he knelt beside the eldest obelisk, however, the damp tiles surrounding its base yielded only the empty scrape of wind.  Puzzled by that void, he stood beneath the low amber sun skimming the horizon and began examining shadow lengths at monuments arrayed across the plaza. 

Seven basalt pillars rose in an arc, engineered as chronological gnomons whose late summer sunset shadows pointed straight to the specific tiles where each generation's leaders had recorded their testament.  Uncoiling his knotted measuring cord, Rowan paced the dark bands cast by the six younger pillars.  Every one stretched precisely three times the width of its square plinth.  Returning to the First Monument, he measured again and found its shadow falling two full paces short of that sacred ratio.  As upward stormlight flared silently across the obelisk's crown, Rowan spotted ancient chisel grooves rubbed smooth with pumice to mimic natural frost wear.  Someone in the second generation had deliberately shortened the stone, pulling its shadow inward so listeners would never visit the outer rim. 

Around him, the evaporating rainwater drew the plaza into a deepening hush, each drying tile extinguishing another faint voice until only scattered sighs remained.  Rowan hurried two paces past the truncated shadow's tip to a weathered slab overhanging the cloud abyss and pressed his ear against the last damp gleam.  No serene astronomer's oath rose from the cold crystal.  Instead, the dying moisture breathed ragged weeping, the iron clang of hammers shattering the lower cliff stairs, and a woman's hoarse whisper begging her children to ignore the terrible knocking beneath the hardening mist.  Then the final droplet vanished into the thin air, leaving the quartz completely mute. 

Those broken echoes offered no explicit chronicle, forcing Rowan to rely on conjecture to unite the shattered stairs, the terror below, and the altered pillar.  He reasoned that something predatory or toxic had woken inside the petrifying fog, driving desperate refugees upward to sever their own bridge, and that an early archivist had later shortened the monument out of mercy so traumatized heirs could grow without dread.  Understanding that mercy had become dangerous once forgotten, Rowan opened his ledger in the silent violet glow.  With steady strokes of his warm reed, he recorded both the fragmentary sounds and his reasoned inference, giving the council the truth required to halt the drills while honoring the compassion that had preserved their sanity.  When he closed the cover, the sun slipped beneath the mute thunderheads, and absolute stillness claimed the mountain.
