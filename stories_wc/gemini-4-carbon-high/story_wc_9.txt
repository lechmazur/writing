Three nights after the birth of her granddaughter, Maren reached the midnight glade above clouds, gasping at a loveliness that felt like silk pulled tight around her throat.  Below the granite rim, a boundless ocean of vapor billowed in silver swells beneath the moon, so blindingly luminous that watching it made her eyes ache and her chest seize.  Frost rimmed the pale gentians along the basin, and every shallow breath of thin alpine air carried a piercingly sweet scent of crushed pine that bruised her lungs.  For twenty years she had labored in shuttered rooms as an onyx-ink cartomancer, grinding black volcanic stone into pigment to paint prophetic cards that mapped disasters before they could touch her daughter, Clara.  Yet guarding tomorrow had cost Maren every shared today, driving her past the timberline to find where lost time accumulates. 

Her pride had broken beside Clara's birthing bed when the midwife unfolded an heirloom linen sheet for a family custom of tracing hands on birthday tablecloths.  Watching the newborn's tiny fist, dipped in marigold water, leave a golden print beside twenty ascending outlines of Clara's hands, Maren had stared in anguish at the stark white space where a mother's matching prints belonged.  Mountain lore held that neglected hours never vanish; unrooted in memory, they evaporate from lonely rooms and drift skyward, hanging as a cold blight over a bloodline until claimed through cloudbound repentance.  To spare the infant that lingering chill, Maren had borrowed the folded cloth and climbed into this dizzying sanctuary of starlight, ready to endure the suffocating splendor of every afternoon she had squandered. 

From her coat she drew a miniature porcelain dish split by a dolls' tea set saucer crack, the relic of her sharpest cruelty.  On Clara's seventh birthday, the girl had crept into Maren's study offering pretend tea, and Maren, terrified of spoiling a wet famine card, had swept the tray onto the flagstones.  By the laws of temporal resonance, sighting through that hairline fissure should have revealed the exact eddy in the glade where their forfeited years pooled.  Yet when Maren raised the tiny saucer toward the moonlit firs, calculating angles as she did with her prophetic spreads, the crack framed only barren, glittering frost. 

Panic constricted her windpipe before the truth struck her: the high sky refuses to yield unmeasured hours to a mind still armored in foresight.  Cartomancy had trained her to dissect every mystery into neat certainties, whereas true penance demanded she stand beneath heaven willingly perplexed, stripped of all power to predict her next breath.  Shivering, Maren gripped the heavy leather harness across her chest and forced her permanently blackened fingers to unfasten its brass buckles.  Her master decks and stone vials dropped into the heather, and a sudden gust swept dozens of painted arcana out over the glowing abyss of clouds.  Instantly, the geometric wards guarding her senses dissolved; the constellations ceased to read like tidy sentences of fate and blazed forth as incomprehensible, aching fire that stole the breath from her body. 

Dizzy with wonder and entirely defenseless against the unknown, she spread the birthday tablecloth across the frosted grass in place of a divination mat.  Kneeling beside the small, hesitant outline Clara had traced at age seven, Maren pressed her bare palm onto the blank weave and drew around her fingers with clear meltwater gathered from the gentians.  At her knee, the miniature porcelain dish gave a faint, crystalline chime, its hairline fracture igniting with a vein of trapped starlight.  From the pearl billows ringing the glade, ribbons of pale amber mist detached and glided inward, condensing above the damp linen into luminous, silent mirages.  Here was the reservoir she had climbed so high to witness: translucent phantoms of a growing girl pouring imaginary tea with solemn grace, braiding wild clover alone on sunlit steps, and searching a dark mirror for her mother's eyes. 

The heartbreaking grace of Clara's solitary blooming crushed Maren's ribs with a beauty so fierce she could only sob into the freezing vapor, breathing every abandoned hour deep into her chest until the cold mist warmed and dissolved.  When she lifted the tiny saucer to catch the last settling droplets, golden dew pooled unbroken inside the glaze, sealed by salt tears rather than stone pigment.  As dawn turned the cloud sea to rose quartz, Maren folded the damp linen and began her descent toward the cradle, achingly uncertain of everything ahead except the warmth of the small hand waiting below.
