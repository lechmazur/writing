Black tidewater lapped at the barnacled pilings of the pirate market hidden beneath a rotting pier.  Overhead, damp timbers groaned in the harbor wind, shedding splinters onto hooded lanterns along the narrow catwalks.  Scarred wreckers and coastal smugglers drifted past Maren, trading salvaged brass and untaxed rum in total silence.  As a moonshine runner's daughter raised among dark cypress creeks and outlawed men, she felt the gloom settle around her with a comforting unease.  Every shadow held a cutthroat, yet the scent of tar and raw corn liquor felt like childhood, cradling her inside a familiar danger.  Down on these slick planks, every transaction obeyed a truce of hush that held from midnight until sunrise.  One raised voice or drawn blade could alert the patrol station above, so the quiet crowd swiftly drowned anyone who broke the peace. 

By an outer piling stood Mercer, her father's longtime coastal broker, waiting beside an upturned barrel.  Two lanterns away lurked Galt, an informant known for selling inland still locations to federal marshals.  Poisonous mash fumes had blinded Maren's father years ago, leaving him reliant on his ears, and he remained curiously steadfast in trusting old friends.  Rumors of Mercer's greed never swayed the blind runner, who swore he knew an honest man by the steady warmth of a voice.  Unless Maren brought back audible proof before her father steered his loaded barge downriver at daybreak, he would sail straight into a raid.  Beneath her oilskin coat hummed a surplus wire recorder, its thin cord threaded to a tiny microphone inside her grandfather's antique watch fob.  She had come not merely to guard their routes, but to capture the sound of trust breaking so her father would finally hear the rot behind Mercer's smile. 

Mercer beckoned her close with an easy nod, expecting a quick swap of forged clearance papers for her father's new creek chart.  Once he held that chart, Mercer needed only a minute to pass it to Galt before the rising tide flooded the catwalks and scattered the market.  If Maren simply fled with the envelope, Mercer would blame youthful nerves at dawn and coax her father into a fresh trap tomorrow.  Instead, she set out to thwart the betrayal through false starts, bleeding away the night while pushing Mercer's composure toward the edge. 

Stepping to the barrel, Maren drew the waxed chart halfway from her pocket, then froze and glanced upward at a creak in the wharf.  She slid the envelope back inside her coat and waited ten slow breaths for phantom footsteps to fade.  When Mercer held out his hand again, she uncorked a sample jar of clear whiskey to test the bead, then paused over an ink smudge on his forged papers.  Folding the papers shut, she retreated into her moored skiff and lifted an oar as if calling off the trade, only to sigh and climb clumsily back aboard the planks.  Trapped by the market's iron hush, Mercer could neither grab her coat nor snap an order without summoning twenty silent knives from the shadows.  Again and again Maren restarted the swap, laying silver coins across the barrelhead, losing count whenever black swell washed over their boots, and sweeping the stack back into her fist. 

Pale grey light finally seeped between the rotting timbers overhead, warning that the harbor bar would soon grow too bright for Galt's escape.  Across the rising water, Galt untied his dory in disgust and dipped his oars toward the open sound.  Watching his bounty slip into the mist, Mercer shed the kindly cadence that had fooled a blind man for ten winters.  Maren touched the tarnished antique watch fob at her collarbone, angling its hollow silver face upward as Mercer lunged halfway across the barrel.  His breath burned against her cheek as he hissed that Galt had already paid half the blood money on her father's stills, cursing the old fool's blindness and demanding the chart before the dory vanished. 

Every venomous word, right down to the jagged hitch where twenty years of false brotherhood snapped in Mercer's throat, spooled onto the hidden wire.  Around them, tallow lamps went dark one by one as the first amber glare of daybreak touched the waves and swallowed the lower catwalks.  Kept safe to the last heartbeat by the quiet cutthroats melting into the pilings, Maren clicked off the switch inside her pocket.  Without a word, she stepped into her skiff, pressed the dry chart against her ribs, and rowed upriver to play the truth before the barge left home.
