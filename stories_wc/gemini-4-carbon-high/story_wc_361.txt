High along the aurora-lit ridgeway, Tarn checked the brass buckles of his pack for the fourth time in fifty paces.  Even after twenty winters as a jittery relic courier for the High Archive, he still flinched and flexed his gloved fingers whenever loose shale skittered into the dark abyss below.  Curtains of emerald and violet light rippled overhead, charging the thin mountain air with a faint static that set his teeth on edge.  He walked alone where two had once traveled, still unsteadied by the absent mentor who had vanished into an early blizzard near the northern crags. 

Master Oren had been calm as bedrock, laughing into the gale while teaching an anxious apprentice how to bear fragile remnants of the elder world.  Since that loss, Tarn had walked his circuit across the fade from memory to myth, watching both his teacher and the cargo they carried lose their living histories.  Younger couriers no longer remembered Oren as a man in patched wool, invoking him instead as a spectral warden of the peaks.  By that same slow erosion, lowland scholars had forgotten what their oldest treasures were, cataloging every sealed piece as inert ceremonial stone. 

Archive rules demanded that trailside exchanges last no longer than five seconds lest the freezing wind stiffen the runners.  Couriers met at granite cairns, traded numbered tubes in grim silence, and retreated down opposite ledges like iron cogs.  Tarn feared the drops, yet he feared that joyless drudgery even more, sustained by a quiet need to coax wonder from the routine.  To keep that spirit alive, he wore the parting gift of Oren, a dragonfly-wing pendant tucked warm beneath his scarf.  Cased in spun silver and clear glass, the translucent membrane preserved the veins of an ancient marsh skimmer extinct among the peaks.  Tuned by forgotten glasswrights to mimic how a dragonfly hovers motionless in midair, the charm holds a rare power that extends brief encounters.  Exposed beneath surging polar lights, the delicate wing hums in harmony with the sky and two nearby heartbeats, stretching a hurried trailside pass into a hushed, unhurried minute. 

Near midnight, Tarn reached Blacktooth Cairn as emerald arcs dipped unusually low across the jagged skyline.  His knees tapped nervously against the frosted marker while wind hissed through his empty harness.  Up from the southern switchbacks scrambled Maren, a young courier barely seventeen, clutching a perforated bronze cylinder to her chest.  Shivering with haste, she kept her gaze fixed strictly on her boots as guild discipline required.  As she held out the cylinder, she whispered the customary runner prayer to Saint Oren of the Lights, asking the legend for safe footing home.  Hearing his warm, imperfect friend flattened into a superstition tightened his chest.  Determined to offer the exhausted girl more than a numb transaction, Tarn parted his collar so the pendant swung free into the green radiance. 

Just then, ice cracked sharply inside a nearby crevice, startling Tarn so badly that he fumbled the exchange.  As he lunged to save the heavy vessel, his trembling hands pinned the silver pendant flat against the narrow slits of the tube.  Immediately, the captured wing blurred into a luminous haze, slowing the bitter wind to a velvet sigh as blown snow hung suspended around them like drifting pollen.  Maren gasped, arrested halfway through her retreat inside that sudden sanctuary of stretched time, her wide eyes lifting at last toward the blazing heavens. 

A swift handoff never let that resonance
 pierce a sealed case, yet this prolonged stillness held wing and metal together.  Within the cylinder lay the supposed Marsh Stone of Verin, long revered in myth as solid jade shaped by sky gods.  Before botanical knowledge slipped into legend, it had been the armored seed pod of an alpine lotus, waiting through ages of ice for the exact wingbeat of its lost pollinator.  Pressed to the grate beneath the electric sky, the pendant woke the ancient husk by vibrating sympathetically with its hollow chamber, splitting the calcified shell with a clear chime.  Right in their cupped hands, the forgotten relic began to germinate, threading pale luminous roots and a coiled silver frond outward through the bronze vents.  Warm summer scent filled the pocket of borrowed time as Maren stared in breathless awe at the living shoot.  Cradling the waking bloom under the green sky, Tarn finally understood what Oren had left him to guard, and for the first night in twenty winters, his hands were steady.
