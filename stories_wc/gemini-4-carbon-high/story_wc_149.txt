Throughout the dead season, over the hush of winter-cracked fields where subzero darkness split the fallow clay, Caelen labored inside his alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night.  Generations earlier, a permanent eclipse had extinguished the sun, leaving survival dependent on heated glass conservatories where grafted metallic trees grew beside roaring furnaces.  Caelen coughed beside a copper alembic, his frail chest rattling with an inherited frost that no ordinary elixir could thaw.  He never suspected that an ashen-veil ghost bride watched him with aching tenderness from limbo, her face obscured beneath drifting gray lace from a chapel burned three centuries ago.  Every ragged breath he drew thrilled Mireille with bittersweet wonder, fueling her sole desire to repay a debt across lifetimes before his weakening heart stopped. 

In their first incarnation, ruthless apothecaries had steeped Mireille's living blood in belladonna and viper salt to fashion an assassin, yet Caelen, then a humble mason, had wed her gladly.  When her former masters torched their sanctuary, Caelen had shielded her body and breathed the blistering smoke himself, condemning his soul to damaged lungs in every subsequent birth while she died hours later in soot-blackened tulle.  Even beyond the grave she remained lovingly venomous, her spectral touch distilling lethal nightshade born equally from ancient poison and fierce devotion.  Lingering inside the sealed glasshouse for five continuous seconds would poison the warm air and kill the mortal she adored.  Consequently, she could assist his desperate alchemy only through ephemeral cameo appearances, manifesting for half a heartbeat before wrenching herself back into the dark. 

For months, Caelen had noticed her solely at the corner of his eye: a smoky veil flashing across polished brass, pale fingers steadying a grafted bough, or a brief shadow behind misted panes.  During each fleeting visit, Mireille brushed microscopic traces of her alkaloid tears into the bark of his dormant quicksilver pears, knowing measured toxin to be alchemy's sharpest catalyst.  Yet venom alone could not achieve the phenomenon detailed in Caelen's open treatise, which sought the lost mastery of resonant awakenings.  His calculations proved that petrified sap and failing flesh would surge into vibrant bloom together only when struck by the exact harmonic pitch of an undying soul bond.  Unbeknownst to Caelen, the instrument already hung wired beneath his central branch: a carved stone bead etched with acoustic spirals, unearthed from the frozen furrow outside without any memory that his own mason's chisel had shaped it centuries ago as his wedding token. 

On the coldest midnight of the year, as furnace embers dimmed and ice groaned across the roof, Caelen collapsed beneath the barren orchard.  His lips darkened with frost, his pulse faltering as he lay upturned beneath the dry canopy, too exhausted to reach his tuning forks.  Refusing to lose him twice, Mireille forced three rapid manifestations that unraveled her very essence.  In her first instant of substance, she kissed the highest silver graft, releasing a single violet pearl of nightshade down the suspension wire.  Blinking out and lunging back a heartbeat later, she waited as the venom threaded the carved stone bead, then struck its grooved basalt with translucent fingertips.  A pure, ancient bridal note rang from the spinning stone, rippling through root and ribcage in a cascade of resonant awakenings.  Every metallic bough erupted into luminous blossom at once, transmuting that trace of deadly poison into warm golden nectar that rained directly onto Caelen's parted lips. 

Caelen inhaled a deep, effortless breath as his cured heart thundered warm, while the sympathetic chord unlocked three centuries of forgotten memory behind his startled eyes.  During her third flicker beside the roots, the resonant chime parted her veil of ash just enough for him to recognize his lost wife and reach upward calling her ancient name.  Swept by a fierce, mournful elation, Mireille rejoiced as color flooded his cheeks even while her spirit wept for the answer she could never give.  One lingering reply would taint his newly healed lungs with fatal nightshade and condemn a living man to spend his restored years grieving against cold glass.  With her centuries-old debt finally paid, she resolved to ghost him forever, severing her tether to the stone and erasing every spectral trace without farewell before his warm hand met empty air.  As she dissolved gladly into oblivion, radiant orchard light spilled outward across the dark frost, illuminating Caelen weeping in solitude yet gloriously, wholly alive.
