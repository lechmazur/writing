In the heavy silence after the last wish is granted, only the tide speaks along the rotting piers.  Corin walks the damp stones of the paper-lantern cove alone, a deserted alchemist surveying the husk of a spent age.  Overhead, thousands of translucent lanterns hang dark on cables strung across the sheltered bay.  For three centuries, those paper globes burned with stray vapors whenever citizens uncorked solar resin to wish away labor, hunger, or grief.  Three nights ago, when the mines yielded their final splinter, the townsfolk pooled it to conjure golden barges bound for a rumored undying isle.  Because Corin warned that the isle was a mirage and guarded his kiln, his apprentices and neighbors sailed into the fog and abandoned him. 

Inside his ransacked shop, a stoppered flask of dream tincture sits beside a farewell note from his youngest pupil.  One drop would fill these silent streets with warm phantom light and the gentle laughter of vanished friends.  Corin pulls the stopper and pours the violet draught down the stone drain.  He is a man who rejects false comfort, knowing that soothing illusions hollowed out his people long before the resin failed.  Through the window, tiny scrub fires flicker along the barren basalt ridge above the bay.  Outcast drifters from older dead towns camp there in the wind, barred for generations from the harbor and unable to drink its brackish, poisoned water. 

Prying loose a hearthstone beneath his cold kiln, Corin lifts out the secret work he hid from the fleet: an amber bottle with no opening.  Fused seamless around one pure drop of primeval solar marrow, its golden walls possess neither neck nor stopper.  Traditional vessels were blown with mouths so mortals could release and consume the marrow inside, trading tomorrow for an effortless today.  Trapped forever within unbroken amber, this captive drop cannot be spent as a wish; instead, when warmed, it radiates perpetual sympathetic order into surrounding matter.  Carrying the vessel and a lit brazier to the central harbor keystone, Corin aims far beyond personal survival.  For this ghost town revival to endure, he must use the resonant core to reimagine an entire civilization.  Centuries of easy magic reduced every city to masters hoarding vials and beggars waiting outside, stripping humanity of cooperative craft.  Here in the ruins, he means to establish a reciprocal commonwealth where natural forces power human skill and every workshop demands shared effort. 

He sets the mouthless amber over the coals until its golden heart beats with steady heat.  No ledger or chalk blueprint lies beside him, because consulting sequential notes imprints disjointed rules that shatter under strain.  Nor can he rely on stored nostalgia, which would blindly reproduce the selfish architecture of the old world.  To attune the vessel, Corin must conceive the whole interlocking civic design simultaneously, holding and balancing every live variable strictly through working memory.  Bracing against the cold spray, he fills the narrow arena of his immediate focus with four linked systems.  First, he shapes sunken shipwrecks into submerged turbines turned by the pull of the tide.  Keeping those blades spinning in active thought, he computes the pressure required to drive seawater through limestone filters and flush the poisoned wells clean.  Third, he couples copper dynamos to the turning shafts so steady current climbs the cables into the unlit lanterns overhead.  Fourth, while juggling tide, water, and spark together, he converts bolted guild vaults into open mills and forges geared with paired counterweights that only two neighbors working together can engage.  Sweat stings his eyes as cognitive strain nears its limit, and an unbidden memory of his lost apprentices threatens to displace the dynamo ratios.  Banishing sentimental solace once more, Corin locks all four dynamic equations into simultaneous harmony and presses both palms against the hot, seamless amber. 

A resonant bell note ripples across the harbor as the glowing bottle sinks halfway into the limestone keystone.  Beneath the piers, shattered hulls groan and interlock into broad wooden wheels that catch the rising tide with a deep, steady thrum.  Stone fountains cough out crusted salt and gush cold, sweet water across the cobbles, while iron vault doors split apart into wide workshop archways.  Then, strand by strand above the bay, thousands of paper lanterns bloom awake with clear golden warmth drawn from the living sea.  High on the dark ridge, dozens of weary figures rise from their dying fires, descend the cliff stairs, and step hesitantly into the illuminated square.  As waking voices gather around the sweet fountains, Corin walks to the open forge with blistered palms, ready to lift his half of the first counterweight beside a newcomer.
