Six months after the cryptocurrency crashes, the server farms along the canal stood gutted, yet strangers climbed four dark flights every evening to reach Corinne's rain-beaded skylight loft.  Beneath slanted glass where storm water clung like quicksilver above pine floorboards, she worked as an onyx-ink cartomancer.  Her late grandmother, Sava, had left her the studio, ciphered ledgers, and jars of crushed black onyx mulled with walnut oil.  Sava had died without explaining her rules, entrusting Corinne with an inherited mystery steeped in mineral pigment and decades of silent observation.  Neighbors who had watched digital fortunes vanish on glowing screens came not for miracles, but for symbols heavy enough to hold in shaking hands. 

Unlike street diviners promising quick rebounds, Corinne refused to predict prices or lucky dates.  Steadily curious, she welcomed ruined traders, sleepless pensioners, and unpaid clerks without a trace of judgment.  Her real aim was to map cultural patterns across the aftermath, charting how a sober city had surrendered its savings to weightless code.  Comparing Sava's undated floor rubbings against archives of older financial manias, Corinne had spent months reverse engineering her grandmother's unrecorded method.  Sava had never practiced prophecy; she had used cards as physical coordinates for collective fever.  At the center of the floorboards lay one card painted in matte black: The Hollow Mint.  Whenever a visitor chose an emblem from Sava's deck and set it on the wood, Corinne knelt with her grandmother's brass measuring tape to log the exact distance and angle from that central ruin. 

Short spans betrayed raw panic, while wide intervals marked older hungers, like a daughter hoping sudden yield might erase family debt.  Pundits still hunted for a lone villain or single broken formula, yet Sava's geometry rejected linear blame.  Reconstructing a crash worked only by multiplying paths, extending each stranger's placement outward from The Hollow Mint into branching chains of linen cards.  Across seventy sessions, those dark spokes had webbed the room like frost, revealing where envy, fatigue, and waning trust converged.  Yet one sector beneath the dripping glass stayed bare, leaving the radial pattern lopsided beside Sava's historical diagrams.  Each night, Corinne stretched her measuring tape across that empty wedge, certain the missing coordinate belonged to someone who had built the machine. 

Late one November evening, a gaunt man in a soaked coat knocked once and stepped inside.  He gave a borrowed name, though Corinne knew his shadowed eyes from broadcasts of the Veridian protocol hearings.  Keeping her manner steadily curious, she gestured toward the lantern circle and offered him Sava's deck.  Passing over painted crowns and shipwrecks, he drew an unnumbered card depicting a sluice gate propped halfway open.  Silently, he set the black card squarely inside the barren sector, closer to The Hollow Mint than any ruined depositor had ventured.  Corinne drew the steel blade of her measuring tape between the cards, noted fourteen centimeters at thirty degrees north, and caught her breath. 

In Sava's oldest ledger, that exact vector bore a margin label Corinne had never deciphered: The Watchman's Flinch.  When she chalked those three words onto the pine beside his card without asking his identity, the engineer's guarded posture broke.  Beneath the patter of cold rain, he leaned forward to confide what he had withheld from prosecutors and furious investors alike.  He had never siphoned the vanished funds; alarmed by how blindly working families worshiped his code, he had quietly exposed an internal risk gauge so small savers might withdraw in time.  Instead, seeing that warning flare public had ignited a contagious stampede that wiped out every account before sunrise.  Bracing for scorn, he watched Corinne dip a reed pen into onyx pigment, ink four fresh cards, and connect his sluice gate outward by multiplying paths until his lone act met the trails of a baker, two machinists, and a teacher.  His breathing steadied as the completed floor map demonstrated what courtroom transcripts never could: his fear had been only one tributary joining a hundred older currents halfway.  Studying the finished wheel of cards, Corinne finally grasped the heart of Sava's legacy.  Her grandmother had ground dull stone instead of glossy varnish so no visitor could mistake a card for a private mirror, turning solitary guilt into a shared human landscape.
