Sera stepped past the broken seals into the subterranean archive of a lost civilization, adjusting the saffron veil that marked her as a sworn historian.  Woven from raw silk steeped in bitter saffron resin, the cloth filtered the air so stray atoms of nostalgia could not enter her lungs and overwrite her mind.  Centuries ago, the vanished Vael had distilled their dearest memories into luminous particulate matter, preserving whole eras of tenderness as drifting golden haze.  Above ground, her monastic order had recently begun rationing surface motes to grieving citizens, preaching that the Vael had ascended peacefully into eternal rapture.  Yet three nights earlier, after inhaling a sanctioned dose, her younger sister Talia had slipped into a radiant, smiling stupor, weeping with delight at invisible yesterdays while forgetting Sera existed.  Torn between terror and aching love, Sera had broken her vows to pursue forbidden knowledge sealed within this deepest vault: the truth of how the Vael actually vanished. 

At the center of the sunken rotunda stood the bronze prism pagoda at twilight, catching a horizontal blade of crimson sunlight through a narrow canyon cleft.  Cast from resonant bell bronze and slotted with angled crystal facets, its nine tapering tiers awakened only during this fleeting threshold between day and night.  As the dying light refracted through the metallic slits, dormant clouds inside the hollow spire ignited with breathtaking, sorrowful splendor.  Carvings along the base revealed that these final memories stretched across the end of innocence, spanning the fatal epoch when the Vael learned to harvest living hope into artificial longing.  Watching that terrible radiance bloom, Sera passed across the end of her own sheltered innocence too, mourning the gentle faith she could never inhabit again. 

Yet she could not read the chronicle in its corrupted form, for centuries of stagnation had encased these atoms of nostalgia within gilded shells of delusion.  Nostalgia survives through repetition, clinging to any steady heartbeat, chant, or familiar cadence to weave an inescapable loop of sweet regret.  Already the golden haze sensed her pulse through the saffron silk and projected piercing mirages of young Talia laughing beneath summer plum trees.  Sera felt a wild surge of sweetness at that vanished sight, followed instantly by dread.  To purify the clouded particulates and strip away their narcotic glamour, she lifted two stone mallets from the altar.  Resonant shockwaves from the bronze prisms could crack those false husks, though only if she rang the metal by avoiding patterns altogether.  Should she hesitate, the twilight beam would die before the mist cleared; should she repeat a rhythm, the dust would crystallize around her melody and claim her mind.  Every strike, breath, and step had to remain steadily unpredictable, unceasing in momentum yet fiercely unrepeatable. 

Sera lunged forward to strike a low bronze eave, then immediately scraped her boot across rough slate and tapped two upper prisms at jagged intervals.  Shrill, asymmetrical chimes echoed off the stone walls as the agitated cloud billowed outward in desperate defense.  Phantom plum blossoms brushed her saffron veil while illusions of Talia sang a cradle song in four even beats, begging Sera to rest inside that warm, familiar measure.  Weeping beneath the bitter cloth, Sera stumbled on purpose, held her breath for seven counts, gasped twice, and slammed a mallet against a dull iron rivet before grazing three high facets.  Her wrists blistered and her chest burned as she whirled around the pagoda, refusing symmetry, refusing comfort, keeping her clamor steadily unpredictable while crimson light bled into deep violet.  With one abrupt, dissonant crash across five uneven tiers, the golden illusion shattered like thin ice, leaving behind a suspended veil of clear, purified silver. 

Saffron resin blocked lie and truth alike; to receive what she had fought to purify, Sera untied her veil and breathed deep before darkness claimed the canyon.  Ten thousand unvarnished lives rushed through her blood at once.  She beheld Vael families starving beside full storehouses, dying in ecstatic blindness because they treasured perfected yesterdays over messy tomorrows, while a few dissident historians wept and rang erratic bronze bells to wake whoever still breathed.  Dropping her mallets, Sera fell to her knees in anguished joy, heartbroken for a civilization that loved beauty unto extinction, yet blazing with exaltation at the miracle they had bequeathed her.  Unpatterned sound could break the trance.  Hot tears scalded her bare cheeks as cold twilight air stung her lungs, every sharp, imperfect sensation singing that she was alive and Talia could still be called back into the present.
