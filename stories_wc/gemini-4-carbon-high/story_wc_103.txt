No dawn or dusk ever reached the misted abyss, for neither hours nor seasons existed there.  Suspended in that hush, Julian drifted through a waking dream where every leaf shivered with a faint and ceaseless tremor.  In the world above, he had been a celebrated hedge maze designer, sculpting labyrinths of rough boxwood and velvety yew to delay the footsteps of kings.  After grief took everyone he loved, he had been left with a pocketful of fading photographs whose slick paper felt cruelly identical beneath his thumb whether they showed laughter or mourning.  Haunted by that flatness, he abandoned mortal estates with a single obsession: to discover the texture of time.  He ached to know whether pure duration possessed a tangible grain like weathered bark, river slate, or warm linen. 

His pilgrimage ended at a halo fragment wedged inside the deepest cleft of this sunless chasm.  Fallen from some forgotten firmament, the curved shard of calcified radiance hummed with condensed eternity, vibrating so fiercely that direct contact would have blinded an unprepared mind.  To slow and disperse that overwhelming frequency until human skin could bear it, Julian cultivated a vast living bridge garden across the void.  Braiding aerial fig roots and supple willow shoots over the emptiness, he trained the suspended walkways to branch through hyperbolic geometry.  Every grafted arch divided and pleated at constant negative curvature, ruffling outward like giant black coral so that boundless surface area folded within the narrow gulf.  Those exponential pleats snared the singing light of the relic and stretched a single instant into miles of trembling botanical corridors. 

Yet the very perfection of his labyrinth left Julian retrocognitively trapped.  Because negative curvature inverted the flow of causality, every woven span he crossed pulled his awareness backward into moments he had already lived.  Pacing the swaying boughs in a hushed trance, he tasted childhood frost, smelled crushed yew on iron shears, and inhabited the quiet room where his sister drew her last breath.  No bridge led toward tomorrow, and his thoughts quivered in a somnolent rapture of perpetual return.  To orient himself amid those recursive echoes, he had pinned his fading photographs along the knotted handrails as spatial markers.  Each sepia square governed a distinct alcove of memory: a courtyard glistening after rain, two clasped hands upon oak, an autumn orchard.  However, the pale luminescence of the central shard drifted endlessly through the mist and overexposed the silver salts on the paper. 

Julian halted upon a shivering cradle of tendrils and lifted the orchard print from its thorn.  Its trees had bleached into a ghostly wash of ivory, and simultaneously within his mind the remembered scent of fallen apples thinned into a colorless hum.  All around him, the living bridge garden vibrated like the taut strings of a struck harp, trembling in sympathy with the core.  Feeling the sap shudder beneath his boots, he perceived at last the folly of his architecture.  Habits forged as a hedge maze designer had persuaded him that eternity could be walled inside leafy chambers and preserved like dried blossoms.  Instead, he had merely turned the entire chasm into another photograph, trapping himself in a bleached stasis where nothing moved and therefore nothing possessed any texture at all. 

Duration could only be felt in passage, and passage demanded loss.  Clutching the nearly blank print, Julian turned toward the radiant origin and traced the converging geodesics inward through hyperbolic geometry.  Every inward stride compressed decades of spectral recollection into a breathless, quaking reverie that rattled his ribs like distant thunder.  At last he reached the innermost knot of braided roots, where the halo fragment blazed in a fever of arrested ages.  With trembling reverence, Julian pressed the faded paper and his bare palm directly against the singing crest of solid light.  A crystalline chime split the hush, and the pent resonance surged outward along every exponentially branched bough.  Shattered by mortal touch, the halo fragment began to disintegrate, dissolving alongside the bleached prints and ancient root spans into a tempest of warm golden dust.  As the woven sanctuary gave way beneath his feet and the first true second rushed through the abyss, Julian closed his eyes amid the falling luminescence.  Plunging into the newly awakened dark, he felt the released stream brush across his skin at last, rough as raw silk spun from lightning and ash.
