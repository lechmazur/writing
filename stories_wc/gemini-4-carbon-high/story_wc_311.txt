Vaelen stood beneath the cracked limestone dome of the ancient observatory at dusk, listening to a wax cylinder turn with a soft hiss.  Three evenings had passed after finding the old recordings hidden inside the hollow pedestal of the meridian transit, yet each replay felt like a hand brushing his sleeve from another room.  From the brass horn came the quiet voice of Elias, pausing now and then to catch a failing breath while explaining the alignment of the mirrors.  For an ordinary mourner, death was a wall, but Vaelen was a heartbroken reaper for whom the grave had always been a meeting place.  He had expected to welcome the dying scholar across the dark threshold at last, only to arrive months ago and find the chamber empty save for a warm, resonant prism. 

Elias had not waited to be gathered into eternal rest; instead, he had stepped into the focal cradle at twilight and burned his own immortal spark into the glass.  Now nothing remained of him in either world except the echo of sacrifice humming steadily through the tall brass frame.  All winter Vaelen had lingered beside that faint vibration without comprehending its purpose, until the hidden cylinders explained the unfinished mechanism.  Wrapped carefully around the last spool of wax was a translucent calfskin atlas page with sea monsters inked across uncharted waters.  Before illness confined him to this high cliff, Elias had studied old mariners who went mad staring at empty horizons.  On the recording, he remarked with quiet tenderness that sailors survived the abyss by painting coiled leviathans upon blank waves, turning infinite nothingness into a beast with borders. 

Vaelen knew too well how the dying faltered before that same blankness.  Every evening, souls stalled along the cliff outside, paralyzed by shapeless dread that rose through the open shutter of the dome like freezing gray mist.  Elias had surrendered his existence to build an instrument meant to give names to nameless fears, trusting Vaelen to tend the lens in his absence.  With careful, pale fingers, the reaper lifted the vellum chart and slid it into the copper clasp directly behind the humming prism.  His thumb lingered for a heartbeat upon a faded smudge of ink at the margin, as close to a clasp of hands as the two of them would ever share again. 

Outside, the sun sank toward the cold sea, sending one low crimson beam through the western slit of the stone wall.  The ray struck the luminous prism and broke apart, casting intricate geometry across the shadowy chamber through scattered light.  Refracted bands of amber and violet passed through the translucent calfskin, projecting the fine grid of latitude lines and the curved spines of sea serpents into the drifting gray vapor.  Caught within those mapped boundaries and touched by the warmth of Elias, the formless panic began to crystallize in midair.  One after another, slender beads of smoky quartz condensed out of the mist and pattered softly onto the slate table below, each stone trapping a distinct, bounded terror inside its facets. 

Yet a boundary alone was only half the mercy; the final step belonged to Vaelen and the strange curse of his kind.  A reaper possesses no native mortal tongue, only a hollow voice that echoes forgotten phrases shed by the departed at their final hour.  When Elias was alive, they had sat whole nights with a table between them, the astronomer speaking freely of constellations while Vaelen could answer only in borrowed fragments of old lullabies and lost harbor greetings.  Now Elias had turned even that lonely limitation into part of their shared craft.  Vaelen gathered the first cold crystal into his palm and breathed across its dark surface.  From his chest rose the murmur of a sailor from centuries past, calling the stone the undertow beneath a winter keel.  Recognized and named, the quartz warmed at once and dissolved into harmless gold dust, while out on the cliff a waiting shadow sighed and stepped peacefully into the quiet dark.  Piece by piece, Vaelen worked through the tray, whispering vanished childhood words for thunder, forgotten prayers against the tide, and obscure titles of painted beasts.  By the time true night claimed the sky and the wax cylinder clicked into silence, the slate table stood bare and the cliffside beyond the walls was calm.  Vaelen rested his palm against the cooling brass housing, separated forever from the mortal he loved, yet held inside the steady warmth of the work they finished together every evening.
