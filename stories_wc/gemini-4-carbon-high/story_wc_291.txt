Arthur stood at the rim of the shadowed quarry in mist, holding his wooden sketching case against his chest in a furrowed hush.  Below him, grooved walls of wet limestone vanished into pale vapor that swallowed every birdcall and footstep.  As a face blind portrait artist, Arthur was accustomed to worlds that refused to cohere.  Whenever he looked at a living person, eyes, lips, and cheekbones floated as disconnected shapes, denying him the instant recognition others took for granted.  To survive professionally, he had become very methodical, translating human heads into strict grids, brass caliper ratios, and numbered tonal steps.  Yet decades of reducing people to geometry had sealed him inside an icy detachment.  Two years ago, he began illustrating county cold cases to awaken dormant empathy, hoping that restoring identities to the nameless dead might teach his numb heart to feel. 

Detectives abandon a file after the evidence disappears, yet Arthur could not let this woman vanish twice.  Two nights earlier, a thief had emptied the county annex locker holding a young woman found dead here fifteen winters past.  Her skull, dental charts, and crime scene photographs were gone before Arthur could measure a single bone.  All that remained in his studio was one item he had signed out early to mix matching oil glazes: a crimson silk masquerade mask ribbon fragment once knotted in her hair.  Because his habits were very methodical, Arthur had already logged every thread of that torn swatch under his microscope.  Snagged inside the frayed stitching where ribbon once met mask were black crumbs of synthetic rubber and spores of cliff fern.  Earlier investigators had ignored pit debris because her remains already lay in plain view.  Arthur realized what those specks meant now: neither fern nor rubber existed on the quarry floor, only along the seepage liner bolted halfway up the north shale bench. 

Moving slowly so as not to disturb the solemn silence, he descended the narrow haul road into the damp gloom.  Chisel marks scored the towering rock faces like the wrinkled brow of a sleeper, deepening the quiet gravity around him.  Where the trail met the middle terrace, a heavy sheet of black diversion rubber hung pinned across a weeping fissure directly beneath the upper rim.  High at one rusted bracket, the top edge had torn open years ago and then plugged shut with hardened clay.  Below that sealed throat, groundwater seeping out of the shale had inflated the elastic liner into a huge, taut blister sagging over the ledge.  Arthur knelt, unlatched his case, and drew a steel scalpel reserved for sharpening charcoal.  Feeling a solid curve resting inside the cold swell, he leaned close to lance the stretched membrane near its bottom seam.  A thin jet of iron dark water hissed into the mist, whispering over the shale until the bladder collapsed around its hidden burden. 

Reaching delicately through the incision, Arthur lifted out a bespoke full face masquerade mask crafted from thick leather hardened in beeswax.  Deprived of air and sunlight inside the frigid pocket, its molded contours had survived fifteen seasons without warping.  At its left temple, a brass eyelet still clutched three crimson threads matching his ribbon swatch.  On the outside, pale lacquer presented a stylized, celebratory smile built for secrecy.  Inside, however, an artisan had molded the leather over a plaster life cast of the wearer, preserving every contour of her skin.  With the stolen bones beyond reach, Arthur propped a sheet of gray paper against a stone block and began her portrait by unifying opposites.  His left thumb explored the dark, concave hollow inside the mask while his right hand constructed bright, convex flesh across the page.  He paired black willow charcoal from the quarry shadows with white limestone chalk rubbed off the misty cliff, turning empty shell into solid bone and festive disguise into bare truth. 

Working through touch rather than fractured sight, his clinical grid yielded to quiet intimacy.  Beneath the painted grin on the shell, his thumb discovered a tiny childhood ridge along her nose, a hesitant lower lip, and two anxious vertical creases pressed between her eyebrows.  She had been afraid long before reaching these cliffs, hiding quiet worry behind gilded leather.  When Arthur lowered his chalk in the damp stillness, his eyes still could not fuse her features into a single whole.  Yet his fingertips remembered the shape of her fear, and a sudden, piercing grief thawed the long numbness in his chest, mourning a woman who would not vanish again.
