Across the hollowed interior of the baroque asteroid Ophir, five years after time became visible, gilded plaster seraphim gazed down upon drifting currents of luminous amber.  Cassian walked alone beneath the scalloped marble arches, a defunct revolutionary whose fiery cause had perished without a final battle.  On the morning the chronal veil tore open, both palace guards and insurgent miners had looked up to behold their remaining days hanging in the air like frail, fraying threads of tarnished gold.  Confronted with the palpable brevity of mortal existence, oligarchs and rebels alike had laid down their rifles in a hush of shared grief, leaving the gilded caverns steeped in a slow, burnished sorrow. 

With the future visibly dwindling toward stillness, the surviving citizens of Ophir turned their passions backward, consumed by a bitter archaeological dispute over the vanished Founders who had carved their world millennia ago.  Chief Archivist Vane maintained that the extravagant frescoes, twisted bronze columns, and velvet vaults proved the Founders were pitiless sybarites who had worked generations to death for imperial vanity.  Cassian, who had always been poetically inclined and had once penned lyric sonnets upon ammunition crates, rejected that bleak indictment with all the fervor his silenced rebellion had left behind.  Denied the chance to build a liberated tomorrow, he burned with a singular longing to reimagine an entire civilization, convinced the ornate stone chambers were crafted not as monuments to pride, but as an elegiac refuge against the cold void. 

Their rivalry converged upon a curved crystal vase shard excavated from the rubble of the sunken Hall of Mirrors.  Vane cataloged the jagged remnant as trivial debris from an aristocratic banquet, preparing to seal the lower gallery forever and publish his cynical history as settled law.  Yet when Cassian tilted the crystal vase shard toward a passing ribbon of visible yesterday, he noticed microscopic bevels cut into its curves like rhythmic stanzas of ancient verse.  His literary memory stirred, recalling an old miner folk ballad that spoke of covering mirrors during storms so the darkened glass might permit a crystal vessel to sing. 

Cassian realized the Founders had endured an earlier epoch of visible time and had crafted ceremonial vases as holographic chronal vessels, meaning even a solitary fragment could project its stored memory under the pressure of a temporal squall.  Whenever temporal gales roared through Ophir, however, the towering silvered mirrors of the sunken hall reflected the surging bronze decades back and forth into a blinding maelstrom that drowned out any faint projection and drove onlookers mad with phantom regrets.  To prove his hypothesis before Vane locked the bronze gates, Cassian had to devise a rapid rigging system that could shroud every reflective wall at the peak of a tempest while funneling a single beam of raw time onto the altar.  Searching the dusty armories of his youth, he gathered crates of heavy crimson banners that had once flown above rebel barricades, stitching brass rings along their hems to fit the ancient hooks hidden behind the baroque cornices. 

Three nights later, a tremendous chronal squall shook the asteroid, sending torrents of radiant, bruised violet centuries howling down the fluted stairwells.  Summoning Vane to witness the trial, Cassian braced himself against the gale of drifting memories and began hauling the hemp cords, practicing the forgotten rite of covering mirrors during storms.  Broad sheets of revolutionary velvet unfurled across the soaring glass frames, extinguishing the chaotic glare of multiplied ages until the cavernous room fell into a hushed, sanctuary darkness.  Only one unreflected shaft of tempestuous amber poured from the ceiling vent directly into the crystal vase shard resting upon the central plinth. 

Freed from the clamor of competing reflections, the etched crystal sang with a low harmonic chime and unfolded a clear, golden apparition in the quiet air between the two men.  Vane stepped backward, his austere certainty crumbling as he beheld not tyrants reveling over chained laborers, but thousands of weary exiles working shoulder to shoulder in tender solidarity.  Masons, scholars, and children pressed delicate gold leaf onto raw asteroid iron while weeping for a lost blue planet, fashioning extravagant beauty in the dark solely so their distant descendants might never succumb to despair.  In the warm glow of that resurrected grace, Vane bowed his head and conceded the archive without a word.  Watching his faded rebel banners tremble gently over the blinded glass, Cassian felt an ache of antique peace; though his uprising had never delivered the future, it had at last redeemed the dead, leaving their memory gleaming softly through the twilight of their world.
