Four hours after the tower falls, the western horizon burns in a tranquil blaze across the salt dunes.  Sheets of silent amber flame rise from the shattered imperial spire, bathing Mara and her desert rose farm inside its domed glass enclosure in warm crimson light.  No thunder from the collapsing capital reaches the gravel paths between her rows of sculpted trees.  Over twelve dry seasons, Mara has carved delicate spiral flutes into the swollen silver trunks so the expanding bark traps every stray vibration in an engraved quiet. 

As the pacifist daughter of a relentless bounty hunter, she built this hushed conservatory to undo the cruel trade of her bloodline.  Wild desert roses brew a milky neurotoxin that trackers harvest to paralyze fugitives for the magistrate courts.  Refusing violence, Mara devotes her days to inculcating plants with values of mercy, patience, and open hospitality.  Through gentle root grafting, sweet mineral rinses, and tensionless pruning, she teaches the defensive flora to surrender chemical malice and store pure drinking water beneath spineless blossoms. 

Above the central blossom bed, one cracked roof pane lets in a thin ribbon of drifting ash.  An hour ago, a listing vessel fleeing the ruined spire jettisoned deadweight, dropping a torn airship ballast sandbag scrap straight through that high glass triangle.  Mara kneels beside a young cultivar, soaking the coarse hemp in cistern water before binding it around a fresh splice.  With empires turning to cinders outside, her sole purpose is to weave continuity through chaos by turning fallen wreckage into living shelter.  Though she despises hunting, she inherited the acute senses of her father to a degree that renders her senselessly perceptive.  All day her mind absorbs useless minutiae, counting nineteen missing scales on a basking lizard, smelling brass oxidation on distant hinges, and distinguishing four separate riverbeds in the spilled ballast sand.  Yet those same overwhelming instincts also read a hidden story inside the frayed canvas.  Beneath the faded black stencil of an imperial armory barge, her fingertips and nose detect oat flour from kitchen stores, clumsy yellow nursery stitches, and crushed fever herbs. 

The iron airlock clicks open, and Garrick steps beneath the fiery vault with his long extraction blade already drawn.  Soot streaks his leather coat, and empty venom vials clink softly at his belt before the carved trunks swallow the sound.  Even from ten paces away, Mara notices sixty tiny ember burns across his cuffs, a split boot welt, and the frightened tremor beneath his jaw.  Without the tower standing to validate his warrants, her aging father knows no way to survive the sudden lawlessness except finishing his final contract.  "An armory dirigible went down beyond the northern ridge, and I need fresh paralytic sap before dark," Garrick says, though the acoustic wood flattens his bark into a murmur.  Spotting the stenciled hemp wrapped around her graft, he strides forward and presses his steel point against the oldest silver caudex. 

Mara rises unhurriedly inside the radiant glow, neither blocking his arm nor flinching as a clear bead swells from the nicked bark.  Reaching out gently, she gathers the droplet from his blade onto her finger and touches it straight to her tongue.  Garrick gasps and jerks the knife back, expecting her heart to seize on the spot.  Instead, Mara breathes evenly while golden firelight shimmers across the silent blossoms.  "There is no poison left in this grove, Father, only clean water coaxed from roots that unlearned fear," she tells him.  She guides his gaze down to the damp airship ballast sandbag scrap cradling the new graft.  Quietly, she points out the yellow nursery thread, the dusting of porridge grain, and the medicinal leaves embedded in the weave.  "No armed mutineers stole that hull, only caretakers and children who will die of thirst tonight unless someone brings them water," she says softly. 

Garrick stares at the mended canvas, his tracker eyes finally confirming the tiny domestic traces he had been too panicked to seek.  Outside the curved glass, the burning skyline settles into a steady, luminous ember bed that illuminates the dark desert without scorching their refuge.  Mara lifts two full wooden canteens tapped from the cistern and holds one toward his scarred hand.  Surrounded by the deep hush of living wood, the old hunter wipes the sweet sap on his sleeve and decides to sheathe his blade.  Together they step out toward the northern ridge, carrying unbroken mercy across the ruins of the old world into the new.
