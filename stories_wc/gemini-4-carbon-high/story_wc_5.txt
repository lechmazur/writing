Deep in the subarctic crater, the gales died at the solstice nadir, leaving the withered orchard locked in sudden, uncanny silence.  Around Oren, ranks of dead plum and apple trees stood skeletal in the gloom, killed years ago when the thermal vents beneath the impact basin cooled.  This was the Polar Slack, the brief span each winter while the world holds its breath between the southward blizzards and the returning northern roar.  Kneeling beside the central graft stump, Oren did not shiver, even as rime crusted his wool sleeves and stiffened his eyelashes.  A flash-paper alchemist trained in volatile instants had to practice the opposite discipline here, locking his knees into the snowpack and bracing his wrists like stone so not a tremor of his own blood disturbed the timber. 

When the orchard froze and the settlement scattered into nomadic ice camps across the outer basin, stone cairns and riverbeds vanished under shifting drifts.  The relief sleds at the southern pass needed no charts of buried rock; Oren had returned alone to translate heartbeats into maps, showing where survivors endured the dark and whose weakening pulse needed firewood first.  Level on the stump lay his base, a heavy brass storybook illustration plate salvaged from the ruined schoolhouse cellar after the surveyors took every clean sheet of copper.  Though its center depicted a fabled child gathering summer plums, the old engraver had carved the twelve jagged peaks of the real crater rim around its beveled border with exact fidelity.  With slow fingers, Oren unstoppered a leaden phial of safekept starlight, distilled through quartz lenses during the last clear autumn nights and hoarded against the winter clouds. 

He tipped a silver film of that captive radiance across the engraved brass until the miniature peaks gleamed like a pale mirror.  In alchemical sympathy, pure starlight answers to warm iron pulsing in living veins, yet wind and falling snow ordinarily drown so faint a summons.  Now, with the curved basalt walls of the crater acting as a vast listening bowl and the petrified roots beneath the stump anchored to bedrock, the still liquid began to stir.  Numbness crept into his calves and frost bound his coat to the crust, yet Oren held his spine motionless and shallowed his breath for long minutes while the ripples clarified.  Near the carved northeastern tooth, six brisk concentric rings pulsed in steady unison, marking a healthy herding tent.  Farther east, quick, fluttering beats betrayed families sheltering with children under the scarp, while below the western glacier two isolated rhythms beat sluggishly, lagging perilously between strokes. 

Ink would freeze solid in this air, and warm fingertips would shatter the surface tension.  Instead, lifting chilled bone forceps, Oren fixed each living coordinate onto the brass by creating patterns with discarded items gleaned earlier from the dead orchard floor.  He set six rusted grafting brads in a tight arc for the northern herders, then arranged shards of broken conservatory glass and chipped pearl buttons in nested rings where the eastern children breathed.  Over the faltering western beats, he laid two bent copper cultivar tags pointing inward like urgent arrowheads, spacing split plum stones behind them to measure the dangerous pause between each throb.  Resting in the luminous pool, the humble litter of a ruined harvest became a precise relief stencil of survival. 

High along the basalt cliffs, a dry hiss warned that the atmosphere had finished holding its breath and the polar gale was rushing back.  Now Oren turned to the perilous heart of his craft.  Ordinary parchment cannot drink faint light in freezing dark, whereas nitrated flash paper is hungrier than tinder, bred to flare into ashless nothingness in the blink of an eye.  He lowered a dry, sensitized sheet onto the plate, letting the fibers soak up the safekept starlight around every engraved peak and arranged relic, then nicked a tiny flint wheel at its corner.  Silver fire raced silently across the page, awakening the image.  At the exact instant the sheet blazed whole, before combustion could devour a single thread, Oren slammed down a heavy block of mica coated in alum resin and cold beeswax to seal the paper. 

Starved of air at its brightest breath, the flash froze permanently inside the translucent glaze.  Gales crashed back through the dead boughs in a curtain of snow as Oren finally unlocked his aching knees and lifted the finished chart.  Warm between his palms, that arrested fire held the whole scattered valley in quiet, luminous poise: twelve dark peaks cradling bright constellations of breath, guiding the sleds west before dawn.
