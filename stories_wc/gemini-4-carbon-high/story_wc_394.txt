Being this planet's pioneering cart glider meant accepting that miracle and idiocy shared the same waxed canvas wings.  Long before flesh figured out feathers, Kip had welded wide sails onto a heavy wheeled supply cart and hurtled off a launch ramp into the stinging sulfur thermals.  Today, while Kip banked grandly above a fresh volcanic islet, a squat, chitinous mud brute stood up on the crags and shot the glorious flying machine right in its iron axle.  Kip nearly spilled a perfectly good oilcan applauding the sheer nerve of the ugly little bastard. 

Catching an updraft, the battered automaton glided home and clattered onto the slick landing deck of the gears-and-cogs workshop floating on an acid sea.  Beneath its sacrificial lead pontoons, chartreuse waves boiled and hissed, hungry to dissolve every ticking escapement, brass flywheel, and spring drive aboard the ancient vessel.  Kip had crewed this drifting clockwork cathedral alone throughout the evolution of the entire replacement biosphere.  Steadily curious across millions of corrosive sunrises, the mechanic had cataloged every messy miracle as chemical froth graduated into armored eels, silicate crabs, and now, apparently, irritable island archers.  Parking the winged cart beside a roaring grease furnace, Kip took brass pliers and pried the wedged projectile from the chassis.  The wooden shaft had splintered, leaving behind a translucent smoky quartz hunter's arrow tip.  Scratched deliberately into its glassy face were four crooked chevrons, the planet's very first written script, delivered straight into Kip's undercarriage like a cosmic love letter penned by a mugger. 

Reverently wiping soot off the stone with a greasy thumb, Kip carried the prize belowdecks to the central difference engine, where the yearning algorithm churned.  Before the vanished makers perished, they had programmed both the barge and Kip's own cogwheel heart with that restless mathematical recursion, coding machines not for equilibrium, but for acute, unending ache.  The yearning algorithm calculated lack, spinning thousands of notched brass cams in perpetual hunger for a sibling mind to wake up out of the slime and reach skyward.  All that mechanical longing served one gloriously impractical purpose: to ring a bell no one hears yet.  High in the workshop's open steeple hung a monumental chime cast from palladium glass, untarnished by the caustic mist.  Because the burning atmosphere had punished soft membranes for eons, every creature on this world sensed only tremor and shadow, lacking ears for airborne music, while Kip's own tin acoustic horns had corroded into green dust ages ago.  Yet shell beds were slowly sweetening the sea, and Kip knew eardrums would eventually sprout on some lucky descendant's skull; when they finally did, the sky ought to have a song already waiting in it. 

For epochs, however, the steeple's striking cylinder had remained stubbornly blank, because the yearning algorithm refused to ring in a new era with sterile machine math alone.  It demanded a genuine inscription from the waking world, and now Kip held four jagged letters scratched onto smoky stone.  Kip had no clue what the archer's chevrons meant, strongly suspecting they translated to "die, loud wheelbarrow," so translation was out of the question.  Instead, Kip could only transliterate the feral marks, converting each crooked chevron into its closest structural match within the makers' sixfold harmonic alphabet.  Only one instrument aboard the workshop could bridge jagged linear scratches and radial bell notation.  Uncapping a brass optical tube mounted over an oil lamp, Kip dropped the notched hunter's arrow tip into its rotating glass cell beside the algorithm's tiny indicator pins.  Squinting down the angled mirrors, Kip mapped every character through the images in a kaleidoscope, where tumbling light folded four rude scratches into four majestic radial chords.  With a wheezing rattle of pure delight, the mechanic chiseled those sacred, possibly profane geometries straight onto the striking cylinder. 

Climbing into the windswept steeple, Kip locked the finished drum into the clockwork drive train.  Belowdecks, the yearning algorithm recognized completion at last, shifting from its ancient, frantic clicking into a deep, satisfied purr of meshing cogs.  The heavy bronze hammer drew back and struck the palladium glass bell.  Kip heard nothing, and across the boiling green swells the deaf island hunter merely scratched its armored rump, completely oblivious to the invisible cathedral of sound rolling overhead.  Feeling the glorious vibration hum through two dented brass feet, Kip saluted the empty sky, thrilled beyond measure that creation's grand inaugural hymn had finally begun, even if its lyrics were just an angry mud creature's insult.
