Inside the candlelit shrine carved into glacier ice, two hundred beeswax wicks trembled within niches of ancient blue frost.  Their pale reflections multiplied through trapped air bubbles until the cavern shone with borrowed starlight, fleeting and fragile against the polar dark.  Soren knelt beside the meltwater altar, peeling heavy rubber gloves from her numb hands.  Along the fjord, fisherfolk knew her as both wreck diver and ghost whisperer, the sole intermediary for sailors lost beneath the ice.  Between her palms rested a melted snow globe, its thick glass dome slumped sideways into a teardrop sealing a miniature porcelain lighthouse inside cloudy water and ash. 

Hours earlier, sixty fathoms down in the blackened hull of the freighter Alba, the spirit of Chief Engineer Tomas Lind had pressed that warped keepsake into her fingers.  Since the sinking three winters ago, Fleet Director Brekke had blamed Tomas for a drunken boiler rupture, denying any pension to his daughter, Freja, and leaving her in disgrace.  Soren had abandoned commercial salvage contracts after years of hauling up corroded bullion and soaked ledgers of greed.  Now she braved the crushing dark solely to salvage something pure from the wreckage, seeking remnants of devotion that salt and catastrophe could not ruin.  Yet ghosts pinned under deep water could never speak in clear words.  Fractured by cold pressure and shock, the shade of Tomas had only hummed against her helmet like a taut wire before fading into silt. 

To decipher what the abyss muted, Soren kept a ledger of travelers sleeping at the hostel above the glacier.  While bereaved relatives built walls of sorrow too dense for delicate spirits to cross, unguarded sleepers caught stray fragments of drowned memory rising through the ice.  Over six months, Soren had reconstructed the final minutes aboard the Alba by analyzing cameo appearances in strangers' dreams.  A visiting surveyor had dreamed of a quiet hallway until, through an iron doorway, a man in a coat with silver buttons slammed a bulkhead shut and wrenched the outer latches down.  Weeks later, two botanists independently described a background figure in a burning cellar unbolting an intake grate to hide a brass shipping tube from white flames before wrapping his wool coat around a glass sphere.  Soren called these brief nocturnal crossings windows of connection, clear apertures where the silenced dead borrowed living slumber so their witness might survive.  Guided by those converging visions, she had swum straight to the engine intake grate during her scarce bottom time that morning. 

Boots crunched at the tunnel threshold as Harbor Magistrate Holm led an anniversary vigil into the ice chapel.  Behind him walked young Freja, staring hollowly downward while Director Brekke, wearing his mourning coat with silver buttons, laid a wreath beside the candles.  When Brekke murmured piously about forgiving careless men who doom their ships, Soren stepped into the pale candlelight.  Because raw terror can scar the grieving as deeply as slander, Soren always weaves truth carefully, parting the cruelty of death from the love inside it.  She spared Freja every detail of suffocating smoke and sealed iron, kneeling instead to set the melted snow globe into the cold palms of the girl. 

Softly, Soren told her how distant strangers had dreamed of Tomas remaining steady amid the heat, sheltering her birthday gift beneath his coat so its tiny lighthouse would reach her whole.  Freja released a broken breath as flecks of ash swirled gently around the intact porcelain tower inside the slumped glass.  Only then did Soren rise to face Magistrate Holm and Brekke, ready to expose the betrayal entombed beneath the fjord.  She noted that a steam explosion in freezing seas shatters glass outward, whereas only undeclared magnesium flares burning inside a latched compartment create dry heat intense enough to slump thick crystal smooth before water floods the hull.  Reaching into her dive bag, she placed the brass shipping tube from the intake grate onto the altar, adding quietly that the outer bulkhead latches still sat dogged shut from the corridor side for any court diver to verify. 

Brekke lunged for the cylinder, paling as Holm intercepted it, unscrewed the watertight cap, and unrolled the contraband flare manifest signed by the director himself.  While the magistrate led the silent executive out toward the harbor lamps, Soren lingered in the hushed sanctuary beside Freja.  Overhead, through a clear seam in the glacier roof, faint winter starlight mingled with reflected candle flames like a quiet promise borrowed from the dark.  Watching the girl cradle the unbroken little lighthouse against her chest, Soren felt the restless current beneath the fjord finally grow still.
