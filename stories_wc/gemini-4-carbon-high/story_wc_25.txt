Ribbons of emerald and violet aurora writhed above Oakhaven Harbor during a violent geomagnetic storm that had killed every radio and frozen the robotic gantry cranes in midair.  Beneath that electric sky stood Sector Nine, a towering labyrinth of shipping containers stacked eighty feet high along the dark docks of the port city.  Director Vane stood at the perimeter gate with a battery bullhorn, roaring directions toward Elena, a rigger stranded inside the darkened steel maze.  Every shout smashed against the corrugated walls and shattered into overlapping thunder that erased all sense of direction.  Beside him, Callum pressed two bare fingers to a corner stanchion and listened.  Twelve years as a labyrinth maintenance worker greasing latches and clearing catwalks inside those shifting canyons had taught him how hollow cargo boxes carried footsteps like drumheads.  He identified people's walks solely by the cadence ringing through the steel.  Filtering out the heavy boot thuds of security near the fence, he caught Elena's stride four tiers up: a crisp right step followed by the slight left heel drag from an old dock sprain.  Worse, each echoing blast from Vane's bullhorn herded her limping steps closer to an unrailed hoist gap. 

Callum reached over and covered the mouth of Vane's megaphone.  Vane planned to replace the human maintenance crew with loud acoustic drones, convinced that brute volume ruled a port, while Callum burned to prove that whispers travel farther than shouts.  Tonight, Elena's life hung on demonstrating that exact truth before another roar drove her off the fourth tier.  Meeting Vane's glare, Callum issued a calm challenge: grant five minutes of silence, and Elena's own voice would reach the gate without anyone speaking above a whisper, or Callum would sign Vane's termination order himself.  Vane glanced at his wildly spinning magnetic compass and flat laminated yard chart, scoffed at the odds, and clicked his mechanical stopwatch. 

Callum darted into the emerald shadows with his trademark blend of strict preparation and agile impulse, drawing a sheaf of folded maps from his coat.  Seconds before the storm killed the power, the cranes had initiated emergency compaction, sliding the twelve radial container alleys, named Aries through Pisces, flush together against harbor gales.  Vane's static blueprint showed wide haul lanes that no longer existed, whereas Callum had creased his paper tier sheets along every bay line so he could pleat vanished corridors away in full stride.  At the central hub, his pleated sheet showed identical distances down the Gemini and Cancer spurs, where hesitating to guess would cost vital seconds.  Without slowing, Callum flipped a worn bronze zodiac coin into the violet light, caught it, felt the embossed crab shell beneath his thumb, and swung briskly into Cancer.  Forty yards deep, a lowered cargo box sealed the passage, yet instead of backtracking, Callum smoothly reversed two map pleats to mirror the stalled hoist.  Across that improvised crease, ink markings from separate rows met cleanly, revealing a continuous ventilation flue back to the gate alongside two permanent ballast containers pressed corner to corner beneath Elena. 

Callum slipped sideways down the narrow gap until he reached the touching steel corner blocks of those ballast boxes.  From his belt he raised a notched iron rod, a key with no apparent lock anywhere on the docks because neither container held a keyhole alone.  Only when compaction married their raw lifting slots did a hidden spring cam align inside the dark seam between them.  Sliding the iron bit into that unmarked structural crevice, Callum turned it once, unlatching an internal service stair while opening the linked storm louvers from tier four down into the ground flue.  He climbed silently so he would not startle Elena into falling, feeling her uneven steps falter inches from the drop.  Rather than shout, he scraped the rim of his zodiac coin briskly along the steel railing, sending its twelve constellation notches clicking in their crew's familiar signal. 

Recognizing those twelve rapid clicks, Elena backed away from the void and limped safely into the stairwell.  Callum tapped the aligned flue crease on his map and guided her close to the open louver lip.  She breathed a quiet report of her bay number and safety into the narrow metal seam.  Spared the shattering canyon echoes, her whisper glided two hundred yards down the smooth waveguide to the outer gate, where Callum felt Vane's clipped, impatient pacing stop dead through the steel.
