Corin climbed the rusted anchor chain onto the Calliope, an abandoned carnival ship drifting in a foggy bay where circular currents never touched shore.  Decades earlier, he had worked as a chimera twin discoverer, hunting rare siblings who shared one braided consciousness and subtle botanical mutations so mainland impresarios could exhibit them.  In his pocket rested his old inspection tool, a lacquered emerald swatch card once held beside suspect eyes to expose the faint green ring around chimeric pupils.  Now he carried that pigment for another purpose: he had rowed into the mist to find the garden of forgotten names, whose mythic leaves reportedly matched the exact verdant hue on his card.  When the coastal fog crept inland last winter, it had stolen the name of his dead daughter, leaving only an ache he hoped those whispering vines could restore. 

Aboard the Calliope, every padlocked menagerie gate swung wide in the salt breeze, offering a strange, trapped freedom upon a boundless sea that permitted no departure.  Anyone who lingered in this bay suffered its quiet curse, for as memories alter inside the thickening vapor, personal history rearranges itself like driftwood.  Already Corin stood beside the rotting carousel and felt his daughter shift from dark curls to pale straw, while his own remorse blurred into older, colder habits.  He had come to beg guidance from the last twins he ever sold to this vessel, because their dual minds resisted the amnesia and rumor named them keepers of the garden.  Yet as memories alter, recent mercy dissolves before deeply grooved instinct, and Corin half believed he was once again hired to recapture the runaways. 

Tracking elusive quarry across blind decks where a hunter forgets his own footsteps required turning their survival method against them.  To cross the sprawling ship without losing direction in the mist, the twins navigated through narrative threads, waxing long puppet cords knotted with sequential painted playbills that told continuous tales from bow to stern.  As long as a traveler read each unfolding scene knot by knot, the forward momentum of the plot anchored the mind until the destination arrived.  Knowing that chimera minds cannot ignore an unfinished origin story, Corin untied the main cord outside the flooded ballroom and routed it straight into the central iron aviary.  Along the diverted twine, he tied pages torn from his old ledger describing a rainy parish loft where two infants were first stolen from their cradle, setting his snare through narrative threads that demanded resolution. 

Soon two figures emerged from the white gloom, fingers sliding along the wet twine as they murmured the ledger entries in staggered, musical harmony.  Ten years without gawking crowds had left the brother and sister blissfully aberrant, delighting openly in the mutations they once hid beneath carnival cloaks.  Feathered moss plumed across her bare collarbones while luminous gills fluttered along his jaw, and they laughed together with unashamed wonder even as they followed the final knot inside.  Corin slammed the rusted aviary door and dropped the iron bar to complete their recapture, thrusting his emerald swatch card through the mesh toward their bright mosaic eyes.  Seeing that deep green against their irises jolted his fractured mind back to the present, and his voice cracked as he begged them to show him where the lost names grew. 

Neither twin rattled the bars; instead, the brother stepped effortlessly through a missing panel at the rear, proving that cages meant nothing upon a vessel nobody could ever leave.  Pitying their aging hunter, the sister stayed within the grate and gently tilted his hand upward toward the shattered glass dome overhead.  What Corin had mistaken in the colorless mist for tangled rigging was a sprawling canopy of wet vines, their leaves matching his emerald swatch card with sudden, breathtaking precision.  As sea wind stirred the dome, hundreds of leaves brushed together in distinct human syllables, whispering every forgotten identity the bay had ever swallowed.  Corin strained to seize one familiar name from that rustling chorus, yet he felt the mist soften his chest again until he could no longer tell which syllable belonged to his child.  Watching the twins listen peacefully without claiming the names they had lost on the mainland, he understood why they never struck the flint of old remembrance.  To reignite grief where tomorrow would only erase it anew was torment, whereas letting his love rest nameless among the vines offered an unkindled solace, unlit and painless as unburned wood.  Corin lifted the iron bar, tucked his card beneath a root, and walked beside the twins onto the open, inescapable deck.
