Below the chalk cliffs, the evening tide draws smooth pebbles down into foam and rocks them softly back toward sleep.  Every wave repeats the same low hush along the bay, singing the gray water into stillness as daylight fades.  After the leash breaks, Clara stands motionless beside the valley boundary stone with half a frayed leather strap warm in her palm.  Twenty paces ahead, where salt grass surrenders to pale sea fog on the veiled ridgeway, her old hound Bram pauses and looks back with calm brown eyes.  For five years she has walked him no farther than this mossy marker, telling herself that the high coastal mist was too perilous for an aging dog.  Now the snapped tether trails harmlessly from his collar, and a single whistle from her lips would bring him trotting obediently home to the cottage hearth. 

Yet Clara leaves her breath unspent, feeling the quiet truth settle over her shoulders like fine spray.  That worn strap never guarded Bram from the cliffs; it only tethered Clara to her own familiar grief.  Deep inside her oilskin pocket rests an unpunched passage card for Lantern Isle, bought five autumns ago when she promised to tend the stone sanctuary across the sound.  After a winter gale claimed her brother at sea, dread turned the open water into a wall she could not face, and she hid the ticket away.  Old harbor songs say that bravery turned back at the pier never vanishes, but drifts across the tide like silver kelp and gathers on the far shore until its owner comes for it.  Stepping past the boundary stone into the cool vapor, Clara climbs upward to find where unused courage waits. 

Far below the headland, rising water drowns the straight shingle road, murmuring softly over submerged shells and tangles of bladderwrack.  Because the lower beach floods before nightfall, anyone seeking the evening boat must ascend the veiled ridgeway while dusk still holds the sky.  Near the crest, two amber lamps bloom softly inside the fog beside a low shelter of stacked slate.  There Oren and Maeve, the wise ferry guides of Greymere Sound, keep a vigil in twilight for travelers too hesitant or weary to depart under bright sun.  Wrapped in gray wool cloaks that smell of cedar smoke and salt, they listen through the hush of surf for footsteps on the turf.  When Bram trots into their circle of lantern light and rests his chin against the knee of Maeve, neither guide expresses surprise at seeing Clara emerge behind him.  They know that timid hearts ripen slowly, and their welcome is quiet and gently persistent, offering warmth without a single urgent word. 

Slack tide will not linger long, so Oren lifts his shuttered lamp and gestures away from the seaward precipice toward a winding sheep track.  To reach the sheltered cove safely, they must traverse the veiled ridgeway through indirect routes rather than risking the crumbled chalk brink.  Direct paths force a fearful walker to stare straight down into dark swells, where vertigo freezes the blood and turns every gust into terror.  Instead, the wise ferry guides lead Clara sideways through heather hollows, curving around leeward cairns and mossy switchbacks that shield her eyes from the sheer drop.  Whenever Clara falters at a steeper bend, Maeve pauses beside her with gently persistent patience, humming an old rowing melody that matches the slow breathing of the shore.  Fold by fold, the crooked descent softens the sea from an enemy into a companion, until its steady murmur feels as safe as rain on slate roofs. 

At last the mist parts above a quiet pebble inlet, where a cedar skiff sways gently against a slick stone jetty.  Bram steps lightly over the gunwale without waiting for command and curls nose to tail upon the forward planking, sighing in deep contentment.  Standing at the lip of the tide, Clara draws the yellowed card from her coat and extends it toward Oren with an unshaking hand.  Daylight boats mark a plain circle for sun, so Oren unhooks his brass ticket punch with star stamp worn silver by decades of dusk crossings.  One crisp click bites through the stiff pasteboard, and when Clara raises the card, the first pale star above Lantern Isle gleams straight through the tiny five pointed window.  She lays the broken leash beside Bram, steps into the rocking boat, and lets the oars dip softly into dark water that sings her forward toward all the bravery kept safe across the bay.
