Cassian had promised a miracle of smoke and refracted moonlight to cross Lake Vael, a theatrical escape meant to blind pursuit.  Now, moments after the magic trick fails, ribbons of false silver bridge dissolve into mist, and the illusionist vanishes beneath freezing black water.  Ahead churns a swirling orchard vortex at the heart of a cursed lake, where an ancient valley of drowned apple trees spins endlessly around a bottomless throat.  Torin steers his narrow ash skiff into that maelstrom with warlike serenity, his breathing slow and measured as winter sap.  Clad in plates of ironbark, the guarded druid keeps his spear balanced across his knees and his grief locked behind ramparts of silence.  Frostbitten branches and dead blossoms whip past his helm like arrows on a familiar battlefield, yet his gaze neither hurries nor flinches. 

Cassian was his twin, a court charlatan who burned their sacred grove three nights past and fled northward.  For thirty years, every polished shield and still basin had shown Torin the exact features of his brother, rendering identical bone, dark eyes, and quiet grace.  Yet Torin braves this lethal funnel not for vengeance, but to discover what mirrors do not show.  Reflections reveal only skin and symmetry, hiding the wound in the marrow that turns a brother toward treason.  Lake Vael exacts a singular toll from every deceiver who drowns within its reach.  As black water fills the lungs, the curse seizes the final exhaled truth and hardens it into crimson rime, a frozen confession that rises while the corpse sinks.  Somewhere amid the grinding timber and the glittering decoys shed by the collapsed spell, that solitary shard of honesty awaits claim. 

Across the marches, Torin is revered as the warden who finds lost things, trusting never in eyesight, which light can trick, but in resonance and weight.  When spinning roots crush his skiff, he steps onto a revolving apple trunk with the tranquil poise of a spearman holding a breach.  While the whirlpool drags every bough inward toward annihilation, Torin hunts through expanding circles.  He strikes the butt of his ash spear against the slick bark, sending concentric rings of druidic sense outward against the inward pull of the current.  Each widening wave passes unimpeded through false crystals of vanishing glamour, dismissing hollow light like chaff upon a threshing floor.  On the seventh ring, beside the roaring lip of the central drop, his awareness strikes true mass snagged within a sunken knot of petrified orchard limbs. 

Torin bounds across three churning trunks to reach the inner edge, landing low and steady amid blinding spray.  Beneath the foam, blackened apple branches clench like an iron fist around a jagged prism of bloodstained ice.  Gentle forest song cannot rouse wood killed by an ancient oath, and steel would shatter against frost so old, while the tangle slides steadily toward the abyss.  From a leaden capsule over his breastbone, Torin draws a forbidden relic, the barbed seed of a marsh plant that eats flesh.  Orchard trees drink sunlight and perish in shadow, whereas this predatory creeper ignores light altogether, hungering only for warm meat, sinew, and mortal blood.  Yet to wake its ravenous strength and bind it to his quarry, a man armored in caution must first drop his shield.  Standing above the hungry roar, Torin wills both his defensive crouch and his guarded spirit to unbend.  He casts aside his ironbark gauntlet, slices his bare palm across his spearhead with calm precision, and closes his bleeding hand around the waking seed. 

Thorny scarlet tendrils burst from the husk, biting greedily into his palm before scenting the identical twin blood trapped inside the submerged rime below.  Torin wrenches the sprouting bulb free and hurls it straight into the petrified knot.  Fed on living marrow, thick carnivorous vines erupt beneath the surface, coiling like red pythons through dead wood to reach the bloodied breath within.  With a crack louder than winter thunder, their savage muscular surge forces the cursed apple boughs to unbend.  Torin snatches the freed shard from the foam at the exact instant the ravenous creeper and shattered trees plunge into the dark throat of the lake.  Riding a drifting log toward calmer shallows, still as a sentinel after combat, he presses the frozen confession against his jaw until his warmth thaws its voice.  The dying whisper of Cassian speaks not of malice, but of hollow dread: whenever the illusionist looked into a mirror, he saw Torin, a true druid, and burned the grove in despair at being merely an empty reflection.  Torin binds his wounded hand, serene and upright upon the black water, carrying home at last the sorrow no glass could ever hold.
