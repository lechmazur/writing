Oren woke at the close of an unspoken dream, that silent vision of sealed iron gates which visited the garrison whenever storms crossed the wastes, though nobody ever named it by daylight.  Outside his quarters, Redoubt Vane stood as a desert fortress after rain, its cracked sandstone steaming in the gray predawn while runoff gurgled through courtyard grates into the deep sand.  Before his boots met the flagstones, Oren pulled on gauntlets of waxed hide and laced them tight.  For a chef who can taste memories, twenty years of sampling the sins of frontier troops had left his bare skin emotionally contagious through touch.  One brush of his ungloved thumb against a sentry would drive decades of borrowed sorrow straight through the flesh and drop the man cold.  Oren bore that isolation the way old cast iron bears weight: rigid, plain, and one sharp blow away from snapping clean in two. 

Taking a brass ladle and a copper kettle, he descended the limestone stair beneath the keep, following rainwater seeping through fissures in the rock.  At the bottom stood the coral icebound pavilion beneath the dunes, an ancient sea shrine locked in perpetual alchemical rime.  Soldiers on this brutal border stayed sane by weeping their worst battlefield regrets into salt jars and freezing the brine onto these pale arches as stored remorse.  In dry months the frost held fast, keeping the garrison upright and functional.  Yet the night storm had warmed the vault ceiling, sending stray drips onto the reef to thaw pockets of raw guilt that had already leaked upward into the sleep of the fortress.  If that meltwater reached the cisterns, or if a guard touched the weeping frost barehanded, contagious despair would sweep the ramparts before noon. 

Oren knelt beside the glistening ledges and began analyzing tidal pool formations melted into the frozen coral shelves.  He read the miniature briny basins like a harbor pilot studying shoals.  Broad, shallow pools ringed by smooth salt crusts held old sorrow that time had nearly settled, whereas narrow channels and foaming eddies marked fresh, jagged trauma ready to cut a mind apart.  Near the central pillar, warm runoff fed a turbulent basin coiled around staghorn coral, its rim bristling with gray needles of ice.  Oren dipped his ladle into the eddy and touched one freezing bead to his tongue.  Sharp brine and rusted iron flooded his mouth, carrying the exact memory trapped inside the frost.  He tasted the dust siege from last month: Commander Sera hauling the gate lever with torn palms, dropping the iron portcullis to save the keep while three scouts pounded outside, his own young apprentice Tam among them.  The flavor hit his throat like crushed glass and scorched tallow.  Behind his ribs, his own private grief gave a dry, hairline creak, yet his gloved fingers held the brass handle dead level as he skimmed the pool clean. 

Carrying the full kettle back to the steaming kitchen, Oren kindled dry thornbrush beneath his iron cauldron.  He could never dump thawed brine into the dunes; soldiers stripped entirely of their past rotted into hollow marauders who forgot why they guarded the frontier at all.  Instead, his duty each storm morning was to sandpaper rough memories, abrading their lethal points through heat and craft until human bone could bear them without splintering.  He built a dense foundation of toasted barley, rendered goat marrow, crushed cumin, and charred desert root.  Slowly, measure by careful measure, he poured the bitter coral meltwater into the boiling pot, working to subsume the sharp, isolating terror of the fallen gate into a broad pottage of shared endurance.  When he sampled the broth again, the final cry of his apprentice no longer sliced his palate like raw flint.  The loss remained, dark and salted, yet worn smooth enough to swallow and survive. 

Pale sun broke hard and clear over the drying battlements as the morning watch filed into the mess hall.  Commander Sera stepped to the counter first, her posture stiff beneath damp wool, her eyes ringed with the shadow of the dream neither of them would ever voice.  Keeping his gloved thumbs hooked low on the clay rim so their skin could not brush, Oren set a steaming bowl on the planks between them.  Sera raised the vessel, drank deep, and went rigid as the smoothed weight of the gate settled back behind her breastbone.  Her breath hitched once against the ache, held firm without cracking, and she gave him one curt nod before turning back to the wall.
