Four years after the Happiness Mandate outlawed sorrow, Mara lived alone in a bleak farmhouse overshadowed by giant wind turbines.  Every three seconds, a colossal white blade sheared across her kitchen window, dropping a cold band of shadow over the table before restoring the pale afternoon glare.  Above the cold iron stove, a brass state microphone blinked green, listening for forbidden quiet or the faint catch of a sob.  Mara sat beneath that blinking lens with her jaw trembling from the effort of maintaining a lawful smile while staring at a dried flower bouquet saved secretly from the burial of her husband.  Beside those brittle brown roses lay his ashwood walking stick and the heavy silver watch she had set out as payment for Venn. 

Venn stood by the sink as a blank-faced performer whose features had been trained in the old pantomime halls to reveal nothing at all.  Since the theaters closed, he had practiced a morally ambiguous trade along the rural circuit, charging desperate mourners their final heirlooms to strip away memories that would otherwise drag them into the state clinics.  Whether he acted as a merciful savior or a scavenger feeding on loss mattered neither to him nor to the clients he left hollowed out.  His entire method existed to cultivate lightness in grief, paring heavy devotion down until sorrow lost its mass and vanished into a survivable emptiness. 

Without a nod of sympathy, Venn slipped the silver watch into his coat pocket and turned toward the tap.  To satisfy the microphone, which demanded energetic domestic speech, he began dramatically narrating mundane tasks in the booming cadence of an announcer.  Keeping his eyes glassy and deadpan, he proclaimed, "Witness the glorious filling of the tin kettle as cold water rushes forth to greet the morning."  His resonant voice rolled over the kitchen while his mouth scarcely seemed to belong to a living man, closing into smooth stillness the instant each syllable ended.  Outside, the giant rotors churned the sky with a low mechanical roar that shook the windowpanes in steady intervals.  True quiet had been abolished under the law, yet Venn timed his grand theatrical phrasing to match the rhythmic thrum of the blades until both sounds canceled each other out.  By inflating trivial chores with hollow pomp, he folded noise upon noise to construct a remade hush, an artificial interior silence hidden directly beneath the surveillance of the state. 

Venn pulled out a wooden chair opposite Mara, drew a narrow steel pocketknife, and lifted the ashwood walking stick from the table.  With practiced precision, he began to whittle the smooth handle where the dead man had rested his palm for twenty winters.  Never altering his expressionless mask, he boomed toward the ceiling, "Behold the triumphant paring of the timber as pale curls descend like ribbons across the cloth."  Thin translucent shavings peeled away from the blade and drifted over the oilcloth, gathering in weightless heaps around the dried flower bouquet.  Mara watched the worn grain disappear slice by slice under the hypnotic pulse of the turbine shadows.  As Venn narrated the wiping of dust from his cuff and the steady scraping of steel against ash, his meaningless grandeur drained the room of urgency.  Inside the remade hush, each shaved curl seemed to remove another fraction of memory from her chest.  The aching tension in her cheeks eased, not into forbidden tears, which carried far too much gravity, but into a flat, unfeeling calm. 

When nothing remained of the walking stick except a slender splinter, Venn rose and swept his open palm across the table, crushing the dried flower bouquet directly into the wood shavings.  Brittle petals and dry stems crumbled without resistance between his fingers while Mara observed the ruin with vacant eyes.  Lifting the iron stove lid, Venn announced in radiant triumph, "Celebrate the diligent clearing of the household hearth as yesterday surrenders its clutter to the grate."  He dropped the mingled shavings and crushed blossoms onto the embers, where a brief orange tongue reduced twenty years of marriage to weightless gray ash drawn swiftly up the flue.  Above the cooling stove, the brass microphone blinked a steady, satisfied green.  Mara sat motionless beside the bare oilcloth, her grief finally light enough to float away because nothing remained inside her to anchor it.  Venn narrated the latching of the front door in a cheerful baritone, stepped out beneath the sweeping shadows of the turbines, and left her safe within the cold shelter of the void.
