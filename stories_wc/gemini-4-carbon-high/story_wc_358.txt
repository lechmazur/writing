Only after the town goes silent do the invisible currents of Oakhaven Harbor reveal their true architecture.  By two in the morning, freight locomotives had halted their iron shunting, warm traffic plumes had faded from the avenues, and coastal fog blurred the amber crane lamps into pale, suspended halos.  Standing between the concrete monoliths of the customs house and the cold storage warehouse, accident investigator Julian Reid raised his ultrasonic anemometer into the damp chill.  He triggered a slender wand of glycol vapor, watching through the beam of his headlamp as a spectral ribbon of mist accelerated sharply down the narrow brick throat.  For the past hour, Julian had begun his nocturnal survey by documenting wind patterns between buildings along the terminal perimeter, logging exact velocities to a hundredth of a knot where gentle harbor air compressed into fast, unseen blades. 

Ahead of those rigid masonry gaps rose the true puzzle of the port city: a towering labyrinth of shipping containers stacked six tiers high against the blackened bay.  Two nights earlier, a harbor medical helicopter descending toward Pier Nine had plunged into the center of this corrugated steel maze under seemingly tranquil skies.  Because rooftop weather stations recorded a mild five knot breeze at impact, the preliminary review board blamed spatial disorientation in the mist, eager to stamp pilot error onto a neat archive file.  Julian refused to settle for tidy hindsight that merely apportioned blame among the dead.  His driving purpose was to cultivate foresight in action, translating ephemeral microclimates into live dispatch warnings so approaching crews could read architectural hazards before committing to a descent. 

Time inside the yard was razor thin.  At five in the morning, automated gantry cranes would awaken to load three waiting freighters, dismantling this exact configuration of steel canyons forever.  Worse, the nocturnal marine layer was cooling faster than forecast, altering barometric pressure across the water every ten minutes.  Stepping from the warehouse alley into the dripping shadow of Row A, Julian worked with quiet urgency, rapidly adapting his sensor grid and vector calculations as each subtle shift in tide and fog reshaped the drafts.  Moisture beaded on his tablet screen while his laser rangefinder sliced the murky corridors, pairing ghostly impressions of salt haze and rust with crisp angular measurements. 

He tracked two separate airstreams that had funneled between the perimeter buildings and entered the stacks along perpendicular avenues.  Boxed walls of crimson and cobalt steel deflected both currents without dispersing their momentum, guiding them deep into the yard toward Junction Seven, forty meters upslope from the charred crash bay.  When Julian released a pulse of glycol mist at that intersection, the vapor did not drift; it snapped violently sideways and coiled upward like a struck spring.  His anemometer spiked to thirty four knots inside a tight horizontal vortex.  Here was the singular convergence that every static instrument had missed: two harmless landward drafts, sliced between port buildings, channeled through staggered container walls, and trapped beneath a nocturnal thermal lid to forge an invisible shear rotor at the exact altitude of the helicopter's approach. 

Calculation demanded physical proof.  Julian aimed his focused inspection lamp up the sheer face of Junction Seven, piercing the luminous drizzle until a fresh scar of white airframe paint glinted along the top container's corner casting.  Clipping his harness onto an adjacent refrigeration scaffold, he climbed the slick steel rungs into the swirling mist above the fifth tier.  Pinched inside a jagged burr of steel beside the paint transfer, sodden with harbor condensate, hung a frayed flight engineer's patch torn clean from its hook and loop backing. 

The entire sequence crystallized with chilling clarity.  Tethered at the open cabin door to spot obstacles through the murk, the crewman had leaned outward to guide his pilot past the stacks just as the hidden rotor slammed the fuselage against Junction Seven, ripping the insignia from his shoulder seconds before the crippled craft tumbled into the next bay.  That engineer had spent his final heartbeat straining for visual warning against an ambush only mathematics and airflow geometry could expose.  Julian photographed the snagged emblem against his scale ruler, sealed the wet fabric inside an evidence pouch, and linked his completed flow matrix directly into the port authority's crane and aviation routing software.  From dawn onward, whenever newly stacked containers aligned with the perimeter buildings to breed a shear corridor, the system would flag the hazard automatically before a rotor ever entered the mist.  Descending through the silver haze as the first distant engine stirred across the waking city, Julian carried the truth out of the labyrinth in time for the living to use it.
