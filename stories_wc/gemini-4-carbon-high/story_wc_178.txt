Violet twilight settled over the terraced ziggurats of Babylon, cooling the glazed lapis bricks and copper conduits threaded through the silent hanging gardens.  Down in the courtyards, the pneumatic fountains had ceased their rhythmic sighing, leaving the vast palace suspended between regimes.  At noon, the old monarch and his diviners had fled down the Euphrates on gilded barges; at dawn, the northern vanguard outside the bronze gates would march in to claim the empty throne.  Only this single evening belonged to no ruler, offering the exact stillness Rimanni had awaited for forty years. 

High within the topmost observatory dome, the frugal astronomer pinched a tallow stub, splitting its braided wick in half before touching a spark to one thin thread.  Even with the royal storehouses unguarded below, decades of strict economy guided his steady hands.  While court astrologers squandered barrels of cedar oil and electrum wire on theatrical omens, Rimanni had hoarded swept brass filings, scraped old clay tablets clean, and rationed every drop of grease.  Thrift had kept him beneath royal suspicion, yet thrift was also an essential law of optics.  Excess light blinded delicate lenses; excess heat warped the meridian wires humming softly in the evening draft.  Through the open ceiling shutter, early stars emerged in dusky harmony with the fading amber horizon, blending earth and sky into one tranquil chord of shadow and cooling bronze.  Now that the palace boilers were cold and the temple drums were mute, no tremor disturbed the ancient masonry or its subtle clockwork. 

Rimanni approached the central pillar, which the priests had assumed to be a plain lead jacket around a hydraulic counterweight.  Reaching beneath carved reliefs of winged bulls, he released three hidden latches to uncloak the slender celestial array he had built from palace scraps.  Heavy leaden petals folded silently outward, exposing nested rings of salvaged tellurium, silvered mirrors, and finely graduated astrolabe arcs.  Had he bared this mechanism yesterday, temple inquisitors would have executed him for blasphemy; if it remained exposed tomorrow, invading soldiers would melt it down for siege hardware.  For half a lifetime, Rimanni had practiced an encrypted devotion, concealing his true reverence inside mundane irrigation ledgers and lunar tables.  Where state priests chanted loud hymns to jealous idols of war, his quiet fidelity belonged to an uncharted gravitational anomaly beyond the orbit of Saturn.  Thirty winters ago he had measured that distant presence as a rhythmic tug upon the outer planets, a deliberate pulse suggesting watchful intellect rather than blind drift.  Every prayer of inquiry he composed had been disguised within sexagesimal ratios so royal censors saw only dry architectural tolerances.  Tonight he worked to seal a covenant with the unknown, pledging enduring human observation to that deep cosmic mind before a new dynasty choked the sky with smoke. 

From his threadbare wool robe, Rimanni drew a palm sized magic square tile kilned from translucent cobalt glass.  Across its nine recessed cells, cuneiform pinholes marked numerical weights that summed along every row, column, and diagonal to the exact orbital constant of the unseen watcher.  He fitted the tile into the focal cradle of the brass loom and set his tiny tallow flame directly behind it.  Because mortal speech could never bridge the void, he addressed the dark solely through schematic representations, rotating perforated copper plates until cobalt rays projected crisp vectors, tangents, and nodal triangles through the zenith lens.  Each luminous diagram rose into the indigo vault as an unadorned theorem of kinship.  Checking his delicate torsion balance, Rimanni contemplated an empirically ironic proof confirmed by every needle in the chamber.  Proud kings had burned whole cedar forests on ziggurat altars to compel a sign from heaven, yet their roaring bonfires generated thermal turbulence that blurred every faint signal from the stars.  Strict measurement demonstrated that boundless mystery answered only extreme conservation, requiring the quiet equilibrium of a split wick filtered through balanced numbers. 

High in the northern dark, a starless point pulsed once in reply, sending a cool gravitational ripple straight down the optic shaft.  Inside the cradle, the glass tile sang with a low harmonic chime, setting a suspended bronze stylus gliding across a dusted wax tablet in an answering spiral of prime intervals.  The accord was struck, uniting mortal witness and distant cosmos in serene symmetry while earthly empires rose and vanished like river mist below.  Calmly, Rimanni pocketed his warm tile, closed the leaden shroud, and pinched out his tiny flame so the remaining tallow would serve the next watch.
