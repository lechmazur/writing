Immediately after the eclipse ends, raw sunlight spills past the black rim of the moon and sets the desert sandstone ablaze.  Soren pulls his woven hood low over his pale brow, shielding unpigmented skin and translucent pink eyes that burn at the slightest touch of ultraviolet glare.  As an albino tracker, he used the brief shadow of totality to cross the salt flats unseen, reading bruised pebbles left by the Sovereign Cartographers.  Ahead of him rises their wind-carved encampment, a labyrinth of hollowed red spires and taut canvas pavilions sculpted by relentless canyon gales.  He enters its whistling stone corridors with an even pulse, guided by a quiet, glacial wrath that neither hurries his step nor clouds his judgment. 

Six months ago, these scholars orchestrated a massacre upon his nomadic kin solely to harvest neural measurements from dying minds.  Amid that slaughter, Soren felt a single heartbeat stretch into a breathless eternity while iron bolts hung nearly motionless in the smoke.  Having buried his family in silence, he set out to discover why time slows in crisis, certain the Cartographers killed his clan to exploit that secret.  City dwellers dull their senses with safety, and cornered beasts drown in blind terror, yet Soren has disciplined himself to remain consciously wild.  He weds raw, feral instinct to razor clarity, sustaining a serene anger as patient and pitiless as the eroding wind. 

Inside the central sandstone rotunda, five sets of fresh bootprints cross the dusty floor and stop dead upon bare rock.  No ropes hang from the fluted ceiling, yet a sharp scent of ozone lingers where the sunlit stone has been swept clean of dust.  On a shaded workbench nearby rests an unstoppered quartz flask half full of disappearing ink, an obsidian fluid synthesized to vaporize the instant daylight strikes it.  Under the cover of totality, the scholars evidently poured half that pigment onto the floor to vanish, trusting the returning noon glare to erase every trace behind them.  Even now, a widening shaft of sunlight creeps toward the workbench, dissolving stray black droplets beside the flask into colorless mist.  High in the rafters, a metallic thrumming slices through the hollow roar of the canyon drafts.  Poised with surgical stability against the violent gusts hangs an artificial hummingbird drone, its brass wings beating eighty times per second beneath a ruby optic.  The Cartographers crafted such clockwork sentinels both to navigate fierce desert vents and to film rapid anomalies invisible to sluggish human eyes. 

Locking onto his pale throat, the miniature guardian tilts and plummets like a javelin, aiming a poisoned steel needle straight at his jugular vein.  Rather than flinching in clumsy dread, Soren embraces the lethal peril, unleashing his primal reflexes while holding his mind completely still.  Instantly, the rushing world congeals into crystalline amber.  Airborne grains of red sand stall in midflight, and the blurred wings of the artificial hummingbird drone slow into languid, countable strokes.  With unhurried precision, Soren inclines his head an inch and sweeps his bone knife sideways, batting the diving machine downward without breaking his stride.  The deflected sentinel smashes directly into the quartz flask, hurling the remaining liquid out across the sunlit flagstones. 

Caught between howling wind and blinding glare, the wide splash of disappearing ink spreads thin and begins to evaporate.  To ordinary observers bound inside linear seconds, the dark stain would simply fade into empty air within an eyeblink.  Yet inside his dilated awareness, Soren watches the vanishing pigment snag against hidden contours in space, illuminating narrow, geometric fissures between one heartbeat and the next.  All at once, the mystery of his survival resolves: nature evolved crisis perception not as a trick of memory, but so imperiled minds could witness the rapid frequency where reality parts.  Panicked prey cannot interpret those fleeting doorways, and numb scholars cannot perceive them naturally, so the Cartographers used hummingbird lenses and volatile ink to force the passage open.  Behind him, the dented automaton recovers in the draft and arcs back for a killing thrust, while merciless daylight blisters his bare pale wrists.  Breathing smoothly, his tranquil fury unbroken, the albino tracker reads the shifting vectors of the evaporating pool before the sun erases the final drop.  Squaring his shoulders with that luminous lattice, Soren takes one deliberate step to disappear through ephemeral patterns in spilled ink, entering the breathless canyon between seconds where his quarry waits.
