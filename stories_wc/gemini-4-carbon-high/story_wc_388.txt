Six months after the liquidation of the Helios Mining Trust, the squatters in Cinder Camp were starving within walking distance of ripe fruit.  Out on the alkali flats stood a wasteland orchard thriving amid radioactive dust storms, its pear boughs heavy and green while toxic amber gales scoured the surrounding valley.  Yet nobody from the camp dared cross its perimeter.  Rumor held that Helios executives had faked bankruptcy, retreated into a luxury bunker beneath the roots, and ringed the trees with invisible nerve agents.  As a conspiracy debunker who had spent decades investigating industrial panics, Soren recognized the seductive geometry of that myth.  Paranoia gave chaos an author.  He strapped on his respirator and marched into the glowing haze, driven to excavate truth from comfortable lies before hunger finished what corporate neglect had begun. 

In his coat pocket rested the prime exhibit of the camp elders: a flawed diamond rumored to be cursed.  Three scavengers had pried the walnut sized crystal from a steel tower at the eastern edge of the grove, only to stumble back coughing blood and die before dawn.  Where the elders saw a poisoned talisman planted by hidden overlords, Soren looked for mechanics.  Sharply observant, he halted beside that eastern tower and studied the crystal under his pocket lens.  A dark metallic cloud marred its core, typical of cheap synthetic stone, while faint concentric grooves encircled its polished base.  Looking up, he noted five identical towers ringing the basin, each topped with a curved steel cowl that pivoted smoothly into the gale.  Only this eastern cowl hung jammed sideways, and only the pear trees directly downwind of it bore leaves blistered by glowing red silt. 

Pushing past the damaged sector, Soren stepped into sudden, uncanny quiet beneath the inner canopy.  Here the air smelled of damp soil and ripe fruit, and his radiation badge fell silent.  At the center stood no concrete bunker, only a weathered tin shed and a wooden grave marker bearing the name of Maya Lin, chief agronomist.  Inside the shed, court receivers had purged all Trust paperwork to shield the bankrupt company from fallout lawsuits, leaving burlap sacks of paper ribbons beside a maintenance jack.  Soren cleared the workbench and lit an oil lamp.  By reassembling shredded letters between Lin and the insolvency board, he uncovered the mundane tragedy behind the legends.  No corporate cabal lived underground; the board had fled months ago, rejecting pleas from Lin to deed the grove to local workers before her failing heart gave out alone. 

Moisture had ruined the schematics in the bottom of the sack, leaving only scattered phrases about dry diamond thrust pivots, gritty gales, and upward boundary shear.  Yet those fragments, paired with what he had observed along the perimeter, were enough for Soren to intuit the invisible architecture keeping the trees alive.  Ordinary greased bearings would have seized instantly in abrasive dust, so Lin had seated each heavy deflector cowl atop an industrial diamond pivot that required zero oil and zero electricity.  Turning freely with every shift of the storm, the six curved cowls lofted incoming gusts skyward into a colliding ring of air, creating a hidden stillpoint across the central orchard where toxic silt could never settle.  Neither curse nor nerve gas had killed the three scavengers.  When they pried the shiny bearing from the eastern mast, the cowl had locked sideways and funneled a concentrated downdraft of radioactive dust straight through their cheap cloth masks.  Worse, that widening breach in the wind wall was slowly poisoning the sanctuary from the east. 

Hauling the maintenance jack from the shed, Soren sealed his respirator tight and climbed the iron rungs of the eastern tower into the roaring ochre squall.  Grit lashed his goggles as he wedged the jack beneath the stalled collar, cranked the steel shaft up a fingerbreadth, and brushed the socket clean.  He seated the flawed diamond into the cup, grooved face down, and released the lever.  With a deep metallic sigh, the heavy cowl swung effortlessly into the wind and hurled the glowing plume straight toward the clouds.  Below him, the turbulent eddy dissolved, restoring calm across the scorched eastern boughs.  Climbing down, Soren filled a canvas sack with clean pears and tucked the taped letters beside them as proof.  Cinder Camp would resent losing its monsters and curses at first, yet a machine built by human hands was something living hands could keep whole.
