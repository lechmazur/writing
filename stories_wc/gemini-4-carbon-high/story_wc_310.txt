After the universe contracts, there is nowhere left to look except inward at the wreckage of everything that ever burned.  Beyond the curved rim of the ambered observatory deck, ten billion collapsed galaxies churned in a dense fog of crushed light, filling Oren with a persistent stellar doubt.  Watching those pulverized suns, he suspected more strongly each cycle that the heavens had never possessed a grand design, having merely stumbled through eternity by blind chemical accident.  Above his brass instruments, thick vaults of golden stasis resin held back infinite pressure, bathing the room in the solemn glow of an insect sealed inside fossilized sap. 

Ordinary calculation could not navigate a cosmos folded into a single violent point, because deterministic equations looped endlessly where spatial distance had ceased to exist.  Recognizing that logic would freeze before infinite density, the builders of this final sanctuary had crewed it with Oren, an arbiter trained as a cast lots decision maker.  He untied a worn leather pouch and poured out his collection of foreign coins, gathered from dozens of vanished worlds before gravity dragged their systems together.  Triangular bronze tokens from the methane seas of Vael clinked beside translucent jade rings from sunken orbital habitats and heavy iron discs stamped by forgotten emperors.  Despite his bleak suspicions about the dead firmament, Oren remained quietly enthusiastic about the ritual itself, warming the varied metals in his cupped palms with a soft, breathless smile. 

With a practiced flick of his wrists, he scattered the currency across the copper sensor board.  Wherever each piece settled, its conductive rim bridged fine contact wires, allowing pure chance to select which slice of the compressed singularity the hull antennas would probe.  Instantly the acoustic horns filled the chamber with a violent hiss of overlapping radiation.  Oren leaned close, eyes shining with patient curiosity, and applied himself to understanding static bursts by tracking subtle shifts of rhythm and thermal decay inside the roar.  Turning his quartz dials with steady fingers, he worked to distill that raw tempest of dead starlight into a single coherent echo.  With no tomorrow left ahead of the collapse, divination operated strictly as backwards prophecy, foretelling unrecorded moments of the ancient past from their surviving ash. 

Every cast served one urgent mandate: to wavelength align with purpose.  The station engineers had believed creation possessed an innate prime harmonic, an enduring note of cosmic intent that would outlast the ruin of matter.  Until Oren tuned the core drive to match that foundational wave, dissonance against the exterior storm would keep splintering the protective amber overhead.  Already thin fractures spiderwebbed across the golden ceiling, groaning softly whenever another reading failed.  This cast, like four thousand before it, yielded only the aimless rumble of a barren pulsar spinning alone in dark space until its fuel ran cold.  Oren sighed, fearing the engineers had built their ark upon a pious illusion, for every star he uncovered seemed just as lost and accidental as the rest. 

Even so, a hushed eagerness brightened his posture as he gathered the alien currency once more, delighted simply to witness whatever forgotten truth the next throw might unearth.  He tossed the handful high, and this time all the heavier pieces bounced off the board entirely, leaving only two modest tokens wedged edge to edge at the center.  A pitted copper circle from ancient Earth leaned against a silver square from Cygnus, bridging a whispery band far beneath the mighty stellar frequencies he usually searched.  Through the horns came rapid, delicate crackles that sounded nothing like collapsing suns.  Holding his breath, Oren narrowed the filters to distill those faint pulses until a vivid backwards prophecy unfolded from the noise. 

He perceived two stranded survey ships from stranger worlds drifting beside a dying red dwarf eons ago, trading pocket tokens across an airlock and locking their radio transceivers in steady unison so neither crew would face the cold dark unheard.  The stars around them had offered no guidance or mercy, confirming his deepest skepticism that the cosmos itself possessed any plan at all.  Yet while giant galaxies had dissolved into incoherent chaos, that deliberate signal of shared witness had survived the crunch as a pure, unbroken standing wave.  Moved by quiet wonder, Oren tuned the station core directly to their modest frequency.  Above him, the fractures in the golden resin ceased their groaning and fused smooth, humming in sudden harmony with the only kind of meaning that had ever been real.  Beneath the indifferent wreckage of the heavens, Oren smiled softly, swept his coins back into his palm, and leaned forward to listen again.
