High in the vaulted ceiling, the iron lift rattled upward through solid rock, carrying the final banquet guests back toward the surface.  Only after the last echo of laughter faded from the towering selenite spires did Julian dare to breathe.  Deep beneath the mountain, the subterranean crystal cave reclaimed its ancient stillness, save for the slow drip of mineral water into unseen pools.  Ribbons of phosphorescent fluorite and pale emerald quartz caught the faint shaft light, casting wavering curtains of violet and green across the cavern floor like a captive polar dawn.  Seated beside the empty banquet tables, the portrait sketch artist rubbed his aching knuckles, every ridge of his fingers stained dark gray with compressed charcoal.  For four hours he had traded rapid, disposable likenesses for silver coins, reducing human faces to brisk smudges of soot for strangers who would forget him by morning. 

Julian himself could forget nothing at all.  Mnemonically gifted since childhood, he carried an immaculate archive behind his eyes where every fleeting expression, every fractured gleam of crystal across five years of underground exile, remained preserved in merciless clarity.  Most vivid of all was Mara, the mineralogist who had first charted these glowing veins before cave sickness stole her breath.  Since burying her, he had sentenced himself to barren monochrome, refusing to mix color or compose an original vision while a thousand luminous paintings accumulated unspoken inside his skull.  Tonight that inward pressure had grown unbearable, driving him from his stool with a fierce need to uncork the suppressed creativity dammed within his chest. 

He crossed the shimmering grotto toward the central limestone pillar, where a large oil canvas hung in a gilded frame.  Commissioned by the cavern proprietors during the final weeks of her illness, it depicted Mara smiling placidly beneath a painted summer sky she had never cared for.  Desperate to pay her physicians, Julian had painted that pleasant commercial falsehood, smoothing away her solemn intensity and the subterranean twilight she loved.  When she died regardless, shame had turned the memorial into a painting with something behind it: a hollowed stone niche where he sealed away his brushes and mineral oils before swearing off true art.  Yet five years of dusty penance had taught him that inescapable callings cannot be starved into silence, for the hand belongs to its vocation even when the heart resists. 

Lifting the heavy frame from its iron pegs, Julian set the canvas upright upon his easel and uncovered a rusted steel grate over the wall cavity.  Through the iron hasp hung a broken combination lock whose numbered outer skirt he had sheared off with a geological hammer on the night of her funeral.  Because his unforgetting mind would never lose the sequence of twelve, thirty, and ten, he had obliterated the numbers and bent the central knob to keep himself from dialing it open.  Aligning a tiny nick on that scarred brass knob with the top seam of the casing, Julian recalled the forty vanished divisions of the circle as pure geometry.  Measuring nine degrees per tick by sight alone, he spun the scraping knob clockwise with blackened fingers to set the nick at one hundred eight degrees.  Carefully he reversed past the seam to two hundred seventy degrees, then turned forward to ninety until the internal tumblers aligned and the corroded shackle sprang free. 

From the damp stone alcove he drew out his cedar box of sable brushes, linseed oil, and glass jars filled with pigments ground from native fluorite, malachite, and azurite.  As the sharp scent of pine spirits rose into the cold air, Julian took up a shard of raw quartz to rectify the betrayal staring back from the easel.  With steady strokes he abraded the glossy varnish across the counterfeit meadow and that hollow, obedient smile, roughening the surface so new oil could bind.  His long drought broke in a rush of iridescent color as his brush swept shimmering curtains of violet and glacial green across the background, painting the living cavern around her.  Working from flawless memory, he reshaped her mouth into quiet resolve and darkened her eyes with the luminous ache of the final nights they had shared underground.  Tears traced clean paths through the charcoal dust on his cheeks, yet his hand never faltered as truth emerged from beneath the lie.  Beneath the glowing crystal spires, grief shone from the wet canvas not as darkness, but as a radiant, sorrowful dawn.
