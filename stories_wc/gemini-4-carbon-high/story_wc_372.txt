High upon Mount Verlas, the frozen library above clouds of endless winter keeps its vigil in a quartz hush.  Hoarfrost coats the vaulted granite arches and turns every breath into a brief silver plume before it settles as dust upon the floor.  Within these crystalline halls, Anselm serves alone as the orderly records chief of a vanishing era.  For thirty years he has guarded the luminous vows of the lowland cities, tending row after row of promises that shine with captured devotion.  In warmer decades, two people swearing fidelity could bind their spoken words into living radiance, trapping a shimmering likeness of the moment inside treated glass.  Now the ancient magic is draining from the world, and even the severe cold of the glacier spire can no longer halt the loss.  One by one the stored phantoms are losing their glow, forcing Anselm to keep his post while illusions fade to memory. 

True to his strict training, he has processed the entire archive in unbroken alphabetical sequence without skipping a single stranger.  Ten thousand citizens have had their oaths documented in his leather ledgers before their glass plates went dark.  Tonight he reaches the final cedar cabinet at the end of Gallery Z, where only one uncataloged record remains.  It belongs to Zora, his late wife, who descended beneath the storm layer five winters ago to carry medicine to the valley towns and never returned.  Had he broken protocol to save their shared promise first, dozens of older plates in the earlier drawers would have perished unrecorded.  With gently meticulous care, Anselm slips his woolen gloves over stiff fingers so the warmth of his skin will not crack the freezing glass.  He lifts the magic lantern slide from its velvet slot and holds it toward the pale starlight slanting through the ice clerestory.  Within the dark emulsion, a faint golden thread still pulses like an ember buried under ash. 

Carrying the fragile square to his brass workbench, he eases the plate into the mirrored lantern carriage and opens the lens shutter.  Instantly the focused beam throws a phantom scene across the smooth alabaster wall.  There stands Zora in her heavy skyfarer coat, clasping his hands as ribbons of gold and violet light braid around their wrists.  No sound emerges in the stillness, yet he remembers every syllable they spoke about enduring separation and keeping faith with the truth.  Already the edges of her coat are fraying into colorless mist as the projection consumes its final reserve of power.  Within minutes the spectacle will vanish forever, and Anselm himself will one day pass away with no successor on the mountain.  His sole purpose in this lonely sanctuary is to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion before total darkness claims their history.  If an image cannot survive as magic, its inner structure must be translated into permanent law. 

Anselm swings a crystal spectrometer into the path of the weakening beam.  Because every sincere emotion vibrates at a distinct optical pitch, a fading vow still reveals its exact nature through the geometry of its waves.  He begins to measure the dying radiance by sampling frequencies across the refracted band of colors.  Turning the micrometer dial a fraction of a degree at a time, he isolates each harmonic vibration and writes the numbers in ink mixed with spirits.  At the lower crimson end of the scale, he records the steady pulse of mortal fear she hid on the morning of her departure.  Moving upward through amber and clear beryl, his gently meticulous hand captures a fierce, unwavering spike of courage.  Highest of all, trembling near the border of invisible violet, shines a pure frequency of tenderness that outlasts both grief and distance. 

On the alabaster wall, Zora lifts her chin and smiles one final time as her luminous form thins into pale static.  Anselm does not look away or falter, sampling the very last oscillation just as the braided ribbons dissolve.  A soft tick echoes from the brass housing when the magic lantern slide cools into a plain square of clear glass.  The wall stands bare, and the living vision now exists nowhere save behind his aging eyes.  Yet upon the open ledger page, three neat columns of figures prove beyond doubt what two human hearts once swore to one another.  Should travelers from a future century ever climb above the clouds and break the ice from these doors, they will read the precise mathematics of love and know the world below was not empty.  Anselm blots the final line, closes the heavy cover, and rests his gloved palms upon the leather in the quartz hush.
