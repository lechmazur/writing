The arctic gale dies completely after the falcon returns, dropping onto the iron rail with warm mist clinging to her slate feathers.  Inside the rusted outpost by a frozen lake, Julian lowers his brass dividers and listens to the sudden, suffocating absence of wind.  For five days, katabatic blizzards have hammered these corroded steel walls, rattling every loose rivet above the shoreline of Lake Mir.  Now a worrying tranquility settles over the white basin, so absolute that Julian can hear the cooling stove tick like an anxious pulse.  Beside the heavy door, Maren unclips the damp leather jesses from her bird and stares warily across the motionless glare.  "Ice only goes this quiet before the whole shelf drops," she murmurs, resting one gloved hand on the latch. 

"If the shelf drops, I will have to renumber four pages of the index, which I refuse to do twice," Julian replies.  He is a dry-witted atlas editor who treats uncharted wilderness mostly as an inconveniently cold archive full of sloppy citations.  Six hundred miles south, his printing presses are waiting on the final copper plates of the tenth decennial edition.  Julian came to this desolate borderland to find where the rivers begin, chasing three major waterways that seem to emerge fully formed from barren permafrost.  Every prior survey left this central plateau blank, a cartographic surrender that offended his tidy sensibilities far more than frostbite. 

While Maren glances toward the emergency sleds outside, Julian remains subtly confident, calmly uncapping his red proofing pen beside the south window.  He has already tethered their dogs along the solid granite spine behind the station, trusting deductions made months ago in a warm basement.  There, he had acquired the stained pocket lexicon of Pavel Koslov, an obsessive polyglot who spoke nine tundra dialects before vanishing in this valley.  Expeditions had combed Koslov's maps in vain, overlooking the polyglot's phrasebook dog-ear creased sharply down on page ninety to point at one obscure idiom.  Translated literally, the marked proverb stated that when the hunting bird brings down wet feathers, the iron house sketches three hidden throats upon its glass. 

"You are wagering our necks on folded paper," Maren says, though she steps closer to his workbench rather than bolting.  Even her gyrfalcon perches unnaturally still on the rafter, plumage sleeked flat in the eerie, breathless hush pressing against the tin roof.  "Editorial marks exist for a reason," Julian murmurs, aligning Folio Sixteen beside the weathered window frame.  Beneath Lake Mir lies a geological oddity theorized as a singular convergence, an event rare enough to wake only once a decade.  Three separate subterranean meltwater channels descend from distant glaciers and collide inside one geothermal vault deep under the ice.  For years, dense basalt traps their combined volume until rising volcanic warmth vents invisible vapor skyward, stalling the valley winds into dead silence.  At that exact threshold, the fused currents surge upward together before splitting into buried limestone conduits that birth the Verkh, Tal, and Nera rivers. 

A faint tingling enters the soles of Julian's boots, conducted straight from the bedrock through the station's oxidized iron pilings.  No sound disturbs the frozen expanse outside, yet an inaudible harmonic tremor sets the thin observation glass vibrating inside its putty seal.  Julian leans forward to study the pattern of condensation on glass where their trapped breath has fogged the cold surface.  Instead of pooling evenly, the microscopic droplets shiver and marshal themselves into crisp acoustic standing waves.  Right before Maren's widening eyes, a clear whorl opens near the center of the mist, spinning off three sharp, branching arteries toward the sill.  Those delicate, trembling channels herald the waking torrent under the lake, revealing both the hidden river mouths and the still nodal zone anchoring their ledge. 

Working swiftly, Julian presses his translucent vellum overlay against the casement and traces the three diverging paths in steady crimson ink.  He marks the central nexus where the resonance peaks, scaling the window's geometry onto his empty grid with practiced precision.  Out across the glare, the pale ice darkens without a single crack of thunder, parting in uncanny silence as the deep chamber overflows.  Three plumes of steaming black water glide smoothly across the basin and pour into distant limestone gorges, vanishing underground toward three separate seas.  Maren releases a long breath that fogs the lower corner of the pane, blurring the fragile map just as the bedrock subsides.  Julian blows gently across his wet lines and caps his pen with a soft click.  "Serviceable," he says, sliding the finished proof into his satchel, "though nature really ought to leave wider margins."
