Long after the shutdown of the singularity engines, Oren walked alone across the time dilation observation deck.  Each bootstep rang against the titanium grating and dissolved into an echoed silence that filled the tiered gallery like cold water.  Every console stood dark, stripped of power during the final evacuation.  To save the departing fleet from a core breach, Oren had manually dropped the damping rods inside the unshielded threshold.  The resulting chronal flash had saved ten thousand lives while knocking his own cells permanently out of phase with normal matter, rendering his touch fatal to anyone outside the gravity well.  So the survivors had welded his airlock shut, painted golden hymns across the outer hatch, stocked his cycling vaults, and fled, leaving him behind as a blessed pariah, canonized in prayer yet forbidden human contact forever. 

Without metric compensators shielding the hull from the spinning black hole below, his existence on the station had become temporally stretched.  One year on his wrist chronometer spanned ten millennia in the flat space beyond the armored glass.  Looking upward from the deck meant watching the cosmos unravel in fast motion.  Familiar constellations warped and scattered like sparks in a gale, giant stars swelled into dying embers, and the central pulsar that once anchored all human charts faded into darkness.  To steady his mind against that disorienting drift, Oren relied upon a family custom of sketching the night sky on anniversaries.  His ancestors had been deep void cartographers who drew the heavens on vellum every charter day to prove their slow haulers were truly moving.  Keeping that ritual every local year since the fleet departed, Oren filled sheet after sheet with charcoal dots, listening to his stylus whisper across the hollow vault. 

For six local years, the mismatching drawings only deepened his grief, confronting him with an alien heaven that erased every memory of home.  Yet on his seventh anniversary, spreading the six prior vellum sheets across a dead navigation table, he felt a sudden urge to turn confusion into exploration.  He was not merely stranded in a tomb; his dilated perch had carried him seventy thousand years into the uncharted future, farther than any scout had ever sailed.  If he could measure how the stars were flowing, he could map this new epoch instead of mourning the old one.  Lacking powered astrometric sensors, he needed a precise geometric reticle to align his drawings against the viewport.  He looked down at the gilded compass rose applique sewn onto the cuff of his hereditary flight coat.  Stiffened with fine gold wire in exact radial symmetry, the octagonal heirloom recalled terrestrial oceans none of his spaceborne kin had ever touched.  With a pocket blade, Oren carefully cut the stitching, each tiny snap of thread echoing faintly off the curved quartz overhead. 

Navigators of the old fleet had spoken lazily of true north, applying the ancient term to whatever bright beacon star lay ahead on their transient routes.  Across tens of thousands of years, however, local stars wandered like windblown sand, proving that true north could only exist anchored to the deep extragalactic frame beyond the Milky Way.  Oren pressed the flat metallic emblem onto the observation glass with a dab of instrument wax, centering its inner ring over a distant lenticular galaxy that had not budged a hairbreadth since the evacuation.  Rotating the longest golden needle until it stood perpendicular to the galactic plane, he established an immutable meridian against the churning sky.  One by one, he held his translucent vellum sketches over the mounted applique, aligning that extragalactic hub so the wild scatter of foreground stars resolved into coherent streams of natural orbital rotation. 

Almost every point of light flowed clockwise along the spiral arm, except for one faint anomaly climbing directly up the gilded northern spire.  Leaning his forehead near the cold pane as a foreground dust lane thinned, Oren squinted past the gleaming wire tip to glimpse what his overlays had uncovered.  Five amber dwarf stars were marching in a crisp, artificial wedge straight out of the galactic disk, each dimmed in rhythmic pulses by vast harvesting shells.  Humanity had not vanished into the dark; across the eons, the children of the fleet had learned to steer suns like ships, voyaging deliberately into the intergalactic void.  Oren exhaled a quiet laugh that rebounded softly through the empty dome and faded into stillness.  Pinning a fresh sheet of vellum beside the golden needle, the lone cartographer raised his charcoal and began charting their course toward the deep tomorrow.
