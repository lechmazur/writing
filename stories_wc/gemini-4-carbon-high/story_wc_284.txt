Ansel climbed Zephyr Bluff in mist so thick it dissolved the chalk cliff into the sky, wrapping the headland in a silver hush.  Far below, the tide breathed unseen against the rocks, muffled to a faint pulse.  Every survival rule of a burned spy warned him never to revisit an old fallback site.  Six months earlier, when the Zurich operation shattered in an ambush, Ansel had bolted into the dark with their joint ledger microdot, leaving his partner Maret trapped behind hostile borders while the directorate disavowed them both.  Since that night, no radio signal or courier between them had stirred. 

His ascent up the exposed ridge had to be prudently daring, balancing mortal risk against strict tradecraft.  Recklessness would lead directorate hunters straight to the cliff, yet excessive caution had already cost Maret her freedom.  Using the dense sea fog to blind distant surveillance optics, he tested each patch of softening crust with his toe before shifting his weight, ensuring no sharp crack carried across the slope.  The calendar forced his hand now, at the tilt of the earth toward spring, when mild westerly winds began eating into the deep highland drifts.  By tomorrow afternoon, the seasonal thaw would slump the snowpack behind the stone windbreak and wash the winter record into mud. 

Years ago, anticipating total electronic blackout, he and Maret had devised a quiet system for leaving codes in accumulated snow.  In the sheltered lee of the granite wall where snow piled undisturbed, a sender could visit after each storm and press flat black slate chips into the fresh layer at measured distances from the corner post.  Subsequent blizzards sealed each line intact, stacking dispatches chronologically like rings inside a tree.  Kneeling beside the post, Ansel drew a narrow blade and sliced a clean vertical pane down through four feet of compressed white strata.  From his coat pocket he produced an engraved pillbox of dull pewter, its rim etched with frost ferns whose uneven fronds formed the only gauge capable of decoding those intervals.  Inside that small metal case rested two relics of Zurich: a gray cyanide tablet he had lacked the nerve to take, and, hidden under the velvet pad, the unredacted ledger microdot. 

His breath caught as three distinct tiers of dark slate glinted within the icy cross section.  Pressing the pewter rim beside the lowest December band, he aligned the etched fern stems with the stones and read her terse confirmation of survival.  The middle January layer noted patrols along the valley road, while the highest row, resting barely an inch beneath the wet surface glaze, promised she would watch the bluff at dawn until thaw.  Maret was alive, and almost certainly hidden in the pale birch scrub right now with a pistol aimed at his spine.  All winter inside silent rented rooms, Ansel had wondered why safety felt like slow suffocation, until he came back here to find where unused courage waits.  He understood now that it never waited in a suicide pill or a bolted door, but in surrendering the shield he had hoarded.  Fixing what's broken between two ghosts required a real price, because the ledger microdot that could clear Maret with neutral authorities also exposed the emergency alias keeping Ansel safe.  Once she traded that file for sanctuary, his quiet refuge would burn and the hunt for him would resume. 

With steady fingers, Ansel clicked open the pewter lid, pinched out the gray cyanide tablet, and tossed it over the cliff into the void.  He lifted the dark velvet pad until the tiny glass microdot shone bare against the metal.  Apologies meant nothing in their trade, so he chose instead to vouchsafe the unredacted proof and yield his survival wholly into her hands.  Setting the open box squarely atop the granite post, he crushed the sliced snow trench beneath his boot so no passing tracker could read her winter vigil.  Then he raised his empty hands, walked ten slow paces toward the precipice, and stood facing the unseen sea with his back exposed to the trees.  For a long minute only meltwater dripped from the stones, pattering softly through the mist.  At last a light boot sole crunched the wet snow behind him, lingered by the post, and snapped the pewter hinge shut.  "You will need a new name once I file this," Maret murmured near his shoulder.  When Ansel turned, she was already walking toward the northern pass, pausing once in the luminous fog to let him follow.
