Elara had warned the council what happens after dreams become shareable: sleepers keep the radiance and sweep away the dark.  Ignoring her caution, the architects below tethered a blooming library above clouds, hanging crystal reels of extracted visions inside high glass conservatories where stratospheric sunlight never failed.  Fed by the warmth of collective slumber, pale orchids and vellum vines climbed the brass stacks in luminous profusion.  For forty years Oren walked those aerial aisles as the last sharp-eyed dusk noticer, trained to study the brief violet interval when the sun dipped beneath the silver cloud sea.  Only in that slanting twilight could a trained observer judge whether the stored visions still held true depth, and lately the diagnosis broke his heart.  Across every gallery, the crystal spools were bleaching into featureless glare, blinding the citizens below who borrowed them each night in search of solace. 

Even the foundational reel on Terrace Nine, donated by Elara during the inaugural twilight before her death, now burned like blank white coal among suffocating blossoms.  Inside his wool coat, Oren turned over the smooth gray river stone she had pressed into his palm that evening, a cold terrestrial weight shaped by centuries of dark current rather than easy wishing.  Because spoken vibrations shattered delicate dream filaments, strict quiet ruled the conservatory, and overhead optical lenses recorded the halls in perpetual hush.  To the naked eye, Terrace Nine showed nothing unusual beneath its tangled white canopy, yet Oren suspected the fading had begun on the night the archive opened. 

It was only by comparing silent video footage from decades apart that he uncovered the wound hidden inside the brightness.  In his dim study, he synchronized the grainy monochrome capture of the inaugural sunset forty years earlier beside the crisp recording from yesterday evening.  On the older screen, young Elara stood at the western balustrade resting her fingers upon the river stone while glass needles drew a vision of human renewal from her brow.  When the extraction finished and she lifted the stone to walk toward Oren, the long dark silhouette behind her ankles tore cleanly away from her boots and stayed upon the limestone floor.  Shifting his gaze to the modern screen, Oren caught his breath in sudden sorrow.  Half masked by forty years of radiant overgrowth, that identical figure still materialized against the balustrade during the three minutes of dusk: a shadow unbound, severed from its dreamer when the machine harvested her brightest longing and left her mortal doubt behind. 

Even as an untethered stain of twilight, the phantom moved with the softly deliberate grace Elara had possessed in life, curving translucent dark fingers over the empty spot on the rail where the stone had rested.  Every other shared vision in the sky had cast off smaller shades that dissipated into the clouds, leaving pure light to rot into emptiness because radiance without boundary cannot hold a recognizable form.  Elara alone had anchored her extraction to earthly stone, and for four decades her abandoned shade had lingered in faithful hunger while Oren hoarded that very token in his pocket out of lonely grief.  Climbing the spiral stair to Terrace Nine as afternoon thinned toward amber, he felt a piercing wish to leave things unchanged so he might visit her silhouette every evening until he died.  Yet watching the frayed edges of her outline tremble as the sun touched the cloud deck, he understood what had to be surrendered to preserve the shape of hope. 

Stepping quietly through the blinding orchids, Oren drew out the cool river stone and placed it upon the limestone rail directly beneath her phantom palm.  At the instant shadow met mineral, the untethered darkness drank the ancient gravity of the riverbed and began to metamorphose.  Her slender human profile dissolved before his aching eyes, unspooling from the stone into living filaments of indigo shade that threaded swiftly up the bleached vines and into the crystal reels.  Where those dark veins wove through the glass, harsh white glare softened into gold and violet, restoring horizon lines, shaded porches, and the patient contours of promises worth keeping.  Across the vast conservatory, bruised twilight petaled alongside the white blooms for the first time, teaching the shared visions of humanity how to hold darkness without despair.  When the sun vanished below the clouds, Elara was gone from the balustrade forever, spent wholly into the merciful night that made the archive legible again.  Oren rested his palm upon the bare stone, mourning the forty years they had missed while grateful for the quiet glow now warming every shelf above the world.
