High sandstone walls held the cold night like a vessel, turning the quiet canyon under stars into a chamber where even a falling grain of grit seemed to chime and linger.  Oren rested his spine against the stone, listening to the thin whistle in his raw lungs hours after surviving the attempt on his life.  Before midnight, the Bailiff's wardens had barred his studio doors and cracked his main furnace, expecting spilled oil and white heat to silence him before dawn.  Instead, he had crawled through the stone annealing culvert into the dry wash, carrying blistered palms, a bruised rib, and a satchel of his finest work.  Now the distant violence felt strangely unmoored, fading into the slow, drifting resonance of wind moving high above the gorge rim. 

For twenty years as a luminous glass artist, Oren had feared stillness above every other flaw.  At the mouth of a roaring kiln, hesitation ruined the gather; if a maker stopped turning the iron rod, molten crystal drooped and died.  Yet tonight his injured chest demanded long rests between shallow breaths, and without a flame left to tend, he felt an unexpected pull to discover purpose in the pauses.  Steadily adaptive by craft and temperament, he did not mourn his shattered tools or his burned workshop.  He bound his stinging fingers in cool seep moss, braced his elbows on a flat boulder, and drew two clear panes from his bag.  Fused with pale canyon spar, the twin sheets drank the starlight and exhaled a heatless sea glow, reminding him that his medium shone clearest in the hush after fire vanished. 

Between those glowing plates rested the true reason his workshop now lay in ashes: a charcoal smuggler's ledger strip.  Maren, who slipped dense ironwood fuel past the valley tolls to feed his unsanctioned kilns, had hidden the narrow ribbon of calfskin inside his cooling flue hours before the Bailiff arrested her.  Its cramped tallies named not only village artisans, but the Bailiff himself, who diverted taxed fuel to private silver smelters while hanging poorer runners for theft.  The arson meant to erase both witness and proof had baked the calfskin ribbon into a brittle, blackened curl.  Dark gall ink had vanished completely against dark char, transforming Maren's careful accounting into an unreadable, scorched testament that would flake into dust at the slightest brush of a thumb. 

Oren could not carry anything so fragile far, nor could he strike a flame without curling the strip to ash.  Instead, he trusted the cold physics of glass, salt, and open sky.  Using the tip of a dry reed, he eased the charred strip flat across the first luminous pane and waited as predawn chill pooled along the canyon floor.  Under the cloudless vault of stars, heat bled rapidly from stone and crystal into the thin air until moisture began to crystallize.  Slowly, delicate patterns in morning frost bloomed across the ruined calfskin.  Because Maren's ink had been brewed with iron salts and gum, tiny ice needles anchored themselves solely along her sunken pen strokes while leaving the barren char untouched.  Lit from beneath by the cool green radiance of the glass, ghostly silver letters, ciphered weights, and the Bailiff's own countersign shimmered back into existence. 

Oren drew a diamond scribe from his coat, laid the second glowing pane beside the first, and adjusted his body to a patient new cadence.  A single warm exhale would melt the rime and blind him, so he turned his head aside to breathe, then bent over the boulder during the quiet suspension of his lungs.  In each held silence, his steady hand worked to retrace every frosted numeral, waypoint, and signature permanently into the hard face of the second glass sheet.  Weakness in his chest became a measure of craft: inhale toward the dark, pause over the light, carve truth while all motion ceased.  The crisp scratch of the diamond echoed softly against the canyon walls and settled into the hush, unhurried and sure. 

When the stars dimmed to pale silver and dawn thinned the ice to mist, a breeze scattered the spent calfskin into soot.  Nothing of the Bailiff's fire could touch what remained.  Every incised line on the luminous pane caught the residual glow from within, holding Maren's witness in material that neither rot nor flame could unmake.  Wrapping the engraved glass in his satchel, Oren rose from the stillness and turned upslope to retrace Maren's hidden trail toward the high courts, carrying light that outlasted the flame.
