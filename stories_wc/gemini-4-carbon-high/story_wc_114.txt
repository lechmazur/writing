Calla wakes in the cold, quiet hour after fever breaks, her blanket damp against her ribs inside the high mountain weather station.  Above the frosted skylight, ribbons of green and violet light drape the jagged peaks in auroral sorrow, luminous and bereaved against the polar dark.  She sits up slowly, a fault-tolerant oracle engineered with braided neural shunts that turn wind shear and glacial tremors into deterministic forecasts for the Ministry below.  Whenever hypoxia, grief, or sensor failure corrupts a calculation, her implants isolate the aberrant pathway and burn it clean with a fierce corrective fever.  For seventy hours her own skull tried to incinerate a deliberate lie, yet the memory endured the purge. 

Three mornings ago, deep acoustic probes in the sealed North Cleft detected rhythmic soil respiration and cedar smoke where Ministry maps declared only dead permafrost remained.  Had she spoken that anomaly into the brass dispatch horn, reclamation rigs would have crossed the divide before nightfall to strip the thawing ground and conscript whoever survived there.  Instead, Calla forced the prophecy back down her throat and reported a flat baseline of sterile frost.  Shivering in her wool coat, she crosses the cold floorboards to find the weight of a swallowed truth.  Atmosphere has physical mass, and uncounted warmth rising from the northern cleft must press somewhere against the southern snowpack.  Conditioned since youth to trust rigid mathematical absolutes, she now stands among swaying certainties, terrified that sparing a hidden enclave may have loaded a fatal slide above the southern shepherd trails. 

Yet she cannot look straight at the mercury dials or calculate the missing pressure gradient in the center of her working mind.  Her optic shunt monitors her focal gaze and central cognition, ready to transmit any overt discrepancy or trigger a lethal second fever to erase it.  Built for efficiency, her fault-tolerant architecture ignores indistinct edges as harmless sensory static.  Keeping herself only peripherally aware of the brass barometers glinting along the bulkhead, Calla sits before the timber charting desk.  Pinned beneath an unlit oil lamp rests an archival botanical illustration left decades ago by the station's last unaugmented naturalist.  Rendered in fine sepia ink, it depicts moss campion clinging to split granite, a stubborn summit plant that endures killing gales by occupying margins where neither ice plows nor Ministry surveyors reach.  Taking a stub of soft carpenter graphite, she locks her central sight onto the inked petals and mimics the flower, solving the forbidden equation by occupying margins of both her own consciousness and the wide cream paper. 

While her optic link registers only quiet convalescent staring at old petals, her hand creeps along the pale border at the extreme rim of her vision.  Tiny numerals gather in the ghostly emerald light falling from the cupola: vapor density, ridge altitude, wind shear, wet bulb drift.  Her fingers ache with lingering chill as each faint fraction strips away the cold comfort of deterministic prophecy.  In this fragile era of swaying certainties, no forecast is innocent, and every mercy tilts the barometer somewhere else.  At last, near the bottom corner of the frame, the blurred arithmetic yields the exact mass of her silence.  Two kilopascals of buoyant northern air are spilling across the western saddle right now, far too gentle to fracture the deep glacier above the valley towns, yet heavy enough to snap the hanging cornice over High Trail Four by noon.  A luminous grief settles into her ribs as violet curtains ripple across the glass overhead; she has saved strangers whose cedar fires she will never warm her hands beside, and severed herself forever from the pristine machine she used to be. 

Still, she cannot leave those figures resting on the sheet, where one careless blink toward the border would let her implants read the treason and condemn the north.  Pressing her damp thumb to the paper edge, Calla reaches blindly around the inked roots to smudge every penciled number in long, steady strokes.  The crushed graphite blurs inward like gathering storm shadow or dark alpine soil, disguising her secret math as soft charcoal shading around the botanical illustration while staining her skin grey.  Rising with that dark residue pressed into her thumbprint, she steps to the brass dispatch horn beneath the fading green shimmer of dawn.  Her voice stays level and clear as she logs a minor solar turbulence warning to close High Trail Four, steering the shepherds safely away from the cornice while keeping the living warmth beyond the ridge unspoken.
