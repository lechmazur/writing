At midnight, Marin climbed the frosted steps of the celestial mapping observatory, clutching the daylily-pressed telegram from her brother Henrik.  Tucked inside its folded yellow paper lay a flattened tawny blossom from their childhood garden, an old emblem for easing sorrow, resting beside five urgent words that summoned her up the mountain.  Inside the high brass dome, red lanterns cast a gentle glow across mahogany star cabinets, wrapping the cold room in midnight solace.  Henrik looked up from his charting table, his eyes tired after a year of mourning his late wife, yet warmed by quiet discovery.  Without fanfare, he poured steaming tea into two tin mugs and set her leather ledger beside his glass photographic plates. 

In the valley below, Marin worked as a lucid dream chronicler, a vocation steeped in nocturnal stillness.  By guiding her own conscious sleep through the tangled memories recounted by bereaved clients, she composed spoken elegies that untied their sharpest regrets.  Townsfolk sought her door because she was a rare listener who heals with words, shaping fragile syllables into genuine relief.  Yet absorbing the sorrow of strangers while failing to comfort Henrik had hollowed her voice, leaving her exhausted and ashamed of her silence.  Desperate for a way to endure such weight, she had resolved to decode temple inscriptions from the collapsed Sanctuary of Somnus, where ancient sleep healers once carved their foundational vow.  Marin had glimpsed that vaulted ceiling only once as a young apprentice before a winter rockslide sealed the coastal sanctuary forever.  For months, she had entered disciplined lucid dreams to walk that remembered stone hall again, meticulously copying every archaic syllable and notched border from her subconscious memory into her waking ledger.  Still, the vertical columns read as scattered nonsense until she mailed the ledger up the mountain as a hesitant peace offering. 

Now Henrik laid a strip of punctured telegraph tape beside her meticulous drawings of the sanctuary frieze.  He explained that watching the village operator tap out his message had revealed what scholars of antiquity always overlooked.  Just as the pressed daylily opened for a brief span of brightness before folding into dusk, the shallow and deep notches above each column recorded intervals of waxing and waning light.  The temple builders had encrypted their sacred vow through variable stars, keying every column of text to the pulsation period of a changing sun.  Only inside a place dedicated to measuring stellar brightness across years could the scattered columns be arranged from shortest cycle to longest.  Side by side in the hush of ticking sidereal clocks, sister and brother matched her dream transcriptions against his magnitude catalogs of Cepheus, Lyra, and Cygnus.  One by one, the ancient syllables slid into place along a parchment sheet, progressing from swift rhythms of hours to the slow crimson breath of Mira. 

A single keystone column remained, matched to Algol in Perseus, which was reaching its deepest dimming at that very minute.  Henrik rotated the great refractor toward the open slit of night sky and gestured for Marin to witness the event herself.  Pressing her eye to the cool brass lens, she beheld a pale star softened to a gentle ember amid velvet darkness.  Henrik murmured that Algol did not falter alone; it was an eclipsing pair of stars locked in mutual gravity, where the dimmer companion periodically crossed before the brighter sun.  Long before the eastern ridge grew pale, they stood together in that quiet hour when understanding dawns, illuminating cipher and soul alike.  Marin stepped back to the lantern and wrote the final syllables into the opening line, then read the restored vow aloud.  The ancient text declared that no speaker of healing words was meant to swallow the darkness of others in solitude.  Grief survived only as shared burdens, held in patient orbit where one soul stood ready to witness the shadow until the light returned. 

Marin looked across the parchment and saw quiet tears glinting beneath his tired lashes in the dim amber glow.  She had stayed away because she lacked a flawless cure for his loss, never realizing that her simple presence beside his sorrow was the medicine itself.  Reaching across the star charts, she took his hand and spoke the translated liturgy a second time, letting her voice steady and warm the cold air.  On the closing cadence, Henrik joined his low voice to hers, speaking the name of his late wife into the peaceful dark without breaking.  Above the open dome, the distant binary pair began its slow climb back toward full brilliance, keeping faithful time until morning.
