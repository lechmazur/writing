Every evening, under a fleeting golden sunset, wealthy passengers gathered on the western terrace of Grand Malachite Terminal to pose before Master Oren's brass lens.  They paid handsomely for selenite plates that flattered departing faces in warm amber light, unaware of what the rare mineral truly recorded.  Julian knew, which was why he had spent the past year working the platform shadows as a golden-hour portrait thief.  Having grown up orphaned beneath these sooty viaducts while trains carried everyone else toward purposeful lives, he never stole the silvered glass for coin.  Years ago, sweeping broken plates behind Oren's tent, he had discovered that dusk selenite occasionally trapped illuminated moments, faint halos hinting at a traveler's approaching fate.  Ever since, he had pilfered those anomalous portraits, desperate to glimpse how futures formed and aching to learn whether a stalled life like his own might ever move. 

Finding the right plate among dozens on Oren's drying rack required more than nimble fingers, and Julian was quietly savvy, attentive to subtle rhythms the station constables missed.  Months earlier, he had noticed Vera, the reserved conservatory florist whose stall bordered the darkroom curtain, arranging fresh centerpieces right as Oren dipped each batch into his chemical bath.  Whenever a plate flared unusually bright in the tray, Vera adjusted her vases, embedding codes in flower arrangements along the marble counter to mark the exact slot for a clandestine collector.  Once Julian cracked her botanical cipher, he began beating that unseen buyer to the prize, yet every plate he carried back to his gaslit garret showed only unreadable smears of golden fog.  Tonight, after watching from the archway as the crimson sun sank behind the river ironworks, Julian drifted past Vera's newly unmanned counter and read her latest vase with a jolt of surprise.  Four stems of white hellebore leaned toward Slot Four, bound with fragrant evening stock that spelled an urgent summons behind the conservatory ferns at moonrise. 

Moving swiftly before the last amber ray vanished, Julian swapped a blank glass square into Slot Four while Oren capped his lens, tucking the warm original inside his coat as twilight fell.  He stepped inside the grand concourse just as a full autumn moon cleared the eastern hills, pouring pale radiance through ten thousand panes of green cathedral glass overhead.  Within moments, the vast hall hushed and shifted into an emerald station by moonlight, its marble pillars swimming in jade luminescence while local commuter crowds thinned.  Ducking behind a pillar to inspect his prize, Julian froze when he saw no wealthy traveler on the selenite, but his own watchful face captured minutes earlier in Vera's angled mirror, wreathed in that same indecipherable gilt haze. 

Any sensible cutpurse would have bolted into the streets, yet curiosity pulled Julian toward the shadowed conservatory alcove, where Vera waited beside two packed leather satchels.  "It is a lonely habit, trying to press your ear against destiny's door," Vera murmured, her dark eyes welcoming rather than accusing.  She explained that her former scholar had retired, leaving her nobody sharp enough to assist her northern light expedition until she noticed Julian decoding her bouquets without ever selling a stolen plate.  "Gaslight blinds the mineral because sunset selenite only records half the truth, locking the rest away until it meets its opposite," she said, lifting a heavy glass jar of captured moonbeams from her workbench.  Harvested beneath the green conservatory dome where refracted lunar zeniths condensed like liquid frost, the vessel swirled with weightless silver mist that cooled the air between their palms. 

When Vera unstoppered the lid, a ribbon of pearlescent vapor spilled across the portrait in Julian's hands, marrying trapped solar gold with living moonlight.  Julian stared in luminous disbelief as the shimmering silver tide dissolved the muddled haze on the glass, forcing him to reinterpret everything he thought the image showed.  What had looked like blurred station ironwork sharpened into the open observation rail of an alpine express winding past snowy peaks, and the solitary thief in the frame was not alone at all.  Standing beside him on that distant mountain train was Vera, holding a portable field camera while he cradled the glowing jar, two partners mapping uncharted light far beyond the city walls.  Down on Track Four, the evening northbound express sounded its boarding whistle beneath the emerald vault, and Vera held out two tickets with a hopeful smile.  Sealing the glowing jar, Julian lifted her second satchel onto his shoulder and stepped out from the shadows, realizing the door had never been locked, only waiting for enough light to show the handle.
