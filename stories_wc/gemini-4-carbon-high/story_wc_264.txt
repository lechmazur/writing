Hush now, little cinder, lay your grey head down.  Long after the last forest burns, the iron train still rocks along its black rails like a cradle that forgot the child.  Tonight a pressure alarm brings the engine sighing to a halt beside a glowing dune in bloom, where pale silica flowers drink the cold ash and shine with brief, feverish light.  Down the catwalk steps Bram, a dreamless freight guard with heavy lead gloves and eyes as dry as salt.  Surgeons pared the visions of sleep from his skull years ago, because a dreaming mind casts wild, unruly glances through the dark and ruins what the railcars carry.  So he walks awake through the broken night, humming half a tune whose ending burned away. 

Inside the sealed vaults hums the divergent archive, a cargo of uncollapsed histories salvaged from branches of time where the great fires never caught.  There, suspended in magnetic dark, sleep the phantom seeds of rain and timber that consensus reality has already judged dead.  Yet beneath car four, a frosted gasket hisses open.  Something delicate has slipped the vault, an unburned white birch unmoored from certainty.  Freed from its cage, the sapling is occasionally everywhere at once.  Bram feels cool phantom bark brush his scarred wrist even as silver leaves flicker across the distant ridge and ghostly roots thread the iron wheels.  Worse, the blossoms along the sand shed stray photons through the gloom, and each tiny collision threatens to measure the tree and reduce it to soot. 

Railway law demands that he ignite his magnesium flare, stare straight into the shimmer, and collapse the stray specimen before it tangles the line.  Hush now, little ghost, do not wake the light.  Bram leaves the flare holstered at his belt.  He keeps this lonely post for one secret purpose, to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners of a dead earth.  Where no eye looks and no stray beam scatters, a fragment of another history can linger without turning to ash.  Beyond the bright crest of the dune yawns a deep basalt sinkhole, roofed in stone and drowned in permanent shadow.  If he can guide the birch down into that blind hollow, the dark itself will shelter it. 

He cannot touch the sapling, nor even turn his gaze toward its trembling haze, lest awareness pin it to this barren century.  Instead, he must charm it through wave functions, coaxing unseen probabilities the way a mother coaxes breath back into a startled infant.  Kneeling with his eyes shut tight, Bram unclips a resonant brass fork and strikes it softly against his boot heel.  His magnetic baton drinks that pure pitch and casts coherent standing waves across the slope, paced by the ragged rhythm of his own quiet singing.  Rest where the stone is hollow, little leaf, rest where no sun counts your ribs.  Where his tuned field meets the bright petals, destructive interference sweeps the sapling gently back from the lethal glow.  Where the troughs pool inside the black sinkhole, the scattered amplitudes gather and deepen like rainwater finding a well. 

Gradually the flickering ceases along the distant ridge, and the phantom roots slip free of the iron wheels, drawn whole into the cavern below.  Still, shadow alone cannot hold back consensus time forever.  Without an anchor, the dead century will eventually press down and smother the probabilities in their sleep.  Reaching into his coat, Bram withdraws a contraband relic built by rogue chronometrists, a chess clock frozen at midnight.  Its left dial was wound inside this world of cinders, while its right was wound inside the green archive, their twin gears entangled like rivals across a board.  Neither timeline can claim the next move until the opposing lever falls, and with both brass hands pinned fast at twelve, local reality remains locked in a quiet truce. 

Eyes still closed, Bram lowers the silent timepiece by a cord until it settles upon the unseen floor of the pit.  Out of the dark rises a sudden, impossible scent of wet bark and sweet sap, brushing his face like the dream he is forbidden to have.  Sleep between the ticks, little bough, neither gone nor here.  Feeling blindly along the rim, he slides a flat slate boulder over the narrow opening to seal the sanctuary against the bright blossoms and the coming dawn.  Down in the blind earth, beside the halted dials, an unburned tree sways softly in an unlit wind.  Bram opens his eyes, walks back to seal the frosted gasket on car four, and climbs aboard, humming his broken lullaby down the iron dark toward the next shadow.
