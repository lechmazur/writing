In this breathless age, the air carries no sound at all, swallowing wind and wingbeat alike into an unbroken hush.  Cael kneels motionless upon damp moss near the center of the siskin alder grove, keeping his breathing shallow as twilight cools the marsh.  Overhead, dozens of small yellow and black finches flit among dark woody cones, shaking loose pale seeds without producing even the faintest rustle.  Beside his bare knee, startled by the slight ground tremor of his arrival, a tight ball of brown spines rests wedged between two slick roots.  He will not lift a single tool from his canvas satchel until after the hedgehog uncurls, because fear knots the subtle pulse of a living woodland and ruins its natural balance. 

As the last tree-song sculptor in the valley, Cael has given his life to one quiet purpose: to create music in a silent world where melodies can travel only through solid matter into waiting skin and bone.  Other builders in nearby settlements strike solitary iron bars or hollow out fallen logs so two people pressing their palms to the timber might share a simple rhythm.  Cael, however, is symphonically minded, perceiving complex arrangements of tactile voices wherever wind, water, and forest creatures intersect.  Beneath this peat, an underground creek nudges submerged alder roots in a steady bass drone, while upper boughs sway like bowed cellos and feeding siskins tap light percussive patterns across the crown. 

For years he failed to weave those scattered vibrations into a single resonant chord without injuring the trees or losing the delicate pulse in wet bark.  His breakthrough came only this morning, by analyzing stress cracks in a long-forgotten sculpture standing bleached and leafless three paces to his left.  Generations ago, when the great hush first settled over the earth, an unknown carver had gouged deep acoustic chambers into that elder trunk and bolted rigid iron wires between it and its neighbors.  While waiting in stillness, Cael lets his gaze trace the jagged spiral fractures climbing from those ancient bolt holes into the dead heartwood.  Every split reveals the exact angle where autumn gales twisted the living alders in separate arcs while the unyielding metal fought their sway until the fibers tore apart.  Reading that preserved pain taught Cael that true forest harmony demands empathy in motion, an instrument designed to glide with each shifting trunk rather than bind them in place. 

Down against the dark peat, the cluster of spines finally parts, revealing a damp black snout that tests the evening air.  Soft paws stretch outward onto the moss, and as the small creature resumes nosing calmly among fallen leaves, the guarded tension beneath the roots melts away. 

Moving with unhurried grace so he disturbs neither beast nor bird, Cael reaches into his bag and lifts out a white porcelain insulator salvaged from a fallen telegraph pole along the old ridge road.  Instead of cutting into tender green bark that would muffle delicate frequencies, he wedges the dense ceramic cylinder snugly into a seasoned, naturally dead fork of the central living alder.  Guided by the stress angles mapped upon the ruined monument, he loops supple copper wire around the smooth glazed groove at the waist of the ceramic piece and anchors the ends loosely to dry knots on three surrounding trees.  When a dusk breeze leans the canopy eastward, the copper slides effortlessly across the glassy porcelain, equalizing the pull between trunks while channeling crisp kinetic energy straight into the core wood. 

Cael plants his bare soles firmly across the broad root arch and rests his cheekbone against the cool silver trunk just beneath the ceramic bridge.  Already his jawbone receives the quiet overture of the marsh: the deep hum of hidden water, the light staccato landings of roosting siskins, and the tiny rhythmic footsteps of the foraging hedgehog.  Waiting until a slow gust bows all four alders in unison and draws the wire to sweet tension, Cael raises his calloused thumb to strum.  Not a whisper stirs the dead air, yet a warm, luminous chord surges through the porcelain saddle, pours down the heartwood, and floods the intertwined root network beneath the grove.  Inside Cael, the bright shimmer of the copper wire marries the swaying timber and the subterranean stream, blooming through his ribs as a complete, living symphony.  Near his ankle, the hedgehog pauses and presses its soft belly flat against the vibrating root with relaxed eyes, while high above, the siskins tighten their claws around the humming twigs to drink the song together in the dark.
