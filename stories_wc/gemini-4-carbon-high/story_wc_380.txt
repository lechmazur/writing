Two nights after the whale beaches on the silver shoals, an unnatural hush settles over the harbor.  Along the wet piers, Oren walks his solitary route as the dock-side lantern lighter, touching a slender taper to every brass lamp until the boardwalk glows above the black tide.  For generations, grieving citizens of this coast have paid apothecaries to extract painful recollections and pour the distilled sorrow into the bay, trusting the deep currents to scatter their past forever.  None of them considered what feeds in the abyssal trenches far from land.  Now the immense leviathan lies motionless in the shallows, gleaming with moonlight and heavy with a century of swallowed human memory. 

Pausing beside the last post on the wharf, Oren reaches inside his wool coat and withdraws a folded square of parchment containing three pressed wildflowers.  Five winters ago, he submitted to the clearing himself, only to discover these dry violet blossoms tucked inside his oil ledger the following morning.  No such blooms grow along this barren salt peninsula, yet looking at them still aches in his ribs like a phantom limb.  Ever since that morning, he has walked the night watches driven by a single quiet purpose: to find where deleted memories go.  Tonight, the dying whale is answering him. 

As the leviathan surrenders its accumulated cargo back into the coastal water, forgotten grief reveals its true physical mass, forming invisible pockets of gravity along the waterfront.  Every unseen well fluctuates mysteriously, tugging lantern flames sideways toward the sea one minute and turning his leather boots heavy as iron the next, before releasing him into sudden weightlessness.  Because human sight cannot perceive these shifting tides of mass, Oren has learned to chart them through patterns in coffee grounds.  Kneeling on the frosted planks, he pours warm black dregs from his flask into a white porcelain cup, swirls the liquid thrice, and sets it flat upon the timber.  Instead of settling evenly at the bottom, the fine dark silt creeps up the northern rim in sharp, feathered ridges, pulled by a dense gravitational current directly beneath the pier. 

Oren lifts his hand lamp and descends the narrow stone stair cut into the sea cliff below the pilings, entering the subterranean salt crystal growing chamber.  Here, in shallow terraced vats fed by filtered tidewater, he cultivates clear halite prisms to focus the beams of his harbor lamps through winter fog.  Because the slightest vibration ruins crystal lattice formation, thick limestone walls seal the cavern from wind and surf alike, wrapping the tall white spires in a gleaming silence.  His single flame fractures across ten thousand geometric facets, filling the soundless vault with pale, diamond brilliance.  Seawater washing past the stranded whale has seeped through the intake grates, carrying dissolved memories into the saturating brine.  All across the chamber, droplets detach from the pools and hover in midair like polished pearls as the local pull surges, falters, and reverses without a whisper. 

Moving slowly between the luminous pillars, Oren swirls his porcelain cup again and watches the coffee sediment gather into a tight, unmistakable spiral at the exact center of the vessel.  He stands over the oldest evaporation bed, where centuries of mineral crust have hardened into a sparkling white floor.  Yet dozens of memories may lie sunken beneath this salt, and claiming the sorrow of a stranger would shatter his mind.  Unfolding his parchment, Oren holds the three pressed wildflowers flat upon his open palm and extends his arm across the basin.  Withered petals should weigh nothing at all, yet above a domed ridge near the eastern vat, the dry blossoms suddenly press into his skin like lead coins, drawn downward by the specific gravity of the hour from which they were plucked. 

Setting his lantern beside the ridge, Oren drops to his knees while the unseen force ebbs and swells around his shoulders like a submarine tide.  He draws his iron wick chisel from his belt and begins to exhume what lies buried beneath the dense crystalline shelf.  Flakes of translucent salt fall away without a sound until his fingers close around a warm geode veined with violet light, condensed from the heaviest brine.  The instant he lifts the stone from its white grave, the crushing pull vanishes, and a sunlit meadow blooms behind his eyes: a laughing woman pressing three violets into his palm before the fever took her, and the love he once feared too heavy to carry alone.  Tears spill down his cheeks and crystallize along his jaw, yet Oren cradles the radiant core against his chest, whole once more amid the thousand shining pillars and the unbroken hush.
