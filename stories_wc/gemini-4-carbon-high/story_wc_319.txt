Beneath the iced arches of the ruined courtyard, Oul crouched on all fours like a beast that had forgotten the shape of chairs.  He was a feral library guardian, his silver pelt matted with dead leaves and frozen ink, his curved claws clicking restlessly against the frost on the flagstones.  Around him stretched a frozen garden in mist, where petrified hedges stood in straight rows like abandoned bookshelves and pale vapor muffled every sound.  Ages ago, the High Index had fractured across the planes and stranded him in this mortal plot, leaving him fiercely dimensionally homesick for a realm of pure geometric order.  Now, lowering his scarred muzzle to the snow, Oul let his consciousness sink through the veil of sleep.  Guardians of his breed never slumbered blindly, so crossing that threshold brought a state of foggy lucidity in which his thoughts grew crystalline even as the outer world dissolved into drifting gray dream. 

In that poised awareness, Oul remembered how the High Index had been governed entirely by the boundaries of light.  In his lost homeland, every lantern cast a border as sharp as a blade, separating known truth from empty nonexistence without a single shadow in between.  Here on earth, the wet mist ruined those edges, scattering the moon into boundless, uncertain halos that defied cataloging.  His ancient duty relied on the art of interiorizing exteriors, a discipline by which guardians inhaled physical rooms and stored whole chambers intact within the vaults of their minds.  Yet only through the veil of sleep did those inner doors unlatch, and every night Oul clamped his jaws shut, afraid that absorbing this blurry courtyard would stain the pristine galleries inside his skull.  Instead of resting, he had grown wild and bitter, refusing to wait for peace while prowling the briars to drive off mortal scavengers. 

Between his forepaws glinted a cultist's pendant, ripped an hour earlier from the throat of a robed zealot who worshiped the vanished Index.  The intruder had crept past the iron gates hoping to pry open an astral rift, fleeing in terror into the night after Oul lunged snarling from the iced yews.  Forged from brass and captive starlight, the amulet answered the dream warmth of his breath as his slumber deepened.  With a low chime, the lens ignited and projected a rigid golden dome that sliced the vapor cleanly in two.  Designed by fanatics who despised mystery, the artifact restored the boundaries of light with pitiless severity, banishing every trace of haze inside its perimeter while plunging the rest of the garden into solid darkness. 

For one breathless moment, the ache of being dimensionally homesick vanished from his chest.  Inside that glowing cage, every ice crystal on the flagstones sat measured and exact, offering a miniature imitation of his native halls.  Yet as Oul studied the edge of the glare in calm, dreamlike focus, his gaze caught a frostbitten winter rose divided precisely by the beam.  The illuminated petals looked flat and lifeless, pinned like dried specimens upon a page, while the unlit half was severed from existence altogether.  Before the amulet woke, that flower had glimmered softly in the vapor, holding the quiet promise of dormant roots beneath the snow.  Oul understood then that the High Index had shattered because perfection left no room for change, and that hiding inside artificial clarity would only turn him into another brittle relic.  What he truly needed was not a cage of old answers, but the courage to make friends with uncertainty. 

Raising one scarred paw, the guardian brought his weight down squarely upon the cultist's pendant until the brass buckled and the starlight lens cracked against the stone.  The harsh golden wall collapsed at once, and silver mist rushed back over the rose, softening the world into gentle, shifting twilight.  Still suspended in tranquil, misty awareness, Oul parted his jaws and began interiorizing exteriors at last, drawing a slow, deep breath of the night air.  He welcomed the entire frozen garden in mist into his mind, letting ghostly vapor coil around his bright mental shelves and wrap his rigid memories in cool, unmeasured grace.  For the first time since the heavens broke, the wildness eased from his spine, and he no longer felt the frantic urge to pace the gates or demand a map of tomorrow.  Curling his silver tail over his nose, the keeper of both clear truth and wandering cloud settled down upon the snow simply to wait, content to discover whatever unwritten bloom the thaw might someday bring.
