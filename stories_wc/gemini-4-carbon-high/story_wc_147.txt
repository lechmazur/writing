Kael breaks the black surface of the moon pool and drags his heavy acoustic beacon onto the flooded marble steps of a forgotten underwater temple.  Beyond the sunken portico, a trench landslide has pinned the research submersible Vesper beneath a granite overhang, trapping four crewmates inside a groaning hull.  As the designated distress signal sender for the dive, Kael has ejected through the forward lock to haul their emergency transponder past the rock lip into clear vertical water.  Instead of open abyss, his ascent carries him into an ancient pocket of breathable air preserved beneath a dome of basalt and salt.  Fluted columns draped in pale bioluminescent polyps and silvered mosaics rise around him in lonesome splendor, unseen by living eyes for millennia. 

Yet the sanctuary is already failing as time runs short for both the ruin and his crew.  The tremor that buried the Vesper has split a jagged seam across the vaulted ceiling, sending ribbons of black seawater cascading down the walls while the air pocket steadily shrinks.  Testing the chamber atmosphere on his wrist sensor, Kael unlatches his faceplate and pulls off his heavy mitts to save his last minutes of reserve oxygen for the wait after transmission.  He kneels beside the beacon on the dry central dais and confronts a worse crisis.  During his scrape beneath the collapsed arch, the spool housing cracked and whipped the slender copper transducer filament into a knotted tangle around his steel harness clips.  If a single crossed loop remains when high voltage fires through the line, the coil will fuse and no call will ever reach the surface fleet.  Cold and rising carbon dioxide trigger immediate panic; his shivering fingers jerk hard at the wire, cinching the knot tighter until the fragile strand nearly snaps. 

Forcing his hands still before he severs their sole lifeline, Kael notices what fills the shrine once his ragged gasps subside: a gathered hush.  Shaped by ancient architects whose porous limestone coffers drink every murmur of sea and stone, centuries of unbroken quiet hang inside the cupola with physical weight.  In the sudden absence of thrumming engines and hissing valves, that gathered hush touches memories long buried beneath years of abyssal service.  With aching clarity he recalls warm dust drifting across his childhood floor and the gentle cadence of his late mother humming at dusk.  Grief over how swiftly human voices and sunlit hours vanish into silence had first led Kael into deep ocean acoustics, driven by an enduring need to harness the ephemeral.  Every brief cry caught from the trenches and every dying echo preserved on his reels has been an act of rescue against oblivion. 

His helmet lamp sweeps the dry marble altar beside him, carved with reliefs of waves swallowing a terraced island.  Cradled in a niche at its crown rests a faceted crystal perfume vial, stoppered with wax and gold wire by drowned priests who shared his exact devotion to preserving transient beauty.  Only solid carved quartz could keep the volatile soul of coastal blossoms sealed intact beneath the sea across thirty centuries.  Kael realizes that the concussive blast of his emergency sonar pulse will collapse the fractured dome overhead and crush this altar forever.  Unwilling to let their fragile offering vanish into salt and rubble, he lifts the crystal perfume vial gently from the stone and seals it inside his padded chest pouch.  A faint breath of cedar and wild neroli clinging to the ancient wax drifts across his face, awakening a calm recollection of summer rain that steadies his racing heart. 

Icy water surges past his knees now, and a sharp crack from the ceiling warns that final seconds remain.  Every animal nerve screams at him to rip the wire loose, gasp the thinning air, and slam the transmit switch.  Instead, anchored by the stillness of the vault, Kael proceeds by overriding instinct.  Refusing to pull against the bind, he feeds slack back into the tangled coils and guides his numb fingers with slow deliberation while dark brine climbs to his waist.  Breathing evenly, he manages to unsnarl loop after delicate loop until the entire copper filament lies smooth and unbroken across his palm. 

He pays the weighted emitter down through the glowing moon pool until it hangs clear beneath the outer granite overhang.  Pulling on his mitts and locking his faceplate shut on reserve oxygen, Kael rests one hand over the saved vial at his chest and looks once more upon the doomed sanctuary.  Then he closes the circuit, sending a thunderous distress call rising toward the light as the ancient hush shatters and the dark sea reclaims the temple.
