Four months after receiving the donor heart, Master Lin climbed the swaying hemp ladder into the floating rock tea house.  Beneath the porous lodestone crag, silver gorge clouds churned in slow currents, swallowing the iron anchor chains until the cedar pavilion seemed to drift untethered through pale sky.  Lin paused beside the low rail, pressing fingers stained with dark indigo against his healing sternum where a borrowed pulse beat with steady warmth.  For forty years as a watchful dye master in the river workshops below, he had observed life from the margins while boiling madder root and walnut hulls into permanent colors, never once becoming the subject of local talk.  Yet during his convalescence, dozens of weathered travelers had visited the clinic ward just to weep in gratitude for Soren, the young cliff courier whose sudden fall had saved Lin, trading luminous accounts of the boy until Lin climbed up through the mist to learn why some people magnetize stories. 

Inside the open pavilion, cool valley vapor curled over woven mats and mingled with fragrant steam rising from iron kettles.  Scattered patrons sat isolated in pockets of pearl gray haze, each wrapped in damp wool and silent fatigue while waiting for the gorge winds to settle.  Old Fen, the proprietor who tended the charcoal braziers, recognized the surgical stiffness in the shoulders of her guest and understood at once whose rhythm carried him up the cliffs.  Without ceremony, she poured pale oolong into a porcelain cup and set down the small cedar box Soren had kept behind her counter.  Expecting a journal of daring rescues or rare maps, Lin lifted the lid and found only a preserved blue morpho wing resting upon black felt. 

In the diffused cloud light, the specimen looked disappointingly drab, the color of dried mud.  From decades of studying pigments, Lin knew the paradox of that fragile membrane better than anyone.  Unlike saffron or lapis, the scales held zero true dye; grind them in a stone mortar, and every trace of azure vanished into gray dust.  Old guild manuals called such microscopic ridge refraction iridescent caution, nature protecting delicate tissue behind plain brown shadow until angled precisely toward a direct ray.  Lin had practiced his own fearful version of iridescent caution ever since waking from surgery, rationing every breath and keeping polite distance from neighbors lest human closeness press too hard upon his mended chest. 

Seeing his confusion, Fen gestured toward faint notches carved across the damp pine floorboards and remarked that Soren never raised his voice or boasted of cliff crossings.  Instead, as the buoyant rock rotated slowly on its tethers and thin shafts of afternoon gold pierced gaps in the high canyon peaks, the courier had simply changed seats with the light.  Intrigued, Lin picked up his teacup and the open box, deciding to reconstruct the habit of the dead boy by tracking sun trails across the misty deck.  A narrow ribbon of amber broke through the cloud bank and crept toward a corner table where a scarred rigger stared grimly at his hands.  Stepping softly to the notched plank beside that table, Lin angled the cedar box into the stray beam. 

The change was quietly explosive, shattering the gray stillness without a sound as dull brown ignited into fierce, electric sapphire.  Refracted brilliance spilled upward through drifting kettle vapor to illuminate the weathered knuckles of the rigger and the pale rim of his empty bowl in shimmering blue.  Startled from his stupor, the rigger exhaled in wonder and began telling Lin how Soren had once shared a freezing cliff ledge with him during a cable snap, tilting that exact wing in a fleeting cloud break to steady the terrified crew until dawn. 

When the boulder pivoted further and the golden shaft glided onward, Lin followed its path toward an elderly potter warming stiff wrists near the rail.  Again the tilted scales flashed across the mist, and again a solitary stranger leaned close, eager to recall not grand heroics, but how Soren had stepped into her cold corner, poured hot tea, and asked patiently about her kiln glazes when nobody else looked twice.  Listening while valley clouds drifted luminous around the suspended stone, the watchful dye master finally understood the pulse beating inside his chest.  Soren had never hoarded color like boiled pigment in a sealed vat, nor demanded center stage; he had magnetized stories simply by carrying fragile wonder into shared light and letting others shine back.  Unclenching his guarded posture, Lin refilled the cup of the potter, tilted the sapphire gleam between them, and asked what colors she hoped to pull from the fire next.
