High above the flooded valley, the glowing viaduct after rain blazed with fierce white luminescence, every wet limestone arch radiating captured storm light into the pale sky.  Aris drifted upside down among spectral spires above the roadway, a solitary gravity defier untethered from the pull of the earth and the crushing weight of mourning.  All around his inverted form, late droplets from the departed storm hung motionless over slick masonry and shining puddles.  His entire reality existed between the drop and the ripple. 

Because mass binds the flow of hours, shedding his physical weight had severed him from ordinary sequence and rendered his solitude periodically eternal.  Whenever a bead of water detached from the stone, time dilated into an endless age, snapping forward by a heartbeat at impact before trapping him inside the next descent.  In his scarred hands he gripped an ancient clay tablet with runes carved deep into its damp ochre surface.  He had unearthed the relic beside his sister before the fever took her, and on the night of her burial he had crossed this bridge and spoken its waking syllables to escape a world that felt intolerably heavy. 

At first, this weightless sanctuary had seemed like pure salvation, a dazzling cathedral of radiant mist where grief could never touch his heels.  Yet the angular symbols on the tablet were no mere prayer for comfort; they formed a relentless mathematical seed designed by forgotten chronomancers.  Feeding upon every hesitation in his mind, those runes spun fractured timelines out of blinding light through procedural generation, assembling phantom bridges and branching colonnades high into the sky.  Upon each newly calculated span stood a translucent echo of Aris enacting some alternate past where he had stayed home, studied medicine, or never loved anyone at all. 

Now the brilliance that once promised freedom burned with a feverish, sterile glare that bleached color and warmth from his memories.  Along the upper reaches of the expanding lattice, veins of ash and bruised violet bled through the incandescent arches as the overburdened stone strained beneath infinite permutations.  Every attempt to drift toward a brighter horizon only instructed the runes to multiply the maze, splitting his spirit thinner with every fugitive thought.  Watching those hollow doubles repeat empty gestures in the waning radiance, Aris understood that his sole hope was to conquer the labyrinth of self before nothing remained of the man who remembered his sister. 

He studied the shifting grooves on the clay and recognized the cold logic governing his prison.  An architecture built through procedural generation possesses no true exit along its frontier, because moving outward merely supplies fresh variables to the engine.  To collapse the equation, he had to retrace every flight from sorrow in exact reverse order, starving the seed back to its single mortal origin down on the bridge.  Pushing off from a luminous keystone, Aris turned away from the dazzling, unborn spires and glided backward into the nearest phantom arch. 

As he passed through the pale double who had chosen forgetfulness, a sharp ache pierced his chest, and that entire branch of fractured timelines dimmed from searing gold to dying ember before dissolving into smoke.  Instantly, forgotten gravity tugged at his ribs, pulling him lower toward the slick roadway below.  Breath by ragged breath, he continued to retrace his evasions down vertical pillars and under vanishing vaults, reclaiming every shard of pain he had refused to carry.  With each collapsed illusion, the manic white noon around him settled into a solemn, darkening brightness, like a fierce flare surrendering to twilight so honest shadows could restore depth to the stone. 

His grace as a gravity defier bled away with the false light until his boots struck the true parapet with a jarring, welcome thud.  Knees buckling beneath the full measure of his grief, Aris knelt beside a dark puddle where a lone bead of rainwater still hovered.  In his trembling palms, the ancient clay tablet with runes drank the damp air, its exhausted symbols smoothing into plain brown mud as the last phantom arch faded into bruised amber dusk.  Only the real glowing viaduct after rain remained, its phosphorescence softening now into quiet silver ribbons along the wet mortar.  Whole at last, Aris bowed his head and wept openly, welcoming the anchor of the earth.  The long vigil between the drop and the ripple ended as the bead finally fell, broke the still surface of the puddle, and released him into the gathering night.
