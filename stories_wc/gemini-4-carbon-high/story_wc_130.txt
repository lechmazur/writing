High overhead, a jagged sinkhole admits the night sky into a cavern orchard nourished by dripping stalactites.  Mineral water beads along the limestone ceiling and patters onto glossy pear leaves, feeding roots anchored in dark silt.  By day, village harvesters haul baskets among the gnarled trunks, seeing Oren only as a mild caretaker.  None of them suspect that the man pruning deadwood is a secretive conjurer who coaxes luminous echoes from thin air.  Because the villagers above dread unseen arts, Oren bolts the iron gate at dusk and begins his true work only after the orchard grows silent beneath the moon. 

In that hushed stillness, the shallow pools flatten into mirrors, undisturbed by footsteps or falling fruit.  Four years have passed since Oren's wife, Clara, descended into the lower aquifers to divert a failing spring and vanished behind a sudden rockfall.  Diggers uncovered neither her body nor her brass lantern, only cold fissures leading toward miles of unmapped caverns.  Left without a grave to mourn or proof of survival, Oren inhabits an ache of ambiguous loss where grief and expectation endlessly collide.  Formless sorrow threatens to crush him, driving him to understand the mathematics of emotion before uncertainty hollows his mind.  He believes that if longing and devotion obey precise geometric ratios, he can chart their angles and build an inner framework strong enough to bear not knowing. 

Unlike the ancient summoners who tore loud rifts with iron and fire, Oren is gently inventive in his solitary craft.  He fashions quiet, harmless instruments from grafting twine, cave water, and humble curiosities bartered at the village market.  Tonight, he unwraps a velvet cloth holding a taxidermist's glass eye pair that he traded two crates of sweet pears to obtain.  Crafted for a preserved mountain fox, the amber spheres were meant to counterfeit life inside a dead husk, an illusion of finality Oren quietly refuses.  Yet their twin convex curves possess the exact focal symmetry needed to split a single lunar beam into measurable vectors.  Carrying the lenses to the heart of the grove, he kneels to work his calculation via the chipped patterns on a mosaic stepping stone that Clara laid beside their oldest tree. 

Winters and falling pebbles have knocked cobalt tiles loose from the mortar, leaving angular recesses now brimming with clear stalactite water.  Oren balances the two glass eyes upright on opposite rims of the stone, facing each other across the broken ceramic grid.  Moonlight pours straight down through the sinkhole, striking the painted pupils and casting twin fans of amber radiance across the wet tiles.  Breathing slowly, Oren murmurs a binding cadence and draws a filament of faint silver mist from his chest, giving visible form to four years of unanswered longing.  During earlier attempts, he tried forcing his conjured mist to fill the missing ceramic fragments, hoping to restore the stone to an unbroken circle.  Every time he denied the fractures, the equation collapsed into erratic sparks, because real sorrow cannot be subtracted to zero. 

Watching a heavy drop fall from a stalactite overhead and ripple one flooded recess, Oren suddenly perceives the true theorem waiting in the clay.  The trapezoidal gaps left by vanished tiles are not errors to erase; they are deliberate notches cut for a matching wedge.  With a slight turn of his wrists, he angles the glass irises so the amber refractions and the silver threads of his conjured memory dovetail inside the jagged grooves.  Like fine joinery shaped by a master carpenter, the sharp edges of absence and the warm lines of remembrance lock together under tension.  Instead of shattering, the luminous lattice holds fast across the scarred mortar, humming with quiet equilibrium. 

Where the twin sightlines intersect, a slender geometric spire rises above the water and leans steadily toward the deep southern aquifers.  The glass lenses cannot tell him whether Clara still walks some distant subterranean shore or endures only in the current nourishing their grove.  Yet the balanced proof reveals a truth no doubt could undo: love exerts measurable force even across an unfinished equation.  Touched by gentle warmth from the interlocked light, a dormant graft on the nearest pear bough swells into an ivory blossom beneath tons of dark stone.  Before the moon slips past the sinkhole, Oren releases the mist, wraps the amber eyes, and rests his palm against the damp mosaic.  Tomorrow he will unlock the gate with steady hands, sustained by a buried optimism as stubborn as roots drawing sweetness from the dark.
