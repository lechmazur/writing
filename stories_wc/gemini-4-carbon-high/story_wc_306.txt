Their contract begins only after the campfire's last spark dies in the sheltered cove.  For hours they have shared that small driftwood heat in silence, allowing darkness to shield Warden Maren from the indignity of being seen afraid.  Now, with nothing left between them but cold ash, she rises and leads him up the spiraling granite steps of the breezy lighthouse at dawn.  As a conscience for hire, Soren is summoned only when a commander's duty grows too heavy to bear alone yet too dangerous to confess to subordinates.  Half a mile out in the pale strait, three battered ketches from the blighted outer isles strain at their anchors, awaiting her sunrise signal.  Maritime law demands the crimson shutter, which will order the harbor batteries to sink any quarantine runners before they reach the tide line. 

Out on the high iron gallery, salt wind whips across Maren's stiff collar and brass epaulets.  She grips the wet railing with locked elbows, guarding the tremor in her hands behind thirty years of naval composure.  Soren does not press her to weep or plead, preserving the quiet space of protected vulnerability that allows proud rulers to examine their own hearts without humiliation.  Instead, he draws a weathered gray wool mantle from his satchel and settles it over her shoulders.  Along this coast, those who pay his fee call the garment the cloak of doubt.  Practical as much as symbolic, its drab folds hide her insignia from any watchman squinting up from the jetty, granting her a brief sanctuary where uncertainty is neither cowardice nor treason. 

Even beneath that shelter, direct interrogation would only harden her defenses.  Soren therefore conducts his inquiry by deciphering wind chime selections hung against the morning gale.  He unrolls a suede case holding twenty chimes crafted from coastal salvage: manacle iron, brass shell casings, cradle cedar, smooth sea glass, and a peculiar hollow mirror shard blown with an empty pocket of air between two thin silvered panes.  Stepping back, he gestures for Maren to suspend any five along the taut wire strung between the gallery stanchions.  Without speaking, she chooses two heavy iron bars and two brass casings that immediately strike loud, martial notes in the stiff breeze.  Yet with her final choice, her gloved fingers pass over a third piece of iron and fasten the fragile glass pendant at the far end of the line.  Too light to clang, it offers only a faint click against the stanchion, catching the first amber light in twin, slightly parted reflections. 

Reading that quiet contradiction, Soren leans against the rail and adopts the sincerely insincere manner that is his sharpest instrument.  "Four voices of iron and brass," he remarks with transparently theatrical admiration, "a splendid chorus for a warden who knows that shelling starving families keeps the harbor ledgers tidy."  His exaggerated praise of cruelty is deliberately hollow, delivered with warm eyes that invite her to dismantle the argument he pretends to champion.  Spared the shame of pleading for mercy, Maren straightens and rebukes him as though correcting a junior officer's sloppy tactics.  "The ledgers gain nothing from drowned children," she replies, her voice steadying against the wind, "not when the old salt wharf beyond the breakwater can isolate all three crews under armed watch and physicians."  Soren inclines his head, understanding at last why she emptied her purse for this vigil.  She did not hire him to excuse dereliction of duty, but to welcome nuance into certainty where the statute books offered only blind absolutes. 

Eastward, the horizon flares with the first rim of the sun, while behind them the great oil lamp burns steadily inside its towering glass lens.  Within the lantern room wait the shutter levers, built to lock into solid crimson or clear white, yet capable of being worked by hand in the slow alternating flash that summons escort cutters without gunfire.  Maren lifts the gray wool mantle from her shoulders and folds it neatly into Soren's arms, reclaiming her visible rank with steady posture.  Turning toward the door, she pauses by the singing wire and reaches up to unhook the hollow mirror shard so the strengthening gusts will not shatter it against the iron stanchion.  Holding the delicate glass to the horizon, she studies her own divided reflection overlapping faintly with the distant sails on the tide.  Then she places the unbroken chime back into his suede case, steps inside to grip both levers at once, and begins working the shutters in a measured, merciful rhythm.
