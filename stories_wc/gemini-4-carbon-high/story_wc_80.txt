After the drawbridge rises, the heavy counterweights lock into silence and seal Upper Carrow behind its granite seawall for the night.  At the same bell, the canal sluices grind shut beneath the piers, halting the fierce outflow that churns the outer basin into opaque white foam from dawn until dusk.  Gareth stands alone on the seaward breakwater beside a hooded brazier of incandescent anthracite coals, watching the turbulence slacken until the lagoon turns as clear and still as black glass.  As the firm shore surveyor of Carrow, he has resisted three council directives to blast this outer apron clean, proving with relentless soundings and core samples that the sunken masonry beyond the pier breaks the winter gales.  Two fathoms below his moored skiff, bathed in the steady golden cone of his underwater lamp, rests the submerged pavilion. 

Twenty years ago, before the coastal shelf sheared into the sound, those drowned limestone colonnades formed the civic rotunda where young artisans displayed masterworks and couples signed betrothal ledgers beneath a cedar dome.  Now the citizens on the bluff survive through disciplined amnesia, skirting stone stairs that end in midair and speaking briskly of upland trade without ever glancing toward the harbor.  Gareth remains stoically sensitive during his daytime inspections, betraying no emotion in council chambers while registering every tremor in an aging voice and every hairline fracture along the cliff.  To his trained eye, the empty beam sockets underwater and the careful silences in the streets above compose one interlaced absence, an unseen lattice of missing timber and unspoken grief that holds the town upright. 

Officially, he lingers outside the gates to maintain the deep pilings of the rotunda, which anchor the drawbridge foundation against tidal scour.  When the lower terrace collapsed, exhibition chests and iron instruments tumbled from the gallery and wedged tightly into settling cracks along the base of those pillars.  Swelling as it corrodes in saltwater, the trapped metal threatens to cleave the supporting stone from within unless someone clears the joints.  Yet a fiercer, quieter devotion drives Gareth through the freezing dark: a vow to become the archeologist of abandoned dreams, recovering the unbuilt futures his neighbors refuse to mourn.  He sheds his heavy coat beside the glowing coals, buckles a lead belt over his woolens, grips his forged pry bar, and slips without a splash down the weighted lamp line.  Gliding through the luminous hush of the submerged pavilion, he passes pale anemones blooming across marble benches until he reaches a jagged fissure in the eastern footing.  He hooks his boots around a fluted column, wedges his iron bar behind a blistered mass of rust and barnacles, and strains with burning lungs to wrest the relic from the stone.  With a muted crunch, the calcified seam surrenders its prize in a rising cloud of silver silt. 

Breaking the surface with one controlled breath, Gareth hauls the dripping bundle onto the granite apron and kneels beside the fierce, silent heat of the brazier.  Using a brass scraper, he flakes away lime and shell from a warped iron frame until an engraved copper drafting plate catches the ember glow.  Etched in fine lines across the copper are elevations for a headland conservatory fitted with bronze pipes tuned to sing with the incoming tide, signed by Elena Voss.  Gareth pictures gray Mistress Voss in the upper customhouse, who tallies salt casks each morning with steady hands and never lifts her gaze toward the barren headland.  Drying his fingers on canvas, he reaches into his survey satchel and unclasps his antique traveling inkwell, a walnut cube cased in brass whose threaded spring stopper kept carbon ink safe aboard pitching coastal cutters a century ago. 

He dips a steel nib and inscribes a strip of oiled vellum with the name of the architect, the harmonic ratios of her sea pipes, and the exact coordinates of her drowned design.  Rolling the parchment inside a thick glass core vial, he seals the stopper with hot pitch, mixes a trough of quick hydraulic lime mortar, and dives once more into the golden stillness below.  At the eastern pillar, he packs fresh mortar deep into the cleared scar, presses the sealed vial into its center, and trowels the joint flush with the ancient stone.  By placing notes in foundation repairs, he swaps destructive rust for imperishable witness, ensuring that the pilings bracing Carrow hold the true measure of all it surrendered.  Surfacing beneath paling stars, Gareth locks the copper plate inside his dry breakwater vault and warms his raw palms over the last red embers, ready to meet the waking town when the chains descend.
