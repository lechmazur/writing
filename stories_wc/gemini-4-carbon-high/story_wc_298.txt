Julian hurried downward within the glow of corridors left half lit by the nightly oil rationing.  He had less than an hour before the subterranean conduits went dark and the cold sealed unbound fates forever.  Against his chest, wrapped in linen, lay a burned tarot card that had once belonged to Linnea.  Terrified by a dark prophecy of lifelong solitude, the young glassblower had cast her Temperance arcana into a furnace, hoping to destroy her future before it could hurt her.  Instead, the flames had torn jagged holes in her soul, leaving her upstairs in a garret bed, mute and leaking warmth like a cracked lamp. 

Every licensed merchant in the upper city dealt in bartered destinies, buying years of vigor from the desperate poor to sell to aging nobles.  Those brokers had laughed Julian out of their parlors, insisting that scorched paper held no trade value and a ruined spirit ought to be left to fade.  Yet Julian was a reckless idealist who refused to accept a ledger that priced human lives by unbroken edges.  He had spent his youth learning forbidden craft for one purpose alone: to patch holes in the soul, regardless of what the mending demanded from him. 

At the bottom of the stairs lay the catacomb orchard, an ancient vault where tree roots snake between bones in silent tiers.  Blind white fig trees fed upon the settled memories of the dead, distilling forgotten mercies and quiet endurance into pale sap.  Beside a stone altar stood Maren, the lone subterranean broker willing to witness desperate trades.  Without hesitation, Julian placed the blackened remnant of Temperance upon the slab beside his own pristine birth card, The Fool, bright with gold leaf and decades of untamed travel. 

"You cannot pour an ocean into ash," Maren warned, eyeing the flaking char.  "Fire strips all structure from an arcana, and even if the graft holds, binding your boundless horizon to her broken span will cage you in this city with half your years consumed," she added.  Julian met her gaze with dusky resolve, feeling no false triumph, only a quiet certainty suited to the gathering twilight.  A grand voyage meant nothing to him if its price was abandoning a friend to hollow silence. 

Because naked ash could not anchor a living fate, Julian set to work rebuilding the lost architecture of the card by inlaying meaning everywhere.  Nothing could remain random dust or blank space.  He tapped resin from a pale root curling through the collarbone of a buried mason, painting the sticky tear along the torn border to signify steadfast shelter.  He crushed dried orchard figs with powdered bone from two skulls resting cheek to cheek, pressing that paste into the missing center so companionship would replace the dread of solitude.  With a silver stylus, he etched microscopic waves for steady breath, an anvil for daily craft, and seven stars for returning memory, turning every millimeter of soot into deliberate testimony. 

At his nod, Maren drew an obsidian knife across The Fool to release the bartered essence.  A blinding torrent of golden wind erupted from the slit, thrashing violently against the vault ceiling as the spirit of his unlived adventures fought to escape so narrow a vessel.  Maren reached for heavy iron clamps to pin the unruly radiance down, though iron always scarred a healing mind.  Julian knocked the cruel metal aside, remaining fiercely gentle even as the searing light blistered his bare palms.  Shielding the fragile char from any rough shock, he coaxed the bucking current downward with unyielding tenderness and wove damp, slender rootlets across the card to trammel the wild brilliance.  Thread by living thread, that delicate net caught and restrained his own wide future, binding its warmth fast into the symbolic lattice he had carved. 

When the last flare subsided, Julian gasped as an invisible weight settled inside his ribs.  His longing for distant seas suddenly felt muted and small, tethered forever to these familiar cobbled wards, while his own faded Fool card lay dull as pewter.  Yet beneath his scorched fingers, Temperance pulsed whole and luminous, veined with pale orchard root and steady breath.  Out in the archway, the oil sconces sputtered toward their final moments of twilight.  Cradling the restored destiny against his heart, Julian climbed back within the glow of corridors still half lit, content to walk a narrower life so Linnea could wake unbroken.
