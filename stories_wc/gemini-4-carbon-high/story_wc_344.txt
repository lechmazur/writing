Far beyond the lowland coast, Oren stepped through the windward arch into a frozen library above clouds.  Below the stone terrace lay an unbroken sea of vapor, holding the vaults under the soft hush of a distant morning.  Forty years along the salt flats had worn his grief smooth and pale as driftwood, stripping away the splinters of youthful pride.  Rime glazed the tiers of folios, preserving the silence just as he and Maren had left it.  By trade, Oren was a lunar-eclipse locksmith, a craftsman whose photic tumblers yielded only to the copper glow of an eclipsed moon. 

In his youth, he had worshiped the solitary click of release, believing any barrier surrendered to a clever hand in the dark.  Maren, the archive's horologist, had lived instead by continuous motion, tending the clockwork engines that warmed the high reading rooms.  Together they had built the spire's regulator, pairing his shadow seal with her train of tracking wheels.  When winter jammed a shutter forty years ago, Oren grew impatient and forced his own lock out of season.  The wrenching torque sheared Maren's escapement, stalling the mirrors at a ruinous angle and driving every scholar beneath the cloud deck.  Maren was gone now, claimed quietly by age in the harbor towns below, yet her memory remained gentle as sea glass.  Oren had climbed the iced stairs one last time, not to conquer another vault, but to learn the rhythm of repair. 

He knew tumblers and springs, not the patient ratios of horology, and the next eclipse lay two winters away.  Under tons of frost, the vaulted skylight would collapse long before earth's shadow touched the moon again.  Yet Maren had worked beside the spire's star diviners, charting human returns with the same care she gave to pendulums.  Only Maren would think to guide a stubborn smith through scraps of prophecy hidden in a library's corner, tucked behind bleached cedar molding where frost never crept.  Kneeling on the cold slate, Oren pieced the brittle vellum strips together until their faded verses formed a single schematic.  One fragment foretold an aging penitent arriving at daybreak, while the next translated stellar cycles into tooth counts for the broken drive.  The final scrap revealed an optical truth he had overlooked all his life: sunlight skimming the planet's misty rim at dawn carries the exact copper refraction of an eclipse.  Wrapped in oiled flannel beside the parchment lay a clockmaker's gear assortment, its brass pinions and spur wheels resting like relics washed ashore from a kinder decade. 

Carrying the flannel roll against his chest, Oren walked into the spine of the archive, a soaring hall of mirrors.  Because the tall panes stood jammed toward the eastern lancets, the rising light multiplied across frost and silver, rendering the gallery hauntingly bright.  For eyes trained to work in shadow, the brilliance felt spectral and merciless.  Pale reflections rippled down the corridor, surrounding him with bleached ghosts of the man who had once mistaken force for mastery.  Worse still, that white glare would blind the regulator's photic lock if a single stray reflection struck its sensor.  Stepping to the central pedestal, Oren draped his weathered wool coat over the bronze casing to tent out the mirrored dazzle.  Only a narrow brass cylinder protruded beneath the cloth, aimed straight between two pillars toward the distant cloud horizon.  Bracing his scarred thumbs against the rimed threads, he twisted hard to uncap the sight tube just as the sun's edge grazed the vapor sea. 

Filtered through leagues of lowland mist, a thin needle of copper light slid down the dark bore and kissed the internal prism.  Beneath the coat, his old eclipse tumblers sighed open with a familiar metallic whisper.  Once, that single sound would have meant his task was finished, yet today it was merely an invitation to begin.  Reaching under the wool by touch alone, he lifted each wheel from the assortment and eased it onto Maren's waiting arbors, feeling for balance rather than leverage.  Twice his stiff fingers slipped in the cold, and twice he breathed slow warmth over the teeth until pinion met spur without resistance.  When the new escapement finally caught, a steady, unhurried pulse began to beat against his palms.  Across the gallery, the great mirrors pivoted a fraction of a degree, softening the harsh glare into an amber wash that coaxed a clear drop of meltwater down the nearest pane.  Oren kept his hand on the warming housing and listened to the quiet cadence filling the stacks, content at last to stay and keep time.
