Oren, a zen-minded potter, stands beside the stone hut and studies the bruised horizon.  Around him lies an abandoned beekeeping oasis in a scorched wasteland, a quiet ring of silver sage and dry cisterns surviving amid leagues of vitrified salt.  Inside the hut, a small test firing of local silt glows on the grate, and Oren watches closely as chimney smoke flattens against the slate roof under the barometric press of an approaching storm.  No rain will fall from those violet clouds, only the grinding static of fractured timelines tearing the desert into mismatched centuries.  Oren walked forty miles across splintered eras to understand why some places resist change when granite fortresses shatter like poorly wedged porcelain. 

He moves down the terraced path with brisk, unsentimental care, stepping around the papery husks of worker bees drifted inside a dry sluice.  Pity is merely uncentered weight on the wheel; Oren offers the dead insects neither tears nor prayers, only a clean sweep of his sleeve to clear grit from their resting ledge.  Before him sit thirty horizontal tube hives coiled from red earthenware, vessels shaped by a kindred hand long before the sky broke.  Beyond the low tamarisk windbreak, the salt flats flicker violently between primordial sea beds and smoking craters, yet inside this apiary the light remains whole.  Every potter knows that rigid clay cracks in the kiln while centered earth endures the flame, so Oren searches the courtyard for whatever quiet discipline centered this ground. 

Along the perimeter wall, shallow notches mark the limestone coping in deliberate seasonal arcs.  Because foraging bees navigate the polarized sky, the vanished keeper had oriented every hive entrance and shade screen by following sun patterns from winter solstice to summer zenith.  Oren traces that solar geometry westward until he reaches the final pillar, where old lime mortar has finally crumbled away.  Half buried in the alkali dust below lies a curved pane of faded lantern glass, bleached from rich honey amber to pale straw by decades of relentless glare.  Without that protective filter in the western notch, raw wasteland light strikes the outermost earthenware hive, where a fresh hairline fracture now creeps along its curved flank. 

Oren lifts the faded lantern glass, wipes its surface clean with his thumb, and raises it to his eye by following sun patterns toward the sinking red disk.  Through the weathered silicate, the blinding desert sheen softens, and the invisible seams of the fractured timelines leap into crisp relief.  Outside the tamarisks, jagged minutes shear against one another like broken stoneware shards.  Inside the courtyard, however, dozens of luminous yesterdays overlap like layers of translucent celadon glaze, holding phantom blossoms and spectral golden swarms in delicate suspension.  Even more astonishing, the frayed strands of duration lean visibly inward toward the worn footpath of the keeper and the smooth ledges where patient hands once lifted honeycomb without crushing a single wing.  Just as desert stems bend toward dawn, the broken threads of reality here are phototropic to kindness, curving away from violence to root themselves around decades of quiet stewardship. 

Behind him, the air grows dense as chimney smoke flattens completely into the courtyard dust, heralding the leading edge of the temporal squall.  Wind hisses across the salt pan, and the hairline crack on the unshaded hive widens with a sharp click, threatening to unravel the western boundary of the sanctuary.  Without haste or panic, Oren uncorks his canteen, wets a handful of sifted oasis clay from his pouch, and wedges it between his palms until every air pocket yields.  With firm, economical strokes, he presses smooth slip deep into the split earthenware, sealing the wound as cleanly as a surgeon closing skin.  Using the remaining coil of damp clay, he seats the faded lantern glass upright inside its limestone solstice notch, aligning its curve precisely with the low autumn glare.  Filtered gold washes across the mended cylinder, and beneath the calloused palm of the potter, the cold terracotta begins to thrum. 

Across twenty overlapping autumns, unseen winter clusters gather behind his fresh patch, beating thousands of wings in steady, warming unison.  The temporal gale roars past the tamarisks, pelting the salt flats with phantom hail, yet inside the courtyard not a single dry sage leaf trembles.  Oren neither pries open the hive for relic wax nor pockets the glass for his kiln, knowing that true craft leaves a mended vessel where it works.  Sitting still beside the warm clay, he breathes with that quiet thrum and finally understands how fragile places outlast the fire: never through hardness, which invites the break, but through unsentimental tenderness that gives fractured time a light to grow toward.
