Far beneath the lit promenades of New Pelagia, Callum worked alone in a forgotten library inside a sunken city.  Above its vaulted ceiling of thick quartz and green bronze, thirty fathoms of ocean pressed down through swaying forests of kelp.  Municipal scholars had abandoned this deep rotunda generations ago for bright digital towers above, leaving behind brittle vellum registries warped by ancient brine.  As a logical page indexer, Callum had requested this solitary post to trace honest origins beneath a century of patrician myth.  Official histories claimed the ruling council alone had engineered the great pressure dome when the coastal shelf collapsed, reducing the caisson crews who worked the floodgates to nameless refugees.  Callum trusted sequence more than monuments, knowing that erased labor always left mathematical scars in municipal records. 

For three months he had mapped the Founding Ledger, cataloging six chapters where the stamped folio numbers leaped forward by irregular amounts.  Earlier curators blamed those gaps on an antique ink stamp preserved in a velvet tray beside his desk, insisting that seawater had jammed its six brass numbering wheels during the subsidence.  If the gears had merely slipped, then no pages were truly missing and the council account stood complete.  Callum doubted that convenient excuse because stripped teeth produce repeating factors, whereas his tabulated jumps of three, seven, two, eight, five, and four followed no mechanical cycle.  Yet under the harsh glare of retrofitted lamps and the ceaseless tremor of the seabed turbines, the tarnished tool had yielded no further proof. 

Everything shifted during the blackout.  Midway through the afternoon watch, a main conduit failed in the thermal spires, and every artificial fixture along the galleries snapped dark.  Heavy pressure doors thudded shut at the top of the access stair, and for the first time in living memory the colossal circulation pumps beneath the floor spun down to silent standby.  Into the chamber poured a vast, reclaimed stillness, as though the granite rotunda had finally shed the nervous pulse of the metropolis and returned to the patience of the seafloor.  Weak sunlight from the distant waves filtered through drifting fronds overhead, scattering pale emerald coins and fluid shadow across the oak tables. 

Tranquilly perceptive where clerks in the upper tiers would have hammered at the bulkheads, Callum remained seated and let his senses widen inside the hush.  For years the turbine hum had vibrated through his fingertips and blurred every faint acoustic clue in the archive.  Now, as he lifted the antique ink stamp into an angled shaft of marine twilight, he felt no gritty corrosion at all, but heard a crisp spring settle inside its ebony handle.  Turning the frame where overhead lamps had previously washed the metal in flat glare, he spotted a hairline pinhole beside the stop collar that prevented the six wheels from rotating past nine. 

In that instant Callum abandoned conventional archival theory and tested an alternative hypothesis.  What if the first registrar had never lost pages or fought a broken ratchet, but had deliberately encoded six calibration offsets into the pagination of the Founding Ledger itself?  Taking a slender brass pin from his binding kit, he pressed the concealed latch to readjust the tension collar and dialed each wheel beyond nine by the exact skipped counts from his index.  Three notches, then seven, two, eight, five, and four clicked smoothly into alignment through the reclaimed stillness, drawing hidden arcs of metal out from beneath the protective shroud.  Where the numerals ended, mirrored brass letters had been waiting inside the casing for a hundred years. 

Callum moistened his carbon pad with a drop of distilled water, inked the newly aligned faces, and pressed the stamp firmly onto a blank catalog slip.  Six characters emerged in sharp black relief to spell PLINTH, pointing straight toward the lone granite pedestal that held the council memorial at the center of the hall.  Stepping through bands of ocean shadow and wavering gold with dappled resolve, Callum crossed the hushed floor and knelt beside the monument base.  Beneath its rear molding, his fingers traced an unmortared joint that yielded inward, sliding free to reveal a waxed copper cylinder.  Inside lay the original drafts of the dome and the unabridged Harbor Charter, signed by three hundred caisson engineers who had designed the vaults and held the lower locks until every citizen was safe.  Long before the distant turbines groaned back to life, Callum carried the parchment to his table, caught a drifting patch of sea light upon a clean ledger sheet, and began indexing the true first page.
