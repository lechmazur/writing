Hollowed from granite root to clouded peak, the labyrinthine bookstore occupied an entire mountain where thousands of winding tiers held the books of vanished lands.  Its founders engineered the archive for the years after remembering becomes painful, when exiled minds can no longer survive the weight of their own history.  To spare its keepers, the mountain substitutes reality nightly.  Every dusk a low vibration rolls beneath the stone staircases like distant thunder trapped underground, and millions of shelved volumes breathe gentle fictions into the dark.  Until dawn, the cold caverns dissolve into invented orchards, calm harbors, and painless summers where nobody has ever lost a home. 

Corin walked the high spiral shelves through a phantom meadow of tall golden grass that smelled faintly of dry ink.  Once he had been a mercury glass seer, trained to wake vanished truths inside hollow blown globes lined with liquid silver.  When the collective grief grew too sharp to bear, Corin locked his silvered vessels beneath the flagstones and chose to become a simple clerk of quiet aisles.  For thirty years he kept those buried talents sealed away in the dark, fearing the merciless clarity they brought.  Yet decades of borrowed dreams had worn his mind dangerously thin.  Waking each morning in his stone cell, he realized he could no longer picture the pitch of his childhood roof or the curve of his native valley.  Beneath his long numbness gathered a heavy, restless pressure, a silent storm urging him onward to understand the shape of home before he faded completely. 

Returning to his quarters, Corin pried up the hearth slate and lifted out his last mercury glass sphere.  Between its two fragile walls of blown crystal, trapped quicksilver rolled with the dark, muted gleam of storm clouds.  Cradling the cold vessel against his coat, he descended through miles of subterranean stacks.  Around him the nightly substitution shifted from false pastures to illusory marble plazas, though deep inside the mountain rock he could hear the low, persistent rumble of the waking bedrock.  Below the lowest history vaults, where no lamps burned and uncataloged folios sagged with damp, he reached the foundation cavern.  In its center stood the lost language stone, a black basalt marker salvaged from their first valley before anyone learned to forget.  Unlike ink on parchment, stone refused to rewrite itself for comfort, yet floodwater and time had worn its ancient script too shallow for eyes or calloused fingers to read.  Worse, touching either the relic or the mercury glass with bare skin would taint the quicksilver with human pulse and longing, producing only another comforting lie. 

Corin knelt beside the basalt slab and drew from his sleeve a stiff primary plume shed by a cliff swift near the peak vents.  Summoning the discipline of his buried talents, he steadied his breathing until his hands grew as still as the mountain core.  In the old seer craft, truths worn beyond sight or touch demanded a finer intermediary.  Holding only the hollow tip of the quill, he laid the smooth grey vane flat against the lost language stone and drew it slowly across the eroded face.  Where human fingertips felt only polished rock, thousands of microscopic barbs snagged against invisible mineral incisions.  The sleek web parted into tiny ragged teeth, crimped ridges, and unbroken spans of silk, capturing the exact physical cadence of forgotten words that named riverbeds, granite sills, and winter winds.  Before the parted barbs could knit back together, Corin lifted the scored plume and drew it lightly across his mercury glass sphere to seed the waiting quicksilver via the texture of a feather. 

Each whisper of frayed barb and smooth silk sent a precise tremor through the thin crystal wall into the liquid metal within.  Inside the globe, the silver ceased its aimless rolling, darkened with the pent force of hidden thunder, and locked into a sharp reflection that resonated upward through every tier of the mountain.  Fed for the first time in decades by a real memory at its root, the archive drank the vibration into its millions of open pages and remade the night.  Above and around him, the painted plazas tore apart like mist.  In their place rose steep terraces of wet slate, narrow stone thresholds scarred by frost, and a swift black river cutting between sheer crags.  Grief struck Corin immediately, heavy and resonant inside his chest like a storm breaking behind solid rock, yet he did not look away.  Breathing air sharp with cold rain and hearth smoke among the towering bookshelves, he stood upright in the ache, whole at last within the true contours of what had shaped him.
