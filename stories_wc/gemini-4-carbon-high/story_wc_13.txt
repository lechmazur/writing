Mara, a natural history illustrator, paddled her narrow skiff through the rooftop canal system weaving between towers.  Far below the sunlit aqueducts and slate parapets, Old Brannoc had curled into the canyon gorge, resting his granite shoulder against the city's primary waterwheel.  With the great wheel stalled, the elevated channels drained low, granting Mara safe passage only across a giant's nap.  Once Brannoc woke and shifted his bulk, river water would surge back into the skyward locks with enough force to splinter oak hulls against the bridges.  Yet Mara pressed upward toward the highest spires, driven to find the place where echoes are born.  University scholars claimed the city's famous reverberations were dead physics bouncing off wet masonry, but Mara's field sketches of acoustic fauna had convinced her something living nested at the water's crown. 

Her waterproof satchel held charcoal sticks and clean vellum, while a silver infinity symbol pendant rested cool against her collarbone.  Guild illustrators prized such charms because twisting the twin loops allowed an artist to study darting wings by borrowing time from the moments ahead.  Once that stolen span expired, however, the debt fell due immediately, locking the wearer in breathless stillness while ordinary seconds caught up.  As Mara glided beneath a mossy bell arch, Brannoc's slumbering breaths rose through the hollow towers like deep organ chords.  Normally, those low vibrations answered back from the upper weirs in a smooth, settling harmony.  Today, Mara's keen ear caught an interrupted cadence trembling through the shallow water.  Each swelling echo climbed toward peaceful resolution, only to fracture abruptly onto a strained, unresolved note that hung aching in the thin mountain air. 

Digging her oar harder into the silt, Mara followed those fractured rings past terraced dovecotes until she reached a sunken culvert normally hidden six feet underwater.  She ducked low as her skiff scraped beneath the stone lip into a vaulted chamber lined with fluted alabaster nests.  Whispered bells from yesterday still shimmered faintly inside those pearly hollows, proving she had finally entered the sanctuary where every echo began.  At the chamber's center, however, a magnificent bird with translucent throat sacs thrashed inside a poacher's brass drift trap wedged against an iron grate.  When another tremor of Brannoc's breathing rolled upward, Mara watched the captive creature inhale the rumble and part its silver bill.  Air rippled like spun glass around its feathers, revealing an organism whose vocal organ sculpts time itself, pleating passing seconds so dying sounds live twice and drift back across the towers.  Cramped inside twisted brass ribs, the bird choked before finishing its phrase, sending another jarring interrupted cadence shivering down the canal. 

Mara reached instinctively for her sketchbook, then froze as a thunderous groan shook dust from the vaulted ceiling.  Down in the valley, Old Brannoc was waking early, rolling his massive shoulder clear of the river wheel.  White foam erupted through the iron grate beneath the trap, and outside the low culvert entrance a dark wall of canal water gathered speed.  Mara had no room to flee while the bird remained pinned, nor enough ordinary seconds to pry open corroded springs before the chamber flooded to its roof.  Letting her satchel stay on the floorboards, she seized her infinity symbol pendant and twisted both silver loops hard.  Acting by borrowing time, she stepped into an eerie hush where rising droplets hung motionless like beads of amber.  She braced her boots against the slick grate, wedged her iron oar tip into the latch, and hauled backward with tearing effort to uncage the battered creature. 

Brass wire snapped away from bruised plumage just as Mara flung herself back across the gunwale of her skiff.  Instantly the charm's cold debt slammed down, freezing her fingers, lungs, and eyelids in absolute paralysis while the roaring flood surged under the stone lip.  Through locked vision, she saw the freed bird leap onto an alabaster nest, spread wide luminous wings, and drink the thunder of the incoming wave.  Swelling its throat unhindered at last, it poured forth the missing final chord, singing a golden note that folded the chamber's immediate past back over the water.  In a living echo of earlier calm, the violent tide dropped flat for five miraculous heartbeats, gliding Mara's skiff safely out beneath the arch before surging upward behind her.  When warmth returned to Mara's hands high among the ringing spires, she pulled out a fresh sheet of vellum and began drawing from memory as pure, resolved echoes danced overhead.
