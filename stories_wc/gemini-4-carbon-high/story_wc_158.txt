Inside a gargantuan shell wedged at the rim of the drowned crater, Oren lived as an insomniac poet who had not closed his eyes since the sky stopped burning.  Like all tide wardens of Pelagis, he aged with the glacial patience of deep coral, yet waking memory kept him pacing the luminous spirals of pearl every night.  Miles of arched chambers rose above the turquoise lagoon, cooling the harsh starlight into green dusk where tiny silver fish darted through flooded ribs.  Whenever he tried to rest, his mind replayed the ancient orbital siege that had cracked the high spire overhead and seeded the surrounding bay with sleeping weapons.  Rather than surrender to nightmares, he spent the quiet hours composing gentle verses that drifted from the mouth of his sanctuary out over the perilous water. 

Beneath the calm ripples lay hundreds of acoustic mines and rusted turrets left by two vanished fleets, each machine still armed to detonate at any sharp sound.  No generals had survived to sign a treaty, so Oren cultivated a living truce across the reef, tending what he called the blossom armistice.  Native glass orchids clung to the sunken steel, their pale buds opening only when vibrated by soft, mournful cadence.  Because the fractured spire bent every sea breeze into a slightly discordant sigh, Oren crouched inside the narrow innermost whorl and woke those dormant spores via half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key wind.  Once roused, the glowing rootlets were shyly unstoppable, retracting at the snap of a crab claw yet creeping back to pry apart titanium casings and drink corrosive fuel.  Bomb by bomb, silent gardens of indigo petals replaced triggers and firing pins beneath the tide. 

Across centuries of longing, Oren drew the breath for this patient work from a single dry relic tucked inside his faded coat: a galaxy postcard.  Ivo, a stranded enemy navigator who had sheltered beside him during the final bombardment, had pressed the stiff rectangle into his palm moments before evacuation ships tore them apart.  Its glossy front pictured a distant silver spiral of stars, while four inked words on the back promised a return when the waters grew gentle.  Whenever loneliness sharpened inside Oren, he spoke that ache into the moonlit mist where spore beds drank both dim light and human breath.  It became his whole purpose to photosynthesize pain into beauty, partnering with the hungry flora so every syllable of grief fed another luminous cluster disarming the bay. 

On a breathless midnight when the lagoon mirrored the constellations, a single amber spark detached itself from the zenith and curved downward through the upper clouds.  A courier skiff from that far spiral was finally descending along the old beacon path toward the center of the crater.  Oren hurried to the scalloped lip of the chamber and looked down into the deepest outer trench, where one massive siege mortar still crouched in cold shadow.  Thick orchid vines wrapped its iron barrel, yet their tight buds had never opened because his voice could not drop low enough to reach the floor of the trench.  Already a faint crimson targeting eye blinked awake beneath the dark water, sensing the distant hum of landing thrusters.  Oren ran back into the narrowest acoustic throat of the spiral, where air whistled through a thin gap in the wet pearl.  To deepen the resonance of the chamber by a full octave, he needed a stiff reed to baffle that gap, and he had nothing stiff enough save the treasure in his coat. 

Without hesitation, he drew out the card and reached up to ensconce it snugly within the damp cleft of living nacre and velvet moss.  Fine white rootlets immediately threaded the painted starlight, sealing the card forever into the architecture of the sanctuary while the rushing draft dropped into a rich, cavernous hum.  Freed even from the fear of losing his last keepsake, Oren leaned his forehead against the cool wall and sang his rawest, unguarded cradle song into the dark.  The low vibration rolled out through the great spiral and plunged into the trench, where the final buds trembled, split wide, and flooded the mortar lens with blue blossoms.  Moments later, the descending skiff glided unharmed onto the glowing lagoon, sending gentle rings of silver foam lapping against the base of the pearl.  As a warm lantern swung out from the open hatch and someone called a remembered name across the water, Oren felt a quiet, reef-shadow joy fill his chest, cool and dappled as sheltered tidepools.  Smiling into the blue gloom after his long vigil, he stepped down to meet the skiff and knew that tonight, at last, he would sleep.
