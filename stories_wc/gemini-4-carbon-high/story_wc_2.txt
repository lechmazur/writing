As a broken-clock tinkerer, Oren earned his keep buying crates of jammed pocket watches and rusted mantel chimes that stricter horologists deemed worthless.  Five years earlier, he had been the harbor's proudest turret engineer, until minor ground subsidence shifted his unyielding bell tower by half an inch and snapped its rigid main axle.  Humbled by how brittle perfection could be, Oren closed his drafting office and resolved to befriend uncertainty in planning, leaving deliberate play inside every gear train he rebuilt.  That quiet habit drew him weekly to the burned cliffside estate of Admiral Vance, where he scavenged scrap brass without a map or schedule.  Treasure hunters had long abandoned those ruins after failing to unearth the admiral's lost compass rose, a famed lodestone dial mounted on loose rings built to roll with violent seas rather than fight them. 

During a rainy Tuesday visit, Oren noticed dry grey soot puffing upward between cracked flagstones near the ruined west wing.  Four days later, the same warm exhale rose through the wet moss forty minutes after high tide.  Every tinkerer recognizes the moment when coincidence becomes pattern, and on his third visit Oren timed the phenomenon with a salvaged pocket watch until the hidden rhythm made sense.  Rising ocean swells were compressing air inside deep sea caves and forcing gusts up an unmapped cooling shaft beneath the house.  Standing in the drizzle, Oren felt uncertainly certain that Vance's upper chartroom had plunged down that stone throat during the fire, carrying the heavy binnacle dial beyond every surface digger's grid. 

Refusing to spend weeks surveying angles and drafting rescue rigs, he returned at the next rising tide with only an iron prybar, a lantern, and his satchel of clock parts.  Rather than prying stones at random, he read the invisible subterranean drafts through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse built against the chartroom's surviving wall.  Shattered roof glass had let decades of crisp sycamore foliage carpet the floor, and as tidal pressure mounted underground, hidden vents spun the dry drifts into tight eddies.  Tracing the sharpest vortex to a fallen cedar potting bench, Oren scraped aside thick mounds of charred joists and pale cinders left by the ancient blaze.  There, waiting beneath the ashes like quiet proof that ruin never has the final word, lay an intact bronze hatchway opening onto steep stone stairs. 

Oren lit his lantern and descended into cool mist until the passage opened onto a cavernous gorge echoing with the roar of a subterranean waterfall.  An underground stream plunged twenty feet down black limestone into a churning tidal pool that boomed whenever ocean swells crowded a submerged sea arch below.  Spanning the ten-foot gulf hung an old iron waterwheel that once powered estate pumps, its half-rotted buckets stalling and lurching wildly as erratic sheets of spray struck its rim.  Just beyond the wheel's far edge, balanced on the splayed iron door of a wrecked dumbwaiter atop a crumbling rock shelf, gleamed the brass points of the compass rose.  Even as Oren watched, rising surf hammered upward from the pool, cracking the limestone shelf and threatening to sweep frame and compass alike into the dark siphon. 

Climbing the rotten spokes would mean certain death, while retreating to forge custom tackle meant returning tomorrow to an empty ledge.  Forced to improvise before the shelf collapsed, Oren linked three coiled steel mainsprings, suspended his curved prybar and a lead pendulum weight beneath them, and hooked the top coil loosely onto a rising wheel spoke while holding a braided chime cable.  Rather than fighting the wheel's irregular lurches, he let the waterfall's shifting bursts carry his rig over the high arch, absorbing every violent jerk through the flexible springs.  When the wheel stalled beside the distant shelf and rocked in the backwash, Oren played his cable in rhythm with its sway until the prybar's claw slid cleanly through the compass rose's outer ring.  One sharp tug freed the top spring from the spoke just as a booming wave shattered the ledge, sending the wrecked dumbwaiter plunging into the foam while Oren hauled his prize safely across the upper rim.  Kneeling on the wet stone, he wiped silt from the heavy lodestone star and marveled at its triple-jointed gimbals.  Even as his tired hands trembled, the dial floated dead level, kept true not by tight locks, but by generous room to yield.  Climbing back toward the grey daylight with the compass rose tucked beneath his arm, Oren welcomed the unwritten days ahead, glad at last not to know every turn they would take.
