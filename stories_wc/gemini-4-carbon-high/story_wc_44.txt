High above the clouded sea, Oren stood alone inside a nocturnal observatory on a floating isle and watched three silver spoons resting on a marble sill.  Pendulums and hourglasses failed at this altitude whenever the nightly aetheric tide rolled across the sky, so astronomers relied on shallow concave metal to gauge the shifting air.  Flat knives sliced cleanly through that unseen current and forked tines let it pass, yet round bowls caught the heavy magic until metal twisted and straight walls bowed inward.  Only after spoons bend reality along the sill can the deep lenses pierce solid stone and glimpse hidden layers of space.  As a budding rune scholar assigned to solitary midnight maintenance, Oren greeted the distortion with quietly deliberate breathing.  Any sudden motion or careless whisper in warped air could fracture chalk sigils and scatter hours of preparation. 

Senior masters slept below, convinced that night watches existed solely to chart distant stars, yet Oren had volunteered for a very different purpose.  Everything he studied was driven by his quiet obsession to find where shadows go at noon.  Across this drifting landmass, whenever the midday sun reached zenith, shadows did not merely shrink beneath boots and eaves.  Even tethered balloons and suspended cages lost their dark projections altogether, as every shadow detached and sank straight into the porous limestone underfoot.  Orthodox scholars dismissed that daily vanishing as a harmless trick of glare, never asking what happened inside the finite rock of an airborne island.  Oren suspected that because open sky lay beneath the cliffs, midnight positioned the sun directly below the island and pushed those sunken noon shadows upward toward this dark dome. 

Proving his theory mattered for more than academic pride, because six silent brass figures stood around the central glass well, housing what ancient records called sealed awakenings.  Centuries ago, when volatile magic first tore this rock from the earth, the founding astronomers locked their living minds inside clockwork shells to slumber through the upheaval.  Those sealed awakenings were meant to stir once later generations stabilized the sky, yet every attempt to rouse them had ended in motionless failure.  According to marginal notes Oren had uncovered, the founders entered their brass shells at high noon, inadvertently leaving their shadows behind as they fell asleep.  Without a shadow to anchor memory and weight, a sleeping mind refused to wake.  Stepping to a velvet pedestal, Oren lifted a heavy clockwork automaton key from its resting cradle. 

Along its bronze shaft ran rows of traditional straight runes, cut with sharp open tips that had baffled every master in the academy.  In ordinary daylight, linear glyphs conducted energy cleanly, yet during the warped night tide, open strokes curved off into nothingness and spilled their charge.  Because Oren was still a novice unbound by centuries of rigid pride, he had forced himself to relearn runic grammar from its most basic foundations.  Month after month in his cramped quarters, he discovered how to preserve structure in curved air by closing loops.  Whenever he bent a linear rune until its head bit its own tail, the resulting circle held fast against spatial distortion and trapped every spark within an unbroken ring.  Using a fine silver graver and quietly deliberate pressure, Oren connected each open groove on the clockwork automaton key, reshaping jagged prongs into seamless interlocking rings. 

Next, he knelt beside the floor lens and drew luminous chalk bands that linked its brass rim directly to the feet of the lead automaton, completing his design by closing loops across the chamber.  Over on the marble sill, the three silver spoons suddenly curled like dry leaves, warping the air until the stone floor rippled like clear water.  Midnight had arrived, and through the downward glass Oren beheld a vast cavern of folded space suspended inside the heart of the island.  Driven upward by the unseen sun beneath the world, thousands of dark silhouettes rose like ink in water, revealing at last where shadows go at noon.  Every lost midday shade pooled unharmed in that deep reservoir until midnight lifted them toward the lens, where six tall silhouettes glided along his chalk rings to settle behind the brass shells. 

Holding his breath, Oren fitted the modified clockwork automaton key into the chest of the nearest astronomer and turned it with one smooth, steady twist.  Warm gold light flared along the circular engravings as the ancient shadow fused onto brass heels, uniting noon and midnight in a single pulse.  Inside the metal ribs, dormant gears began to chime, and the first of the sealed awakenings opened luminous eyes to greet the apprentice who had made the sky whole.
