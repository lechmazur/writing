Jonas worked in a funeral home prep room because dead weight never bluffs.  As a con artist's honest child, he had watched his mother cheat desperate travelers with forged visas, leaving him driven to find the mathematics of a fair trade.  Arterial preservation obeyed pure equivalence.  Beneath cold lamps and sharp wintergreen vapor, one ounce of crimson preservative displaced one ounce of dark venous blood, lending corpses a startling, deathly vitality.  Hollow cheeks flushed coral and slack lips grew plump with urgent counterfeit health above the porcelain drain.  On his steel desk sat his mother's vintage ink pot of beveled glass and tarnished brass.  She had filled it with vanishing blue dye for crooked contracts, so Jonas kept it brimming instead with permanent black carbon fluid made for burial tags that outlasted damp earth. 

Upon the center table lay Envoy Soren of the Northern Legation, felled by a stroke while meeting Nadia, a state archivist seeking political asylum.  Regime police had ringed the Legation with iron barricades to block defectors, and two armed officers stood outside the bolted prep room door.  Slipping in through the coal alley with stolen arrest rosters Soren had died pursuing, Nadia had offered Jonas gold to forge a pass or seal her inside Soren's zinc coffin.  Jonas refused her gold and both schemes.  Counterfeit papers failed at checkpoints, and customs scales weighed every coffin to the pound.  If her rosters equaled a human sanctuary, he promised, they would balance the equation honestly. 

By bilateral treaty, Consul Breck entered the locked room alone to witness Soren's preparation before claiming the body for the Legation hearse.  Waiting near the sink in a rubber apron, Nadia presented the rosters while Jonas named the price: full intelligence for Breck's signature adding her to his immune escort manifest.  Breck stiffened inside his wool coat and refused.  Bound by rigid orthodoxy, the Consul cited statutes declaring that political asylum could only be requested inside Legation gates, demanding she surrender the files gratis and face the corridor police afterward.  Like Jonas's mother, the diplomat wanted value for nothing, using legalism to dignify a swindle.  Yet health statutes held Breck inside the tiled room until embalming finished, or else Soren's unsealed corpse and coded pocketbook would pass to state coroners. 

Just then, a dense clot halted the glass gravity percolator above Soren's shoulder.  Nadia moved to plead, while Breck braced himself for threats or blackmail.  Jonas lifted a gloved hand for silence.  Vascular work had taught him that brute pressure ruptures a closed channel; you breach an obstinate barrier only by waiting actively.  He raised the crimson tank two inches for steady poise, kneaded Soren's cold collarbone with patient thumbs, and tapped the brass tube without forcing the line.  With matching calm, he uncapped the vintage ink pot, set a steel pen beside Breck's portfolio, and aligned Nadia's rosters with the blank escort sheet.  He offered no argument, keeping every instrument ready while quiet tension filled the room. 

The stark ritual of a funeral home prep room carries an intimacy that liquefies rigid thinking.  Standing inches from his dead friend, Breck watched rigor mortis soften under Jonas's hands as the clot suddenly yielded.  Rose dye flooded Soren's temples, banishing gray pallor until the fallen envoy looked radiant enough to sit up and judge his colleague's refusal.  Beside the steady trickle of mortal blood down the trough, borders and bureaucratic subsections dissolved into paper myths, leaving only living breath and honest debt.  Jonas sutured the vessel, rinsed his gloves, and spoke evenly.  "Soren spent his life to secure two hundred names, and one seat in your hearse balances his account." 

Breck looked from Soren's flushed, lifelike brow to the open ledger, and his bureaucratic armor gave way.  Stepping to the desk without another excuse, the Consul dipped the steel pen deep into the vintage ink pot and inscribed Nadia's name onto the courier manifest in permanent black carbon.  He pressed his consular seal beside the wet letters and pocketed the rosters, sealing a trade neither weather nor state decree could undo.  When Jonas slid back the iron bolt, Soren rested in gleaming zinc wearing the warm blush of sleep, and Nadia shed her apron to walk openly beside Breck past the guards, carrying honest credentials to breach the Legation blockade in plain sight.
