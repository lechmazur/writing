Morning light slanted like waking music beneath the vaulted ribs of the extinct language archive.  Along towering cedar shelves, thousands of forgotten dialects slumbered inside wax cylinders and basalt tablets, waiting for an arcane linguist bold enough to breathe them back into sound.  At the center of the rotunda, Corin balanced on the balls of his feet beside a brass reading desk, his pulse thrumming with nervous hope as Mara fastened the last buckle on her traveling trunk.  Outside the tall lancet windows, a waiting carriage rattled over bright cobblestones.  Soon Mara would sail for the northern fellowships while Corin remained in the capital.  For six months, ever since their first attempt at the Vaelish Concordat had scorched her palms and frightened him into muting his voice, only formal inventory notes had crossed their table.  Yet Corin had spent sleepless weeks studying the fault, driven not by scholarly pride but by an urgent wish to restore trust after silence. 

"Twenty minutes remain before paths diverge," Corin said, setting an unshielded glass ink well beside the damaged Vaelish scroll with a steady, expectant smile.  Mara paused beside her luggage, her dark eyes widening as she recognized the volatile medium.  Behind the thin transparent curve of the glass ink well, distilled harmonic fluid glowed like molten gold, trembling in eager rings at the sound of his voice.  Beside it lay the Concordat, where silver glyphs coiled in a tight, buzzing knot of broken syntax from the autumn day Corin had pulled back in fear.  "Help me unsnarl the closing stanza before you go," he urged softly, extending a slender silver pen. 

Mara approached the desk, drawn despite her caution by the luminous puzzle they had once chased with breathless delight.  High Vaelish never conveyed mere dry definitions, because when spoken and written together, its clauses rose off the vellum as symphonies of truth, building layered musical chords that harmonized only with complete honesty.  Any hidden doubt soured the melody into destructive static, yet Corin had deliberately stripped the leaden containment bands from the crystal reservoir.  "Working with unwarded resonance ink is wild," Mara murmured, though a suppressed grin tugged at the corner of her mouth.  "Lead bands dull the overtones, and holding back is what tangled the script before," Corin answered, embracing a strategy that was carefully reckless, daring enough to risk an acoustic shatter, yet calibrated down to every precise breath and vowel.  Moreover, no single scholar could mend Vaelish alone, for the ancient grammar bound its clauses exclusively via interconnected promises.  One speaker had to inflect a root verb with a sincere personal pledge, and a partner had to loop an answering pledge directly through the wet stroke to complete the chord. 

Exhaling slowly, Mara took the silver pen and stepped beside him, her shoulder brushing his sleeve.  Corin dipped his nib into the glass ink well first, lifting a droplet of singing light to the outermost snarl on the parchment.  Tracing the opening curve, he intoned the Vaelish prefix and anchored it to his vow never again to abandon a shared risk or hide behind silence when fear struck.  A rich cello note rose from the page, vibrating through the floorboards while the fragile crystal vessel shivered intact upon the brass tabletop.  Because his confession rang true, the golden fluid brightened rather than cracking its walls, waiting hungrily for its counterpart. 

Eyes shining, Mara dipped her pen and swept an interlocking arc directly across his glowing line.  Her clear alto joined his baritone, promising to pardon honest mistakes and to send candid letters from the northern coast.  At once, a silver flute overtone braided around the bass note, causing the darkest knot on the scroll to unsnarl into graceful, living script.  Cautious exuberance flooded the room as they exchanged a radiant, breathless glance, thrilled by the rising music yet careful to keep every wrist movement disciplined lest an overeager flourish break the spell.  Line after line, they wove rapid reciprocal vows of shared research, patient listening, and steadfast friendship until every twisted glyph relaxed across the vellum.  When the final period touched down, triumphant symphonies of truth soared into the high dome of the extinct language archive, waking gentle answering hums from a hundred sleeping tablets. 

Between their hands, the glass ink well chimed like a clear temple bell in the golden sunbeams.  Below the windows, the coachman blew a brisk horn to summon his passenger.  Laughing softly at last, Mara squeezed his fingers with warm certainty before lifting her trunk, carrying their restored harmony outward toward the sea.
