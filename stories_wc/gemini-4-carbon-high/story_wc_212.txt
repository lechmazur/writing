A faint click echoed across the granite bench as the translucent shell parted.  Only after the egg hatches can the ancient cycle turn again, and Mara had waited thirty winters for that fragile sound.  She stood alone within an icebound atrium under starlight, where vaulted ribs of iron and glass held back the endless glacier outside.  Above the frosted panes, fierce constellations blazed down upon rows of stone planters locked in silver rime.  As an heirloom seed saver, Mara kept thousands of forgotten lineages lined along the walls in waxed clay vials: crimson clover, hulless barley, painted millet, and mountain rye. 

Even in deep cold, dormant seeds slowly lose their spark unless planted, pollinated, and harvested anew, while forest oaks perish outright if dried or frozen.  To save both, her ancestors had forged the vortex inheritance, a contained cyclone of stolen autumn turning silently at the heart of the conservatory.  Behind a thick dome of opaque frost, a dwarf white oak and rich woodland soil revolved through a luminous afternoon looped across centuries.  Duty required Mara to sow her oldest fading seeds into that living eddy and claim the ripened pods left by her predecessor.  Beneath duty, however, burned a deeper hunger: having known only brittle ice and dry husks, she ached to discover the texture of borrowed time against her own bare skin. 

On the granite bench, a pale silk moth pulled itself free from the split shell.  Bred to pollinate the hidden sanctuary and bridge two eras, the insect unfurled crystalline wings that shimmered in the blue starlight.  Its emergence proved that the inner current had slowed near the outer rim, offering a brief window of harmonic alignment.  Mara reached into her wool coat and withdrew an oak acorn cap, dark with age and polished smooth inside its concave cup.  Taken from the founder tree before the oceans froze, its overlapping scales formed a flawless geometric spiral.  With a linen pouch of aging clover seeds slung across her shoulder, she coaxed the moth onto the woody rim of the cap and approached the frosted dome. 

No chisel or flame could breach that shimmering boundary.  Heat and force multiply molecular chaos, and any spike in turbulence would unravel the temporal knot, scattering its warmth into the hungry night.  To reopen the sealed vault, she had to work in reverse by decreasing entropy along its frozen crust until outer ice and inner vortex shared one coherent order.  Mara set the oak acorn cap into a shallow depression at the zenith of the shell.  Instantly, the moth anchored its silver tarsi to the scaled cupule and began to vibrate.  Its wings beat in an inaudible standing wave that quieted stray air currents and drew wandering vapor into strict symmetry around the spiral scales.  Even a ragged breath from Mara could spoil that delicate geometry.  Kneeling on the freezing flagstones, she locked her racing pulse to the rhythm of the insect, becoming furiously tranquil, every nerve ablaze with fierce intent inside an unbreakable stillness. 

Outward from the spiral template of the cap, chaotic frost needles snapped into mirror lattices.  Cloudy fractures in the dome clarified into flawless crystal as random thermal jitter bled away.  Starlight speared straight through the purified ice to meet the wheeling amber glow within.  When the lattice reached total regularity, the crown of the vault did not shatter, but parted silently like petals of clear quartz.  Up through the opening curled the breath of an extinct October, heavy with damp leaf mold, oak sap, and wildflower honey, spinning tightly enough to hold its warmth in the freezing hall. 

Slipping off her mitten, Mara plunged her bare hand past the crystalline rim into the revolving current.  At last she felt what preserved centuries were made of: not thin phantom air, but a dense, velvet undertow that pressed against her scarred knuckles like warm river water threaded with coarse pollen and living static.  Every stolen minute inside the eddy hummed with untamed vigor and hushed peace, wrapping her skin in wild serenity.  Working smoothly so her pulse would not disturb the balance, she plucked dried seedpods of regenerated vetch from the oak roots and pressed her aging clover seeds deep into the warm, turning loam.  When she lifted the oak acorn cap away, the silk moth darted downward into the golden foliage to begin its work.  Disorder returned gently to the rim, drawing the frost petals shut once more beneath the silent blaze of the stars.
