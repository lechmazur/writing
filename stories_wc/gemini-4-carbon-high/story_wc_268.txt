Vorin, the last diminutive titan, knelt upon the cold flagstone floor to mend the cracked spine of a leather ledger.  Millennia ago he had risen tall enough to shoulder thunderheads, yet age by age he had poured his granite mass into the preservation wards until he measured no higher than a mortal child.  His reduced stature suited the delicate cedar galleries of the Sanctum of Oros, an ancient temple library lost in time where lamp flames hung motionless like amber glass.  Though early monarchs had once bowed at his ankles, Vorin remained regally unpretentious, wearing a faded linen apron as he swept stray dust and sipped water from a dented tin cup.  For centuries no living breath had disturbed his quiet vigil.  Only across the pause of shifting constellations, when the winter stars halted between epochs before reversing their slow wheel, did these forgotten halls overlap with the Royal Scriptorium of the waking capital. 

High beneath the domed skylight, the bronze astrolabe clicked once and fell still.  Translucent shapes of mortal courtiers shimmered into view along the aisles, gathered around a gilded dais where Lord Malen waited to swear the sovereign oath.  Studying the heavy signet ring on the hand of Malen, Vorin perceived the sour alchemy of stolen blood masking the murder of the true prince.  Once the stars resumed their march, the ancient compact of the kingdom would bind the land to a usurper forever.  Vorin knew his final duty was to unmask a pretender to the throne before the celestial stillness ended.  Yet he could neither cross the temporal membrane nor speak aloud to mortal ears without crumbling instantly into mute gravel.  Reaching into his apron pocket, he withdrew a single iron gall ink pellet, pressed ages past from sacred oak galls and dark filings shaved off the first crown.  He placed the dry bead into a stone inkwell, added three drops of water from his cup, and stirred with a reed pen until the biting black tincture woke, sharp enough to bleed onto mortal parchment. 

Near the eastern stacks paced Archivist Seraphina, her spectral hands trembling as she searched the succession records for any flaw in the claim of Malen.  Because hired sorcerers watched the dais to burn any plain charge of treason, Vorin could guide Seraphina only through cryptic messages left in library books.  Slipping unseen beneath her elbow on quiet stone feet, he opened his copy of the Herbal of Kings and penned a brief marginal riddle concerning winter nightshade and a fallen stag.  Seraphina caught her breath when the antique script darkened upon her open folio, recalling the stag badge of the poisoned heir and hurrying toward the Foundry Rolls.  There Vorin inscribed a second gloss asking why counterfeit gold feared the weight of true iron, directing her eye toward the Litany of Succession.  In that third volume his final couplet invited the new sovereign to press his signet across all three open margins to prove their harmony.  Woven subtly through the curved tails of those three separate verses lay a rune of drift, the primordial glyph that unmoors anything severed from honest history. 

Grasping the hidden design, Seraphina bore the three heavy volumes to the dais and laid their outer borders edge to edge before the council.  When she recited the seemingly innocent riddles aloud as a customary test of wit, Malen smiled with easy vanity and pressed his golden signet firmly across the aligned pages.  At once the ancestral crown iron within the ink bit fiercely into the false metal above it.  A silent tide of silver light rose from the completed glyph to sunder the stolen blood glamour, splitting the forged ring in two and stripping the princely illusion from the startled impostor.  Yet Vorin had known from his first stroke that waking a spell of drift inside a suspended sanctuary would likewise untether the ancient wards keeping his own halls outside decay. 

While royal guards seized the exposed traitor, the constellations overhead trembled back into motion and drew the waking world safely away.  Inside the unmoored temple, centuries rushed softly inward in a graceful collapse that held no sorrow at all.  Amber lamp flames bowed like tired petals and went dark, while tall cedar shelves leaned gently together, shedding pale vellum leaves like quiet snow.  Vorin wiped his reed pen upon his apron, set his tin cup neatly beside the mended ledger, and sat upon his low wooden stool.  Folding his stained granite hands in his lap, the small custodian closed his eyes as vaulted stone softened into silver dust, yielding his long watch at last to the gentle mercy of time.
