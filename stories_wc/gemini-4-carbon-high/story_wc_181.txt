Beneath the astral orchard blossoming on a lonely asteroid, Ansel broke the leaden seal of the Origin Codex and turned its opening leaf.  Immediately its clockwork spine clicked awake, priming twenty vellum sheets with canopy glare in an irreversible cascade that flipped and bleached one leaf after another.  Overhead, thousands of radiant pistachio boughs chimed against the glass dome, filling the conservatory with a brightened disquiet where festive golden light met the suffocating black abyss of deep space.  For years he had served here as a bewildered curator, cataloguing estates that arrived through temporal root wells via nonlinear inheritance.  Botanical journals, tools, and grafted scions dropped from unborn successors just as readily as from ancient predecessors, forming a lineage in which effects routinely preceded their causes.  Statute forbade any archivist to ask how such a circle had begun, yet chronic confusion had driven Ansel to pursue forbidden knowledge despite the ruinous trap of the book.  Whatever truth anchored his solitary post would have to be grasped between the first and last page before every word vanished into blankness. 

Nestled in velvet inside the same reliquary box lay no sacred portrait of a Prime Founder, only an uncarved pistachio shell cameo blank.  Every other bequest in the halls bore a miniature relief scraped through pale outer hull to dark inner core, depicting the benefactor who
 sent it across folded centuries.  Ansel lifted this smooth oval and froze with a jolt of uncanny recognition.  Across its polished ivory curve ran a crescent mineral vein identical to a scar he had noted that very morning on an unripe nut hanging from Bough Seven.  Scanning the early leaves of the Codex as their golden script dissolved into shimmer, he read a terse craft maxim stating that fresh astral shells splinter under any blade unless cured for a millennium in dry stone. 

Heart hammering, he cradled both book and shell and sprinted down the luminous gravel path toward Bough Seven.  Blossoms flared overhead with joyous pink luminescence while his stomach hollowed out, as though the orchard were celebrating a snare he could not yet perceive.  Sure enough, the living green fruit still clung to its twig above him, bearing that exact crescent vein.  Beside the trunk stood the brass acoustic horn of the Probate Chorus, wired into the memory sap of the roots.  Abandoning his final scruple, Ansel leaned into the horn to inquire who had sealed the Origin vault ten centuries before.  Out of the brass throat murmured a hundred layered voices of unborn apprentices and departed masters, reciting a donor cipher identical to his own service mark while insisting that no mortal eye had ever beheld the Founder.  In his palm, the Codex ticked past its midpoint, shedding bright ghosts of ink into the perfumed air. 

Blinded by blossom glare and dizzy with temporal vertigo, Ansel retreated to his strangest scholarly method: having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms.  Ever since his novice examinations, whenever paradoxes knotted his mind under the vast dome, only windowless washrooms with plain porcelain geometry could restore his reason.  He lunged into the cramped tiled lavatory behind the potting shed, kicked the door shut against the oppressive radiance, and twisted the cold tap.  As clear water swirled down the basin drain into the exposed glass purifier beneath the sink, rising straight back toward the faucet in a sealed station cycle, comprehension struck him cold.  Inside a closed pipe, no droplet precedes the rest, yet fresh matter must still enter once to set the endless circuit running.  The secret architecture of the orchard was simply complicated, elementary as a bent copper tube yet maddeningly tangled to anyone standing inside its flow. 

Under the steady hum of the washroom bulb, he read the penultimate leaf of the Codex just before the cascading bleach turned its vellum bare.  There had never been an ancient stranger at the dawn of time, only a custodian brave enough to author the past that sustained him.  Tomorrow he would pluck the ripened fruit from Bough Seven, plant its kernel, polish its hull smooth, copy these vanishing instructions from memory, and drop everything down the deepest root well a thousand years backward.  Resting silently inside that sealed vault across ten dry centuries had petrified the shell until it was finally hard enough to accept a blade without shattering.  Outside the door, golden orchard light seeped beneath the threshold with unsettling splendor, reminding him that no providence watched over this rock save his own.  Before the final sheet settled utterly bare, Ansel angled the hinged vanity mirror to catch his own side profile, drew his steel burin, and carved the first line of the Founder.
