Soren stepped onto the echoing terrace in spring, moving as an elusive mirage painter must, unseen among columns of damp limestone.  Tight plum buds glistened overhead, shedding the last drops of an April squall onto the flagstones outside the convalescent ward.  She could practice her forbidden craft only over the hush before bells ring, that brief twilight interval when street noise fades and bronze chimes have not yet stiffened the air.  Along the inner arcade stood tall silvered looking glasses, placed beside sickroom windows by the Physicians of Fact so patients would confront their frailty without comforting delusions.  Once curfew tolled, heavy iron vibrations would lock those reflections into merciless certainty until dawn. 

Soren glided to the outer balustrade, where the prism parapet after rain caught the violet and amber glow of dusk.  Carved from mineral crystal to scatter summer glare, the wet beveled rail now held thousands of rainwater beads that bent dim twilight into liquid spectrums.  Tucked inside a dry niche beneath that crystal ledge lay a coiled copper shell, still warm from the chest of young Tomas behind the third transom.  Soren pressed the resonant vessel against her collarbone, accepting a borrowed heartbeat into her own ribs.  Pure illusions carried no pulse, so Physician silver shed them instantly like oil off cold water.  Soren never climbed these walls to paint gaudy falsehoods, which both waking minds and honest glass would reject by morning.  Instead, she risked capture each evening to persuade mirrors to show possibilities, anchoring a plausible tomorrow to a living pulse so despairing eyes could envision recovery. 

Against her sternum, the captured rhythm of the boy hammered fast and uneven, frayed by months of winter lung fever.  Down in the courtyard, heavy boots clicked across wet cobbles as a watchman made his rounds.  One upward glance from skeptical eyes would collapse nascent refractions into ordinary glare, yet haste would make Soren tremble and sever the sympathetic link inside the shell.  Standing in shadow, she remained perceptively calm, registering the fading crunch of gravel below, the scent of damp moss, and every hesitant skip within the copper coil.  Slowly she lengthened her inhalations until the frantic thrum beneath her fingers eased into a steady, trusting cadence. 

Now she needed to soften the rigid silver backing of the third mirror before drawing light across its face.  Generations earlier, dissident glassmakers had preserved their forbidden acoustic formulas through coded lullabies in children's songs, knowing stern magistrates would overlook simple bedtime rhymes.  Every gentle interval matched a resonant pitch that loosened metallic tension, while verses about sleepy wrens and silver brooks told the painter which prism angle to gather.  Soren breathed the opening notes of a cradle tune toward the vaulted ceiling.  Damp stone arches multiplied her quiet alto, blending her melody with dripping plum boughs into a dusky harmony that set the tall mirror shimmering like thawed water.  Behind the tilted transom, Tomas recognized the nursery song from infancy and hummed a faint counterpoint, unwittingly completing the chord that unlocked his own reflection. 

Angled toward his narrow bed, the darkened looking glass currently showed a hollow boy slumped beneath gray wool.  Past rebels had smashed such mirrors with iron bars, leaving only jagged shards that multiplied fear and brought harsher replacements.  Soren knew violence only proved the Physicians right; her true calling was to grace the unforgiving glass, meeting its cold rigor with gentleness until silver learned mercy.  Dipping her fingertips into droplets along the crystal rail, she drew slender filaments of rose, gold, and indigo through the cool air.  With unhurried grace, she wove those luminous threads into the softened mirror in exact time with the borrowed pulse warming her chest.  Stroke by quiet stroke, the reflection shifted: color warmed sunken cheeks, bent shoulders squared, and Tomas stood barefoot beneath open white blossoms on this very terrace, breathing deep and whole.  Recognizing a living human rhythm inside the painted light, the mirror drank the vision inward and held it steady. 

In the distant tower, heavy ropes creaked against timber pulleys as bell ringers braced their weight.  Soren slipped the warm copper shell onto the outer sill so the boy could reclaim his steadied pulse at dawn, then melted into violet shadows along the wet stone.  When the first bronze toll rolled across the city, its iron resonance sealed the newborn possibility safely inside the silver instead of freezing grief.  From the garden wall below, Soren heard a soft gasp of wonder through the open transom, followed by a child drawing a deeper, easier breath.
