Deep inside the polar mountain, the prismatic escondite hummed like a cathedral holding its breath.  Towering ribs of tourmaline and smoky quartz split stray auroral light into bruised ribbons of violet and amber across the vault.  High above the ice, a ferocious solar storm battered the planetary shield, pouring silent rivers of charged fire across the sky while the deep stone trembled in sympathy.  Caelum stood motionless at the center of his secret refuge, a bored immortal who had watched empires rise and erode until eternity itself tasted of gray ash.  Because he was dimensionally aware, his gaze saw past the three visible walls into six higher spatial folds, all of them shivering like taut canvas beneath a distant drumbeat. 

Footsteps echoed softly along the crystal gallery as Vespera emerged from the refracted gloom, cradling a leaden reliquary against her cloak.  As warden of the Undying Synod, she believed she was delivering a forbidden treasure to the sole sanctuary hidden from rebel spies.  She did not yet suspect the three centuries of patient intrigue Caelum had woven to bring her here.  Every forged cipher, every whispered rumor of schism among the ancients, and every staged ambush near the Synod vaults had been his quiet handiwork.  Setting the sealed coffer upon a plinth of dark basalt, Vespera lifted the latch to verify her cargo, revealing the cursed amulet fragment within.  It was a jagged crescent of meteoric iron, forged before terrestrial memory and hexed with a geometric blight so absolute that creation itself loathed its presence. 

The instant the lead parted, a low, subaudible tremor rolled across the chamber, and Vespera froze as her eyes traced the deliberate alignment of the surrounding spires.  "There was never a cabal," she murmured, her voice scarcely louder than snowfall yet heavy with dread.  "You orchestrated the entire panic yourself, waiting to unseal this iron during a solar storm."  Caelum inclined his head, feeling no triumph, only the solemn relief of a traveler reaching the coast at dusk.  "We have tasted every pleasure and mapped every corner of the continuum, Vespera, yet we remain prisoners of permanence."  Across ages of fleeting mortal friendships, he had listened to countless dying companions whisper their final words before vanishing utterly.  Though he could peer into all nine planes of existence, the departed never arrived in any of them, leaving a mystery that hollowed out his endless years.  Nothing in the cosmos remained worth conspiring for except one forbidden hunger: to discover what happens after goodbye. 

Before Vespera could slam the lid shut, the tempest raging in the heavens struck its terrible crescendo.  Coronal fire compressed the magnetosphere above the pole, driving colossal currents downward into the ferric veins of the mountain.  Caelum had carved every facet of his prismatic escondite to harvest that stellar wrath through magnetic fields, braiding invisible tides of planetary flux into a single focal point.  A sound like distant, smothered artillery vibrated in their marrow as those unseen lines of force seized the cursed amulet fragment.  Ripped from its leaden bed, the black iron glided across the luminous air and locked itself flat against the sternum of its architect.  Vespera lunged forward, only to be pushed gently back by a dense wall of electromagnetic pressure that turned the rainbow light between them into shimmering armor. 

Fed through magnetic fields of continental scale, the dormant curse awoke and poisoned the coordinates Caelum occupied, inciting an immediate spatial mutiny.  The sanctuary did not shatter; instead, local geometry revolted against its ancient duty to house him.  Depth refused his shoulders, verticality peeled away from his spine, and distance itself coiled inward like an angry tide determined to cast driftwood off the shore of reality.  With his dimensionally aware sight, Caelum watched all nine manifolds curl backward in unison, severing the immortal tether that the Synod believed unbreakable.  Between the rebellious angles opened an aperture of profound, velvety quiet that belonged to no axis he had ever named.  Around them, the deep vibration of the mutinous chamber swelled like distant thunder trapped behind miles of glacial ice.  Through the bending prism light, Caelum met the wide, sorrowful eyes of Vespera one last time, feeling the forgotten thrill of genuine uncertainty rise in his chest.  "Farewell, old friend," he whispered into the hushed expanse.  Space completed its insurrection with a single, resonant pulse, casting both Caelum and the iron shard clean outside the architecture of the world.  Behind him, the crystal vault folded seamlessly back together, leaving Vespera alone in the colored dark as the last low echo faded into stone.
