Twenty years after the fossil fuel ban quieted the shipping lanes, the ocean around Outpost Nine held only the sound of wind and slack iron chains.  The floating sea level monitoring station swung in wide, lazy circles above the sunken reef, tethered to a concrete anchor block yet never resting in the same spot twice.  Every morning Aris walked the narrow deck with a brass stylus, relying on a practice of tapping rhythms on surfaces to inspect the instruments.  He tapped three short beats and one slow beat against the steel stilling well, listening for the clear metallic ring that proved no barnacles choked the float gauge inside.  Then he tapped the solar battery housing and the wooden gunwale, finding comfort in how each material answered with its own honest voice across the silent water. 

Inside the cabin, weighted down at the corners by lead sinkers, lay an atlas page torn out of the final printed maritime survey before the coastal bureau switched to satellite files.  Aris had ripped the sheet from the binding on the day clerks discarded the volume, refusing to forget the dotted footpaths drawn between the outer limestone cays.  For generations those shallow sandbars had been periodically eternal, drowning beneath every high tide only to emerge whole and pale silver six hours later without fail.  Yet eight years ago two of the eastern walkways sank beneath the chop and never surfaced again, stranding the old shrines on the farthest islet.  Because the local water column had risen less than three inches in all that time, Aris had volunteered for this solitary mooring to discover why some paths disappear while adjacent shoals endure. 

His obsession reached back thirty summers, when he swam this lagoon as a young surveyor and became a coral bleaching witness during the fiercest heatwave of the century.  Floating in water as warm as broth, he had watched vast gardens of staghorn and brain coral expel their golden algae until the entire barrier glowed white like bone.  Back then he had tapped those newly dead domes with his dive knife and heard solid stone ring back through the water.  Even as he grieved the vanished color, he assumed the mineral fortress itself would stand forever, especially once nations outlawed carbon fuel and clean winds returned to the sky. 

At slack tide that afternoon, Aris winched up a wire crate holding the final sequence of limestone core samples drilled along the vanished eastern causeway.  Carrying the dripping cylinders to his table, he began to assemble them in exact geographic order directly across the faded ink of the torn map.  Twelve pale cylinders soon stretched from the sheltered inner lagoon out to the seaward rim where ocean swells broke.  Once the entire cross section of the reef flat lay joined together, seawater soaked into the paper and darkened the old dotted line beneath the stone.  By habit he lifted his brass stylus and tapped his familiar four beat cadence along the row from shoreward end to outer edge.  The first seven cores answered with a sharp, dense chime, firm enough to bear a walking person for another thousand years.  When the metal tip struck the eighth and ninth segments from the outer crest, the pitch dropped abruptly into a hollow, brittle rattle, and the tenth cylinder crumbled into wet lace under the light blow. 

Bending close, Aris saw thousands of tiny channels riddling the interior where boring sponges and worms had quietly eaten away the unguarded limestone.  He stood motionless as the cabin swayed, struck by a delayed revelation that bridged thirty years in a single breath.  On that scorching summer day in his youth, he had believed he was watching the end of the reef, never grasping that he was only witnessing the start of a slow collapse.  Without living polyps rebuilding the outer wall against decay, the dead crest had hollowed in secret for two decades until an ordinary swell broke it open and let deep currents scour the inner sandbars away.  Adjacent paths survived only where cold seabed springs had spared small patches of living builder coral along the rim during the heat.  Beneath the floorboards, the anchor chain groaned softly as the turning tide pushed the barge toward the west and then held it fast against the evening drift.  Aris tapped one final, quiet rhythm on the damp edge of his table, recording the truth of a world healed above the surface yet still answering to old ghosts below.
