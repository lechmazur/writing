Noon bleaches the limestone terraces of Ostraka, a city built on the shells of gargantuan turtles wading endlessly across a blinding brine sea.  Every shadow boils away against the chalky scutes beneath the streets, leaving only white sky and shallow, shimmering water.  Hope grows scarce in the seasons after the gate rusts shut.  Five years ago, the council lowered that massive iron ramp beneath the throat of the lead beast, sending forty scouts toward the eastern rim in search of fresh springs.  No word ever returned from the lost expedition, and relentless salt spray eventually welded the neglected hinges into solid red stone, marooning three thousand citizens thirty cubits above the pan. 

While elders doze beneath bleached canvas awnings and ration lukewarm cistern water, Venn works alone on the scorching parapet directly above the fused ramp.  As the last spiral-shell cartographer in Ostraka, he remains quietly driven by a debt he never speaks aloud.  His own calculations sent those forty walkers eastward before the iron gears seized.  In a featureless waste where stars drown in haze and landmarks do not exist, Venn maps the wandering beasts through pattern languages carved into coiled whelk shells.  Each precise sequence of notches and blank intervals records stride vibrations, sun angles, and the vast logarithmic gyre of the three living islands. 

For three weeks, that cyclical gyre has carried Ostraka steadily back toward the eastern rim.  Out in the lethal brightness stand slender, motionless silhouettes arranged along a pale coral ridge.  Superstitious priests avert their eyes from the dazzle and call those shapes salt ghosts, yet Venn climbs to the prow every afternoon with one obsession: to question the silent watchers on the horizon.  Today the churning legs of the turtles narrow the gap to barely a league, bringing the ridge within acoustic reach across the flat water.  Shouts dissolve instantly in the rising thermal wind, and signal flags bleach invisible against the glare, so Venn lifts his slender reed whistle from its oiled leather case.  Cut from the last marsh cane grown in the royal greywater basins, the instrument converts carved shell ratios into piercing rhythmic tones that carry for miles. 

Venn presses the dry mouthpiece to his blistered lips and blows the scouting guild query, translating his outer whelk notches into sharp bursts of prime intervals.  The high notes skitter out over the dazzling brine and vanish into the heat shimmer.  For ten slow heartbeats, nothing answers except the heavy splash of scaly limbs below and the hum of blood in his sunburned ears.  Then, thin and unmistakable across the white expanse, a dozen distant pipes blow back in unison.  Venn grips the hot stone balustrade as the opening signature confirms his wildest hope.  Those figures on the ridge are the surviving scouts, marooned on that barren reef five years ago when the unstoppable turtles strode past them faster than wading humans could follow.  Yet his triumph curdles into cold dread as the chorus abandons standard coordinates and repeats a rigid, unfamiliar cadence of four descending ratios.  At that exact moment, the gargantuan lead turtle fails to turn parallel to the shoal and instead aims its broad, barnacled head straight past the watchers. 

Desperate to translate the frantic fourfold rhythm, Venn snatches up the Founders Helix, a heavy fossilized shell whose outer whorls hold only recent centuries of drift.  The oldest laws of the beasts lie sealed inside its overgrown core, hidden by early guild masters who feared panic.  Without hesitation, Venn braces the priceless relic against an iron rivet of the gate and swings his brass plumb weight down to crack the calcified spiral open.  Chalky fragments scatter across his sandals, exposing the primeval central spindle to the pitiless sun.  Carved deep along that inner column is the exact fourfold sequence screaming across the water, paired with the foundational glyph for deep submergence.  Once every five hundred years, the turtles march off the shallow pan into an oceanic trench to drown parasites and scour crushing stone from their aching backs. 

Squinting past the frantic figures on the coral lip, Venn finally perceives what the noon glare concealed: a sudden cobalt abyss where drowned masonry from older cities glimmers far below the clear tide.  Those stranded scouts endured five blistering years on that narrow ledge solely to warn their kin to disembark onto the reef as the beasts plunged.  Across the bright water, the urgent whistles keep begging Ostraka to lower its ramp and walk free.  Beneath the unblinking white sky, Venn lowers his reed whistle as cool ocean water laps the ankles of the descending beast, sealing their fate behind thirty tons of rusted iron.
