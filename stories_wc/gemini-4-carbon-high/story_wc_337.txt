Under starlight, the vaulted quartz walls of the prism sanctuary split the night into razor bands of violet and silver across the black sand floor.  Soren knelt beside the burial pit in sharp, sleepless dread, feeling the high tremor in the glass vibrate inside his own crooked ribs.  As the parish's broken mask restorer, crippled years ago beneath a fallen kiln yet sworn to mend the fractured porcelain visages of the dead, he knew a ghost was straining to shatter the walls.  Opposite him knelt old Marek, whose weathered lips parted to begin sharing a secret he had locked behind his teeth for forty winters. 

"Tomas did not vanish in the outer squall," Marek whispered, staring at the cloven clay visage resting incomplete beside Soren's resin pot.  Every mariner on this coast wore a consecrated mask through winter gales so that, if the sea claimed him, an intact face could be sealed above his grave to ground restless spirits.  While one cheek shard remained missing, Tomas walked the cliff winds in waking torment, scraping at Soren's raw mind and threatening to tear the sanctuary apart.  "He swam to my swamped dory, and when his weight dragged the gunwale down, I struck his masked jaw with an iron pin," Marek continued, his knuckles trembling under thin bands of cold violet light. 

The broken porcelain cheek had fallen into Marek's bilge, and fearing the gallows, the young boatman had crept here during the gale rites to bury his crime in the pit.  No spade could ever touch this sand without disturbing hundreds of offerings and loosing a choir of furious dead.  Instead, the founders had rigged the vault so a keeper could exhume any single relic by unraveling knots in a centuries-old fisherman's net threaded vertically through every stratum of black sand.  As surface hitches along a specific cord gave way, counterweighted leads deep below shifted and drew that lone pocket smoothly upward. 

Only refracted starlight revealed the faint, phosphorescent strain along haunted hemp, and Soren needed every detail of Marek's guilt to find the right mesh among thousands.  "I knotted it beneath the third tarred float on the eastern tier, beside Vane's sextant," Marek breathed as frost bloomed across the ringing quartz overhead.  Soren probed the exposed collar of stiffened cordage with a long bone tapestry needle.  A sharp blade would sever brittle fibers and wake the entire pit, whereas the blunt, rounded bone slipped cleanly between petrified twists without snapping a strand.  More vital still, carved from the dense ear bone of a stranded whale, the ancient tapestry needle accumulates silence whenever friction warms its pale shaft. 

As Soren pried open the first crown knot, the tool drank the dry hiss of shifting sand and swallowed the rasp of his uneven breath.  Any stray sound carried down the taut web would rouse the neighboring dead, yet Soren still had to hear Marek's confession to trace the buried line.  Each whispered admission crossed the pit, pricked Soren's taut nerves, and vanished straight into the working bone as he drew the loosened twine through its loop.  Spoken truth and devoured sound wove together across the vault in an interlaced hush, blanketing the lower graves in heavy quietude while isolating the single glowing cord between Soren's scarred fingers. 

"He called my name twice through the broken mask before the surf took him," Marek choked out as a bright blue crack split the eastern prism.  Soren's pulse hammered with cold, crystalline clarity against the spectral pressure in the room while his needle slipped into the seventh binding knot and levered the tarred hemp open.  Subterranean leads dropped silently into the void below, hauling the released mesh upward until a curved shard of glazed porcelain, stained dark along its jagged edge, broke through the swirling sand.  Soren snatched the exhumed fragment from the slackened net, coated its break with warm pine resin, and pressed it tight against the waiting mask while Marek drew one last ragged breath.  "Nobody saw me strike him, and nobody else ever knew," Marek whispered, yielding the final splinter of his secret into the bone needle's pull just as the resin hardened.  The violent hum inside the starlit quartz died at once as the mended visage anchored Tomas deep in the earth, though Soren stayed wide awake in the fractured dark, already sensing the restless stir of other broken dead beneath his knees.
