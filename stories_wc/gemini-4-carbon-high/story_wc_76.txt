High in the mysterious lighthouse on a rocky island, Ansel felt his pulse quicken with cold thrill as the ocean withdrew beyond the black reefs.  No ships ever braved these jagged shoals, and the lantern room held neither oil lamps nor silvered mirrors.  Instead, a massive drum of opaque black crystal straddled an open shaft plunging straight through the granite crag into a flooded cavern.  Tonight, under the slow exhale of low tide, four fathoms of frigid brine were draining from that vault for the first time in three centuries.  Suspended from the drum by a thick brazen cable, the ancient hunger stone was emerging inch by dripping inch into the freezing dark.  Chiseled across its basalt ribs was the merciless decree that whenever it breathed dry air, seven years of famine and preordained ruin would scour the coast. 

Mainland priests bowed to such omens as unalterable law, which was why Ansel had abandoned them twelve winters ago for this barren solitude.  Trained as a counter clockwise clock maker, he had spent his youth cutting widdershins gears whose hands swept backward in quiet mutiny against time.  Yet those early mechanisms had mocked his sole obsession: to step beyond fate's design.  Even a backward dial relied on lead weights sinking toward the floor, proving that downward gravity still owned every hour until the cord ran out.  Undeterred, Ansel had applied his neatly inventive mind to the silent tower, arraying brass calipers, numbered pawls, and spotless linen squares beside the crystal drum. 

Its true function had baffled him until he pried open a corroded reliquary sealed beneath the spiral stair.  Only through reverse-engineering the puzzle of an ancient yoyo locked inside that chest had he uncovered what the vanished architects intended.  Rotating its concentric meteoric rings in surgical order, Ansel had exposed a miniature centrifugal clutch coiled around an axle of identical black crystal.  Unlike a clock weight dying at the bottom of its drop, the heavy disc slept spinning inside its terminal loop, turning descent into fierce rotational fury.  When snapped at peak spin, its stressed core blazed with cold latent radiance and climbed straight back up its tether against gravity.  The lighthouse was that exact engine built to monumental scale, compressing centuries of tidal strain inside its dark crystal mass.  Because rust had destroyed the original return clutch ages ago, the stone had previously dropped dead at extreme ebb, letting ruin unfold unchecked. 

Over twelve austere years, Ansel had fabricated a massive widdershins escapement of tempered steel and bolted it onto the primary spindle.  Now, deep inside the shaft, the last supporting seawater sucked away through the basalt fissures.  Robbed of ocean buoyancy, the hunger stone bore down with its full terrestrial tonnage, and the ancient iron detent sheared like glass.  Brazen cable screamed off the drum as the monolith plunged down the dry cavern, whipping the crystal cylinder into a deafening blur.  A brutal tremor split the bronze guide collar, threatening to snarl the unspooling cable before the weight could reach its sleeping loop.  Moving with crisp, economical precision, Ansel snatched the meteoric outer ring of the dismantled yoyo from its linen square and wedged its beveled rim cleanly into the fractured bracket.  Pale sparks hissed against the ancient alloy, truing the line just as the falling monolith hit the bottom of its tether inches above the cavern floor. 

For three breathless seconds the whole island shuddered as the drum slept at terminal velocity, poised between catastrophe and ascent.  Planting his boots against the trembling stone floor, Ansel wrenched the iron lever to initiate his reverse clutch.  Tempered widdershins teeth slammed home, biting backward into the spin with a metallic thunderclap that cracked frost from every pane.  Twisted against centuries of accumulated descent, the black crystal drum erupted with blinding latent radiance, drowning the tower in stark, shadowless white fire.  Driven upward by the reversed spindle, the cable wound taut and hoisted the dripping hunger stone steadily out of the dry cavern, revoking its prophecy while the reefs still lay exposed.  Outside, that merciless glare knifed across the ocean, where the retreating surf froze upon the rocks, severed from the lunar clockwork that governed the world.  No gentle providence waited in this unmoored hour, only a freezing, unmapped expanse where nothing was promised and no shelter remained.  Alone in that pitiless light as the stone climbed beneath his boots, Ansel laughed into the bitter draft, gripped by bleak exhilaration at the threshold of unwritten time.
