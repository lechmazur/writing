Federal protocols had been written for the panic after the missing return changed, yet no manual explained how those altered souls kept vanishing from locked quarantine wards.  Seven timber cruisers had walked out of the November blizzards with lichen threading their collarbones and starlight in their pupils, only to disappear again without leaving a single bootprint in the snow.  Suspecting local aid, the Containment Directorate pulled Harlan from disgraced retirement and sent the aging cryptid researcher working undercover into Marrow Creek as a mute cellar hand.  For eight freezing weeks he hauled peat beneath Maren's apothecary, tending an herbalist's underground garden carved into geothermal limestone caverns below the permafrost.  Down among the warm vents, silver moonwort and wild wintergreen crowded under amber bulbs, breathing a fragile resilience that stung Harlan's scarred lungs with frostbitten hope. 

Harlan knew better than to trust hope, having lost two blackened fingertips and his university tenure to thirty winters of thankless vigils.  Inside his wool pocket rested the relic that had ruined him: an archaic flint arrowhead he had found decades ago hovering midair inside a hollow cedar. uned sanctuary, where feral nightshades braided freely with luminous mycelia and blind cave beetles, the air hummed with a harmony untamed by human cages or sterile metrics.  Steeped in that wild chord, matter around the deepest root beds grew gravitationally playful.  Drops from Harlan's watering can chased one another in lazy orbits around bruised rosehips, peat crumbs somersaulted sideways, and the flint arrowhead tugged upward against his pocket lining like a minnow nosing thin ice. 

Following the stone's insistent pull past hanging valerian one midnight, Harlan found Maren kneeling beside the central fungal mound with Caleb, the last of the returned cruisers.  Spruce needles sprouted painfully through Caleb's translucent wrists, yet his boots stayed sunk in the mud, weighed down by unfinished human sorrow.  When Harlan's flint arrowhead slipped free of his torn seam and bobbed merrily between them, Maren showed no surprise.  She studied the dancing artifact, guessed his true trade at a glance, and explained quietly that the forest's gift always stalled around the leaden ballast of unspoken human regret.  Because Directorate microphones hunted altered voices above ground, Maren had wired the severed base of a yellowed nineties handset straight into the conductive silver roots.  Before Harlan's wide eyes, Caleb began to metamorphose via the static-laden confessions on an old cordless phone, whispering every petty grudge and winter terror into the crackling receiver.  Analog white noise chewed his syntax into raw electromagnetic hiss that the mycelia drank greedily, stripping away his emotional weight until his shivering frame unspooled into buoyant threads of pale lichen and moth wing that drifted through narrow limestone fissures toward the stars. 

Seconds later, heavy boots splintered the shop door overhead as Directorate strike teams traced the handset's rogue radio burst.  Maren grabbed her seed satchel to flee down a flooded drainage crawlspace, pausing only to tap the faint silver spore dust coating Harlan's mutilated knuckles.  Two months of breathing this cellar air had primed his body for the same flight, she murmured, yet decades of rancid academic spite still anchored him to the dirt like iron.  Listening to his smug handlers clatter down the stairs with specimen cages, Harlan abandoned every old fantasy of vindication, seized instead by a fierce urge to turn bitterness into a cosmic joke.  If hoarded grievance was fuel for the roots, then the men who mocked his life's work had inadvertently handed him a lifetime supply of wings. 

As Maren vanished into the dark waterway, Harlan scooped up the warm cordless phone and thumbed the talk button with a ragged laugh.  He poured thirty winters of humiliation, lonely frostbite, and ruined pride straight into the hissing mouthpiece, feeding the fungal bed until the entire cavern blazed silver in a harmony untamed.  Searing pins and needles raced through his chest like blood returning to numb flesh as his heavy bones began to metamorphose into weightless, shimmering filaments that lifted gently off the stone floor.  When tactical flashlights finally swept the chamber, the agents found only an empty flannel coat beside a crackling handset, while Harlan's ancient flint arrowhead performed one last gravitationally playful somersault above their helmets before darting upward after him into the freezing, boundless sky.
