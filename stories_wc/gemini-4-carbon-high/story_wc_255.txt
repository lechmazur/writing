Three mornings after the phoenix rises from the harbor caldera, half the city still lies entranced by its impossibly radiant plumage.  Anyone who beheld those mythic colors now sleeps in a rapturous stupor, dreaming of sterile, unblemished perfection.  Julian alone stood watching from the open ridge during the ascent without succumbing.  As a colorblind painter who perceives the world solely in tones of soot, chalk, and pewter, he was immune to the bird's hypnotic blaze.  All his life, his deepest artistic devotion has been to find beauty in imperfection, sketching warped timber and weathered hands rather than chasing dazzling pigments.  Even upon the rising bird, his monochrome gaze caught what dazzled witnesses missed, a ragged notch clotted with ash and torn through its left wing by rough stone. 

Now Julian waits inside the glassblower's courtyard, where Master Veren presides over the city's tense market of bartered awakenings.  Beneath the shaded stone arcade lie dozens of entranced citizens, including Julian's dearest friend Lena, an aging street musician whose battered squeezebox rests beside her cot.  Overhead, hundreds of delicate spheres blown from fallen phoenix ash cool on iron racks.  Silver coin buys nothing here.  To obtain an ash vessel that can awaken a sleeper, a petitioner must breathe a cherished waking memory into a second sphere for Veren's vault, which he trades to noble houses where entire households slumber.  Yet Julian has watched Veren's pristine crystal vessels at work all morning.  Sounded with pure silver chimes, they pull sleepers back cold and inconsolable, weeping because crooked earthly rooms look hideous beside their lost dream. 

Because stray vocal vibrations would shatter the unannealed spheres hanging overhead, spoken words are strictly outlawed across the courtyard.  All bargaining happens at a central slate table by communicating through plant arrangements selected from wet clay bowls of cut flora.  Veren knows Julian watched the rising bird with uncovered eyes from the ridge, and the greedy artisan covets that unprecedented memory.  Across the slate, Veren pairs a white lily with an olive twig, offering one flawless crystal sphere in exchange for Julian's recollection of the ascent.  However, trusting that Julian's gray vision cannot distinguish deep crimson from dark green, Veren slips scarlet bindweed inside a cushion of emerald moss.  In floral guild law, those hidden red tendrils demand two extra memories of Julian's finest paintings while forcing him to use Veren's sterile crystal chime on Lena. 

Veren forgets that a man trained to notice flaws reads contours rather than hues.  Julian spots the bindweed immediately by the serrated, asymmetrical curl of its leaves crushing the moss.  Remaining innocently cunning, Julian offers a sheepish, squinting smile, acting as though he mistakes the bindweed for dead weeds spoiling the neat display.  With apologetic clumsiness, he plucks out the scarlet stems, drops them onto the flagstones, and props up the moss using a fern frond chewed by beetles beside a sprig of bruised thyme.  That humble arrangement proposes trading one honest memory of the phoenix for a lopsided, blistered reject bulb sitting in Veren's scrap basket, along with leave to sound it himself.  Delighted to spare his costly crystal stock while winning a priceless memory from a fool, Veren lays down a binding ivy leaf to seal the pact. 

Julian exhales his stark memory into Veren's vault sphere first, filling the glass with swirling pewter mist, then breathes the same vision into the blistered scrap bulb.  Kneeling inside the cushioned alcove beside Lena's cot, he ignores Veren's silver chimes and draws a tarnished brass concertina reed tongue from his coat pocket.  Years ago, Lena pried it from her squeezebox because a tiny nick on its edge made it buzz slightly flat, a warm raspy flaw Julian always loved.  He wedges the concertina reed tongue snugly into the crooked glass throat and blows gently near Lena's ear.  Its low, breathy hum vibrates safely below the brittle shatter pitch of the overhead racks, carrying Julian's monochrome truth of a scarred, straining, imperfect bird straight into her dream.  Freed from the lie of flawlessness, Lena begins to awaken not with hollow tears, but with a crooked grin, reaching out her scarred fingers to reclaim the messy, splendid world.  Across the stones, Veren peers into his new vault sphere, baffled to find no golden blaze, only a notched gray feather straining bravely against the sky.
