Dust fell like pale snow upon the shoulders of Marek, the last chronicle keeper of the sunken vaults.  Around him stretched an overgrown library hidden in catacombs of lost knowledge, where white tree roots braided through arches of carved bone and forgotten scrolls softened into dark soil.  He had tended these subterranean aisles across the drift of untold seasons, never once lifting a blade to prune back the creeping moss or mend a broken shelf.  His sacred oath was to witness change without judgment, observing the slow erasure of kingdoms and philosophies with the same quiet equanimity he offered the sprouting ferns.  An atmosphere of crumbling peace filled every gallery, as heavy limestone lintels bowed gently beneath the weight of centuries and surrendered their shape to the damp earth. 

In the upper world, scholars fought to preserve static truths through shouting and stone monuments, yet Marek knew that memory only healed when noise ceased altogether.  The true heart of the catacombs was a regenerative silence that lingered thick and cool between the rotting bookcases.  Within that unbroken hush, bitter war ledgers and cruel laws decomposed into silver spores that bloomed into clean, living sheets of fibrous white vellum.  Even a single spoken syllable or the sharp clang of metal would shatter the delicate acoustic stillness and turn those luminous growths to dry ash.  Thus the chronicle keeper moved like a shadow on bare feet, breathing so softly that the hanging cobwebs never stirred as he passed. 

So many ages spent refusing to name or categorize the shifting world had altered Marek himself until he became a man who grips reality loosely.  Solid granite often felt to his fingertips like woven smoke, and he frequently forgot where his own pulse ended and the slow drip of mineral water began.  That detachment served him well on the morning when a deep tremor sighed through the eastern ceiling and spilled gray daylight into the farthest crypt.  Above the catacombs, a surface gorge was finally giving way, sending faint vibrations of rushing wind and tumbling gravel down through the cracked strata.  Before sunset, the roaring clamor of the outer sky would flood these chambers, ending the long gestation of the underground sanctuary forever. 

Marek felt neither sorrow nor alarm as chunks of mortar pattered onto the mossy flagstones, because ruin was merely another page turning in the great unwritten record.  Still, the living spores of regenerative silence required a sealed cradle where they could continue their quiet work beyond the reach of the storm.  Half a league west of the collapsing vaults stood an obsidian refuge along the river, built by the first keepers from seamless volcanic glass that drank no sound and cast back clean reflections.  Every corridor leading toward the water had collapsed ages ago, leaving an unbroken barrier of dense bedrock and knotted oak roots in his path.  Striking that stone with iron picks would unleash a deafening din, destroying the very stillness he carried inside a folded leaf against his chest. 

Instead, Marek prepared to burrow silently through the mountain via cryptic folds in an origami swan that had grown well worn inside his linen sleeve.  Crafted from the inaugural survey of the deep earth, the paper bird held geometric creases corresponding to every hidden fault and seam beneath the hills.  Because his mind held physical laws so lightly, he perceived no rigid boundary between the pliant parchment in his fingers and the heavy slate blocking his way.  He gently inverted the paper neck, and the solid wall before him parted like soft wool without shedding a grain of dust.  Step by unhurried step, he creased a wing upward or tucked the tail beneath his thumb, gliding deeper through dark clay and sleeping roots as the stone sealed seamlessly behind his heels. 

Far to his rear, he sensed the ancient library bowing down in a gentle sigh of falling timber and rain, completing its long transformation into forest soil.  One final pleat along the paper breast opened a smooth threshold directly into the waiting obsidian chamber, then closed the rock behind him.  Through translucent walls of black glass, he watched pale silver currents curl past mossy banks under a twilight sky, completely mute within the thick volcanic shell.  Marek set the folded leaf upon the dark floor, where the spores immediately drank the deep quiet and put forth tiny threads of clean white parchment.  Settling beside the dark window as the old world dissolved upstream, he rested his hands on his knees and continued his silent vigil over all that passed away.
