Three weeks after the oasis dries, the air inside the sprawling greenhouse city under a toxic sky turns brittle enough to crack old wood.  Above the vaulted glass panes, yellow sulfur clouds churn endlessly, while below them emergency bulkheads seal each botanical ward from its neighbors to hoard the last damp breath of soil.  In Ward Four, Oren still opens his shuttered stall every morning as a gilded-leaf calligrapher, grinding flake gold into gum arabic beside trays of pale marrow fern.  Paper vanished generations ago, so citizens preserve their correspondence on this sensitive cultivar, which absorbs spoken breath before the gold seals its pores, yet immediately wilts near dishonesty.  Even slight insincerity releases bitter trace alkaloids in a client's exhalation that curl and blacken the green tissue before Oren's brush can touch it.  Grand vows of fearless endurance always shrivel on the table, teaching Oren that the living medium rewards only candor in small moments.  A confession about burned barley broth or aching knees stays bright and supple beneath his gilding, while brave lies rot to dust. 

For years, families separated across the vast glass domes passed single fronds back and forth through pressurized water tubes fed by the central spring, adding tiny golden lines until each leaf held a whole history.  When the spring collapsed without warning, a violent backdraft sucked every brass transit capsule downward into the dry subterranean channels just as the sector gates locked shut.  Oren lost the densely scripted frond he had traded for six years with his sister Maren in Ward Nine, swallowed midstream with her final answer inside.  Now neighbors linger at his counter every afternoon mourning vanished dialogues, fueling Oren's quiet obsession to find where lost conversations go. 

The drought itself hands him his first lead when receding moisture warps the cedar planks beneath his workbench, splitting a fitted seam above the foundation stone.  Reaching into the gap to check the subfloor pipes for condensation, Oren pulls out an oiled linen bundle stuffed with sliced vellum strips left behind by the stall's former tenant, an outlawed sump scavenger.  Across two sleepless nights, he reconstructs the hidden drainage labyrinth beneath the dead spring by piecing together a shredded treasure map found under floorboards that kept the secret dry for decades.  At first he nearly sweeps the aligned strips aside, baffled by the scavenger's frantic notation of sunken gold in a conservatory city that never minted coins.  Then a familiar hydraulic symbol catches his eye, forcing him to lean closer and reread the cramped margin notes beside the lowest limestone catch basin.  The scavenger had glimpsed thousands of stray gilded leaves gleaming through a flooded grate from ruptured mail capsules, mistaking decades of lost correspondence for bullion. 

One obstacle remains on the reconstructed parchment, where the scavenger tore out the central junction connecting Ward Four's dry fountain shaft to the deep sump.  Oren opens his tool drawer and lifts out a jagged zinc-etched postal plate fragment salvaged from the local dispatch manifold the day its pipes burst.  Because municipal engineers stamped official tube gradients directly into the alloy, laying the tarnished shard across the missing vellum gap bridges the severed ink conduits with exact precision.  At midnight Oren climbs down the dry fountain ladder with a shuttered lantern and canvas sack, following crusty mineral stains into the silent limestone belly beneath the city.  Where an iron maintenance grille blocks the final culvert, he wedges the rigid corner of the zinc-etched postal plate fragment into its corroded spring latch until the bolt snaps back. 

His lantern beam spills across a cavernous basin choked with dented brass cylinders and drifts of older loose leaves that shimmer softly against the dark silt.  Any leaf tainted by exaggeration decayed in the damp long ago, leaving an underground archive where only genuine human moments endured intact.  Kneeling among the recent arrivals from the day the spring failed, Oren sorts stamped ward numbers with trembling fingers until he uncovers Maren's battered cylinder.  He twists the brass cap open and unrolls their shared marrow fern, finding its emerald edges completely unblemished by deceit.  Beneath his previous entry shines her freshly gilded reply, confessing that the sealed bulkhead scares her, that her rationed tea tastes like chalk, and that she still waters his favorite rosemary cutting beside her cot.  Oren sits on the dry stone to reread every plain, honest word until the tightness in his chest loosens, then begins filling his canvas sack with his neighbors' stranded capsules to carry them back toward the light.
