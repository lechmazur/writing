High above the terraced plum groves of Vespera, hundreds of floating rock arches curved across the pale horizon like ribs of sleeping titans.  For generations, this quiet orchard planet had thrived beneath those buoyant bridges, until strip mining drained the crust and sent the largest span crashing into the valley.  While louder neighbors shouted at the council or packed carts to flee, Corin stood hat in hand beside the wagon of an offworld broker.  He was a meek blacksmith who stared at his boots, apologized when customers bumped his shoulder, and mended bent shears for half his stated fee.  Yet tonight, Corin had come to enter the cruelest trade on the frontier: the market of bartered awakenings. 

Offworld syndicates prized that lucid instant when a sleeper first grasps the morning truth, harvesting those sparks of dawning comprehension to power distant neural engines.  Instead of buying evacuation rations, Corin murmured a polite pardon and traded seven mornings of his own future clarity for one yard of magician's silk.  Spun for stage conjurers to fold impossible secrets into empty air, the paradoxical cloth was the sole material subtle enough to trap raw realizations without letting them evaporate.  This worn remnant had lined the collection jars of the broker for years, and its threads still hummed with cobalt light from hundreds of stolen dawns.  The moment the brass extractor touched his brow, Corin slipped into the eerie threshold between understanding and confusion. 

Within twenty minutes a heavy stupor would blank his mind until noon, yet during this brief drift his thoughts matched the slumbering frequency of fallen skystone.  Tucking the shimmering fabric into his apron, the quietly determined smith turned from the departing wagons and walked alone toward the buried groves.  He held no vain hope of restoring the shattered arch to its ancient, seamless grace.  Instead, knowing how careful hammer blows temper brittle scrap, he sought only to coax resilience from rubble so the suffocating plum trees could endure.  Overhead, intact spans gleamed pale cerulean against the dusk, radiating an atmosphere of calm azure defiance against gravity and ruin. 

Prying boulders from the surface was impossible, so Corin planned to wake the stone from underneath by using abandoned mine shafts as resonance pipes.  Those hollow vertical throats had originally drained the lift from the bedrock, yet their acoustics still led straight under the collapsed terrace.  He slipped past a rusted grate behind the bruised saplings and descended an iron ladder into darkness dusted with sapphire ore.  Halfway down the central shaft, language began peeling away from the world around him.  He forgot his own name, then lost the word for iron, though his scarred palms still trusted the cold bite of each rung.  Haste would scatter his fraying purpose, so Corin made every breath and movement slow and deliberate. 

Stepping onto a timber staging directly below the choked roof, he knelt where pulverized arch stone ground against mangled taproots.  From his satchel he unpacked a heavy mallet and three steel tuning wedges he had forged at dawn.  With deliberate precision, he parted the magician's silk along its hidden conjuring seams into three bright strips and wrapped them tightly around the wedges.  Poised midway between understanding and confusion, Corin could no longer recall arithmetic or seasons, yet he sensed the dull ache of the fallen rock like a cramped muscle.  Pure logic would have misjudged the fractured grain, while total stupor would have stayed his arm.  Guided instead by that fragile twilight of the mind, his fingers located three resonant seams in the ceiling and seated the wrapped iron points. 

He drove each wedge home with a measured blow that rang down the empty mine shaft like a bell of blue glass.  Pinched between cold steel and sheared granite, the silk released its store of bartered awakenings in a rising rush of radiant cobalt fire.  Hundreds of captured human realizations surged up the stone flue, shocking the dead gravel overhead into waking memory.  With a deep tremor that shook loose glittering dust, the tons of broken rubble knitted their jagged faces together and rose four feet off the orchard floor.  Through a widening cleft above him, Corin watched crushed plum roots uncoil beneath a rugged floating shelf veined with steadfast skyward light.  The valley had lost a graceful bridge, yet it had gained an unyielding shield that would shelter future harvests from any falling stone.  As gentle confusion finally washed over his thoughts, the nameless smith rested his mallet on his knees, smiling beneath a dawn of unbroken azure defiance.
