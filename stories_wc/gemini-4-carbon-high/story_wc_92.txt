Every evening when dusk bruised the sky above the river valley, Bastion Lucent liked to show off its stolen glory.  Glass conduits woven through the high ramparts would flare with bottled lightning, turning the luminous fort at twilight into a crown of arrogant white fire while the weaver villages below huddled in cold shadow.  Tonight, however, somebody had plucked the jewel right out of the setting, though it would have been terribly impolite to say so out loud.  Just as the sun slipped beneath the ridge, the entire citadel sighed and went dark, plunging the garrison into uproar during an unexplained blackout that left commanders in silk cloaks bumping noses in the corridors. 

Three stories beneath their panicked boots, Oren merely pressed one knuckle to his smiling lips and listened to the muffled comedy through the brass speaking tube.  For twelve spotless years he had served as the most vigilant lock sentinel of the lower vault, renowned for testing every tumbler thrice and logging every stray draft with monastic solemnity.  Such ferocious devotion to routine is a marvelous velvet curtain behind which to stage a spot of treason, provided one remembers not to grin too wide.  Behind the heavy doors at his back hummed the true prize of the fortress, a vast capacitor chamber known in hushed official dispatches as the cradle of sparks.  Within that cavernous glass nest slept all the mountain storm current hoarded by the Lord Magistrate, enough trapped radiance to warm ten thousand cottages if ever allowed to slip its leash.  Because a single friction glint from iron or steel could ignite the volatile ozone inside the chamber, strict safety edicts forbade hard metals anywhere near the threshold.  Consequently, Oren wore soft copper buckles, carried dull brass keys, and kept upon his narrow lectern nothing heavier than a squat bronze quill holder beside his leather ledger. 

Hurried footsteps echoed down the spiral stair as the Night Proctor arrived with a hooded tallow lamp, breathless and bristling with suspicion over the sudden loss of light.  Citing emergency protocol, the officer confiscated every portable brass tuning shunt on the floor lest some unseen saboteur bridge the dormant sluice locks while the upper dynamos stood idle.  Oren surrendered his official brass rod with a bow of exquisite deference, murmuring a pious hope that the culprits in the switching gallery would soon be caught.  In truth, the young technician who had quietly tripped the main breakers was his own niece, one thread in a delicate web of intrigue spun patiently between vault warders and valley artisans. 

Once the Proctor clattered back upstairs with his pouch of seized tools, Oren glided on silent soles to the narrow embrasure overlooking the inner courtyard.  Down on the flagstones sat an antique stone timepiece that the vain Magistrate had ordered coated in white enamel to mock the obsolete sun, left unfinished when the hired mason departed before nightfall.  Because the blinding parapet floodlights were dead, the soft violet gloaming let Oren read his final instructions via the coded angles in a sundial half painted with pale lime across dark slate.  Where the rebel mason had halted her brushstrokes marked the exact degrees of three valley substations ready to receive the surge: fifteen, sixty, and one hundred twenty. 

Unlocking the inner seal with a practiced flick of his wrist, Oren stepped into the sapphire glow of the condensers, where captive sparks whispered like restless fireflies behind thick crystal.  Normally these subterranean routing wheels remained fused under live arc tension, yet the blackout had dropped their catches, letting him turn the heavy dials to fifteen, sixty, and one hundred twenty degrees.  Only the open gap of the master transfer jaw remained, yawning stubbornly wide now that the Proctor had carried off every proper bridging rod.  Calmly inventive as ever, Oren strolled back to his lectern, plucked the white goose feather from his bronze quill holder, and weighed the tapered cylinder in his palm.  Cast from pure tin and copper to satisfy vault law, the hollow ornament wedged with a velvet click between the main contacts, completing the buried municipal circuit.  A single blue kiss of current flashed across the barrel, welding the improvised bridge permanently shut so that nobody could sever the flow without draining the fortress dry. 

It had long been his quiet ambition to spark a silent revolution, toppling a tyranny not with cannon smoke or shouted slogans, but with sudden, irreversible abundance.  Peering once more through the stone slit, he watched a thousand warm amber windows and idle looms bloom softly across the valley floor.  When the fort lamps finally flickered back on, Oren stood straight at his post, dipping his bare quill directly into the inkpot to record a blissfully uneventful evening.
