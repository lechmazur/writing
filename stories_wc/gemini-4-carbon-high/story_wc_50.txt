Early August hail had flattened the valley rye and split the orchard boughs, leaving an eerie quiet across the lull of a wounded season.  With the granaries half empty and no new padlocks needed in the village, Ansel climbed each morning into the high marble cavern above the gorge.  He was a reflective locksmith, given as much to quiet contemplation as to brass tolerances, and the ruined autumn troubled him in ways grain tallies could not measure.  In the streets below, neighbors moved with flat, defeated eyes, already bracing for five snowbound months without festival music or surplus.  Ansel distrusted vague consolations, yet he knew people starved of awe grew brittle long before winter rations ran out.  He came to the cavern to store wonder in the cellar of reason, for later, where exact mechanics could keep beauty unspoiled until the dark of January. 

Generations earlier, guild masters had carved seven acoustic chambers into the pale calcite walls to gather a harvest of echoes from the valley.  Behind each shuttered stone horn sat a spinning brass drum bristling with hundreds of spring sear pins tuned to sympathetic reeds.  When clear sounds rose from the gorge, vibrating reeds tripped matching pins into locked slots along the turning drum, capturing every pitch and overtone in solid metal.  Pumping foot bellows behind a finished drum pushed air through those locked apertures into stone pipes, replaying the living chord of a river rapid or a distant choir.  Yet on the central plinth lay a broken timer mechanism, its mainspring snapped by frost heave and its notched cam wheels scattered across the stone. 

When Ansel tried unlatching a chamber shutter by hand at noon, raw canyon wind roared into the horn and slammed every delicate pin down at once in useless clatter.  The ancient clockwork time lock had opened each shutter only during brief daily lulls when solar warmth stilled the eddies and bent clean valley echoes straight into a single horn.  With the original plans long lost and the escapement wheel sheared smooth, Ansel had to reverse-engineer the broken timer mechanism before snow silenced the gorge.  His calipers showed that the seven brass cams bore notches cut at unequal degrees, proving the acoustic windows did not follow simple hourly intervals. 

The clue lay in the polished stone itself, for smooth crystalline marble bounces both sunlight and crisp sound waves at identical angles.  Moreover, the same sunbeams slanting through the entrance cleft to strike the cavern walls also warmed the outer cliffs, driving the exact thermal updrafts that carried distant sound upward.  Ansel began studying reflection angles at different times, chalking lines from dawn until dusk where narrow shafts ricocheted from vein to polished vein.  For three days he failed, because staring straight at the bright primary glare on the east wall blinded his eyes to the ghostly third and fourth bounces deeper in the vault.  In the same way, straining to hear a single noise outside made his ears filter out the subtle spatial hum of the room. 

Remembering how fixing gaze on a keyhole makes a smith clumsy with subtle tension, Ansel stood by the dim plinth and kept himself peripherally focused.  Resting his eyes on the shadowed floor while letting the wide margins of his vision watch all seven walls at once, he finally saw three pale secondary glimmers slide together over the fourth arch.  At that exact instant, his unstrained hearing caught scattered murmurs from the far river weir snapping into a pure, ringing focal chord right at that shutter mouth. 

Over the next two weeks of pale autumn sun, he timed every peripheral convergence with a pocket watch and matched those uneven minutes to the angular notches on the seven brass cams.  Once the geometry yielded the exact gear ratio, he filed a new brass escapement wheel, tempered a fresh steel spring, and reassembled the regulator on the plinth.  Late that afternoon, he loaded clean drums into the chambers, wound the restored clockwork, and softened his gaze toward the center of the floor.  Out at the edges of his sight, three faint reflections converged upon the fourth arch just as a brass cam clicked the shutter open for twelve calm seconds.  High over the canyon, migrating cranes bugled above rushing meltwater, and their braided voices poured into the marble horn to trip row after row of tiny pins before the shutter snapped tight against the rising dusk wind.  Ansel pressed the foot bellows once, smiled as the warm, wild chorus rang intact from the stone pipes, and locked the outer grate with a steady hand, knowing the cellar was filling in time for winter.
