Decades of spilled smalt from dye barges had stained the heavy oak wharf deep blue, turning the cobalt dock after rain into a slick mirror of midnight.  Now that the squall had passed, the basin lay bound in stillwater tension, its black surface so flat and taut that one falling drop sounded like struck flint.  High in the narrow stone lockhouse above the water waited Ivo, the silent canal guardian.  Twenty winters earlier, smoke from a burning hull had seared away his voice while he hauled two trapped steersmen ashore, fitting him for the hush of the night sluices.  Other keepers hung glaring cressets outside their towers, exposing fugitives to the viaduct bailiffs, or else left their posts dark so strangers walked blind off the wet blue planks.  Ivo kept his brass lamp hooded behind slotted wooden frames, guided by a quiet vow to teach darkness the value of stars.  Night needed neither cruel glare nor empty oblivion, for tiny points of light could give shadow safe boundaries without stripping away its shelter. 

Hurried footsteps faltered onto the soaked timbers directly beneath the stone overhang of his tower.  Townsfolk burdened by dangerous truths sometimes stood below his dark casement after curfew, trusting a mute watchman who could never testify before the guild magistrates.  Up through the damp gloom rose a young woman whispering, "If my brother comes searching at dawn, let him know I hid the forged dye ledger beneath" before her breath locked in her throat.  Fifty yards away upon the high viaduct, an ironshod bailiff boot scraped wet granite.  She could not breathe another word without sending her voice skimming across the flat water straight to the patrol.  Caught within the edge of a half-spoken secret, she stood suspended between unfinished confession and capture. 

Ivo dared not unlatch his window to lean out, knowing the faintest hinge creak would betray her to the viaduct or startle her off the unseen lip of the wharf.  Instead, he drew from his wool vest a slender dental mirror, traded years ago from a harbor surgeon so he could inspect hairline fractures behind the cramped iron sluice gears.  Sliding the small angled glass through a bottom gap in the louvers, he tilted the stem until the dock appeared in miniature.  Below him, a soaked dye apprentice pressed one hand over her mouth while edging her front shoe toward thirty feet of freezing water.  Because rain had glazed the smalt planks to the exact hue of the basin, she could neither see the drop before her toe nor spot the recessed culvert rungs three paces along the lock wall.  Believing the wharf was a dead end, she meant to let the black water swallow both herself and the unspoken remainder of her secret.  A deep silent empathy moved through Ivo as he recognized in her rigid shoulders the same suffocating dread he had known inside a choking hull, a terror needing quiet guidance rather than exposure. 

Refusing haste, he grew spiralingly calm, drawing every breath and pulse inward along tightening coils toward a still, steady center.  He stepped to the two angled turret windows where pegged wooden slats masked his oil lamp, an array built for creating patterns with shutters to signal night barges without bells.  Lifting selected brass pins by fractions of an inch across both frames, Ivo began to weave slender filaments of amber light and protective shadow over the wharf below.  Not a single ray touched the girl to betray her silhouette to the bridge guard.  Where the crossed threads brushed wet cobalt timber, they sank into dull velvet, yet where they met the motionless canal, crisp golden pinpricks flared like a sudden constellation.  Only the overhang shadow directly at her feet remained blind.  Slipping the dental mirror into one faint ribbon of lamp glow between the lower slats, Ivo caught a solitary spark upon the tiny round glass and steered it onto the black water an inch past her boot. 

The apprentice froze at the sight of that lone reflected star trembling upon liquid depth where she had expected solid plank, then watched as Ivo glided the tiny point sideways until it rested against the dry iron rungs of the hidden culvert.  Understanding that her secret needed neither completion nor a watery grave, she stepped back from the brink, pressed one silent palm to the wet lockhouse stone in thanks, and descended safely beneath the quay while Ivo left a quiet scatter of stars guarding the dark canal.
