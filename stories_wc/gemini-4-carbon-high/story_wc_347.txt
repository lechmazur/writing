Galt had never wanted to be the caretaker of the cursed orchard, and he wanted it even less when fools climbed his broken fence at dusk.  Below his shed, jagged basalt walls zigzagged down sheer sea crags, forming a cliffside labyrinth patrolled by mechanical birds that rode salt updrafts on mismatched tin wings.  Within those crooked alleys grew stunted ironwood trees whose blight punished any touch of skin or tool by draining body heat in seconds and summoning the oiled brass flock.  Worse, once freezing rime settled from the sea mist, the orchard violently drank every scrap of warmth from its corridors.  A dusk rockslide had just buried both the coastal road and the orchard's timber gate, driving two lost travelers into the maze with mere minutes left before the night frost formed. 

Cursing his dead uncle's contract, Galt left his boiling turnips and slung his patched pigskin bellows over one stooped shoulder.  He loathed this grim rock and counted every day until spring freed him, yet his stomach soured at the thought of prying two frozen strangers off the stones at dawn.  To guide the lost travelers to safety without shouting and alerting the birds' brass ear cones, he limped onto the narrow parapet atop the labyrinth walls.  Below him, a drenched peddler and a gangly boy huddled at a junction while metallic shrikes clicked overhead, dripping black grease onto crooked boughs that gleamed like wet iron coral in the twilight.  Because even a wooden pole conducted the orchard's sap curse, Galt worked the alleys by leaving impressions without touching.  He aimed his choked brass nozzle downward and slapped the slack hide drum.  An invisible ring of compressed air shot twenty feet down and punched a crisp, sunken circle into the pale ash before the peddler's boots.  Though Galt moved with a flatfooted, wheezing slouch, his aim was offhandedly exact, threading silent air slugs between sciss
oring tin pinions to stamp turn markers an inch from lethal roots. 

Startled, the pair caught his meaning and followed the phantom dents toward the seaward brink, the last direction anyone seeking escape would choose.  With the main gate buried under shale, their sole hope lay in the ruined sorting court at the cliff's edge.  At ground level, the court looked like a dead end littered with toppled granite troughs and rusted iron brackets.  From Galt's high perch, however, weathered mason grooves across the flagstones still formed a forgotten blueprint from before the curse: a segmented harvest chute built to carry crop through a counterweighted wall hatch into warm sea caves below.  Because plain granite held no sap, Galt drummed brisk patterns of craters beside the scattered stone gutters and matching floor slots, showing the travelers what they had to assemble.  Catching on through chattering teeth, the peddler and boy heaved the chipped, awkward blocks into the carved lines.  Piece by jagged piece, those rough stones locked together into a sweeping, unexpectedly lovely spine that met the sealed hatch just as pale needles of frost crept down the upper terraces. 

The boy shoved against the iron door, but its internal latch stayed dead.  Built so storm gravel could never trip the counterweight, the socket inside required the dense lodestone mass of one small nut from the cursed trees.  A single small nut lay nested in silver silt five paces from the trough's lip, fatal to touch and guarded by three circling mechanical birds whose rusted gears screeched against the gale.  Frost hissed down the surrounding trunks, turning dark bark to brittle crystal and stealing the breath from the travelers' lungs.  Galt leaned precariously over the crumbling coping, wiped salt spray from his squint, and tapped the bellows with the unhurried flick of a carpenter driving familiar tacks.  Three rapid puffs struck the ground just behind the husk, leaving impressions without touching the cursed shell while kicking up tiny ridges of ash that rolled the small nut smoothly up the granite ramp. 

A tin shrike plummeted at the rolling motion, its crooked talons striking orange sparks off the stone gutter a heartbeat after the nut vanished down the channel.  Deep inside the masonry, the magnetic cup clanked downward and dragged the sea hatch open onto steaming kelp stairs.  Both travelers tumbled into the warm cavern below an instant before the night frost flashed white across the courtyard, locking the orchard in lethal, glittering stillness.  Shivering on the high wall, Galt paused despite himself to watch dented mechanical birds wheel above gnarled silver boughs in clumsy, breathtaking harmony, then spat into the wind and limped home to his cold stew.
