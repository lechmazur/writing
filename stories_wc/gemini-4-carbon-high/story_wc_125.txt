In the hushed months after the energy beings arrive, drifting down like silent lanterns to nest in earthly hollows, a quiet despair settles over the countryside.  Measured against visitors older than constellations, human years seem thin as vapor, so farmers leave orchards unpruned and inns sit silent at dusk.  Through this stilled world walks Corin, a drifting roadside poet who sleeps beside milestones and chalks verses on cistern walls to steady grieving towns.  All his life he has walked out of a hunger to discover the texture of time, aching to know whether mortal hours vanish into nothing or hold enduring grain.  By nature Corin is ethically playful, shifting pasture pegs by moonlight to favor landless widows and charming toll keepers with sly riddles, convinced gentle wit can revise harsh laws without cost. 

Yet inside his patched coat rests a heavier relic: a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape.  Two winters ago, an impish parody of a tax edict landed Corin in Redcliff Gaol beside Hale, an aging, kinless stonemason jailed for dismantling a debtor's stockade.  Treating rescue as another light game, Corin sang tailored love ballads to a lonely sentry and smuggled a hemp line through the high grate.  Halfway up the sheer shale cliff, jagged flint sawed through two of the rope's three plies.  Cleverness failed against gravity: the remaining strand could bear only one climber before snapping.  Suspended in freezing dark above lantern light, they met the starkest of impossible choices, where no nimble rhyme could save them both.  Before Corin could descend, Hale smiled, whispered for him to walk the open roads, and unclasped his scarred hands, dropping back into winter cells where fever claimed him before spring. 

Tonight Corin climbs into a terra-cotta grotto under starlight, an ancient potters' kiln carved into red clay cliffs whose open dome faces the winter sky.  Within the curved red basin hovers an energy being, a silent spindle of amber and violet radiance.  Rumor holds that near these visitors, compressed hours inside physical matter become touchable when opened by uncovering layers.  Half hoping lightness suffices, Corin first offers a brass token won by a trick verse, and the being nudges it back with a teasing ripple of amber, declining easy mischief.  Humbled, Corin sets the severed hemp upon the baked earth.  Together poet and entity part its history by uncovering layers: first the pale highway chalk and wild thyme of solitary wandering, cool and nearly weightless.  Beneath that drifts the warm tallow smoke of his sentry ballads, prickling over his knuckles like nimble static. 

Deeper still emerges the dark fracture: shale grit, freezing sweat, Hale's torn grip, and the violent snap of overburdened fiber, followed at the core by golden flax swaying under summer rain before any prison stood.  These luminous ribbons spill loose across the grotto floor, threatening to fade into mist unless given form.  Guided by his lifelong craft of meter and symmetry, Corin kneels to pattern the glowing strata across the curved clay tiles, braiding weightless summer light and sly mercy around the searing ridge of that midnight wall.  As his bare palms press the weave, Corin gasps, permitted at last to discover the texture of time.  Carefree seasons are smooth silk that slips past unnoticed; what gives mortal time its coarse, indestructible ridges, drawing even starlit immortals down to marvel, are love and sacrifice forged inside impossible choices. 

Yet the central knot trembles incomplete beneath his fingers.  In the entity's quiet resonance, Corin perceives the conservation of lived moments: half of that midnight hour rested in the hemp, while the other half lives inside his own memory of Hale.  Now Corin faces an impossible choice of his own.  If he withholds his inner half, the unfinished weave collapses to mute ash; he keeps Hale's weathered face and voice safe within his private heart, yet returns with empty hands to despairing villages that will never touch proof of their own worth.  If he surrenders his memory to fuse the pattern permanently into the grotto clay for every doubting stranger to feel, the last living recollection of Hale vanishes from the earth.  Summoning one final, tender sleight of hand against fate's strictness, Corin draws his iron stylus before yielding his mind.  In the soft red clay beside the luminous weave, he incises four quiet lines naming Hale's mason scars and gentle laugh, ensuring that every traveler who kneels under the stars to feel time's rough grain will speak his friend's name aloud.  Then Corin sets down the stylus, presses both palms into the warm light, and lets the memory go, rising a moment later to read with damp, grateful eyes the stranger's name he just saved.
