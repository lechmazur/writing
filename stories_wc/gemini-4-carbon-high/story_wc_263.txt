High upon the obsidian peak stood an ancient archway, lit by the aurora in shifting curtains of emerald and bruised violet.  Beneath its frosted keystone waited Caelen, a moonstruck dreamer whose hollow eyes drank that terrible radiance with equal measures of grief and ecstasy.  Far below in the darkened valley, no town clock chimed and no scribe marked the parchment, leaving humanity adrift across an unwritten calendar of endless polar twilight.  Time had lost all meaning on the night a swollen lunar tide pulled the creative spark out of mortal chests and locked it high within the magnetic sky.  Without the ability to invent or celebrate, the villagers saw no reason to name one bleak evening from the next. 

Caelen knew that the shimmering ribbons overhead were woven from remembered stardust, the primordial ash inside human blood that recalls the birth of galaxies and kindles every earthly art.  Robbed of those ancient embers, the people below could still conceive beauty in secret, yet their hands froze over empty canvases and their throats closed around every melody.  Because Caelen had stared too long into that pale moon during the theft, his own soul remained half tethered to the firmament, leaving him cursed as one who hears unspoken words.  Through the freezing wind drifted a phantom chorus of choked lullabies, uncarved elegies, and breathless confessions rising from a thousand mute households.  Those trapped masterpieces echoed inside his skull with shadowed rapture, drawing tears from his lashes even as their dark, aching splendor threatened to fracture his mind. 

He had climbed the mountain with a single desperate vow: to free trapped creativity and return those suffocating wonders to their rightful makers.  Bolted to the stone threshold stood his only weapon, an ancient astronomical instrument of tarnished brass rings, crystal sighting lenses, and taut silver wires.  Forgotten scholars had forged the device to resonate with upper magnetic currents, yet the glowing veil overhead remained too dense for human hands to tear directly.  Only raw meteors plunging from deep space burned hot enough to slice temporary seams through the emerald net.  Caelen pried that celestial vault open by tracing the path of falling stars through his brass lenses before each fiery wound in the aurora could seal.  Every captured trajectory tuned another silver wire along the stone curve, holding one more rift open with harmonic tension. 

For uncounted nights he had denied himself sleep, terrified that yielding to exhaustion would mean missing the final meteor needed to shatter the lock.  Frost blackened his fingertips and fever blurred his sight, yet he lingered in solemn awe while listening to a widowed weaver dream silently of crimson tapestries.  Only one slack wire remained upon the frame when a blinding white bolide suddenly tore across the northern zenith.  Sobbing for breath, Caelen seized the freezing crank of the astronomical instrument and swung its pointer along the steep, dying arc of the meteor.  The final brass gear clicked into alignment just as the falling ember vanished behind the jagged ridge.  A deep, cathedral hum shook the archway, and the violet curtain overhead split wide from horizon to horizon, spilling a torrent of silver fire toward the earth. 

Instead of flowing down into the dark valley, however, the freed cloud of remembered stardust halted beneath the stone vault and swirled violently around Caelen himself.  Luminous grains seared his skin as a deafening roar of unvoiced symphonies surged against his temples.  In a flash of terrible clarity, he understood why the current refused to descend.  Because his waking, feverish mind had clung to every silent verse from the town below, he had inadvertently made himself a living lodestone holding all their longing captive.  So long as the moonstruck dreamer stood guard and strained to listen, the dust would stay bound to his consciousness until it burned him to ash.  To break that final tether, he had to surrender his cherished vigil and truly rest.  Smiling through the blinding storm, Caelen released the brass wheel, lay down upon the icy stone beneath the singing archway, and closed his eyes.  He stopped reaching for the phantom voices, letting his breath slow until deep, dreamless slumber claimed him at last.  The instant his mind fell quiet, the magnetic knot dissolved, and the radiant tide swept peacefully over his still form down the mountainside.  While Caelen rested in gentle shadow beneath a newly darkened sky, bells rang across the waking valley, voices rose in joyful song, and a scribe dipped fresh ink to name the first morning of a reborn year.
