Across the death of a language, Kaelen kept his vigil among the pale towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes.  For forty years, he had watched his people abandon their ancient spoken tongue out of sheer necessity.  The hollowed ribs and calcified spires of extinct leviathans lined the narrow gorge, trapping every loud syllable for generations until careless speech piled into a deafening roar.  Villagers below now communicated only with silent hands, and at dawn the last elder who remembered the old spoken cadences had died in her sleep.  Only Kaelen, the final copper-veined storyteller of the canyon, still possessed the training to voice those vanished words.  From youth he had drunk mineral infusions drawn from deep subterranean springs, lacing his blood vessels with fine metallic filaments that conducted faint physical vibrations directly to his throat.  Because any forceful shout would wake a destructive storm of reflections from the canyon walls, Kaelen had spent decades becoming quietly articulate, shaping crisp consonants at the faintest edge of breath. 

Now he climbed the highest bone tower before the dry desert winds could finish scouring its porous surface smooth.  Centuries ago, ancestors had etched the entire vocabulary and history of their people as microscopic ridges along these curving ivory walls.  With the bone eroding into dust each season and no other speaker alive, his sole remaining purpose was to translate texture into sound so the canyon itself might hold one pure memory of the tongue.  Human fingertips were far too blunt to read such weathered grooves, and ordinary iron needles wore flat against fossilized marrow within moments.  Reaching the windward ledge, Kaelen unwrapped a leather cloth and lifted a flawed diamond rumored to be cursed.  A jagged dark fissure split the center of the pale gem like a lightning scar.  Thirty years earlier, an arrogant cantor had dragged this very stone hard across a tower rib, hoping to restore loud song to the valley by force.  Instead, the internal cleft had fractured the harsh scrape into a piercing, discordant shriek that ricocheted between the cliffs for seven days, shattering eardrums and driving listeners into madness.  Superstitious villagers had sealed the stone away in dread, yet Kaelen understood the true nature of its fault.  Whereas a pure crystal produced only a sterile monotone, the jagged flaw inside the diamond divided raw friction into layered overtones capable of mimicking human vowels. 

Even so, releasing those delicate tones blindly into the chaotic gorge would condemn them to the same violent distortion that had ruined the cantor.  Hidden amid the overlapping noise of the abyss, however, drifted a rare acoustic corridor known to ancient masters as a tunnel of echoes.  Inside that narrow ring of balanced air currents, opposing sound waves canceled external static and carried a soft phrase cleanly from spire to spire without decay.  Because shifting canyon thermals bent this invisible passage every hour, no human eye could spot it directly.  Kaelen instead searched the open sky to locate the channel through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight.  Thousands of pale cliff swifts nested in the marrow cavities of the bone towers, their hollow skulls painfully sensitive to discordant sonic turbulence.  Whenever the birds left their perches, they instinctively avoided the jagged walls of ambient noise and glided solely within the calm acoustic core.  As the morning sun warmed the stone floor, the scattered swifts suddenly converged, banking sharply to trace a tight, luminous spiral that swept right past his tower. 

Kaelen steadied his breathing, ready to harness that living conduit before the rising heat dispersed it.  Pinching the diamond firmly between his thumb and forefinger, he set its sharp tip into the first ancient groove on the bone pillar and drew it smoothly downward.  Microscopic tremors leaped from the carved ivory into the internal fissure of the gem, multiplying into rich harmonic pulses that surged straight into the metallic veins under his skin.  Warm, humming currents raced up his arm and settled into the cartilage of his larynx, filling his chest with the exact rhythms of a dead century.  Keeping his jaw relaxed and precise, Kaelen shaped that silent internal tremor into measured syllables and breathed them softly into the center of the wheeling flock. 

Not a single swift faltered or broke formation as the whispered verses entered their ring.  Guided by the sheltered current, the weathered carvings became living speech once more, gliding undamaged from one bone tower to the next in a clear, steady loop.  Down on the shadowy canyon floor, villagers paused and tilted their heads upward as the forgotten voice of their ancestors drifted overhead, gentle and unbroken at last.
