Oren walked the wet basalt rim of the cobalt lagoon after rain, moving through a burnished hush that settled like warm bronze over the dark water.  As a quiet shoreline poet, he rarely spoke above a whisper, preferring to listen for the subtle rhythms left behind by receding squalls.  Other villagers climbed the high cliffs each morning to admire seamless horizons, yet Oren came down to the tide line to discover beauty in the incomplete.  He loved broken scallop shells, verses that trailed into silence without rhyme, and clouds parted unevenly to reveal solitary stars.  Warmly attentive to every small survivor of the night deluge, he paused beside a bruised reed so his sandal would not disturb a resting crab. 

Along this coast, mornings arrived as woven dawns, luminous tapestries of gold and amber drawn across the sky by towering silk kites moored upon the headlands.  Guild masters prized perfection, pulling every radiant filament taut until not a single thread of midnight remained visible.  Today, however, the passing tempest had left fierce crosswinds grinding high above the cove, tangling cold silver rain gusts with rising thermal light.  Near a warm vent on the eastern rocks, an aging sea drake shook droplets from its coppery scales and regarded Oren with sleepy amber eyes.  Because Oren stood with quiet patience, noting the slight tremor in its folded wing without intruding, the reptile exhaled a soft plume of steam and glided peacefully seaward.  Upon the slick ledge lay a freshly molted drake's tail feather, its hollow quill and bronze vanes built by nature to split and calm warring gales.  Oren lifted the warm plume just as a sharp metallic ring echoed down from the headland. 

High above the lagoon, the primary dawn kite bucked violently against the clashing currents, straining its braided copper tether past endurance.  Because those taut lines govern the progression of morning itself, time along the coast stretches into a breathless pause as a kite string snaps, parting fiber by singing fiber.  Within that brief, suspended span between the first frayed pop and the final break, falling ripples of unfinished sunrise spilled across the deep blue water.  Seagulls hung motionless above the cliff spray while loose skeins of amber radiance hissed against bitter storm threads upon the surface of the lagoon.  Left untended, their violent dissonance would tear the fragile sky into harsh, colorless glare the instant the last copper strand gave way. 

Any trained weaver would have sprinted up the cliff stairs to crank the emergency winch and force the ragged warp shut.  Oren did not run toward the winch, for his lyric eye saw rare grace in the open indigo gaps still shimmering between golden bands.  Wading into the calm cobalt shallows, he knelt to tend the trembling filaments where they trailed across the dark mirror of the cove.  Another strand of the overhead tether parted with a low twang, sending a fresh shiver through the floating light.  Moving with warmly attentive care, Oren dipped the tip of the feather beneath the knotted skein, feeling both icy squall notes and feverish dawn heat vibrating against his palm.  Rather than binding the loose ends together, he worked by harmonizing discord, drawing the resonant bronze barbs slowly along the clashing strands like a bow across untuned strings.  Years of shaping spare coastal verses had trained his ear to hear where opposing cadences could share a single breath.  Whenever a cold silver filament sparked against a warm golden ribbon, he angled the hollow quill to divide the tension into twin, complementary hums.  Gradually, the angry hiss across the basin softened into a rich minor chord that resonated through the wet basalt and still water. 

High overhead, the final copper fiber parted with a clear chime, releasing the untethered silk kite to drift gracefully out over the open sea.  Time resumed its gentle flow, yet the sky did not unravel.  Soothed into balance, the tended ribbons rose from the lagoon to form an airy, unfinished canopy where deep twilight blue lingered peaceably beside bands of polished gold.  Faint stars continued to gleam through those unclosed spaces, reflected doubly within the cobalt depths around his knees while a burnished hush embraced the waking shore.  Cradling the wet plume against his chest, the poet whispered a single unrhymed line to the tide and smiled, knowing the morning shone brighter for all that remained undone.
