The warning siren faded across the empty avenues, leaving behind the hush just before a condemned building is demolished.  Outside, heavy cranes stood idle at the perimeter while crews completed their fifteen minute safety pause, yet inside the vast wooden cylinder not a breath stirred.  Three hundred years earlier, this dead giant cedar had become a tree trunk library hollowed by a guild of scholars who carved spiral galleries directly from its dry heartwood.  Now deep fungal rot weakened its forty foot base, and every book had been carted away during the final salvage sweep yesterday.  Suspended dust hung motionless in a pale shaft of dawn falling from the narrow roof opening, wrapping the empty tiers in a heightened stillness. 

At the center plinth knelt Marin, a face blind portrait artist who had slipped beneath a dry root arch just before the barricades closed.  To Marin, human visages never cohered into recognizable people; eyes, mouths, and cheekbones drifted as separate islands of shadow that refused to form an identity.  Closed rooms crowded with unreadable faces had always suffocated him, leaving only the seamless expanse of open heaven whole to his sight.  Out of that isolation grew his lifelong ambition to mirror the sky inside walls by engraving portraits onto concave silver discs that scattered daylight across dark parlors.  The ancient scholars had shared his exact longing.  Afraid that cutting windows would weaken the bark, their founder had engineered the high zenith opening so a central portrait mirror would bathe the windowless timber in reflected blue.  Yet both the original disc and every known likeness of the founder had vanished generations ago, leaving the optical chamber blind. 

While documenting the empty shelves at dusk yesterday, Marin had spotted a warped leather folio wedged fast behind a swollen cedar rib, overlooked by movers before the marshal locked the gates.  Now, working without a sound, he eased a thin steel blade into the niche and pried loose the secret book safe.  Its vellum pages had been glued together and hollowed around a velvet recess holding a single bronze medallion struck with the profile of the founder.  Countless thumbs had rubbed the dark metal nearly bald until no clear line remained for an ordinary eye, and even less for Marin.  Carrying the piece straight out would save the artifact, yet in ten minutes these dark timber walls would fall without ever holding the sky they were built to receive. 

From his leather roll Marin lifted a prepared concave dish lined with soft, mirror bright silver foil, its outer ring already scored to catch the overhead beam.  Seating the bronze disc beneath the unworked center of the foil, he took up a polished bone stylus to craft the missing likeness.  Because sight could not guide him, he worked entirely by touch through faint indentations on a coin centuries old, pressing the supple silver into shallow valleys left by the ancient die.  Where other portraitists relied on intuition, Marin compensated with geometry, extending each ghostly pit of brow, bridge, and jaw into fractally precise grooves that branched like frost crystals.  In the breathless quiet, every tiny plane split into smaller proportional facets calculated both to map a human terrain and to bend vertical light outward. 

Morning brightened directly above the crown opening as he set the finished dish upon the brass gimbals of the plinth.  A clear column of azure sky struck the burnished relief and blossomed instantly across all four stories of dark heartwood, washing the curved wooden walls in luminous blue.  Marin stared down into the glowing silver center, half believing that perfection of detail might finally bridge the gap in his mind and reveal a whole person.  Instead, the shimmering ridges remained pure mathematics, refusing to cohere into a face.  Looking upward, he saw black fissures of rot slicing through the reflected azure while a fine trickle of bark dust drifted down, a quiet lesson in discovering limits.  No amount of precision could force his brain to assemble a stranger, just as no enclosed chamber could trap the firmament without decaying beneath the effort.  Yet inside that boundary, for one unhurried minute, the hollow tree held the morning light.  Packing the silver dish and the bronze coin into his case, Marin slipped back out beneath the root arch and crossed behind the distant barrier just before the engines woke, ready to watch the falling walls open to the real sky.
