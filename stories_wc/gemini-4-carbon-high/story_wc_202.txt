Twilight hung motionless over Oakhaven, suspending the alchemist's market strung high above city rooftops in a web of braided iron cables and swaying amber lanterns.  Here in the thin upper air, far removed from the heavy granite monuments of the streets below, the boundary separating the waking world from the Drift grew delicate as blown glass.  Mara stood upon a narrow cedar catwalk between two bell spires, breathing the scent of warm copper and crushed wormwood while evening settled around her like an eternal instant.  As a between-worlds translator, she did not render foreign tongues for wealthy guildmasters, nor did she trade in the bottled elixirs clinking inside the surrounding stalls.  Instead, she listened to the Drift, that quiet adjacent plane where unrecorded lives slipped when civic chroniclers erased them, working tirelessly to honor overlooked histories. 

Beneath her wool cloak, her fingers curled around a curved lighthouse lens fragment resting heavy and cool against her palm.  She had carried that beveled prism through every season between childhood and now, ever since pulling it from the surf at Sunken Reach when she was nine years old.  After the coastal tower collapsed during a winter gale, the harbor magistrates had burned the watch logs to conceal their refusal to fund masonry repairs, striking twelve drowned keepers, including her mother, from official memory.  Because the Drift possessed no spoken alphabet, Mara had spent her youth learning to navigate its silence by anchors of intuition, trusting sudden visceral pulls behind her ribs that revealed where forgotten truth pressed against waking matter. 

One such unmistakable tug drew her across the swaying planks toward the smoky awning of Master Vane, a distiller who dissolved unsigned oil canvases in nitric baths to harvest the human yearning trapped within old pigment.  Alchemists strung their workshops at this dizzying altitude precisely because the rarefied sky kept spectral residue potent, yet they treated those echoes as mere raw fuel rather than voices pleading for remembrance.  Mara knew better, having discovered years ago that the Drift communicated through hidden details in paintings, subtly guiding the brushes of dockside artists to embed vanished realities inside background shadows and wave crests.  Propped beside a bubbling glass retort sat a weathered seascape of jagged coastal rocks, clamped inside a heavy leaden containment frame that Vane used to seal volatile impressions until boiling.  Without hesitation, Mara emptied her leather purse onto his counter, trading her entire winter savings of silver coin for the darkened panel before the alchemist could lift his tongs. 

Carrying her prize to an empty lookout platform lashed against a sooty chimney crown, she knelt beneath a lantern whose golden flame stood straight in the windless hush.  With steady fingertips, she reached down to unfasten the four leaden clasps binding the containment frame, freeing the captive canvas from its alchemical cage.  As the final latch clicked open, a cool exhalation of brine and wet slate rose from the cracked varnish, turning the air around her hands elusively tangible, dense enough to press against her knuckles like ocean swell yet vanishing whenever she closed her fingers.  Mara raised the lighthouse lens fragment between the overhead lantern and the painting, knowing only Fresnel glass cut for the Sunken Reach beacon possessed the exact angle of refraction needed to untangle light scattered across worlds.  Where ordinary eyes saw only smeared gray storm foam, the focused prism revealed twelve tiny figures painted inside the translucent hollow of a breaking wave, standing shoulder to shoulder around a crumbling stone gallery.  Each miniature keeper held a tilted brass mirror in the maritime cipher of the coast, spelling out how they had manually reflected emergency firelight until the tower fell so three incoming grain ships could clear the shoals. 

At the center of those microscopic brushstrokes stood a tall woman in a knotted shawl, her painted gaze meeting Mara across the threshold of two worlds.  High above the darkened roofs, clockwork bells seemed to pause in midair as every ache between childhood and now folded seamlessly into one crystalline, eternal instant.  Steady upon her anchors of intuition, Mara opened her archival journal and spoke the translated message aloud while inscribing every name and deed in permanent iron gall ink.  A warm, calloused phantom palm brushed her cheek in quiet gratitude, lingering with unmistakable weight for a heartbeat before the restless tension inside the oil paint dissolved into tranquil gold.  Though the city below slept on in ignorance, twelve courageous lives now rested safe within waking record, forever tethered to the living world.
