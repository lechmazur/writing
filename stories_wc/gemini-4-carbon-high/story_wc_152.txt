High within the cobalt observatory in autumn, dry maple leaves scraped across the arched glass and vanished into the freezing wind.  Beneath that blue dome, a cavernous echoed silence filled the chamber, returning every breath and needle click as a faint, hollow twin.  Corin, the court galaxy-chart embroiderer, leaned over his circular loom of black velvet where spun meteoric wire caught the cold starlight.  Emperors paid him to stitch blazing constellations that foretold conquests, for taut metallic threads hummed with approaching events inside the acoustic stone hall.  Yet Corin ignored the bright stars of rulers, having taken this solitary post to listen for unheard stories.  Bending near the dark voids between galaxies, he heard tiny copper knots vibrate with the quiet lives of scullery maids, tired clerks, and shivering sentries across the sleeping capital. 

Tonight, less than an hour before the coup, dozens of those humble threads were suddenly going mute.  At dusk, General Vane and two armed captains had climbed the tower stairs to sever the rope of the great alarm bell.  They had spared Corin only because ruthless men rarely suspect an elder who disguises wisdom as foolishness.  Wearing his spectacles crookedly, Corin had apologized to a brass stool, pricked his thumb, and babbled anxiously about stitching warm scarves onto the northern comets before frost arrived.  Scoffing at the senile weaver, Vane had bolted the heavy bronze doors from outside, locking Corin high above the palace while rebel troops crept toward the royal apartments. 

Once their boots faded down the steps and echoed silence reclaimed the glazed cobalt walls, Corin straightened his back and leveled his spectacles.  Across his velvet chart, coarse iron filaments marking Vane and his mercenaries were tightening like a snare around the sleeping garrison.  Beneath that snare, three dim copper stitches trembled near death: a young sentry on the eastern rampart, a clerk cornered over a stolen bribery ledger, and a kitchen girl carrying coals beside the inner arch.  Corin held no weapon to shatter bronze bolts, yet his quiet craft excelled at unraveling conspiracies.  Because the master tapestry shared a sympathetic resonance with the capital below, reweaving tension among undecided fates could tip fragile moments before they hardened into certainty. 

Common oil lamps revealed only the likeliest outcome, so Corin stepped to the central stone plinth where an ancient taper burned without smoke.  With a silver reed, he drew a pale flame from the eternal candle and positioned it inside a brazier directly beneath the taut velvet.  At once, spectral silver filaments shimmered around every stitch, letting Corin search through adjacent possibilities that hovered a breath away from reality.  He traced a faint branch where the freezing sentry dropped his spear to chafe his fingers, ducking an assassin by inches only to perish a second later.  Nearby glimmered a path where the trapped clerk tossed his ledger out a window into dark autumn leaves unseen, and another where the frightened kitchen girl spilled live coals moments before rebel horsemen trampled her. 

Separately, each small act ended in ruin, yet Corin realized they could rescue one another if he could synchronize all three overlooked lives.  Working rapidly in the breathless hush, he unpicked the heavy iron knots of Vane and his vanguard, loosening their rigid schedule thread by thread.  Then he passed his needle above the sacred glow, catching those three silver shadow filaments and binding them taut across a single meridian.  As he tied the final stitch, a clear metallic note rang through the dome and lingered in the stillness. 

Hurrying to the curved casement, Corin peered down through swirling leaves into the outer ward.  Below, the kitchen girl stumbled backward, scattering glowing coals across the flagstones just as Vane and his lead horses rounded the arch, rearing them in panic and lighting the gloom.  By that sudden flare, a loyal watch patrol spotted the fallen bribery ledger open among the blown leaves and stormed upstairs to save the clerk.  At the exact same heartbeat, the clatter of hooves drew the gaze of the rampart assassin, giving the stooping sentry time to snatch his brass bugle.  One piercing blast shattered the ambush, waking hundreds of royal pikemen who surrounded the conspirators before a single blade neared the Empress.  Returning the borrowed flame to its plinth before guards came to unbolt his door, Corin crooked his spectacles and smiled into the echoed silence, where three copper threads hummed steadily toward dawn.
