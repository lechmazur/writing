High within the crooked boughs of the ashen skyborne arbor, Lorn rested his palms upon a cushion of dewy bryophytes and breathed in the sweet, cool scent of rainwater.  Centuries ago, celestial lightning had charred this colossal floating tree into pale gray timber, yet lush carpets of emerald moss now upholstered every limb in velvety abundance.  As an orderly seed sorter, Lorn maintained three hundred cedar trays arranged by weight, ridge, and shell, ensuring the drifting ecosystem preserved its rare botanical lineage.  Even so, his quiet heart beat fastest for the plush green margins between his worktables, where plump spore capsules bobbed like tiny lanterns whenever his sleeve brushed past. 

Just before dusk, the steady altitude winds ceased altogether, wrapping the upper branches in a profound, cushioned silence.  During the hush where past meets prophecy, the wandering grove halted between cloud strata to shed its seasonal script.  Overhead, translucent gray foliage detached without a rustle, spiraling straight down toward his terrace in slow, deliberate patterns.  Lorn set down his brass tweezers, eager to decode the message in falling leaves so he could prepare the proper seed vaults for whichever climate waited ahead.  Regional folklore among the skyward tenders taught that the great canopy never repeated a form, for anything surrendered to its silver ash emerges transformed always. 

Yet as the first delicate blades touched his immaculate slate sorting board, they skittered sideways off the polished stone and piled into illegible heaps.  Frowning gently, Lorn realized that his devotion to bare, tidy surfaces had swept away the very textures needed to catch the omen.  The old ballads claimed that tomorrow could only be read when snagging upon the shapes of yesterday.  Smiling with sudden inspiration, he pulled open his bottom drawer of barren relics and knelt beside the spongy emerald turf bordering the veranda.  With nimble, reverent fingers, Lorn began building tiny monuments to forgotten things across the lush expanse of damp moss.  He stacked hollow husks from a cloud orchid extinct for six decades into a miniature tower upon a pillow of fern liverwort.  Beside it, he balanced translucent cicada wings from a vanished drought into a fragile arch, then arranged dry, sterile pine scales to honor a bough lost in winter storms.  Working down among the dewy green tufts filled him with mossy delight, each soft cushion springing warmly against his knuckles as his miniature skyline rose. 

Soon the descending ash leaves settled directly over these petite shrines, catching on their ridges instead of sliding away.  Each notched blade bridged one memory to the next, forming an intricate lattice of angles that mapped the upper atmosphere.  Only the center of the lattice remained dark and unreadable, where three broad leaves overlapped in dim twilight without revealing their internal veins.  Lorn needed one final monument at the heart of the green bed, something luminous enough to shine upward from forgotten days.  Hurrying to his finest brass sieve, where he catalogued stray mineral grit, he retrieved a tiny glass vial labeled for unclassified shimmer.  Inside rested a solitary moonstone cabochon dust speck, the last microscopic grain weathered from the smooth navigation rings worn by ancient cloud pilots long forgotten.  Lorn shaped a thimble mound of silver ash and velvety star moss right beneath the overlapping leaves to honor those lost wayfinders.  Tipping the vial, he caught the pale blue glint on his cedar needle and reached under the foliage to prod the speck snugly into the wet emerald crown. 

At that gentle touch, the beaded moisture around the moss acted as a natural lens, magnifying the adularescent gleam of the buried gem fragment.  Soft cerulean radiance bloomed upward through the three central blades, illuminating hidden vascular lines that joined every surrounding monument into one unmistakable glyph.  The canopy was steering toward warm monsoon swells, promising abundant mist for the tender marsh spores sleeping in his lower vaults.  Before Lorn could reach for his ledger, however, a quiet tremor rippled from the glowing speck through the damp bryophytes and into his miniature shrines.  Drinking the mineral light and rich ash, the hollow orchid husks and dry pine scales softened, split open, and unfurled luminous teal fronds never recorded in any archive.  The folklore was no mere metaphor, for bridged by reverence and living green, even barren remnants woke into novel flora.  Laughing softly, the keeper left his neat cedar trays untouched and cupped his hands around the thriving miniature forest as warm rain began to patter through the waking boughs.
