Moments after the anesthesia wears off enough to restore conscious thought, its chemical chill still fogs the memory while waking peripheral nerves in jagged stages.  Venn opened his eyes to an ultraviolet forest where towering silica trees drank away visible warmth and scattered cold, blistering light across beds of pale moss.  He knew neither the season nor which hemisphere held this glowing basin, leaving his mind completely exiled from its past.  Yet because he was proprioceptively enhanced, surgical spindle grafts along his joints and inner ears tethered him to the foreign ground with unyielding precision.  Displaced from every familiar coordinate yet rooted deep into the four degree slope of damp mineral loam, he felt the tangled roots beneath his spine like extensions of his own bone. 

Thin ozone smoke curled from his bare forearms as the canopy began his trial of ultraviolet penance.  Under Orthodoxy law, any enhanced interrogation survivor who outlasted the cells without confessing was cast beneath these actinic boughs, where shortwave radiation ignited tracer salts injected into the bloodstream.  Through dark quartz lenses that spared his corneas while filtering out gentle daylight, Venn watched old subcutaneous bruises along his wrists fluoresce in accusing violet patterns.  The visor stayed clamped to his head by a heavy pewter mask strap, forged from dull malleable alloy because leather and cloth rapidly crumble to ash under ionizing light.  Most exiles crawled up the valley to the Recantation Cairn before high zenith burned them alive, signing the Orthodoxy creed that all justice is pitiless symmetry.  Venn refused to move toward that altar until he answered the heresy that had put him in irons: his lifelong quest to discover the mathematics of mercy. 

Before his arrest as a structural dynamicist, he had proposed an unfinished theorem proving that shared oblique yielding could ground catastrophic force without breaking either bearer.  To mock that claim, inquisitors had locked him inside the Dyad Press, a coupled hydraulic rig linking two unseen captives across a steel partition.  In that frame, collapsing crushed your own spine, while pushing straight back drove twelve kilonewtons across the beam to snap the bones of your counterpart.  The transport sedative had scrubbed his cortical recall of the ninth and final loading cycle, leaving an unbearable blank.  Waking alive meant either that he had killed a stranger to survive, proving the Orthodoxy right, or that two bodies in the dark had completed his proof. 

With conscious memory erased and his extremities still tingling from the fading drug, only one method remained to recover the truth: remembering through trauma responses.  Venn reached behind his scarred mastoid bone and gripped the cold pewter mask strap.  Crimped tight with pliers while he lay sedated, the dead soft metal band reproduced the exact cranial pinch of the interrogation head clamp.  He pressed the pewter rim harder into his skull and thrust one bare shoulder straight into a lance of searing canopy glare that matched the cell floodlamps.  Terror detonated through his brainstem at once, locking his throat and driving his pulse past one hundred eighty beats per minute.  Refusing to calm the flashback, he rode the autonomic surge and lunged against a broad silica buttress root to brace against the phantom press. 

His muscles locked first into the brutal ninety degree stance of early sessions, pure symmetrical resistance meant to crush whoever stood behind the wall, and dread hollowed his chest.  Then a deeper, violent convulsion seized his flanks as muscle memory replayed the erased ninth hour.  Ragged gasps tore from his lungs while his proprioceptive grafts measured the exact vectors hidden inside his trembling tendons.  His knees had canted fifteen degrees inward and his spine had rotated off the primary axis, voluntarily absorbing controlled rib shear so the lethal load routed tangentially through his heels into bedrock.  Better still, his palms recalled three rhythmic taps through the steel partition, proving the stranger opposite him had mirrored that exact angle to form a reciprocal arch that spared them both. 

Breathing hard against the glowing bark, Venn looked down at the violet bruise lattice across his ribs, no longer marks of shame, but the living coordinates of mercy.  With a sharp flake of silica bark, he reached back and scored the fifteen degree ratio deep into the soft inner face of the pewter mask strap where no chemical scrub could touch it.  Somewhere in this alien basin his unseen partner had also awakened beneath the burning violet sky, so Venn planted his boots into the strange earth, turned fifteen degrees off the shrine trail, and walked into the trees.
