High above the faceted roof of the topaz workshop, a white star convulsed and vanished into permanent night.  The shockwave of its death rang through the crystalline golden walls, filling the room beneath the remaining constellations with a sharp, humming vertigo.  Cassian did not flinch as his trays of lead type rattled in their wooden cases.  His heavy iron letterpress could rewrite human interiors only between vanishing stars, during the brief, turbulent interval when cosmic gravity loosened its grip on the soul.  For twenty years, the cryptic operator had labored under the dying sky for one purpose: to understand the mathematics of emotion.  Born empty of native feeling, he believed that grief and devotion were merely unruly equations awaiting exact measurement in picas, angles, and pressure. 

The brass door slammed open, admitting Mara and a swirl of freezing ash.  To greet her, Cassian slipped into the warm posture of an orchardist whose sorrow he had printed winters ago.  Lacking an identity of his own, he habitually borrowed personalities from past patrons, collecting their voices like dry specimens without ever feeling their pain.  "A narrow hour brings a heavy sum," he said, shifting abruptly to the clipped irony of a disgraced magistrate.  Mara unwrapped a linen bundle upon his stone table, revealing a jagged halo fragment scavenged from a fallen seraph.  The curved shard blazed with fierce white radiance, throwing wild, refracted prisms across the yellow topaz facets overhead.  "Grief for my dead brother nearly drowned me, and touching this relic magnified every ache tenfold," she whispered, her hands shaking violently.  She begged him to lock the shard into his press for transposed empathy, inverting their emotional coordinates so she could inherit his geometric calm while leaving him her raw capacity to ache. 

Examining the luminous arc through his brass loupe, Cassian felt a cold thrill of ambition.  Ordinary lead type deformed under extreme passion, blurring the ratios he sought to chart.  A seraphic shard, however, was an absolute constant, pure compassion crystallized into indestructible form.  By transposing the axes of his detached mind with her burning empathy, he could measure every vector of sorrow upon impact, finally reducing the heart to solvable algebra.  Yet the press could never invert a guarded psyche, for certainty anchored feeling stubbornly to its host.  To pry her spirit loose before the next star collapsed, he needed to disorient her completely, and the cleanest way to fracture certainty was through deliberate ambiguity. 

Clamping the scorching halo fragment into the iron chase with steel quoins, Cassian began cycling rapidly through stolen selves.  "The debt you carry leaves this chamber whole," he murmured in the tender rasp of a midwife, then smiled with the cold poise of a usurer.  Mara stumbled backward against a glowing topaz pillar, unable to tell whether his cryptic promise meant her pain would depart intact or remain wholly hers.  Around the blinding curve of the relic, Cassian set a single line of movable lead letters to read, "Mercy ends where your sorrow meets mine," locking the blocks tight.  He rolled silver ink across both lead and divine bone while the crystal workshop spun with fierce, geometric glare.  Every word and borrowed gesture offered two opposite meanings at once, suspending Mara in lucid turbulence between deliverance and betrayal. 

Directly above the amber dome, a second star bruised violet, swelling toward sudden extinction.  Cassian laid damp rag paper over the form and hauled the cast iron lever down with all his weight.  The platen crushed paper against the halo fragment like splintering bells, unleashing silent pressure that shattered the oil lamps.  Peeling back the smoking sheet, Cassian thrust the luminous print before Mara.  As her eyes seized the double meaning on the page, uncertain whether mercy had fulfilled her rescue or ceased forever, her footing in reality gave way.  In that instant of total disorientation, her defenses dissolved, and the transposition snapped shut with mathematical precision. 

Mara inhaled sharply, straightening with the crisp detachment Cassian had worn for decades, her eyes dry as she regarded the crumbling sky as harmless geometry.  Beside the press, Cassian fell to his knees as his newly claimed empathy ignited the dormant grief of every personality he had ever borrowed.  Crushed by dozens of awakened sorrows magnified through seraphic light, he reached desperately for numbers to plot the ache tearing open his chest.  Instead, with terrifying clarity, he grasped the proof at last: love and sorrow were irrational numbers, infinite, indivisible, and wild beyond any boundary.  Overhead, the violet star vanished, leaving Mara walking steadily into the night while Cassian wept in the brilliant amber dark.
