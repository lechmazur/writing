Three winters after the great fire, frost coated the silent statues of Mirath, though the statues were made of breathing flesh.  Dozens of citizens stood motionless across the ancient plaza beneath a shattered sky, where jagged plates of the broken atmospheric vault hung suspended in pale, refracted light.  For centuries, the city had bound the lineage and memory of every resident into resonant glass underfoot so no ancestor would ever be forgotten, never imagining how such devotion might turn cruel.  When the inferno swept the courtyard, the superheated tiles fused human consciousness to that single hour of terror, leaving the survivors retrocognitively trapped inside a vanished blaze.  Ice gathered upon their lashes even as silent tears scalded their cheeks, their minds burning endlessly in yesterday while their bodies froze in tonight. 

Tarek walked among them with noiseless wool boots and steady, ungloved hands.  The civic magistrates hunted him as a gentle radical, an insurgent who carried neither torch nor dagger yet dismantled the foundational law of Mirath one quiet night at a time.  Official doctrine declared past identity sacred, forbidding any alteration to the ancestral stones despite the agony locked within them.  Tarek had once been a master glazier, and he had watched softhearted healers weep themselves blind beside the victims without easing a single blister of phantom heat.  Grief without discipline was merely another spectator to torment, so Tarek had taught himself to cradle empathy in arms of steel.  He loved these strangers far too deeply to offer them tears when what they required was a scalpel.  Beneath his coat he carried a leather pouch of spire glass, each shard a cool minaret mosaic tessera salvaged from the fallen high tower where the ground flames had never reached. 

He halted before Nadia, a young weaver whose trembling fingers still shielded her face from invisible cinders.  Steel scraped softly against marble as Warden Galen stepped from a ruined colonnade, leveling his halberd across the frost.  Galen stopped short at the perimeter of the glyph because every guard knew a heavy boot or violent blow upon the brittle, scorched stone would shatter its resonance and kill the bound sleeper instantly.  "Leave my sister untouched," Galen whispered, his voice cracking under three years of helpless vigil.  "The council calls your work aberrant mercy, and they are right, because saving her flesh by murdering every memory of our family leaves nothing of Nadia behind," the Warden pleaded.  Tarek met the trembling spear point with a warm coldness, feeling genuine sorrow for the brother yet allowing none of it to slow his pulse.  "Your love keeps her burning so that you will not feel alone," Tarek murmured, kneeling gently beside her frozen hem. 

With bare, steady fingers, he brushed dry rime away from the inlaid script beneath her soles.  A mind bound to a damaged inscription could never be pried loose by force, for the only way to sever the backward loop was to rename the victim via the chipped patterns on a mosaic stepping stone.  Where the conflagration had popped four crimson cubes from Nadia, the fractured phonetic curve folded back upon itself in an endless, choking spiral.  Restoring crimson glass would only reinforce the memory of flame, so Tarek read the jagged absences instead, seeing how the wound itself formed half of a different letter.  Using a slender silver pick, he cleared vitrified soot from the central fissure without disturbing her balance.  Galen drew a ragged breath to shout for distant patrols, then froze as Tarek selected a cerulean prism from his pouch and pressed it cleanly into the scar. 

The unburned tile bridged the broken geometry, turning the scarred characters of Nadia into the quiet ancient word Sela.  A crystalline note chimed beneath her boots as the stone sought a woman named Nadia in the flames and found only a stranger standing in winter.  Her lungs unlocked in a sharp, shuddering gasp, and she pitched forward out of yesterday.  Tarek caught her shoulders with firm, practiced grace, checking the steady rhythm at her wrist like an impartial physician before draping his own wool shawl around her shivering frame.  Galen let his halberd clatter onto the flagstones and dropped to his knees as she blinked up at the broken heavens with clear, painless, unrecognizing eyes.  Tarek stepped back before she could reach for his hand, denying himself the comfort of her gratitude.  "Her name is Sela now, so take her somewhere warm," Tarek said softly, already turning through the cold ash light toward the next weeping silhouette in the dark.
