Only after the last ship sails beyond the granite breakwater of Vaelis does Soren ascend the wide marble stair to the summer palace.  Early winter ice already films the empty anchorage below, sealing the royal port from the southern world for six silent months.  Gilded domes catch the dying amber light above deserted colonnades, steeping the cliffside estate in a hush of lonesome splendor.  Soren comes to forsaken ports like this as a traveler who writes obituaries for the living, arriving only when seasonal waters close and departures turn irreversible.  Inside a weathered leather satchel rests a handmade folio filled with ink maps of thresholds, harbors, and windy courtyards.  Every commission adds another contour to that lifelong survey, driven by a quiet hunger to understand the geography of healing and discover why sorrow loosens at one boundary while petrifying at another. 

Lady Clara waits alone in a shadowed garden in autumn behind the western wing, where tall black cypresses cast lengthening violet bars across beds of bronze chrysanthemum and fallen quince.  Alabaster statues stand draped in scarlet creeper beside dry porphyry basins pooled with dark rainwater.  Her consort, Prince Julian, boarded the final outbound vessel at daybreak without leaving a single letter upon his study desk.  Death offers the severe mercy of certainty, leaving a body to prove why a room stays cold, whereas a breathing departure leaves a restless phantom behind every curtain.  Clara summoned Soren to convert this raw, nameless void into one of those explained absences that permit a grieving house to breathe again.  Until she learns which version of Julian perished at the pier, unanswered questions will haunt every gilded hall of the empty citadel. 

From the folds of her heavy mourning cloak, Clara produces the sole token Julian left behind: a long palace carpet weft thread spun from crimson and tarnished gold wool.  Before dawn, he knelt in the Sea Gallery and drew the strand cleanly out of the ceremonial runner linking the throne room to the harbor stairs, exposing a narrow seam of bare linen warp.  Soren draws the fiber slowly between bare fingers, reading its worn texture like an honest chronicle.  Upon a royal loom, warp cords stretch taut from beginning to end, rigid as bloodline and state duty, while the horizontal weft weaves warmth, color, and mortal choice back and forth across that iron frame.  One end of the crimson strand stays plush from years sheltered beneath a velvet stool; its center is bleached pale apricot by October window glare and rubbed thin under pacing heels; its severed tip feels stiff with dried ocean spray. 

Julian did not slash the dynastic loom in anger; he quietly slipped his frayed daily spirit out of its crushing tension before vanishing altogether.  Guided by years mapping where broken hearts mend, Soren leads Clara down the frosty gravel path to the rusted iron postern gate, knowing a severance heals best upon the exact threshold where footsteps ceased.  Polite abstractions cannot lay a living specter to rest, so Soren spreads heavy vellum across a mossy sundial near the gate and pens an obituary that is vividly descriptive in every cadence.  Sharp black ink resurrects the courtier whose palace life ended at sunrise: the scent of crushed bergamot lingering on threadbare velvet cuffs, golden carpet dust caught in pale knuckles, the soft catch of his breath beside locked casements when autumn gales rose.  Lifting the page, Soren counteracts the cavernous emptiness of the terrace by projecting presence into the twilight, standing tall upon the flagstones where Julian lingered and speaking with warm, grounded gravity until the departed prince seems tangibly gathered in the cold air between them. 

Across the sundial, Clara shivers inside stiff brocade, locked within decades of royal reserve that forbade open sorrow as strictly as human touch.  Soren sets the quill aside, steps close enough for shared breath to silver the dusk beside her cheek, and slips the kidskin glove from her icy hand.  In a quietly intimate act of witness, Soren winds the crimson wool around both their bare palms and clasps skin against skin, steadying a warm thumb over her racing wrist until the frozen composure breaks.  Silent tears darken the parchment, mourning the prince who suffered here while releasing the living man now sailing south.  When Soren binds the finished scroll with that salted crimson weft and rests it in her steady hands, the shadowy alleys behind them lose their ghosts, their emptiness finally named and understood.  Sketching the rusted postern gate into the leather folio beside the darkening sea, Soren notes how recovery begins where stone yields to tide, leaving Clara at peace amid the solitary grandeur of approaching winter.
