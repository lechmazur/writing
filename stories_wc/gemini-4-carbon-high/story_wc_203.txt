Miles stood inside the abandoned circus ticket office of the Borealis Hippodrome, checking his watch and waiting for the site timer to trip.  Demolition crews had strung a temporary worklight outside the brass wicket, filling the octagonal booth with a harsh electrical rattle.  Experience had taught him that lingering spirits only sharpen into clear view when the overhead fluorescent buzz dies off.  Right on schedule, the lobby relay clicked out, the glowing tube went dark, and a profound hush settled over the peeling mahogany counter.  In the sudden gloom, the translucent figure of an elderly clerk in sleeve garters materialized beside the empty floor safe, staring desperately upward.  Such encounters defined the solitary routine of an elevator repair technician who sees ghosts.  Phantoms never tripped an overload switch or strained a hoist cable, yet witnessing their mute sorrow remained a weightless burden he carried from shaft to shaft. 

Contractors hired Miles ahead of wrecking balls to locate and ground unmapped dumbwaiters or suspended counterweights before walls came down.  He accepted these forgotten commercial surveys for a quieter reason of his own, driven always to discover what ceilings hide.  Most people trusted solid plaster, never guessing how much concealed machinery and unfinished human business hung inches above their heads.  Switching on his flashlight, Miles swept the beam across murals of grinning clowns and prancing white horses that wrapped the booth.  Decades of settling iron had split those cheerful faces with jagged cracks in the facade, exposing raw lath behind the gilded spectacle.  Old newspapers recorded that when the bankrupt circus closed forty years ago, court receivers had found the night cashier dead of a stroke beside an empty safe.  With the final gate receipts missing, authorities had branded the faithful clerk a thief, leaving his spirit snared behind the painted illusion of circus joy. 

Unable to speak, the pale figure lifted trembling spectral palms toward the domed canopy painted like a summer sky.  No blueprints survived for the lobby, and no visible trapdoor broke the painted clouds overhead.  Fortunately, twenty years of diagnosing silent hoistways had made Miles skillfully observant.  Rather than prying randomly at brittle plaster, he began analyzing dust accumulation patterns across the booth from floor to crown.  Every concealed vertical shaft breathes like a chimney, pulling room air upward through the tiniest structural seams.  While an even grey blanket coated the solid dome, dark velvety ridges of drawn soot outlined a neat square panel hidden inside a painted cloudbank.  Directly below that seam, a faint line of sifted mortar grit marked the wainscot above the safe where hidden guides ran inside the wall. 

Miles unfolded his fiberglass stepladder, climbed beneath the dome, and pressed a recessed spring catch concealed along the soot line.  The service hatch swung down on dry hinges, releasing a stale draft of trapped shaft air.  Shining his beam into the brick pocket, he discovered a custom burglar hoist designed to hold overnight cash out of reach above the ceiling until morning.  Suspended inside its miniature iron cage sat a padlocked canvas bank bag and a balanced leather tally ledger.  Beside the cable drum rested the timer meant to lower the cage at dawn, driven by a heavy brass horologist's mainspring barrel salvaged from a lobby clockwork automaton.  On closing night, a loose mounting collar had let that wound drum tilt slightly on its arbor, pulling its drive teeth free of the escapement pinion.  With the timer frozen and the late founder gone, nobody had ever known the missing receipts were waiting three feet overhead. 

Down on the checkered tile, the ghostly clerk watched Miles with hollow, breathless hope.  Respecting forty years of stored kinetic tension, Miles wedged a wooden safety chock under the hoist sheave before drawing a narrow spanner from his pouch.  He eased the tilted brass housing square onto its shaft and tightened the collar screw until he felt the milled teeth and steel pinion cleanly engage.  Immediately, a crisp horological ticking woke inside the dark chimney as the coiled spring resumed its interrupted countdown.  Miles cleared his wooden chock just before the final gear revolution tripped the iron brake pawl.  Checked by its governor fan, the cage glided smoothly down the wall chute and clicked into the open floor safe below, delivering the untouched bag and ledger where the morning salvage crew could not miss them.  Beside the counter, the old cashier gazed down at the balanced record of his honesty, lowered his tired hands, and bowed his head once in quiet gratitude before dissolving into the dark.  Miles folded his ladder in the empty booth, lighter by one forgotten grief yet already bracing for the next silent shaft across town.
