Nineteen days after the last fish was caught, the eastern horizon tilted three degrees toward the pale floorboards of the sky.  For ages, vast schools of silver shad had swum hard against the planetary spin, lending their small, stubborn weight to keep earthly gravity level.  Once the final net came up empty and the canal grew still, a quiet listing overtook the valley.  Loose pebbles rolled uphill across dry pastures, milk stood slanted in every pitcher, and heavy water pooled motionless overhead.  Beneath the tall arches of the sapphire viaduct at dawn, Oren climbed onto the narrow inspection catwalk with a canvas sack clinking against his hip.  He was a lukewarm mage, dismissed from the high colleges because his palms could never conjure anything fiercer than the mild warmth of steeped chamomile tea. 

Kneeling upon the cold planks, Oren laid out six jagged fragments of a broken ship's bell upon a grooved wooden cradle.  For centuries, this bronze vessel had graced the flagship of the Tide Keepers.  Those sailors had worshiped the living canal with fierce, unbending zeal, swearing eternal fidelity so long as the waters yielded abundance.  Yet extreme passion bred brittle devotion, rigid as overtempered glass and utterly unable to endure loss.  When the final silver shad died flopping on the deck, the grieving captain smashed the watch bell with an iron maul and marched away into the dust.  Master pyromancers later tried welding the pieces with white flame, only to boil the marine salts from the alloy and leave its edges crumbling. 

Every respected scholar had since fled westward, leaving Oren alone to right a cosmic imbalance before the slanting seas spilled over the rim of the continent.  Townsfolk called him earnestly naive because he trusted an old nursery almanac, which claimed that ringing the morning watch taught idle water how to remember its proper measure.  Learned men dismissed that rhyme as childish fable, never imagining that pelagic metal resonated at the exact frequency of ten thousand swimming fins.  No primer explained what an apprentice should do after the last fish is caught, yet Oren trusted that neglected tools still wished to serve.  He tied a brass plumb bob to the guardrail, watching its pointed weight hang crookedly toward the east, and set to work. 

Overhead, the first slanting rays of morning pierced the translucent blue blocks of the viaduct, turning the massive waterway into a ribbon of glowing jewel.  As sunlight gently warmed the chilly corundum stone, microscopic pores along the underside of the trough dilated and released beads of mineral brine rich with dissolved copper.  To rebuild the shattered bell without forge fire required patience, steady hands, and a precise knack for understanding dripping rates.  Because warped gravity pulled unevenly across the span, eastern joints leaked in frantic, splashing streams while western seams clung to reluctant drops for whole minutes.  If heavy brine struck the bronze faster than fifteen drops per minute, the liquid washed away nascent crystals before any bond could form.  Slower than twelve per minute, the film dried into chalky crust that would fracture at a tap, proving as fragile as the old pride of the Keepers. 

Pressing his bare palms against two adjoining wedges, Oren breathed his modest, tepid magic into the metal until it reached the mild temperature of summer shoals.  Counting the rhythm against his own unhurried pulse, he nudged the wooden cradle beneath a sapphire fissure that wept one bright bead every four heartbeats.  Each falling drop hissed faintly against the lukewarm seam, depositing a hairbreadth layer of gleaming copper salt that knitted the jagged edges together.  As dawn brightened across the blue stone and thermal expansion quickened the overhead leaks, Oren shifted his cradle steadily westward, chasing the exact boundary where night chill yielded to morning sun.  Drop by measured drop, mild heat and humble arithmetic accomplished what grand infernos had ruined, joining all six wedges into a seamless cone just as the sun cleared the ridge. 

Looping a hemp cord through the restored crown, Oren hoisted the heavy bronze bell onto an iron bracket directly beneath the central sapphire keystone.  He did not strike the metal in triumph or demand a miracle; instead, he tapped the warm rim once with his bare knuckle.  A pure, golden chime rippled upward through the blue arches, humming with the remembered cadence of countless unseen fins.  High overhead, the dark canal shuddered awake and began flowing steadily against the turn of the earth, while beside Oren on the rail, the brass plumb bob swung quietly back to true vertical.
