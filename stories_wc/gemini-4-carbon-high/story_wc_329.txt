High above the piston alleys of Vaelen, Corin worked as a sky-bridge violinist, bowing bleak calibration notes into the iron suspension cables to test the span for hidden fractures.  Centuries ago, his guild had played melodies that turned the wind towers into singing harps, before the ministries replaced all wonder with clockwork efficiency.  At dusk, clinging to a cable slicked with grease, Corin found a translucent locust shell hooked upon a rivet, split cleanly down the spine where a winged creature had emerged.  Holding that amber husk, he lingered in the gap between knowing and understanding, a breathless twilight interval where certainty had not yet become courage.  He had long known from forbidden blueprints that an ancient grove survived beneath the central viaduct, yet he still did not grasp what liberation truly demanded.  Driven by a quiet ache to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world, Corin pocketed the fragile shell, unhooked his safety chain, and climbed down into the exhaust mist. 

Beneath the girders hung a quarantined conservatory, an enchanted orchard policed by living statues of cracked alabaster and tarnished bronze.  Dropping silently onto its mossy iron floor, Corin breathed air heavy with boiler oil and bruised night jasmine.  All around him, luminous silver plum trees arched toward glass vaults darkened with soot, their glowing petals filmed in coal dust like fallen stars drowning in grease.  Down the shadowy aisles marched the tall stone wardens, their chipped wings green with verdigris and their marble joints grinding in strict metronomic time.  Though blind, the sentinels hunted any sharp or metallic vibration that disturbed the deadened hush of their cage.  Even stained by decades of factory smoke, the neglected sanctuary shimmered with a broken majesty that made Corin grieve for everything the city had entombed. 

He had risked the descent to claim a living sapling and plant it in the sunlit ash yards below, where its drifting spores could split the iron avenues with wild bloom.  Yet the orchard possessed an ancient defense: if anyone tried to wrench a tree free by force, its silver roots locked tight around the floor grates and rang like struck crystal, summoning the wardens.  According to old guild lore, the vanished caretakers had coaxed saplings loose by mimicking a human pulse, proving to the wary flora that warm blood approached rather than cold machinery.  Crouching beside a slender sapling near the edge, Corin wedged the hollow locust shell over his violin bridge to mute the harsh steel strings.  When he drew his bow gently across them, the metallic bite disappeared, emerging resonantly soft, warm as breath through dry reeds and pitched beneath the brittle frequencies that alerted the guards. 

Pressing his knee to the damp moss, Corin began signaling through heart rhythms, bowing the double beat of his own pulse to whisper safety into the dark loam.  Thirty paces away, one of the statues halted beneath a tarnished halo, tilting its featureless marble face while rusty condensation dripped from its bronze knuckles.  Panic constricted his chest inside the heavy brass guild harness bolted around his ribs.  His bow slipped into the jagged, racing tempo of his terror, and instantly the sapling tightened its luminous roots around the iron grate.  In that suspended instant, staring at the split shell trembling upon his strings, cold knowledge finally crossed into understanding.  No creature could coax another into breaking free while clinging to its own rigid armor.  Setting his jaw, Corin unlatched the state iron harness that had braced him to the bridge cables since boyhood and eased its weight noiselessly onto the moss. 

Unbound at last, he drew a deep, unhurried breath until his heartbeat settled into a steady, fearless measure.  He played that calm cadence once more through the amber chitin, letting each low velvet twin note sink like warm rain into the soil.  In answer, pale light pulsed through the sapling in time with his chest, and its silver roots uncurled willingly from the corroded iron.  Sliding bare fingers into the warm earth to uproot the young tree without tearing a single thread, Corin tucked its glowing roots inside his coat while the stone warden resumed its blind march.  As he climbed into the night wind, golden pollen trailed from the silver leaves dusted with soot, drifting out toward the dark gears of Vaelen.
