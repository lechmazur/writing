Amber light leaks through the ceiling grate above Elias, turning the salt dust into a slow, glowing snowfall that burns his eyes.  Slumped over his easel, the crestfallen portrait artist stares past brushes dried to brittle reeds at his failed likeness of Clara.  Around him curves a forgotten library inside a lighthouse, where rotting ship logs and mildewed atlases line the damp granite shaft midway between surf and sky.  High overhead, the cracked prism of the unmanned beacon spins lazily, refracting his refusal to grieve into a fragmented reality.  Jagged panes of yesterday hover in midair, shimmering with a soft, luminescent ache that shows Clara turning toward him yet never arriving. 

A deep groan shudders through the circular masonry, heralding midnight, the hour when the walls close in.  Because no single frozen memory can sustain a whole room, the curved oak shelves physically contract every night, grinding mortar into pale powder as the floor shrinks.  Tonight the tightening circle has crept within reach of his stool, threatening to crush him inside this radiant trap before dawn.  Safety does not lie upward near the blinding lantern that splinters everything it touches.  Instead, Elias longs to open the iron hatch beneath his boots and climb down into the unlit sea cellar, driven at last to descend into necessary darkness.  Down in that quiet gloom, sorrow will hurt without shining, and time will finally flow whole again.  Yet the fractured geometry of the library has split the iron hatch into three overlapping planes of cold metal that no hand can lift. 

Beside his elbow, one suspended shard of golden air brightens to reveal Clara standing by the reading table, cradling her broken tarot card box.  Its cedar lid hangs split along the grain, just as the tide left it on the rocks after the storm took her.  Her luminous echo murmurs the fatalistic sigh she favored in life, asking, "Who can mend what the ocean shatters, love?"  Even in ruin, Elias questions rhetorical questions, unable to surrender to inquiries disguised as defeat.  He leans toward the glowing sliver and asks softly, "Why ask who, Clara, while both of my hands still work?"  The memory ripples and whispers back, "Yet what artist paints with night when he still possesses gold?"  Elias freezes, interrogating that hollow premise as well: why assume gold alone can shape a human face when light without shadow is merely glare? 

Truth strikes him with sudden clarity as he turns back to his easel.  Weeks ago, having ruined his last canvas, he plucked sturdy linen cords from the spines of the forgotten books and wove a taut grid across his wooden frame.  Onto that web he tied only the brightest arcana from Clara's deck, painting her eyes and mouth across The Sun and The Star while leaving every somber card shut inside the broken tarot card box on the table.  Deprived of contrast, his shadowless mosaic caught the beacon's stray gleam and snagged the fabric of the room itself, binding space into painful, glowing knots.  Another lurch of the contracting shelves scrapes stone against his shoulders and showers his sleeves with salt.  To mend the room before the granite crushes his ribs, he must redesign the portrait of his wife, and without wet pigment he can do it only by untangling threads. 

Elias plunges his trembling fingers into the radiant web upon the easel.  Each knotted linen cord sears his skin with fierce, golden nostalgia as he teases the tight loops apart, releasing bright cards that blinded her likeness.  Once he clears spaces across her cheeks and throat, he reaches toward the reading table just before the advancing shelves pin his arm.  He snatches the broken tarot card box from the oak surface, pries back its splintered lid, and draws out the dark cards he once feared to touch.  Working fast while heavy volumes tumble around his knees, he weaves Death, The Moon, and the ink of the Ten of Swords into the loosened cords.  Indigo and soot settle beneath Clara's painted cheekbones, hollowing her temples with honest loss and deepening her gaze until her eyes finally hold weight. 

With the final knot tied, the redesigned portrait exhales a long, merciful hush.  All around him, the floating shards of Clara fade like dying embers into the unified air, and the grinding walls halt an inch from his chest.  Beneath his boots, the three ghostly planes of the cellar hatch snap together with a solid iron clank.  Gathering her completed likeness and the empty box against his heart, Elias hauls open the door and climbs down the unlit ladder toward the sound of the sea, welcoming the dark that finally makes her whole.
