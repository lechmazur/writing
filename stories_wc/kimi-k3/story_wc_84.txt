<story>The ancient observatory opened its bronze eyelid at dusk, and Ila Voss, a sharp-eyed drone pilot who was perpetually surprised by anything older than fear, saw the pilgrim arrive. [29] He climbed without breath or hurry, carrying a tin reliquary against his chest as if it were a lung. [48] Inside slept a type foundry matrix chip, dark as cooled pitch, its square face crowded with molds for letters no alphabet had kept. [71] Three molds were empty, three little absences shaped like teeth. [81] The pilgrim said the observatory had been built to print the sky's answer, but the press had lost its pieces before learning the question. [105] Ila should have doubted him, yet the dome above them was a skull of equations, and every cracked panel glittered with brilliant dimness. [128] Her drones unfolded from her pack, four moths with brass bones, each lens drinking the last blue minute. [146] The pilgrim set the chip in the central press, where a bed of type waited like a mouth missing its prayer. [167] Nothing sang. [169] The pilgrim did not beg; he only said that exiles had a frequency too, and he had crossed salt deserts to decode the frequency of belonging. [195] Ila heard her own surname in the word belonging and hated how her hand steadied. [210] So she sent the moths upward by following star trails, those first faint scratches Polaris made while the sun withdrew its testimony. [232] One trail bent toward the eastern pier, where a gear hid under pigeon lime and still turned from centuries of wind. [253] Another crossed the meridian slit and pointed down, impossibly, into the floor. [265] The third trail had no end; it kept returning to the chip, as if the missing pieces were already homesick. [285] Ila laughed once, surprised again, because a map made of departure had begun to behave like a summons. [303] She ordered Moth Two to pull the eastern gear with a graphene thread, gently, the way a midwife turns a shoulder. [324] The gear gave up a wedge of lead no bigger than a thumbnail, engraved with a matrix for the word almost. [345] Almost belonged in the first empty mold, though it fit badly, the way hope fits a rational mouth. [363] Moth Three and Moth Four took opposite corners of a floor slab and pulled until dust rose in a slow gray galaxy. [385] Beneath lay a mirrored disc, blind with tarnish, holding the second matrix in a rim of soldered hairlines. [403] This one spelled remain, and Ila felt the press lean closer like an animal recognizing scent. [419] The last mold still refused its letter. [426] Then the pilgrim opened his reliquary wider and showed that the tin itself had been lined with a foundry label: PROPERTY OF NO HOUSE. [450] He said his people had melted their printing plates for bullets, saving only matrices for words that could not defend them. [471] Ila understood that the third piece was not under stone but in use, worn thin by being carried. [489] From the label she lifted a foil slip with Moth One's claws, no pull stronger than a breath, and laid it trembling in the final mold. [515] It read enough. [518] The press closed on almost, remain, enough, and the observatory inhaled through every crack. [532] Starlight gathered in the mirrored disc, separated into tones, and struck the matrix chip until type became tuning forks. [551] A frequency walked the walls, low as bread baking, bright as a blade remembered by the hand. [568] The drones answered first, because machines are honest when their work resembles worship. [581] Then the pilgrim wept without moving, and Ila, sharp-eyed to the end, saw that his face was not arriving but being recognized. [603] She expected revelation to name a country; instead it returned her own pulse, corrected by one note. [620] The observatory printed no map, only a warm pressure behind the ribs, as if the sky had found its missing piece in the space between two strangers listening. [648] When dawn came to erase the trails, Ila kept her drones grounded and let belonging keep broadcasting through the dim, brilliant ruin. [670]</story>
