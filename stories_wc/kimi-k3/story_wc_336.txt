<story>Upon the hush of budding hope, before the orchard admitted gold, Sera Vey climbed to the verdant parapet beside the waterfall with her hooked knife, a basket of cuttings, and one heirloom seed warm in her cheek. [37] She was the wise orchard pruner of Mirrorgorge, hired to shape apples, yet insatiably creative enough to ask whether a branch might be taught to hold a season, a face, or to preserve the color of wonder. [74] Below, the falls combed mist into silver threads; above, clouds opened like pale grafts, and between those waters Sera had learned the art of weaving between worlds. [101] The seed came from her mother's mother, a black speck swearing it remembered blossom, snow, and the exact blue that children see before language shears it away. [128] That spring the orchard awoke wrong, with buds clenched like fists and bee-song thinning, because the neighboring world had begun to sip through the falls and drink color from the leaves. [159] Sera did not call it theft; she called it thirst, and thirst could be answered, if one moved gently enough to offer succor without teaching the storm to beg. [188] So she set the heirloom seed in a hollow of moss on the parapet, where spray kissed stone and rainbows unmade themselves into breath. [212] She worked by capturing essence, not body, trimming a curling shoot from the oldest apple and laying it across the seed like a bridge no wider than grief. [240] Her knife did not wound; it listened, loosening a scent of green skin, noon sugar, and rain remembered by roots from the far side of the water. [267] The fragrance rose, folded through mist, and became a thin luminous ribbon that Sera wove with patient fingers into the waterfall's white grammar. [290] A world leaned nearer: orchards there were glass, birds were sparks, and every fruit rang hollow because wonder had been harvested before it ripened. [314] From that side came a caretaker made of reflections, polite as frost, begging for one uncorrupted blossom to prove that color could survive being seen. [339] Sera felt the old appetite in herself too, the wish to keep every dawn in a labeled jar, but wisdom had taught her that preservation without passage becomes a beautiful cage. [370] Therefore she pruned her own longing first, cutting away the branch that wanted ownership and leaving the branch that knew how to bless. [393] The seed split with a sound softer than petal-fall, offering not a sprout but a doorway shaped like the moment before bees decide. [416] Through it Sera poured the captured essence: apple-green for beginning, mist-blue for distance, and a gold so quiet it made the glass orchard blush. [440] The reflective caretaker received the ribbon and changed, seams filling with leaf-shadow, until its frozen face learned the warm disorder of a smile. [463] In return it released stolen hues back through the falls: plum dusk, ember dawn, and the shy violet that hides inside thunder. [485] Color crossed without conquest; wonder crossed without capture; each world kept its shape by lending the other a way to shine. [506] Around Sera the clenched buds loosened, not all at once, but in a radiant hush, as if the orchard were counting miracles and had reached enough. [532] She pressed the opened seed-shell into the moss and planted beside it the pruned shoot, grafting threshold to root so the parapet would remember both mercy and boundary. [560] When the caretaker asked what price remained, Sera answered that the debt was creative: return each spring with one new color imagined in common. [584] Years later travelers found two orchards flowering in one spray, apples on this side carrying starlight, glass fruit on that side learning juice. [607] They said the pruner still worked at dawn, thin as a watermark, singing branches open while the waterfall braided worlds without breaking their names. [631] And when children asked why blossoms kept their impossible blue, Sera smiled toward the seed's green descendant and said wonder lives longest where it is given passage home. [659]</story>
