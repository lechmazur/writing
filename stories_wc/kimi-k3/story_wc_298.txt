Beneath the city, the catacomb orchard where tree roots snake between bones kept its own patient season. [17]
Mira Vale entered it within the glow of half-lit corridors, carrying a burned tarot card against her ribs. [35]
The card had once shown the Star, but fire had eaten the woman and left one silver point. [53]
To Mira, that point was a door; to the orchard, it was a price. [67]
She had come as a reckless idealist, which meant she believed mercy could be engineered. [82]
She had also come to patch holes in the soul, hers first, then anyone's the roots had drained. [100]
The orchard traded in bartered destinies, knotting one fate to another until both ripened wrong. [115]

Mira called that theft with flowers on it. [142]
The rootwardens called it balance and set their lanterns low. [152]

He said he could return Mira's brother, Ash, if she carried another person's last winter. [195]

The roots recoiled from meaning they had not authored. [312]
Avel opened his ledger, and pages fluttered like moths stunned by lamplight. [324]

Mira felt the old hole widen, the one shaped like a door slamming. [353]

Dusky resolve rose in her, not hard as iron, but warm as bread before dawn. [477]
