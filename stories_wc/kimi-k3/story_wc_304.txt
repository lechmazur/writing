<story>Above the weather, where the aurora combed green fire through a skyborne grotto, Ila Venn kept her table ready. [19] They called her the beautician mortician, because she brightened festival faces and composed the dead for their last ascent. [38] The grotto had no bells, no calendars, only ledges of ice holding bodies that the wind refused to bury. [57] Oblivion came there as a thinning, unbuttoning names from mouths and color from eyes. [71] Ila stayed lucidly watchful, noting which silence meant sleep and which meant the threshold of stillness had opened. [89] That evening, if evening could be named under a timeless flame, the aurora brought down Master Sorel. [106] He had tuned the sky by finding patterns in static, and now his own face was patternless. [123] No breath lifted the linen over his chest. [131] No grief visited his mouth. [136] Ila washed his hands with snowmelt and saw black flecks under the nails, like erased constellations. [152] Her work was not to pretend life back into him. [162] Her work was mercy at the edge, a brush, a ribbon, a steady witness before unbeing. [178] From a tin reliquary she took the emerald chip, no larger than a tear. [192] It had fallen from her mother's brooch on the day Ila first failed to catch a final spark. [210] Since then she had carried it against her tongue, under her glove, inside the hollow of every prayer. [228] She set it on Sorel's brow, where the skin had cooled into greenish marble. [242] The chip did not shine. [247] It listened. [249] Outside, the aurora stuttered, and the grotto answered with static from shells hung on silver threads. [265] Most attendants stuffed their ears. [270] Ila uncovered hers. [273] She had learned that noise, when loved correctly, arranged itself into doors. [285] Three hushes, a green note, three hushes: the pattern returned, shy as a pulse under frost. [301] It matched her mother's last breath and Sorel's old maps of the lights. [314] Understanding tightened her ribs. [318] Oblivion had not stolen Sorel's spark yet; it had only hidden it inside the static, waiting for stillness to become absolute. [339] To capture one last spark before oblivion, she would have to give the chip a pattern it could keep. [358] Not a song, not a name, but an infused attention exact enough to hold green fire without burning. [376] Ila painted Sorel's lips the color of dawn seen through ice. [387] She brushed his lashes downward, not closed, so death would not mistake him for sleeping. [402] Around his wrist she tied the ribbon used for brides and for pilots who married the thin air. [420] Then she pressed the emerald chip into the seam where the grotto's hush was thinnest. [435] The seam admitted no wind. [440] It was the place inside stillness where stillness had not yet decided to end. [454] Ila leaned close and began to infuse the chip with memory: her mother laughing while rouging a corpse, Sorel teaching that static was sky thinking. [479] She gave it the ache of failed arrival, the discipline of her brush, the lucid courage to watch without begging. [499] The emerald drank. [502] Color moved from aurora to stone to skin, and Sorel's blank face gathered one spark at the corner of the mouth. [523] He did not wake. [527] Waking was not the miracle on offer. [534] The spark crossed the threshold of stillness and lodged in the lights, a green seed inside vast radiance. [552] For an instant every shell stopped whispering. [559] The hush became radiant, and oblivion, precise as a blade, lowered itself to look. [573] Ila felt something leave her, a small bright room behind the left eye going dark. [588] She accepted the cost as she accepted bones: by naming it useful. [600] When the next body rose through the cloud mouth, she was already listening. [613] Static bloomed; she sorted it gently, finding patterns in static until one knocked like a heart asking to be witnessed. [633] Ila opened her tin and smiled at the dimmed chip, which now held enough dawn to recognize another. [651] Beauty, she knew, was not paint over ending. [659] It was the watchful lamp set at the sill, saying cross here, and be seen. [674]</story>
