<story>Ilsin Vale, a hedge maze designer, kept the living bridge garden. [11] Its yew arches crossed the ravine and rooted in both broken banks. [23] The hedges breathed, the paths healed, and every leaf leaned toward footsteps. [35] Ilsin was retrocognitively trapped, so touch spilled her backward into other lives. [47] A hand on bark became a drowned stair, a wedding, a knife, a cradle. [61] She clipped green rooms to slow those seizures and give the past doors. [74] Her motive stayed simple and impossible: to discover the texture of time. [86] Beside her bench, fading photographs kept losing faces to a gray bloom. [98] The portraits showed the bridge before it rooted, yet their smiles thinned daily. [111] Under the middle arch she found a halo fragment biting the living rail. [124] It was no saint's whole circle, only a bright tooth of hammered bronze. [137] Where it touched sap, rings opened like eyes and refused to close. [149] Ilsin named it Mercy, because it spared nothing and answered everything. [160] The garden trembled around her tools, a hush learning how to shake. [172] She measured the bridge and found its angles starving inside triangles. [183] Three turns made less than a flat embrace, then less than memory. [195] So she drew the maze through hyperbolic geometry, letting parallels run apart. [207] Each corridor widened by going inward; each choice returned more possible. [218] The hedges drank that impossible map and shook with green applause. [229] Ilsin set Mercy at the center, where distance should have failed. [240] At once the photographs did not fade; they began to disintegrate. [251] Silver freckled the air, and every lost cheek became weather. [261] She reached for one and fell through a woman's last laugh. [272] The laugh had texture, linen dragged over stone, salt split by bells. [412] The living bridge garden answered by growing a new inner crossing. [423] It looped beneath Mercy and made the center no center at all. [435] There the past stopped dragging her and began holding the lantern. [446] Ilsin pruned one arch into a threshold only uncertainty could enter. [457] Beyond it, blank photographs waited like ponds before wind. [466] She did not fill them; she taught the garden their grammar. [477] Leaves arranged themselves into portraits no camera had stolen. [486] The images would fade, because fading meant touch still mattered. [548] It did not vanish; it became the grain under every step. [559] Ilsin pressed both palms there and felt time answer back. [569] It felt rough as bark, bright as bells, tender as skin remembering rain. [582] The garden kept quaking, not with fear, but with recognition.
