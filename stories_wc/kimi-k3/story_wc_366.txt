Mara Venn came to Gull Crown after the lamp flickered out, with salt on her cuffs and no extra words. [20]
She was the supply skipper for the dark stations, reticent as a gull reading weather from inside its own feathers. [40]
People mistook her silence for coldness, though she stayed warmly observant of rope burn, tired eyes, and doors left one inch open. [62]
At the landing she found the stones stacked in the old coast code, three flat, one upright, one split on top. [83]
It meant the light had been harmed, the keeper was gone, and the unsigned letter must move before dawn. [102]
Beside the cairn lay a piece of sea glass, green as bottled dusk, still warm from somebody's pocket. [120]
Mara closed her fingers around it and felt the small ridiculous wish rise again, to bottle the feeling of safety and carry it below deck. [145]
She had been six when a lantern room lifted her off a black wave and set her living on the sand. [166]
Since then she had delivered coal, wicks, oil, batteries, soup, and lies about calm weather to anyone too proud to ask. [187]
The tower door opened before she touched it, letting out air that smelled of cold brass and lavender polish. [206]
Inside, the stairs turned like a shell, and every step repeated her name in a voice almost kind. [224]
Mara did not answer, because answering ghosts made them bold, and because her tongue worked best when tied. [242]
In the lantern room the great lens stood dark, one prism missing, the bronze frame sweating though the night was clear. [263]
The haunt was not a chain rattler but a careful pressure, the way a mother checks a sleeping child. [282]
It guided her gaze to the pedestal, where dust had been brushed aside by a hand that left no print. [302]
There she found the packet, oilskin sewn shut, addressed only to the one who notices. [317]
The first letter inside was dated before Mara was born, and it named the reef by the sound it made under keel. [339]
The second warned of Brack Ilder, who bought wrecks before they happened and sold prayers back to the drowned. [358]
The third held a recipe for broth, because fear burns sugar faster than storms burn rope. [374]
Letter after letter passed from keeper to keeper, unsigned so no grateful town could turn mercy into debt. [392]
Each writer added one danger, one comfort, and one instruction for stacking stones where only the observant would look. [411]
The last page said the lamp would fail tonight, and that a boy named Tam would run the cliff path with Brack behind him. [435]
Below the tower, gravel scattered, too light for a man and too desperate for a deer. [451]
Mara pocketed the letters, then set the sea glass into the empty prism seat because it fit like a remembered tooth. [472]
She primed the spare burner with shaking hands, not from fear of ghosts but from fear of being almost enough. [492]
Flame took, small and blue, and the green glass gathered it until the dark room wore one soft star. [511]
The haunt leaned close, not stealing breath but lending it, warm against her ear like a lamp kept all night. [531]
Mara opened the seaward vent and whistled the landing code, two short, one long, the tune of come home alive. [551]
On the path a thin whistle answered, broke, then found itself again. [563]
She stacked stones on the sill with her free hand, upright for pursuit, flat for hidden door, split for trust the sea mark. [586]
Brack Ilder came out of the fog smiling, all white cuffs and legal paper, certain that dark water made him innocent. [607]
He said the light was dead property, the letters were slander, and the boy had taken what drowned men leave. [627]
Mara stood in front of the burner, not tall, not armed, only placed exactly where the beam needed a body. [647]
Protect was a verb she had saved for ropes and cargo, but now it climbed her and set its feet. [667]

Brack reached for him, and the lantern room inhaled, every old kindness tightening at once. [702]
The little beam swung across Brack's eyes, green as the glass, and showed him the reef waiting inside his own chest. [723]
He stumbled backward into the keepers' ropes, which had been coiled for years by hands that refused to forget knots. [743]
Mara did not strike him; she simply kept the light on his lie until the mainland boat answered with its horn. [764]
