<story>On the lonely asteroid Vael, the astral orchard blossomed in blue rows, each petal a small lamp lit against the dark. [21] Mira Sol, its bewildered curator, had catalogued comets, reliquaries, and silence, yet nothing prepared her for fruit that ripened into names. [42] The bequest arrived between the first and last page of the estate ledger, where no entry should have lived: one pistachio shell cameo blank, cool as a withheld answer. [71] Archive law called such objects simply complicated, which meant harmless to touch and ruinous to understand. [87] Mira was forbidden to inquire after provenance, because the orchard's donors had signed their names in pollen that vanished when read aloud. [109] Still, Mira chose to pursue forbidden knowledge: the blank drew her thumb across its green husk, promising a face before any face had been carved. [134] At grafting dusk she carried it under the glass boughs, where stars rooted in black soil and fruit hung like lanterns listening. [156] If I ask wrong, Mira thought, the tree will give me an ancestor; if I ask rightly, it may give me a warning. [179] She set the cameo on the irrigation stone and said, I inquire not for owners, but for the direction of the gift. [201] The nearest apple split open without falling, revealing a seed printed with her own initials in handwriting she had not yet learned. [223] A pear three rows over answered with her mother's voice, though her mother had died before Vael was named. [242] Then every blossom turned toward the blank, and Mira understood the orchard practiced nonlinear inheritance: bequests leapt sideways through time, choosing heirs by need rather than blood. [269] The knowledge was forbidden because it made lineage useless; a child could inherit from a stranger not born, and a curator could receive orders from her final mistake. [297] Mira fled to the lavatory, the asteroid's only room with plumbing, locks, and a mirror old enough to tell the truth badly. [319] There, washing pistachio dust from the shell, she caught herself having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms, the kind that feel ridiculous until the universe agrees. [343] Blank did not mean empty, she realized; blank meant the cameo was a graft site where incompatible centuries could share one face. [365] Inheritance, then, was not the arrow but the orchard: every ending sent pollen backward to fertilize a first page. [384] If she carved the expected portrait, she would close the circuit and become merely an ancestor; if she left it smooth, the future would have to keep explaining itself. [413] The mirror fogged, although the air was cold, and wrote BE MORE SPECIFIC in the condensation. [429] Mira laughed once, bright and frightened, then returned to the orchard with the blank held like an unanswered bell. [448] She did not ask who had sent it; she asked which act of care would make the sender unnecessary. [467] The trees brightened, all at once, and their light showed every root grafted to a different century. [484] On one branch hung the first page, blank except for a seed catalogue; on another, the last page wore her obituary like frost. [507] Between them, the orchard made room for revisions, apologies, and strangers brave enough to become kin. [523] Mira saw the forbidden pattern plainly: Vael had been planted by refugees from finished stories, people escaping endings that demanded their faces remain fixed. [547] The cameo blank was their passport, and she was not its owner but its border. [562] A lesser curator would have engraved certainty; Mira chose to polish the shell until it reflected whoever looked with honest need. [583] When the next comet crossed, she buried the cameo beneath the youngest tree, still blank, still warm from her hands. [603] The orchard answered by blooming out of season, white flowers opening over roots that had not been born. [621] In the ledger, between the first and last page, a new line appeared: Given to the keeper who refuses to keep. [642] Mira read it aloud, and the pollen did not vanish; it settled on her sleeves like dawn with teeth. [661] By morning the asteroid remained lonely, the orchard remained astral, and the fruit whispered in voices scheduled for next century. [681] Mira, still bewildered and finally useful, locked the lavatory from the inside and began cataloguing miracles under Forbidden, ongoing. [700]</story>
