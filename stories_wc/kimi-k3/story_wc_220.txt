<story>After the last resource war, the scrapyard settlement sprawled across the cracked wasteland like a rumor of scrap. [18] Mara kept a reading stall at its heart, and beside her, gray-muzzled and solemn, slept Dirge, a bloodhound retired from finding bodies. [40] During the war he had found two hundred and eleven dead; now he found only lost bolts, runaway chickens, and, sometimes, Mara's own misplaced grief. [65] He was, the scavengers said, intuitively descriptive: his lifted ear described storms a day away, his low whine described liars, his stillness described the truth. [90] Radio had died with the grid, so words crossed the waste on wax. [103] Mara's trade was reading secret texts in the grooves of phonograph cylinders, messages etched between the spirals of music, invisible unless lit through tinted glass. [128] Each tint offered its own revelation: red confessed anger, green forgave, blue remembered. [141] People called her revelations tinted, and paid double for amber, the color of things almost lost. [157] The cylinders began arriving at thaw's end, wrapped in oilcloth, addressed in a hand Mara had buried years ago. [176] Her sister Lark had vanished the winter the wells were poisoned, yet here was her tilted script, cut fine as hair into the wax. [200] Every cylinder carried one riddle, and every riddle arrived with instructions about which lens to use. [216] Through red, the first read: I am longest when you start and gone when you arrive; what am I? [235] The road, Mara answered, and wept, because Lark had posed that riddle on every childhood walk. [251] Through green: What breaks the moment you say its name? [261] Silence, Mara whispered, and the settlement's wild hush leaned close to hear itself confirmed. [275] The last cylinder was no cylinder at all but a block of wax, and preserved inside it lay a single autumn leaf, rust-red, veined like a map of somewhere green. [305] A note said: read me with amber, and bring the dog. [316] Dirge had already risen. [320] He pressed his nose to the wax and his whole body described the finding: tail low, breath quick, ears lifted toward the west, alive, alive, alive. [346] Mara held the amber lens over the leaf, and the tinted revelation surfaced slow as a photograph in developer: the leaf's veins aligned with grooves finer than hair, and words rose where the two patterns crossed. [382] You asked me once why I collect riddles, it read. [392] Because a riddle is a door that only opens inward, from the far side to the near. [409] Every mile I sent you, you turned into a word; every word, into a lantern. [424] I have spent three years translating distance into closeness, sister. [434] The final conversion is yours to make. [441] Follow the hound. [444] They went out at dusk, when the wasteland held its wild hush like a lung holds breath, and the scrap towers stood black against a sky the color of the amber lens. [476] Dirge moved through the maze of hulls and girders as though reading sentences with his nose, pausing, correcting, turning pages of air. [498] Mara had watched him work the dead fields after the war, how he would sit down gently beside what he found, as if apologizing. [522] Now he did not sit. [527] He stopped before a tanker's hollow belly and bayed once, a sound so large the hush itself stepped back. [546] Lark stepped out thin as a reed, hair gone white, eyes the same tilted script as her letters. [564] You solved the leaf, she said, which was not a question, because between sisters some riddles arrive pre-answered. [582] Mara crossed the last ten feet of distance the way the letters had crossed the waste: slowly, then all at once. [603] Dirge, retired from finding bodies, sat down gently between the living, and for the first time in years the settlement's hush felt less like absence and more like everything it had been holding. [636] That night Lark pressed the wax leaf into Mara's palm, and Mara understood it for what it was: not a keepsake of autumn, but proof that something once falling had been caught. [668]</story>
