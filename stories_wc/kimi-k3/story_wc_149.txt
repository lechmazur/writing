<story>Over the hush of winter-cracked fields, the greenhouse kept its own weather. [12] It was an alchemical orchard shrouded in endless night, where glass ribs held back a sky that had forgotten dawn. [32] I came there owing nothing except a name I could not remember spending. [45] Then the ashen-veil ghost bride made her first cameo inside a drop of sap on the nearest graft. [63] She did not step from it so much as let the drop lean toward me, bright as a held note. [83] Her veil was gray pollen, her mouth a crushed plum, and her gaze carried a lovingly venomous patience. [101] In my palm woke a carved stone bead, cold from the pocket I had sworn was empty. [118] Its hole was no larger than a tear, yet the orchard turned toward it like one body hearing home. [137] The bride vanished before the branches finished shivering, leaving only the taste of iron and honey. [153] That was her method, I learned: never a haunting, only passage through ephemeral cameo appearances brief enough to be doubted. [173] She surfaced in the black shine of a pruning blade when the alchemist entered with his ledger of souls. [192] Master Vey ruled the rows by grafting promises to bark and feeding the roots with stolen anniversaries. [209] He smiled when he saw the bead, because debt recognizes debt even across lifetimes. [223] Once, he said, I had been the keeper of these winter fields and had sold my name to spare a condemned bride. [245] Once, the bride answered from a kettle's round reflection, she had accepted that mercy too late to thank the hand that offered it. [268] So she had crossed the long orchards of death to repay a debt across lifetimes, though repayment now required a little poison. [290] Her love was the kind that kissed the wound and taught it to bloom venom. [305] Vey lifted his shears toward my throat, but every tree rang as the bead knocked once against my teeth. [324] The sound opened resonant awakenings in the grafted bark: first one bud remembering sun, then hundreds recalling the hour before frost. [345] Fruit split without falling, and from each pale seam came the ghost of a season Vey had murdered. [363] I understood then that to ghost was not to vanish but to pass through a locked lie by consent of the truth. [385] The bride appeared for three heartbeats in the vapor above the compost, beautiful enough to make grief stand upright. [404] Take back your name, she mouthed, and let my thanks become the knife. [417] Mournful elation moved through me like warm sap through a severed stem. [429] I bit the bead until the stone tasted of weddings and battlefields, until every borrowed year inside Vey's ledger answered to its owner. [452] Pages loosened like white moths; dates flew to the branches and perched as frost-bright apples. [467] Vey reached for them and found his hands blooming with the names he had eaten. [482] He begged the orchard to forget him, and the orchard, lovingly venomous to the end, remembered everything gently. [500] The bride's veil lifted in a wind that existed only between one life and the next. [516] Her face was my old face softened by mercy, and I knew she had carried my hidden name beside her tongue like a seed. [540] When she spoke it, the bead cracked and released a green spark no larger than forgiveness. [556] The spark entered the graft where Vey had bound our bargain, and the tree flowered black, white, then gold. [575] Dawn did not return, because the greenhouse was faithful to endless night, but a small sun rose inside every fruit. [595] We ate one together, the ghost bride and I, while the winter-cracked fields outside held their hush like cupped hands. [615] Its juice burned sweetly down my borrowed centuries, paying the last coin of her debt by giving me back to myself. [636] She faded in cameo after cameo: a bright bruise on glass, a ring in water, a bride-shaped pause between two apples. [657] Each disappearance hurt, and each hurt flowered into elation, because being found once teaches the lost where to knock. [676] Now I tend the alchemical orchard by night, carving stone beads for names that still sleep underground. [693] When one wakes, I set it in a drop of sap and wait for a gray veil to lean close with mournful, venomous joy. [717] If she comes, I do not call it haunting; I call it the orchard answering itself. [733]</story>
