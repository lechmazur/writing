<story>Marr built bunkers for people who feared the sky, but his daughter Ila feared the rooms he made. [18] Each door swallowed air, and each stair pressed its shoulder against her claustrophobic breath. [32] So when gravity inverted for an hour, she did not hide under the city; she fell upward toward the xenial trellis above the clouds. [56] The trellis was built for guests, a cedar lattice whose vines opened like hands and never asked a name. [75] In her pocket rode a tiny porcelain doll, no taller than a thumb, with hairline cracks bright as spilled milk. [95] Its face stayed smooth, yet the cracks made it intangibly tangible, a solid little cup for things that could not be held. [117] Ila wanted to collect the flavors of different silences before her father sold them all to the gilded hush. [136] The hush was a company and a perfume, gold leaf pressed over quiet until quiet suffocated and shone. [154] Marr called it safety; Ila called it a throat with wallpaper. [165] At the first rung, the trellis tasted her fear and offered a silence of rain on warm dust. [183] She let the doll sip it for one breath, then shut her hand, practicing rescue by limiting exposure. [201] Too long, the gardeners warned, and any silence would yellow into coin. [213] Too brief, and it would sprout without root, a green idea torn up by wind. [228] Ila climbed because falling had become direction, and because below her the bunker doors were opening like mouths. [246] From them came bells, soft and rich, ringing the price of peace into the inverted rain. [262] The gilded hush had followed her upward, wearing her father's measurements as gloves. [275] Its agents carried silver funnels to pour quiet into vials and seal the clouds for later sale. [292] Ila's palms slicked; the lattice narrowed; her old panic knocked with a hammer made of rooms. [308] She almost fled down, which now meant into the sky, where silence would be wide and ownerless. [325] Instead she wedged the doll into a node of vine and whispered the names of hushes she knew. [343] Cellar silence tasted of chalk and potatoes. [350] Hospital silence tasted of coins under tongues. [357] Her mother's last silence tasted of bread before it burns. [367] The doll drank each name through its cracks, and the porcelain warmed as if a cheek had answered. [385] Then the first agent reached the rung and smiled with Marr's careful mouth. [398] He said a child underground was a seed, and Ila felt the lie try to plant her. [415] She answered that seeds choose the light that does not own them. [427] The hour turned stretching contracted, every second pulled long while her ribs drew small. [441] Gravity rang like a bowl; gold dust rose from the agents and stuck to the vines. [457] If the trellis drank that shine, hospitality would harden into admission. [468] Ila opened her hand for exactly three breaths, no more, and fed the doll to the vine without letting go. [488] On the first breath it tasted cellar chalk; on the second, bread; on the third, the rain of the trellis giving back her fear changed. [513] The crack across the doll's face split wider, and from inside came a root so white it made the gold look dirty. [535] It did not conquer; it chose to sprout, tender as a question, stitching vine to vine with doors that swung both ways. [557] The agents funneled faster, but the new doors admitted only what a guest could carry and remain a guest. [576] Their vials burst into dandelion clocks, and the hush lost its polish in a billion small departures. [593] Marr, climbing after his contracts, saw his daughter standing on air with dirt under her nails. [609] She did not forgive him; she handed him a root and showed him where a wall could become a trellis. [629] When gravity remembered itself, the hour closed gently, returning stones to fields and debts to ledgers. [645] The trellis stayed, not above the world only but threaded through every bunker Marr unmade into stairs. [662] Ila kept the doll against her tongue when speeches rose, tasting which silences fed and which devoured. [679] Years later, visitors found a child sized door in the lattice, painted the color of breath on porcelain. [697] It opened by limiting exposure to wonder: one breath for grief, one for beginning, and one for the hush learning to be bare. [720]
