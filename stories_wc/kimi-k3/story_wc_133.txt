<story>After the water wars, the tidal island of Tarn kept its own hours, opening to the mainland only when the tide went thin. [23] Noe Venn, an insomniac painter with a wryly earnest mouth, crossed that wet road carrying one amber brooch and too much night. [45] He had not slept since the reservoirs were declared borders, since his sister Lio traded breath for a flag and came home as rain on a window. [72] People said he painted because he loved color; he said he painted to thresh meaning from experience before it mildewed in the skull. [95] The brooch held no portrait, only a fly sealed in honey light, yet Lio had sworn it drank storms and remembered where they fell. [119] At dawn the causeway shone like a scar, and Noe began by following rain trails that combed the drowned grass toward the chapel. [142] One thread ran from a gutter shaped like a mouth, one from a bullet nick in the saint's blue hand, one from his own sleeve. [167] He smiled at that, because even grief had learned plumbing after the pipes were bombed into scripture. [184] The chapel door opened on Mera Sol, tide keeper, ration clerk, and part time executioner of mercies. [201] Her face had the haggard awe of someone who had watched the sea audit every promise and still expected change. [221] If you want the undercroft, she said, you do not pay with coin, since coins only remember hands. [239] Noe offered the amber brooch, not as a relic, but as the warmest fact he owned. [255] Mera turned it until the fly seemed to wake, then named the exchange cruel and fair: one drowned keepsake for one living hour below. [279] She gave him a key made of fish bone, a lamp filled with rain, and permission to leave if meaning refused to be threshed. [303] The stair went down through salt rot, through a door the tide had chewed, into dark that tasted faintly of metal. [324] There the island confessed its unearthed convergence: aquifer vein, siege tunnel, sea cave, and chapel crypt all leaning into one bright seam. [346] On the wall, old ration marks met older pilgrim scratches, and under both ran a child's chalk map of rain. [366] Noe saw Lio in the chalk's crooked mercy, in the way every line chose downward and still found company. [385] The lamp burned lower, and the brooch in Mera's pocket above him answered with a small amber tick. [403] He understood then that water had never been the altar; it was the knife people held to the same throat. [423] Fear made him witty, as usual, so he told the seam, if you flood, please number the bones for revision. [443] The dark did not laugh, but it moved aside from his brush when he opened the lamp and let the captive rain fall. [466] He painted the convergence on stone with rust, crushed mussel, and the clear drop Mera had sold him for a ghost. [487] Each stroke made a path; each path found another thirsty one; the picture behaved less like art than weather. [506] When the tide returned, it touched the floor with tongues of foam and read the painting aloud in a voice of dragged shells. [529] Noe waited for erasure and got instead a shine, as if the sea approved of being imagined without permission. [548] He climbed out hollowed, ringing, absurdly grateful, while behind him the seam closed like a mouth deciding not to bite. [568] Mera stood at the door wearing his brooch above her ration seal, the fly inside turned toward the mainland. [587] Keep it, Noe said, and she answered, I intend to, until someone brings me a fact warmer than yours. [606] He walked home over the narrowing road, watching new rain trails divide and reunite as if choice were only practice for arrival. [628] By dusk he had sketched the island without defenses: roof, wound, cistern, altar, hand. [642] It was not forgiveness, and it was not victory; it was a way to carry both without calling them water. [662] That night Noe lay down with paint still bright under his nails and heard the fly in amber turn once, patiently. [683] Sleep came like low tide, late, salt bitten, and wide enough to cross. [696]</story>
