<story>Beneath an alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night, the withdrawn knight Ser Isembard kept vigil among glass panes black with stars. [22] The trees were factually mystical, because their silver fruit ripened into small doors that opened when no hand touched them. [42] He had come to capture the shape of possibility before the border between promise and ruin thinned again. [60] As reality shifts, the orchard rearranges its rows, yet tonight it held still like an audience before music. [78] On the potting bench lay an abandoned marionette theater program, its paper soft as breath and its ink bright as wounds. [99] Isembard did not unsnap his gauntlet for courage; he did it because the program named him in a cast of impossible people. [121] The listing promised one performance only: The King Who Borrowed Tomorrow, with strings by memory and scenery by regret. [140] A shivering bliss moved under his armor when the marionettes inside the glass began to breathe without waking. [158] Their faces were unpainted pears, their crowns were little grafts of brass, and their eyes reflected his neglected name. [177] He had withdrawn from court after swearing that no banner deserved his grief, but the orchard offered a murmured allegiance. [197] It rose from root and valve, not loud enough to command, only certain enough to be obeyed. [214] The program fluttered open through forgotten patterns, each crease becoming a corridor he had marched in dreams. [231] He followed those corridors past seven dead footlights and found the stage set inside a hollow apple of lead. [250] There the possible hung like fruit that had not decided whether to be food, poison, or dawn. [267] Isembard lifted his bare hand, and every string in the greenhouse tightened toward his pulse. [282] If he cut them, choice would fall; if he bound them, choice would kneel; if he listened, choice might answer. [302] The marionette king stepped forward with no step, wearing Isembard's own mouth sealed by a silver clasp. [319] He almost unsnapped that clasp, but pity stayed him, for some locks are doors practicing mercy. [335] Instead he whispered the old refusal that had emptied his halls and sweetened his exile. [350] The orchard answered by blossoming backward, petals returning to buds, buds returning to the first green thought. [367] In that reversal he saw possibility was not a prize but a posture, a way of standing while worlds changed. [387] So he set the program upon the stage and read aloud the parts no actor had survived. [404] His voice made the glass sweat gold; his silence made the roots spell his childhood prayers. [420] The marionettes turned as one, not to worship him, but to show where his shadow ended and theirs began. [439] Then the king doll offered the silver clasp, and inside it lay a seed shaped exactly like an unopened question. [459] Isembard understood that capture did not mean cage; it meant giving form a place to land before it vanished. [478] He unsnapped the clasp with fingers steady from loneliness and caught the seed against his tongue. [494] Taste flooded him: iron rain, pear smoke, chalk dust, and the blue hush before a name is born. [512] The greenhouse inhaled, night folded inward, and every fruit door opened onto the same undecided morning. [528] He did not step through; he held the frame so another pilgrim could choose the hour. [544] When dawn finally remembered the orchard, it found only footprints circling a bench and a program warm as skin. [563] On its last page, in ink still learning to dry, the cast list bore one new role: Isembard, Keeper of the Almost. [585] He was gone from the bench, yet not absent, for absence had become the lantern he carried through rows of patient glass. [607] If a hand later unsnaps the clasp, the seed will answer in that pilgrim's voice, and the orchard will bow without breaking. [629] Then possibility, wearing a human outline, will learn again that mercy is the bravest shape of all. [646]</story>
