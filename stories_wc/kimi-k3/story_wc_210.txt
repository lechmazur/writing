<story>After the last wish is granted, paper-lantern cove keeps burning though every house has learned the habit of absence. [19] The deserted alchemist, Mira Sol, remains because someone must remember which hands made brightness and which hands only took it. [39] In her window stands an amber bottle with no opening, smooth as a held breath and heavier than an anchor. [59] It answered the final wish by granting plenty without work, and the town mistook that mercy for a future. [78] Nets mended themselves, ovens woke before dawn, and boats returned full while nobody needed to learn the water. [96] So the people drifted inland seeking difficulty, leaving Mira with lanterns that glowed like patient, unpaid witnesses. [113] She rejects false comfort now, refusing to call abandonment peace or to name a curse a cradle. [130] She will not smash the bottle, because breaking the only proof would let the town pretend it had been innocent. [150] Neither will she pray at its seamless flank, since worship would only gild the same old hunger. [167] Her motive is larger than grief: to reimagine an entire civilization that can survive being answered. [183] Each dusk she climbs the tower and practices through working memory, holding the cove awake inside her skull. [201] She keeps seven quays, forty roofs, three tides, two debts, one song, and every absent face moving at once. [220] When a detail slips, she walks to that street before moonrise and repairs the model with her shoes. [238] Only after the whole town stands steady behind her eyes does she conceive the shape of return. [255] Not a restoration, she writes, but a charter for people who have been spared too much. [271] Let no lantern shine unless a hand explains its need, and let no meal appear before thanks has a task. [291] Let the bottle sit in the meeting house under clear glass, unopened, illegible, and finally unable to command. [309] Its amber weight shall be our question, not our answer: what do we owe one another when nothing is scarce? [329] Mira copies the charter onto lantern skins, because paper that can burn at dawn will never become scripture. [347] Then she opens the harbor sheds and finds rust teaching the hinges a slow grammar of use. [364] The first strangers arrive in winter, drawn by rumors of a ghost town revival that refuses miracles. [381] They expect easy lamps and are handed needles, bait, slate, rope, seed, and the names of the dead. [399] Some leave before the second bell, calling Mira cruel for making hunger into a school. [414] She lets them go, for pity without practice is only another smooth bottle wearing the shape of love. [432] Others stay, and she teaches them to read tides by holding yesterday, today, and the storm in one thought. [451] Their working memory becomes civic, shared aloud at supper until errors blush and correct themselves. [466] A cooper recalls barrels; a midwife recalls courage; children recall that doors sound different when someone is expected. [484] They hang paper lanterns with chores written inside, so light must pass through labor before reaching the street. [502] At dawn they burn the finished pages and keep the ash for ink, vowing that law must remain warm. [521] The bottle darkens on its shelf as if jealous, yet no hand can find a seam to obey. [539] When storms split the pier, the old plenty does not return; ropes do, and shoulders learn their ancient arithmetic. [558] When fever comes, no oven sings by itself; broth still requires choosing who wakes beside the pot. [575] The town grows slower than pride and faster than loneliness, which Mira marks as the first honest wealth. [593] Years later, sailors call the place paper-lantern cove again, meaning not magic but a harbor that keeps promises visible. [612] They ask why the charter begins beside a bottle nobody can open, and Mira answers with the tide. [630] A sealed gift is still a debt, she says, and payment begins when comfort stops impersonating love. [647] If another last wish came begging, we would feed it soup, give it a broom, and introduce it to tomorrow. [667] Then the meeting house laughs, not at sorrow, but at the old belief that being spared was the same as being saved. [689] Mira keeps the amber bottle in sight while children write new rules on paper thin enough for dawn. [707] Outside, every lantern hums with work unfinished, and the revived town leans toward morning like a question finally glad to be answered by hands. [731]</story>
