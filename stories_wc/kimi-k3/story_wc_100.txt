<story>After the mysterious package arrived, the forgotten underwater city library began answering itself. [13] Mara Ilex, weary undertaker of the drowned district, found it on the catalog steps where no courier could swim. [32] Inside lay a broken lighthouse lens, heavy as a vow and crossed by one silver crack. [48] When she lifted it, every aisle exhaled distress signals in bubbles shaped like names. [62] The library had been forgotten by the surface, but never by the dead who still knocked from its shelves. [81] Mara's work was to give rest a permanent address, though rest kept moving like fish through black water. [99] She was not brave; she was inadvertently stubborn, and her refusals had outlived three mayors and one tide. [117] The lens pulsed against her apron, spelling a coordinate that belonged to no map in the circulating collection. [135] It pointed to the Reserves, a drowned reading room sealed when the city sank with its patrons still debating margins. [155] Stillness chased her there, always one shelf behind, clicking in the hinges of her knees. [170] She told it to wait, as she told grieving families that paperwork made mercy durable. [185] The door refused every key until Mara stopped unlocking and listened through the crack. [199] Inside, the impossible space was narrower than a coffin and wider than the sea. [213] By occupying impossible spaces, she entered where pressure should have unmade her: between stacks, inside margins, beneath the ink. [232] There the drowned patrons sat upright, each holding a book whose last page had been stolen by dark water. [251] Their mouths opened in perfect distress signals, no sound, only the grammar of bubbles rising and breaking. [268] Mara set the broken lens on the reference desk and saw the crack drink light from their panic. [286] The flaw split each signal into directions, and directions into rooms, and rooms into names. [301] She understood then that the package was not a gift but a summons addressed to her trade. [318] To revitalize the library would not mean waking it; it would mean shelving every borrowed breath. [334] Mara opened her undertaker's ledger and wrote coordinates where epitaphs should be. [346] A child patron named Sef demanded the lighthouse first, because he had drowned chasing a lamp across a stair. [365] His signal struck the lens and became a small clear window on the roofless dome. [380] Through it Mara saw the surface storm still rehearsing the night the city went under. [395] She could have climbed toward that remembered thunder, but the dead below began misplacing themselves. [410] Her stubbornness, accidental as barnacles, fastened her boots to the floor. [421] She read Sef's true name backward through the crack until it stopped tasting like alarm. [436] Then she assigned him Shelf One, Sea Gate, Lamp End, and felt the room accept the address. [453] One by one the patrons released their books, and the books released the pages, and the pages released the dark. [473] The library did not brighten; it settled, the way a body settles when someone finally closes the correct door. [492] Distress still arrived from far wrecks, but now each signal found a desk, a clerk, a grave with coordinates. [511] Mara kept the lens broken, because wholeness would have shone outward and missed the voices inside. [527] At dawn, if dawn could be said to visit that depth, she stamped the package RETURNED TO SENDER and fed it to the catalog. [551] She slept then in the index drawer, not because death required her, but because tired hands deserve evidence. [569] When she woke, Sef's window held no storm, only a lamp keeping its small appointment with the dark. [587] Outside, the drowned district rearranged its streets around the library, polite as mourners who had found the right pew. [606] The machine spat back a card reading PERMANENT ADDRESS: HERE, and the chased stillness finally sat down beside her. [625]</story>
