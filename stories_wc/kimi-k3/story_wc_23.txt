<story>After the hydraulics failed, the signal station stopped breathing and became a rib of iron under the stars. [18] Mara Voss, patchwork navigator by necessity and burial clerk by memory, climbed the service ladder with a canvas of sewn charts on her back. [42] The old dish should have turned toward the returning boats, but its pistons lay limp as drowned arms. [60] Below, the bay carried the reverse migration home: skiffs, rafts, and one black tender moving back into streets the sea had taken. [82] They were not fleeing the ruined coast now; they were coming to stand where their doorbells still slept in silt. [102] Mara's motive was simple and unbearable: to navigate the geography of loss before the tide edited it again. [120] Her sister Noor had vanished on the outward migration, in the season when mothers burned rosemary in a tripod censer and lied about safe land. [145] One patina bronze censer foot remained in Mara's pocket, green as a harbor bruise, heavy with the ash it had outlived. [166] At midnight the field telephone rang, though its wire ended in a dead cabinet. [180] A voice like static over bone said, New moon, foot west, build the small fire first. [196] Mara did not ask who was speaking; grief had taught her that cryptic phone calls at midnight can arrive wearing a stranger. [218] She unrolled the patchwork chart: sail from a ferry, sleeve from a hospital gown, road atlas browned by flood, receipt for Noor's last oranges. [242] Each scrap held a coordinate of absence, and together they made a country only mourners could cross. [259] The station lamp had failed with the pumps, so Mara built by starlight, starlit and salt blind, her hands reading bolts like Braille. [282] The beacon's reach still fluctuates with moon phases, the old manuals confessed, because the sea lifts the whole cliff a finger nearer heaven. [305] On the next midnight call, the voice said, Full moon, foot east, give the mast three legs or none. [324] Mara understood then that the censer foot was not keepsake but ballast, one third of an answer waiting to be assembled. [345] She cut two bronze struts from the dead pump housing and bound them with wire stripped from the phone's dumb cord. [366] The censer foot cooled or warmed in her palm as the moon thinned, and she set the mast's lean by that small betrayal. [389] Another midnight brought only breathing, then three words: Count the bells. [400] So Mara counted underwater doorbells in her chart, each one a house that had refused to become only fish dark. [420] When she reached Noor's street, the sewn sleeve showed not an address but a current, and grief became useful as a keel. [442] She raised the tripod on the roof, bronze feet biting the tar, and hung a lamp inside the censer cup she rebuilt from tin. [466] No pump moved it; no switch blessed it; the flame answered to oil, wick, and the moon hauling at the metal. [487] At the dark of the moon the beam leaned west over the drowned school; at full moon it reached the old ferry stairs. [510] The boats read that wavering not as failure but as a hand trembling and still pointing. [526] On the last midnight the phone said, Do not answer tomorrow, and Mara laughed until the roof stopped holding her up. [547] She recognized the voice then by its mercy: Noor calling from every boat that had learned the way back. [566] By dawn the tender entered the bay slow as forgiveness, guided to the street where oranges once rolled from a split bag. [588] Mara did not run down; she kept the flame fed so the living could land and name their dead without disappearing. [609] When Noor's foot touched the broken stair, the bronze foot in the censer warmed, and loss opened one gate wide enough for return. [632] The station still had no hydraulics, but it had a pulse, and Mara found raw solace enough to begin the next chart. [654]</story>
