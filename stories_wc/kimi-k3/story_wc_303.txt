<story>In the velvet observatory at midsummer, the Promise hung under the open dome like a bruised star. [17] Ilsa Vey, provenance researcher, had until dawn to certify a transparent violin made of ice crystal. [33] Her warrant said before the promise loses its glow, as if law could humble light. [48] The room had a mulberry ache, sweet and bruised and impossible to swallow. [61] Outside, the imperial terraces waited for one pure note to bless their chains. [103] The old mulberry in its brass tub pressed a bruise-colored sorrow through her teeth. [117] It remembered well ice, a woman's hands, and a seed sealed where a heart should cool. [133] The violin lay on black silk, clear as frozen breath, with no imperial mark inside. [148] Its f-holes were not carved but formed around absence, two doors for escaping music. [162] When Ilsa lifted the bow, every leaf in the room leaned away from the coming note. [178] She set the bow down and brewed coffee in the little brass pot allowed to researchers. [194] The Regent expected documents; she expected stains. [201] By reading the future in coffee stains, she had learned that destiny was seldom solid. [216] Tonight the saucer showed a scale, a leaf, and three dark holes shaped like bells. [231] Around them the brown edge feathered into terraces burning under a polite sun. [244] Perforated destiny, the plants seemed to murmur, is mercy with places to pass through. [258] Ilsa understood the Regent's bargain then: certify the fiddle, and her brother's debt would vanish. [273] Refuse, and the observatory would be scoured of roots, names, and song. [285] The empire offered order with plum-dark banners and bread priced by obedience. [297] The dream offered no bread, only a tune her sister Lior had hummed while grafting trees. [313] To weigh a dream against an empire was obscene until the empire put both on one scale. [330] Her brother Nox had once forged seals because hunger made his ethics soluble. [343] Lior had died planting quince in ration dust, certain roots could outvote cannon. [356] Ilsa opened a floorboard where the mulberry roots tapped four times for yes. [442] First bell would come when the Promise thinned to a thread above the dome. [456] Ilsa had come to authenticate an empire; she chose instead to propagate a crack in it. [502] She threaded one slip through each f-hole of the violin until ice held a green pulse. [518] The seed at its center answered by splitting with a sound too small for soldiers. [643] From the melt rose one note, and every terrace mulberry answered with fruit dark as kept promises. [660] The guards entered as the last glow failed, finding no forgery, only soil under Ilsa's nails. [676] Some called it treason; some called it gardening.
