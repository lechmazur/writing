<story>In the hush of a hidden sunrise, Echo Gorge kept its light folded under stone. [15] Mara Vey came there modestly fearless, meaning she named her fear and walked beside it. [30] She was a lost soul collector, though her satchel held no cages, only cloth, salt, and questions. [47] Around her neck hung a crocodile's tooth, pale as a seed that had survived being swallowed. [63] The gorge answered every step with three echoes, and each echo arrived older than the last. [79] Mara had learned that the dead here did not haunt people; they confessed to the cliffs. [95] These spectral confessions glowed faintly, like heat left in ash after a house has gone. [110] Most collectors bottled them for churches, but Mara listened until the ache became a door. [125] Her work was to integrate laughter into healing, not as medicine over the wound, but as air inside it. [144] That morning a small soul ran from her, bright and ragged, carrying a laugh with blood on it. [162] Mara had to pursue it before the sunrise found the canyon and made every secret visible. [178] The path narrowed above black water where crocodiles once turned like slow, patient drums. [192] She touched the tooth and remembered her brother saying fear could be a lantern if held low. [209] At the first bend stood a harp no hand had built, wedged between two leaning stones. [225] Its frame was whalebone colored by rain, its strings too loose for prayer and too tight for sleep. [243] The little soul slipped through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp and vanished into the wall. [260] Mara followed, and each wrong note bent her shadow until it pointed somewhere she had refused to look. [278] Inside the stone she found a theater of echoes, all seated, all holding their mouths. [293] Onstage stood her brother at eleven, wearing the wet shirt from the day the river took him. [310] His confession rose in sparks, saying he had joked on the bank so Mara would not dive. [327] For years she had hated that joke, because she lived and because living felt like betrayal. [343] The small soul beside him was Mara herself, split off at the splash, still waiting for permission to laugh. [362] The harp demanded payment, one true sound for every lie a survivor tells at noon. [377] Mara set the crocodile's tooth against the lowest peg and turned it with her numb fingers. [393] The string did not sweeten; it cleared, the way a window clears when breath is wiped away. [410] A note opened, crooked and gold, and the gorge repeated it softer than any mother could. [426] Her brother's mouth trembled, and from it came the old joke, terrible, tender, impossible. [440] Mara laughed before she forgave herself, and the laugh hurt enough to be honest. [454] The child-soul looked up, astonished that joy had not arrived as an enemy. [467] Mara did not gather her into cloth; she offered both hands and asked what game came next. [484] Outside, the hidden sunrise stayed hidden, yet its hush loosened into colors no one needed to name. [501] The confessions did not stop; they changed key, becoming bright wounds that could close without vanishing. [517] When Mara left Echo Gorge, the harp kept one string misaligned for the next frightened singer. [533] She carried no new soul in her satchel, only a warmer silence and the tooth against her heart. [551] At the gorge mouth, the ferryman asked what she had stolen, and Mara said only a splinter of dawn. [570] He crossed himself, then laughed when her brother's voice finished the blessing with a hiccup. [585] That hiccup followed them home, nesting in kettles, church bells, and the pause before bad news. [601] Mara still feared deep water, but fear no longer got to choose the song. [615] When children died too soon, she brought the tooth and the wrong string and stayed until a joke found its body. [636] She taught the grieving not to smile on command, but to leave one window unlatched for mischief. [653] Some nights the gorge still echoed her name like a debt, and she answered with a breath, a note, a grin. [674] The luminous ache remained, polished rather than cured, a small sun carried under the tongue. [689] There healing kept its teeth, and love, at last, was allowed to laugh back. [703]</story>
