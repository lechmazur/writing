<story>Mira Vale was a heartfelt artisan in the repair shop at the mist coated rail depot. [16] She oversaw the work while blind, and the room answered her hands with patient signals. [31] Her compass was an artificial hummingbird drone, small as a secret, built to carry motion back as sound. [49] It woke on the morning an anchor lifted for a ship's maiden voyage beyond the freight yards. [66] Fog softened the cranes, the couplers, and the brass name of the ship waiting at the pier. [83] Mira had one task before departure: to decode feedback into direction before the tide turned. [98] The drone had fallen against a switch lamp, and its left wing wore a silver tear. [114] Without it, the new cradle brake spoke only in static, and static could not guide a hull. [131] Mira set the bird on cloth and chose to patch the wing with film from a dead signal prism. [150] The film had once split lantern light for ships; now it would bend vibration into meaning. [166] Her apprentice Tomas watched her thumb find the tear by heat, by grain, by memory. [181] He offered worry; she offered the quiet pressure of a palm laid flat on steel. [196] Their calm had a pull, the way rails draw rain toward the same bright line. [211] Long ago her sight thinned, but her attention widened until every bolt acquired weather. [225] She kept names for sounds: shy brass, tired timber, honest steel, and the lie of loose pins. [242] Mira's method was examining light refraction through water features hidden around the depot. [255] She read the eave trough, the boiler drip basin, and the black pool between tracks. [270] Where color stood round and steady, metal was honest; where it trembled, stress was speaking. [285] Tomas filled the basin from a can, then set three spoons as markers on the rim. [301] He wanted numbers; Mira wanted behavior, because behavior confessed before it measured. [313] The patched wing dried while the anchor chain climbed, link by link, from the harbor mouth. [329] Tomas primed the drone with three drops of oil and one breath held like a tool. [345] Mira touched the launch rail and felt the ship answer through rope, horn, and waiting water. [361] Around them the depot made its slow liturgy of brakes, gulls, wet rope, and waiting doors. [377] She released the hummingbird into mist that smelled of salt, coal, and new paint. [391] It flew by pulses, tipping toward every echo that returned with a clean edge. [405] At the first water feature it hovered, scattering light into a brief rose over Mira's knuckles. [421] The rose leaned left, so she marked the cradle left by one warm degree. [435] At the basin the drone dipped, and green broke apart into needles pointing toward switch three. [451] Tomas repeated the number, not louder, only clearer, as if clarity itself were instruction. [465] A harbor bell crossed the yard, and the spoons trembled without agreeing. [477] Mira waited for the disagreement to choose a side, because haste was only blindness with noise. [493] The middle spoon settled first, pointing past the switch toward a hairline in the rail. [508] Mira heard the maiden voyage hold its breath inside the depot's old iron ribs. [522] The bird returned with mist jeweled on its back and a tremor stitched through its song. [538] That tremor matched the false static, but reversed, like a footprint found facing home. [552] Mira understood then that the brake was not lying; it was begging to be aimed. [567] She had Tomas patch the gasket seam, then ease the screw half left until the color stood. [584] The rail gave a low note, round as the rose, and the cradle accepted the ship's weight. [601] Outside, the anchor broke free of water, and the first voyage leaned toward open gray. [616] The hummingbird landed on Mira's sleeve, its patched wing flashing with borrowed lantern fire. [630] Tomas laughed once, then covered it, ashamed of disturbing the exact shape of relief. [644] Mira smiled toward the sound and gave him the bird to clean, not as reward but trust. [661] She did not cheer; she listened until feedback folded into direction, and direction became departure. [676] When the ship cleared the points, the water features released their colors back into ordinary rain. [692] Mira kept the dead prism film in a tin labeled with one word: return. [706]</story>
