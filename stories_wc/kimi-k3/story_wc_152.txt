<story>In the cobalt observatory, autumn pressed its bronze hands to the glass while Mira Solune threaded night through linen. [19] By title she was the republic's galaxy-chart embroiderer, by rumor a fool who talked to moths and mislaid stars. [38] Her wisdom wore foolishness like a patched coat, because powerful men happily confess to any woman they believe cannot add. [58] Before the coup, three dawns remained, and every bell in the city practiced ringing without sound. [74] The minister of navigation had ordered a new firmament for the coronation, with loyal constellations and no empty spaces. [93] Mira heard the order as silence entering a room, the kind that learned your name and came back wearing it. [113] She listened for unheard stories the way others read charts, with both hands open and her mouth carefully wrong. [132] Years ago her brother Nox had vanished after mapping a harbor where no ships drowned, though seventeen families kept empty plates. [153] The observatory called his route a defect and burned his notebook under the eternal candle that never bent in wind. [173] Mira stole one flame from that candle inside a hollow bone bead, where it glowed blue as a held breath. [193] When thread passed near the bead, neighboring stitches loosened and showed adjacent possibilities shimmering like fish under ice. [211] In one possible sky the minister died a hero; in another he sold the coast; in the truest he was only punctuation. [233] Mira pricked her finger, let blood mark the margin, and began unraveling conspiracies knot by patient knot. [250] Each loosened warp released a voice: a lighthouse keeper bribed to blink late, a cadet taught to salute the wrong moon. [271] The voices did not accuse; they asked whether anyone had noticed the tide arriving in polished boots. [288] Mira giggled at the wrong moon, spilled crimson floss, and let the general's bride call her sweetly simple. [306] The bride laughed too, then whispered that the signal mirrors would synchronize at midnight with the eastern fleet. [324] If those mirrors agreed, cannon would name the regent a traitor before he finished denying it. [340] Mira bowed so low her hair swept the floor, counting screws in the mirror cradle beneath her embroidery hoop. [359] All day she stitched the minister's false Milky Way while secretly exchanging silver thread for strands cooled by the stolen flame. [380] Autumn darkened early; leaves struck the dome like soft hands asking to be remembered. [394] At dusk the observatory filled with officers whose medals ticked against glass, small clocks hungry for agreement. [411] Mira set the bone bead inside the cradle and hummed a lullaby backward, the tune Nox used to calm instruments. [431] The eternal flame did not brighten; it narrowed until every shadow in the room faced the same absent star. [450] Then the adjacent possibilities opened around midnight, each mirror holding a slightly different version of the fleet. [467] In one, cannon fired; in one, sails turned; in one, the minister's secretary wept because she had forged the tide tables. [488] Mira chose the hush between those futures and drew her needle through, bidding flame, mirror, bell, and heartbeat synchronize. [507] For three counted breaths the coup stood beside itself, close enough to hear its own instructions stammer. [524] The secretary's unheard story rose first, naming the forged tables, the bought harbor, the cell where Nox still sang to metal. [545] Then the lighthouse keeper answered, then the cadet, then seventeen plates struck once like a constellation finding its spoons. [564] No trumpet sounded; the eastern fleet received mirrors that disagreed by one honest star and turned away from murder. [583] Officers reached for swords and found only thread tightening softly around their wrists, blue as distance, patient as frost. [602] Mira hid the bead in her cheek and babbled about moths until even suspicion grew embarrassed and looked away. [621] Dawn opened the dome to gulls, to rust, to the regent alive and furious in a palace suddenly undefended. [640] She unpicked one final knot, and Nox's map unfolded from the hem, showing the prison island beneath a renamed moon. [660] The room after was not loud; it was an echoed silence, wide enough for every missing name to enter and be counted. [682] Mira smiled like a simpleton, stitched the sun crooked on purpose, and listened as the republic learned to stutter back its dead. [704]</story>
