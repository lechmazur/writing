<story>Vey had outlived cities, lovers, languages, and every reason to knock on death's polite door. [15] A bored immortal, Vey wore boredom as climate, chapel, chains, compass, and one unanswered prayer. [30] She hid inside the prismatic escondite, where rainbows kept secrets under stone without asking leave. [45] During a solar storm, bright thumbs pressed the sky and whispered through iron all night. [60] The hushed thunder pressed close, as if heaven argued behind glass with knives for years. [75] Dimensionally aware, Vey felt corridors lean before any mason heard them crack inside her marrow. [90] Then came spatial mutiny: doorways refused their assigned distances and elected stranger laws of elsewhere. [105] Around her throat, the cursed amulet fragment woke, tasting farewells like metal and old blood. [120] It promised one answer: what happens after goodbye when goodbye finally happens to the speaker. [135] Intrigue moved softly among the prisms wearing Master Cael's patient face and borrowed honest hands. [150] Cael tended magnetic fields like vines, teaching them to pull sideways while compasses learned disobedience. [165] He said the storm would bless migration; Vey heard doors choosing exile in lower voices. [180] The escondite had been built from hidden refuges stolen during old wars and folded inward. [195] Now every refuge wanted out, and colors carried maps across skin that burned without heat. [210] Vey followed Cael beneath the crystal wells where magnets dreamed in rows of captive north. [225] There she found letters addressed to nobody, sealed with black aurora wax and salted light. [240] Each letter begged the recipient not to return from any farewell, no matter how gently. [255] The curse in the fragment fed on unfinished endings, growing teeth whenever love delayed permission. [270] Vey understood then why immortality had made even mercy feel administrative, like filing away snow. [285] Cael admitted his bargain: surrender the escondite and learn death's customs from the storm itself. [300] He feared goodbye because he had practiced it badly for centuries with faces still warm. [315] Vey feared it because no ending had ever agreed to keep her past the threshold. [330] Outside, the storm combed the world with slow magnetic fingers until every buried boundary answered. [345] Inside, north divorced needle; west signed papers; depth demanded witnesses, and altitude rehearsed speeches downstairs. [360] The amulet fragment pulled Vey toward the First Door, the forbidden hinge made from separation. [375] Beyond it, Cael claimed, farewell continued walking after feet stopped and turned around waving gently. [390] Vey wanted that sight with a greed finally larger than survival and quieter than prayer. [405] She asked whether after goodbye meant reunion, oblivion, or another room with kinder geometry inside. [420] The fragment answered by cutting her palm and drinking the question until it shone homeward. [435] Cael moved to chain the door; prisms bent around his lie and exposed the clasp. [450] Vey smiled, because centuries of boredom had made betrayal almost refreshing, like rain after paperwork. [465] She let the mutiny take her elbow and choose the nearer far beyond Cael's reach. [480] Together they crossed corridors that argued with mirrors about distance while hush carried thunder underfoot. [495] At the First Door, Cael offered his name as payment for passage and future obedience. [510] The door refused names; it wanted the exact weight of leaving measured without witnesses present. [525] Vey gave back every borrowed century, one heartbeat at a time until her shadow lightened. [540] She told Cael goodbye without saving any road for return or revenge dressed as mercy. [555] The curse starved; the fragment became only metal, honest and cold against her fading pulse. [570] Spatial mutiny paused, astonished that an ending had volunteered first before the storm could bargain. [585] Through magnetic fields the escondite exhaled, returning stolen rooms to their grief and rightful distances. [600] Vey stepped past goodbye and found no trumpet, only listening, as if eternity exhaled too. [615] There, the dead were not waiting; they were continuing without witnesses and without old maps. [630] Vey understood at last that after goodbye was still a verb practiced by open hands. [645] She let go, and thunder kept its hush as dawn arrived wearing ordinary mortal light. [660]</story>
