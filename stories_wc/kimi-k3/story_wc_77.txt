<story>After the resuscitation, Mara glowed. [5] The doctors called it trauma; the nurses called it imagination; Mara called it the lingering luminance, a pale afterlight that had followed her back from wherever flatliners go. [33] It also ruined photographs. [37] Every snapshot developed wrong: her face washed to a skull-white blur, her eyes two wet pits, as though the camera remembered her dead and refused to forget. [64] The wedding photographer called it photographically cursed, laughing, until he developed the proofs. [77] So Mara called off the wedding and drove back to the pier. [89] The light whispered at night, and the voice was June's. [99] June, who had vanished eleven years ago inside the abandoned carnival funhouse on the rotting pier, while Mara stood outside holding both their tickets and none of her courage. [128] Her whispering advisor gave instructions in a voice like static through lace: go back, go in, trick fear into revealing its weakness. [150] Mara went at dusk, when the funhouse was most itself: paint peeling like skin in slow sunburn, the clown's painted grin glistening with something sweetly fungal, the whole wreck breathing salt and sugar-rot, a smell she hated and kept inhaling. [190] Her own glow lit the way, pooling on the warped floorboards like milk that refused to sour. [207] In the fortune booth she found the dream dictionary, swollen by damp, its pages furred with mold the color of old bruises. [229] Someone had annotated every entry in green ink, paradoxical notes in June's patient hand. [243] Under NIGHTMARE: to wake from it, lie down inside it. [253] Under DROWNING: the water is dry to whoever stops swimming. [263] Under FEAR: it eats only secrets; feed it yours and watch it starve. [276] Mara understood then why the light had brought her, and what the trick would cost. [291] The funhouse arranged its seductions: mirrors that made her lovely and wrong, a corridor of painted velvet whose rot perfumed the air like a lover's cologne gone rancid. [319] At the heart of the maze, on a velvet chair under a dead chandelier, Fear waited wearing an opera cloak. [339] It had June's face the way a coin has a face: stamped, official, spendable. [353] The cloak was fastened by a clasp Mara knew before her eyes agreed: their mother's opera cloak clasp, twin silver moths kissing, lost with June eleven years ago. [381] You came back, Fear said, and its voice was gorgeous, and its voice was a drain. [397] The whispering advisor stirred along Mara's shoulders, faint as candle-heat: remember the notes. [410] So Mara did not run; she lay down inside the nightmare, in the chair's hushed halo of her own light, and she unbosomed herself. [434] I dared her, Mara said. [439] I was eleven and cruel with love, and I said only babies knock first, and I watched her go in, and I did not follow, and I let everyone believe the door swung shut by itself. [475] Fear leaned closer, radiant and famished, inhaling each confession like steam off soup. [488] Mara kept feeding it: every small cowardice since, every photograph she had burned, every mirror she had turned to the wall. [509] And the paradox held: the fuller Fear grew, the thinner it became, bloating translucent, a tick of light. [527] Its weakness, whispered June, is that it is only ever ours. [538] Fear revealed it then, panting, triumphant, stupid: I cannot hold a secret already told. [552] Mara reached out, gentle as a sister, and unhooked the moth clasp. [564] The opera cloak sighed open, and there was nothing beneath it but a child's handful of darkness, already shrinking from her light. [586] She pinned the clasp at her own throat, where it beat once like a second pulse. [602] The whispering advisor exhaled and was quiet, and the lingering luminance went with June at last, softly, the way candles forgive a room. [625] In the exit corridor stood the old souvenir camera, which had once sold terror for a quarter. [642] Mara fed it her last coin and sat in the mouth of its flash. [656] The photograph slid out wet and developed true: a woman, unlit, unblurred, alive, with two silver moths asleep at her throat. [677] She kept it anyway, the first true likeness in years: proof that fear, once fed, dies of the meal. [696]</story>
