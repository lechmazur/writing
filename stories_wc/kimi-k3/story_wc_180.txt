After the breach of contract, when the lead merchants nailed their writ to the baptistry door, Sera Quill climbed into the abandoned cathedral with her mother's forgotten bag bumping her hip. [31] Below the rafters, pigeons complained like tiny landlords, while above them a lush orchard spanned the stone ribs in green defiance. [52] Apples shouldered through broken lancets, pears leaned over the choir, and plum roots gripped the old altar rail like hands. [72] Sera was a quartz-eye mender: her milk-bright left lens found cracks in bells, bones, promises, and peach bark when honesty had failed at last. [96] She was endearingly feisty, the sort of woman who blessed ladders by kicking them and apologized only after the rung held. [117] Her daughter Nomi waited on the floor, arms crossed, insisting that contracts were paper and trees were lumber with patience. [137] The breached paper had promised the orchard forever to the town if the town promised never to cut the nave for firewood. [159] Forever had lasted eleven years, which Sera called a decent innings for a sentence with no sleeves. [176] Nomi said, Mother, climb down before your smart mouth makes gravity legal. [188] Sera answered, If gravity wants a lawyer, it can queue behind the pigeons. [201] She meant to sound brave, but the quartz eye showed every graft above the choir standing one dry season from letting go. [223] Still she persisted, because the bag at her hip had begun ticking like a heart that remembered stairs. [241] Inside she found no relic, only forgotten contents: a horn spoon, three rusted staples, beeswax, a salt twist, a plum stone, and her daughter's hair ribbon gone pale. [269] Nomi flushed and said, I threw that ribbon away when you made me vow to a mulberry. [286] Sera almost scolded her, then saw the ribbon's faded knot matched the first stitch her own mother had taught her during a winter of thin soup. [312] Accidental wisdom came out of her before dignity could stop it: a knot is just a promise practicing how to loosen. [333] Nomi rolled her eyes, but she reached up and steadied the ladder. [345] The magistrate shouted from the porch that breach made the orchard forfeit, roots, fruit, rafters, and all. [362] Sera called down, Then come collect the roots, sir, and mind the bees in your jurisprudence. [378] The town laughed despite itself, and even Nomi's mouth betrayed her by becoming briefly glad. [393] Yet laughter did not pack the split in the master beam, where the oldest apple had driven a root into oak and made both argue. [418] Sera opened the beeswax with her thumb, salted it for courage, and told the beam, Hold, you difficult ancestor. [437] She worked the wax into the crack, not to imprison the root but to give the wood a softer place to change its mind. [461] The staples bridged a worm highway; the spoon ladled rainwater from a dead soldier's helmet into three waiting graft cups in turn. [483] When Nomi asked why save a cathedral no god paid rent on, Sera said, Because your grandmother trusted no darn that refused to breathe. [507] That made no sense and entire sense, which is how generational wisdom crosses a room when the bridge is out. [527] At dusk the lead merchants returned with hooks, and Sera tied her daughter's hair ribbon to the writ, the mulberry twig, and the split beam in one impolite bow. [556] It did not bind anything; it only marked where holding hard should turn into holding on. [572] The magistrate reached for the bow, and the plum stone slipped from Sera's pocket into his boot. [589] He hopped, kicked a basket, and woke the bees, who read his face as bad weather. [605] In the buzzing bright panic, the hook men dropped their chains, and one chain struck the old bell that had forgotten it could speak. [629] Its note shook loose a rust-colored leaf from every branch, though summer still ruled the orchard. [645] Nomi caught one leaf and whispered, They are deciduous vows, not broken ones; they fall so the next promise can feed on the last. [669] Sera stared at her daughter and said, I was going to make soup; how did you get wise from inventory. [689] By morning the merchants were gone, the beam held, and the town drafted a contract with blank spaces for autumn each year. [711] Nomi kept the ribbon but untied the knot once each season, learning from Sera that persistence is not clenching; it is returning with sap on your hands. [738]
