<story>When gravity inverts for an hour, the mist inside the serpentine grotto learns to fall upward. [16] Ila Venn, the prism-minded archivist of the sunken catalog, waited for the floor to release her boots. [33] Around her, stalactites became bright teeth above, while pools lifted like slow coins and refused to spill. [50] She carried no lantern, only a manuscript margin doodle folded inside a brass tooth. [64] The doodle showed a little gate drawn in blue ash, clandestinely luminous whenever nobody admitted looking. [80] Its ink had been made from crushed beetles, patient salt, and the seeds of vigilance. [95] Those seeds did not sprout in soil; they opened in choices, tiny hinges inside the blood. [111] Ila needed to reenter the grotto before the hour ended, though every archivist swore return was vulgar. [128] Rules were for weather that stayed down. [135] Her motive was older than fear: to find where forgotten combinations hide before the city forgot its own locks. [154] Combinations were not numbers here; they were the pause between drip and answer, the bruise a name leaves on stone. [174] The first threshold effect came as taste: iron on her tongue, then music trying to remember a mouth. [192] She stepped anyway, and the mist admitted her by narrowing. [202] In that narrowed place she saw margin lines crawl off the doodle and stitch themselves to the cave. [220] Each stitch burned low, a latent blaze under gray water, heating nothing that could be forgiven. [236] The grotto coiled back upon itself, serpent eating serpent, and every scale was a shelf. [251] On the shelves lay abolished keys, wedding knots, plague doors, and lullabies with teeth. [265] Ila read them by almost closing her eyes, because direct sight made the luminous gate pretend to be stone. [284] A second threshold effect pressed sound through her bones until each vertebra chose a different childhood. [300] In one she was loyal; in another she had already betrayed the catalog for bread. [315] The doodle warmed against her palm, drawing a combination from the difference between those lives. [330] Three turns left, one held breath, a mercy refused. [339] She did not speak it; speech would have made the lock proud. [351] Above, or below, the city began its inverted applause as roofs shed rain into the sky. [367] Ila moved through threshold effects the way other thieves moved through windows, bruised and exact. [382] At the final coil the doodle unfolded by itself, larger than mercy, and showed not a gate but a garden. [402] Every plant was a forgotten combination, root gripping the moment before somebody surrendered. [415] She understood then that hiding was not absence; it was a promise postponed by cowards. [430] The seeds of vigilance split open in her chest, each one a small sentry learning her name. [447] If she took the combinations home, the city would wake armed; if she left them, morning would inherit unlocked throats. [467] Her hand hovered, and the latent blaze became choice, quiet as a wick before it remembers fire. [484] She chose one seed only: the combination that opens from inside. [495] The garden exhaled; shelves unscaled themselves; the serpentine grotto in mist turned her gently toward descent. [511] Gravity remembered its manners with nine minutes left, and all upward things came down changed. [526] Ila reentered the archive carrying nothing visible, yet every lock she passed leaned nearer to listen. [542] She shelved the doodle where margin meets meaning, knowing some doors must remain able to answer knocking. [559] Then she planted the seed under her tongue, a clandestinely luminous coal for the next inverted hour. [576] Outside, bells tested the restored weight of air, and the city mistook survival for peace. [591] Ila smiled without showing teeth, because vigilance had found a mouth and was learning to wait.
