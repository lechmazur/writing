<story>In the hush beyond the murmur of lost languages, Vey came to the crystalline orchard perched at the edge of a shimmering crater. [23] She was a marsh-reed sculptor with salt in her cuffs and a boldly sympathetic habit of listening until frightened things stepped into light. [46] The orchard people kept their doors narrow, for the crater had once sung their bridge away and left every root shaking. [67] Vey wanted to graft herself onto community the way reed splits will accept one green shoot and drink as one. [87] At dawn she found the glimmering obsidian arbor black as a held breath, arching over fruit that rang softly when the wind remembered names. [111] On its inner curve burned an elusive street artist's tag, three reeds knotted around a moth, and no elder would name it aloud. [134] The mark meant the web was failing, said Pio, whose sister had crossed the old bridge and returned with crystal dust instead of words. [158] Rather than seize the pigment and accuse, Vey asked the orchard to make the finding shared, a search braided from baskets, ladders, and confessions. [182] Some laughed because grief makes a clean spear, but her courage to feel first turned the spear into a handle. [202] They began where crystal pears leaned over the shimmer, each fruit holding a pale reflection of someone who had been loved. [223] The tag's left reed pointed toward the cider press, so Vey paired stern Anka with Milo, the boy who had broken her harvest bell. [247] Anka found ochre under the press stone; Milo found the courage to say the bell had drowned his small brother's name. [268] When the bell spoke again, dust rose from its mouth and spelled nothing, yet the two stood closer than kin by blaming less. [291] The middle reed bent to the crater stair, where steps of fused glass descended into blue brightness and never touched bottom. [312] Down there Vey matched Pio with Soma, a keeper of lamps who called every missing person a coward. [330] Pio almost struck her, but Vey set one reed between their hands and asked what the crater had taken from Soma first. [352] Soma's answer came as a whisper that the light had swallowed her mother's lullaby and left only rules for keeping lamps lit. [374] Then Pio opened his fist, showing crystal dust that hummed with his sister's unfinished sentence, and the lamp keeper wept oil into flame. [397] The last reed curled back to the arbor, and the searchers understood the artist had never been outside the web but pulling at its cut threads. [423] They gathered after moonrise, when the orchard threw long silver shadows and the crater answered each footstep with a softer one. [444] Beneath the obsidian arch crouched Ilex, a child of bridge builders, painting moths because wings could cross what stone could not. [465] Elder Bane lifted rope to bind the vandal, and Vey felt the old clean spear rise in every chest around her. [486] She stepped into its path with both hands open, boldly sympathetic enough to be called fool, traitor, and finally door. [506] Vey said the tag was not damage but a grafting cut, the wound a scion makes so a stronger root may choose it. [529] Then she unwrapped her finest sculpture, a reed cradle grown flexible by marsh tides, and dedicated it to the orchard's unfinished kinship. [551] The word dedicate frightened her more than falling, because it asked her to remain after being seen. [568] Ilex pressed the moth brush to Vey's palm and guided one stroke, adding a fourth reed that reached toward every watcher. [589] Anka tied the cradle to the arbor with bell cord; Milo promised to teach Ilex the names his brother had carried. [610] Soma poured lamp oil over the black stone until it glimmered like water holding stars, while Pio spoke his sister's sentence to the end. [634] The crater brightened, not with forgiveness exactly, but with the luminescent ache of mouths learning one another again. [652] Crystal branches clicked overhead, and fine threads ran from tree to tree, from wrist to wrist, making webs of kinship visible. [673] They did not become pure or painless; Bane still muttered, the lost still glittered below, and Ilex's tag kept changing doors. [694] Yet when strangers climbed from the marsh roads, the orchard sent Vey forward with reeds in her hair and room in her hands. [717] She would carve no wall around belonging, only arches dark enough to hold light and open enough to be entered. [737]</story>
