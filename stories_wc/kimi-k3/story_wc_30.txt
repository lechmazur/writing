<story>After tomorrow disappeared, the tide still entered the atrium at dawn, polite as a reader returning a book. [18] Sef Vale, titan-gear librarian of the Tidal Atrium, opened the brass gates with shoulders borrowed from a drowned giant. [37] The suit's pistons had been cast from a titan's heart, and their strength fluctuated with moon phases, surging at full silver, loosening when the sky went dark. [64] Since the calendars lost their next page, people woke wearing other memories the way boats wear different lights. [82] Migrating identities moved through the flooded halls like fry through estuary grass. [94] A mason might answer to his sister's name at noon, then shelve law books with hands that remembered kneading bread. [114] Most citizens begged Sef to choose one self for them and nail it fast. [128] She refused, because wonder dies when routine becomes a cage. [138] Her work was to preserve wonder in routine: stamp, shelve, light, feed, mend, and listen. [153] Every low tide she carried a fisherman's bamboo creel down the salt-stained steps to the moon furnace. [170] The creel had belonged to Itto, who once filled it with smelt and onions for his crew. [187] After the disappearance, Itto sometimes returned as Nari, sometimes as a child named Bellow, always clutching the same woven handle. [207] "If I am many," he asked on a thin moon, "which mouth should eat breakfast?" [222] "All of them," Sef said, "but slowly." [229] She lined the creel with clay from the atrium floor and set inside it three black stones that sweated silver. [249] These were tomorrow-ore, fallen from calendars that had burned when the future noticed itself missing. [264] At dark moon, when the titan-gear loosened and names drifted farthest, Sef would smelt the ore in a shell of brass. [285] She did not smelt to purify away contradiction; she smelted by accepting contradictions until metal learned to hold two shapes. [305] The first drop made a rivet bright as wintersun on snow. [316] Sef pressed it between Itto's palm and Nari's palm, though both hands belonged to one trembling person. [333] "Be fisher and witness," she said. [339] "Be child who bellows and elder who forgives the sea." [349] The rivet cooled without choosing, and Itto laughed for the first time since tomorrow vanished. [364] Word traveled through stairwells wet as whale throats. [372] Soon the atrium filled with citizens carrying contradictory relics: wedding rings beside apprentice badges, cradles roped to swords. [390] Sef gave each person a shelf mark with two call numbers and one blank line. [405] The blank line was not erasure; it was the tide's margin, where a returning self could sign its new old name. [426] Routine held the doors: morning tea, noon dusting, evening count of lanterns before the water rose. [442] Within those repeated acts, wonder kept its small animal warmth. [452] On the first full moon after tomorrow disappeared, the titan-gear lifted every shelf at once and revealed a lower room breathing silver. [474] There lay the lost page of the world, not blank, but crowded with crossings-out. [488] Sef read that tomorrow had not been stolen; people had fed it to certainty until it starved. [505] She did not restore the date. [511] Instead she smelted the page's edge into a thin ring and dropped it into the bamboo creel. [528] When Itto Nari Bellow lifted the lid, the ring had become a key with three teeth and no lock. [547] They laughed, all mouths at breakfast, and the atrium answered with a tide that came in without promising to go out. [568] Sef hung the key above the returns desk, where each borrowed book could touch it before entering sleep. [586] New migrations still came with the moon, but no one called them theft anymore. [600] They called them weather, and checked the sky before choosing which name would carry the creel. [616] At dusk the wintersun lay level and unalarmed across the water, gilding routine until it looked almost chosen. [634] Sef wound the titan-gear down gently, stamped the last card, and left tomorrow open like a door with sea light under it. [656]</story>
