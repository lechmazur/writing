<story>The fog came off the harbor before dawn, and the town never woke. [13] Mara Venn stood at the port gate with her case of instruments, listening to a silence so complete she could hear the tide worrying the pilings three streets away. [42] She was an accident investigator, and the accident here had been enormous: a wall of stacked shipping containers, nine high, had come down in the night and crushed the crane operator's cab like a paper cup. [78] No storm had been recorded, no cable found snapped, no weld failed. [90] So the port authority had emptied the town, and now Mara walked alone into the towering labyrinth of containers, where corridors of corrugated steel rose on both sides like the streets of a city built by giants in a hurry. [130] Her work was a survey, and her method was unfashionable: she documented wind patterns between buildings. [146] Other investigators modeled loads and metallurgy; Mara tied ribbons to door handles, chalked arrows on the pavement, and released pinches of powdered pigment into the alley mouths, watching where the air actually went. [179] She had learned the habit from her brother, a flight engineer, whose crew patch she wore sewn inside her cuff, wings and a worn constellation pressed against her pulse. [208] He used to say that instruments disagreed, but air never lied. [219] He died when his freighter went down in weather that had not been forecast, and Mara, who read the report a hundred times, had promised herself she would cultivate foresight in action, training her attention until she could see the accident before it happened. [263] By midmorning the fog had thinned to a gauze, and her survey filled three notebooks. [278] The corridors behaved like canyons, each one combing the wind into a different temper. [292] The wind shifted every few minutes as the sun warmed the steel, and she was rapidly adapting, retying ribbons higher, reweighting pigment with chalk dust, moving her stations when a lane's flow reversed. [325] Pattern by pattern, a shape emerged on her pages, every arrow bending toward the same junction, a four-way crossing where the fallen stack had stood. [350] It was a singular convergence: at certain hours, when the harbor breeze met the streets' reflected breath, all the lanes delivered their air to that one point, and the stack, nine stories of hollow steel, had stood squarely in the lung of the maze. [394] Containers flexed, loads shifted millimeter by millimeter, until the night the whole wall leaned too far and came down. [413] The crane operator had simply been sitting in the place where the wind wanted to push. [429] Mara stood at the junction and felt the hair lift on her arms. [442] Her ribbons, all of them, pointed inward now. [450] The harbor breeze had veered with the tide, and the labyrinth was breathing toward its heart again. [467] Across the crossing, a second wall of containers, identical to the first, ticked softly in the warming air. [485] Then she heard what the silence had been hiding: a boy's whistle, thin as a thread, somewhere in the lanes, and a small dog barking. [510] Old reflex would have been to follow procedure, to note the hazard and phone the authority and wait. [528] Foresight, her brother's gift drilled into her for years, moved her feet instead. [541] She found the boy two lanes over, hunting for his dog between the towering boxes, and she did not explain; she took his wrist and walked fast, tracing her chalk arrows backward, out of the lanes that fed the junction, while behind them the ticking grew into a groan. [590] The dog burst from a gap and ran ahead of them as the second wall folded down into the crossing with a sound like the end of the world, filling the exact footprint of the first collapse. [627] Dust rolled over them, and the fog took it, and for a while the maze was only white. [645] When it cleared, Mara sat on a bollard with the boy and the dog and wrote her report in the same hazy precision she had walked with all day, every arrow accounted for, every wind named. [681] She noted the convergence, the adaptable survey, the ribbon that had snapped upward like a hand pointing the way out. [701] That evening the town's siren tested itself, and the sound, thin and ordinary, meant people could come home. [719] Mara touched the patch inside her cuff and told her brother, quietly, that the air had not lied today either, and this time she had been early. [746]</story>
