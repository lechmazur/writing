<story>The storm held its breath over the floating rock tea ceremony platform. [12] Below, the first gale had spent itself, and above, the second gathered black wool. [56] Its ink crystallizes when stressed, her master had warned, blooming into facets no apology could soften. [72] Ila had come to transform scars into stories, not to decorate them. [84] A saturated hush dyed the moss indigo and made even the kettle seem thoughtful. [98] So she revisited youthful superstitions because terror had kept them alphabetical in her bones. [235] Ila had hated that habit after the lightning chose her left arm. [247] Hatred was easier than grammar. [252] Grammar asked why the burn branched like a river searching for its sea. [265] Osen lifted the cup, and the platform lifted with him, balanced on silence. [278] "Now," he said, though no steam dared move. [286] Everyone drew breath and did not give it back. [329] They hurt only when storms compared themselves to her childhood. [339] Ila dipped the crow quill, trimmed tip first, into the tea instead of ink. [661] Osen bowed to the empty cup first. [668] Ila gathered the shed scales into her sleeve, light as forgiven snow. [680] Later she would grind them for ink and sign the margins of dangerous maps.
