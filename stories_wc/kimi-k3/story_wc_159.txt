<story>Above the weather, where stairs ended in milk-blue air, Ione kept the opal library above clouds. [16] She was the glass-orchid caretaker, gently systematic before sunrise and after the last bell of birds. [32] Her gloves carried foxglove regret: beautiful, poisonous, and careful never to root too deep. [46] The shelves held deserted marvels, each dome humming with pollen that had outlived its bee. [61] No readers came, only cloud-shouldered storms pressing their gray foreheads to the panes. [74] Ione fed the orchids cooled moonwater and listened for names loosening from spines. [87] A book sighed Aurel; another tasted of brine and said Nox before forgetting itself. [101] She wrote nothing down, because ink made graves too easily. [111] Instead she carried a wind chime bamboo tube cracked like a vein of frost. [125] It had belonged to the museum below, reached by a ladder of condemned rainbows. [139] Across the museum of extinct feelings, labels curled over empty pedestals: tenderness, awe, hometown. [153] Ione descended there each dusk to light the listening wicks with a blue taper. [167] The flame bent toward absence as if absence were a window left open. [180] Her task was simple and endless: to preserve the sound of forgotten names. [193] She had once said a sister's name into an orchid and watched the petals turn translucent. [209] Since then, every blossom rang faintly when the tower leaned into evening. [221] One storm tore the ladder away and left the library marooned in brightness. [234] The tube's crack widened, sipping wind, giving back a note too thin to carry. [248] Ione pressed beeswax into the seam, then stopped, hearing the damage answer in tune. [262] It wanted not mending but a mouth shaped for loss. [272] So she arranged three orchids beneath it, white for hush, violet for warning, gold for debt. [288] Their glass roots held drops of names she had rescued from burned ledgers. [301] Maram for the cartographer. [305] Seth for the bell founder who feared his own music. [315] Una for the child who invented hiding and was never found. [326] Ione touched each petal in order, patient as water counting stairs. [337] Then she breathed through the cracked bamboo by the echo of a long-forgotten song. [351] The note climbed, broke, and found its fracture useful. [360] Down in the museum, extinct feelings lifted their dusty faces. [370] Awe opened one umbrella under a ceiling that had forgotten rain. [381] Tenderness took off its glove and touched nobody, carefully. [390] Hometown unlocked a door onto fields no foot had agreed to remember. [402] The library answered with pollen bright as small bells. [411] Every orchid leaned toward the tube, thirsty for the names riding that flawed air. [425] Ione heard Maram sharpen into coastline, Seth darken into bronze, Una vanish laughing. [438] For an instant, the deserted marvels were inhabited by echoes choosing to stay. [451] Then the clouds shouldered in, soft as mourners, and asked for their share. [464] Ione gave them the names of storms already spent: Cilka, Bront, Hesper. [476] They carried those syllables outward and dropped them gently over cities asleep. [488] In exchange, the sky returned one breath smelling of rain on dust. [500] It entered the crack and stayed there, a green pulse inside pale bamboo. [513] By morning, the tube had flowered. [519] A single glass-orchid grew from the split, ringing whenever anyone below almost remembered. [532] Ione did not smile; joy required witnesses, and she had trained for solitude. [545] Still she changed the ledger rule: no name was lost if its sound could feed a root. [562] Visitors never climbed, yet echoes arrived wearing shoes of thin thunder. [573] They asked whether preservation was mercy or only a slower theft. [584] Ione answered by opening the orchids at dusk and letting each name choose its petal. [599] Some stayed one hour, some seven years, and some returned as weather with human pauses. [614] She kept her gloves on, because foxglove regret could heal and still stain the hands. [629] At last the tube split fully, not apart but into two throats. [641] One sang upward to the opal shelves; one sang down across the museum of extinct feelings. [657] Between them Ione stood, lighting nothing now, listening as forgotten names practiced being bright. [671] When the first true reader knocked from inside a cloud, she was ready with air. [686] She opened the door and said, without writing, every name the wind had saved. [700]</story>
