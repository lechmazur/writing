After the beekeeper's last visit, the waterfall stopped sounding like rain and began sounding like a held breath. [18]
Mara Vell, museum guide for the grotto city behind the monumental waterfall, polished the emerald-tinted opera glass lens with a handkerchief that smelled of rosewater. [43]
She was cultured in the old courtly way and historically oriented to the bone, which made her bow to cracked saints and correct emperors before breakfast. [69]
The displaced persons lodged in the lower galleries called her Guide, though most had lost the countries that taught them to say it. [92]
Their rooms were niches carved from spray, their lamps were fish oil, and their dread wore perfume because rosewater was cheaper than tears. [115]
When the thaw came, it did not sing; it loosened the black ice inside the lens and woke a green spark under its rim. [139]
Through it Mara saw the falls part like curtains, not water but threads of glass spelling names in a dead court hand. [161]
One name was Aurel, beekeeper, traitor, saint, the man who had sealed the archive and promised to return with spring. [181]
His last visit had left three hives empty, one queen dead, and a loaf cooling on the museum step. [200]
The loaf was braided into initials, M V, the family custom Mara used when grief required witnesses who could eat. [220]
By custom, the first letter fed the living; by custom, the second accused the dead. [235]
Mara broke the V and tasted honey, ash, and the bitter wax of a city taught to forget. [253]
Her motive had never been nostalgia; she meant to rescue dying arts before their makers were renamed as refugees, then as rain. [275]
The archive held lace patterns, bell metal recipes, lullabies for thunder, and the surgery that saved violins from damp. [294]
Without those arts, the grotto would remain beautiful in the way of a tomb with good lighting. [311]
So Mara gathered the displaced bakers first, because hunger makes an honest parliament. [324]
She asked each family to bake bread shaped like initials, not for saints, but for the skills their hands still remembered. [345]
A potter shaped R L and found, under the crust, the wrist turn for glazing river clay. [362]
A widow shaped S T and wept when the crumb showed the stitch that keeps surgical silk from cutting skin. [382]

Rosewater dread moved through the crowd, sweet at the lips and metal at the back of the tongue. [453]

Mara climbed to the beekeeper's niche at dusk, carrying the lens, the V crust, and a knife for cutting comb. [509]

She understood then that Aurel had not abandoned the arts; he had frozen them inside the people most likely to be searched last. [553]
Displaced persons crossed borders with recipes under their tongues and stitches sleeping in their cuffs. [568]
