<story>After the collapse of civilization, Maren kept the beacon tower anyway, polishing lenses no ship would ever answer. [18] She was the last custodian, retrocognitively blessed, cursed, really, to see every yesterday layered over today like soot on glass. [38] Below the tower stood the glass humming pavilion, its panes singing thin notes whenever the wind combed the dunes. [57] Maren swept its floor each morning because ritual was the only rope she had left to hold. [74] On the equinox of ash, she found a broken crystal prism beneath the pavilion's central plinth, split clean through its heart. [95] When her fingers closed around it, the blessing flared, and the past opened like a door. [111] She saw the old world: engineers tuning the pavilion's panes, then sealing a shard of hush inside the prism, a sliver of pure captured silence meant to steady the beacon's flame. [142] She saw the day it shattered, a looter's elbow, an accident, nobody's plan. [155] And she saw herself, years ago, a girl refusing to board the last evacuation convoy because a stray moth had landed on the tower door. [180] Everyone called that coincidence; Maren had spent her whole life aching to prove that coincidences have meaning. [197] Now the prism's halves lay in her palms, and the shard of hush leaked out in slow, soundless ripples that flattened the pavilion's song. [221] The tower's lamp had been cold for eleven years. [230] But the old records said the beacon could be rekindled only by tracking star paths, aligning the lens with three specific constellations as they crossed the meridian on one named night. [261] Tonight was that night, if the ash clouds broke. [270] Maren carried the prism halves up the spiral stair, past rusted gears that remembered motion. [285] At the lamp room she set them into the cradle, and the shard of hush settled between them like a held breath, drinking every creak of the wind. [313] Silence, she understood now, was not emptiness; it was the tuning fork the flame had always needed. [330] She climbed to the roof where the sky was clearing in ragged seams, stars pricking through like stitches. [348] By tracking star paths the way her grandmother had taught her, thumb to horizon, eye to the wheeling drift, she marked the first constellation and rang the pavilion once with a copper rod. [381] The note rose, met the leaking hush, and steadied into a single pure tone that pointed true north. [399] The second constellation wheeled into place; she turned the great lens, and somewhere below, the prism halves glowed as the hush sealed their seam. [423] Then the third stars arrived, exactly where the records promised, exactly when her heart most doubted. [439] Maren struck the igniter. [443] The flame caught, small as a thistle seed, then climbed the oil-soaked wick and bloomed white-gold through the mended prism, splitting into every color the dead world had forgotten. [472] Light rolled off the tower and across the black water in long, patient spokes. [486] Maren laughed until the hush in her chest loosened, because a moth had kept her here, a looter's elbow had freed the silence, and tonight's sky had cleared at the exact hour the stars aligned. [521] Three accidents, braided into one burning proof. [528] Before dawn, an answering light winked from far across the strait, faint, flickering, deliberate. [542] Someone else had survived; someone else had kept a tower. [552] Maren lifted her lamp shutter and answered in the old code, pulse by pulse, her hands steadier than they had any right to be. [576] Below, the glass humming pavilion sang again, and this time the hush inside the prism did not smother the song but gave it shape, the way silence shapes music. [605] Hope, Maren decided, was a thistle-blown thing: fragile, wind-scattered, and stubbornly certain to root wherever it landed. [622] She kept the beacon burning until sunrise, then climbed down to sweep the pavilion floor, because some rituals hold the world together, and some coincidences are simply the world holding back. [653] The moth returned at dusk and settled on the warm lens, and Maren, smiling, logged it in the keeper's book as evidence. [675]</story>
