Mira, a wistful loom spinner, worked where the forgotten orchard deep in a bamboo forest kept its last gold apples. [20]
Her shuttle wore one tree sap amber bead, stoically sparkling whenever wind combed the hollow canes above the patient loom. [40]
She crossed through the hush of time's unraveling tapestry after roads forgot their names and all bells lost mouths completely. [60]

Mira heard it thinning inside her ribs, where her brother's last promise still circled like a pale moth around lamps. [100]
He had gone to carry a message across impossible distance before the silence learned to bite through maps and borders. [120]
The bead held his words because sap remembers light long after branches surrender their green to autumn's first returning frost. [140]
To wake it, Mira needed edges, for only boundaries can teach scattered music where a center waits still in patience. [160]
She found them through jigsaw puzzle edges found in a rummage sale box beneath paper stars and buttons and thimbles. [180]
The pieces were not cardboard but pear bark, bamboo scale, robin bone, and pale porcelain from broken cups and saucers. [200]
Each edge would join when matched by vibration, so Mira pressed them against the amber bead and held breath there. [220]
The first pair clicked, and an apple somewhere remembered blush, though no tongue remained to name it in autumn again. [240]
With that click, one thread on Mira's loom tightened, drawing a line toward the east beyond sight and beyond fear. [260]
The price arrived gently as the taste of rain left her mouth and pooled inside the glowing bead like coin. [280]
She did not bargain; sacrifice stayed latent, a seed under stone, until the border demanded bloom in the dark orchard. [300]
Another edge fit, and frogs returned to one black pond, while Mira forgot her mother's lullaby note by silver note. [320]
Grief tried to stop her hands, but bamboo knocked together, counting like solemn patients outside a birth room and waited. [340]
Mira fed that grief to the loom, weaving it as weft so absence could carry weight without breaking apart tonight. [360]

Each hum was a syllable of her brother's message, blistering the amber from within with patient blue fire and ash. [400]
It said continue; it said do not follow; it said plant me where silence first opened its mouth and swallowed. [420]
To recommence the world, Mira saw, she must finish the edge and give the bead a road made of sound. [440]

Their pictures had been cut crooked by grief, yet their sides answered Mira's rib cage exactly when she breathed in. [480]
She joined ocean to pear bark and paid with the color of her first honest kiss under the cedar bridge. [500]

If she released it, dawn would still happen, but no witness inside her would confirm the warmth or his face. [600]

She lifted it, placed her brother's laughter inside the bead, and pressed the final border closed until it sang once. [640]

The amber bead cracked like an egg, releasing a bright moth that knew the way east over salt and thorn. [680]
Mira could not call after it; her voice lived in the border now, holding every rescued note from coming apart. [700]
Yet when the moth vanished, apples answered with leaves, and Mira smiled, wistful, present, finally heard by the orchard itself. [720]
