On the tidal obsidian ridge, Ila Venn wound the tower as the sea rehearsed its bright confession beneath black noon. [20]
She was the precise local clock tower winder, ethically diligent because time here fed boats, graves, and ovens without mercy. [40]
One dawn the clock tower pendulum bob answered her pull with silence, heavy as an unburied name inside the case. [60]
Below, fishers and glass cutters watched the tide hesitate, their shared balance tilted toward quarrel and salt on every tongue. [80]
Ila climbed to restore shared balance, not by pardon, but by measuring each harm until it could breathe without drowning. [100]
At low water she found a broken rake handle furred with brine, lying where the ridge kept its spare skeletons. [120]
Its wood remembered kelp fields, a boy's small theft, three shorted weights, and her own careful cowardice after winter bells. [140]
She did not carve forgiveness; she carved coordinates through tiny inscriptions carved along a broken rake handle for true harm. [160]
Before any mark she returned rope, coin, and credit, refusing absolution that cost nothing but warm breath from the guilty. [180]
