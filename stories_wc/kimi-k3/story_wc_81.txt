<story>During the blackout, Mara Venn returned to the drowned archive with a lamp hooded by blue glass. [17] She was the logical page indexer for Pelagos, a forgotten library inside a sunken city that still breathed through its stacks. [38] No generators answered, no elevators prayed upward, only the pressure of water made the bronze doors tick. [55] Mara had come to trace honest origins, not for glory, but because every borrowed book in Pelagos had begun returning false. [76] False meant margins rewritten after midnight, dates kneaded soft, and dedications pointing toward donors who had never lived. [94] In her satchel lay an antique ink stamp, heavy as a locked room, its seal cut with a leaping fish. [114] The first director had used it to mark acquisitions when the city above believed sunlight was a contract. [132] Now the stamp felt cold from the black water, and Mara, tranquilly perceptive, noticed that its fish faced the wrong current. [153] A stamp should remember motion by repeating it, yet this one remembered intention by refusing the lie. [170] She set it beside Catalogue Zero, the chained volume that listed every shelf before Pelagos sank. [186] The index entry for the lantern room said repaired, but the paper grain under her thumb said replaced. [204] Her first hypothesis blamed thieves in dive suits, practical men with knives where mercy should be. [220] Then an alternative hypothesis opened like an umbrella turned inside out: the library itself was editing memory to survive the flood. [241] The thought should have frightened her; instead it asked for better light. [253] Mara chose to readjust the lamp, then readjust the chain, until Catalogue Zero lay flat enough to confess. [271] Ink pooled in the fish seal, blacker than the blackout, and she pressed it onto the entry. [288] The mark rose through the page like heat through winter cloth, revealing a second line beneath the first. [306] It named no thief; it named the founding ledger, donated by Mara's grandmother under a borrowed tide. [323] A donor who had never lived was impossible unless the city had invented a mother for its oldest book. [342] Mara heard stacks drip in sonar patterns, each drop returning an answer from a lower room. [358] Between those answers moved reclaimed stillness, not silence, but the poised second after a lie admits its weight. [376] She followed that poised second down the spiral aisle where biography had swollen into myth. [391] At every landing she stamped the air first, watching whether the fish would turn toward proof or toward comfort. [410] Comfort always smelled of camphor; proof smelled of iron, salt, and ink before it dries. [425] Dappled resolve steadied her hands when the stairs ended in a reading room full of chairs facing one empty desk. [445] On the desk lay her own apprenticeship card, stamped though she remembered refusing the ceremony. [460] The date placed her oath before her birth, a clerical cruelty too neat for accident. [475] If Pelagos had authored her, then tracing origins meant choosing which sentence deserved to remain true. [491] She pressed the stamp to her card, not to erase the false date, but to give the true one somewhere to stand. [513] The fish bit through both dates and left a small door of paper, open on the honest word begin. [532] Above them something vast readjusted in its sleep, and the shelves leaned closer without falling. [547] Mara understood then that the library had not been lying to win; it had been lying to keep one reader brave. [568] She carried Catalogue Zero back to the bronze doors while the blackout thinned into gray seams. [584] Behind her, pages shut one by one, careful as eyelids, keeping the dark from learning their names. [601] At the threshold she stamped the final index and wrote beneath it: origin is where courage first checks its facts. [621] When light returned, Pelagos remained sunken, forgotten, and enormous; but every false donor had been readjusted into weather. [639] Mara left the stamp wet on the step so the next honest coward would find it heavy, warm, and facing home. [660]</story>
