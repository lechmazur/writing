<story>By trade, Sela was an analytical street performer, though her license said juggler and her pulse said tide chart. [19] Her theater was the tidal arcade under stars, where the opera across the square exhaled strangers between acts. [37] There the sea combed the tiles, withdrew, and returned with a brighter coin of foam. [52] Sela stayed lightly attentive, letting coughs, heel strikes, and gull shadows register without being seized. [67] Her motive was not applause; it was to serenade monotony with creativity before repetition could rust the hour. [85] She called her method orchestrated serendipity, a calculated wilderness of small permissions. [97] Nothing was shoved; events were invited to happen by ordering randomness until accident kept time. [112] In her cuff lived a bishop's chess piece, white as a tooth, nicked at the miter from some old drowning. [132] She had found it in a storm drain after the winter regatta, when the arcade rang like a cup. [151] Most nights she used it as a metronome, rolling it once across her palm before each impossible catch. [169] Tonight the piece felt warmer, because Vale had come back to work the intermission crowd. [184] Vale stole diagonally, sliding from pillar to pillar only when applause folded the air. [198] He had once been a chess hustler on the night boats, and rumor said his pocket set lacked one bishop. [218] Sela did not chase him; a chase would make the arcade honest in the wrong way. [234] Instead she spent three intermissions tuning the floor. [242] She chalked four faint slants from column base to drain grate, each pale enough to be mistaken for salt. [261] She paid two siblings in barley sugar to race paper boats at the seventh minute and never at the sixth. [281] She asked Nero the violinist to sour one string before the bell, because a crooked note makes thieves look up. [301] She loosened a lantern hook by half a turn, teaching its shadow to lean toward the eastern arch. [319] All of it looked like weather, which was the point. [329] At the next bell, the opera doors opened and poured out silk, wet wool, and impatient perfume. [346] Sela began with three hoops and a lie about gravity. [356] The hoops crossed, hesitated, and chose to fall around a sailor's wrist. [368] Laughter rose; Vale moved with it, soft as a page turning. [379] His hand visited a conductor's coat and borrowed the watch that had been keeping the overture honest. [396] Sela missed a catch on purpose, and one hoop rolled to the first chalk line. [411] The siblings launched their boats; Nero's sour string lifted every chin by an inch. [425] Vale looked up, found no danger, and stepped back along his practiced diagonal. [438] The tide, punctual as debt, sent one clear sheet through the grates. [450] It lifted the bishop from Sela's cuff where she had let it rest, and set it sliding down the pale seam. [471] A bishop cannot cross to a color it does not own, so the little piece kept faith with the dark tiles and reached Vale's shoe. [496] He saw the missing bishop and forgot, for one breath, that he was being hunted by geometry. [513] He bent; the lantern shadow leaned; the paper boats arrived like witnesses. [525] The watch slipped from his sleeve and landed inside Sela's fallen hoop with a sound like a coin confessing. [544] Sela caught it as if it had been thrown, then bowed to the conductor before understanding could harden into alarm. [564] Applause returned, wider this time, and Vale straightened with empty hands and a face full of tide. [581] He ran toward the eastern arch, where the loosened lantern swung once and marked him for the constable Sela had never seemed to call. [605] No whistle spoiled the show; only Nero changed keys, which was enough. [617] By the second bell, the conductor had his watch, the siblings had their sugar, and the sea had taken back the chalk. [639] Sela palmed the bishop and felt it cool again, satisfied that chance had not been captured, only given stairs. [658] When the next crowd drifted out, she rolled the white piece across her palm and listened for the hour to admit its pattern. [681] The stars kept their distance; the tiles kept their shine; monotony, bruised and singing, went home early. [698]</story>
