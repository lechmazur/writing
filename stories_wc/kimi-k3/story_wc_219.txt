<story>After making first contact, the rainwashed tower held spring in its stones, a bronze quiet that made every drip sound deliberate. [21] Mara Venn, a luminous skeptic with academically rebellious margins in every notebook, had come to understand the chemistry of homecoming. [99] The method, if it deserved the word, was via dusty footprints left on an overgrown sculpture path after winter thaw. [119] Those prints appeared whenever the storm gutters overflowed, too narrow for deer and too purposeful for wind. [136] Mara brought reagents, brass probes, and the fly amber she had inherited from Professor Ilex, her advisor in heresy. [155] Inside the cloudy bead, a prehistoric fly kept one wing raised, as if interrupting extinction to correct a footnote. [174] She believed evidence should glow less than doubt, but the amber shone whenever the tower bells leaned toward evening. [193] At the first plinth, a marble astronomer wore ivy over its equations, and dust outlined feet climbing its lips. [212] The footprints converged there with Mara's shadow, though she stood still and refused the comfort of destiny. [229] Homecoming, she wrote in pencil, might be a reaction between distance and recognition, catalyzed by belonging without consent. [247] The aliens replied through static on her cracked radio, translating chemistry into weather: copper, rain, leaf, bone, return. [265] A bronze quiet settled over the path, not empty but minted, as though the air had been alloyed for listening. [285] Mara followed print after print past a lion with mossy teeth and a saint whose hands had eroded into brackets. [342] That memory betrayed her method, because control groups rarely weep or remember bread. [355] The path bent toward the tower door, where footprints and roots had braided a threshold no faculty committee approved. [391] Mara set the fly amber on the banister; its insect prisoner seemed to lean toward the hum above. [409] In the bell room, first contact repeated itself as chemistry, not language: a slow precipitation of meaning from noise. [428] The visitors were not bodies but conditions, a pressure that taught molecules in her blood to remember before birth. [447] Understanding homecoming became dangerous, since it asked whether Earth had made her or merely caught her falling. [481] Mara, academically rebellious to the last, declined to worship and instead labeled the miracle unknown, pending tenderness.
