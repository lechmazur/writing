<story>At nautical twilight the opal dock in mist glowed like a held breath, and Eben Quill, eccentric lighthouse keeper, wound the fog clock with a brass key he wore on a ribbon. [32] He named the gulls after debts, fed them only crusts, and believed any true answer arrived wearing a gap. [51] The lamp above him turned its softly persistent white eye over the water, counting hulls that were not there. [70] From that counted nothing came a stranger, dry as paper, though the planks behind him darkened with sea. [88] His left hand held a dried Edelweiss boutonniere, alpine and impossible, while his right hand made a circle over his heart and broke it. [112] Then he set the flower in Eben's palm, closed Eben's fingers around it, and stepped backward into mist without a splash. [133] Eben did not shout, because shouts made the fog thicker and the dead less polite. [148] Instead he chose to investigate the way he cleaned lenses: by observing gaps before touching glass. [164] First gap: no line tied the stranger to any boat, yet his sleeve carried salt from far off. [182] Second gap: between two blinkings of the lamp his reflection stood alone on the opal skin, though he had already gone. [203] Third gap: the boutonniere would not wet, and its stem was sewn shut with blue thread. [219] Eben knew that thread; he had bought it for Liora's coat the winter the mountain gave her to the sea. [239] In Rill Cove the drowning poor could leave one memory inside a kept thing, and the lonely could borrow it until dawn forgot their faces. [264] Eben had called that trade wicked, then used it, then built rules around using it so his sin looked like weather. [285] Rule one said borrowed memories must sleep below the lamp, where light could audit their dreams. [301] Rule two said never open them at nautical twilight, when the horizon loses its witness and calls every loss by your name. [323] The stranger had broken rule two for him, which meant the final gesture was a lock, not a farewell. [342] Eben climbed toward the lamp room with the flower held forward like a candle that accused him. [359] On the stair he counted the missing things: one gull cry, one iron step's cold, one heartbeat spent elsewhere. [378] The logbook lay open to March, and March had a hole cut neat as a mouth. [394] He did not remember cutting it, which proved nothing, because his hands kept secrets from his face. [411] Inside the boutonniere stem he found a paper no wider than a tear, folded around a strand of red hair. [431] The words were his own small carpenter print: Keep the fall, return the choosing. [445] Below the lamp, behind a panel he had sworn was blank, slept a glass float warm as skin. [463] It held Liora on the cliff path, Edelweiss in her buttonhole, laughing because Eben feared edges more than death. [482] For eighteen years he had borrowed the fall: her heel slipping, his hand late, the sea receiving what pride dropped. [502] Now the borrowed memory opened its other side, and he saw the gap he had paid to avoid. [520] Liora had not stumbled; she had stepped between Eben and the edge, trading her balance for his life. [538] Her last gesture, stolen from him by terror, matched the stranger's: circle the heart, break the circle, give away the piece. [559] To decode a stranger's final gesture, Eben only needed to admit whose hand had taught it. [575] He carried float and flower down to the dock while the light swept over him and did not look away. [595] Alone was too small a word for that witnessed isolation; even the mist seemed to hold its coat closed. [614] He found the gap where no star repeated on the water, a black seam between two moored boats. [632] There he set the glass float, the blue thread, and the dried Edelweiss boutonniere inside the broken circle of his palms. [653] Return the choosing, he said, and the words left him cleaner than prayer. [666] The borrowed memories rose as one breath: Liora steady on the path, the stranger rowing under no moon, Eben's hand finally arriving. [688] When the lamp came around, its softly persistent beam found the seam and filled it without owning it. [706] Eben kept one rule then, and broke the rest: he left the logbook open so dawn could read the hole. [726]</story>
