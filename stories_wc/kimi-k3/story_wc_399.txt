<story>After the prophet arrives, the murmuring pavilion lowers its canvas ribs and listens. [13] Dunes lean close, and the kinetic parapet beneath the dunes turns like a buried shoulder. [58] In each clearing stands Ila Venn, half-masked line walker, with linen over the left side of her face. [76] The bare half is strangely honest, while the hidden half keeps her mercy from becoming applause. [92] She has come to consecrate the clearings before the prophet's wisdom cools into quotation. [106] Her motive is plain and private: to preserve the echo of wisdom, not the statue of it. [123] A direct gaze makes the pavilion repeat words; a sideways attention makes it reveal pauses. [138] So Ila works through peripheral awareness, reading the edges where sleeves almost touch and breath changes. [154] The prophet enters with dust on his hem and a silence that seems already remembered. [169] He blesses no one, which makes the nearest mothers loosen their grip on frightened children. [184] He says that cities fall when answers harden faster than questions can kneel. [197] The pavilion drinks the saying, but Ila watches a boy's sandal press the outer line. [244] A clearing opens around three old women who have been arguing over borrowed salt. [258] Their quarrel stops, not from shame, but because each hears the other finish. [381] Yet the prophet notices her and does not ask her to uncover. [393] He has brought wisdom like rain; she is making roof and cistern from intervals. [497] She does not correct them, because correction would steal the room where wisdom returns. [511] Instead she sets the cradle rocking with one finger and lets the motion finish the sentence.
