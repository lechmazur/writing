<story>After receiving the donor heart, Ila Venn returned to the floating rock tea house with a watchful dye master's patience and a new thunder under her ribs. [27] The heart did not race; it was quietly explosive, a small coal breaking open whenever a voice made strangers lean toward it. [49] She had survived the valley surgeons, yet survival felt like a borrowed cup of tea, fragrant, warm, and still belonging to someone missing. [72] Ila wanted to learn why some people magnetize stories, why a simple telling could pin bright eyes to one mouth and pull feet over stone. [97] At dawn the rocks woke first, their iron veins answering the sun while mist filled the valley below like unspoken ink. [118] She opened her dye book, ground indigo with ash, and set the blue morpho wing inside a clear cup no guest was allowed to touch. [143] The wing had come with the heart in a donor box labeled only with a warning: illuminate gently, or be illuminated. [164] Ila obeyed by tracking sun trails across the floor, marking where light climbed a table, crossed a sleeve, and stopped at the wing. [187] When the trail reached the cup, scales flashed blue, green, then a warning violet she named iridescent caution. [205] It meant that wonder had begun to sharpen, that beauty was holding a knife behind its back. [222] Most days the tea house drifted in valley-mist wonder, with kettles humming and travelers trading routes as if routes were prayers. [243] Then came Nox Ara, smiling like weather, saying he knew a ledge where the mist parted and showed each listener his lost name. [266] The room leaned; cups paused halfway to lips; even the rocks seemed to tilt their ears. [282] Ila's heart broke its coal open, and heat spread through her wrist into the spoon. [297] She kept her face kind, because a watchful dye master never accuses color before it dries. [313] Nox told of a child beneath the valley who sold names back to the dead, and every guest heard the child calling from below. [337] The blue morpho wing went black at the edges, then bright enough to illuminate the salt in a young porter's tears. [358] Ila saw the porter rise without deciding to rise, saw three merchants choose the cliff stairs as if choosing breakfast. [378] She understood then that stories magnetize by offering the heart a rhythm to borrow, and hers had been borrowed before. [398] The donor, whoever they had been, must have followed such a rhythm until it beat them hollow. [415] Ila lifted the cup into the latest sun trail and let the wing burn violet over every bowl. [433] To Nox she said, "Drink first, story keeper, and taste the color that caution makes when it chooses to shine." [453] He laughed, but the dye touched his tongue, and his next sentence arrived with its cost attached. [470] The lost name beneath the valley was his sister's, given up by him for applause, and the ledge he praised had eaten the searchers. [494] Guests blinked as if a lamp had been turned inside them; the porter sat down hard and spilled tea back into his own shadow. [518] Nox reached for the old rhythm again, but Ila rang the brass cup once, not loud, only exact. [536] The sound set the sun trail trembling, and the wing poured caution across the floor in blue footprints leading away from the stairs. [559] No one followed Nox when he left; the mist kept its wonder, and the rocks kept their balance. [577] That evening Ila wrote in the dye book that some people magnetize stories because a hollow room will mistake any pulse for its own. [601] She touched the scar above her donor heart and thanked the stranger for failing to be endless. [618] Then she set the blue morpho wing where dawn would find it, ready to illuminate gently when beauty next hid a blade. [640]</story>
