<story>Seno Vale was the obliging sky mechanic of Vireo, a xenobiology research station cupped in the storms of a violet giant. [21] He answered every bell with a mechanically warm courtesy, though his gloves were cold and his smile had frost on it. [42] At twilight the sky herds folded their glassy wings along the mooring spars, and Vireo listened. [58] The magnetic nexus at twilight was below the aviary, a black seed in a cradle of copper fields that should have slept. [80] Instead it began the subdued awakenings that Doctor Yven feared, stirring eggs without cracking them. [95] Larvae brightened by one pale degree, then dimmed, as if trying a future and putting it back. [112] Yven asked Seno only to tighten a loose field shoe, but her eyes asked for a confession. [129] He gave her neither refusal nor comfort, because three winters ago her brother had stepped through a door and left no body. [151] Seno had polished that door every week since, needing to prove that time moves sideways when no one survives to correct it. [173] The herds knew before the instruments did. [180] Their rest made a hush so complete that loose screws ticked toward the nexus like iron snow. [197] Seno hung from the service rail and set his palm against the cage. [210] Through his glove the metal felt almost alive, not with heat but with attention. [224] He understood then that the station was not malfunctioning; it was being answered. [237] Yven wanted sedation for the eggs, but sedation would only make the sideways room darker. [252] Seno proposed proof through holographic principles, using the folded wings as plates, the nexus as reference beam, and twilight as the shutter. [274] If each membrane held one instant, interference could show whether instants stood in line or side by side. [292] Yven said the math was indecent. [298] Seno said grief was worse, and she let him build. [308] He tuned beacon kites until their light crossed the aviary without touching a feather. [322] He bribed the air scrubbers with his own heat ration so the hush would not break. [338] Everything had to occur within the hush of beating wings at rest, when pulse became architecture and mercy kept its tools clean. [360] On the first pass, the hologram showed eggs waking one after another, obedient as beads on a string. [378] On the second, the same eggs woke beside themselves, each larva turning toward a neighbor that had not hatched. [397] Yven whispered that mirrors could lie. [403] Seno opened the maintenance throat and found the manual lever, made for hands when every clever system failed. [421] The instruction plate said pull once for isolation, twice for burial, never for truth. [435] He chose never, because never had been waiting for him since the door took Yven's brother. [451] The lever resisted with the courtesy of ice, then warmed under his mechanically patient grip. [466] Fields folded inward; the aviary filled with standing light, and every sleeping wing cast two shadows. [482] One shadow pointed down the ordinary corridor of cause and effect. [493] The other lay flat across the wall, where a man in an old orange suit was still knocking. [511] Yven did not scream; she named him, and the name returned to her changed by use. [527] Seno saw that sideways was not escape but adjacency, a bench beside the road where the lost kept breathing. [546] The nexus offered to widen the seam if he held the lever past pain. [560] He thought of eggs waking beside unborn sisters, of Yven reaching into cold radiance, of his own warm lies. [579] An obliging mechanic fixes the thing in front of him, not the universe that made it break. [596] So Seno let the lever rise by one tooth, enough to leave a witness and not a wound. [614] The standing light collapsed into twilight, and the herds slept on while two larvae opened neighboring eyes. [631] In the log Yven wrote that time had been proven sideways, then struck out proven and wrote invited. [649] Seno kept his burned glove in a drawer, mechanically warm for hours, an icy warmth after the station cooled. [668] When bells rang after shift, he still answered, but now he listened first for wings choosing which beside to beat in. [689]</story>
