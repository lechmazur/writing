<story>The ancient temple library lost in time could only be entered across the pause of shifting constellations, when the stars held their breath between one sky and the next. [29] For three nights the doors stood open in the desert, and for three nights Senna the diminutive titan kept watch beside them with a lamp. [54] She stood barely five feet tall, though her shoulders bore the broad strength of her mountain kin and her hands could lift stone shelves that broke mortal backs. [82] Her people had built the temple before kings learned to write their names, and they had left her there as its last keeper when the roads sank under sand. [111] On the second night a rider came veiled in royal purple, claiming the lost Chronicle of Descent that would prove his right to the vacant throne. [137] He called himself Prince Aldric, son of the late king, and his voice carried the practiced sorrow of a man rehearsed by mirrors. [160] Senna bowed without kneeling, regally unpretentious in her plain gray robe, and asked what proof he offered besides a fine horse and a finer story. [185] He showed her a signet ring and a birth record sealed in gold, both bright as new coins, and demanded entrance before the constellations moved again. [211] Senna knew gold that old should have dulled, and ink that old should have bitten deep into the page. [230] She suspected him at once, but suspicion without proof would only martyr a liar and crown him in the eyes of his followers waiting beyond the dunes. [257] Her purpose hardened quietly to unmask a pretender to the throne before he could carry a forged past into the capital and make it law. [282] She could not bar him by force, for temple law bound her to serve any seeker who arrived during the pause, so she led him inward and planned to sunder his claim from its false roots. [318] Inside, towers of scrolls rose into perfumed darkness, and every aisle smelled of cedar, dust, and rain that had fallen a thousand years before. [342] Aldric reached eagerly for the Chronicle where it lay chained beneath glass, but Senna placed one broad hand upon the case and shook her head. [367] The true record, she explained, could not be read directly, because its guardians had hidden its testimony through cryptic messages left in library books across a hundred harmless shelves. [396] Each message gave only a shelf mark, a date, or a single word, and only a reader patient enough to gather them all could rebuild the royal line. [424] What bound the scattered clues together, and what guarded them from thieves, was a rune of drift cut into the Chronicle's first page and echoed faintly in every volume it had touched. [456] The rune never rested, for at each pause it slipped from margin to margin like a minnow through dark water, marking only the next true clue and abandoning the last. [486] A false seeker who copied one message would therefore hold nothing but stale directions by morning, while a true keeper could follow the drift by memory and by heart. [515] Senna set Aldric to searching the east aisles while she searched the west, and through the long night she left him fair clues but watched which ones his hands avoided. [545] He skipped every book bound in undyed leather, though the rune glimmered plainly there, and he lingered instead over splendid volumes lettered in fresh gilt. [570] Near midnight she took from her sleeve the iron gall ink pellet she had carried for sixty years, a hard black bead no larger than a plum stone. [598] Ground and wetted, it made the only ink the rune would answer, the same bitter ink with which the guardians had written both the Chronicle and every royal birth record. [628] She wetted the pellet, touched its black tear to the corner of Aldric's offered record, and held the page beside the Chronicle under her lamp. [653] On the true page the old ink stood iron brown and sunken, while on his the fresh lines beaded, shone blue, and lifted away like paint on glass. [681] Then the rune answered her test, drifting from the Chronicle across both pages and circling only the true names, among which no Aldric had ever stood. [707] Aldric stared at the moving mark, then confessed he was a steward's son paid in promises, and his fine sorrow folded without a sound. [731] Senna let him leave with water and bread before the pause ended, because even a pretender deserved to outrun what the temple became when time returned. [757] Behind her the shelves bowed, the towers leaned like tired dancers, and the library sank through sand in a graceful collapse as the constellations resumed their slow turning. [785]</story>
