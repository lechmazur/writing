Mira was the last sea-foam recollectionist on the drowned coast, and she kept her shop in an iridescent cave inside a giant crystal geode. [24]
Every dawn she gathered foam from the tide pools, because each bubble held a memory someone had lost while sleeping. [44]
She did not read the memories directly, for she transliterates emotions into letters first, turning grief to ink and joy to bright vowels. [67]
Written out, a sorrow could be shelved, a delight could be mailed home, and a fear could be folded small enough to burn. [90]
It was cheerful work, mostly, though the cave always carried a cheerful gloom, as if it were smiling while it mourned. [111]
The crystal walls caught the sea light and threw it back in pinks and greens, even on days when funerals filled the harbor. [134]
That morning the foam arrived thin and trembling, and most of its bubbles popped before Mira could lift them. [153]
When she transliterated what remained, the letters made a map she knew well, the cliff path to the lighthouse where her brother kept the lamp. [178]
But the map had missing pieces, whole bends and stairways gone, leaving cheerful little gaps that hummed like lost teeth. [198]
Mira laughed, because laughing was her habit against dread, and she said the path itself must be forgetting how to exist. [219]
For months other maps had thinned the same way, and she had come to want one thing above payment or praise. [240]
She worked to discover why some paths disappear while others remain, even when nobody walks them for years. [258]
Her brother Tomas had sent word by gull that the lighthouse stair vanished under his feet at dusk, though he landed laughing in soft sand. [283]
Nobody was ever hurt when a path went, which made the loss stranger and, in the cave light, almost merry. [303]
People simply found their intentions rerouted, their errands forgotten, and their letters arriving years late with apologetic stamps. [321]
Mira packed her satchel before memory fades at noon, when all foam maps melt back into salt and guesswork. [340]
She took a lens of crater glass, dark and bubbled stone born when a fallen star kissed the bay a century ago. [362]
Through crater glass, the emotions she had written stopped behaving like words and showed their true routes as threads of colored light. [384]
Where a path still lived, the thread ran gold and steady. [395]
Where a path had died, the thread snapped, curled, and sang a note too sweet for such a sad small ending. [416]
Mira followed the gold thread out of the geode and up the shore, whistling to keep the gloom cheerful and the cheer honest. [439]
At the cliff base the thread broke exactly where the first missing piece began, and no footprint crossed the bright bare stone. [461]
Then a migrating bird crossed the sun, huge and white, flying north alone because its flock had forgotten the way too. [482]
As it passed, it shook its wings and scattered shining seeds across the cliff, each seed landing with a soft green tick. [504]
Mira understood at once that the bird fed on foam at sea and carried undigested memories in its crop like tiny lanterns. [526]
She would travel via the scattered seeds dropped by a migrating bird, letting each sprout tell her where a step had been loved. [549]
For where the seeds touched stone they sprouted instantly, opening silver leaves that pointed, trembling, toward the next true foothold. [569]
Where no seed fell, Mira had to conjecture, and conjecture felt like stepping on ice while singing to persuade it not to crack. [592]
She conjectured a bend where Tomas always paused to wave, and the stone warmed under her shoe as if remembering his weight. [614]
She conjectured a stair where lovers carved initials, and two pale steps bloomed out of the cliff with a delighted creak. [635]
Then she understood the pattern the seeds and her guessing had drawn together, bright as a joke told at a graveside. [656]
Paths did not vanish from age or storm, but from unwitnessed love, from moments nobody bothered to transliterate into any lasting form. [678]
A step that held only routine faded, while a step that held grief or joy endured and fed new seeds to passing birds. [701]
The missing pieces were never truly missing, only unwritten, waiting for someone to feel them loudly enough to make letters stand up. [723]
So Mira climbed the last conjectured stair singing Tomas a welcome, and the lighthouse door, delighted to be remembered, opened before she knocked. [746]
Tomas poured tea, they mourned the lost bends together, and then they laughed and wrote them back, seed by seed, before night fell. [769]
