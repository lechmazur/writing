<story>Mara Venn had been the bewildered curator of the Hollow Archive for eleven years, and she still checked the visitor log each morning hoping it would explain her job. [29] The Archive stood, if standing was the word, inside an astral orchard blossoming on a lonely asteroid at the edge of charted light. [52] Its trees grew through vacuum without air, opened white flowers toward dead stars, and dropped fruit that ticked softly like cooling clocks. [74] Tourists never came, which made the log easy to keep and hard to trust. [88] On the morning the box arrived, the log already contained an entry in her handwriting thanking herself for a donation she had not yet made. [113] The box held no note, only tissue, a smell of green dust, and a pistachio shell cameo blank polished to the soft shine of a thumbnail moon. [140] A cameo blank was meant to wait for a face, but this one felt less empty than patient, as if it already knew whose portrait it refused to carry. [169] Mara turned it over and found her own initials carved so shallowly on the back that they appeared only when her breath fogged the shell. [194] Because bewilderment had become a professional hazard, she did what she always did first and went to inquire of the Registrar Tree at the center of the orchard. [222] The tree answered by blossoming out of season, each new flower showing a brief scene from the life of whoever would inherit the Archive after her. [248] In one blossom a gray haired Mara carved the blank, in another a child Mara received it, and in a third a woman Mara had never met buried it under roots that glowed. [281] The scenes did not line up, and the tree, when pressed, dropped a single fruit that split open to reveal a library card stamped with dates before her birth and after her death. [314] This, she understood, was nonlinear inheritance, not a tidy passing down from old to young but a handing back and forth until giver and receiver exchanged places. [341] The card named the next curator as Venn, the previous curator as Venn, and the donor of the blank as Venn, with the ink still wet in every century. [370] It was simply complicated, the way a knot is simple until you learn that pulling one end tightens your own throat. [391] Mara was not supposed to pursue forbidden knowledge, and the Archive had been built precisely to tempt someone exactly like her to pursue forbidden knowledge anyway. [417] The forbidden part was not the future itself but the Index of Unlived Lives, a catalogue of faces people might wear if their choices changed. [442] If she could read the Index entry for the blank, she would know whose face belonged on it and whether carving it would free that person or trap her. [471] The Index was sealed behind the root door, and the root door opened only for a curator who could state, without lying, why she wanted in. [497] Mara tried duty, then curiosity, then professional care, and each answer made the roots knit tighter with an almost polite finality. [518] Defeated, she retreated to the staff bathroom, the one room where the orchard could not listen because its pipes sang too loudly with recycled water. [543] There, among cracked tiles and a mirror that fogged from the inside, Mara had spent years having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms because embarrassment loosened what reverence locked. [570] She sat on the closed toilet lid, held the blank under the humming light, and admitted aloud that she wanted the truth even if it ruined the consolation of being only herself. [602] The pipes stopped singing, and in the sudden quiet she understood that inheritance was not property moving through time but time using objects to introduce people to themselves. [630] The blank did not need a face added; it needed a face withheld, so that whoever received it next could remain unfinished long enough to choose. [656] When she returned, the root door stood open, not because the answer was noble but because it was finally accurate. [676] Beyond it lay no vast hall, only a narrow reading room suspended between the first and last page of the Archive ledger, where every entry waited to be written by its reader. [708] Mara did not read the face intended for her blank, though the page turned toward her with eager brightness. [727] Instead she asked the room a better question, inquiring who would be harmed if she left the portrait uncarved and let the future arrive unnamed. [752] So she catalogued the blank as intentionally unfinished, thanked herself in the log, and left the box for a younger Venn, carrying home only brightened disquiet. [778]</story>
