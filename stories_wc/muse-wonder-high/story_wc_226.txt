<story>Mara had lived her whole life in the shadow of towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes. [19] Every dawn the wind struck the hollow ribs and returned her own breathing a hundred times changed. [36] At the canyon heart stood the pale spire in mist, taller than any tower and smooth as a tooth. [55] The priests would climb it before the ritual began and speak the One Truth over the gathered clans. [73] Until then a forged hush held the canyon, a quiet hammered thin by fear and waiting. [89] Mara was the defiant artist who had refused to paint that Truth upon the spire. [104] She painted instead whatever people whispered when they thought the echoes were asleep. [117] For this the priests had chained her brushes and promised her forgiveness at a price. [132] If she climbed at noon and painted the Truth alone, they would forgive her defiance and free her brother. [151] Tobin had stolen sacred pigment to save her work, and now he waited bound inside the spire. [168] Forgiveness, Mara learned, always asked someone to become smaller. [177] She wanted Tobin free, but she could not make one sentence carry every living grief. [192] Her secret purpose was to refract a single truth into many before the priests could seal it shut. [210] Light did it through glass, and sound, she reasoned, might do it through people. [224] She was innocently mischievous by nature, more likely to hide a bell in a priest's shoe than to raise a knife. [245] That morning she used that mischief as a key. [254] While guards changed posts, she slipped into the market and began the first of her impromptu poetry sessions. [272] She only clapped twice and asked a baker what the morning had cost him. [286] He laughed, surprised, and answered in a rough verse about flour, debt, and his mother's hands. [302] The canyon caught his last word and threw it from bone to bone until it sounded like prayer. [320] A child repeated the line wrong on purpose and made the baker grin instead of weep. [336] Mara wrote both versions on a shard with charcoal stolen from a cooking fire. [350] At the well she asked two rival sisters to finish one poem without agreeing on its ending. [367] One ended with pardon, the other with departure, and both women wept because both were true. [383] At the tannery she coaxed an old soldier to rhyme his shame with the name of the man he had abandoned. [404] He spoke haltingly, then straighter, as if each echo handed his voice back less cruelly. [419] By midmorning dozens followed her, not as rebels, but as neighbors curious about their own mouths. [435] The priests sent runners, yet no law forbade people from trading verses while waiting for bread. [451] Near the spire she found Tobin's small prism, dropped when the guards had taken him. [466] She held it in the misty light, and one pale beam split into trembling colors across the bone. [484] Look, she whispered, even light refuses to travel as only one thing. [496] A little girl placed the prism on the lowest step and begged another poem, this time about fear. [514] Seven people answered, and seven fears stood side by side without devouring one another. [528] Then the high priest descended, white robes gray in mist, and offered Mara her last bargain. [544] Climb now, paint the One Truth, reject these scattered verses, and Tobin goes free forgiven. [559] Refuse, and he stays in chains to hear his sentence spoken from the height. [573] Mara looked at the shards in her hands, each dark with a different honest ending. [588] If she bought Tobin's freedom by erasing them, his chains would only move into her mouth. [604] She set one shard on the step and, smiling like a child caught stealing plums, invited the priest to begin. [624] Begin what, he asked, though the whole crowd had gone still behind her. [637] Your poem, she said, but only yours, and we will keep it beside ours. [651] The priest stared at the prism, where his white sleeve broke into red, gold, and blue. [667] For a long moment no one spoke, and the forged hush deepened until it became listening. [683] Then the baker, shy as a boy, repeated his verse from the lowest shadow of the spire. [700] The sisters answered, the soldier answered, and children stole lines from adults and improved them by accident. [717] Echoes carried every version upward, tangling around the pale spire until no single voice could claim the air. [735] The high priest lowered his gaze, not in surrender, but because Tobin was laughing behind the barred door. [753] Mara heard him and understood that freedom might begin before any pardon was granted. [767] She placed the prism in the priest's palm and waited, with all the canyon, before the ritual.
