It always began in the murmur of half-remembered dreams, when the abandoned spaceport repair bay smelled of hot copper and rain that had never fallen there. [26] June Park was a sensory map maker, hired by salvage crews to chart places too unstable for cameras or drones. [46] She did not draw coastlines or corridors. [53] She recorded how a deck hummed under bare feet, how air tasted on her tongue, and how darkness pressed in rooms where something had gone wrong. [79] The bay kept returning at night with details that belonged to no waking survey. [93]

In one dream the floor was warm as skin. [102] In the next it rang like a struck bell and left her teeth aching until morning. [118] The changes were cyclically spontaneous, swelling open every ninth night without warning, then sealing before she could carry proof out. [138] Seventeen years earlier, her father Tomas had vanished from that bay during the last launch, and her mother had called it desertion ever since. [162] June had come to discover the weight of a withheld truth, and she meant that almost literally. [179]

In her left palm she carried a green glass shard, all that salvage had returned from his co-pilot seat after the accident. [201] In the bay on the ninth night, it grew heavy enough to ache. [214] She believed the shard reacted not to metal or heat, but to nearness, to a version of events pressed close enough to bruise this one. [239] Her mother had felt that heaviness once, then locked the shard away and refused every question with a shaking head. [259] Before she died that spring, she left June a key, a tide chart with no sea, and three words written hard enough to tear the paper. [285] Do not pull. [288]

Now June stood under dead gantries while wind through broken vents made the chains sway in a rhythm she knew from sleep. [310] The bay in this world was stripped and silent, its launch rail cut, its fuel lines capped, its windows blind with dust. [332] But when she closed her eyes, other bays laid themselves over it like wet pages sticking together. [349] In one, the air smelled of oranges and the rail sang high as workers in yellow suits ran toward a ship that had landed safely. [374] In another, smoke settled as grit on her tongue and every alarm tasted of blood. [389]

This was her work and her inheritance: weaving between worlds not with engines, but with attention. [405] She walked slowly, barefoot despite the cold, naming each sense aloud so the map would hold when the dream thinned. [425] The third rib of the floor hummed. [432] The shard dragged her hand down toward a maintenance pit she had never seen open in waking life. [450] Below the grate, something knocked twice, paused, and knocked twice again. [461] That rhythm was the family code Tomas had taught her for lost radios and dark stairwells. [477] Her throat closed. [480]

She hooked her fingers through the grate and pulled, obeying instinct before the written warning could rise in her mind. [500] Cold light spilled from seams where other versions of the pit showed through like cracks in ice. [517] Through them she saw Tomas at fifty and at thirty and at an age she did not know, each holding a tether that ran into darkness. [543] The nearest Tomas looked up, older than her memory and thinner than grief, and his mouth formed her name without sound. [564] Then the map in her body snapped into sense. [573]

He was holding the bay steady while a damaged colony ship threaded through parallel universes, using his heartbeat as its needle. [594] Every cycle, its broken guidance woke, mistook that heartbeat for a beacon, and tried to land in a bay no longer whole. [616] Her mother had withheld the rest because she had been the one on launch control that night. [633] She had frozen, missed the window to redirect the ship into an empty universe, and left Tomas gripping a tether that should have been cut. [658] Do not pull meant do not drag him home and tear every held seam with him. [674]

June set the shard on the humming third rib, where its weight finally steadied. [688] Then she stamped the family rhythm on the floor and sang the safe bay into focus: oranges, yellow suits, a rail singing high. [711] The guidance heard a new beacon, one made of place instead of pulse, and turned. [726] Through parallel universes the ship slid sideways toward the landing her senses had mapped. [740] Tomas let go. [743] He fell six inches into her world, laughing and weeping, while the shard grew light in her palm. [761] The withheld truth had weighed exactly one man's life, and now she could help him carry it. [778]
