<story>Mara had tended the glass orchids for forty years in the opal library above clouds. [15] She was the last glass-orchid caretaker, and she knew each bloom by the sound its petals made at dawn. [34] The library held no books of paper, but shelves of opal tablets that stored light from vanished cities below. [53] Below the clouds lay the deserted marvels, towers and gardens and singing bridges abandoned when their makers forgot why they had built them. [76] Mara worked in a gently systematic way, polishing leaves on Mondays, listening for roots on Tuesdays, and naming new buds on Wednesdays. [98] Her purpose, written on the first tablet, was to preserve the sound of forgotten names. [113] Each orchid held one name inside its transparent heart, and when the wind moved through the greenhouse, the flowers chimed it softly. [135] If a name was never heard again, its orchid would turn to dust and the person it remembered would be truly gone. [157] On a still morning late in her life, Mara found the oldest orchid dark for the first time. [175] Its name was Elian, her brother, who had built the lift that once carried children up from the world below. [195] He had died young, and she had planted his orchid the day the lift stopped and the cities emptied. [214] She felt the familiar foxglove regret rise in her throat, sweet and poisonous at once, because she had not spoken his name aloud that week. [239] Her schedule had slipped after a storm shook the library and scattered her careful rows of pots. [256] She touched Elian's glass petals and heard only a dull click where a clear note should have rung. [274] Above the pot hung the wind chime he had made for her, five bamboo tubes tied with blue thread. [293] As she reached for it, the wind chime bamboo tube cracked along its length with a sound like a held breath breaking. [315] Mara understood at once, because the chime had always echoed the orchids and kept their names in tune. [333] Without its note, Elian's flower could not remember how his name was supposed to sound. [348] She could repair the tube with resin, but resin would change the note and perhaps lose him forever. [366] She needed the true note, and the library kept true notes only where feelings had been stored before they vanished. [386] So she locked the greenhouse, took her lamp, and set out across the museum of extinct feelings that filled the lower floors. [408] The museum was not a place of hours but of distances, so the walk would cross years as well as rooms. [429] In the Hall of First Wonder, glass cases held the awe people had felt at oceans they would never see again. [450] In the Room of Unembarrassed Joy, empty chairs still faced a stage where no one performed. [466] Mara passed them gently and systematically, pausing at each case to read its label and speak the name written there. [486] Speaking kept the cases from dimming, and it reminded her that preservation was an act, not a wish. [504] At the far end she found the Gallery of Last Songs, where the air smelled of rain on hot stone. [524] There, behind a curtain of woven light, a single opal tablet held the tune Elian used to hum while he worked. [545] Mara did not play it at once, because tablets gave their songs only to those who remembered why they sought them. [566] She set down her lamp and tried to light its wick, but her hands shook and the flame would not catch. [587] Then, faint through the floor, she heard the cracked tube in the greenhouse answer a wind she could not feel. [607] By the echo of a long-forgotten song, she found her rhythm again and lit the lamp on the third try. [627] In its glow the tablet woke and played Elian's humming, note by note, exactly as he had left it. [646] Mara repeated the notes aloud, not perfectly, but steadily, until the tablet brightened and released them into the air. [665] The song rose through the floors, past extinct wonder and joy, and reached the greenhouse before she did. [683] When she returned, carrying the lamp like a small sun, Elian's orchid was trembling with stored sound. [700] She placed the cracked tube beneath it, not to fix it, but so its broken edge could learn the note by listening. [722] The orchid opened, and light spilled from its heart as it spoke his name clearly for the first time that day. [743] Mara answered with his full name, her voice joining the flower and the chime until all three held the same note. [764] From then on she spoke every name each morning, gently systematic in grief and love, so none would darken again. [784]</story>
