Mara Voss was an orchestra pit musician who believed silence had a shape, if you stayed long enough to learn it. [21]
For months she had played on automatic, counting rests and watching dust, but this autumn she felt lately inspired by something she could not name. [46]
The feeling had come after the river broke its banks and filled the lower theatre with black water. [64]
Now she returned alone to the flooded opera house lit by flickering lanterns, because the caretaker had called about her missing violin case. [87]
Water covered the marble lobby to her ankles and turned every lantern into two, one steady and one drowning below. [107]
Her case floated in the pit beside the overturned music stands, its lid open and its velvet dark with silt. [127]
Tucked under the strings, where she had left no paper, lay an antique opera program for a performance that never opened. [148]
Its cover showed La Traviata, dated October 1913, the night the first flood took the soprano Elene Marr. [166]
Mara knew the house legend, that Elene had vanished between the final rehearsal and the premiere, leaving only wet footprints on stage. [188]
She lifted the program with two fingers, expecting it to fall apart, but the pages held, swollen and rippled. [207]
Inside, water damage creating images across the cast list had turned smeared ink into something deliberate. [223]
Where Violetta should have been printed, a dark stain shaped itself into a woman standing in a doorway that did not exist on stage. [247]
On the next page, the overture notes bloomed into a row of submerged stairs descending behind the orchestra seats. [266]
Mara understood then that she was not looking at ruin, but at directions left in the only material the dead could still move. [289]
She came back the next three nights, not for the case, but to unravel the code left by a ghost. [309]
Each night, amid the hush of unstruck chords, she set a lantern on the conductor podium and waited for the water to speak first. [333]
The house answered without sound, in what became a wordless conversation between her bow, held still, and the images changing under the surface. [356]
If she moved the lantern left, the stain on page three lengthened into a corridor behind the boxes. [374]
If she waited, breathing shallow, another image rose as the damp spread, a hand pointing toward the wall beneath the royal box. [396]
The theatre had always been a building of in between spaces, of curtains, wings, trap rooms, and passages meant for people the audience never saw. [421]
Mara had lived in one herself, below the stage and above the music, close enough to touch the singers' feet but invisible. [443]
That was why Elene had chosen her, she thought, because a ghost also lived between performance and oblivion, heard but never held. [465]
On the fourth night the program showed a new image, a sealed door under the stage, flooded to its iron handle. [486]
Beneath the image, in ink that bled as she watched, were seven small notes with no staff lines to hold them. [507]
She copied them onto her wrist with a pencil, feeling their order tug at her fingers like a remembered exercise. [527]
Without deciding to, she lifted her violin and played them, low and slow, into the wet dark. [544]
The notes did not echo as notes should, but seemed to drop straight down and knock, softly, under the floorboards. [564]
Something knocked back, three times, from behind the wall the stained hand had named. [577]
Mara should have run, but inspiration is a stubborn form of listening, and lately hers had taught her to answer. [597]
She played the seven notes again, and this time she chose to disrupt the pattern, leaving the fifth space empty. [617]
The silence in that empty beat stretched until the water in the pit began to pull backward, away from her ankles. [638]
It drained through a seam she had never seen, revealing wet stone steps that matched the second page exactly. [657]
She understood the code now: the images were not a map of the building, but of the pauses Elene had hidden inside her final song. [682]
Each missing note opened a space the flood could enter, and each played note told it where to go. [701]
Down the steps, lantern high, she found the sealed door standing open and a dry room beyond it, lit by nothing. [722]
Inside lay Elene's dressing table, a mirror still silver, and a score weighted by a single dry rose. [740]
Beneath the table, under a loose board, lay Elene's bones curled around a silent metronome. [755]
Mara covered her with her coat and played the missing fifth note aloud. [768]
Above them, the flooded pit sighed, settled, and gave back its silence, no longer asking. [783]
