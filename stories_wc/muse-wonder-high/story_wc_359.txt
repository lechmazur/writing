<story> The city of Caldra held its breath at the moment a sculptor's chisel first strikes marble. [16] Above Sanna, the blow struck the block that would become the Register of Essential Things. [31] Below, in the ice cavern under the atelier, Sanna felt the blow arrive as a thread of blue pain. [50] She was synesthetically blessed, so every sound bloomed in color and every color rang faintly in her teeth. [68] It was why the temple had made her its prism-song bell ringer when she was nine. [84] Her bells were glass, hung in a circle, and one true note could split through them into a rainbow chord. [104] Today the Council of Measures had given her a single task before the marble dust settled. [120] She had come to certify wonder as essential, or watch the bells be melted for cannon. [136] The Council trusted scales, sealed lungs, and ribbons tied at throats to record a breath. [151] If wonder moved breath like air and steadied hearts like food, they would carve it beside bread, water, fire, and shelter. [172] If not, the sculptor would finish only four stern figures, and the tower would go silent before spring. [190] At the center of the cavern stood the sculpted hush. [200] The first ringers had carved it from the echo after the first bell, the way sculptors carve a hand by removing stone. [222] They had given that silence a rounded shape, a listening curve, and chains of black iron to keep it from rushing upward all at once. [247] For two hundred years it had taught the city how to pause between notes, prayers, and heartbeats. [264] Now its chains were frozen into blue walls, and only a ringer could unfetter it without breaking it. [282] Sanna laid her palm against the hush, and the strike above stopped. [294] Inside its stillness, a moment could widen the way a held breath widens a chest. [309] The chisel hung unfinished in the air, the marble waited, and Sanna had the length of one suspended blow to work. [330] From her coat she drew the amber music sheet, thin as ice and warm from her heart. [347] Ancient notes lay trapped inside it like dark seeds in honeyed glass. [359] In plain light they meant nothing, because the first composer had written music for eyes that could hear. [377] Sanna lifted the sheet toward the cavern roof, where clear ice opened to the winter sky. [393] Beyond it, the aurora moved in slow rivers, green and rose and violet, indifferent to councils and cannon. [411] She did not read the sheet with light alone but through the colors of the aurora passing across the ice. [431] As green crossed the amber, the trapped notes lit in sequence and sang a low green tone behind her eyes. [451] As rose followed, a higher note warmed her tongue like summer fruit, and violet came last with a cold bright sting. [472] She struck the smallest bell, then the next, letting color decide pitch and her hands remember order. [489] The cavern answered in oscillating repose, one bright chord rolling outward, then perfect calm, then another chord, then calm again. [509] That rhythm was the proof she had rehearsed but never dared to release at full strength. [525] Sound alone the Council could dismiss as pleasure, but sound married to hush could enter stone. [541] She rang the sequence again, slower, and with each pause she loosened one chain from its ring of ice. [560] In silence, the iron heard its own weight and slipped. [570] In sound, the ice remembered the sun and let go. [580] One by one, the chains fell without a clang, because the hush caught every fall and folded it away. [599] When the last ring opened, Sanna stepped back and unfettered the sculpted hush with the final violet note. [617] It rose not like smoke but like attention, filling the cavern, the stairs, the tower, and the held marble moment above. [638] The chisel strike at last found its sound, and instead of a crack it became a chord in the marble. [658] In the atelier, every throat ribbon stilled, then swelled together, as councillors, guards, children, and the sculptor breathed in one shared pause. [680] Their sealed lung gauges opened, closed, and opened again in the same oscillating repose, though none of them heard a bell. [701] The oldest councillor, deaf since childhood, wept because the marble under his palm had turned violet, green, and rose. [720] He lifted his slate and wrote the measure the law required: essential, because without it breath loses its music and bread its savor. [743] The sculptor turned his unfinished block and began the fifth figure, Wonder, a girl with bells and eyes raised to colored light. [765] Below, Sanna lowered the amber sheet, folded it carefully into her coat, and listened to spring begin inside the ice. [785] </story>
