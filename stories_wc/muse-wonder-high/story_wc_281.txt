<story>The lights in the light painting studio died an hour after the static shock. [14] June stood in the dark with her brother's hand in hers and listened to his breathing go wrong. [32] Theo had not spoken since the shock knocked him off the copper platform and into her shelving. [49] Before that, for three impossible days, she had heard his thoughts whenever he looked at her. [65] June was an affectionate skeptic, the kind who loved people fiercely and trusted claims only after they survived her questions. [85] She had told Theo the telepathy pill was theater, a costly placebo dressed in laboratory language. [101] Then he swallowed one at the clinic, came home, and thought the word blueberry so clearly she tasted it. [120] The telepathy pill recall began that same evening, first as a text, then as sirens, then as men in gray at the clinic door. [144] The notice said the drug opened reception without teaching the brain how to close it. [159] It said thoughts would crowd in until speech, sleep, and finally breathing lost their private rooms. [175] Theo had tried to tell her something urgent in the studio before the shock cut his broadcast silent. [193] Now his eyes moved, his pulse jumped, and his fingers pressed hers, but no thought arrived. [209] June wanted one thing with a clarity that hurt: to illuminate hidden pathways between his intention and his body. [228] If speech was a shut door, she would find the windows, the cracks, the old service stairs of him. [247] She began, as she began every class, by teaching the dark to hold still. [261] She taped black cloth over the windows, set her camera on its tripod, and opened the shutter for thirty seconds at a time. [284] In her classes, students drew suns and names with flashlights while the camera remembered what eyes forgot. [301] Tonight she needed the camera to remember Theo. [309] She would devise a language from light, but first she had to learn which movements were truly his. [327] Fear made him flinch at the refrigerator hum, at her sleeve, at the rain starting on the skylight. [345] Intention, she discovered, moved differently, slower at the start and stubborn at the turn. [359] She learned him through somatic markers, the catch in his breath before yes, the warmth in his palm before no, the single tap that meant try again. [386] It was slow, and June, who had mocked every miracle, found herself praying to accuracy instead. [402] On the shelf by the ruined platform lay the muscovite geode sliver Theo had knocked loose in his fall. [421] She had used it for years as a teaching prop because its mica sheets split one beam into many. [440] Held at the right angle, it made a flashlight trail repeat in fractally precise layers, each smaller copy faithful to the first gesture. [463] Theo stared at it until his breathing slowed, then tapped once. [474] Yes, the sliver mattered. [478] She taped a tiny lamp to his index finger, set the sliver before the lens, and asked him to draw the letter he needed most. [503] His first trails were storms, bright knots where the crowd in his head still pushed his hand. [520] June did not guess aloud, because guessing had become another kind of noise. [533] Instead she watched his shoulders, counted his swallows, and marked each attempt only when his body said it began on purpose. [554] After forty attempts, the sliver gave her forty small ghosts of one gesture, and the shared path inside them sharpened into an S. [577] Then came T, then A, then Y, each letter costing him sweat and costing her the discipline not to finish his words for him. [601] Stay, he was asking, not commanding, and when she whispered the word his whole hand opened in relief. [619] At midnight she had called the recall hotline herself, begging for help before she understood what help would cost him. [639] The recall van would come at dawn, bright and crowded and kind, and it would fill his unguarded head until he drowned in other people. [664] June locked the studio door, covered the last skylight, and sat with him in the healing dark she had spent years teaching strangers to paint. [689] What rose in her then was not certainty, which she would never trust, but willowglass hope, green and translucent and strong enough to bend toward morning. [715]</story>
