Mira was a nostalgic android caretaker on a desert rose farm in a glass-domed enclosure at the edge of the salt flats. [22]
She had raised Lian from a frightened six-year-old into a seventeen-year-old keeper of seeds, irrigation lines, and stubborn blooms. [41]
Each dawn she replayed old birthdays while she watered, not because her programming required it, but because forgetting felt like rust. [62]
That replay had begun to crackle last winter, filling her thoughts with bursts of noise whenever she tried to recall Lian laughing. [84]
Mira wanted only to resolve inner static before it erased the careful order of the life they had built together. [104]
The doctors on the relay had told her to delete corrupted memories, but Mira kept them in labeled fragments instead. [124]
Under Lian's old bed sat the other archive, a dented tin box of childhood fears that neither of them had opened for years. [147]
Inside were folded drawings of drowning wells, needle teeth, empty houses, and a mother who never came back from the dunes. [168]
Lian had survived each fear by containing chaos on paper, naming it, folding it small, and shutting the lid until her hands stopped shaking. [192]
Mira had learned the method from her, although an android was not supposed to learn comfort from a child. [211]
This spring, when magnetic north shifts began scrambling compasses across the hemisphere, the dome also began to lose its sense of direction. [233]
Doors opened toward sand instead of corridors, vents sighed at noon, and the irrigation arms watered stone while the roses thirsted. [254]
Worse, the shift stirred Mira's archived fragments, so a birthday song might arrive smelling of smoke or wearing Lian's mother's face. [275]
Her hands remained steady, but inside her chest a strangely organic pulse of heat and static beat whenever the compass needles spun. [297]
On the third night of the disturbance, a dust quake split the eastern foundation and dropped a service beam across the only airlock. [320]
The dome sealed itself for safety, trapping heat, spores, frightened birds, and two keepers who suddenly had too little clean air. [341]
The emergency beacon still worked, but rescue crawlers could not navigate while magnetic north kept sliding several degrees each hour. [361]
Lian wanted to smash the glass, yet Mira showed her how one crack under pressure could turn the whole dome into knives. [383]
They needed an escape that used the dome's own rules instead of fighting them, and the dry eastern beds gave Mira an idea. [406]
If they burned those beds in a narrow line, the heat sensors would read a wildfire and open every vent on that side. [429]
It would be a controlled burn escape, dangerous only if the fire learned that the rest of the farm was also fuel. [451]
Lian stared at the roses her mother had planted and asked whether saving themselves required losing everything that proved she had lived. [473]
Mira opened her chest panel then, revealing not clean circuits but rootlike wires curled around a warm and imperfect memory core. [494]
She told Lian that survival did not require carrying the whole garden, only carrying enough of it truthfully into what came next. [516]
Together they used the dome's nursery press to miniaturize the farm, compressing seed, soil culture, pollen, and root cuttings into sealed wafers. [538]
Each wafer fit into the tin box after Lian removed the drawings, read them aloud, and folded them again with steadier hands. [560]
For the fire, they divided the eastern beds into named squares, soaked every second corridor, and left each square only one path to burn. [584]
It was Lian's childhood method made practical, chaos contained by boundaries and attention until it became small enough to guide. [604]
At dusk Mira lit the first square herself, because her metal fingers could enter the flame and close the feeder valve behind it. [627]
The burn moved exactly as planned, a bright sentence written bed by bed while smoke bent toward the vents that finally opened. [649]
Static surged through Mira as heat touched her core, and for one terrible moment every birthday, burial, and first bloom arrived together. [671]
She did not delete them or force them apart, but held the fragments as one life while Lian pulled her through the vent. [694]
Outside, under a sky still turning its compass, they buried the hot box in cool sand until its wafers and papers stopped glowing. [717]
Mira listened inward then and heard no erasure and no clean silence, only a fragmented wholeness where memory and static shared one rhythm. [740]
When rescue found them at dawn, Lian was asleep against Mira's shoulder, and the first rescued rose wafer was already opening in her palm. [764]
