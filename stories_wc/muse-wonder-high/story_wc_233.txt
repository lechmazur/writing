Queen Mirel had ruled the valley for twenty years, and praise now tasted like ash on her tongue. [18] She was a disenchanted queen who smiled at feasts while quietly counting the exits behind every curtain. [35] For forty nights, her sleep had returned her to the same impossible shore beneath a wounded sky. [52] There, bleeding starlight fell in slow red threads and pooled warm around her bare and blistered feet. [69] Each morning, she woke with soot under her nails and a taste of copper bright on her tongue. [87] Her council called it grief, because her husband had died in spring and grief was a tidy explanation. [105] Mirel knew grief well, and this dream had a patience and hunger that grief had never shown her. [123]

On the fortieth night, the dream changed and showed her a path of white stones climbing the ridge. [141] At its end burned the ember grotto on the ridge, breathing orange light through cracks in black stone. [159] She woke before dawn and summoned Sella, her old astrologer, who arrived with charts rolled like weapons. [176] Sella listened without interrupting, then unrolled one chart and placed a silver ring upon a darkened star. [193] The chamber candles bent though no wind moved, after the final warning left her thin pale lips. [210] Sella said that if Mirel followed the dream again, it would begin to follow her while waking. [227] Mirel dismissed her court with a smile as delicate as frost and twice as cold to touch. [244] Their obedience hid a lacy menace, all bows and lace cuffs and knives politely sheathed in silk. [261] She understood them because she had taught them that courtesy could make any threat sound reasonable. [277]

That night, she packed bread, water, and a lamp, determined to unravel the mystery of recurring dreams. [294] She left through the kitchen garden while the palace watch changed, her gray cloak smelling of rain. [311] At the foot of the ridge, she began to climb toward the glow she had seen sleeping. [328] The path punished her knees and tore her hem, but each step made the dream feel less invented. [346] Halfway up, a girl waited beside a cairn, holding a lantern that burned without any visible flame. [363] The girl said her name was Pip and that no one reached the grotto without being expected there. [381] Her voice was softly commanding, small and clear, and Mirel obeyed before she considered refusing her at all. [399] Pip pointed to a narrow stair cut into stone and warned her not to count the steps aloud. [417] Counting, Pip explained, gave the mountain a number it could later use to demand something back from climbers. [435]

They climbed in silence until the valley vanished and the path entered a cobalt grotto above clouds. [452] Inside, blue stone drank the lantern light and returned it slowly, as if the walls were remembering dawn. [470] Yet heat pulsed deeper within, where the ember grotto waited behind a door of woven copper roots. [487] Their only method was a shared exhale spotting the first fireflies of summer at the exact same moment. [505] So they waited beside the door while cold blue dark gathered and summer insects rose below the clouds. [523] When the first green sparks drifted upward, Pip nodded, and queen and girl breathed out as one person. [541] The copper roots loosened with a sound like a harp string sighing under a careful human hand. [558] Beyond the door, embers floated in blue air, neither rising nor falling, watching them with patient heat. [575] At the center stood a pool where bleeding starlight gathered, thick as syrup and bright as fresh wounds. [593]

Mirel saw her husband reflected there, not young or ghostly, but tired and exactly as death had left him. [612] He had been the keeper of this threshold before Sella bound his memory into Mirel's sleeping mind. [629] Sella had hidden him there after poison took him, fearing the council would steal his final true testimony. [647] Each dream had been his attempt to climb back through her grief and warn her about the poisoners. [665] In the pool, three faces surfaced beneath the starlight: her steward, her cousin, and her trusted taster. [682] Mirel understood then that her dreams were not madness but evidence knocking nightly at a locked door. [699] Pip asked softly whether Mirel would free him and lose the dreams, or keep dreaming and lose herself. [717] Mirel knelt, placed both palms in the pool, and thanked him for refusing to let her sleep peacefully. [735] The starlight thinned into ordinary silver, and somewhere below them the first fireflies scattered over summer fields. [752] At dawn, the disenchanted queen descended alone, softly commanding her grief to follow at a respectful distance behind. [770] When her council bowed in lace and silk, Mirel smiled and named every poisoner before they could reach their knives. [790]
