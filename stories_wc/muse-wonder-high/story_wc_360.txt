Marek Vane was the cryptic letterpress operator of the topaz workshop under stars, a room with a glass roof above the observatory where night staff brought letters they could not send. [31]
He worked between vanishing stars, in the hour after the last constellation faded and before the first returned wrong. [50]
No one knew why the sky lost and regained its lights, only that each absence lasted eleven minutes longer than the last. [72]
Marek printed then, because his press ran truest when no star watched it. [85]
On his bench lay a halo fragment, a curved shard of gold glass fallen from the observatory saint during the first vanishing. [107]
Set in lead, it served as lens, paperweight, and the only perfect O in his type case. [124]
Through it, ink looked deeper, margins looked honest, and unfinished sentences showed what their writers had hidden. [141]
Marek sought to understand the mathematics of emotion, believing his press could calculate what prayer could not. [158]
His method was transposed empathy, the exchange that occurred when he set another person's words backward for printing. [176]
To read reversed type, he slowed his mind until the writer's pulse seemed to move his hands. [193]
By the final line, grief written in anger arrived in him as shame, and joy written in fear arrived as tenderness. [214]
Elsewhere, the writer briefly felt his steadiness instead, like a cool palm over a fever. [229]
He recorded ink weight, tear salt, pauses between words, and the hour each borrowed feeling faded. [245]
If sorrow had ratios and longing decayed at a fixed rate, he would find the constants and suffer less by knowing them. [267]
Yet Marek habitually borrows personalities with feelings, wearing each writer for a day like a still warm coat. [285]
After a sea captain, he salted his tea and addressed the press as a tired crew. [301]
After a midwife, he folded rags with ceremonial patience and listened at doors for other people's beginnings. [318]
He spoke of himself only through deliberate ambiguity, because a clear answer would fix him to one grieving life. [337]
Ila Sen found him during the longest vanishing yet, climbing the stairs with a letter sealed by shaking hands. [356]
She charted missing stars for the observatory and had just named a new absence after her dead brother Tomas. [375]
Her farewell, rewritten monthly, always stopped before admitting she had survived the accident and Tomas had not. [392]
She asked for fifty exact copies for the family shrine, as if repetition could replace an ending. [409]
Marek read it twice, then through the halo fragment, and saw her neatest lines carried the wildest omissions. [427]
Every I was crossed like a fence, and every mention of Tomas lacked a verb, leaving him suspended rather than gone. [448]
Printing her version cleanly would preserve the numbness that kept her returning. [460]
So he chose to disorient her, not with lies, but with faithful type set one breath out of order. [479]
He exchanged two pronouns, left her unfinished sentence open, and placed the halo fragment where its missing O belonged. [498]
The proof seemed to say Tomas had survived her, and she was the one who had not come home. [517]
Ila read it standing, then sat hard among type drawers as the room tilted into lucid turbulence. [534]
Her charting mind saw every displacement at once, yet clarity did not soften the storm it revealed. [551]
She accused him of cruelty until transposed empathy took hold through her fingertips on the wet ink. [568]
Set as Tomas might have read them, her words let her feel his imagined relief that she lived before her guilt. [589]

He wept with her exact restraint, counting tears because counting was the only prayer he trusted. [625]
Secondhand, his steadiness reached Ila, and she finally wrote the missing sentence in pencil across the proof. [642]
It said she had lived, that living was not betrayal, and that Tomas would have mocked fifty identical farewells. [661]
Marek printed one copy, using the halo fragment as the O in home, so the word held a ring of flawed gold light. [684]
When the first stars returned, he wrote his final constant in the ledger: emotion divided between two carriers does not vanish, it becomes bearable. [708]
