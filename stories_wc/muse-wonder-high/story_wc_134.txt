<story>Mira had lived for twenty years in the musical crystal cave, listening to water strike quartz and calling the notes by name. [22] She was the village clairvoyant, keeper of tides and lost keys, and she told fortunes to magnify everyday magic for people who thought their lives were plain. [49] A farmer would leave believing his well held moonlight, and a widow would hear music in her kettle again. [68] That was her gift and her work, until the morning the cave went silent. [82] Silence in that place was not empty, but thick, like bright darkness pressing soft hands over every shining wall. [101] Mira lit her lamp and found the pool black, the crystals dull, and her own reflection missing from the water. [121] She, the clairvoyant who didn't see it coming, laughed once in disbelief and then felt true fear move through her ribs. [142] For three days she tried coaxing the cave with songs, with salt, with stories of the sea that had carved it long ago. [165] Nothing answered, and on the fourth day, after the pilgrim arrives in the village above, everything changed. [182] He came down the pilgrim steps at noon, a thin young man named Tomas, steadily unpredictable in step and speech. [202] Sometimes he hurried as if chased, sometimes he stopped to thank a stone, and sometimes he walked backward to see where he had been. [226] He carried no offering but a cracked cup, and he asked Mira not for his future, but for a place to sit out of the rain. [252] Rain had not fallen in weeks, yet his shoulders were wet, and where he stood the crystals gave a faint, uncertain hum. [274] She asked why he had come to a cave that had lost its music, and he smiled as if the question itself were a door. [299] He said the silence had called him from the road, because silence, like sorrow, wanted company rather than cure. [318] Mira, who had foreseen droughts and births and the exact hour of the mill fire, could foresee nothing about him at all. [340] When she looked ahead for Tomas, she saw only overlapping lamplight, as if his days refused to stand in single file. [361] Still, she let him sit by the black pool, and she gave him bread, because hospitality was a spell that needed no sight. [384] That night the unseen forces that had stilled the cave returned, not as wind or voice, but as pressure behind her eyes. [406] She woke to find Tomas standing in the pool to his ankles, holding her lamp so its light fell across his face in bands. [430] He was not praying or searching, only listening with his whole body, the way animals listen before earth moves. [449] Then the smallest crystal above the pool answered his stillness with one clear note, and the note made the water shiver. [470] Mira understood suddenly that the cave had not been punished or emptied, but had been waiting for a listener who did not demand marvels. [494] For years she had sung to force brightness from stone, while Tomas simply let the darkness be bright on its own terms. [516] Under his feet something pale loosened from the silt, round as a moon and warm from the living rock. [535] It was a cave pearl, grown grain by grain where dripping water had turned around a single seed of grit for a hundred years. [559] Mira had read of such pearls but had never seen one, because they formed only where no hand disturbed the slow turning. [581] When Tomas lifted it, their two shadows met on the pool, and through overlapping shadows the cave seemed to breathe again at last. [604] A low chorus rose from wall to wall, each crystal taking the note before it and giving it back changed, steadier and stranger. [627] Mira felt the unseen presence not enter from outside, but inhabit the familiar places again, the pool, the pearl, her own listening heart. [650] She saw then that her foresight had failed because the future was not hiding, but arriving sideways through kindness, patience, and accident. [672] Tomas placed the cave pearl in her palm and closed her fingers over it, saying a keeper should hold what she could not command. [696] From that day the cave sang again, but never on schedule, and villagers learned to bring their ordinary days down like offerings. [718] They left with wet hems, steadier hearts, and the sense that unseen forces had brushed their sleeves without asking to be believed. [740] And Mira, who no longer tried to see everything, kept the pearl by her lamp to remind her that wonder grows best when it is simply lived in. [768]</story>
