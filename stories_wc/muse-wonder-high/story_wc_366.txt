<story>After the lamp flickers out, Elias Venn stands in the haunted lantern room and listens to the dark breathe around him. [21] Below, the sea strikes Gull Rock as if testing every stone. [32] He is a reticent lighthouse supply skipper, a man who delivers oil, flour, mail, and medicine and seldom delivers an opinion. [53] Yet he is warmly observant, and tonight he notices everything that matters before anyone says it aloud. [70] He has seen the tremor in Mara Kade's hands when she signs for the crates. [85] He has seen her daughter Wren hide wet sleeves behind her back and pretend she is not afraid. [103] Most telling, he has seen the stones Wren stacked this morning on the breakwater, leaning together like shoulders. [121] Her family has a way of stacking stones as messages when fog steals their bell and wind tears the flag. [141] Two upright stones with one flat stone across them means all is well and no boat should risk the landing. [161] Three leaning stones mean a child is frightened and an adult is too proud to ask for company. [179] Elias reads the message at dawn and quietly finds reasons to mend a pump until the storm traps him there. [199] Now the great lens stands cold, and somewhere beyond the rain a fishing boat feels for the channel. [217] Mara climbs the iron stairs with a storm lantern, her face lit from below and her grief lit from within. [237] Her husband Jonah kept this light for eleven years before a burst heart left him dead beside the lens. [256] Keepers swear his step still circles the gallery on black nights, patient and unwilling to leave his post. [274] Elias does not believe in ghosts, but he believes rooms remember the hands that loved them. [290] Mara sets the lantern down and whispers that the generator has drowned and the radio has died with it. [309] Without a light or signal, the Anne Marie will cross the shoal before her crew sees the rock. [327] From the supply crate at her feet, Mara pulls a folded oilcloth packet tied with blue twine. [344] Inside lies the latest page of the anonymous letter chain that Elias carries from light to light along the coast. [364] No names ever sign it, so lonely keepers can confess fear, fury, or tenderness without shame following them home. [383] Each keeper adds a paragraph by candlelight, folds the page small, and trusts Elias never to reveal a hand he recognizes. [404] One paragraph gives exact instructions for lighting the old oil lamp that stands behind the electric lens. [421] Mara stares at the familiar slant of the letters and asks if Jonah wrote that page before he died. [440] Elias wants to protect the chain and every keeper who has trusted it, so he only says the words belong to everyone now. [463] What matters, he says, is that a keeper learned the old lamp and left the knowledge where another keeper could find it. [485] Together they clean the soot from the old lamp, trim its wick, and fill it with oil from his emergency can. [506] When Elias strikes the match, a step sounds on the gallery outside, slow and unmistakably wet. [522] Mara freezes, but Wren appears on the stairs below, clutching an empty jam jar and something bright. [539] In her palm lies a piece of sea glass, cloudy green and smooth as a held breath. [556] Jonah found it the morning Wren was born and told her calm water had made it gentle. [573] For two winters Elias has searched beaches after each safe delivery, trying to bottle the feeling of safety he feels when a light brings him home. [599] He sets the glass before the lamp, and its green heart catches the newborn flame. [614] Through lens and glass the light throws a warm green road across the rain toward the Anne Marie. [632] Far out, the fishing boat turns, slow and sure, and its horn answers twice. [646] At the same instant, another step crosses the gallery and stops where Jonah used to stand and watch over his family. [667] Mara does not run; she lays her palm against the warm lens housing and thanks the room for keeping him close. [688] Before dawn, Elias rebuilds Wren's leaning stones into two uprights with one flat stone across, the sign that all is well. [709] He slips the sea glass into the jam jar, where the lamplight gathers and stays like something she can carry. [729] Wren holds the jar to her chest, and for the first time that night her shoulders drop. [746] In the glowing room, Elias understands safety is a light passed hand to hand, letter to letter, stone to stone, and grief can ache without darkening it. [773]</story>
