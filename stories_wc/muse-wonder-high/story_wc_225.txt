Marek Vane arrived in the time-frozen village of Hushmere at noon, and no one noticed him because time had stopped noticing anything at all. [24]
A carter stood with one boot raised, a girl held a bucket with water hanging from its lip, and the mill wheel leaned against an unfalling river. [51]
Over the roofs, during the lull of distant thunder, the hush had paused the storm in mid-rumble and learned how to stand still. [74]
Marek was the only moving thing, and even he moved as if apologizing to the air. [90]
He was an exiled chess tutor, with a coat mended in squares and a mind that still set up boards in empty rooms. [113]
Ten years earlier in the capital, he had taught Prince Aldan to love the beautiful sacrifice more than the patient move. [134]
Aldan, grown vain and crowned, later sacrificed a border regiment to save his shining cavalry, exactly as Marek taught him to prize glory over ground. [159]
Seven hundred men had not come home, and the court had found it cleaner to exile the tutor than to bury its own lesson with the king. [186]
Marek had agreed with the court every night since. [195]
His hands would not obey his guilt, for he fidgeted productively, tapping knight moves on his cuff until loose threads, crumbs, and dropped pins arranged themselves into answers. [223]
It was that restless tapping, small clicks against the woven hush, that had kept a narrow path of time open around his boots. [246]
The woven hush was Hushmere's pride and wound, a great tapestry in the hall where every grieving household knotted one unsaid thing into wool so the village could bear living. [276]
Widows knotted names they could not speak at table, fathers knotted apologies, and children knotted questions that made adults turn away. [297]
For years the weaving had softened sorrow the way deep snow softens a road. [311]
Then tax riders brought news that Aldan and his regiment had fallen, and every throat in Hushmere closed at once. [331]
Too many names tried to enter the tapestry in one hour, and the hush, overfed, tightened until sound, grief, and noon itself were caught. [355]
In the hall doorway Marek found the cause and the key together, for the tapestry hung from a platinum labyrinth no wider than a chessboard. [380]
Its bright walls made a hundred turns above the cloth, and every thread of the border passed through one gate before it could join the weaving. [406]
If he tore the cloth, the village might wake all at once under a grief too large for any single chest. [427]
If he did nothing, the lull would hold, the thunder would never finish, and Hushmere would keep its fierce repose until it became a tomb. [452]
Marek sat before the tapestry anyway, because among the new knots he had seen one black thread twisted into the shape of his old classroom gambit. [478]
He understood then that rescue, if it came, would come by decoding frayed threads in the tapestry's border, where the weavers had hidden the order of their sorrow. [506]
His fingers began their useful trembling, tapping pawn, rook, and knight across his knee while his eyes followed each fray where color changed or a knot leaned left. [534]
The border was a notation, not of one game but of many losses end to end, each household repeating its worst move until it looked like fate. [561]
To read it truly, he would have to winnow the threads, letting his tapped breath shake empty husks of habit from the living grain of grief. [587]
So he gathered fallen clippings beneath the loom and winnowed them on the hall steps until true wool settled heavy in his left palm and lint clung to his coat. [617]
Among the heavy threads lay a strip of scarlet he knew, Aldan's color, but its final knot had been retied by another hand, clumsier and younger than his own. [646]
Decoded in order, the scarlet moves taught the gambit, then Marek's old warning against using it without retreat, then Aldan's own reversed knot spending the retreat first. [673]
Marek wept without sound, not because he was innocent, but because winnowing had separated his real fault, arrogance, from the endless chaff of believing he could have moved another man's hand. [704]
He could forgive himself for the lesson he had truly given only by finishing the work before him, so he fed the heavy threads through the platinum labyrinth in the decoded order, one gate, one grief, one breath at a time. [745]
Then Hushmere inhaled, the thunder finished far away, and time began again, thread by thread. [760]
