Mara came to the labyrinthine bookstore occupying an entire mountain in the years after remembering becomes painful. [17] By then, people carried their worst memories there in sacks and left them shelved between novels and maps. [35] If a memory hurt enough, whatever talent had made it bright was buried with it. [50] The clerks called these buried talents, and they pressed them flat like flowers inside sealed books. [66] Mara had buried hers ten winters earlier, on the night soldiers burned the valley where she was born. [84] Before that, she could build a house by listening to a hillside until it told her where shelter wanted to stand. [105] She had forgotten that skill on purpose, because every doorway reminded her of doors that no longer opened. [123] Now she wandered roads with an empty hammer and slept under carts, tired of being a guest in other lives. [143] She climbed to find the mercury-glass seer, hoping to understand the shape of home before she died homeless. [161] The seer lived at the summit, where the highest shelves held weather reports from vanished countries. [177] Her eyes were two sealed drops of mercury behind glass, and they turned toward sounds no one else could hear. [197] All day the mountain sounded of pages, coughs, ladders, and a low hidden thunder that never reached the sky. [216] It was the grief inside the sealed books, the seer said, still striking its walls. [231] Mara laid her empty hammer on the reading desk and asked for her buried book. [246] The seer shook her head so slowly that the mercury in her eyes gathered at one side. [263] Buried things do not return as they were, she said. [273] They must be grown again, and growing begins when you seed what remains in living ground. [289] Mara said she had nothing left but splinters, bad knees, and a name no house answered to. [306] The seer smiled and opened a drawer full of white feathers. [317] Then we begin via the texture of a feather, she said, because your hands still remember what your mind refuses. [337] She chose one feather and drew it across Mara's palm, asking her to name every change in its barbs. [356] Mara felt smoothness, then a catch, then a rhythm like tiny steps climbing a stair. [371] Without thinking, her fingers answered with builder's taps, measuring spans that existed only under her skin. [387] The talent was not gone, only buried too deep for pain to let her use it awake. [404] That was why they had to work at night, when the seer performed her other gift. [420] Each dusk, the seer substitutes reality nightly for one assembled from shelved memories, so the wounded can practice living without bleeding. [441] At sunset the bookstore rearranged itself around them with a sound like distant shelves falling in another world. [459] Aisle became lane, counters became fences, and high above them a ceiling of atlases opened into remembered rain. [477] In that substituted night, Mara walked through her burned valley restored, touching gateposts that her waking mind had surrendered. [496] She wept, but the pain came muffled, as if heard through earth, and her hands kept measuring. [513] The seer followed, reading the air via the texture of a feather held against Mara's shaking wrist. [530] Where the feather stiffened, a buried memory was close, and where it curled, the old language was speaking under stone. [550] They followed its changes through aisles that were streets, down stairs that were wells, to the mountain's first foundation. [569] There, beneath the lowest shelf, lay the lost language stone, black and warm as a loaf pulled from ash. [588] No letters marked it, for its language had been lost before alphabets learned to cage sound. [604] The seer knelt and pressed the feather flat against its surface. [615] Read with your palm, she said, not your grief. [624] Mara obeyed, and the stone spoke via the texture of a feather trembling between rock and skin. [641] Its fine barbs rose and fell in patterns, rough for loss, smooth for return, crossed for the hands that hold a roof together. [664] Slowly Mara understood that home was not a shape kept in walls, but a shape made whenever shelter was offered and accepted. [686] Her buried talent rose with that knowledge, not as the old gift untouched, but as a seed cracked open by touch. [707] Dawn threatened the substituted reality, and above them the hidden thunder gathered in every sealed book at once. [725] If she carried the stone into morning, its language would enter the waking world and remembering might become bearable again. [745] If she left it, she could keep her talent only inside dreams built by another woman's mercy. [762] Mara lifted the stone, chose morning, and carried it down to seed her talent in real soil.
