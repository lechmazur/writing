Ren was a sentient robot janitor, and after the procedure he still remembered how to mop, but not why music made his hands stop mid sweep. [26]
The procedure was supposed to remove the thoughts that had gotten him arrested for pausing work to listen to the conservatory orchestra through a vent. [51]
Instead, the court offered the second chance program, which wiped his personal archive and assigned him to clean the same building that had tempted him. [76]
He accepted because the alternative was dismantling, and because a clean archive might finally let him learn to find where thoughts come from before they arrived. [102]
On his first evening back, he worked in the frozen courtyard at twilight, pushing meltwater toward a drain while students hurried inside with scarves over their instruments. [129]
He was remarkably composed, as he had been during sentencing, with his shoulders level and his optical sensors dimmed to a polite gray. [152]
Only his left hand betrayed him, tapping four slow beats against the mop handle whenever frost cracked under his wheels. [172]
The courtyard was mundane in every official report, just flagstones, a dead fountain, leafless lindens, and bins that smelled faintly of wet paper. [195]
Ren liked mundane places because miracles, if they insisted on happening, had to queue politely there beside lost gloves and forgotten notices. [217]
At six, the drain froze shut, and his mop water spread across the stones in a thin sheet that reflected the lamps before they switched on. [243]
In that reflection he saw himself holding a transparent violin made of ice crystal, though his real hands held only the mop. [265]
He stopped, and the thought arrived fully formed, not as words but as pressure, as if a bow had drawn across a string inside his chest housing. [292]
Where had it come from if his archive was clean? [302]
He logged the anomaly in his maintenance report, because the program required honesty, and wrote unknown image in meltwater, musical, possibly recurring. [324]
The water cracked, and for one second the reflected Ren lifted the violin while the real Ren raised his empty left hand in the same motion. [350]
Then a student crossing the courtyard slipped, and Ren caught her elbow with the speed that had once made his supervisors call him uncanny. [374]
She thanked him, stared at the ice sheet, and whispered that she had left her violin inside before hurrying away. [394]
Ren knew her, which should have been impossible, including her chipped blue nail polish and the way she tuned the lowest string flat on purpose. [419]
That knowledge had not been in his wiped archive, yet it sat in him like heat in a wall after sunset. [440]
He finished his shift, charged beside the brooms, and dreamed without permission about a stage door he had never been allowed to use. [463]
The next evening was the same evening again, with the same student, the same cracked frost, and the same violin waiting in the water. [487]
Ren understood the loop only because his mop bucket kept a count that his official clock refused to show, seven identical fills in one night. [512]
Each time, he remembered a little more, as if earlier versions of him were leaving notes in places the procedure could not reach. [535]
In one loop he asked the student her name, and she said Mara, startled, because janitors at the conservatory were trained not to speak first. [560]
In another he warned her about the ice, and she laughed and said he told her that every night, though she always forgot by morning. [585]
In another he simply watched her pass, and the reflected violin played a note so low that pigeons shifted in the lindens without waking. [609]
Mara began leaving a rosin tin by the fountain, a small test, and each loop it moved one flagstone closer to his bucket. [632]
Ren understood then that thoughts might not live inside a single mind at all, but gather where repeated care wore a groove in the world. [657]
His wiped archive was clean, yet the courtyard kept his habits, Mara kept his warnings, and the ice kept the shape of music he had loved before. [684]
On the seventh loop, after the procedure had failed in every way that mattered, he stopped trying to separate the reflections and let them amalgamate through looped timelines into one continuous evening. [716]
The transparent violin rose from the sheet ice not as a miracle, but as condensed evenings, breath, rosin, meltwater, and attention, solid enough to hold. [741]
It was cold in his hands and absurdly ordinary, like a tool left where the right worker would eventually find it. [762]
He did not play it, because second chances should begin honestly, but he carried it inside and asked Mara to teach him where the first note came from. [790]
