<story>Each autumn at Calen, after the tide pools empty, the boats rest on mud and the wharf smells of salt and rot. [22] That was when Sella, the poetic wharf weaver, found the storybook illustration plate beneath her brother's overturned skiff. [40] She mended fleet nets, naming each knot for a star, a debt, or a drowned saint, as if language could keep rope from fraying. [64] The copper plate, green at its edges, showed a centuries-old labyrinth carved into red canyon walls behind the village, with an orchid at its center. [89] Every child knew that engraving from the winter storybook, but none had seen the plate that printed it. [107] Sella stared because three nights earlier her brother Tam had sleepwalked from his cot toward those walls and woke at the labyrinth mouth, feet bleeding, laughing that the stones sang. [137] Since the pools emptied, fisherfolk across Calen had risen nightly and followed routes they never chose awake. [154] Their paths crossed on the wharf, diverged at the net lofts, and met again at the canyon gate, as if drawn by one remembered map. [179] The elders nailed that gate shut, because precedent said the labyrinth stayed closed when the sea withdrew, and no one followed sleepers lest the dream spread. [205] Precedent also assigned Sella to mend nets and forget the flood-ruined chapel at the labyrinth heart, though she had left orchids there as a girl. [230] Now Tam left an orchid at her door after each sleepwalk, though none grew near Calen in autumn and he swore he had picked none. [255] Turning the plate over, Sella found her grandmother's handwriting scratched into the copper. [268] "If the village dreams with one pair of feet, sew the dream before it tears." [283] Below it, smaller letters explained that the cheerful picture was not a curse but instructions generations had misread. [301] Sella understood that the sleepers were not being lured away; they were retracing the safe line through fallen stone that waking memory had lost. [325] One path was too faint, but many laid together made a map no flood could erase. [341] In Calen, broken precedents were feared like dropped stitches, gaps where the world might unravel. [356] Yet Sella decided to break this one, not from rebellion, but to carve a path from ruin to reverence for her grandmother's chapel. [379] Because she was hauntingly optimistic, she chose to follow her neighbors via overlapping sleepwalking paths, trusting agreement more than any single footprint. [401] For seven nights she trailed the sleepers without waking them, carrying sailcloth, chalk, and red yarn from Tam's winter shirt. [421] Where one hesitated at a fork, another crossed surely; where a third turned back, two pressed on, so she chalked only ground they shared. [445] On the eighth night she entered alone before dawn and began to sew pale sailcloth panels together along the chalk line, anchoring each beneath stones. [470] Her needle rose and fell like a small gull, stitching net to cloth and cloth to net wherever fallen blocks had split the old pilgrim track. [496] The labyrinth resisted with blind turns, stairs ending at sky, and red corridors that wept iron after rain. [514] Twice rubble buried the shared footprints, and twice the plate, held to lantern light, showed which shadow hid a turn. [534] Near noon she reached the chapel, roofless at the center, and stopped with her needle raised. [550] Orchids covered the floor, fed by a spring the flood had uncovered rather than destroyed. [565] Their roots held cracked flagstones together, and their petals lay thick on an altar cleared of silt. [582] Sella saw shells, salt, and smooth pebbles arranged there, offerings left by hands that woke with no memory of giving. [602] The sleepers had never been escaping Calen; they had been tending this place in dreams, returning reverence to ruin one night at a time. [626] She sewed the final panel across the chapel threshold, then rang its cracked bell until the note rolled down every corridor. [647] Awake, the villagers followed her white path into the canyon, guided by red stitches bright against sailcloth and stone. [666] They found Tam asleep beside the altar, an orchid in his open hand, breathing as peacefully as a child in a storybook picture. [689] Sella woke him gently, and for the first time he remembered the spring, the flowers, and the way home. [708] In that orchid solace, Calen kept the path open and learned that some precedents break so reverence can return. [727]</story>
