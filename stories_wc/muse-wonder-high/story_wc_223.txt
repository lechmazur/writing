For seventeen days the city had lived during an interregnum, with a dead king below the chapel and no crowned hand above it. [23] In that pause the palace kept its doors open but its records shut, as if openness could excuse the silence. [43] Sella Marne worked in the whispering gallery that curved around the dome, where a word breathed against one wall arrived clear at the other. [67] She had been hired there as a crisis actor for the court drills, paid to scream, faint, and bleed from packets hidden in her sleeves. [92] Then the drill bell rang on the night the kitchens caught true fire, and the rehearsed smoke learned teeth. [111] By dawn she was a real crisis survivor, with ash in her hair and a scar mapping her left wrist. [131]

The court called the fire a successful exercise and listed no one dead, because a list would have admitted that servants slept beside the ovens. [156] Seven servants had died, and eleven survivors had signed silence papers before their hands stopped shaking. [172] Those papers were cords in spirit, and the clerk bound copies of them into a black ledger sealed with wax and wire. [194] Sella wanted to become the historian of small gestures because decrees had never told her how the dying reached for one another. [216] She remembered Tomas turning a cup upright before he ran, and old Ana folding a cloth even while the roof cracked above her. [239] Official history would keep the date and lose those motions, so she wrote them in the margins of the silence papers before the clerk bound them. [265]

The survivors lived as a nomadic penumbra, drifting along the shaded rim of the gallery and moving whenever the sun moved its bright blade. [289] They never gathered in full light, never stayed where guards expected grief, and never spoke above the breath that the dome could carry for them. [314] At the center of the gallery, forgotten by the fleeing council, stood the late king's chessboard missing all the pawns. [334] Queens, rooks, bishops, and knights waited in polished rows, splendid and useless, like a court that had misplaced the people who fed it. [357] From that board she learned their method, for a history with only great pieces could not move truthfully until ordinary hands returned. [379] She was benevolently ruthless with memory, keeping every tender detail but cutting away any lie, however kind, that made the dead easier to abandon. [403]

That ruthlessness brought her at dusk to the niche where the black ledger lay chained beside the empty seat of the absent regent. [426] The interim guards would burn it at midnight, not from cruelty, but from fear that names would gather a crowd before a ruler could bless them. [452] If she stole it whole, they would hunt the penumbra from shadow to shadow until no shaded rim remained. [471] So she chose to unbind it instead, leaf from cord and name from silence, and let each survivor carry one page into separate streets. [495] A shout would summon spears, and a written note would be searched from every sleeve at the stair. [513] She used instead a pattern of walking that spells words, a slow script her drill troupe had invented for crossing a stage without speaking. [537] From the high rail the penumbra could read her feet in dust and lamplight, each turn a letter and each pause a space. [560] She walked COME, then GATHER, then TAKE, and felt the gallery breathe back the shapes from a dozen hidden throats. [580]

One by one the survivors entered the shade she had chosen, not as mourners, but as pages loosening from a spine. [601] Sella cut the wire with a kitchen knife while Tomas held the ledger steady, though his steadiness cost him visible effort. [622] She gave Ana's page to Ana's daughter, who pressed it flat against her heart before she vanished down the service stair. [643] She gave the cooks their page, the grooms theirs, and the laundresses theirs, refusing to rank burns above hunger or terror above fatigue. [666] When the guards came at midnight with oil and a brazier, they found only the empty binding, the cut cord, and the board with its grand pieces staring at vacancies. [696] Beyond them the throne room held its luminous absence, a bright empty seat that seemed less powerful than the shadows now carrying names through the city. [722] Sella walked last, spelling MEMORY in dust no broom had yet touched, and behind her the gallery whispered it from wall to receiving wall. [746]
