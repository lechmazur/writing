<story>Mara had always been a grim optimist, the kind who expected the fall and still reached for the rail. [19] The infinite staircase gave her plenty of chances to practice both habits. [31] It rose without a top and sank without a bottom, a single flight turning forever inside a tower with no outside. [52] She could climb until her knees sang and still find the same chipped landing waiting above her. [69] She could descend and meet it again below, patient as a held breath. [82] That was the cruelty and the comfort of boundless confinement. [92] Everything was open, and nothing was reachable. [99] Mara kept climbing anyway because the staircase ran on perpetual motion, and she wanted to learn its secret. [117] Each step sank a fraction as her foot left it, then rose to meet the next climber who never came. [137] No engine fed it, no wind turned it, and no rest ever slowed it. [151] If she could prove how it moved forever, she thought, the proof might show her where it could stop. [170] Once, on a climb she could not number, a plain door had appeared beside that landing. [186] Beyond it she had heard rain, bread ovens, and people calling names that were not hers. [202] She had lifted one hand toward the handle and then stopped to think. [215] Thinking had cost her the door. [221] It folded into the wall with a soft click she still heard whenever the steps changed rhythm. [238] Since that loss, her whole purpose narrowed to capture the texture of a missed opportunity. [253] She did not want the door itself so much as the exact roughness of its vanishing under her fingers. [272] If she could hold that feeling clearly, she would know whether she had chosen caution or fear. [289] For that work she carried chalk, a notebook swollen with figures, and a pair of dice, one chipped. [307] The chipped die had lost its corner on the five face, so it leaned toward loss in a way she understood. [328] She rolled them not to gamble but to choose which risks were worth taking. [342] That made her balancedly daring, bold enough to leap a broken tread and careful enough to test it first with chalk dust. [364] On the landing she rolled again, watched the chipped die settle on five, and smiled despite herself. [381] Five meant she would write the proof she feared, the one that treated her own steps as part of the machine. [402] She had avoided it because the conclusion looked too much like blame. [414] Still, a grim optimist could accept blame if blame came with a handle. [427] She knelt and began to write through mathematical proofs directly onto the stone. [440] First she proved that each rising step returned exactly the energy spent to leave it. [455] Then she proved that the staircase borrowed the remainder from the climber who kept climbing. [470] The chalk squeaked, the dice rested beside her knee, and the steps slowed to listen. [485] Her final line showed that perpetual motion here was not a gift but a loop made of returning. [503] Every ascent paid for a descent she never took, and every hesitation fed the landing that waited for her. [522] The missed door had not been taken from her by the staircase. [534] It had been held shut by her habit of finishing the proof before touching the handle. [550] Mara read the last line twice and felt the old click move through her teeth. [565] Now the balanced roll of the dice asked for the other half of the method. [580] Proof could name the loop, but only a rupture could break it. [592] She stood, left the final symbol unfinished, and placed her full weight on the step that begged her to continue upward. [613] Then, trusting the chipped die more than her caution, she jumped backward into the open air between flights. [631] For one suspended instant the staircase forgot its rhythm. [640] Stone cracked with a clean rupture from the landing to the turning wall, and cold rain blew through. [658] Not a door appeared, but a torn opening shaped by the unfinished proof and her unfinished fall. [675] She caught its edge, and the stone felt rough, warm, and heartbreakingly like the handle she had failed to touch. [695] There it was, captured at last, the texture itself: grit under nails, hope under grief, and a choice arriving before it could be solved. [719] Mara laughed, grim and glad, and pulled herself through without finishing the climb. [732] Behind her the steps resumed, endless and unneeded, still rising for anyone who preferred a perfect proof to a living risk. [753]</story>
