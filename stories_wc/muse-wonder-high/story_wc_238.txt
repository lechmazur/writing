Marta Vale was the Millerton Gazette's gossip-column exorcist, writing readers' scandals by day and, on Tuesdays, casting out whatever listened back from their houses. [24] She was not ordained, which suited her, because she believed imperfect miracles were the only honest kind, the sort that left a window cracked. [48] A miracle that fixed everything, she told callers, was just a debt wearing perfume. [62] That October she returned to the county sheet music archive, a low brick room behind the library where dust hung like low fog over every cabinet. [88] She had arrived during the hush before the orchard's trees were to be cut down, when pickers had gone, saws had not arrived, and crows seemed to hold their breath. [118] Her brother Ellis kept the archive, and he had not spoken to her since she printed that his late wife communed with their dead mother through the piano. [146] She had come to deliver an overdue apology, ten years overdue, and she carried it the way she carried everything difficult, hidden inside something breakable. [171] In her coat pocket sat their mother's antique perfume bottle, its silver cap tarnished, its glass still holding a breath of lilac and smoke. [195] Ellis looked up from a box of hymnals, thinner than she remembered, with ink under his nails and orchard grit ground into his knuckles. [219] Ellis asked if she had come to exorcise the cabinets or just haunt him professionally. [234] Marta set the bottle on his desk between a metronome and a stack of unsorted waltzes, and the glass ticked once though nothing had touched it. [260] She had advised a hundred strangers through metaphors because plain speech failed her, and now it failed her again, so she said, "A family is a roof, Ellis, and I pulled off a shingle to sell papers." [297] "I was wrong to print June's seances, wrong to make your grief into a headline, and I am sorry I never said so while you could still believe me quickly." [327] Ellis did not answer, but from the cabinets came a faint chord, as if damp paper had remembered hands. [346] For three nights that month, scores throughout the archive had played themselves at closing, always songs June had copied by hand before the fever took her. [372] Ellis believed Marta's column had invited reporters, curiosity seekers, and something less kind that now lived in the paper and would not leave. [395] He wanted it gone before the orchard fell, because June had planted half those trees and he could not bear another loss learning her name. [420] Marta understood then that her apology and her exorcism were the same work, and both would have to reconnect what her words had split. [444] "We coax it out through induced currents," she said, winding Ellis's copper binding wire around the bottle's silver cap and then around each cabinet handle in turn. [471] She clipped the free ends to the hand-cranked magneto Ellis kept beside the reading lamps, set a magnet swinging on the metronome, and told him to crank when the ticking matched his pulse. [504] "Think of it as weather in a wire," she said, because metaphors were safer than fear in her mouth. [523] "We are not smashing the cloud, Ellis, we are giving its rain a drain." [537] He cranked, the wire hummed against the bottle, and every drawer in the archive shivered with a soft, gritty chord that rose through their shoes. [562] The scores lifted at their edges though the windows were shut, and June's handwriting brightened where the current found iron in her old ink. [586] Then a voice came not from any throat but from a hundred pages vibrating together, thin and furious and heartbreakingly ordinary. [607] "You sold me," June said through the paper, and Marta felt the sentence land harder than any haunting she had ever described in print. [631] "I did," Marta said, and did not hide behind a metaphor this time. [644] "I wanted the byline more than I wanted my brother, and I have carried that fact like grit under a nail ever since." [667] The current faltered as Ellis stopped cranking, his shoulders shaking, but he caught the rhythm again when Marta steadied the bottle with both hands. [691] Heat warmed the silver cap, the last lilac breath escaped, and for one impossible second the archive smelled like their mother's kitchen after rain. [715] June laughed once through the scores, surprised, and the fury in the pages loosened into something almost like forgiveness, but not quite and not all at once. [742] That was the imperfect miracle Marta had always trusted, not a door slammed on sorrow, but a door finally unlocked from both sides. [765] Outside, the first saw coughed beyond the orchard, and Ellis took her hand above the warm bottle, choosing his sister again while there was still time to choose. [793]
