For three winters, Joren had been the ascending servant of the icebound spire above the clouds, and he still counted every floor as if it might change its mind. [29]
The service elevator was a brass cage that climbed ten floors from the kitchen to Captain Ilya Marrow, groaning through ice and old prayer. [53]
This morning it carried Joren, a covered tray, and a parcel that the night steward had pressed into his hands without explanation. [75]
He was warmly inquisitive by nature, the sort of boy who asked kettles why they sang and thanked doors that opened kindly. [97]
So of course he opened the parcel before the elevator passed the first floor. [111]
Inside lay the airship captain's goggles, newly mended, and beneath their leather strap, a small diary bound in blue canvas. [131]
Joren knew the goggles because he polished their brass rims each week while Captain Marrow told him which clouds had once tried to kill her. [156]
He did not know the diary, and that ignorance sat in his chest like a warm coal. [173]
On its cover, in ink faded to the color of weak tea, someone had written, For Ilya, when looking hurts less than remembering. [196]
Joren should have waited, but the elevator had nine floors to go, and sorrow kept such poor company when it was left alone. [219]
For a year, since her ship the Larkspur fell beyond the northern ice, Captain Marrow had eaten little, laughed less, and spoken mostly to the wind. [245]
Joren served her with affectionate detachment because love, he had learned, sometimes meant carrying soup without demanding that grief finish it. [266]
Still, beneath his careful manners, he wanted one thing with stubborn brightness, to seek out the joy beneath anguish and set it gently on her tray. [292]
He opened the diary as the second floor slid past, its window white with frost and morning. [309]
The pages seemed blank except for pinpricks, tiny holes pricked in wandering lines that meant nothing in the elevator shade. [329]
Then the cage reached a broken shutter on the third floor, and sunlight entered in thin, bright blades. [347]
Joren, curious rather than wise, lifted the goggles and held the diary behind one amber lens. [363]
Through scattered light, the pinpricks joined like stars, and letters bloomed where the lens softened the glare into separate threads. [383]
The first readable line said, If you found this by accident, you are probably Joren, so stop apologizing to the book. [404]
Joren laughed aloud, then covered his mouth, because the handwriting belonged to Tomas Marrow, who had captained the Larkspur beside his wife and had not come home. [431]
He had always left jokes in coat pockets and silver spoons in flowerpots, as if sorrow might trip over something playful and lose its way. [456]
At the fourth floor, Joren turned the page, and the holes aligned again through the lens into careful, slanted words. [476]
Tomas had written that the diary was encrypted because grief makes thieves of kind people, and he would not have pity stealing these pages first. [501]
Each entry, he promised, described not the ship or its fall, but one ordinary happiness that he and Ilya had failed to notice while it was happening. [528]
The fifth floor brought a smell of coal smoke and a page about Ilya asleep in a hammock, one boot on, snoring at the moon. [553]
The sixth gave him Ilya again, furious and radiant, teaching frightened cadets to dance on deck so their hands would stop shaking before a storm. [578]
Joren read with wet eyes and steady hands, understanding now why the parcel had waited until the goggles came back from the mender. [601]
Without the amber lens, Tomas had known, the diary would keep its silence, and without silence, Ilya might have burned it unread on the worst night. [627]
At the seventh floor, the elevator slowed beside the archive window, where pale light fell sideways through cracked ice. [646]
There the last page resisted him, its holes scattering into nonsense no matter how he tilted the goggles. [664]
Joren frowned, then saw that the page was not one page but two, pressed together by damp and folded at the corner. [686]
With a thumbnail, careful as a surgeon and twice as nosy, he began to unfold the hidden sheet. [704]
The eighth floor chimed, the ninth floor answered with a shiver of cables, and the sheet opened under his hands like a small sail catching weather. [730]
Through scattered light one last time, he read Tomas asking Ilya to keep living loudly, and to forgive the dead for being unable to knock. [755]
At the tenth floor, Joren wiped his eyes, set the goggles over the open diary, and knocked himself, ready to watch anguish uncover its buried joy. [781]
