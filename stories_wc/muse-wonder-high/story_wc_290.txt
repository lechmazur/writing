During the fallow period, when frost sealed the fields and no plow needed her hands, Mara Venn returned to the abbey she had sworn to forget. [26] She was a claustrophobic explorer by trade and curse, mapping flooded mines and collapsed granaries for villages that feared their dark places. [48] Tight stone thinned her breath, yet she entered because someone had to learn where the earth had moved and might move again. [70] Her neighbors called her prudently adventurous, for she tested every rope twice, marked every threshold in chalk, and never chased wonder beyond a counted retreat. [95] This time wonder had a purpose. [101] She had come to uncover a vital key lost to time, the iron tuning key that kept the abbey organ true and opened the crypt beneath its choir. [129] Without it, the crypt stayed sealed against winter stores and medicines hidden there before the roof fell. [146] The elders had forbidden the search after her brother Tomas vanished, but their caution sounded to Mara like surrender dressed as wisdom. [168] She carried his unfinished blueprint in a waxed roll, its ink faded where his hand trembled near the crossing of nave and transept. [191] At its center he had circled a warning in lead: the rooms do not stay. [206] Mara had laughed at that warning until the year the abbey stair delivered her to the same locked door three times from three directions. [230] Tomas had named that wrongness a spatial mutiny, as if corridors and chapels had risen against the blueprint and refused their appointed places. [253] She waited until starlight thinned the mist, then slipped past the watch with lantern shuttered and compass case against her ribs. [274] The abbey crossing formed a shadowed nexus under starlight, where nave, transept, and tower stairs met in darkness no doorway could explain. [296] There the air pressed close, and panic climbed her throat as the walls seemed to lean in to listen. [315] She knelt, unrolled the blueprint on a slab, and pinned its corners with stones, saving the last corner for the point itself. [337] Her only steel instrument was a blueprint drafting compass point, broken from its hinge years ago and kept because its needle could scratch wood, wax, or stone. [364] She drove that point through the blueprint at the exact crossing Tomas had circled, anchoring paper to the slab crack beneath. [385] When she rose, the north doorway stood where the south doorway had been, and a stair she had never climbed descended into the floor. [409] Her chalk marks appeared ahead on walls she had not reached, each arrow reversed, as if the abbey mocked her careful method. [431] Panic narrowed the crossing further, or seemed to, until her shoulders brushed both sides of a passage that had seemed wide enough for a cart. [456] Then, from the choir beyond the mutinous dark, the lonely church organ breathed through a broken pipe and gave one low note. [478] The reverberation rolled through the crossing and seemed to transfix every threshold, holding door, stair, and shadow in their true places for the length of one held breath. [506] Mara counted the stillness, as Tomas had taught her, and understood that sound could give the mutiny a brief, merciful order. [527] She pulled the point free, left the stones to hold the blueprint, and ran only while the echo lasted, scratching each stone that remained beneath her feet. [554] Three echoes bought her the choir steps, because after each fading she waited, listened, and let a gust through the broken pipe teach her the interval. [580] In the loft she found the bellows handle worn smooth and Tomas's chalk tally marks counting notes, not days. [599] Mara set her shoulder to the handle, drew breath with the bellows, and pressed the lowest key Tomas had circled above the stops. [622] Through the reverberation of a lonely church organ, the crossing below steadied, unfolded, and showed its shape at last: one nexus, four doors, and a narrow stair descending where a blank wall had pretended to be. [658] As the note held, something iron slid inside the longest pipe and dropped ringing into the loft dust beside her knee. [679] It was the tuning key, its handle cut with Tomas's initials, its head fitted to both organ valve and crypt lock. [700] She wedged the sounding key down with the compass point, lashed the bellows to a rubble weight, and descended with the iron key in her shaking hand. [727] The stair squeezed her ribs, but rope, counted steps, and the held note carried her to the crypt lock, which turned at last. [750] Inside lay stores, Tomas's lantern, and his scratched request to leave the door open to the sky. [767] At dawn she led the elders in, grieving the brother whose map had brought her home. [783]
