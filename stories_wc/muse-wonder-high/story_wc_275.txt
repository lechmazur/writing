<story>After the tower fell, the desert learned a new kind of quiet. [12] Mira kept the desert rose farm inside its glass-domed enclosure as if nothing beyond the dunes had broken. [30] She was a bounty hunter's daughter, raised on road maps and rewards, and she had chosen pacifism the way other people chose a trade. [54] Her father, Tomas Rook, had taught her to track a man by a bent stem, but she used the skill to find thirsty seedlings. [78] The blast that toppled the tower took her hearing, and the quiet that followed felt engraved into her bones rather than merely heard. [101] Yet she became senselessly perceptive, reading weather in the tremor of leaves and strangers in the shiver of sand against the dome. [123] Outside, caravans scattered and towns argued over wells, so she kept planting to weave continuity through chaos one rooted season at a time. [146] Each morning she worked the rows while inculcating plants with values, pressing patience into the soil beside seeds that wanted to rush. [168] She spoke aloud though she could not hear herself, naming mercy while she spared weak shoots and honesty while she thinned crowded beds. [191] She believed roots remembered handling, and that a rose tended without cruelty would bloom without demanding blood from bees or hands. [212] A scrap of airship ballast sandbag shaded the youngest bed, its coarse weave softening noon light into stripes the seedlings could bear. [234] The airship had fallen with the tower beacon, and Mira had cut that scrap from its ruptured side before salvagers stripped the wreck. [257] Stitched lettering on the canvas named the ship and its lost crew, so watering beneath it became a small act of remembrance. [279] Inside the dome the air stayed tranquil, yet every rose blazed red against green leaves, a quiet fire that never asked to spread. [302] At midday the sand against the glass changed rhythm, and Mira looked up before the gate bell rope began to sway. [323] A boy stood outside clutching his bleeding arm, with her father's bounty knife already loose in his other shaking hand. [343] He mouthed that hunters were coming, then pointed toward three dust plumes moving fast beyond the eastern dune. [361] Mira opened the gate because the farm rule, taught to every rose and guest, was that thirst and wounds entered before questions. [383] She washed his cut with stored rain, bound it with clean linen, and set warm flatbread where he could reach it without asking. [406] Writing on her slate, Joss explained that Tomas had spared him, given him the knife, and sent him here for shelter. [427] Tomas had refused a bounty on a child thief who stole medicine, and the other hunters had called that mercy a betrayal. [449] The hunters would search for tracks, blood, and fear, so Mira hid Joss beneath the nursery bench where the sandbag shadow kept him cool. [473] She swept his footprints into the wind patterns she knew, then returned to pruning as if the morning had never been interrupted. [495] On the potting bench the bounty knife lay exposed where Joss had left it, bright enough to accuse them both. [515] When the hunters reached the dome, their leader held up a reward notice and shouted words Mira read imperfectly from his lips. [537] The leader stepped inside with one hand near his own blade, watching Mira as hunters had always watched her father. [557] Mira lifted the knife slowly, turned it from the light, and made herself sheathe it in the leather loop beside her pruning tools. [580] The gesture settled the dome more firmly than a threat, because her hands stayed steady and her eyes burned with tranquil refusal. [602] She poured water for the hunters first, equally and without flinching, as she had taught the roses to share a scarce irrigation line. [625] When the leader glanced toward the nursery bench, a rose branch trembled from hidden breathing, and Mira felt the warning through the floor. [648] She wrote on her slate that mercy was not betrayal but the oldest continuity, the thread Tomas had carried from his mother into them. [672] For the first time the leader looked at the rows instead of the bench, and his shoulders lowered among the red blooms. [694] He stepped back outside, told his men the farm held only flowers and a deaf woman, and led them away along the eastern road. [718] After their dust faded, Joss came out shaking, and Mira placed the sheathed knife where he could see it without needing to touch it. [742] He wrote that he wanted to stay and learn, if a thief could be taught as patiently as a rose. [762] Mira gave him the sandbag scrap to carry seedlings, and together they planted another row, extending the engraved quiet beyond loss into living memory. [786]</story>
