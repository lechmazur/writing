<story>When Ellis returned to his sister's house, he came not as a mourner but as the neighborhood's leaf blower symphony conductor, a man who timed six machines into almost music. [30] For eleven months that skill had let him avoid silence, because every yard he cleared gave grief somewhere else to go. [50] Now, after the final grain slips through the hourglass on June's mantel, the year of formal mourning was officially over, and no one in the house knew how to begin again. [81] June had turned the hourglass herself when her daughter died, promising that when it emptied she would speak Mara's name aloud and clear the small bedroom upstairs. [108] She had done neither. [112] Instead she rewound wound clocks, refolded folded laundry, and smiled at Tomas with a politeness that frightened him more than weeping. [133] Tomas worked late, left meals untouched, and listened at the bedroom door for sounds he would never admit hoping to hear. [154] Ellis understood that his real work here was tending to the unspoken, the name, the birthday, the accident, and all the love that had lost its grammar. [181] His private vow, foolish and stubborn, was to restore lost laughter to a household that had forgotten it was allowed to laugh. [203] He was mournfully eager for any sign that the house could hold joy again without betraying Mara. [220]

The sign came at dusk, when his leaf blower coughed, died, and left the yard in a quiet so complete that he heard breathing under the porch. [247] Beneath the porch steps, half hidden by lattice and wet leaves, he found a low concrete mouth he had never noticed as a child. [271] June, watching from the kitchen window, went pale and said their father had sealed that old echo chamber tunnel after their mother began hearing voices in it. [298] The tunnel had once carried heat and gossip between farmhouse, root cellar, and well house, amplifying footfalls until private sorrow sounded public. [320] That night the breathing became broken childish humming, repeating the lullaby June used to sing through Mara's bedroom door. [339] No one slept. [342] By morning Tomas insisted it was wind in the vents, June insisted it was nothing, and both of them set an extra plate neither would explain. [368] Ellis knew a true haunting was not a sheet or a slammed door but a sound a family made by refusing to answer it. [392]

He took his blower apart on the porch, not to fix it yet, but because its broken pull cord handle fit exactly into the rusted latch of the tunnel grate. [422] His father had shaped both after one truck key, June whispered, because he believed a house should open only for tools that had served it. [447] At midnight Ellis turned the handle, entered on his belly, and dragged the silent blower through dust, mouse bones, and sweet rotted leaves. [470] Inside, the tunnel widened into a round chamber where whispers returned altered, as if Mara were learning his voice from the walls. [492] He did not call her name, because conductors know entrances matter and grief breaks if forced too early. [510] Instead he let fallen leaves macerate in the tunnel's shallow water while he worked, softening their brittle edges so moving air would lift music from them, not dust. [538] For three nights he practiced there, blowing through the dead blower's tube until damp leaves and tunnel air answered in uneven puffs rather than roars. [563] Above him, through floorboards and vents, June began without knowing it to answer via half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key wind, her voice finding Mara's tune where words had failed. [593] On the fourth night she came barefoot to the grate, Tomas behind her holding the empty hourglass like an unlit lantern. [614] Ellis raised his hands, absurd and trembling, and conducted. [623] The chamber sighed, wet leaves rose, and the tunnel returned June's humming layered with a smaller laugh none of them had heard for a year. [648] June cried out Mara's name at last, not in apology but in greeting, and the echo answered with the name softened rather than stolen. [672] Tomas laughed then, startled and guilty and alive, while tears cut clean lines through the dust on his face. [691] It did not bring Mara back, only permission for the living to remember her without lowering their voices. [709]</story>
