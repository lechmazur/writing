<story>Mira had been the canyon echo singer since she was twelve, when the elders first led her blindfolded to the serpentine nexus under starlight. [24] There, five gorges twisted together above a black pool, and every voice thrown into the dark returned changed or did not return at all. [48] Her gift was not a loud voice but a richly perceptive ear, able to hear grief hiding inside anger and fear hiding inside laughter. [72] She had accepted the lonely post for one reason: to learn why some people echo for generations while others vanish after a single call. [96] On market days, lovers, miners, and mourning mothers climbed down to ask her to sing their names into the nexus and listen for an answer. [121] Some names came back seven times, warm and clear, as if the canyon itself had learned to love them. [140] Others fell straight into silence, and Mira had to watch hope leave a face like light leaving a window. [159] For ten years she kept a ledger of returns, certain that kindness, guilt, or blood must explain the difference. [178] None of it held, until the night a stranger arrived carrying the whispering seashell half she had buried with her brother. [199] Mira knew that shell because she had found it whole on the riverbank and split it with Tomas so each could hear the other across the gorges. [226] When Tomas died in a rockfall, she placed his half in his hands and kept hers pressed to her ear for a year, hearing only wind. [252] The stranger set the shell on her ledger and said her brother was not dead in a grave, but alive beneath the nexus. [275] His name was Pell, a reed cutter with dust in his beard, and his voice carried the reed-blown melancholy of men who work beside deep water. [301] He would not say more until she sang, claiming the walls themselves had ears and traded secrets for silver. [320] That was her first thread in the webs of intrigue that had quietly grown around her canyon while she listened only for the dead. [344] She sang her brother's name in a single low note, and the nexus answered not once but eleven times, each return softer and younger than the last. [371] Pell flinched at the seventh echo, and Mira understood that he had heard that voice below ground, calling names that were not his own. [395] She proceeded through progressive disclosure, note by note and question by question, drawing his story out the way she drew echoes, never demanding the whole truth at once. [423] Pell had cut reeds along the underground river where the council hid its prison, a place built to ensure that certain voices would never reach the canyon. [447] The prisoners sang into stone vents at night, and their songs traveled through flooded tunnels until they broke the surface as echoes. [472] Tomas, who had mapped those tunnels for the council, had freed three prisoners and hidden them among the reed cutters before the guards found him. [497] The council had drugged Tomas after the rockfall and carried him from his coffin before burial, leaving the shell lost in its lining. [520] Pell found it when the council ordered the empty grave opened, fearing Tomas had hidden a map of the tunnels before his capture. [543] Inside the shell, fine grooves caught the light, and Mira read the names of the missing in Tomas's tiny, patient hand. [564] Her ledger suddenly made sense, for every name that echoed seven times matched a prisoner, and every vanished name matched a grave Pell knew. [588] The canyon had never chosen the worthy; desperate people below had answered the living, repeating names to prove they still existed. [609] At the festival that night, the council would expect her to ensure the crowd heard only comfort, then they would flood the tunnels forever. [633] Instead, while the universe hums its secret song and the pool sinks lowest, she would sing each hidden name and let its owner answer. [657] She hid Pell among the singers, gave him her half of the shell to take to Tomas, and kept her brother's half pressed against her ear. [683] An hour later the shell trembled, Tomas whispered that the vents were open, and Mira stepped to the nexus to sing the oldest missing name. [708] From beneath the starlit stone a living voice answered before the council, and answered, and answered, until the crowd rose shouting every name in her ledger. [734]</story>
