Elian Marr worked nights in the funeral home prep room because the dead never asked him to lie for them. [20]
His father Silas had been a con artist who sold forged death certificates to frightened people with excellent penmanship and no conscience. [42]
Elian was the con artist's honest child, incapable of saying a coat was blue when it was black. [60]
That honesty had cost him schools, jobs, girlfriends, and one trial where his testimony jailed Silas for nine years. [79]
On the steel shelf above the embalming table he kept his father's vintage ink pot like a warning. [97]
Its ink smelled of clove and iron, and watching it spread liquefies rigid thinking before it dries. [114]
At midnight the delivery door buzzed twice, the signal Okafor used when a family came without an appointment. [132]
Elian opened it to find a soaked woman dragging a laundry cart stacked with a body bag that was breathing. [152]
The woman said her name was Dara Venn and that the man inside the bag was her brother Nilo, a radio journalist from Kestravia. [176]
Three hours earlier police in his home capital had named him a traitor for broadcasting names of prisoners who had vanished. [197]
Nilo had swallowed a sedative, ridden in a coffin across two checkpoints, and woken just enough to whisper for water. [217]
Dara did not beg, which Elian respected, because begging had been the music under every dinner of his childhood. [236]
She asked for two things that belonged to Silas rather than to him: one forged death certificate for Nilo and a path to political asylum. [261]
The funeral home shared its rear brick wall with the consulate of Alden, a small country that took Kestravian refugees and guarded its courtyard day and night. [288]
If Nilo could stand inside that courtyard before dawn and speak his claim aloud, Alden law would hear him instead of returning him. [311]
Elian said no with the clean speed of a man who had rehearsed refusal until it became a spine. [330]
Dara then set a cloth ledger on the wet prep table beside a row of polished steel clamps. [348]
Inside were thirty seven names in Silas's hand, every client he had cheated with promises of resurrection insurance and prepaid graves that never existed. [372]
She would give Elian the ledger if he gave Nilo a recorded death strong enough to stop hunters from squeezing their mother for his hiding place. [398]
All his adult life Elian had tried to find the mathematics of a fair trade for his father's debts, adding apologies and wages like coins that never balanced. [426]
One lie for thirty seven repayments sounded generous until he divided it by the terror in Dara's steady hands. [445]
Dara pointed at the drainage vent set low in the shared wall, an old breach from Prohibition days that Okafor sealed with boards and freezers every spring. [472]
Consulate guards crossed the courtyard every eighteen minutes, Kestravian watchers parked outside the front gate, and Nilo could barely lift his head. [494]
Running now would kill him, so Elian chose the harder discipline he had learned among corpses: by waiting actively, watching clocks, hands, breath, and doors. [519]
For the next hour he embalmed Ana Ruiz, who had died that evening at ninety, while Dara timed guard steps and counted Nilo's pulse aloud. [544]
Ana's painted nails, a radio playing dance music through static, and Nilo's returning color filled the cold room with deathly vitality. [565]
Between sutures Elian opened the vintage ink pot and copied the registrar's signature from an authentic certificate until his own refusal softened into a question. [590]
What exactly did a fair trade weigh when one side offered paper and the other side offered a man's remaining life? [611]
He wrote Nilo Venn, dead of heart failure at eleven forty, then blotted the page and felt no triumph, only room to breathe. [634]
When the guard clock reached its blind minute, Dara pulled the freezer aside and Elian used a bone saw on the rotten boards behind the vent. [660]
Together they breached the vent until its grille swung inward on one rusted screw and cold courtyard air smelled suddenly of rain and cut grass. [685]
Nilo crawled on elbows polished by pain, laughing once through cracked lips because his funeral suit made him look already mourned. [706]
Inside the courtyard he stood, swayed, and said his name and country and fear clearly enough that the Alden guard lowered his rifle and called the duty officer. [734]
The officer recorded a claim for political asylum, wrapped Nilo in a blanket, and warned Dara that paper deaths could protect only if the living stayed disciplined. [761]
Dawn found Elian mailing the false certificate toward Kestravia, holding the true ledger like a balance sheet, and beginning with the first name. [784]
