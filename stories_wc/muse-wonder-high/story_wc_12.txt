<story>Father Tomas was the loop pedal priest of the dimensional anchor, a stone chapel bolted where two worlds ground against each other. [22] Every Mass, he recorded his chanting, looped it through the speakers, and sang over it until prayer became architecture holding the anchor steady. [45] If the chanting stopped for thirteen seconds, the walls began showing other skies. [58] On the last night of October, Mara arrived carrying an artist's palette with one color, a wet black that swallowed candlelight. [79] She placed it on the altar like an offering she already regretted. [91] A voice had moved into her mouth three days earlier and was learning her tongue. [106] Tomas knew that before the possession completes at the third midnight, the host can still answer in her own name. [126] Afterward, questions only teach the intruder to imitate her. [135] The chapel had a muffled clarity that night, every sound arriving through wool yet leaving its meaning bare. [153] Mara said the voice painted through her hands while she slept, leaving canvases covered in black doors, each opened wider than the last. [176] Tomas did not reach for a crucifix. [183] He reached for the pedal, because his calling was not to shout at demons but to illuminate hidden assumptions. [202] Other priests decorate with incense and gold, but he decorates with silence, leaving shaped gaps where a soul can hear itself think. [224] He recorded one low organ note, let it loop, added Mara breathing, then recorded four beats of deliberate nothing. [243] The silence sat inside the loop like a clean chair in a crowded room. [257] His aim was to plumb her buried memory through rhetorical devices, since direct commands gave the intruder something solid to twist. [278] "Who paints when you sleep?" he asked. [351] Since then she had painted his portrait weekly, using less color each time, until grief edited her palette down to black alone. [373] The intruder nested in that narrowing and taught her that sorrow was a door and opening it wider was love. [417] He pressed the pedal, letting her confession loop back framed by recorded silence. [508] Outside, the cliff repeated into fog, each copy thinner, while the anchor stone hummed through their shoes. [690] "You are not grief," she said. [696] "You are a habit wearing grief as a coat." [705] As midnight pressed against their ears, Tomas set the pedal to record and nodded, leaving her the choice. [723] Mara dipped a finger into the black and drew not a door but a window with a returning ferry. [742] The intruder left without drama, because drama required her belief, and the walls settled into one sky.
