<story>On the third morning after the hydraulics fail, the drawbridge over the Alwen estuary locks half-open and will not move for anyone. [22] The fleet that left in spring for the southern canneries must now come home against season and tide. [40] The only guidance left is the starlit signal station on Gull Rock, where I keep the lamps by hand. [59] People call me the patchwork navigator because no single chart survived the flood, so I stitched five together. [77] In my coat pocket, wrapped in oilcloth, I carry a patina bronze censer foot from my mother's burned altar. [96] It is small, lion-pawed, green at the edges, and heavy enough to hold a map corner down in wind. [115] My brother Tomas drowned in the north channel three winters ago, on a night when the markers burned out. [134] Since then I have worked, quite literally, to navigate the geography of loss, one missing buoy and one remembered kitchen at a time. [157] This autumn the fleet is attempting a reverse migration, returning north for winter instead of following warmth and work south. [177] The southern grounds have gone warm and empty, and the men and women aboard are coming back to cold water that might still feed them. [202] Our beacon is old, fed by a tidal dynamo, and its strength fluctuates with moon phases in a way no engineer has explained to my satisfaction. [228] At full moon it throws a hard white blade across the marsh; at new moon it barely sighs. [246] Tonight is new moon, the return is due, and my stitched chart disagrees with itself where the flood erased the banks. [267] I have to build a second signal, low and amber, that boats can follow when the white beacon fades. [286] I learn where to place it through cryptic phone calls at midnight from Ansel, who keeps the upstream lock and speaks only in weather and scripture. [312] At midnight the hand-crank telephone shrieks, and Ansel says, 'The sparrow has lost the eave, but the river remembers the roof.' [333] I write it down and look at my patchwork, where five maps disagree most violently around Widow's Eave, a sandbar shaped like a broken roofline. [358] I set the censer foot on that sandbar's corner, because grief has taught me that what survives fire can anchor what survives water. [381] All week I build the amber signal from cannery lanterns, bicycle mirrors, copper wire, and a bellows pump I pedal myself. [402] Children bring me jars for lenses and do not ask why my hands shake when the north channel bell rings. [422] On the second midnight Ansel says, 'Do not trust the road that shines,' and I understand that moonlight on the flooded towpath will look like a safe channel. [450] So I move the amber signal six yards west, onto black mud where no reflection can imitate it. [468] That mud is where we found Tomas's boat, upright and empty, its lantern still warm inside a nest of reeds. [488] I had expected standing there to hurt without relief, but working there gives me a kind of raw solace: my hands know what to repair even when my heart does not. [519] On the seventh night the wind drops, the stars sharpen, and from the south I hear engines idling where no engines should be. [542] The white beacon dims to a thread, exactly as the moon promised, and I light the amber signal and pedal until my legs burn. [566] The first boat answers with three short horn blasts, then turns not toward the shining towpath but toward my low, honest mud. [588] One by one the fleet follows, hulls scraping reeds, crews silent so they can hear the shallow water change its note. [609] A girl on the last boat waves a cannery scarf at me, and I recognize my own stitching on its torn edge. [631] She is Tomas's daughter, come north with the fleet, carrying his compass in a tin that clicks against her teeth when she smiles. [654] At midnight, after the last hull grounds safely, Ansel calls and says only, 'The roof holds,' and hangs up before I can thank him. [678] I take the censer foot from my pocket and set it at the base of the amber signal, where its green paw will hold against rust and tide. [706] The loss has not left my map; it has become a channel marker, warning and guiding at once. [724] Tomorrow we will build a proper footbridge, mend the chart together, and teach the girl which stars mean home. [743]</story>
