For seven years, Mara Sol worked as a contact tracer for hearts at Saint Brigid Transplant Network. [17] She did not trace infections, fevers, or the ordinary paths of disease through a city. [32] She traced the living route left by a donated heart, from the family who released it to the stranger who carried it onward. [55] If both sides agreed after a year, she arranged one anonymous call and sat between them. [71] No surnames, addresses, faces, or debts were allowed to cross the line she guarded. [85] Her work was to protect vulnerable voices from gratitude that could crush and grief that could demand. [102] Nurses called the scheduled hour when the dead call, because a widow might hear her husband beating inside another chest. [122] Mara disliked the phrase, yet she understood why no one could surrender its terrible hope. [137]

That evening she waited in the mist-clad atrium at twilight, where the fountain breathed fog across the glass and benches. [157] The damp air gave every voice a reeling hush, as if the room itself leaned closer and then swayed back. [177] Iona Pell arrived with wet shoes, a green coat, and a decades-old hearing aid fitted behind her left ear. [196] At eighty-two, she still moved as though delay were an insult she refused to accept. [211] Three years earlier, her husband Tomas had died after a fall, and his heart had gone to an unnamed recipient. [231] Mara had traced that recipient through sealed records, hospital transfers, and one frightened sister who finally answered the telephone. [250] Tonight the recipient had agreed to press a telephone to his chest while Iona listened from a private booth. [269]

Before Mara could guide her onward, Iona opened her purse and removed the last photograph ever taken of Tomas. [288] He stood laughing in this same atrium, one hand raised, minutes before the fall that ended his life. [306] Iona wanted the stranger to have it, although photographs broke every rule Mara was hired to keep. [323] Mara held the photograph gently and assured her, 'He will know he is remembered without needing to carry your sorrow.' [343] Iona searched her face, then nodded and let Mara keep the picture face down. [357] At that moment, the old hearing aid gave a faint click, paused, and clicked again. [372] Iona touched her ear and whispered that the aid had made that sound only near Tomas and his implanted monitor. [392]

Mara should have called the booth, confirmed the connection, and followed the careful script on her card. [409] Instead, being persistently curious, she asked Iona to turn slowly toward the fountain and then toward the dark service corridor. [429] Mara followed, tracking faint pulses in a decades-old hearing aid that no modern device would have noticed. [446] The pulses quickened toward the service corridor, weakened near the elevators, and stumbled unevenly behind the fountain. [463] There, half hidden by mist and broad leaves, a young man lay collapsed with one hand pressed to his chest. [483] A telephone beside him kept displaying the private number Mara had issued for the call that night. [500] Mara understood before reason could catch her, because only one recipient had been traced to this hour and this place. [520]

She knelt in the wet leaves and assured him, 'Stay with us, because someone has waited three years to hear you breathe.' [542] His eyes opened briefly, and his fingers found the photograph that had slipped from her folder onto the tiles. [561] He looked at Tomas laughing, then at Iona, and his whole body shook with recognition he had never been given. [581] Mara shouted for the transplant team, gave the protocol code from his sealed tracing file, and warned them that a common sedative could endanger the graft. [607] While nurses lifted him, Iona held his wrist and counted each uneven beat aloud so he would not drift away. [627] Mara assured Iona that names could wait, that rules could bend, and that no one would turn this accident into a debt. [649]

Later a surgeon said the man would have died unseen if another ten minutes had passed behind the fountain. [668] Reporters would call Mara brave, but she knew she had only followed a sound because she could not leave a question alone. [690] At midnight, from his hospital bed, the recipient chose to call, and Iona chose to keep the photograph until he asked for it himself. [714] When his heart sounded through the telephone, neither spoke at first, and the silence protected everything words might have broken. [734] Mara listened from the doorway, an accidental witness, grateful that curiosity had arrived before it was too late. [752]
