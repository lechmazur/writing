<story>Three days after the phoenix rises, the town still smelled of hot ash and oranges. [15] Everyone who had watched it climb from the harbor furnace had fallen asleep where they stood. [31] They did not snore or dream aloud in the streets. [41] They waited, because after such a rising, no sleeper could awaken alone. [53] Each watcher who stayed home received one spare waking, warm as a swallowed coal. [67] That gift could be kept for kin, sold for bread, or traded, and so the age of bartered awakenings began. [87] Joren, a colorblind painter, arrived at the glassblower's courtyard with his spare waking burning under his ribs. [104] To him, rose red and leaf green were the same kind of brown, and ripe fruit hid among leaves. [123] He painted by warmth and weight instead, dark against pale, rough against smooth. [136] He had come not to sell his waking, but to find beauty in imperfection before the brokers priced it away. [156] Sella the glassblower lay asleep beside her cold furnace, her hands still curled around unfinished tongs. [172] Her courtyard held forty sleepers on mats, between pots of ash lilies, reeds, and blackened thyme. [188] The phoenix ash had taken every waking voice, leaving the town to speak without tongues or shouting. [205] So people had begun communicating through plant arrangements, setting sorrow, hunger, warning, and hope in stems. [221] A lily bent left meant no entry, while thyme under a stone meant medicine was needed. [237] Three reeds tied upright meant a family would trade food, labor, or love for one waking. [253] By the gate, seven upright reeds and a cracked pot told Joren that Sella's own kin had nothing left to trade. [274] Joren was innocently cunning, the way children are when they hide medicine in jam without believing it is deceit. [293] He saw brokers circling with ledgers, buying cheap awakenings from the frightened and selling them dear. [309] He also saw Sella's son Perin moving among the mats, rearranging stems whenever a broker looked away. [326] Perin, nine and voiceless, kept turning hopeless arrangements toward hope before despair could be read. [341] Joren knelt and placed a pebble beside a fallen lily, asking in the new language who Sella trusted with music. [361] Perin answered with one bent reed for himself, then pointed at his throat and shook his head slowly. [379] Then Perin dragged a broken concertina from under Sella's mat and laid its loose concertina reed tongue in Joren's palm. [399] The brass tongue was bent at one corner and blackened, too ruined for any musician to keep. [416] When Joren flicked it with a thumbnail, it gave a wheezing note that stumbled, caught, and stumbled again. [434] Sella's curled fingers tightened on her tongs. [441] Joren flicked it again, and a sleeping girl sighed like a kettle nearing song. [455] Perfect glass bells above the furnace, made by Sella, copied the phoenix call note for note. [471] The brokers had rung them for three days over every sleeper, and not one eyelid had moved. [488] Joren looked from the flawless bells to the bent brass, and his painter's eye trusted the flaw. [505] The phoenix had not sung cleanly as it rose, but cracked like green wood in fire. [521] Its beauty lived in that break, where failure carried farther than perfection. [533] A broker in a yellow coat, which Joren saw only as pale dust, stopped beside Sella's mat. [550] Through Perin's quick hands among the pots, the broker offered three loaves and a coin for Joren's waking. [568] Joren smiled with perfect innocence and set four upright reeds, a rose, and a split stone in a row. [587] The crowd read it at once: his waking for Sella, and Sella's first waking work for everyone, not for sale. [607] The broker laughed, thinking of flawless bells sold by the dozen, and pressed his seal into clay. [624] Joren knelt, laid his warm palm on Sella's brow, and gave her the waking meant to keep him safe. [643] Heat left his chest in one slow breath, and the courtyard tilted as sleep came hunting him. [660] With thickening hands he set the bent reed tongue between Sella's waking fingers and pointed at her perfect bells. [679] Sella understood, because glassblowers know a bubble can weaken a bowl or teach it to hold light. [696] She took one bell, cracked its rim with her tongs, fitted the reed inside, and swung it over Joren. [715] The flawed note stumbled through glass, bright and broken, and Joren awoke gasping as if hauled from deep water. [734] As sleepers rose around him, Joren painted them not in colors he lacked, but in glorious, imperfect waking light. [753]</story>
