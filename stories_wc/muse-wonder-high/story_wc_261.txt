Mara wakes in the quiet tower at dawn after the anesthesia wears off, and the world lies in a meteor hush. [21] No bells ring below, no gulls cry outside, and even the machines beside her bed seem to wait before daring to beep again. [44] On the table stands a frosted hourglass, its glass clouded by cold, its sand stuck halfway as if time had stopped to watch her survive. [69] She should not have survived, and she knows it before anyone tells her. [82] The surgeon had given her brother Tomas those exact odds in the hall, one chance in fifty, and Tomas had signed the consent form because she could not hold a pen. [113] For three years Mara has chased closure the wrong way, by opening wounds and demanding that they explain themselves. [132] When their mother died in this same tower, she had picked at that grief until it bled, then wondered why nothing ever healed. [155] She came to the tower clinic to learn to read the stories in scars, her own and other people's, believing each raised line was a sentence she could finally force to end. [187] Now her chest carries a fresh scar under white bandages, curved like a hook, and her fingers move toward it before her mind wakes fully. [212] That is when the ivy on the sill speaks, not in words at first, but in a green pressure behind her eyes. [234] She understands, suddenly and accidentally, that she is telepathic with plants, and the understanding arrives with the taste of soil and rain. [256] The ivy does not congratulate her, it grieves, curling one leaf toward the floor below where a boy named Ansel still lies between heartbeats. [280] Through leaf and root she sees last night plainly, the meteor crossing white and silent, and a miracle dropping toward Ansel like slow lightning. [304] Her failing heart had risen in the same moment, loud in the wrong room, and the miracle had turned aside and entered her instead. [328] It was meant to close the hole in Ansel's heart, the ivy tells her, but it sealed hers and left his open. [350] Mara presses her bandage and feels the stolen strength move there, warm and orderly, while below a child thins toward morning. [371] She swings her legs down, dizzy, and finds Tomas asleep at the small table, his head beside three cups and her chart. [393] By examining coffee rings on the chart and on the tower plan beneath it, she reads what fear made everyone too polite to say. [417] The darkest ring circles her midnight dose, doubled where the nurse hesitated, and another ring marks the old root cellar hatch under the pantry floor. [442] During the meteor hush the outer stair had cracked and settled, sealing the upper ward, but roots remember passages that people forget. [464] The ivy shows her roots tunneling through mortar and dark soil, each root scarred where stone cut it, each scar pointing down. [486] Mara understands that a scar is not an ending but a direction, and for the first time she stops pulling at her bandage. [509] She takes the frosted hourglass because its stuck sand tells the truth her pulse will not, that borrowed time does not flow until it is returned. [535] In the pantry she lifts the hatch where the coffee ring promised, and cold air rises smelling of earth, stone, and crushed leaves. [558] The passage is too narrow for Tomas, who has followed her barefoot and frightened, so she kisses his scarred knuckles and goes alone. [581] She must tunnel where the cellar roof has fallen, moving earth by handfuls, following the ivy roots that think toward Ansel in pictures of light. [606] Her bandage darkens, her breath shortens, and every plant in the buried dark leans its small mind toward her hands. [626] Do not dig for an ending, they tell her, dig for the place where the wound can give something back. [646] She tunnels past broken bottles and old roots thick as wrists, reading their rings and scars like braille, until wood gives way to the lower ward door. [673] Ansel lies gray under a lamp, his mother asleep with one hand on his ankle, as if love could hold a soul by its smallest bone. [699] Mara kneels, opens her bandage with steady fingers, and presses her warm scar to the scar the surgeons left on his thin chest. [722] This time she does not open a wound to interrogate it, she opens it to release what was never hers to keep. [744] Heat leaves her in a hush deeper than silence, and Ansel breathes, once, twice, then with the outraged strength of a boy returned to his life. [770] Beside them the frosted hourglass clears in the first sun, its sand running again, while Mara finally lets her own wound close. [792]
