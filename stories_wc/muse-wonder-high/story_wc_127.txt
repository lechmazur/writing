Mara Vane was the most professionally thorough counterfeit emotion dealer in the harbor city, and she kept records of every lie she sold. [23]
She bottled joy that had never been felt, grief borrowed from actors, and courage distilled from soldiers who had only pretended to be brave. [47]
Each vial came with a certificate, a warning, and a small discount for customers who admitted they knew the feeling was false. [69]
That honesty was her favorite half truth, because wanting to be fooled is itself a real emotion. [86]
On the last evening of October, a boy named Tomas arrived at her shop with wet shoes and no money. [106]
He said his sister Lina was changing in the amber grotto at dusk, and she no longer answered to her name. [127]
"I need the feeling that will bring her back," he said. [138]
Mara, who was twelve years older and tired of heroes, told him she did not sell rescue. [155]
She said this with affectionate detachment, her tone for anyone about to make a sincere mistake. [171]
Still, she locked the shop, filed his request under hopeless cases, and followed him into rainy streets. [188]
In her case she carried one genuine article, a fragrance chemist's blotter petal Lina had made when they still shared a workshop and a bed. [213]
The petal was paper shaped like a rose petal, but it drank scent the way skin drinks warmth, and it never forgot what it touched. [238]
The grotto lay below the old perfume works, where amber resin sweated from the walls and pooled in clear, slow veins. [259]
At dusk the resin caught the last light and held it, so the cave glowed as if evening had been stored there for winter. [283]
Lina stood in the shallow pool with resin up to her ankles, calmly letting it climb. [299]
Her left hand had already turned translucent, and through it Mara could see small bones lit gold like insects in a paperweight. [321]
Lina smiled when she saw them, and the smile was exactly as Mara remembered, which made it harder to trust. [341]
"You brought a customer," Lina said. [347]
"I brought his sister a chance to explain herself," Mara said. [358]
Tomas begged Lina to come home, promised to sell his boat, promised to be good, promised all the clean simple bargains of the frightened. [382]
Lina explained, patiently, that the change was not an accident and not a curse, but a treatment she had chosen before her lungs began to fail. [408]
The resin would preserve her nerves, her scent, and the electrical weather of her thoughts, but it would also subsume her voice, her hunger, and her name. [435]
That was the other half truth at the center of the night, because preservation and loss can wear the same kind face. [457]
Mara opened her case and laid out twelve empty vials, labels, gloves, and a pencil sharpened at both ends. [476]
If she worked, she would work properly, even here, even with her hands shaking. [490]
For years she had used insight like a locksmith's picks, but tonight she wanted to fuse insight with empathy and hold a person without owning him. [516]
She began by sensing through static electricity, brushing the blotter petal lightly across Lina's wrist where the resin made the fine hairs rise and crackle. [541]
Each tiny discharge carried a scent to the petal: salt for fear, crushed iris for tenderness, hot metal for the pain Lina kept politely hidden. [566]
Mara wrote each one down, because memory, like grief, becomes a counterfeit when nobody bothers to check it. [584]
Then the resin spoke through Lina's mouth in her own warm voice and promised Tomas that nothing would change if he only stepped closer. [608]
It was a beautiful lie built from half truths, for Lina would stay visible and near, yet never again answer his hunger or loneliness. [632]
Mara lifted the petal between them, and where Lina's true feelings had made it spark and darken, the resin's words left it dry, pale, and silent. [658]
Mara pulled Tomas back by the collar, gently, before his shoes touched the resin. [672]
"Take the tenderness," Lina whispered as the resin reached her throat. [683]
"As the transformation completes, let it stay loose in the world, not sealed inside me." [698]
Mara pressed the blotter petal to Lina's heart, felt the last bright crackle pass into it, and sealed the tenderness in a vial. [721]
On the label she wrote, Not her, but true, and gave the vial to Tomas, who wept without stepping forward. [741]
Then Lina closed her eyes, and the amber took her face as gently as a hand taking a lamp at bedtime. [762]
