Ilen had been the harmonic stone skimmer of the canyon for forty years, and she had not taken a step beyond her ledger stone in the last ten of them. [30] The towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes rose on every side, rib and spine and skull locked together by hands that had been dust long before her grandmother was born. [63] She was historically reverent in the way tired sailors are careful with a frayed rope, not from softness, but because she knew what would be lost when it snapped. [92] Through centuries of layered dust, the canyon kept its dead in order, each pale stratum a year, each dark line a flood, each handprint a name she read with her fingertips before she sat. [126]

She did not skim for sport. [132] A flat stone struck right rang one clean note, bounced, rang another, and sent its voice out between the towers to learn the shape of every hidden ledge and fallen wall. [163] If she listened long enough, the echoes came home changed, and she could draw maps for caravans she would never follow. [184] That was her vow and her work, to learn to navigate without moving, because the canyon punished feet that wandered without knowledge. [206] Her own left leg, crooked since a rockfall in her youth, had taught her the price early. [223]

On the morning the trader came, a pulse left wandering by her grandmother still moved somewhere in the deep branches of the canyon, sixty years after the stone that made it had sunk and gone silent. [259] It returned some dawns as a low note under all the others, patient and familiar, proof that a sound could keep traveling after its maker had stopped. [286] The trader climbed to her ledger stone with dust on his boots and a wrapped bundle under one arm, careful not to meet her eyes too soon. [313] He said his name was Corv, that he had bought the bundle from a digger near the south mouth, and that three collectors would pay a fortune if the thing inside was true. [346] Inside the cloth lay a stone arrowhead, black as wet ash, edged so fine that the morning light seemed to bend rather than break along its sides. [373]

He asked her to appraise it. [379] Not to price it, he said quickly, though his fingers tightened on the cloth, but to say whether the ancestors had made it or whether some living hand had faked it for gold. [412] Ilen did not touch it at first. [419] A reverent appraisal began with refusal, because old stone remembered every careless hand, and hers were rough from work. [438] She set three flat skimming stones beside the arrowhead, smallest to largest, and waited for the canyon swifts to rise. [458] Corv frowned and asked why a bird should know more than a lens or a scale. [474] She told him a lens saw a surface, a scale saw a weight, but the canyon itself could see a history if it was asked in its own language. [503]

When the swifts burst from the bone towers in a single dark ribbon, she skimmed the first stone low across the dry basin. [526] It rang, skipped, rang again, and its echoes split around the towers while the flock turned as one body above the sound. [548] She watched the appraisal through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight, where each gap and fold marked a returning echo too faint for human ears to separate. [578] The second stone sang higher, and the swifts climbed with it, opening a bright hole in their ranks above the eastern tower. [600] Then she lifted the arrowhead at last, held it by its blunt base between thumb and forefinger, and let the third stone strike the basin and send its note past the black edge. [633] The flock broke. [636] Half the birds sheared left toward the old hunting terraces, and half dropped straight down along a line no living eye could see, tracing a path the canyon had kept since before the dust had names. [672] Under the returning notes, faint but exact, her grandmother's pulse answered from the deep branches, and the arrowhead hummed against her skin in the same key. [698]

It was true, and it was not a weapon. [707] The ancestors had shaped it as a pointer for skimmers, tuned to catch a wandering pulse and show which canyon branch was calling it home. [732] With weathered resolve, she told Corv it would stay here, returned to its layer, where no collector could buy the dead out of their road. [757] Then she sat still, followed the joined echoes home in her mind, and marked for Corv a safe southern road in charcoal. [779]
