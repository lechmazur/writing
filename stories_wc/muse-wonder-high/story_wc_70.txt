<story>Three days after the city-wide blackout, the light at dusk still smelled of nectar and hot iron. [17] Mira Sol kept the toll gate at the foot of the lightning collection tower, where the road narrowed to a bridge over the orchard gorge. [42] She had held the post for eleven years, collecting copper coins, jars of honey, and sometimes a song from climbers who had nothing else. [66] Since a stray arc struck her at sixteen, Mira was involuntarily truthful, unable to let even a kind lie leave her mouth. [88] If a child asked whether the climb was easy, she said no, her legs ached daily, but the view made brave liars of poets. [112] That bluntness had cost her friends and once a husband, yet it made her the only toll keeper the farmers wholly trusted. [134] Her deeper reason for staying was simpler and, because she could not hide it, she said it aloud each morning while opening the gate. [158] She stayed to preserve wonder in routine, turning each toll into a small ceremony rather than a tax. [176] She stamped receipts with a bee, warmed coins in her palm before returning change, and asked every traveler for one true thing they had seen that day. [203] On the tower roof above her, the drone beehive had failed at noon, and ten thousand brass bees hung silent in their magnetic frames. [227] They had pollinated the rooftop orchards and combed pollen from the tower rods, guided by a hive mind that drank lightning and sang through the rails. [253] When the grid collapsed in the storm, the collectors drank too much sky and threw the city into darkness to save themselves. [275] Now engineers called the hive dead and feared the orchards would follow within a week. [290] At closing time, Chief Engineer Daro climbed the bridge with a cutter crew and a permit to melt the drones for replacement parts. [313] He set the permit on her lacquer box, the red toll box her mother had painted with gold bees and a black storm cloud. [337] Mira tried to say the required words, that the toll was waived for city business, but the lie tightened her throat until she coughed. [361] "The toll is not waived," she blurted, hating and trusting her own voice at once. [376] I am afraid that if you melt them today, we will lose something we do not know how to rebuild. [396] Daro stared, then paid with the only coin in his pocket and one true thing, that he had wept when the drones fell silent. [420] The coin rang strangely in the lacquer box, striking something that had not been there at dawn. [437] Mira lifted the false bottom her mother had never mentioned and found a matchbox containing a cryptic riddle in ink so fresh it shone. [461] No one had crossed since noon except wind, dust, and a single living bee that had bumped her window and flown toward the tower cellar. [486] She opened the matchbox with shaking fingers and read the riddle aloud because silence felt like another kind of lie. [506] "I drink no flower, I keep its fire, I sleep below the dead choir, turn the wheel where first bees dream, and wake me only if you mean it." [535] Daro went still, for choir was what his crew called the humming frames, and first bees meant the original living hive kept under the tower. [560] Mira understood then that the hive mind had not died in the blackout but had hidden itself where its makers would look last. [583] Before the surge peaked, it had used a maintenance drone to slip the matchbox into her trusted box and sent a living bee as its witness. [609] That was the reveal she had missed all afternoon, not a failure but a retreat, the hive folding its mind into the warm dark below its children. [636] They carried the lacquer box down the cellar stairs, past sleeping batteries, to a cedar door that smelled of wax and summer rain. [659] Behind it, living bees moved slowly over golden comb while a brass wheel waited beside a core no larger than Mira's heart. [681] Daro warned that turning it could kill them, and Mira answered truthfully that she was terrified but would not let grief be solved by melting. [706] Together they turned the wheel, meaning it. [713] Light ran up the tower in amber veins, and above them the dead choir answered note by note until every brass bee lifted into nectar dusk. [739] At the gate, Mira returned Daro's coin and asked the waking city for one true thing, and somewhere below, real bees kept dreaming. [762]</story>
