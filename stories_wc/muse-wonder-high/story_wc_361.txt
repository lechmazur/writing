<story>Wren was a jittery relic courier, and her hands shook even before the cold found them on the aurora-lit ridgeway. [20] Every dusk she crossed the high stone road between Lorn and Sable, carrying saints' bones, sealed letters, and broken tools in an oilcloth pack. [44] The work was safe, paid, and so routine that she knew which flagstone would wobble before her boot touched it. [64] She hated that she knew it, because she had taken the route to coax wonder from the routine, not to be buried by it. [88] Her mentor Tam had promised her the ridgeway would teach her something new each crossing, if she learned to linger. [108] Then last winter Tam walked into a white squall with a mailbag and never walked out, and no body was ever found. [130] His absence sat in every familiar place: his chipped mug at the waystation, his route notes in the margin of her map, his laugh missing from the wind. [158] All he left her was the dragonfly-wing pendant, a real wing sealed in amber, veined with green and gold. [177] Tam had worn it for forty years and claimed it extends brief encounters, though Wren thought that was only his excuse for talking too long. [202] On her first solo crossing, a shepherd nodded at the summit shrine and should have been gone in ten steps, yet found himself telling her about his newborn daughter while the aurora moved overhead and the moment stretched like warm honey. [243] Afterward her pendant felt warm, and her shaking hands had steadied around its amber edge without her noticing. [261] She began to test it. [266] At each shrine she would pause, let the pendant swing free, and ask whoever passed the question Tam used to ask her when she was small and afraid of the road. [297] "What surprised you today?" [301] Most people meant to answer shortly and leave, but the wing caught the aurora and threw green light across their faces, and they stayed. [325] A miller described ice singing under his wheel. [333] A soldier admitted she rehearsed apologies to her dead brother while she marched. [346] A child swore a fox had bowed to her, and Wren, jittery with cold and delight, believed her completely. [365] Each confession did what Tam's stories once did for Wren: it made a stranger briefly into company, and company into a reason not to hurry into the dark. [393] In spring the abbess at Lorn gave her the last relic of the season, a pouch of black seeds saved from the orchard that burned before Wren was born. [422] "Plant them beyond Sable if they quicken," she said, "but Tam never told us how he made the old ones germinate." [443] For three crossings the seeds lay silent against Wren's ribs, beside the pendant, while rain polished the ridgeway and nobody stopped for long in the wet. [469] Wren grew frantic, shook the pouch while walking, warmed it in her palms, and whispered Tam's name until it sounded like an accusation. [492] On the fourth night the aurora came early, green rivers opening above the ridge, and the pendant began to tremble against her chest though no wind touched it. [520] At the summit shrine an old goatherd tried to pass with only a nod, but the extended moment caught them both, and he asked why she was crying over a bag of ash. [553] So she told him everything: Tam gone, the seeds dead, her fear that she had kept the route but lost its meaning. [575] The goatherd listened, then laughed with sudden wonder and said Tam had once fished his lost wedding ring from a crevice with a hooked stick and carried it home for him. [606] As he spoke, the pendant answered his voice by vibrating sympathetically, its amber humming in time with his laugh and with the low crackle of the aurora above them. [635] Against her ribs the pouch stirred, and one black seed split with a sound so small she felt it in her teeth. [657] By dawn, held in that unhurried encounter, every seed had begun to germinate, green threads pressing through the pouch like stitches mending cloth. [680] Wren understood: Tam had left her no technique, only company, because seeds and people quicken the same way, when someone stays long enough for hidden life to answer life aloud. [710] She planted them beyond Sable with the goatherd's help, and that summer she kept asking her question at every shrine, not from duty but because each answer fed the ridge another small astonishment. [743] Years thinned into decades, and Tam passed from a man Wren mourned into a tale, then a myth whispered to new couriers. [765] By then the orchard bloomed under the aurora, and brief crossings lingered long enough to bloom too. [782]</story>
