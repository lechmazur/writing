Milo Wren had been the corn maze architect's lost child for exactly one night, and it had lasted ten years. [20]
He was eight when he slipped past his father in the October maze and chose the wrong green corridor. [39]

He felt nothing at the terrace rail, where a fall would kill him cleanly and without ceremony. [278]
The difference troubled him more than fear, because fear at least had a shape he could name. [295]

Milo opened it on the shelter table, where the pages smelled of rain, pencil lead, and old heat. [338]

"Stay high," she had written, because love from too close can erase the trail. [401]
