On the night the full moon should have risen, Sera climbed to the storm-worn windblown parapet in mist that neither army claimed. [22]
She was a prisoner exchange negotiator, armed only with names, and this cracked walk above the river was neutral territory, marked by a white line across its middle stones. [51]
After forged written dates caused an ambush, the two kingdoms stopped trusting calendars and began synchronizing through moon phases, which no clerk could alter and no general could advance. [80]
At the new moon they exchanged prisoner lists, at the first quarter they confirmed the living, and at the full moon they met here to make the trade. [108]
In Sera's pocket lay an antique drawer pull of green brass, a lion holding a ring in its teeth, kept from the archive that burned during her first exchange. [137]
That night she had trusted the paper date, confused Tomas Hale with Tomas Hael, and sent Hale home while Hael stayed in a cell that flooded before morning. [165]
Everyone believed Hael drowned, and Sera unscrewed the pull from the blackened archive cabinet as a reminder she could neither misplace nor explain away. [189]
Determined to recycle mistakes into wisdom, and uncommonly thoughtful by nature, she now required each prisoner to spell their name, show a scar, and name one useless thing they loved. [219]
Tonight's trade fell between apology and forgiveness, after the northern king admitted his men burned the mill town but before the southern queen could forgive the burning. [246]
The apology had halted executions, yet without forgiveness neither side would grant an extra name, an extra hour, or an extra kindness. [268]
Through the mist, Captain Rook waited across the white line with nine prisoners for the north, all tethered by one yellow rope, while nine prisoners for the south stood tethered behind Sera. [300]
Rook read the confirmed names slowly, and at each one Sera pressed the drawer pull until the lion's ring marked her palm and a living voice answered. [327]
When the eighth man called himself Tomas Hael, though the list named a fevered captain, Sera raised her hand and the parapet fell into a furrowed hush. [354]
Rook said the listed captain had died at dawn, and after the king's apology the queen ordered every southern cell opened, seeking proof that his remorse deserved an answer. [383]
In the lowest cell her guards found Hael alive, forgotten since the flood, thin as winter grass and furious that the world had spelled him dead. [409]
A substitute could be a spy, a trick, or an unexpected mercy, and in the present mist all three wore the same gray face. [433]
Once Sera would have obeyed the list or obeyed her guilt, but both had already buried enough men, so she set the pull on the white line and demanded her three proofs. [465]
Hael spelled his name without the clerk's extra letter, bared a crescent scar on his chin, and said he loved sour green plums because they made his eyes water like his mother's scolding. [498]
His mother had given Sera those same details three winters ago, after the flood, while begging her not to turn a spelling error into a grave. [524]
Behind her the northern general objected that Hael had not been confirmed at the first quarter, and Rook answered that death had not been confirmed either, only assumed by frightened clerks. [555]
Sera looked from the dead captain's empty place to the living man before her and chose the evidence of breath over the comfort of a perfect list. [582]
Still no one moved, because mist hid the full moon, and neither side would begin until both armies saw the same phase shine at the same moment. [609]
While they waited, Sera passed her water flask across the line to Hael first, knowing thirst makes prisoners reckless and reckless men fall from high, wet stone. [636]
Then wind stripped the mist from the parapet, the full moon appeared round and unarguable above both towers, and Sera and Rook lifted their hands together. [662]
At that signal Sera stepped to her iron anchor ring to untether the southern prisoners, while Rook untethered the northern prisoners, and eighteen bound people began crossing in careful single file. [693]
Hael crossed last and paused at the white line, where Sera untethered the yellow rope from his wrist and felt his pulse hammering under her thumbs. [719]
He picked up the antique drawer pull and offered it back, but Sera left it on the white line, where the next negotiator would find a handle for the hardest lesson. [750]
The prisoners went down toward lamps and waiting mothers, while the apology remained unfinished and forgiveness remained possible, which was enough ground for peace to stand on for one more phase. [781]
