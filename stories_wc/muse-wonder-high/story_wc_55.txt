Tomas had been the abbey's introverted clock scribe for twenty three years, and he still ate lunch alone behind the tower stairs. [22]
His work was not to build clocks but to record them, one line for every hour they kept or lost. [42]
In ruled ledgers he noted which bell lagged in frost, which gear sang in heat, and which pendulum dreamed of stopping. [63]
The monks valued his accuracy and excused his silence, because a man who listened so well rarely needed to speak. [83]
Only Tomas knew the ledgers were also a hiding place. [93]
At seventeen he had wanted to compose music for bells, not merely count their failures. [108]
He had filled a cheap notebook with tunes for dawn and rain and funerals, each one timed to the abbey chimes. [129]
Then his mother died on a winter morning when the great clock stopped, and no bell marked her going. [148]
After that, every melody he wrote sounded like an apology he could not bear to finish. [164]
So he abandoned the notebook, took the scribe's oath, and promised himself that counting time was safer than shaping it. [184]
For twenty three years the promise held, because routine can bear a grief that honesty cannot. [200]
It broke on the morning the abbey woke to find its courtyard transformed. [213]
Rain had fallen all night without thunder, washing soot from the roofs and waking the smell of stone and earth. [233]
In the hush of a hidden sunrise, with cloud still covering the east, Tomas crossed to the tower and stopped at the crystal whispering forecourt after rain. [260]
The old forecourt was paved with pale quartz set between flagstones, and after rain each seam held water that caught light and seemed to whisper as it drained. [288]
The abbey around it had become a soundless sanctuary, for the monks were at prayer behind closed doors and even the rooks had not yet woken. [314]
Above that quiet lay a deeper one Tomas felt in his teeth and ribs, a glacial hush that slowed his breath until his heartbeat sounded presumptuous. [340]
He understood suddenly that the hush was not empty but full, like a glacier holding whole summers locked and shining inside its patience. [363]
Something in that patience accused him, because he had called his own frozen years faithful when they were only afraid. [383]
From the forecourt he could hear the tower clock prepare to strike six, its gears gathering themselves with familiar iron reluctance. [404]
Then, between one tick and the next, the whispering water found a rhythm his notebook would have loved. [422]
Three soft notes fell from the eaves, one low note rose from the drain, and the quartz seams answered with a bright scatter like fingers on glass. [449]
It was the unfinished tune for his mother, or near enough that his body knew it before his mind dared to. [470]
He had written it to make peace with an abandoned dream he named only in ledgers, where in the margin for the stopped hour he had drawn a single staff and left it blank. [504]
His hands shook, not from cold, but from the old wish rising with petrichor nostalgia so sharp it felt like youth returning to claim a debt. [530]
He wanted to flee to his ledger and record that the clock had lost four seconds, as if numbers could bear him back to safety. [555]
Instead he stayed, because the glacial hush would not let him hurry, and hurry had been his true hiding place all along. [577]
When the clock struck, its bell was excruciatingly gentle, muffled by wet air until the note entered him without wounding the silence around it. [601]
Tomas wept then, quietly and without shame, for his mother, for the boy who had buried his music, and for every hour he had counted instead of lived. [629]
When the monks emerged, he did not explain, but asked Brother Anselm if the small hand bell still hung in the chapel porch. [652]
It did, green with age, and Tomas carried it to the forecourt as carefully as a man might bear a sleeping child. [674]
There, while the quartz still whispered and the hidden sun warmed the clouds, he rang the lost tune once, badly, and then again with growing certainty. [700]
Afterward he returned to his desk, entered the hour as true, and beneath it wrote not a correction but the notes themselves, finally able to bear an ending that did not erase the beginning. [734]
