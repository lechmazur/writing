<story>The wrecking crew had cut their engines, and in the hush just before the condemned building was demolished, no one in the yard dared to cough. [26] Inside the hall, inside the great oak it had been built to shelter, portrait artist Jonah Vale stood alone with his brushes and did not move. [52] Dust hung without falling. [56] A bell rope waited without ringing. [62] Even the pigeons on the broken roof had gathered their wings close and gone watchful and still. [79] Centuries ago, a scholars guild had hollowed the living trunk into a library, carving shelves, stairs, and a round reading room into wood that still grew around them. [107] The hall around the oak was condemned and would fall at eight, taking its roof, its annex, and, the crew believed, the tree with it. [132] Jonah had been given one last hour by the foreman, out of pity, to remove his unfinished work. [150] He had not come for the canvases stacked by the door. [161] He had come for the secret book safe that Guildmaster Sera Lonn had shown him once, behind a false spine titled On Limits. [184] Jonah was face blind, and though Sera had sat for him eleven times, he could not have picked her face from a crowd of two. [209] He knew her by the green coat she always wore, by her ink blackened thumb, and by the way she knocked twice before speaking. [233] For her portrait he had relied on measurements, on notes pinned to studies, and on a patience so fractally precise that every eyelash contained smaller lines faithfully branching within it. [263] Strangers called the result uncanny, because they recognized Sera at once in details Jonah himself could assemble but never grasp as a whole. [286] Above his easel, the domed heart of the trunk held the other work Sera had commissioned, his attempt to mirror the sky inside walls for apprentices sealed indoors through winter. [316] He had painted dawn there in bands of blue, with cloud edges edged again in finer cloud, so a reader lifting tired eyes would feel weather rather than timber. [345] Both portrait and sky remained unfinished, and Sera had died in spring without telling him how the safe could be opened. [366] Then, while lifting his paint box, he heard the small false note he had ignored for months, a coin sliding under the tray. [389] It was a centuries-old guild coin, soft edged and heavy, bearing a first guildmaster whose profile had worn almost smooth. [409] Jonah turned it once, then again, and stopped when his thumbnail caught on faint indentations cut in a pattern along the rim. [431] They were not damage. [435] Each notch held finer notches, depth within depth, the same branching care Sera used when she copied a charter by hand. [456] On the morning of her last sitting, she had pressed this coin into his palm and told him that limits were a kind of key. [481] He had smiled and forgotten, because her words had been attached to a face that his mind could not keep. [501] Now, kneeling on the library floor, Jonah used the faint indentations on the centuries-old coin as a template and began to craft a narrow brass key. [527] A drawer beneath the shelves held brass key blanks and files left by the guild locksmith. [543] He filed, paused, felt the coin, and filed again, copying notch and sub-notch until the new teeth matched the pattern his thumb had memorized. [567] Outside, a crane cable ticked once against its hook and then, as if corrected, was silent. [583] The key turned in the false spine with a sound no louder than a page settling. [599] Inside the secret book safe lay no money, only the guild charter and Sera's final diary. [615] Her first page told him not to mourn the face he could not summon, because she had written herself down in measurements for the portrait and in boundaries for the tree. [646] The charter map was fractally precise, each inset magnifying the property line until one final inset showed the oak trunk standing three feet outside the condemned hall and outside the demolition grant. [678] The limit he had feared was real, but it did not run where everyone had assumed. [694] Jonah carried the charter through the still yard and laid it on the foreman's dented hood without raising his voice. [714] The foreman read, signaled once, and every machine that had waited to strike the tree backed away from the chalk line. [735] At eight the hall fell beyond the line, and dust above the oak briefly made a second, truer sky. [754] Inside again, Jonah crafted the portrait's final lines from her measurements, content to keep Sera in precise details rather than a grasped whole. [777]</story>
