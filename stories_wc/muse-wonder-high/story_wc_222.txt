Mara had been an apprentice of wonder for seven years, and she had never once seen the sun rise in the east. [22]
In the icebound spire above the clouds, where she swept observatories and fed clockwork doves, dawn arrived whenever it pleased. [42]
That winter it arrived at midnight, spilling gold across the snow while stars still burned overhead. [58]
Her master called that season the time when the sky is wrong, and he said it with grief, not fear. [78]
Master Ansel had built the spire to read the heavens and gently correct them when they drifted. [95]
His instrument was a pocket sundial whose brass lid held the one true map of the turning stars. [113]
Ten years before Mara came, a thief had lifted that lid from Ansel's coat during a single game of chess. [133]
He had left coin for the poor, a rose for the master, and an apology in flawless verse. [151]
Because of those courtesies, and because he stole only from hoarders of miracles, the valleys named him the chivalrous thief. [171]
Ansel never cursed him, for the old man understood why Cassian had taken the lid. [186]
A duke below had meant to use its map to chain dawn itself and tax every sunrise. [203]
Cassian stole it first, and in saving the valleys he unknowingly loosened the hinge of the sky. [220]
Now the hinge had slipped enough that winter noon glowed black and summer midnight burned white. [236]
Mara found Cassian at dawn that was not dawn, clinging to the outer stair with frost in his beard. [255]
He had climbed through cloud and storm with cautious abandon, testing each icy step yet never turning back. [273]
In his open palm lay the pocket sundial lid, bright as the day he stole it. [289]
"I came to amend what my mercy broke," he said, and bowed despite the wind tearing at his cloak. [308]
Mara should have called the wardens, but wonder had taught her to listen before she judged. [324]
"Ansel died in spring," she told him, "and he left the mending of the sky to me." [341]
Above them the observatory door stood sealed, guarded by a stone saint who permitted prayer and games but forbade force. [361]
Ansel, who believed reverence without laughter was only fear in fine clothes, had made his saint sacredly playful. [379]
Each evening Mara played chess with the saint, and each evening the saint cheated by blessing her worst moves. [398]
The board held Ansel's hand-carved chess pieces, and inside each hollow piece slept a spoken secret of the sky. [417]
Cassian smiled when he saw them, because swapping hand-carved chess pieces with secrets inside had once been his favorite trick. [437]
As a young thief he would carve a twin, hide a stolen whisper within, and exchange it during play. [456]
No lock was broken, no guard was harmed, and no opponent noticed until the piece grew too heavy with truth. [476]
Now, he said, the same trick could carry the lid past the saint, hidden where only a game might go. [496]
Mara opened the white queen and found she could fit the lid inside its hollow crown. [512]
They would swap the light queen for the heavy one as the saint pondered, amending theft into restitution through play. [532]
Mara feared what waited beyond the door, for Ansel had never told her what corrected the heavens. [549]
Cassian offered no map and no promise, only his hand and the truth that some doors open inwardly. [567]
Her calling, Ansel had always said, was not to master the unknown but to embrace the unknown with open arms. [587]
That night Mara set the board before the saint while Cassian, pardoned by her authority, sat veiled beside her. [606]
She played boldly and badly at first, sacrificing knights to make the saint lean close in delighted correction. [624]
When stone fingers lifted to bless her folly, Cassian swapped the queens with a thief's tenderness and a penitent's care. [644]
The saint paused, weighed the heavy queen in one palm, and laughed like bells under ice. [660]
Instead of wrath, the statue moved the queen herself and set her upon the final square. [676]
The board clicked, the lid sang within its wooden crown, and the observatory door opened on warm starlight. [694]
Inside the queen, the lid turned itself toward home, and every hidden secret rose from the pieces in soft voices. [714]
They named the true dawn together, not commanding it, and Mara stepped onto the threshold with open arms. [732]
Light came, cautious at first and then abandoned to joy, pouring eastward over clouds and waking valleys. [749]
Mara kept the empty queen on her windowsill afterward, a sacredly playful reminder that wonder begins where certainty ends. [768]
