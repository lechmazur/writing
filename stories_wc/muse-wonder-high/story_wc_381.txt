For nine years, Neris had lived as a solitary summoner in a nocturnal haven beside a quiet brook, summoning nothing louder than moths. [23] She had chosen exile after her teacher died, because every voice in the village asked her for wonders she no longer trusted herself to control. [48] Her cottage smelled of clay, ash, and dried mint, and its single hearth burned each night with a tranquil blaze that never spat or smoked. [73] Outside, water slipped over black stones, carrying leaves, moonlight, and the soft confessions people brought after dark. [90] It was the week amid whispered secrets, when custom freed tongues that shame kept bound all year. [107] Farmers whispered debts into reeds, lovers whispered names into cups of water, and the dying whispered apologies they could not bear to give the living. [132] Neris listened without answering, for a summoner's first duty was to know which sounds wanted help and which wanted only passage. [153] On the third night, a new pattern entered the brook, cold and deliberate, repeating under the honest secrets like a hook under silk. [176] It spoke in code, using heron for king, ash for army, and a drowned bell for the gate between the living and the dead. [200] By dawn she understood enough to feel fear move cleanly through her, bright and steady. [215] It was a prophecy, and if its final line was spoken whole at the brook's mouth, the gate it named would learn to open. [239] Neris had one purpose now, clear as hunger: to hush a prophecy spoken in code before the current carried it to the sleeping village below. [264] Ordinary summoners called beasts, winds, or dead grandmothers, but Neris had trained in the rarer art of calling quiet itself. [284] Her teachers called that quiet a smoothed hush, a silence pressed by patience until it could lie over sound without breaking it. [306] It was terrestrially cosmic, born where river clay touched fallen starlight, and it obeyed only hands that did not grasp. [326] To call it, she could not shout over the prophecy or dam the brook, because forced silence always cracked and let worse noise through. [350] She had to let the hush flow, by mapping silence where it already gathered in pools, under roots, and behind listening stones. [372] At dusk she took her reed mat, a bowl of clay, and the crane's origami wing from the cedar box beside her bed. [395] The wing was all that remained of the first creature she had ever summoned, a paper crane that had flown one autumn night and returned without its other wing. [424] She had kept it not from sentiment alone, but because folded paper remembered air, and air remembered every word it had carried. [446] Kneeling at the bank, she set stones in the shallows wherever a whisper thinned, marking each true pocket of quiet with a white pebble. [470] Where voices thickened, she left the water open, so grief and love could keep their road and not be mistaken for doom. [492] Hour by hour her map grew, not drawn on skin or paper, but known by knee, ankle, and the changing pressure of cold water. [516] Near midnight, the coded prophecy quickened, trading heron for ash, ash for bell, as if something upstream had begun to count aloud. [538] Then a small shape came down the path, barefoot, holding her own secret with both hands as if it might spill. [559] It was Lio, the miller's daughter, who had once left milk for Neris and never asked for magic in return. [579] 'I told the brook my brother is not dead,' Lio whispered, 'and the brook answered with a bell.' [597] Neris felt the final line coiling inside the child's innocent secret, using her hope as its last clean syllable. [616] If she silenced Lio outright, the prophecy might break, but so might the girl's one thread back to her lost brother. [637] If she let the words finish, the gate would hear itself named and begin, patiently, to turn. [654] So Neris did the harder thing her teacher had once praised but never taught: she listened all the way to the edge of the secret. [679] She placed the crane's origami wing on Lio's palm and asked her to breathe across it, once, without saying what she feared. [701] Lio breathed, and the wing shivered, lifting a fraction, carrying her unsaid hope into the map Neris had made of the dark. [723] At that breath, the smoothed hush rose from clay and starlight, not as a wall, but as a current under the current. [745] It flowed from pebble to pebble, gathered the bell note without waking it, and bore it past the sleeping village into the wide, forgetful marsh. [770] Lio still had her hope, the village still slept, and Neris remained alone to listen. [785]
