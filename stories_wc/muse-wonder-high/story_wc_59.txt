Prince Lio returned to the suspended palace anchored to giant stalactites with mud on his boots and ash in his hair. [21]
He was a bedraggled prince by then, not from war but from crawling through service tunnels no royal had entered in a century. [44]
Above the cavern roof, the mechanical world ticked without pause, sorting rain, grinding grain, and stamping identical songs onto tin disks. [65]
Below that roof, his palace hung from iron chains, swaying whenever the great pumps above changed their rhythm. [83]
Lio loved the sway because it proved that even stone and iron could tremble. [97]
On that morning he carried something wrapped in his wet cloak, and his hands shook so hard that the palace bells answered. [119]
His keeper, old Marta, met him at the glass door and saw the shape before she saw his face. [138]
Inside the cloak lay an extinct bird's egg, blue as deep water and warm despite a hundred years in a sealed vault. [160]
Lio had found it beneath the lowest pump, where a cracked stalactite wept mineral water into a nest of copper wire. [181]
The vault door had borne one warning, that no genesis in this world had been perfect. [197]
Marta whispered that the first builders had made a flawed genesis when they taught machines to sing before they taught them to listen. [220]
Because of that error, every machine song repeated one missing note, and every child above grew up humming a silence they could not name. [244]
Lio believed the egg held that note inside a living throat. [255]
It was a foolish belief, and he knew it, which was why he began hypothesizing about everything that could prove him wrong. [277]
Perhaps the warmth was stored heat, perhaps the blue shell was painted, and perhaps the faint pulse was only the palace swaying. [299]
He wrote each doubt on brass tags and tied them around the egg like a cautious crown. [316]
His motive was not power, although the council above would have paid a province for a true bird. [334]
He wanted only to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world, to give its children one sound that no wheel had planned. [355]
So through the discovery he stayed awake, not celebrating, but testing the miracle until wonder became a discipline. [373]
First he assembled a cradle from clock springs, lamp glass, and feathers cut from old ceremonial coats. [390]
Then he assembled a warmer that breathed in slow cycles, because he hypothesized that a shell might trust rhythm more than heat. [412]
Marta assembled the third device, a listening horn, and she did it without smiling because hope had hurt this palace before. [433]
Years earlier, Lio's mother had assembled a garden of wire flowers that bloomed when struck, and the council had melted it for gears. [456]
Lio still kept one petal in his pocket, and its edge had worn his thumb smooth. [472]
For three days the egg did nothing but gather condensation and tick faintly when the pumps changed speed. [490]
Lio hypothesized that the tick was an answer, then that it was mockery, then that it was a heart learning the palace tempo. [513]
He fed his hypotheses into the palace log, page after page, until Marta said his mind was a second machine. [533]
On the fourth day, a crack crossed the shell like lightning crossing a small and private sky. [550]
The sound was bitterly sweet, a bright note breaking under a sour smell of stone, milk, and old rain. [569]
Lio froze with his ear to the horn while the whole suspended palace seemed to lean toward the cradle. [588]
Chains groaned, stalactites dripped faster, and somewhere above a pump missed its beat for the first time in living memory. [608]
Out came a chick with wet gray feathers, one bright eye, and one wing folded wrong against its side. [627]
For a breath Lio grieved, because even resurrection had obeyed the flawed genesis of his world. [643]
Then the chick opened its beak and sang the missing note, but it sang it cracked, trembling, and too short. [663]
It was not the perfect beauty he had assembled in his dreams during his long quaking reverie beside the cradle. [683]
It was better, because the crack in the song left room for an answer. [697]
Above them, one machine repeated the note wrong, then another tried it softer, and a third stopped entirely to listen. [717]
Marta wept while Lio lifted the chick in both hands, feeling its uneven heart argue gently with his own. [736]
He did not announce a triumph to the council, because beauty newly awake was still small enough to be crushed by praise. [758]
Instead he opened the palace windows, set the listening horn toward the shafts, and let the flawed song climb. [777]
