<story>Mara Venn had been a burned spy for eleven days, long enough for her name to travel where her face could not. [22] Her handlers in the capital had cut her loose after a depot camera caught her beside the dead courier she was meant to meet. [46] She did not blame them, but she blamed the new silence in herself, where she once reached for nerve and found it ready. [69] She had come north at the tilt of the earth toward spring, when light lingered longer and snow still held its winter weight. [92] On Zephyr Bluff in mist, the wind learned to move around the pines instead of tearing straight through her coat. [112] The silver hush there suited her, a bright quiet of fog, thaw, and no voice asking her to explain the depot. [133] Below the bluff stood the relay cabin, its broken aerial clicking against a guy wire in every gust. [151] Fixing what was broken was the last order her handler gave before he refused to vouchsafe any promise of extraction. [171] So she repaired in sequence, stove first, then pump, then the shortwave set that coughed static each noon. [189] In her pocket she kept the engraved pillbox taken from the courier's coat, its lid cut with a swallow whose tail pointed left. [212] Inside lay no pills, only two brass keys and oil paper that smelled of machine oil and cloves. [230] One key fit the cabin, but the other fit nothing she found in eleven days of careful searching. [248] The paper bore one line in the courier's hand: leave the second key where unused courage waits. [265] Mara distrusted the riddle, yet she turned it over while she worked, as if it were a third key. [284] Training had made her prudently daring, willing to enter a watched building by its laundry door at noon, never reckless enough to grandstand after dark. [309] On the twelfth morning, the shortwave cleared for nine seconds and gave her Ilya Brandt, who had taught her codes at training school. [332] He spoke no name, only three numbers twice, then breath held too close to the microphone. [348] The numbers named the old snow code, which used smoothed patches in accumulated snow that shade kept firm until melt erased every mark. [371] Short cuts meant consonants, long cuts vowels, and the whole message could vanish under fresh snowfall before any patrol learned to read it. [394] Mara climbed with a cedar spade to the north drift, where hare tracks stopped at the pines and the surface lay otherwise untouched. [417] Below its crust, a gloved finger had written in blocky strokes that the relay was cut, not weather broken, and its cache lay beneath the cabin floor. [444] A second line warned that a border patrol would come at dusk to burn the cabin and call it a stove fire. [466] She read it twice, memorized each angle, then smoothed it away as if clearing a path for wood. [484] Prudently daring, she did not run downhill, though every muscle urged it, but circled the ridge and counted patrol smoke in the valley. [507] She entered from the creek side with her pistol holstered and lifted the floorboard marked by a swallow cut tail left into the grain. [531] The second key turned in a rust filmed lock, opening a cache with a transmitter coil, a forged patrol order, and a list of hidden names. [557] The depot camera, she understood, had been staged to bury those names, and the courier had died carrying proof toward her. [578] Her aerial had not failed by chance; someone had swapped its coil so distress calls bent back on themselves and sounded like weather. [601] Fixing it would expose her, because a true signal would tell her old service and the patrol exactly where she stood. [622] She sat with the coil in her lap while mist pressed the windows and the silver hush thickened around her pulse. [643] Then she knew unused courage had never waited in a grand act, but in this unspent choice to act without any promised reward. [666] She fitted the coil, aligned the aerial by creek wind, and waited for the white hour when patrol radios choked on mist. [688] Then she sent the names three times on the training frequency, vouchsafing the evidence to anyone listening rather than to the service that burned her. [713] Afterward she folded the forged order into the engraved pillbox and carried it up through wet mist to the north drift. [734] There she cut her answer in accumulated snow, relay true, cabin empty by dusk, and set the pillbox at the first stroke for Ilya to find. [760] She left by the creek as spring loosened the drift behind her, and above her the fixed wire sang over Zephyr Bluff. [782]</story>
