In the ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog, Elias Venn worked as a mirror maker in a cottage built from fallen applewood and salvaged stone. [28]
His mirrors never showed a face exactly as it was, but as it remembered being in a dream the night before. [49]
That was why the astronomer came to him upon the shimmer of twilight frost, when the dead trees wore white coats and every breath rang faintly against the cold. [78]
Mara Ilex carried the astronomer's broken sextant wrapped in oilcloth, its brass arc bent and its small mirror clouded since the night her observatory burned. [103]
She did not want it repaired to measure stars, she told him, but to measure the angle by which a dream had escaped her. [127]
For seven nights she had dreamed of glass mountains rising beyond the orchard, their peaks holding a second dawn that never reached the valley. [151]
On the eighth night the dream had not returned when she woke, and she had found frost on her pillow in the shape of a footprint. [177]
Elias understood then that the dream was runaway, loose in the waking world and looking for a body or a place to inhabit. [200]
He agreed to help her, not for coin, but because a loose dream in fog could settle into his mirrors and teach every reflection to wander. [226]
His trade depended on boundaries, silvered glass that said this side is flesh and that side is image, and he intended to keep that law. [251]
Yet Mara asked him to do the opposite for one hour, to practice erasing boundaries until dream, breath, metal, and glass occupied the same thin place. [277]
Elias was firmly grounded in ordinary work, in callused hands and measured cuts and feet planted bare on cold earth even when his customers spoke of spirits. [304]
That steadiness, Mara said, was exactly what the work required, because an ungrounded man would follow the dream and never return to tell where it went. [330]
They set the sextant on the cottage table and Elias cut a new mirror to fit its frame, polishing until his own face vanished and only fog remained. [358]
He explained that a sextant once trusted the horizon to divide sea from sky, so its broken form could now be taught to distrust every division. [384]
To capture a runaway dream, they would not chase it through the orchard, because a chased dream learned roads and doors and became harder to hold. [410]
Instead they would recreate the eighth night exactly, the cold room, the unlit lamp, and Mara waking with her hand reaching for a dawn that was not there. [438]
If the room remembered perfectly, Elias said, the dream would mistake it for its own skin and slip back inside to be caught. [461]
They darkened the cottage, laid the coat where Mara had slept in the dream, and set the repaired sextant aiming through the window toward the invisible peaks. [488]
Then came the method Mara had learned from divers and singers, to align two lives through synchronized breathing until neither could tell whose lungs moved first. [514]
They sat knee to knee with the sextant between them, breathed in for seven counts as frost formed, and breathed out for seven as it melted on the glass. [543]
At first Elias counted his tools and debts to stay himself, but Mara's steady rhythm pulled his breath into hers like tide entering a narrow channel. [569]
The mirror in the sextant cleared, and in it the ruined orchard ceased to end at the cottage wall and instead continued across the table. [594]
Apple branches crossed the ceiling beams, fog moved through Mara's hair without wetting it, and the glass mountains stood in the room with their bases in the hearth. [622]
The dream arrived not as a creature but as light moving backward, pooling on the floor where Mara had slept and rising into the shape of her younger self. [651]
The younger Mara held the second dawn in cupped hands and looked at the older woman with grief, because she had run to find a sky wide enough for both of them. [683]
Elias felt his own reflection loosen in every mirror in the cottage, yet he kept his bare feet flat on the boards and breathed with Mara, firmly grounded while the boundary thinned. [715]
Mara spoke through their shared breath and promised not to cage the dawn, only to visit it and return, and the younger self stepped willingly into the sextant mirror. [744]
Elias turned the brass arc one final degree, and the dream settled there as a bright horizon that would appear whenever Mara raised the instrument at twilight. [771]
Then Elias opened the door, and fog and morning entered together without asking which was which. [787]
