After the egg hatches, Sella does not cheer. [8]
She sits beside the oak acorn cap where it has rested all winter and watches a pale moth loosen itself from the split shell. [32]
The atrium has been sealed since the first ice took the river, and the moth is the only clock her grandmother trusted. [54]
If it lived, the air inside would still remember how to be kind to living things. [70]
Sella is an heirloom seed saver, third in her line, and she is furiously tranquil as she lifts the cap in both hands. [93]
The moth climbs her wrist, dries its wings, and turns toward the faintest warmth beyond the glass. [110]
Above her, the icebound atrium under starlight holds its breath, its roof panes webbed with frost and its rows of sleeping beds dim under snow light. [136]
Outside, the storm that killed the orchards still turns in a slow white spiral over the valley. [153]
Her family did not inherit land, because the land had gone, but a vortex inheritance, the duty to keep one ordered wind alive inside the glass. [179]
Grandmother taught that a vortex is a promise made by motion, a circle that holds difference together without crushing it. [199]
Warm air rose through the beds, cold air fell along the panes, and between them the seeds slept in turning air that never let damp settle. [225]
When the river froze hard enough to crack its banks, Sella had shut the vents and let the vortex slow to a thread. [248]
Tonight that thread is broken. [253]
The fans are still, the beds are out of season with one another, and a spill of beans and squash seeds covers the central floor where a crate fell in the dark. [285]
To reopen the atrium before dawn, she must restart the turning air, and to do that she must first learn what winter has disordered. [309]
She wants this not only for the planting, but to discover the texture of borrowed time, for every seed in her keeping belongs to a future she will not eat from. [340]
A bean is a meal lent backward by grandchildren, a tomato is a summer held in trust, and her hands are only the middle of the loan. [367]
She begins by decreasing entropy, not with a machine, but with attention. [379]
Entropy here is simple and visible, kinds mixed together, labels loosened by damp, warm pockets hiding in cold beds, and rot waiting to become a general rule. [406]
Order is colder and clearer, each kind alone, each packet dry, each bed assigned one depth and one thirst. [425]
She kneels and uses the oak acorn cap as her only measure, because its bowl holds exactly the seeds for one hand span of soil. [450]
Cap by cap, she sorts beans from squash, squash from amaranth, and the good seed from the blackened seed that smells of ash. [473]
Her mother had sorted by song, one verse for each family of plants, and Sella hears the verses now in the order her fingers choose. [498]
She sings none of them aloud, saving her breath to warm nothing, waste nothing, and let the silence keep the cold where it belongs. [522]
The moth follows the small lantern she carries, and wherever its shadow crosses a damp packet, she moves that packet to the dry stones by the stove. [549]
As the floor clears and the beds regain their separate names, the air itself seems to remember the shape of its turning. [571]
She opens one vent a finger width, then another, and listens for the quarrel between warm soil and cold glass to settle into rhythm. [595]
It does, slowly, a leaf lifting here, a label trembling there, until the whole atrium turns around her without touching her. [616]
A wild serenity moves through her then, not peace as stillness, but peace as many living things each allowed their own motion within one shared circle. [642]
Borrowed time, she understands, feels like seed husk under a thumbnail, rough with waiting, thin as paper, and stronger than it looks because it is meant to tear at the right moment. [674]
At last she turns the iron key, sets her shoulder to the frozen door, and begins to reopen it an inch at a time. [698]
Dawn has not come, but starlight thins on the panes, and beyond the opening waits the black valley where meltwater has begun to speak under the ice. [725]
The moth slips through first, Sella follows with the sorted seeds in their packets, and the vortex turns behind her, inherited again by being given away. [751]
