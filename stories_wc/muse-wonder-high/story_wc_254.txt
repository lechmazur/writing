Mira returned alone, after the ban lifts, to the cobalt atrium by a frozen lake. [15] For nine winters, its doors had been chained because the ice sang too loudly beneath the stone. [32] Now the council seal hung broken from the lock, and snow filled the keyhole like salt. [48] Mira was the last cloud-shadow poet in the valley, trained to read poems in weather before they fell. [66] She carried no lantern, though dusk was coming, because she trusted the blue light stored in the walls. [84] Inside, the air held a lantern-moth hush, so still that her breath seemed impolite. [98] Moths slept in the lamp housings, their wings dusted cobalt, waiting for a warmth that might not come. [116] At the center stood the humming quartz colonnade, twelve pillars ringing the sealed well where spring water dreamed under ice. [136] Each pillar sounded a different note when cloud shadows crossed the glass roof. [149] Mira had come to finish the winter poem her brother began before the accident on the lake. [166] His last verse was locked in the well house scriptorium beyond the pillars, where only keepers had walked. [184] The boundary was not a door but a rule everyone could recite and no one could explain. [201] She was aesthetically driven, not reckless, and beauty for her was a form of accuracy. [216] A crooked pillar offended her more than danger, because crookedness meant someone had stopped listening. [231] Tonight the colonnade leaned its music toward harmony, and that small rightness told her the ice had settled. [249] On the first pillar, frost had written the keeper oath in old, prohibitive language. [263] It forbade any spoken repetition, lest exact words wake the water too suddenly. [276] To pass, a poet had to paraphrase the oath, proving she understood its mercy rather than its fear. [294] Mira pressed her palm to the quartz and felt the note travel up her wrist like a bee. [312] She said that what is loved must be guarded gently, and guarding must never become a cage. [329] The pillar brightened, but the inner ring stayed dark and the well kept its locked silence. [345] A second oath waited there, cut deeper, promising exile to anyone who crossed for curiosity alone. [361] Mira thought of her brother laughing through thin ice, calling her to see the fish suspended like commas. [379] Curiosity had been his kindness, because he noticed the world before he judged it. [393] She chose to paraphrase that oath too, saying attention is innocent when it kneels and does not take. [411] Light ran between the pillars then, thread by thread, until the ring resembled a loom of blue fire. [429] Yet the floor beyond remained separated by a sheet of black ice that mirrored the roof exactly. [446] If she stepped there too soon, her weight would find the one flaw the music had not forgiven. [464] So she waited for the hour when lake, sky, and atrium shared one color and no edge argued. [482] Clouds crossed the moon, and their shadows crossed the glass, and the pillars answered in lower, braver tones. [500] She advanced through dissolving boundaries, where reflection met substance and black ice became a doorway of light. [517] Mira felt a tremor of certainty rise from the soles of her feet to her teeth. [533] It was not confidence that nothing could break, but knowledge that breaking was not the only truth. [550] She had not come to defy the sacred, but to stride through boundaries once thought sacred. [566] Her first step made no crack, only a note so soft the sleeping moths opened their wings. [583] They rose around her without sound, carrying lamp dust, and settled on the far pillars like living punctuation. [601] In the scriptorium, her brother's verse waited in a cedar box, unfinished because its final line pointed beyond itself. [620] It read, when winter shuts the mouth of the lake, say the world again in kinder words. [637] Mira understood then that he had left her not an ending, but instructions for entering one. [653] She wrote the final line where light, ice, and breath met, letting each word keep its own weather. [671] When she recited it aloud, the well answered with clear water rising, and the frozen lake beyond the atrium sighed but held. [693] Outside, dawn loosened the clouds, and Mira walked home across blue shadow, carrying a poem that had learned how to open. [714]
