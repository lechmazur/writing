<story>Before the collapse, Harrowmere was already empty enough to hear grass growing through its streets. [15] Its houses stood with shutters open, as if their owners had stepped out for bread and forgotten the way home. [35] Only the ancient archway remained stubbornly whole, though its stones had begun to lean above the narrowed valley gate. [54] A bell hung inside that arch, green with age and silent for nine winters. [68] When it had rung true, millers timed their wheels to it and shepherds brought their flocks down before dark. [87] When it slipped out of tune, neighbors argued over boundaries, debts, and water until leaving felt easier than listening. [106] That was why old Sera sent for a tuner, though no road now led anywhere people still wanted to go. [126] He arrived on foot at dusk, carrying nothing but a small case and a coat patched with dark cloth. [145] Sera asked his name, and he said names changed faster than bells and were therefore less useful. [162] She asked where he had learned his trade, and he pointed toward the hills and then toward his own ear. [182] From that night the town called him only the cryptic bell tuner, and he accepted the title without correcting it. [202] Three people still lived in Harrowmere then, and each watched him from a different window on the first morning. [221] He did not climb to the bell, ring it, or even look up at the archway. [237] Instead he sat beneath it on an overturned bucket and opened his case upon his knees. [253] Inside lay a single ebony tuning fork, black as river stone and polished by generations of careful hands. [271] He struck it once against his boot, held it near the ground, and listened until its hum disappeared. [289] Then he did nothing at all, which in Harrowmere felt more alarming than any work could have felt. [307] Sera brought him soup at noon and found him watching the empty square with his head slightly tilted. [325] She asked what he heard, and he said the town was still deciding whether to return. [341] On the second day he walked the main street slowly, stopping wherever a loose cobble answered his boot unevenly. [360] He marked each stone with chalk, never two marks alike, and left the marks unexplained when Tomas questioned him. [379] Tomas was the miller, broad and angry, and he wanted the bell rung loudly enough to frighten sense back home. [399] The tuner told him a shout could gather a crowd but only a pulse could keep one together. [417] Wren, who kept the last goats beyond the south wall, laughed and asked whether stones now gave orders. [435] He answered that stones remembered every order feet had ever given them, especially the order to leave. [452] That night a wild hush came down from the fields, so complete that the goats stopped shifting in their pen. [472] In that silence the tuner finally climbed the narrow stair inside the ancient archway and touched the bell. [490] He did not strike it, but laid his palm flat against its cold side until his breathing slowed. [508] Below him, Sera crossed the square with a lantern, and her steps rang unevenly between the shuttered shops. [526] He struck the ebony tuning fork, listened to her pace, and smiled for the first time since arriving. [544] Her left foot fell heavier than her right because of an old injury, giving her walk a limping rhythm. [563] Tomas walked in hard even strides, always hurrying even when there was nowhere left to hurry toward. [580] Wren moved lightly and paused often, listening for goats, wind, and any change in the dark fields. [597] The tuner understood then that the bell had failed because it demanded one perfect beat from imperfect people. [615] Its old note had marched above them, while their lives below had learned too many different kinds of sorrow. [634] He said his task was not to command them back, but to restore shared balance between bell and street. [653] So he began to rebuild Harrowmere via the rhythm of footsteps on cobblestones, beginning with the three who remained. [672] First he reset the loose stones Sera favored, lowering some and raising others until her steps crossed without a stumble. [692] Then he set a broad flat stone where Tomas turned toward the mill, wide enough to make hurry briefly soften. [712] For Wren he left a hollow sounding stone near the south steps, so her pause could answer the bell like another voice. [734] He worked for nineteen days without urging anyone onward, infinitely patient with stone, weather, grief, and delay. [751] Each dawn he tuned the bell a fraction lower, then rang it once so its note could hold their steps together. [772] When exiles returned hearing their own walks inside the bell, they raised roofs together, beginning Harrowmere ghost town revival before the collapse could take it. [797]</story>
