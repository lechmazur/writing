<story>Ten years after discovering aliens in the trench below us, our benthic city still dimmed its lamps when their songs passed through the hull. [24] I was the navigation instructor for children who had been born under two miles of water and had never seen an unbroken sky. [47] My name was Sera Voss, and each morning I wound the dead chronometers and taught certainty to the uncertain. [66] The aliens, whom we called the Vei, had not invaded or rescued us, but their presence had ended every easy map we trusted. [89] Their craft did not travel between points, and their pilots laughed, if laughter was the word, at our compasses and charts. [110] They taught us edge geometry instead, a way of reading where light bent, broke, and resumed along the borders of things. [131] A reef was not a place to them, but an agreement among edges, and a course was the promise those edges kept. [153] I kept one surface relic on my desk, a boat anchor chain link scarred by rust and older than the city itself. [175] My grandmother had carried it down during the Descent, when coasts drowned and the first domes were sealed in grief. [195] When I held the link to the lamp, its inner edge made a small hard horizon inside the room. [214] That was how I taught the Vei method to my class, though none of us yet understood what it would demand. [235] This winter the council ordered our benthic city to expand beyond the vent fields into the black canyon the Vei called the Fold. [258] The new domes would house three thousand people, double our farms, and place our drills beside reefs the aliens considered sacred. [279] Sonar was forbidden there because its pulses tore the hearing of young Vei, and satellite signals died long before they reached us. [301] So the first survey boat would travel dark, guided only by edges of lamplight, current, stone, and remembered stars. [320] The council chose me to lead it because I taught navigation, and because I had confessed my private motivation in public. [341] More than safety or promotion, I wanted to learn to navigate by starlight with my own eyes, not through recordings rotting in the archive. [365] I was balancedly curious by training and by temperament, eager to test the dark but unwilling to gamble a child on a guess. [388] That balance kept my students alive in pressure drills, and kept me awake beside charts ending in blank water. [407] Among those students was my brother Jonah, nineteen and solemn, who polished the chain link each evening as if it were an instrument. [430] He asked whether stars were truly fixed, or whether distance only made their wandering too slow for grief to notice. [450] I told him no sailor trusted a star itself, only the measured edge where its light met a known and faithful object. [472] The evening before departure, the eldest Vei pilot entered our school through the flooded lock and unfolded a membrane of living light. [494] Within it we saw not one horizon but many, layered like wet pages, each hiding a route inside the seam of the next. [517] She called these unfolded horizons, and she warned that expansion without attention would tear them and leave both peoples lost between layers. [539] Then she touched the chain link with one translucent hand and set it ringing at a pitch our teeth remembered. [559] The sound opened no door, but taught me iron could hold direction as water held a fading song. [577] At dawn, which for us was a change in lamp color, Jonah and I took the survey boat beyond the last dome. [599] Behind us the city narrowed to a string of amber windows, and before us the canyon expanded into a silence without landmarks. [621] We moved by edge geometry alone, reading where our weak lamps cut the rock and where alien membranes brightened along hidden seams. [643] Twice I stopped the boat when curiosity pulled toward bright reefs, and twice Jonah checked the tether, the valves, and my shaking hands. [666] At the canyon mouth a current seized us and turned every edge into foam, glare, and brief meaningless angles. [685] Our lamps failed then, with the small tired clicks of things that had served beyond their promised years. [703] Jonah lifted the boat anchor chain link into the membrane light the Vei had left upon our bow. [721] Through its rusted oval, one folded layer opened above the canyon, and real starlight fell for the first time onto my upturned face. [744] I set the brightest star against the inner edge, and its line led us home. [759] In somber marvel, we marked a gentler boundary, and expansion began without breaking the sacred reef.
