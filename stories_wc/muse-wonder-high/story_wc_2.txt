<story>Juno Vale repaired broken clocks in a shop that smelled of oil, ash, and rain. [15] She was not a watchmaker by title, but a broken-clock tinkerer by trade, coaxing dead pendulums back into reluctant motion. [35] Her gift was patience, and her flaw was planning every minute as if uncertainty were an enemy to outwit. [54] She kept lists for lists, spare keys for spare keys, and three routes to every address in the port. [73] That autumn, the clocks began arriving stopped at exactly four seventeen, and Juno told herself it meant nothing. [91] First came a captain's mantel clock, its hands frozen after a kitchen fire blackened its case. [107] Then came a lighthouse clock, then a station clock, each silent, each pointing to the same impossible minute. [125] When coincidence becomes pattern, Juno's mother used to say, a wise woman stops arguing and starts listening. [142] On the fourth morning, Admiral Wren's widow knocked, carrying a velvet box that held ash instead of treasure. [160] Fifty years earlier, Admiral Wren had vanished at sea, and his prized compass rose had vanished with him, or so the town believed. [183] The widow said divers had found his wreck that summer, and inside his cabin they recovered his log, unburned and impossibly dry. [205] Its last entry claimed the compass rose was not lost at sea, but hidden beneath the ashes of his own greenhouse before he sailed. [229] If Juno recovered it, the widow would pay enough to save the shop from the landlord's winter deadline. [247] Juno accepted, then spent two days making a perfect plan with maps, lamps, gloves, rope, and a labeled bag for ash samples. [269] She told herself the plan would keep her safe, because she feared what she could not schedule more than darkness itself. [290] Yet some private part of her accepted this job to befriend uncertainty in planning, to learn whether trust could replace control. [311] The Admiral's estate stood beyond the harbor wall, its neglected greenhouse leaning like a glass ship wrecked against the house. [331] Inside, dead vines held the panes, and dry leaves covered the floor so deep that Juno's boots vanished to the ankle. [352] She followed her plan for six exact minutes, checking the log's sketch against the central fountain, the potting bench, and the iron grate. [375] Then a gust through a broken pane lifted the leaves, and they did not settle as leaves should. [393] They turned in a slow spiral above the floor, again and again, though the air inside felt still. [411] Juno was uncertainly certain then, certain the swirling marked something hidden and uncertain whether she should follow it or flee. [431] A fifth clock ticked somewhere under her feet, although she had brought no clock and the greenhouse held none. [450] It repeated four seventeen in tick after tick, as if time below ground refused to move past the Admiral's final departure. [471] Her plan said mark the anomaly, return tomorrow, and bring witnesses, but the leaves were already sinking through a gap she had not mapped. [495] So Juno chose, for once, not to obey the paper in her pocket. [508] She went through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse, stepping where they vanished, and found a stair cut into black stone beneath the ash-thick floor. [534] The stair was not in the log, and her lamps, rope, and labeled bag waited uselessly by the door where her perfect plan had left them. [560] She had to improvise, so she tore a strip from her sleeve, soaked it in lamp oil from her pocket flask, and lit a vine-wrapped torch. [586] Halfway down, cold spray struck her face, and a low roar grew until the stair opened onto a vast cavern. [606] A subterranean waterfall fell from a crack high above, turning a black pool that fed, impossibly, toward the harbor and the sea. [628] On a stone island at the pool's edge stood a brass pedestal, and on it rested a clock with no hands. [649] Beneath its glass, instead of numbers, twelve small stars formed the admiral's lost compass rose, each point cut from pale sapphire. [670] Juno understood then why every recovered clock stopped at four seventeen, the minute the Admiral had set this one ticking below his house. [693] In a niche behind the pedestal she found his final note, sealed in tin against the damp. [710] He had hidden the rose where thieves would see only a burned greenhouse, trusting falling water to turn its wheel and ring its hidden bell homeward. [736] The climb back required choices no map could make, wet footholds, a fallen beam, and trust in leaves that still spiraled above the hidden stair. [761] She delivered the rose at dusk, muddy, torchless, and laughing at her ruined plan, because the unplanned path had brought her home. [783]</story>
