<story>At dawn, the courtyard behind Marigold Row woke in color before the rest of the city did. [93] For three weeks, the locked mailbox by the gate had been their quiet mystery. [107] Its green paint was blistered, its slot was taped shut, and no one admitted to having a key. [125] Yet each Sunday a soft click sounded inside, as if a letter had landed where no letter could enter. [144] The click had divided neighbors into those who wanted the box pried open and those who feared what it held. [183] So she brought no tools, only the aged locket that had reached her shop yesterday, wrapped in a 1987 grocery receipt. [225] Rosa had moved to her daughter's home in February, leaving no forwarding address, yet her empty doorway still gathered offerings. [245] June set the locket on the courtyard table and let the neighbors follow its story, following each of them as she led. [267] That was her leadership, never standing first, but hearing every speaker out to the end of need.
