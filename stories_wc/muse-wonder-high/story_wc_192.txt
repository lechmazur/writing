Three winters after cutting off all contact, Wren still listened for a knock that she had sworn would never come. [20]
She lived in the ashen charcoal sanctuary under starlight, where the roof had burned away and left the rooms open to the cold sky. [44]
The walls were black timbers and packed ash, and nothing green grew inside except moss in the rain barrels. [63]
By day the place looked abandoned, but at night Wren did the work that had chosen her. [80]
She was the tender shadow keeper, the one who fed the shadows of the burned so they would not starve and wander. [102]
Each shadow arrived stuck to a scrap of door or a child's shoe, thin as soot and hungry for the life it had outlined. [126]
Wren washed them with ash water, sang to them without words, and pinned their edges down with smooth river stones. [146]
If she neglected them, they would encroach, first on the paths, then on the beds, then on the throats of sleeping visitors. [168]
That was why the sanctuary existed, to teach the suffocating to breathe again before their own shadows learned to press too hard. [190]
Survivors came at dusk with coughs in their chests and burns on their hands, and Wren taught them to inhale through the nose for four counts, hold, and release. [219]
Most left before dawn steadier, but none stayed, and none knew her name from before the fire. [236]
Wren burns bridges carefully, even now, and she had burned the one to her family with the same care she gave the dead. [259]
After her mother died in the house fire that scarred her wrists, her sister June had called her reckless, then cruel, then nothing at all. [284]
So Wren had answered silence with silence, changed her road, left no address, and carried only one thing from the ruined house. [306]
It was the iron doorknocker ring, blackened but unbent, which she set into the sanctuary gate where there had never been a door. [329]
The gate marked the fading threshold, the thinning place where the shadows of the burned could touch the living and be soothed, or slip through and be lost. [357]
Each night the threshold grew paler, because fewer people remembered the fire, and a threshold lives only while someone on both sides still knocks. [381]
Wren told herself the quiet did not hurt, but the rooms held a loaded emptiness, like a held breath in a house that expects bad news. [407]
Then, on the first clear night of spring, the doorknocker ring lifted and fell three times, though no wind moved the ash. [429]
Wren knew the hand before she saw it, because only June had ever knocked as if apologizing to the wood. [449]
June stood beyond the gate in a travel coat, thinner, grayer, with a scarf at her throat and her own scarred wrists hidden in her sleeves. [475]
June said she had followed a survivor who spoke of a keeper who taught the burned to breathe, and she had hoped against hope it was a stranger. [503]
Her voice broke on the last word, and then her breath locked, as it had since smoke filled the stairwell three winters ago. [526]
She clawed at her scarf, eyes wide, making the small terrible sound Wren had heard from a dozen survivors at the edge of panic. [550]
The shadows along the walls noticed first. [557]
They lifted from their stones and began to encroach across the courtyard, drawn to a living throat that had forgotten how to open. [580]
If June's shadow tore loose while she suffocated, it would slip through the fading threshold and take her breath with it for good. [603]
Wren did not waste time deciding whether forgiveness was possible, because breath comes before every other reckoning. [620]
She pulled June inside, bared their wrists, and pressed scar to scar, linking through scars the way keepers anchor a straying shadow to a living body. [646]
The burned skin warmed where it touched, and June's shadow, already thin at the edges, leaned back toward her sister's stronger one and held. [670]
Then Wren counted aloud, four in, four held, six out, again and again, lending June the rhythm until her sister could find it without help. [695]
On the ninth round June sobbed, which was ugly and blessed, because a sob is still air moving where no air had moved. [718]
The shadows settled back under their stones, disappointed or relieved, and the threshold brightened a fraction, as if someone had knocked from the living side at last. [745]
June whispered that she had not come to be forgiven, only to know that her sister had survived being erased by her. [767]
Wren said that survival was enough to begin with, and that some bridges stay burned while people learn to meet at the gate instead. [791]
