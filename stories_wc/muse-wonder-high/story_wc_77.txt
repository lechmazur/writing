<story>Mara woke on the carnival stretcher with lake water in her lungs and light trapped under her skin. [18] The paramedics called it a miracle after the resuscitation, but none of them would look directly at her. [36] Where the defibrillator paddles had touched, two pale handprints glowed for hours, fading so slowly that nurses covered them with gauze. [57] They called that lingering luminance a side effect of electricity, a trick of damaged nerves. [72] Mara knew better because the glow followed the shape of whatever had held her down in the funhouse. [90] Three nights later she returned to the abandoned carnival funhouse with a flashlight, a crowbar, and her brother's old dream dictionary. [111] The place had been closed since the drowning, though officially she was the only one who had drowned. [129] That contradiction was why she had come. [136] She had not come to remember, but to trick fear into revealing its weakness. [150] Fear, she had learned while dead for eleven minutes, fed on what a person refused to say. [167] While her heart was stopped, something in the mirrored maze had leaned close and listened to her unsaid things. [186] It had worn her mother's face stretched over wet wood, beautiful and rotting in the same glance. [203] That repulsive allure had kept her from screaming, and keeping still had kept it from finding her throat. [221] Now the mirrors inside waited under dust sheets, humming faintly where moonlight struck the cracked entrance sign. [238] Behind the ticket booth stood Elias, her whispering advisor since childhood, dead twenty years and still afraid of being overheard by God. [260] "You brought the book," he whispered, though his lips were sewn shut with black thread. [275] Mara had first seen him in a photograph the summer he died, standing behind her with that same stitching. [294] Every family picture since then proved she was photographically cursed, because Elias appeared clearer each year while Mara faded a little more. [316] The doctors blamed camera faults for the glow around her hospital bed, but Elias knew light stayed where death had been refused. [338] "Inside, do not answer it in your own voice," Elias whispered into her left ear, although he stood ten feet away. [359] She pried the door and entered smelling mildew, sugar, and the hot iron smell of her own burned skin. [378] The funhouse corridors leaned at wrong angles, lined with mirrors that showed her three seconds too late. [395] In each reflection her luminous handprints opened and closed like slow jellyfish. [407] On the floor of the central chamber she found the opera cloak clasp she had lost at age nine. [426] It was silver, shaped like a moth, and warm as a freshly held coin. [440] When she picked it up, every mirror lit at once, not with reflection but with stored daylight from the day she died. [462] That was the lingering luminance made visible, all the light the house had stolen and never learned to digest. [481] In the brightest mirror her mother stood waist-deep in black water, smiling with green teeth. [496] "Unbosom yourself, darling," the thing said, in a voice sweet as spoiled cream. [509] It wanted her confession spoken plainly, because plain speech would give it the shape of her mouth. [526] Elias pressed his cold warning along the back of her neck, a whisper that felt like moth wings. [544] "Through the book," he breathed. [549] Mara opened the dream dictionary, where her brother had written paradoxical notes in margins she had never understood. [567] Under MIRROR he had written, 'A door that opens inward eats the key.' [580] Under MOTHER he had written, 'The safest face is the one that has never fed.' [595] Under FEAR he had written only, 'It starves when named by what it is not.' [610] Mara understood then that her brother, who vanished in this house, had left her a method rather than a map. [630] She would unbosom herself through paradoxical notes in a dream dictionary, telling every secret backward until fear mistook the confession for a lie. [653] She pressed the opera cloak clasp to her throat like a second voice box. [667] "I never loved my mother," she read aloud, though the truth was she had loved her ruinously. [684] "I was not afraid of drowning," she said, though the water still filled her sleeping mouth. [700] With each inverted confession the luminous handprints dimmed, and the thing in the mirror thrashed, suddenly smaller inside her mother's loosening face. [722] Its weakness spilled out at last, not hunger but literalness; it could only believe a secret spoken straight. [740] Starved of a usable truth, it clawed for the clasp, mistaking polished silver for another mirror. [756] Mara snapped the opera cloak clasp shut on its reaching reflection, and the moth wings closed like a mouth biting light. [777] Then, at last, the stolen light went out for good. [787]</story>
