<story>Ivo had not dreamed since the fever took him at nine, and the freight line hired him for exactly that lack. [21] A dreamless freight guard could ride the night cars without hearing the cargo call him by a false name. [40] His cargo that spring was the divergent archive, forty sealed crates of seeds, songs, maps, and witness stones that refused to keep one history. [64] Each crate held many versions of the same summer, and clerks chose which version survived by reading it aloud. [83] If no one read them, the versions multiplied in the dark like unpruned vines. [97] The train crossed the glowing dune in bloom at dusk, where ash fed white flowers that opened only for heat lightning. [118] It was the third year after the last forest burns in the old counting, when even the rivers carried a thin skin of soot. [142] Ivo walked the roofs with a lantern, a short iron baton, and a lullaby his mother had left unfinished. [161] He hummed it when the wind rose, not whole, but in pieces, because the missing notes kept him awake. [180] On the caboose wall hung the chess clock frozen at midnight, its two faces stopped between a move made and a move answered. [203] The captain said the clock marked the hour the archive had split, and until it ticked again, no road behind them was certain. [226] That night the blooms turned toward the crates and began to tap their petals against the hot iron sides. [245] Inside, paper rustled though no window stood open, and a child in every crate began crying in a different key. [265] Ivo knew that sound from manifests he was never meant to read. [277] The archive was not merely dividing on paper but reaching through wave functions for ears that could hold its branches without breaking. [299] Because he never dreamed, no branch could enter him while he slept and wear his face into a false siding. [319] The captain found him at the brake wheel and told him the forward dune had eaten the rails. [337] Beyond the headlamp, flowers covered the track in a bright drift, and each flower held a tiny black seed like a pupil. [359] If the train forced through, the seeds would burst and plant their hunger in the archive soil, unmaking every stored spring. [380] If they waited, the multiplying versions would crowd the crates until the doors failed and history spilled onto the sand. [400] Ivo asked what the flowers wanted, and the captain laughed without mirth. [412] She said they wanted a witness who could charm them without promising rain, because rain was a lie no honest throat could sing anymore. [436] His wages mattered little to him beside the private work that had carried him from town to town and job to job. [458] He traveled to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners, the small survivals no ledger praised and no temple housed. [478] A seed under a stair, a name whispered in a cellar, a tune kept by one toothless cook were sparks to him. [500] So he climbed down with the lantern unlit, because a flame would have told the flowers to fear him. [519] He sang his fractured lullaby into the drift, giving each bloom the verse it asked for and withholding the verse it demanded. [541] Sleep, little ember, sleep unburned, his voice cracked, and the nearest petals loosened. [554] The archive answered through wave functions, not with words, but with pressure behind his eyes as its branches aligned like iron filings. [576] In that alignment Ivo was occasionally everywhere, one hand on every crate latch, one ear at every keyhole, one foot balanced on every car. [600] He did not become many men but became the brief agreement among his possible selves, each one awake, each one dreamless, each one listening. [624] That was how he learned the flowers were not blocking the train but guarding a crossing where three lost children had once slept. [647] The children had vanished before the ash years, yet every divergent telling saved them in a different place and condemned them in another. [670] To force one ending, he saw, would charm nothing and would only prune the archive until its sparks went out. [690] So he opened no crate and chose no child, but sang each saved name into a hollow the blooms had left between the rails. [714] For every name he planted a witness stone with its face uncarved, so remembrance could grow without a fence. [733] The petals closed over the stones, not to hide them, but to keep them warm through the cold hours before dawn. [754] Then, from the caboose, the chess clock ticked once and settled at a breath past midnight, granting the game another move. [775] The train rolled on, and behind it the dune bloomed darker and kinder where his song had taken root. [794]</story>
