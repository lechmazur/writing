Selin Voss had served as lore keeper of the Hollow Palace for twenty three years, cataloguing songs that no living choir still sang. [23]
She was wisely adventurous, a trait her masters praised in private and punished in public whenever she slipped beyond the archive doors. [45]
On the last night of the Interregnum, she received a lacquered box that had waited a century in the palace ventilation ducts. [67]
Inside lay a ruby flake, thin as frost and warm as a held coin, beside a tag naming the first Empress. [88]
The tag gave her a reason to find the stairway of ascension before the law erased her chance. [106]
For nine generations, the unclaimed throne had remained lost while regents ruled from smaller chairs and called it mere prudent mercy. [127]
At sunrise, the regents would melt those chairs into guns and crown whichever captain brought them the largest army. [146]
Selin had until sunrise to prove that ascension was a test, not a bloodline, and that the palace itself still remembered it. [168]
She pressed the ruby flake against her scanner, expecting pigment, and found a living key grown inside synthetic stone. [187]
It woke the box, which unfolded into a star chart made from tram tickets, harvest prayers, and obsolete weather codes. [207]
Every layer mourned a future her grandparents had imagined in chrome and candlelight, bright colonies lit by patient machines. [226]
That aching futuristic nostalgia had taught Selin caution, because old dreams often hid instructions inside their sentimental decoration. [244]
The chart pointed not to the throne room, but to the service gallery where generations of regents had painted over forbidden history. [266]
She packed a solvent brush, a ration lamp, and her grandmother's paper atlas, then waited for the patrol drones to recharge. [287]
At midnight, Selin chose to sally from the archive through a laundry chute that still smelled of rain and hot iron. [308]
She landed in the gallery, listened for wings, and began to work through the peel of paint layers revealing hidden murals. [329]
She did not tear, because wisdom meant softening each coat, lifting it in curls, and reading what the palace had endured. [350]
The first mural showed Earth orchards under glass, where emigrants trained young trees to survive a sky they had never seen. [371]
The second showed the first Empress refusing a crown until engineers, farmers, and children could climb beside her in procession. [391]
The third showed a door behind falling water, and beneath it a warning written in the Empress's own uneven hand. [411]
Only a keeper who valued the past without obeying it could open the way, and only before the first true light. [432]
The ruby flake warmed, matching a red seam in the mural that marked coolant falling through the old garden engines. [452]
Selin followed service pipes past sleeping kitchens and memorial halls where portraits blinked awake, then politely pretended not to see her. [473]
Behind the kitchens, a maintenance iris opened onto light that shifted from green to violet to gold across falling coolant. [493]
It was the hidden orchard behind a kaleidoscopic waterfall, preserved by machines that had mistaken devotion for a maintenance schedule. [513]
Ancient apple trees grew beside reactor lilies, their roots braided around brass rails, their fruit lit from within by stored sunlight. [534]
Selin tasted nothing, touched no branch, and bowed to the gardener machines before asking where the buried stairway began. [553]
A gardener extended a pruning arm toward the waterfall, where the colors separated whenever her ruby key caught the spray. [573]
Behind the parted coolant stood a narrow stair, each step inlaid with a name that history had tried to paint away. [594]
Children, cooks, navigators, and keepers rose together there, proving ascension had always meant shared labor made visible in public. [613]
At the top waited the unclaimed throne, not above the orchard, but encircled by roots that fed it living sap. [633]
Selin understood then that sitting would end the search, while summoning witnesses would begin the rule the Empress had intended. [653]
She sent the mural record through every palace speaker, waking cooks, captains, children, and even regents to climb before dawn. [673]
They came in sleep clothes and armor, carrying lanterns, tools, arguments, and bread, while sunrise thinned the waterfall to amber. [693]
Selin placed the ruby flake upon the empty seat, and the throne opened like a blossom to reveal no weapon, only records. [715]
The records named no heir, but required the first climber each generation to govern with the witnesses who had climbed beside them. [737]
So Selin, lore keeper and reluctant sovereign, took her seat only after every witness had found a place upon the stair. [758]
Above them, sunrise entered the orchard, and the old palace finally looked like the future its founders had lovingly remembered ahead. [779]
