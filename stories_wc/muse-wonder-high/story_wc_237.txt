<story>Between the first storm and the second, the master took one breath and held it. [15] In that held breath, the world went still in a saturated hush so thick that Tova could hear steam rising from the kettle. [38] She stood on the floating rock tea ceremony platform, a flat stone hovering above the gorge, chained only by roots to the cliff behind her. [63] Below, clouds bruised purple and gathered for their return, but here no wind moved. [77] This pause was the law of the examination. [85] While the master held his breath between storms, the apprentice must pour, write, and be read. [101] Tova was a budding rune scholar, seventeen, with ink under her nails and more hope than skill. [118] Today she would either earn the copper ring of a reader or be sent down to scrub pots until next spring. [139] Before her sat three cups, a pot of dark tea, and the tool she feared most. [155] It was a crow quill with a trimmed tip slanting to a point, meant to write runes on the skin of hot tea without breaking it. [181] If the surface tore, the rune drowned and the meaning was lost. [193] If it held, the master would drink the word itself. [203] Her task was not the tea. [209] Her task was her arms. [214] From wrist to elbow, pale scars crossed her skin where practice runes had burned her for every mistimed stroke. [233] The school called them failures and covered them with sleeves. [243] Tova had come to transform scars into stories, to prove they were sentences the body had been trying to write. [263] For each cup, she must copy a scar in tea, read it true, and let the master drink it clean. [283] Only then could she shed the old shame and take the ring uncovered. [296] The master sat opposite, cheeks full, eyes closed, his held breath making a small warm weather over the cups. [315] She reached for the pot and felt the first chill along her fingers. [328] It was happening again. [332] Whenever she was stressed, her skin crystallized, first as frost at the knuckles, then as clear scales creeping toward her scars. [353] The teachers said the crystals were raw rune salt forcing its way out when her mind pressed too hard. [372] They made her grip clumsy and her scars shine like cracked ice. [384] If the shell closed over her hands before the third cup, she would spill, tear the tea, and fail. [403] She poured the first cup anyway. [409] The tea settled dark and perfect, holding the light without a ripple. [421] With the crow quill she traced the longest scar, the one from the day she had reversed fire and flower. [441] Her hand shook. [444] The crystal clicked against the cup. [450] The line in the tea bent, thinned, and began to tear. [461] Panic rose bright and sour in her throat. [469] The frost spread to her palm. [475] She set the quill down before she ruined it and stared at her stiffening fingers. [490] Under the platform, thunder muttered, impatient to be allowed back into the world. [503] The master did not open his eyes. [510] He could not help her, because help would be a breath, and a breath would end the stillness that made reading possible. [532] Tova tried to remember the school remedy, which was to empty the mind and name each rune by its true shape. [553] Fire was a lifted torch, flower was a cupped hand, river was a road that forgave mistakes. [570] The names scattered. [573] What came instead were older lessons, learned before school, in her grandmother's kitchen below the cliff. [589] Grandmother had trusted youthful superstitions the way scholars trusted ink. [599] Never pour for sorrow with your left hand, she used to say, because sorrow is already left handed. [617] Tap the stone three times before reading, so the stone knows you are listening. [631] If your hands go strange, hum the tune you hummed when you hid from thunder, and let your body remember before your mind does. [655] The scholars called these sayings foolish and made the children unlearn them at the gate. [670] Now, with crystal locking her wrist, Tova chose to revisit them on purpose. [683] She switched the pot to her right hand, though it felt awkward, because this cup was for sorrow and sorrow did not need help. [707] Under the table, where the master need not see, she tapped the floating stone three times with her heel. [726] One for the scar, one for the girl who got it, one for the reader she might become. [744] Then she hummed, almost without sound, the hiding tune from childhood storms. [756] The tune slowed her pulse, and warmth returned first to her fingertips, then to her wrists. [772] The crystal shell cracked, shed in bright flakes into her lap, and freed her hand. [787] She wrote each scar true, he drank, and exhaled as thunder returned. [799]</story>
