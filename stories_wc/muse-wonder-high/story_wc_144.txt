<story>Before dawn, the mist-coated rail depot stood in a soft gray hush, and only the repair shop glowed awake behind Platform Three. [22] Inside, Tess Amari ran her palms over a workbench she knew better than sight, sorting screws by weight and warmth. [42] Blind since a boiler flare at nineteen, she nevertheless oversaw every repair that entered, and no apprentice dared argue with her ears. [64] That morning her calm was magnetic, drawing porters, conductors, and anxious travelers toward her bench as if toward a hearth. [84] Beyond the arched windows, in the harbor cut beside the tracks, the new ferry ship Meridian waited to carry rail cars across the bay. [108] Today was her maiden voyage, and when her anchor lifted, the depot clock would start a departure no one could delay. [129] Tess had been hired for one ceremonial task: to patch the artificial hummingbird drone that would fly the first brass ticket from depot to ship. [154] At six, courier Jonah Bell arrived out of breath, holding the drone in both hands like a wounded bird. [173] Its left wing hung cracked, its jeweled eye flickered, and each attempt to rise filled the shop with a thin, pleading squeal. [195] 'It only turns left,' Jonah said. [201] 'It hears something we do not, and it keeps trying to follow.' [213] Tess smiled and held out one steady hand until his shaking placed the machine against her fingers. [230] She was a heartfelt artisan in the way the depot meant it: she mended owners as carefully as objects, and she never hurried either. [254] 'Then we will not silence the squeal first,' she said. [264] 'We will listen until its feedback becomes direction.' [272] She set the drone on a square of black felt and told him to lift the depot shutters exactly two inches. [293] Mist curled through the gap, carrying harbor salt, coal smoke, and the low groan of Meridian testing her engines. [312] Tess could not see light, but she knew precisely where it broke, because she had built the water features that taught this shop its lessons. [337] A shallow fountain fed the waiting room, a copper gutter sang above the door, and mist collectors beaded every cold pipe in the arcade. [361] 'Hold the drone beside the fountain,' she said, 'and tell me where the morning bends in the spray.' [379] Jonah obeyed, squinting as gray light entered the water and split into brief bands of green, gold, and blue across the drone's eye. [402] 'Green holds steady,' he reported. [407] 'Gold vanishes when I tilt left, and blue stutters like a bad wire.' [420] Tess nodded, translating the refraction his eyes described into touch, pitch, and memory. [433] 'Its eye is not blind,' she said. [440] 'Its patch was laid over the wrong seam last winter, so it reads bent light as a turn.' [458] She warmed a sliver of resin between her palms, smelling pine and iron, and asked Jonah what the gutter drips did to the signal lamp glow. [484] Through that second sheet of falling water, the red lamp softened, doubled, then sharpened when the drone turned its head by itself toward the harbor. [509] The squeal changed with it, dropping from complaint to a clear two-note call, repeated each time the harbor light cleared the mist. [531] 'There,' Tess whispered. [534] 'Feedback is not noise when a machine is lost; it is a compass asking to be believed.' [551] She found the cracked wing root by following heat from the tiny motor, then laid her new patch along the seam her fingertips remembered. [575] The patch was not mere resin; it carried a hair of copper angled toward true north, so corrected light would pull the drone straight. [599] Outside, a bell rang from Meridian, and through the floor Tess felt the first links of the anchor begin to lift. [620] 'Now,' she said, 'hold it in the fountain spray once more, and do not help it choose.' [637] Jonah released the hummingbird, and for one held breath the whole depot seemed to lean in without a sound. [656] Then the drone rose, green and gold steady in its eye, and answered the harbor light with a clean rising note. [677] It circled the fountain once, then turned not from error but by choice toward the open shutter and the brightening mist. [698] The brass ticket swung beneath it, catching split light from the gutter as the drone crossed the platform and vanished toward the ship. [721] Far off, Meridian answered with her horn just as the anchor cleared the water, and the waiting crowd exhaled as one. [742] Tess folded her tools away by touch, listening to the fading call until feedback had become, at last, a direction home. [763]</story>
