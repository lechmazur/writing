<story>In the vanishing point gallery, the walls leaned closer with every step until distance itself seemed to hold its breath. [20] Upstairs, as the old rocking chair finally slows to a stop, Ines listened to each creak grow longer and quieter. [40] She was the gallery's charcoal portrait ash scatterer, keeper of faces that families could describe but could no longer see. [60] Visitors brought her a laugh remembered at a kitchen table, the tilt of a chin, a scar misplaced by grief. [80] She worked through incomplete information, coaxing a likeness from fragments that love had saved and loss had blurred. [98] Then she burned each finished portrait in a copper bowl and scattered its ash beyond the harbor wall at dawn. [118] The burning was not cruelty, but a promise that memory could be honored without being required to stay. [136] Tonight the portrait on her easel was her mother, who had founded the gallery and now rocked above its narrow ceiling. [157] Maren had worn a mask made from shattered porcelain shards for twenty years, ever since the sea returned her husband without his boat. [180] She had gathered shards from their wedding plate, shattered that first widowed night, and wired their bright edges together. [199] Behind it she could receive widows, frame their portraits, and never let anyone watch her own face change. [217] Ines understood that the mask was only the mirage of safety, beautiful from afar and cutting wherever it touched. [236] That morning, through the stairwell mirror, she had seen her mother lift the mask and smile at the sunrise. [255] The smile held terror and peace together, and it vanished before Ines could be certain she had truly seen it. [275] Now she wanted to crystallize a fleeting emotion before the chair stopped and took every chance of asking with it. [295] She had no photograph of that smile, only half a remembered childhood face, letters unsent, and the mirror's brief mercy. [315] So she drew through incomplete information again, but this time every missing piece belonged to her own life. [333] She laid charcoal lines for the brow she remembered from before the wreck and softened them with uncertainty. [351] She left the mouth unfinished, afraid that invention would turn reverence into theft. [364] With a folded cloth she began to powder charcoal across the paper, dusting shadow under the eyes until weariness became tenderness. [385] Upstairs the rocking chair answered with a slow creak, then a longer silence. [398] Ines climbed with the portrait, her hands black to the wrist and shaking. [411] Maren sat masked beside the harbor window, her white hands locked around the chair arms as if stillness could be resisted. [432] "Is that supposed to be me," Maren asked. [440] Ines said it was the woman the mirror had allowed her to see that morning. [455] Ines weaves truth carefully, even with strangers, and she would not begin lying now to spare herself pain. [473] She told Maren that the mouth was missing because a daughter should not invent what a mother had chosen to hide. [494] For a moment the only sound was water touching the harbor wall and the chair easing toward stillness. [512] Then Maren untied the mask and let Ines see the face that grief had altered but not erased. [530] Her left cheek bore a thin scar where a shard had pressed on nights she slept in the mask. [549] Yet her eyes held the same brief peace from the mirror, now steadied by being witnessed. [565] Maren said she had built the mask because looking breakable had frightened her more than being cut by it. [584] She asked whether hiding had kept them safe, and her voice made the question smaller than its years. [602] Ines answered that hiding had kept them near, which was true, and that nearness had sometimes felt like safety, which was also true. [625] It had not kept them known, she added, and the words hurt without accusing. [639] Maren reached out, touched the unfinished mouth on the paper, and then, astonishingly, smiled the sunrise smile again. [657] Ines drew the mouth in two quick strokes, not perfect, but alive with terror and peace held together. [675] The chair gave one last creak. [681] Then it stopped, and Maren's hand loosened around Ines's fingers as her breath went out with the tide. [699] Ines did not burn this portrait. [705] Instead she ground one porcelain shard from the mask to powder and mingled its white dust with charcoal ash from the copper bowl. [728] With her thumb she pressed the mixture along the drawn smile, so the thing that had hidden Maren now helped reveal her. [750] Then she carried the remaining ash to the harbor wall and scattered it, not to erase her mother, but to release her. [772] Behind her, in the vanishing point gallery, the portrait remained where distance narrowed and yet, marvelously, did not disappear. [791]</story>
