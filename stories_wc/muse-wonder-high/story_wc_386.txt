Wreck-diver and ghost whisperer Sella Marne dove because the dead were quieter underwater than in her head. [17] On land she heard them as pressure behind her eyes, but below the surface a ghost could only reach her through a living dreamer. [41] Divers called that a gift, though to Sella it felt like listening at windows of connection that never opened for her alone. [63] The wreck that mattered lay at the foot of the Isvar Glacier, where the mail ship Vesper had grounded twelve winters before her first dive. [88] Officially, a boiler had burst, the captain had panicked, and Sella's younger brother Tomas, the junior engineer, had opened the wrong valve. [110] Sella did not believe it, not because Tomas was innocent by blood, but because his ghost kept failing to arrive in her own dreams. [134] So she worked the way she always had, by analyzing cameo appearances in strangers' dreams and following where the background figures pointed. [156] Above the wreck, families had cut a candle-lit shrine carved into glacier ice, with benches of black wood and a round window facing the black water. [182] There, by candlelight as thin as borrowed starlight, strangers told Sella what they had dreamed the night before. [200] A fisherman dreamed of his wedding and saw Tomas behind the altar, holding a wrench and mouthing the word aft. [220] A schoolteacher dreamed of drowning in a classroom while Tomas carried dry ledgers past the windows and never once looked at her. [242] A ferryman who had never met Tomas dreamed of him in the engine room, turning not a valve but a small brass key. [265] She weaves truth carefully in her waterproof ledger, giving families the shape of a haunting without handing them its sharpest edges. [286] After three winters of listening, a pattern rose from the dreams like wreckage surfacing after a storm. [303] Tomas was never the center of any dream, only a cameo at its edge, always moving aft, always carrying proof away from the boiler. [327] If he had caused the explosion, Sella reasoned, guilt would have dragged him to the middle of every sleeping mind he entered. [349] Instead he behaved like a man still on duty, using other people's dreams as narrow windows because the wreck had sealed every other door. [373] On the last dive of autumn, Sella took the pattern down with her, past the Vesper's tilted portholes and into the flooded purser's corridor. [397] The dreams had placed Tomas beside a locker painted with a white star, a detail no newspaper had ever printed. [417] She found the locker behind a fallen beam, its brass key still turning sweetly after all the salt and cold. [437] Inside lay the captain's true log in a waxed tin, and beneath it, wrapped in oilcloth, a melted snow globe from the gift shop. [461] The glass dome had slumped in the boiler fire before the sea came in, but the little lighthouse inside stood upright and absurdly clean. [485] Sella carried both finds up slowly, guarding her breath, because she wanted to salvage something pure from the wreckage and not merely another accusation. [509] The log said the company had ordered the captain to sail with a cracked boiler plate and to blame the glacier if the ship failed. [534] It said Tomas had locked the log away, flooded the aft compartment himself to slow the fire, and helped steer the dying ship toward the ice so passengers could climb out. [565] That was the pure thing, not the globe itself but the unsullied fact that Tomas had spent his last minutes making a window where none existed. [591] The company men arrived at the shrine before the hearing, smiling with lawyers' teeth, and asked Sella to sell them the log for the families' own good. [618] She refused in the careful way she refused all cruelties, by placing the melted snow globe on the ice altar and setting the log beside it in candlelight. [646] Then, before widows, reporters, and the harbor magistrate, she read the dream ledger aloud and let each stranger stand and confirm the dream they had reported. [672] One by one they confirmed the white star, the brass key, the aftward path, details they could not have invented together because she had never shared them. [699] That was how Sella chose to expose the company, not with a ghost's accusation but with a chorus of ordinary sleepers whose dreams corroborated the tin box from the deep. [729] The magistrate seized the log, the reporters stopped smiling for the company, and Tomas's name came off the bronze plaque of blame by noon. [753] That night Sella dreamed of an open window above calm water, and Tomas, at last in the center, set the little lighthouse glowing and walked through. [779]
