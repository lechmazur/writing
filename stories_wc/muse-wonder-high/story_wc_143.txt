In October 1969, June Harrow climbed to the moonlit summit of Meteor Crater with a camera, three chemical vials, and one borrowed grain of sand. [25] She was the crater inspector assigned to certify the floor safe for Apollo crews before engineers remade it as a lunar training field. [48] At dawn, trucks would gravel over the last untouched sheet of impact glass, a black pool frozen where the meteor had melted stone. [71] June meant to photograph that sheet in its final natural light, before floodlights and bootprints claimed it forever. [89] In her report she prudently called that motive a stability survey. [100] Tomas Rey, the nightwatch at the gatehouse, had laughed without unkindness when she told him the truth. [117] He was retiring at sunrise, replaced by an electric fence and a clock that needed no winding. [134] His hourglass stood beside his ledger, its white sand measuring thirty years of rounds more faithfully than any supervisor. [153] Its neck had once caught a single black grain, desert sand fused by the ancient impact and carried there by chance. [174] He kept that grain in a fold of paper and called it the crater remembering itself. [190] Tonight he pressed the paper into June's glove and told her to return the grain with her photograph. [208] The crater walls swallowed radio signals, and nearby Apollo crews demanded silence for their tests. [223] So Tomas and June had devised messages from chemical reactions, timed like patient clocks. [237] Each message was a sealed flask of pale liquid set against a lantern at the gatehouse or summit cairn. [256] A measured wax seal slowed acid until it reached a second chamber and turned the liquid amber, green, or fierce red. [277] They started a flask thirty minutes before each agreed glance, so its color bloomed exactly when the other looked up. [297] Amber meant proceed, green meant caution, and red meant return at once. [310] June was purposefully curious, the trait that had earned her this post when cautious geologists dismissed the crater as mapped and finished. [332] On every inspection she asked what a crack concealed, what a color remembered, and what the official map had politely ignored. [353] That habit had found the impact glass last spring, hidden under windblown silt at the center where surveyors expected only rubble. [374] Now her habit made her pause halfway up, despite gathering cloud, because fresh tracks crossed the trail and vanished toward the forbidden floor. [397] They were too narrow for boots and too regular for coyotes, each print holding a neat crescent of damp soil. [417] June followed them only to the rim overlook, where her lamp showed a fox limping below with one paw lifted from the cold stone. [441] The crater was not an exhibit yet, and it still kept its own wounded citizens. [456] At the summit cairn she anchored her tripod against the wind and set one flask beside a small shielded lantern. [476] Below, Tomas would read its color through binoculars when the delayed reaction finished at midnight. [491] He had placed three gatehouse flasks in a row, ready to start as conditions changed during his final rounds. [510] At ten, through her binoculars, his first flask bloomed amber behind its lantern, telling her to proceed despite the rising wind. [531] June tightened the tripod ropes, ate cold beans, and chose to outlast the weather rather than surrender the last dawn to it. [553] At eleven thirty she started her summit flask, then descended by the service path while its wax seal slowly thinned. [573] She followed reflective markers her curiosity had made her repair all summer. [585] At midnight the flask above turned amber, and Tomas raised his lantern twice to confirm that he had seen her signal. [606] At the center, moonlight found the glass sheet through a break in cloud and lit its buried bubbles like trapped stars. [627] She placed the black grain on white card beside the glass, giving her close photograph a scale older and smaller than any ruler. [650] She exposed three plates slowly, sheltering each with her body while gusts worried the card but never claimed the grain. [670] At two, Tomas's second flask bloomed green through her binoculars, warning caution as cloud thickened and the path markers began to vanish. [692] She could have retreated then, but the glass still held moonlight and her last chance was narrowing with the storm. [712] She stayed through spitting rain and a failed plate, outlasting both until the eastern rim thinned from black to blue. [732] Then came the dawning hush, when wind, rain, engines, and breath paused before first light. [747] In that hush she exposed the final plate, retrieved the grain, and reached Tomas as his hourglass emptied. [765] He kept both in his ledger after the floodlights came. [775]
