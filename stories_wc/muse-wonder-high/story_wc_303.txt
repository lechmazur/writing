Mara Voss had been a provenance researcher for eleven years, authenticating stolen gods and disputed crowns for the Empire. [19] That summer she was sent alone to the velvet observatory at midsummer, when the sun refused to set. [37] The observatory crowned a hill above mulberry orchards, its dome lined with purple velvet to swallow echoes. [54] Inside waited the disputed treasure, a transparent violin made of ice crystal that had not melted in thirty years. [73] The Empire claimed an imperial luthier had carved it for the first Empress and demanded Mara certify that origin. [92] The northern villages claimed their grandmothers had grown it from winter water and song, and demanded its return. [110] If Mara signed for the Empire, armies would take the northern orchards as imperial property by old law. [128] If she signed for the villages, she would be branded a traitor before the promise loses its glow. [146] That promise burned as a thin gold line around her wrist, made to her dying sister ten years earlier. [165] She had sworn never to certify a lie, and the light would fade at the end of midsummer. [183] When it faded, the Empire could legally compel her signature, whether the certificate was true or not. [200] On her first night she drank bitter coffee among the potted mulberries kept for shade inside the dome. [218] A leaf brushed her cup, and a voice bloomed inside her skull, green and startled and not her own. [237] She had become accidentally telepathic with plants by drinking meltwater from the violin, though it never shrank at all. [256] The mulberry said the violin had wept that water for weeks, lonely for cold soil and living roots. [274] Mara laughed from fear, then stopped, because another leaf whispered her sister's name in perfect imitation. [290] Plants remembered everyone who watered them, and her sister had once worked here as a gardener. [306] That memory opened a sweet grief in her chest, a mulberry ache for summers when they stole fruit and stained their mouths purple. [329] She asked what the tree knew about the violin, and it showed her hands planting a seed inside river ice. [349] The hands wore village blue, not imperial gold, and hummed while the ice closed like clear skin. [366] But memory was not proof the Empire would accept, especially when proof served her own heart. [382] Mara needed physical provenance, a mark inside the object that strangers could test and repeat. [397] She turned the violin toward the midnight sun and saw suspended pollen, dark seeds, and one trapped air bubble shaped like a thumbprint. [418] Under magnification the pollen matched living mulberries below, but that alone could mean contamination during storage. [434] The seeds were stranger, still viable, humming faintly through the ice when she rested her palm against the curve. [453] Exhausted, she spilled her morning coffee across her notebook and saw tomorrow pooled in the brown stain. [470] She had always read omens badly, but the mulberries leaned close and translated the stain without words. [487] By reading the future in coffee stains, she learned that destiny was not a road but a cloth. [505] Every choice pricked it, making the perforated destiny through which other futures leaked like light through moth holes. [523] In one stain she signed for the Empire and watched soldiers burn the orchards to plant parade grounds. [541] In another she signed for the villages and died in prison while the violin was melted for imperial glass. [560] Between those stains ran a narrow white gap where she did neither and instead let living evidence multiply. [578] The mulberries understood before she did and pushed their roots toward the violin's weeping water. [593] To propagate was their oldest verb, meaning do not hoard life in one fragile body. [608] Now her task was to weigh a dream against an empire, and the scale trembled in her hands. [626] The dream was not escape but evidence everywhere, too widespread for soldiers to burn without burning their own food. [645] She extracted three seeds through a hairline crack that the violin opened willingly when the mulberries sang. [662] She planted each seed in coffee grounds, river ice, and orchard soil beneath the observatory floor. [678] Within hours green shoots pierced the ice, each carrying a transparent curl of crystal along its stem. [695] The curls resonated when she touched the violin, proving they shared one origin and one living mother. [711] At dawn the imperial inspector arrived early, expecting a signature that would settle ownership forever. [726] Before he climbed the hill, Mara distributed cuttings to gardeners, servants, merchants, and soldiers waiting below. [742] By noon a hundred crystal veined mulberries rooted across the valley, each testifying that the violin was grown, not carved. [762] She signed the true certificate while her promise still glowed, then watched its gold line soften into ordinary skin. [781] The Empire kept its observatory, but it could not own a forest that belonged everywhere. [796]
