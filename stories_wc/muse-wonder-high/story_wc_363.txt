<story>Mira had lived so long as the wistful loom spinner of the forgotten orchard deep in a bamboo forest that even the bamboo seemed to lean toward her cottage to listen. [31] Beyond the green walls of stalks lay a gorge no foot could cross, and on its far ridge stood the house where her sister Sana still kept a lamp in the window. [63] They had waved to each other as girls before the landslide took the bridge, the road, and finally every path by which a voice might travel whole. [90] What remained between them was a raveling silence, widening each season as old signals faded from memory and the forest closed its ranks. [113] Mira had not touched her loom since the winter Sana failed to answer her last cloth signal from the ridge. [133] The shuttle lay in dust, the warp had slackened and grayed, and spiders had claimed the pedals for their own patient weaving. [155] Then, in the last week of summer, a hill trader reached the orchard by a fallen trunk and told her that Sana was alive but believed Mira dead. [183] He would travel onward at dawn by the hunters trail, but no reply could go back with him, for the trunk was already splitting and no boat could live in the white water below. [217] That night Mira resolved to carry a message across impossible distance, though her hands shook whenever she looked at the silent loom. [239] From her neck she took the tree sap amber bead Sana had pressed into her palm on the morning the bridge fell. [261] It had hung there through every lonely year, stoically sparkling even when dust, smoke, and grief had dulled everything else she owned. [283] She turned it in her fingers and remembered the rummage sale box she had carried home from the valley market on that same morning, thinking only that its painted lid was pretty. [315] The box still sat beneath her bed, full of jigsaw puzzle pieces that smelled faintly of cedar and another family's rain. [336] Mira poured them onto the floor and discovered, as she had never troubled to do before, that only the edges remained. [357] Someone long ago had lost or kept the middle, leaving a bright border of sky, bamboo leaves, blossom, and dark water. [378] Kneeling among them, she understood that a border alone could speak if the person waiting for it knew what the missing center meant. [401] As children, she and Sana had sewn coded borders around plain cloth, each flower a letter and each knot a pause, while the middle stayed empty for the words only family could supply. [434] That was how she would reach the far ridge, through jigsaw puzzle edges found in a rummage sale box, copied large enough for Sana to read across the gorge. [463] Before dawn she cleaned the spiders from the pedals, cut away the ruined warp, and made herself recommence the work her grief had interrupted. [487] She had blue wool for sky, green twists for bamboo, and white silk saved from her mother's shawl for blossom, but nothing bright enough to mark the living heart of the pattern. [519] So she threaded the amber bead onto the central cord, where distance would shrink it to a steady point of sun. [540] The work went on through the hush of time's unraveling tapestry, through frost on the orchard grass, through spring blossom falling into her hair, and through another summer of storms. [570] Twice floods tore the half finished cloth from its frame, and twice she began again without complaint, drying each rescued thread over her small fire. [595] When the red yarn ran out, she unraveled her own winter blanket for its madder colored yarn and slept that season under orchard leaves and sacking. [621] She never wrote of this in the border, for the cloth was meant to say only that she lived, that the orchard endured, and that Sana must not mourn her anymore. [652] On the first clear morning of autumn, Mira raised the finished tapestry between the two oldest apple trees, hauling each corner upward with orchard ladders and patient knots. [680] Its border blazed against the bamboo, sky blue, leaf green, blossom white, and dark water black, with the amber bead burning at the empty center. [705] For three days nothing answered except wind worrying the cloth and birds stitching quick shadows across its face. [723] On the fourth morning a border of white cloth bloomed along Sana's ridge, flower after flower spelling the old family answer, I see you and I have not forgotten. [752] Mira laughed then, alone among the apple trees, with her bare loom behind her and the bead already fading into weather, distance, and light. [776]</story>
