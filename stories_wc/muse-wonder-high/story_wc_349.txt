Noor arrived before dawn to sell beginnings that had never been used. [12]
She was the clean slate vendor, keeper of polished glass tablets on which a writer could set a first line without any old ending pressing through. [38]
Her stall stood at the entrance of the glass orchard library floating upon a swirling sea of clouds, where ripe books grew like clear fruit among transparent leaves. [66]
That morning the library opened as a store, and its first customers climbed the cloud stairs hungry for plots, prologues, and second chances. [89]
Noor sold eleven slates before noon, and each buyer left holding a bright emptiness against the chest like a held breath. [110]
Then came the quiet during the lull on a store's very first opening day, when the bell slept and even the glass leaves stopped chiming. [135]
In that quiet Noor could finally pursue the errand that had brought her here, to find the place where stories begin. [156]
Her own story had begun in the middle, or so her memory insisted, with soup cooling and a woman calling a name that was not hers. [182]
Before that scene there was only a white margin, clean as her merchandise and just as unwilling to explain itself. [202]
Fragment one: a thumbprint in blue ink on a borrowed first page. [214]
Noor kept such fragments in her pockets, in her shoes, and between unsold slates, because she was mindfully scattered on purpose. [235]
If she kept every clue in one place, grief sorted them into a single sad order, but scattered with care they stayed able to surprise her. [261]
For her stall she used the same method, hiding one slate in cookery, one in maps, and one beneath the ladder where reluctant readers paused. [286]
Beneath her counter stood a crate of bottled moonlight, gathered last winter from the library roof and stoppered while it still remembered the night. [310]
The head librarian allowed its sale for one use only, to grant a glimmering parole to a story locked too long in unopened glass. [334]
Under that borrowed light a captive opening could step outside its fruit for seven minutes, speak, stretch, and search for a reader, provided it returned before the glow thinned. [363]
Fragment two: a child laughing behind a door that had no room beyond it. [377]
During the lull Noor inventoried the orchard not by what hung ready, but by quantifying absence, counting bare twigs, pale rings, and hooks that swung empty. [403]
She marked each missing book in chalk on her own forearm, because paper lists had a way of becoming endings too soon. [425]
By midday nineteen absences lined her skin, and they were not random, for together they formed a path narrowing toward the oldest tree. [448]
Its trunk was a spiral stair, its branches bore dictionaries and lullabies, and at its heart one clear fruit pulsed with a sentence trying to turn over. [475]
Fragment three: a warm hand spelling a name against her back, letter by patient letter. [490]
Inside the fruit a girl no older than Noor sat at a desk that was also a shore, writing and erasing the same bright line. [515]
The girl looked up with Noor's own eyes, younger and furious, and said that no reader had ever arrived to let her begin. [538]
Noor uncorked the bottled moonlight with her teeth and poured it over the fruit, not to sell freedom cheaply, but to lend it honestly. [562]
Light ran down the glass skin in silver seams, the glimmering parole widened, and the girl stepped out holding her unfinished line like a wet bird. [588]

Then the scattered pieces moved at once in Noor's pockets, the thumbprint warming, the laugh unfolding, the remembered hand completing its patient spelling. [635]
They were not clues to an earlier chapter hidden by someone else, but pieces of one morning when her mother had taught her to read at that shore desk. [664]
The place where stories begin was not behind the tree, but in the brief mercy between a locked line and a witness willing to stay. [689]
So Noor took her cleanest slate and chose to incise not the perfect line, but the true one the girl had been afraid to keep. [714]
She cut the words slowly into the glass, We were loved before we were ready, and each letter held the light instead of letting it leak away. [741]
The girl touched the cut line and vanished into its glow just as the store bell woke, and Noor turned toward her first returning customer whole. [767]
