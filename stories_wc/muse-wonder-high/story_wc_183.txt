<story>Mira Solen had been a chimera twin discoverer for three years before she understood what she had found in herself. [20] A blood test after the ferry accident showed two sets of DNA tangled in one body, hers and her unborn sister's. [41] The doctor called the result rare, but Mira called her sister Liss and began listening for her. [58] Liss never spoke in words, only in sudden tastes, wrong memories, and a pull toward places Mira had never visited. [78] That pull led them, in the last week of October, to an abandoned carnival ship drifting in a foggy bay beyond the harbor lights. [102] No crew answered her hail, and no gulls circled the silent decks. [114] The gangway was raised, yet Mira felt blissfully aberrant standing barefoot on the cold dock, free to board a place the town had forbidden. [138] She climbed a mooring rope while fog sealed the shore behind her, and that sealing felt less like loss than permission. [159] On deck, calliope pipes wore torn bunting, and painted horses leaned from a carousel that had forgotten how to turn. [179] Every mirror she passed showed her face a breath too late, as if Liss were looking first. [196] In the ticket booth she found the emerald swatch card wedged beneath a brass bell. [211] It was not paper for matching paint, though it looked like one, but a stiff leaf pressed flat and lacquered green. [232] On its back, in handwriting that shifted when she blinked, were three words: garden, forgotten, names. [248] Liss surged then, flooding Mira with the smell of wet soil and the grief of being almost born. [266] Mira understood their shared errand at last was to find the garden of forgotten names and plant the card where Liss could be remembered. [290] Yet the ship resisted plain searching, because its corridors rearranged themselves as memories alter inside a single skull. [308] A stair she climbed for the ballroom delivered her to the engine room, where cotton candy sugar crusted dead pistons. [328] A door marked pantry opened onto the captain's cabin, and the captain's log listed Mira as passenger, crew, and cargo. [348] She was trapped, but the trap had no walls she could hate, only choices that kept widening. [365] This trapped freedom steadied her, because if no route was fixed, then no route could truly exclude Liss. [383] From the carousel hung hundreds of narrative threads, each tied to a horse and humming with a remembered scene. [402] Mira touched a red thread and instantly relived her mother sewing two christening gowns, then hiding one in a drawer. [422] A blue thread showed the ferry accident differently, with Liss, not Mira, clawing toward the surface light. [439] She realized the ship did not store the past but rewove it whenever a thread was pulled. [456] If she could recapture the first thread, the one before doctors named her condition, she might follow it straight to the garden. [478] The first thread was emerald, matching the card, and it ran from the smallest carousel horse into a painted forest door. [499] But when she grasped it, the horse spoke with her mother's voice and asked which daughter deserved a name. [518] Mira answered that names were not bread to be divided, and the thread burned cold in her palm. [536] Through narrative threads she then descended, not by stairs but by scenes, each landing softer and stranger than the last. [556] She passed the nursery that never was, the birthday with two cakes, and the funeral no one was allowed to hold. [577] At the bottom of memory she found a greenhouse built into the hull, where fog pressed its face to green glass. [598] Inside grew rows of unlabeled plants, each leaning toward her card like iron toward a magnet. [614] This was the garden, but nothing bloomed, because every plant waited for a name no living mouth remembered. [632] Mira felt the old unkindled solace she had carried since childhood, a comfort kept unlit so it could never be taken or used. [655] Liss pushed that solace aside, not with anger but with hunger, and Mira understood unlit comfort was only another locked room. [676] She planted the emerald swatch card in the central bed and spoke both their names aloud, one after the other, without choosing. [698] The card split, rooted, and bloomed into a flower whose petals bore every erased twin, sibling, and self the ship had collected. [720] Fog lifted from the bay, the carousel turned once, and the ship, free at last, let Mira walk ashore still trapped inside one body and glad of it. [748]</story>
