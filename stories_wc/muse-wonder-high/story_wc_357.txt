<story>Mara Venn was the intrepid orchard pilot of Tessell, an orchard planet riddled with floating rock arches above a silver sea. [21] Each dawn she flew her small glider between the arches, pruning roots that trailed in the air and harvesting fruit that ripened without soil. [45] The trees survived drought, frost, and the yearly blight because their roots drank mist and held fast when the wind tore through the sky. [69] Mara had flown north to find the source of resilience after the blight took her home grove and left its branches black. [91] Elders said the oldest trees grew at the planet heart, where no chart reached and no signal returned. [109] They also said that place could only be entered when gravity inverts for an hour at the turning of the season. [130] In that hour the sea lifted, stones rose, and every loose thing sought the clouds. [145] Mara waited in her glider on the highest arch, holding the broken arrow tip her grandmother had carved from heartwood and stone. [167] Its edge was chipped, but green moss always crowded toward it, because the tip accelerates plant growth wherever its shadow falls. [188] Seedlings touched by that shadow sprouted in minutes, and wounded bark closed before sunset. [202] Her grandmother had called it a compass for living things, and Mara trusted it more than any instrument. [220] When the inversion bell tolled from the harbor below, the world answered with a whirling silence that pressed against her ears. [241] Leaves spun upward without a sound, waterfalls climbed the arches, and her glider tipped as if the sky had become the ground. [263] She cut her engine, because motors failed in the inversion, and let the lifted water decide her course. [281] Navigating by water flow, she followed the bright streams that poured up through the arches toward the hidden heart. [300] Where a stream split, she held out the arrow tip and chose the branch where new shoots burst fastest from the rock. [322] The shoots leaned upstream like hands pointing home, and each choice carried her deeper into rings of stone no pilot had mapped. [344] Half the hour was gone when the streams began to bend inward, all of them curving toward a single dark arch. [365] Beyond it lay a hollow sphere of rock, wide as a harbor, with one ancient tree growing at its center. [385] Its roots wrapped the whole sphere, its branches held hundreds of smaller arches, and its trunk bore scars from centuries of inversion. [407] As Mara drifted closer, faint voices brushed past her, though no one stood among the roots. [423] A child laughed, an old man sang a pruning song, and a woman counted seeds aloud in a language Mara almost knew. [445] The glider entered the hollow, and the voices gathered around her in a gathering of echoes left by every keeper who had tended the first tree. [471] Their words had caught in the curved stone during each inversion, layer upon layer, until sound had become a kind of memory the rock could keep. [497] Mara listened while the echoes repeated warnings about dry years, deep frost, and roots that learned to drink from stone. [517] Then one voice, clear as her grandmother, said to place the broken tip against the trunk and look where the green rushed first. [540] Mara obeyed, though her hands shook as the inverted sea roared silently beyond the arch. [555] Where the tip touched bark, light spread through the grain, new leaves unfurled, and a seam in the trunk opened like a slow eye. [579] Inside the seam she began to discover the truth was not a spring or a crystal, but thousands of seeds pressed together in dark, breathing rows. [605] Each seed carried a different strength, one against blight, one against thirst, one against cold, and the tree endured because it never relied on only one. [631] When one kind failed, another sprouted from the seam, fed by the arrow tip gift that woke sleeping growth. [650] Understanding steadied her hands, and she filled her coat pockets with fallen seeds while the echoes named each one for her. [671] She took only what the tree had already shed, because the voices warned that a taken heart weakens but a shared heart spreads. [694] Above her, the lifted sea began to tremble, which meant gravity would soon return and crush any glider still caught in the hollow. [717] Mara pushed from the trunk, caught a falling stream, and rode its downward turn through the arches as the last minutes drained away. [740] Water that had climbed now plunged, and navigating by its reversing flow she slipped out just as stones ground back into their old places. [764] At dawn she planted the mixed seeds in her blackened grove, set the broken arrow tip among them, and watched green answer blight with a thousand different leaves. [792]</story>
