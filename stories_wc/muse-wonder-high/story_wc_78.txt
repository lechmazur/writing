<story>At the blue hour, the orchard afloat on a circle of driftwood in open seas rocked beneath a sky the color of deep bells. [24] Mira lived there alone since the storm that took the mainland harbors, and the sailors who still passed called her the Leeward tide prophet. [48] She interprets cloud movements the way shore priests once read entrails, finding in each torn edge the hour when wind would turn. [70] That evening the clouds came in low ribbons, broke, reformed, and broke again, promising a leeward tide at dawn but a violent crossing before it. [95] Mira climbed the central apple tree with a wet sketchbook, a stick of charcoal, and a single xylophone mallet worn smooth by salt. [118] The mallet had belonged to her mother, who played harbor signals on iron bells before the orchard tore free and learned to float. [141] The outer circle had cracked on its northern side, and every wave drove loose logs against the roots like fists against a door. [164] By midnight the gaps would widen, the soil raft would tear, and twenty three apple trees would scatter into the dark water. [186] Prayer had never tightened a rope or taught a broken thing to sing. [199] Her mother had left one rule written inside the sketchbook cover, and Mira whispered it while the first rain needled her shoulders. [221] To turn obstacles into instruments, first listen until the obstacle tells you its pitch. [235] Mira struck the cracked northern log once with the mallet, softly, and heard a dull note buried under water and splinters. [256] She struck the nearest root, then a low branch, then a hanging apple, and each answer rose brighter than the last. [277] In the sketchbook she drew not the log but its sound, a short dark bar fading into rings. [295] This was her sketched resonance, a map of where wood held water, where rot hid, and where a rope could bear weight without cutting the living bark. [322] Page after page, the drawings repeated at smaller scales, root echoing branch and branch echoing twig, until the whole orchard seemed drawn by one branching hand. [348] Mira understood then how to save it, not by fighting the pattern of breakage, but by fractal generating a pattern of sound strong enough to organize work. [375] She would tap a rhythm on the great root, let each tree answer in smaller faster copies, and use the echoes to find every hidden crack before she bound it. [405] First she had to dub each obstacle with a name and a part, because unnamed wreckage only frightened her hands into haste. [427] She dubbed the cracked log the Bass Door, the loose barrel the Rain Drum, and the torn sail snagged in branches the high shivering Veil. [452] She tied the Bass Door with sail rope where its sketch sang deepest, leaving the hollow end free to boom against the waves. [475] She wedged the Rain Drum between two roots, cut a slot in its lid, and filled it with apples that thumped when the raft rolled. [500] She raised the Veil on a bent bough, where gusts combed it into bright tearing chords above the leaves. [519] Then she ran from tree to tree in bare feet, striking trunk, bucket, branch, and gourd, until the separate answers gathered into a restless euphony that made even her fear keep time. [551] The pattern began with her mallet on the great root, four slow notes, then split into eights on the branches, sixteens on the twigs, and a silver shimmer on the wet leaves. [583] Echo located echo, pitch corrected pitch, and Mira followed the failures in the music the way she followed gaps in clouds. [604] Where a trunk answered late, rot had softened its heart, so she braced it with a driftwood spar and bound it to two sound neighbors. [629] Where the driftwood circle knocked too sharply, she hung apples in nets, and their soft knocking turned wave blows into measures she could count. [653] Near midnight the wind rose exactly as the clouds had promised, and the orchard leaned hard toward the leeward side where calmer water waited. [677] Mira did not stop playing, because ropes held best when each knot was tightened on the beat its own sketch had revealed. [699] Waves struck the Bass Door and answered as bass, the Rain Drum thumped under its apples, and the Veil tore wider and sang even higher. [724] What had threatened to pull the orchard apart became the very music that told her where to stand, when to pull, and how long to hold. [750] At dawn the leeward tide arrived and bore the bruised but unbroken orchard into quiet water. [766]</story>
