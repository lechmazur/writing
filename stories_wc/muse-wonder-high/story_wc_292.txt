After the jet stream shifted north for good, the coastline Ines had memorized as a girl no longer matched the sea. [21]

Official history said he had moved a lantern to draw the ship onto rocks, then looted its cargo while its crew drowned. [231]

Ines wanted to honor her ancestor's overlooked bravery with more than a story, so she volunteered to rechart the rock before the next storm season. [285]

She asked a question at once, because questions were how she steadied herself when her hands shook. [331]
'If exile is reversible for the dead,' she said, 'who signs for their return?' [345]

The wood had gone soft as bread, but one copper nail from the coffin lid still held bright where Ines pried the nameplate loose. [406]

It was not a confession but a warning copied from the Corinna's last journal, arranged as verses about gulls, bells, and a coast that walked at night. [455]
