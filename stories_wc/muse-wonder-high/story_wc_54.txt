<story>Mara Venn arrived at the abandoned spaceport passenger terminal on the first morning after the carnival leaves town, when the midway lights were gone and only crushed cups and rocket-shaped balloons littered the apron. [34] She was a curious civil engineer intern, sent by the county to decide whether the building should be demolished or given a second life. [58] Her supervisor had given her a clipboard, a key ring, and one instruction that sounded less like engineering than philosophy. [78] He wanted her to puzzle out new equations for hope, formulas that weighed concrete and cost against what a stranded town still needed to believe. [103] The terminal had served ore haulers and tourists when the launch pads were active, but the pads had been silent for eleven years. [126] Inside, dust lay thick on check-in counters, and departure boards still promised flights to places that no longer sent ships back. [147] Mara did not feel sorrow, only a stoic wonder at how much care remained visible in a place everyone had agreed to abandon. [170] Brass handrails had been polished by thousands of palms, and the floor tiles still formed arrows toward gates, customs, prayer rooms, and cafes. [193] Yet every corridor seemed to turn against its own arrow, ending in locked doors, flooded stairs, or walls added during some forgotten renovation. [216] On her tablet, three decades of blueprints disagreed with one another, each layer signed by architects who had expected different futures. [237] It was a maze of intentions, built by miners who wanted speed, officials who wanted grandeur, and families who wanted one last room that felt like home. [264] Mara understood that demolition would be simple only if she pretended those intentions had never conflicted. [280] She was functionally creative in the way good interns often are, able to repair a survey drone with a hairpin and to see a culvert in a broken fountain. [309] By noon, the lower concourse had flooded again from a blocked drain, exactly as it had during the carnival storm two nights before. [332] The carnival crews had pumped that water into the street, where it ruined their painted midway and carried confetti into the river. [354] Mara knelt in the cold water and found the drain grate held shut not by rust, but by a deliberate plate bolted from below. [378] Below it, in a maintenance crawlspace lit by her helmet lamp, she discovered a dry channel that sloped toward the old cooling ponds. [401] Someone had once planned to move floodwater away from passengers and toward machinery that needed cooling, but the connection had never been opened. [424] Wedged beside the unopened valve was an explorer's sextant piece, a brass arc etched with degrees and initials worn nearly smooth. [445] Mara lifted it, and through its sight she saw the crawlspace markings align into a line that pointed not north, but toward the terminal roof. [470] She climbed service stairs that the blueprints called storage, and found a shuttered skylight dome built to track ships by star and sun. [493] The sextant piece fit the missing arc of the dome mount perfectly, as if a navigator had left it below to be found by whoever finished the work. [521] When she seated it, the dome gears turned an inch, then stopped against a nest of carnival ribbons blown in through a vent. [544] Mara cleared the ribbons without hurry, because the mechanism had waited this long and deserved not to be forced. [563] Sunlight then moved across the dome scale in a clean line, showing her the true angle of the roof gutters that every later blueprint had drawn wrong. [590] That error explained everything, because rain, crowds, power, and waste had all been routed as if the building leaned toward a hill it did not face. [616] Mara spent three days drawing a plan that did not erase the maze, but taught each intention to take its turn. [637] Her plan would work through redirected flows of stormwater to the cooling ponds, sunlight to panels in the dome, and foot traffic through the old customs hall, without pretending the terminal could be new again. [672] On the fourth morning, county crews followed her drawings to resurrect the building, opening the sealed drain and turning the dome until clear water left the concourse in a steady, unhurried stream. [704] The batteries woke the departure boards, which flickered once and then, absurdly, displayed the single word ARRIVING in calm green letters. [725] No ship was coming, but children from town were already entering with library books, farmers carried seed trays toward the sunlit gates, and the lower hall was becoming a clinic waiting room. [757] Mara wrote her final equation on the clipboard, not numbers but a balance she could defend: memory plus water plus light equals a place that refuses to waste any of them. [788]</story>
