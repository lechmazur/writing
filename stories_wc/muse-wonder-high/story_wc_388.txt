After the liquidation of the Bureau, Mara Venn kept her brass badge out of habit, though it no longer opened any doors. [22]
For eleven years she had worked as a conspiracy debunker, disproving staged moon signals, poisoned rain, and haunted mayors. [41]
She was sharply observant by training and by temperament, the sort of woman who noticed a changed lock before she noticed a changed face. [65]
That gift became a burden after the Bureau closed, because neighbors now wanted verdicts on beloved mysteries. [82]
Mara still wanted only one thing, to excavate truth from comfortable lies, even when truth paid nothing and lies kept people warm. [104]
So when the orchard cooperative begged her to examine their curse, she packed a notebook, a dosimeter, gloves, and a sack for evidence. [127]
The cooperative farmed a wasteland orchard thriving amid radioactive dust storms, forty miles beyond the last paved road and the last reliable well. [150]
Outside its broken boundary walls, the wind stripped paint from signs and drove grit through sealed shutters. [167]
Inside, plum and apple trees stood in green rows, their leaves clean, their fruit heavy, their roots sunk into black and fragrant soil. [190]
At the center, under the oldest plum tree, the workers had set the flawed diamond rumored to be cursed into a post of blackened oak. [215]
It was no larger than a thumbnail, cloudy at one edge, with a dark vein branching through it like a crack in ice. [238]
Three owners had owned it in nine years, and all three had died coughing blood before their fortieth birthdays. [257]
Their widows blamed the diamond, their workers blamed buried waste, and traveling preachers blamed the orchard itself for growing where nothing should grow. [280]
Sella, the cooperative manager, met Mara at the gate with a lowered shotgun and a ledger against her chest. [299]
Sella said the trees would stop bearing if the diamond was moved, and the storms would finally enter and kill them all. [321]
Mara asked why a stone would care about trees, and Sella answered that stones remembered longer than people did. [340]
That night the storm divided at the boundary, one arm grinding north, one south, leaving the orchard in uncanny calm. [360]
Mara stood among the trees and watched dust fall everywhere except in a narrow spiral of still air above the central tree. [382]
She marked the edge of the calm with twine, then found the same clean line repeated in fallen leaves, ash, and old footprints below. [406]
The orchard, she realized, contained a hidden stillpoint, a sheltered heart where moving air canceled itself and let clean rain settle instead of poisoned dust. [431]
Such a place could explain the trees, but it could not explain why three careful owners had died in the same ugly way. [454]
In the morning she inspected the post and saw fresh tool marks beneath older scratches, as if someone still polished the diamond in secret. [478]
Sella denied it until Mara pointed to the blue polish on her thumb, the same color smeared along the oak below the stone. [501]
Then Sella opened a flour tin in the packing shed, filled with shredded paper, ash, and bent staples. [519]
She said Ana Rook, the first owner, had shredded her letters the night reactor crews abandoned the valley. [537]
Ana had feared looters would read them and dig for buried reactor metal worth a fortune. [553]
Mara spent two days by reassembling shredded letters on the shed floor, sorting strips by ink, fold, watermark, and the pressure of Ana's hand. [577]
Some strips were missing, so she had to intuit whole phrases from grammar, context, and stubborn hope. [594]
The letters showed Ana had built the stillpoint herself, raising angled walls from reactor shielding so opposing winds weakened and dropped their poison beyond the trees. [620]
Beneath the central tree she had buried a sealed waste capsule, and above it she had set the diamond as a warning instrument. [643]
Its flaw was radiation damage that darkened as exposure rose, and Ana had polished it weekly to read the growing vein. [664]
The later owners had died because each had dug beneath the tree for imagined gold and breathed the capsule's leaking dust. [685]
Ana warned that if the vein reached the diamond's edge, the seal had failed and the orchard must be abandoned. [705]
Mara looked up and saw the vein had almost reached that edge beneath Sella's fresh polish. [721]
She told them the curse was a warning, ordered the harvest burned, and led everyone beyond the walls. [739]
