<story>June poled us into the mangrove labyrinth at slack tide, in the space where an apology waits unspoken, to find the cryptic medium who could evoke our drowned brother. [29] She had not looked at me since the dock, and the unsent letters in my coat felt heavier than the oar she left across my knees. [55] There were ten, nine for Kit, one for every year since he drowned, and one for June that I had written and rewritten but never sent. [81] I had not come to ask whether Kit forgave me, whatever June believed, though I had spent nine years blaming her for teaching him the channel. [107] I had come to find the moment before deciding, the last clean second before I told him to go if he was so determined to prove himself. [134] After that came the storm, the swollen channel, and searchers finding his lantern snagged in roots that closed like fingers. [154] Odile Vane waited where the mangroves thickened around a house on stilts, its salvaged windows already white with breath that did not belong to the living. [180] The fisherwomen said the drowned were singing spirits here, singing through water in hollow roots, and that Odile answered grief only with questions and the names of weather. [208] Her scarred throat made her voice a thread by day, but she heals in moonlight, and tonight a white moon stood open above the canopy. [233] As the light reached her, her breathing eased and her scar softened, though her eyes stayed hard enough to cut rope. [254] She looked at my coat and said the dead did not collect paper. [267] When I held out the letters anyway, she ignored them and lifted a gargoyle's rainspout shard from her table, black stone smooth on one side and cruelly sharp on the other. [298] She said it had broken from the flooded chapel the night Kit died and still remembered every storm that had passed through its mouth. [322] Then, with fierce tenderness, she caught my wrist and drew the sharp edge once across my palm before I understood she meant to do it. [347] She pressed my bleeding hand to the cold stone and held it there, not unkindly, until the shard warmed and the singing below us gathered into one note. [375] Odile said blood gave the dead a family name to follow, and set the shard upright in a bowl of channel water to evoke Kit by the hour he kept, not the death I kept rehearsing. [411] Around the room, spirits rising from the cold water laid messages in condensation on windows, first as drifting mist, then as letters that gathered backward on the glass. [439] Odile told me to ask with the stone, so I pressed its smooth side to a pane and wrote Kit's name in the wet shine. [464] Beneath my writing another hand answered, uneven as a child's, asking why I kept looking after the shout instead of before it. [486] Odile read the reversed words aloud in a voice made whole by moonlight, then turned my face toward the door with two fingers under my chin. [512] Memory arrived with wet wool and lantern oil: Kit already dressed for the storm, one foot over the threshold, looking back before I had spoken at all. [539] In that look he was not defying me, and he was not yet gone; he was asking whether his sister would stop him, guide him, or let him vanish alone. [569] I had found the moment before deciding, and it gave me no pardon, only the truth that my cruelty had not created his longing to be believed brave. [597] More letters bloomed beside my hand, slower this time, while Odile gripped my shoulder hard enough to keep me from begging the glass for absolution. [622] The message said he had looked back for both his sisters, because even then he knew a drowned boy could become a locked door between the living. [649] When the moon slipped behind leaves, the words ran down as ordinary water, and my cut palm stopped aching in the last of its light. [674] Odile gathered Kit's nine letters without opening them and laid them in the bowl, where the ink loosened and darkened the channel water. [697] She kept June's letter dry on the table, though, and pushed it toward me with the smooth side of the shard. [718] Outside, June waited by the boat with her hands empty in her lap, exactly as she had waited all these years for me to stop making her the lock. [747] I gave her the letter, then spoke the apology it had carried too long: I was sorry I had blamed her for the brother I also failed to stop. [776]</story>
