<story>Wren Solace had not worn boots since the rebellion went quiet. [11] She crossed the abandoned spaceport repair bay barefoot, as she always did, feeling cold oil and grit bite her soles. [31] Above the torn roof, stars still burned in the margin of daylight, that thin blue hour when night had loosened but morning had not yet claimed the sky. [59] It was the only hour she could be both mechanic and stargazer, with grease on her hands and heaven reflected in her eyes. [82] Three weeks earlier, the dock crews had risen against the Meridian Combine and shut down every launch pad on Theros. [102] They had demanded shared ownership of the port their grandparents built, and the Combine had answered with locked fuel vaults and orbital threats. [125] Now there was only a lull in rebellion, a held breath stretched so long that hope had begun to sound like surrender. [147] No ships rose, no wages paid, and the great cranes stood still while negotiators traded careful lies above the planet. [167] Wren came to Bay Nine because its mess, unlike the argument in the council hall, seemed honest enough to teach her something. [189] Broken coolant loops, orphaned bolts, and a ferry engine stripped for parts lay where frightened crews had dropped them in the first night. [212] She was not searching for a weapon at first, only a pattern, some proof that ruined things still pointed toward a purpose. [234] From the cord at her throat she lifted her great grandmother's vintage opera glasses, brass rimmed and absurdly delicate beside the engine blocks. [257] They had crossed three star systems in a coat pocket when opera still needed singers and ships still needed hands. [277] Through them the fading stars sharpened into blue nails, and the Combine watch satellite became a slow red eye sliding toward dawn. [299] Wren lowered the glasses and smiled at the satellite with awkwardly confident defiance, although her knees knocked and her voice, when she practiced speeches, tripped over its own courage. [328] That was her gift and her curse in the strike meetings, to stand too quickly, speak too plainly, and then apologize to the chair she had bumped on the way up. [359] Still, people listened, because she said what their exhaustion had polished down to bone. [373] Beneath her bare feet, then, she felt it for the first time, not through the floor plates but through the older stone sleeping under them. [398] The repair bay had been poured over a ring the first surveyors called a ruin and the first settlers called a warning. [420] Nine black stones formed an ancient stone circle under the concrete, their tops exposed where decades of landing shocks had cracked the floor. [443] Wren knelt and pressed her palm to the nearest stone, and a low hum climbed through her bones with thunderous serenity, vast and calm at once. [469] It was not loud in the air, but everything loose in the bay answered it, wrenches trembling in trays and dust lifting in slow spirals. [494] Through the opera glasses, raised more from habit than sense, she saw the ferry engine's dead resonance coils glow faintly in time with the stones. [519] Her breath stopped, because every Combine engineer knew those coils needed refined fuel cells, and every local mechanic knew the vaults were sealed. [542] Yet the stones were feeding the machine a note it remembered, a power older than fuel and patient as gravity. [562] Wren laughed once, too loudly, then dragged cables across the mess with the certainty of a girl conducting weather. [581] She coupled the ferry coils to the exposed stones with grounding straps, copper mesh, and clamps scavenged from three different wrecks. [602] If she was wrong, the surge would cook the only engine the rebels might still claim. [618] If she was right, the Combine would learn that locking a vault did not lock a planet. [635] She ran barefoot to the council hall through streets silvered with first light and dragged the strike leaders back before their doubt could dress itself. [660] Old Tomas called her reckless, young Priya called her blessed, and Wren, scarlet and stammering, told both of them to hold a cable and watch the sky. [687] By the hum of an ancient stone circle, tuned through bare soles and shaking hands, she coaxed the dead ferry coils awake. [709] The engine did not roar so much as exhale, and then, impossibly, it began to reignite in a pillar of clean blue fire. [732] Heat washed over mechanics who had not cheered in weeks, and in that thunderous quiet they remembered they were not beggars waiting for permission. [756] Wren saw it then, the meaning in the mess, not that broken things were beautiful, but that broken things could still be joined truthfully. [780] Above them, through smoke and daylight, the stars withdrew, and the rebellion woke with engines singing under its feet. [799]</story>
