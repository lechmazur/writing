<story>Joren Hale was the last music box repairman in the city of Lumen, and at midnight he heard the light go out. [22] It left not with darkness alone, but with the soft click of a thousand lids closing at once. [40] Since spring, Midnight had come as a tall thief in a coat of blue ash and pocketed every dawn before it could rise. [63] Joren climbed to the resonance chamber beneath the clock tower because the mayor had summoned the only hands that still knew the Dawn Box. [87] The chamber was round, ribbed with copper pipes, and built to make a whisper travel like a bell. [105] Tonight its pipes were silent, and the whole city hung in the balance between the last tick and the first that would not come. [129] Mayor Sella met him at the iron door with a lantern whose flame leaned away from her mouth. [147] She said thieves had shattered the Dawn Box in the night and begged him to save what little remained. [166] Joren set his tool roll on the floor and opened the great cedar case at the center of the chamber. [186] Inside lay three hundred gears, a silver cylinder, and a mainspring bright as a held breath. [202] Nothing was shattered, and nothing was even scratched. [210] Above the cylinder bloomed the reason Joren trusted his eyes more than any report from the mayor. [227] A living lily grew from the chip from philosopher's stone that served as the bearing for the highest gear. [246] The stone turned true music into light, and its lily opened only when the music was honestly played. [264] It also had one plain habit that no liar could charm away, for it wilts near dishonesty. [281] Now the white petals curled tight whenever Sella spoke, though they loosened each time she fell silent. [298] Joren looked from the lily to the unwounded gears and understood that his work would be mending what isn't torn. [318] He asked who had wound the box last, and Sella answered too quickly that her brother had done it. [337] The lily folded so hard that a petal dropped onto the cylinder with a note like a dropped pin. [356] Her brother had been dead for seven years, and Joren had made the tiny box that still sang beside his grave. [377] Sella caught the fallen petal and, for the first time, did not try to explain it away. [394] She whispered that she had stopped winding the Dawn Box on the night her brother died. [410] While night lasted, his shadow still came to the tower stair and hummed the tune he had composed for sunrise. [430] To keep him, she had struck a covenant of rust with the tall thief who called himself Midnight. [448] Rust would take the bridges, pumps, and bells of Lumen a little each year, but never the shadow of her brother. [469] Midnight would hold back dawn as surety, pocketing its light until the debt of iron was paid. [486] Now red rust crept over the tower bell outside, and the city pumps coughed in their flooded pits below. [505] Joren had not come for Sella, the mayor, or even the honor of his trade. [520] He had come to reclaim the light stolen by midnight, because his granddaughter had been born in spring and had never seen morning. [543] He told Sella that a box could carry a tune, but it could not carry the dead without stealing room from the living. [566] Then he took her brother's grave box from his coat, having lifted it from the headstone before the climb began. [586] With a silver chain from his tool roll, Joren began to tether the little box to the Dawn Box so their springs would turn as one. [612] At the first linked note, the lantern died, and Midnight stepped out of the copper pipes with dawn shining inside his coat. [634] He reached for the stone chip, but the resonance chamber caught the little tune and threw it back from every wall. [655] The thief staggered, and inside his tall shape Joren saw a younger shadow lift its head and listen. [673] Sella pressed both hands to the cedar case and told her brother that she loved him and could finally let the song finish. [696] The lily opened wide, white and fearless, and the philosopher's stone turned the linked tune from silver to gold. [715] Midnight unbuttoned his coat; the younger shadow stepped into the pouring mornings, and the stolen light carried him beyond the tower. [736] Outside, bells, pumps, spoons, and gates shook off their rust as the city rose in one bright tinkersong wonder. [755] At his own doorway, Joren held his granddaughter toward the first true sunrise, and around them a hundred small boxes began to sing. [778]</story>
