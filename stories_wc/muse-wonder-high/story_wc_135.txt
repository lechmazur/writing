Sella Vane had kept the spray for seven years, because no one else in the ivory harbor after rain knew how to read it. [24] As the ocean-spray archivist, she bottled each morning's wash against the white quay and labeled it with the names of the absent. [46] The exile had emptied the harbor of fishers, pilots, and her own brother, all sent beyond the reef for refusing the Regent's tithe. [69] Now, after the exile ends, the first sails showed through thinning rain, and every family crowded the quay asking who had survived. [91] Sella's task was not to promise miracles, but to compile fragments into meaning from spray, splinters, and remembered routes. [110] She was reactively visionary, which meant the sea gave her nothing until a fragment touched her wet hands and demanded an answer. [132] Then, for a breath, she saw where a boat had turned, where a hand had slipped, or where a harbor light had held. [155] The gift drowned the unwary, so she worked through staggered intervals, opening one bottle, resting, listening to bells, then opening another. [176] That method had kept her sane while the harbor stood empty and the gulls nested in the customs house. [195] On the morning of return, the herald climbed the quay tower with his trumpet and produced only a choking wheeze. [215] A missing herald's trumpet valve, bright as a knuckle of brass, had likely rolled somewhere into Sella's unsorted wreck crates. [235] Without that valve, he could not shape the notes that announced each returning captain by name. [251] Without names, the crowd heard only noise, and noise after seven years sounded too much like loss. [268] Sella understood that fear because she had navigated absences for years, charting not the ships present but the spaces they left behind. [290] Her charts showed blank lanes where convoys should have crossed, white gaps where spray held no salt from familiar hulls. [310] Those gaps had told her which widows to visit first and which doors needed bread left quietly outside. [327] Now she opened the crates in her staggered rhythm while the first ship scraped home against the ivory stone. [346] Rain slicked the quay, fresh water rinsing old salt into bright runnels that made the whole harbor shine like bone. [366] In the first crate she found a kettle, a child's shoe, and a logbook swollen shut by the sea. [385] Spray struck her face as she pried the logbook open, and the vision came in reaction, unasked and exact. [404] She saw her brother Tomas bailing beside Captain Iven while a torn sail beat the mast like a frantic wing. [424] She also saw a second boat, unnamed in the spray, turning back toward them through the storm. [441] Then the bell rang, the interval closed, and Sella set the book down before the vision could pull her under. [461] In the next interval she dried the pages page by page with warm ash, until three names emerged from the ink. [482] Iven had lived, Tomas had lived, and the unnamed rescuer had been Pilot Ansel, whose own ship was still absent. [502] The crowd pressed closer, but Sella lifted one palm and made them wait for the herald's silence to become speech. [522] In the second crate, under netting stiff with spray, her fingers found the herald's trumpet valve cold and green at the edges. [544] Brass touched her wet skin, and she saw the herald drop it during the exile's first riot, then choose mercy over searching. [566] He had used both hands to lift a fallen child from the stampede, and the valve had rolled into a departing crate. [588] Sella rinsed the valve in rainwater, fitted it into the trumpet beside the herald, and felt its click settle like a key. [610] When he blew the first true note, the sound crossed the wet harbor and every face lifted at once. [629] That note did not erase grief, but it did hearten the waiting families enough to hear hard news without breaking. [649] Sella read from her compiled fragments between trumpet calls, giving each interval its names, its rescue, or its confirmed wreck. [669] For Ansel, she left a deliberate pause, and the herald rested his trumpet so the absence could be heard cleanly. [689] Then, through staggered intervals of spray and bell, another sail entered the rain-washed light beyond the breakwater. [706] It was Ansel, limping home on a patched hull, with Tomas waving from his deck beside the rescued Iven. [725] Sella added his name to the chart not by filling the blank, but by circling it, so the harbor would remember both the absence and the return. [752]
