Mina introduced herself at every meeting as a pyramid scheme survivor, because survivor sounded braver than fool. [17] Three years earlier she had recruited cousins, neighbors, and choir members into Luminous Levels, a company promising wealth in rising tiers. [38] When the tiers collapsed, the money vanished, but her guilt remained and grew heavier every month. [54] That was normal now, in the strange season when concepts become physical and feelings develop weight, texture, and weather. [73] Shame fell as fine black rain. [79] Grief pooled in shoes. [83] Pressure, worst of all, gathered as invisible stones inside the chest and pressed until breathing became work. [100] Mina found relief only underground, volunteering nights at a bioluminescent mushroom farm built inside a decommissioned submarine pressure hall. [119] Rows of blue caps glowed along damp timber, and green spores drifted like slow fireflies through the humid dark. [138] The farm fed the town, because the mushrooms thrived on sorrow exhaled by workers and turned it into sweet, clean protein. [159]

Mina, who was virtuously mischievous, relabeled the saddest beds with hopeful names so the pickers would greet them kindly each morning. [180] She also hid tiny bells among the stems, so exhausted volunteers laughed when the harvest seemed to answer them. [199] Her private hope was harder and brighter than relief. [208] She wanted to translate pressure into diamonds, real diamonds she could sell to repay every person she had recruited. [227] The farm manager, Odo, claimed the deepest vault could do it, if someone made a quantum promise and meant it in two worlds at once. [252] A quantum promise, he explained, stayed both kept and broken until the promiser acted, and that suspense generated useful force. [272] Mina did not understand the physics, but she understood owing people a future and watching interest grow teeth. [290]

The night the vault finally ripened, her phone buzzed with a message from her own number. [306] It read: Do not volunteer unless you bring the bolt, and then dissolved into squares and stars. [323] More followed via glitchy text messages from parallel dimensions, each signed by a Mina who had chosen differently. [341] One Mina had become rich and lonely among white, silent mushrooms. [352] Another had never joined Luminous Levels and wrote only, Tell Tomas I am proud of him, whoever he was. [371] The kindest messages arrived broken, as if mercy had poor reception between worlds. [384] Still, they agreed on three things: bring the submarine porthole bolt, speak the promise aloud, and leave one shadow unsewn. [404] The bolt hung by the farm entrance, heavy brass salvaged from the old submarine, keeping a cracked porthole fastened like an eyelid. [426] Every worker touched it for luck, because it had held back the sea and therefore understood pressure personally. [444]

Mina waited until Odo was arguing with a leaking pipe, then borrowed the bolt with a grin and left an IOU in its place. [468] The note promised him two lunches and one sincere apology, in that order of importance. [483] At midnight she chose to volunteer as the vault promiser, although no one had asked and everyone had quietly hoped someone else would. [506] The vault door was a round submarine hatch at the end of the mushroom rows, sweating cold light. [524] Inside, black mushrooms grew in a circle around a stone seat, their caps shining like wet constellations. [541] Mina sat, set the submarine porthole bolt across her knees, and felt her chest stones grind softly together. [559] She spoke to the recruited faces she carried in memory, naming cousins, neighbors, choir members, and Tomas, who had sold his boat. [581] She promised to repay them without demanding forgiveness, and to stop turning shame into another scheme for applause. [599] The words left her mouth twice, once solid and once translucent, and hovered facing each other above the mushrooms. [618] That was a true quantum promise, suspended between kept and broken, and the vault drank its tension like roots drink rain. [639] Her phone lit with a dozen broken replies, all repeating, Now press, now press, now leave the seam open. [658] Mina laughed because even parallel versions of herself were bossy when frightened. [670] Then she screwed the bolt into the empty socket at the center of the hatch, although she had no idea it belonged there until her hands remembered. [697] The hatch sighed, the circle tightened, and the invisible stones in her chest rolled toward the seat and vanished into black caps. [719] Beneath each cap, pressure folded and folded until tiny diamonds formed, clear as held breath. [734] She did not pry them free, because the promise was still becoming true, and shadowseam wonder glimmered between kept and broken. [755] At dawn Odo found her asleep, the bolt warm, and seven modest diamonds cooling in her lap like forgiven teeth. [775] She sold them anonymously, repaid Tomas first, and kept working the night rows, mischievously ringing her bells for every tired volunteer. [796]
