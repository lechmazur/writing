After the eclipse ends, the light returns too clean and too white over the wind-carved encampment. [16]
Tents of pale canvas lean in bowls that the desert wind has hollowed from red stone. [32]
In the lowest bowl, Sera the albino tracker kneels beside a cooling fire and does not blink. [49]
Her skin burns in minutes, so she has learned shade, timing, and patience better than any hunter alive. [67]
Above her, an artificial hummingbird drone hangs motionless, its glass wings beating only when it thinks no one watches. [86]
She calls it Pip, though its makers called it a witness, built to record crises without interfering. [103]
Three hours ago, during totality, raiders came on silent electric sleds and took the camp water and her brother. [122]
She remembers every second of that theft in impossible detail, and that is the wound she cannot leave alone. [141]
She wants to discover why time slows in crisis, because in that stretched minute she saw a face she should not have been able to see. [167]
The face belonged to Councilor Venn, who claimed to guard the camp but stood among the raiders and smiled. [186]
If time had moved honestly, Pip could prove that smile, but Pip went blind when the moon covered the sun. [206]
Its solar eye shut in darkness, and its memory filled with white noise and her own slowed heartbeat. [224]
Sera therefore trusts an older witness, the small clay bottle of disappearing ink tied at her belt. [241]
Her grandmother made it from ash, cactus milk, and iron dust, and it shows only while light is changing. [260]
In steady noon it fades within breaths, leaving paper and sand apparently blank. [273]
During the raid, frightened children spilled a whole jar across the council tent floor as they ran. [290]
Venn stepped through it in the dark, not knowing his boots carried a story that sunlight would erase. [308]
Sera is consciously wild, not careless, and her wildness is a discipline she chose after years of being sheltered like glass. [329]
She does not rage or weep over her brother, though both impulses sit steady in her chest. [346]
Instead she feels a serene anger, clear as well water, that tells her exactly where to place each foot. [365]
She leads Pip into the empty council tent, where dust still holds the shallow prints of fleeing feet. [383]
There she uncorks her bottle and deliberately spills fresh ink across the floor in a thin black sheet. [401]
Pip dims its glass dome in a slow artificial eclipse, because Sera has taught it to imitate returning light. [420]
As the sheet fades from black to gray, old boot marks rise inside it, dark and brief, before sunlight eats their edges. [442]
The marks show children scattering, guards turning away by order, and Venn walking calmly toward the water sleds. [460]
One print turns outward at the heel, the limp Venn hides in public and could not hide while stealing in darkness. [481]
The last prints do not end at the tent wall but narrow into a bright seam, because the ink still holds the minute when fear stretched time around the children. [511]
Her grandmother called such seams borrowed seconds, left when terrified minds notice more than a passing second can carry. [530]
If Sera steps into the seam before it fades, she will disappear through ephemeral patterns in spilled ink and arrive walking inside that borrowed minute. [555]
There she can follow Venn unseen, because the living moment will look past a woman moving between its seconds. [574]
Pip hovers at the tent pole, afraid in its own mechanical way, and asks if the seam will remember to return her. [596]
She ties a red thread between Pip and her wrist, trusting its small loyal motor to pull her back toward ordinary light. [618]
She sets her bare foot exactly inside Venn's turning print, breathing once as cold climbs her ankle like floodwater. [637]
The tent walls, the white noon, and the watching camp thin out, and Sera disappears without a sound. [655]
She stands again in totality, but her anger remains serene, and by that calm she knows she is herself and not a memory. [678]
Ahead, Venn crosses the stolen minute at an unhurried pace, leading water sleds toward the canyon while her brother clutches a rail in silence. [702]
She follows beyond his lamplight, counting guards, memorizing the canyon turn, and learning the taps he uses to open the sled locks. [724]
She could kill him inside this hidden minute, but her serene anger chooses her brother and rescue over revenge. [743]
Then the red thread tightens, Pip pulls, and the borrowed minute folds her back into noon beside the now blank ink. [764]
She understands at last that crisis time slows because sharpened attention makes each second carry more life, and she opens her eyes already planning the canyon rescue. [791]
