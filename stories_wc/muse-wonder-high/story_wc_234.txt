June was a pocket-sun alchemist, which meant she carried small captive dawns in the lined pockets of her coat. [19]
Each dawn weighed almost nothing until someone she loved stood near it, and then it grew warm, dense, and difficult to ignore. [41]
At the ember nexus at twilight, where seven furnace vents met beneath the harbor bridge, her pockets ticked like patient clocks. [62]
She had come there to perform the experiment she had rehearsed for three years, ever since Wren accepted the long survey. [83]
At dawn Wren would sail beyond the ash reefs, charting currents no living cartographer had crossed and survived to describe accurately. [104]
June, scientifically romantic to the marrow, believed absence should obey measurements if affection was honest enough to record them properly. [124]
So she kept notebooks recording Wren in temperatures, pulse rates, reflected brightness, and the exact pressure of a familiar shoulder. [144]
Her life's ambition was to capture the density of presence, not as memory, but as matter that would not cool. [164]
If she succeeded tonight, one sun would retain Wren's nearness after distance, habit, and silence had done their ordinary erasing work. [185]
She arranged her instruments on the warm iron grating with the care of a surgeon laying out bright scalpels. [204]
Between the scales and the silver crucible she placed the nautilus shell columella curl Wren had cut from their childhood shell. [225]
The curl was the shell's hidden central spiral, a calcium staircase built by an animal that outgrew room after room. [245]
For years June had called it a keepsake, because calling it equipment would have admitted how much she expected to lose. [266]
Before it stood her suns in a palette of resolve, each color a decision she might still be brave enough to make. [288]
Gold meant bless the voyage, red meant beg her to stay, and white meant open her hand without bargaining afterward. [308]
She had also prepared a violet vial for release, although her hand shook whenever she tried to imagine using it. [328]
Her teacher had named this method directed spontaneity: build the apparatus exactly, then obey the first true impulse it revealed. [348]
June hated that rule, because her first impulses were usually possessive, theatrical, and far less noble than her careful plans. [368]
Footsteps crossed the bridge above, scattering sparks through the grating, and Wren arrived smelling of salt, ink, and ship tar. [388]
She was late, breathless, and carrying no bag, which in Wren's language meant she had changed her mind three times. [408]
"You brought the whole sunrise," Wren said, looking at the suns, and June heard affection trying to disguise itself as teasing. [429]
"I brought options," June said. [434]
Wren knelt and touched the columella curl, turning it so its spiral caught the red breath of the nearest vent. [454]
"Tonight it needs a secret," June said, "spoken close enough to fog the silver and honest enough to bend light." [474]
Wren looked toward the harbor, where the survey ship waited with its lanterns doubled in black water. [491]
Then she leaned in, and everything important happened in the moment a secret is whispered between friends. [508]
The nexus caught the first syllable and held it, stretching one warm breath until sparks hung motionless around their faces. [528]
Outside that breath, gulls froze over the harbor and the bridge bell stopped with its iron tongue raised. [546]
Inside it, June had time to hear not only Wren's words but the fear moving underneath them like a tide. [566]
"I am not afraid of being forgotten," Wren whispered, "I am afraid you will preserve me so perfectly that returning becomes impossible." [588]
June felt the sentence enter her apparatus before it entered her heart, changing every reading she had prepared to trust. [608]
Her suns leaned toward Wren, bright with accumulated lunches, arguments, fevers nursed, maps corrected, and hands found in crowds. [627]
That was presence, she realized, not a fixed weight but a history of mutual alteration, and freezing it would falsify it. [648]
The revelation forced her to revalue the curl, the suns, and the friend whose living warmth no instrument should arrest. [668]
A keepsake was worthless because it demanded the past remain intact; as equipment, the spiral could guide change without preventing it. [689]
June lifted the violet vial, then obeyed a stronger impulse and poured all her colors around the brightest sun inside the curl. [711]
"Take it," she said, closing Wren's fingers around the glowing spiral, "and let it come back changed with you." [730]
Wren understood at once, because friendship had taught her June's grammar better than any alchemical manual ever could. [748]
"Then I will practice by returning differently," Wren whispered, "not as the girl who left, but as whoever the currents make." [769]
The held breath released; sparks fell, gulls cried, and the little sun inside the curl pulsed once, not preserved, but alive to change. [792]
