At first light, in the hush of a hidden sunrise, the echo gorge held its breath between two black cliffs. [20] Maren walked its narrow floor with a lantern that burned blue for the dead who had lost their way. [39] She was a lost soul collector, paid not in coin but in memories the living could no longer carry. [58] For nine years she had gathered whispers from drowned roads, fever rooms, and battlefields where names dissolved before bodies did. [78] She was modestly fearless, the kind of brave that knocked softly and entered disaster without needing anyone to applaud. [97] This morning her lantern swung hard toward the gorge wall, though no wind moved the dust at her feet. [116] Behind the stone, something laughed once, then tried desperately to turn the sound into crying. [131] Maren set down her collecting jars and listened, because laughter from the dead was rarer than gold and twice as dangerous. [152] Ordinary souls confessed and loosened, but a laughing soul often hid a wound so deep it refused every door. [171] She had come to believe her true work was to integrate laughter into healing, not banish it as mockery or madness. [192] The laugh came again, thin and bright, and her smallest jar cracked straight down its side. [208] Maren lifted the crocodile's tooth she wore on a cord and pressed it to the cold glass for steadiness. [227] A river spirit had given it to her after she pulled a drowned boy's soul from a flooded millrace without flinching. [248] The tooth remembered deep water and older hungers, so lies could not comfortably settle anywhere near its white point. [267] When she touched it to the jar, the laugh stopped hiding and became a name whispered backward. [284] Tobin Hale, who had played harp at weddings until fever stole his breath at nineteen, was trapped inside the rock. [304] To pursue him she would have to enter the gorge's memory, where the dead kept what daylight had edited out. [324] Maren unstrapped the small traveling harp from her back and found its highest string had slipped a quarter tone flat overnight. [345] She did not correct it, because lost souls heard truth more clearly through imperfection than through any polished prayer. [364] She began to pursue his echo through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp, each wrong interval opening a seam in the stone. [387] The gorge answered note for note, throwing her music back older, slower, and burdened with voices between the strings. [406] These were spectral confessions, the truths souls spoke only when no living face could shame them into silence. [424] A mother admitted she had loved sleep more than vigil beside her dying son, and wept with relief at last. [444] A soldier confessed he had hidden during the charge, then carried that cowardice like a second spine for sixty years. [464] Each confession lit the seam wider until Maren could see Tobin crouched in a chamber shaped like the inside of a bell. [486] He clutched his throat with both hands, laughing without joy whenever he tried to speak his final regret. [504] "I stole the bride's song," he managed at last, and the words themselves giggled and fled around the chamber. [523] For his wedding feast, Tobin had sold the melody his sister wrote, then claimed her silence afterward was only jealous grief. [544] When fever came, she sang that stolen tune at his bedside, forgiving him in a language he could not bear to hear. [566] So his soul had turned forgiveness into laughter, and laughter into a lock, because remorse felt safer when it sounded like mirth. [588] Maren did not demand an apology or offer easy absolution, since both would have slammed the bright lock tighter. [607] Instead she played his sister's melody on the wrong string and deliberately missed the note where his theft had begun. [627] Into that gap she placed her own raw memory of laughing helplessly at her father's funeral, when grief felt suddenly absurd and holy. [650] Tobin laughed then for real, startled and boyish, and the sound cracked into sobs that finally knew their proper name. [670] He confessed without giggling that he had wanted to be remembered more than he had wanted to be kind. [689] Maren touched the crocodile's tooth to his forehead, and it drank the false brightness while leaving the true laughter clean. [709] The chamber opened like an eye at noon, although outside the gorge the hidden sunrise still kept its amber secret. [729] Tobin stepped into her smallest jar willingly, still humming, and its cracked glass healed around a luminous ache that did not fade. [751] Maren sealed it with wax and, modestly fearless still, lifted her face to the returning echoes and laughed with him until healing sounded human. [775]
