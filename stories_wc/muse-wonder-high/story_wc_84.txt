<story>After the pilgrim arrives, the ancient observatory at dusk belongs to neither of them. [14] It stands above the salt flats, its dome split like a cracked eggshell. [27] Inside, the light has a brilliant dimness, bright at the edges and dark where it matters. [43] Sera Venn, the sharp-eyed drone pilot, watches the pilgrim climb the last hundred steps. [57] She has mapped this ruin for nine evenings, hunting for its missing pieces. [70] Her drone hangs beside her, one rotor ticking against the cooling air. [82] The pilgrim reaches the threshold and stops, perpetually surprised by everything he sees. [95] His mouth opens at the telescope, at the moths, at Sera herself. [107] She is used to men arriving certain, and his wonder disarms her. [119] "You fly that thing by starlight?" he asks. [127] "I fly it by following star trails," she says. [136] She taps her tablet, where long exposures arc like white scratches overhead. [148] The drone learns where the dome blocks heaven, and where heaven leaks through. [161] The pilgrim holds out a cloth bundle with both scarred hands. [172] Inside the cloth lies a type foundry matrix chip, blackened and cold. [184] It is a brass square that once stamped a single letter into hot lead. [198] Sera turns it over and feels her sharp eyes snag on the engraving. [211] The chip bears not a letter but a tiny, toothed frequency dial. [223] Her drone dips, as if bowing, and its camera finds the same teeth missing elsewhere. [238] For nine evenings she has catalogued the observatory gear train slot by slot. [251] Three slots gape empty along the main wheel, exactly this size and shape. [264] She assumed vandals had stolen them for scrap during the long abandonment. [276] Now she understands the builders hid them with trusted families instead. [287] The pilgrim, Tomas Rill, inherited one piece and walked six hundred miles home. [300] He does not know the observatory, yet his boots knew every turning. [312] That is why Sera was hired to decode the frequency of belonging. [324] The valley towns hear it in fillings, in well water, in dreams. [336] It calls scattered families back each generation, but lately the call stutters. [348] Without all its teeth, the transmitter speaks a word with letters missing. [360] People arrive at wrong valleys, wrong doors, wrong graves, and weep there. [372] Sera wants the whole word restored before the autumn migration begins. [383] Tomas wants only to know why his father wept at that sound. [395] He unwraps two more chips from the lining of his coat. [406] His perpetual surprise deepens when the brass warms in his palm. [417] Sera slots each chip home, and the wheel shivers like a waking animal. [430] Still the dome will not turn, because its pull chain snapped decades ago. [443] Sera launches the drone through the cracked dome into the violet sky. [455] She ties its tow cable around the dome rib with three practiced loops. [468] On her tablet, the first stars smear into trails as Earth turns. [480] She flies by those trails, correcting for drift no compass can feel. [492] The drone leans back, cable taut, and begins to pull the dome open. [505] Metal screams, dust falls in sheets, and Tomas laughs in startled delight. [517] Through the widening slit, the observatory finally sees its own forgotten sky. [529] The wheel below answers with a low tone that climbs through their bones. [542] Sera adjusts the last chip by a hair, listening for the stutter to vanish. [556] The tone steadies, deepens, and resolves into something almost like a name. [568] On her screen, the frequency graph spells a valley word in clean peaks. [581] It is not coordinates, as the town council had confidently expected. [592] It is an invitation, repeated in the old foundry code of the builders. [605] Tomas reads the peaks aloud, his voice shaking on the missing syllables restored. [618] Below them, valley windows light one by one across the darkening flats. [630] Doors open in villages Sera has only ever mapped from above. [641] A bell answers, then another, then a train whistle far to the south. [654] People are turning homeward without knowing why their feet suddenly remember. [665] Tomas stares at the returning lights, surprised as a child at snow. [677] Sera, who notices everything, finally notices she is crying with him. [688] She takes his hand and together they pull the transmitter lever down. [700] The observatory sings its whole name into the brilliant dimness above them. [712] For the first time, no piece of the call is missing. [723] For the first time, neither of them feels like a missing piece. [735]</story>
