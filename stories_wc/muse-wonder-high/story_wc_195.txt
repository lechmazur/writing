Ser Ilvar had not spoken to the village in seven years, not since he returned without his squire and hung his sword above a cold hearth. [26] The withdrawn knight tended only his bees, mended his own roof, and left coins on the miller step before dawn so no one need thank him. [52] Grief had made a discipline of him, and discipline had kept the grief from spilling. [67] Beyond his cottage stood the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night, though noon often burned white over the valley. [87] Its glass panes drank the sun and gave back starlight, and inside it, plum trees grew silver fruit that chimed when the wind found a crack. [113] The old queen had planted it to study how metal might ripen, and how mercy might be grafted onto a harder root. [135] After her death, the gardeners fled its changing seasons, but Ilvar kept the key because he had once sworn to guard her door. [158] That night, while frost formed inside his boots by the fire, he heard music from the greenhouse where no musician lived. [179] It was a waltz played slightly too slow, as if remembered by hands that had forgotten joy but not the steps. [200] He took his sword down for the first time in years and felt its weight accuse him of rest. [219] Inside, the air smelled of warm copper, wet earth, and plums left too long in brandy. [235] Stars moved across the glass ceiling even when he blinked, and the trees leaned toward sounds that had not yet happened. [256] This was not a trick of grief, for the silver fruit cast shadows upward, a factually mystical property the queen had recorded without embarrassment. [280] On the potting bench lay an abandoned marionette theater program, its paper soft as moth wing and printed with tonight's date in wet ink. [304] The title read The Knight Who Stayed, and beneath it his own name appeared in a hand he recognized as his squire's. [326] Tobin had died at nineteen holding a broken gate against men who never learned his name. [342] Ilvar had buried him under plum blossom because there were no flags left clean enough. [357] Now the program listed Tobin as puppeteer, and the first act was set to begin as reality shifts beneath the trees. [378] Between the rows, a small stage had grown from roots overnight, its curtain woven from shed snakeskin and winter ivy. [398] Marionettes hung above it, each carved with a face Ilvar had loved, feared, or failed. [413] The smallest wore Tobin's grin, too wide and earnest, and its wooden hands already moved though no strings rose into the dark. [435] Ilvar raised his sword, then lowered it when the puppet knight beside Tobin bowed with his own stiff courtesy. [454] He understood then that the greenhouse was not haunted but horticultural, growing scenes from unspent lives as other trees grow fruit. [475] Each silver plum held a possible hour, warm and sealed, waiting for a hand brave enough to open it without devouring it. [497] The queen had sought not gold from lead, but to capture the shape of possibility before sorrow made men call it false. [519] That was his true commission, Ilvar realized, and he had mistaken guarding a door for guarding the purpose behind it. [539] The Tobin puppet pointed to the largest plum, which pulsed like a heart embarrassed to be seen. [556] Along its skin ran fine seams and brass clasps, the alchemy of a fruit that wanted choosing rather than picking. [576] Ilvar knelt, laid his sword across his knees like an offered bridge, and learned how to unsnap each clasp without breaking the skin beneath. [600] He worked through forgotten patterns his mother had taught his fingers for mending fishing nets, patterns Tobin had later borrowed for tying banners before battle. [625] Each clasp opened on a different life, not better in every part, but honest in the parts he had refused to imagine. [647] In one, Tobin limped and laughed and grew stubborn with age, and Ilvar felt pain move through him like weather through a repaired roof. [671] In another, Ilvar laid down knighthood, taught village children to prune silver trees, and was teased at supper by friends who knew his silences. [695] None promised that loss could be undone, and that mercy made the visions bearable enough to love. [712] The stage curtain trembled, and every marionette turned toward him, waiting not for orders but for permission. [729] Ilvar pressed his forehead to the warm plum and gave a murmured allegiance not to the queen, nor to the dead, but to the living world that still asked his hands. [760] A shivering bliss ran through him as the fruit opened like dawn inside the endless night, and he rose to unlock the gate. [783]
