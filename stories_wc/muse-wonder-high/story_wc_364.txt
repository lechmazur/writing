On the orchard planet of Thallo, floating rock arches crossed the azure sky above endless rows of sleeping apple trees. [20]
Each arch held a vein of pale iron that pushed against the ground and kept the stone aloft over the orchards below. [42]
When miners emptied those veins through abandoned mine shafts, the weakened arches began to crack, groan, and sometimes fall. [61]
One fell on the morning the magician died, crushing the council hall, the village forge, and the oldest grove in a single white cloud of dust. [87]
Beneath that rubble slept Wren, the sister of Joss Hale, the meek blacksmith who apologized to his anvil whenever he struck it too hard. [111]
Joss spoke so softly that customers leaned across his counter, yet his hands never shook when hot iron demanded precision. [131]
He wanted only to coax resilience from rubble, to prove that broken trees and fallen stone could learn to bear weight again. [153]
Thallo had survived its long winters through bartered awakenings, an old bargain taught by the dead magician and enforced by the council. [175]
Every spring, one healthy volunteer slept through the whole season so the groves could wake, blossom, and feed everyone else. [195]
The magician's silk made the bargain possible, because a strip laid across closed eyes could carry wakefulness from one sleeper to another. [217]
This year the silk lay torn in the magician's dead hand, and one end still trailed toward Wren under the stones. [238]
The council declared that Joss must choose before dawn whether Wren or the oldest grove would receive her awakening. [257]
If Wren woke, the grove would stay asleep and the village would face an empty harvest and a hungry winter. [277]
If the grove woke, Wren would dream under rubble until spring returned her, thin and weak, if it returned her at all. [299]
Joss listened with his head bowed, thanked the council in a whisper, and then made the most deliberate refusal of his life. [321]
He would accept no bargain that required one life to be spent like a coin for another. [338]
Instead, quietly determined, he picked up the magician's silk, his smallest hammer, a lamp, and a coil of copper wire. [358]
The abandoned mine shafts still ran beneath the orchards, linking root cellars, old iron seams, and the hollow hearts of the fallen arches. [381]
Joss had mapped them as a boy, not from courage, but because the cool tunnels were the one place nobody demanded he speak louder. [405]
Now he crawled through them with silk tied around his wrist, following the faint tapping Wren made in her sleep against a buried pipe. [429]
He found her in a side chamber where fallen beams formed a narrow shelter and living roots had pushed through the cracked ceiling. [452]
The roots belonged to the oldest grove above, and they trembled whenever Wren breathed, as if tree and girl shared one interrupted dream. [475]
That sight gave Joss his answer, though understanding it came slowly and left him shaking in the lamplight. [493]
The silk did not create wakefulness, so perhaps it did not need to steal a whole awakening from a single sleeper. [514]
Perhaps every sleeper in the village, every root in the grove, and every branch that still waited in cold darkness could lend a little. [538]
Using abandoned mine shafts as his loom, Joss threaded silk and copper wire from Wren to the roots, then from chamber to chamber beneath the village. [564]
He worked without raising his voice, asking each household through written notes to tie one thread to a bedpost before dawn. [585]
Dawn on Thallo arrived between understanding and confusion, in a brief blue interval when sleepers knew their dreams clearly but waking minds had not yet misplaced them. [612]
In that interval, the silk could not tell where one awakening ended and another began. [627]
Joss waited beside Wren with his hammer resting on the buried pipe, counting her breaths while the tunnels filled with pale azure light. [650]
When the first dream bell sounded from the council tower, he struck the pipe once, softly, and sent that single note through every shaft. [674]
Thread by thread, the village lent minutes rather than lives, a yawn from a baker, a drowsy blink from a child, a slow waking from an old miner. [702]
No one lost a season, but everyone rose a little later, rubbing their eyes and laughing at dreams they almost remembered. [723]
Wren opened her eyes as the roots above her shivered, then pushed white blossoms through the rubble toward the morning. [743]
By noon, Joss had forged the fallen iron into crutches for the cracked arch and braces for the newborn branches. [763]
The village raised a banner of magician's silk against the sky, its azure defiance bright above blossom, stone, and smoke. [783]
