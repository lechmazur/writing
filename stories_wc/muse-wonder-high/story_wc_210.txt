<story>In Paper-Lantern Cove, the houses still bow toward the harbor, though no one has lived in them for three years. [20] Only Soren Vale remains, the deserted alchemist whom the town left behind when it chose the sky over the shore. [40] It is the third winter after the last wish is granted, and the cove has learned how complete a granted wish can be. [63] In the hunger winter before that granting, the people lit ten thousand lanterns with one shared prayer for a life without need. [85] By morning, every boat and bed stood empty, because the prayer had carried the people to a shore where nothing was ever lacking. [108] Soren refused to light his lantern, not from courage, but because he feared a kindness that removed all choosing. [127] On the shrine above the harbor sits an amber bottle with no opening, left by the power that answered the town. [148] Inside its sealed glass, a miniature cove glows, complete with tiny lanterns, busy markets, and his wife Mara laughing at their door. [170] If he holds the bottle at dusk, the small figures live their easy day again and Mara whispers that he can join them by wishing only once. [197] He knows the voice is only the wish remembering her shape, yet for three winters he has gone to listen whenever the wind cut deepest. [222] This spring he wants more than company, because he has sworn to reimagine an entire civilization that can survive without wishes. [243] His old master taught that amber creates only through working memory, the brief inner room where a mind holds several living details at once. [267] A copied list makes nothing, a spoken charm makes nothing, and a detail forgotten before the lantern dies makes a life unable to begin. [291] To conceive a person in the amber, Soren must carry every needed trait together until the glass accepts them as one pattern. [313] His first attempts fail because he tries to restore the old cove exactly, street by street, face by face. [332] There are too many names for one mind, and every time the harbor bell tolls in his memory, another trade slips away. [354] On the seventh night, kneeling among cold alembics, he understands that no civilization should depend on a single memory any more than on a single wish. [380] He will conceive seven founders instead, each carrying one craft, one kindness, and one question they must teach to someone else. [401] He imagines a grower who shares seed, a smith who repairs before replacing, a sailor who maps danger for strangers, and four others shaped around useful mercy. [428] To hold them, he carries the bottle through the empty streets and gives each founder a house whose smell, tools, and broken stair refresh a fading detail. [455] The walk does not remember for him, but its turning corners bring each trait back just as working memory begins to drop it. [478] At his own house, the last station, Mara waits in the amber light with his dead life arranged exactly as he left it. [501] She offers him the easiest pattern of all, her face copied whole, her voice restored, and their old rooms warmed against loneliness. [523] Soren sets the bottle down, closes his eyes, and rejects false comfort because a rescued past would teach the new people only how to beg the sky again. [551] He keeps Mara's patience, learned from real years beside her, but lets her face remain the past and walks on without it. [573] At the shrine he lights one plain lantern, lifts the amber bottle, and runs the seven founders through his mind while the flame leans in the harbor wind. [601] Seed shared, iron mended, reefs marked, broth carried, walls plumbed, stories divided, and lessons given freely circle faster until they stop feeling like separate burdens. [626] For one clear moment, all seven lives stand complete in his working memory, distinct yet bound by the needs they will meet in one another. [651] The amber does not break, but softens around his hands, and the sealed glass becomes a bright doorway no wider than a held breath. [675] Seven strangers step onto the shrine stones, blinking at a real sea, already knowing their names and crafts but none of his memories. [698] The grower, Ines, first asks why he fallowed the best field, then plants it before he can explain his fear of another failed harvest. [722] Their choices please him more than obedience would, because a civilization begins when conceived people answer back. [739] Through summer, their ghost town revival raises roofs, mends nets, and hangs paper lanterns for guidance, not prayer. [757] By winter, passing sailors follow those lights into harbor, hear seven trades taught in seven houses, and some choose to stay. [778] Soren leaves the bottle whole on the shrine and goes down to a shared dinner. [793]</story>
