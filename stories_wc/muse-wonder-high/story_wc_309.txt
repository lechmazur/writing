<story>Tobin Vale was an underachieving mage in an age when creativity gets automated, and the Guild had run out of grand places to hide him. [25] They sent him to keep the icebound spire above the clouds, where wind filed the towers to needles and inspectors never stayed long. [48] His duties were simple because his record was not. [57] He had failed transmutation twice, turned a dean into a teapot once, and graduated only because the hall needed repairs he accidentally provided. [80] In the attic, dreamlooms invented poems, murals, and flawless spells all night, doing easily the work he could never match. [100] Below, the cloud sea closed over the world, leaving only one garden within a hundred miles, the foolish little one on the roof. [123] It held six frost roses, a bench for nobody, and a stone arch that led nowhere and opened onto weather. [143] It was a garden folly, built by the founder for delight alone, and Tobin loved it because no loom could measure its purpose. [166] One grey morning, the arch shivered in a gust and its garden folly keystone slipped a finger width from the crown. [187] If the keystone fell, the arch would fall, and by charter a spire without its folly was a warehouse to be sold for parts. [211] Tobin asked the valley for help, and the dreamlooms instantly offered nine hundred perfect replacement designs. [227] Each required silver grown in sunlight, phoenix resin, or stone that had never existed above the clouds. [244] The machines could invent anything except a use for what the cold roof had, which was ice, rope, and one plain granite wedge in the shed. [270] Tobin stared at the wedge until the obvious became a dare. [281] More than rescue or promotion, he wanted to find the sacred in the simple, though he would never say so aloud. [302] The folly stood across a cloud gulf, and the roof bridge between had iced into a ribbon too slick for boots. [323] Only one old method crossed such a place without metal or fuel, and Tobin had failed it at school like everything else. [345] Mist walking required no power, only slow bare feet, steady breath, and a refusal to look down and argue with terror. [366] Its principle was the pride of the spire, for mist under patient weight accommodates impossibilities and briefly holds what should fall. [387] The founder had built the folly on that principle, its stones leaning on cold air and trust as much as on each other. [410] Tobin packed the wedge, a hammer, and a flask of tea, taking nothing the looms suggested because simple tools steadied his hands. [432] At the gulf edge, he unlaced his boots and set his bare soles against a cold that burned like bright iron. [453] His automated safety line blinked green above the harness buckle and promised to catch him sensibly. [469] But its tension would drag sideways and break the mist before his weight could settle. [484] He took one breath for courage and one for stubbornness, then reached behind him to unstrap the harness and let the line fall. [507] He stepped onto nothing, and nothing received him with the soft resistance of a held note. [523] Each step sank, steadied, and bloomed under his foot with lingering luminance, the pale gold light mist retained after sunrise. [543] It was neither daylight nor darkness, but a brilliant dimness where every crystal showed its edge and nothing hurt his eyes. [564] He thought of loom spells arriving finished and leaving no warmth in his hands, and he understood why this crossing felt different. [586] Halfway across, wind rose, the mist thinned to lace, and below his toes the clouds opened on a drop without a visible bottom. [609] His school failure rose with the wind, neat in memory, a red mark on a perfect page. [626] He did not run, because running was a demand, and instead he thanked the mist for each step as if it could refuse. [649] It did not refuse, and the far roof received his frozen feet in a silence that felt like approval. [668] The slipped keystone was cracked through, so Tobin lifted it free and set the plain granite wedge in its place. [688] He expected a spell, a chime, or at least an argument from the stone, but there was only the small tap of his hammer. [712] The arch settled, held, and leaned once more on air, trust, and a wedge nobody would ever praise in a catalogue. [733] The dreamlooms hummed on above him, inventing wonders, but the simple stone stayed warm under his palm long after their light faded. [755]</story>
