<story>At noon the sun made the whole city shine, and that was when Sela feared it most. [17] Light poured over white streets, salt roofs, and market awnings, yet every shadow seemed too thin to hide in. [36] Below the streets, three gargantuan turtles swam in slow formation, and the city built on the shells of gargantuan turtles rose and fell with their breathing. [62] Bridges of rope linked shell to shell, bells rang when a flipper turned, and houses leaned together like passengers on one patient boat. [85] Sela worked above the harbor as a spiral-shell cartographer, drawing each new coastline inside shells that curled like listening ears. [105] When a turtle changed course, old maps lied, so she scraped them clean and began again without complaint. [123] People called her quietly driven because she never argued at council, never wept at memorials, and never stopped measuring the horizon. [144] Her shell calendars began each year with the same line, written after the gate rusts shut in the harbor ledger, because that rust had sealed the city from every shore. [174] In that first sealed winter, Sela's brother Joren led a lost expedition beyond the turtles to find a current that might carry them home. [198] Forty sailors took two low boats under the gate before it closed, and none of their lanterns ever answered the harbor bells. [220] A month later, the sea returned only Joren's reed whistle, tied to a buoy and worn smooth by salt. [239] Sela kept it on a cord against her ribs, where its silence felt heavier than any storm bell. [257] The watchers appeared that same spring, standing far off where the sun broke into white pieces on the water. [276] They never waved, never sailed closer, and never cast reflections, though at noon their tall shapes darkened the glare like cracks in glass. [299] Council called them heat spirits and forbade questions, so the city learned to lower its eyes while it worked in the brightness. [321] Sela looked, but through her trade rather than defiance, recording each watcher's place at the edge of every unfinished map. [341] For ten years she filled spiral shells with noon marks, patient as tide, because she needed to question the silent watchers on the horizon. [365] The turtles also spoke in patterns, pressing ridges of shell against one another in slow sequences that steered the herd. [385] Expedition crews had answered them through pattern languages of whistle calls, three short notes for hunger, two long notes for danger, and spirals of sound for turning. [412] Sela began to suspect that distance had flattened the watchers into another such language, waiting for someone literate enough to read them standing still. [436] She laid ten years of shells in a spiral on her roof and walked it at noon, one step for each bright day of watching. [461] The marks refused a coastline, yet they made a sequence, and the sequence repeated the expedition call for crews trapped beyond sight of home. [485] Every seventh day the tallest watcher shifted left, every ninth day two figures closed the gap between them, and together their stillness spelled distress without a single lifted arm. [514] On the roof she raised the reed whistle, afraid that hope itself might be a kind of haunting, and blew the old question used by lost crews. [541] Who holds the far shell, and why do you not come home, the notes asked, bright and thin under the sun. [562] For one held breath nothing changed, and the dread of the city seemed to stand inside her chest with them. [582] Then the tallest watcher lifted both arms, the others opened a path of bodies, and their new pattern named a dead turtle stranded beyond the current. [608] Sela read survivors in their spacing, Joren in the tallest figure's familiar stance, and warning in the slow crossing of their arms. [630] Their boats could not return because the current beyond the gate ran in a bright circle, always carrying them back to the dead shell and its empty water. [658] The answer came next in whistle echoes from the horizon, faint but exact, and Sela wrote each answering note inside her largest shell. [681] To reach them, she would have to steer the living turtles against the circle, yet the rusted gate chain still held the lead shells in their harbor turn. [709] At dusk, though dread stayed sunwashed in her memory of noon, she set her chisel to the gate chain and chose to crack its weakest rusted link. [736] When the link gave way, the turtles lifted their great heads toward the watchers, and Sela lifted the whistle to ask Joren, at last, how to bring them home. [765]</story>
