<story>When the geomagnetic storm hit Port Vale at dusk, every compass in the container stacks began to spin toward steel instead of north. [23] Overhead, auroras rippled green above the cranes, and below them the towering labyrinth of shipping containers groaned as its catwalks swayed in charged wind. [47] Joss Mirek, the night maintenance worker assigned to keep that labyrinth passable, stood still and listened to the metal breathe. [67] Radios spat static, drones dropped from their lanes, and the port loudspeakers ordered everyone to shout their positions so control could track them. [90] Joss knew those shouts would ricochet between container walls until direction meant nothing. [103] He could identify people's walks through those walls, feeling a limp, a hurry, or a heavy boot heel in the vibrations under his palms. [127] That was how he heard the wrong walk at 7:14, too light for a docker and stopping every few steps to listen. [149] He pressed his ear to a blue container and recognized Pip Alden, the sixteen-year-old apprentice who dragged his left toe after an old skateboard break. [174] Pip should have been in the gatehouse, but his steps were circling somewhere deep beyond the Taurus rows. [192] Joss unfolded the paper stack map from his chest pocket, then folded it again along its soft, familiar creases. [211] Digital charts died with the storm, but his folded maps changed with the stacks because each new fold made distant aisles touch on paper exactly as their container walls touched in steel. [243] For nine years he had redrawn lanes after every crane move, creasing the sheet so sound, shortcuts, and contact points lined up. [265] Foreman Dara Venn found him with her flashlight already dimming. [275] "Your boy is lost in my stacks," Joss said. [284] "Then shout until he answers," she snapped. [291] Joss shook his head and pointed at the loudspeaker still bellowing names into useless echoes. [306] "Give me one challenge instead," he said. [313] "If I bring Pip out before the storm peaks, without radios or shouting, you retire the loudspeakers and let crews use the whisper line." [337] She looked at the spinning compass on her wrist and called it a wager to prove that whispers travel farther than shouts. [359] Joss accepted with a nod that looked impulsive, though he had already chosen his first three folds, his first three walls, and the order in which he would touch them. [389] From his other pocket he took the brass zodiac coin his mother had carried for luck, its twelve signs worn bright around the rim. [413] The coin was not luck to Joss, because each sign matched a stack row, and the tiny notch under Aries marked the row where sound carried best. [440] He also carried a long iron key with no apparent lock, issued to a maintenance chief thirty years before any living supervisor arrived. [463] New workers laughed at the key, but Joss knew its flat teeth made it a perfect tongue for steel. [482] He laid the coin flat against the blue container, set the key teeth against its edge, and put the key bow against the bone behind his ear. [509] Pip's dragging step came through at once, clearer than any shout, because iron and brass carried the vibration without letting the aisles scramble it. [533] Joss refolded the map until Pip's guessed circle touched the Aries notch, and the crease showed him three touching walls between them instead of eleven blocked lanes. [560] He moved fast, apparently choosing turns at random, slapping a wall here and ducking a swinging hook there, but every turn followed the fold under his thumb. [587] At the first touching wall he whispered Pip's name against the coin, then waited with the key pressed to his ear. [608] Nothing answered except rain beginning to tick on the roofs and the storm humming in every cable. [625] Joss smiled, because Pip would be scared, and scared boys held still before they risked a sound. [642] He whispered again, lower rather than louder, telling Pip to knock once if the voice seemed to come from inside the steel itself. [665] One knock arrived, so faint through the air that Dara frowned, yet so sharp through the key that Joss felt it in his jaw. [689] He folded the map the other way, matching the knock to the Scorpio row on his coin, where a fallen light had blocked the only marked exit. [716] He followed the last touching wall, slipped the key into a seam no one had called a lock, and opened the maintenance hatch behind the fallen light. [743] Pip crawled out whispering, because Joss had reached him through steel long before any shout could have found its way home. [764] Dara heard that whisper, looked up at the useless loudspeakers, and ordered them taken down at dawn. [781]</story>
