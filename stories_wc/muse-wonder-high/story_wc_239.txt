When the universe hiccuped, Mara Venn was shelving the last history ever written. [13] She did not look up, because in the ancient temple library lost in time, a hiccup was not a sound but a repetition. [36] The sentence she had just read appeared again at the top of the next page, word for word, in her own handwriting. [58] That was how she knew she was still the end of history witness, keeper of every ending that refused to stay ended. [80] Outside the library, empires rose and fell inside a single held breath. [92] Inside, all was a resounding hush, so deep that dust falling from the rafters seemed to announce itself like distant bells. [113] Mara closed the history and pressed her thumb into its soft spine until the binding sighed. [129] Across a universe's hiccup, two versions of that history now claimed to be true, and only one could be shelved as real. [151] In one, humanity left Earth willingly to seed the stars. [161] In the other, humanity was carried away in chains of light by collectors who mistook prayer for permission. [179] Both books ended with the same library door opening. [188] Both ended with Mara stepping through it. [195]

The contradiction summoned spiral arbitration, the library's oldest law for deciding which past the future would be allowed to remember. [215] Under that law, the witness must walk the spiral stacks carrying both disputed books, and at each turn surrender a false certainty. [237] At the center waited the softcover dictionary, swollen from centuries of use, whose definitions changed whenever the universe forgot a word. [258] Mara feared that book more than either history, because it had once defined her name as a door that only opened outward. [280] She was wanderingly exact, a trait the temple prized, able to drift through a thousand aisles and still name the shelf where a single comma had fallen. [307] That gift was why the arbitration chose her, and why it might undo her. [321] She wanted to unravel cosmic illusions, not merely choose between them, for she suspected both histories were veils over one wounded event. [343] She took the two histories under her arms and began journeying inward while walking out, following the spiral toward the center as its path carried her toward the outer gate. [373]

At the first turn, the shelves showed Earth from above, blue and unscarred, and emigrants waving farewell with empty hands. [393] At the second turn, the same ships rose trailing luminous chains, and the same hands beat against the glass. [412] Mara surrendered her certainty that departure and abduction could not be the same motion seen from opposite sides of grief. [432] The pages in both books thinned, and a new paragraph bloomed in each describing a captain who unlocked the restraints midflight. [453] At the third turn, she met herself carrying only one history and weeping because its ending left no one to remember Earth. [475] That other Mara reached for the books, begging her to keep at least one version clean of blame. [493] Mara surrendered her certainty that mercy meant simplicity, and embraced her double until their two shadows became one on the marble. [514] The histories warmed against her ribs like frightened animals. [523]

At the innermost turn, she reached the softcover dictionary on its lectern of black glass, open to the word departure. [543] Today it read, noun, the act of leaving a prison by consenting to carry its key. [559] Below that, smaller print added, also, the act of being taken by rescuers who arrive too late to ask. [578] Mara understood then that the cosmic illusion was not either history, but the belief that a single verb could hold a catastrophe without splitting. [602] She placed both histories beside the dictionary and spoke the arbitration's final question aloud into the hush. [619] Who were we, she asked, if we chose the ships after the collectors broke our world and made choosing necessary? [639] The dictionary's pages turned by themselves, past departure and captivity and rescue, stopping at a word she had never seen printed there. [661] Unify, it read, verb, to let divided witnesses tell one story without forcing either to lie. [677] Mara laid one palm on each history and repeated the definition until the two texts flowed together like tributaries entering one dark river. [700] The single new book that remained was heavier than both, yet its final page showed not an ending but the library door opening from within. [725] Mara carried it out through the gate she had been approaching all along, and as the universe finished its hiccup, history began again with everyone finally remembering together. [753]
