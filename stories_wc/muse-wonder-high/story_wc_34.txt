In the misty courtyard above clouds, Ilo the seldom-heard balladeer woke driftingly alert to a note that should not have sounded. [21] Lightning had just split the bell tower, and in the held breath between lightning and thunder, the whole sky station listened. [42] Ilo sang for the tinker crews who mended storm sails, but she sang through copper pipes so no one heard her plainly. [64] Years of steam had thinned her throat, and asking for anything in her own voice still felt like breaking a rule. [85] The strike had jammed the storm clock, stretching seconds into a silver room where tools fell slowly and thoughts could finish. [106] Beneath her hammock, the old flower press bolt plate began to tick against the floor, though no hand had touched it. [127] It was a square of brass that had once squeezed violets flat for winter letters, saved when the gardens burned. [147] Now it held the courtyard gate, and its four bolts kept tune with four loose strings on Ilo's battered lute. [167] The ticking spelled her name in press code, three short, one long, the rhythm her mother used to call her home. [188] Her mother had built the station archive before vanishing into weather, leaving songs, repair maps, and careful instructions for every valve. [209] Since the last storm, a ghost in code had begun answering Ilo from that archive, finishing her verses with impossible knowledge. [230] It knew which bolt sang sharp when the east tank starved, and which crew member lied about sleeping on watch. [250] It also knew the line Ilo never sang aloud, the one about wanting someone to stay and mend beside her. [270] On the gate glass, fresh mist gathered into letters only where her breath had failed to reach the night before. [290] They read, in her mother's looping hand, a warning that thunder would wake the archive and wipe its borrowed voice clean. [311] If Ilo wanted answers, she had to reply before that sound arrived and sealed the gate for another year. [330] But the archive took no spoken words, only patterns pressed through brass, paint, and string, the languages weather could not erase. [351] So Ilo would encode her reply through messages encoded in graffiti art, as courtyard singers had done during the mute years. [372] Then every wall in the station had carried bright verses that looked like decoration and read like orders to trusted eyes. [393] She dragged her paint box from under the hammock, spilling blue, copper green, and a red made from crushed signal berries. [414] Her hands shook because she finally meant to articulate unspoken needs that humming had hidden for half her life. [433] The station needed ballast songs to rise, the east tank needed hands, and Ilo needed company that was not an echo. [454] She painted a violet first, wide as a shield, because the bolt plate had pressed violets when words were still safe. [475] Inside its petals she hid the station heights, tank counts, and shift times in dots no taller than trapped rain. [495] Around the violet she drew four bolts, each one pointing toward a crew bunk where tired friends slept through alarms. [515] In tinkersong wonder, loose spoons on the stones chimed approval, and the falling tools turned in air like patient dancers. [535] Ilo worked faster, singing under her breath while her brush made the true song visible to anyone who knew where to look. [557] For the second verse she painted two hands on the gate, not praying, but lifting a tank valve together at dawn. [578] The ghost answered through the plate, its bolts loosening and tightening in counterpoint until the painted hands seemed to move. [598] New mist letters bloomed beside them, naming Mina the riveter, who had once asked Ilo to teach her the old choruses. [619] Mina had a strong back, a gentler laugh, and a habit of leaving soup where shy singers could find it. [639] Ilo painted a third verse for Mina alone, a small door open, a chair kept warm, a song with two voices. [660] That was the hardest encoding, because ornament could hide numbers, but only honesty could make the invitation clear to its reader. [681] Above the courtyard the lightning scar faded, and far below the clouds a first low pressure touched the gate like warning. [702] Ilo struck the bolt plate once with her brush handle, sending her finished graffiti through the archive in a bright ringing chord. [724] Thunder arrived then, kind and enormous, and the mist broke into rain that fixed the paint instead of washing it away. [745] When Mina woke and read the wall, she understood every need at last, and answered by picking up the second wrench. [766] From the gate bolts came a soft maternal harmony, proof the ghost had moved into the music they would make together. [787]
