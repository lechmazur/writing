Fifty years after violence becomes impossible on the water, Low Tern still kept its harbor knives sheathed in tar and its grudges sharp in the mouth. [26]
The whales had seen to that during the Harrow War, rising under fleets and singing until cannonballs softened, blades dulled, and every raised fist forgot how to close. [54]
What remained was argument, stubborn and loud, which suited Juna fine. [65]
She was the graceful dock worker of the sublittoral fringe, crossing slick pontoons on bare toes, balancing crates of salt plums as if they weighed less than gossip. [93]
They called her grace luck, because it was easier than asking why her eyes stayed open underwater or why she never gasped coming up from the kelp beds. [121]
Juna had a pirate grandmother and a whale somewhere under her skin, and she had spent twenty nine years pretending the second was only a story. [147]
From that grandmother she kept one thing in a cedar box: a pirate's whalebone scrimshaw, yellowed, palm long, carved with a coastline that matched no chart. [173]
Its edge showed an island shaped like a sleeping hand, five inlets for fingers, and under it her grandmother had cut the words: Thren holds those who hold nothing back. [203]
The scrimshaw did more than remember a place; in Low Tern they swore it unlocks guarded hearts, and Juna had tested that swear often enough to fear it. [231]
Hold it while you lied, and your tongue ached; hold it while someone else lied, and the carved whale on its back wept a bead of seawater down your wrist. [261]
On the morning the tide went out farther than prayer, Juna found the coastline on her palm alive and wet. [281]
Beyond the fringe, where the water thinned over black rock and bright anemones, Thren was rising again. [298]
The disappearing island came once every nine years, for a single turning of the tide, bearing black sand, hot springs, and trees that fruited only for the unashamed. [326]
This time, her brother Tomas meant to keep it. [335]
Tomas was harbormaster now, broad, sleepless, tender only in the way a storm door is tender when it slams before the rain can enter. [359]
He had sunk three anchor chains into the outer rocks, each thick as his wrist, and meant to marry Thren to Low Tern before it could sink again. [387]
"If this port dies, it dies polite," he told her, tightening a coupling while she held the wrench he kept dropping. [408]
"If you chain a tide, it drowns the town," she said, and slipped the warm scrimshaw into his bleeding hand before he noticed the cut. [433]
His fingers closed despite himself, and the carved whale wept. [443]
"Say it plain, Tomas," she said, gentler, which only made him glare harder. [456]
He tried to hand it back, failed, and the truth came out of him like a hook from a lip: their mother had not drowned by accident off Thren. [485]
She had gone down singing to the whale that sired her children, Tomas said, and he had chained his grief to the harbor office, the ledgers, and Juna herself ever since. [516]
"You think I want an island," he snapped, wrapping her scraped knuckles with his own bandage while she cursed him for a tyrant, a coward, and the only family worth feeding. [547]
"I want one thing the sea cannot take while I am watching." [559]
That was how they loved, in combative tenderness, every insult set like a splint around a break neither would name. [579]
Then, from under Thren, under the harbor, under the soles of Juna's feet, came the call. [595]
It was not loud; it loosened fillings, bucket seams, and the old iron certainty in Tomas that holding fast was the same as holding dear. [620]
Juna understood, all at once, what the island was: not land at all, but the broad back of the world whale's mate, resting briefly where the deep leaned toward the shore. [651]
The chains were not anchoring Thren; they were pinning a living throat shut. [664]
Juna ran, not away from Tomas but past him, kicking off her boots, her dock apron, and twenty nine years of careful human shame in one clean motion. [692]
She dove from the pontoon and, by the call of a whale from the deep, answered with the voice she had locked behind her teeth since childhood. [719]
The song shook the couplings open one by one, and Tomas, cursing her with tears in his eyes, hauled the last lever so she could detach the chain from Thren's scarred skin. [751]
Freed, Thren sank without anger, and Juna surfaced laughing, at last willing to find freedom in embracing one's true nature, half dock girl, wholly whale kin. [777]
