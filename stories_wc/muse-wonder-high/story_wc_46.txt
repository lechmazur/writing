Liora had been an earthbound star watcher for seven years, ever since the fall from the observatory stairs broke her ankle and left her unable to climb to the great telescope. [31]
She still charted the heavens for the fishing village, but now she did it from her cottage garden with a brass astrolabe and charts weighted against the wind. [59]
Below the garden, a serpentine grotto in mist wound through the cliff, its two mouths joining the village to the only safe landing beach. [83]
At low tide children crossed through it with messages and baskets, but after sunset its wet turns swallowed every sound and direction. [105]
A torch would have helped, except no one trusted fire there after the lighthouse blaze that had taken Liora's brother and scarred the nets drying along the grotto walls. [134]
So each clear night she promised the children another kind of guide, made only from borrowed starlight. [151]
Her motive, simple and stubborn, was to share light without using fire. [163]
On the morning the spring tides withdrew farthest, she limped into the grotto as memories resurface with every familiar pool. [183]
The mist smelled of salt and early thrift, and a soft zephyr moved through the arches while pink blooms trembled above the entrance. [206]
Here, when she was twelve, her brother Tomas had taught her to find north by holding a wet thumb to the wind and watching where the first stars pricked through. [236]
Here, too, he had shown her sea glass in the shingle, green and amber fragments the ocean had already softened for patient hands. [259]
She knelt by the lowest pool and gathered new pieces left by the winter storms, rinsing each one until its color woke in her palm. [284]
Most were dull as bottle ends, but one piece, curved and deep blue, caught even the veiled daylight and threw it back in a clean bright line. [311]
It reminded her that polishing could reveal what dulling weather had only hidden, not destroyed. [326]
At the pool's center an oyster shell gaped around a matte pearl, so untouched by the restless water that she named it, without thinking, a pearl of stillness. [354]
She did not pry it loose, because its stillness belonged to the pool, but she understood suddenly what her guide needed at its heart. [378]
Not a flame that ate air and spat sparks, but a calm center that let moving light do the traveling. [398]
Back in her cottage she spread the sea glass on Tomas's old sailcloth and set to work by the open window. [419]
She used her father's gem polishing craft, learned at his bench before the sea took his strength, grading each fragment first with coarse sand and water, then finer grit, then ash on wet leather. [453]
Gem polishing was slow enough to make memory honest. [462]
As she rubbed the blue piece in small circles, she remembered Tomas laughing when she complained that earthbound work was only waiting. [484]
He had told her that waiting, done properly, was a form of navigation. [497]
By dusk her fingertips were raw and the polished pieces lay reflectively vibrant on the cloth, each holding a different weather of green, amber, white, or blue. [524]
Yet a hanging guide would fail if it spun too wildly in the grotto draft or listed and struck the stone. [545]
So she built a light frame from driftwood and copper wire and began to weigh every fragment in her palm scale, adding and swapping until both sides rested level. [574]
For the center she chose not bright glass but a smooth white shell around a small dull pearl from a dead oyster, a borrowed pearl of stillness to steady the turning colors. [606]
That night mist returned thicker than breath, and two children pounding at her door said their father had not come round by boat and would try the grotto path blind. [636]
Liora took the frame, her astrolabe, and a cord, and went down because the moon was rising behind the mist and the tide was holding its breath. [663]
She hung the guide beneath the highest arch where the serpentine passage turned twice and even sure feet lost the shore. [684]
At first there was only gray, then the zephyr found the balanced frame and turned it slowly, bloom scent riding the same gentle air from above. [710]
Moonlight entered the polished glass without heat or smoke and broke into quiet ribbons that laid themselves, one after another, along the wet walls toward home. [736]
The pearl at the center barely shone, yet everything turned around its calm, and Liora understood that stillness was not the absence of light but its patience. [763]
Minutes later the fisherman emerged, following the ribbons, with his torn sail over one shoulder and wonder widening his tired eyes. [784]
She smiled, earthbound and exactly where she needed to be. [794]
