Mara Venn had been the wistful station pianist at Skye Terminus for eleven years, playing arrivals that no timetable could promise. [21] The terminus floated above a sea of cloud, moored to a mountain by cables that sang whenever the wind changed. [41] Each morning she opened the piano, set her broken anniversary clock upon it, and wound its key though the hands never moved past seven. [65] At seven her husband Theo had boarded the last northbound car, waving through steam until the clouds closed behind him. [85] The clock had stopped that evening, and no repairer since had made its second hand forgive her for letting him go. [106] She kept time instead by arrivals, by cups cooling beside her, and by the flickering compasses mounted above every platform gate. [127]

Those compasses did not merely point north, for above the clouds iron winds pulled their needles between true bearings and remembered places. [149] When a needle settled, a train might come, but when it flickered, something lost was trying to find the station again. [170] That anniversary morning, every compass on Platform Three began to flicker in the same patient rhythm, three beats and then a pause. [192] Mara stopped mid-phrase, her fingers hovering, because Theo had tapped that rhythm on their kitchen table whenever he was thinking. [212] Steely longing tightened in her chest, not soft enough to weep and not weak enough to turn away. [230] A porter rolled in a dented canister labeled for the newsreel booth, though Skye Terminus had shown no films since the war. [252] Inside lay a note in Theo's hand, asking the pianist to play the reel alone and to listen where the picture failed. [274]

Mara was strategically creative, a gift Theo had always praised more than her perfect technique or her stubborn memory. [293] She locked the waiting room, threaded the projector by touch, and used a piano string to mend its frayed drive belt. [314] The black-and-white film showed this same station younger, its platforms bright, Theo laughing as he planted saplings in iron tubs beside the tracks. [337] Then the image stuttered into darkness, and Theo's voice, preserved on a wire behind the screen, whispered coordinates hidden between the frames. [359] Only through hidden sections in a black-and-white film reel could he speak safely, because censors then had searched letters but never silence. [381] He had built a refuge beyond the timetables, he said, for crews stranded when compasses failed and clouds rose like floodwater. [402] It lay in the tidal arbor above the clouds, where grafted pines drank mist and their roots held a hidden platform out of the wind. [427] Trains reached it only when the cloud tide turned, and only if a living signal guided them through the final unmapped mile. [449] Theo's last message ended with seven chimes and a warning that the northbound car had never crashed, only waited, holding its breath. [471]

Mara understood then that the anniversary was not an ending the station expected her to mourn again in obedient silence. [491] She had until the horizon invites night to reach the arbor, because after that the tide would lock the hidden platform for another eleven years. [516] The stationmaster refused her a handcar, citing regulations, insurance, and the useful cowardice of men who had never loved anyone inconveniently. [537] So Mara improvised an engine from what grief had taught her to keep close, useful, and tuned. [554] She mounted the broken clock on a luggage trolley, wired its escapement to piano hammers, and let each remembered tempo drive the wheels forward. [578] She played from the trolley seat with one hand and steered with the other, her music rolling ahead of her like a lantern. [601] Clouds broke against the rails in silver waves, and every flickering compass she passed swung toward her notes, then steadied, then pointed onward. [624] At the final slope the trolley slowed, its clockwork heart faltering exactly where Theo's train had vanished from the world. [644]

Mara did not play the scheduled lament the station had requested for the anniversary ceremony back home. [661] Instead, with cold hands and deliberate joy, she chose to surprise fate with an unplanned encore, Theo's favorite waltz played backward into hope. [684] The backward waltz woke the clock, whose hands shuddered past seven at last and struck a bright, impossible eight. [703] Ahead, pines emerged from mist, their branches heavy with lanterns, and beneath them a single northbound car waited with its doors open. [725] Theo stepped down older, thinner, alive, holding the other half of the reel like a promise he had been afraid to finish alone. [748] Mara took his hand and led him toward refuge, not backward into mourning, but forward into the music neither of them had planned. [771]
