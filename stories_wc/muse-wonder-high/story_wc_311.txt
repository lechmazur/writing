Seven nights after finding the old recordings, the heartbroken reaper returned to the ancient observatory at dusk. [17] He had played them until their batteries thinned Ilya's voice to a whisper he could almost touch. [34] She had kept the observatory before she became the one soul he could not bring himself to reap. [52] Her light had splintered there, held by every fear she had taken in and left unnamed. [68] He had come to give names to nameless fears, because hers were the last he could bear to leave unsaid. [88]

In his coat lay a folded atlas page with sea monsters coiled where constellations should have been. [105] Ilya had drawn it during the winter they learned how little living charts admitted about the dark. [122] She mapped the sky as an ocean because sailors at least confessed that some waters had teeth. [139] He unfolded it on the chart table beneath the open dome, where dusk entered in a narrow band. [157] The telescope, its prism cracked years before, pointed toward a patch of evening no star had claimed. [174] He set the recorder beside the page and pressed play with an unsteady thumb. [188]

Ilya spoke first of the weather, then called him Thomasin, as if death were only a late appointment. [206] Thomasin answered before he could stop himself, and the sound shamed him with its hope. [221] Grief always loosened his tongue until he echoes forgotten phrases, borrowing words the dead had left unfinished. [238] Stay the course, he whispered, though the words belonged to a drowned ferryman from his first year reaping. [256] On the recording, Ilya described the gaps between constellations as rooms where fear lived without a name. [273] She had matched thirteen gaps to thirteen monsters, each drawn from sailors' tales and her own careful sight. [291] To name a fear, she explained, was to bring it close enough to learn whether it wanted blood or company. [311]

Thomasin finally understood why she had not followed him on the night she died. [325] Six years earlier, a sickness of dread had moved through the valley while a comet burned above the observatory. [344] People woke certain something stood behind them, and by noon they could not recall their children's faces. [361] Alone in the dome, Ilya had offered those homeless fears her own mind as a harbor. [377] She bore them without witness, bargain, or prayer until her heart failed under their accumulated weight. [393] Thomasin, newly made a reaper, had gathered only her brightest fragment and mistaken it for the whole of her. [412] The rest had stayed in the lenses, the walls, and the cold spaces between familiar stars. [428]

Near the end of the tape, Ilya explained that setting sunlight could reveal what moonlight merely softened. [445] Through scattered light, she said, loose fears would crystallize along familiar shapes and stand still long enough to be named. [465] Thomasin turned the cracked prism into the sunbeam and laid the atlas page beneath its broken colors. [482] Light scattered across the parchment, and each monster lifted from its ink with a cold, bright edge. [499] The whale with hands was the fear of being forgotten, so he wrote FORGETTING beneath its vast belly. [517] The serpent covered in mouths was the fear of a wrong farewell, and he named it PARTING. [534] The small shelled creature beneath the table was the fear of being seen and still unloved, so he named it SHAME. [555] At each name, a crystal formed on the page, clear as ice yet warm as a held coin. [573] He named nine more, from hunger and falling to the silence that followed laughter in an empty house. [591]

Her recorded voice counted with him, pausing where she had once paused for his real answers. [607] They never met in time, yet they worked in their old rhythm, intimate across an impossible distance. [624] That nearness hurt more than absence because it showed love could stay exact while remaining unreachable. [640] Only the largest monster remained, filling the blank center where Ilya had drawn no stars at all. [657] It wore Thomasin's listening face and crouched forever beside a door that would not open. [672] He knew it as the fear of surviving what love could not, and his borrowed phrases deserted him. [690] He named it GRIEF aloud, in his own plain voice, the name he had avoided for six years. [708] The crystal at the center opened like a clear eye, and Ilya's fragments gathered there through the fading scattered light. [728] That was the echo of sacrifice, her gift returning through his voice to set them both free. [745] Whole at last, Ilya crossed beyond the dome, and Thomasin let the recorder play into silence without calling her back. [765]
