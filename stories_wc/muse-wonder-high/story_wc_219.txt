<story>In the third spring after making first contact, Mara Venn returned to the rainwashed tower in spring with a piece of fly amber in her coat pocket. [27] She had not come to worship the Returned, or to thank the silent visitors who had left without language, law, or warning. [49] She had come to understand the chemistry of homecoming, and to prove that a body could miss a planet the way blood misses salt. [73] Mara was the university's luminous skeptic, named first for the pale light in her skin since the contact night, and second for refusing to call it a blessing. [101] Her glow brightened near the Returned and faded in empty rooms, yet she insisted it was only chemistry until chemistry confessed otherwise. [123] That insistence made her academically rebellious in a faculty that now preferred incense to instruments and miracles to measurements. [142] They had suspended her for publishing the hormone charts of weeping astronauts, then quietly rehired her when the weeping spread to generals and priests. [166] Now the Ministry had built an artificial pilgrimage, and it needed a skeptic who glowed to certify that the cure was real. [188] The pilgrimage began at the coastal quarantine fields, wound through drowned orchards, and ended at the tower where the first signal had been answered. [212] It was artificial because no saint had walked it, no god had demanded it, and every shrine along the road had been installed last winter. [237] Yet the Ministry argued that returning minds needed a rehearsed road home, since rockets delivered bodies faster than grief could follow. [258] Mara despised the theater and suspected the data, but she accepted because the final station offered something no laboratory could fake. [279] Inside the tower, three hundred Returned would sleep one night in their old clothes, breathe unfiltered valley air, and be measured before and after dreaming. [304] If cortisol fell and oxytocin steadied there, the Ministry would declare the pilgrimage a treatment and sell the route to other wounded nations. [327] If nothing changed, Mara would publish that too, and let the bronze quiet of the tower statues judge her career. [347] She reached the grounds at dawn, when spring rain had washed the stone bright and left the lower fields smelling of iron and lilac. [371] The sculpture path from the gate had been overgrown for years, its saints and cosmonauts sunk to their knees in wet ivy. [393] No pilgrim was due until noon, but a line of prints already crossed the grass and vanished between the statues. [413] They were dusty footprints on wet stone, impossible in the rain, each toe edged with fine gray powder that did not dissolve. [435] Mara knelt, touched one print, and felt her skin flare softly in answer. [448] The powder smelled not of ash or ship fuel, but of her childhood kitchen after bread had burned and her mother had opened every window. [473] That was the first warning, because the visitors had never spoken, but they had listened to everything the Returned remembered about home. [495] She followed the trail via dusty footprints left on an overgrown sculpture path, past bronze mothers, bronze pilots, and a bronze child holding an empty bowl. [521] At each statue the prints paused and deepened, as if whoever made them had stopped to learn the posture of being mourned. [543] Mara took samples with shaking hands, angry at her own wonder, and sealed the powder beside the fly amber she had carried since she was twelve. [569] The amber held a housefly from the last summer before her mother died, trapped while the kitchen radio counted down to a harvest that failed. [594] For years she had used it as a control, a perfect piece of home that time could touch only as light. [615] Now the powder warmed against the amber through two layers of cloth, and the fly inside seemed to darken, as if remembering flight. [638] By noon the pilgrims arrived by bus and boat and slow train, and all the separate roads of the artificial pilgrimage began to converge on the tower. [665] The Returned were old and young, decorated and forgotten, and each one stopped at the first statue because the footprints fit their own bare feet. [690] Mara understood then that the trail was not made by one visitor, but by the tower remembering every departure in reverse. [711] The visitors had given the Returned no message for Earth, only a way to let Earth recognize them when they finally came back. [734] That night she measured no miracle in the sleeping hall, only slower breath, loosened hands, and blood learning again the familiar salt of rain, soil, bread, and skin. [762] At dawn the footprints were gone, the amber was warm, and Mara wrote that homecoming was not a place returning to you, but your chemistry agreeing to stay. [790]</story>
