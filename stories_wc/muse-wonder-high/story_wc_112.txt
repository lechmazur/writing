<story>Juno Vale was the obliging sky mechanic aboard the xenobiology research station Aerie Nine, where alien moths slept in glass bays above the clouds. [24] She repaired lift sails, tuned turbine feathers, and never refused a frightened biologist who knocked at her workshop during a storm. [45] That evening, Doctor Senn came with frost on his sleeves and a request he had rehearsed too carefully. [63] He wanted her to prove that time moves sideways, not forward, before the funding council erased his life work at dawn. [84] His evidence lived in the moths, whose wings recorded every hour they had ever flown, even hours they had not yet lived. [106] Senn called those folded memories subdued awakenings, because each moth woke knowing a little of its future and trembling from it. [127] The council called it contamination, bad data, and a reason to close Aerie Nine forever. [142] Juno listened while holding a dead navigation gyroscope, already deciding which tools the impossible experiment would need. [159] Outside, the station hung in icy warmth, its hull freezing in shadow while its engines breathed heat against her boots. [179] At the heart of Aerie Nine turned the magnetic nexus at twilight, a ring of blue fire that only stabilized when day and night balanced. [204] It powered the bays, bent compass needles toward forgotten stars, and made loose screws orbit slowly like patient moons. [223] Tonight the ring was drifting out of phase, and every clock aboard disagreed politely with every other clock. [241] Senn believed that drift was not failure but a doorway, if someone could read the whole station at once. [260] No single sensor could do it, because each sensor saw only its own narrow slice of cause and effect. [279] Juno could, through holographic principles, because every polished surface aboard already carried a faint image of everything touching its light. [299] She had learned that truth while fixing mirrors for moths who navigated by reflections no human eye could see. [318] If she tuned the nexus correctly, its field would print the entire night onto one wing, past and future side by side. [340] The attempt had to happen within the hush of beating wings at rest, when six hundred sleeping moths made no vibration to blur the print. [365] That hush would last eleven minutes, beginning when the last moth folded its wings after feeding. [381] Juno gathered silver thread, three cracked lenses, a kettle for courage, and the brass lever that manually governed the nexus. [401] The lever had resisted every motor for twenty years, yet it always yielded to a steady human hand. [419] Senn watched her work and asked, with sudden honesty, whether she believed him or only pitied his ruined career. [438] Juno tightened a bolt, felt its mechanically warm head through gloves chilled stiff, and said belief was a poor substitute for evidence. [460] She told him she would build the test fairly, and if the moths proved him wrong, she would help him accept it. [482] Something eased in his face then, as if obligation shared kindly could become trust. [496] They carried her apparatus into Bay Six, where sleeping moths hung like gray lanterns and breathed in slow unison. [515] Each wing bore spiral veins of living crystal, grown in the station tanks to store scent, starlight, hunger, and remembered weather. [536] Juno chose Mira, the oldest moth, whose left wing carried a scar shaped exactly like a question mark. [554] Mira had survived three migrations and once returned before she had technically departed, which Senn recorded and everyone else ignored. [574] Juno wired silver thread from Mira to the cracked lenses, then from the lenses to every mirror in the bay. [594] Through those mirrors, each fragment would borrow the whole pattern, so one clear wing could reveal the entire hidden night. [614] Above them, feeding moths crossed the twilight glass, their wings loud as soft drums against the cooling air. [632] One by one they settled, until the final wing folded and the bay entered its brief perfect silence. [650] Juno placed her palm on the lever, feeling cold brass above and living warmth humming below her skin. [668] She pulled, not down toward consequence, but sideways along a slot Senn had cut for a direction clocks refused. [687] The nexus bloomed blue, every mirror flashed, and Mira opened her eyes before the light could possibly reach her. [706] On her wing appeared Juno pulling the lever, Senn weeping, and Mira flying toward a flower that had not yet bloomed. [727] Seconds later, Senn wept exactly as printed, while outside the glass a winter bud opened early to meet returning dawn. [747] Juno smiled, because sideways time had left its evidence sleeping peacefully in her obliging hands, asking only to be witnessed gently. [768]</story>
