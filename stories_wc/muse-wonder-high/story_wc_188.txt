<story>Joren Vale, a wandering fireworks designer, arrived at the blooming fortress at dusk with a cart of damp powder and unlit stars. [22] Vines had claimed the cracked walls, and trumpet flowers spilled from every arrow slit as if the stone had decided to laugh. [44] Inside, however, no one laughed, because the town had buried its captain that morning and expected another siege by winter. [64] Children sat on the steps sorting lentils from ash while their parents spoke in the low voices people use when hope has become impolite. [88] As always, Joren chants while walking, soft nonsense rhymes under his breath to measure his pace and lift his mood. [108] He let the rhymes filter the courtyard noise until fear, hunger, and grief separated into sounds he could work with. [128] Through ambient awareness, rather than questions, he learned that the well was low, the ovens were cold, and no bell had rung for joy in a year. [155] The mayor met him by the gate and said the council had canceled the harvest festival and wanted no false light. [176] Joren bowed as if she had offered him a throne, then asked where the old captain used to stand during celebrations. [197] She pointed to the central courtyard, where a dry fountain held rainwater black as ink. [212] There, under fallen petals, Joren found colored tiles arranged not as a picture but as a note in mosaic. [231] Blue tiles said to gather at the fountain when the sky forgot them, and white tiles added a recipe for soup. [252] It was not a grand prophecy, which made Joren trust it at once, because real hope usually arrives wearing work clothes. [273] As he brushed the last tiles clean, he uncovered a fire spinner's kerosene rag knot wedged beneath the fountain rim. [293] The knot was still supple, still faintly sweet with fuel, and tied in the captain's own impatient double loop. [312] Before she commanded soldiers, the captain had spun fire at festivals, and this was her signal that the celebration kit survived. [333] The mayor warned him that grief had made people literal, and a promised light that failed would finish what the siege began. [355] Joren grinned with serious playfulness and said that was exactly why he had come, to defy the gravity of hopelessness with something that must rise or fail honestly. [383] He followed the smell of kerosene through ambient awareness of small things, noting a warm draft, a scorched petal, and a mouse that nested unafraid by the fountain. [411] Beneath a loose tile behind the mouse nest lay oil, powder jars, colored salts, and a bundle of sky rockets wrapped in captain's canvas. [435] Half the powder was damp, so Joren spent the afternoon teaching children to filter ash from salt and salt from sand with sieves, scarves, and patient jokes. [462] What began as chores became a game of naming each color after someone the town refused to forget. [480] Red was for the baker who sang off key, gold for the captain, and green for a goat that had once eaten a war banner. [505] When the goat's owner laughed until she cried, no one apologized for either sound, and the courtyard seemed to stand straighter. [526] At dusk the blossoms closed their bright throats, and Joren laid his rockets along the fountain like a choir waiting for permission to sing. [550] Then night arrived early and complete, during the hush of a night with no stars, when even the river seemed to lower its voice. [574] Joren unwound the fire spinner's kerosene rag knot, soaked its heart again, and tied it to his staff as the captain once had. [597] The mayor held the torch but did not light it, asking if he was certain, and Joren said certainty was a luxury for people with spare skies. [624] So she lit the torch herself, because some decisions should belong to the living. [638] Joren spun the burning staff in slow circles, then faster, until a ring of fire wrote a brief sun around his body and every child gasped on cue. [666] He led the crowd around the fountain, and soon everyone chants while walking, not a prayer exactly, but the soup recipe from the mosaic set to a marching tune. [695] On the third chorus, Joren touched the torch to the fuses, and the rockets rose through flower scent and wood smoke into the starless dark. [720] Red, gold, and green opened above the fortress, brief gardens that fell as sparks and taught the night a temporary alphabet of names. [743] No one mistook the light for rescue, or the soup for plenty, but people ate together under falling ash and made plans for winter without lowering their voices. [771]</story>
