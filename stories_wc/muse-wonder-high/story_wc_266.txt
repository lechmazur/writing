<story>Wren held the empty loop long after the leash breaks, staring at the snapped cord swinging from her wrist. [19] Bram, her brother's old sheepdog, had bolted up the hill when the harbor bell startled him. [35] Now his bark came thin and far from the veiled ridgeway, where mist hid the drop to the sea. [54] Wren wanted to run straight after him, but the direct path had already vanished into white. [70] Below her, where the lagoon met the shore, two lanterns swayed toward her in patient rhythm. [86] They belonged to the wise ferry guides, sisters Ansel and Miri, who knew every tide the fog tried to keep secret. [107] Ansel carried an oar over her shoulder, and Miri carried their ticket punch with star stamp, clicking it softly as she walked. [129] "He went high," Wren told them, holding out the broken cord as if it explained her shaking hands. [147] "Then we will not chase him high," Ansel said, "not while the ridge hides its edges." [163] Miri took Wren's return ferry ticket from her pocket and punched one clean star near its corner. [180] "One star for standing still when running would be easier," she said. [192] Wren followed them, though all she truly wanted was to find where unused courage waits inside her before Bram was lost. [213] Since her brother Jonah drowned two winters ago, deep water had emptied her of every brave word she knew. [232] She walked Bram at dusk because he still watched the ferry for Jonah, and because no one else could bear his waiting. [254] Now Bram's barking rose and fell beyond the mist, always stopping whenever Wren shouted his name. [270] Ansel laid a hand on her shoulder and kept it there, gently persistent, until Wren slowed her breath to match the lapping water. [293] "We traverse through indirect routes when the straight road lies," Miri said, turning away from the ridge toward the lagoon. [313] The sisters led her through reed beds, across a sheep bridge, and along the inside curve of the bay where water stayed shallow. [336] At each turn, Miri punched another star into the ticket, not for payment, but so Wren could see that waiting had its own map. [360] By the third star, Bram's bark had changed to a whine from below the far side of the ridge, near old ferry steps cut into rock. [386] The tide had trapped him on a ledge while he chased a gull, and rising water worried the stones beneath him. [407] Ansel looked at the sky, then at the water, and sat down deliberately on a flat stone. [424] "We keep a vigil in twilight," she said, "until the tide loosens its grip and the lower steps appear." [443] So they kept watch as light thinned, sharing cold bread and answering each whine from Bram with a low, steady call. [464] Miri hummed a shoreline lullaby their mother had sung to restless passengers, and the tune moved over the water like a hand smoothing a blanket. [489] Wren joined in spite of her fear, verse after verse, because Bram quieted when she sang and because singing kept her teeth from chattering. [513] When the last blue left the sky, the lower steps rose black and shining from the falling tide. [531] The gap beyond the steps was narrow enough for Wren, but too slick for Ansel's bad knee and too tight for Miri with the lanterns. [556] Wren looked at the ticket in her palm and saw three stars, each earned not by rushing, but by staying. [576] "Courage was not waiting up the ridge," Ansel said, reading her face, "it was waiting in the part of you that could sing while afraid." [601] Wren tied the sisters' rope around her waist, tucked the ticket into her sleeve for luck, and began to traverse the wet steps alone. [625] She moved sideways, one hand on the rock, feeling each foothold before trusting it, while the guides held the rope and kept the lullaby behind her like another line. [654] Bram pressed his cold nose into her neck when she reached him, too tired to bark, his paws cut and trembling against the stone. [678] She coaxed him ahead step by step, speaking softly all the way back, and he obeyed as if her voice were the only dry path left. [704] On solid ground, Miri lifted the ticket punch with star stamp and punched one final star beside the others while Ansel untied the rope. [728] Then they walked home through lifting mist, Bram limping between them, while the sea behind them kept humming its shoreline lullaby to the ridge. [752]</story>
