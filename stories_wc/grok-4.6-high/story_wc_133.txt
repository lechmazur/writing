The insomniac painter had not closed his eyes in four days when the first rains of the season struck the tidal island. [22]
After the water wars the world had grown cautious with its moisture, and islands like this one surfaced only at low tide to remind survivors of what had been lost. [52]
He lived there alone, wryly earnest in his explanations to the occasional trader that sleep was a luxury the wars had taught him to distrust. [77]
His motivation never wavered: to thresh meaning from experience by capturing every scarred horizon on canvas before the next flood erased it. [99]
That morning he decided to work by following rain trails that snaked across the exposed seabed, believing they would lead him to fresh subjects. [123]
The water carved glistening paths that revealed secrets the wars had drowned. [135]
Hours later the trail ended at a freshly scoured dune where an amber brooch lay exposed, its surface warm even in the chill air. [159]
He picked it up with hands that trembled from exhaustion and wonder, an expression of haggard awe settling on his face as he studied the ancient insect trapped inside. [188]
This find felt like an unearthed convergence of deep time and recent catastrophe, two histories colliding in one golden relic. [208]
A shadow fell across the sand as a woman stepped from behind a wreck, her own path having crossed his by following similar rain trails from the opposite shore. [237]
She was as haggard as he, a fellow remnant of the conflicts, and she recognized the brooch immediately. [255]
"That belonged to my mother," she said, her voice wryly earnest despite the tremble, "lost when the reservoirs broke and the armies marched for the last aquifers." [282]
The painter, still holding the amber brooch, proposed an exchange that surprised them both. [296]
He would keep the brooch only long enough to paint it, then return it in trade for her account of the wars, so he could thresh more meaning from the shared experience. [328]
She agreed, sitting beside him on a slab of concrete that had once been part of a desalination plant. [347]
As the tide lingered at its lowest, she spoke of cities turning to dust, of children drinking seawater and dying, of the final treaties signed in blood and brine. [376]
He listened with haggard awe, his insomnia sharpening every detail so that he could later transfer it to pigment. [395]
The amber brooch sat between them, a silent witness to the unearthed convergence of their separate sufferings. [412]
When she finished, the painter opened his satchel and began to sketch rapidly, his wryly earnest comment cutting the silence: "If I sleep now I might miss the exact shade of that regret in your eyes." [448]
She watched him work, the rain trails now drying into faint scars on the sand. [463]
Hours passed and the sea began its return, forcing them to higher ground on the island's rocky spine. [481]
There he completed the painting of the brooch against the backdrop of receding rain, incorporating elements of her tale so the canvas itself became another kind of exchange. [509]
True to his word he returned the amber brooch, and she in turn left him a small flask of collected rainwater, precious after the wars. [534]
As she departed before the waters closed the path, he felt the weight of what they had unearthed together. [553]
Back in his weathered hut the insomniac painter hung the new canvas and stared at it through another sleepless night, threshing still more meaning from the experience of their meeting. [583]
The amber glow in the painting seemed to pulse with the unearthed convergence of insect, heirloom, war, and two worn souls. [604]

Outside the tidal island waited, patient and scarred, a stage for whatever meaning the waters and rains might yet unearth. [645]
