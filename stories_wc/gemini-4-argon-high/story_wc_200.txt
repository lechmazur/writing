Oren walked the narrow floor of Echo Gorge in the hush of a hidden sunrise, where the amber light crowned the high basalt rims but left the canyon floor steeped in violet shadow. [33] As a lost soul collector, he did not carry a scythe or chains, for the dead who lingered here were already bound tightly enough by their own regret. [61] Instead, he carried an ancient wooden frame strung with tarnished brass, its neck warped by decades of damp mist. [80] Oren was a modestly fearless man, possessing neither the swagger of a demon hunter nor the trembling piety of a priest, greeting the chilling phantoms with the quiet patience of a shepherd tending tangled sheep. [115]

The canyon walls around him hummed with spectral confessions, a continuous, sorrowful murmur of mortal sins that bounced infinitely between the stones. [137] Most ghosts believed that endless weeping would eventually wash away their guilt, not realizing that the acoustic trap of the ravine only magnified their misery and anchored them to the earth. [168] Oren knew a different truth, one born from a luminous ache that lived in his own chest ever since he had buried his young daughter decades ago. [195] Grief was a heavy stone, and the only way to lift it was to integrate laughter into healing, transforming the crushing weight of human folly into something light enough to float skyward. [227]

Today, he had come to pursue a particularly stubborn shade who had been wailing near the dry riverbed for three moons. [248] To draw the spirit out from the crevices, Oren needed a catalyst that matched the fractured state of the ghost's mind. [269] Pure music only made the dead hide in shame, so Oren hunted them through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp, using dissonance as his net. [295] From his leather pouch, he retrieved his favored plectrum, a yellowed crocodile's tooth he had scavenged from the southern marshes years ago. [317] The curved ivory was notched and dense, perfect for striking the slack, uneven wires to produce a comical, twanging vibration that cut right through the solemn echoes of the chasm. [347]

He dragged the tooth across the strings, unleashing a warbling, sour arpeggio that sounded like a drunken frog attempting a love song. [369] Ahead, a translucent figure coalesced from the pale mist, shimmering with the silver melancholy of a man who had died in utter disgrace. [392] "Leave me to my penance," the apparition groaned, his voice overlapping with the rebounding whispers of his own past declarations from the cliffside. [415] "I am Sir Garrick, who fled the great beast of the delta and left my squire to drop my banner in the mud!" [438] The gorge dutifully repeated the words "coward" and "mud" from a dozen stony angles, pressing the phantom knight to his knees. [459]

Oren stepped closer, entirely unbothered by the freezing aura of death radiating from the knight's spectral armor, and plucked another gloriously clumsy chord. [482] The sharp clack of the reptile bone against the brass made Garrick flinch and glare at the instrument in bewildered annoyance. [503] Oren gently held up the crocodile's tooth so it caught the growing glow of the sky above, asking, "Do you recognize this pick, brave sir?" [528] "It belonged to an ancient, toothless lizard that choked to death on a silk banner right after you sprinted up a cypress tree." [551]

Garrick blinked his hollow eyes, the grand tragedy of his eternal torment suddenly colliding with the ridiculous reality of what had actually happened in the swamp. [577] "Your squire lived to eighty and told the tavern every winter how you tried to bribe a reptile with a wheel of cheese before falling out of the branches," Oren added, strumming a jaunty, terribly out of tune jig. [616] The absurdity of the melody, paired with the memory of the airborne Gouda, cracked the solemn armor of the knight's remorse. [637] A strange sound bubbled up from Garrick's phantom throat, starting as a startled snort before blooming into a rich, resonant belly laugh that shook his misty shoulders. [664]

As the laughter struck the walls of Echo Gorge, it shattered the old, heavy loop of his confession, replacing the groans of despair with a bright, cascading mirth. [692] The knight looked down at his fading hands, smiling through a bittersweet tear as the unforgiving weight of his mortal failure finally dissolved into a harmless jest. [719] Drawn upward by the warmth of the hidden dawn, Garrick's spirit rose like golden dust, leaving Oren alone in the quiet canyon with his crooked instrument and a heart that hurt so beautifully.
