In the era when creativity gets automated, the grandest spells are no longer cast by human hands, but woven by the relentless pistons of the Aether Engines. [27] High within an icebound spire above the clouds, thousands of brass cylinders calculated the exact mathematical parameters of beauty, projecting flawless, soulless auroras into the stratosphere. [53] Corin, an underachieving mage by every metric of the New Guild, swept the frost from the exterior observation deck and ignored the synthetic masterpieces flashing overhead. [79] His peers designed complex algorithms to mass produce wonder, yet Corin had failed his licensing exams three times because he refused to surrender his wand to the clockwork looms. [108] He possessed a stubborn, quiet ache to find the sacred in the simple, believing that true magic lived in the trembling breath of a mortal caster rather than the tireless hum of a machine. [142]

The atmosphere at this extreme altitude existed in a state of brilliant dimness, where the dying twilight refracted through floating ice crystals to create a radiant, shadowy gloom. [170] It was a lighting condition the engines could never properly replicate, too paradoxical for their binary matrices. [187] Shivering in his threadbare robes, Corin adjusted the heavy leather harness cutting into his shoulders. [202] Bound to his back was an old garden folly keystone, salvaged from the ruins of a seventeenth century mortal estate before the valleys below were paved over for mana turbines. [232] A folly was architecture built purely for whimsy, serving no practical function, and this wedge of limestone had once held together an arch that led nowhere at all. [260] To the automated looms, such an object was an error to be deleted, a waste of effort and stone. [279] To Corin, the chisel marks left by a long dead mason were holy scriptures. [293]

The relentless vibrations of the spire threatened to shatter the delicate memory dormant within the limestone. [309] To awaken it, he needed distance from the engines, which meant leaving the stone balcony behind. [325] He stepped onto the frozen parapet, looking out over the churning sea of silver vapor that stretched to the horizon. [345] The Guild had outlawed mist walking decades ago, deeming the ancient method of treading upon condensation too erratic for modern transport. [366] A machine could not calculate how to stand on nothing, because the spell required a mind that accommodates impossibilities without trying to solve them. [390] Corin closed his eyes, let go of rigid logic, and stepped off the ledge into the freezing air. [408]

His boot did not plummet; instead, it sank an inch into a billowing cloud bank that firmed beneath his weight like freshly packed snow. [432] He exhaled a plume of white steam and took another step, walking out into the vast, luminous dark. [450] Fifty paces from the icebound spire above the clouds, the mechanical drone faded into a profound, velvet silence. [468] Here, suspended between the stars and the smothered earth, the paradoxical physics of mist walking held him steady despite the crushing burden on his back. [493] Gravity demanded that the dense rock drag him down through the vapor, yet the ancient art gently defied the law of mass. [515]

Kneeling upon the firmament of fog, Corin reached behind his freezing neck with numb fingers to unstrap the soaked leather bindings. [536] The buckles fought him, stiffened by rime, until he managed to unstrap the final belt and slide the garden folly keystone into his lap. [560] He pressed his bare, shivering palms against the rough face of the stone, pouring his modest, unrefined mana directly into the ancient tool marks. [584] He did not ask the rock to become a palace, or a dragon, or any of the grandiose spectacles the Aether Engines spat out by the thousands every hour. [613] He merely asked the stone to remember the warm afternoon it was carved, and the quiet joy of the mortal who shaped it for no reason other than love. [642]

A soft warmth bloomed beneath his frostbitten skin, pushing back the bitter cold of the upper atmosphere. [659] The limestone began to emanate a lingering luminance, a pale, golden glow that did not flash or strobe, but settled deeply into the surrounding vapor. [684] As Corin set the keystone down upon the cloud, an invisible archway of pure, tranquil memory formed around it, holding up nothing but the sky itself. [710] The automated auroras of the spire flickered and died as their nightly cycle ended, plunging the peak into shadow, yet the humble stone kept shining. [735] Bathed in that lingering luminance, the underachieving mage smiled, knowing he had anchored a piece of human soul in a world that had forgotten how to dream. [762]
