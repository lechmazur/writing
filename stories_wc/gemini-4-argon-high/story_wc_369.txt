Every evening after the gates locked, Corin oiled his shears and walked the green alleys as the sole labyrinth maintenance worker. [21] His job was half custodial and half sacred, a routine of mundane miracles performed in dirty canvas coveralls. [39] All day, grieving citizens wandered the miles of ancient yew hedges to weep in private, and all day the thirsty roots drank their tears. [63] By dusk, the branches drooped under thick beads of dark amber resin that hardened along the bark. [80] The city council called this substance quantified sorrow, paying Corin by the ounce to scrape it into tin pails and weigh it on a grocer's scale so the hedges would not snap under the accumulation of human heartache. [118] Yesterday had been a four pound Tuesday, heavy with the regrets of widows and bankrupt clerks, yet Corin never threw the harvest into the furnace as his contract instructed. [147] Instead, he hoarded the resin because he burned with a quiet obsession to discover what trees witness when people think they are completely alone. [171] He believed the yews did not merely soak up misery like sponges, but kept a faithful record of the souls they sheltered. [193]

Carrying his heavy pail, Corin unlocked the door to the central spire, climbing the winding stairs into a stormlit tower by moonlight. [215] Through the tall, unglazed arches of the belfry, a summer squall prowled the western hills with silent forks of lightning, while directly overhead the full moon poured cold silver across his workbench. [247] This dual illumination was essential for his midnight experiments. [256] From a velvet lined box, he lifted an astronomer's broken sextant that he had rescued years ago from the municipal dustbins. [277] Its brass frame was snapped in two and its index mirror was webbed with cracks, rendering it useless for measuring the altitude of stars. [301] However, anything steeped long enough in the breath of the living labyrinth acquires the wood's strange botanical grace and heals in moonlight. [323] As the lunar rays touched the workbench, the jagged bramble scratches on Corin's knuckles faded into smooth skin, and the fractures in the sextant's glass began to knit themselves together like freezing water. [356]

If he left the tool whole, it would merely mend itself into an ordinary navigational instrument by dawn. [374] To peer inside the resin, he had to deconstruct the device while the silver light was actively trying to make it whole, harnessing the tension between ruin and repair. [403] Working quickly with a jeweler's screwdriver, Corin separated the healing horizon glass, the shade lenses, and the telescope barrel, spacing them across a sheet of butcher paper around a fist sized lump of amber. [437] The dismantled pieces hummed, reaching for one another across the table with invisible threads of lunar magnetism that passed straight through the crystallized grief. [461] When the distant storm flashed, the lightning caught inside the mending lenses and projected the amber's contents onto the paper, allowing Corin to decipher the trees' memories by mapping scattered dreams. [492] People never wept for abstract pain; they wept for specific, abandoned hopes, and those discarded visions were what the roots actually consumed. [514] With a carpenter's pencil, Corin traced the glowing projections of unbuilt houses, reconciliations that never happened, and children who had grown into strangers, connecting them with dotted lines of latitude and longitude. [546]

Still, the image remained blurry, lacking one final degree of calibration to reveal what the yews themselves made of all this human yearning. [656] Across the butcher paper, the disparate mirages of grief converged into a single, breathtaking canopy of light. [673] Corin saw then what the ancient hedges truly witnessed: not the crushing weight of the sorrow left behind, but the sudden lightness of the shoulders that walked away without it. [703] Every tear absorbed by the bark was a burden set down so a baker could return to his ovens or a mother could whistle on her walk home. [731] The trees stood as quiet partners in a thousand daily resurrections, turning private despair into the green vigour of new shoots.
