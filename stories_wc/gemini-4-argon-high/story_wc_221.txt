The breath Elian exhaled took forty minutes of standard galactic time to fog the cold glass. [16] Around him, the time dilation observation deck curved into the shadows, a cavern of steel and quartz suspended just above the singularity. [38] Five subjective years had passed after the shutdown, when the Syndicate cut the main power and evacuated the crew back to normal space. [61] They had abandoned Elian to the echoed silence of the dead facility, where the metallic ring of his boots lingered in the thin air like a haunting. [88] Among the departing scientists, he was known as the blessed pariah. [99] The core leak that doomed the station had rewritten his nervous system, leaving him temporally stretched so that his heartbeat matched the sluggish flow of the abyss. [126] He was blessed to survive without life support in the heavy zone, yet a pariah because normal people could not bear a man who moved like continental drift. [154]

Today marked the fifth anniversary of his solitude. [162] Outside the gravity well, tens of thousands of years had flashed by in a blur of dying stars. [180] At first, watching familiar constellations dissolve and scatter across the warped glass had nearly driven him mad. [197] The universe had lost all anchor, spinning around the black hole in chaotic ribbons of light. [213] Yet Elian refused to drown in the vertigo. [221] His sole purpose now was to turn confusion into exploration, transforming his prison into a vessel of discovery. [239]

To do so, he relied on a family custom of sketching the night sky on anniversaries. [255] Generations ago on Earth, his ancestors had drawn the seasonal drift of Orion in charcoal ledgers to celebrate each wedding and birth. [277] Elian opened his own translucent folio on the console. [286] Sewn into the corner of his heavy thermal sleeve was an heirloom from that terrestrial past, a gilded compass rose applique. [307] When he pressed his forearm against the observation pane, the metallic gold threads caught the dim emergency lamps and cast a sharp, thirty two point reflection onto the dark glass. [337]

That reflection served as his reticle. [343] Without a magnetic pole in deep space, the gilded compass rose applique provided the only fixed geometry in his world. [363] Aligning his stylus with the reflected golden points, he traced the current positions of the lensed galaxies onto a fresh sheet of vellum. [386] Every dot represented a star system dragged out of shape by the gravitational monster below. [401] For four years, the drawings had looked like random splatter, a mocking testament to his isolation. [417] The silence of the deck seemed to press against his eardrums, hollow and vast, amplified by the empty corridors behind him. [438]

He finished the fifth chart and laid it over the previous four, aligning their borders with the reflected spire of the golden emblem. [461] As the five epochs of deep time stacked together, Elian caught a glimpse of something impossible. [477] While ninety nine percent of the stars swirled in violent arcs around the gravity well, a faint filament of seven blue quasars had not curved at all. [504] Instead, across the five anniversary sketches, their dots formed a ruler straight line pointing directly through the upper needle of the compass rose. [527]

His slow, heavy heart gave a thunderous thud that vibrated through his ribs. [540] A normal observer, living in standard seconds, would never have lived long enough to record the subtle alignment across millennia. [560] Only someone temporally stretched could perceive the macro currents of the cosmos with the naked eye. [576] That unbending vector of light was a gravitational wake, a stable corridor piercing the distortion field. [592] In a realm where space and time were twisted into knots, he had found true north. [608] It was not a magnetic pole, but an invariant cosmic axis along which a ship could ride free of the singularity. [629]

The Syndicate had believed the inner horizon was an inescapable wall, which was why they had abandoned the station and left one spare escape pod docked in the lower bay. [659] They had lacked the patience of centuries required to chart the exit. [671] Gathering his five translucent sheets, Elian tucked them beneath his arm, right below the gleaming threads of his sleeve. [690] He turned away from the glass and walked toward the hangar airlock, his footsteps ringing out in the echoed silence like the slow, steady tolling of a bell. [718] With his hand on the manual release wheel, he took one last glimpse of the darkened time dilation observation deck. [738] He was no longer an outcast waiting for the end of time, but a navigator setting sail toward true north. [758]
