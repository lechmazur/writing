Ten years after the reef dies, the sea no longer roars against the coast; it merely exhales a flat, chalky breath across the ruins of the littoral siege. [28] Corvin, once a gloom-hill coachman who drove ironclad supply wagons through the perpetually shadowed upland passes, walks out alone onto the blooming jetty at twilight. [53] Beneath his boots, the causeway is scarcely wider than a wagon axle, yet it stretches for miles into an unbroken, darkening emptiness of water and sky, trapping him in a sensation of narrow vastness. [87] To his left and right, the bleached skeletons of brain coral and staghorn jut from the slack tide like the ribs of the horses he used to bury in the high mud. [119] During the war, this stone spine served as a firing platform where two empires ground each other into the surf, poisoning the polyps with copper shelling and spilled oil until the entire barrier perished. [153] Now, Corvin comes every evening with a single purpose: to let a garden reclaim a battlefield. [169]

Already, the landward half of the pier is thick with saltwort, twilight primrose, and nightshade that spill over the masonry in violet cascades, drinking the purple dusk. [196] Slung across his weathered greatcoat is a heavy leather satchel containing his collection of dried seed pods, gathered from the cold, foggy valleys of his youth. [222] They rattle together like castanets with every step he takes along the narrow, endless ribbon of granite. [239] However, upland seeds cannot sprout in bare, briny basalt and dead limestone alone. [252] To make them take root, Corvin must fertilize the sterile crevices of the jetty with a very specific marrow drawn from the sea floor. [276] Ten fathoms down, where the hulls of sunken troop barges rest among the collapsed coral shelves, the warring soldiers have achieved a submerged concord. [300] In the airless dark, the bones of invading marines and defending gunners have dissolved together, their calcium and decaying iron mixing with the rotted reef to form pockets of rich, black pelagic silt. [333] This drowned peace treaty is the only soil potent enough to nourish the mountain flora, yet the deep currents push it to the surface in shifting, unpredictable eddies. [361]

Corvin locates these hidden upwellings of truce mud by understanding bird calls. [373] The littoral starlings and grey curlews that roost along the shattered reef are generational mimics, having learned the cries of the dying armies decades ago and passed those phonetic ghosts down to their fledglings. [407] Because both empires collapsed into extinction at the end of the war, their dialects have vanished from human mouths, but Corvin speaks dead languages learned during his years ferrying foreign envoys and ancient texts through the high passes. [445] When the birds hover over a spot where the subterranean tide churns up the mineral remains of a sunken regiment, they chatter in the exact archaic vocabulary of the men who drowned there. [478]

He stops near the tip of the jetty, where the blooming vines give way to bare, scarred stone, and listens to the immense quiet of the horizon. [505] Three yards out, perched on a calcified turret of dead fire coral, a pied curlew tilts its head and whistles a sharp, four syllable cadence. [530] To anyone else, it is merely a shorebird piping at the dusk, but Corvin recognizes the Low Vaelish imperative for lower the ramp, followed by the Old Pelagian response for hold the line. [563] The bird is feeding on bristle worms that have just ridden a thermal plume up from the intermingled wreckage below. [583] Corvin kneels on the slender ledge of the causeway, whispers a soft absolution in both extinct tongues, and lowers his long brass dredging scoop into the eddy beside the coral turret. [614] When he hauls the bucket up, it brims with heavy, iridescent mire that smells of iron, phosphorus, and ancient peace. [634] He packs the rich mud into the mortar gaps of the stone, draws three woody husks from his satchel, and cracks them open with his thumb so the dormant life inside can touch the silt. [669] As the last amber light drains from the sky, the curlew cries out once more in a forgotten grammar of truce, and behind the old driver, a hundred twilight blossoms open along the thin stone bridge between the hills and the abyss. [711]
