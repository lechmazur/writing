Barnaby Finch, the most elusive disaster-relief logistician on earth, operated entirely from a disused railway station overgrown with ivy. [19] Vines strangled the rusted tracks and crept through the shattered ticket windows, hiding the man who illegally rerouted cargo ships with courteous apologies. [42] His methods embodied polite anarchy, a system where pirates commandeered medical trains only after leaving fresh scones and sincere compliments for the tied conductors. [66] Though he saved thousands from floods and famines, his true motivation was to find the cure for loneliness. [84] He had spent ten years alone on the mossy platform, connecting every corner of the fractured globe while remaining completely untouched by human warmth. [108] Whenever he communicated via shortwave radio, he became desperately jocular, broadcasting frantic, terrible puns into the static just to hear a smuggler chuckle. [131] He would wheeze silly riddles about contraband crossing the border into the microphone, laughing too loudly at his own punchline before weeping into his ledger. [156]

Because official channels were monitored by hostile governments, Barnaby directed his clandestine syndicate through improbable rumor chains. [173] He would whisper a joke and a manifest to a passing tramp, who told a fishmonger, who gossiped with a corrupt duchess, until a barge of grain magically appeared in a starving province. [206] No currency ever changed hands, for every participant in his web was bound by reciprocity. [221] If a rebel province accepted fifty tents, etiquette demanded they eventually hijack something useful for a stranger, always attaching a neat note of gratitude. [245] On the rotting mahogany counter before him sat the strangest payment this obligation had ever yielded: a jagged fragment of moon rock. [267] A grateful cosmonaut, rescued from a flooded steppe by one of the rogue helicopters, had politely pilfered it from a state museum to settle his moral account. [294] Barnaby stared at the gray, pitted stone, realizing it was a monument to ultimate isolation, chipped from a dead world just as barren as his silent waiting room. [322]

He could not eat it, and he could not use it to purify water, yet the law of his network dictated that every object must circulate to serve a need. [352] His own need was no longer a secret he could laugh away. [364] He decided to use the lunar pebble to catalyze a different kind of emergency operation. [379] Summoning a trespassing poacher who often slept in the signal box, Barnaby put on a wide, trembling grin and delivered a frenetic slapstick joke. [403] While the poacher stared in sympathetic discomfort at this forced hilarity, Barnaby planted a new whisper. [419] He claimed that the fragment of moon rock would be awarded to whoever could deliver a shipment capable of filling an empty station. [442] He expected them to send crates of banned literature or perhaps a herd of apologetically rustled cattle, giving him a brief excuse to chat with the drovers. [469]

However, the magic of gossip always occurs when the story changes. [480] As the message leapt from the poacher to a mutinous postal worker, and then to a convent of bootlegging nuns, the mention of his strained, manic laughter overtook the details of the stone. [513] The underworld realized that their unseen benefactor was not asking for freight, because the empty vessel in the rumor was Barnaby himself. [535] Three weeks later, the rusted iron gates of the station were gently lifted off their hinges by a crew of fugitive dockworkers who immediately wiped their boots on the welcome mat. [566] Behind them marched hundreds of outlaws, paramedics, and skyjackers, all trespassing with extreme decorum as they parted the hanging curtains of green ivy. [589] They carried contraband folding tables, requisitioned violins, and barrels of hot soup, setting up an unauthorized banquet right over the abandoned sleepers. [611]

"Pardon the intrusion," said a heavily armed tugboat captain, placing a porcelain teacup beside the fragment of moon rock, "but our ledgers showed we owed you a visit." [639] Barnaby opened his mouth to tell a frantic gag about derailed locomotives, hoping to shield himself behind his usual routine. [659] Instead, the duchess tapped a stolen silver spoon against her glass and told a dreadful riddle of her own, prompting a chorus of warm, authentic groans from the assembled criminals. [689] For the first time in a decade, the logistician did not have to force a laugh. [705] He quietly slid the lunar stone into the calloused palm of the captain, satisfying the ancient rules of trade, and pulled up a crate to join the feast. [733]
