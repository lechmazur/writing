High in the stormlit observatory above clouds that smelled of wet pennies and burnt sugar, Barnaby polished his brass ear trumpet with a greasy rag. [25] As a lonely visionary with soot in his sideburns and starlight in his cataracts, he conversed almost exclusively with family portraits drawn on turnips. [49] Outside his crooked window, jagged lightning danced across the cobalt glass portico that jutted into the tempest like the frozen tongue of a blue dragon. [74] Centuries ago, forgotten artisans had baked the silhouettes of extinct moths, laughing kings, and rebel saints directly into those thick azure panes. [96] Whenever a thunderhead flashed, the glass awoke. [103] Barnaby watched the dark patterns unfold across his oilcloth floor, stretching and capering like tipsy marionettes. [119] His singular ambition was to shape a new dawn using ancient shadows, rescuing the miserable world below from its monochrome drudgery. [140]

Down beneath the suffocating blanket of dirty clouds lay the Sump, a sprawling mire where citizens pledged eternal fealty to the Iron Aldermen. [163] The Aldermen owned the mud, the shovels, and the very coughs in the workers' lungs. [178] Barnaby despised such dreary ownership. [183] Every night, he cranked his pedal powered transmitter, which wheezed like an asthmatic accordion and spat blue sparks into his teacup. [204] He translated the dancing silhouettes into verse, broadcasting his observations downward through fragmented radio frequencies that dodged the Aldermen's copper jamming towers. [226] Because the storm constantly chewed up his signal, his broadcasts arrived in the Sump as lyrically prophetic riddles tucked between bursts of frying bacon static. [251] He sang of paper crowns melting in the rain and of moths devouring iron thrones. [266]

At first, Barnaby assumed only stray rats and rusted weathervanes were listening. [278] Then, his homemade receiver began picking up whispers from the muck below, allowing him to listen across the rise of a prophet who called herself Little Tallow. [305] She had started as an orphan grease scraper with a tin funnel strapped to her ear, catching Barnaby's stray syllables in the alleys. [328] Year by year, Barnaby tracked her ascension through panicked police dispatches and tavern songs drifting up through the mist. [347] Little Tallow did not preach rebellion by the sword, which the Aldermen could easily crush. [362] Instead, inspired by Barnaby's poems about shifting silhouettes, she preached the heresy of variant allegiance. [377] She taught the shovelers to swear loyalty to the Tuesday rain, to a lost button, or to a passing beetle, changing their masters every hour. [402] When tax collectors arrived, the workers politely explained that they currently served the Kingdom of Boiled Cabbage and therefore owed no iron tithes. [425] The Sump was dissolving into glorious, ungovernable nonsense. [433] Barnaby giggled so hard he spilled turnip soup on his slippers. [444]

However, the Iron Aldermen were not amused by whimsy. [453] On the night of the Equinox Gale, Barnaby heard Little Tallow's voice trembling over the emergency band. [470] The Aldermen had cornered her congregation in the plaza with steam cannons, demanding they swear a permanent blood oath or be boiled alive. [493] Her followers wavered, terrified by the hissing brass artillery, begging the sky for one final sign from the Ghost in the Static. [515] Simultaneously, the Aldermen fired a massive magnetic pulse from their spires to deafen the airwaves forever. [531] Barnaby's transmitter exploded in a shower of pickled vacuum tubes and scorched springs. [544] Outside, a giant hailstone smashed through the cobalt glass portico, leaving the ancient silhouettes dangling over the abyss in a web of lead. [567] Without the glass to focus the lightning, the shadows vanished from his floor. [580] Without his transmitter, the prophet below was mute. [588]

Barnaby looked at his turnip portraits, sighed, and grabbed a spool of copper wire and his brass ear trumpet. [607] He crawled out onto the shattered portico as the gale tried to peel his whiskers off. [623] If he could not broadcast through vacuum tubes, he would use the storm itself as an amplifier. [640] He lashed the copper wire directly to the lead framing of the broken blue glass, tying the other end to the iron lightning rod below the balcony. [667] Pulling the jagged panes together with bleeding hands, he held the silhouette of the laughing king upright against the boiling sky. [688] He pressed his brass trumpet to the wire and screamed a rhyme about empires of rust bowing to dandelions. [707] A bolt of white lightning struck the portico. [715] The blast vaporized the lead, turning Barnaby's voice into a titanic howl that resonated inside every iron cannon in the plaza below. [737] The artillery sang his ridiculous poem, shaking their own rivets loose while Little Tallow's people laughed and claimed allegiance to the thunder. [759] Up above, with smoking eyebrows and a melted trumpet, Barnaby smiled as the clouds finally parted for morning. [777]
