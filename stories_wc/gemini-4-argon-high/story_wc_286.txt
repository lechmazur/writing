Oren centers the wet clay on his portable wheel as chimney smoke flattens against the bruising evening sky. [18] Around his small kiln, an abandoned beekeeping oasis in a scorched wasteland holds its breath between the heat of day and the biting cold of night. [44] Rows of bleached wooden boxes sit beneath skeletal acacia trees, surrounded by leagues of vitrified ash. [60] As a zen-minded potter, Oren does not mourn the dead world, nor does he demand that the desert yield water. [80] He simply observes the boundary where destruction stopped, driven by a quiet need to understand why some places resist change. [100]

For three months, he navigated the ash wastes by following sun patterns that bent strangely around pockets of surviving earth. [120] Where the light fell harsh and straight, the land was dead, locked in the singular catastrophe that had boiled the seas. [141] Yet where the shadows curved like the lip of a thrown bowl, the world suffered from fractured timelines. [159] In these rare hollows, yesterday and tomorrow overlap like poorly fitting shards of a broken amphora. [175] A traveler might step through a veil of blown dust and briefly smell damp clover from a century ago, only to choke on sulfur a heartbeat later. [202]

Oren trims the base of his spinning vessel with a bone blade, his movements possessing a sharp gentleness. [220] There is no room for sloppy sentiment when working with unforgiving stonewares, just as there is no survival in the wasteland for those who weep over ghosts. [247] Care must be precise, severe, and entirely devoid of greed. [257] He stops the wheel and reaches into his canvas satchel, withdrawing a curved piece of faded lantern glass he excavated from the oasis silt that morning. [283] The glass is cloudy with age and scratched by ancient sandstorms, yet it retains a faint, honeyed tint. [301]

The apiary keeper who once lived in the ruined stone cottage had understood the nature of this hollow. [319] Scavengers had passed through here before Oren, leaving behind rusted shell casings and boot prints, seeing nothing but dry timber and dead thorns. [342] They took nothing because there was nothing for violent hands to seize. [354] Oren, however, noticed how the brittle acacia branches leaned toward his campfires whenever he sang his low kiln chants, and how the temporal mirages thickened when he mended the broken hive roofs. [386] The remnants of this sanctuary are phototropic to kindness. [447] The heavy air presses the pale smoke from his firing stove down into the dry irrigation ditches, blanketing the ground in a fragrant haze of burning scrub oak. [475] Low sunlight strikes the oasis at a steep angle. [484] Oren lifts the damp vessel and sets it atop the central queen hive, aligning the embedded glass by following sun patterns etched into the ancient stone wellhead. [511]

[674] It pauses on the rim of his wet clay jar, drinking the moisture from the mud before vanishing into the flattening smoke. [696] He pulls his hands back, leaving the vessel unglazed and unowned upon the box. [710] The oasis endures not because it is strong, he realizes, but because the keeper who built it loved the bees without ever trying to trap the afternoon. [737] Satisfied, Oren cleans his bone blade in the dust and prepares his wheel for the dark. [753]
