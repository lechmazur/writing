The great brass carousel finally ground to a halt, leaving the kinetic lagoon above the clouds in a state of fractured stillness. [22] For hours, the spinning wooden horses had churned the suspended waters into a glowing froth of raw energy, powering the remnants of the sky pier. [47] Now, after the carousel halts, the surface flattens into shards of liquid glass that reflect a sky both ancient and unborn. [68] Vance waded into the calf deep ripples, a resolute scavenger determined to harvest the quiet before the gears resumed their rotation. [89] His mind was chronologically confused, a side effect of breathing the thin, ionized mist of the high altitude basin for too many decades. [112] He often believed that his youth was waiting for him next Tuesday, or that the rusted boardwalk had not yet been built by his younger hands. [138]

Between his gloved fingers, he pinched a salvaged disposable camera wheel, its serrated black plastic edges worn smooth by his constant, anxious thumbing. [161] In this elevated wasteland, the little notched ring was not used for advancing photographic film. [176] Instead, it served as a tuning dial to catch the acoustic sediment drifting through the currents. [192] Vance worked by collecting fragments of overheard stories that had been dropped into the water by tourists long ago, or perhaps tomorrow. [214] Click, went the plastic gear beneath his thumb. [222] From a glowing eddy near his knees rose the voice of a woman, speaking of a train that never arrived in a city of glass. [247] He rotated the tiny wheel three more notches, and the water murmured with a confession from a disgraced pilot who had sold his wings for bread. [273] Vance captured each syllable in a copper jar, weaving the strangers and their incomplete tales into a dense, resonant bait. [293]

He needed this narrative weight for a singular, desperate purpose: to negotiate with his own ghost. [309] Across the static surface of the lagoon, a figure began to coalesce from the rising vapor, wearing the pristine drafting coat Vance had discarded forty years in the future, or forty years in the past. [344] This specter was the relentless embodiment of his eroded ambitions. [354] Once, Vance had been the chief engineer of the cloud archipelago, a visionary who promised to anchor whole continents to the stratosphere. [376] Instead, the funding had dried up, the storms had torn the scaffolding apart, and his grand designs had shrunk to the size of a single, decaying amusement pier. [404] The ghost glided over the stilled waves, its eyes burning with the terrifying optimism Vance could no longer endure. [423] Every time the carousel stopped, the phantom appeared to demand why the sky cities remained unfinished, haunting Vance across his tangled timeline. [445]

Vance uncorked the copper jar and held it out toward the apparition. [457] He offered the vessel of stolen narratives as a bribe, hoping the ghost would consume the unrealized dreams of others and finally leave him in peace. [483] The phantom looked at the jar, then down at the disposable camera wheel still clutched in the old scavenger hand. [503] Without speaking, the apparition reached out and turned the plastic wheel backward against the natural catch of the pawl. [522] A sharp snap echoed across the lagoon, and the stolen stories spilled out, dissolving into meaningless static upon the water. [542] The ghost did not want the borrowed tragedies of strangers; it demanded that Vance finally regret the surrender of his own life. [564] For years, Vance had hidden behind his muddled sense of time, pretending that he could still build his empire yesterday, thus avoiding the pain of his failure. [591] The negotiation required honesty, not scavenging. [597]

Looking into the eyes of his younger self, Vance stopped winding the dial and let the crushing weight of his squandered potential flood his chest. [622] He allowed himself to deeply regret the blueprints he had burned, the compromises he had made, and the cowardice that had kept him tethered to this shallow basin. [650] A single tear dropped from his chin, striking the lagoon and sending a tiny, genuine ripple through the fractured stillness. [670] The ghost inhaled the bitter truth of that sorrow, its rigid posture softening as the debt between past and future was finally settled. [693] With a faint nod of forgiveness, the specter dissolved back into the white clouds below. [708] Vance dropped the plastic wheel into the water, watching it sink as the great carousel behind him groaned and began to turn once more. [732]
