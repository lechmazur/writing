Outside the bark walls, the iron cranes waited with their engines idling, creating a heightened stillness that pressed against the ancient wood. [22] Oren stood alone at the hush just before a condemned building is demolished, his brushes poised above the curved cedar palette. [43] Around him rose the vast tree trunk library hollowed by a guild of scholars three hundred years ago. [61] The scholars had evacuated their scrolls when the deep taproots rotted, leaving the colossal timber shell to the city wreckers. [81] Yet Oren had refused to abandon the scaffolding beneath the high interior canopy. [94] His lifelong commission, dictated by the founding charter of the sanctuary, was to mirror the sky inside walls of living heartwood. [115] For decades, the guild had believed that enclosing a perfect reflection of the heavens within the windowless timber would trap infinite wisdom in a finite space. [141] Oren had already painted the swirling nebulae and the pale morning clouds across the upper vault, blending lapis lazuli into the dark grain. [164] Only the central zenith remained bare, reserved for the visage of the first archivist gazing down like a waking sun. [184]

The irony of the task was known only to Oren, who worked as a face blind portrait artist. [202] When he looked at a person, features swam together into an incomprehensible blur of skin and shadow, devoid of unity or familiar recognition. [225] To compensate for his neurological blindness, he had trained himself to craft every likeness through pure geometry rather than intuition. [245] His method was fractally precise, breaking a human countenance down into thousands of interlocking triangles, microscopic pore clusters, and calculated ratios of bone. [268] Where other painters saw a warm smile or a stern brow, Oren saw only endless mathematical topography waiting to be mapped. [289] Because the first archivist had died long before living memory, no painted reference existed in the public archives. [307] However, during the frantic evacuation of the lower tiers, Oren had uncovered a secret book safe wedged inside a gnarled burl of the reading alcove. [332] Disguised as a leather ledger of botanical taxonomy, its hollowed pages concealed a velvet cavity containing a single silver minting from the founding year. [356] Now, perched high under the painted firmament, Oren held that relic between his thumb and forefinger. [372] He had to reconstruct the lost founder through faint indentations on a centuries old coin, translating worn metal into monumental life. [393]

The air inside the trunk was motionless, thick with the scent of dry sap and old parchment dust that refused to settle. [415] Not a single bird called from the dead branches above, and the distant city traffic seemed muted by a heavy bell of anticipation. [438] Oren closed his eyes and ran a fine brass caliper across the tarnished surface of the token. [455] Every tiny scratch and shallow groove where the minting die had struck the silver became a coordinate in his mind. [475] He scaled the submillimeter valleys up by a factor of ten thousand, plotting the ridge of a nose and the hollow of a cheek onto the primed timber overhead. [504] Stroke by silent stroke, he subdivided each plane into smaller and smaller facets of ochre and umber. [521] He believed that if his measurements were small enough, the illusion of a whole human spirit would finally emerge from the math, just as the painted sky was meant to become boundless. [553] For an hour of suspended breath, the only sound in the hollow trunk was the whisper of sable bristles against dry wood. [575]

When the final microscopic highlight was placed upon the painted pupil, Oren lowered his arm and stepped back to the edge of the plank. [599] He tilted his head upward, waiting for the triumph of completion, but instead found himself discovering limits he had spent his life denying. [622] The portrait above him was an astonishing web of crystalline geometry, yet it remained a cold map of eroded metal rather than a living gaze. [647] Dividing a surface into infinite fractions could never bridge the gap of his missing perception, just as blue pigment on dead bark could never truly hold the wind. [675] The scholars had been wrong to think a ceiling could contain the cosmos, and he had been wrong to think calculation could replace sight. [699] A low vibration shivered through the floorboards as the heavy iron cable tightened around the base of the trunk outside. [719] Dust drifted down from the painted clouds like silent snow, blurring the sharp edges of the founder and the false firmament alike. [741] Feeling a strange liberation in the failure of his art, Oren placed the silver coin back into the hollowed ledger and descended the ladder toward the open door, ready to step beneath the real, unmeasurable sky before the walls came down.
