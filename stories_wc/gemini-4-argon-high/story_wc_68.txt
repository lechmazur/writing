History always rots fastest right after the plague ends. [9] From my dry ledge inside the grand main culvert, I watch the green tide lap against the sunken city ruins of Oakhaven. [31] The survivors clinging to the upper cathedral spires call me a madman, or worse, a wannabe philosopher living in a sewer. [52] They are welcome to their petty insults. [59] I possess a dry brick for a pillow, an endless supply of blind eels, and an abrasive calm that their frantic preachers will never comprehend. [84] When you have watched ten thousand bloated citizens float past your kitchen grate, human vanity loses its power to impress you. [105] I spend my mornings scraping lime off my leather apron and composing treatises on the stupidity of the living. [124] Admittedly, my treatises mostly consist of misquoted fragments from ancient logic primers, because the ocean swallowed the university library before I could finish my second semester. [150] Yet even an amateur thinker can recognize a lie when it echoes down a drainpipe. [165]

Up on the dry rooftops, the remnants of society are already tearing each other apart over two competing narratives. [184] The High Synod claims the fever arrived first as divine punishment for our secular arrogance, and that the sea walls only collapsed after the dying engineers abandoned their posts. [213] Conversely, the Port Admiralty insists a sudden subterranean earthquake shattered the sea walls first, bringing stagnant marsh water that birthed the contagion. [235] Both factions drop weighted pamphlets into the flooded streets, demanding that scavengers swear allegiance to either the priests' guilt or the admirals' innocence. [258] Both factions are lying through their rotting teeth. [266] Because I am rationally rebellious, I refuse to sign their loyalty oaths or shout slogans at the sky. [284] Rioting with a pitchfork is for fools who enjoy getting shot by nervous watchmen. [298] True defiance requires a trowel, a measuring rod, and an utter disdain for authority. [312] My motivation is simple: I work to preserve ancient methods of empirical observation and mechanical timekeeping before the priests outlaw clocks and the admirals burn the old harbor logs. [341]

Down here in the dark, truth is not written on parchment; it is stacked in mud. [357] For thirty days, I have been excavating the main junction box, reading the city's demise through sedimentary layers of hardened muck. [378] Filth never fabricates an alibi. [383] The top stratum is a pale, chalky crust of quicklime, washed down from the streets during the final weeks of the mass burials. [406] Directly beneath that lies four inches of black pelagic silt, rich with crushed barnacles and deep ocean kelp. [424] Beneath the ocean silt sits a thin, greasy band of red coal ash mixed with apothecary glass, marking the first week the quarantine hospitals burned. [449] This stratigraphy alone disproves the Admiralty, as the plague clearly preceded the flooding. [462]

However, what I uncovered this morning at the exact boundary between the hospital ash and the marine silt destroys the Synod's story as well. [486] Wedged tightly into the petrified clay is a gleaming brass seafarer's chronometer cog. [499] I wiped the rim with my thumb and read the microstamped serial number through my jeweler's loupe. [516] It belonged to the master timepiece aboard the Sovereign, the flagship that supposedly vanished at sea months before the sickness began. [537] Surrounding the brass wheel are splinters of charred mahogany and granules of military blasting powder. [552] The deduction is elementary. [556] The Sovereign brought the plague home, and the two factions jointly dynamited the sea locks to scuttle the infected ship, accidentally flooding the city to hide their failure. [584] Their bitter public feud is merely a theatrical distraction to keep the survivors divided. [598]

Yet as I crouch by the lantern, I do not celebrate my discovery; instead, I agonize over a brutal philosophical dilemma. [619] In my dry alcove sits the last surviving pelagic regulator of the Old Guild, an intricate astronomical clock that lacks only a single beveled escapement wheel to function. [647] This embedded gear is that exact part. [654] If I leave the artifact undisturbed in the dirt wall and bring neutral witnesses down from the spires, the physical context will expose the rulers' conspiracy forever. [681] However, the brackish moisture will corrode the delicate brass teeth within a month, and the ancient craft of precision horology will die with it. [705] Conversely, if I pry the metal free to repair my regulator, I destroy the stratigraphic proof, reducing hard geology to just another unverified story in a city drowning in lies. [735] My fingers hover over the cold mud as I weigh justice for the dead against knowledge for the future. [754] With a dry, humorless chuckle, I wedge my iron trowel behind the brass wheel and pull it free. [772] The mobs above will always prefer comforting myths, so I might as well keep accurate time while they rot. [791]
