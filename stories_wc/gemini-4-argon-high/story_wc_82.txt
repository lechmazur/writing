In the floating island tea garden, the bereavement ritual always begins immediately after the shadow detaches. [16] When a citizen of the sky dies, their dark silhouette peels away from their cooling heels and plunges through the clouds, leaving behind a soul of pure, blinding agony. [45] Orin, a crafty lantern maker with calloused thumbs and squinted eyes, watched the silhouette of his wife slip between the manicured rows of silverleaf camellias. [70] What remained of Elara was an unbearable, radiant aura that scorched the delicate tea blossoms and seared his retinas with memories he could not hold. [95] In this high realm, mourning was a luminous torment, a brilliance so fierce it blinded the bereaved unless it was properly contained. [117] The priests claimed only they could harvest such light, locking it forever inside the great glass basilica on a floating isle tethered just above the tea terraces. [144] However, Orin had no intention of surrendering Elara to their cold vaults. [156] His sole desire was to fold grief into origami cranes, giving her trapped spirit wings to escape the merciless glare of the upper atmosphere. [180] To do that, he first had to steal the raw material of her final slumber through the forbidden art of dream recording. [227] The glass basilica on a floating isle loomed before him, its transparent buttresses humming with the captured, blazing nightmares of a thousand departed souls. [251] Because he had repaired the sanctuary lamps for decades, Orin knew the secret flaw in the sacred architecture. [269] He bypassed the guarded nave and stepped onto the Prismatic Helix, a miraculous stair of blown quartz that descends while ascending. [290] With every step upward toward the zenith of the dome, the bent light pulled his physical body downward into the inverted well of the sanctuary foundation. [316] This optical paradox was the only place where the atmospheric pressure was heavy enough to condense a loose spirit after the shadow detaches. [339] At the bottomless peak of the stair that descends while ascending, Elara waited as a swirling vortex of incandescent, weeping gold. [360] The heat of her unspooling final vision blistered Orin on his face, filling his mind with a dazzling, silent scream of her last feverish hours. [385] Weeping behind his dark lenses, he pulled a rough, uncarved beryl and a miniature treadle wheel from his leather apron. [405] The priests used liquid mercury for dream recording, which froze the visions into static agony, but Orin relied on the ancient method of gem polishing. [430] He pressed the raw beryl against the spinning copper disk, coating the surface with diamond dust and his own falling tears. [451] As he ground the first facet, the friction drew a thread of blinding gold into the stone. [518] The stone grew scorching hot, radiating a luminous torment that threatened to crack his calloused fingers and char his wooden tools. [595] Back beneath the quiet, misty arbors of the floating island tea garden, Orin knelt beside his workbench and locked the glowing beryl inside his finest brass lantern frame. [659] The angled facets did not merely project the agonizing vision; they began to transform the nature of the sorrow itself. [696] This was the secret of his trade, allowing him to transform the blinding agony of a nightmare into something gentle enough to touch. [719] Orin removed the glowing vellum and began his final task, driven by the need to fold grief into origami cranes. [739] With each crisp crease of a wing, the searing pain in his chest subsided into a quiet warmth.
