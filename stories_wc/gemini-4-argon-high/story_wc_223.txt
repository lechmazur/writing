[26] This wandering half shadow was cast by the unmoored solar mirrors high above, which no longer had a living monarch's signet to anchor their light over the palace. [78] For years he had worked for the crown as a crisis actor, paid to weep in staged collapses and smear theatrical soot across his cheeks to test the civic guard. [108] Then the royal foundational vaults had truly ruptured three weeks ago, turning a performer of counterfeit panics into a real crisis survivor who had clawed his way out from under genuine stone. In the center of the acoustic dome sat Censor Maren, her fingers resting lightly beside a marble chessboard missing all the pawns. [162] Only the tall, crowned figures of ivory and onyx remained on the squares, facing each other across an empty expanse where the foot soldiers belonged. [187] "When the next ruler ascends, the citizenry cannot be burdened by the ghosts of three hundred buried clerks and bakers," Maren murmured toward the curving wall. [239] Maren was benevolently ruthless in her curation of the state archives, erasing the names of the common dead not out of malice, but from a fierce, maternal determination to spare the survivors any lingering sorrow. [274] She had swept the carved pawns from her board that very morning to illustrate her philosophy: history required only the survival of the major pieces to restart the game. [303] "You survived the rubble, Julian, so let me grant you the mercy of forgetting it," she whispered into the stone. [323]

Julian looked at the empty middle ranks of the game table and felt the profound weight of everything that had been removed from the room. [507] "I am not here to argue with your mercy, Censor," Julian replied, his voice skimming the curved plaster as he began to pace the perimeter.
