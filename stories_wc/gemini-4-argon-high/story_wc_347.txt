Corin spat over the jagged parapet, watching the violet sea smash against the base of the cliffside labyrinth. [18] All around him, crooked trees sprouted from the stone trenches, their bloated boughs weeping a luminescent amber sap that smelled of vinegar and crushed marigolds. [43] As the reluctant caretaker of a cursed orchard, he despised every gnarled root and blistered leaf, having inherited the post only because his late uncle owed a blood debt to the local magistrate. [76] Above the tangled canopy, a flock of mechanical birds wheeled in jerky, stuttering arcs, their rusted tin feathers and brass talons catching the sunset in a display of graceless beauty. [106] They moved like dropped marionettes, clanking and wheezing through the salt wind, yet the crimson light burning across their dented steel breasts made the whole wretched sky look like a shattered cathedral window. [139]

A terrified shout echoed from the lower tiers of the maze, interrupting Corin as he tried to light his evening pipe. [160] He groaned, shoving the unlit pipe back into his patched coat, because he knew his only real purpose here was to guide lost travelers to safety. [186] Peering over the mossy ledge, he spotted two wool merchants huddled on a narrow stone island surrounded by deep silver ash. [207] If those fools stepped into the ash, the slightest vibration would alert the clockwork shrikes patrolling the cliffside labyrinth, and the birds would tear them to ribbons. [234] Worse still, the sea air was turning frigid, and in this blighted place, every living soul must escape the stone corridors before the night frost forms. [260] The moment the rime crystallized upon the cursed trees, their glowing sap would hiss into a freezing, poisonous fog that choked the lungs and locked the iron exit gates until dawn. [291]

There was a safe walkway hidden beneath the silver ash, a narrow vein of solid basalt that did not transmit tremors to the listening talons of the mechanical birds. [320] However, its winding route belonged to a forgotten blueprint, drafted centuries ago by a mad architect who burned his own parchments before leaping into the surf. [346] No human memory held the geometry of that safe passage, yet the cursed orchard remembered, because its roots had grown directly along the magnetized iron veins buried inside the safe basalt path. [378] Corin turned to the nearest marrow tree, an ugly, hunched specimen whose bark looked like boiled leather, and plucked a small nut from a cluster of thorny husks. [406] This seed was denser than lead, humming faintly against his calloused palm because it had drunk decades of magnetic current from the foundation stones below. [431] Working quickly in the fading twilight, he emptied his pockets of salvaged brass wire, three bent clockwork springs, and a spool of waxed linen thread to assemble a makeshift dowsing pendulum. [462] His movements were offhandedly exact, born from years of resentful practice; he did not bother measuring the wire or counting the twists, yet his fingers crimped the small nut into the brass cradle at the precise center of gravity. [501]

He whistled sharply to catch the attention of the shivering merchants below, gesturing for them to watch the ash at their feet. [523] Lowering the pendulum from the upper parapet, Corin let the weighted line drop until the seed hovered three inches above the metallic dust. [546] Immediately, the fierce polarity of the small nut tugged toward the hidden basalt vein while pushing violently against the iron filings mixed into the ash, leaving impressions without touching the perilous floor. [578] Because the suspended seed never made physical contact with the ground, no acoustic shockwave traveled through the stone to wake the mechanical birds roosting in the crooked branches overhead. [607] Corin strolled along the high wall, paying out thread with that same careless precision as the swinging pendulum carved a clean, silent trench through the silver powder below. [635] Line by line, the unseen magnetism traced the forgotten blueprint across the labyrinth floor, revealing a sharp, zigzagging path of bare basalt where the travelers could plant their boots in complete silence. [667]

The merchants followed the phantom furrow with desperate haste, stepping only where the dust had parted, while above them the clockwork birds tilted their tarnished heads and clicked their scissor beaks in baffled frustration. [701] White needles of ice were already creeping up the seaward rocks when the two men finally stumbled through the outer archway onto the safe gravel of the highway. [729] Seconds later, the frost struck the orchard in a wave of glittering white, turning the grotesque, weeping trees into a forest of glowing amber chandeliers and freezing the awkward brass birds motionless upon their perches. [764] Corin reeled in his thread, dismantled his rig, and stared out at the hideously radiant vista before retreating to his smoky cabin to finally light his pipe. [791]
