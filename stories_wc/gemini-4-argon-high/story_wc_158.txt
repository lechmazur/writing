Inside the gargantuan shell of a vanished leviathan, twilight pooled like cool water over pearlescent terraces. [16] For ages, two splintered clans had warred within these curving chambers, scarring the nacre with iron and bitter fire. [35] Now, a fragile peace held fast, known to the survivors as the blossom armistice. [49] So long as the luminescent anemones of the inner spiral remained in bloom, neither clan could draw a blade. [68] Tending this delicate truce was Vaelen, an insomniac poet whose heavy eyelids had not closed in a hundred seasons. [87] He walked the fluted corridors in a state of reef-shadow joy, a quiet, dappled gladness born from living safely beneath the shelter of the calcified arches. [113] Even when exhaustion tugged at his marrow, that cool, submarine delight sustained him. [126]

Vaelen understood that the flora of the sanctuary did not feed on ordinary sunlight. [140] Instead, his sleepless calling was to photosynthesize pain into beauty, coaxing the dark roots to drink the lingering sorrow of the clans and exhale it as radiant petals. [168] Whenever old hatreds murmured in the lower vaults, Vaelen composed verses of forgiveness to feed the hungry vines. [186] He delivered this nourishment via half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key wind that whistled through ancient siege fractures in the outer wall. [208] Because the shell had been cracked by catapults generations ago, the sea breeze never blew true, warbling in a discordant minor third through the spiral acoustic chambers. [235] Rather than fighting the draft, Vaelen pitched his weary voice to match its strange intervals, letting the breath of the ocean carry his unfinished rhymes into every hidden crevice. [264]

In response, the pale creepers advanced with a momentum that was shyly unstoppable. [277] If a sentry stomped near a tendril, it would bashfully curl inward for an hour, yet by morning it would have claimed another inch of bloodstained stone, splitting granite with soft, relentless buds. [310] Yet one chamber at the very apex of the whorl remained barren and perilous. [324] There, the original grievance had begun when a starfaring sister of the upper clan departed into the void, leaving her betrothed from the lower clan weeping on the shore. [353] Each family had blamed the other for her exile, nursing their wrath across centuries of longing while staring up through the shell's broken aperture at the indifferent sky. [381]

Only Vaelen possessed the final relic of that sundered love: a worn galaxy postcard that had fallen from a courier pod fifty years ago. [405] Its glossy front captured a swirling nebula of violet dust, while the reverse bore a single faded line in the sister's handwriting, promising that she had found peace among the stars and wished them the same. [441] Vaelen had kept the card tucked against his ribs, waiting until the creeping flora reached the threshold of the apex chamber. [462] Tonight, under a low tide, the timid runners had finally touched the ancient altar of bone where the first sword had been drawn. [485]

With trembling fingers, the poet climbed the steep, iridescent stair. [495] The air here tasted of salt and old iron, heavy with the unhealed ache of two houses that had forgotten how to weep together. [519] He knelt beside the altar and parted the hesitant, glowing leaves. [530] To seal the treaty forever, he needed to ensconce the galaxy postcard deep within the central root knot, wedding the memory of the distant sky to the living marrow of the shell. [562] Gently, he pressed the stiff paper into the damp, bioluminescent tangle until the pale fibers wove over the violet nebula and held it snug. [586]

Then Vaelen leaned back against the cold mother of pearl and opened his parched throat. [601] A gust rose from the surf below, howling crookedly through the jagged fissures, and he met it with a soft, wavering croon. [623] The melody lacked the grandeur of the old battle hymns, sounding more like a mother hushing a feverish child at midnight. [644] Yet as the crooked breeze caught his verse, the roots drank the ink of the sister's farewell and the poet's centuries of vigil. [667] A tremor of pure, cool light surged upward through the stems. [678] All across the vault, buds the color of deep sea coral unfurled in silent unison, casting shimmering teal reflections across the ceiling. [700] Below, sleepers from both clans stirred in their alcoves, smiling as the bitter weight lifted from their chests like silt settling in a calm lagoon. [725] The ancient grief had been wholly transformed into a canopy of living starlight. [738] Bathed in the gentle glow of the completed truce, the insomniac poet finally let his head rest upon the cushioned moss and closed his eyes. [763]
