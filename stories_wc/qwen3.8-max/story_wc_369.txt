Brin was a labyrinth maintenance worker, which meant he oiled hinges, swept moss, and apologized to hedges when storms bent them. [21]
The labyrinth grew inside a tower, and the tower stood on a cliff where weather argued with the sea. [40]
On that night it was a stormlit tower by moonlight, every window blinking with rain. [55]

Its arc was cracked, its mirror clouded, but the trees leaned toward it as if it remembered their names. [95]
Brin wanted to discover what trees witness, because trees never left the scene of grief. [110]

The beads healed in moonlight, slowly, like bruises turning from violet to faint gold. [154]
When clouds covered the moon, the sorrow stayed sharp and official. [165]

Brin did not trust men who measured safety with straight lines. [206]

Under the tile lay a drawer of scattered dreams, each one a thread of sleep torn loose by lightning. [277]
Brin understood that he must map them before the tower's sorrow overflowed its ledgers. [291]
