Mara Voss was a counter clockwise clock maker, which meant she repaired hours that refused to move forward. [18]
Her workshop stood in a mysterious lighthouse on a rocky island, where salt climbed the stairs and rust listened at the door. [40]

Mara knew the hunger stone fed on latent radiance, the hidden warmth sleeping inside unspent mornings and unspoken vows. [127]
She had spent her life neatly inventive, bending gears and glass until small machines could hold large sorrows. [145]

That chart hung beside her bench, inked in a line that looked like a sentence no court would overturn. [185]
