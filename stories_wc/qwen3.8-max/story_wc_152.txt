Before the coup, the cobalt observatory in autumn sits among red maple leaves, its dome the color of deep sky. [20]
In its cold gallery works Maren, a galaxy-chart embroiderer who stitches the heavens into cloth so fine that starlight seems caught in thread. [43]
She has come not to glorify the court but to listen for unheard stories, because every missing servant, silenced astronomer, and erased date leaves a gap in the night. [72]
When fools are safe, she disguises wisdom as foolishness, humming nonsense and asking lords which star resembles their aunt. [91]
They answer, eager to mock her, and in their careless answers she hears the shape of unraveling conspiracies. [109]
On the longest autumn night, a flame from eternal candle is brought into the dome in a brass lantern, because no ordinary fire may touch the galaxy chart. [137]
The flame burns blue and patient, and under it the stitched stars reveal marks hidden beneath the silk. [155]
Maren sees that the official chart has been altered, one future made to look inevitable while all other futures are trimmed away. [177]
The alteration is small, a planet shifted by a thread, but it will make the regent's coup seem written in the heavens. [199]
If the astronomers announce that omen before the coup, no loyal citizen will dare resist. [214]
Maren cannot shout, so she decides to synchronize the observatory itself against the lie. [228]
She works through adjacent possibilities, stitching side charts for every hour the coup might begin. [243]
Each chart is a neighboring world, a version of the sky where the regent's star rises honestly or not at all. [264]
By comparing them, she finds the single false thread that binds the forged omen to the ministers' secret letters. [283]
Those letters had been burned, but their ashes had fallen onto the chart, and the eternal flame makes the residue shine like faint dust. [307]
Maren reads the dust as servants' whispers once read it, names of prisoners moved below the cellar before dawn. [326]
One name repeats in the echoed silence of the dome, a child astronomer who had warned the court and vanished. [346]
Maren embroiders that name into the hem of the great chart, using a stitch that looks like a mistake. [365]
When the regent arrives with armed ministers, she bows and says the stars are shy and must be coaxed with silly songs. [387]
The regent laughs, but his eyes watch the lantern flame from eternal candle trembling against the forged planet. [405]
Maren sings a fool's counting rhyme and pulls one loose thread, not the false one, but the thread beside it. [425]
That small motion releases the side charts, and they fall around the dome like falling leaves, each showing a different possible hour. [447]
In every adjacent possibility, the regent's omen fails except in the single chart stitched by treason. [463]
The astronomers step closer, their faces pale, because they recognize the true sky and see how cleanly their own signatures were copied into the lie. [488]
The regent orders the chart burned, but the eternal flame does not obey ordinary command, and the brass lantern closes around it like a mouth. [513]
In the echoed silence that follows, the child astronomer's name catches light, and every servant in the gallery repeats it without sound. [535]
Maren then synchronizes the dome's shutters with the true rising of Mars, and the building answers with a bell long forbidden. [556]
The bell does not call for battle, but for hearing, and the city below lifts its face from market, alley, and prison gate. [579]
Before the coup can begin, the unheard stories arrive as witnesses, servants, prisoners, clerks, and mothers carrying names like lanterns. [599]
The regent's men lower their swords, not because they are good, but because the truth has made their planned future impossible. [620]
Maren gathers the false chart, the true chart, and the ashes, then folds them into a bundle no larger than a prayer. [642]
She does not claim victory, for conspiracies unravel one thread at a time, and autumn always leaves more silence to hear. [663]
When dawn touches the cobalt observatory, the flame from eternal candle is still burning, small and steady, waiting for the next unheard story. [686]
