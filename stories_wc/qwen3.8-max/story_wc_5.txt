In a withered orchard deep in a subarctic crater, Mira the flash-paper alchemist knelt beneath a sky paused like a held breath. [22]
She worked while the world holds its breath, because no season dared move in that hollow after the last comet passed. [43]
Around her roots rose like cold hands, and every branch carried the memory of fruit that never returned. [61]
Mira had come to translate heartbeats into maps, for the buried village below still sent faint pulses through the frozen soil. [82]
Those pulses were not ghosts, she believed, but directions left by people who had loved the land enough to hide inside it. [104]
From her satchel she lifted a storybook illustration plate, its copper edge worn smooth by generations of careful thumbs. [123]
The plate showed a garden no one remembered, and its engraved moon looked hungry for real light. [140]
Mira placed it on a flat stone, then opened a glass vial containing safekept starlight. [155]

Now the ash glowed softly against her palm, patient as a sleeping bird. [191]
To use it, she needed a pattern, and her method was creating patterns with discarded items. [207]
She gathered torn netting, cracked seed bowls, rusted pruning hooks, white bark, and buttons lost from coats that no longer walked. [228]
Each scrap had failed its first purpose, and failure made it honest enough to carry a second one. [246]
Mira demonstrated patience physically, laying each object down with fingers so slow that frost had time to lace her knuckles. [266]
She did not hurry, because a map made from heartbeats could not be forced any more than grief could be commanded. [287]
First she arranged the hooks in a spiral, matching the rhythm she had felt through the ground all morning. [306]
Then she set buttons along the spiral like stars, and the torn netting became a lattice over their silver faces. [326]
The pattern looked like a broken orchard seen from above, or perhaps like a chest opened to reveal its hidden roads. [347]
When the last seed bowl clicked into place, a moving stillness filled the crater like water finding its level. [366]
Mira breathed without breaking the silence, and the stillness answered by leaning closer. [379]
