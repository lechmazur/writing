In her satchel lay a tattoo stencil on vellum, cut with the last constellation her daughter had drawn before the fever took her voice. [48]
The observatory did not keep hours, only dust, brass rings, and curtains of intention that hung before every dark window. [68]
Visitors believed that if a curtain was touched honestly, it would show the wish hidden beneath a sorrow. [86]
Nara had come to illuminate them, though she despised hope enough to spit at its smallest lamp. [103]

Every ring she had ever sold was a cage for absence, and absence had made her wealthy in silence. [145]
Yet wealth could not buy the one lesson she wanted, to teach withering to bloom again. [161]
