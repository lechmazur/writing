After the happiness mandate, the blank-faced performer named Mara kept a farmhouse overshadowed by giant wind turbines. [17] The blades cut the sun into measured pieces, and the rooms lived in an emotional void. [33] Mara had been a performer before the mandate, trained to carry joy like a dangerous tool. [49] Now she narrated mundane tasks dramatically because the law required brightness and her heart refused noise. [65] She would say, "Behold, I lift the kettle, and the kettle becomes a small sun." [80] The words made no warmth, but they made a remade hush where grief could sit safely. [96] On the table stood a dried flower bouquet, tied with string from her mother's funeral. [111] Each brittle petal had browned into a color like blood that had learned patience. [125] Mara touched it only when the turbines paused for inspection, as if silence might break it. [141] That morning, an inspector from the Happiness Office came up the lane with a smiling mask. [157] His boots were polished, and his eyes were morally ambiguous, willing to pardon or erase. [172] Mara invited him in without moving her face, because blankness was her last honest art. [187] He asked if she had cultivated lightness in grief, as the mandate demanded of all mourners. [203] She answered by beginning to whittle a wooden bird at the edge of the table. [218] The knife curled thin ribbons from the pine, and she narrated each curl as a triumph. [234] "I peel away the weight," she said, "and the state receives a feather." [247] The inspector watched the blade, not her hands, and asked whether her performances hid rebellion. [262] Mara did not deny it, because denial was another kind of performance under the turbines. [277] She remembered the night her brother fell from the service ladder while painting a blade white. [293] The Office had called it a joyful accident, and that phrase had emptied her. [307] She kept the dried flower bouquet from his grave, though keeping sorrow was inefficient. [321] To cultivate lightness in grief, she had learned to trade small truths for larger silences. [336] She reported a neighbor's illegal radio once, and the report bought her three months of solitude. [352] That bargain still sat in her throat like a stone trying to become bread. [366] The inspector pointed to the bouquet and asked whether dead flowers served happiness. [379] Mara set down the whittled bird, now smooth and beakless, and began her demonstration. [393] She poured water into a glass and announced it as the filling of a quiet ocean. [409] She folded a cloth and called it the burial of a storm. [421] Each ordinary motion became a stage, and the emotional void in the room gained a shape. [437] The inspector's mask tilted, and for a moment his official smile failed. [449] He whispered that the mandate wanted citizens lighter, not hollow. [459] Mara said, "Lightness is what remains when a person has been hollowed fairly." [472] Outside, the turbines turned grief into power and power into something that resembled permission. [486] The inspector reached for the bouquet, then stopped, as if the remade hush had weight. [501] He asked what she would perform at the seasonal review. [511] Mara lifted the wooden bird and said, "I will make absence fly." [523] It was not a promise of joy, and it was not a confession. [536] It was a method for surviving a law that feared tears more than silence. [550] The inspector wrote a mark on his tablet, and the mark could mean mercy or erasure. [566] Mara placed the dried flower bouquet beside the whittled bird, letting them argue without sound. [581] When he left, the farmhouse seemed smaller beneath the giant wind turbines. [593] She stood in the kitchen, blank-faced, and waited for the blades to resume. [606] She did not hope exactly, but she carved the next day from quiet wood. [620] The turbines took their slow turns above her, measuring the fields without forgiveness. [633] In the remade hush, the dried bouquet and the bird waited for her next breath. [648]
