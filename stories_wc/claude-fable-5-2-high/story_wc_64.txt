The flag on Abel Quist's chess clock fell at a quarter past nine, three nights after Abel himself did.  Wren Hollis, gardener of Saint Odo's cemetery, had sat through his last forty minutes because stopping a dead man's clock felt like cheating.  "I win on time, you old spider," she said, and whistled two bars of a polka.  The silo took the tune, kept it a while, and gave it back from the wrong direction. 

Abel had rented the old grain silo at the cemetery's edge for thirty years, painted it cobalt inside and out, and woven there under a roof hatch he never closed.  Tonight the hatch held a coin of stars, and the blue walls drank the lamplight until the dark felt soft and napped, like the lining of a jewel box.  His spools still stood on their shelves, tenderly organized by shade, moss beside lichen beside yew. 

Beside his captured pawns sat the thing he had left her: an antique sovereign case, brass, round as a pocket watch, with a card tucked beneath it.  Not before my clock runs down, the card said, in his cramped weaver's hand.  Wren had obeyed, which was unlike her, because she badly wanted to know what he meant by it.  Her whole trade was guessing what people meant: she read the living by what they left on graves, a toy tractor, a peeled orange, plastic lilies chosen so they would never need a second visit.  Abel, who never said a straight thing in his life, had always been the one intention whose shape she could not find. 

She thumbed the catch.  Inside, where the spring should have pressed a stack of gold, lay a single sovereign, a skein of cobalt thread, and a slip of paper: The moths did not fray my border. 

The tapestry hung on the curved wall behind his chair, and she had admired it for years without once reading it.  It showed Saint Odo's from above, every bed she kept stitched in its season, her wheelbarrow a red fleck by the gate.  Only the north corner was plain green, as it was in life, a lawn the parish register called empty.  The border was a band of checkered squares, eight along each side, and from it hung seven snarls of frayed thread. 

Wren fetched her dibber and began to untangle them, humming, patient as she was with bindweed.  Each snarl, teased straight, became one cord tied with a deliberate run of knots, three on this one, six on that.  Where each cord hung gave a file; its knots gave a rank.  She laughed, sat down at the board, and tried them as moves, his last seven, played from beyond the flag.  They were nonsense; no piece of his could reach a single one of those squares. 

Then she looked up at the plain green corner and remembered Abel on his evening walks, rapping the north lawn with his cane and tilting his head at the answer.  "Uncharted echoes," he used to say, and she had assumed he meant the silo.  He had meant the ground: hollow places that answered a cane, paupers buried before the register began, charted nowhere but here. 

She traced each file up and each rank across the woven cemetery, pinning the cobalt thread from point to point.  All seven fell in the north corner, and together they drew a ring with one square left open at its heart. 

So that was the shape of it.  One sovereign was the old price of a common grave; he had joked about it for years, and she had taken it, like everything, for a joke.  He did not want the marble his nephew had ordered.  He wanted the open square, among the people nobody had counted, and he wanted them gardened.  He had not asked her aloud, she understood, because a request can be refused politely, while a puzzle, once solved, belongs to the solver. 

The gloom pressed close and plush around the lamp, and for one breath her whistle would not come.  Then she tipped her own king over, gently, because a win on time against a man that far ahead was no win. 

"Seven beds of snowdrops and a weaver in the middle," she told the blue dark.  "I'll see the vicar at dawn and bring my spade." 

She pocketed the sovereign case, blew out the lamp, and whistled the polka up toward the stars.  The silo kept it a while, and this time gave it back from the north.
