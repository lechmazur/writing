Six weeks after stopping the medication, Wren Calloway could hear the River Marl again at its true volume.  The pills had turned it into a hum behind glass, and her brother's death into a fact she filed.  Now everything arrived unfiltered, so she walked to Hollin Halt, the one place she had refused to chronicle.  For eleven years she had kept the Marl's logbook: levels, silt, drownings, the truth in plain numbers.  Only once had she lied to it.  One wet March morning she had copied the previous day's reading instead of walking to the gauge board, and the next afternoon Dov tried the ford below the station and did not come out. 

The station had been shut since before they were born.  Ivy had taken the canopy, the clock, the platform edge, and it pressed green hands against every window.  Dov used to play his accordion in the waiting room because the tiles, he said, sang back.  She went through to the gentlemen's lavatory only because the roof still held there and the rain had started. 

The walls were crowded with decades of strangers: phone numbers, insults, and the odd piece of wisdom.  "Nobody misses the last train on purpose," someone had scratched above the cistern.  Beneath it, in green marker, in handwriting she knew like her own pulse, ran five numbered lines.  Each was a small proverb followed by a six-figure map reference.  "One: trust the gauge, not the gossip, SK 412 668." 

Her birthday had fallen a week after the funeral, and Dov had always given her treasure hunts, ever since she taught him to read a grid.  This one had waited three years on a toilet wall for her to be awake enough to find it. 

She wanted to run straight to the fifth reference.  Instead she copied all five into the logbook, checked each against her map twice, and resolved to take one a day, in order, measuring the river at every stop before she touched anything.  No more copied numbers. 

The first reference was the gauge board itself, where a film canister held a heron's feather.  The second and third were the culvert and the willow, each with its own feather and its own honest reading entered in her shaking hand.  The fourth line said, "Whoever fears the middle never learns the river," and its reference sat on the gravel bar in midstream, at the ford.  She roped herself to the old signal post, probed each step with the gauge rod, and counted aloud, thigh-deep in the water that had taken him.  Without the pills, the fear was enormous and entirely hers.  She wrote the depth on her wrist, unwired a rusted tin from the ford post, and waded back. 

The fifth line would only allude to its prize, the way Dov always had: "The lungs are in the lamp room, SK 409 671."  The reference was the station itself, where she had started. 

In the lamp room, behind a curtain of ivy, his accordion lay wrapped in oilcloth, its bellows patched with tape where the folds had split.  One strip of tape was newer than the rest, and under it was a folded page from his pocket diary, dated the day before the ford. 

"Happy birthday, chronicler," it said.  "Read the board myself today: 2.3 and climbing, though your log says 1.4, so I caught you copying."  "I will cross where I like anyway, I always have, but you are better than a borrowed number." 

Wren sat down on the lamp room floor among the leaves.  He had known the true level.  He had seen her lie, forgiven it in a sentence, and walked into the river on his own stubborn reckoning.  That did not make the number true.  It did not make him less dead.  But it was evidence, and evidence was the only mercy she had ever trusted. 

She cried the way the pills had never allowed, loud and ugly, until the room rang with it.  Then she lifted the accordion and drew the taped bellows open.  It wheezed, leaked, and gave one cracked chord, like something breathing after a long time under. 

Before dusk she went back to the lavatory wall with her own pen.  She did not confess there; she only alluded, as he would have, and let the map carry the rest.  "Six: a patched thing still holds air, SK 409 671." 

Then she opened the logbook, corrected the March entry to 2.3 in ink, initialed it, and wrote today's level beneath, measured by her own hand.  It was not forgiveness yet, but it was a reference she could walk to.
