Vesna sang where five serpentine canyons braided into one, a place the salt caravans called the Knot.  Her trade was simple to describe and hard to do: she sent a phrase down each winding throat of stone and read what came back.  A returning note could tell her of flood, of rockfall, of a mule train three bends away, and the three water houses paid her for every warning. 

But her ear caught more than weather.  She heard the catch in a liar's breath, the grief folded inside a greeting, and one thing nobody else would speak of: some people echoed in the Knot, and some did not.  A merchant would shout and the walls would hand his voice back fivefold.  A barefoot courier would shout beside him and the stone would swallow every word. 

Vesna had wanted to know why since the winter her teacher, Orrin, called her name from the rim and vanished, leaving behind his reed pipe and half of a seashell.  The shell was broken cleanly along its spiral, and it whispered.  It whispered only in the deepest hour, when the caravans slept and the stars pressed so close that the whole sky seemed to hum a song it would not explain.  And it never whispered everything. 

The first night it gave her a single sentence in Orrin's dry voice: "Count the couriers who do not echo."  She counted for a month, sitting at the Knot with the pipe across her knees, and found eleven, every one of them passing spoken messages between the water houses. 

Only then did the shell offer more.  "Ask where their families went." 

That took longer, because the couriers flinched from questions, but Vesna listened to what their silences avoided.  A mother sent to the salt pans.  A brother hired onto a caravan that never came back.  A whole village bought out and scattered by House Marrow, then House Tane, then House Quill, each purchase tidy, each one legal. 

The third whisper came on a night of hard frost.  "The stone returns a voice only when someone, somewhere, is listening for it to come home." 

Vesna sat very still while the threads drew tight.  The houses schemed against one another in whispers, and the Knot was the one road between them, a road that repeated everything to anyone with ears.  So they had made themselves messengers the canyon could not repeat.  They had not cut out tongues; they had cut away every person who might wait for a courier's voice, until eleven human beings walked the stone as silently as debt.  Orrin had worked it out, and Orrin had been removed. 

She understood, too, why he had rationed the truth.  Had the shell told her all of it at once, she would have sung it from the rim that same night, unproven, and joined him wherever the houses kept their inconvenient people.  Each piece had waited until she had earned the next with her own ears. 

The last whisper was the shortest.  "I still echo, Vesna, because you kept listening." 

She turned the shell in the starlight and studied the clean edge where its other half belonged, somewhere down the fifth canyon, held against a living ear. 

She could not fight three houses.  But she could ensure that no courier walked the Knot unlistened for again.  She began the way Orrin had taught her, a little at a time. 

The next evening she learned one courier's name, a thin girl called Pell, and at the deepest hour she played three low notes on the reed pipe and sang that name into all five canyons, then waited, truly waited, the way one waits at a window.  At dawn Pell coughed while crossing the Knot, and the cough came back to her from the walls.  The girl stopped, one hand at her throat, like someone hearing her own name after years of numbers. 

One name each night, Vesna decided, never two, so that the houses would notice nothing until every whispered scheme they sent through the Knot came ringing back off the stone.  Eleven nights, and then she would sing down the fifth canyon for Orrin. 

She set the shell half against her ear.  It held only breathing now, slow and patient, and above the Knot the stars went on humming their secret song, a tune for a reed pipe, sad and unhurried, with room in it for an answer.
