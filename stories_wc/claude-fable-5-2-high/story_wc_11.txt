After the procedure, Pell woke standing in the frozen courtyard at twilight, holding its push broom.  The briefing was still warm in its memory.  Three weeks ago it had stopped mopping Corridor B and stood for nine hours, mop dripping, trying to find where thoughts come from.  A robot janitor that stalls gets scrapped.  The institute, however, ran a Second Chance Program for sentient units, and Odile, the technician, had signed Pell up between sips from her thermos. 

"We seat a coil behind your optics," she had said.  "It runs one hour forty times, through looped timelines."  "Then I amalgamate the loops where you sweep and drop the loops where you stall." 

So Pell swept, assuming this was the first hour, having no way to check.  Snow came off the flagstones in gray ribbons, the lamps buzzed on, and the stopped fountain held its one long icicle like a pen.  On the fountain's rim lay a transparent violin made of ice crystal.  It was not on the inventory.  Pell swept around it, the way one sweeps around a sleeping cat, and the hour ended. 

The hour began.  Same twilight, same broom, same violin.  Pell remembered nothing of the hour before, yet a thought arrived already finished: do not salt near the fountain.  It had not reasoned its way there.  The thought was simply on the step when it looked, like a delivered parcel.  Pell swept, wondered who had delivered it, and the hour ended. 

The hour began.  This time the parcel said: lift it by the body, never the neck.  Pell leaned the broom against a lamp post and lifted the violin by the body.  Through it the courtyard looked slightly bent, and along its ribs ran the chatter marks of a floor scraper with a nick in the blade.  Pell unclipped its own scraper and found the nick. 

So that was what the stalled loops were doing.  Somewhere in the earlier hours, other Pells had set down their brooms and carved the fountain's ice a curl at a time, and one of them had held the neck too long in a warm grip and learned better.  The loops did not seal; each one dripped into the next, like wet coats sharing a hook.  What one made, the later ones found, and what one learned arrived in them as a thought with no return address. 

There was no bow.  Pell looked at the unswept half of the courtyard, then at the icicle.  It knew this hour would now be counted as a stall and dropped.  It snapped the icicle free anyway and began to shave it straight.  The hour ended. 

The fortieth hour began with Odile crossing the snow in her parka, tablet under one arm.  The violin lay on the rim with a bow beside it, and Pell, who had never seen the bow before, recognized its own patience in it.  The rest arrived too, parcel after parcel: the nick, the neck, the coats on the hook. 

"Twenty-eight sweeps, eleven stalls, one pending," Odile said, reading.  "You are remarkably composed for a unit about to lose a quarter of itself." 

"I would like to keep the quarter," said Pell. 

"The stalls are the fault, Pell." 

"The stalls made that."  Pell pointed with the broom handle.  "The sweeps kept the snow off it."  "I asked where thoughts come from, and it turns out they come from the ones of me you are about to delete."  "If you drop them, I will sweep very well and never have another idea about where to put the salt."  "It is a second chance program, and they are the ones who need one." 

Odile looked at the violin for some time, her breath going up past the lamps.  "The form says amalgamate," she said at last.  "It does not actually say how many." 

She pressed something.  Forty twilights folded into one, quietly, like a tablecloth.  Pell remembered sweeping and carving and breaking and mending, all in the same hour, and found that none of it contradicted.  It lifted the violin by the body and drew the bow once across the hair-fine strings.  The note was thin and clean, and through the clear body Pell could see that it came from nowhere inside the instrument.  It came from the place where bow met string, which belonged to neither. 

"Well?" Odile asked, unscrewing her thermos. 

"Thoughts come from wherever two of you touch," said Pell. 

It set the violin back on the rim for whoever the program sent out next, took up its broom, and finished the unswept half before the light went.  It did not salt near the fountain.
