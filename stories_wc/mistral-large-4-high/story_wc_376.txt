Elian worked as a gilded-leaf calligrapher, pressing gold ink onto the broad leaves of the city's hardiest vines to record the day's truths for those who could afford honesty. [88]
His studio occupied a converted potting shed near the central canal, where the humidity kept his materials supple and his conscience clear. [110]
In the eastern conservatory, the candor lilies grew thick along the irrigation channels, their white petals a biological lie detector that wilted within minutes of any falsehood spoken nearby. [139]
Elian had learned to speak only in small, careful truths, nurturing the lilies that served as the city's moral compass, for he sought something stranger than gold or water. [168]
His motivation was simple and obsessive: he wanted to find where lost conversations go. [182]

One morning, while repairing a floorboard in his studio, he discovered a shredded parchment and a fragment of zinc-etched postal plate wedged between the joists. [256]
The metal bore the ghost of a postmark from before the drying, etched with coordinates that matched no known district. [276]

He worked by lamplight, matching the water stains and fiber patterns, treating each fragment with the reverence of a sacred text. [325]
