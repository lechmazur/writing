Mira descended into the catacomb orchard where apple trees grew from human skulls, their pale roots snaking between femurs and ribcages like ghostly fingers. [24] She carried a lantern casting half-light, leaving the stone corridors in perpetual dusk where shadows seemed to breathe. [42] She was a reckless idealist, convinced that destinies were negotiable, that fate could be rewritten if only she dared to touch the invisible threads binding souls to paths. [70] In her pocket lay a burned tarot card, edges charred black, but the center still visible: The Hanged Man, suspended between heaven and earth, eyes serene. [96] Mira had come to barter destinies, offering her own remaining years for the chance to patch the jagged holes in her sister's soul. [119] The orchard was a marketplace of last resorts, where roots grew through bone to drink memories, and the air tasted of copper and overripe fruit. [144] Mira moved through the half-lit corridors with a fiercely gentle tread, her boots silent against the stone, her heart hammering against her ribs. [167] She knelt beside a femur, her fingers brushing the cold bone with a tenderness that belied the desperate gamble, her touch soft as moth wings. [192] This was her method: inlaying meaning everywhere, pressing her palm against the skull-trees and seeing their rings as rings of fate, each bone a sentence in a larger story. [221] "It is not enough to wish for change," she whispered to the darkness, her voice steady despite trembling hands, "one must become the vessel, must embed oneself in the bargain." [251] The roots responded, writhing upward to trammel her ankles, not to trap her but to anchor her to the transaction, ensuring she could not flee when the price came due. [281] This was the ancient law of bartered destinies: to be held in place while the universe weighed souls on invisible scales, to be trammelled by the earth that drank the offering. [312] Mira pressed the burned tarot card into a hollow eye socket, and the orchard hummed with recognition, the sound vibrating in her teeth and bones. [337] She felt her life thread unwinding from her chest, a silver filament stretching toward the roots, offering itself to fill the cracks in her sister's spirit, and she did not flinch. [368] The half-lit corridors glowed amber as the roots tightened around her legs, weaving her essence into the network of bone and branch, binding her fate to the hungry soil. [397] Inlaying meaning everywhere had always been her gift and curse, seeing significance in dust and cracks, finding patterns where others saw only chaos, but here it was necessary magic. [426] She was reckless to believe she could fix what was broken, yet fiercely gentle in how she gave herself away, piece by piece. [449] The trammel of roots grew tighter, winding around her calves, ensuring she could not escape the bargain once the exchange began, locking her into sacrifice. [497] The catacomb orchard drank deep, roots snaking between bones to carry her sacrifice upward through the half-lit corridors, threading her offering through the network like a needle.
