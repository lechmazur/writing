The storm had not relented in seventy-two hours, yet Mara descended the rope bridges of the hanging orchard each dawn, her boots finding purchase on moss-slicked planks suspended three hundred feet above the ravine where wind screamed like the dying. [40]
She was a rational truffle forager by training and temperament, approaching the black diamonds beneath the storm-battered apple trees with the same methodical precision she applied to soil pH readings and mycelial network mapping, never swayed by superstition or hope. [80]
In the time after the empathy virus had swept through the valley two years prior, leaving survivors neurologically fused in a constant hemorrhage of involuntary feeling, Mara had discovered her devastating attribute: she was incapable of deception, her body betraying every secret thought the moment it formed behind her lips. [130]
This involuntary truthfulness had rendered her a pariah among the remaining orchard keepers, for she could not hide her revulsion at their suffering, could not mask her relief when they wept, could not pretend comfort she did not feel. [169]
Three weeks ago, she had smuggled a relic from the sealed quarantine zone below: an antique glass eye, milky and opalescent, dating from the Victorian era, its painted iris cracked but still seeming to track movement in the dim light of her pack. [212]
She had not stolen it for value, but for the rumor that attached to such prosthetics, that they had once been used in experimental surgeries to block sensory input, to create artificial barriers against overwhelming stimulus. [248]
Her motivation was singular and desperate: to cultivate psychological safety in a world where minds had no walls, where the joy of one stranger could trigger euphoria in another, where grief spread like the storm currently battering her hanging orchard. [288]
She sought to occlude the residual neural pathways of the virus, to physically block the empathic tendrils that still connected every survivor in the ravine, forcing them to feel each other's trauma across impossible distances. [323]
In the toolshed beneath the orchard's central platform, she spread discredited textbooks across a warped workbench, their spines cracked, their medical theories long debunked by the Academy, their pages yellowed with warnings about cures that had failed or killed. [362]
The method she pursued existed only in these rumored cures, in marginalia describing tinctures of belladonna and ground crystal applied to the temples to create a membrane of isolation, a way to wall the mind against the emotional contagion. [401]
Mara had already tried the modern treatments, the pharmaceutical blockers that left patients drooling and vacant, but she wanted something with fractured grace, a solution as broken and beautiful as the glass eye in her pocket. [437]
She climbed to the highest branch where the wind tore at her hair, holding the antique glass eye up to the lightning, watching it catch the light in fractured patterns that resembled grace despite its damage. [473]
"I am terrified of what I will become," she said aloud, and the truth poured from her involuntarily, raw and bleeding, witnessed only by the storm and the ravine below. [503]
Yet as she pressed the cold glass against her temple, following the discredited textbook's instructions to grind the antique eye into powder and mix it with storm-water and crushed apple blossom, she felt the occlusion beginning, a door closing in her mind. [545]
The empathy virus had made the world a place of constant exposure, but here, in this hanging orchard battered by wind, she was building a sanctuary, using smuggled relics and forbidden knowledge to cultivate psychological safety for herself and perhaps for others who could not bear the weight of feeling everything. [596]
She was a rational truffle forager, and this was her harvest: not mushrooms, but silence, gathered grain by grain from the broken pages of history. [621]
The storm raged on, but Mara stood calm in her new solitude, the antique glass eye dissolving against her skin, the truth still flowing from her lips but no longer carrying the crushing weight of others' emotions. [658]
In the ravine below, the wind howled, but she felt safe, finally, in the space she had occluded, a fractured grace achieved through smuggled wisdom and involuntary honesty. [686]
