The insomniac poet had not closed her eyes in four hundred years, not since the ancient tide first rolled the gargantuan shell onto the coral shelf where darkness gathered like a held breath and bioluminescence flickered in the deep. [39]
Within its spiral chambers, she had learned to ensconce herself among the nacreous curves, arranging her thin body along the logarithmic whorls as though the shell were a cradle designed specifically for sleepless things that needed to hide from the sun. [80]
Her fingers, stained with squid ink from decades of verse, traced the ridges of the inner wall where a galaxy postcard had wedged itself centuries ago, its holographic nebula still pulsing with violet and gold light against the dim reef-shadow that never quite became night. [125]
The postcard depicted a blossom armistice written across the stars, flowers of light blooming between warring constellations in a truce that lasted only as long as a supernova, and the poet recognized in its image her own lifelong project of negotiation with the dark. [169]
She was shyly unstoppable in her devotion, a quiet force of nature that persisted without fanfare, determined to photosynthesize pain into beauty the way deep-sea anemones converted crushing pressure into radiant color that glowed in the absence of sunlight. [208]
Across centuries of longing, she had tried every remedy the ocean offered, from the soporific pollen of underwater lilies to the rhythmic pressure of the tide against the shell's opening, but her mind remained a lantern that refused to dim even in the deepest dark. [253]
It was in this endless vigil that she discovered the method that would save her, not from wakefulness, but from the despair of it, learning to catch the half-sung lullabies that drifted down from the surface world on an off-key wind that smelled of salt and distant storms and carried the voices of those who slept. [309]
These melodies, incomplete and wavering, carried the fragments of dreams from sailors and mermaids and lost children, and the poet gathered them in her palms like precious water, letting them seep into the cracks of the shell where they took root in the mineral-rich darkness. [354]
From these half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key wind grew the blossom armistice, a garden of moonflowers and ghost orchids that sprouted between her poetry tablets, their luminous petals softening the hard edges of her grief until the pain became fertile soil for something new. [399]
The reef-shadow joy of this place was not the bright happiness of sunlit shallows, but a deeper, pulsing gladness that lived in the blue-dark, in the bioluminescent plankton that danced around her cot like fireflies made of starlight and captured sorrow. [440]
She ensconced herself deeper each night, nestling into the shell's heart where the galaxy postcard glowed softly against the wall, its nebula seeming to breathe in time with her own restless heart that had learned to beat in rhythms of its own. [482]
Across centuries of longing, she had accumulated thousands of verses scratched into mother-of-pearl tablets, each one a transformation, each stanza a leaf turning pain toward light, and now the blossoms responded to her insomnia with a truce of their own making. [523]
The poet was shyly unstoppable, her quiet persistence wearing down the resistance of her own sorrow until it surrendered completely, blooming into beauty that required no sleep to sustain it, only the endless dark and her willing, ink-stained hands. [562]
She had learned that to photosynthesize pain into beauty was not to deny the suffering but to feed it to the blossoms, to let the roots drink deep from the well of her wakefulness and produce flowers that glowed in the reef-shadow. [604]
When the great current finally shifted after all these centuries, pulling the gargantuan shell back toward the abyssal plain where no light reached, she did not struggle, for she had become shyly unstoppable, and the off-key wind still carried lullabies to wherever she drifted. [648]
In the shell's spiraling depths, surrounded by the blossom armistice and the galaxy postcard's eternal bloom, the insomniac poet smiled, ensconced in her creation, her centuries of longing finally converted into reef-shadow joy that needed no dawn to validate it, only the dark and her endless song.
