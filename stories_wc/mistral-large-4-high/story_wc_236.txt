[39] She had been an aromatherapist in another life, before the high fever had stolen her sense of smell three winters ago, leaving her with only the phantom memory of bergamot and jasmine on her skin, scents that now existed only in the ghost-rooms of her mind. [85] Now she worked with clepsydras, ancient timepieces that measured existence through the steady, hypnotic drip of water into graduated vessels, finding in their brass mechanisms a language that did not require scent to understand. [119] Her fingers, once trained to blend essential oils with the precision of a chemist, now traced the intricate gear trains with a sensitivity that could detect the slightest misalignment, the tiniest burr that might stop time itself. [156] The shop smelled of linseed oil and yellowed paper, scents she could only imagine through memory, yet she moved through her morning ritual with the trembling calm of a priestess preparing for a ceremony she was not entirely sure she believed in. [266] She withdrew a birthing clamp from the bag, its curved steel jaws scarred by nearly two centuries of use in delivery rooms, the screw that had once held its pivot point now missing entirely, lost to time or perhaps to the chaos of a long-ago birth.
