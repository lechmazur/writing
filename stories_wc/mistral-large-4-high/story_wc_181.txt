Dr. Elara Voss floated through the airlock of Asteroid 734-B, her curator's badge swinging against the glass like a pendulum refusing to settle. [23] The mission brief had described an astral orchard blossoming in the vacuum, trees of crystallized starlight bearing fruit that hummed with temporal radiation, but nothing prepared her for the bewilderment that seized her chest when she saw the grove glowing amber and violet against the black. [69] She was meant to catalog the inheritance of a civilization that had vanished before it began, a collection arriving out of chronological sequence, each artifact deposited before its own creation. [99] Her motivation burned bright against the void: to pursue forbidden knowledge that the Galactic Archive had sealed behind seven locks of quantum encryption. [122] The situation was simply complicated, beautiful in its impossibility, like trying to alphabetize a library where the books wrote themselves backwards. [143] The trees did not grow upward but spiraled inward, their roots drinking from pools of liquid time that reflected yesterday's storms and tomorrow's droughts. [167] In the orchard's center stood a pedestal of compressed moonrock, and upon it rested a pistachio shell cameo blank, its surface carved with spirals that seemed to move when viewed peripherally. [198] Elara reached out, her fingers trembling not from fear but from the vertigo of nonlinear inheritance, for the moment she touched the shell, she saw her own hands releasing it, the image playing in reverse loop behind her forward motion. [238] She needed to inquire about the mechanism, to ask the orchard itself why it had chosen her as the bewildered curator of its impossible timeline, but the trees offered only rustling silence that sounded like pages turning. [275] She pressed her palm against a trunk and felt the bark pulse with centuries compressed into heartbeats, each ring containing a century lived in reverse. [300] She retreated to the habitat module's only bathroom, a cramped cylinder with frosted glass walls that looked out upon the stars, for it was here, surrounded by the hum of recycled air and the echo of her own heartbeat, that philosophical breakthroughs always arrived. [344] The mirror showed her a woman she did not recognize yet, someone who would exist only after she had finished her work, someone who already knew the answers. [372] The knowledge was forbidden because it existed outside the timeframe between the first and last page of any book, slipping through the cracks of causality like water through fingers. [401] The Archive feared this knowledge because it turned archivists into prophets and prophets into unwitting gardeners of causality. [419] She understood now that the pistachio shell was not an object but a question, and she was the answer being written in real-time. [442] Every question she asked the shell echoed backward, so that by the time she finished speaking, she had already heard the answer three seconds before she thought it. [470] When she returned to the orchard, the blossoms had shifted, revealing fruit that contained not seeds but memories of futures that would never branch. [494] Elara placed the cameo blank into her satchel, feeling the brightened disquiet settle over her like a familiar coat, the strange comfort of knowing that some inheritances arrive before you are born and depart after you have already left. [533] The disquiet was not unpleasant; it tasted of copper and starlight, the flavor of truths that illuminated while they unsettled the mind. [555] She would inquire further, catalog every anomaly, and in doing so, become the nonlinear inheritance herself, passing backward through time to plant the orchard she was currently admiring. [583] She realized the pistachio shell was merely a placeholder, a blank canvas shaped like a nut, waiting for her consciousness to fill it with the specific gravity of her understanding. [613] The asteroid spun on, lonely and luminous, while between the first page of her arrival and the last page of her departure, she existed everywhere at once, simply complicated and finally understood.
