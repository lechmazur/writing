Mist curled through the structure's open arches, softening the edges of the world until the boundaries between stone and air seemed negotiable, permeable, alive with possibility. [62]
At the center of this luminous space stood Mira, an attentive mystic whose reputation had been built on decades of observing the invisible currents that flow between living things and the objects they touch. [96]
She stood firmly grounded, her boots sinking slightly into the loam, her spine straight as a compass needle pointing toward true north, refusing to sway in the shifting winds of doubt that had plagued her research for so long. [135]
Her motivation had crystallized years ago, during a winter night when she had watched her grandmother's teacup shatter on the kitchen floor, and in the fragments, she had sensed something impossible: the weight of seventy years of morning rituals, the accumulated love of countless breakfasts, the emotional residue clinging to porcelain like steam. [188]

Today was the timeframe when assumptions shatter, when the solid ground of scientific materialism would crack open like thin ice, revealing the rushing water of truth beneath. [267]
Mira had spent the previous night preparing her method, for she would not commune with the spirits of objects through Ouija boards or seance circles, but through the patterns in frost on a car window, those delicate crystalline structures that formed when breath met glass in the cold dark hours before dawn. [319]
She believed these frost patterns were not random decorations but maps, emotional cartography written in ice, showing how feelings moved through the world and settled on surfaces like condensation. [348]
The object of her study sat upon the stone altar before her: a glass prism, clear and sharp-edged, purchased from an estate sale, heavy with the unknown history of its previous owner. [380]
Mira had chosen it specifically because it was firmly grounded in its own physicality, dense and unyielding, a material that refused to be metaphorical, that insisted on being exactly what it was while somehow holding more than its atomic weight suggested. [421]
