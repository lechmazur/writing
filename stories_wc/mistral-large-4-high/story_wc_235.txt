The grave had moved again while I slept, sliding three feet north across the frost-heaved earth, the headstone now leaning against a granite boulder that had not been there last Tuesday, or last year, or perhaps last century, for I am chronologically confused in a way that makes calendars weep and clocks run backwards like frightened crabs scuttling across a kitchen floor. [62]
I am a chronicler of lucid dreams, a profession that requires me to remain conscious while the world dissolves into symbol and sensation, mapping the architecture of the subconscious with the precision of a cartographer charting enemy territory, documenting the impossible geographies that unfold when the mind releases its grip on consensus reality and allows the impossible to bloom. [121]
The mountain ranger cabin sits at the edge of the treeline, a structure of weathered pine and stubborn stone that has witnessed the collapse of my sense of sequence, the moment when yesterday began to bleed into tomorrow and both dissolved into a continuous, shimmering present that tastes of ozone and old snow and the particular silence of high altitude. [181]
I write upon a birch bark scroll, peeling the white skin from fallen trees and flattening it between heated stones until it becomes a surface that accepts ink with the reluctance of a wild animal, curling at the edges, resisting the domestication of the page, smelling of sap and forest floor and the ancient memory of the tree from which it was torn. [244]
My method for surviving this temporal chaos is getting jealous of past selves, envying the woman who woke with certainty, who knew that morning followed night because the sun had risen in the east, not because the mountain had decided to rearrange its own geography in the dark hours before dawn, shifting valleys and ridges like a sleeper shifting blankets. [304]
She wore wool socks and believed in gravity; she did not know that graves could migrate like caribou across the tundra, that time could fold upon itself like a letter never sent, that the dead could change their minds about where they wished to rest, that the earth itself could become as fluid as water. [359]
This is feral nostalgia, not the gentle ache of memory but a predatory longing that hunts through the corridors of my mind with teeth bared and claws extended, wanting to tear apart the present to get at the girl who was whole, who was linear, who had not yet seen the hole in the ground that waits for her with patient, mineral patience. [422]
My motivation remains constant despite the confusion: to distill chaos into narrative, to take the shattered fragments of chronology and forge them into a story that makes sense, that provides a rope to climb out of the labyrinth, a thread to follow through the maze of my own unraveling, a map to guide me back to myself. [479]
But the story wants to be wild, wants to run naked through the pines, shedding plot and character like a snake sheds skin, leaving me naked in the cabin with my scroll and my envy, my hunger for selves that are gone and will not return, my jealousy burning like a low fire in the grate. [535]
Last night, while recording a dream in which I drowned in a lake of liquid hours, I found myself on the roof of the cabin, arms spread wide against a wind that tasted of copper and pine resin and the cold breath of approaching winter, and I began to soar. [585]
Not falling, not the controlled descent of a parachute or the plummeting terror of the cliff, but soaring the way eagles do, riding updrafts that exist only in the liminal space between sleeping and waking, between the grave I have seen and the life I am still living, suspended in the amber of the moment, weightless and free. [643]
From above, the grave looked like a punctuation mark, a period at the end of a sentence that refused to end, and I felt the jaded wonder of a woman who has seen too much to be surprised by miracles, yet is still amazed by the impossible weight of her own survival, the strange grace of continuing to exist. [702]
I am jealous of my past selves with an intensity that frightens me, watching them through the window of memory, the child who collected stones, the girl who loved without calculation, the woman who built this cabin before the confusion set in. [744]
The scroll curls in my hands like a question mark, and I answer it with every stroke of charcoal, every record of the impossible, every dream that visits when I am awake enough to remember. [779]
This is my life now, after seeing my own grave, a wilderness I chronicle with jaded wonder at my own survival. [800]
