The onyx-ink cartomancer stood alone in the midnight glade where silver grass swayed beneath a sky bruised purple with approaching dawn. [21]
She had climbed above the clouds on a ladder of braided starlight, driven by a singular motivation: to find where lost time accumulates when it slips from human grasp. [50]
Seventeen years had passed since the birth of her daughter, yet in this cloudbound realm, repentance had frozen her in the moment of that delivery, the choking beauty of the sterile room pressing against her memory like a fist. [89]
She was willingly perplexed by the cartomancy that had led her here, her cards refusing to show anything but blank onyx surfaces, as if the future itself were ashamed to exist in this space between breaths. [125]
In her pocket sat a cracked saucer from a dolls' tea set, porcelain spiderwebbed with a fracture that appeared the morning after the birth, when time began to leak from her fingers like sand. [159]
The family custom demanded that each birthday she trace her child's hand upon a linen tablecloth with charcoal, pressing the small palm into fabric so the outline would grow alongside the girl, a method meant to anchor the child to linear time. [201]
But the ritual had failed, for the cloths now held only ghostly impressions, the charcoal hands trapped in two dimensions while the girl moved into a future the cartomancer could not reach. [233]
She felt the weight of cloudbound repentance settling over her shoulders like a mantle woven from guilt, for she had blinked during the birth and missed the second her daughter's first breath became the first theft of time. [271]
The midnight glade above the clouds was a place of choking beauty, where the air tasted of ozone and forgotten lullabies, where each blade of silver grass held a dewdrop containing a lost minute. [305]
She knew she must unfasten the leather thong of her satchel containing the seventeen birthday cloths, for only by releasing them could she follow the trail of her ritual into the deepest part of the cloud-sea where time pooled. [344]
Her fingers, stained with onyx ink, trembled as she worked the knot loose, willingly perplexed by the absurdity of her quest, yet unable to stop. [369]
As the satchel opened, the tablecloths rose like white birds, each bearing the charcoal outline of a hand that had grown smaller instead of larger, the tracings shrinking as the years accumulated elsewhere. [402]
She followed them through the luminescent mist, the choking beauty intensifying with each step until she could barely breathe, her lungs full of the heavy scent of lilies and regret. [432]
The cards in her other hand showed only the cracked saucer, over and over, as if the porcelain fracture were the only future worth reading. [457]
She understood she was not searching for her daughter, but for the place where time pooled when it fell from human hands, a reservoir of accumulated seconds where the cracked saucer waited, patient and perplexing. [492]
The family custom had been a method of preservation, but she had misunderstood its purpose; the tracings were not anchors but breadcrumbs, leading the willingly perplexed mother back to the source of the temporal leak. [527]
She found the accumulation at the glade's heart, a lake of liquid amber where hours swirled like cream in tea, and there, floating on the surface, sat the dolls' tea set saucer, its crack a dark line across the porcelain moon. [568]
The choking beauty stopped her breath, for every lost moment had gathered here in this cloudbound space, forming a weight that kept her repentant and tethered. [594]
She knelt at the edge of the temporal pool, her onyx-stained fingers hovering over the liquid, and she knew she must unfasten her own grip on the past, must release the cloths she had clutched for seventeen years. [632]

The tablecloths descended like snow, settling on the silver grass, the hand-tracings fading as the time returned to its place, flowing back through the clouds to the daughter who had grown without her mother. [702]
She remained kneeling in the midnight glade, willingly perplexed by the mercy of the moment, cloudbound repentance lifting from her shoulders like a cloak, leaving her free above the choking beauty of dawn. [735]
The onyx ink on her fingers dried to dust and blew away, and she understood that some futures could only be read in the space between birth and loss, in the accumulation of what was gone. [771]
