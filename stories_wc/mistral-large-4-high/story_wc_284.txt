Mara Voss stood at the precipice, her fingers tracing the engraved pillbox hidden deep in her coat pocket. [18]

Now, she had come to this bluff not to die, but to fix what was broken, to repair the fracture in her soul and the rupture between herself and the one man who might still believe in redemption. [38]

The wind howled across the bluff, threatening to erase her work, but she wrote quickly, the silver hush of the place muffling even her own heartbeat. [26]
She had chosen Zephyr Bluff because it was neutral ground, suspended between the old world and the new, between the spy she had been and the person she might yet become. [31]
