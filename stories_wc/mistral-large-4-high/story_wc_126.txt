It was a chronic hush, thick and permanent as the tropical humidity, muffling the dripping condensation that traced the glass ribs overhead in endless silver threads, swallowing footsteps and breath alike into its velvet depths until the air itself seemed to hold its breath. [84]
Mira played her violin each dawn among the shipwrecks that ringed the flooded grove, her fingers finding delicate notes in the residual hush that only an acoustically perceptive ear could catch, resonances trapped between rusted hulls and the submerged bones of apple trees, hearing the slow creak of timber against timber, the sigh of water moving through broken panes, the secret songs of rust consuming iron in the deep places. [154]
She was an echo-harbor violinist, trained since childhood by her grandmother to coax reverberations from spaces where sound should have died, and the greenhouse had become her cathedral of unfinished things, a sanctuary for broken beauty that the world above had abandoned to the rising waters.
