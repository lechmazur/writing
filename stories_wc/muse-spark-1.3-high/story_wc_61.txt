<story>Oue had lived longer than any human record and remembered every gardener by the shape of their hands. [18] She was an otherworldly sentimentalist who pressed fallen petals between sheets of cold starlight and labeled them with names she could not forget. [41] Her home was a nebula garden grown inside the Veiled Reach where dust and ice had been coaxed into soil. [61] There the flower beds floated in long ribbons and the ponds held reflections of newborn suns. [77] In the center stood a trellis of bone white branches that curved into a loop which approximates infinity when seen from above. [99] Generations of the Voss family had planted that loop with glow lilies meant to guide passing ships. [116] They worked with troubling serenity moving slowly among the blooms while the void hummed softly beyond the membrane. [134] Old Ana Voss arrived that cycle with her granddaughter Wren for the autumn transit of the seed barge. [152] Ana carried a small brass calculating machine gear worn smooth by a century of thumbs. [167] It had once turned inside the navigation engine of her grandmother and clicked each time a new course was set. [187] The family kept it because their generational patterns demanded that every keeper add one tooth of memory before passing it on. [208] Wren did not want to keep anything and said so with the blunt honesty of twelve year olds. [226] She wanted to sell the relic at Port Meridian and buy passage away from soil and duty. [243] Oue listened from behind the lilies and felt the old quiet ache of watching children repeat parental departures. [261] She loved humans precisely because they repeated and revised in equal measure. [273] That evening Ana taught Wren to tend the light beds without flame or fuel. [287] Their purpose from the first planting had been to share light without using fire so lost ships could find harbor without burning. [309] Combustion was forbidden under the membrane where oxygen ran rich and a single spark could unmake a century. [327] Instead the lilies stored starlight in their throats and released it slowly through the night cycle. [343] When the nebula fog thickened Ana showed Wren how to find the root lines by sonar pinging with a hollow seed pod. [365] She tapped the pod and listened as the sound returned in soft rings from stem and stone. [382] Wren learned quickly and soon mapped three beds by ear alone while Oue hummed approval from the shadows. [400] Across the quiet sigh of falling petals the garden shifted and the lilies began to open one by one. [419] Each bloom exhaled light in a slow pulse that traveled down the loop and outward toward the dark lanes. [438] A distant freighter answered with a blink and altered course toward the glow. [451] Wren watched the ship turn and felt pride wrestle with resentment in her chest. [465] If she left no one would keep the lamps and the lane would go dark again. [481] If she stayed she would become another notch on the gear another name pressed in starlight. [497] She confronted Ana beside the pond and demanded why the family clung to such fragile work. [513] Ana turned the calculating machine gear over in her palm and told the story of its first keeper. [531] Her grandmother had wrecked on an unlit rock and sworn that no other crew would die for want of a lamp. [552] She built the first bed from wreckage and taught her daughter to tap roots in the dark. [569] Each daughter had wanted to leave and each had chosen to stay long enough to teach the next. [587] Oue stepped into the lamplight then unable to remain a witness and offered Wren a petal marked with the name of her great grandmother. [611] The girl held the translucent skin and saw veins that glowed like charted courses. [625] She understood that memory could be a transit as real as any voyage between ports. [640] That night Wren placed the calculating machine gear at the root of the oldest lily and pinged it gently to hear it sing. [663] The tone came back clear and layered with a century of careful hands. [676] She decided not to sell the gear but to carry it with her when she shipped out at dawn and return with new light. [700] Ana wept quietly and Oue recorded the choice with troubling serenity for patterns can hold and also release. [718] By morning the petals were falling faster and Wren walked the loop once memorizing every turn. [734]</story>
