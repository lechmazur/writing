<story>The energy beings arrived on a Tuesday without sound or shadow and left the highways humming faintly afterward. [18] Ellis had been a drifting roadside poet for eleven years, trading verses for rides and soup and sleeping under overpasses when rain found him. [42] He was ethically playful in the way of old tricksters, stealing only loneliness and repaying every kindness with interest and a better line. [65] His one constant possession was a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape, lifted from a ditch outside Marfa where a man had cut himself free and run. [94] Inside its twisted fibers the escapee had knotted tiny scraps of paper, each marked with a date he had tried not to forget. [117] Ellis kept the rope looped at his waist, not to climb but to remember that freedom always leaves a frayed edge. [138] After the energy beings arrived, time no longer flowed but folded, and at dusk people could hear yesterday brushing against tomorrow like dry grass. [162] Ellis wanted only to discover the texture of time, to learn whether it felt like river stone or loom cloth or human skin. [185] He followed the humming off Route Nine to a terra-cotta grotto under starlight, its walls red as cooled embers and cool to the touch. [209] Inside, two figures waited beside a low fire: Mara, who had guarded the Marfa prison for twenty years, and Luis, the man who had cut his rope and run. [238] Above them drifted three energy beings, tall transparencies of heat and attention, patient as librarians between storms. [255] The beings had offered Mara and Luis impossible choices wrapped in mercy, one remembered life saved at the cost of another remembered life erased. [279] Mara could keep the night Luis escaped, when she looked away on purpose and lost her pension and name, or she could forget it and be honored again. [307] Luis could keep the morning his daughter was born while he hid in creosote, or he could trade it to restore the guard he had struck on the fence. [336] Neither would choose, so the beings waited and the grotto walls sweated red dust in the heat of their hovering. [356] Ellis bowed to all four and offered tea he did not have, then sat to listen because poems grow best in unchosen ground. [379] With passionate subtlety he drew them out, asking not for facts but for small weathers of memory, the smell of bleach, the sound of keys. [404] Mara spoke of mercy as a heavy door, Luis spoke of freedom as a thirst, and the beings brightened faintly at each honest word. [428] Ellis unwound his frayed coil and invited them to feel its roughness, by uncovering layers of fiber and ink and sweat. [449] He showed how Luis had hidden dates inside the strands, and how Mara had written her report on the back of a prayer card. [473] He began to pattern pebbles on the grotto floor into a loose spiral, each stone holding a moment no one wanted to lose. [496] This stone is the looked away second, he whispered, and this is the newborn cry, and this is the struck guard breathing again. [519] The beings leaned closer, curious, for they collected textures but had never understood why humans clung to pain with love. [539] Ellis proposed a game, ethically playful and binding, that no prison and no star could refuse. [555] Let Mara and Luis each place their impossible choices upon the stones, he said, and let time itself keep both memories for one turning of the earth. [582] For one night the grotto would hold every version true, and at dawn the beings could taste which texture endured without forcing a cut. [606] The beings consented with a chord like distant bells, and the terra-cotta walls bloomed with layered light. [623] Mara saw herself honored and disgraced at once, Luis held his daughter while kneeling beside the wounded guard, and neither heart broke. [645] Ellis walked among them touching wall and rope and brow, learning at last the texture of time as braided and frayed and warm. [668] He understood that choice need not always be a blade, but could be a holding, patient and rough as hemp against palm. [690] At dawn the beings dimmed, sated, and left the memories intact, having learned subtlety from a poet who owned nothing but attention. [712] Mara kept her scar and her honor, Luis kept his daughter and his debt, each carrying both weights more lightly for being shared. [735] Ellis rewound his rope, now holding three new knots from the night, and stepped back onto the humming highway under fading stars. [757]</story>
