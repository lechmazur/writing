<story>Ilen was born where great beasts had died and left their ribs standing like towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes. [23] Her people called the place Kethra, and they spoke Tesh, a language built for stone and wind. [40] As a girl she worked the copper cuts, and dust settled into her scars until she became a copper-veined storyteller known in every camp. [64] She was quietly articulate, choosing few words and shaping each one cleanly so listeners leaned closer. [80] When the company men came for diamonds, they found one flawed diamond rumored to be cursed because three owners had died coughing blood. [103] The company sold it cheap to Ilen, thinking a storyteller would not fear a stone that sang faintly at night. [123] These things happened slowly across the death of a language, for Tesh did not fall in battle but thinned with each school year. [146] Trade talk replaced prayer talk, then kitchen talk, until only Ilen and her blind grandson Jor cared for the old sounds. [167] Jor could not see the history cuts carved on the bone towers, and that sorrow gave Ilen a purpose to translate texture into sound. [191] She remembered a hollow rib deep in the canyon, a tunnel of echoes where a whisper returned for days layered upon itself. [213] She decided to harness that tunnel to keep Tesh alive as sound even after no mouth spoke it daily. [232] At first her shouts tangled and died, for the echoes fought each other like angry children. [248] Then she watched the razor swifts that nested in the bone towers and learned to read them through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight. [275] When the flock folded left, sound curved left, and when it split around a tower, echoes split also. [293] She began to time her syllables to their turns, speaking softly between wingbeats so the tunnel could carry each tone cleanly. [314] She set the flawed diamond on her tongue as a weight, for its fault made her voice buzz and the bone walls answer stronger. [338] Men said the curse woke, for at night the tunnel repeated names of the dead owners in her own voice. [358] Ilen did not fear it, understanding that the stone only held what the echoes gave it. [374] Each morning she led Jor into the cool dark and placed his hands upon the carved texture of ancestors. [393] Then she pressed the diamond to the bone and sang the meaning, letting the tunnel of echoes lift and hold the sound. [415] Rough cuts became low drums, smooth polish became flutes, and deep cracks broke into the clicking consonants of Tesh. [434] Jor laughed aloud, for he could hear shape for the first time and answer in perfect old words. [452] Year by year the last speakers died, and traders wrote Tesh down as extinct in their ledgers. [469] Yet in Kethra the tunnel kept speaking, fed by wind and swift wings and the memory of a quietly articulate woman. [490] Ilen grew thin, her copper veins darkening to green, but she taught Jor to harness the echoes without her. [509] She showed him how to watch the birds before singing, how to set the stone, how to listen for the returning wave. [531] On the final winter she could no longer walk, so Jor carried water to her and described the flock in Tesh words she had saved. [556] She smiled to hear her language living in a blind boy who had never seen bone or diamond or sky. [576] When she died, Jor placed the flawed diamond in her mouth as custom demanded for storytellers going silent. [594] The curse rumor ended there, for no death followed except the slow fading of grief. [609] Now travelers who enter the canyon hear layered voices teaching texture, and they swear the bone towers themselves repeat Tesh lullabies through circling birds. [633]</story>
