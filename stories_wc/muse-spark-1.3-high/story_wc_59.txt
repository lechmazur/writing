<story>Prince Cael had not been washed since the gears stopped singing. [11] He was still called a bedraggled prince in the lower workshops, though no one said it unkindly. [28] Above the iron city hung his inheritance, a suspended palace anchored to giant stalactites in the hollow roof of the world. [49] Its chains swayed in warm updrafts while below pistons thumped in endless rhythm through day and night. [66] Cael lived among clocks and ledgers because his father had traded songbirds for steam valves. [81] The world below had become precise, oiled, and gray, a mechanical world that measured sunrise in kilowatts. [98] He wanted, with a private ache, to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world before his own heart rusted. [116] That longing settled into a quaking reverie whenever he climbed to the cold observatory and listened to the palace groan. [136] In the vaults he spent his nights hypothesizing about everything the engineers had discarded as useless. [152] He pieced together broken music boxes, moth wings, and letters his mother wrote before the smokestacks rose. [169] One rainless winter he found a manifest listing an extinct bird's egg kept in mineral oil since the last forest fell. [190] The entry trembled under his thumb like a living pulse through the discovery that followed. [205] He traded his coronet for the key and descended to Cistern Nine with a lantern shaking in his hand. [224] There on velvet gone to dust lay the egg, speckled blue and larger than his two fists together. [242] No heat remained in it, only a bitterly sweet smell of almonds and old rain. [257] The keeper whispered that its species had sung in chords no machine could reproduce. [271] Cael carried it up through dripping tunnels to his workshop in the palace keel. [285] He did not pray, for prayer had been standardized, but he began to assemble what feeling demanded. [302] He would build a womb of brass and glass to coax the dead back toward song. [318] Copper coils became arteries, watch springs became breath, and a phonograph horn became a throat for lessons. [335] He called his work a flawed genesis, knowing no gear could truly replace a mother. [350] Each evening he warmed the oil bath and played fractured recordings of wind through hollow reeds. [366] The egg ticked faintly as metal expanded, and in his tired mind it seemed to listen. [382] Through the discovery of marginal notes in his mother's journal, he learned she had once heard the living birds. [401] She described dawn breaking open like a yolk and voices stitching light to leaf. [415] Her words made his hands steadier even as the palace shuddered in a deep earth tremor. [431] Stalactites creaked, chains sang, and dust fell like snow upon his instruments. [443] He dreamed the palace would fall if he failed, and he worked with fever brightening his eyes. [460] On the fortieth night the shell showed a hairline crack that had not been there before. [476] He held his breath, afraid his hope was only condensation. [486] With tweezers he lifted the fragment and saw not feathers but tiny filament wires blooming toward the light. [504] His machine had invaded the egg, or the egg had accepted the machine, and he could not tell which. [523] Sorrow rose in him, bitterly sweet as the first taste of remembered spring. [536] He had not resurrected life but had grown a hybrid that shivered and chimed when touched. [552] When he played the reed recording, the thing inside answered with a thin perfect third. [567] It was flawed, it was uncanny, and it was beautiful in a way the ledgers could never price. [585] He wept then, not from triumph but from recognition that beauty could return changed. [599] He carried the trembling incubator to the grand balcony where workers below could hear. [613] The palace swayed gently, its anchors groaning among the giant stones above. [625] He lifted the horn toward the smoke and let the small voice sing out over the pistons. [642] For a full minute the great engines faltered as men paused with wrenches in midair. [657] Even the foremen looked up, caught through the discovery of sound into sudden stillness. [671] No forest returned, no extinction undone, yet something unmeasured entered the air. [683] The bedraggled prince bowed to the city that had forgotten how to listen. [696] He understood his flawed genesis would not save the world, only soften its turning. [710] And in that quaking reverie between chain and stone, machine and feather, he finally felt at home. [727]</story>
