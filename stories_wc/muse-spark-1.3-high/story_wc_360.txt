<story>Mara kept her topaz workshop open only between vanishing stars. [10] The roof was cut glass the color of old honey, and through it the sky bled from black to blue. [30] She was a cryptic letterpress operator, famous for answering questions with other questions set in type. [46] Clients brought grief and love on scraps of paper, and she promised to print them true. [62] What she really wanted was to understand the mathematics of emotion. [73] She believed sorrow could be divided, carried, and proved like a theorem. [85] She habitually borrows personalities the way other printers borrow ink. [95] With widows she became soft and slow, with sailors she swore and laughed too loud. [110] It was not mimicry but study, a lens ground for each heart. [122] On the last night of the meteor fall, a boy brought her a halo fragment. [137] It glowed faint white and hummed against the iron chase, too light and too cold to be metal. [155] He said his sister had vanished where the stars went out, leaving only this curve of light. [172] Print her, he begged, so I can feel what she felt. [183] Mara set her composing stick shaking, because pity is an equation she had never solved. [198] She decided to disorient herself on purpose, to lose north and find the boy. [212] Her method was through deliberate ambiguity, leaving every proof half inked and half white. [226] She pulled two type cases, one of joy and one of mourning, and mixed them blind. [242] She asked the boy if his sister sang when afraid, and he said he did not know. [259] She asked if loss felt heavy or hollow, and he said both, and neither. [273] Good, she said, then we will print both and neither. [283] All night under the thinning stars she borrowed the boy, becoming twelve and sleepless and angry at heaven. [301] Her hands moved in lucid turbulence, calm and exact while her mind spun like washers in a flood. [319] She locked the halo fragment itself into the press as a sort, beside lead letters that shivered. [336] When she rolled ink, the fragment drank black and gave back silver. [348] The first proof read YOU ARE GONE AND YOU ARE HERE. [359] The second proof, pulled upside down, read I AM THE ONE WHO STAYED. [372] The boy wept because both sentences were true and neither was his. [384] Mara felt transposed empathy rise in her chest, his grief playing in her body a half note higher. [402] It was not his feeling copied but shifted, as a melody shifts when scored for a different instrument. [420] She understood then that emotion has no single sum, only translations with remainder. [433] She printed a third sheet through deliberate ambiguity, leaving the sister's name half uninked. [447] The halo fragment flared and threw cold light across the topaz walls and the boy's wet face. [464] Between vanishing stars, the press groaned and the sky outside thinned to a last few nails of fire. [482] Mara, still wearing the borrowed boy-self, wanted to run and to stay in equal measure. [497] She disoriented the type further, shuffling lines so grief could not settle into habit. [511] She told him the mathematics he asked for was not addition but resonance. [524] If I carry your note in my body, she said, you can rest your hands. [539] He touched the fresh print and the halo cooled, dimming to the color of milk in rain. [556] Dawn took the last star, and the topaz roof turned from night mirror to amber lamp. [572] The boy folded the ambiguous proof into his jacket, calmer because someone else remembered the tune. [588] Mara cleaned the press, returned the borrowed self gently, and felt her own age settle back on her shoulders. [607] She kept no copy, for some equations balance only when one side lets go. [621] When clients came next evening, she was cryptic as ever, asking what weight their words should weigh. [638] But under her calm hands the turbulence remained, lucid and useful as a river under ice. [654]</story>
