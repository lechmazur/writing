<story>Sarn had been a titan when titans still knelt to fold the sky. [13] Now he stood no taller than a temple door, a diminutive titan kept small by the vow he had sworn to remember. [35] He kept the ancient temple library lost in time, where dust hung like slow snow and the aisles breathed out of sequence. [57] The empire outside had fallen and risen and fallen again, but here the lamps never quite went out. [75] Sarn was regally unpretentious, mending his own robe and sharing his rice with mice who nested in the commentaries. [94] He remembered the true queen as a child who hid among these shelves and learned to read by lamplight. [113] After her ship was lost, a cousin took the throne in the capital and sealed his claim with a stolen signet. [134] Sarn could not march or accuse, for his feet were bound to the threshold by old magic and his voice could not cross the years. [159] He resolved to unmask a pretender to the throne, not with armies but with proof no court could burn. [178] It happened across the pause of shifting constellations, when the stars held their breath between one age and the next, the library doors thinned. [202] In that stillness, books borrowed themselves and drifted outward into other libraries, other hands, other centuries. [218] That wandering was governed by a rune of drift carved beneath the central desk, a slow spiral that taught parchment how to stray and return. [243] Sarn fed the rune with patience and polished its grooves with oil, and in exchange it let him choose which volumes would travel. [266] He wrote not in margins but through cryptic messages left in library books, sewing truth where only the attentive would find it. [288] His ink came from a single iron gall ink pellet, dark as a plum stone, gifted by the true queen on her last visit. [312] When moistened, it bloomed black and bitter and permanent, and a single pellet could outlast a dynasty if used sparingly. [332] Night after night he dotted its water with a brush tip and added false colophons, altered genealogies, and marginal stars. [352] To the careless eye they were scribal errors, but to scholars they formed a chain leading back to the stolen signet. [373] He copied the nursery rhyme the queen had invented, where each first letter spelled her nurse's name and village. [392] He transcribed the harbor log showing the cousin's ship anchored far from the wreck on the night she vanished. [411] He slipped a pressed temple flower between pages, one that bloomed only in the royal garden and withered at another's touch. [432] For decades of outside time, which passed here as seasons of quiet coughing from the stone, no answer came. [451] Then a young cataloger in a distant city noticed the same odd correction repeated in three unrelated donations. [469] She followed the thread with growing wonder, cross checking watermarks and finding Sarn's small hand everywhere. [485] When soldiers came to seize the books, she smuggled them out and read them aloud before the council, voice shaking but clear. [507] The pretender laughed and ordered the seal brought forth to sunder her argument with one stamp of authority. [525] Instead the wax cracked wrong around the stolen signet, and the old nurse stepped forward and named the village in the rhyme. [547] The court did not riot or sing, for the revelation felt less like triumph than like a graceful collapse of a long held breath. [571] Far away, Sarn felt the rune cool under his palm as the traveling books settled back onto his shelves, their work done. [593] He set the last sliver of the pellet into its box, bowed to the mice, and slept at last with his forehead on the true queen's primer. [620]</story>
