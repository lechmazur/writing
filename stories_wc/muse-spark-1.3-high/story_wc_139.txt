<story>Lena learned honesty the way other children learn shoplifting, by watching her father do it wrong. [16] Ray could forge a signature, a deed, a tearful goodbye, and he taught her the tell of wet ink while she vowed never to use it. [42] After his heart gave out in a motel outside Reno, she took the night job in the Harlow Funeral Home prep room to pay back what she could calculate. [71] She kept his old brass vintage ink pot on the steel counter beside the trocars, filled now with honest black ink. [92] In it she dipped a plain nib each night to enter names and sums in a ledger, her attempt to find the mathematics of a fair trade for a lifetime of takes. [124] The prep room smelled of bleach and mint and cold, and everything hummed with a deathly vitality that kept her awake and oddly tender. [148] Bodies came in stiff with stories, and she washed them, stitched them, and listened as if listening could balance a scale. [169] On a Tuesday in late fall, the back door shuddered under a soft knock instead of the usual gurney rattle. [189] Old Mama Aguilar wheeled in her nephew Tomas, a thin man with gray pallor who was very much alive. [208] He had fled detention after his brother drowned in the river, and he was asking for political asylum with papers still wet from a lawyer who had stopped answering. [237] Mama Aguilar begged Harlow to hide him among the quiet dead until morning, when the church van could carry him to the hearing in Phoenix. [262] Harlow, who feared licenses more than ghosts, said no and then went home, leaving Lena with the humming cooler and a choice. [284] Lena understood cons, and this was not one, so she sat Tomas on a stool by the sink and gave him tea in a paper cup. [310] You can stay while I work, she said, because the dead do not inform and the living must earn their stillness. [331] He told her about posters he pasted, prisons he named, and a brother who taught him to swim against current. [351] She told him about Ray and the ledger, and how no sum ever closed without someone forgiving the remainder. [370] Working steadies her, and she began by waiting actively, keeping her hands busy with the night's preparation while keeping her eyes on the door. [394] There is a careful attention in the prep room that liquefies rigid thinking, turning fear into simple tasks of washing and covering. [416] She showed Tomas how to fold sheets, how to label, how to breathe through the mouth when the chemical air burned. [437] He learned quickly, his hands gentle on an old miner's shoulders, tucking cotton with reverence no school had taught him. [457] At three the back alarm chirped and headlights swept the frosted glass, and two officers came with a paper that was not quite a warrant. [482] Tomas went pale and slid behind the cooler, and Lena felt her father's old instinct flare to lie brightly and fast. [503] Instead she uncapped the vintage ink pot, wrote the time and badge numbers in her ledger in plain view, and kept washing the miner. [527] We are in mid preparation, she said, and state law forbids interruption once the cavity work starts. [544] It was a bluff built from truth, and her steady voice made it hold. [558] The younger officer stepped back, but the older one shouldered past the curtain rail and caused a breach of the quiet sanctuary they had kept. [583] Cold air rushed in, a tray clattered, and Tomas stood up rather than let a dead man take a knock meant for him. [606] Lena stepped between them with wet gloves, not blocking but witnessing, her ledger open and ink still shining. [624] This man is under my care till dawn, she said, and every mark you make here will be written down and signed. [646] The older officer stared at the miner's peaceful face, at Tomas with folded sheets in his hands, at Lena's honest handwriting. [667] Something in the room's deathly vitality softened his jaw, as if life insisted louder where death was handled gently. [686] He lowered his flashlight and said the hearing notice could be served at sunrise without dragging a witness from holy ground. [707] They left with the frosted door sighing shut, and Tomas wept quietly into a paper towel that smelled of mint. [727] At dawn Lena entered two lines in Ray's ink, one debt balanced by shelter given, one life carried forward without price. [748] Tomas boarded the church van with Mama Aguilar, holding a copy of her entry as proof he had been seen and cared for.
