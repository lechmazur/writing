<story>Toben had always been a meek blacksmith on Marrow, an orchard planet riddled with floating rock arches. [17] He mended pruning hooks and wagon tires while louder men drilled for copper beneath the trees. [33] When the company left, they left rubble, dry pits, and orchards choked in dust. [47] Toben stayed because no one else knew how to coax resilience from rubble. [60] He was quietly determined, though his voice shook when he spoke of replanting. [73] Each morning he made one deliberate choice and followed it until dusk. [85] First he forged his hammers into grafting blades and narrow spades. [96] Then he began using abandoned mine shafts as cisterns and nurseries for young trees. [110] The shafts breathed cool air and caught drip water from the arches above. [123] Marrow's arches floated silent and pale, their shadows crossing the rows like slow hands. [137] Under them the apple and quince roots struggled through crushed stone and ash. [150] Toben lived between understanding and confusion in those early months after the collapse. [163] He understood soil and iron, but not hunger or how fear emptied a town. [177] Most settlers slept in the lower tunnels to save food, waking only when needed. [191] So Toben offered tools and repairs in exchange for bartered awakenings. [202] For one sharpened saw, a family would wake for three days to clear stones. [216] For a mended pump, two brothers would wake for a week to carry water. [230] He recorded each bargain in chalk on a shaft wall, careful and fair. [243] A traveling show had crashed years before, leaving crates of costumes and paint. [256] Among them Toben found a length of magician's silk, thin as breath and blue as morning. [272] He learned to wrap fresh grafts in the silk to hold moisture against the wind. [287] At dawn the wrapped branches shone under the arches with azure defiance. [299] People laughed at first, then stopped laughing when buds pushed through the cloth. [312] A girl named Sella woke from her family's bargain and stayed to help without counting days. [328] She asked why he did not leave with the company ships. [339] He said iron remembers the hand, and land remembers care. [349] She told him the sleepers dreamed of orchards and woke crying. [360] Together they lowered saplings into shaft mouths where light fell in soft shafts. [373] Toben taught her to hammer a loose band, to test bark, to listen for roots. [388] She taught him to sing while working so the waking hours felt shorter. [401] One storm cracked an arch and dropped stone through a nursery shaft. [413] Toben dug with bleeding hands until he found the buried pots unbroken. [425] Sella wrapped the shaken stems in magician's silk and tied them upright. [437] That night more sleepers woke without bargain, drawn by lamps and singing. [449] They cleared rubble by lantern light and shared cold soup from iron kettles. [462] Toben moved among them, meek no longer in purpose though gentle still in manner. [476] By spring the lower terraces held green lines where nothing should have grown. [489] The floating arches caught the blue and threw it back onto the leaves. [502] Travelers from the dry side came to trade, amazed at fruit forming in stone. [516] Toben asked only for help waking the last sleepers still waiting in the dark. [530] They carried the sleepers up into light and wrapped their shoulders in silk for warmth. [545] No one spoke of the company or the hunger that winter, only of work ahead. [560] Toben chalked the final bargain closed and washed his hands in shaft water. [573] Sella planted the last quince where the cracked arch had fallen, marking the spot. [587] When wind moved through new leaves, it sounded like soft rain on iron. [600] Toben stood quietly in that sound, a blacksmith turned gardener at last. [612] Above him the orchards and arches held against the empty sky. [623]</story>
