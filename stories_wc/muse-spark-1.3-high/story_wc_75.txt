<story>My name is Ana Lucic and I was the cultured museum guide of Vrelska, the grotto city behind a monumental waterfall. [21] I am historically oriented by training and by stubbornness, which is why I kept the catalog even after the beekeeper's last visit. [43] He came through the damp arcade in March with empty jars and said the upland hives had failed again. [62] After that no honey sweetened our rosewater tea, and a thin dread settled over the caverns like perfume over rot. [82] The city lived on echoes, for the fall thundered outside our limestone shell day and night. [98] When the thaw began in April, water seeped through cracks and loosened the iron doors of the lower museum. [117] My supervisor fled with the archives, but I stayed to rescue dying arts from indifference. [132] The displaced persons arrived a week later, thin families from flooded villages above the falls who had lost looms and names. [153] The council housed them in the empty concert hall and asked me to keep them quiet with tours. [171] I took my grandmother's emerald-tinted opera glass lens from its case because the tunnel lights were dim. [188] Through that green glass the faded frescoes sharpened, and painted beekeepers and bakers seemed to breathe. [204] I told them Vrelska was built by refugees who carved homes behind water to hide from soldiers. [221] A boy named Tomas laughed and said hiding behind water fails when the river rises. [236] I showed them the loom room where tapestries hung like ghosts, and the kiln where potters fired rose-glazed cups. [255] To rescue dying arts I needed living hands, and theirs were empty and skilled. [269] Old Sefa knew how to spin nettle fiber, and Mina remembered songs for glazing pots. [284] I asked them not to watch but to work, and the museum thawed faster than the upper ice. [302] We cleared cots and set up benches, and the smell of clay replaced disinfectant. [316] Each morning I baked using my family custom of baking bread shaped like initials, each loaf marked with its baker's letter. [337] My grandmother started it in wartime so exiles sharing one oven could find their loaf. [352] Tomas shaped a crooked T, Sefa a trembling S, and Mina a proud M with extra flour. [369] The ritual steadied us more than lectures, because hunger respects history. [380] One evening Sefa poured cracked rosewater into our tea to mask the musty cistern taste. [395] The sweetness made us smile then fall silent, for beauty curdles into dread when supplies run out. [412] That night the upper ice thawed too fast and the grotto shuddered behind its curtain of water. [429] Lights failed and newcomers pressed against the glass fearing we would drown in the dark. [444] I held the emerald-tinted opera glass lens to the lamp, casting a green circle on the wall. [461] Look, I whispered, moving the light across the fresco of refugees carrying hives and violins through spray. [478] They are us, Mina said, and Tomas fit his hand inside the painted hand of a boy holding bread. [497] By morning the pumps held, though the lower museum stood ankle-deep and cold. [510] We waded in and saved tools and songbooks and rose glaze recipes before mold claimed them. [526] The council called us foolish, but Sefa said old things were the only dry land she owned. [543] In summer the displaced persons were offered concrete flats above, and half chose to leave. [558] Those who stayed became keepers with me, historically oriented by loss and practice. [571] Tomas now guides visitors through the grotto city behind a monumental waterfall, holding my lens to show true colors. [590] Mina fires rose-glazed cups that smell of roses and dread, sweet and unsettling. [603] Sefa spins and teaches, and each morning we bake bread shaped like initials so each claims a name. [621] After the beekeeper's last visit we feared no sweetness would return, yet work sweetens memory. [636] The fall still thunders, and at dusk I taste rosewater and feel afraid and grateful. [651] We did not save the city so much as thaw it, and let it remember how to feed and sing. [671]</story>
