<story>Ilya was a crafty lantern maker who tended a floating island tea garden. [13] Life changed after the shadow detaches from her feet on mourning morning. [25] She woke to find it standing apart, thin and watchful by the kettle. [38] Instead of fear she felt purpose rise to fold grief into origami cranes. [51] Her sorrow became a luminous torment that lit her hands from within. [63] Each evening she brewed bitter tea and listened to wind through trellised vines. [76] Across the mist stood a glass basilica on a floating isle, empty and ringing. [90] Ilya believed the basilica kept echoes of everyone she had ever loved. [102] For years she had perfected dream recording inside paper lanterns with copper seams. [115] At night she slept beneath mulberry leaves and captured whatever visions came. [127] Morning brought fragile films of memory clinging to the lantern walls like frost. [140] She learned gem polishing from her mother to clarify clouded stones and hearts. [153] She applied the same slow patience to each recorded dream, rubbing gently. [165] With powdered ash and silk she smoothed the glare until images turned clear. [178] Polished dreams could transform pain into color, sound into warm light. [189] Ilya folded one crane each dawn from paper brushed with tea and salt. [202] She whispered her grief into the fold so the paper remembered her daughter. [215] Lena had died in spring when the tethers snapped and the lower orchard fell. [229] Since then Ilya had not climbed down to gather fallen fruit or flowers. [242] Her detached shadow refused to climb stairs and waited near the garden gate. [255] On the basilica isle a stair descends while ascending through layered light. [267] Pilgrims said walking it felt like sinking upward into a bright lung. [279] Ilya packed seven polished dream lanterns and two hundred paper cranes for passage. [292] Her shadow followed only after she promised to return before the moon thinned. [305] They crossed by rope ferry strung between fig roots and crystal anchors. [317] The tea garden shrank behind them into a green coin floating on cloud. [330] Inside the basilica, glass walls held daylight even after sunset had passed. [342] Thousands of unclaimed lanterns hung from arches, humming faintly with old dreams. [354] Ilya hung her own lanterns among them and watched them steady their glow. [367] One lantern held Lena laughing as she chased geese through steaming tea rows. [380] Another held Lena sick in winter, breathing shallow under quilted stars. [391] Ilya polished each projection with ash paste until the edges stopped bleeding. [403] The basilica priestess warned that clear dreams demand a price in waking hours. [416] Ilya answered that grief unpaid grows heavier than any honest exchange. [427] She released her paper cranes into the nave where they caught rising air. [440] Each crane carried a folded dream fragment sealed tight against forgetting. [451] They circled higher, glowing amber and rose, then stalled near the dome. [463] Her shadow shivered on the glass floor and stretched thin toward the stairs. [476] Ilya understood she must climb the stair that descends while ascending alone. [488] She tied a lantern to her wrist and began stepping into brightness. [500] Every step downward lifted her higher through panes of blue and gold. [512] The lantern projected Lena running, then falling, then rising as light motes. [524] Pain sharpened into luminous torment as memory burned clean without blurring. [535] Ilya kept polishing the air with her sleeve as if clearing gem facets. [548] Higher she saw her shadow below detach again and flutter like paper. [560] She called down forgiveness and watched it fold itself into crane shape. [572] The shadow crane rose to join the flock circling beneath the dome. [584] At the summit no floor remained, only light pouring through endless glass. [596] Ilya opened her hands and let polished dreams transform into living breath. [608] The cranes drank the light and beat their wings with sudden strength. [620] Lena stepped briefly from brightness, older than memory, kind and unhurried. [631] She touched Ilya on the cheek and thanked her for keeping dreams warm. [644] Then she scattered into cranes that sailed outward over cloud and tea fields. [657] Ilya descended the stair feeling lighter, her shadow restored and softly golden. [669] In the nave the hanging lanterns burned clearer for her patient tending. [681] She returned by ferry to her floating island tea garden before dawn. [693] There she brews fresh tea for travelers who bring clouded dreams for polishing. [706]</story>
