<story>After the resuscitation, June could still taste copper pennies and cold lake water on her tongue. [16] The paramedics had called her back on the midway beside the abandoned carnival funhouse, while music wheezed from a dead speaker. [37] She returned three nights later because every photograph taken since showed a second pale face hovering behind her own. [56] A nurse had whispered that she was photographically cursed, marked by whatever had touched her while her heart was stopped. [76] June pushed through the warped plywood door and found the air thick with mildew, sugar, and something sweetly rotten. [95] It held a repulsive allure, like a wound she wanted to keep touching with her eyes. [111] Inside the hall of mirrors, a lingering luminance clung to the cracked glass, though no bulbs had burned for years. [131] Her flashlight flickered out, but the mirrors kept glowing faintly, holding daylight long after it should have died. [149] In the tallest mirror she saw him, a thin man in a rotted tuxedo, fastened at the throat with an opera cloak clasp. [172] He did not step out so much as lean from the silver, and his voice came low against her ear. [192] He called himself her whispering advisor, paid in secrets and kept alive by listening. [206] June wanted to run, but her legs remembered the lake and refused to obey. [220] You brought fear home with you, he breathed, and it is wearing your face in pictures. [236] June asked how to be rid of it, and he smiled with teeth like wet piano keys. [253] Unbosom yourself, he said, speak the true thing you have never told, and let it echo. [269] She shook her head, because the true thing was that she had held her sister under the water as children and lied about it for years. [295] The whispering advisor nodded as if he had already tasted that memory. [307] Fear guards itself, he said, so you must coax it forward to trick fear into revealing its weakness. [325] He drew a swollen dream dictionary from inside his coat, its pages furred with mold and penciled margins. [343] He told her to speak to it only through paradoxical notes in a dream dictionary, writing what was both true and false. [365] Write that you saved her, he whispered, and also that you did not. [378] Write that the lake was cold, and that it was warm as blood. [391] June knelt on popcorn turned to paste and wrote until her hand cramped, filling margins with trembling contradictions. [409] With each line the lingering luminance brightened, pooling like spilled milk across the mirrors. [423] The pale second face surfaced in the glass, bloated and curious, sniffing at her words. [438] Its beauty was awful, its gums black, its eyes kind, and June could not look away. [454] She understood then that fear had loved her guilt because guilt kept feeding it silence. [469] The whispering advisor pressed his cold forehead to the back of her neck and urged her on. [486] She wrote that she wanted punishment, and that she wanted mercy, and that both were the same hunger. [504] The mirrors shuddered, and the opera cloak clasp on his throat began to burn with green light. [521] Fear screamed from every throat at once, furious at being named and lured into shape. [536] June finally unbosomed the plain fact aloud, saying she had let go too late and her sister had lived but never forgiven her. [559] The words cracked the glass down the center, and the second face split open like rotten fruit. [576] The lingering luminance rushed out in a long sigh and faded into the night air beyond the broken roof. [595] June took a photo with her shaking phone and saw only herself, tired and wet eyed and alone. [613] The whispering advisor was gone, leaving only his opera cloak clasp cold upon the floor.
