<story>June had been an intrusion detection specialist in Portland before the data centers burned. [14] Now she worked nights at a bioluminescent mushroom farm sunk into basalt caves east of Bend. [30] She was spatially gifted, able to walk the dark tunnels once and keep their bends and valves in her head like breath. [52] The owners hired her to stop the losses, for someone had been slipping in and harvesting Ghost Caps by hand. [72] She set tripwires of light and sound, but the caves breathed and fooled her sensors with drips and shifting spores. [92] On the shuttle she rode with familiar strangers, night workers she recognized by boots and lunch pails but never by names. [113] There was the older man who always saved her a seat, and the girl with chapped hands who braided twine during the ride. [136] June learned their rhythms without speaking, the way you learn weather, and she told herself distance was professional. [154] In truth she had come here to understand the physics of letting go after her brother died in the Lookout fire. [175] He had been a hotshot, certain he could hold a line, and she had believed safety meant tighter seals and louder alarms. [197] Grief sat in her like a quiet wildfire, low and smokeless, consuming old certainties from the inside. [214] The farm glowed each midnight, rows of caps exhaling blue green light that pulsed when the fans changed. [232] Her instruments failed because the rooms were sealed with plastic and locked doors that trapped heat and made the mycelium sweat. [253] She carried a laser pointer button in her pocket from her conference days, and she used it to test the caps without touching them. [277] A brief red dot would dim the glow around it, proving the organism noticed light and remembered it for hours. [297] One shift, before the echo of morning birdsong, her panel flagged movement in Hall C where no one was scheduled. [317] She went down with her flashlight off, navigating by memory and by the living light that washed over her sleeves. [337] Through the curtain she saw the girl from the shuttle, hands quick and gentle, lifting only the mature caps and leaving the pins. [360] The older man stood watch, and his face folded when June stepped into the blue. [375] She should have shouted, but she recognized hunger, not malice, in the way they bagged the mushrooms for a sick child. [396] The girl whispered that store pills had failed, and the Ghost tea brought down fevers when nothing else would. [415] June thought of alarms and arrests, of lines that could not hold, and her hand stayed empty at her side. [435] She let them finish, then walked them out through permeable barriers of hanging mylar that sighed but never locked. [454] No siren sounded, and the farm kept glowing, indifferent and generous in the dark. [468] The next night she did not add more locks, for she finally saw that sealing had caused the rot and the thefts alike. [491] Tight rooms starved the air, stressed the fungus, and forced workers to sneak where open hands might have asked. [510] She proposed to revolutionize the farm by replacing doors with layered veils that let air and spores drift but tracked passage. [531] Each veil would be sensor laced, recording heat and breath without stopping movement, learning like skin rather than wall. [550] The owners laughed until she showed maps drawn from memory, airflow models no computer had caught, and theft logs that matched fever seasons. [573] They let her cut vents, hang conductive threads, and tune the laser pointer button into a calibrator for the new membranes. [594] Red light swept across veils to test response, and the mushrooms brightened after, as if relieved to breathe. [612] The shuttle changed after that, for June learned names, shared soup, and admitted she had once feared every kindness as a breach. [634] The older man was Ellis, the girl was Priya, and their need had made them thieves only because no door had listened. [656] Yields rose, rot fell, and night losses turned into logged gifts, measured and freely given to families in Bend. [675] June still walks the tunnels without light, feeling pressure shift across her skin before gauges register change.
