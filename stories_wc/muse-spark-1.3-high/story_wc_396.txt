<story>Sela stood between the orchard rows and the sea ice with a question burning in her throat. [17] The lighthouse orchard perched above shifting ice floes hummed with wind through bare apple limbs and distant groaning bergs. [36] Below her, floe families waited in fur-lined boats while above her, orchard keepers gripped pruning hooks like weapons. [54] Old Joren had just demanded she choose which people deserved the winter stores. [67] Sela was a shadow-laced peacekeeper, her gray cloak stitched with dark threads that moved when she breathed. [84] She was calmly intuitive, trained to listen longer than anyone thought necessary. [96] She did not answer at once but held herself during the weighted breath before reply. [111] In that pause, memory widened and offered her a path to map empathy onto action. [126] She touched the broken crystal ball fragment sewn into her cuff for steadiness. [139] The shard had come from her mentor, shattered the night a truce failed in the south. [155] She had kept one piece to remind herself that sight is partial and must be rebuilt. [171] To reconstitute what had broken here, she needed more than rules and rations. [184] She thought of the vaulted storeroom beneath the lighthouse, once a chandlery and now a vintage store of wreck salvage. [204] Its walls were lined with vintage store mirrors salvaged from drowned liners and fishing smacks. [219] For weeks she had studied those mirrors, catching glimpses of past quarrels and kindnesses reflected at odd angles. [237] Via glimpses in vintage store mirrors, she had learned how the two peoples once shared harvests without ledgers. [255] She remembered a girl from the floes laughing as an orchard boy traded her a warm apple for a carved seal. [276] She remembered the storm winter when keepers lowered lanterns down the cliff to guide lost hunters home. [293] She remembered also the fire and the accusations after stores went missing three years ago. [308] No single mirror held the whole truth, but together the fragments taught her the pattern. [323] The floe folk feared starvation if the channel froze early. [333] The orchard folk feared ruin if their grafted trees were stripped for firewood. [346] Both fears were reasonable, and both had hardened into pride. [356] Sela understood then that she herself was the courageous anomaly the situation required. [369] Not a judge from afar but someone willing to risk failure by refusing to choose one side. [386] The wind lifted salt and apple scent together as she finally drew breath to speak. [401] She proposed that the orchard open its upper loft as a shared storehouse guarded by both groups. [418] She asked floe pilots to chart safe ice paths and carry apples south before the deep freeze. [435] She asked keepers to prune deadwood openly and apportion it daily as fuel rather than hoarding it. [452] She laid her broken crystal ball fragment on the cold stone wall between them as a pledge. [469] She said no one could see the future whole, but each could carry a shard of responsibility. [486] Joren frowned, and a young floe mother named Aki stepped forward first. [498] Aki placed her bone knife beside the shard and promised two boats for transport. [512] A keeper boy who had once traded apples blushed and offered his loft key. [526] Slowly hands followed, tools laid down, breath visible in the bright cold air. [539] The lighthouse beam swept across the orchard and the floes as if sealing the bargain. [554] Sela felt her shadows settle, no longer restless, as empathy became labor and watching and sharing. [570] She knew peace was not a perfect sphere but something remade daily from broken pieces. [585] She breathed out and gave her answer, steady and plain for all to hold. [599] Spring would test them, but they would meet it together. [609]</story>
