<story>Upon the hush of budding hope, when spring light first touched the valley, old Maren climbed to the orchard above the falls. [22] She was a wise orchard pruner, her hands scarred by decades of graft and frost, her eyes patient as root water. [43] Maren carried in a linen pouch one last heirloom seed saved from the mother tree that had fed seven generations. [63] The mother tree had died in winter, and with it the orchard seemed to lose its color of wonder. [82] Maren had vowed to preserve the color of wonder, not as paint or memory, but as living fruit. [100] She stopped at the verdant parapet beside a waterfall where moss covered old stones and mist rose like breath. [119] There in radiant hush she listened, for the falls softened every sound into a low and golden quiet. [137] Her granddaughter Liri followed, a child insatiably creative, who braided bark into crowns and sang to buds. [154] Liri held Maren's sleeve with sticky fingers and asked if a seed could remember sweetness. [169] Maren smiled and knelt so their foreheads touched beneath the flowering branches. [181] A seed remembers by capturing essence, she said, pressing the heirloom seed into Liri's palm. [196] It drinks the soil and sun and sorrow of a place and keeps them folded inside. [212] Liri closed her fist around the seed and felt its small weight grow warm. [226] As mist drifted over the parapet, the air thinned and the world began weaving between worlds. [242] The waterfall slowed until each drop hung shining, and behind it opened an orchard of light. [258] There stood the mother tree, vast and silver barked, its roots spanning two sides of time. [274] Around it moved the quiet ones, the keepers of pollen and rain, neither old nor young. [290] Maren bowed to them, for she had come many times in dreams to learn their pruning. [306] They did not speak in words but offered succor to the grieving, which Maren now returned. [322] She lifted the heirloom seed toward the mother tree and asked leave to plant its child. [338] The keepers leaned close and breathed upon the seed until it glowed amber in Liri's hand. [354] Liri gasped, for she could see tiny rivers and summers turning inside the shell. [368] Maren took her pruning knife and cut a small branch from the tree of light. [383] With practiced care she grafted memory to matter, weaving between worlds with bark and breath. [398] The cut healed instantly, and sap like sunrise ran gold beneath the silver skin. [412] In return the keepers gave Maren a single blossom that would never wilt. [425] Plant the seed where water sings, they chimed without sound, and tend it with joy. [440] The shining drops fell again, the veil closed, and only the falls remained. [453] Maren and Liri stood again on the verdant parapet beside a waterfall, damp cheeked and dazzled. [469] Without delay Maren knelt in the terraced earth above the parapet and dug a careful hollow. [485] Liri placed the heirloom seed inside and covered it with dark soil and fallen petals. [500] They poured cold waterfall water over it and waited in radiant hush for a sign. [515] For a long moment nothing moved except mist and the trembling of leaves. [528] Then the soil cracked and a green spear rose, unfurling two leaves bright as morning. [543] Liri laughed aloud and clapped, and the falls seemed to echo her delight. [556] Maren pruned a nearby wild shoot to give the seedling light and air. [569] With every cut she whispered thanks, teaching Liri where to trim and where to spare. [584] In seasons that followed, the tree grew swift and strong, its blossoms holding amber light. [599] Travelers who tasted its fruit wept quietly, remembering childhood summers they thought lost. [612] Maren grew older and slower, but Liri grew skilled with knife and song. [625] When Maren could no longer climb, Liri carried her to the parapet at dawn. [639] Together they watched the young tree bloom above the falls in radiant hush. [652]</story>
