<story>Mara had worn her grey veil for three years, and the town called her the veiled dreamer. [17] She lived between two fateful letters, the first inviting her to find the library of unspoken words. [52] She felt cautious exuberance each time she traced its lines, afraid to hope and unable to stop. [87] Its hands were cyclically reversed, turning backward through twelve hours before leaping forward again. [101] Mara learned to tell true time through propagation delays, listening for the echo of its tick against stone. [166] Shelves rose like cliffs, and every book glowed faintly as if remembering readers who never spoke. [182] She understood then that the library kept words people swallowed, apologies and confessions left unsaid. [513] Outside, dawn thinned the fog, and the glowing library in mist faded like a held breath. [529] In her pack lay the notebook, proof that she had entered the library of unspoken words. [580] She knew she would spend years copying those pages for others who swallowed their speech. [595] Between the memory of the first letter and the warning of the second, she had found her voice.
