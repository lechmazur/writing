<story>I found the cabin the morning after I found my grave. [11] That sounds backwards, and it was, because dying had left me chronologically confused. [24] I am, or was, a lucid dream chronicler by trade, paid in grants and strange mail to catalog other people's nights. [45] The marker above my own plot had my name spelled right and my dates wrong in both directions, and I stood there feeling jaded wonder instead of fear. [73] The mountain ranger cabin was supposed to be empty for the winter, shutters latched and wood stacked, but the door opened for me anyway. [97] Inside, the air smelled of pine sap and cold ash, and on the table someone had left a birch bark scroll tied with fishing line. [122] My handwriting crawled across it, though I had no memory of writing there. [135] The first line said do not trust the calm version of yourself. [147] I built a fire and read on, trying to distill chaos into narrative the way I had been trained to do for clients. [170] Every dream I had ever recorded came back at once, unfiled and snarling, and with them came a feral nostalgia for places I had never lived. [196] I remembered pine forests as a wolf might, by scent and hunger rather than by story. [212] I remembered birthdays with teeth bared, wanting to bite the cake and the hands that offered it. [229] The scroll listed my past selves like trail markers, the earnest intern, the lover who slept with a notebook, the cynic who charged by the hour. [255] Reading them, I felt the petty heat of getting jealous of past selves who had dreamed more cleanly than I ever would again. [278] The intern believed every flying dream meant freedom, and I hated him for that simplicity. [293] The lover believed sharing dreams could save a relationship, and I envied his stupid hope. [308] Outside, snow began to fall sideways, erasing the path to the graveyard below and sealing me in. [325] I wrapped myself in a ranger blanket that smelled of someone else's dog and kept reading. [341] The scroll claimed the cabin was a waystation between my death and my final dream, and that I had built it myself from remembered lumber. [366] Of course I did not believe it, and of course I checked the axe marks on the beams for my initials. [387] They were there, cut deep and still oozing sap, fresh as this morning's grave dirt under my nails. [405] That was when sleep took me sitting up, the chronicler's trick I had taught insomniacs for years. [457] I stepped through and told myself to soar, not fly, because soaring requires weather and trust. [473] The wind caught the loose parts of me, the jealousy and the filing systems, and scattered them like sparks. [492] Below, my grave glowed faintly beside the frozen lake, a small tidy lie about a messy life. [509] Above, my younger selves were already airborne, graceless and laughing, racing me toward the ridge. [524] Instead of competing, I drafted behind them, letting their ridiculous joy pull me higher. [538] The feral part of me howled at the moonless sky, and the chronicler part finally took notes without judgment. [557] I understood then that my job was never to tame dreams but to follow their tracks in the snow. [576] I woke with bark dust on my tongue and added my own line to the scroll before the fire died. [596] It said forgive the man who cataloged wonders and forgot to live inside them. [610] Outside, dawn thinned the snow, and my grave, my cabin, and my selves all seemed briefly true at once.
