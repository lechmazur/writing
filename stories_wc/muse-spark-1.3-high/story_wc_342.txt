<story>Elena was a face blind portrait artist who never trusted memory for a nose or mouth. [16] She learned to craft each likeness from live measurement and patient repeated looking. [29] Her lines grew fractally precise, branching from broad shape into pore and faint scar. [43] The city had hired her to record the tree trunk library hollowed by a scholar's guild generations before. [61] The vast oak had grown inside a brick exchange hall, and both tree and hall were condemned. [78] She arrived at the hush just before a condemned building is demolished, when dust hangs without falling. [95] A heightened stillness filled the hollow trunk, as if the wood itself held its breath. [110] Shelves curved along the inner rings, holding ledgers and pressed leaves and quiet light. [124] Her commission was to mirror the sky inside walls before the wreckers erased them. [138] The guild founders had painted constellations on the inner bark, now faded to ghosts. [152] Elena could not picture faces, and she feared she could not picture stars either. [166] She set her easel in the center where a shaft fell from a cracked skylight. [181] For hours she drew the grain, the shelves, the stair that spiraled with the growth. [196] She avoided the portraits tucked between books, small oil heads of long dead scholars. [210] Without a sitter present, her blindness left only blank ovals where features should live. [224] Discovering limits had always shaped her work, teaching her where observation must replace recall. [238] She worked until the light thinned, then searched for a stool and found a false spine. [254] One thick almanac was a secret book safe, its pages cut into a square hollow. [269] Inside lay a centuries-old coin, dark and lightened only along its raised rim. [282] She tilted it toward the shaft and read history through faint indentations on a centuries-old coin. [298] The indentations formed tiny stars and numbers, a printer's map of the original ceiling. [312] Some thoughtful librarian had pressed the die into soft metal to save the design. [326] Elena understood that she need not invent the sky but recover its measure. [339] She laid tracing paper over the coin and rubbed graphite until points emerged like frost. [354] Each point split into smaller points in her notes, a patient and fractally precise chart. [369] She climbed the spiral stair with lamp and chalk, testing scale against curved bark. [383] The trunk breathed around her, cool and resinous, narrowing toward the broken roof. [396] Outside, engines idled beyond the barricades, but inside no leaf of paper trembled. [409] She marked the vanished constellations point by point, letting the coin guide her hand. [423] Where paint remained, she matched its blue, mixing ash and linseed into night. [436] Where paint was gone, she left chalk stars, honest about loss and record. [449] A foreman called that time was short, and his voice sank into bark without echo. [464] She did not hurry, because hurry blurred the seeing she had trained for years. [478] At last she descended and turned to the scholar portraits she had feared to face. [493] One by one she brought each small canvas into the shaft and copied it stroke by stroke. [510] She could not remember a cheek minutes later, but she could hold its edge while looking. [526] Her copies grew true not from likeness in mind but from attention without interval. [540] When the warning horn sounded, she rolled her charts and closed the safe book. [554] She slipped the coin into her pocket, meaning to return it to the city archive. [569] Outside, neighbors gathered in cold air to watch the hall fall with its tree. [583] Elena unrolled her long drawing against a barricade, its stars and faces pale in daylight. [598] Children traced the chalk constellations with fingers, connecting dots into animals and boats. [611] An old scholar wept to see his teachers preserved, each gaze steady and particular. [625] Elena realized her blindness had taught her to honor what remains while it is present. [640] She had not conquered forgetting, but she had crafted a mirror that would outlast walls. [655] As the first beam cracked, dust rose slowly in the heightened stillness before collapse.
