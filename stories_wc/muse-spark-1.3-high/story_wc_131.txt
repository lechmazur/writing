<story>Dawn came blue through the tall windows of the Carnegie library. [11] It happened while the city's lights flicker after an unexpected outage, holding at a dim hum. [42] Below the floor, in the crawl space beneath the west wing, June listened to the building breathe. [59] She was a crawl space dweller by necessity and by preference, small enough to move where others could not. [78] For three years she had lived kindly methodical routines among pipes and joists and forgotten book carts. [95] Each morning she checked the pipe chases for leaks, for mice, for things patrons dropped through vents. [112] She repaired what she could with tape and wire and left everything cleaner than she found it. [129] She did not steal, but she did cache small rescued things for the day they might matter. [146] A single mitten, a brass key, a love letter folded into a paper crane. [160] Uncovering in pipe chases was her work and her prayer, slow hands brushing rust and lint aside. [177] That dawn, while the emergency lamps pulsed amber against blue shadows, her fingers found something different. [221] She lifted it to her ear though she knew it made no sound alone, and sense memory struck her hard. [241] The smell of warm plastic brought back her grandmother's front room in summer, curtains drawn against heat. [258] A box fan rattled, a reel to reel player turned, and an old voice read aloud from a library book. [278] June had been seven, feverish on the couch, listening because her eyes hurt too much to read. [295] Her grandmother had said stories live in the walls for people who cannot yet find them. [311] June pressed her forehead to the cool pipe and wept without noise, grateful and suddenly lonely.
