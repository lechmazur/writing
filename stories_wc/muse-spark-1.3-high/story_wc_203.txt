<story>Marcus Hale fixed elevators for a living and carried ghosts as a second trade. [14] He never advertised the second trade, but he was skillfully observant in both. [27] Tuesday sent him to the Meridian Arcade, a dead mall with cracks in the facade wide enough to fit a hand. [48] The work order said freight elevator stuck between floors, but the ground floor still held the abandoned circus ticket office. [68] Faded tigers leapt across its plywood walls and a crooked window sold tickets to nobody. [83] Marcus propped his toolbox and tried to engage the manual brake, but the lever only shuddered. [99] Above him the car hung in the shaft like a held breath. [111] The girl was already waiting by the ticket window. [120] She wore a paper crown from the circus and pointed straight up at the stained tiles. [136] Most ghosts mumbled or wept, but this one burned with a single purpose to discover what ceilings hide. [154] Marcus had learned not to ask them questions aloud in public places. [166] Instead he started analyzing dust accumulation patterns the way other men read newspapers. [179] The ticket counter was thick with gray dust except for a clean arc where a door had swung recently. [198] Work boots had tracked plaster across the checkerboard floor toward the back closet. [211] Yet the mall had been locked for three years and the elevator had not run in five. [228] Someone living or dead was still riding it. [236] He waited until closing time, when the overhead fluorescent buzz dies off and the building settles. [252] The security guard locked the front grates and left Marcus alone with his lamp and his ghosts. [269] By then three more figures had gathered, all circus workers in heavy canvas coats. [283] They did not look at Marcus but at the ceiling above the ticket office. [297] He climbed a stepladder and pressed his palm to the tiles, feeling cold air slip through. [313] Water stains mapped old leaks, but one square showed fresh scratches around its metal frame. [328] Under his lamp the dust on that tile was disturbed in fingertip swirls, as if lifted often from below. [347] His heart lifted with a strange weightless burden, grief without weight, duty without dread. [361] He lifted the tile and found a dark cavity smelling of oil and old popcorn. [376] Inside the shaft space sat a brass cylinder the size of a lunch pail, toothed and coiled. [393] Even in dim light he knew a horologist's mainspring barrel when he saw one. [407] Someone had bolted it to the elevator cables as a homemade counterweight and time delay. [422] A frayed rope ran from its arbor up through a drilled joist into the stalled car above. [439] Marcus understood at once, the way a mechanic understands a lie told by a machine. [454] Decades ago the circus wintered here and the owner, once a horologist, had rigged the freight lift to smuggle cash. [474] He hid the nightly take above the ticket office ceiling where inspectors never looked. [488] The barrel released slowly, lowering the loot after midnight while the elevator appeared broken. [502] The girl with the paper crown had seen him hide it and climbed up for a dare. [519] The mechanism tripped early and sealed the car between floors with her inside the ceiling void. [535] No one heard her over the calliope, and the circus left town without its lost child. [551] Now her small bones lay curled beside a rotted canvas sack spilling green bills. [565] Marcus breathed out and the ghosts leaned closer, lighter than dust yet heavy as history. [580] He carefully disengaged the spring with his pliers so it would not snap and harm her remains. [597] Then he lifted the bones down as gently as a repairman frees a jammed door. [612] The workers watched without anger, only relief, and the girl dropped her pointing arm. [626] In the morning he called the police and the coroner, and told the truth about the ceiling. [643] He kept the barrel for the report, its brass teeth still holding sixty years of tension. [659] The mall owners will patch the cracks in the facade, but they cannot patch what happened inside. [676] Marcus filed his invoice, oiled the brake, and left the ticket office open to daylight. [691] As he walked out the ghosts did not follow, and that absence felt like forgiveness. [706]</story>
