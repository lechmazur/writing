<story>The withdrawn knight had not lifted his visor in three years, not since the war of glass bells ended. [19] He walked now through ash and frozen petals toward the only light left in the world. [35] That light burned inside an alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night, where brass pipes sweated honey and copper trees bore bottled stars. [58] Ser Odo had come to capture the shape of possibility, though he would never have said it so grandly. [77] He only knew his sword hand shook and his prayers came back empty, and he wanted something unbroken. [95] He had heard the greenhouse was factually mystical, not a poet's trick but a place where measurements failed and mercy grew like moss. [118] Inside, the air smelled of cinnamon and ozone, and rows of apple saplings grew in alembics of green glass. [137] On a potting bench beneath a moonvine, he found an abandoned marionette theater program, its paper soft as a moth wing. [158] The ink listed acts that had never been performed: The Orchard Wakes, The String Is Cut, The Knight Kneels. [177] Odo laughed once, a rusted sound, because his whole life felt printed there without his permission. [193] As he turned the page, the glass roof chimed, and the night outside thickened like cooling wax. [210] Then, as reality shifts when a lens is breathed upon, the saplings shivered and rearranged their shadows. [227] From between the alembics stepped a gardener with bark for skin and fireflies nested in her throat. [244] You carry strings, she said, pointing to his shoulders where no strings could be seen. [259] Odo stepped back, for no one had spoken to him gently since the siege of White Harbor. [276] I cut my own, he muttered, and touched the scar where his knightly cord had been. [292] The gardener smiled and lifted a pair of shears made from fused spoons and hummingbird bones. [308] No, she said, those deeper ones that move your tongue when you dream of orders. [323] She spread the program on the soil and pressed a seed into each printed act. [338] To heal, she whispered, you must water them through forgotten patterns, not by command but by listening. [355] Odo knelt because his knees remembered courtesy before his mind could refuse. [367] Together they hummed old watering songs his grandmother sang to beans, songs the army had forbidden as idle. [385] With each note, a seed cracked, and a green thread rose, seeking his hands. [399] He felt a murmured allegiance rise in his chest, not to king or chapel, but to the small living things that persisted. [421] The threads touched his gauntlets and waited, trembling, for permission. [431] He tried to unsnap the clasps, but his fingers were stiff, so the gardener helped him with patient thumbs. [450] The armor fell away in plates like dead leaves, revealing skin pale from years without sun. [466] Beneath, his shoulders bore red grooves where the invisible strings had pulled for decades. [480] The gardener guided the green threads to those grooves, and Odo gasped at the cool touch. [496] It was not pain but shivering bliss, as if winter water had learned how to sing. [512] The threads did not bind him but rooted him, drinking old fear and exhaling light. [527] Above, the bottled stars ripened and burst softly, scattering pollen that spelled names of the fallen. [543] Odo read Tomas and Miren and Little Joss, and tears cut clean paths through grime. [558] I left them strung up to orders, he whispered, I marched while they called for water. [574] The gardener shook her head and placed his hands around a swelling gourd. [587] Then tend them now, she said, for allegiance is a seed, not a chain. [601] Odo stayed through the endless night, learning to prune and graft and wait without orders. [616] When dawn at last threatened the glass, he did not flinch or reach for his sword. [632] He lifted the empty armor and hung it as a trellis for beans, and smiled at its new use. [651] In the quiet, the marionettes of the program rotted into soil, and the knight finally grew.
