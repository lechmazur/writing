<story>Mara was a grim optimist who kept climbing because stopping felt like dying. [13] The infinite staircase rose above her and fell below her without landing or door. [27] Each step was gray stone wide enough for sitting, narrow enough for longing. [40] She carried in her pocket a pair of dice, one chipped, that had belonged to her brother Tomas. [58] Tomas had loved games of chance and had vanished down the stairs on a dare. [73] Mara told herself he was still falling somewhere in the boundless confinement of the stair. [88] She was balancedly daring, careful enough to test each tread and bold enough to keep ascending. [104] Her purpose was to capture the texture of a missed opportunity she could still feel on her skin. [122] Once they had dreamed together of building perpetual motion from spare parts. [134] He would roll the dice to decide their designs, laughing when the chipped one landed wrong. [150] She would correct him through mathematical proofs scratched in chalk on the workshop floor. [164] The night he challenged the stair itself, she had hesitated one breath too long. [178] That hesitation was the missed chance, the hand not grabbed, the word not shouted. [192] Now she climbed to replay it until the memory gained weight and edges. [205] On step four hundred and twelve she stopped to draw circles and arrows in chalk. [220] She believed the stair itself might hold the secret of return, a loop that fed itself. [236] If she could prove perpetual motion here, where up never ended, Tomas could not truly be lost. [253] Each morning without sun she rolled the pair of dice to choose whether to climb or study. [270] If doubles came, she climbed hard, trusting luck more than reason. [281] If the chipped die stumbled, she sat and worked through mathematical proofs on the risers. [296] The proofs grew longer and cleaner, lines of numbers marching up like footprints. [309] She derived wheels that lifted their own weights and pendulums that borrowed from their future swings. [325] For a grim optimist, failure was data, and grief was fuel that never quite burned out. [341] Higher up the air thinned into silence and the stone grew warm under her palms. [356] She began to notice a rhythm in the stairs, a pattern of seven wide steps then one narrow. [374] She marked the narrow ones with chalk stars and counted the intervals between them. [388] Her balancedly daring mind saw a machine in the architecture, a vast wheel disguised as ascent. [404] Perhaps the infinite staircase was not a trap but an engine waiting for understanding. [418] On a star marked step she rolled the dice once more and both showed sixes. [433] She laughed aloud, a harsh kind sound, and stood to climb beyond her usual limit. [448] Three steps higher, the stone under her left foot gave a soft sigh and began to rupture. [465] A crack split the tread, dust rose, and for an instant she saw hollow darkness below. [481] She threw her weight back, dropped flat, and clutched the dice so hard they cut her palm. [498] The step held by a finger width, trembling under her, refusing to finish falling. [512] Inside the crack something glinted, not gears but a small chalk drawing of dice. [526] Tomas must have marked this high place before his fall, proving he had climbed farther than she knew. [544] Her throat closed, because her proofs had told her he could not have come this far. [560] Through mathematical proofs she had built a clean world where loss obeyed rules. [573] The chalk dice in the crack broke those rules, rough and hopeful and true. [587] She understood then that perpetual motion was not a wheel but her own returning grief. [602] It rose each day without fuel and fell each night without rest, endless and confined. [617] Carefully she reached into the rupture and lifted out a folded paper Tomas had left. [632] It held no proof, only a drawing of two figures climbing a stair that looped like a smile. [650] Under it he had written that the chipped die rolls crooked so we must walk straight for it. [668] Mara pressed the paper to her chest and felt the missed opportunity change texture from sharp to warm. [686] She would not solve the stair, she would keep it, step by step, dice in hand.
