<story>I came to the labyrinthine bookstore occupying an entire mountain after remembering becomes painful and I could no longer live above the flooded valley town. [25] I had burned maps and letters to understand the shape of home, but ash remembers longer than paper and followed me up the stone stairs. [50] The shelves rose like strata through cold tunnels, and every corridor smelled of dust and snowmelt and patient ink. [69] On the third night I found her, the mercury-glass seer who substitutes reality nightly for travelers too tired to dream safely alone. [91] Her face caught lamplight in silvered ripples, age and youth moving beneath the surface like fish under ice. [109] You carry buried talents, she said, not the clever kind but the planted kind, griefs you put down deep so they would not sprout in your kitchen. [136] I told her my mother taught me a lullaby in a dialect no one speaks now, and that I had forgotten all but its weight. [161] The seer nodded and led me deeper, past catalogs carved into slate, to a vault where hidden thunder rolled softly behind the walls. [184] Inside a cedar box lay the lost language stone, a gray river pebble veined with script too small and too alive to read. [207] When I touched it my fingers remembered the song before my mind did, and my throat ached with the pressure of unsung syllables. [230] She warned that the stone does not translate, it awakens, and what wakes too fast can crack its vessel. [249] I asked what I should pay, and she asked only that I seed one true memory in the mountain and let the bookstore keep it. [274] She gave me a flight feather from a mountain owl and told me to learn its barbs via the texture of a feather against my lips. [300] Each night the seer lit a blue lamp and laid the stone on my forehead while I slept among dictionaries. [320] She substitutes reality nightly with a gentler version, she explained, so the buried thing can rise without dragging your whole house after it. [343] In that softer dream I stood again in our kitchen while rain hammered the tin roof like distant drums. [362] My mother hummed and folded dough, and the lost words were folded in also, warm and yeasted and unbroken. [381] I learned the song not by meaning but via the texture of a feather, breath against softness, sound before sense. [401] Morning after morning I woke crying without knowing why, and copied strange letters that my hand knew better than my eyes. [422] The seer read them aloud and the vault answered with hidden thunder, a low approval vibrating through rock and paper. [442] On the seventh day she told me to choose what to seed, because the mountain only keeps what is freely given and fully remembered. [466] I chose the evening my mother stopped singing halfway when the river took the bridge and the town siren wailed. [486] I had buried that night deepest because after remembering becomes painful, silence feels safer than song. [502] The seer pressed the feather to my cheek and the stone to my palm until both felt like extensions of my skin. [524] Speak it here and let it root, she whispered, and the hidden thunder leaned closer to listen. [541] I sang the broken lullaby aloud, unsteady and shaking, feeling each buried talent stir like seeds splitting in spring soil. [561] My voice cracked on the missing verse, but the stone warmed and supplied the texture, not the words, of what I had lost. [584] Suddenly the kitchen returned whole, the smell of wet wool and soup, the shape of home settling around me like a fitted shawl. [607] I understood then that home was not the town nor the house but the cadence we carried between us when water rose. [629] The seer smiled with mercury light shifting in her eyes and said the mountain would keep the memory safe for other lost daughters. [652] I left the stone in its cedar box, lighter without its weight, and carried only the feather down the winding stairs toward morning. [675] Outside snow fell on the valley scar below, and I sang clearly at last, planting sound where flood had once taken root. [697]</story>
