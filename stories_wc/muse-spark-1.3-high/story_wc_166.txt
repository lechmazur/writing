<story>After the nostalgia ban, remembering the sea aloud became a punishable ache. [12] Selene chose elective exile over forgetting, trading the tidal choirs for silence inland. [25] She had once been a skilled tide dancer, reading the pull of water with her ankles and spine. [43] Now she kept a twilight garden under stars, where black roses opened like slow mouths at dusk. [60] The garden held its breath in obsidian grace, every leaf polished by shadow and patience. [75] She learned its rhythms not from books but by observing gaps between birdcall and wind. [90] Between one cricket pulse and the next she found where to plant, where to prune, where to wait. [108] Between waves she had once found the same stillness, the hollow before the break where the dance lived. [126] The Council had ordered all tide dancers to unlearn the ocean, to surrender shells and salt songs. [143] Selene refused, and carried inland only a moonstone cabochon dust speck caught in her cuff. [158] It was too small to sell and too bright to discard, a ghost of tide light. [174] At night she set it on soil and watched it gather starlight like a patient eye. [190] A boy from the village came to her wall, drawn by rumors that she inked skin with constellations. [208] She had vowed to pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin, mapping lost tides into stars. [224] Ilya arrived with that same hunger, asking for his dead mother's laugh line to become Orion. [240] Selene first refused, for ink after the nostalgia ban was contraband and memory was treason. [255] Yet his hands trembled with the particular grief of the second generation, who had never seen the sea. [273] She led him into the garden and taught him stillness by observing gaps in the dark. [289] Look between stars, she whispered, not at them, and tell me what holds them apart. [304] He stared until his eyes watered, until the black softened into depth and distance breathed. [319] What do you see, she asked, and he answered only space that listens. [332] Good, she said, for verses live in pauses as dances live in the undertow. [346] That night a storm clipped sparrow nest from the eaves, spilling three blind chicks onto wet stone. [363] Two died quickly, but one gasped and opened a beak like a cracked seed. [377] Ilya begged to warm it, and Selene showed him how to cup without crushing. [391] For days they fed it moths and rainwater while the moonstone speck watched from the altar stone. [408] Selene felt herself spontaneously anchored to that small routine, to soil and boy and bird. [423] Her exile had been elective, yet rooting felt unchosen, sudden as weather. [435] She had fled the coast to keep the tide inside her, and now the garden was teaching her to stay. [455] When the sparrow grew pinfeathers, Ilya charted its growth against the constellations she named. [469] Vega for hunger, Deneb for first flight, Altair for the return no one promises. [483] Selene ground ink from charred rosemary and iron, mixing it with starlight and resolve. [497] She traced Orion along the lifeline of his palm, where his mother's laugh would have rested. [513] Each dot burned, each line sang, and he did not flinch but breathed through the gaps in pain. [531] By observing gaps in his breathing, she timed her needle to his calm. [544] Above them the garden arched in twilight, and the stars leaned close to witness. [558] The moonstone dust caught the lamplight and flared, seeding his fresh tattoo with pale fire. [573] You carry tide and sky now, she told him, a verse no ban can erase. [588] At dawn the sparrow tested the edge of the basin, wings shivering with unspent weather. [603] Selene lifted it to the rosemary crown and whispered the tide song she was forbidden to sing. [620] Fledge, she breathed, not as command but as tide coaxing water from shore. [633] The bird hesitated, remembered wind by observing gaps in leaves, then hurled itself into blue. [648] It fell, caught air, rose again with obsidian grace that mirrored her old dances. [662] Ilya wept and laughed, pressing his marked palm to his heart where memory now lived legally as art. [680] Selene understood that exile had not emptied her but refined her into vessel and verse. [695] She would remain in the garden, spontaneously anchored no longer by grief but by giving. [710]</story>
