<story>Mara had lived in echo gorge so long that the cliffs knew the shape of her breathing. [17] By trade she was a lost soul collector, gathering those who died laughing or crying and could not tell the difference. [38] She was modestly fearless, the way river women are when they have already drowned once in memory. [55] Around her neck she wore a crocodile's tooth on a cord of braided nettle. [69] The tooth had belonged to the old river crocodile that took her brother, and she kept it not for revenge but for remembrance. [92] Each dawn she climbed to the narrow ledge above the black water to listen for spectral confessions. [109] The dead in the gorge did not wail at first, they whispered the small truths they had hidden while alive. [129] She came always in the hush of a hidden sunrise, when light entered sideways and never touched the bottom. [148] On that morning the mist was thick and the cliffs returned every sound twice, softened and delayed. [165] Mara brought her harp, a weathered frame with seven strings gone slack from damp. [179] It could no longer play true music, only bent and sighing tones that matched the gorge. [195] She had learned to hear the dead not by silence but through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp. [214] She plucked one sour string and the echo came back rounder, as if the gorge were trying to correct her. [234] Then a boy stepped from the mist, barefoot and bright eyed and no older than nine. [250] I am Tomas, he said, and I died laughing, which is why no one will collect me. [267] Mara nodded and touched the crocodile's tooth for steadiness, for laughing dead were the hardest to hold. [284] Tell me, she said, and the gorge leaned in to listen with her. [297] Tomas confessed that on the last day of the fever he had tricked his mother into smiling by pretending the spoon was a boat. [321] She laughed while tears ran down her face, and he laughed too, and then his chest stopped. [338] Now he feared that his laugh had been cruel, a theft of her last hope. [353] Mara had vowed long ago to integrate laughter into healing, believing joy was not the opposite of grief but its companion. [374] That was why she did not flinch from laughing ghosts, and why she would pursue even one who tried to hide. [395] Your laugh was medicine, she told him, not mockery, and your mother remembers it that way. [411] The boy shook his head, and the gorge threw his doubt back at them in doubling echoes. [428] Mara plucked three strings together, letting the ugly chord rise and split against the stone. [443] Through the warped sound she heard older spectral confessions joining his, a chorus of mothers and brothers and drowned sailors. [463] One woman whispered that she had sung while her house burned, one man that he had joked as the boat sank. [484] Their luminous ache filled the gorge with pale gold light, sorrow made bearable by being shared. [500] Tomas listened with wide eyes as the echoes taught him that laughter at the edge of death is courage. [519] He laughed again, a small startled sound, and this time the gorge returned it warm and unbroken. [536] Mara felt the crocodile's tooth grow warm against her skin, a sign a soul was loosening its grip on regret. [556] She reached out her hand, modestly fearless still, and did not promise heaven, only passage. [571] Come, she said, your mother has kept the spoon, and she tells the story without crying now. [588] Tomas took her hand, and his weight was no more than mist and music. [602] Side by side they walked down toward the black water where the sun at last struck gold. [619] The harp strings hummed on their own in the rising wind, almost in tune for the first time in years. [639] Mara understood that healing had come not from forgetting sorrow but from letting laughter live inside it. [656] When the boy stepped into the light, his form thinned into brightness and lifted like breath. [672] Only his laugh remained a moment longer, hanging in echo gorge like a bell fading to silence. [689] Mara touched the empty air where his hand had been and smiled with that luminous ache that never left her. [709]</story>
