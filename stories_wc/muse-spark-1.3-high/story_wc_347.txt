<story>Mara had never asked to become the reluctant caretaker of a cursed orchard. [13] The trees grew twisted inside a cliffside labyrinth patrolled by mechanical birds. [25] Each autumn the branches bore glass fruit that rang in the wind and poisoned anyone who tasted it. [43] Mara had inherited the duty from her grandmother with a warning to keep the paths clear before the night frost forms. [64] Her true work was not pruning but to guide lost travelers to safety. [77] Travelers used the maze because it was the only shortcut between the harbor market and the high villages. [95] The mechanical birds circled above with brass wings clicking as they recorded every footstep in the hedges. [112] Mara hated the birds and the orchard and the cold that crept up the cliffs each evening. [129] Yet she walked the maze daily and memorized each turn the way other people memorized prayers. [145] On the last warm afternoon she found a boy asleep under a blackened tree with frost whitening his shoes. [164] He said his sister had run ahead and vanished near the center where the hedges grew tallest. [181] Mara wanted to tell him to wait until morning but the sky was paling fast. [196] She reached into her coat pocket and found only a small nut her grandmother had left there years before. [215] The shell was carved with tiny lines like paths and gates and stairs descending to the sea. [232] The boy whispered that it looked like a forgotten blueprint for the whole maze. [246] Mara laughed with a graceless beauty that surprised them both. [256] She turned the nut over and saw the carving matched the labyrinth including a sealed well she had never opened. [276] Her grandmother had been offhandedly exact about never trusting maps drawn by strangers. [289] Now Mara understood that warning had been a clue left for this hour. [302] She decided they must assemble string and chalk and lantern oil and move quickly. [316] She poured oil along the threshold stones leaving impressions without touching the frozen earth with bare hands. [333] She used a stick to press guiding marks into soft mud for the boy to follow if they separated. [352] The birds tilted their heads at shallow grooves that looked like wind damage rather than human work. [369] Step by step they entered the deeper rings where glass fruit hung low enough to brush their shoulders. [387] Mara told the boy not to listen when the fruit sang because the song made wanderers sit and sleep. [406] He held her sleeve and asked why she stayed if she hated the orchard so much. [422] She answered that hatred was easier than grief and the trees had once been ordinary apples before the curse. [441] The curse had come when engineers built the birds to guard the harbor and spilled hot metal into the roots. [461] Since then the orchard remembered pain and tried to share it with anyone who lingered. [476] Near the center they found the sister curled beside the sealed well half asleep and humming. [492] The well lid showed a round hollow exactly the size of the small nut Mara carried. [508] Her hands shook as she fitted the nut into place and turned it like a key. [524] With a soft sigh the lid slid aside revealing coiled pipes and warm light below. [539] Inside lay the forgotten blueprint her grandmother had hidden inked on oilcloth and weighted with stones. [555] The drawing showed service tunnels beneath every hedge built for repair crews long before the curse. [571] Mara saw how to assemble an escape not through the maze but under it. [585] She was offhandedly exact as she traced the route naming each turn and counting each ladder. [601] They climbed down into warmth while above them the mechanical birds screeched at empty paths. [616] The tunnels smelled of rust and apples where tangled roots embraced copper wires with graceless beauty. [632] When they emerged beyond the outer hedge the harbor lights were blooming in the dusk. [647] The frost was silvering the grass and their breath showed white as they ran. [661] Mara looked back at the labyrinth and felt the old reluctance loosen inside her chest. [676] The boy pressed the small nut into her hand and thanked her for refusing to leave his sister. [694] Mara kept the nut but left the blueprint open on the well lid to guide lost travelers to safety in years to come. [717]</story>
