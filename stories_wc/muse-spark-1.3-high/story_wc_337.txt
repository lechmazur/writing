<story>I am Mara Voss, a broken mask restorer for the drowned town of Kestrel Harbor. [15] For three generations my family has mended the death masks hung in the cliff chapel to ground restless spirits before storm season. [37] This year seven masks came back from the sea wall cracked down the middle, humming faintly when the wind changed. [57] The elders said the drowned fishermen were waking, angry that no one remembered their true names. [73] I carried the masks at dusk to the prism sanctuary under starlight on Gull Rock. [88] The sanctuary is not a church but a dome pieced from ship glass and salt prisms. [104] By day it throws rainbows, but by night it accumulates silence like cold water in a hull. [121] Jonah, the lighthouse keeper's mute grandson, followed me with a lantern and my grandmother's tapestry needle. [137] He had found the net last winter, a centuries-old fisherman's net tangled around the bell buoy. [153] Every knot in it was tied for a man lost, and every lost man had knotted his own name into the cord before sailing. [177] The masks would not hold paint or prayer until those names were spoken again. [191] I knew I had to exhume the names, not bodies, from the deep. [204] Jonah sat beside me on the glass floor and promised he would speak if I first told him why my hands shook. [226] So the whole night unfolded across sharing a secret neither of us had dared to say aloud. [243] My secret was that I had dropped my brother's mask on the rocks last spring and glued it crooked. [262] Since then his voice has called from the surf, thin and disappointed, on foggy mornings. [277] Jonah listened with a clarity that made my fear feel like lucid unrest rather than shame. [293] Then he opened his mouth, rusty from years of quiet, and told me his own secret. [309] He had watched his father cut the buoy rope in a storm to save the catch, dooming two men. [328] He had never told because the guilt felt like his inheritance. [339] Above us the stars sharpened through the prisms and scattered cold fire across our hands. [354] I threaded moonlight through the tapestry needle and began my work by unraveling knots in a centuries-old fisherman's net. [373] Each knot resisted like a clenched tooth, swollen with salt and time. [385] I used the needle's blunt eye to coax the fibers apart without breaking the inner thread that held the name. [405] The first knot gave with a sigh and released the name Tomas Elk, drowned eighteen forty nine. [422] As I whispered it, one cracked mask on the floor grew still and cool to the touch. [439] Jonah wrote the name on a shard of glass with charcoal and set it beneath the mask. [456] Knot by knot we worked, and the dome filled with an interlaced hush of breathing and surf. [473] It was not empty quiet but woven quiet, each released name a thread pulled through the night. [490] By the fourth name my fingers bled, and Jonah took the needle with surprising gentleness. [505] His small hands remembered the sailor's loops better than mine, and he freed old Pieter without tearing. [522] When he said the name aloud his voice cracked, then steadied, louder than the sea. [537] Two more masks settled, their humming fading into the glass. [547] At midnight we reached the hardest knot, black with tar and shaped like a fist. [562] Inside it was my brother's name, Caleb Voss, knotted in his own hurried hand. [576] I wept because I had hoped the sea had taken him cleanly without anger. [590] Jonah pressed the tapestry needle into my palm and nodded toward the mask with the crooked seam. [607] With steady breath I picked the knot open and spoke, Caleb, I am sorry, you are remembered. [624] The last mask cooled, and the dome's strange silence deepened into peace. [636] At dawn we carried seven quiet masks down to Kestrel Harbor as gulls cried. [650] The fishermen crossed themselves when they saw the names glowing faintly under fresh varnish. [664] That spring no boats were lost, and Jonah spoke a little more each tide. [678] I still mend what breaks, knowing silence can be rewoven into shelter. [690]</story>
