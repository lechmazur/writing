<story>The platform hung still above the clouds, a flat slab of basalt veined with old anchor runes. [17] It was a floating rock tea ceremony platform, tethered by three singing chains to nothing visible. [33] Mara climbed barefoot onto its warmth, a budding rune scholar sent to earn her second mark. [49] Master Ilya waited cross-legged by the low brazier, his hands scarred white from decades of etching. [65] Around them lay a saturated hush so dense the steam from the kettle rose in one unbroken thread. [83] Below boiled a black storm, above gathered a pale storm, and between them the air held clear and trembling. [102] This was a breath between storms, brief and deliberate, the only hour when the chains stopped humming. [119] Mara knelt on the woven mat and set down her case of inks, her throat tight with duty. [137] Her task was simple to speak and hard to live, to transform scars into stories that could hold weight. [156] On her left forearm, three raised burns from her first year still pulled whenever she wrote fast. [173] She had hidden them under long sleeves, ashamed of error, until Ilya asked her to bring them into light. [192] He poured bitter tea without speaking, then slid toward her a crow quill, trimmed tip sharp as a needle. [211] The ink he offered was stormglass ink, which crystallizes when stressed and ruins the line if the hand shakes. [230] You will write your true rune during a held breath, he said, or the storm will write you. [248] Mara lifted the cup with both hands and drank, letting the heat settle behind her ribs. [264] The tea tasted of iron and pine smoke, and her heart began to beat too loudly for the hush. [283] She shed her heavy outer robe to free her arms, folding it with care beside the mat. [300] Cool air touched her scars and she flinched, certain every old mistake showed in the clear light. [317] The upper storm growled and the platform swayed, sending a tremor through the ink bowl. [332] Tiny bright shards bloomed on its surface as her fear reached the liquid. [345] She remembered her grandmother warning that spilled salt invited ghosts and that whistling indoors called wind. [361] As a child she had believed each creak was a spirit listening, and she had learned to hold her breath to pass unseen. [384] Now she steadied herself by revisiting youthful superstitions she thought scholarship had burned away. [398] She tapped the cup three times for luck, tucked her tongue against her teeth to trap bad words, and counted her heartbeats to seven. [488] She had lunged to save her charts and branded herself, weeping not from pain but from shame at clumsiness. [507] She had hidden that shame for a year, polishing theories while the skin healed thick and voiceless. [524] Now, with lungs full and storms paused, she let the quill trace the ridge of each scar in one continuous rune of return. [547] The line held black and smooth, drinking light, binding pain to purpose without trembling into crystal.
