<story>Mara had lived for eleven winters as a guarded druid on the black shore. [14] Villagers sought her because she finds lost things that water and weather had stolen. [28] She kept her door barred and her promises short, and she trusted roots more than people. [44] Her calm was not softness but warlike serenity learned from years of border fighting. [58] When the miller lost his son Tomas, he begged her to look into still water. [73] She agreed to try a showing trick with a silver mirror laid upon the lake ice. [89] She whispered the names of the drowned and waited for the face to rise. [103] Nothing rose except her own tired eyes, and the village muttered that the charm was empty. [119] So after the magic trick fails she packed her staff and iron filings for deeper work. [135] She wanted to discover what mirrors don't show, the truth beneath reflection. [147] Old charts said all lost things drift toward the swirling orchard vortex at the heart of a cursed lake. [166] No boat would go there, so she walked the ice at dawn with measured breath. [181] The ice sang under her boots and the mist opened like a held lung. [195] In her left pocket she carried a flesh-eating plant seed wrapped in waxed linen. [209] It had been payment from a southern widow for finding a wedding ring in a well. [225] Mara hated the thing and needed it, for hungry roots can bite through spells. [239] At noon she reached the edge where solid ice broke into black rotating water. [253] Apple trees grew impossibly from the current, their branches circling and dripping with frost. [267] Fruit and chairs and coats and bells spun slowly in the foam, all things ever lost. [283] She knelt and set her palms flat, sending her hearing outward through expanding circles. [297] Each ripple returned a sound, a spoon, a dog bark, a child crying far below. [312] She breathed in warlike serenity and stepped into the cold ring without fear. [325] The water seized her knees and tried to drag her staff from her grip. [339] She planted her feet like a soldier and sang the root holding chant under her breath. [355] The vortex slowed enough for her to see a small blue mitten caught in branches. [370] It was Tomas mitten, and beneath it a lantern still burned green underwater. [383] She reached and the current bit her wrist, sharp as teeth of winter. [396] Then she saw the boy himself curled asleep in a pocket of air between roots. [411] He was alive and pale, breathing shallow beside a block of clear ice. [424] Inside the ice stood a folded paper and a man voice trapped like a fly. [439] She lifted it and knew it for a frozen confession left by someone desperate to be heard. [456] With numb fingers she broke the edge and read the miller own shaking hand. [470] He had traded his son sleep for a good harvest, promising the lake a winter child. [486] He had rowed the boy out during the orchard bloom and left the lantern as a marker. [503] Shame had frozen his words into the water after he lost courage to speak. [517] Mara felt old anger rise, then forced her spine to unbend from its guarded curl. [532] She had spent years bent around her pain, and now she stood straight in the flood. [548] Tomas woke and stared, too cold to cry, and she wrapped him in her dry cloak. [564] The branches tightened, for the lake demands payment, and the vortex began to spin faster. [579] Mara unwrapped the flesh-eating plant seed and pressed it into the wet bark. [592] She fed it with blood from her bitten wrist and ordered it to grow and bite. [608] Green tendrils burst and chewed the cursed wood, opening a path toward solid ice. [622] She carried the boy through expanding circles of calming song until the water lay flat. [637] Behind her the plant devoured the frozen confession and the lantern and the debt. [651] On shore she returned the mitten and the boy, and told the village the whole truth. [667] The miller wept and did not deny his crime, and the ice on the lake cracked like judgment. [685] Mara walked home empty handed but light, for she had found what was truly lost. [700]</story>
