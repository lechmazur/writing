<story>Tansy Wren sorted seeds the way rivers sorted stones, patiently and with mossy delight. [14] High above the cindered valley hung her home, an ashen skyborne arbor woven from living branches and tethered clouds. [33] She was the orderly seed sorter, keeper of bins and ledgers, lover of small exact rows. [49] Every autumn the great boughs released their leaves all at once, and the whole arbor held its breath during the hush where past meets prophecy. [74] The elders called it the Listening, when falling leaves spelled messages for those wise enough to read them. [92] Tansy burned to decode the message in falling leaves, not for glory but because unanswered questions itched like burrs in her socks. [114] Regional folklore insisted the arbor remembered everything the lowlands chose to forget, and its leaves repeated those memories to patient listeners. [135] Grandmothers below told of seed children who climbed into the sky and never returned unchanged, and Tansy believed every word with green growing joy. [159] Her method was peculiar, even for the arbor folk, for she spent her evenings building tiny monuments to forgotten things. [179] From bark chips she raised a barn for a lost farm, from thistledown she shaped a cradle for a lullaby no one sang. [202] From acorn caps she built a schoolhouse for a drowned village, and moss softened each roof with delight. [220] She set the monuments along the sorting table, hoping the leaves would recognize their kin and fall truer. [238] On the eve of the Listening, gray light filtered through ash and the air smelled of rain and cold apples. [258] Tansy swept her bins into perfect lines, labeled each with charcoal, and tied back her hair with vine. [276] Then she climbed to the crown platform with her monuments packed in seed sacks, humming to keep her courage sweet. [296] The hush arrived like dusk poured into water, silencing birds and bells and even the creak of branches. [314] Leaves loosened above her, gold and rust, turning slowly as if considering where a story should land. [331] Tansy knelt and arranged her tiny monuments in a spiral on the mossy planks, her hands trembling with happy awe. [351] A broad sycamore leaf landed across the barn, another veiled the cradle, a third roofed the schoolhouse like a blessing. [371] She laughed aloud, for the pattern looked less like chance and more like guests choosing their chairs at supper. [390] She leaned close to study the veins, and a glint caught her eye, a moonstone cabochon dust speck trembling on a leaf edge. [413] It must have lodged years ago from the old moonstone cabochon set in the arbor helm, ground fine by wind and time. [435] Tansy held her breath as the speck pulsed with pale fire, lighting the leaf veins like rivers on a map. [455] Beneath its glow she saw not random lines but a trail, a seed route winding between monuments in deliberate order. [475] Her orderly heart leapt, for here was sorting of a higher kind, a ledger written by wind and memory. [494] Gently she used a pine needle to prod the speck forward, guiding its light along the veins without scratching the leaf. [515] The speck rolled, brightened, and projected tiny shapes onto the moss, a barn door, a cradle song, a bell. [534] Each shape matched one monument, and Tansy understood the leaves were thanking her for remembering what the lowlands had discarded. [554] Tears pricked her eyes, moss soft and joyful, and she sang the half remembered lullaby aloud to fill its empty cradle. [575] More leaves fell, layering words upon words, and the speck drifted down onto her sorting ledger, illuminating a blank page. [595] The folklore spoke of a door in the crown that opens only when the past is honored, and now bark groaned and parted before her. [620] Inside pulsed a seed pod large as a kettle, lined with moonlit down, humming the lullaby back to her in harmony. [641] She knew the old warning, that whoever enters the pod emerges transformed always, never quite the sorter who climbed inside. [661] Tansy gathered her monuments, tucked the ledger under her arm, and stepped inside with mossy delight fizzing in her chest. [681] Darkness closed soft as soil, the pod rocked, and the dust speck settled warm upon her brow like a seed. [701] She dreamed of barns rebuilt, cradles rocked, school bells ringing across clear valleys, and awoke to morning light through leaves. [721] The pod had opened, the hush had lifted, and Tansy emerged transformed always, her hair threaded with living moss and light. [742] In her hands the ledger bloomed with new script, a prophecy sown from past kindness, readable only to orderly patient eyes. [763] She descended laughing, scattering seeds that sprouted midair, and the arbor folk cheered to see forgotten things remembered and growing. [783]</story>
