<story>After the mysterious package arrives on a rain thick Tuesday, Silas Cole does not open it until nightfall. [18] He is the weary undertaker of Grayport, a man who has folded more cold hands than he can remember. [37] Inside the crate, packed in salt and newspaper, lies a broken lighthouse lens that still smells of smoke and sea. [57] Beneath it rests a water swollen ledger filled with names no stone in his graveyard carries. [73] A note in a drowned hand asks him to give rest a permanent address. [87] Silas laughs once, short and tired, because the town council stopped paying for pauper funerals years ago. [104] He is inadvertently stubborn in the way old doors are, staying shut not from pride but from warped habit. [123] So he keeps the lens on his worktable and means to forget it. [136] At midnight the shard begins to blink with faint distress signals, though it has no lamp and no power. [155] The light throbs in slow triplets, pause, triplets again, like a heart trying to remember SOS. [171] Silas, who knows the language of last breaths, understands it is calling from below rather than from sea level. [190] The ledger pages, when dried by his stove, reveal a map inked in tide lines leading to the sunken ward of Pell. [212] Old charts say Pell fell in one storm night a century past, church bells and books and all. [230] Its library, the map claims, still holds air in its dome because the drowned sealed it with wax and prayer. [250] Silas borrows a diving suit from the harbor master and tells no one where he is going. [267] He descends at slack tide, following the pulses that now beat behind his eyes even on land. [284] The water is cold and green and utterly silent, a chased stillness that seems to flee as he enters it. [304] Pell rises from the gloom with streets intact and fish drifting through window frames like slow birds. [321] At its center stands the forgotten underwater city library, its doors open and its dome glowing faintly gold. [339] Inside, the water thins to breathable air, held back by some old pressure Silas does not try to understand. [358] Shelves tower in circles, every book swollen shut except one dry reading desk at the heart of the room. [377] There, occupying impossible spaces between shelf and shadow and memory, wait the unburied whose names fill his ledger. [395] They are not ghosts as the living imagine, but imprints kept awake by being never recorded nor mourned. [413] A girl with kelp in her hair steps forward and points to the broken lens in his pack. [431] You brought the light, she says, though her mouth does not move in any normal way. [447] Silas sets the lens upon the desk, and its fractured facets catch the dome glow and scatter distress signals across the walls. [469] Each flash becomes a name projected in light, each name a face stepping briefly into focus. [485] He reads aloud from the ledger, giving each soul date and tide and witness, to give rest a permanent address at last. [507] His voice shakes at first, then steadies, and the chased stillness settles around him like dust after running feet. [526] The girl tells him the library has kept them only because no living hand would revitalize their stories. [544] Silas understands then that burial is not always a body in earth but a name kept where it can be found. [565] He copies every ledger name onto the dry pages of the library catalog, pressing hard so the ink holds. [584] As each entry dries, one figure thins and quiets, no longer flickering with need. [598] The lens flares once, clear and whole in purpose if not in glass, then darkens into ordinary breakage. [616] Silas climbs back through green water with empty lungs and a full heart, pursued only by silence. [633] In Grayport he builds a small memorial wall beside his chapel, carving each Pell name into slate. [650] When the fog comes thick and ships sound their horns, he places the broken lighthouse lens above the wall to catch their light. [673] Travelers think it is mere decoration, but sailors swear its cracks still pulse in answer to distress signals far at sea. [694] Silas tends the wall until his own hands tremble, content at last to have given rest a place that will not wash away. [717]</story>
