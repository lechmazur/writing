<story>After the procedure, Mara remembered nothing on purpose and everything by accident. [12] She had paid to become a clean slate seeker with a dirty past scrubbed into static. [28] The clinic took her guilt, her names, her capacity to flinch, and left her functional. [43] For three months she felt clean in the way empty houses feel clean. [56] Then the wonder started leaking back in, jaded and unwelcome, like light under a door. [71] She wanted to kindle empathy where none remains, starting with herself, because numbness was worse than shame. [88] So she began a journey she told no one about, down and inward. [101] Her only map was learned by reading between newspaper lines in the public archive. [115] The obituaries praised people she had once betrayed, and the classifieds beside them hid directions in plain syntax. [133] Lost dog, found watch, piano lessons, each ad held a second sentence for those who knew loss as a code. [153] Mara had written that code herself, before, when she sold routes and names to quiet men. [169] Now she followed it backward like a thread through a labyrinth of choices she had pretended were not choices. [188] The final ad read FOR SALE: childhood globe, slightly haunted, meet where the river goes blind. [204] She laughed, because she had placed that ad seven years ago and forgotten the placement. [219] Below the limestone quarry, past the sealed transit tunnel, the earth opened into an underground bioluminescent lake. [236] The water glowed a delicately robust blue, fragile as breath yet strong enough to paint her face. [253] Cave walls sweated light, and blind fish drew slow calligraphy beneath the surface. [266] Mara stood a long time, jaded enough to catalogue the algae species, wondering anyway at the impossible chandelier of the dark. [287] A boy lived there, or something that had learned to look like a boy to avoid questions. [304] He tended a rowboat and a crate of damp newspapers dating back forty years. [318] You came late, he said, not unkindly, and Mara felt an old interrogator habit rise to meet innocence. [336] I used to hurt people efficiently, she said, testing if the lake would echo her confession. [352] The boy nodded as if weather had been described. [361] From the crate he lifted a baseball signed by a time traveler, its ink still wet across decades. [379] The signature read Edwin Voss, 2147, with a looped 7 that swallowed its own tail. [394] Mara turned it over and felt sick, because the ball was the one she had lost at nine, before any betrayals. [447] He unfolded a newspaper from next week, her name in the drownings column, then in the rescues. [464] Two futures, he explained, and the lake will only carry one weight across. [477] Beyond the water, three tunnels breathed cold air, each marked with her handwriting in different ages. [493] Left promised exposure and prison, center promised forgetting again, right promised remembering everything she did. [508] Mara understood this was not magic but consequence made visible, and that made it worse and better. [525] She asked the boy who he was, and he said he was the part of her that had waited to feel sorry. [547] Jaded as she was, she almost dismissed him, yet wonder kept her hands gentle on the baseball seams. [565] If I go back remembering, she said, will I be able to stand it. [579] No, he said cheerfully, but you will be able to carry others across, which is the actual trick. [597] She chose the right tunnel, not because she was brave but because she was tired of being light. [615] The rowboat moved without oars, guided by the current and the soft pulse of living light. [631] Midway across, Mara pressed the baseball into the boy's hands and told him to throw it far into time. [650] Let some other lost kid find it early enough to choose differently, she said, crying for the first time since winter. [671] The lake flared, the tunnels exhaled, and the weight in her chest shifted from stone to seed.
