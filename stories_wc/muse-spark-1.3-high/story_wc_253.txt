<story>The farmhouse overshadowed by giant wind turbines stood empty after the happiness mandate was repealed. [15] Mara had been a blank-faced performer in the capital before she stopped smiling on order. [30] Now her face stayed empty by habit, not by paint, and neighbors found that harder to forgive. [47] She lived with her brother Tomas, who was morally ambiguous in the small useful ways survivors had to be. [66] He traded ration flour for fuel, lied on census forms, and kept the radio working when radios were forbidden. [85] Mara did not judge him because judgment required feeling and she had cultivated a careful emotional void instead. [103] In the mornings she would whittle spoons from fallen fence posts while the turbines turned overhead. [119] She worked slowly, shaving thin curls that fell like snow onto her boots. [132] Tomas said idleness would get them reported, so Mara began dramatically narrating mundane tasks to seem busy and bright. [151] She announced the sweeping of ash like a battle, the boiling of water like a resurrection. [167] The neighbors laughed at first, then listened, then came closer to the fence to hear her. [183] Her motivation was not fame but to cultivate lightness in grief after their mother died in the final mandated festival. [203] Their mother had clutched a dried flower bouquet while officials checked her smile with a wooden stick. [220] Mara kept that bouquet on the mantel, its petals brown and crackling whenever the door slammed. [236] She could not throw it away because it smelled faintly of dust and summer and forced joy. [253] One evening Tomas brought home a stranger who offered seeds for shelter from the patrols. [268] The stranger recognized Mara from the stage and asked why she no longer wore the white mask. [285] Mara replied that her own skin had learned the shape and no longer needed plaster. [300] The stranger stayed for supper and praised her narration of potato peeling as if it were a coronation. [318] After supper Tomas whispered that they should report the stranger for a bounty of flour and coal. [335] Mara felt nothing rise in her chest, only a flat gray calm that worried her more than anger would. [354] That night the turbines stalled in dead air and left what the old people called a remade hush over the fields. [375] It was not the old silence from before the mandate, but a new one patched from absence and relief. [394] In that hush Mara heard her mother coughing behind a smile, heard the crowd chanting happy slogans off key. [413] She rose and took the dried flower bouquet down and crumbled one stem between her fingers. [429] Tomas found her at the table, sorting petals and whittling a small box to hold them. [445] He said the stranger had fever and would die without medicine they could buy with bounty money. [462] Mara continued dramatically narrating mundane tasks even in whisper, describing the sorting of seeds like counting stars. [479] The stranger listened from the cot and wept, saying her voice made ordinary mercy feel possible again. [496] Tomas argued that mercy was a luxury and grief was safer when it stayed light and portable. [513] Mara understood his logic, for she too wanted to cultivate lightness in grief rather than drown in its weight. [532] Yet she knew lightness could not be built on betrayal, or it would sink like wet wood. [549] At dawn she gave the stranger her winter coat and pointed to the path between the turbines. [566] She told Tomas she had dreamed their mother asked for quiet, not happiness, not performance. [581] Tomas called her a fool and a traitor, but he did not stop the stranger at the door. [599] His morally ambiguous heart wavered, choosing kin loyalty over profit for once, and he looked ashamed of the softness. [618] After they left, Mara swept the floor and narrated it as the clearing of a stage after a long run. [638] The remade hush settled again as wind returned and the blades began their slow patient circling. [654] She placed the empty stems back on the mantel and kept only dust in the new box. [671] Tomas sat beside her and did not speak, and the emotional void between them felt less like absence than rest. [691] Outside the turbines hummed and the farmhouse held its shadow, small and stubborn beneath them. [706] Mara whittled another spoon and described its birth in a low theatrical whisper for no audience at all. [724]</story>
