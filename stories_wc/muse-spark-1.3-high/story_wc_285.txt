<story>The gloom-hill coachman kept his horses still at the fog-bound river crossing. [12] He was closely attentive to the water, to the ropes, to the breathing of his two mares. [29] Rain had ended an hour before, but the eaves and gutters still ticked with runoff. [44] He had promised the miller's widow he would wait until rain gutters stop ticking before risking the ferry cable. [63] It was a palimpsest promise, written over older vows to his late wife, to his son lost at sea, to himself. [84] Each layer showed through the next, and he meant to keep them all by keeping this one. [101] In the carriage behind him slept a girl clutching a sampler and a yarn needle. [116] She was bound for her aunt in Lowwick, sent with a shawl half mended to distill complexity into essence. [135] Her mother had told her that mending was prayer made plain, one loop at a time. [151] The coachman had lifted her up at Gloom Hill when the road turned to mire. [166] Now fog lay thick on the river, soft and white as carded wool. [179] There was a spindrift serenity in the air, a lightness that did not deny the cold. [195] He listened through ambient awareness rather than staring, letting sounds percolate slowly into meaning. [209] The cable hummed low, the water lapped the pilings, the horses shifted and sighed. [223] Somewhere across the river a ferryman coughed and dragged a chain across wood. [236] The girl woke and pressed her face to the damp window, watching him without fear. [251] She asked if they were stuck, and he smiled under his beard. [263] Only waiting for the river to settle, he said, and for the gutters to finish their talk. [280] She nodded as if she understood waiting, then held up her torn shawl for him to see. [297] The pattern was a river in blue wool, with one bank unraveled by travel. [311] My aunt will think me careless, she whispered, picking at the loose edge. [324] The coachman climbed down, opened the carriage door, and sat carefully on the step. [338] With thick fingers he threaded her yarn needle on the second try, then returned it with a bow. [356] Mend it here, he told her, where the light is gray but even. [369] She bent over the work, tongue caught between teeth, hands slow at first then surer. [384] He watched the thread pull the blue river closed, stitch by stitch, while he listened for silence from the eaves. [404] At last the ticking thinned to a single drop, then to nothing, and the cable sang tighter. [421] The ferryman called across the fog, and the coachman stood and called back in answer. [436] He led the horses forward onto the wooden ferry, coaxed the wheels over the ramps, and set the brake. [455] Midriver the fog opened for a breath, showing pale sky and gulls wheeling high above the current. [472] The girl finished her last stitch, bit the thread, and held the mended shawl around her shoulders. [489] When they reached the far bank, she thanked him and offered him the needle for luck. [505] He shook his head and closed her fingers over it, saying keepers mend best with their own tools. [523] Driving up from the river into thin sunlight, he felt the old promises settle quietly into one kept vow. [542] The road ahead was clear, the horses eager, and the girl humming softly against the clatter of hooves. [560] He understood at last that patience had distilled his grief and duty into simple care for this crossing. [578] It was enough to bring them safely over, to watch and wait and mend what could be mended. [596] Behind them the fog closed again over the water, gentle and absolving as a held breath released.
