<story>Long after the last human is born, the recycled spaceship Mercy drifts beyond the orbit of a cooling sun. [19] Its hull is patched from carriers and stations and temples, and no living breath warms its corridors. [36] Only memory ghosts remain, held by the ship in thin fields of light. [49] Among them lingers Suni, a steadfast pacifist who had been a field medic in the final wars. [66] She had never lifted a weapon, even when soldiers burned her clinic and called her mercy a treason. [84] Death did not end her duty, and with ghostly resolve she still walks the ward deck each dim cycle. [103] The ship measures grief as mass, for ghosts here grow gravitationally lighter when happy and heavy when they mourn. [122] Suni often sinks through the floor plates when she remembers the children she could not save. [138] In the chapel of reclaimed things she guards Kwan Yin's tear, a clear bead sealed in copper wire. [156] Legend says the bodhisattva wept for a world that would not stop hurting, and the tear did not fall but stayed to work. [179] Suni believes the bead remembers compassion better than any ghost, and she speaks to it when guilt grows loud. [198] The Mercy hides a sealed door that is not a door, a thin place where physics folds and refuses entry. [218] The old navigators called it a locked dimension, a possible future walled off during the wars. [234] Suni has made it her purpose to push open a locked dimension where unborn kindness might take root again. [253] Force cannot move it, for every weapon pressed against it only thickens the wall. [267] Prayer alone cannot move it either, and Suni has prayed until her light grew thin. [282] Then the ship shows her another path, written not in manuals but in faces. [296] In the archive are faded photographs of refugees, mothers and medics and tired children staring at the lens. [314] The dead reach her via the faint shimmer of long-dried tears on faded photographs when the archive lamp burns low. [334] Each speck of salt catches the light and opens a voice, brief and honest. [348] At first the voices comfort her, thanking her for bandages and water and calm hands. [363] Then one night a boy she failed asks why she did not fight the men who came with fire. [382] Shame rises in her like black water, and the floor plates groan under her sudden weight. [398] She wants to turn away and polish her peace like a clean badge. [411] Instead the ship asks for shadow work, the hard task of holding her buried rage up to the light. [430] For a pacifist can be steadfast only if she knows the violence inside her and chooses otherwise each day. [449] Suni sits among the photographs and lets herself remember wanting to kill. [461] She remembers the torchlight on the clinic wall and her hands shaking not with fear but with hatred. [479] She had buried that hatred under vows, calling it unworthy, until it poisoned her kindness from below. [496] Now she names it aloud, telling the boy she was furious and helpless and human. [511] The admission does not excuse her, but it steadies her, and her form flickers a little brighter. [528] Night after night she returns, listening to anger and grief without flinching or striking back. [543] Slowly her weight lessens, for joy is not the absence of pain but pain fully faced and forgiven. [561] One cycle she laughs while mending a torn shroud, and she rises a handspan above the deck. [578] She learns to stitch the frayed edges of memory to mercy, binding what was broken without hiding the seam. [597] She threads sorrow through compassion until the cloth holds light. [607] She carries that stitched shroud and Kwan Yin's tear to the thin place where the wall resists. [624] She does not demand or batter, but lays her forehead against the cold fold and tells the truth of herself. [644] She says she has hated and has chosen care, and she will choose care again though the cost remains. [663] The tear warms in its copper cage, and the shroud lifts as her heart grows light and buoyant. [681] A seam of brightness parts in the dark, wide enough for a breath, then for a song. [698] Through it Suni glimpses green fields and unbuilt cities, a dimension waiting for gentle hands. [713] She does not enter, for ghosts cannot plant, but she holds the opening with steady love. [729] She sings the names from the photographs until the wall learns the shape of mercy that has faced its shadow. [749]</story>
