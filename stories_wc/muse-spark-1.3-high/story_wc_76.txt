<story>Mara had spent eleven years tending the mysterious lighthouse on a rocky island that the charts refused to name. [19] She was a counter clockwise clock maker by trade, building timepieces whose hands ran backward to honor the dead. [38] The island gave her salt and silence, and under the slow exhale of low tide it gave her something stranger. [58] A hunger stone rose from the black sand, carved with rings and marked with a date that had not yet happened. [79] Old sailors said such stones appeared in famine years to warn the river towns, but this one warned her alone. [99] She touched its cold face and felt a bleak exhilaration, as if grief had finally found a sharp and useful edge. [120] Her brother had drowned on a night the official clocks called safe, and she had come here to step beyond fate's design. [142] Inside the lantern room she kept his ancient yoyo, its wooden halves locked together and refusing to spin. [160] She had always thought it a toy, until she saw that its carved spiral matched the rings on the hunger stone exactly. [182] She was neatly inventive even in mourning, and she decided to initiate a new mechanism that joined stone and toy and tower. [204] She would learn the stones warning through reverse-engineering the puzzle of an ancient yoyo left by a drowned boy. [223] She carried the yoyo down to the exposed reef and set it against the hunger stone while gulls screamed overhead. [243] The wood was swollen with years, and the axle hid a second chamber that turned only counterclockwise. [260] Inside the chamber lay a coil of brass wire, a vial of phosphor oil, and a slip of paper in her brothers hand. [283] If the tide takes me, read the light backward, the note said, and Mara understood at last. [300] Her brother had discovered a latent radiance sleeping in the phosphor, a light that only woke when starved of sun. [320] He had meant the lighthouse to burn cold at low water, guiding boats that official charts abandoned. [337] Mara climbed back to the lamp with shaking legs, the hunger stone looming larger behind her with every step. [356] The great lens was built to throw warm light outward, but she reversed its gears to drink light inward instead. [376] With wire and oil she wired the yoyo axle into the clockwork, letting the counterweight fall as the sea fell. [396] Her counter clockwise training made the inversion simple, and soon the whole tower ticked toward darkness rather than dawn. [415] At the exact ebb she opened the vial, and the phosphor drank the stored lamplight and released a pale blue fire. [436] It was not bright, but every fisherman for twenty miles would see a cold star where no star should burn. [456] The hunger stone below seemed to answer, its carved date glowing faintly as the tide licked its base. [474] Mara laughed aloud in the empty lantern room, hollow and triumphant, for fate had scheduled a wreck that night. [493] A coal freighter named Mercy was due to follow the old channel, trusting a light that would lie to save fuel. [514] Her new cold beacon told the truth instead, warning of the reef the company charts had erased. [531] She watched the ship slow, turn, and anchor beyond the rocks, its horn sounding once in confusion and thanks. [550] Dawn returned with the flood, and the hunger stone sank again beneath the gray and indifferent water. [567] Mara reset her backward clocks, knowing she had broken the pattern without breaking her love for the lost. [585] The tower would still be called haunted, the stone would still hunger, but no law of tide or time owned her now. [607] She oiled the yoyo and set it spinning in the lantern light, free at last to rise and fall. [626]</story>
