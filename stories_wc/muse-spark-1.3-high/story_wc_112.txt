<story>Juno Kall was an obliging sky mechanic who had traded open clouds for sealed corridors. [15] She took work at a xenobiology research station anchored above the ice fields of Varra Nine. [31] The scientists studied vast mothlike drifters that slept in vaulted hangars during the cold cycle. [46] Juno repaired their heating ducts and flight harnesses without complaint and learned their quiet rhythms. [61] Her hands were mechanically warm from old grafts that let her work bare fingered in frost. [77] She spoke little but listened well, and the night staff trusted her with locked doors. [92] Juno had a private theory she rarely shared, to prove that time moves sideways. [106] Her brother had vanished in a storm fold over the southern reach, and clocks never agreed afterward. [123] She believed loss was not forward but adjacent, a room one could step into. [137] The station held a magnetic nexus at twilight that hummed when the low sun skimmed the horizon. [154] Engineers used it to calibrate navigation, but Juno felt it pull at her grafts like memory. [170] She mapped its pulses through holographic principles borrowed from a dead pilot manual. [183] According to the diagrams, overlapping fields could thin the wall between parallel moments. [196] Juno waited for the right evening within the hush of beating wings at rest. [210] The drifters folded their great wings and sank into slow breath, and the hangars grew silent. [226] Frost traced the windows, and the station air held an icy warmth that soothed and stung. [242] Juno carried her tools to the service shaft beneath the nexus ring and disabled the alarm. [258] She did not want rescue or witness, only a brief alignment she could enter alone.
