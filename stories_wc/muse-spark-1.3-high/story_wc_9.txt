<story>After the birth of her daughter, Mara climbed the stair of wind to the midnight glade above clouds. [53] Mara carried nothing but a chipped cup and a dolls' tea set saucer crack she had kept since childhood. [92] She had missed her own mother's last birthday, and the empty outline haunted her like an unpaid debt. [110] The cartomancer spread cards inked black as onyx, their edges soft with cloud and handling. [154] With shaking fingers she began to unfasten the bundle holding her daughter's first cloth. [168] Tell me where the hours went, she whispered, the feedings and fears I cannot recall. [196] You seek cloudbound repentance, she said, the kind that cannot kneel in churches but only in high lonely places. [215] Mara nodded, tears catching the moon as she laid the cracked saucer among the cards. [230] This crack came when I was seven and reached for more cake before my hand was traced, she said. [249] My mother glued it and poured my tea through the crack as a joke, calling it my shortcut to growing. [302] She turned a second card, and Mara saw herself rocking a cradle while staring at the wall, exhausted and absent. [355] The cartomancer turned a third card, blank except for an outline of a small hand. [370] To find where lost time accumulates you must follow what was never finished, she whispered. [456] The cartomancer gathered moonlight into the cracked saucer and told her to drink the reflection. [471] Mara lifted the saucer, and the silver light stung her lips with cold fire. [485] Suddenly she heard her daughter crying down below in the warm house, thin and real through cloud. [502] Guilt seized her, for she had left the child to chase repentance instead of living it. [570] Go down, the cartomancer said, and lay a clean cloth for your daughter's first birthday before the year stains it.
