<story>The queen had been dead for forty days and no new ruler had been named. [15] During the waiting the palace kept its doors open but its throne empty. [28] Mara sat each morning in the whispering gallery beneath the high dome. [40] She had been hired as a crisis actor to weep on cue for visiting delegations. [55] The officials did not know she was also a real crisis survivor from the drowned southern towns. [72] Three years earlier the river had taken her house and her brother and her name from the registers. [90] Now she lent her grief for pay while keeping her true grief hidden. [103] The gallery was a perfect circle where even a breath traveled along the wall. [117] Light fell through the oculus in a pale shaft that never seemed to touch anyone directly. [133] The old keeper called that moving half light a nomadic penumbra. [144] He said it belonged to no one and followed the sun like a displaced citizen. [159] Keeper Ansel was benevolently ruthless with everyone who performed sorrow in his hall. [172] He fed the actors soup and corrected their posture and dismissed them without pity if they lied badly. [190] You wail too loudly, he told Mara, real loss is quieter and stays longer. [204] She wanted to tell him she was not acting when her hands shook. [217] Instead she asked what she could do to become the historian of small gestures. [231] He laughed and pointed to a chessboard missing all the pawns left on the marble ledge. [247] The kings and queens and knights stood without any common soldiers to protect or to mourn. [263] During an interregnum, he said, the small pieces always disappear first. [274] Mara understood because in her town the fishermen and nurses had vanished from records before the nobles. [291] She began to watch the other women in the gallery and to memorize the way they folded scarves and touched foreheads. [312] She wrote these details in a bound ledger the palace gave her for false testimonies. [327] At noon the light left the center empty and the whole dome held a luminous absence. [343] It was bright and hollow at once, as if presence had been carefully removed. [357] Mara learned that whispers spoken against one curve could be heard clearly on the opposite side. [373] Ansel taught her a pattern of walking that spells words for those who listen from afar. [389] If she walked slowly along the eastern arc then paused then crossed quickly, her footsteps made the shape of letters. [409] A long drag meant a vowel and a short tap meant a break between names. [424] Together they spelled the names of the drowned that the official chronicle had omitted. [438] One evening Mara decided to unbind the ledger and to sew her true notes into its spine. [455] She loosened the thread with a kitchen knife and felt her heart beat with the wall echoes. [472] Ansel found her at midnight with pages spread around her like fallen leaves. [485] He could have reported her and ended her pay and her shelter in the upper rooms. [501] Instead he sat beside her and read each small gesture aloud so the dome would remember. [517] The way old Ilsa tucked bread into her sleeve for her grandson. [529] The way the young guard touched his cap before crying though no king ordered him. [544] The whisper carried each line around the circle and returned stronger. [555] When the new ruler finally arrived, the court demanded a record of mourning during an interregnum. [571] Mara offered the rebound book with false pages outside and true names hidden inside the binding. [587] The court saw only proper sorrow and praised her skill as an actor. [600] But those who pressed an ear to the gallery wall could still hear the other history walking. [617] In that sound the lost remained present and the empty center stayed bright. [630]
