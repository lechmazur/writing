<story>June lived alone in a floating rock dojo above the cloud sea. [12] The dojo drifted on a broken piece of mountain and held one hall one garden and one bell. [30] June had come there as an ego death survivor after the temple dissolved her name and past. [47] The rite had cured her fear but left her numb and unable to weep or laugh. [63] She had grown a child from her own cells to defeat emotional numbness and learn love as practice. [81] She named the child Kit and taught her balance breath and soft steps. [94] The temple declared the child unlicensed and sent a clone to claim her. [107] The clone called herself Ilya and wore the old face of June with perfect health. [122] Their dispute became a clone custody battle heard under temple law. [133] The hearing was set in the dojo while history rewrites itself in the lowlands. [147] Archives flickered and birth records changed each hour as the time vaults failed. [160] No paper could prove who was original and who was copy. [171] Ilya brought proof in a sealed case and set it on the mat. [184] Inside the case rested a brain in a jar labeled duplicate in faded ink. [198] The clerks said the jar held the first failed clone of June and proved the line was temple property. [217] June bowed to the jar and felt old grief move under her numbness. [230] Ilya demanded sole custody and cited doctrine stability and clean lineage. [241] June claimed Kit by care and daily touch rather than by blood or law. [255] The arbiter ordered them to settle the matter through complementary opposites on the practice floor. [270] One would move as stone and the other as water until truth showed. [283] They circled in silence while Kit sat beside the bell with wide eyes. [296] Ilya struck first with hard precise forms learned from temple masters. [307] June answered with soft yielding turns learned from wind and age. [318] Stone pressed and water gave and neither could hold ground alone. [329] Kit watched and then spoke in a voice that slices through pretense without cruelty. [343] She asked why two mothers fought to own her instead of raising her. [356] The question stopped both women more cleanly than any blow. [366] June lowered her staff and saw her own hunger for feeling had used the child. [381] Ilya lowered her blade and saw her own hunger for order had done the same. [396] The arbiter told them law could not cut a living bond while records burned. [410] He proposed shared custody sealed by training rather than by ownership. [421] June would teach Kit stillness and Ilya would teach Kit strength. [432] Together they would vivify the dead garden on the rock as a sign of trust. [447] They knelt in the thin soil and set the jar aside with honor under the bell. [463] They mixed ash and compost and pressed seeds into the cold ground together. [476] June breathed slow and Ilya breathed fast and their rhythms steadied Kit. [488] Day by day the sprouts rose green against gray stone and the bell rang clear. [503] June felt a sharp ache when Kit laughed and knew numbness was cracking. [516] Ilya felt tears when Kit stumbled and knew doctrine had not taught her this care. [531] They did not own the child but guarded her growth as gardeners guard light. [545] When the lowland vaults settled the records named both women as mother. [557] History kept the scar of the dispute and the dojo kept its peace. [570] Kit placed small stones around the sprouts and sang the watering chant each morning. [584] The floating stone drank the rain and held warmth from the sun through cold nights. [599] June found herself able to mourn the jar under the bell and to smile at new leaves. [616] Ilya found herself able to question orders and to sit still without purpose or report. [631] The dojo remained a school for opposites learning to hold together without breaking. [644]</story>
