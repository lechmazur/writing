<story>Li Mei had been called a chaste courtesan for ten years without ever touching a client in desire. [18] In the capital her role was to pour tea and remember poems while powerful men negotiated futures around her silence. [38] She learned to defy the lullaby of conformity not with shouting but with perfect stillness that made listeners uneasy. [57] Her rebellion lived in her memory because she kept every forbidden song the court tried to bury. [74] On the night of first snow the Empress ordered all courtesans to sing the same approved lullaby at midnight. [93] Li Mei bowed and smiled and slipped away through the service gate with her lute wrapped in linen. [111] She walked to the abandoned silver courtyard under snow where no guards patrolled and no music was allowed. [129] The courtyard held an alarming serenity as snow settled pale and empty over silent stones. [144] In the center stood what children called the prism sanctuary under starlight because broken glass still caught the sky. [163] It was not a temple but a collapsed pavilion whose shattered roof prisms threw thin colors across the snow. [182] Li Mei knelt inside the ring of light and tuned her lute with fingers numb from cold. [199] She did not intend to perform but to unlock the voices hidden in the stones themselves. [215] For years women had whispered farewells and curses and prayers in that courtyard knowing the snow would keep them. [234] Li Mei believed those sounds remained frozen waiting for a harvest of echoes in the right hands. [251] She plucked one string and listened as the note traveled outward and returned changed. [265] Another pluck brought back a laugh she had never uttered and a sob she had swallowed at fourteen. [283] The prisms trembled and multiplied her music into colors that moved across her skin like living ink. [300] Her response was bodily poetic as she swayed and breathed and let vibration settle in her throat and ribs. [319] She understood that memory here was not a record but a tangle of threads left by many feet. [337] Each thread hummed with a different weight of grief and hope and anger carefully knotted together. [353] She could free them only by untangling threads one by one without breaking their delicate fibers. [369] So she played slowly naming each feeling aloud and letting the prisms answer in widening light. [385] A maid who drowned her newborn sang through the ice with a voice sharp as needles. [401] An old musician who lost his hands hummed fingerings that Li Mei repeated faithfully on her lute. [418] A girl sold at twelve laughed between sobs and taught Li Mei a verse banned for thirty years. [436] Snow fell harder and the colors deepened until the courtyard breathed between sigh and smile in fragile balance. [454] That pause held everything unsaid in the palace and Li Mei felt its alarming calm enter her bones. [472] She realized conformity was a lullaby sung to keep the threads tangled and the women quiet. [488] To defy it she must not add noise but separate each voice so it could be heard clearly. [506] Her fingers bled on the strings and blood spotted the snow but she did not stop playing. [523] With each clear tone a prism brightened and projected a woman walking away from the palace gates. [540] Dozens of lights moved across the snow like a procession of freed shadows seeking their names. [556] Li Mei sang the banned verse aloud and her own voice joined the harvest rising into cold air. [574] At midnight the palace bells called for the approved lullaby but the courtyard answered with many songs instead. [592] Guards who entered the gate stopped in confusion because serenity met them like a blade wrapped in silk. [610] They expected riot and found stillness and they expected silence and found unbearable clarity. [624] Li Mei stood with bloody hands and bowed to them without fear and without submission. [639] She had unlocked the courtyard and herself and there was no lullaby strong enough to bind her again. [657] By dawn the snow kept only footprints and faint color but the women remembered every word they had heard. [676] In tearooms and kitchens and nurseries they hummed the banned verse softly until the palace itself seemed to listen. [695]</story>
