<story>Mara had been a wreck-diver ghost whisperer long before the northern sea swallowed the passenger liner Astrid. [17] Now she lived above the icefield, listening for voices trapped under water and under winter. [32] The villagers called her to the candle-lit shrine carved into glacier ice when the dreams began. [48] Three strangers on three nights dreamed of a drowned steward knocking on windows of connection between the living and the dead. [69] Mara weaves truth carefully, never promising reunion, only passage. [78] She knew the steward from the dive, a young man named Tomas who had stayed at his post as the halls flooded. [100] To salvage something pure from the wreckage, she had already brought up letters, a brass bell, and a child's melted snow globe. [122] The globe had lost its winter scene to heat from the engine fire, its glass sagged and its snow turned to cloudy resin. [145] Tomas haunted it, she thought, because it had belonged to the little girl in cabin nine. [161] Mara built her method by analyzing cameo appearances in strangers' dreams, tracing how the dead borrowed faces to send messages. [181] Tomas did not speak in the dreams but pointed downward, again and again, toward a sealed pantry. [198] The shipping company claimed the Astrid carried only grain and timber, and paid the families quickly to forget. [216] Mara suspected they wanted to expose nothing, so she descended once more through black water with borrowed starlight clinging to her helmet. [238] Below, the wreck loomed like a sleeping cathedral, its portholes blinded with silt and frost. [253] She whispered as divers are taught not to do, calling Tomas by name until the current shifted. [270] He came as cold pressure on her gloves, guiding her past the collapsed stairwell to the pantry door. [288] Inside, behind tins of coffee and salt pork, stood a crate marked medical glass, still latched. [304] Her lamp caught small vials of vaccine packed in sawdust, enough to save a mountain town through diphtheria winter. [323] The company had hidden the shipment to avoid liability, letting rumor blame the crew for the slow evacuation. [341] Tomas had died locking that pantry to keep the vials from shattering when the ship rolled. [357] Mara understood then why his ghost knocked on windows of connection in sleep, begging the living to look lower. [376] She surfaced with two vials sealed in her suit and the truth burning brighter than grief. [392] At the shrine, candles guttered against blue walls while families waited for word of blame or blessing. [409] Mara weaves truth carefully still, telling first of courage before speaking of corporate cowardice. [423] She set the melted snow globe among the candles so its warped glass caught and threw soft light. [441] This is Tomas, she said, who kept his post and kept this medicine whole. [455] This company left it unlisted and left him unnamed to salvage profit from the wreckage. [470] To expose them, she showed the crate number burned into her slate and the dreams that three strangers had written down before ever meeting. [494] The crowd grew silent, and even the ice seemed to listen as Tomas brushed frost from the shrine arch. [513] The girl from cabin nine, now grown with children of her own, stepped forward weeping and claimed the globe. [532] You kept my winter, she whispered to the cold, and now we keep your name. [547] That night Mara dreamed herself for once, not as diver but as passenger watching snow fall inside unbroken glass. [566] Tomas stood outside the globe, smiling, no longer knocking, his hand resting lightly on the window. [582] She woke knowing she had salvaged something pure from the wreckage, not the vaccine alone but the memory of duty. [602] Outside, dawn poured over the glacier like borrowed starlight returning to the sky that lent it. [618]</story>
