<story>Queen Maren had ruled for eleven years and no longer believed in omens. [13] They called her a disenchanted queen behind fans of black lace, and she let them. [28] Every night for a month she dreamed of light falling through stone like blood through linen. [44] She woke to unravel the mystery of recurring dreams that left salt on her tongue and ash under her nails. [64] The court seer warned her twice, then delivered after the final warning only silence and a folded map. [82] The map showed the ember grotto on the ridge, marked in red ink that had bled through the parchment. [101] Above it, drawn in blue so deep it hurt, was the cobalt grotto above clouds. [116] Maren knew she would climb, though the mountain had killed stronger and younger sovereigns. [130] Her voice remained softly commanding when she ordered no guards and no bells to follow her. [146] She took only Tova, a kitchen girl who remembered the old paths and owed the queen a debt. [164] They left at dusk with a shared exhale spotting the first fireflies of summer over the barley fields. [182] The path rose through pine and cold air, and the town below shrank to a scatter of lamps. [200] Tova spoke of her mother who had vanished on the ridge, and Maren listened without turning her head. [218] Mist closed around them like lace pulled tight, beautiful and full of lacy menace. [232] By midnight they reached the ember grotto on the ridge and found its mouth glowing faintly orange. [249] Coals that had slept for a hundred years breathed when Maren stepped inside. [262] The walls were ribbed with black glass, and in each rib ran a vein of bleeding starlight. [279] It did not shine so much as leak, slow and silver and wrong. [292] Tova pressed her palm to the stone and whispered that she had seen this light in her own sleep. [311] Maren understood then that the dreams were not hers alone but a summons sent to many. [327] Deeper in the grotto a stair of cooled lava rose beyond the heat into darkness and wind. [344] The climb grew steeper, and the air thinned until each breath felt borrowed. [357] Tova tied their wrists together with a strip torn from her apron so neither would fall unseen. [374] They emerged above the clouds into cold blue silence that rang in their teeth. [388] The cobalt grotto above clouds opened before them, vast and round as the inside of a bell. [405] Its walls were not stone but solid night, veined with fractures of bleeding starlight. [419] In the center stood a pool of still water holding the sky upside down. [433] Maren saw herself as a child, then as a bride, then as the disenchanted queen she had become. [451] Tova saw herself older, crowned, weeping, and she laughed in fear at the image. [465] A figure formed from the mist, tall and thin, with eyes like moth holes in lace. [481] You came after the final warning, it said in a voice softly commanding and sad. [496] Maren did not kneel, for she had left kneeling to priests and to husbands. [510] Tell me why the dreams bleed, she said, and why you chose us. [523] The figure touched the pool, and the bleeding starlight gathered like milk in water. [537] Every star that dies sends its light downward, and some lights lodge in stone and in sleep. [554] Your ridge caught one, and now it calls for witnesses before it cools forever. [568] Maren felt anger loosen in her chest, not at the magic but at her own long numbness. [585] She asked what price the light demanded, and the figure answered only remembrance. [598] So they sat together on the cold floor and watched until dawn thinned the blue to gray. [615] Tova leaned against the queen, and for the first time in years Maren felt useful rather than adorned. [633] When the last starlight faded from the stone, Maren wept without sound and without shame. [648] They descended past the ember grotto on the ridge as morning lit the pines below. [663] The town did not notice their return, and Maren preferred it that way. [676] She abolished the seer tax, opened the granaries, and taught Tova to read maps by candlelight. [692] She never dreamed of bleeding starlight again, but she kept a shard of black glass by her bed. [710] When doubt returned, she held it until it warmed, and remembered how to climb toward light.
