<story>I wear the saffron veil because historians must not weep where the dead can listen. [15] The lost civilization archive sleeps beneath the salt plain, a vault of clay tablets and glass seeds that hold stolen voices. [36] I was sent to catalog the lower galleries across the end of innocence, when the city still trusted memory more than stone. [58] My masters forbade the sealed strata, yet I burned to pursue forbidden knowledge like a root seeks water. [76] At its heart stands a bronze prism pagoda at twilight, a tiered tower of dark bronze and cut glass. [95] Each evening the shaft from the surface well strikes it, and the light turns steadily unpredictable, flaring and failing. [114] The elders say the tower can purify corrupted records, burning lies while sparing true sorrow. [129] I doubted them until I found my brother Tomas named on a nursery seed. [143] He died when the cisterns failed, when I was twelve and he was seven. [157] The seed held what the index called atoms of nostalgia, bright motes that smelled of rain on hot stone. [176] To open it was forbidden, and the ban filled me with anguished joy, grief edged with hope. [193] For three twilights I studied the blind stone sentinels that guard the sealed strata. [207] I moved by avoiding patterns, never using the same stair twice or pausing at the same hour. [224] On the fourth evening I slipped to the pagoda base with the seed hidden in my sleeve. [241] The bronze warmed under my palm and the seed split with a sound like a breaking bell. [258] Dust rose into a kitchen where Tomas laughed with milk on his chin while mother mended nets. [275] The vision lived, rebuilt from atoms of nostalgia that clung to his touch and refused to scatter. [292] I understood why such openings were banned, for each memory takes payment in breath. [306] My veil slipped and saffron cloth pooled at my knees as I watched him chase lizards across a lost roof. [326] Above me the pagoda flared, seeking rot to purify with patient fire. [338] I feared it would burn Tomas as falsehood, because joy feels false after long loss. [353] Instead the light passed through him and struck the black mold strangling the shelves, the flattery that rewrites true names. [373] The archive was dying, choked by praise for kings, and only humble grief could cleanse it. [389] The sentinels woke at the scent of open memory and ground their gears toward me. [404] I lifted the seed higher and let more atoms of nostalgia spill into the twilight beam. [420] The air filled with markets and lullabies and quarrels over bread, all the moments kings had ordered erased. [438] As a saffron-veil historian trained to keep chronicles, I now chose to keep kitchen laughter. [453] The pagoda drank that ordinary flood and cast prisms that cut mold from tablet and glass. [469] The light touched my skin and began to purify me, drawing out my obedient lies. [484] I laughed through tears in anguished joy as dust burned clean upon my face. [498] The sentinels halted, baffled by avoiding patterns in my earlier steps, unable to track a steadily unpredictable heart. [516] I stepped left when they expected stillness and knelt when they expected flight. [529] Tomas turned and looked at me, no longer merely seven but aware of the woman I became. [546] He spoke my name without reproach, and the sound made the end of innocence bearable. [561] I whispered I had come to pursue forbidden knowledge and found him waiting inside it like rain in a seed. [581] Twilight failed and the pagoda rang once, sealing cleansed tablets with bronze fire. [594] Darkness settled on the lost civilization archive, but the shelves glowed with honest memory. [608] The sentinels bowed and withdrew, for one purified by grief is returning, not trespassing. [622] I rewound my veil and set the empty seed at the tower foot as offering. [637] Tomorrow the order will question the shining galleries, and I will answer with steadily unpredictable truth, by avoiding patterns of denial. [658] Nostalgia is not weakness but matter, atoms from which we rebuild a city worthy of children. [674]</story>
