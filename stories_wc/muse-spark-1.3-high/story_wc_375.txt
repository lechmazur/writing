<story>Mara had been a charcoal-fingered portrait sketch artist for eleven years on the gray ferry docks. [16] She was mnemonically gifted, able to recall every face she had ever drawn with painful precision. [32] Yet commissions demanded flattering smiles, and she learned to uncork suppressed creativity only in secret midnight sketches. [49] The habit became one of those inescapable callings that rang louder each time she tried to ignore it. [67] When a caving tour descended into the northern hills, she followed with her pad strapped tight against her ribs. [86] The guide joked about echoes, and the tourists laughed until the walls threw their voices back. [102] Then, after the last echo of laughter fades, Mara slipped away from the lantern light into a side passage. [121] Her headlamp found a subterranean crystal cave where violet and green points bloomed like frozen fireworks. [137] In the center stood an old iron locker, its door secured by a broken combination lock hanging open. [155] Someone had tried to rectify a theft long ago, for scratches around the dial spelled numbers in desperate strokes. [174] Inside, wrapped in oilcloth, lay a painting with something behind it bulging against the canvas. [189] The painting showed a miner girl with solemn eyes, rendered in clumsy but tender brushwork. [204] Mara lifted the frame with trembling hands, feeling an auroral sorrow wash over her like dawn through ice. [222] The crystals caught her lamp and threw rose and gold ribbons across the dark, beautiful and mournful at once. [241] Carefully she peeled back the canvas tacks and discovered a packet of charcoal portraits tied with twine. [258] Each face stared out with unflinching honesty, wrinkles and scars and missing teeth preserved without shame. [274] A folded note in a careful hand read, Forgive me for hiding what I was told to forget. [292] The miner artist had drawn coworkers the company wished to erase after a collapse killed eleven men. [309] Management had ordered cheerful landscapes instead, and he had locked his truth underground. [322] Mara recognized her own bargain instantly, the years of smoothing noses and whitening smiles for tips. [338] Her throat tightened, for she too had buried difficult faces to keep customers comfortable. [352] She spread the hidden portraits in a circle on the crystal floor and studied them one by one. [370] Because she was mnemonically gifted in a different way now, grief sharpened rather than dulled her memory. [387] She memorized each line, each shadow, letting the dead men imprint themselves behind her eyes. [402] Then she opened her own pad and began to copy them, her charcoal fingers black to the wrist. [420] The work was not theft but witness, a promise to carry their likenesses back into daylight. [436] Cold seeped through her knees, and water ticked from the crystals like a slow clock. [451] She worked until her lamp dimmed, until her hand cramped around the familiar stick. [465] When she finished, she placed her fresh copies beside the originals and rewrapped the old packet tenderly. [482] She left the painting propped against the locker so future wanderers would find the miner girl first. [499] Climbing back toward the voices, she felt the cave release her with reluctant affection. [513] On the ferry docks the next morning, she pinned the miners faces in a row above her stall. [531] Customers expecting quick caricatures stopped, unsettled by the stern and sorrowful gazes. [543] She drew them truly now, every wart and worry line, refusing to sweeten sorrow for sale. [559] Some walked away, but others wept and paid double, saying nobody had ever looked so closely. [575] Each evening she returned to the cave in memory, and the crystals lit her dreams with quiet dawns. [593] She no longer feared the calling that had pulled her underground, for it had taught her how to see. [612] To rectify the past, she would draw the living and the dead with the same unflinching love. [629] And the auroral sorrow remained, a soft light that made every honest face glow. [643]</story>
