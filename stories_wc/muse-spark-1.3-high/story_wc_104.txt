<story>Silas had not seen the sky in three years except through the cracked dome of the abandoned carnival prize redemption center. [21] They kept him chained to the old prize counter because they called him a chained liberator who freed seeds instead of people. [43] He did not argue because he was patiently strategic and knew anger would only tighten the locks. [60] The regime had outlawed heirloom seeds after building the wall to control food and memory alike. [76] Silas had answered by building a clandestine seed vault beneath the floorboards of the redemption booths. [92] His purpose was simple and stubborn, to plant resilience in dry soil when no one else dared. [109] The guards thought the shelves of stuffed bears and plastic trophies were only rot and childhood trash. [126] They did not know each hollow bear held packets of sorghum and desert beans sealed in wax. [143] Mara came twice a week with cleaning water and whispered news through the clink of her bucket. [160] She had been a piano teacher before music was restricted to state anthems and approved marches. [176] She told Silas about the piano bench in the ruined bandstand where someone had left old keys in a strange order. [197] Silas understood at once that the Burners meant to impede the last growers by decoding the same pattern first. [216] He had to impede their search without leaving his chain or raising the alarm in the hall. [233] He asked Mara to bring him the arrow with strange fletching that hung above the duck shoot gallery. [251] No one else had noticed that its shaft was hollow and its fletching was cut from old seed catalogs. [270] For three nights he worked by the buzzing carnival lights that never fully died. [284] He scraped the catalog feathers flat and read the tiny varietal names printed along each barb. [300] Those names gave him the order for the keys, and he memorized it by decoding the arrangement of keys left in a piano bench as Mara described it. [328] The low C key meant tepary beans and the chipped E meant drought corn from his own farm. [346] Each key matched a drawer in the vault and a planting depth for the dry fields beyond the wall. [365] He wrote nothing down, but tapped the sequence into the counter until his fingers bled. [380] Mara learned the tapping song and carried it out in her mop rhythm so no microphone would catch words. [399] The Burners searched the midway with dogs and scanners while the vault breathed quietly below them. [415] When they shook Silas awake, he smiled with staggering grace and offered them dust from empty prize cups. [433] They called it surrender, but he was counting days until the rains and the rumors of cracks in concrete. [452] He taught Mara how to pack seeds into the hollow arrow shaft for the crossing. [467] She would shoot it over the checkpoint garden where her cousin tended a hidden plot. [482] The arrow with strange fletching would look like carnival junk to any patrol who found it fallen. [499] On the night the sirens changed pitch, Mara ran barefoot across broken tiles to his counter. [515] She whispered that the northern checkpoint was empty and people were climbing with children on their shoulders. [532] Silas knew at once that this was the hour they had waited for, when the wall comes down. [550] He gave her the last combination by humming the key order while she pretended to sweep. [566] She knelt and turned the rusted prize dials below the floor in the sequence he sang. [582] The vault sighed open, revealing jars of amaranth and melon and cotton glowing in lamplight. [597] Together they filled sacks while plaster fell from the dome and distant cheering shook the midway. [613] The chain cut his ankle as he stood for the first time to lift a sack to her back. [632] He did not follow because his leg was ruined and the dogs were already loose in the streets. [650] He told her to go and impede any pursuer only by scattering handfuls of seed behind her. [667] The Burners would slip and hesitate, unwilling to crush what they could later steal and claim. [683] Mara vanished into dust with the arrow with strange fletching nocked loosely in a scavenged bow. [699] Silas sat back against the counter and listened to concrete breaking like distant carnival drums. [714] By morning the center was empty except for feathers of catalog paper and opened jars. [729] People said the desert bloomed along the fallen wall in straight lines. [741] They found corn where no corn should grow and beans in cracked asphalt. [754] No one found Silas, but his empty chain hung from the counter in a perfect circle. [770] Mara plants the same crops every spring and teaches children the song. [782] She says he waits in every sprout that breaks hard ground with staggering grace. [796]</story>
