<story>Mara Kessler earned her bread as a natural history illustrator, painting moths and drowned birds for museums that no longer believed in living things. [24] Around her throat she wore a dull copper infinity symbol pendant, her grandmother's promise that no line ever truly ended. [44] When the Academy offered coin for a map to find the place where echoes are born, she volunteered before the old men could laugh. [68] Everyone knew echoes began somewhere above the city, in the rooftop canal system weaving between towers where barges carried rainwater and rumors. [90] The only safe crossing was across a giant's nap, the shallow sleep of old Hush who lay curled beneath the lower districts with rivers in his lungs. [117] While Hush slept, his breath pulled the canals level and slow, and while he woke, the water stood up and remembered being a flood. [141] Mara hired a pole boy named Tin to push her sketch skiff through the high water, paying him by borrowing time from her own future sleep. [167] She had learned the trick from her grandmother, who sculpts time itself, or so Tin claimed, by drawing each second until it lengthened and held. [192] On the first bridge they passed a brass cage where the Tower Wardens kept a storm wren, its throat bound to warn of waking. [216] Free it and we drown, Tin whispered, but Mara knew she would have to uncage the bird before the end. [236] The canals narrowed between towers grown thick with moss and antennae, and every oar stroke returned twice as sound. [255] This is where echoes are born, Tin said, but Mara shook her head and pressed her pencil to paper, listening for older water. [278] She drew the wren from memory, each feather a careful study, and felt the minute stretch the way warm wax stretches, slow and willing. [302] Tin poled in rhythm to her breathing, and soon he began to hum the ferryman's lullaby that kept Hush dreaming. [322] The song climbed toward rest the way all lullabies do, toward the soft chord that tells a sleeper to stay under. [343] But at the central lock, where seven canals met in a star, the humming faltered against a wall of returning sound. [364] Every note they made came back doubled and tripled, crowding the air until the lullaby could not land. [382] If he finishes the song cleanly, the echo will finish it louder and Hush will hear his own name in it and wake. [405] Mara understood then that only an interrupted cadence could pass, an ending that refused to end, turning aside just before home. [426] She touched her infinity symbol pendant for luck and whispered to Tin to sing the last phrase wrong on purpose. [446] He stared at her, terrified of bad music, but she showed him her sketchbook where every finished line looped back into another beginning. [469] He sang, his voice cracking on the climb, and instead of resolving to the tonic he lifted to the strange sixth chord that hung like fog. [495] The echoes chasing them tried to resolve it for him, tangling and thinning as they reached for a home that never came. [517] The canals went glassy and silent, and in that withheld breath Mara heard it, a hollow below all sound where new echoes drew their first air. [543] It rose from the storm wren cage, which pulsed with each stolen note, fattening on music it never released. [562] The Wardens had not caged a warning, she realized, but a mother, and every echo in the towers was her caged children calling out. [586] Mara set down her pencil, lifted the brass latch, and chose to uncage the small trembling bird despite Tin's cry. [606] The wren burst upward, and Hush beneath the city inhaled sharply in his nap, tilting the canals toward waking. [625] Tin seized his pole and Mara seized her borrowed minutes, sketching furiously to stretch the second before the giant turned. [645] She drew water, tower, boy, bird, and her own shaking hand, and each true line sculpted time a little longer, holding the breath. [668] The wren sang one clear phrase and then, wise as any ferryman, broke it with an interrupted cadence of her own. [689] Her song refused to close, looping outward like the pendant at Mara's throat, teaching every echo how to be born without ending. [711] Hush sighed and settled deeper, the canals easing flat, and Tin laughed in relief as the skiff slid toward the star lock's far gate. [735] Afterward Mara did not map the place where echoes are born, for maps end and this did not. [753] Instead she painted a natural history of a moment that never resolved, a bird in flight above still water between high towers, and left the final feather unfinished. [781]</story>
