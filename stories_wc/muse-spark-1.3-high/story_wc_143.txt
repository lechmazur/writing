<story>Harlan Webb kept his post on the cold rock through July of nineteen sixty nine. [15] Hired as a crater inspector for the survey mapping the Moon for Apollo, he knew his work would soon end. [35] During the space age every observatory ridge had become a waiting room for launch day. [50] His assignment was simple and strange, to photograph the last of something precious before men touched it. [67] The untouched floor of the Sea of Tranquillity still held shadows no boot had broken. [82] From the moonlit summit above Flagstaff he could study it with a clarity the desert air allowed. [99] He was purposefully curious, the kind of man who logged every rill and asked why each wall slumped. [117] In his coat pocket he carried a small brass locket holding a single nightwatch's hourglass sand grain. [134] His father had walked a factory through the war years, turning the glass each hour till it cracked one winter. [154] Harlan had kept one grain as a promise that time could be held and examined. [169] Radio silence was ordered after midnight, so he and his young assistant Mara spoke by chemistry. [185] They communicated using chemical reactions for timed messages, iodine flasks that bloomed blue when an exposure should begin or end. [205] Mara mixed the solutions below the dome while Harlan called the crater names softly into the dark. [222] The telescope breathed cold, and the photographic plates waited in their black sleeves like undeveloped memory. [238] Clouds had ruined eleven nights, and Houston wanted the final mosaic before the countdown rehearsal. [253] Harlan needed to outlast the weather, the fatigue, and the slow creep of dawn in the east. [270] At two in the morning the sky cleared, and Tranquillity hung sharp as etched glass. [285] Mara set three flasks in a row, each timed to turn at seven minute intervals for the long exposure series. [305] When the first flask flushed blue, Harlan opened the shutter and held his breath against the eyepiece chill. [323] He thought of how many mornings his father had watched sand fall and had believed vigilance could keep things whole. [343] The second flask turned, and he shifted the plate, his fingers numb but exact from years of practice. [361] Mara watched the ridge line, where coyotes called and the pines stood black against the silver sky. [378] She had asked once why map what would soon be walked on and photographed by other hands. [395] He had answered that maps made by care would guide boots more gently than maps made in haste. [413] The third flask was slow, its solution pale, holding back its color as if reluctant to speak. [430] Harlan tapped the brass locket once for luck and felt the weight of that single grain like an anchor. [449] Wind moved over the summit, thinning the last haze until the craterlets inside the sea stood clear. [466] He exposed the final plate as the eastern sky paled, knowing Apollo would render his vigil obsolete within days. [485] A thin line of rose touched the dome, and the stars began to withdraw one by one. [502] Then came the dawning hush, that brief quiet when night instruments tick cool and birds have not yet decided to sing. [523] Harlan closed the shutter and felt a dawnlit melancholy settle, sweet and heavy as developer on paper. [540] The Moon was still there, paling above the pines, already less his and more a shared future. [557] Mara climbed to collect the plates, her hands shaking from cold and from understanding what they held. [574] Together they developed the first sheet in the darkroom hut as daylight strengthened outside. [588] The image emerged slowly, seas and craters rising out of gray like islands out of fog. [604] Harlan saw the tiny pits near the landing ellipse, perfect and undisturbed, and his throat tightened. [620] He had caught the last quiet face, and soon footprints would write a new geology over the old. [638] Mara touched his sleeve and said the work would outlast them both, even if no one remembered their names. [657] He nodded, slipped the locket back into his pocket, and left the dome to face the ordinary morning. [675] Below, the desert woke, and above, the Moon faded, both kept for a little longer by his care. [693]</story>
