<story>Tomas Hale had been the reticent lighthouse supply skipper for eleven winters, speaking more to gulls than to people. [19] He was warmly observant in the way of quiet men, noticing who left an extra biscuit for the keeper's dog and who mended coats by lamplight. [45] Every Tuesday he rowed provisions to Gull Rock, coconut oil and flour and kerosene and news he never quite delivered. [65] The lighthouse itself was decommissioned, but Keeper Maren stayed on as caretaker, claiming the haunted lantern room kept her late husband's voice alive. [88] Sailors swore that after the lamp flickers out each evening at nine, footsteps paced the glass and someone hummed a low shanty. [110] Tomas did not believe in ghosts, but he believed in loneliness, which felt haunt enough. [125] That spring he found a piece of sea glass on the supply beach, cobalt blue and smoothed to a soft jewel. [146] He meant to pocket it and forget it, yet that night he held it to his lamp and felt a luminescent ache he could not name. [172] It was grief for his drowned brother and love for the living all mixed, bright and painful. [189] Wanting to bottle the feeling of safety his mother once gave in warm soup and dry socks, he did something uncharacteristic. [210] He wrote an unsigned note on a scrap of chart paper, You are seen and the sea is not only taking, and left it with the glass at the jetty cairn. [241] He used a way of stacking stones as messages that his brother had taught him, two flat and one round to mean I am thinking of you. [268] Maren found the glass and note on her morning walk and wept, because no one had noticed her mourning. [287] She answered by adding her own letter beneath a new stack, three tall stones for carry on, I am here. [307] She wrote of sleepless nights in the haunted lantern room and how she polished brass to hear an echo like company. [328] Tomas read it on his next run and felt startled to be trusted, so he replied and protected his identity with block capitals and his left hand. [355] To protect the fragile exchange from gossip on the mainland, they never signed or spoke of it aloud. [373] Slowly the cairn became an anonymous letter chain, joined by others who learned the code. [388] Old Jonah the netmender left a chipped cup handle and a note about his son in prison, stacked under a cross of pebbles meaning do not give up. [416] Widow Alva left dried lavender and admitted she feared the dark stairwell even after sixty years on the island. [435] A teenage deckhand named Siff left a rusted hook and confessed he could not swim, asking if cowardice could be forgiven. [456] Each writer was warmly observant of the last, answering with specific kindness rather than empty comfort. [472] Tomas ferried paper and pencils without comment, his silence becoming a kind of shelter. [486] He learned to protect each letter from rain with oilskin squares and weighted them with quartz. [502] In late August a gale knocked the temporary beacon loose, and the island went truly dark for three nights. [521] Frightened voices gathered at the jetty, and someone suggested abandoning the cairn as childish nonsense in a crisis. [539] Tomas surprised everyone by speaking at length, his voice rough from disuse, saying the stones were navigation marks for the heart. [560] On the second dark night he climbed to the haunted lantern room with extra blankets and the piece of sea glass now returned by Maren. [585] After the lamp flickers out, or rather after the emergency light sputtered and died, the room filled with cold and the old humming began. [609] He did not run but set out blankets and read aloud the stacked letters, letting each voice protect the others from despair. [631] Maren lit a candle and admitted the humming was her own, a recording of her husband she played to bottle the feeling of safety. [655] She had been ashamed of the trick, fearing people would call her mad and leave her truly alone. [673] Tomas placed the cobalt glass in her palm and told her his name for the first time, breaking his long reticence to show she was known. [699] By candlelight they expanded the anonymous letter chain, promising company to anyone afraid without need for explanation. [716] When the beacon was repaired, islanders still climbed to the haunted lantern room to share stories and tea. [734] Tomas remained quiet, yet warmly observant, noticing trembling hands and offering warmth before being asked. [749] He kept stacking stones as messages, each small tower glowing with luminescent ache turned to signal. [765] Visitors still find sea glass and folded notes among the cairns, feeling safe and strangely missed. [781]</story>
