<story>After the carousel halts, the station stops pretending to be a world. [12] Mara Vess had been a resolute scavenger for eleven rusted years. [23] She worked the kinetic lagoon above the clouds, where wreckage never rested. [35] Old shuttles and solar sails drifted in slow collisions, ringing softly in the thin air. [50] It was a place of fractured stillness, motion without progress and silence full of echoes. [65] Mara was chronologically confused since the accident, mixing launch day with yesterday. [77] Sometimes she called the lagoon by its construction name, sometimes by the name her mother gave it. [94] She did not mind the confusion, because it let her keep working. [106] Her task was simple and impossible, to negotiate with one's own ghost. [118] Three years ago, during a spin shift, she had cut her tether to save the carousel. [134] The carousel survived, but her younger self, bright with plans, did not. [146] Or rather, that self lingered as a voice on dead channels, repeating takeoff checklists. [160] Mara felt clean regret each time she heard it, sharp as a new weld. [174] She hoped to answer it properly one day, and be forgiven. [185] She prepared by collecting fragments of overheard stories from the drifting wrecks. [197] She patched into suit radios left on, galley recorders, and children's lessons looping in escape pods. [213] Each fragment taught her how other people had carried their eroded ambitions. [225] A pilot who wanted to map storms now fished metal with a magnet. [238] A botanist who dreamed of forests now grew mold in a helmet. [250] Mara logged them all in a paper notebook, because digital memory failed in the lagoon static. [266] On the last salvage run, she found a plastic disposable camera wedged in a coolant pipe. [282] Its disposable camera wheel still turned, stiff but willing, clicking past ruined exposures. [295] Mara kept it, because it could keep images when her mind could not keep order. [310] That evening the lagoon turned amber, and the wrecks knocked together like slow bells. [324] Mara anchored her skiff to a gutted ferry and listened for her ghost. [337] The radio hissed, then a young voice said, Ferry Kite requesting departure vector. [350] It was her own voice at nineteen, bright and certain that the colonies needed her. [365] Mara answered, her throat dry, This is Mara Vess, holding position. [376] She expected silence, but the ghost replied with a laugh, asking why she sounded old. [391] Mara told the story of the cut tether, the halted spin, and the years of salvage. [407] She confessed she had traded flight for maintenance, and wonder for routine. [419] The ghost was quiet, and Mara wound the disposable camera wheel to calm her hands. [434] She took one photograph of the amber lagoon, though the film was surely dead. [448] The click steadied her, a small proof that time could still move forward. [461] Then she offered the ghost the fragments she had gathered, one by one. [474] She repeated the pilot's joke about magnets, and the botanist's pride in helmet mold. [488] She said, We all lose altitude, but we learn to float in what remains. [502] The young voice asked if Mara regretted cutting the tether that day. [514] Mara looked at her scarred gloves and said yes, and also no, and both were true. [530] She regretted losing the girl who believed in straight trajectories, and honored the woman who learned to drift. [548] The radio warmed, and for a moment past and future sat together without fighting. [562] The ghost said, Then keep scavenging, and remember me kindly. [572] Mara promised she would, and felt the fractured stillness settle into peace. [584] Afterward she developed nothing, because the camera held no film, only the will to record. [599] She turned her skiff home, carrying stories heavier and kinder than metal. [611]</story>
