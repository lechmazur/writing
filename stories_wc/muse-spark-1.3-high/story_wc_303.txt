<story>Mara Venn had spent eleven years as a provenance researcher for the Meridian Empire, tracing stolen instruments to their true makers. [21] She was summoned to the velvet observatory at midsummer above Vela Harbor, where the dome was lined in deep purple cloth to soften the starlight. [46] On a stand of black oak lay the disputed object, a transparent violin made of ice crystal that had not melted in sixty years. [70] She was ordered to weigh a dream against an empire and give the Empire its legal claim. [87] Her private dream was simpler, to return unclaimed music to the villages it was taken from, before the promise loses its glow. [109] As a child she had promised her grandmother she would do such work, and the memory still shone but felt thinner each year. [132] The curator left her alone with cold tea and a file of forged bills, and she drank the tea to steady her hands. [155] When she tilted the cup she learned her path by reading the future in coffee stains, for dark grounds clung to the porcelain in branching lines. [181] In one stain the violin shattered under a seal stamp, and in another it sang on a snowy porch while children listened. [203] A third stain showed her perforated destiny as a line of tiny holes she could tear or mend. [221] Shaken, she set down the cup and heard the observatory ivy sighing against the velvet walls, thin and green and worried. [242] Since a fever at nineteen she had been accidentally telepathic with plants, catching their slow thoughts without wanting them. [261] The ivy remembered workmen who carried glacier moss up the hill in salt barrels, and women who hummed while they worked. [282] The wall moss added its colder memory, of a northern luthier named Aune who grew the violin instead of carving it, coaxing ice around a living frame. [309] It was never imperial, the plants insisted, but a communal instrument meant to hold winter sound through summer hunger. [328] Mara touched the violin and felt sorrow rise in her like mulberry ache, sweet and dark and staining, for beauty kept prisoner. [350] The file claimed a flawless imperial origin, yet the oak stand whispered of swapped papers and a bribed harbor clerk. [370] If she signed, the Empire would lock the violin in a vault and her career would be secure forever. [389] If she refused, she would lose her post and the violin would likely be seized anyway, melting unwatched without its moss. [410] The violin survived only because living frost moss breathed cold across its ribs, and the moss in the stand was dying. [431] The plants begged her to propagate the moss before it failed, to press green threads into new soil and keep the cold alive. [454] Mara found shallow trays under the telescope, filled them with damp velvet scrapings and shade, and laid the brightest strands with tweezers. [476] As she worked the ivy taught her the old watering song Aune had used, a low tune that slowed evaporation. [496] Outside the dome the midsummer sky stayed pale blue past midnight, and harbor bells counted ships that carried imperial orders. [516] Mara rinsed her cup and poured fresh coffee, then studied the new stains to test whether mercy could survive reporting truth. [537] The stains showed a hard road north with the trays wrapped in her coat, and the violin humming faintly against her back. [559] They also showed arrest and disgrace, but further on a village hall where the violin voiced winter again and children learned the watering song. [583] Her perforated destiny trembled, each hole a small escape, each tear a choice that could not be untorn. [601] She wrote two reports, one true for the hidden archive and one false with careful doubt that would delay the vault order. [623] She sealed moss samples and Aune's name inside the true report, so future researchers would know the violin was grown not seized. [645] Before dawn she lifted the violin into a padded case, tucked the new trays around it, and walked past the sleeping guard toward the north road. [671] The ivy brushed her sleeve in farewell, and the mulberry ache in her chest ripened into resolve, bitter and tender and chosen. [693] She did not know if the promise would hold, but she moved while it still glowed, carrying winter toward those who remembered its song. [717]</story>
