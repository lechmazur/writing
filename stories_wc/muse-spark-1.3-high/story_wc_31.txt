<story>Tomas Reyes was the late-shift pier lamplighter at Brindle Harbor. [10] Every night he walked the long planks with oil and matches, coaxing yellow light from iron lamps. [27] He had taken the job to discover the color of sound after his sister, a singer, drowned the previous winter. [47] She used to say high C was pale blue, and he needed to prove her right. [63] The town held its remembrance during a sky lantern release each August, when families sent paper stars over black water. [83] That night Tomas lit each lamp early, for the crowd would need steady light against the moving dark. [101] A hush lay over the harbor, a meteor hush as if the sky itself held its breath. [118] While freeing a tangled rope near piling nine, his fingers closed on a hotel skeleton key tag. [135] The brass was green with age, stamped SEABORN HOUSE ROOM TWELVE. [146] Seaborn House had burned thirty years ago, yet its wrecked annex barge lay sunk beyond the breakwater with all its fittings. [167] Old divers whispered of a forgotten library inside a sunken ship, sealed in oilskin and silence. [183] As lanterns rose, Tomas heard humming rise from beneath the pier, low and amber at the edges. [200] He leaned over and saw light below, not reflection but a steady glow pulsing with the song. [217] Curiosity steadied him more than duty, so he locked his oil cart and followed the sound down the service ladder. [237] The tide was out, exposing a ribbed hull half buried in sand, its portholes shining like drowned lanterns. [255] The key tag grew warm in his palm, as if it recognized home. [268] He fitted his boots through a split in the hull and entered the forgotten library inside a sunken ship. [287] Shelves rose in curved ribs, books wrapped in waxed cloth, aisles ankle deep in cold clear water. [304] Small glass floats hung from the ceiling and burned with slow phosphorescence, lighting ink without heat. [320] Tomas moved by wandering purposefully, letting the humming guide his feet between the stacks. [334] Each step changed the tone, and he began to see edges of color trembling at the rim of hearing. [353] In the stern alcove he found a ledger open to pages covered in luminous vows. [368] They were promises written by sailors and wives, inked with ground lantern ash that glowed when sung to. [386] He touched a line that read I will listen until you return, and the words brightened gold. [403] The humming deepened, and for an instant he saw the sound as a broad ribbon of deep amber veined with blue. [424] Tears stung his eyes, for here was the proof his sister had wanted. [437] A voice behind him said You should not be here, and he turned to find Mara, the harbormaster, holding a lantern. [458] She was a woman who hides behind truth, speaking plain facts to avoid her own grief. [474] This barge was my mothers floating library, she said, and those vows were her attempt to signify that love outlasts wreckage. [495] Tomas held up the hotel skeleton key tag and asked if Room Twelve had kept the music books. [513] Mara nodded and unlocked a glass cabinet with a duplicate key, revealing damp songbooks and a brass tuning fork. [532] Strike it, she told him, and let the room answer. [542] He struck the fork against the shelf, and the library sang back in layered light. [557] Low notes bloomed green as kelp, high notes stitched silver across the water ceiling. [571] Above them, through cracked timbers, they could see new sky lanterns rising to join the release. [587] Mara sang her mothers vow aloud, and the shelves pulsed in time, turning sound to color and color to memory. [607] Tomas understood then that his sister had been right, and that grief could be tuned like an instrument. [625] He promised to tend the wreck as he tended his lamps, keeping its light alive for others who listened. [644] When they climbed back to the pier, the lanterns were distant embers and his lamps still burned steady. [662] He logged the night in his book, noting that sound was amber and blue, and signed his name beneath Mara's. [682]</story>
