<story>After the tower falls dust settles over the valley in soft red curtains. [13] Wren had always been known as the bounty hunter's pacifist daughter and she carried no gun. [29] While her father tracked men for money across the dunes she learned the language of roots and thorns. [47] Now she keeps a desert rose farm in a glass-domed enclosure on the edge of the wreck field. [65] The dome holds heat like a held breath and the air inside burns with a tranquil blaze. [82] Outside the wind moves scrap and bone but inside there is only work and water. [97] Wren lost her sight to fever as a child yet she remains senselessly perceptive in the rows. [114] She reads moisture by smell and hears aphids chewing before any sighted worker sees damage. [129] Her father returns once a season with blood on his boots and stories he does not tell. [146] He mocks her faith in inculcating plants with values as if roses could learn mercy. [161] She answers by pressing his rough hand to a bud and waiting until he feels the pulse. [178] To weave continuity through chaos she saves seed from every bloom that survives the heat. [193] She labels each jar with the name of a fallen town so nothing is forgotten. [208] The tower had guided airships before its collapse and its shadow once cooled her dome at noon. [225] After the tower falls no ships come except broken hulls dragged by scavengers for parts. [240] Wren trades roses for water and repairs the dome with an airship ballast sandbag scrap. [255] She stitches the heavy canvas over a cracked pane and plants beans along its shade. [270] The scrap smells of oil and high cold air and rain from other places. [284] Her father brings her a knife taken from a prisoner and tells her to keep it close. [301] The blade carries an inscription and in the metal she finds an engraved quiet. [315] She runs her fingers over the tiny letters that spell hold and breathe and stay. [330] She understands the maker once wanted stillness more than victory. [340] Raiders come at dusk seeking water and they kick open the outer door. [353] Her father rises with his rifle but Wren steps between him and the hungry men. [368] She does not lift the knife to strike but holds it flat so all can see. [384] She speaks of trade and seed and thirst and offers roses for peace. [397] One raider laughs and swings a pipe toward her shoulder. [407] Her father shouts but she sidesteps because she heard his breath change first. [420] She turns the blade and will not draw blood but will sheathe it slow and loud. [436] The click of metal entering leather fills the dome like a promise kept. [449] The sound steadies the men more than threats ever could. [459] Her father lowers his rifle because he sees his daughter leading without killing. [472] The raiders drink and leave beans and wire in payment for three red roses. [486] Later her father sits among the rows and asks why she refused to fight. [500] She places his hand on warm soil and says killing breaks the thread she mends. [515] He weeps without noise and leaves his rifle outside the door that night. [528] Under twin moons the dome glows amber and the roses open wider. [540] Wren walks the rows touching each bloom and whispering patience and courage and care. [554] The plants lean toward her voice as if they remember every kindness. [566] In the morning her father helps patch the door and learns the names of seeds. [581] The desert keeps burning beyond the glass but inside they build a gentle order. [595] And the farm endures because someone chose tending over hunting. [605]</story>
