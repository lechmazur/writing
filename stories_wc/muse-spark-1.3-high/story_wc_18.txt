<story>Ena was a mirror maker who kept her workshop firmly grounded in the cellar of a fallen cider house. [19] Above her lay the ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog, where apple trees stood like blackened ribs and no star had been seen for a hundred years. [49] Upon the shimmer of twilight frost, when the fog turned pale blue and every branch wore ice, she carried her last clear mirror up the broken lane. [76] She did not go to sell it, but to capture a runaway dream that had slipped from her sleeping son three nights before. [99] The boy Tomas had dreamed of the orchard in blossom, and the dream had grown too bright for his chest and fled into the fog. [124] Since then he would not wake fully and would not sleep deeply, caught between worlds while his breath grew thin. [144] Ena understood mirrors, and she knew a true mirror does not reflect but keeps. [158] In her satchel she carried the astronomer's broken sextant, left by her grandfather when the sky closed and the observatory fell. [179] Its mirrors were cracked and its arm was bent, yet it could still catch a slant of light and hold it steady. [201] She meant to use it not for stars but for finding the path where the dream had run. [219] At the heart of the district the trees opened around a frozen well, and there the air rang faint and sharp as glass mountains in the distance of sleep. [248] The dream waited there, a small bright shape like a blossom petal refusing to fall. [263] It trembled each time the fog moved, and Ena feared her own hands would scatter it. [279] She set her mirror against the stones and knelt on frozen roots while her heart lifted with hope and fear. [299] Come back, she whispered, I have not come to break you. [310] The dream pulsed and drifted toward the orchard rows, longing for trees that no longer bore. [326] Ena realized she could not force it, for dreams obey only invitation, and mirrors obey only patience. [343] She lifted the astronomer's broken sextant and angled its cracked glass to gather the frost light into one thin beam. [363] The beam touched the mirror and the mirror touched the dream, linking them in a quiet triangle. [380] Slowly she began to breathe, and she asked the dream to breathe with her through synchronized breathing that her grandfather had taught for polishing glass without shaking. [407] In and out they moved together, her chest rising as its light widened, her chest falling as its light narrowed. [427] With each shared breath the boundary between waking and dreaming grew thinner, erasing boundaries that had kept mother and son and tree and ghost apart. [452] Fog curled closer to listen, and frost brightened on the bark like tiny answering stars. [467] Ena did not chase the dream but offered it a place, and she began to recreate the lost spring inside the mirror from memory and breath. [493] She pictured white blossoms and bees and warm rain, and the mirror fogged then cleared with each exhale. [511] The dream leaned nearer, recognizing its home, and touched the glass with trembling light. [525] The surface shivered like thin ice on high peaks, cold and clear and singing, and Ena held steady. [543] She sealed the light with one final slow breath, and the mirror accepted it without cracking. [559] Silence fell, deeper than fog, and then the orchard seemed to exhale around her. [573] She wrapped the mirror in wool and hurried home, guided by the sextant glinting faintly through the dark. [591] Tomas lay pale beneath blankets, his breath shallow and uneven, and she set the mirror beside his pillow so its blossom light fell across his face. [617] She matched her breathing to his until their chests rose together, and the dream slipped gently from glass to boy. [637] He sighed and smiled in sleep, and color returned to his cheeks like sunrise on snow. [653] Outside the fog thinned for the first time in memory, and a single real star pricked the frost sky. [672] Ena looked up through the broken sextant and laughed softly, knowing some borders are made to be crossed with care. [692]</story>
