<story>After the whale beaches itself on the black rocks below Lighthouse Point, Mara lights her lamps an hour early. [19] She is the dock-side lantern lighter, sixty-two and bowlegged, trusted with kerosene and keys for thirty years. [36] The town gathers with buckets and cameras, but by midnight only she remains beside the great heaving flank. [54] It breathes once more, and something cold and heavy lifts out of the water around them. [70] In the weeks that follow, fishermen speak of pockets of gravity drifting through the harbor like weather. [87] A gull freezes mid-glide, a dropped wrench falls sideways, and Mara watches her lantern flames bend toward empty air. [106] The cannery basement has been rebuilt as a salt crystal growing chamber, funded by the university and the memory clinic. [126] There, on steel racks in gleaming silence, alum strings fatten into perfect cubes day after day. [142] Mara cleans the chamber at dawn, mopping brine while the researchers sleep upstairs. [155] She took the job to find where deleted memories go, after her husband Tomas paid for an erasure. [173] He could not bear the memory of their son Jonah drowning, so the clinic cut that summer from his mind. [193] Now he smiles at the empty chair and asks why Mara keeps pressed wildflowers in Jonah's dictionary. [210] She cannot answer without crying, so she studies the dregs left in her morning cup. [225] Her grandmother read futures through patterns in coffee grounds, and Mara reads them for lost coordinates. [241] One Tuesday the grounds show a spiral with three teeth, exactly the shape of the whale's barnacled jaw. [259] That night the harbor gravity fluctuates mysteriously, and three lanterns along Pier Four go dark at once. [276] Mara rows out with fresh wicks and finds water pooling upward in a trembling dome near the rocks. [294] Inside the dome her oar weighs nothing, then everything, then nothing again as her heart hammers. [310] She remembers the researchers saying salt reveals what eyes cannot, because each crystal leans toward true down. [327] At dawn she carries a bucket of harbor water into the salt crystal growing chamber and seals the door. [346] In gleaming silence she sets out eleven dishes, each with string and seed, and pours the strange tide among them. [366] By noon ten dishes grow square and honest, but the eleventh sprouts sideways in a knotted white horn. [384] The horn points east, toward the old quarantine field where the town buries its unsaved dead. [400] Mara understands she must exhume what the whale left behind, not flesh but weight. [414] She waits for low tide, borrows a spade from the gravedigger, and walks past Tomas sleeping open-mouthed. [431] Through patterns in coffee grounds she has drawn a map on her palm, and the field matches its dark scatter. [451] She digs where the horn pointed, between Jonah's empty grave marker and a sailor with no name. [468] Six inches down her blade strikes metal, a clinic archive box, slick with brine and labeled for incineration. [486] Inside, sealed in wax, are glass vials etched with dates and a packet of pressed wildflowers identical to her own. [506] She lifts one vial marked with Jonah's summer, and the air around her lightens as if forgiven. [523] Tomas's laugh pours out first, then seaweed, then a small boy shouting for his mother to watch him swim. [542] The pocket releases, and memory rushes back into the world like tide returning over sand. [557] Across town Tomas wakes weeping with salt on his lips, calling Jonah's name into the morning. [573] Mara re-buries the empty box, sets the wildflowers upon the soil, and relights the pier against the coming dark. [592] The lanterns hold steady now, burning in gleaming silence while the water finds its proper weight again. [609]</story>
