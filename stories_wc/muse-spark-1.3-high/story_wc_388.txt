<story>After the liquidation of the Meridian Trust, June Park made a living as a conspiracy debunker for desert radio stations. [20] She was sharply observant in a way that made liars nervous and honest people oddly relieved. [36] She had one rule which kept her solvent, to excavate truth from comfortable lies without selling new lies. [54] That rule brought her to the Kestrel Tract, a wasteland orchard thriving amid radioactive dust storms forty miles east of Fallow. [75] Everyone said no trees should live there, yet rows of black barked apples pushed green through ash. [92] The orchard crew credited a miracle stone, a flawed diamond rumored to be cursed by its first owner. [110] June did not believe in curses, but she believed in people who needed curses to explain luck. [127] The owner, Silas Vane, had vanished after the liquidation, leaving ledgers shredded and debts unpaid. [142] His daughter Liora now ran the orchard and swore the diamond kept the storms from stripping the blossoms. [160] She wore it on a wire around her neck, and the stone flashed gray whenever dust thickened. [177] Three workers had died in the past year, and the settlement whispered that the curse was collecting. [194] Liora hired June not to prove the stone safe, but to prove the deaths were ordinary. [210] June set up in the old packing shed with respirator filters, a lamp, and bins of shredded paper. [228] Vane had ordered his records destroyed, and June began by reassembling shredded letters from his office safe. [245] Night after night she fitted strips together, matching fiber edges and faded ink like a patient puzzle. [262] The letters were to a geologist, and they described a pocket of calm air beneath the orchard rows. [280] Vane called it a hidden stillpoint where wind shear cancelled and dust fell harmless to the ground. [297] June cross checked soil maps and found the orchard sat in a shallow basin ringed by broken ridges. [315] The ridges broke the storms and let cold night air settle, holding moisture long enough for roots. [332] No miracle was needed, only topography and relentless irrigation with filtered well water. [345] Then she found the second set of letters, shorter and angrier, about money and the diamond. [361] Vane had bought the flawed stone cheap because jewelers feared its dark inclusion shaped like an eye. [378] He planned to use the curse story to keep thieves and inspectors away from his water theft. [395] After the liquidation, he had been pumping from a protected aquifer and hiding the usage in false logs. [413] The shredded letters proved he knew the pumps were poisoning two workers with solvent runoff. [428] The third death was his own, staged to look like a curse taking him during a storm. [445] June sat back with paper cuts on her fingers and began to intuit the shape of his fear. [463] He had not feared ghosts, but exposure, and the orchard workers had paid for his cover story. [480] Liora listened without moving when June laid the taped pages across the sorting table. [494] You are saying my father murdered men and blamed a rock, she said flatly. [508] June answered that comfortable stories about curses had let everyone avoid asking about water and coughing lungs. [525] Liora touched the diamond and asked if she should throw it into the dust. [539] June told her to keep it as evidence of how easily fear could be farmed like apples. [556] Together they took the letters to the settlement council, and the council ordered the wells tested. [572] Tests confirmed solvent poison, and families of the dead finally received compensation from the liquidation fund. [588] Liora kept farming, but she published the wind data and removed the guards from the gates. [604] The orchard survived its next three storms without any magic, sheltered by the quiet physics of the basin. [622] June filed her broadcast from the shed, saying the real stillpoint was a community willing to look. [639] She left the diamond on the table, flawed and ordinary, catching lamplight without malice. [653] Dust moved over the trees as she drove out, and the apples held firm in the calm center.
