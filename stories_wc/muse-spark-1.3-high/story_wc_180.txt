<story>After the breach of contract, no one locked the cathedral doors because there was nothing left worth stealing except light. [20] The parish had paid the grafters guild for a tithe orchard and then sold the land twice, and the guild walked away with their ladders. [45] Old Fen, the quartz-eye mender, stayed because his trees had already learned the way up the pillars. [62] Thirty years ago surgeons had set crushed quartz into his left socket after a grafting knife slipped, and through it he could see water moving in wood. [89] Now the nave held soil and a lush orchard spanning the rafters of an abandoned cathedral. [105] Apple and pear limbs threaded through the broken rose window, and roots combed the old hymnals into pulp. [123] Fen brought his granddaughter Posy on a Tuesday, meaning to bridge generational wisdom before his hands forgot everything. [141] She was nine and endearingly feisty, the kind of child who argued with pigeons and usually won. [158] You said cathedrals are for God, she said, kicking a fallen finial, so why does God need cider? [176] Fen laughed until his quartz eye fogged, and said God probably preferred pie. [189] He had not come to pray but to persist, to keep one mother tree alive through another winter. [207] The mother was a winter russet he had spliced for his daughter June, who had loved the orchard and then left it for the city. [232] June was gone two years now, taken by fever, and his daughter's hair ribbon still marked the trunk where she had tied it at twelve. [257] The ribbon had faded to the color of rain, but Posy touched it like a relic and stopped arguing. [276] The trellis wires had snapped under fruit weight, and Fen had brought no tools, trusting a young back and an old eye. [298] Then Posy found his forgotten bag behind the font, a canvas sack left the day the guild quit. [316] Inside were brittle twine, copper nails gone green, a bent spoon, stale altar bread, and a pocket catechism swollen with rain. [337] Using forgotten bag contents, they propped limbs with broken pew slats, tied them with twine, and sealed cuts with chewed bread paste. [359] Posy hammered copper nails with a rock to pin the ribbon higher so the wind would not worry it loose. [379] You make vows to trees, Fen said, but you must make deciduous vows, the kind that know when to fall. [399] Posy frowned and asked if that meant it was all right to break promises. [413] Fen had meant it as a joke about pruning, but accidental wisdom came out anyway, the way sap finds a crack. [434] He said no, it meant you keep the root promise and let the leaf promises drop when the season turns. [454] The guild had promised to stay forever, which was an evergreen vow in a deciduous world, and so it snapped. [474] June had promised to return each harvest, and when fever stopped her, Fen had to let that leaf go or hate the tree. [497] Posy was quiet a long time, which worried Fen more than her questions, and then she tied her own shoelace beside the ribbon. [520] For Mom, she said, so the tree knows two generations, and Fen felt his throat close like bark over a wound. [541] By dusk they had lifted three limbs back into the light shaft where the roof had caved. [558] Fen showed Posy how to read water with the quartz eye, letting her press her forehead to his temple to see the pale flow. [582] Cool, she whispered, its like the tree is drinking stars, and Fen decided that was better theology than any sermon. [602] They ate a russet each, sour and hard, and spat the seeds into the nave soil for whatever came next. [622] When the light failed, Posy read aloud from the swollen catechism in a dramatic priest voice, absolving the pears of blight. [643] Fen corrected her Latin with his mouth full, and they both laughed, and the rafters threw the sound back like leaves. [664] He knew the city might reclaim the cathedral and cut the orchard for beams, and he knew Posy might leave like June. [686] He tied those fears to the trellis with the rest of the twine and let them hang there, deciduous, for the winter to take. [710] As they left, Posy looped the bent spoon over a branch as a bell to scare starlings, accidental wisdom again in work boots. [733]</story>
