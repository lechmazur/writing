<story>The old launch field hummed with mothballed drones dreaming of orbits they would never touch again. [16] Mara had tended its archive for forty years, a lore keeper who remembered every launch by the taste of ozone on the wind. [39] She was wisely adventurous, bold enough to chase forbidden maps but patient enough to pack spare batteries and warm tea. [59] Tonight the sky port authority had announced the final purge at dawn, when all analog records would be melted for reactor fuel. [81] She had until sunrise to find the stairway of ascension, the half mythical elevator her grandmother swore still linked earth to the uncrewed ring. [105] The young techs laughed at the story, calling it futuristic nostalgia for a tomorrow that never arrived. [122] Mara did not laugh, because she had held a ruby flake pried from the hull of the first climber car. [142] The flake was no mere paint, but a storage medium layered with data dust that glowed faintly when breathed upon. [162] Under magnification its edges showed strata, red over gold over blue, each coat a decade of repainted lies. [180] The archive index whispered of a hidden orchard behind a kaleidoscopic waterfall where the original engineers used to rest. [199] At midnight she decided to sally out past the perimeter fence with her lamp, her scraper, and her grandmother's brass compass. [220] The waterfall was not water at all, but overflow from the cooling towers bent through broken prism shields. [238] Light fell in shifting sheets of green and violet, hissing softly where it struck the black sand. [255] Mara held her breath and stepped through the glare, her coat shedding droplets of color like startled fireflies. [273] Beyond lay the orchard, gnarled apple trees wired with ancient sensors, their roots cradling rusted toolboxes and prayer flags. [292] In the center stood a dead climber car, its door sealed by a century of enamel, its windows blind with dust. [313] Mara knelt and began her real work through the peel of paint layers revealing hidden murals. [329] With the scraper she lifted the outer red skin, exposing a gold fresco of workers raising a ribbon toward the stars. [350] Beneath gold lay blue, and beneath blue lay silver, each layer a mural of the stairway growing taller and stranger. [370] One showed children planting orchard seeds in zero gravity, roots curling around floating drops of water. [386] Another showed a vast ring station with a single empty chair at its heart, the unclaimed throne waiting for a guide rather than a ruler. [411] Mara understood then that ascension was never about conquest, but about stewardship remembered by lore. [426] Her hands trembled as the silver gave way to bare alloy etched with coordinates and a small handprint. [444] She pressed her own palm to the print, and the ruby flake in her pocket warmed as if recognizing kin. [464] A seam sighed open in the orchard floor, revealing narrow steps that descended before rising as light. [481] The stairway of ascension was not steel cable but a projected lattice of memory, rebuilt photon by photon. [499] It needed a keeper to speak the route aloud, lest the pattern scatter at sunrise when the purge reactors spiked. [519] Mara climbed three steps and spoke the old launch litany, names and dates and winds, her voice steady in the colored mist. [541] The orchard answered, sensors blinking awake, branches lifting toward the waterfall as the light steadied to gold. [558] Above the clouds the ring must have heard, for a thin line kindled and held, a thread against the coming dawn. [579] Mara knew she would not sit the unclaimed throne herself, but she could point the way for those wisely adventurous enough to follow. [602] As sunrise bled over the prisms, she recorded the coordinates on apple leaves and scattered them for the morning crew to find. [624] They would think it a prank born of futuristic nostalgia, until one leaf led them back behind the waterfall.
