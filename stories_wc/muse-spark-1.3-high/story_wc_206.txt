<story>Silas Hobb was a music box repairman in the crooked lanes of Cog Hollow. [14] His shop was an old resonance chamber lined with brass horns and pine shelves. [28] He worked with tinkersong wonder, humming while springs and combs learned to sing again. [42] On the autumn equinox, when day and night hung in the balance, a girl brought him a silent box. [61] The box was black walnut, its lid carved with a sun swallowed by midnight. [75] My grandmother said it holds the morning, the girl whispered, but midnight stole its voice. [90] Silas opened the back and found the mainspring whole, the comb whole, nothing torn. [104] His true craft was mending what isn't torn, listening for breaks no eye can see. [119] Beneath the cylinder he found a glowing chip from philosopher's stone, warm as a coal. [134] Coiled around it was a tiny vine with silver leaves that served as the guardian of truth. [151] The vine wilts near dishonesty, the girl warned, so you must speak plain. [164] Silas nodded and set the box upon his bench inside the humming chamber. [177] When he plucked the comb, no note rose, though the chip pulsed with trapped light. [192] He remembered the old tales of a covenant of rust between makers and time. [206] Long ago the tinkers promised rust that they would let all tired songs fade and die. [222] In return rust agreed to sleep softly on gears that still carried hope. [235] Midnight had broken that pact by stealing dawn and locking it in iron. [248] For years the town lived dim, to reclaim the light stolen by midnight seeming only a dream. [265] Silas understood his task was not to oil wheels but to free a captive dawn. [280] He cleaned his lenses and studied how the chip was wedged against the cylinder. [294] The stone wanted to sing, but rust had grown like ivy across the music roll. [309] If he scraped it away, he would break the covenant and wake its hunger. [323] If he left it, the song would stay choked and midnight would keep the light. [338] He brewed tea for the girl and asked what morning her grandmother remembered. [351] She spoke of bread ovens and roosters and gold spilling over the hills. [364] The vine lifted its leaves as she spoke, drinking in honest memory. [376] Tell me truly, Silas said, did your grandmother ever try to force the box. [390] The girl flushed and admitted she herself had pried at it with a nail. [404] The vine curled tighter, and the chamber air grew sour with shame. [416] Silas did not scold, but showed her his own scarred hands and early failures. [430] I once oiled a lullaby into silence, he confessed, and had to learn patience. [444] At his honest words the vine eased and bloomed with a small white bell. [458] Encouraged, Silas devised a way to honor rust while freeing sound. [469] He would not file the rust away but tether the chip to the spring with silver wire. [486] The wire would carry the song around the rust, letting age rest undisturbed. [499] With tweezers steady as a heartbeat, he looped the shining tether into place. [512] The resonance chamber began to hum as brass horns caught the faint vibration. [525] The chip warmed and poured thin light along the wire like sunrise through frost. [539] Silas held his breath and turned the key slowly through three full clicks. [552] For a moment nothing moved, and the balance of night and day trembled. [565] Then the cylinder turned, the comb shivered, and one clear note rang out. [578] Another followed, then a tide of melody that smelled of rain and bread. [591] The rust sighed and settled deeper, content to guard instead of devour. [603] The vine rang its tiny bell in answer, bright and unafraid. [614] Outside the windows, midnight thinned as stolen light rose from the box. [626] Gold edged the hills, roosters called, and Cog Hollow remembered morning. [637] The girl laughed through tears and wound the box again to keep it singing. [651] Silas logged the repair not as mended spring but as covenant kept. [663] He closed his shop knowing some music returns only when trust is repaired. [676]</story>
