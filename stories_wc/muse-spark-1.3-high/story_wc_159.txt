<story>Ione had served for forty years as the glass-orchid caretaker of the opal library above clouds. [16] She was gently systematic in all things, watering each transparent petal with measured care and noting every change in her ledger. [37] The library itself was one of many deserted marvels left after the Ascension, when readers abandoned paper for dreams. [56] Its opal shelves floated high and silent, holding books no one opened and seeds no one planted. [73] Ione remained because she had sworn to preserve the sound of forgotten names. [86] Each glass orchid held a name inside its stem, spoken once by someone below and then lost. [103] When wind passed through the aisles, the orchids rang faintly by the echo of a long-forgotten song. [120] Ione could hear her mother in that chorus, and her brother, and a thousand strangers. [135] Every evening she would climb the stair of cloud glass and light the memorial lanterns one by one. [153] The work demanded patience, for the flames remembered only when coaxed slowly. [165] One dusk she found a wind chime bamboo tube cracked upon the floor beneath the orchid wall. [182] It had hung for decades at the west window, voicing the drafts that fed the flowers. [198] Now its voice was broken, and the orchids near it had dulled to gray. [212] Ione felt a foxglove regret settle in her chest, tall and purple and quietly poisonous. [227] She knew that plant well from the lower gardens, beautiful to see and bitter to taste. [243] Regret, she thought, was much the same, lovely in memory and harmful if kept. [257] She lifted the broken tube gently and carried it to her worktable of bone and light. [273] Her hands, though old, were steady from years of mending fragile things. [285] She cleaned the crack with cloud water and bound it with thread spun from orchid roots. [301] As she worked, her mind traveled across the museum of extinct feelings she kept within herself. [317] There was the bright hall of first snow, and the dim room of last goodbyes. [332] There was the gallery of childhood laughter, now dusty and without visitors. [344] She had filed each feeling away instead of speaking it, thinking silence would protect her. [359] The cracked chime reminded her of her own voice, unused for years except to whisper names. [375] Long ago she had stopped singing because no one remained to listen. [387] That night she hung the mended tube back at the window and waited for wind. [402] At first there was nothing but cold air and the creak of opal beams. [416] Then a slow draft rose from below, carrying salt and rain and distant cooking smoke. [431] The mended tube sounded, lower than before but clear, and the orchids answered in turn. [446] Name after name floated through the library, bright as pollen in the lantern light. [460] Ione walked among them, speaking each aloud so none would vanish unheard. [472] She recorded every syllable in her ledger, her script small and careful and complete. [486] When dawn thinned the clouds below, the wind softened and the music faded. [499] The orchids shone again, their stems humming with remembered voices. [509] Ione touched the mended crack and understood that broken things could still carry sound. [523] She was no longer only a keeper of flowers but a keeper of those who had named them. [541] And as morning filled the opal library above clouds, she began softly to sing. [555] Her song was simple and low, learned in childhood and kept quiet for decades. [569] The orchids trembled, and the mended chime joined her, steady and true. [581] For the first time in years, the vast rooms did not feel abandoned. [594] They felt waiting, patient as seeds beneath snow, ready to bloom when called. [607]</story>
