<story>The exiled chess tutor came to the time-frozen village during the lull of distant thunder. [15] His name was Tomas Ash, once trusted in the capital, now seeking to forgive oneself for past mistakes. [33] Years before he had taught a young prince a reckless queen sacrifice, and the boy had repeated it in war and died. [94] Above the hills thunder rolled and paused, and Tomas walked forward in that held breath between sounds. [111] He fidgets productively even while afraid, turning a worn pawn between his fingers to steady his thoughts. [128] A woven hush lay over the roofs, as if sound itself had been looped and knotted into cloth. [164] Its center showed harvesters in a field, but its border writhed with loose ends and broken patterns. [181] At the heart of the field the weaver had stitched a tiny platinum labyrinth that caught no dust. [215] Tomas understood that he must winnow the true holding thread from a hundred false leads before the thunder returned. [234] He could free them only by decoding frayed threads in a tapestry's border, reading knots as if they were chess notation. [339] His hands kept moving as he worked, sorting threads by weight and color while his eyes tracked the maze. [358] The platinum labyrinth at the center began to answer, its silver lines warming under his gaze. [374] The woven hush pressed closer, tasting of flax and rain, daring him to hurry and to err. [472] He fidgeted no longer from nerves but from purpose, his pawn clicking softly against the altar stone. [489] Slowly he began to winnow memory from guilt, keeping the love of teaching while discarding the vanity. [506] The threads under his fingers separated cleanly, true gold from dyed hemp, strong warp from rotted weft. [652] He had not undone the past, yet he had at last used its lesson to preserve rather than to dazzle.
