<story>Elias Vane waited on the cobalt terrace at dusk, and the sea below him held its breath. [17] He was a guilt-ridden black market genealogist, and every pedigree he had sold now sat like stones in his coat. [37] For twenty years he manufactures false memories for the childless rich, stitching dead names into their empty lines. [55] He had asked his sister Mara to meet him here, and they stood together under the weight of unspoken words. [75] He had come to discover the weight of a withheld truth, not to ask for pardon. [91] He spoke with tempered regret, measuring each admission like medicine. [101] Mara, I sold our brother's name, he said, and left our mother to mourn an empty grave. [118] Ten years ago a merchant wanted a drowned heir, and I gave him Jonah Vane with papers and dates. [137] In truth Jonah lived, and hid, and died nameless because I erased him for money. [152] Mara did not answer, but turned a small brass watch hand over in her palm. [167] It was all that remained of their father's drowned watch, stopped at the hour Jonah disappeared. [183] I cannot bring him back, Elias said, but I can quell the lie before it roots deeper. [200] The merchant's son now claims the Vane blood, and he will sell the shore to wreckers. [216] If the lineage stands, no court will listen to our kin. [227] Mara pressed the watch hand into his hand and said, Then prove what you buried. [242] He would do it by sifting through driftwood carvings along a forgotten shore, where fisher families cut their dead into wood. [263] No paper register held Jonah, but the shore remembered every lost son. [275] That night he walked down with a lantern and the watch hand sharpened to a pick. [291] The tide was low, and pale carvings lay scattered like bones in the sand. [305] He lifted each board, brushed salt away, and read with his fingers. [317] Some named storms, some named debts, and one near the rocks bore his mother's mark. [332] Beneath it, faint and crossed, he found J V 1879, scratched and then gouged out. [347] His own knife had done the gouging, paid for in merchant silver. [359] He sank to his knees, and used the watch hand to clear the grooves with care. [375] Around him settled the sieved hush, the shore sound strained through wood and water until only truth remained. [393] No gulls cried, no wind pushed, and his breathing sounded borrowed. [404] He understood then that silence, rightly sifted, accuses louder than any voice. [416] He carried three carvings back up the cliff, his hands bleeding from salt and splinters. [431] Mara waited where he had left her, still on the terrace as dawn thinned the blue. [447] He laid the wood at her feet and confessed every coin, every forged date, every letter. [463] She listened without mercy and without leaving, which was more than he deserved. [476] Together they took the carvings to the magistrate, and Elias signed a true genealogy in ink. [492] He surrendered his ledgers, his fees, and his freedom for one season in jail. [506] The merchant's claim failed, the shore stayed with the fishers, and Jonah's name was cut clean again. [523] Elias never forged again, but kept the watch hand to teach apprentices what a line costs. [539] In his last years he mended true trees for poor families and asked no payment but patience. [556] When children asked why he limped, Mara would say the sea keeps accounts. [569] He would nod, and feel again that sieved hush loosen the knot behind his ribs. [584] It did not absolve him, but it let him live beside his fault without lying. [599] On the night he died, Mara set the watch hand on his chest and thanked him for returning. [617]</story>
