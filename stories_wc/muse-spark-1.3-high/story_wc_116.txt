<story>I became the chronicle keeper before I learned my own name. [11] The overgrown library hidden in catacombs of lost knowledge chose me, not the other way. [26] Roots broke through vaulted ceilings and pages sprouted moss, and I learned to catalogue rot as faithfully as ink. [45] My task was simple and endless, to witness change without judgment. [56] Across the drift of untold seasons I watched empires donate their maps and plagues donate their dead. [73] I swept dust from atlases while vines rewrote the aisles. [83] I do not fight time, for a keeper who grips reality loosely outlasts every stone edict. [99] That looseness became mercy when the river people came with their burned archives. [112] They carried a child who remembered everything and spoke nothing. [122] Her name was Sella, and silence pooled around her like water in a deep well. [137] The priests above wanted her voice for prophecy, the generals wanted it for warning. [151] I wanted only to keep her safe until she chose her own telling. [164] One dusk she placed in my hands a gift, a small and softened bird made via cryptic folds in a well-worn origami swan. [187] Its wings were thinned by unfolding, its creases dark with handling. [198] Unfold this when they come, she whispered, and refold what remains. [209] That night torchlight bled down the stairwell and boots shook loose mortar from the arches. [224] There was no door to bar, only catalogues and roots and crumbling peace. [237] I took Sella by the hand and led her deeper than any index goes. [251] Past the drowned philosophy stacks we ran, past histories gnawed to lace. [263] The catacombs exhaled cold air, and the river answered through the stone. [275] I unfolded the swan with shaking fingers and found inside a map drawn in a hand I had buried centuries before. [296] It showed a fissure behind the cistern, a low throat leading out toward the bank. [311] We knelt and began to burrow through soft earth and old bone dust. [324] My nails broke, her small shoulders bled against rock, and the library shed plaster like dry skin behind us. [343] The soldiers shouted names of kings, and the roots tightened as if listening. [356] We emerged at dawn into reeds, into the gray rush of open water. [369] There on the far shore stood the obsidian refuge along the river, black glass half swallowed by willows. [387] My predecessor had built it for censored poets, and I had recorded its ruin without ever seeking it. [405] We swam with the swan held high between us, and the cold stripped the pursuit from our backs. [423] Inside the refuge there were no books, only smooth walls and light moving like fish. [438] We sat and breathed, and the quiet there was not empty but regenerative silence. [452] It mended the torn edges in us without asking for story or confession. [465] Days passed, perhaps years, for time grips a keeper even more loosely than she grips it. [481] Sella began to speak, not in prophecies but in small true things, about herons and bread and my crooked handwriting. [501] I wrote them on the black walls with chalk, and the river kept the rhythm of her sentences. [519] When soldiers at last found the empty library, they found only vines and a refolded swan upon my desk. [538] I had left it as both record and release, for some chronicles must end in crumbling peace rather than in capture. [559] Sella sleeps now behind glass grown green, dreaming in a language no army can translate. [574] I tend a small garden on the roof, trading memories for seedlings with ferrymen who ask no questions. [592] Sometimes I miss the weight of catalogues, the dust that taught me patience. [605] Then the river fog rises and restores the regenerative silence, and I remember why keepers endure. [621] Not to own the past, but to shelter its smallest voices until the world grows gentle enough to hear them. [641]</story>
