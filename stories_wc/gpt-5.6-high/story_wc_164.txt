In the iridescent cave inside a giant crystal geode, morning arrived as a rumor of blue light, and Nella woke smiling beneath a blanket that had forgotten her feet. [29]

She was the village's sea-foam recollectionist, gathering memories from the pale froth that seeped through crystalline walls whenever the distant ocean dreamed. [51]

Lately, however, roads through the cave had been losing sections overnight, leaving doorways facing cliffs, bridges ending in glittering air, and maps full of polite blankness. [77]

Nella wanted to discover why some paths disappear, because each vanished turning also erased a little of everyone who had walked it. [99]

Her grandmother still remembered having a bakery, but not its location, its oven, or the name of the honey cake she baked for funerals. [123]

"A cheerful gloom is healthier than a miserable breakfast," Grandmother declared, buttering an empty plate. [138]

Nella laughed, then hurried to the eastern gallery, for "before memory fades" was the recollectionists' warning, and lately it felt like the weather. [161]

At the newest gap, she found black crater glass wedged between amethyst ribs, warm as a recently held hand. [180]

When she pressed sea foam against it, the glass showed no scene; it produced only a tightness behind her eyes and the taste of farewell. [205]

Her guild certificate described her gift simply: she transliterates emotions, converting feelings into the alphabets of places, weather, and sound. [225]

The glass's farewell became three notes of rain, a crooked footpath, and the dry rattle of seeds. [242]

Above her, a silver-breasted tern fluttered through a shaft leading to the surface, dropping tiny red seeds from its muddy claws. [263]

Migrating terns crossed the outer ocean only twice yearly, and no red-seeded plant grew anywhere within the geode. [281]

Nella formed a conjecture: the bird had flown along a forgotten route, and the crater glass was what remained when that route was cut from memory. [307]

She could test this only via the scattered seeds dropped by a migrating bird, each seed marking a point where an invisible path still touched the world. [334]

Cheerfully announcing that impossible errands improved the complexion, she packed foam jars, chalk, and Grandmother's useless but comforting honey spoon. [354]

The seeds led across a shattered bridge, down a prism chimney, and toward a wall whose pearly surface admitted no passage. [375]

Yet every seed rolled through the wall and vanished. [384]

Nella touched crater glass to crystal, translated its sorrow into a descending whistle, and heard an answering note beyond the barrier. [405]

She whistled back, and a missing corridor shimmered into being, thin as moonlight and crowded with translucent footprints. [423]

Halfway along it, she met Orro, the geode's elderly cartographer, sitting beside a brass machine with shears poised over a ribbon of light. [446]

"I removed dangerous paths," he said, looking relieved and ashamed, "but every road carries memories into every other road." [465]

After his daughter fell from a crystal ledge, Orro had cut away the path to the ledge, hoping to spare other families. [487]

But the machine could not understand edges; each cut unraveled journeys, then destinations, then the people held together by arriving. [507]

"I meant to remove grief," Orro whispered. [514]

Nella placed foam on his wrist, and his grief transliterated itself into red seeds tapping patiently against glass. [532]

"Grief is not a path," she said; "it is proof that someone reached us." [546]

Together they reversed the machine, feeding it crater glass while Nella sounded each trapped emotion: fear as thunder, love as bells, regret as a door unlatched. [572]

Corridors returned in luminous loops, bringing bakeries, gardens, stairways, and bewildered neighbors who had been absent without knowing it. [591]

The final ribbon restored Orro's ledge, but Nella redrew its approach with rails, warning chimes, and a bench bearing his daughter's name. [613]

By evening, Grandmother's bakery reappeared beside a turquoise arch, its oven glowing and its funeral honey cakes tasting confusingly festive. [633]

The villagers ate beneath crystal rainbows and toasted missing pieces, not because loss was pleasant, but because gaps revealed what needed mending. [655]

Orro became keeper of safe crossings, while Nella stored the last crater glass in a foam-lined box labeled, "For remembering what forgetting costs." [678]

Thereafter, whenever a tern scattered seeds through the geode, she followed them before memory could fade, laughing softly at the gloom and carrying chalk for the road home. [706]
