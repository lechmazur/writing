<story>After the last ship sailed, the harbor of Vey closed its blue gates, and every remaining bell sounded like a spoon striking an empty cup. [25]
Mara arrived then, carrying three black notebooks and the peculiar profession that had made her welcome nowhere: she was a traveler who wrote obituaries for the living. [52]
Her clients believed a finished life, described before death, might reveal which losses had shaped it and which roads remained open. [73]
She believed them because her own brother, Tomas, had vanished in Vey seven autumns earlier, leaving only a map with the palace garden circled. [97]
Mara had come to understand the geography of healing, and whether a wound became bearable because one departed, or because another learned where to stand. [122]
At dusk she entered the shadowed garden in autumn, where copper leaves lay across the paths like coins offered to forgotten gods. [144]
The abandoned palace rose around it in terraces of pearl stone, its windows holding the sunset long after the streets went gray. [166]
Among black roses stood an old woman in a coat of green velvet, pruning stems with silver scissors. [184]
She introduced herself as Ilyen, last keeper of the garden, and asked if Mara had come to explain Tomas's absence. [204]
"I came to learn whether it can be explained," Mara said. [215]
Ilyen led her into a ruined pavilion, where rain had eaten the painted ceiling but spared one magnificent palace carpet. [235]
Its crimson field portrayed Vey as an archipelago, with golden roads crossing islands of indigo wool and tiny ivory ships fleeing a silver storm. [259]
A narrow gap divided the harbor from the garden because a single palace carpet weft thread had been pulled free. [279]
Ilyen placed the loose thread in Mara's palm and closed her fingers around it with a tenderness that made accusation impossible. [300]
"Your brother pulled it loose," she said, "because he discovered what this carpet was made to do." [317]
The old kings summoned images of absent heirs by projecting presence through its threads, allowing a voice, warmth, and remembered gesture to occupy empty air. [342]
The illusion was intimate enough to comfort mourners and dangerous enough to keep them from accepting who would never return. [362]
Tomas, Ilyen explained, had come seeking their mother, whose fevered death Mara had answered by wandering while he remained beside the grave. [384]
But the carpet did not summon souls; it borrowed the observer's memories, making longing vivid and obedient. [401]
Each night grieving citizens crowded the pavilion and saw soldiers, lovers, and children return beneath the roses, brighter than lanterns and equally untouchable. [424]
Tomas recognized that Vey had stopped burying its dead, repairing its homes, or boarding ships, for projected presences occupied every necessary absence. [446]
"He said absences must be explained, not filled," Ilyen whispered. [456]
When he pulled the harbor thread, every apparition dissolved, but the severed enchantment gathered itself around him and carried his body into the pattern. [480]
Mara bent over the carpet and found, among the indigo knots of the garden, a figure no longer than her smallest fingernail. [502]
He wore Tomas's red scarf, and one woven hand gripped the golden road toward the harbor. [518]
"If I replace the thread, he can cross out," Mara said. [529]
"And every beloved ghost can cross in," Ilyen answered. [538]
Mara understood then that her work had always drawn outlines around loss, preserving a person's shape without pretending the person remained inside it. [561]
She asked Ilyen for ink, then questioned her until moonrise about Tomas's hidden season in the garden before the enchantment claimed him. [583]
Ilyen described how he grafted white pears, feared deep water, sang badly to seedlings, and forgave Mara long before she forgave herself. [605]
From those details, Mara composed his obituary, not as a notice of death, but as a vividly descriptive map of where love had carried him. [630]
She wrote of the boy beside the grave, the man among roses, and the brother who tore open a false heaven so strangers could mourn honestly. [656]
Then Mara knelt, pressed her forehead to the carpet in an intimate gesture remembered from childhood, and read the pages aloud while projecting her presence toward him. [683]
The carpet stirred like grass under wind, and the tiny figure followed her voice across the missing channel, walking upon the loose weft thread she held taut. [710]
At dawn, Tomas emerged beside her, thin, with silver hair, smelling of wool, rain, and the bitter leaves of seven lost autumns. [732]
Mara did not ask why he had left; his absence now had shape, distance, and a bridge, and his hand was warm in hers. [756]
Beyond the garden, winter ships would someday sail again, but for that luminous morning, the siblings stood together where healing began: not where absence ended, but where truth gave it a shore. [788]</story>
