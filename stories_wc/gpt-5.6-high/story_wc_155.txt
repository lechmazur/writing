<story>When Mara Vale fell asleep over her unpaid studio rent, she slipped within the fold of a daydream and woke laughing. [21]
The laugh embarrassed her, for she had not heard its bright, foolish sound since her brother Tomas vanished into the fog. [42]

Yet her recent walls were skillful and dead, commissioned smiles arranged over cafes whose customers stared through them. [101]
She had entered sleep asking for one thing, not fame or money, but a way to rediscover lost joy. [120]

Mara pursued it into a collapsed conservatory, where strips of exposed film hung from branches like black, translucent fruit. [155]
Each frame held a tiny face, but moisture had washed away mouths, leaving the remembered citizens solemn and incomplete. [174]

"Admission," said the fox, "is one honest sorrow, preferably small enough to carry." [207]
