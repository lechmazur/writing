<story>After the rainforest burned, Mara came home to Echo Harbor, where even the gulls seemed ashamed to cry. [18]

For eleven years, a chronic hush had lain over the inlet, swallowing bells, engines, thunder, and finally conversation. [36]

She followed those small sounds beyond the drowned quay to a sunken orchard greenhouse ringed by shipwrecks. [78]

He had carried it through villages after the fires, accepting no coins, only endings people could not bear to speak. [130]

Each night he followed their family tradition of storytelling around a bonfire, repeating the fragments and giving them to everyone who warmed beside it. [154]

Mara had hated that custom as a child because no tale ever finished; Iven stopped at the brightest image and let darkness answer. [177]

Now she understood why his bowl waited here. [185]

When Mara touched the knot, the greenhouse gave a deep wooden sigh, too low for ordinary hearing. [225]

The hush was not emptiness, she realized, but thousands of untold conclusions cinched together by grief. [241]

Iven had gathered them to save the survivors from their pain, then bound them into the seed before they silenced him too. [263]

But grief preserved becomes grief enthroned, and the seed had fed on every sound the harbor made. [280]
