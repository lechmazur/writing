<story>Three years after discovering aliens, Mara Venn descended through black water toward Pelagos, the benthic city where sunlight had become folklore. [21]

As the academy's youngest navigation instructor, she taught pilots to cross the abyss by sonar, current, and disciplined suspicion. [40]

Yet she had volunteered for this forbidden ascent because she wanted to learn to navigate by starlight. [57]

The aliens had offered no engines or weapons; they had sent a map folded into seventeen dimensions and one invitation: Come upward. [79]

Pelagos feared the surface, where storms erased ships and old nations had drowned, but Mara feared a horizon she could not read. [100]

She entered the observatory lock carrying the sole object the visitors had requested, a corroded boat anchor chain link recovered from the founding wreck. [124]

Waiting inside was Ilyr, tall as a mast, translucent beneath its pressure suit, with five eyes arranged like an unfinished constellation. [145]

Mara's grief for her drowned brother made her cautious, but not closed; she was balancedly curious, asking questions without surrendering judgment. [166]

"Why this?" she asked, raising the link. [173]

Ilyr touched its rust and projected a globe of pale lines around them. [186]

"Because your people once trusted weight to hold them still," it said, "and now must trust emptiness to carry them." [206]

The ascent capsule rose before midnight, tethered to Pelagos until the city's lamps dwindled below like embers under glass. [225]

Mara expected a chart lesson, but Ilyr darkened every instrument and set the chain link floating between them. [243]

Through its oval, a single star trembled. [250]

"A navigator does not begin with destinations," Ilyr said. [259]

"Begin with edges." [262]

It taught her edge geometry, the alien practice of locating an observer by how boundaries changed when seen from shifting angles. [283]

The ring framed one star, then two, then a bright wound of galactic dust; each movement turned absence into measurement. [303]

Mara understood why sonar had never prepared her: echoes returned answers, while light preserved questions from ages before her birth. [323]

Above the last storm bank, the sea flattened into darkness, and the sky opened with such multitude that she forgot breathing. [344]

These were not the neat ceiling lights of Pelagos but unfolded horizons, distances released from every wall she had known. [364]

It was a somber marvel, for somewhere among those fires circled the silent world of the visitors, emptied by a starquake. [385]

Ilyr had not come as a conqueror, she realized, but as the last instructor of a homeless route. [403]

Then the tether alarm sounded. [408]

A deep current had dragged Pelagos eastward, pulling the capsule sideways while a surface cyclone bent the cable toward failure. [428]

Instruments remained blind, their antennas iced by spray, and the city could not calculate where the capsule would fall. [447]

If Mara cut free, she and Ilyr might survive, but the whipping tether would strike the city's oxygen towers. [466]

She stared through the link at Polaris, then at two lower stars vanishing along the storm's edge. [483]

Edge geometry made their angular drift visible; the chain's pitted oval gave her a crude but honest frame. [501]

She plotted the capsule against the moving cloud boundary, found the current's hidden vector, and fired the maneuvering jets. [520]

"We cannot overpower the storm," Ilyr warned. [527]

"No," Mara said, feeling the old anchor turn warm in her hand, "but we can expand the edge we measure." [547]

She rotated the capsule until the link framed Polaris, the cyclone wall, and Pelagos's beacon in one trembling alignment. [566]

That geometry revealed a narrow slack-water corridor behind the cyclone, invisible to sonar because the storm filled every frequency with thunder. [587]

Mara pulsed the jets, not fighting the tether but describing a widening spiral that guided its violence away from the towers. [608]

The cable snapped only after the capsule had crossed the corridor, dropping harmlessly across an abandoned quarry beyond the city. [628]

At dawn, Pelagos called her home, yet Mara remained on the surface long enough to chart the fading stars. [647]

Ilyr watched silently as she threaded the chain link onto her instructor's cord. [660]

"Will you teach them?" it asked. [666]

Mara looked down through clearing water, where Pelagos glimmered like a constellation learning its own reflection. [682]
