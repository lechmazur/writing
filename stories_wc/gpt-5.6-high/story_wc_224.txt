<story>After remembering became painful, Mara climbed toward the bookstore that occupied Mount Orison from root cellar to snow-capped summit. [19] Its halls doubled back through the mountain, and ladders rose beside black shafts where index bells murmured like hidden thunder. [39] Mara carried no map, only a white rook feather and one question: what shape had home possessed before memory sharpened? [59] Her island village had sunk twelve years earlier, leaving her with disconnected rooms, her mother's hands, and the taste of rain. [80] She wanted not to recover every wound, but to understand the shape of home well enough to build one elsewhere. [100]

At dusk, a bookseller directed her downward, toward a chamber paneled with mirrors whose silver had clouded at the edges. [120] There waited Ilyon, the mercury-glass seer, his skin reflective and darkly mottled, his eyes bright droplets that never held one face. [141] "I substitute reality nightly," he said, "because truth alone cannot guide those whose memories have become prisons." [158] Each midnight, his mirrors covered the bookstore with a possible world assembled from whatever its sleeping readers had refused to remember. [179] By dawn the vision vanished, but sometimes a stair, a scar, or a marriage created inside it remained. [197]

Mara distrusted him immediately, which Ilyon accepted as evidence that she might survive his help. [212] From a locked atlas he removed the lost language stone, a palm-sized slab furred with shallow marks. [229] It had been quarried beneath Mara's drowned island, and no scholar could agree whether its marks were letters, roads, or waves. [250]

"Read it," Ilyon said. [254]

"I cannot read any language but trade script." [262]

"That is the talent you buried, not the talent you possess." [273]

As a child, Mara had recognized people by footsteps and storms by pressure, until teachers punished every answer she could not explain. [295] She had buried those talents under useful facts, as thoroughly as the mountain had buried its oldest books. [313] Ilyon laid her feather upon the stone and told her to seek meaning via the texture of a feather, not sight. [334] When she drew the rook plume across the grooves, each barb snagged differently, plucking forgotten sounds through her fingertips. [353]

"Not words," she whispered, while the floor answered with a distant rumble. [365] "Instructions for seeding words." [369]

The stone could seed a language in any imagined world, but it required a first word shaped by genuine longing. [389] Ilyon confessed that his nightly substitutions were failing because the mountain contained too many private griefs and no common grammar. [409] Soon one invented reality would harden permanently, trapping every reader inside a stranger's perfect past. [424] He wanted Mara to name home, seed the next vision, and give the labyrinth a world its occupants could share. [444]

She nearly fled, for naming her island might raise its houses, its dead, and the water entering her mother's lungs. [464] Then midnight struck, softly at first, and the hidden thunder opened beneath every shelf. [478] Reality peeled away; warm surf flooded the chamber, and Mara stood before her childhood door beneath a flawlessly blue sky. [498] Her mother emerged alive, smiling exactly as remembered, and Mara understood the cruelty in that precision. [514] A home without change was merely a tomb that welcomed its mourner. [526]

She pressed the feather harder, listening through texture as the stone offered the island's lost syllables. [542] Among them she found no word for house, only avara, meaning the shelter made when one person leaves room for another. [563] Mara spoke avara into the stone. [569]

Letters spread like roots across the flooded floor, climbed shelves and mirrors, and entered every dreamer in the mountain. [588] The perfect island broke into thousands of incomplete rooms: kitchens without roofs, boats with garden gates, beds beside foreign windows. [608] Readers woke within them and began exchanging doors, recipes, songs, and names, using the seeded language to ask permission. [627] Every gift altered the vision, so no single memory could close around them. [640]

Ilyon's mirrored body cracked from brow to heel as realities passed through him, yet beneath the mercury Mara saw ordinary skin. [661] He had not remembered his own face for centuries. [670]

At dawn, the sea withdrew, leaving a new reading room where the locked chamber had been. [686] Its walls were mismatched doors, and its tables bore alphabets contributed by sleepers who no longer seemed like strangers. [705] Ilyon, dull-eyed and human, asked Mara whether she had recovered her home. [717]

"No," she said, opening the mountain's front ledger to a blank page. [729] "But I have learned its shape." [735]

Then she wrote avara, and all through the labyrinth, buried talents stirred like thunder choosing at last to become music. [755]</story>
