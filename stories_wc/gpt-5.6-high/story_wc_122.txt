During the faint rustle of turning seasons, Mara Vale descended beneath Bellweather with one hand on the limestone wall and the other guarding a velvet box. [26]

She had been the town's deaf music teacher for eleven years, long enough for children to stop pitying her and start watching her hands. [50]

Through soles, fingertips, and the hollow of her ribs, she taught rhythm as contact: a promise that no silence was truly empty. [72]

Yet Bellweather had gone beyond silence. [78]

Babies opened their mouths without wailing; bakers sold wedding cakes without smiles; even arguments ended in soft, indifferent shrugs. [117]

Mara alone remembered feeling, perhaps because fever could not erase what sound had already taught her to rebuild by other means. [138]

She entered the drum maker's cavern at dusk, when autumn leaves skittered above like brushes sweeping a distant snare. [157]

Hundreds of unfinished drums lined the galleries, their skins breathing with underground drafts, while copper lamps doubled themselves in black pools. [178]

The place held a mirrored hush: every tremor seemed to travel outward, meet its reflection, and return changed. [196]

At the deepest pool waited Orin, last of Bellweather's drum makers, beside a phonograph mounted upon a bass drum. [215]

"You brought it?" he signed. [220]

Mara opened the box, revealing a broken phonograph needle, its snapped tip bright as frozen lightning. [236]

Her mother had broken it deliberately on the fever's first night, after recording the town choir and discovering the plague nested inside harmony. [259]

Orin fitted the fragment into a silver cradle, then connected the phonograph's horn to pipes ending beneath every drum. [299]

Mara arranged wax cylinders of backward recorded songs: lullabies, funeral chants, harvest rounds, and the foolish dance tune her students once adored. [321]

"The needle cannot carry them," Orin signed, touching the fracture. [331]

"Then the cavern must," Mara answered. [337]

Her plan was not merely to wake the town, but to defeat emotional numbness without unleashing the fatal hymn again. [380]

Mara pressed her injured thumb onto the strand, binding silk to blood, and signaled Orin to crank faster. [490]
