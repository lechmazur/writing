Eleven years after the fossil fuel ban, Mara Venn kept the outermost sea level monitoring station, a steel room balanced on pilings above the drowned streets of Bellweather. [28]

The tides lifted cables beneath her floor, then lowered them, so the station felt like a drifting thing that happened, by stubbornness, to remain moored. [53]

The water fell, the float moved, but the ink pen drew a clean bridge between hours, as if that particular path through time had never existed. [130]

She wanted to discover why some paths disappear, whether they belonged to currents, migrating people, or facts that frightened whoever kept the maps. [153]

Official manuals blamed a software conversion, although the gauge was mechanical and older than Mara. [168]
