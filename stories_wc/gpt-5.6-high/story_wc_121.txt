After rain, the old viaduct glowed above drowned Veyra, each arch lit from within like a rib of captured dawn. [20]

Mara Vale stepped from the parapet and did not fall; the city called her a gravity defier, though she preferred cartographer. [41]

Beneath her boots, silver water hung in the air, arrested between the drop and the ripple, because midnight had broken again. [62]

It always broke here, where seven versions of the viaduct crossed one another and fractured timelines leaked their unfinished hours. [82]

From one future came smoke; from another, birds with human shadows; from the past came Mara's own voice begging her to turn back. [105]

She ignored it and opened her satchel, revealing an ancient clay tablet with runes pressed by fingers erased from every history but this one. [129]

The tablet had belonged to Orin, her brother, before the labyrinth beneath the bridge copied him into a thousand possible men. [150]

Only one Orin had escaped, and he had returned kind, frightened, and unable to remember which Mara was real. [169]

She had killed him when the other Orins spoke through his mouth. [181]

Since then, she had come each midnight not to rescue him, but to conquer the labyrinth of self that had made her choose murder. [205]

The runes brightened as her suspended tears drifted upward, and the bridge unfolded stairs toward an opening blacker than the rain. [226]

Inside, no mason had designed the passages; they renewed themselves through procedural generation, using each visitor's regrets as rules and memories as seed. [249]

Thus Mara found the kitchen of her childhood joined to a prison corridor, then to the viaduct on the morning Orin vanished. [271]

At every junction stood another Mara: a coward who had fled, a saint who had forgiven, a butcher who had enjoyed the knife. [294]

To proceed, she had to retrace their choices in reverse, stepping backward while the tablet burned directions into her palms. [314]

Each choice reduced the crowd, yet each also darkened the radiance pouring through the walls, a darkening brightness that revealed truth by blinding hope. [338]

At the center waited the clockmaker, a sexless figure whose life was periodically eternal: immortal for one minute, dust for the next. [360]

While flesh covered its bones, it said, "You mistake victory for becoming the only version left." [376]

"What else could conquest mean?" Mara asked, hovering above a floor tiled with Orin's faces. [391]

The clockmaker turned to dust, and in its silence the faces answered, "It means knowing which enemy is yours." [410]

Then the labyrinth generated its final chamber: the rain-bright bridge, Orin kneeling, and Mara holding the knife before she had plunged it. [432]

Her younger self shouted that all the Orins would escape unless one death sealed the breach. [448]

The kneeling Orin looked up, his eyes flickering with countless lives, and begged exactly as Mara remembered. [465]

She raised the tablet to command the scene, but one rune remained dark, shaped like two hands releasing each other. [485]

Mara understood then that the maze had never fed on guilt alone; it fed on her belief that every possible failure belonged to her. [509]

She had made herself judge of infinite Maras, accepting all their crimes so none could contradict her innocence. [527]

"Mine is the hand," she told her younger self, "but not every fear that guided it." [543]

She stepped between knife and brother. [549]

The younger Mara stabbed her, yet the blade passed through her chest and pinned the accusing shadow behind her to the stones. [571]

Across the labyrinth, alternate corridors collapsed, not erased, but released from the duty of proving this Mara right. [589]

Orin rose, no longer singular, his many voices settling into harmony rather than war. [603]

"You cannot bring me home," he said, "because I am all the homes I entered." [618]

Mara wanted to deny him, but conquest now meant allowing an answer she could not own. [634]

She pressed the final rune, and the clay tablet cracked into seven pieces, each falling toward a different history. [653]

Time resumed. [655]

Rain struck the glowing viaduct, ripples widened below, and the stolen futures withdrew like lights beneath deep water. [673]

At dawn, Mara stood on the parapet with gravity tugging honestly at her coat and no brother beside her. [692]

For the first time, falling was not defeat, and flying was not escape. [705]

She climbed down by the stairs, carrying only her own remembered hand, heavy enough to be hers. [722]
