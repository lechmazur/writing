<story>After the nostalgia ban, remembering became a civic crime, and Liora survived by dancing tides for the Ministry of Forgetting. [20]

She was a skilled tide dancer, trained to draw flooded memories from citizens and send them seaward before dawn. [39]

Her art possessed obsidian grace: black ribbons, bare feet, and gestures sharp enough to cut longing from the heart. [58]

Yet each extracted memory left a silver scar on her palms, a private constellation no inspector could explain. [76]

Liora wanted neither pardon nor rebellion; she wanted to pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin, using those scars as letters. [97]

Such a poem might restore memory without restoring obedience, but no ink known to the Ministry could bind starlight to flesh. [118]

The answer arrived in a confiscated locket, where a single pale grain shimmered like a trapped, unfinished moon. [136]

Its evidence label named it a moonstone cabochon dust speck, the last residue of an astronomer executed for mapping forbidden yesterdays. [157]

When Liora touched it, every scar brightened, and the locket became spontaneously anchored to her wrist by roots of cold light. [178]

An alarm began counting the seconds until wardens came, so she chose elective exile, a sentence available only to artists deemed dangerously sincere. [201]

Exiles could leave the capital alive, provided they crossed the Salt Gate before sunrise and never carried an audience with them. [222]

Liora carried no audience, only the stolen grain and a newborn poem beating wordlessly beneath her scars. [239]

Beyond the gate lay the abandoned remembrance quarter, its houses emptied, its statues faceless, its clocks ordered never to look backward. [260]

At its center waited a twilight garden under stars, once planted for weddings and now cultivated by nobody but wind. [280]

"Can your poem make them remember me?" he asked. [322]

"It may make them remember themselves," Liora said, which was the more honest promise and the crueler one. [340]

But the verse lacked a final line, because Liora had remembered everyone she cleansed except the person she had been before serving. [443]

"Wings are only the courage to enter what will hold you," her mother had said. [562]
