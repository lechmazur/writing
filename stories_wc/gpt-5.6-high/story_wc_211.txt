After the foghorn fell silent, the city discovered how much of its courage had depended on that single iron note. [20]

Without it, mist climbed the towers unchallenged, muffling traffic and turning every window into a blind gray eye. [38]

There, beneath roots gripping six hundred feet of steel, worked Vey Ansel, the venerable oath scribe. [77]

For sixty years, Vey had recorded promises before witnesses, then indexed not their words but the tremors, hesitations, and hopes beneath them. [99]

His private purpose was to archive emotional patterns, because civilizations lost feelings long before they lost alphabets. [116]

He spoke nine dead languages, and believed each had died when its people could no longer recognize one another's grief. [136]

His class had toured a sound museum, where their teacher lowered a paper cone onto a turning cylinder. [245]
