<story>In the autumn before the coup, Mara Vey worked as the palace's galaxy-chart embroiderer beneath the cobalt observatory's whispering dome. [20] Each night she stitched silver routes among constellations, while fallen leaves scraped the blue walls like letters nobody had opened. [40] She had asked for the post not to advise admirals or flatter astrologers, but to listen for unheard stories. [59] Space, she believed, went silent only when powerful people decided which voices deserved an echo. [74] Mara disguised her wisdom as foolishness, tangling threads, misnaming moons, and asking questions so simple that ministers answered carelessly. [93] Thus she learned that Chancellor Ors planned to seize the throne when the first winter comet crossed the northern lens. [113] The conspiracy hid inside observatory corrections, each false coordinate redirecting royal beacons and isolating loyal provinces. [129] Yet proof vanished whenever she followed one trail because Ors's agents rewrote the archive before dawn. [145] Mara therefore began unraveling conspiracies as she unraveled damaged silk: not by pulling one thread, but by testing the threads beside it. [167] She called this traveling through adjacent possibilities, imagining each lie's nearest truth and checking what the liars would need to change next. [189] If a star map falsely closed the western road, for example, then someone must also falsify weather, courier, and fuel records. [210] Those neighboring alterations formed a shape: a crown with eight severed roads. [222] She embroidered that shape into a ceremonial galaxy-chart, disguising it as the mythical Web of the Drunken Spider. [240] At court, she wore bells on her sleeves and announced that the Spider predicted a rain of blue turnips. [259] The courtiers laughed, except Prince Talan, who noticed her needle had pierced every province whose governor still supported his mother. [279] He found Mara at midnight beneath the observatory, where open shutters admitted leaf-scented wind and an echoed silence. [297] "Tell me the joke," he said, though fear made his voice smaller than the ticking instruments. [313] Mara showed him the stitched crown and explained that no single record condemned Ors, but their synchronized errors did. [332] To expose him, they would synchronize the observatory's eight beacon bells with the palace's eternal candle during the comet ceremony. [352] Talan frowned because bells carried sound, while candles carried only light. [363] Mara plucked a loose brass gear from the floor, balanced it on her nose, and said foolish machines merely needed better neighbors. [385] Then she explained: each beacon lens had an unused possibility, a nearby alignment that could catch one flame and cast the hidden archive onto clouds. [410] During the ceremony, Ors arrived with guards already stationed at every gate and the queen smiling under a veil of fatigue. [431] Above them, the comet brightened, white as a blade being drawn. [442] Mara stumbled theatrically into the altar, caught a flame from the eternal candle on a silver embroidery needle, and ran laughing upstairs. [464] Guards pursued the apparent idiot, leaving Talan free to lock the archive wheels into Mara's neighboring alignments. [481] At the dome, Mara touched the captive fire to the central lens exactly when the comet crossed its thread on her chart. [503] Eight bells struck together. [507] Blue light leaped from tower to tower, and the falsified orders, signatures, payments, and planned executions appeared enormous upon the low autumn clouds. [530] Every courtyard saw the conspiracy at once, not as accusation, but as Ors's own handwriting magnified across heaven. [548] Ors commanded his guards to arrest the stars, then the clouds, and finally everyone who could read, which was one command too many. [571] The captain lowered her sword, and the queen, hearing the crowd's anger gather beyond the walls, ordered Ors seized. [590] By dawn, the coup had failed before it could begin, although the city remained awake beneath slowly fading evidence. [609] Talan offered Mara a ministry, but she refused, saying ministers had doors and doors made poor ears. [626] She returned instead to her loom in the cobalt observatory, where the first frost silvered the leaves, and repaired the galaxy-chart. [647] In its border she added tiny figures, a courier, a cook, a jailed printer, and a frightened guard, whose overlooked stories had led her through the lies. [674] When visitors asked why heroes surrounded a drunken spider, Mara jingled her bells and claimed they were turnip farmers lost in space. [696] Most laughed, but some leaned closer, and in their listening the observatory's silence began at last to echo with voices power had missed. [719]</story>
