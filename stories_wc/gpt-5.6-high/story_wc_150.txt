When Mara Vey returned to Saint Orra after twenty years abroad, she found the hill split open and the village digging downward. [22]

Village elders had uncovered a catacomb orchard, a buried grove where pale fig trees survived without sun and their roots snaked between bones. [45]

At the center lay a sealed tomb believed to hold Ilyon, the pilgrim who had founded Mara's discarded faith. [64]

Mara had left as a believer and become an engineer overseas, learning to trust load tables more than prayers and arrivals more than promises. [88]

Her father had vanished on pilgrimage during her first year away, and every unanswered letter had made return feel like surrender. [109]

Yet after her mother's death, a final note summoned her home: Find what stopped us. [124]

The villagers remembered a solemn girl, but exile had made Mara warmly curious, quick to ask masons about their chisels and children about the orchard's whispering fruit. [151]

The tomb excavation was expected to take seven days, and Elder Senn allowed Mara below because the cracked entrance arch needed an engineer. [174]

On the first morning, workers raised a round stone from the soil, scarred by hour lines but missing its bronze needle. [195]

Mara recognized it as a sundial base, absurd underground, until she noticed tiny roads carved between the hours. [213]

Each road ended abruptly at the rim. [220]

An inscription named them the Interrupted Journeys, routes taken by refugees whose destinations history had erased. [236]

Senn confessed that Mara's father had searched for this stone before disappearing, convinced it mapped the founder's true pilgrimage. [255]

Mara felt old anger stir, then agreed to excavate, hoping the founder might restore her lost faith. [272]

Instead of advancing straight toward the tomb, she proposed purposeful wandering: each day the crew would follow one carved road through side chambers and record where it failed. [300]

Senn objected that wandering wasted time, but Mara showed him stress cracks radiating from blocked corridors; understanding the whole orchard was the safest way inward. [325]

On the second day, the eastern road led beneath roots wrapped around rib cages like careful fingers. [342]

There they found travelers buried with shoes facing opposite directions, suggesting not failure but deliberate return. [358]

On the third day, a northern passage ended at a collapsed cistern, where Mara rebuilt a stone channel and water began flowing toward the figs. [383]

The trees shivered though no wind entered, dropping silver fruit whose scent recalled kitchens each digger had lost. [401]

No one ate it, for wonder below ground deserved caution, yet they carried the fruit like lanterns. [418]

On the fourth day, soot marks along a western chamber matched rubbings from Mara's father's notebooks. [434]

His final line read: Faith is not reaching the promised place; it is choosing whom to guide when the road breaks. [455]

The words did not absolve his abandonment, but they changed its shape: perhaps he too had turned back for someone. [475]

On the fifth day, Mara set the sundial base into a socket beside the cistern and replaced its needle with her mother's brass hairpin. [499]

Underground there was no sun, but the silver fruit brightened as water reached each root, casting shadows across the dial. [519]

The shadows joined the interrupted roads, not at their vanished destinations but at the places where travelers had reversed course. [539]

Together those turns formed a new path spiraling toward the tomb while avoiding every weakened vault. [555]

On the sixth day, guided by that path, the crew excavated the tomb's threshold and found its door already open from within. [577]

Within stood no saint's coffin, only shelves of clay tablets naming generations who had sheltered strangers in the orchard. [596]

The last tablet said Ilyon was not one pilgrim but the title given whoever abandoned a sacred destination to lead the lost home. [619]

Beneath it, sealed in wax, lay her father's final letter beside a narrow breach opening toward the neighboring valley. [638]

He wrote that a flood had trapped families beyond the mountain, and that he was interrupting his pilgrimage to lead them through the orchard's hidden exit. [664]

The letter ended with a promise to return, a promise Mara could neither believe nor despise. [680]

On the seventh day, they cleared the breach and emerged above the neighboring valley, where an old woman had followed their hammering. [702]

She had been a child in the flood, and she wore Mara's father's compass around her neck. [719]

He had brought thirty people home, she said, but a final rockfall killed him while he held the passage. [738]

Mara wept without recovering certainty, yet felt faith return as a smaller, sturdier thing: trust in the turning back. [757]

She rebuilt the entrance around the sundial base, leaving its broken roads visible and the hairpin upright for whatever light found it. [779]

Below, the orchard stirred, roots easing among the bones, as if making room for another beginning. [795]
