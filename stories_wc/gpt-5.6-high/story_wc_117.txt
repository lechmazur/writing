On Sage Ridge, where wind combed silver grass above a buried forest, Mara Vale kept a one room clinic between the bakery and the bell tower. [26]

She was a mellow music therapist, known less for curing people than for helping them hear what pain had hidden. [46]

The curl had been clipped by his mother on his first birthday, tied with blue thread, and labeled in her careful slanting hand. [118]

Mara never asked him to explain it; instead, she played low chords on a cedar harp while he rolled the box beneath his palms. [142]

Her practice rested on ordinary magic: breath finding rhythm, grief finding shape, and one nervous system learning safety from another. [162]

Softly adaptive, her music changed with each twitch of Eli's shoulders or brightening of his eyes. [178]
