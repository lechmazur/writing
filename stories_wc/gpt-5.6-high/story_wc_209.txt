<story>In October, when the hill gardens burned copper beneath an early frost, Mara Vale arrived at the amber pavilion with ladders, pigments, and the quietly driven patience that had made her the city's most vivid mural artist. [37]

She had been hired to paint a procession of native flowers around the pavilion's circular wall, but its owner, an elderly botanist named Elian Rook, gave her one peculiar instruction: depict every blossom as it appeared after sunset. [75]

Mara accepted because years earlier her younger sister Nessa had vanished after asking the question Mara could never answer: why flowers bloom at night. [99]

While clearing a paint-stained cabinet, Mara found a collection of old postcards tied with black thread, each showing the same garden under different moons. [123]

The messages were addressed to Elian in Nessa's hand, although their dates ran from November to March, the winter after her disappearance. [145]

When Mara confronted him, Elian did not deny knowing Nessa; he merely said she believed the flowers were answering the cathedral. [166]

Before she could demand more, the first snow sealed the hill, and Elian collapsed from a fever, leaving Mara the pavilion's key. [188]

All winter Mara painted from the postcards, staining the plaster with moon-white lilies, crimson catchfly, and evening primrose whose petals seemed to breathe whenever clouds crossed the skylight. [216]

Yet she kept one blank arc above the doorway and watched the snow retreat by inches, waiting for Elian to wake and explain. [239]

Across the melting of winter's last snow, his fever eased while meltwater exposed a path of blue stones leading from the pavilion to Saint Orra's abandoned cathedral. [266]

On the first thawing dawn, Mara followed it, carrying the postcards, a lantern, and a shard of painted plaster. [285]

Inside, darkness pooled beneath the rafters, and the eastern window held a dawning hush so complete that her bootsteps felt like confessions. [307]

She aligned each postcard with the view through the hidden layers of etched glass in a cathedral window and discovered that scratches invisible alone joined into a map when dawn entered them. [339]

The map led not to a grave but to the cathedral's buried conservatory, where roots threaded cracked tiles and pale buds waited beneath sheets of ice. [365]

At the center stood a glass table etched with botanical diagrams, and beside it, Nessa's field journal sealed in wax. [385]

Its final pages explained that certain flowers stored sunlight by day, then opened in darkness to guide moths, but Nessa had suspected a deeper exchange. [410]

Minerals in the old glass bent dawn into wavelengths that woke the buried roots, while their nocturnal pollen preserved traces of whatever breath passed among them. [436]

She had come seeking a cure for Elian's failing lungs and had remained too long during a freeze, sheltering the experimental beds with her own body. [462]

Beneath the roots Mara found Nessa's silver bracelet, but no bones, only a human-shaped hollow warmed by new shoots. [481]

Mara had to reckon with the cruel possibility that her sister had not been taken from the garden but had chosen it, knowing the cost. [506]

Anger rose first, bright and useful, then softened into the umbral tenderness of loving someone whose last decision could never be forgiven or undone. [530]

As sunrise touched the etched saints, the ice loosened, and the buds opened one by one, not toward the sun but toward Mara's lantern. [554]

Their petals released a scent of turpentine and rain, Nessa's scent from childhood, and pollen settled on the plaster shard in lines resembling her handwriting. [579]

Not a ghost, not quite a message, but memory carried through root, moth, mineral, and light, the patient grammar of living things. [601]

Mara finally understood why flowers bloom at night: because darkness is not an absence to them, but a room where faint exchanges can be heard. [626]

She returned to the amber pavilion as the last snow slid from its roof and told Elian the truth, sparing neither Nessa's courage nor his guilt in encouraging her. [655]

He wept and admitted he had hidden the postcards because each had arrived after Nessa vanished, delivered without stamp by moths dusted gold. [678]

Together they carried the collection back to the wall, where Mara filled the blank arc with a woman kneeling among open flowers, her face turned away so grief would not become a cage. [711]

By autumn the pavilion glowed like a held ember, and visitors said its painted garden changed after dusk, though Mara knew the change belonged to those who stood quietly enough to meet what darkness kept. [746]</story>
