After the falcon returned, Mara Venn unlocked the rusted outpost by the frozen lake and listened to the hinges complain into the worrying tranquility. [24]

She was an atlas editor by trade, dry-witted by survival, and subtly confident in the way of people who corrected coastlines for a living. [48]

Her employer in the southern capital had sent her north to verify an old cartographic embarrassment: three great rivers whose printed headwaters occupied three different mountains. [74]

Mara had come for a less professional reason, to find where the rivers begin, because her missing brother's last letter claimed they began together. [98]

The falcon, Kest, had belonged to Elias, and its return that morning after six days away brought blue thread on its jesses but no message. [123]

Inside, the stove held cold ash, the radio muttered static, and every window wore a skin of frost except one clear oval above the map table. [149]

Mara breathed on that glass, then paused when condensation gathered not as a cloud but as branching silver lines. [168]

They resembled a watershed seen from heaven, though the droplets flowed upward and joined at a single point near the northern sill. [190]

Wedged beneath the sill was the dog-ear torn from her polyglot brother's phrasebook, its creases crowded with tiny translations of the word source. [213]

On its reverse, his cramped hand had written four words: Glass remembers warm water. [227]

Mara said, "An excellent note, if one is mapping a teapot," but her pulse quickened. [242]

She lit the stove, filled its kettle with chipped ice, and held panes from each wall above the steam, testing the pattern of condensation on glass. [268]

Each pane made one branching line, and all four pointed beyond the outpost toward a black ridge where no river appeared on any official chart. [293]

By noon, Kest was circling that ridge, a dark comma editing the blank sky. [307]

Mara packed rope, the phrasebook scrap, and her brother's letter, then crossed the lake because the shore route would cost a day she no longer trusted. [333]

The ice boomed gently beneath her, sounds too deep to be called cracks, while snow fell without wind. [351]

Worry came not as panic but as the calm certainty that something below was matching her steps. [368]

At the ridge, Kest landed beside a rusted survey marker stamped with the insignia of the vanished Northern Water Commission. [388]

The blue thread on his leg matched a strand frozen inside the marker's hollow cap. [403]

Mara unscrewed it and found a brass key and three coordinates, one for each falsely mapped headwater. [420]

When plotted in her pocket atlas, the coordinates formed a triangle centered exactly beneath her boots. [436]

A tremor heralded the opening of a narrow seam in the rock, breathing warm mist into the cold. [454]

She unlocked an iron door below and descended through pipes furred with mineral salt. [468]

The stair ended in a cavern where three underground torrents rose from separate fissures, collided in a luminous pool, and departed through channels aimed east, south, and west. [496]

This was the singular convergence her brother had described: not one river dividing, but three rivers arriving before beginning again. [516]

Beside the pool sat Elias, thinner, bearded, and very much alive, keeping one hand on a wheel that shuddered against the water's force. [539]

He smiled as if she were merely late for supper. [549]

"You took six days to read a window," he said. [559]

"The typography was poor," Mara replied, and hugged him without releasing the wheel. [572]

He explained that the Commission had built gates to keep the convergence balanced, then abandoned the outpost when maps proved cheaper than maintenance. [595]

A gate had jammed shortly before her arrival, and if he let go, pressure would reverse one channel, split the lake ice, and drown villages hundreds of miles away. [624]

He had sent Kest and left the clue above, trusting Mara to notice what ordinary surveyors wiped from glass. [643]

Mara studied the pipes, the three coordinates, and the colored mineral stains branching across the cavern roof like the condensation. [663]

Then she moved the eastern counterweight to the southern chain, making the three flows bear one another instead of the wheel. [684]

The mechanism settled with a sigh, and the tranquillity above ceased to feel like held breath. [700]

At dusk, brother and sister emerged to find fine cracks shining safely empty beneath the lake's blue ice. [718]

Kest flew ahead to the outpost, herald of a truth Mara intended to print despite every ministry in the capital. [738]

On the corrected atlas, she would mark no heroic mountain, only a small circle beneath the ridge and a note: Here, rivers begin by meeting. [763]
