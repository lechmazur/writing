At the bottom of Saint Orison, where four realities pressed together, Brother Vale kept watch over the dimensional anchor. [19]

The anchor was not a machine so much as a room whose walls remembered mutually exclusive histories. [36]

One wall bore scorch marks from a war that had never happened, while another smelled of orchards not yet planted. [56]

Vale was the abbey's loop pedal priest, trained to catch a spoken phrase, return it, and stack its echoes until a congregation heard the truth hiding beneath certainty. [84]

A loop pedal priest decorates with silence, so Vale furnished his chamber with felt, cork, and curtains that swallowed every accidental sound. [106]

Only chosen words were allowed to live twice. [114]

On the night the stillness began to unravel, the anchor's caretaker, Mara Venn, arrived carrying an artist's palette with one color. [135]

The pigment was Anchor White, a mineral paste that glowed only where reality contradicted itself, and its pallor gave the chamber a muffled clarity. [159]

"Something crossed over," Mara whispered, holding out the palette. [168]

Her voice reached Vale as two voices, the second arriving half a heartbeat early. [182]

The echo smiled before she did. [188]

Vale knew the symptoms: an intelligence from the Unmade Country had entered Mara and was learning to wear her sequence of causes. [210]

When it could predict her every choice, the possession would complete, and Mara's own future would become inaccessible. [228]

They had perhaps twenty minutes. [233]

The usual exorcism demanded bells, but bells would shake the anchor loose and let all four realities flood the abbey. [253]

Vale instead set his pedal between them and asked Mara to paint the intruder's outline. [268]

She touched her brush to Anchor White, yet laid down a stripe of absolute black. [283]

The contradiction spread like a crack across the floor. [292]

Around them arose an unraveled stillness, not quiet but the sensation that every possible sound had forgotten which world should receive it. [314]

Vale pressed the pedal and began to plumb the invader, not with commands, but through rhetorical devices. [331]

"If you are Mara," he asked, "why must you learn her?" [342]

The pedal returned, "Why must you learn her, learn her, learn her?" [354]

Mara's borrowed smile faltered, and white sparks trembled in the black stripe. [366]

"If perfect knowledge grants ownership, does ignorance grant freedom?" [375]

His task was not merely to expel it, but to illuminate hidden assumptions until their own shadows trapped them. [437]

He looked at Mara, whose left hand clenched against the right hand lifting the brush. [452]

"Can a person surprise herself while knowing herself?" [460]

"No," the intruder said too quickly. [466]

It was the childhood signal she and Vale had used when smuggling pears from the orchard, a deliberately senseless rhythm neither could ever predict. [505]

Yet the possessing voice continued denying surprise, unaware that its own vessel had just produced it. [521]

Anchor White blazed across the palette. [527]

His pedal multiplied the question, then clipped away words until only before remained. [674]

Then Mara tapped three, one, four, two, and changed the ending just to see him smile. [783]
