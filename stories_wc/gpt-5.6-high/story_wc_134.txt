In Bellglass Hollow, every column held a note, and footsteps played the musical crystal cave like rain tapping a thousand silver cups. [22]

Mara Venn lived beside its entrance, a clairvoyant famous for warning miners of split ropes, bad air, and marriages likely to echo poorly. [45]

Her visions were steadily unpredictable: sharp as glass about missing buttons and tomorrow's soup, but clouded whenever anyone asked about death or destiny. [68]

She preferred small prophecies, believing her gift existed to magnify everyday magic, the humble astonishment of reaching home before the bread burned. [90]

That belief began to crack after the pilgrim arrived, carrying no luggage except a lantern whose flame cast darkness instead of light. [112]

His name was Ilyan, and he had crossed seven salt deserts because an old lullaby promised a cave pearl beneath Bellglass Hollow's deepest choir. [136]

He wanted the pearl not for wealth, but to amplify his lantern until ordinary people could see the secret kindness holding their world together. [160]

Mara saw him drowning in music, then saw him crowned with dawn, two futures alternating whenever she blinked. [178]

"You must leave," she said, although the cave behind her chimed a welcoming chord it had never played for anyone. [198]

Ilyan answered, "If danger can hide from you, perhaps it is because it needs your choice, not your prediction." [217]

Irritated but unwilling to abandon him to either future, Mara followed him downward past quartz organs and violet stalactites singing beside his dark lantern. [241]

The deeper chambers glowed with bright darkness, each black ray outlining tiny beetles, mineral veins, and roots drinking patiently beyond the stone. [263]

At the lowest vault, overlapping shadows from Mara, Ilyan, and the lantern crossed upon a milky sphere nested inside a crystal harp. [285]

Through overlapping shadows, unseen forces came to inhabit the cave pearl, and the entire mountain inhaled with the soft surprise of a waking child. [309]

Mara had foreseen flood, fire, and falling stone, yet she had never imagined that memories themselves could hunger for human witnesses. [330]

They poured from the pearl as translucent hands, touching the cave walls and making forgotten daily sounds blossom into radiant music. [351]

A kettle whistled in amber crystal; a broom brushed rhythm from blue stone; a mother's tired laugh rang clear and golden. [372]

Ilyan smiled, believing the lullaby fulfilled, but every sound grew louder, until tenderness became thunder and the crystal harp began to fracture. [394]

The unseen forces were not kind spirits, Mara understood, but abandoned moments craving witnesses, multiplying because neither prophet nor pilgrim could release them. [417]

Her sight split again: in one future Ilyan shattered the pearl and silenced the mountain; in another, music buried the valley. [438]

Between those grand disasters, Mara noticed something her visions always overlooked: Ilyan's blistered hand trembling around the lantern handle. [457]

She wrapped his palm with her plain linen handkerchief, the same one she used each morning to polish dust from her doorbell. [479]

The cloth carried memories of countless unremarkable mornings, each freely cherished, and when it entered the crossing shadows, it sounded one modest note. [502]

That note gave the hungry moments a home without spectacle, and one by one they settled into linen threads instead of stone. [524]

The mountain exhaled; cracks closed, the harp softened, and the cave pearl dimmed to the luster of a common shirt button. [545]

Ilyan sank to his knees, grieving that his quest had ended not in revelation, but in a handkerchief that smelled faintly of soap. [568]

Mara laughed, partly from relief and partly because a clairvoyant who didn't see it coming was finally seeing what had always mattered. [590]

"Everyday magic grows visible not when we make it enormous, but when someone chooses to notice it," she told him. [610]

Together they carried the quiet pearl upstairs, while the cave replayed breakfasts, mended socks, shared water, and apologies in a gentle major key. [633]

At dawn, Ilyan placed the pearl above Mara's door, where travelers mistook it for decoration until their own shadows crossed beneath it. [655]

Then each heard one overlooked kindness from home, and Bellglass Hollow shone darkest and brightest whenever somebody stopped, listened, and remembered. [676]
