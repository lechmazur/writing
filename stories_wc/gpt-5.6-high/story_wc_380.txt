<story>Three days after the whale beaches itself beneath the east pier, Iven Vale notices that the harbor lanterns no longer cast their reflections downward. [24]

They hang instead in the air, thin golden coins suspended inside a gleaming silence that has settled over gull, wave, and bell. [46]

Iven is the dock-side lantern lighter, a widower who knows every wick by smell and every piling by the complaint it makes under tide. [70]

Since his wife, Sela, died, he has carried her pressed wildflowers in his breast pocket, though he cannot remember where she gathered them or why she laughed when she gave them to him. [103]

That absence is no ordinary grief; whole hours with Sela have vanished from his mind, cleanly cut away. [121]

He has remained on the docks to find where deleted memories go. [133]

The beached whale offers the first answer. [140]

At dusk, as Iven lights the lamp above its enormous glazed eye, the flame bends toward the creature and burns blue. [161]

In the whale's open mouth, seawater climbs its baleen against the slope, carrying loose nails, shells, and one silver button from Sela's coat. [184]

Iven follows the impossible current below the pier, where the old desalination works contain a salt crystal growing chamber abandoned after Sela's final experiment. [208]

Its walls glitter like frozen rain, and at its center float several pockets of gravity, invisible except where salt dust circles them like tiny moons. [233]

Their pull fluctuates mysteriously: one lifts his boots, another drags tears from his eyes, and a third tugs the flowers through his shirt. [256]

On Sela's rusted desk, Iven finds a burner, a copper pot, and a note in her hand: "What weight forgets, bitterness maps." [278]

She had always read danger through patterns in coffee grounds, an old fisher custom Iven had dismissed as theater. [297]

Now he brews the grounds left in her sealed tin, drinks, and turns the cup upon a salt slab. [316]

The residue forms a whale, then a flower, then a door beneath branching black roots. [331]

Iven understands: Sela made the chamber to catch memories before sorrow erased them, giving each recollection weight enough to survive. [351]

But the pockets grew unstable, stealing nearby memories, and when she tried to shut them down, they took her knowledge of the controls. [374]

The whale, crossing above the submerged machinery, had been seized by their combined pull and stranded as the tide withdrew. [394]

The coffee roots point beneath the slab. [401]

Using a lantern hook and his bare hands, Iven begins to exhume what Sela buried there. [417]

He uncovers a brass cage packed with labeled salt crystals: FIRST KISS, STORM NIGHT, HERON COVE, and IVEN FORGIVES ME. [437]

The last crystal is cracked. [442]

When he touches it, memory enters him with the force of a falling anchor. [456]

He sees Sela pressing seaside asters between tide tables, sees her confess that the experiment has begun feeding on the harbor, and hears himself promise to destroy it. [484]

Then he sees his own terror, his refusal, and Sela choosing to delete his memories so he will not follow her into the chamber during its collapse. [511]

She died sealing the largest pocket, but left the flowers as a key, their stems arranged in the same branching pattern as the grounds. [535]

Iven lays the pressed wildflowers across the cage. [543]

Each blossom points toward a crystal socket, and when he fits the memories into their proper places, the orbiting salt becomes still. [565]

The chamber offers him a final choice: release the memories back into every mind they came from, or preserve his recovered life with Sela alone. [590]

Above him, the whale groans, and the sound passes through iron, salt, and bone. [604]

Iven thinks of sailors forgetting their children's faces, vendors losing songs, and a great animal unable to remember the road of tides. [626]

He opens the cage. [630]

Light pours without noise through the chamber, across the harbor, and into the whale, while forgotten names rise on every tongue. [651]

The gravity pockets fold inward, bead small as dew, and vanish among the flower petals. [666]

By dawn, the returning tide lifts the whale. [674]

It turns once beyond the pier, breathing a silver tower into the brightening air, then swims toward deep water. [693]

Iven resumes his rounds, lighter in one way and heavier in another, carrying Sela's laughter where no pocket can hold it and no sorrow can erase it. [720]</story>
