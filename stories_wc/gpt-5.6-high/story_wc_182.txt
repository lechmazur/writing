<story>Three nights after the time traveler arrived, the bells inside Mount Orison began ringing notes no living clerk had ever catalogued. [21]

The mountain was a single labyrinthine bookstore, its galleries tunneled through granite, its shelves spiraling upward from damp fern cellars to a summit reading room above the clouds. [49]

Each bell sounded only once, leaving silence shaped like missing music, and Ilyan Vale, the resident arcane linguist, heard in those absences an unheard symphony. [74]

The traveler, Eda Rook, had fallen from tomorrow carrying a botanical diary bound in silver bark and a warning that six children were trapped in the store's erased corridors. [103]

Her rescue missions had all failed because every map redrew itself when she opened it, and with each attempt another child's name vanished from her memory. [129]

Only the diary resisted the change, although alternate pages described plants that had never evolved and blank spaces interrupted every description at the same grammatical joints. [155]

Eda believed the children had sought the legendary Library of Unlearned Lessons, a hidden collection containing every truth a person had once refused to understand. [180]

Ilyan had pursued it for twenty years, not for wisdom, but to recover the lesson he had neglected when his daughter Mara disappeared between two shelves. [206]

If Eda was right, Mara was one of the six, preserved where the bookstore kept mistakes that readers were not yet ready to name. [230]

Ilyan was carefully resourceful rather than brave; he packed chalk, twine, dried pears, a tuning fork, and receipts from books that no longer existed. [254]

At the Index Gate, a blind bookseller blocked them and recited the old bargain: one rescuer could enter an erased corridor only by surrendering the memory of whom they meant to save. [286]

Eda reached for the gate, desperate enough to pay again, but Ilyan noticed that the bells paused whenever the bookseller spoke a noun. [309]

The diary's blanks also replaced nouns, while its impossible flowers supplied verbs for opening, returning, remembering, and forgiving. [327]

By researching gaps instead of entries, comparing bell pauses with missing words and missing children, Ilyan discovered that the bargain had never specified whose memory must be offered. [355]

He asked to renegotiate, offering the bookstore its own forgotten memory: the botanical diary's unwritten account of how Mount Orison had first learned to grow shelves. [381]

The blind bookseller smiled as if a rest had finally resolved into harmony, stamped the blank pages, and unlocked a door drawn in dust. [405]

Beyond it, stairways crossed like silent violin bows, ladders descended into darkness, and millions of unopened volumes trembled to a rhythm too low for ears. [430]

Eda tied the twine at each turn, but the path consumed it, so Ilyan planted pear seeds in cracks and read their sudden roots as arrows. [456]

Where stone refused seed, he rubbed chalk over old receipts, revealing purchase dates that formed coordinates when translated through the diary's extinct flower names. [480]

Thus they reached a circular chamber where six children slept inside glass cases, each case shelved beneath a book bearing one rescuer's face. [503]

Mara remained nine years old, yet Ilyan recognized the stubborn tilt of her chin before he recognized his own younger portrait above her. [526]

A brass inscription said the cases would open when the rescuers read what they had refused, but Eda warned that her earlier selves had read aloud and been swallowed by their books. [558]

Ilyan listened instead, striking the tuning fork against each cover until the chamber filled with vibrations that his arcane grammar rendered as speech. [581]

His book did not accuse him of losing Mara; it repeated her last request, ignored while he pursued a rare inscription: Father, please walk with me. [607]

The lesson was not that knowledge had stolen his child, but that he had mistaken attention for a treasure he could postpone and later retrieve. [632]

He spoke no spell; he answered, "I hear you now," and Mara's case cracked from top to bottom. [650]

Following him, Eda and the echoes of four forgotten rescuers listened to their own books, and the remaining glass opened in bright, soundless chords. [674]

The chamber unfolded around them, cases becoming desks and blank walls becoming shelves, and Ilyan understood that they had found the Library of Unlearned Lessons only by learning one. [703]

Words flowed into the botanical diary's gaps, recording not impossible species but small acts of care that might keep each rescued child from returning there. [728]

Mara took her father's hand, Eda gathered the others for the journey home, and throughout the mountain the bells withheld their final note. [751]

In that living silence, Ilyan heard the truest music the bookstore possessed: not a triumphant ending, but room for an answer. [772]</story>
