In the dry age, towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes stood, each ribbed spire humming with the names of vanished clans. [24]
Through centuries of layered dust, those names had blurred into one low chord that trembled beneath every footstep. [42]
Orrin, the canyon's last harmonic stone skimmer, could ride such chords by laying his palms on shale and letting tuned vibrations draw him forward. [66]
Yet a fall had crushed his hip, anchoring him beside the western tower while winter approached. [82]
He met pain with weathered resolve, not because he hoped to walk again, but because he intended to learn to navigate without moving. [105]
The canyon people were historically reverent, reading old remains as counsel and forbidding any hand from altering the ancestral towers. [125]
Their Keeper, Maela, therefore refused Orrin's request to retune the western spire into a beacon. [140]
"Bone remembers what ambition forgets," she said, setting water beside him. [151]
Orrin answered that memory which could not guide the living was only another tomb. [165]
That night, while testing the floor for hidden harmonics, he found a stone arrowhead buried under his mat. [183]
It was older than the clans' flint, polished smooth except for three notches along its base. [199]
Unable to travel to the archive, Orrin asked Maela what she knew of it. [213]
She weighed it and frowned, for the notches matched forbidden cuts inside the tower's lowest chamber. [229]
"Our founders called this the Listening Point," she admitted, "but their record says its signal failed." [245]
Orrin set the arrowhead against the floor and struck its edge. [256]
A beat passed through him, climbed the tower, vanished, then returned from far upriver with a second beat trailing behind it. [277]
It was not an echo but a pulse left wandering, trapped among bends of stone since the founders had abandoned it. [298]
At dawn, Orrin began to appraise the arrowhead's pulse through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight, as skimmers once read ripples cast across sand. [325]
Each turn of the canyon pulse lifted the birds from one warm current and dropped them toward another. [343]
Their wheeling bodies made the invisible route visible. [351]
He spent seven days striking the arrowhead at different angles, mapping the returning intervals with pebbles arranged around his useless legs. [372]
Maela watched skepticism wear into awe as his pebble map reproduced every safe ledge, spring, and thermal in the canyon. [392]
On the eighth morning, the returning flock broke formation, and the pulse came back jagged with alarm. [409]
From the changed intervals, Orrin understood that an upriver shelf had split and would bury the migration road by noon. [429]
Forty gatherers were already beyond the bend, where ordinary shouted warnings would shatter uselessly among the echoes. [446]
Maela seized the tower key but stopped before the sealed chamber. [457]
To retune the spire would cut the ancient name chord from its walls. [470]
"Reverence is not silence," Orrin told her. [477]
"It is carrying the dead far enough that they can still save someone." [490]
Maela opened the chamber. [494]
Inside, carved channels converged on a socket shaped for the arrowhead, proving it had never been a weapon. [512]
It was a tuning tooth, removed long ago by guardians who had mistaken change for desecration. [528]
Orrin remained on the floor, listening as Maela fitted the tooth and turned it according to his measured beats. [547]
The tower groaned, shed curtains of dust, and sent the founders' pulse racing along a new course. [564]
Far upriver, the gatherers felt three clear tremors under their boots: left at the fork, climb at the split cedar, shelter beneath the red wall. [589]
Those directions belonged to the oldest traveling song, preserved in children's games after its purpose had been forgotten. [607]
The gatherers obeyed just as the shelf collapsed, and the flock spiraled above their refuge instead of scattering into storm. [627]
Hours later, Orrin felt forty answering footfalls join the pulse, and he smiled before anyone entered the chamber. [645]
Maela feared the names were lost, but Orrin showed her how to braid their old chord beneath the beacon's beat. [665]
Every name remained, no longer a weight upon the canyon but a compass within it. [680]
When winter sealed the roads, Orrin became the first Still Skimmer, guiding hunters, children, and migrating birds from his place beside the tower. [703]
The arrowhead rested under his hand, and the wandering pulse at last had a home without being imprisoned. [721]
Across the bone spires, the dead kept their voices, the living found their way, and Orrin traveled farther than walking had ever carried him. [745]
