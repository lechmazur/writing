After the shutdown, Mara Venn lived alone beneath the time dilation observation deck, where the station's echoed silence made every footstep sound inherited. [23]

The colony called her the blessed pariah because she had survived the reactor's blue flash and emerged able to sense the direction in which time ran cleanest. [50]

That gift had predicted the cascading clocks, yet her warning had also caused the panicked evacuation that killed eleven people in the eastern lock. [74]

Gratitude and blame had fused around her so tightly that, when the station closed, nobody asked her to board the final shuttle. [96]

She told herself she had remained to guard the dead, but really she stayed to turn confusion into exploration. [115]

Each year on the accident's date, Mara followed a family custom of sketching the night sky on anniversaries, begun by navigators who mistrusted maps drawn by strangers. [142]

Her mother had taught that stars remembered where a traveler stood, even when the traveler wished to forget. [160]

Holding it near the glass, Mara felt her strange sense pull not toward the interval, but through it. [272]
