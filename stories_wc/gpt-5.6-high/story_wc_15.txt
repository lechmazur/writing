After the tide pools emptied beneath Saint Orra Wharf, Mara Vey descended into the red canyon carrying a needle, blue thread, and a stolen copper plate. [26]

By daylight, Mara was the wharf's last weaver, mending nets while turning sailors' grief into verses they pretended not to hear. [47]

By moonlight, she sleepwalked, as everyone in Orra had since the earthquake broke open the centuries-old labyrinth carved into red canyon walls. [69]

Their wandering paths overlapped in dust, forming knots around collapsed shrines and ending at a door no waking hand could find. [90]

The council called the maze a ruin and had ordered its stones quarried before the next spring tide. [108]

Mara, hauntingly optimistic even at gravesides, called it an unfinished poem. [119]

She had stolen the storybook illustration plate from the council archive because it showed children following orchids through the same maze. [140]

In the old tale, the flowers led survivors from a drowned kingdom toward a city where loss was honored rather than hidden. [162]

Someone had scratched out the final city and engraved a quarry over it. [175]

That alteration was older than any living councilor, which made it precedent, and precedent was Orra's favorite name for fear. [195]

Mara entered after the tide pools emptied because only then did moon water drain from the labyrinth and reveal violet orchid roots. [217]

She threaded her needle through the copper plate's border, then through each root, sewing metal to living fiber without piercing a flower. [239]

Her grandmother had taught her that a net could guide as well as capture, depending on what the weaver loved. [259]

Mara loved the dead too fiercely to let their sanctuary become paving stones. [272]

At the hidden door, she tied off the thread, lay down, and waited for sleep. [287]

Her plan could work only via overlapping sleepwalking paths, because generations of unconscious feet had remembered turns that official maps erased. [308]

When Mara rose dreaming, blue thread unspooled from the plate at her breast, and every sleeper in Orra rose with her. [329]

Fishermen, widows, quarrymen, and councilors crossed the canyon in silence, their separate routes weaving one luminous pattern beneath the stars. [349]

Where two paths crossed, an orchid opened, pale as dawn seen through closed eyes. [363]

The flowers did not banish sorrow; their fragrance made sorrow spacious enough to hold a future. [379]

At the labyrinth's center, the sleepers surrounded a chamber split by the earthquake. [392]

Inside stood hundreds of carved names, including those lost at sea, those buried in canyon slides, and those removed from public record. [414]

Mara found her brother Iven among them, though the council had always insisted his failed rescue made him unworthy of memorial. [435]

The sight woke her, and waking broke the dream's protection. [445]

Councilor Senn woke beside her, saw the stolen plate, and seized the thread. [458]

"You would bind us to failures," he said, pulling until orchid roots tore from the floor. [474]

Mara grasped the other end. [479]

"No," she answered, "I would sew our paths together, then carve a path from ruin to reverence." [496]

Senn pulled harder, and the ancient plate bent along the engraved quarry line. [509]

Copper screamed, snapped, and divided the false image from the children and their flowers. [523]

The broken halves flashed, projecting both pictures across the chamber: quarry on one wall, sanctuary on the other. [541]

Around them, the sleepers woke and recognized their own night paths in the illustration's curling vines. [557]

They also recognized the names the quarry would destroy. [566]

Senn stared at his mother's name, hidden low among those condemned for opposing the old council. [582]

His grip loosened. [585]

"We cannot overturn precedent because a dream embarrasses us," he whispered, but his voice had lost its stone. [603]

Mara placed the two pieces in his palms and showed him the scratched seam. [617]

"We inherited broken precedents," she said, "so let us mend what they broke." [630]

By sunrise, Senn had revoked the quarry order, not as an act of mercy, but as correction of a fraud. [650]

The townspeople returned awake, carrying chisels, needles, and coils of wharf rope. [662]

Guided by the pattern their sleeping feet had drawn, they repaired stairs, bridged fissures, and stitched safety lines through the maze. [683]

They left every crack visible, for Mara argued that repair should remember the wound rather than impersonate innocence. [701]

Years later, pilgrims entered the labyrinth after the tide pools emptied and followed orchids to the chamber of names. [720]

At its door hung the mended storybook plate, its quarry half turned backward, its bright children facing in. [738]

Mara sat nearby weaving memorial ribbons, her verses gentle but unsparing. [749]

When visitors wept, she gave them the labyrinth's orchid solace and an honest path onward. [764]
