<story>After the first snowmelt, Mara descended through the thawing sinkhole into the bronze grotto under starlight. [16]
She carried no rope, only a bone-handled scale, a notebook, and the kerosene lamp burner that had survived her mother's drowning. [37]
In the villages above, people called her a fractal weaver, because she spun patterns that repeated themselves from cathedral windows down to the veins of a moth's wing. [65]
Yet Mara had come below not to weave, but to collect the weights of different burdens. [81]
Since childhood, she had believed sorrow possessed mass, and that grief shared precisely might cease to crush its bearer. [100]
The grotto breathed a shingled hush: overlapping drips, clicks, and mineral sighs settling over one another like slate on a drowned roof. [122]

"Hunger," read one; "Regret," another; then "Oath," "Exile," and "The Child I Could Not Save." [246]
When Mara touched the last cocoon, the grotto spoke in her mother's voice. [259]
"You weigh what you are willing to carry." [267]
