<story>Above the cloud sea, where towers rose like tuning forks from a drowned world, the monastery kept a misty courtyard above clouds. [22] Its brass gutters chimed with rain, its weather vanes hummed in six keys, and every loose screw seemed ready to join the tinkersong wonder of the place. [49] Only Lio, the seldom-heard balladeer, refused to sing there, although the monks had hired him to wake their ancient weather engine before the long drought emptied the wells. [77] He rarely performed because ordinary songs pleased crowds while hiding what listeners could not bear to ask for, and Lio wanted music to articulate unspoken needs. [103] Driftingly alert, he crossed the wet tiles as if sleepwalking, yet noticed a wrong note trembling beneath the drip of each eave. [125] At the courtyard center stood the engine, a glass organ wrapped around a lightning rod, silent except for a tapping inside its control cabinet. [149] Lio opened it and found no trapped bird, only a corroded flower press bolt plate knocking against relays whenever blue fire gathered overhead. [172] Stamped upon the plate was his dead sister's maker mark, a tiny moth, though Mara had vanished into the engine ten years earlier. [195] Then lightning divided the sky, and in the breath between lightning and thunder, green letters rippled across the courtyard walls. [215] They appeared through messages encoded in graffiti art: bright birds pecked painted locks, vines curled into musical notation, and a faceless child pointed toward Lio. [240] He understood that the old wall cleaning automaton had been stealing sparks from the storm to repaint one question: WILL YOU HEAR ME. [263] Mara was not haunting the machine as a memory preserved whole, but as a ghost in code, scattered through pressure valves, bell timings, and the automaton's pigments. [290] When thunder finally struck, the letters washed blank, and the tapping weakened. [302] Brother Ansel, keeper of the engine, confessed that Mara had designed it to summon rain, but during its first trial she had entered the cabinet to replace that very plate and lightning had taken her. [337] The monks had sealed her unfinished program, fearing it would burn the tower, and their silence had condemned both valley and ghost to thirst. [361] Lio touched the moth mark and realized why Mara's signals used pictures instead of speech: she had no single voice left, only systems that could paint, tap, or chime. [390] To answer, he would have to encode a ballad across all of them before the next thunder closed the circuit. [410] He set the flower press bolt plate back into its slot, tightened it with a tuning peg, and climbed the organ as rain began to hiss. [436] On the first flash, Lio sang one low note, a note nobody in the monastery had heard from him. [455] The gutters carried it into pipes, the vanes stretched it across the wind, and the automaton sprayed silver moths over Mara's painted child. [478] He sang not come back, which no code could grant, but tell us what you need. [494] Lightning answered, holding the whole courtyard white and weightless. [503] In that charged pause, vines of notation raced under Lio's feet and joined his melody, while valves knocked out words: OPEN THE EAST CISTERN. [527] Lio pulled the rusted lever there, and the engine groaned awake, not as a weapon against the sky but as an instrument asking it politely. [552] Thunder arrived inside the organ, colossal and tuneful, and every glass pipe poured rain into channels leading down through the clouds. [573] The graffiti child gained a mouth. [579] THANK YOU, it painted, then changed into Mara's moth and settled above the repaired plate. [594] By dawn, cistern bells rang from the valley far below, and the monks begged Lio to sing a victory hymn. [614] Instead he listened to the soft relay tapping, now steady as a second heartbeat, and sang a duet with the sister he could not restore but had finally heard. [643] Thereafter travelers climbing above the cloud sea found new murals blooming after storms, each carrying some quiet request from machine, monk, or mountain village. [667] Lio answered them all, for he had learned that wonder was not the engine's voice or even the rain, but the brave machinery of asking and being answered. [695]</story>
