<story>Upon the hush of budding hope, when dawn made every leaf seem newly invented, Mara climbed to the king's abandoned orchard. [21]
It crowned a verdant parapet beside a waterfall, where silver spray drifted through branches trained against the sky. [39]

Yet that morning she carried no pruning saw, only an heirloom seed sealed in a blue glass locket. [96]
Her mother had called it the Last Azure, though its shell was dull as an old tooth. [113]

Then the waterfall began forgetting its source, and each season another blue thing faded toward gray. [148]

"Plant it where two waters meet," she had whispered, "and give succor by capturing essence, never by caging life." [189]
For seven years Mara had puzzled over those words while blue retreated from the world. [204]

Its bark opened and closed like sleeping eyelids, revealing not wood but brief glimpses of another country. [243]
Beyond it, rivers climbed mountains, clouds nested underground, and forests blazed with colors this world had lost. [260]

The sound summoned Iven, the king's last gardener, who emerged from the ruined gatehouse with an ax. [312]

"Stand aside," he said, raising the ax. [336]

But the foreign river dimmed, and a startled cry echoed from across the trunk. [389]
Mara snatched the fruit away, realizing that simple theft would save one world by impoverishing another. [405]
Iven lowered his ax, for his fear had recognized itself in that distant cry. [419]
Together they studied the tree until Mara saw pollen shining on both sides of each opening bud. [436]
The worlds were not mirrors, she decided, but blossoms awaiting a shared bee. [449]

"Wonder keeps its color only when it travels," she said. [690]
