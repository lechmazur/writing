After the energy beings arrived, every clock in the borderlands began losing a different hour. [15]

No one knew whether the radiant visitors had damaged time, eaten it, or merely taught it to hide. [33]

Mara, a drifting roadside poet, followed their silent migrations from town to town, trading verses for meals and sleeping beneath abandoned toll signs. [56]

She claimed she wanted new metaphors, but secretly she traveled to discover the texture of time, which since her brother's execution felt rough as unfinished clay. [82]

At dusk outside Bellweather, a fugitive named Iven stumbled from a drainage ditch and offered her a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape. [107]

Its fibers were stained with blood, moon dust, and the blue sparks shed by an energy being when it passed through solid walls. [130]

"I climbed down, but my sister Lio remains inside," Iven said, "and tomorrow the magistrate will erase her past to repair the clocks." [153]

He had learned that the beings nested in a terra-cotta grotto under starlight, where they arranged stolen hours in luminous spirals. [174]

The prison stood above that grotto, and only Mara could enter because the beings answered not to keys, but to patterned language. [196]

Iven's impossible choice was plain: save Lio and leave the region temporally broken, or surrender her memories so thousands recovered their missing hours. [219]

Mara saw another choice beneath his, sharper and more private: help the man who had escaped, or protect the law that had killed her innocent brother. [245]

She tied the rope around her waist and smiled with ethically playful defiance. [258]

"Let us obey every rule," she said, "especially the ones nobody bothered to write." [272]

They entered through the ditch, descended the rope, and reached the grotto as constellations appeared through cracks in the prison floor. [293]

Terra-cotta saints lined the chamber, each figure holding a bowl of trembling light, while the beings hovered like flames remembering human shapes. [315]

Mara approached their central spiral by uncovering layers: first dust from tiles, then prison records pasted beneath it, then older poems scratched into stone. [339]

Every layer repeated the same pattern, three long marks beside one short mark, like breath hesitating before confession. [357]

She recognized it as the meter of a childhood rhyme her brother had used when afraid. [373]

Speaking that rhythm made the nearest being open, not like a door, but like a memory willing to be questioned. [393]

Inside it, Mara felt seconds brush her palms: velvet birthdays, glassy illnesses, coarse years of labor, and the slick instant of a blade descending. [417]

Time had texture because choice gave it grain; without choice, an hour was only light. [432]

Then it offered Mara authority over one preserved hour, enough to free Lio or restore the valley, never both. [476]

Mara refused the grammar of the offer. [483]

A poem, she knew, survived by making one sound carry several meanings, so perhaps an hour could carry several consequences. [503]

She looped the escape rope around the spiral, placing each frayed section over a long mark and its single unbroken strand over the short one. [528]

Then she recited her brother's rhyme backward, turning the pattern from a lock into a hinge. [544]

It revealed that her brother's final hour had not been erased; the magistrate had hidden it among the records because innocence would have ruined his career. [633]

Mara could expose him, yet doing so would also expose Iven, whose escape had broken seven laws and saved one life. [654]

At dawn she wrote the truth as a ballad, naming the magistrate exactly and naming the fugitive only as "a loyal brother." [676]

It was not pure justice, but justice with mercy hidden inside its meter. [689]

Lio carried the song through Bellweather, citizens added verses, and soon no prison wall could contain the multiplying account. [708]
