In the seventh year after the sun vanished, Sir Oran entered the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night. [19]

The withdrawn knight had crossed three provinces without speaking, carrying a rusted sword and the shame of surviving his entire company. [40]

Here, trees grew behind black glass, their silver pears chiming softly while roots drank tinctures from copper veins. [58]

The place was factually mystical: every marvel could be weighed, repeated, and entered into the dead royal alchemists' ledgers. [77]

Oran had come because one ledger promised a fruit able to capture the shape of possibility before reality chose its final form. [99]

If he found it, he might return to the battle at Harrow Ford and discover the road on which his soldiers lived. [121]

At the central tree, beneath branches grafted with tiny brass hands, he found no fruit, only an abandoned marionette theater program pinned to the bark. [146]

Its painted cover showed a knight kneeling before a queen whose strings rose into darkness. [161]

Inside, the cast list bore Oran's name, although the theater had burned eighty years before his birth. [178]

When he touched the ink, wooden applause rattled among the trees, and a waist-high stage unfolded from the soil. [197]

A marionette queen stepped through velvet curtains, moving through forgotten patterns that his sword hand recognized as the secret drills of his slain captain. [221]

She raised one carved palm. [226]

"Unsnap the scabbard," she said, "or the orchard will make your grief permanent." [239]

Oran obeyed, and his sword sprang free, not as steel, but as a bright cord tied to his wrist. [258]

The cord pulled him onto the stage. [265]

At once the greenhouse multiplied around him: in one aisle his company feasted beneath a restored sun; in another, they lived but cursed him for cowardice; in a third, he died heroically and left them leaderless. [301]

Each future hung from the branches like translucent fruit, ripening and rotting as reality shifts under the pressure of a single choice. [323]

Oran reached for the happiest vision, but the puppet queen looped her strings around his fingers. [339]

"That is not possibility," she whispered; "it is hunger wearing a mask." [351]

Her voice was his queen's voice, the one that had ordered the doomed charge and afterward denied giving it. [370]

Oran's silence cracked. [373]

"I followed her," he said. [378]

"I chose obedience over them." [383]

Speaking the truth made the orchard tremble, yet terror passed through him as shivering bliss, painful and clean as blood returning to a frozen hand. [408]

The program changed in his pocket, its final blank page filling with a diagram of strings converging upon the central tree. [429]

Oran understood then that the fruit was not grown by choosing a perfect future, but by releasing every future from the will that would imprison it. [455]

The royal alchemists had built the theater to test a guardian, and failed because each had tried to become the puppeteer. [476]

He lifted the bright cord to his lips and offered a murmured allegiance, not to the dead queen, nor to victory, but to whatever lives might freely grow beyond his knowledge. [507]

Then he cut the cord. [512]

Every marionette fell, including the tiny likeness of himself, while the stage collapsed into rich black earth. [529]

The possible worlds rushed inward, not erased but mingled, and the central tree opened one white flower. [546]

Within its petals rested a seed shaped differently whenever he blinked. [557]

Oran could have planted it at Harrow Ford, where it might have rewritten the charge, but he now saw that resurrection chosen for others was only another command. [585]

He tucked the seed into the program and asked the wooden queen, now inert upon the soil, what he should do. [606]

No voice answered, and for the first time he accepted silence as permission rather than punishment. [622]

At the hour that had once been dawn, he opened the greenhouse doors. [635]

Refugees waited outside, driven north by roads that rearranged themselves nightly and by rulers who sold certainty in iron cages. [655]

Oran, still withdrawn but no longer sealed, showed them the seed and told them honestly that he did not know what it would become. [679]

Together they planted it beyond the glass, each hand adding soil without claiming the harvest. [694]

Behind them, the orchard bells rang through endless night; before them, the seed sent down roots and waited, gloriously undecided. [714]
