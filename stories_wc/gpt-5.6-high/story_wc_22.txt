<story>After the reef died, the sea around Orison Strait lost its blue patience and began returning every wreck the old war had fed it. [24] At twilight, the channel looked like a blade laid between two islands, narrow enough to cross by cannon shot and vast enough to swallow history. [49] Along its eastern shore, a blooming jetty thrust through the gray water, crowded with bean flowers, salt roses, and lantern vines. [70] Mara Venn, the gloom-hill coachman, tended them after driving her black carriage down from the cemetery road. [87] During the war she had carried officers toward the batteries and brought back whatever names remained. [103] Now she carried compost, rain barrels, and a collection of dried seed pods that clicked behind her like gentle bones. [123] Each pod had come from a widow on one island or the other, payment for journeys no widow wished to make. [144] Mara meant to let a garden reclaim the battlefield, not because flowers forgave blood, but because roots gave blood no vote. [165]

The councils of both islands had tolerated her work while the living reef broke the storms and kept warships outside. [185] When the coral whitened, crumbled, and sank, each council claimed the exposed mud and sent surveyors to mark new gun emplacements. [206] They gave Mara seven days to clear the jetty. [215] She answered in Pelagic, a dead language once shared by the strait's fishermen, and watched the surveyors mistake her blessing for a curse. [238] She spoke six dead languages, learned from epitaphs, monastery ledgers, and the last passengers who still dreamed in them. [257] Yet no grammar could tell her where the poisoned mud would accept roots. [270]

For that, she listened to birds. [276] Over years of night journeys, she had learned that a curlew's falling whistle meant worms beneath clean silt, while three clipped cries from a tern marked floating fish and phosphorus. [306] Plovers stuttered over buried iron, where shells might still wake. [316] Thus Mara could fertilize the battlefield by understanding bird calls: she tipped compost where curlews fed, scattered crushed fish below the terns, and left the plovers' ground untouched. [344] Within a season, green lines began crossing the mud between old trenches, but the surveyors returned with soldiers before the lines could meet. [367] Their captain, Ilyan Ro, had once ridden in Mara's carriage as a terrified drummer. [381] He quietly warned her that both fleets would arrive at dawn, and whichever side planted its flag first would start the war again. [404]

That evening, as twilight compressed the whole sea into the shining lane beside the blooming jetty, Mara heard two black gulls trading an unfamiliar call. [429] Its rhythm matched the Pelagic words inked on her oldest pod: "Two voices above, one promise below." [446] The pod held seeds of water fennel, a plant that grew only above freshwater springs. [461] She followed the gulls' replies from post to post until they converged over a patch where fennel had flowered without compost. [482] Beneath its roots, Mara found a bronze ring set in stone. [493]

Ro helped her hitch the carriage team to the ring, and horses, roots, and tide together lifted a black tablet from the channel. [516] Across it ran Pelagic letters and the carved wings of both islands. [528] Mara translated aloud: the strait was to belong to neither throne, its battlefield was to be seeded after the reef died, and any dispute was to be judged where birds of both shores fed. [562] This was the submerged concord, sunk by ancestors who had trusted no palace archive to keep peace. [579]

By moonrise, soldiers faced one another across the garden, and their officers called the tablet a coachman's fraud. [597] Then Ro struck no drum; he broke his sword across the stone and read his island's wing beneath Mara's translation. [617] From the western line, an old widow recognized the fennel seed she had given Mara beside her son's coffin, and she stepped between the guns. [642] Others followed, first to inspect the concord, then to name their own flowers growing from the mud. [659] The officers could command soldiers to fire, but not mothers to stop kneeling among bean blossoms. [675]

At dawn, the fleets entered the reef's ruined gap and found no flags, only people from both islands carrying baskets along the green trenches. [699] The councils did not become wise, but they became outnumbered, and signed new copies of the old agreement on Mara's carriage roof. [721] Mara kept the bronze original beneath the fennel, where water could guard what paper had betrayed. [737] Each twilight thereafter, she drove more seed pods to the blooming jetty and let their small dry thunder answer the birds across the narrow vastness. [762]</story>
