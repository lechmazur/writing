When expectations collapse, they do not fall cleanly; they splinter, and every sharp piece remembers the hand that raised them. [20]

At dawn, Elias Vale sat on the warped ramp of the coastal kayak launch, listening to fog erase the harbor bell. [41]

Once, shipping companies had called him the surest navigator on the northern coast, before one bad bearing drove the Mercy Bell onto Blacktooth Reef. [65]

His error killed no one, yet it broke his inner ear, his career, and the trust of Mara, his only daughter. [86]

Now Mara was somewhere beyond the fog in a survey kayak while an unexpected squall shouldered toward shore. [104]

The forecast had promised a clear morning, the grant committee had promised funding, and Elias had promised himself he would not interfere. [126]

All three expectations had collapsed by sunrise. [133]

The harbor master had taken the motor launch south, chasing a false distress flare, so the rack beside Elias held one narrow red tandem kayak. [158]

In Elias's pocket lay his father's astronomical compass, brass rimmed and absurd beneath a sky with neither sun nor star. [178]

He had carried it on the Mercy Bell and blamed it for years although the fault had been his own hurried arithmetic. [200]

This was the backlash of regrets: the more fiercely he pushed the past away, the harder it swung forward to strike him. [222]

His handheld radio crackled, and Mara's voice surfaced through static: "Dad, my rudder cable snapped, and the tide is pulling me north." [244]

North meant the drowned cannery, a maze of pilings that could open a kayak like a paper envelope. [262]

Her voice returned, thinner: "I can manage, so please do not make this another apology." [277]

Elias almost obeyed, because cowardice often dressed itself in him as respect, neat and reasonable. [292]

Then she began to sing over the radio, merrily melancholic, the old shanty her mother had loved when storms frightened her. [313]

The signal died midway through the line about sailors who gravitate home, and silence made his decision before courage could. [333]

He strapped on a faded life jacket, shoved the red hull into the chop, and entered water that looked hammered from lead. [355]

Without a horizon, his damaged balance told him every wave was level and every level stroke was tipping him over. [375]
