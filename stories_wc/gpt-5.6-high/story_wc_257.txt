<story>After hitting rock bottom, Mara Vale stopped making mourning jewelry and slept beneath her workbench among curls of tarnished silver. [20]

For sixteen years she had set hair, ash, and tiny portraits into lockets, giving grief a hinge and a clasp. [40]

On the seventh morning of her collapse, she found a paper moth perched in the locked window, its wings folded from an old star chart. [88]

Coordinates had been written inside in Ivo's careful hand. [97]

They led beyond the city, through a quarry drowned in mist, to a brass door standing upright without any wall. [117]

Behind it waited a timeless observatory where dawn and midnight occupied the same blue dome. [132]

No clocks hung there; planets moved only when Mara breathed, and dust remained suspended like patient snow. [149]

A blind astronomer named Sen called them curtains of intention, woven to reveal not desire, but the purpose beneath desire. [187]

"Ivo came here to learn why your memorial flowers always seemed alive," Sen said, touching the moth. [204]

"Before the fever took him, he asked us to send you his unfinished lesson." [218]

"He had no right to leave me another thing to save," she whispered. [324]
