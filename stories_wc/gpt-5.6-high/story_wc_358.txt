<story>After the town went silent, Mara Venn arrived at the port with an accident investigator's case and no official welcome. [20]

Three nights earlier, a cargo drone had fallen without warning, striking the municipal siren tower at precisely midnight. [80]

The siren had sounded one immense note, then stopped, and by dawn the town had gone silent: not deserted, simply silent. [101]

Mara had investigated ruptured wings, false telemetry, and pilots who lied to survive, but never an accident that seemed to erase sound. [123]

Her only clue was a flight engineer's patch recovered from the drone's melted guidance cage. [138]

It showed a silver swallow crossing three blue rings, the insignia of a research unit dissolved twenty years ago after predicting an impossible storm. [162]

Mara pinned it inside her coat, where its rough edge touched her pulse, and began her survey. [179]

She did not start with wreckage, because wreckage preserved consequence, while she had come to cultivate foresight in action. [198]

That phrase had been her father's lesson before a preventable ferry collision took him and left her suspicious of hindsight. [218]

If she could learn how danger assembled itself, she believed, warning might become a deed rather than an epitaph. [237]

She raised a spool of silk thread, a compass, and a pocket recorder, then entered the container canyons. [255]

Her method seemed archaic, even foolish: documenting wind patterns between buildings by tying threads at corners and noting their angle, tremor, temperature, and scent. [279]

The port's navigation grid was dead, so she marked each junction in chalk and watched the fog breathe. [297]
