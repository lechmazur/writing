After the Butterfly Effect, maps stopped promising that two places would remain the same distance apart. [16]

A footstep could cross a kitchen, while the next wandered for a week through blue weather and returned older. [35]

In that fractured hush, cities anchored their doors to measured rituals, and nobody mocked a threshold for being sacred. [54]

Iria Vale, the corn maze architect's lost child, made her living testing those rituals for the Ministry of Necessary Distances. [74]

She had joined to discover why some thresholds feel sacred, although privately she wanted to know why one threshold had stolen her. [96]

At nine, she had followed her father into his celebrated maze, crossed an unmarked gap between stalks, and emerged alone three hundred miles away. [120]

Her father, Oran, vanished that same afternoon. [127]

Twenty years later, a summons carried Iria by cable balloon to the drifting prism terrace above Bellweather Harbor. [145]

The terrace was a slab of glassy stone that migrated with sunlight, casting seven pale versions of every passenger onto the clouds. [167]

At its center stood a single cedar desk bolted beneath a doorframe that opened onto empty air. [184]

Chief Surveyor Pell met her there, speaking softly because loud voices sometimes widened local corridors. [199]

"Three pilots have crossed this frame and disappeared," he said. [209]

"The fourth turned back, leaving this." [215]

He laid down the fourth pilot's logbook, open to a weather code obsolete even before the sky broke. [233]

The entry read: Butterflies west, cedar rain, home is nine grains beyond north. [246]

Pell expected instruments, but Iria ignored the frame and crouched beside the desk. [259]

Her father had taught her that wood remembered pressure, weather, and the intentions of patient hands. [275]

By analyzing the grain in wooden furniture, he could intuit where a room wished its exits to be. [293]

People called that nonsense before the Butterfly Effect and civil engineering afterward. [305]

Iria was featherly determined as she traced the cedar, touching it lightly but refusing to retreat from any splinter. [324]

This desk had once stood in the maze's planning shed, where she had watched her father design choices with string and corn kernels. [381]

The code was not predicting weather but describing the grain: butterflies meant cross-grain curls, cedar rain meant descending lines, and nine grains marked a route. [406]
