On the acid sea of Vitrion, where green waves ate stone and burped silver vapor, a gears-and-cogs workshop floated on ceramic pontoons. [22]
Its proudest invention was Pip, a pioneering cart glider with brass wings, three wheels, and the social grace of a falling cupboard. [44]
Pip was steadily curious, which meant he investigated every lever, insulted every safety notice, and survived mostly because disasters found him entertaining. [66]
His maker, old Mara Quench, had built him for one purpose: to ring a bell no one hears yet. [85]
The bell hung beneath the northern aurora, according to Mara, and would sound only when the world became ready for its warning. [107]
To locate that unborn readiness, she installed a yearning algorithm in Pip's axle, teaching desire to calculate what maps could not. [128]
Throughout the evolution of Vitrion, from crawling reef beasts to cities that argued with weather, the algorithm gathered every unfulfilled wish. [149]
It finally pointed not north, but downward, into the acid that kept the workshop afloat and everyone sensibly above it. [169]
Mara called this result mathematically rude, then gave Pip her kaleidoscope and a hunter's arrow tip recovered from a fossilized forest. [190]
The arrow tip bore marks in no living script, but Pip could transliterate them through the images in a kaleidoscope. [210]
Each turn shattered the marks into colored symmetries, and each symmetry matched a species whose longing had trained his algorithm. [230]
Together they read: THE BELL IS BELOW, AND IT IS NOT A BELL UNTIL SOMEONE LISTENS. [246]
Pip bolted his wheels to a diving rail, spread his brass wings as fins, and plunged before Mara could invent a prohibition. [268]
The sea nibbled his paint, swallowed his left mudguard, and whispered directions in the voices of extinct sailors. [286]
Pip followed, partly from courage and partly because losing a mudguard had made retreat look embarrassingly asymmetrical. [303]
At the sea floor, he found an immense mechanism assembled from shells, bones, anchors, and the discarded teeth of patient gods. [324]
Every creature in history had added something: a hinge, a prayer, a bad idea that later generations polished into wisdom. [344]
At its center stood a dome shaped like an ear, beside a bow whose string was drawn by the tides. [364]
The hunter's arrow tip clicked onto the waiting shaft, and Pip understood that it had never been meant to kill. [384]
It was a needle for the world's first phonograph, aimed at the groove carved throughout the evolution of life. [403]
Yet the bow would release only if Pip supplied the final wish, his own, and the algorithm had never asked what he wanted. [426]
For once, curiosity stopped moving steadily and became fear, because a machine's private yearning might be merely an error wearing perfume. [447]
Far above, Mara's voice crackled through his speaking tube, ordering him home with several inventive remarks about corroded warranties. [466]
Pip looked through the kaleidoscope again, not at the inscription, but at his reflection multiplied into a caravan of possible selves. [487]
One hauled kings, one carried turnips, one rusted unopened in a museum, and one rolled onward beneath unfamiliar stars. [506]
He wanted not merely to find the future bell, but to become the road by which its listeners arrived. [525]
Pip fed that yearning into his axle, and the bow fired with a noise like thunder discovering comedy. [543]
The arrow traced the ancient groove, while the ear dome amplified every hunger the world had preserved. [560]
No sound reached Pip, Mara, or the acid sea; instead, the vibration traveled forward through time, hunting a listener. [579]
A thousand years later, a child on a quiet planet woke with a clear note ringing inside her newest dream. [599]
She drew a winged cart, a floating workshop, and a route across stars humanity had not learned to name. [618]
Back in the deep, the mechanism opened a launch ramp, having received her answer before she was born. [636]
Pip shot upward, burst from the sea smoking and triumphant, and landed in Mara's workshop with two fewer wheels. [655]
Mara stared at him, at the hissing holes in her floor, and at the impossible star route glowing on his chassis. [676]
Mara asked whether he had rung it, trying and failing to sound like someone unimpressed by miracles. [693]
"Not yet," Pip said, unfolding his battered wings; then the unheard bell pulled him toward tomorrow, and curiosity supplied the wheels. [714]
