After the spirit leaves, the body becomes honest about its weight. [11]

Nara learned this from her father, who died beneath the magnetic arbor above the clouds, one hand closed around a knowledge fruit seed. [34]

The arbor's iron-veined trees leaned toward the northern star, while their roots gripped a floating shelf of black stone over an endless white weather. [58]

Travelers came there to ask the dead for direction, but Nara had come to reestablish an experiment her father abandoned when dying interrupted him. [82]

As a cuneiform translator, she was trained to resurrect meanings from wedges pressed into clay, not voices pressed into air. [102]

She was kindly methodical, labeling each brass instrument in large script for Orin, her father's aging assistant, whose sight had blurred. [123]

Orin feared her haunted ambitions, because grief had made the old scholar reckless, and he recognized the same bright fever in his daughter. [146]

"Your father wanted a bridge," he warned, "not an understanding." [156]

Nara set the seed inside a copper cradle and answered, "I want to understand the physics of transition, so no one mistakes a door for a road." [183]

Only the seed remained, hard as fired clay and striped with lines resembling the river signs in the oldest funeral texts. [227]

Through analogical thinking, Nara treated each stripe as a cuneiform stroke, each magnetic pulse as grammar, and each silence as the space between words. [251]

If writing carried speech across time, she reasoned, perhaps magnetism carried identity across matter. [265]

At sunset, Orin turned the arbor's ring-shaped branches toward the star, and Nara released her father's final recorded breath from a glass ampule. [288]

The trees shuddered, blue sparks walked between their leaves, and the seed rose from its cradle. [304]

For one heartbeat, her father's spirit appeared beside it, not transparent, but terribly solid, with rain shining on his beard from the day Nara was born. [330]

He looked at her with pride, then at the hungry machine, and said, "Do not make my leaving your life's only arrival." [352]

After the spirit leaves, instruments should settle, yet the needles swung harder and the seed split with a sound like a small bone. [375]

Roots tore free from the stone, tilting the arbor, and the cloud sea rushed close enough to touch. [393]

Nara saw the error at once: she had translated resemblance as identity, confusing the echo made by transition with the traveler. [414]

But the stolen memories included his, and Nara could not cure one absence by giving her gentle friend another. [471]

She studied the pulsing stripes, no longer reading them as a sentence, but as a clay tablet cracked along a repeated sign. [493]

Repetition in cuneiform could mark plurality; here, the doubled pulse meant the machine had mistaken many memories for one soul. [513]

Nara never heard her father's voice again, although every northward branch leaned within touching distance when she worked. [708]
