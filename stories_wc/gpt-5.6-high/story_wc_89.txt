The season called After the Phoenix Rises began when silver blossoms opened across Evernight, the fairy tale orchard, though no dawn had touched its branches in a thousand years. [29]

Beneath permanent moonlight, pears shone like bottled stars, foxes wore crowns of clover, and moths carried messages between sleeping roots. [49]

Mara, the orchard's beast whisperer, heard every message, yet listened most closely for the voice that had vanished from the world. [70]

That voice belonged to Ilyan, her husband and fellow keeper, who had died sealing the orchard's gate against the winter wolves. [91]

Mara remained because she meant to reforge their bond beyond death, even if the task offended every law of root, moon, and bone. [114]

On the first night of the new season, a magpie dropped a cracked fortune cookie into her palm. [132]

Inside lay a narrow paper fortune: "What love cannot summon, change may invite." [145]

Mara hated riddles, especially hopeful ones, but Ilyan had baked those cookies before his death and hidden them among the magpies' winter hoard. [168]

His handwriting made the scrap feel warmer than her skin, and grief became, for one breath, almost touch. [186]

At midnight, the antlered owl that guarded the eastern boughs bowed and told her the Moon Hart knew a road into death. [208]

"But he will answer only one of his own kind," the owl warned. [221]

Mara understood the fortune then, and wished she did not. [231]

To conjure the Moon Hart, she climbed the oldest pear tree carrying saffron, star anise, blue cardamom, and the last pepper from Ilyan's garden. [255]

The Hart could be called only via the scent of rare spices, because such fragrances reminded immortal creatures that small mortal pleasures mattered. [278]

Mara crushed the spices upon a moonstone and breathed over them until their perfume threaded the orchard. [295]

Constellations leaned lower; roots trembled; every animal fell silent. [304]

Then a white stag stepped from between two stars, vast enough to carry galaxies in his branching antlers. [322]

"You seek a dead man," he said, "but only a creature of my blood can follow his echo." [340]

He offered her a mouthful of luminous fruit, warning that it would begin a metamorphosis no human will could fully reverse. [361]

Mara recoiled, not from antlers or hooves, but from surrendering the hands Ilyan had held through twenty ordinary years. [380]

Her heartbeat remained paradoxically steady while terror crowded her throat, as if her body had already chosen what her mind refused. [401]

"Will I bring him back?" she asked. [408]

"No," the Hart replied, "for death is not a gate with a stolen key." [422]

"You may meet his living echo and choose what bond can exist between what he became and what you become." [442]

The fortune rustled in Mara's fist, and she remembered Ilyan saying love was attention, not possession. [458]

She ate the fruit. [462]

Moonlight entered her teeth; silver fur unfurled along her arms; her fingers tightened into delicate black hooves. [479]

She fought each change, weeping as her human shape loosened, yet did not spit out the shining flesh. [497]

By the time antlers budded from her brow, reluctance had not vanished; it had merely learned to walk beside devotion. [517]

The Hart touched her new forehead, and the orchard expanded into a dark meadow where every star was a listening eye. [538]

Across that cosmic meadow stood Ilyan, no longer a man but a gray wolf with a scar of fire across his chest. [560]

"Mara," he whispered, and because she was still a beast whisperer, she heard her name inside his single breath. [579]

She stepped toward him, but he retreated, ashamed that dying had broken the promise to grow old beside her. [598]

She told him the promise had changed shape, as they had, and changing was not the same as breaking. [617]

When their noses touched, the fortune's paper dissolved into golden letters that circled them like a tiny patient galaxy. [636]

Their bond did not pull Ilyan from death; instead, it gave his echo a path into every creature Mara could hear. [657]

The meadow folded, and Mara woke beneath the pear tree, changed forever, with the Moon Hart gone and dawn still impossible. [678]

A fox laughed with Ilyan's warmth, a moth beat his favorite rhythm, and deep below, roots spoke her new name. [698]

She mourned her lost hands and would mourn them always, but she lowered her antlered head to the listening earth. [718]

"I am here," she said, and from beasts, blossoms, and wheeling stars came his answer: "So am I." [736]
