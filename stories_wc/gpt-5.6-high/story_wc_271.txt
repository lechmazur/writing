<story>After the last lighthouse keeper left Cape Orison, the abandoned lantern tower became the rain cistern for an arboretum perched on a seaside cliff. [24]
Mara Venn tended its wind bent pines, salt roses, and medicinal beds, speaking little and grafting damaged branches with quietly talented hands. [46]
Visitors knew her as the reserved curator who could coax green buds from gray wood, not as a drone strike wedding survivor. [68]
Twelve years earlier, across another sea, she had been carrying jasmine toward her brother's bride when the music vanished beneath a mechanical hum. [91]
She remembered obeying her aunt's shout to freeze, then heat, earth, and a silence so complete that it seemed solid. [111]
Only Mara and a crumpled photograph of the laughing couple came out from under the collapsed courtyard wall. [129]
Now she kept the photograph in her pruning apron, where its creases pressed against her ribs whenever engines crossed the sky. [150]
The arboretum's trustees wanted to replace the dark lighthouse dome with a glass cafe, promising tourists, jobs, and cheerful views. [170]
Mara opposed them, but she also distrusted easy hope, the sort printed on charity banners while ruins still smoked beyond the frame. [192]
Her plan was to reopen the tower as a weather station and seed bank, to anchor optimism in realism with measurements, stored life, and shelter. [217]
At the public inspection, trustee chairman Pell dismissed her proposal because the old foundation appeared sound and storms, he said, were becoming milder. [240]
Mara's body contradicted him before her voice could: her jaw tightened, her injured heel burned, and nausea pooled beneath her tongue. [261]
A trauma physician had taught her to read such warnings through somatic markers, sensations by which the body recognized danger before thought assembled proof. [285]
She looked from the fresh hairline cracks in the tower floor to the gulls abruptly abandoning the lower ledges. [304]
Then the sea withdrew from the rocks. [311]
Mara shouted for everyone to freeze. [317]
The command was old terror, but what followed was trained attention: she felt the vibration traveling upward through her soles and watched dust pulse from the mortar. [344]
An earthquake rolled offshore, ringing the empty lantern chamber like a bell. [356]
Pell lunged for the stairs, but Mara seized his coat and pulled him beneath the central arch as window glass burst inward. [378]
When the shaking paused, she guided the group into the open cedar meadow, counting each person twice while the cliff shed slabs into foaming water. [403]
A child named Ivo was missing. [409]
His mother screamed that he had run toward the wedding garden to retrieve his red kite. [425]
For one breath Mara stood inside the courtyard of twelve years ago, unable to distinguish rosemary smoke from burning jasmine. [445]
Her fingers found the crumpled photograph, and the familiar ridge of its deepest fold became a pearl of stillness around which panic could break without carrying her away. [473]
She did not tell herself that Ivo would be safe; she noted the wind direction, the strongest path, and the twenty seconds between tremors. [497]
Realism gave her something hope could stand on. [505]
Mara crossed the meadow low, entered the garden behind a retaining wall, and found Ivo pinned by a fallen trellis, still clutching the kite. [529]
The next tremor began as she cut the vine cords with her grafting knife. [543]
She could not lift the trellis, so she sliced its loaded joints, the same precise angled cuts she used to save storm split trees. [567]
It folded harmlessly aside, and she carried Ivo back as stones rattled down the path. [582]
By evening, surveyors had found seawater in the cracked foundations and declared the tower unfit for crowds, though its central arch remained sound. [605]
Pell, bandaged across one cheek, withdrew the cafe proposal and asked Mara whether her weather station could include an emergency refuge. [626]
She answered that it must, along with sirens, cliff sensors, and drills that taught people when to move rather than merely telling them not to fear. [652]
Months later, the restored lantern room held barometers instead of tables, and drawers of seeds occupied the keeper's old quarters. [672]
Mara hung her photograph beside the storm map, not flattened or hidden, its damage visible beneath protective glass. [690]
On clear days, Ivo flew his repaired kite above the wedding garden while visitors learned evacuation routes among the roses. [710]
When distant engines made Mara's heel burn, she touched the photograph, named the present facts, and listened until birdsong returned. [730]
She never promised the cliff would hold forever; she planted, measured, prepared, and let that honest promise be enough. [749]</story>
