<story>After planting the wrong seeds in the monastery garden, Mara Venn noticed that the sky had begun to lean. [19]

She had expected silver barley, whose chaff preserved memories, but black vines rose instead and curled toward the gravitational anomaly above Saint Orra. [42]

The anomaly was a dim, circular absence that made rain climb, bells ring before they were struck, and yesterday occasionally arrive with different shoes. [66]

Mara was the abbey's celestial signal interpreter, trained to read the pulses that stars sent through the absence. [84]

For nine years she had translated those pulses into warnings about frost, births, and wars, always confidently uncertain: firm enough to advise, humble enough to revise. [110]

Now each signal repeated one sentence: UNBEND WHAT YOU WROTE. [120]

In the archive beneath the garden, history rested in a single enormous book called the Ledger, and whatever its appointed editor wrote there became true. [145]

The editor had died in winter, leaving Mara a fountain pen whose ink had dried up and whose exotic nib was fashioned from the translucent beak of a moon squid. [175]

Its ink was exhausted, yet the nib could still scratch words from the Ledger, erasing events as neatly as footprints from snow. [197]

Mara guessed the warning concerned her seeds. [204]

She opened the Ledger and found the line she had added last spring, when famine threatened: "The abbey receives silver barley from the southern caravan." [229]

She had edited history to save Saint Orra, but the caravan's merchant had then missed another village, where children starved. [249]

Worse, the barley's remembered harvest had displaced the local seed stock, and her mistaken black seeds were history correcting its own wound. [271]

"So I erase my rescue," Mara said, though her hand shook. [282]

Brother Elian, the gardener, caught her wrist. [289]

"Then our dead return to dying," he said. [297]

The strange comfort of his objection steadied her; someone else remembered that a clean correction could be another cruelty. [316]

Mara understood that the anomaly did not demand reversal. [325]

It demanded that she unbend history, which was different: a bent branch could not be made whole by forcing it backward. [346]

To thresh meaning from experience, she would need every grain of consequence, including those she wished were chaff. [364]

She summoned everyone linked to the altered harvest: Elian, the caravan merchant, two survivors from the bypassed village, the abbey cook, and a child born during the saved winter. [393]

The Ledger's oldest instructions said durable revisions could be made only through contingent relationships, because no event belonged to one cause or one owner. [417]

Beneath the hovering garden, where loose stones orbited their ankles, Mara asked each person what the barley had changed. [436]

Elian had lived, but his husband had not; the merchant had gained a monastery and lost a village; survivor Nessa had lost her daughters and learned medicine from the burial woman. [467]

The cook had stretched barley with bark, inventing a bread now traded across the valley. [482]

The child, Tovin, simply said, "I am here." [490]

Their testimonies rose as pale threads, tugged by the anomaly into a knot above the open Ledger. [507]

Mara placed the dead pen against her tongue, tasting dust, salt, and a faint bitterness like an unsent apology. [526]

No ink appeared. [529]

Of course not, she thought; history was not filled by authority. [540]

It was filled by relation. [545]

She passed the pen around, asking each witness to hold it while naming what they owed another person. [563]

Elian owed Nessa seeds; Nessa owed Tovin a future; the merchant owed the lost village a road; Tovin owed the dead remembrance. [585]

At the final confession, black sap welled from the exotic nib. [596]

Together, their hands around Mara's, they scratched out only three words: "from the southern caravan." [611]

Then, using sap from the vines, they added: "from fields shared by monastery and village, harvested in years when either hungers." [632]

The Ledger shuddered. [635]

Outside, the black vines loosened, not vanishing but flowering into silver heads streaked with grain as dark as mourning. [654]

Memories changed carefully. [657]

The starved children remained dead, because repair was not theft from truth, but a road now bore their names, and annual wagons carried food both ways. [683]

Elian still mourned his husband, yet remembered Nessa tending him, an encounter that had planted the promise of shared fields. [703]

Above them, the gravitational anomaly became a dark ring rather than a hole, holding the sky together like a repaired handle. [724]

Mara listened as the stars sent a final pulse, one she translated without confidence and without fear: NOTHING TRUE STANDS ALONE. [745]

Then Tovin took her hand, and beneath the gently falling rain, everyone went to harvest what their mistakes had grown. [765]</story>
