After the leash broke, Mara did not run toward the village, though freedom had always sounded like a road going there. [21]

She climbed instead to the veiled ridgeway, where fog braided the pines and the sea breathed below like a sleeping mother. [42]

Around her wrist hung the snapped leather, its silver clasp knocking softly against the old brass ticket punch she carried. [62]

The punch had belonged to her father, ferryman of the western inlets, and its jaws pressed a tiny star into any ticket. [84]

He had vanished three winters before, leaving Mara with his tools, his debts, and a final unsigned note. [102]

"Unused courage waits where the shore forgets its name," the note said, and nothing more. [117]

Mara had kept a vigil in twilight each evening since, watching from the ridge for his green lantern. [135]

Her aunt had tied the leash to Mara's wrist during the worst storms, fearing grief would lead the girl over the cliffs. [157]

Tonight lightning had freed her, and the broken strap felt less like escape than permission. [172]

At the ridgeway's end stood a white gate that opened not onto land, but onto a narrow quay under the mist. [193]

Three wise ferry guides waited there: Sella with her shell earrings, Oren with his blind eyes, and little Bram, oldest of all. [215]

"We open only after the leash breaks," Sella said, as if weather and sorrow kept appointments. [231]

Mara showed them the note, and Oren turned his sightless face toward the breathing dark. [246]

"I need to find where unused courage waits," she said. [256]

"Then you must traverse the Unnamed Shore," Bram replied, "but no boat reaches it directly." [271]

They boarded a blue ferry whose mast leaned like beach grass, and Sella asked Mara for her fare. [289]

Mara had no coin, so she fed her father's blank ticket into the punch and squeezed. [305]

A star fell from its jaws, neat and bright, and all three guides bowed. [319]

"That stamp buys passage through indirect routes," Oren said, "which are costly only to those in haste." [336]

They sailed first upriver, away from the sea, while the guides hummed a shoreline lullaby about foam returning every borrowed name. [357]

The tune was low and tidal, and Mara's fear settled beside her instead of steering. [372]

At Reed Widow Island, they rescued a cormorant tangled in red cord; at Bell Narrows, they waited for drowned bells to finish ringing. [395]

At every delay Mara asked whether they had lost the way, but Bram was gently persistent: "The way is learning you." [416]

Near midnight they found a boy clinging to a buoy, too ashamed to admit he had rowed out to prove himself brave. [438]

Mara threw him the broken leash, braced her feet, and pulled until her palms bled. [453]

When the boy was safe, the fog opened upon a crescent beach beneath the far side of the same veiled ridgeway. [474]

Hundreds of green lanterns stood unlit along the sand, each bearing the name of someone who had mistaken courage for fearlessness. [495]

Mara found her father's lantern beside one marked with her own name. [507]

"Is he here?" she whispered, but Sella shook her head. [517]

"He reached this shore," Sella said, "then spent his courage sailing into a storm to save strangers." [534]

"Courage does not wait here because it was unused," Oren added; "it waits because someone has not yet chosen its use." [555]

Mara touched her lantern, understanding that her father had not abandoned her, and that grief had never been a leash he tied. [577]

She lifted the punch, stamped her star into the metal door, and placed the lantern in the rescued boy's hands. [597]

"For tomorrow," she told him. [602]

Her own lantern kindled at once, not green like her father's, but gold as the last line of sunset. [621]

The guides smiled, for the indirect journey had brought Mara to the place she watched from home, yet changed who was watching. [643]

They ferried back beneath paling stars, still humming, while the sea rocked the rescued boy into sleep. [660]

By dawn, Mara stood again at the white gate, with the snapped leash coiled in her pocket and the ticket punch warm in her hand. [685]

She could have gone home, but below the ridge a storm bell called, and one ferry berth remained empty. [704]

Sella offered her the oar without ceremony. [711]

Mara took it, not because fear had ended, but because fear now knew its proper seat beside her. [729]

Then four wise guides pushed from the veiled ridgeway, following the long route toward whoever waited beyond the fog. [748]
