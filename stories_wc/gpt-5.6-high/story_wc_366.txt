<story>After the lamp flickered out on Saint Orra Light, Elias Vane kept his supply boat beyond the reef until dawn. [20]

Elias was a reticent lighthouse supply skipper, more comfortable reading tides than faces, but he noticed what lonely people left unsaid. [41] His silence was warmly observant: he remembered which keeper needed cinnamon, whose boots leaked, and who pretended not to fear fog. [62] Yet Saint Orra had no keeper now; Mara Quill had vanished from its haunted lantern room three winters earlier. [81] Since then, sailors claimed her shadow polished the dead lens, while the harbor master kept ordering oil for an empty tower. [102]

That morning, duty finally pushed Elias ashore with a crate of fuel and a coil of wick. [119] On the path, five beach stones stood in a stack: broad, narrow, broad, narrow, white. [134] Mara had taught him that way of stacking stones as messages when storms stole flags: alternating sizes meant danger, and white meant wait. [157] Someone living had built it after the lamp flickered out. [167]

Elias waited, hearing only gulls and the bell beneath the drowned chapel. [179] Then he found a green piece of sea glass wedged under the white stone, wrapped in paper. [196] The letter bore no name. [201]

"I am afraid upstairs," it read. [207] "If you have ever been afraid, answer, but do not sign." [218]

Elias knew the harbor's anonymous letter chain, begun by widows who slipped confessions into outgoing supply crates and received kindness from unseen hands. [241] He had carried it for years without adding a word. [251] Now he wrote on the fuel invoice, "Fear is weather, not a verdict." [264] He left the unsigned answer beneath a new stack meaning I will enter, then climbed. [279]

Inside, damp walls breathed salt, and portraits of former keepers watched him ascend with silvered eyes. [295] At the lantern room, cold blue radiance pulsed through the extinguished lens, though no flame burned. [311] Mara stood within it, translucent as moonlit surf, one hand pressed against the glass. [325] Beside her crouched a girl of twelve, the harbor master's daughter Nell, clutching a packet of anonymous letters. [343]

Nell had come seeking the unknown person whose replies had steadied her through her mother's illness. [359] Instead, when darkness fell, the room had locked around her, and Mara's ghost could not cross the lens to help. [379]

"She wrote the answers," Nell whispered, pointing to Mara. [388]

Mara shook her head and pointed to a rusted speaking tube where hundreds of folded notes glimmered like captive moths. [408] The chain had passed through Saint Orra for decades, each frightened stranger protecting another without needing recognition. [425] That accumulated mercy had lit the beacon after Mara died trying to protect a ship in a gale. [442] But tonight Nell had opened every letter, searching for one savior, and the shared light had broken. [459]

Beyond the windows, pale figures rose from the shoals toward the dark tower, sailors whom Saint Orra had failed. [478] Elias wanted to run, yet Nell looked exactly as every silent passenger had looked when boarding his boat: ashamed of needing shelter. [500]

For years, he had wished to bottle the feeling of safety, to cork it against loss and hand it intact to whoever trembled. [523] He understood now that safety spoiled when hoarded; it lived only while being passed onward. [538]

He placed the sea glass in the cold lamp cup, where its green curve gathered Mara's glow. [555] Then he took Nell's first letter, added his unsigned reply, and fed both into the flame channel. [572]

"Write to the next afraid person," he told her. [581]

Nell wrote quickly, apologizing for opening the chain and promising to protect its next secret. [596] When she slid the page after his, the sea glass kindled, not with fire, but with the tender green of shallow water. [618] Mara smiled, and the lens began turning. [625]

Its beam swept the approaching dead, changing each hard face into the remembered face of someone once welcomed home. [644] One by one, they lowered their hands and dissolved into luminous rain. [656] Mara faded last, leaving Elias with a luminescent ache, grief bright enough to guide by. [671]

At dawn, he and Nell descended to find a fresh stack on the path: white, broad, broad, narrow. [689] In Mara's code it meant safe passage, and beneath it waited another nameless letter. [703] Elias tucked the letter inside his coat instead of a bottle. [714]

Thereafter, he answered on every voyage, while Nell rebuilt the stacks and Saint Orra shone with all the fears that kindness had carried. [737]</story>
