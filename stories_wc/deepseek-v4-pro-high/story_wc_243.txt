The walnut-ink archivist came to the blooming jetty at twilight, when the salt wind reversed and the first white flowers opened in the cracks of the old stone. [28]
She carried a leather satchel of notebooks, a tin of crushed galls, and a brass locket whose photograph had faded to a pale question. [52]
Her work was to preserve the weight of small moments, and she did it with a gently thorough attention that made even the fall of a petal seem necessary. [81]
She knelt on the jetty, unlatched the locket, and looked at the photograph of a woman standing in this same spot years ago. [104]
The face was almost gone, but the memory was not. [114]
At this hour, she always let the past steep like dark tea in the last light. [130]
Tonight, however, the context changed. [135]
A flight of terns wheeled upward from the rocks, and the water stilled as if the sea had drawn a breath. [156]
The archivist understood events not through grand designs but through proximate causes, so she sat very still and asked what had shifted. [178]
The answer arrived as a warm pressure at her wrist, then a voice she had not heard since the winter of the covenant. [201]
"I knew you would keep the archive," the voice said, and it came not from the air but from the locket, where the photograph now glowed like a sunken coin. [231]
The archivist closed her eyes and let the haunted euphoria rise through her chest. [245]
"This covenant is not a grave," she whispered. [253]
"It is a thing that regenerates when context changes," the photograph answered. [265]

The woman from the photograph stood at the edge of the jetty, barefoot among the flowering stems, solid enough to cast a shadow in the violet dusk. [296]
"Show me what you have kept," the woman said. [305]

"This is how you keep me alive," the woman said. [371]
The archivist replied, "I trace you through proximate causes, because one small moment leads to the next, and I cannot let the chain break." [395]
