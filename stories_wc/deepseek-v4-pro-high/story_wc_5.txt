In a withered orchard deep in a subarctic crater, Sable knelt among frost-cracked apple trees. [15]
The world held its breath around her, windless and gray with the long arctic night. [30]
She was a flash-paper alchemist, and her hands knew how to turn old rags into sheets that burned without leaving ash. [51]
In front of her, a shallow bowl of frozen clay held a storybook illustration plate, copper-dark and small enough to fit in her palm. [75]
The plate showed a girl carrying a lantern through an orchard of flowering trees, but Sable had never seen the printed page it came from. [100]
She needed that image because it was the only map of what the orchard had once been. [117]
All around her lay discarded things: broken ladder rungs, rusted tin lids, cracked jars, and the black knots of long-dead roots. [138]
Sable arranged them into patterns, spirals and branching lines that copied the rhythm she felt through the frozen ground. [157]
Her fingers placed each scrap while her pulse beat inside her ears, and she listened for the slower heartbeat beneath the orchard. [179]
That was her work, to translate heartbeats into maps. [188]
She smoothed a sheet of flash paper over the plate and folded its edges into tiny pockets. [205]
Into each pocket she tipped a pinch of safekept starlight, a silver dust she had collected in stoppered glass on clear nights. [227]
Starlight could not be held by iron or stone, but flash paper would cradle it long enough for her to work. [248]
The world held its breath more tightly, and even the brittle twigs stopped ticking as the temperature dropped. [266]
Sable demonstrated patience physically: she did not shiver, did not blow on her fingers, did not let her shoulders rise. [286]
Frost grew in fine needles along her sleeves while she waited for the pulse under the ground to align with her own. [308]
She began to move only when the pulse came, faint as the tap of a root against stone. [326]
Her hands snapped open, and the first pocket of flash paper caught the cold air. [341]
A point of starlight flared white, then blue, and the paper vanished into a brief flower of light. [359]
She pressed the storybook plate down over the burning dust and heard the copper tick. [374]
The image of the lantern-bearing girl drank the light, and a new line etched itself across the orchard scene. [393]
That was how she sealed each heartbeat into the plate, not with wax or solder but with a flash of starlight. [414]
One by one, she touched the pockets lined along the spirals and made the light leap from scrap to scrap. [434]
Rusted tin reflected the blue flares, and broken glass bent them into tiny rainbows on the snow. [451]
The pattern was no longer a pile of discarded things; it was a nervous system drawn in fire. [469]
Sable moved along its branches with her last sheet of flash paper, following the heartbeat as it strengthened. [487]
The plate grew warm in her palm, and the etched lines began to glow like a live map. [505]
At the center of the spiral, she set the plate on the frozen clay and folded her hands beneath her coat. [526]
She bowed her head, and for seven heartbeats she did not move. [538]
The orchard answered. [541]
A sigh of warmth rose from the crater floor, not enough to melt the frost but enough to make the dead twigs straighten. [564]
The safekept starlight in the plate flared once, gentle as a lamp turned low, and then settled into the copper. [584]
Sable lifted the storybook illustration plate and saw the new map burning faintly on its surface: a heart-shaped pattern of lines where every discarded scrap had lain. [611]
She pressed it to her chest, and the warmth of the sealed map matched the beating beneath her ribs. [630]
For the first time in years, the withered orchard did not feel like a grave. [645]
Somewhere under the crater, a root was still alive, and its heartbeat had been translated into silver lines. [663]
Sable stood slowly, leaving the scattered scraps in their pattern, because a finished map should not be disturbed before it cooled. [684]
The world still had not exhaled. [690]
She walked out of the orchard with the plate tucked inside her coat, her boots printing the only sound into the snow. [712]
Behind her, a faint blue light pulsed once along the discarded patterns, then faded into the moving stillness of the arctic night. [734]
