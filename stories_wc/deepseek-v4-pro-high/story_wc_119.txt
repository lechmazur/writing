After last orders at the Gull's Cry, Silas Marlin rowed back to the rocky island alone, carrying a tin of blank fortune slips and an old unease. [27] He was the cryptic fortune cookie writer for the Noodle Lantern, and his guarded one-liners had become an open secret along the waterfront. [50] People passed the little slips around the pub because, without intending to, Silas had become accidentally prophetic. [67] The island itself was a familiar enigma, all its dangers known and ignored until the tide changed. [84] Last month he had written, "A cold bell wakes a warmer door," and the harbor master found his missing son at a snowbound church. [108] The week before he had written, "Your quietest tool will speak loudest," and the locksmith won a radio contest. [127] Silas hated the accident of it; he wanted to transpose life into a new key, to be the composer rather than the struck chord. [151] Tonight, after last orders, the mysterious lighthouse on the rocky island seemed to lean toward him as if it knew his decision. [173] He climbed the spiral stairs, set down his tin, and opened the keeper's log he had read a hundred times. [193] This time the leather spine crackled wrong, and a pair of spycraft microdot film specs slid from a split in the binding. [215] They looked like ordinary reading glasses, but the lenses held tiny concentric rings that made the shadows shift. [233] He put them on and turned to his blank fortune slips. [244] Under the specs, each slip shimmered with a microdot beside the printed red star. [258] Silas had never placed those dots there. [265] He carried the slips to the lens room and held one to the low moon. [280] The microdot opened under the film specs into a line of text: "North current, two bells after last orders, keep the light steady." [303] He checked another slip and found a different rendezvous, and another, all written in miniature logistics. [319] His fortunes were open secrets in the truest sense: anyone could read the vague poetry, but only the film specs revealed the instructions underneath. [343] Someone in the supply chain had been using his cryptic lines as cover for spycraft traffic. [359] The accidental prophecies were not accidents at all; the messages directed actions that made his words seem fated. [377] Silas felt the shock turn to a strange, clarifying anger. [387] He would not simply expose the traffic; he would orchestrate one final message and break the loop himself. [405] He selected a blank slip and wrote a fortune in his usual unknowable style: "When the tide forgets its name, follow the light that blinks twice." [431] Then he drew a microdot of his own beneath the red star, copying the method with a needle and a drop of iron gall ink. [456] His hidden text read: "Meeting moved to the south reef, one hour after midnight; come alone." [472] He folded the slip and planted it in the courier's dead drop beneath the lighthouse steps. [488] He knew the south reef was a trap of rips and stone, and he would do it by directing flow rather than forcing the outcome. [513] He climbed to the lamp and set the clockwork so the beam would hold longer on the false channel toward the south reef. [536] At midnight he watched from the gallery as a trawler without lights aimed for the path he had directed. [555] The boat made it forty yards before its hull struck the submerged reef and listed hard. [571] Silas rowed out and pulled a sodden courier from the water. [582] He left the courier, bound with fishing line, at the Gull's Cry with a note for the coast guard. [601] By morning his accidental prophecies had ended. [608] He returned to the lighthouse, wiped the spycraft microdot film specs clean, and burned the remaining blank slips in the brazier. [629] He wrote one last fortune for himself, not hidden, on a clean white slip: "You are the light, not the lamp." [650] That one did not need to come true; he had already transposed his life into a new key. [668]
