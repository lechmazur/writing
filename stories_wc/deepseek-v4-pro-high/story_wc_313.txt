Lena Voss walked the levee at twilight, her boots finding cracks in the old concrete long before her eyes could. [20]
She had been a vigilant levee warden for eleven years, and she trusted the weight of the reservoir against her spine like a living thing. [45]
On Midnight Ridgeway above the water, the wind chime on her cabin porch fell silent, and the world dropped into a mirrored hush. [68]
The drought had lowered the lake enough for the old town of Marrow to begin resurfacing, and tonight the mud flats stretched like a bruise under the first stars. [97]
She scanned the flats with a lens, counting the wet seams where the levee needed repair. [113]
A small blue rectangle glinted near a rusted pump. [122]
Lena slid down the riprap and knelt beside it. [131]
It was a sapphire-hued liqueur label, still gummed to a curve of old glass. [145]
She used her sleeve to expunge the silt, careful not to erase the faded letters. [160]
The label read Marrow Sapphire Cordial, and beneath it someone had scrawled the year of the flood. [177]
Farther on, a photograph lay flat against the mud as if the water had placed it there. [194]
Another picture showed the same cordial bottle raised in a toast inside a bright saloon. [209]
Behind it, a third photograph curled around a broken porch post farther along the drowned street. [225]
She followed the trail of photographs past the old church steps and the collapsed hotel, each image tugging her down the resurfaced main road. [249]
The pictures were not random. [254]
They were arranged in a deliberate line that led toward a sagging house at the end of Marrow's old orchard road. [275]
Lena hesitated, because she had never admitted that she knew that house. [287]
The next photograph showed a woman in a green dress holding a child on the same porch. [304]
The child was Lena, age four, squinting at the camera. [314]
Beside them on the rail stood the sapphire cordial, its label bright and new. [328]
She remembered the sweet burn of a stolen sip and the laughter of her grandmother, a forgotten memory the flood had buried until now. [352]
She had been born in Marrow before the valley was drowned, but her family had moved away so young that she had let the town become only a name on a map. [384]

Lena gathered each picture with the precisely humane care she used when evacuating families before a storm. [423]
She wiped the mud from the final photograph and tucked the broken label into her jacket pocket. [440]
Her reason for patrolling no longer felt like only concrete and seepage. [452]
She wanted to spin these scattered stories into shared meaning, to give the returning town a memory instead of a spectacle. [473]
By dawn she would notify the historical society and ask for help preserving what the water gave back. [491]

The wind chime stayed silent, and the lake mirrored the stars with a hush that asked nothing of her. [549]
Lena climbed back to the levee, carrying the photographs like evidence of an inheritance. [563]
She would map every structure that resurfaced and mark the places where stories had been lived. [579]

The levee still needed her, but now she guarded more than the valley below. [646]

She would let this one rise, but she would not let it drown anyone. [670]
