<story>In the mirror world, the alchemical orchard greenhouse stood shrouded in endless night. [13] Lian, the scattered memory keeper, wandered between rows of silver-leaved trees whose fruit distilled moonlight into memory. [30] She carried a mica window, a thin pane that held the last reflections of a sun the realm had forgotten. [50] Her boots left no prints on the mirrored soil, because every step belonged to another version of herself. [68] The orchard's trees were alchemical, each branch grafted with glass retorts and copper tubing that breathed pale vapors. [86] In this endless night, the greenhouse was both prison and laboratory, and Lian's task was to rekindle a dying realm. [106] She pressed one hand to the mica window and listened for forgotten songs. [119] The pane answered with a faint vibration, a tune from a village that had lost its name. [136] Lian could not remember the village herself, but the mica caught what her scattered mind could not hold. [154] She followed the tune through the orchard to a tree whose bark was split like a dry mouth. [172] Beneath the tree lay a bundle of roots shaped like a sleeping child, cold and unlit. [188] Lian knelt, and the mica window showed a dawn trapped inside the root bundle. [202] This was how dawns were woven in the mirror world. [212] Each dawn had to be sung out of the dark by someone who remembered enough to begin. [229] Lian began the first fragment of the forgotten song, and the roots stirred. [242] Her voice was cracked, but the mica window caught the sound and folded it into a thread of gold. [261] She drew the thread across her lap and sang the second fragment, a verse about rivers running upward. [279] A second thread, the color of apple blossoms, joined the first. [290] Lian kept singing, and the forgotten song gathered itself into a shimmering length of woven dawn. [306] The fabric glowed with the pink of first light and the blue of a sky that had never known noon. [326] She used the mica window as a loom, passing the dawn threads through its mineral veins. [342] As she worked, the woven dawn began to knit communities together. [353] In the dark mirrors of distant villages, people remembered a harvest song their grandmothers had sung. [369] They opened their doors, hummed the tune, and found neighbors already singing in the street. [384] Lian felt the realm shiver with connection, but the root child still lay cold. [398] The mica window showed her a memory that was not hers: a crown of woven dawns placed on a stone cradle. [419] She understood then that the dawn could not be hung in the sky until it first swaddled the sleeping child. [439] Lian lifted the root bundle and wrapped it slowly in the woven dawn. [452] The fabric tightened around the roots like a mother binding an infant to her chest. [467] The root child exhaled, and a warm wind moved through the alchemical orchard for the first time in a century. [487] But the endless night resisted. [492] Shadows slithered down the glass panes and pressed against Lian's back, urging her to stop. [507] The mica window began to crack along its edges, leaking thin streams of dawn. [521] Lian knew that release and binding were the same act in the mirror world. [535] To free the dawn, she had to bind herself to the song completely. [548] She sang the last verse, the one even the mica had hidden from her. [562] The words came from a place deeper than memory, from the mirrored root of her own scattered mind. [580] The woven dawn flared, and the root child opened eyes of brilliant quartz. [593] With a sound like a bell splitting, the mica window shattered into a thousand falling stars. [609] The greenhouse roof tore open, and the bound release rushed upward, carrying the swaddled child into the sky. [627] Lian fell back, empty-handed, as the first true dawn broke over the mirror world. [641] The alchemical trees drank the light and turned from silver to green. [653] In the villages, the forgotten songs no longer had to be remembered alone. [666] They were carried from mouth to mouth, door to door, and the communities held each other in the new warmth. [686] Lian lay on the mirrored soil, her memories scattering like petals, but she smiled. [700] She had rekindled the dying realm not by keeping its dawns, but by releasing them. [715] The mica window was gone, yet its fragments lay everywhere, tiny windows into a brighter future. [731] In the mirror world, night still returned, but now it came woven with the memory of dawn. [748] The communities, once isolated, gathered beneath the orchard's new leaves to sing the forgotten songs together. [764] And whenever a child asked how dawn was made, the elders pointed to the mica shards and began, "First, you must find a memory that scatters." [790]</story>
