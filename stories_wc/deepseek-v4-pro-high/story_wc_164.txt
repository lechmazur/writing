<story>The sea-foam recollectionist arrived at the geode with a pocket of cold foam. [13] She lived inside an iridescent cave within a giant crystal geode, where every wall refracted dawn into cheerful gloom. [32] This place kept memory stable, but only before memory fades with the tide. [45] Her task was to find missing pieces. [52] She knelt by a pool and set her crater glass on the stone. [65] The glass, born from an old meteor wound, transliterated emotions into shapes and colors when wet with sea foam. [84] She spread the foam across the crater glass and watched a woman's goodbye become a thin emerald path. [102] The path led from the shore to a hill, then simply stopped. [114] "Another vanished road," she said, with the cheerful gloom of someone used to incomplete maps. [129] She needed to understand why some paths disappear before memory fades. [140] Outside, a migrating bird passed over the geode and dropped scattered seeds through a crack in the crystal. [158] The seeds clicked against the floor like tiny obsidian questions. [168] She collected seven of them and placed each one on the crater glass. [181] The glass recognized them as an old method: seeds dropped by a migrating bird could mark where a path had once existed. [203] She conjectured that the bird carried lost routes in its gut and planted them as seeds. [219] If she could read the seed pattern, she might find the missing piece of the emerald path. [236] She arranged the seeds in the order they had fallen and breathed on the glass. [251] A pale blue thread appeared, running from the third seed to a dark corner of the cave. [268] She followed it, holding the crater glass like a lantern. [278] The thread ended at a hollow beneath a crystal shelf. [288] Inside lay a small nest of dried sea grass and a single seed the migrating bird had not dropped. [307] She touched the seed to the crater glass. [315] The glass transliterated the seed's emotion, and the emerald path reappeared, longer this time. [329] It now curved beyond the hill to a gate and a woman waiting. [342] The missing piece was not a lost road, but a hesitation. [353] The woman had paused so long that the path had forgotten to continue. [366] The sea-foam recollectionist smiled with cheerful gloom and let the crater glass cool. [379] Then she planted the seed in the geode floor and watched a new path sprout, fragile and green, toward the missing pieces of every map she had ever kept. [408] But not every missing piece could be mended by one seed. [419] She gathered the remaining six seeds and pressed them into the crater glass. [432] Their emotions surfaced as small waves: grief, relief, a laugh that had nowhere to land. [447] She followed each wave before memory fades, marking the cave walls with wet fingertips. [461] The bird's seed method was not random. [468] It scattered missing pieces along the exact routes that people had abandoned. [480] Some paths disappeared because they were never walked. [488] Others disappeared because the walker stopped to feel too much. [498] The recollectionist transliterated each seed's emotion into a map that could hold both. [511] At dawn the geode flared iridescent, and the maps glowed like veins. [523] She saw the missing pieces not as failures, but as pauses. [534] The emerald path finally reached the woman, and the woman stepped forward. [546] That motion sent tiny seeds scattering from her hem, each one a moment she had once abandoned. [563] The recollectionist caught them in the crater glass before the tide erased the foam. [577] Now the cave held a full map of disappearing paths, and none were entirely gone. [592] She whispered a conjecture to the migrating bird that returned at dusk. [604] "You drop seeds so the lost can find their way back." [615] The bird did not answer, but it perched on the crater glass and sang a note of cheerful gloom. [634] Then she understood that the cave inside the geode was not a storehouse of memories. [649] It was a nest for missing pieces, and she was its sea-foam recollectionist. [662]</story>
