After the energy beings arrived, the roads learned a new silence. [11] I was a drifting roadside poet, and I carried a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape. [29] The rope had scraped against a stone windowsill the night I chose not to die in that cell. [47] My only reason for living was to discover the texture of time. [59] The energy beings had promised that texture could be found in a terra-cotta grotto under starlight. [75] For three seasons I walked toward that grotto with road dust in my teeth. [89] At night the energy beings drifted overhead like slow blue lanterns. [100] They did not speak often, but their arrival had made every hour feel ridged and measurable. [116] I reached the grotto at the edge of a dry river plain. [128] Its mouth opened like a clay throat lined with mica. [138] Starlight fell through holes above and dusted the walls. [147] I lowered myself into the dark with the prison rope. [157] The fibers creaked but held. [162] At the bottom, a being made of slow blue current waited. [173] It said my name was already written in the layers. [183] It offered me an impossible choice. [189] I could feel the past completely and lose my future road, or feel the future completely and lose every memory of escape. [211] I laughed because the choice was not a choice at all. [222] Ethically playful, I asked the being whether it had ever tasted a question. [235] It pulsed brighter, which I took as a no. [244] I knelt and began to pattern the frayed rope across the terra-cotta floor. [257] Each loop was a road I had walked after the energy beings arrived. [270] Each knot was a locked door I had chosen to leave closed. [282] The being asked what third path I imagined. [290] I said I would trade a poem for one minute of touching the wall. [304] The being accepted, but only if the poem contained a true pattern. [316] I spoke slowly, uncovering layers of memory as the starlight shifted. [327] I told it about the prison yard where I learned that time has grain like wood. [343] I told it about the rope biting my palms as I dropped from the high window. [359] I told it how the road after prison felt smooth in some places and splintered in others. [376] I told it about the small roadside shrines where I had sometimes traded poems for bread. [392] The poem ended with the word for time I had carved into a roadside stone. [407] The energy being faded into the wall like rain into clay. [418] A section of terra-cotta loosened. [423] I pressed my palm against it. [429] The texture of time passed through me. [436] It felt like rope fibers, old blood, the cold edge of a windowsill, and then a long warm road. [455] I understood that every layer in the grotto was one of the impossible choices someone had refused. [472] The impossible choice had been a test of attention. [481] I did not need to lose either road or memory. [491] I needed to notice the layers between them. [499] The being reappeared, fainter now, and called me ethically playful. [509] I coiled the rope slowly. [514] Some fibers had turned to dust and left a snaking pattern on the floor. [528] I left the rope there, tied loosely around a central stone. [539] The being asked if I was not afraid to walk unroped. [550] I said the road was my rope now. [558] I climbed out before dawn. [563] Above the grotto, the road lay silver under the arriving energy beings. [575] I walked with my hands empty and my shoulder light. [585] I still did not know what time was. [593] I had touched its texture, and that was enough. [602] The energy beings sent a pulse of starlight down the road ahead. [614] I followed it because the texture of time was not a thing to keep but a thing to walk through. [634] My footprints made their own pattern beside the old tire tracks. [645] The rope stayed behind in the grotto, holding the memory of my escape. [658] That was its layer now. [663] The stars leaned close enough to listen. [670]
