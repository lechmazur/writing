<story>After the liquidation, Mara Venn arrived at the last orchard in the radioactive dust belt. [15] She was a conspiracy debunker by trade, and the orchard had become a myth. [29] The trees bore fruit despite the storms, their leaves green in a gray land. [43] Locals whispered that a flawed diamond rumored to be cursed held the orchard alive. [57] Mara intended to debunk that comfortable lie. [64] Her employer, a mining syndicate, had acquired the land during the liquidation and wanted the legend dead before resale. [83] She was sharply observant; she noticed that the orchard's wind rows bent inward, not with the storm. [100] That small contradiction became her first thread. [107] At the orchard's heart, she found a stone plinth. [116] On it rested the diamond, flawed by a black inclusion shaped like a splinter. [130] Mara began to intuit that the diamond was not a cause but a marker. [144] She found the ground beneath the plinth strangely still. [153] Dust devils spun at the orchard's edge, yet in this exact spot the air did not tremble. [170] She thought of the old surveyor's phrase: a hidden stillpoint. [180] The phrase was not magic; it was geology. [188] Mara needed evidence. [191] Inside the abandoned farmhouse, she found a drawer of shredded letters. [202] They had been torn by hand, not machine, probably during the liquidation panic. [215] She spent three nights reassembling shredded letters on a warped table. [226] The fragments revealed a correspondence between the orchard's former owner and a government geologist. [240] They wrote about a "null point" under the soil where radioactive fallout never settled. [254] The geologist called it a hidden stillpoint, a stable air pocket created by old collapsed tunnels and mineral springs. [273] The diamond, the letters claimed, was flawed by a carbon needle that locked in radiation signatures, making it seem cursed. [293] Mara reassembled the final letter. [298] It contained a map reference to the stillpoint. [306] She compared it to the diamond's inclusion. [313] Under magnification, the splinter's angle matched the map's bearing from the plinth to the source of the spring. [331] Mara laughed quietly. [334] The comfortable lie was that the diamond was cursed; the uncomfortable truth was that the orchard thrived because of an unmapped stillpoint, and the diamond merely marked it. [362] She filed a report recommending preservation, not debunking. [370] Her employer refused. [373] The syndicate wanted the stillpoint drilled and drained, then the orchard cleared. [385] Mara felt her old purpose shift. [391] She had come to excavate truth from comfortable lies, but now she saw that truth could be buried by greed. [411] That night, she took the diamond from the plinth. [420] She placed it in a lead-lined case and drove toward the capital, where the geologist's letters could testify. [438] Behind her, the dust storm swallowed the orchard. [446] But without the diamond, she knew the stillpoint would not fail. [457] It had existed before the miners, before the orchard, before the comfortable lie. [470] At the highway checkpoint, guards stopped her. [477] They had been told she was a looter. [485] Mara did not argue. [489] She simply asked them to open the case and reassemble the truth themselves. [502] The diamond's flaw caught the checkpoint light. [509] One guard, sharply observant, noticed the inclusion pointed to a symbol etched inside the case lid: the government geologist's seal. [529] Mara said, "The curse is real, but it is not what they fear." [542] "The stillpoint keeps the poisons out." [548] "If you drill it, the orchard dies and the dust spreads." [559] The guards let her pass. [564] Mara did not debunk the diamond. [570] She carried it into the capital as evidence of a hidden stillpoint, a place where the wasteland could learn to breathe again. [592] The orchard behind her had already begun to outlast the lie. [603]</story>
