Callum had no use for words; the sea carried what mattered. [11] He was the lighthouse supply skipper, and he ferried crates to a gray island where the lamp had burned through three keepers. [33] He rarely spoke to the harbormaster, the villagers, or even the gulls that followed his wake. [49] But he watched. [52] He noticed which widow lingered at the post office, which boy left notes under the church rail, which dockhand folded paper too carefully to be sending nothing. [79] An anonymous letter chain had grown along the coast like a rope of kelp, each letter passed hand to hand or hidden under stones. [103] Callum carried its letters in a waxed canvas pouch, never reading them, only moving them. [118] He understood that some people needed to be heard without being seen. [130] On his belt he kept one piece of sea glass, green as a wave held still. [146] It had come from a bottle that once held a letter from his mother, sent after the accident that took his father. [168] The sea had chewed the bottle into softness, and he kept the glass as proof that broken things could become safe to hold. [191] He also had a way of stacking stones as messages. [201] Three flat stones meant a letter was hidden beneath the capstone. [212] A tall cairn with a tilt meant danger at the landing. [223] A ring of stones around a stick meant he would wait after dark. [236] No one had taught him this language, but the island understood it. [248] On a night when the wind came low and warm, the lighthouse lamp flickered out. [263] Callum saw the dark from his boat before he heard the bell. [275] He crossed the rough channel with a spare mantle in his coat and a letter in his pouch for the keeper who was no longer there. [301] The lantern room was empty except for the huge dead lamp and the hum of the wind through cracked glass. [321] Sailors called it haunted because a figure moved behind the glass after dark. [334] Callum did not believe in ghosts, but he believed in unfinished messages. [346] He climbed the spiral stairs slowly, one hand on the cold wall. [358] The air smelled of brass, salt, and old kerosene. [367] After the lamp flickered out, the room turned strange, as if it was waiting for someone to remember it. [386] From the gallery he looked down at the shore and saw his own stone stack from that morning, still standing. [406] He needed to protect it. [411] A storm was coming, and the letters beneath the capstone would wash into the sea. [426] He also needed to protect the last piece of the chain that only he knew: the keeper had been writing to someone for years. [450] The old keeper, Elias, had left letters in the lantern room behind a loose panel. [465] Callum found the loose panel now and eased it open. [475] Inside lay a bundle of envelopes, dry as moth wings. [485] He added the letter from his pouch to the bundle. [495] Then he took out the piece of sea glass and set it in the hollow of the dead lamp. [514] It held a faint green glow from the moon through the storm clouds. [527] He stacked three stones on the floor beneath the lamp, a message that said I was here, and you are safe to follow. [550] He did this because he had spent his life trying to bottle the feeling of safety. [566] As a boy, he had watched the lighthouse beam sweep across the water and believed nothing could be lost. [585] After his father's boat went down in a sudden squall, that belief cracked. [598] The letter from his mother had arrived by bottle, and when he held it, the fear in his chest loosened. [618] He wanted to give that loosening to someone else. [627] The anonymous letter chain was his way, and this haunted room was the safest place he knew. [644] He closed the panel, left the lamp dark, and went back down the stairs. [658] The wind had already pulled apart his morning stone stack on the shore. [671] He knelt in the rain and rebuilt it, stone by stone, with the letters tucked deep in a crevice beneath. [691] He placed the capstone so its shadow pointed toward the lighthouse, a sign that the cache was protected. [709] He never told anyone about the sea glass in the lamp or the bundle behind the panel. [726] But the next morning, a mainland woman found a letter under her blue boat cushion and smiled for the first time in weeks. [749] Callum tied his skiff to the supply dock and watched her from a distance, saying nothing. [765] The lighthouse remained dark, but the lantern room held its small green light, a luminescent ache. [781] He carried safety like a bottled tide and let the stones speak for him. [795]
