Liora was a canyon echo singer, trained to cast her voice into the Serpentine Nexus under starlight and read the returning echoes like a map of hidden grief. [28] She cradled a whispering seashell half in her palm, its broken edge warm as if a distant tide still breathed through it. [50] The canyon around her lay like a serpentine nexus under starlight, a maze of wind-cut stone that swallowed and reshaped every call. [72] Her reason for standing there was to learn why some people echo, why a few voices returned again and again while others vanished into the rock. [98] She knew the canyon had begun to answer her differently ever since the webs of intrigue around the echo trade had tightened. [120] Tonight, while the universe hums its secret song, low and endless, she felt it through the soles of her boots. [140] She raised her chin and sang a dead woman's name into the dark. [153] The echo came back in three fragments, each one clearer than the last, as if the canyon was learning to speak. [174] The first fragment held the woman's laughter, the second her weeping, and the third only the word "sorrow." [192] Liora pressed the whispering seashell half to her temple, and the shell answered in a voice like wind through dry reeds. [213] Its counsel came through progressive disclosure, not as a full answer but as a slow unrolling of clues meant to protect her from the truth. [238] "Some people become stone, and some people become song," the shell whispered. [250] It told her that the woman whose name she had sung had left an unfinished vow in the canyon, and the vow still beat against the walls. [277] Liora was richly perceptive, so she heard the omission beneath the shell's words. [290] The shell was not telling her why the vow remained, only that it did. [304] She moved deeper into the serpentine nexus under starlight, following a narrow passage where her own footsteps returned as whispers. [324] A figure waited at the junction, holding the other half of the seashell. [337] The figure was Morn, the echo cartographer, and his face carried the same reed-blown melancholy that haunted every map he drew. [358] "You want to know why some people echo," he said, "but you already knew the answer before you sang." [377] He held out his half, and the two broken edges fit together with a sound like a door closing under water. [398] The joined shell did not roar; it fell silent, and in that silence the canyon told her the rest through progressive disclosure. [420] She saw the unfinished vow as a knot of love and rage tied into the stone by the woman's last breath. [441] People did not echo because the canyon remembered them; they echoed because they refused to let go of someone who still listened. [463] Morn nodded and said the webs of intrigue had not been about maps at all, but about hiding this truth from those who would use it to bind the dead. [493] He asked Liora to ensure the two shell halves stayed together, so the dead could finish their vows and fall quiet. [514] She wanted to refuse, because if she understood the echo, she would lose the only voice that had never left her. [535] But the canyon hummed its secret song inside her, and she knew that keeping the halves apart would only chain the woman to her grief. [560] She pressed the joined shell to her lips and sang the woman's name one last time while the universe hummed its secret song around them. [585] The echo returned whole, and then it faded, leaving a silence that felt like forgiveness. [600] Morn took the shell and buried it at the junction, where the halves could hold the canyon's mouth open for the next unfinished voice. [624] Liora walked back through the serpentine nexus under starlight with the reed-blown melancholy of a singer who finally knew why some people echo. [647]
