Sera had not touched the loom since the last plum fell in the forgotten orchard deep in the bamboo forest. [20]
Around her, the bamboo stood hushed, and the silence between the trees had begun to ravel at its edges, pulling loose like thread from an old wound. [47]
She sat on the worn bench with a bead of tree sap amber in her palm, and the bead caught the low light with a stoically sparkling gleam. [75]
That amber was a gift from her sister, who had walked beyond the orchard years ago to carry a message across an impossible distance and had never returned. [103]
Now the raveling silence followed the same path her sister had taken, and it was erasing every sound that tried to cross. [125]
It swallowed birdsong, then wind, then even the sound of her own breathing when she let herself listen. [143]
For months Sera had been too wistful and heartsick to recommence. [154]
The loom's bamboo reed had shattered during her last attempt to weave a path through the silence, and she had no replacement teeth. [177]
Then, beneath a mossy crate at the orchard's edge, she found a rummage sale box that smelled of dust and someone else's abandoned hope. [201]
Inside were only jigsaw puzzle edges, dozens of them, their curls and knobs worn smooth by hands that had once searched for a fit. [225]
Sera nearly threw them away, but a single notch matched the broken reed's groove exactly. [240]
She fitted the jigsaw puzzle edges into the loom one by one, and the ragged teeth became a new reed, not beautiful but whole. [264]
That was the method hiding in the rummage sale box. [274]
With the amber bead tied above the shuttle, its stoically sparkling surface steady as a heartbeat, she began to weave again. [295]
She did not speak. [299]
She drew the first thread through the hush of time's unraveling tapestry and let the loom remember its rhythm. [318]
The raveling silence pressed close, trying to pull the new reed apart, but the jigsaw puzzle edges held because they had already learned how to wait. [344]
Sera wove her sister's name into the weft, not with sound but with the spacing between threads. [361]
Each pass of the shuttle carried a syllable across the impossible distance, where no voice could travel and no letter could survive. [383]
The message was not words alone. [389]
It was the shape of the orchard in autumn, the taste of plums, the particular silence of a house shared by two sisters, and the promise that someone still waited. [419]
As she wove, the silence around her stopped raveling and began to gather, drawn into the pattern instead of against it. [440]

Sera understood what the sacrifice would require before she reached the final row. [466]
To carry a message across impossible distance, the messenger had to become part of the thread, and the thread had to be cut from the living world. [493]
She did not slow. [497]
With each pass, her hands grew lighter, and the amber bead glowed warmer, as if it recognized the message it had been keeping all these years. [523]

The raveling silence closed over the final thread, not as an ending but as a seam. [564]

Far beyond the silence, a sister paused at the edge of a familiar dream and heard, at last, not words but the shape of a heartbeat woven through the hush of time's unraveling tapestry. [638]

And in the silence that followed, the loom began to wait for the next hand brave enough to recommence. [674]
