Maren had never asked to be the caretaker of the cursed orchard. [12] It inherited her after her father vanished into the cliffside labyrinth, leaving only a ring of keys and a warning not to touch the stones. [37] The orchard grew along the cliff's broken edge, silver-barked and thorny, beautiful in a graceless way that made her teeth ache. [58] Below it, the labyrinth coiled through pale rock like a half-erased drawing. [70] Mechanical birds patrolled its corridors with copper wings and jeweled eyes, each one taught to kill anything that left a false step. [92] That evening, three travelers entered the maze just as the sun dropped behind the western ridge. [108] They were already lost, and the night frost would form in less than an hour. [123] Maren did not want to save them. [130] She wanted to throw every remaining nut into the sea and let the curse rot. [145] But the orchard kept its roots in her pulse, and the one thing it still asked was that no lost traveler die inside the maze. [170] So she gathered a pouch of small nuts and went to the cliff's crumbling lip. [185] She could not touch the labyrinth herself. [192] If her bare foot met its stone, the orchard's curse would root her there, and she would become another twisted tree above the path. [216] Instead, she used the nuts as her hands. [224] Each one, when thrown against rock, shattered and left a pale impression, a handprint of sap and dust that glowed faintly for a few minutes. [249] Those impressions were not for the travelers to follow. [258] They were decoys. [261] The mechanical birds hunted by the scatter of human footsteps, and false impressions drew them away from the real path. [281] Maren began to assemble a route of diversions. [289] Her throws were offhandedly exact, lazy flicks that cracked into exactly the right crevice, leaving an impression without ever touching the stone herself. [312] One nut landed on the eastern spur and drew a bird toward its copper shadow. [327] Another opened a seam between two leaning slabs. [335] A third made a splintered pattern that echoed like a child stumbling. [347] The travelers below saw the pale marks and hesitated until the oldest among them understood. [362] She pulled the others into the clear channel while the birds tore at the false impressions. [378] Maren had one nut left, smaller than the rest and ridged with a spiral that her father had called the forgotten blueprint. [400] It was not a drawing on paper. [407] Inside its shell, the kernel rattled in a sequence that matched the old acoustic map of the labyrinth's only safe exit. [428] She could not read maps, but she had learned to hear the route in that rattle. [444] She fitted the small nut into her sling and waited. [454] The final bird had taken the true exit as its roost. [465] Its bronze head turned in shallow increments, scanning the stones for the heat of living feet. [481] Frost was already needling the grass at the cliff's edge. [491] Maren let the nut fly. [496] It wobbled without grace, spun once, and struck the stone lintel above the bird's head with a sound like a broken bell. [518] The impact was not meant to kill. [525] It left an impression, a small spiral of sap and dust, exactly where the forgotten blueprint said the exit's echo point lay. [547] The bird turned toward the mark, clicked its iron beak, and hopped down to inspect the false signal. [565] The true exit opened behind it. [571] The travelers ran. [574] Maren shouted at them to follow the hollow path, not the bright one. [587] They did. [589] Before the night frost formed fully, the last of them slipped through the cliff gate and onto the harbor road. [609] Maren stood alone among the silver trees. [616] The orchard's beauty was graceless, thorned and unapologetic, the same as her. [628] She swept the broken shells aside with her boot and did not watch the travelers go. [644] The small nut was gone, but the forgotten blueprint had finally done its work. [658] She was still the reluctant caretaker, bound to the cursed orchard above the labyrinth. [672] But before the frost took the last heat from the stone, three sets of footprints had become a road. [691]
