The storm-worn windblown parapet at Greyhollow rose out of the mist like the spine of a drowned animal, and Idris Vane, a prisoner exchange negotiator, stood at its center because this was neutral territory. [34]
He kept one hand wrapped around an antique drawer pull in his pocket, a brass lion's head worn smooth by years of anxious fingers. [58]
Mist beaded on his knuckles and on the brass lion's unseeing eyes. [70]
He was uncommonly thoughtful for a man in his trade, so he had arrived three hours early to read the wind, the tide, and the thin slip of moon that still hung in the western sky. [106]
The Maren delegation would not trust a clock, but both sides synchronized through moon phases, and the last quarter meant they could cross the causeway before the tide turned. [135]
He had learned that method the hard way, and he was here to recycle a fatal mistake into wisdom. [154]
Below, through the mist, a rope ladder shuddered, and the first Maren guards climbed onto the parapet with furrowed brows and muted voices. [177]
Their officer, Ilyana Marn, came last, and she did not greet him. [189]
Idris bowed enough to show respect but not enough to beg. [200]
Between them lay the unfinished space of an apology he had sent after the last failed exchange, and her silence told him it had not yet become forgiveness. [228]
He produced the drawer pull and set it on the stone ledge, where the wind could not steal it. [247]
"Your brother had this in his hand when he was taken," Idris said. [260]
Ilyana's jaw tightened, but she did not look away from the little brass face. [274]
"I meant to return it after the exchange that went wrong." [285]
He had wrenched that drawer pull from a shattered cabinet in her family's burned house, thinking it was only a useful bit of brass, and by the time he understood it was a grave-marker of memory, her brother was dead in the causeway panic. [329]
"I am not asking you to forget," he continued, "but I am asking you to let the exchange proceed." [348]
She gave a single nod and signaled to the guards below. [359]
The prisoners came up one by one, three Diric soldiers and five Maren, all of them blinking in the grey light. [380]
Idris counted faces, checked the sealed manifests, and placed the drawer pull atop them so the wind would not carry the papers away. [403]
He read each name aloud, and the wind carried every syllable nowhere. [415]
The last prisoner, a thin Maren boy, could not grip the ladder. [427]
Idris knelt and spoke to him until the boy's shaking stopped. [438]
He remembered being that age and afraid of losing his grip. [449]
He ordered the escort to untether the causeway coracle from its mooring ring so the boy could be lowered by rope instead of climbing. [473]
When the rope went slack, the mist swallowed the boat's stern, and for one heartbeat no one breathed. [491]
Ilyana watched him untie the knot himself, her brow furrowed. [502]
"You have changed," she said at last, low enough that only the wind could have heard if it wanted to. [522]
"I am trying to recycle my mistakes into wisdom," he said. [533]
She picked up the drawer pull and closed it in her palm. [545]
"My forgiveness is not in the manifest," she said. [554]
"It is not something you can negotiate." [561]
"I know," he said. [565]
She nodded, and for a moment the furrowed hush was deeper than the sea below. [580]
The exchange finished before the moon set. [587]
Idris untied the last line himself and watched the coracle move toward the Maren shore, its lantern swinging in exact time with the last quarter's pale path on the water. [617]
Somewhere between his apology and her forgiveness, he thought, the tide would turn again, and he would be there to meet it. [639]
