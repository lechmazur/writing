<story>The abandoned carnival funhouse sagged against the salt marsh like a rotted tooth, and I stepped through its splintered mouth because the whispering advisor had promised me the only cure waited inside. [32] I clutched the opera cloak clasp in my fist, its cracked garnet still warm from the ambulance ride. [50] After the resuscitation, I had returned with a photographically cursed affliction: every picture of me developed as a smear of lingering luminance, a bright ghost where my face should have been. [81] The advisor's voice slithered through my teeth: "Keep walking past the tilting mirrors." [94] I obeyed, because fear had been stalking me since the flatline, and my only plan to trick fear into revealing its weakness was to walk deeper into a place it already owned. [126] The funhouse breathed around me with the odor of wet velvet and mouse bones, a repulsive allure that pulled my sternum forward even as my stomach turned. [153] Faded bulbs sputtered to life without current, and their glow stuck to the air long after each filament died. [172] The whispering advisor told me to open the dream dictionary I had stolen from the hospital chaplain's shelf. [190] Its margins were crammed with paradoxical notes in a cramped hand: "To find the exit, stop seeking; to become invisible, be fully seen; to defeat fear, feed it your most guarded name." [222] I recited the lines as I passed a hall of concave mirrors, and my reflections fragmented into a dozen photographs that were not me. [246] Each image showed the same scene: a drowned woman in an opera cloak, clasp undone, her mouth opening to speak. [266] I stopped, because the woman was me before the resuscitation, and the fear I wanted to trick was beginning to breathe against the back of my neck. [293] The advisor whispered, "Fear is watching you through the lens of the clasp." [306] I held the opera cloak clasp to my eye and saw through its cracked garnet a narrow room behind the mirror where a blackness stood upright. [332] It wore my shape but not my edges. [340] The dream dictionary's next paradox said, "To unbosom a secret to the dark is to steal the dark's tongue." [359] I understood then that I had to confess not to a priest or a lover but to the thing that had followed me home from the water. [386] The whispering advisor pressed the words into my ear: "Tell it why you stopped fighting." [401] The blackness lurched closer, and its repulsive allure sharpened until I wanted both to run and to let it swallow me. [422] I unbosomed myself in the mirror's cold frame. [430] "I did not slip from the pier by accident." [439] "I let go because I was tired of being the one who smiled in every photograph while the light moved through me like I was already gone." [466] As the secret left my mouth, the blackness flickered and shrank, and its weakness showed itself: it could only live inside the space between what I feared and what I refused to say. [499] The lingering luminance that had ruined every photograph now detached from the walls and braided itself into the garnet clasp. [519] The funhouse went dark, not empty but ordinary, and the whispering advisor spoke once more from the threshold. [537] "You tricked fear by giving it the truth it thought it wanted." [549] I turned, but the advisor was only an old stagehand's coat hanging on a hook, its collar rustling with a final whisper. [571] I walked out into the grey marsh dawn carrying the opera cloak clasp closed tight at my throat. [589] Behind me, the funhouse collapsed into its own shadow without a sound. [601] For the first time since the resuscitation, I let someone take my photograph with a borrowed camera. [618] The print was clear. [622] I was not a bright ghost anymore, only a woman standing in ordinary morning light. [637]</story>
