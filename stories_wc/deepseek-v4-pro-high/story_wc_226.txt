<story>The towers of bone in the canyon of endless echoes stood gray and listening before the ritual. [17] Mara, a defiant artist, crouched at the foot of the highest tower and pressed her palm to its warm surface. [37] Mist crawled between the bone spires, hiding the pale spire at the canyon's mouth. [51] Her little brother Penn followed her with an innocently mischievous grin, tapping a rib bone until it hummed. [69] They will hear you, Mara said. [75] Let them, Penn whispered, and the canyon whispered back in three directions. [87] The ritual would begin at moonrise, and the elders had already chosen the single truth to be forgiven. [105] Mara refused to let that truth stand unchallenged. [113] A year ago, a rockslide had killed seven herders, and the elders blamed Penn's stray spark from a forbidden fire. [133] The boy had only been chasing a lizard with fireflies tied to its tail, but the canyon had turned that small mischief into a long echo. [159] Forgiving him was the ritual's price, the elders said, and the pale spire in mist would accept one voice at dawn. [180] Mara knew the price of forgiveness was not mercy but silence. [191] Once the chosen confession entered the spire, every echo of the rockslide would be erased, along with the seven names. [211] That was why she had spent the last month holding impromptu poetry sessions under the bone arches. [228] She taught the herders' children to rhyme, the old women to break lines, the masons to chant. [245] Each poem described the rockslide from a different set of eyes. [256] Her motivation was simple: to refract a single truth into many. [267] If truth could splinter, the ritual could not feed on one clean lie. [280] Penn did not understand the danger; he just loved the way his voice split into five Penns when he yodeled from a hollow skull. [304] At the final session before the ritual, Mara gathered thirty people in the seventh tower. [319] They sat in a ring of marrow dust and echoed her first line. [332] The mountain did not fall for one boy's spark, they repeated, their voices layering. [346] It fell because the elders cut too deep into the ridge. [357] The words flew outward, struck the bone walls, and came back as a hundred quarreling versions. [373] Mara smiled, but the expression felt forged in a hush, like a blade hammered from held breath. [390] Then Penn, innocently mischievous, added a line of his own. [400] The lizard dropped its tail and the tail kept running. [410] The canyon caught the line and began to refract it before the echo could die. [425] Some heard it as a joke; others heard it as a clue. [437] Mara stopped breathing. [440] The lizard had been headed toward the old spillway, not the ridge, and the tail had kept running downslope. [459] She seized the echo and called for another poem, right there before the ritual. [473] Tell it from the water's view. [479] A mason named Orin stood and shaped the story of rain-swollen channels beneath the ridge. [494] A herder's widow recited the sound of splitting stone from inside the caves. [507] A child repeated Penn's lizard line until it became a map. [518] The elders arrived in a column of gray robes, their faces tight as drumskin. [532] Enough, the eldest commanded. [536] One truth must enter the spire, or the dead will wander the mist forever. [550] Mara stepped between the elders and the bone wall. [559] Then let the spire choose among many, she said. [568] She gestured toward the pale spire in mist, now faintly glowing before the ritual. [582] The truth you want to forgive is not the truth that cracked the ridge. [596] The eldest raised his hand, but the echoes were already refracting Mara's words into the towers of bone. [614] No one could stop the canyon from multiplying a voice. [624] Penn added, "I am sorry for the fire, but not for the lizard." [637] The confession split into warmth and grief, mischief and sorrow, until the elders' single truth became unrecognizable. [654] At the mouth of the canyon, the pale spire flickered and refused to open. [668] The price of forgiveness, the eldest finally said, was that they had to listen to every version before one could be freed. [690] Mara bowed her head, not in defeat but in a forged hush that held the whole canyon still. [708] The impromptu poetry sessions had not destroyed the ritual; they had made it too large for a single lie. [727] Moonlight touched the bone towers, and every version of the rockslide began to speak at once. [743] Mara took Penn's hand. [747] Now the ritual has to carry all of us, she said. [758]</story>
