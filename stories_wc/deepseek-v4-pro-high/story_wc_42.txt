The sage camera repairer Mara Voss had spent four decades alone in the lunar observatory above the clouds. [18]
Its silver dome rose from the highest ridge like a moon-flower, and below it the cloud sea ran white to the horizon. [40]
She mended shutters and lenses for astronomers who had stopped visiting years ago, though she still honored their handwritten notes. [60]
Her whole life had bent toward one desire: to harness the ephemeral. [72]
She wanted the brief silver light of a departure, the wobble of a star, the instant when a moving object became a streak. [95]
On her bench lay the brass hand of the old transit clock, a clock hand that won't stop spinning. [114]
It had begun when foundations first shook, and it had never paused. [126]
The hand's motion was changelessly constant, even when the walls shifted and the power failed. [141]
Its thin arc cut the luminous ache of her solitude into measurable slivers. [154]
Each morning she timed its spin by the sound of a leaf falling from the ancient maple in the glass courtyard. [175]
The leaf took the same breath to fall, and the soft click of its landing told her the hand had not lied. [197]
When foundations shake, she had told herself, every instrument lies except the leaf and the spin. [213]
The maple had once been a sapling brought from a lowland forest, and it kept above the clouds a memory of ground she had not seen in years. [241]
Now the last supply ship sat in the valley below, fueled for a launch that would carry it beyond the blue. [262]
Its hull bore the name of her daughter, who had left on another ship years before and never returned. [281]
The ship would climb until it reached escape velocity, and then it would leave Earth forever. [297]
Mara wanted the ship's light, to hold that final streak in emulsion. [309]
She had already missed one departure because a shutter jammed, and she would not miss this one. [326]
But her best camera had a shattered focal-plane shutter, and the observatory's electronic triggers were dead from the tremors. [345]
So she began to restructure the camera around the spinning clock hand. [357]
She removed the broken shutter and mounted the hand at the focal plane, letting it sweep across the film like a constant blade. [380]
She cushioned the hand's spindle with felt so its spin would not blur the whole body. [396]
Because its rate never changed, each pass would expose the film for exactly one two-hundredth of a second. [414]
She aligned the camera with the launch window, pointed it through the dome slit at the cloud sea, and waited. [434]

The foundations of the observatory shook for three long seconds, and the little maple dropped its last remaining leaf. [480]
The leaf fell in silence until it struck the copper tray. [491]
By the sound of a leaf falling, the circuit closed, the rebuilt shutter spun, and the film caught the silver needle of the ship at the exact moment it pierced the clouds and fled toward escape velocity. [528]
Mara stayed very still. [532]
The spinning clock hand never slowed. [538]
Later she developed the frame in red light and saw a bright line cut through the dark like a thread pulled from the ache of heaven. [564]
She touched the outline of the hull and let herself cry for the first time in years. [581]
