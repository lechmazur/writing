The mirror maker lived alone in the ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog. [16]
Her name was Eira, and she remained firmly grounded by habit, walking barefoot over warped roots and broken flagstones so the ground could never forget her. [42]
She kept an astronomer's broken sextant wrapped in oilcloth beneath her workbench, its index mirror shattered and its brass limb bent like a question. [66]
The dream had escaped through that crack three winters ago, and since then the fog had begun erasing boundaries between sleeping and waking. [89]
People spoke in their dreams while awake, and orchard paths led into rooms that had burned down before memory. [108]
Eira meant to capture a runaway dream. [115]
To do that, she had to recreate the instrument that had first let it slip. [130]
The sextant could not be mended with ordinary silvered glass. [140]
It needed a mirror made from the same desire that had cracked it, polished by the maker's own breath and cut upon the shimmer of twilight frost. [167]

At twilight frost, the fog turned to billions of suspended mirrors. [191]

The dream paused on a ridge of ice. [329]
