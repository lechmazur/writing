During the sky lantern release, the late-shift pier lamplighter Mara Voss left the lamp room because her hotel skeleton key tag had begun to hum against her collarbone. [28]
The tag read 306, and she had always let it signify a room in the old Hotel Meridian, even though the hotel had been underwater since before she was born. [58]
She told herself a shore-truth: she kept the pier lamps burning so that ships would not tear themselves open on the reef. [80]
That truth hid a sharper one, because she was searching for the color of sound, and the humming brass tag was the only map she had. [106]
Outside, paper lanterns drifted upward in slow fire, and the whole harbor fell into a meteor hush, that held breath after light crosses the sky. [131]
Mara moved through the crowd by wandering purposefully, not toward any stall or path but toward the low-tide ladder at the pier's end. [154]
She descended the iron rungs to the tidal flats, where the black water had drawn back like a curtain. [173]

The library leaned hard to starboard, but its shelves still held rows of books, and a pale blue glow moved between them like tide-light. [236]
That glow came from luminous vows, letters and logbook entries sealed in glass jars, each one still burning with the promise made inside it. [260]
Mara had discovered this place a month ago, and she had learned that the jars only brightened for the person meant to keep the vow. [285]
Now the skeleton key tag on her chest flared, and she followed that new light by wandering purposefully down the slanted aisles. [307]
The tag did not grow hot the way metal does in the sun; it grew urgent, pulling her toward a low shelf near the shattered stern windows. [334]
There, among the jars, sat a small tin box labeled in her mother's handwriting. [348]
Mara opened it with the skeleton key, though the box had no lock that she could see. [365]
Inside lay a single glass jar, and inside that jar a strip of paper glowed with blue-violet light. [383]
She brought the jar close and read the vow written in her mother's uneven script. [398]
"I promise my daughter will not stop at safety, and I promise she will find the color of the sea's voice." [419]
Mara tightened her fingers around the key tag. [427]
The words were not a command; they were a vow that had waited for her before she was born. [446]
Her mother had told her only one truth, that the sea at night could kill, but she had hidden behind that truth a luminous inheritance. [471]
Mara had never understood why she lit the lamps at the latest hour, why she listened to the flame hiss like a held note. [495]
Now she knew the hum was not the pier and not the wind, but a sound with a color she could finally name. [518]
It was the blue of the jar, the violet at the edge of a lantern flame, the color of a promise kept underwater. [541]
She placed the jar back, not because she was finished, but because the color of sound was already reading her. [561]
Then the tide began to return, and the library shuddered as the first wave licked the broken doorframe. [579]
Mara climbed out through the split hull and back up the ladder, the key tag cooling against her skin. [598]
Above, the last sky lanterns were vanishing into the sea-haze, leaving only a meteor hush behind them. [615]
She walked the pier and lit each lamp with a new steadiness, letting every flame signify the vow she had found. [636]
She still told the harbor master the same truth, that the lamps kept the boats safe. [652]
But now that truth hid nothing at all, because it and the deeper vow had become the same light. [671]
