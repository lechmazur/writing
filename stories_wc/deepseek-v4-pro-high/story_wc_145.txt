Mara Pell had edited atlases long enough to distrust any map that looked too certain. [15]
She stood at the frosted window of a rusted outpost by a frozen lake, watching for the falcon. [33]
The cold outside was so still it felt like the whole world was holding its breath, a worrying tranquility she had learned not to trust. [58]
The falcon returned just after noon, a dark mote dropping from low cloud and settling on the outpost's bent antenna. [78]
Its arrival did not herald anything so simple as a message. [89]
It heralded the thaw, and Mara had no time left to hesitate. [101]
She was a dry-witted atlas editor, not an explorer, but a dead polyglot's phrasebook had brought her here. [119]
The book's dog-ear marked a page of river words in seven languages, and in the margin someone had written a coordinate that did not exist on any published sheet. [148]
She opened it again and read the phrase she had translated into a note too neat for the chaos around her. [169]
"To find where the rivers begin, follow the wet glass north." [180]
It sounded like a riddle from a cheap fortune, and she had almost thrown the phrasebook into the lake twice. [200]
But the phrase kept her at the window, breathing on the pane until condensation bloomed across the glass. [218]
At first the fog was ordinary, a grey smear that softened the lake's white surface. [233]
Then the pattern of condensation on the glass began to branch, as if the water were remembering old channels beneath the ice. [255]
Mara traced one line with a fingertip and saw where it joined another, then another, all sliding toward a single point at the lake's northern end. [281]
A singular convergence, she thought, the kind of thing mapmakers dream about and editors quietly remove for being too improbable. [301]
She pulled on her coat and stepped outside before doubt could catch up. [314]
The falcon watched from the antenna, preening one wing with the unbearable calm of a thing that already knew the outcome. [335]
The lake did not groan under her boots. [343]
It was silent in a way that should have frightened her, but she walked with a subtly confident stride, because a lifetime of correcting bad maps had given her an ear for when the world was about to reveal itself. [383]
At the convergence point she found no spring, only a circle of black ice where the lake refused to freeze. [403]
She knelt and placed the phrasebook's dog-eared page against the ice, not from sentiment but because it held the only true translation of what she was seeing. [430]
Beneath the page, lines of current moved, darker rivers trapped under a lid of winter. [445]
They all fed the same dark circle, a silent mouth drinking the sky. [458]
She laughed once, dry and low. [464]
"Of course," she said to the black ice, "the rivers begin where the map gives up." [480]
The falcon screamed, and the sound carried across the lake like a crack that took its time arriving. [498]
Mara stood as the ice began to shift, not violently, but with a slow terrible grace. [514]
The pattern of condensation had been a herald, she realized, not a map. [527]
It had announced the exact moment the hidden source would wake. [538]
She backed toward the outpost, tucking the phrasebook into her coat, and watched the black circle widen. [555]
Water rose through the ice in thin bright threads and began mapping the lake in reverse, every river unspooling from the same point. [578]
The outpost's rusted walls ticked as warmth returned to the air, and the worrying tranquility finally made sense. [596]
It was not peace. [600]
It was the patience of something that had been waiting for her to arrive and look closely. [617]
She made it to the outpost steps and turned once more. [628]
The lake was no longer a lake. [635]
It had become a braided system of open water, all strands pulling toward the place where the rivers began and, she suspected, where every map she had ever edited would have to begin again. [669]
The falcon launched from the antenna and flew north, following the new water. [682]
Mara opened the phrasebook to the dog-eared page and found the margin note had changed. [697]
It now read, "Singular convergence is not a place on a map; it is the moment a map becomes unnecessary." [717]
She stared at the phrase for a long time, then closed the book and went inside to dry her gloves by the stove. [740]
Outside, the thaw spread with the quiet force of a fact she had finally accepted. [755]
Every river had to begin somewhere, and now she knew the somewhere was also a when. [771]
That was enough, and it was terrifying. [778]
