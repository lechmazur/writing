Mira Voss, curator of the Orchard Archive, woke on a lonely asteroid where pale trees grew in spirals and their blossoms opened into tiny maps of stars. [27]
The astral orchard was blossoming in a silent season, and the air carried a brightened disquiet, as if every petal remembered a secret. [50]
She walked the rows, checking the bark for new faces, because the orchard did not inherit seeds but memories. [69]
That morning she found something in the soil beneath a silver pear tree: a pistachio shell cameo blank, oval and unmarked, warm as a held breath. [95]
Mira turned it over in her palm, bewildered. [103]
The archive's manifest listed no such object, yet the shell matched the archive's oldest catalog photograph exactly. [120]
She brought it inside and did what any orderly curator would do: she began to inquire. [136]
First she asked the index. [141]
The index opened to a page between the first and last page, a page that had not existed until she asked the question. [164]
It described a bequest with no donor, no date, and a single phrase: nonlinear inheritance. [179]
Mira read the phrase again. [184]
The orchard had always transferred memory along bloodlines or apprenticeships, a clean line from one keeper to the next. [203]
But nonlinear inheritance meant the past could belong to a future heir, and the future could leave its face in an object before any ancestor had carved it. [231]
The cameo blank was not empty; it was simply complicated, holding every possible face until a recipient claimed one. [250]
That was forbidden knowledge. [254]
The archive charter warned that to pursue forbidden knowledge was to unmake the order of the trees, and yet Mira could not stop rereading the page. [280]
She needed to know whose face the blank would choose. [290]
Her hands trembled as she set the shell on her desk, and the orchard outside rustled though there was no wind. [311]
The pale blossoms seemed to turn toward her window like listening ears. [323]
Mira locked the object in a humidity drawer and retreated to the only place the pollen could not follow: the archive bathroom. [345]
She believed in having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms. [353]
There, with her back against cold tile, she had a philosophical breakthrough. [365]
She realized that inheritance was not a line but a knot. [376]
The blank had been sent backward from someone not yet born, or sideways from a life that would overlap hers only briefly. [398]
Every attempt to carve it would be an answer, not a question. [410]
She could not inquire without deciding. [416]
The bathroom mirror fogged as the station's atmospheric recycler hummed, and she drew a spiral in the steam with one finger. [437]
It was the same spiral as the orchard rows. [446]
Mira understood then that the orchard had been waiting for her to ask. [459]
She returned to the desk, opened the drawer, and lifted the pistachio shell cameo blank. [474]
It felt lighter, as if it had exhaled. [482]
The index opened again on its own. [489]
A new line had appeared beneath the phrase: one question accepted, one face to be assigned. [505]
Mira asked aloud, "Whose face will you hold?" [513]
The shell warmed in her hand. [519]
For a long moment, nothing happened, and then the unmarked surface shifted. [531]
A faint profile emerged, cut not by a tool but by the pressure of future memory. [547]
It was her own face, but older, with a small scar above the eyebrow she did not yet have. [566]
The orchard outside burst into full blossom, and every flower turned toward the sunless sky. [581]
Mira felt the brightened disquiet settle into her chest as she understood the inheritance. [595]
She was not the curator passing the orchard to an heir. [606]
She was the heir, receiving it from herself between the first and last page of a life she had not finished living. [628]
The cameo blank was no longer blank, and the forbidden knowledge was this: she had chosen herself. [645]
