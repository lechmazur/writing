<story>At the close of an unspoken dream, Mina woke to rain hammering the clay roofs of Qasr al-Rimal. [18] The fortress had not seen such water in seven years, and its walls exhaled a smell like wet fossils. [37] Mina was the fortress chef, and she could taste memories in everything she cooked. [106] Mina walked out into the gray light, her sandals sinking with each step. [119] She needed to find the coral icebound pavilion beneath the dunes before the water dried. [134] The pavilion held the fortress's stored remorse, frozen in cells of sea ice older than the desert. [151] Remorse, she knew, was emotionally contagious through touch. [159] One bare finger on those cells would flood her with every regret the fortress had ever buried. [176] Her plan was to shave a sliver of that remorse into a broth. [189] The broth would sandpaper rough memories, smoothing their edges without erasing them. [201] She needed it for the feast that evening, but also for herself.
