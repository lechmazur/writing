Before the coup, Sera worked as the galaxy-chart embroiderer inside the cobalt observatory in autumn. [15]
Every night she climbed the spiral stairs to the dome, carrying a small lantern lit by a flame from the eternal candle in the meridian hall. [41]
The autumn air smelled of cold copper and fallen maple leaves. [52]
She was known as a simple woman because she asked foolish questions. [64]
Sera never corrected that impression. [69]
She had learned that disguising wisdom as foolishness let her move through the observatory unnoticed, and she needed that invisibility to listen for unheard stories. [94]
She listened to apprentices, lamplighters, and the old clock-keeper because they carried rumors that never reached official histories. [112]
The observatory's blue domes held an echoed silence, and in that quiet the smallest voice left a trace. [130]
Unraveling conspiracies was not a proper task for an embroiderer, but Sera's needle could trace secrets as cleanly as star paths. [151]
One night, while stitching silver thread through a new chart from Overseer Voss, she noticed a faint star marked near the constellation of the Loom where no star should shine. [181]
She asked Voss whether the sky had adopted a new guest. [192]
He laughed and told her to mind the needle. [201]
Sera bowed with a fool's empty smile and returned to the stitching gallery. [214]
She copied the suspicious chart onto linen and laid it beside the observatory's older star maps. [230]
The false star appeared in five official charts, always near the same coordinates, always added within the past month. [249]
Sera realized it was not an error. [256]
It was a signal. [260]
Someone was using the official sky to move orders through the observatory without speaking. [274]
She laid the charts side by side and studied the negative space around the false star, where other possible paths branched like threads. [297]
Through adjacent possibilities, she began to read the pattern. [306]
If she treated the false star as a clock hand, each nearby chart moved that hand by one small increment. [326]
That was how she could synchronize the encrypted orders with the old festival calendar in the vestibule. [343]
The dates aligned with the harvest festival, when the city guard would be drunk and the palace gates would stand open. [364]
Sera threaded crimson silk and began to unravel the conspiracy stitch by stitch. [377]
She could not run to the magistrates. [384]

She asked the palace astronomer, with her usual foolish smile, whether autumn stars ever changed their minds. [456]
The palace astronomer studied her stitching under the lantern. [465]
In the echoed silence of the observatory nave, he whispered that she might have saved the realm. [482]
Sera only bowed again and returned to her work. [491]

The false stars were erased from the official charts. [515]
Sera was not praised publicly. [520]

Her eyes ached, but she continued stitching the true stars in silver. [560]
She knew the realm was full of unheard stories, and they would not all reveal themselves so clearly. [578]
