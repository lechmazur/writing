Between two vanishing stars, Maris worked alone in her topaz workshop. [11] The walls glittered with raw topaz crystals that caught the failing constellations and threw them back in amber splinters. [30] She was a cryptic letterpress operator, and her press waited like a black animal under the high windows. [48] In her palm, the halo fragment burned gently, a crescent of something that had once circled a dying star. [67] She set it into the bed of the press, because the fragment did what no metal type could do: it transposed empathy. [89] Whatever emotion she pressed into the page came out as a tactile equation, readable by the skin more than the eye. [110] For years she had chased a private proof, the mathematics of emotion that could explain why the stars were going out one by one. [134] A bell above the door struck once, and a young man entered with star charts folded under his arm. [153] He looked at her as if he had expected an old man, or a machine, or maybe a saint. [172] "You are the one who prints the impossible proofs," he said, not quite making it a question. [189] Maris did not answer in her own voice, because she had long ago stopped keeping one. [205] She habitually borrowed personalities the way other printers borrowed ink, and tonight she wore the calm, clipped manner of a lighthouse keeper. [227] "Sit where the topaz light forgets its own edge," she said, pointing to a stool half in shadow. [245] He blinked, uncertain, and that small disorientation was exactly what she needed. [257] "You want me to map a feeling you cannot name," she continued, deliberately ambiguous enough to make his certainties loosen. [277] "The stars are vanishing because their emotional equations have become illegible," he said. [290] "Then bring me the illegible part," Maris replied. [298] He unrolled a chart marked with a blank patch where a star should have been. [313] "This star, Arcturine, will vanish before dawn unless we know what it feels." [326] "You assume stars feel one thing at a time," she said, and began to set type. [342] "I cannot reach a star's feeling directly, but I can borrow yours and find its harmonic." [358] "You will feel what the star feels, briefly, and I will print the result before you shatter." [375] The young man hesitated, then nodded. [381] Maris lifted the halo fragment and pressed its cold edge to the hollow of his throat. [397] The transposed empathy flooded through her as a lucid turbulence, a bright and drowning sensation that had no single name. [417] She felt the star's loneliness, yes, but beneath it a need for witnesses. [430] Her hands moved while the feeling was still raw, setting lines that used no common alphabet. [446] The young man gasped and gripped the edge of the worktable. [457] Maris chose words that would disorient the star itself, an address meant to be felt, not decoded. [474] "I am the space between two vanishings" was the first line she locked. [487] "Hold still," she told the young man, "or the empathy will transpose back and leave you empty." [504] Outside, through the topaz windows, another star dimmed as if testing the strength of the night. [520] They were between vanishing stars, in the only hour when an emotion could be rewritten. [535] Maris inked the type. [539] "Emotion is not arithmetic," she said, "it is topology." [548] "Loss and wonder can be the same point seen from different angles." [594] The young man collapsed to his knees, but his eyes cleared. [605] "What did you print?" he whispered. [611] Maris turned the paper toward him. [649] "Its emotion needed to see itself." [655] Outside, the dimming star steadied. [660] The young man stared at his shaking hands. [668] "I felt it," he said. [673] "For a moment, I was the gap between the stars." [683] Maris removed the halo fragment and wrapped it in black cloth. [694] "That is the mathematics of emotion," she said. [702] "It cannot be solved, only shared." [708] The next star did not vanish that night, and the topaz workshop grew quiet beneath the remaining constellations. [726] In the silent pressroom, Maris sat with the borrowed personality of a lighthouse keeper, letting the lucid turbulence of shared feeling settle like ink into paper. [752]
