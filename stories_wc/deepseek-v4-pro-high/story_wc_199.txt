Lome was the most orderly seed sorter in the ashen skyborne arbor. [12]
She worked alone on a platform of silver-gray branches, sorting seeds by weight, shape, and the faint heat they remembered from the soil below. [36]
Every seed had a drawer, and every drawer had a label etched with moss. [50]
The arbor floated in a pale sky where the sun never burned, only silvered the leaves like old pewter. [69]
Moss grew in the joints of the railing, soft and green, and Lome let it spread because it remembered the names she forgot. [92]
Regional folklore said that during the hush where past meets prophecy, the falling leaves wrote a single message across the arbor. [113]
Lome needed to decode that message in falling leaves. [122]
Her method was building tiny monuments to forgotten things. [131]
She gathered lost seed hulls, empty chrysalids, and the curled edges of old leaves, then stacked them into small cairns along the platform. [154]
Each monument marked something the arbor had forgotten: a pollinator, a drought year, a tree that bloomed before memory began. [174]
When the hush began, the air stilled. [181]
Leaves dropped straight down, not spinning, and each one landed beside a different monument. [195]
Lome read the pattern by the order of monument to leaf, but for nine seasons the message refused to resolve. [215]
On the tenth morning of the hush, a leaf struck the monument she had built for the first green shoot. [235]
Something glittered on its gray surface. [241]
Lome bent close and saw a moonstone cabochon dust speck, no larger than a grain of ash. [258]
She did not brush it away, because regional folklore warned that moonstone dust was a key, not a waste. [277]
The old tales also said that a seed touched by such a speck emerges transformed always. [293]
Lome prodded the speck with a splinter of cedar. [302]
The dust split cleanly, and one half sank into the leaf while the other half sat in her palm. [321]
The leaf's veins lit like silver water under the ashen sky. [332]
They spelled a sentence in the curling script of old roots. [343]
She read it aloud: "Sort the last seed by what it has forgotten, not by what it will become." [362]
Lome frowned and walked to the unsorted pile. [370]
There, hidden between a maple key and a thistle tuft, lay a seed as dark as the bottom of a well. [391]
It was warm, and its coat was cracked with the shape of a tiny, lost river. [407]
She knew this seed from the older records. [415]
It belonged to a tree called the Silver Crown, which had fallen from the arbor before any living sorter was born. [436]
No one remembered its true name, only that it had once held the moon in its branches. [453]
Lome built one last tiny monument to the forgotten Silver Crown. [464]
She used a shelf of bark, three dried anthers, and a strand of moss from the railing. [481]
She placed the dark seed in the center and set the moonstone cabochon dust speck upon its cracked coat. [500]
Then she prodded the seed once, exactly as the old tales instructed. [512]
The husk opened with a sound like a small green bell. [523]
A shoot rose from the seed and emerged transformed always, pearled with gray light and mossy at the edges. [542]
It grew not upward but outward, weaving its stem through the tiny monuments she had built. [558]
Each forgotten thing she had marked began to tremble. [567]
From the monuments, new leaves unfurled, silver underneath and soft with moss. [579]
The falling leaves of the arbor changed their course and spiraled once around the seedling. [594]
The pattern at last resolved. [599]
Lome saw the message whole, written in leaf shadow and moonstone light. [611]
It said that the past and the prophecy were not opposites; they were the same seed turning in the hush. [631]
The arbor had wanted her to remember that order was only memory with a steady hand. [647]
She returned to her drawers and opened the one labeled with moss for the Silver Crown. [663]
A single leaf from the new seedling drifted into it. [673]
Lome closed the drawer and smiled with mossy delight, because the message was no longer falling away from her. [692]
She would keep sorting, but now each seed went into its drawer with a tiny monument placed beside it. [711]
The ashen skyborne arbor drifted on, quiet and green at the edges, holding its past like a seed and its prophecy like a leaf. [735]
