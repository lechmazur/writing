The feral library guardian crouched at the edge of the frozen garden in mist, where the first boundary of light bled silver across the ice. [25] It did not remember choosing this post, only that the shelves behind it had become a den and the cold had become a kind of pelt. [51] A cultist's pendant, strung on a frayed cord, rested against its chest and ticked faintly like a second heart. [70] The guardian was dimensionally homesick, and it had learned to wait. [81] It waited the way frost waits on a stem, without needing the stem to bloom. [96] Every night through the veil of sleep, the mist thickened and the garden became a room the world had forgotten. [116] The guardian had one method for surviving that hour: interiorizing exteriors. [127] It would draw the frozen garden inside itself, storing the black branches and white paths as if they were memories, until the outside was no longer larger than its own breath. [158] That was how it befriended the uncertainty that came with the dark. [170] The pendant grew warm exactly when the boundary of light began to dissolve, a warning that the veil had thinned enough for something to cross. [195] It did not attack the sound that followed, only listened as if the sound were a question. [212] From the far side of the ice, a figure made of condensed fog knelt among the frozen roses. [230] The guardian's first instinct was to guard, but guarding felt like an old language it no longer spoke fluently. [249] Instead, it waited, and the figure waited back. [257] The pendant's tick aligned with the figure's breathing, and the frozen garden in mist became a single held note. [276] The guardian understood then that the figure was not an intruder. [287] It was a piece of the home-dimension, shaped like a visitor, sent to ask if the guardian still wanted to be found. [309] The guardian did not answer with words. [316] It interiorized the figure too, placing the fog-made knees and the slow breath inside the same inner room where it kept the stars from its lost sky. [343] The boundaries of light returned, one thin line at a time, and the garden turned lucid without losing its fog. [363] The figure faded, but the warmth of the cultist's pendant remained, not as a warning now but as a hand held open. [385] The guardian rose and walked the perimeter of the library, running its fingers over ice-cased spines. [401] It still felt dimensionally homesick. [406] But for the first time, homesickness felt less like a wound and more like a weather system it could carry. [426] It understood that the veil of sleep was not a wall to guard against. [440] It was a door that opened only for those who could wait in foggy lucidity. [455] The feral library guardian made its rounds until dawn, not patrolling but listening. [468] It listened to the ice tick, to the mist settle, to the far-off hum of a dimension that might one day call it back. [492] When morning came, the garden remained frozen but the mist thinned to a bright membrane. [507] The guardian sat on the library steps and held the pendant out to the light. [522] It no longer needed the cultist's pendant to tell it when the veil would thin. [537] It had learned to read the boundary of light in its own ribcage, a gift from interiorizing exteriors. [555] But it kept the pendant because friendship with uncertainty requires a keepsake. [567] The next night, through the veil of sleep, it did not wait for the figure. [582] It waited with the uncertainty itself, letting the fog fill the spaces between its thoughts. [597] The garden became a place the guardian no longer needed to defend. [609] It belonged there by standing just outside belonging. [617] The figure returned, but this time the guardian did not interiorize it. [629] Instead, it let the figure remain exterior, a fog-made companion in the frozen garden in mist. [645] The cultist's pendant beat softly between them, no longer a boundary but a bridge. [659] The guardian knew that home might never be a place it could enter again. [673] But it also knew that through the veil of sleep, every exile could learn to be a threshold. [691] So it remained, feral and patient, guarding the one thing worth guarding: the space where light and fog agree to meet. [712] That space was not a fortress. [718] That space was a friendship with uncertainty, kept in the belly of a frozen garden. [733] The figure raised one fog-made hand, and the guardian raised the pendant to the boundary of light. [750] They waited together until the mist became the only wall the library needed. [763] And the guardian, still dimensionally homesick, finally felt at home enough to keep waiting. [777]
