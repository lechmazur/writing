Sera hummed to the service lift as it began its crawl up the icebound spire. [15] She was only a servant, but she was an ascending one, climbing ten floors each dawn with a crate of clean linen. [37] Around her neck hung her father's airship captain's goggles, brass frames cold against her collarbone. [52] She wore them always because they helped her ask the small questions that others forgot. [67] The lift rattled past the first floor, and dawn light bent through the ice sheath outside the shaft. [85] Through scattered light, the frost on the cage door glittered like a coded message. [99] Sera pulled the goggles over her eyes, a warmly inquisitive habit from years of watching clouds. [115] The blue left lens and amber right lens turned the glare into gentle ribbons. [129] By the second floor, she noticed a thread of copper winking behind a loose panel join. [145] She set down the linen crate and pressed the panel with her thumb. [158] The metal unfolded away from the wall with a sigh. [168] Behind it lay a thin, oilcloth-wrapped diary, jammed beside the lift's greasy gears. [181] Sera lifted it out as the elevator climbed past the third floor. [193] The cover was unmarked, but the pages inside were dense with symbols that meant nothing. [208] This was an encrypted diary, and it had been waiting in the dark. [221] She turned a page and held it toward the scattered light. [232] Nothing appeared at first, only the ordinary ink of some private, guarded hand. [245] Then she looked through the amber lens, and the symbols folded into letters. [258] Her breath caught. [261] The goggles did not merely shade the light; their twin lenses separated and recombined it. [276] Her father had once told her that airship captains read clouds this way, seeking the warm seam between storms. [295] Now his diary was answering the same question. [303] By the fourth floor, she had unfolded the first entry. [313] It spoke of a crash that left the ship stranded on a frozen ridge above the clouds. [330] Her father wrote of the silence after the engines died, and of the guilt he carried like a second cold. [350] Sera's fingers trembled, but she kept reading as the lift passed floor five. [363] The next entry was not about the crash itself, but about the breakfast he had made from emergency rations and melted snow. [385] He described the absurd pleasure of a warm biscuit eaten while his hands went numb. [400] That was the pattern she began to see: each page of anguish ended with a small, stubborn joy. [418] Her father had written in cipher not to hide his sorrow, but to protect the joy he had finally learned to notice. [440] Floor six brought a violent gust, and the lift swayed in its icebound track. [454] Sera braced herself against the wall and kept the diary open. [465] Through scattered light, another page unfolded its meaning. [473] It held a list of things he wanted her to remember: the color of frost on a closed petal, the weight of a warm cup, the sound of a stranger's laugh. [504] She laughed once, quietly, though her eyes stung. [512] The lift climbed past floor seven, and the light shifted from gold to silver. [526] In the silver light, the diary's final pages revealed a folded sketch of the spire, drawn from the sky. [545] It was the place he had dreamed of returning to, where his servant daughter would rise each day to meet the clouds. [567] Sera realized that he had hidden the diary in the service lift because only she would pass this way every morning. [588] He had trusted her curiosity, her affectionate hunger for the world behind the world. [602] Floor eight arrived with a whine, and she refolded the sketch carefully. [614] She wanted to read more, but the elevator was climbing too fast now. [627] Floor nine passed before she could decode the last symbol. [637] She pressed her eye to the amber lens one more time. [648] The symbol became a single word: "Look." [655] Sera lifted her head. [659] Through the shaft's ice, the sun had broken fully over the cloud sea. [672] The light scattered into a thousand soft colors across the diary, her hands, and her father's goggles. [689] She finally understood that the encryption was not a puzzle to defeat but a door to stand in. [707] She had sought the joy beneath anguish and found it woven through the ordinary when she wore the right eyes. [727] The elevator chimed for the tenth floor. [734] Sera tucked the diary into her apron and slid the goggles back around her neck. [749] The doors opened on the bright corridor where her duties waited. [760] She stepped out, partly inside that private sky, and smiled with affectionate detachment at the clean linen still in the crate. [781] Then she walked, an ascending servant carrying a secret that would not let the day be small. [798]
