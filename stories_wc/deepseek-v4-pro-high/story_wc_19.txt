Mara was the claustrophobic child of a bunker builder, and every underground wall pressed toward her like a slow exhale. [20]
She inherited his fear of the open sky and inverted it into a hunger. [34]
Her only inheritance from her mother was a tiny porcelain doll with a hollow belly and a mouth shaped like a small, unfinished word. [58]
She kept it not for comfort but for capacity. [67]
For years she had been collecting the flavors of different silences, and the doll was the only vessel small enough to hold what had no weight. [93]
Some silences were chalky, some were salted with old rain, but she wanted the rare ones that tasted like height. [113]
The bunker's quiet tasted of iron and old breath; she stored it only once, then sealed that flavor away because it stung. [135]
Her father designed shelters against every disaster except the disaster of being buried alive in your own chest. [153]
Safety, he believed, was a ceiling; Mara believed it was a lid. [165]
Mara needed the opposite: the high places, the thin air, the sky's enormous lack of walls. [181]
Above the clouds, a xenial trellis waited for anyone willing to climb its weather-smoothed rungs. [196]
Wind had polished the rungs, and old carvings promised welcome to anyone with empty hands. [211]
Nobody knew who built it, but its handholds angled like open arms, and its slender beams sprouted new leaves each time the gravity hour passed. [236]
Her father called it the inversion, the hour when gravity inverts for an hour, as if saying it twice made it safer. [258]
During that single hour, Mara could stand on the underside of the trellis and look down into the cloud sea with her hair falling upward. [283]
Clouds drifted below like a second, softer earth. [291]
The gilded hush began at the first tremor of reversal, a silence that felt gold-plated and warm, pressed into the air like a coin. [315]
It was intangibly tangible: she could feel its weight on her tongue, yet her fingers closed through it. [333]
Mara had learned to collect it by limiting exposure; the hush only kept its gilt edges if she listened for less than one full minute. [357]
She held the doll open at the mouth, and the silence poured into its hollow belly like honey that had forgotten how to stick. [381]
That day she wanted more than a minute. [389]
The bunker had shrunk her for fourteen years, and the open hush offered a space wide enough to stretch every contracted part of her. [413]
She left the doll open too long, and the gilded hush began to thicken into a visible gold thread that wrapped around her wrist. [437]
The trellis sprouted a new shoot at her touch, pale and trembling, as if the silence were putting out roots. [457]
Mara tried to close the doll, but the porcelain mouth had grown warm and slippery in her palm. [475]
She understood then that she had mistaken capacity for permission. [485]
The gilded hush was not hers to keep; it belonged to the hour, and the hour was already stretching into its final minutes. [508]

The golden thread loosened and dissolved into an intangibly tangible dust that settled back into the cloud sea. [551]

The bunker door opened for her, but for once its iron silence tasted less like burial and more like the pause before flight. [624]
She placed the doll beside her cot, its hollow belly holding one drop of sky. [639]
In the morning she would climb again, because there were more flavors of silence above the clouds, and she meant to collect each one slowly. [664]
