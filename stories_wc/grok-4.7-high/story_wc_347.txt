<story>Mara kept the gate because the previous caretaker had walked into the cliffside labyrinth and never come back up. [19] The orchard on the rim was cursed in a plain way. [30] Its trees bent toward the drop, and each offered only one small nut in a season. [46] People still climbed to her door. [52] The harbor lay below, and the only descent before the night frost formed ran through stone corridors patrolled by mechanical birds. [73] Those birds wheeled on brass hinges and cried in short metal notes. [85] They struck any hand that brushed a warm tile. [94] She had not wanted the post. [100] Reluctance sat on her like salt grit that never left her cuffs. [112] Yet lost travelers arrived with maps that lied. [120] If she turned them away, the birds finished what the frost began. [132] She meant to guide them to safety, then return to pruning, then forget their faces. [147] The curse made even that modest aim difficult. [155] Old charts could not be touched. [161] A palm on ink rearranged corridors by dawn. [169] What the orchard remembered, and people did not, was a forgotten blueprint grown fresh inside the nuts. [186] To assemble it she had to obey the only method the curse permitted. [199] That method was leaving impressions without touching. [206] She set clay boards along the orchard wall where sea wind came in clean. [220] She tipped the small nut from a wooden spoon onto the first board. [233] The nut rolled. [236] It pressed a shallow groove and stopped. [243] She angled the board with a forked stick, never with her skin, and the nut went on. [260] Each impression was a piece of the night path. [269] A stair appeared. [272] A gap opened where the mechanical birds banked and left a human width of shade. [287] A turn showed that the frost would lock if they waited too long. [300] She worked without grace and without waste. [307] Sap stained her sleeve, and her knot of hair came loose. [318] The lines were ugly and exact, a graceless beauty no painter would claim and no frightened person could ignore. [337] Travelers learned quickly that she was offhandedly exact. [345] Ask her how far and she answered thirty-eight steps, then a left at the third bird shadow, as if naming a dull knife. [368] She did not soften counts. [373] Soft counts killed. [376] Accuracy was the only kindness she could stand to give. [386] That afternoon two travelers reached the gate with tickets folded to rags. [398] Frost already showed as a pale thread on the lower stones. [409] She did not greet them with warmth. [416] She nodded, took the day's small nut from the oldest tree, and told them to watch. [432] Watching mattered. [434] If she assembled the forgotten blueprint alone, later lines smeared. [444] Witnesses kept the impressions honest. [449] Attention itself seemed a weight the curse respected. [457] She rolled the nut across seven boards. [464] The forgotten blueprint gathered under their eyes, dent by dent. [474] She spoke the numbers as they appeared. [481] Twelve. [482] Nine. [483] Twenty-three. [484] Four. [485] She was offhandedly exact, as if the figures had waited in her mouth since morning. [500] The pair repeated them until the counts sat steady. [509] Wind worried the clay. [513] Mechanical birds clicked along the upper rim, close enough to flash their glass eyes and not close enough to strike. [533] They patrolled. [535] They did not yet forbid. [540] The gap was a rule of the hour before the night frost formed. [553] Mara knew that rule the way she knew which branch would drop its nut if she breathed wrong. [571] Near the end the nut hesitated. [577] Old stories said one scrap of the blueprint was always lost. [588] Mara had learned that the hesitation was the scrap. [597] She let the nut rest until the clay took a round dent the size of the nut itself. [615] That dent was the harbor door. [621] Beyond it the birds did not patrol, because the curse ended where salt water started. [636] She had bought that knowledge with failure. [643] Once she had touched a board to hurry a line, and the path had folded. [658] Two travelers had spent the night in a loop while frost wrote over their tracks. [673] Reluctance after that was not laziness. [679] It was respect for a thing that punished haste and punished love of one's own hands equally. [696] She raised the boards so they could watch her assemble the ridges into one path. [711] Nothing about it was elegant. [716] Still the shape held. [720] It was a map for boots and not for fingers. [730] She gave the rule in the same flat voice. [739] Match your steps to these impressions. [745] Do not brush the walls. [750] Do not offer the birds a hand. [757] They went before the night frost formed. [764] Mara stayed with the small nut cooling in a bowl she refused to touch. [778] She listened until the clicks faded toward the harbor and stopped. [789] Silence was the only thanks she trusted. [796] </story>
