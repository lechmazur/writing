Mara kept her plant nursery open through storm season because closed glass taught her nothing about how living things endure weather. [21]
She was the owner, and the title had once meant catalogs, irrigation schedules, and a pride in making growth obey. [41]
Lately the title felt like a coat she wore wrong. [51]

She would say the beds looked tired, then mention the ditches, then admit she had stopped forcing blooms so she could watch what the plants chose instead. [99]
People called that evasive. [103]
She knew it was only circuitously sincere, an honesty that needed room to turn before it faced anyone. [121]
Her real motive was narrower and stranger than sales. [130]
She wanted to study plant behaviors while the sky kept breaking and mending, because calm weeks made every stem look obedient and therefore false. [154]

The moss had thickened overnight into a green that seemed to breathe. [188]
