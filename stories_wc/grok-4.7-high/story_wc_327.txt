The copper-veined storyteller came to the canyon of endless echoes when the last speakers of her mother tongue could no longer name the wind. [24]
Veins of dull copper traced her throat and wrists, bright only when she swallowed a word she refused to waste. [44]
She was quietly articulate, and the canyon seemed to lean closer to hear a voice that never rose. [62]
Around her, towers of bone rose from the canyon floor like the ribs of a beast that had died speaking. [82]
Between two such towers she found the mouth of a tunnel of echoes, a throat of stone that repeated not sound but the shape of sound. [108]
She carried a flawed diamond rumored to be cursed, a stone the size of a thumbnail with a crack like a dry river. [131]
Old tellers said the flaw drank languages and left speakers with only the memory of having meant something. [149]
She did not believe the rumor in the way a child believes a warning, yet she held the diamond as if belief were a tool. [174]
Her motive was narrower than legend and harder to refuse. [184]
She had come to translate texture into sound before the last grain of her language wore away. [201]
Words, she knew, were not only meanings. [208]
They were the rough of bark under a name, the cool of river stone, the catch in a throat when grief arrived too fast. [232]
Across the death of a language those textures were vanishing first, while dictionaries still pretended the words remained. [250]
She stepped into the tunnel of echoes and spoke one word in the old tongue, the word for the nap of woven cloth. [273]
The tunnel returned the word stripped of touch, a clean bell with nothing under it. [288]
She tried again, pressing the flawed diamond to the bone wall so the crack faced the dark. [305]
A faint scrape answered, like cloth dragged over stone, and then silence took it. [319]
The curse, if curse it was, did not steal her voice. [330]
It only refused to keep what she could not yet give shape. [342]
Days passed in the towers of bone, and each day another speaker far beyond the canyon forgot a verb for carrying. [363]
She felt the death of the language as a thinning in her own mouth, copper veins cooling when a phrase would not come. [386]
Still she remained quietly articulate, saving breath for the sentences that still held grain. [400]
On the seventh morning a flock of birds lifted from the bone spires and turned as one dark page above the canyon of endless echoes. [425]
Their flight wrote and rewrote a pattern, a tight wheel, a sudden shear, a slow braid that opened and closed like a lung. [448]
She watched until the pattern settled in her mind the way a melody settles, not as picture but as sequence. [468]
The tunnel of echoes, she understood then, did not want louder speech. [480]
It wanted a score. [484]
Texture was not a single note but a change across time, the way cloth changes under a hand, the way a flock changes the sky. [509]
She returned to the tunnel mouth with the flawed diamond and waited for the birds. [524]
When they rose again she began to speak, not reciting words but timing them through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight. [548]
A wheel of wings, and she gave the rough of bark. [559]
A shear, and she gave the cool of stone. [568]
A braid opening, and she gave the catch of grief, her voice still low, still quietly articulate, never begging the canyon to listen. [591]
The flawed diamond warmed against the bone. [598]
Its crack drank the timed speech and spilled it back as layered sound, each layer a texture the dying language had almost lost. [621]

The rumor of a curse loosened its grip. [652]
What had seemed theft was only hunger for order, a stone that could keep texture if texture arrived as pattern. [672]
Through the long afternoon she worked, and the work spanned what remained of the language's life. [688]
Far away, the final speaker set down a cup and could not recall the word for the heat in its clay. [709]
In the same hour the copper-veined storyteller caught that missing heat in a rising turn of wings and sent it into the tunnel. [732]

She did not smile. [761]

She lifted the flawed diamond, no longer rumored only as a curse, and listened while the flock wrote the next line. [794]
