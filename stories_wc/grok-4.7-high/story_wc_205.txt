On the baroque asteroid of Vesperine, after time became visible, the halls glittered with slow gold filaments that drifted like pollen through carved cornices and gilt stairwells. [27]
Elian Voss walked those halls as a defunct revolutionary, his old banners long since folded into the museum cases he had once meant to burn. [52]
He was poetically inclined, and sorrow sat on his tongue like a burnished coin he could not spend. [70]
The archaeological dispute had cracked the asteroid's scholars into two bitter camps. [82]
At the center of their quarrel lay a crystal vase shard no larger than a palm, its edge still sharp enough to draw a thin bright line of blood from the careless. [114]
One faction swore the shard proved the lost people of Lumenfall had been shatterers of beauty, a warrior cult whose rites justified the iron laws Elian had failed to overthrow. [144]
The other faction, smaller and mocked, held that the break was ceremonial, a deliberate gift of fragments to the dark so that light might be remembered. [170]
Elian wanted neither verdict as it stood. [177]
His motivation, nursed through years of failed marches and quiet exile, was to reimagine an entire civilization, not merely to win a footnote in a catalog. [203]
If Lumenfall could be seen anew, the living laws built upon its ghost might loosen, and his dead cause might find a gentler afterlife than victory. [229]
Time, now visible, complicated every claim. [235]
It hung in the air as fine amber threads, and during storms those threads thickened into ropes that lashed the mirrors until each glass showed not a face but a century. [266]
The old custom of the baroque courts had been covering mirrors during storms, a courtesy to the dead and a shield for the living. [290]
Scholars now called the custom superstition. [296]
Elian, poetically inclined and therefore dangerous to literal men, believed the custom was a method of seeing by refusal. [315]
He began to devise a rite that would settle the dispute without letting either camp own the shard. [333]
For seven nights he studied the shard under cloth, noting how the visible time pooled in its fracture like honey in a crack. [356]
On the eighth night a storm gathered over the asteroid's gilt horizon, and the filaments rose into a shining weather. [376]
Elian carried the crystal vase shard into the Hall of Returning Faces, where a hundred mirrors waited like open mouths. [396]
He did not uncover them. [401]
Instead he practiced covering mirrors during storms with black velvet cut from his old revolutionary coat, the same cloth that had once flown above barricades. [426]

He told the covered mirrors that Lumenfall had not been a cult of ruin nor a cult of pretty grief. [486]

The shard warmed. [518]
Visible time threaded itself through the fracture and wrote, in a script of slow gold, a scene no catalog had recorded. [539]

He saw the storm come, and he saw those same hands covering mirrors, not from fear but from mercy. [587]
The archaeological dispute had assumed the shard was evidence of a single truth. [600]
Elian understood then that truth, after time becomes visible, is a weather one must dress for. [616]
He had devised not a proof but a practice: covering mirrors during storms while holding the shard, so that the past could speak without being forced to serve a present hunger. [647]

To reimagine an entire civilization, he told them, one does not seize its relics. [718]

Elian remained a defunct revolutionary, for no banner rose and no law fell that night. [772]
