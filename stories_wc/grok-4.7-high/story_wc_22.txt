The gloom-hill coachman came to the blooming jetty at twilight and stood where the planks narrowed into a vast pale sea. [21]
This was the season people called after the reef dies, a name that refused the past tense, as if the dying had not finished taking what it wanted. [49]
He had not carried a passenger since the coastal inns closed. [60]
The road from the inland hills still took a coach, but it ended now in wind and a smell of spoiled kelp. [82]
He came because the flats under the jetty had been a battlefield, and he meant to let a garden reclaim a battlefield before the salt sealed it shut. [110]
That motive had ridden with him longer than any fare. [120]
In the satchel on the boards lay a collection of dried seed pods gathered from orchards soldiers had cut for firewood and never replanted. [144]
They clicked when he shifted them, light as knucklebones. [153]
He speaks dead languages, and he used one now, softly, so the words would not startle the birds working the shallows. [174]
The speech belonged to divers who had read the reef as a ledger. [187]
In that ledger lived the submerged concord, an agreement set into living coral: the war dead would be left to the water, and no one would mine the shallows for iron, bone, or revenge. [221]
When the reef died, the concord sank with it, clauses and all, and only those who could still pronounce the old tongue could say what had been promised. [249]
He was such a man, a coachman by trade and a keeper of vanished speech by compulsion. [266]
Above the jetty the birds were already translating the mud. [276]
He worked by understanding bird calls, which was slower than prayer and more exact. [290]
A tern's double cry meant sweet silt under the far pilings. [301]
A sandpiper's whistle meant salt too sharp for any seed but the gray vine. [315]
The heron's single low note, when it came, marked a line he must not cross, because beyond it the tide still hid unexploded charges from the last year of fighting. [345]
He answered each call in the dead language, repeating the boundary so the submerged concord would have a witness even if it could not rise. [370]
Then he began to fertilize. [375]
The word felt plain for so much care, yet it was the action the ground required. [391]
He split a pod and mixed its seeds with crumbled kelp and the white dust of shell, then pressed the mixture into a crack where rain had gathered between blooming boards. [422]

Narrowness was the method's other half. [437]
