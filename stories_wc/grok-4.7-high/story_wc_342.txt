The tree trunk library stood in a square of pale dust, its bark walls still holding the shelves a scholar's guild had hollowed centuries before. [25]
Around it the condemned building waited, windows already gone, and the hush just before demolition lay over every grain of air. [46]
Mara knelt on the worn floor with her brushes aligned like quiet instruments. [59]
She was a face blind portrait artist, and faces had never assembled for her into persons. [75]
A nose was a ridge, a mouth a brief weather, an eye a well she could measure but never name. [95]
What she could do, and had done all her working life, was craft interiors that held people as structures of light. [116]
Her motivation was simple and severe: to mirror the sky inside walls, so that a room might keep noon even after the door was shut. [141]
The guild had commissioned that work when the trunk was still a living library and the building around it was only a plan. [164]
Now the city had condemned the whole mass, bark and brick together, and the crews would arrive when the hush broke. [185]
She had one task left before that break. [193]
On a high shelf, between treatises on proportion, sat a volume whose spine was too thick for its title. [212]
It was the secret book safe the last guild warden had shown her, its pages glued into a shallow box. [232]
Inside lay a centuries-old coin, worn almost smooth, and a slip of instructions in a hand she trusted because it never tried to draw a face. [258]
The slip said the coin had been pressed, year after year, against the inner curve of the trunk where dawn first entered. [280]
Faint indentations remained: not a portrait, not a map, but a pressure record of sky as the wood had received it. [301]
Through faint indentations on a centuries-old coin, she was meant to craft the final mirroring. [316]
She set the coin on a cloth and tilted a lamp until the worn face threw a shallow relief. [335]
Her eyes, useless for people, were exact for edges. [344]
She read each nick as a fraction of brightness, then the fractions inside those fractions, a habit the guild had once called fractally precise. [368]
Where a shallow dent caught light, she mixed a cooler blue. [379]
Where metal had been spared, she laid a thin gold that would read, in the right hour, as cloud. [398]
She worked on the inner wall of the trunk, the only surface the wrecking ball might strike last. [416]
Stroke by stroke she tried to mirror the sky inside walls that would not stand. [431]
The hush deepened rather than lifted. [437]
No engine yet, no shouted count, only the small sound of bristle on plastered bark. [452]
She had painted patrons this way for thirty years, translating what she could not recognize into rooms they could inhabit. [472]
They had thanked a woman whose features they later failed to recall, which had always seemed to her a fair exchange. [493]
Today the patron was the sky itself, and the coin was its only remaining face. [508]
Halfway up the curve she stopped. [514]
The indentations grew too faint to resolve, even under the lamp, even with the patience that had made her work fractally precise. [536]
Beyond a certain wear the metal kept its history and refused to lend it. [550]
She pressed her thumb beside the last clear nick and felt nothing the eye had missed. [566]
This was the discovery she had postponed for decades: discovering limits was not a failure of nerve but a property of the world. [589]
Her face blindness had a border, her craft had a border, and the coin, which she had trusted as infinite detail, had a border too. [614]
She did not invent what the metal would not give. [624]
Instead she left the upper wall bare, a pale band of plastered bark, and painted only to the line where evidence ended. [646]
The bare band was not emptiness. [652]
It was the honest edge of a sky she could mirror and a sky she could not. [669]
In the heightened stillness she heard her own breath and nothing else. [681]
She returned the coin to the secret book safe and set the volume back between the treatises, as if the library might yet be consulted. [706]
Outside, a gull crossed the open roof and its shadow slid down the painted curve, brief as a passing face. [726]
She did not try to know the gull. [734]
She watched the shadow meet the line she had refused to cross, and she let it go. [751]
When the first engine coughed at the edge of the square, the hush did not shatter so much as step aside. [772]
Mara closed her paint box. [777]
The walls would fall holding only the sky the coin had allowed, and that limit was the portrait. [795]
