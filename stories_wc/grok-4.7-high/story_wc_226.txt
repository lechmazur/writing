The canyon of endless echoes lay under a forged hush that felt hammered into place by centuries of withheld cries. [17] Towers of bone stood in crooked ranks, each one a monument to some unquiet life that had once climbed and never descended. [36] Before the ritual the air itself seemed to wait, thick with the knowledge that soon a single truth would be demanded and then locked away. [58] A pale spire in mist rose at the canyon's throat, its surface the color of old moonlight and its tip lost in drifting vapor. [80] The defiant artist approached it without haste, her movements innocently mischievous, as if she were late to a game rather than early to a reckoning. [103] She carried no tools except her voice, and her motivation was simple and absolute: to refract a single truth into many before the ritual could claim it whole. [129] The price of forgiveness, she had learned, was the surrender of that truth to the spire, after which the speaker would hear only silence for the rest of their days. [157] She would not pay it. [162] Instead she began the work through impromptu poetry sessions, speaking verses that the endless echoes immediately seized and altered. [180] I struck the bell that should have stayed still, she said at the foot of the first tower, and the bones returned the line as I stilled the bell that should have struck. [209] Each repetition bent the meaning a fraction further from its origin. [220] She walked on, smiling the small smile of someone who has just hidden a pebble in a friend's shoe and is waiting to see the limp. [245] That innocently mischievous spark never left her face, even as the gravity of her defiance deepened. [261] At the second tower she offered a new poem about hands that both built and broke the same bridge. [278] The echoes multiplied the image until the canyon rang, softly, with bridges that existed and bridges that did not. [298] No single confession could survive such refraction. [305] She understood that the ritual's power lay in unity; a fractured truth could not be weighed, could not be purchased, could not be silenced. [329] So she kept speaking, hour after hour, letting the towers of bone become her collaborators. [344] One verse imagined the pale spire as a throat that had forgotten how to swallow. [358] Another asked the mist whether it forgave the spire for piercing it every dawn. [373] The answers came back tangled, contradictory, and therefore useless to any priest who might later try to gather them. [393] The forged hush never broke into clamor; it only grew more intricate, a quiet woven from too many nearly identical threads. [415] Listeners far above the canyon rim, if they existed, would have heard not a crime but a weather of almost-crimes. [435] The artist paused to rest against a femur-thick column and watched her breath join the mist. [451] She felt the price of forgiveness hovering like a debt that someone else would now have to negotiate in pieces. [472] Her own name would not be the one erased. [481] That knowledge did not lighten her step so much as steady it. [493] She resumed the sessions as the light thinned. [501] Climbing the lower ribs of the pale spire itself, she recited a longer piece about a bowl that had been broken, mended, lost, and never owned. [527] The echoes rushed up the shaft and returned as a rain of syllables that no longer formed a sentence anyone could swear to. [550] For the first time the forged hush seemed almost kind. [560] She sat on a ledge and let the fragments drift. [570] Moonrise was still an hour away, and the ritual would not start until then. [584] She had time for one last poem. [591] Count the truths and they become weather, she whispered. [600] Count the weathers and they become mist. [607] Count the mist and you will find the spire still standing, but no longer hungry. [623] The canyon of endless echoes held those words without returning them. [634] In the sudden deeper quiet she understood that the refraction was complete. [646] The single truth had been spent into shares too small for any one voice to reclaim or surrender. [664] Whatever forgiveness the ritual might still offer would arrive already divided, and the price would be paid in fractions no single throat could feel as total silence. [692] She remained on the ledge, innocently mischievous even in stillness, watching the pale spire in mist as if it were a friend she had just outwitted at cards. [720] The towers of bone stood around her like an audience that had already begun to forget the plot. [737] Before the ritual she closed her eyes and listened to the forged hush she had helped to shape, a hush that now contained multitudes and therefore could not be owned. [767]
