After blowing the whistle, the hydraulic clock shepherd remained on the balcony of the horizon measurement station and let the last echo die against the fjord. [26]
No wind moved the water, and the pipes that fed his clocks settled into a murmur so low it might have been the stone itself breathing. [52]
He had sounded the whistle to close the dawn horizon fix, exactly as the station manual required, and the signal had done its narrow duty. [77]
What followed the signal was the part no manual described. [87]
Colleagues at the coastal posts called him eminently peculiar for staying after the blast, and for treating the clocks as a flock rather than as instruments. [113]
He accepted the phrase without protest, because peculiarity had kept him employed when tidier men were rotated inland. [131]
His real reason for remaining was older than their jokes and harder to explain at a mess table. [149]
He wanted to understand what clocks really measure. [157]
The ledgers said duration, a clean interval between one sighting of the horizon and the next, but duration had begun to feel like a curtain drawn over a sharper fact. [187]
In his coat he carried a mantle clock key that had no lock anywhere on the station. [204]
It was a small brass piece from a city parlor, darkened by years of a household hand, and its teeth had been worn nearly to razor edges. [231]
He did not know why he had brought it north, except that a key implied a door, and he had not yet found the door his work was meant to open. [262]
He went into the clock room, where six hydraulic vessels stood in iron cradles along the inner wall. [280]
Water entered each vessel from the fjord main, rose against a float, and fell again through a governed valve, and the fall turned a hand on a dial ruled for the horizon watches. [313]
Cold made springs lie, the old engineers had said, but water, if watched, told a truer hour. [330]
So he shepherded them. [334]
He listened for a vessel that hurried, for a float that stuttered, for any animal in the row that broke from the common pace. [358]
This morning, after the whistle, all six held the same second. [369]
Their agreement should have comforted him, and it did not. [379]
He took the mantle clock key to the western window and lifted it until its worn teeth crossed the line of the sea. [402]
The horizon that day was a single stroke, bright and severe, and the clear air gave it no mercy of haze. [423]
He had to squint to hold the stroke steady, because looking straight at it made the line tremble and double, as if his eyes refused a boundary with no width. [453]
The squint was how he read the station arc as well, narrowing the world until only the mark and the moment remained. [475]
He chose to test that old question through counting backwards from the whistle. [488]
Each number he released had to match a visible retreat of water in the nearest vessel, or he discarded it and began again. [511]
Sixty, fifty-nine, fifty-eight, and the float sank a hair he could only see by the squint. [527]
The numbers were not devotion. [532]
They were a path walked in reverse, so that he might notice what he had stepped over while facing forward. [552]
At forty the whistle dropped out of the count, and what remained was the first hard look he had given the horizon that day. [576]
At twenty he met a tilt he thought he had seen, a lean in the line that none of the arcs had confirmed. [599]
At ten he met his own breath, which no dial on the station had ever been built to record. [618]
When the count reached zero, zero was not the whistle and not the start of the watch. [635]
Zero was the razor edge where sea ceased and sky began, a cut with no thickness, and therefore a thing no flow of water could cross in measurable time. [664]
He understood, with a stillness that belonged to the water and not to any triumph, that his clocks did not measure time. [686]
They measured how long a person could stand before an edge and refuse to say which side of it was his. [707]
The water kept falling. [711]
The hands kept moving. [715]
What thinned was patience, not the hour. [722]
He set the mantle clock key on the sill where daylight found the razor edges of its teeth. [740]
The key and the horizon were one instrument, and neither needed to wind a machine in that room. [758]
Both had opened the question he carried up the coast. [768]
Outside, the line held. [772]
Inside, the vessels murmured, faithful and insufficient. [779]
He did not blow the whistle again. [786]
He stayed, and the name no longer bothered him. [795]
