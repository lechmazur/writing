During the migration of clouds, Mara came down through the dunes to the water. [14] They moved in a long pale file, gray bellies sliding inland, and the light they left was the color of cooled glass. [36] She had walked because the obsidian lagoon beneath the dunes waited in that moving shade, black as poured stone, and because this hour alone held the surface still enough to hear what old burns had kept. [72]

She was an arsonist's daughter and a firefighter, and both facts lived in the set of her hands. [90] Her father had loved the bright appetite of flame. [99] In stations and on blackened hillsides she had learned to answer that appetite with water, with blankets, and with the patient work of keeping people alive. [125] What steadied her was unyielding compassion, a discipline she refused to set down even when the old name followed her into briefing rooms and men glanced at her gloves as if soot might leap from her blood. [162]

In a canvas pouch she carried two small things. [171] One was black sand from a volcanic beach three days south, grains sharp as tiny cinders and cool now against her palm. [193] The other was a foreign coin drilled and strung on red thread, the metal worn smooth by a pocket that was not hers. [216] A medic on a joint crew had given it to her after a night when a roof came down and she stayed under the beam with a child until the saws arrived. [248] She had kept the coin and the sand because gifts, like scars, were not finished until they were used. [267]

The lagoon lay in a bowl the dunes had made and then forgotten. [280] Its obsidian water, not true glass but near enough, mirrored the migrating clouds in broken plates. [296] Kelp drifted at the margins in loose olive ropes, and the air smelled of salt and wet stone. [314] Mara moved through that kelp-drift peace with a care that never stiffened into fear. [328] She was gracefully alert, each step placed where the crust of sand would not collapse into the hidden seep. [347] Peace here was not emptiness but the drift of kelp and the refusal of the water to hurry. [365]

On the near shore a charred snag leaned into the shallows, remnant of the fire her father had set when she was twelve. [388] That fire had jumped the road and taken the reed beds and a shed and, later, his freedom. [406] The snag was split, and inside the split pale wood still lived, a thin tongue of green the seasons had not yet convinced to die. [431] Mara knelt in the wet margin because this was why she had come. [444] She meant to graft living tissue to the wounded wood and prove, if only to the lagoon and to herself, that scars can sing. [468]

She worked without rush, taking from the pouch a cutting lifted at dawn from a healthy stand beyond the point, a finger of reed with a node still wet. [497] She set the black sand from the volcanic beach in a small mound at the lip of the split, a dark bed that would hold moisture and minerals the burned soil had lost. [530] Then she fitted the cutting into the wound, matching green to pale, and bound the graft by the only method she trusted. [552] She passed a foreign coin drilled and strung on red thread through the split and around the cutting, and let the coin hang as clasp and weight so the tide could not tease the join apart. [588] When wind or water moved the snag, the metal would tick very softly against charred bark. [604]

Her fingers did not shake. [609] Gracefully alert, she watched the waterline, the cloud shadow, and the minute quiver at the cutting's node. [626] Unyielding compassion was the pressure she applied and then released, firm enough to hold and gentle enough not to crush the living part. [649] She thought of her father, of the people she had carried from smoke, and of the child who had not let go. [671] None of those memories asked her to be hard. [680] They asked her to stay. [685]

When the graft was secure she cupped lagoon water over the sand and the thread. [700] The coin knocked once, a small bright note, then settled. [710] Kelp drifted past her wrist and did not cling. [719] Above the dunes the clouds went on with their slow migration. [730]

Mara sat back on her heels and waited in the kelp-drift peace. [742] The wind found the red thread, and a thin singing rose, salt-sweet and steady. [756] Scar and new growth answered each other. [763] She closed her eyes, black sand cooling under her nails, and heard the proof she had walked to find. [782] Scars could sing. [785] They sang because she stayed. [790]
