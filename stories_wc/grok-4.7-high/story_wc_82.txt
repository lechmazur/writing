After the shadow detaches, the crafty lantern maker stood alone in the floating island tea garden and found the dawn indecently complete. [22]
No dark twin lay across the moss, and luminous torment filled the space where comfort should have been. [40]
He had lost the shadow on the night his sister's boat missed the lower quay, and the isle had taken the darkness as payment for a body it would not return. [71]
Since then every lantern he crafted burned too honestly, showing customers the raw color of their own rooms. [89]
Craft could bend bamboo and oil, but it could not bend grief into a size his chest would hold. [108]
So he chose a smaller labor, the only one that still made sense: to fold grief into origami cranes and trust the wind more than he trusted memory. [136]
Paper alone was not enough, because the grief had no crease, only glare. [149]
Beyond the last tea row, a glass basilica on a floating isle caught the same sun and broke it into quiet shelves of stored sleep. [174]
Pilgrims called the practice dream recording, a trade in which the sleepless sold their nights and the grieving bought a little borrowed dark. [197]
He had mocked that trade when his shadow still walked with him. [209]

The keepers had left no priests, only a bench, a basin of water, and the warning cut into the rail. [322]

Each circle of the cloth drew a thread of dream recording out of the nearest pane and wound it into the stone. [371]
The first dream was a harbor bell heard from under a blanket, not his sister's bell, yet close enough to make his throat close. [395]
