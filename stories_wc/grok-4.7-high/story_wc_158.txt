Inside the gargantuan shell, the insomniac poet kept a single lamp and a longer memory. [15]
She had not slept across centuries of longing, and the dark knew her better than any friend. [32]
Each night she tried to photosynthesize pain into beauty, turning old hurts toward whatever thin light reached the inner whorl. [52]
The shell itself was a city of chambers, ribbed like a cathedral and cool as reef-shadow joy. [69]
Fish passed its mouth like slow thoughts, and their silver sides wrote brief poems she could not keep. [87]
She was shyly unstoppable, a quiet engine that never announced itself and never quit. [101]

On the lowest shelf of the spiral she found the galaxy postcard. [113]
It had no stamp and no date, only a spill of stars that looked wet, as if the night had been painted and not yet dried. [139]
She held it to the lamp and felt the paper breathe. [150]
Someone had wanted her to see a sky the reef could never offer. [163]
When longing grew too heavy for her ribs, she pressed the card against the shell wall and listened. [181]

War had lived in the reef longer than her name. [191]

Anemones had closed like fists. [202]
The poet heard their grievances in the click of crabs and the sour taste of stirred sand. [219]

Instead she began the work she trusted, to photosynthesize pain into beauty until enemies might recognize their faces in the light. [257]

She called the peace she wanted a blossom armistice. [266]

The only way to ensconce it was via half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key wind. [334]

So she sang. [337]

An off-key wind found the mouth of the shell and carried those broken melodies across the reef. [372]
The wind was never in tune. [378]

Night after night the half-sung lullabies rode the off-key wind. [413]

The poet did not watch for victory. [434]
