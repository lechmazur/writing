Edmund Hale, an urbane calligrapher with ink still dried in the seams of his fingers, stepped into the decommissioned radar dome and closed the hatch on the wind. [28]
He had come up the ridge in the hush left behind by a heated argument, and he did not yet trust his voice to do anything but keep quiet. [57]
In his hand was the campfire story stick, a pale length of ash notched for every tale he had turned, smooth where his thumb had worried the grain for years. [87]
He set it across his knees and sat, uncommonly serene, while the curved metal ticked as it cooled. [105]
For years he had tried to explore ethical storytelling through subversive puppet shows in seedy bars, where the lamps were weak and the listeners wanted to be implicated. [133]
He lettered tiny scripts in a fine hand, then let cloth figures speak them under tables sticky with beer. [152]
His favored action was always to reverse the old stories until the moral faced the other way. [169]
The careful wolf kept the door, the child admitted she had wanted the dark, and kings lost their names while servants kept them. [192]
He believed the reversal made an audience feel the cost of a comfortable ending, and that feeling, he thought, was the beginning of ethics. [216]
The woman in the last bar had not applauded. [225]
She said that reversal was only a cleaner costume for the same appetite, and that a calligrapher who loved his own cleverness was still a man moving mouths that were not his. [257]
The argument rose, bright and brief, like something falling through the upper air. [270]
Then it was over, and the meteor hush of the room made every glass seem louder than speech. [288]
He had gathered the stick, left the puppets in their box, and walked until the bar lights dropped behind the ridge. [309]
Now the dome held a similar quiet. [316]
It had once listened for objects coming down from the high dark, and it had been decommissioned when the sky stopped being a threat worth measuring. [342]
Half-light from a narrow slit fell across the curved wall and stopped short of the floor, so that Edmund sat in a shallow dusk of his own choosing. [370]
Half-lit epiphanies arrived the way they always did for him, not as lightning but as a slow correction of the hand. [391]
He saw that he had used the campfire story stick as a gavel. [404]
At campfires in his youth the stick had passed from teller to teller, and no one spoke until they held it. [425]
In the bars he had kept it for himself, and the puppets had spoken only when he struck the table. [445]
That was not an exploration of ethics. [452]
That was a monopoly dressed as play. [459]
The notches in the wood were not a record of courage. [470]
They were a tally of times he had refused to leave a story in another person's mouth. [487]
He turned the stick until the blunt end pointed back toward the hatch, a small reverse of its usual aim, and the gesture clarified the larger one he had postponed. [517]
To reverse familiar tales had been easy, a flourish his urbane manner could sell to a room that wanted wit more than responsibility. [540]
To reverse himself would mean writing scripts no puppet would perform for a crowd eager to be shocked and then absolved. [561]
It would mean returning the stick, in imagination if not yet in miles, to a circle where the next speaker was not him. [584]
The epiphany stayed half-lit, which he trusted more than any full blaze. [596]
A complete revelation would have flattered him into thinking the work was finished. [609]
Instead he saw only the next honest motion, and he trusted that partial light because the dome itself had been built to catch signals, not to invent them. [637]
He uncapped his pen. [641]
On the rolled sheet he began a script that did not reverse a hero into a villain or a villain into a saint. [664]
It simply followed a listener home after the bar had closed, into the long quiet after sharp words, and asked what that listener owed the story once the teller was gone. [695]
His hand stayed steady. [699]
The patience of his craft, the care with edges and the refusal to blot, served him better here than it ever had under stage lamps. [724]
He remained uncommonly serene, not because the argument had been won, but because he had stopped needing a victory in order to continue. [747]
Outside, the ridge wind moved like a delayed echo of something that had already crossed the sky and left no scar worth tracking. [770]
Inside, the decommissioned radar dome kept its old silence, and the campfire story stick lay across his knees without a new notch. [792]
