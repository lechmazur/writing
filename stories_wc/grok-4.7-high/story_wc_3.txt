The cuneiform translator kept her desk among the iron branches of the magnetic arbor above the clouds, where the garden held itself aloft by a quiet pull she had never fully named. [32]
She was kindly methodical in the way she sharpened her reed and lined her clay, as if gentleness were a form of accuracy. [55]
For years a spirit had sat across from her, not quite solid, close enough that their knees nearly touched, and together they had chased haunted ambitions: to read what the dead had meant, and then to follow meaning past the place where meaning failed. [99]

The spirit had called it a loan from a tree that remembered every hand that had ever reached for it. [203]

Kindness, in her practice, meant refusing to hurry a thing that could not speak for itself. [226]
