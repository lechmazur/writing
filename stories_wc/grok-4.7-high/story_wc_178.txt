In the hush between regimes, when one king's banners had been burned and the next king's scribes had not yet arrived, the frugal astronomer kept his lamp low in the ancient Babylonian palace reimagined as a house of measured shadow. [40]
He owned little: a reed pen, a clay tablet scored with star paths, a bowl of barley, and the magic square tile he never sold. [65]
The tile was small, fired clay the color of dusk, its face a lattice of numbers that summed to the same quiet total along every row, column, and diagonal. [94]
Courtiers had once called such squares amulets, but he treated the object as a ledger of attention, because devotion, he believed, survived only when it was encrypted against the hunger of changing powers. [127]
His own devotion was not to any throne. [135]
It was a covenant he hoped to seal with the unknown, the dark field beyond the last visible star, where names failed and only proportion remained. [161]
The palace around him had been reimagined by successive builders who could not agree on glory, so its walls held unfinished reliefs, faded processions, and corridors that turned toward courtyards open to the evening sky. [196]
Dusky harmony lived in those turns: ochre stone cooling, a wind that did not choose a master, the soft click of his sandals on floors no longer polished for reception. [226]
He ate once a day and saved the oil. [235]
Frugality was not a costume; it was the method by which he stayed when others fled with gold. [253]
Between regimes, gold invited knives, while a poor man with a tablet invited only boredom, and boredom let him work. [273]
What he needed to uncloak was not a spy or a treasure but the meaning pressed into the magic square, a meaning he had set there years ago in a cipher of sums and omissions. [308]
He had encrypted his devotion so thoroughly that even he could no longer read it as feeling, only as arithmetic, and that was the empirically ironic fact he had to face. [339]
The more exact his counts became, the less they resembled love, yet every proof of their cold balance pointed back to the night he had sworn himself to what he could not name. [372]
Empirical method had made the vow look like a puzzle, which was ironic because the puzzle had been the vow's only shelter. [394]
To recover it he would not pray and he would not guess. [406]
He would proceed through schematic representations. [412]
On fresh clay he drew the palace as a plan: gates, stair, observatory roof, the narrow slot through which Venus rose in the month of thinning light. [439]
He overlaid the star paths he had logged since the last regime began to rot, reducing each night's altitude to a line, each line to a node, each node to a number that could sit inside a square. [477]
The schematic was severe and beautiful in the way a drained canal is beautiful, showing structure without the noise of water. [498]
He placed the magic square tile at the center of the drawing and turned it until its constant sum matched the count of steps from the old throne room to the roof. [530]
That match was not mystic decoration. [536]
It was a check, and it held. [543]
When the tile aligned, a gap in the schematic revealed itself, a missing cell he had left blank so that no looter could complete the figure without knowing which absence mattered. [574]
Into that cell he set the only number his frugality had taught him to trust: one, the unit of a single watched night, not multiplied by tribute or fear. [603]
The square completed, and the encrypted devotion uncloaked not as a confession of a person's name but as a pattern of returns, the same ratio appearing in the palace plan, in the star altitudes, and in the barley he portioned against the length of uncertain rule. [649]

By withholding wealth, speech, and even clear feeling, he had preserved a signal that louder ages would have drowned, and the proof of that preservation was the very dryness that had made him doubt he still loved anything. [697]
Love, measured, had looked like absence. [703]
Absence, mapped, was the covenant. [708]
He carried the tile and the schematic to the roof as the sky went from copper to the color of wet ash, a dusky harmony in which no anthem was sung and no decree was posted. [744]
The city below was between names. [750]
He set the tile on the parapet, faced the dark quarter where no star had yet declared a successor, and spoke only the sums, not as spell but as terms. [780]
