At dusk, when every window in Bellweather turned to honey, Mara Venn stole faces from their frames. [17]

She was the city's golden-hour portrait thief, famous for taking only paintings made during the last amber minutes of day. [37]

Such portraits were called illuminated moments because the light preserved not merely a likeness, but the choice the sitter most regretted. [58]

Collectors locked them away, while Mara, quietly savvy about locks and sorrows, returned them to those willing to choose again. [78]

Her mother had taught her the craft and its dangerous purpose: to press your ear against destiny's door and decide whether to knock. [101]

Under a fleeting golden sunset, a delivery boy brought Mara a bouquet of white asters, blue irises, and one snapped fern. [122]

These were codes in flower arrangements, the secret language her missing brother, Silas, had used when the Ministry watched their home. [143]

Five asters meant "alive," blue irises meant "northbound," and the broken fern warned, "Bring stolen light, or do not come." [163]

She had believed Silas dead for nine years, yet disbelief rose within her, luminous and trembling, as if hope itself had struck a match. [187]

Carrying the bouquet, Mara took from a safe behind her stove a jar of captured moonbeams, contraband bright enough to expose every hidden choice in a portrait. [214]

At midnight she reached Emerald Station by moonlight, its green glass roof glowing like the inside of a wave. [233]

No public train had stopped there since Silas vanished, but tonight one black locomotive waited beneath clocks running backward. [252]

Vases lined the platform: east-facing roses, west-facing lilies, and marigolds spaced between them like dashes. [267]

A less attentive reader would decode their order as ENGINE, but Mara noticed that every vase stood before a mirror. [287]

She chose to reinterpret the reflected arrangement, which spelled LAST CAR. [298]

Following that reversed command, she entered the luggage car, where hundreds of sunset portraits hung from brass hooks and breathed in unison. [320]

At the center hung Silas's portrait, older than he had been when he disappeared, his painted knuckles rapping silently against the canvas. [342]

"Don't open it," said Curator Vale behind her. [350]

His silver uniform bore the Ministry's key emblem, and fresh fern dust stained his gloves. [365]

"I sent the bouquet," he said, "because your jar can open what your brother refused to reveal." [382]

Vale explained that the train ran on abandoned choices, and Silas had hidden its final destination inside his own illuminated moment. [403]

With that destination, the Ministry could arrive before any rebellion, marriage, invention, or murder and purchase the outcome. [421]

Mara raised the jar as though surrendering, studying Silas's painted hands while Vale watched the light. [437]

His knocks came in groups matching the bouquet, but his painted eyes looked toward its shadow, cast across the canvas by the jar. [460]

The shadow reversed Vale's command: stolen light belonged to the moments from which the Ministry had taken it. [478]

She hurled the jar against the brass floor. [486]

Moonlight flooded the car, entering every canvas at once, and the painted passengers stepped through their frames in a blaze of gold and pearl. [510]

Vale lunged for Silas, but a hundred freed hands seized the curator, pinning him beneath the backward clock as the locomotive screamed awake. [533]

Silas fell into Mara's arms, thin, warm, and astonished to discover that nine years had passed outside his prison. [552]

"We have one minute," he said. [558]

The freed passengers crowded the windows, each seeing a different life racing toward them along the moonlit rails. [576]

Silas told Mara the train would stop only when someone chose the present over every promised future. [593]

For years, Mara had stolen portraits to give others second chances, while secretly longing to find the one perfect door meant for herself. [616]

Now each window offered it: her mother alive, Silas unscarred, Mara innocent and unknown. [630]

In luminous disbelief, she saw that destiny's door was not an entrance but a question, and listening did not require obedience. [651]

"I choose this moment," she said, taking Silas's hand, "including everything it cost." [664]

The rails folded into silver ribbons, the locomotive sighed, and Emerald Station settled around them beneath an ordinary, merciful moon. [684]

At dawn, the freed passengers carried Vale to the city watch, then reclaimed the lives that had waited beyond their frames. [705]

Silas opened a flower stall beneath the station clock, arranging blossoms into messages for people afraid to choose. [723]

Mara kept her old title, but now the golden-hour portrait thief stole locks from portrait frames, leaving every owner free to choose again. [746]

Whenever sunset gilded the city, she pressed her ear to the warm wood and heard, with wonder, the present knocking back. [767]
