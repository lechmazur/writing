<story>At midsummer, on the ancient terrace above the salt white ruins of Orison, Mara Vey waited for the two enemy moons to cross. [23]

For twenty seven years, she had served as the forever war peace negotiator, a title invented after every shorter war had outlived its name. [47]

Her ebony clipboard lay against her knee, polished by anxious hands and scarred where an assassin's bullet had failed to reach her heart. [70]

Below, delegations climbed opposite stairways while the sun lowered between the moons, during the slow dance of celestial bodies that Orison's dead had once believed could judge an oath. [99]

Mara did not share their faith, but she had learned that exhausted people often approached truth through ritual significance when argument alone sounded like another weapon. [125]

That was why she had summoned General Sorn of the River Host and Marshal Ilyan of the Ash Provinces without guards, each carrying one clay cup filled from his homeland. [155]

Both men despised her, although for different reasons: Sorn because her first truce had stranded his son behind enemy lines, and Ilyan because she had exposed the prison where he buried captives alive. [188]

She had told herself the betrayal saved twelve thousand civilians, but arithmetic had become the coward's chapel where she went to avoid one young man's face. [214]

Tonight she meant to anchor ideals in practice, even if the first anchor had to be dropped through her own reputation. [235]

Her manner remained operatively gentle: she placed each cup in a carved basin, checked each man's empty sleeves, and asked them to muster the names of everyone whose return would make peace tangible. [268]

They had expected borders and grain quotas; instead, they wrote prisoners, missing children, stolen bells, poisoned wells, and the unburied dead upon strips of linen. [293]

Mara copied every claim onto the ebony clipboard, whose black surface made the white pages seem painfully clean. [311]

When the moons' rims touched, she poured Sorn's river water and Ilyan's ash cloud rain into the basin, then lowered the linen until the inks bled together. [338]

"The old rite says shared water carries shared guilt," she said, "but guilt without payment is only theater." [356]

She unclipped the last page, her signed confession that the fatal truce had been a calculated lie, and offered Sorn the document that could end her career and perhaps her life. [387]

"This is the price of redemption I can pay: not forgiveness, which belongs to you, but the truth I withheld and the judgment it earns." [412]

Sorn's hand closed over the confession, and for one terrible breath Mara saw triumph restore youth to his ruined face. [432]

Then Ilyan laughed, saying Sorn could hang her after signing the border accord, as though justice were a garnish beside conquest. [453]

The laughter broke the ritual's spell, yet it also revealed its purpose: Ilyan wanted absolution without opening his prisons, while Sorn wanted vengeance more than his displaced villages wanted roofs. [483]

Mara turned the clipboard toward them, showing not a treaty but paired columns of acts to begin before sunrise: gates opened, mines mapped, surgeons exchanged, and burial teams protected. [512]

"If these are not done, my confession goes to both armies with records of your refusals." [528]

"If they are done, it still goes, but beside proof that tonight none of us chose innocence over repair." [547]

Ilyan reached for his cup, perhaps intending to smash it, but Sorn placed the confession inside the basin, where mixed water climbed the paper and softened Mara's signature. [575]

"Your name is not mine to wash away," Sorn said, "but my son believed promises should become roads, so give me the page with the roads." [601]

Mara gave it to him, and he signed; after a long silence, Ilyan signed because refusing before the watching delegations would confess more than any tribunal could prove. [629]

Messengers ran down both stairways, horns called engineers and healers from their camps, and men who had mustered for battle instead assembled with shovels, keys, ledgers, and lanterns. [657]

No miracle followed: one prison gate jammed, a sniper killed a woman digging graves, and three officers fled rather than disclose their minefields. [680]

Yet by dawn, forty captives crossed the terrace, two poisoned wells were marked, and soldiers from opposite armies carried the same small coffins toward Orison's cemetery. [706]

Mara watched the flawed beauty of the sky reflected in the muddy basin and understood that redemption was neither acquittal nor purity, but the daily surrender of excuses. [734]

She fastened the stained confession back onto the ebony clipboard and descended to face judgment, while behind her the moons separated and the work continued. [759]</story>
