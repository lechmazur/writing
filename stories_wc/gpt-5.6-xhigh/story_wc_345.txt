<story>By the third harvest on Pomona, the castaways no longer remembered which lifeboat had carried whom. [16] Strangers had become family beneath an amber sky, sharing patched roofs, grief, and the tart blue apples that grew across their orchard planet. [39] Above the trees, floating rock arches drifted like immense ribs, beautiful until their shadows crossed and crushed whole rows without warning. [60] The survivors trusted weather, rope, and one another, but they did not trust ghosts. [74] Then messages began appearing, chalked onto deserted sidewalks at dawn in a hand shaped by looping flourishes: MOVE THE EAST NURSERY, BANK THE RESERVOIR, WAIT. [99] Each warning proved exact. [103] The eastern arch fell where the nursery had stood; the reservoir caught a week of sudden rain; waiting spared three pickers from a lightning vine. [128] Their author was Lio, the colony's first dead child, an altruistic ghost who had slipped from an arch years before and now shimmered beside the oldest pear tree. [156] He was strangely trustworthy for an apparition, perhaps because he never asked for candles, worship, or even remembrance, only work that kept others alive. [180] Mara, the engineer who had once blamed him for frightening her daughter, became his reluctant interpreter. [196] One dawn he led her to the butterfly garden, where bronze wingmoths slept among thorn roses and a watering bell hung over the dry central basin. [222] The butterfly garden watering bell had been cast from their broken navigation dish; when filled, it tipped, chimed, and scattered a silver shower. [245] Lio passed his translucent hand through its empty bowl, and a map of stars kindled inside the metal. [263] At its center pulsed the Nebula of Respite, a violet cloud their instruments had dismissed as harmless dust. [281] Lio chalked beneath the bell, REEXAMINE WHAT SAVED US. [290] Mara remembered their lifeboats emerging from that cloud together, engines dead but hulls cooled, as if the nebula had cupped them until the solar storm passed. [316] She also remembered the fine violet pollen coating every keel. [326] Under her microscope, the pollen matched crystals now webbing the roots of every orchard tree and drawing the floating arches lower. [347] The cloud had granted respite, not rescue, and its gift had ripened into a noose. [362] By noon, calculations showed that all seven arches would collide above the settlement during the next dawn rain. [380] Lio could see the crystal currents, but dead hands could neither turn valves nor climb the arches, so his warnings had always been rehearsals for this day. [407] He had not meant to command them; he had meant to orchestrate collective effort until fear learned the rhythm of trust. [428] Mara read his final plan aloud in the dining hall: harvest every apple, flood every root trench, and suspend the watering bell from the highest arch. [454] Its chime, magnified through the crystal web, could loosen the pollen while reservoir water washed it from the roots, but only if every trench opened together. [480] Some called the plan suicide, and Mara nearly agreed when she saw Lio watching his mother tie a climbing rope around her waist. [503] His face held no hunger to keep her near, only the brambled hope that love might release what love could not protect alone. [526] At dusk, elders stripped fruit, children cleared channels, mechanics rigged pumps, and former rivals braided cable from the last spools of lifeboat wire. [549] Mara climbed with Lio's mother while rock groaned around them and sparks crawled across the orchard like hunting insects. [568] They hung the bell as dawn silvered the abandoned sidewalks below, where Lio's chalk had dissolved into luminous streams pointing toward each valve. [591] Then the rain struck. [595] Seven arches plunged, the colony opened its trenches, and Mara kicked the bell. [608] The note rolled through stone, trees, bones, and memory, a deep bright peal that sent millions of violet crystals bursting upward. [629] Water followed, carrying the glitter into the sky, where it spread beneath the falling arches as a small new nebula. [649] The rocks slowed within it, then settled harmlessly above the orchard, their curves woven together like sheltering hands. [667] When the cheering ebbed, Mara found Lio fading beside the bell, because the crystal web that anchored the arches had also anchored him. [690] His mother reached for him, and though her fingers met rain, he smiled at the family the strangers had chosen to become. [712] The next dawn, no warning waited on the sidewalk, only one last chalked sentence beneath a wild tangle of rose runners: RESPITE IS WHAT WE GIVE EACH OTHER. [740] Above it, wingmoths drank from the ringing bell, and every thorn held a drop of light. [756]</story>
