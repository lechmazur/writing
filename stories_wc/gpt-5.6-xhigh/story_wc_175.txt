During the migration of the last monarch butterfly, Ilyan climbed through cloud mist into the verdant grotto above the clouds. [20]

He was an esoteric realist, which meant he trusted stones, hunger, and weather, yet also believed forgotten events left pressure in the air. [43]

The grotto opened beneath a ceiling of leaves so green they seemed to tint the light with memory, while dawn laid a faint rose color over every pool. [71]

In that blushing stillness, roots descended from floating fig trees, touched the moss for an hour, and withdrew when winds shifted. [92]

Everything here was transiently rooted, including Ilyan, who had lived in twelve valleys without claiming one as home. [110]

Below him, the final monarch in existence beat northward alone, carrying a seed capsule tied beneath her body by the dead lepidopterists of the lowlands. [135]

Her wingbeats should have made an even fourfold rhythm, but Ilyan heard an interrupted cadence, three strokes, a pause, then two. [156]

It was the distress pattern once tapped by miners trapped inside Mount Vey, though official histories claimed the mine had been empty. [178]

Ilyan had come to honor overlooked histories, especially those erased because their witnesses were poor, winged, foreign, or dead. [197]

In his satchel lay a moth wing sealed between two mica sheets, its silver scales patterned with the miners' map of hidden ventilation shafts. [221]

A survivor had drawn the map on that fragile wing with soot and salt, then entrusted it to Ilyan's grandmother before the governors burned every paper account. [248]

His task was building temporary structures as signals for the butterfly, using the old miners' cadence to mark safe currents through the grotto. [271]

In earlier years, whole orange rivers had followed his reed arches, but now only one traveler approached the cloud wall. [291]

He cut willow switches, bent them into hoops, and used grass fiber to knot each crossing tight enough to survive until sunset. [313]

From each hoop he hung three leaves, left a gap, and hung two more, translating the interrupted cadence into a shape visible from flight. [337]

Yet when the monarch reached the first hoop, she veered west, away from the flowering path and toward a cliff of rain. [359]

Ilyan cursed his interpretation, then held the moth wing against the rosy sky and saw one broken line flare silver. [379]

It did not mark a ventilation shaft as his grandmother had believed, but a stair cut behind the western waterfall. [399]

The butterfly's cadence was not asking for rescue; it was repeating directions carried through generations, too quiet for people to notice. [420]

Beyond the cliff lay the miners' abandoned refuge, where records said mutineers had perished, but descendants whispered that families had built a hidden garden. [444]

The capsule contained last seeds of cloud milkweed gathered before the lowlands burned, and the monarch needed that garden if her lineage was to continue. [469]

Ilyan tore down the eastern hoops, saving every length of fiber, because reverence without usefulness was only decoration. [487]

He rebuilt them along the western ledge, wedging reeds into cracks and spanning empty air with gates no larger than his shoulders. [509]

Each gate bore the three and two leaf pattern, and each knot held a scrap of bright fungus that glowed through rain. [531]

The wind rose as if the cloud sea had inhaled, and his signals shuddered, their roots merely borrowed from stone. [551]

One arch ripped free, struck his temple, and sailed into whiteness, leaving blood warm beside his eye. [568]

He nearly followed, but a fig root descended beside him, and he caught it with both hands. [585]

It held only moments, then began lifting toward its drifting tree, forcing him to accept the brief mercy and move. [605]

At the western fall, the ledge ended before the hidden stair, and he had no reeds left for a final marker. [626]

He unsealed the mica, took out the moth wing, and knotted it to a single thread above the abyss. [645]

The relic spun silver, then steadied, pointing west, a tiny truthful flag made from one overlooked life. [662]

The monarch saw it, altered course, and passed so near Ilyan that her orange shadow crossed his bleeding face. [681]

She slipped behind the waterfall, and the seed capsule clicked against stone three times, paused, and clicked twice. [699]

Ilyan followed into a cavern where pale milkweed grew among hundreds of names carved waist high by miners, their spouses, and children. [721]

History had not been empty or silent; it had simply chosen walls, wings, and rhythm when paper became dangerous. [740]

The monarch settled on the oldest plant while Ilyan pressed his bloodied thumb beneath the names, not to sign them, but to promise witness. [764]

Behind them, sunset blushed across the grotto, and his temporary arches loosened into harmless reeds, but the interrupted cadence continued softly within the stone. [788]
