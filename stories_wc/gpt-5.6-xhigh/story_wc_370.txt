<story>In Bellweather, the Library of Common Hours occupied a bright storefront between a bakery and a watchmaker, so ordinary that nobody wondered why its windows reflected stars before sunset. [29]

Her solitude was not disdain; she was keenly empathetic, and another person's grief reached her with the force of weather through broken glass. [119]
She hid because feeling everyone had once left no room in her to hear herself. [134]

Mara recognized the handwriting as her own. [205]

There, a map contained the city, the city contained the library, and the library contained the map, while lamps burned darkness into neat pools. [300]
