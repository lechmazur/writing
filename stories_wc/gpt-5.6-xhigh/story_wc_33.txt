At twilight, the cobalt arcade held its breath beneath steel shutters, blue lamps, and the wires that divided one exhausted city into two suspicious countries. [25]

Ilyan Voss, the disputed territory mailman, waited there at the day's lone mail drop with a canvas sack chained to his wrist. [47]

A brittle hush surrounded him, sharpened by distant checkpoints closing and shopkeepers pretending not to watch. [63]

Officially, Ilyan carried ration notices and censored family letters across the armistice line; secretly, he studied every envelope that never arrived. [84]

He did this to find where broken promises heal, because his younger sister Mara had vanished after both governments promised her safe passage. [107]

For three years, he had mapped the vanished by linking absences: a widow without medicine, a school without ink, a prisoner's mother without replies. [131]

The pattern led not to a camp, but to the smuggling drone border above the river, where contraband machines crossed while human gates remained locked. [156]

Inside rested a thunderstorm in a bottle, no taller than his palm, with black clouds turning around a filament of white fire. [199]

A paper wound around its neck read, "For the dead letters; open under the Customs Tower bell." [216]

"Mail property must be surrendered," she said, softly enough that the order sounded like condolence. [270]
