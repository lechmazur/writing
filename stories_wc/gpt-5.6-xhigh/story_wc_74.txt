<story>In the rooftop greenhouse above Bellweather, Ilyan Voss painted while the smog-choked city failed to sleep below him. [18]
He was an insomniac painter, thin as a ladder, with yellow on every cuff and no faith in cures. [37]
At three each morning, grow lamps breathed a saffron quiet through the tomato vines, and Ilyan copied old words onto scraps of sailcloth. [60]
The words came from market lintels, burial jars, and lullabies in lost languages the Ministry had declared extinct. [78]
Officials called them useless marks, but Ilyan's dead sister, Sela, had called each one a door that still expected a knock. [99]
Since her death in a clinic queue, he had worked to prove that strangers remember kindness, even when governments erase the words for it. [123]
His evidence was small: translated thank-you notes, names of unknown blood donors, and recipes shared during blackouts. [140]
He cataloged everything by date, district, ink, and probable grammar, methodically hopeful where Sela had been brilliantly certain. [158]
On the seventh night of the waxing moon, a parcel appeared beside the roof hatch, though Ilyan had heard no visitor. [179]
Inside lay a calligrapher's moon-tipped brush, its black handle ending in a silver crescent, wrapped with a note in Sela's hand. [200]
"Let borrowed light finish what we began," it said. [209]
Ilyan tested the bristles in rainwater and soot, making a single character for shelter on the eastern glass. [227]
Under a waxing moon's glow, the crescent gathered light, and the painted character opened inside his waking mind. [245]
He daydreamed a woman lifting her coat over an unknown child's head while acid rain hissed around them. [263]
The woman whispered a phrase Ilyan had copied from a cracked tram station, and the child answered in another lost language. [284]
When the vision faded, he understood both: You are covered; I will cover another. [298]
Sleep remained impossible, yet wonder steadied him. [305]
Night after night, he painted one recovered word, then waited, measuring every vision against maps, archives, and surviving elders. [324]
Moonlight sparked daydreams of dockers passing bread along a flood line, nurses concealing refugees, and rivals carrying a fallen courier. [344]
Every scene contained a spoken gift, and every gift supplied the missing sound of a mark he already possessed. [363]

That morning, a Ministry drone tagged the greenhouse for demolition, claiming its unlicensed soil worsened the municipal air. [401]
Ilyan had until dawn, when machines would come, and the moon was only two nights from full. [418]
He could preserve Sela's notebooks by fleeing, or spend the remaining hours turning the greenhouse into a lantern no archive could hide. [440]
He chose the lantern, not because he trusted miracles, but because his ledgers showed how often desperate people chose one another. [461]
All afternoon he ground marigold petals, soot, and rainwater into ink, while the vines trembled in the scrubbers' hot breath. [481]
After sunset, he brushed hundreds of linked characters across the glass walls and roof, joining languages once divided by avenues and law. [503]
The waxing moon climbed through copper haze and touched the silver crescent. [515]
