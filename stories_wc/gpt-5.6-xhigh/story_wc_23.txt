After the hydraulics failed, the great lens sank crooked in its cradle, and Bellwether Signal Station went dark. [18] Above it, stars burned with the pitiless clarity of salt rubbed into a wound. [32] Mara Venn, the coast's last patchwork navigator, slept beneath dead gauges in a coat sewn from obsolete charts. [50] Each colored scrap marked somewhere she had fled, and each seam marked someone who had not. [66]

For eleven months, flood survivors had migrated north, away from drowned orchards, roofless towns, and graves without stones. [84] Then the inland camps ran out of medicine and mercy. [94] Boats began slipping south again, families steering toward forbidden homes in what officials called the reverse migration. [111] Without Bellwether's beam, the returning flotilla would meet Splitjaw Reef before dawn. [123]

Mara volunteered to relight it because her daughter, Lio, had vanished on that reef during the first evacuation. [141] Before the flood, Lio had repaired radios and apprenticed under Bellwether's keeper. [153] She told no one that she wanted more than rescue; she wanted to navigate the geography of loss. [171] Perhaps, if she mapped every channel of grief, she could discover where love went after the body disappeared. [189]

The station's backup current fluctuated with moon phases, whispering at the new moon and surging dangerously when full. [207] Mara could power the lamp, but the broken lift left its light aimed into the floor. [223] On her third night in the starlit signal station, an unplugged telephone rang at midnight. [238]

A voice behind static said, 'Three teeth need a green heel,' then disconnected. [251] Mara searched the station stores until she found a patina bronze censer foot, three-clawed and heavy, in a box from Saint Orra's drowned chapel. [275] Its socket fitted the lens axle as if some grieving engineer had designed both altar and beacon. [292]

At midnight the next night, the telephone said, 'Give the heel a spine of railway bone.' [308] Mara dragged in a rusted signal rod and bolted it through the censer foot. [322] Night after night, through cryptic phone calls at midnight, she learned to build what no manual described. [339] 'Let a bicycle dream of turning the sun,' meant chain the rod to the maintenance pedals. [355] 'Teach the moon not to shout,' meant wire a ballast coil between the lunar battery and lamp. [372] The caller never answered Mara's questions, yet breathed sharply whenever she spoke Lio's name. [386]

By the waxing half-moon, Mara had made a hand-cranked skeleton capable of lifting and sweeping the lens. [403] The censer foot carried its weight, but bronze pins screamed whenever the beam crossed south. [418] That scream sounded like a chapel door opening underwater. [427]

On the full moon, the returning boats appeared: forty black seeds scattered across a silver, murderous sea. [444] The battery surged, the new mechanism kicked, and the lens began hammering its cradle. [458] Mara caught the midnight call with blood already slick on her palms. [470]

'Remove the green heel,' the voice ordered. [477]

Without the censer foot, the axle would collapse, and every boat would lose the channel. [492] Mara shouted that fact into the receiver. [499]

For once, the caller replied plainly: 'It was never meant to hold the light, Mama.' [514]

Mara stopped breathing. [517] Lio had called the chapel censer a green heel when she was six, after polishing one bright claw with her sleeve. [538] No official search had found her body, but no living voice could travel an unplugged wire. [554] The truth hurt less than choosing between truths. [562]

Mara looked from the trembling bronze to the boats entering Splitjaw's white teeth. [575] Then she understood the riddle. [580] She pulled the censer foot, caught the falling axle on her shoulder, and became the missing support herself. [598] With her free hand, she set the bronze foot beneath the ballast lever, where its sacred weight steadied the moon's excess. [619]

The mechanism softened. [622] Mara pedaled, bone grinding against iron, and Bellwether cast one clean road across the water. [637] Boats turned along it, away from the reef and toward the ruined harbor. [650] As each passed, deck lights blinked thanks, small as candles carried home from a funeral. [665]

The telephone remained silent. [669]

At dawn, rescuers found Mara pinned under the axle, alive, laughing once and then sobbing without shame. [686] Beyond them, the reverse migrants stepped onto mud that remembered their houses. [698] They had not defeated loss; they had given it an address, a lamp, and room among the living. [716] Mara touched the blood-dark seam over her heart and felt no healing, only raw solace, which was enough to steer by. [737]
