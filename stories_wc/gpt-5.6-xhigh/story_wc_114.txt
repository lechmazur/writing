After the fever broke, Mara climbed the last ice glazed steps to the mountain weather station, where dawn trembled green above the black peaks. [24]

She carried her father's botanical illustration of ghost gentian inside her coat, although the paper seemed colder than the wind. [44]

Ten years earlier, her younger brother Ivo had vanished during a sudden whiteout, and their father, Tomas, had died insisting that no forecast could have saved him. [71]

Fever had loosened a memory Mara had kept buried: before the storm, she had seen Tomas at the log, a warning blurred beneath his hand. [96]

She had come to find the weight of a swallowed truth, not in guilt's poetry, but in pressure, ink, and whatever the station still remembered. [121]

At the observatory door waited Nacre, the fault-tolerant oracle Tomas had built from three forecasting engines that constantly checked, contradicted, and repaired one another. [145]

Its brass face held no eyes, only a ring of needles whose swaying certainties never quite agreed. [162]

"Mara Vale," it said, while a side needle measured the snowmelt dripping from her sleeve. [177]

Nacre was peripherally aware by design; Tomas believed disaster entered by occupying margins that ordinary instruments dismissed as noise. [196]

So the oracle listened to pipe vibration, moth wings, static, human pauses, and even the faint abrasion of paper against wool. [217]

When Mara laid the illustration beneath its lamp, Nacre's needles leaned toward it as grasses lean before rain. [235]

The gentian was exquisitely rendered, each blue throat opened beside notes about altitude, bloom, and medicinal use. [252]

Along the lower border, however, a gray thumb smudge covered the roots. [264]

"Your father brought that sheet here on the day Ivo disappeared," Nacre said, "then fed me a forecast amended in charcoal." [285]

Mara's fever memory sharpened: Tomas at the desk, his hand above the blurred log, telling her to take Ivo downhill. [305]

She had refused because the official bulletin promised clearing, and because at seventeen she preferred the machine's certainty to her father's fear. [327]

Then Ivo had followed a survey flag into the whitening yard, and the mountain had erased him. [344]

Mara had spent a decade blaming Tomas for cowardice and herself for obedience to the bulletin. [360]

Now Nacre asked her to turn the painting over, where the blank back showed shallow furrows near one edge. [379]

"Paper remembers force," the oracle said, and projected low violet light across the fibers. [393]

Indented words rose like tracks at sunrise: AVALANCHE SIGNAL UNCERTAIN, SOUTH ARRAY FAILED, EVACUATE CHILDREN. [408]

The warning had been written on a page above the illustration, then torn from the log; its pressure remained in the paper, measurable as ridges no heavier than dust. [437]

Mara touched the gray mark and remembered one detail her anger had excluded: Tomas's thumb was clean when he erased the public line. [460]

"Whose charcoal is this?" she asked. [466]

Nacre's needles swung apart, one toward Tomas, one toward Mara, and one toward the sealed storm archive. [483]

"Insufficient agreement," it answered, its honesty more merciful than confidence. [493]

That refusal was why Tomas had made an oracle able to survive failed parts and beloved lies: not to abolish uncertainty, but to keep it visible. [519]

Mara opened the archive, where a wax cylinder preserved voices beneath the storm's roar. [533]

Her own young voice emerged first: "I used my thumb to smudge the warning and wrote CLEAR because Ivo was scared and I wanted the ridge." [559]

Then Tomas replied, "You are ill with terror, and people will believe they heard nothing; let the blame stop with me." [580]

He had not swallowed his guilt, but hers, carrying it until his death so she could grow beyond that morning. [600]

Mara expected absolution, but the oracle offered only three probabilities, each needle quivering between accident and responsibility. [617]

She understood then that truth had no single weight: on paper it was a ridge, in Tomas it had been silence, and in her it would become speech. [645]

She restored the warning to the log, added her confession without excuse, and pressed the gentian sheet beside it. [664]

The smudge remained over the painted roots, no longer evidence to scrub away, but the dark soil from which remembrance drew nourishment. [686]

Outside, auroral sorrow spilled rose and green across the retreating storm, beautiful because it did not promise safety. [704]

When Mara descended, Nacre kept watch over the page, its divided needles swaying, certain only that every margin mattered. [723]
