<story>Three months after receiving the donor heart, Mara climbed through valley mist to the floating rock tea house. [18] It hung above a gorge on chains no eye could see, while waterfalls unraveled beneath its cedar floor. [36]

Mara had come to find Soren, a watchful dye master whose colors were said to remember every hand they touched. [56] She wanted his teaching for one private reason: to learn why some people magnetize stories, as her dead donor apparently had. [77] Since the surgery, strangers had mistaken her for a confidante, confessing betrayals, births, buried coins, and once a murder beside a pear tree. [100] Their words crowded her new pulse until she feared it was not a heart but an unlocked door. [118]

Soren received her without surprise and poured white tea into cups thin as dawn. [132] Around him, silk banners breathed in mineral reds and mossy greens, but he wore undyed gray. [148] Only a blue morpho wing, pinned beneath glass at his throat, flashed whenever the house turned toward morning. [166]

"That blue is not pigment," Mara said. [173]

"It is structure, angle, and borrowed light," Soren replied, covering the wing with two fingers. [188] "Its beauty practices iridescent caution: seen from one place, hidden from another." [200]

"Then teach me to hide," Mara said. [207]

"No," he answered, listening not to her voice but to the stutter beneath it. [221] "I will teach you to choose what you reveal." [230]

His first lesson was to illuminate six hanging vats by tracking sun trails across mirrors fastened to the drifting cliffs. [250] Each hour, Mara tilted the mirrors, catching pale fire and guiding it through the tea house windows. [267] The dyes answered warmly, indigo deepening like evening rain, saffron blooming like a bell no one could hear. [285]

Yet one covered vat remained cold. [291] Whenever Mara approached it, her heart struck once, hard enough to tremble the spoons. [306] Soren always noticed, and always changed the subject. [314]

Days passed above the clouded valley, measured by tea steam, wheeling swallows, and the blue wing's vanishing gleam. [332] Mara discovered that Soren's silence drew tales as surely as her face did. [345] Pilgrims told him what grief had dyed them, and he never seized their endings. [359] He simply waited, watchful, until each speaker found the color of the truth. [372]

Perhaps stories were not iron filings dragged toward a gifted heart, Mara thought; perhaps they were moths seeking an unshielded flame. [393]

On the seventh dawn, thunder formed below them, quietly explosive, turning the mist pearl white before any sound arrived. [412] The house lurched, the covered vat slid, and Mara caught it against her chest. [426] Its lid fell away. [430]

Inside floated hospital bracelets in a dye so dark they resembled bones at midnight. [444] One bracelet bore her donor's name: Elian Vale. [452]

"My son," Soren said, as the first thunder shook the windows. [463]

Mara's shock sharpened into anger. [468] "You summoned me here and pretended chance?" [475]

"I invited no one," he said. [481] "Elian did, if anyone can invite after death." [489]

Soren explained that Elian had served tea there, listening recklessly, promising everyone that their pain could become adventure if repeated brightly enough. [511] People adored him, then mistook his appetite for care. [520] He magnetized stories because he made tellers feel briefly legendary, but he carried their secrets into rooms where they did not belong. [542] The murder by the pear tree was one of them. [552]

"I kept these to discover whether memory survived the body," Soren said, "but the dye only reflects the watcher." [571]

He angled a mirror, and sun pierced the storm's rim, striking the black vat. [585] Its surface burst into shifting blues, and Mara saw not Elian's memories but her own hungry relief whenever a stranger chose her. [607] She had feared the stories, yes, yet she had also treasured them as proof that the borrowed heart made her special. [628] The knowledge proved quietly explosive within her, dangerous precisely because no one else could hear it. [644]

"What should I do?" she asked. [650]

"Turn the light," Soren said, "before brightness becomes theft." [659]

Mara redirected the mirror. [663] Blue vanished from the vat and returned to the wing at Soren's throat, a small sky that existed only from her chosen place. [686]

When the storm passed, she closed the lid over the bracelets, veiling their names rather than exposing them. [704] Soren bowed, not as master to pupil, but as one keeper of silence to another. [719]

Descending through the valley mist, Mara still heard confessions in every village, for her steady gaze remained an open invitation. [739] Now she let each story illuminate its teller, and carried away only what was freely given. [755]</story>
