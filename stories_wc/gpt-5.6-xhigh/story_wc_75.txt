<story>After the beekeeper's last visit, the monumental waterfall froze, and rosewater began seeping through the walls of Veyra. [18] The perfume should have suggested weddings, but in the grotto city it meant the upper cisterns were cracking. [36] Mara Venn smelled it each morning as she unlocked the Museum of Vanished Countries, where she worked as its most cultured guide. [58] Historically oriented by temperament and training, she could date a cracked violin by its varnish and identify five exiled schools of puppetry by the stitching of their eyelids. [86] Her polished lectures delighted visitors, yet her private purpose was fiercer: to rescue dying arts before their makers disappeared. [105] Most makers now lived in Lantern Ward, displaced persons from valleys drowned when Veyra diverted the river behind the mountain. [125] The diverted torrent became the Silver Curtain, a waterfall so vast that daylight entered the caverns through it as green trembling bands. [147] Old Sava, the beekeeper, had crossed the Curtain yearly with cliff-hive honey, beeswax, and news of the outside thaw. [166] On his last visit, three months earlier, he warned Mara that the sluice keepers had abandoned their posts, then died before naming why. [189] Soon the Curtain hardened into a white wall, trapping the city with dwindling flour and a museum full of mute inheritances. [210] While cataloging Sava's effects, Mara found an emerald-tinted opera glass lens hidden inside a wax honeycomb stamped with a tiny M. [231] She recognized the M as the mark of Mirel Oss, last scene painter of the drowned Merrow Opera and a Lantern Ward refugee. [254] Mara found Mirel baking hard loaves shaped like initials, a family custom used to remember relatives after even their portraits were lost. [276] At sight of the lens, Mirel whispered, "That is not for watching performances; it is for finding the machinery behind them." [297] He set an M-shaped loaf beneath the glass, and green light revealed yeast bubbles arranged like a map of the city's forgotten waterworks. [320] For generations, his family had pressed route diagrams into ceremonial dough, disguising hydraulic knowledge as mourning when rulers forbade refugees to keep maps. [343] Sava had carried the lens between scattered Merrow families, letting each household read its portion without possessing the dangerous whole. [363] Now only seven initial loaves remained, and together they showed a maintenance passage climbing above the frozen Curtain to the abandoned sluices. [385] Opening them could thaw the waterfall and save Veyra, Mirel said, or burst the ice inward and erase the displaced twice. [406] The council refused the risk, ordering citizens to burn museum frames for warmth and grind the memorial loaves into ration flour. [427] Mara had spent years preserving art behind glass, but preservation that watched people perish now seemed another elegant form of burial. [448] She invited Lantern Ward's remaining singers, puppeteers, bakers, and stagehands to perform one final opera inside the museum that night. [468] While the council attended to confiscate the loaves, a lament rose sweetly through galleries scented with leaking rosewater. [486] Its beauty made Mara's skin prickle, for every tender note sounded like a goodbye rehearsed beneath a collapsing ceiling. [505] When painted puppets reenacted Merrow's drowning, the bakers replaced the mapped loaves with blank initials and slipped the originals to the stagehands. [527] Mara then guided the troupe through a sealed exhibit door, narrating each tunnel's historical purpose so fear could not turn them around. [549] Above them, ice groaned like an audience withholding applause, and pink water dripped from roots into their lantern flames. [568] At the sluice chamber, Mirel arranged the loaves alphabetically, and Mara passed the emerald lens across their crusts to join the hidden lines. [591] The resulting map was also a stage score: each painted gesture marked a valve, each choral interval the seconds between turns. [612] Merrow artists had hidden engineering inside their arts, trusting that no conqueror could operate the city without the culture it despised. [633] The singers began, puppeteers pulled levers in measured arcs, and bakers packed hot dough around pipes that threatened to split. [653] At the final note, Mara turned Sava's honey-greased wheel, though rosewater mist filled her mouth with the taste of funeral sweets. [674] Warm reservoir water entered the Curtain in narrow channels, and the great waterfall began to thaw from its heart outward. [694] For one terrible breath the mountain shook; then silver water resumed its fall, carrying broken ice harmlessly into the lower gorge. [715] Daylight flooded Veyra, and beyond the water Mara saw Sava's untended hives gleaming gold on the cliffs. [732] By spring, the museum no longer called Merrow's people displaced persons, as if displacement were their only history. [750] Its labels named them engineers, singers, painters, and bakers, while its galleries became workshops rather than tombs. [767] At every reopening, Mara baked an S-shaped loaf, then raised the green lens as remembrance became rehearsal and survival learned to sing. [789]</story>
