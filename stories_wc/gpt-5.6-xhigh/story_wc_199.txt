<story>During the hush where past meets prophecy, the Ashen Skyborne Arbor drifted above seven green valleys, trailing roots through clouds like fingers combing wool. [24]
In its lowest bough lived Tavin, an orderly seed sorter who arranged every harvest by shape, dream, and the small music each kernel made. [48]
He loved rules because the wind did not, and because his vanished grandmother had taught him that careful hands could make even grief germinate. [72]
That autumn, however, silver leaves began falling upward from the orchard floor, each bearing a dark vein shaped like one letter of an unknown alphabet. [97]
Tavin yearned to decode the message in falling leaves, for they rose only when the valley bells went silent and tomorrow seemed briefly rememberable. [121]
Regional folklore claimed that the arbor spoke during this hush, warning one valley through riddles while blessing another with mushrooms shaped like laughing umbrellas. [145]
Grandmother had believed a kinder version: the tree mourned whatever people ceased naming, and its warnings became clear only when the forgotten were properly welcomed. [170]
So Tavin abandoned his ledgers, gathered bark crumbs, snail shells, and berry stems, and began building tiny monuments to forgotten things along the eastern limb. [195]
For a lost lullaby he raised nine acorn cups; for an extinct blue moth, a fern arch; for Grandmother, a throne of moss and cinnamon pips. [221]
Each dawn, one upward leaf settled upon a monument, but its vein changed before Tavin could copy it, curling into another maddening letter. [244]
At last Mosswife Ilex, a beetle robed in lichen, crawled from Grandmother's throne and told him he was reading endings instead of beginnings. [267]
"Whatever enters the arbor's heart hollow emerges transformed, always," she said, tapping his ruler, "so prod the oldest monument and watch what wakes." [290]
The oldest monument was no shrine of Tavin's, but a mud lump beneath his sorting table, crusted around something pale and perfectly oval. [313]
He prodded it with a hawthorn pin, and the crust opened to reveal a moonstone cabochon bearing one impossible dust speck at its center. [337]
When moonlight touched the stone, the speck expanded into a miniature arbor where a tiny woman sorted seeds beside monuments identical to his own. [361]
Tavin recognized Grandmother, younger than memory, placing the cabochon inside a clay memorial for the brother whom the valleys had erased from every song. [385]
Then the vision shivered forward, showing seven valley lords hacking the arbor's cloud roots to anchor their towers against a prophesied storm. [407]
The wounded tree burned gray, seeds rained without names, and every future forest grew obedient, square, and silent beneath an ashen sky. [429]
Tavin saw that each leaf vein was not a letter but a root map, altered nightly because each new monument restored one missing path. [453]
Far below, axes flashed on the pilgrimage road, and the seven lords climbed toward the arbor while their heralds praised necessary progress. [475]
His orderly instinct urged him to finish the map first, yet Grandmother's tiny face shook its head from within the moonstone. [496]
Understanding at last, Tavin swept every sorted seed into one joyous heap, added the cabochon, and poured the unruly mixture down the heart hollow. [520]
For one breath nothing happened; then the seeds emerged transformed as bright green beetles, winging into cloud roots and carrying remembered names in golden pollen. [545]
The cabochon emerged last as a round white door, and through it came Grandmother with her forgotten brother, both old as rain and grinning. [569]
He spoke his name, Oren, and the arbor answered by lifting its roots beyond the approaching axes, gently tangling every lord in flowering bindweed. [593]
Across the valleys, the leaf maps completed themselves upon rooftops and ponds, spelling no command, but showing where severed roots could be replanted. [616]
People came with spades instead of prayers, guided by beetles to buried springs, abandoned graves, vanished gardens, and toys whose owners had grown solemn. [640]
For each recovered thing, Tavin made a small mossy monument, and from each monument sprang some delightful error: blue strawberries, humming radishes, or shy bells. [665]
With Grandmother beside him, he kept his shelves, labels, and beloved straight rows, but left one empty compartment in every cabinet so wonder would have somewhere to arrive. [693]
Thus the region's folklore changed: during the hush where past meets prophecy, children now prod little shrines, not to wake warnings, but to invite memories home. [719]
And high in the ashen skyborne arbor, Tavin sorted seeds by shape, dream, music, and one new category he named mossy delight. [741]</story>
