<story>Eleven months after going off the grid, Mara Vale entered the buried archive of Iskar through a vent breathing sulfurous warmth.[21]

Above her, a mining company was preparing to crack Mount Orra open for lithium, and beneath her waited the last archive of a civilization lost before history learned its name.[51]

Mara carried no radio, only charcoal, a knife, and a faded tapestry fragment her mother had pressed into her hand before dying.[73]

"Find what they promised," she had whispered, "and make it remember us."[85]

It was for that sentence Mara had become the lending library arsonist.[97]

The state kiosks called themselves libraries, but they lent censored tablets, recorded every reader, and quietly erased Iskar from schoolbooks.[117]

Mara burned three after closing, harmed no one, and left their forbidden books projected in white light across the smoke.[137]

The name survived because scandal travels farther than scholarship.[146]

She was analytically intuitive, a patient sorter of evidence who also trusted the small inner flare that arrived before proof.[166]

In the first chamber, clay dancers circled a dry fountain, each pointing not toward the next figure but toward a painted bird.[188]

The bird's mosaic wing angled to a bronze poem, whose final letters named a corridor sealed by ash.[206]

Someone had made a trail of artistic creations leading to a hidden message, trusting beauty where maps could be confiscated.[226]

Mara followed, brushing ash aside, until she reached a gallery warm as the hollow beneath a sleeping animal.[244]

Its murals did not portray conquests; they portrayed bakers opening ovens, elders washing strangers' feet, and children carrying embers between houses.[265]

Every image held the same gesture: two cupped hands sheltering a flame.[277]

At the gallery's end hung a vandalized self-portrait of Archivist Sera, her painted face slashed to the stone by furious diagonal cuts.[299]

A detonation test thumped above, and red dust sifted down like the mountain's slow blood.[314]

Mara almost hurried past the ruined face, but intuition snagged on the cuts, which were too evenly spaced for rage.[334]

She spread the faded tapestry fragment over the portrait, and its worn threads fitted the scars as veins fit a leaf.[355]

Through holes in the cloth, selected pieces of Sera's painted features appeared: one eye, one tear, the corner of a resolute mouth.[377]

Together they seemed to portray not Sera but Mara's mother at the instant she had made Mara promise.[395]

Grief rose hot and immense, yet she held it as the painted hands held fire: not extinguishing it, not letting it consume her.[418]

The visible features corresponded to symbols in the bronze poem, and their positions spelled BENEATH THE WELCOME.[435]

Mara pressed both palms beneath the portrait, and a stone panel yielded, exhaling air preserved for three thousand years.[454]

Inside, a crystal cylinder held Sera's voice, thin with age but steady as magma beneath winter.[470]

"Iskar promises shelter, water, and learning to every future settlement of Orra, and no ruler or owner may close this mountain while one seeker stands outside."[496]

Beneath the recording lay Iskar's fired clay charter and a geological relief showing that the company's blast would pierce a pressurized chamber.[518]

Another test shook the gallery, and Mara understood the mountain had hours, perhaps less.[532]

Boots scraped the corridor, and her younger brother Ansel, the company's survey chief, emerged with two guards and the exhausted anger of someone she had abandoned.[558]

"You burned the only places that might have carried your work, vanished, and left me to bury her," he said.[578]

Mara offered him the cylinder because apology without proof had failed them too many times.[593]

"I left to rescue the forgotten promise she died believing, but it is also a warning, so hate me after you listen."[615]

Sera's voice filled the gallery, and Ansel read the geological relief with the swift competence their mother had taught them.[635]

A guard's receiver announced ten minutes until the main charge.[645]

When the senior guard reached for the cylinder, Ansel struck his hand away and opened a live survey channel on his tablet.[667]

Mara held the tapestry against the scarred portrait while he filmed the alignment, the charter, and the relief in one unbroken sweep.[689]

Across the mountain, miners saw a dead civilization speak through cloth, paint, clay, and the face of an accused arsonist.[709]

Across the slopes, blasting crews cut one relay, then another, and the countdown stopped at three.[725]

Ansel lowered the tablet but not his anger, which remained between them, bright and living.[740]

"I cannot forgive the fires today," he said, taking one edge of the tapestry, "but I can help carry this."[760]

Together they climbed toward morning, bearing the promise between them as carefully as cupped hands bearing a coal.[778]</story>
