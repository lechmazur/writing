In the forgotten orchard deep in a bamboo forest, Mara kept a loom beneath the last pear tree. [18] Villagers had once called her the wistful loom spinner, because every cloth leaving her shuttle pictured a road she had not taken. [40] Now no villagers remained; bamboo had swallowed the roofs, and white roots lifted the kiln stones like old teeth. [59] Only Mara's loom continued, stoically sparkling whenever moonlight struck its brass heddles. [71]

She wove against a raveling silence that came each winter from the mountain and unmade whatever no living voice remembered. [91] First it erased birdcalls, then names carved on gates, then the paths between one hour and the next. [109] Mara had resisted it for seventeen years, since her son Iven entered the royal observatory and the mountain folded distance around him. [131] His final signal, delivered as a shiver in her warp, said the observatory still hung beyond the fold, unreachable but intact. [152]

Since then, her one motivation had been to carry a message across impossible distance: Come home; I remember you. [171] Ordinary thread snapped at the fold, and spoken words vanished before crossing the orchard wall. [186] Mara therefore spun memory itself: wool combed with laughter, flax dampened by grief, silk reeled from dreams. [203] Each attempt carried farther, but each also took the memory from her forever. [216] She had already forgotten Iven's first step and the tune he sang while gathering pears. [231]

One rainless morning, scavengers abandoned a rummage sale beside the buried village shrine. [244] Mara discovered a possible bridge through jigsaw puzzle edges found in a rummage sale box, all center pieces missing. [263] The edges depicted blue sky, yet their tabs and hollows matched no neighboring piece. [277] When arranged around the loom, they formed a crooked frame, a boundary describing absence rather than containing a picture. [296] Mara understood: a message could not cross the fold, but perhaps the fold could be persuaded to finish what lay at its edges. [319]

She set the frame into the warp and went seeking a heart for it. [333] Inside the last pear tree, beneath bark split by lightning, she found a tree sap amber bead enclosing one black seed. [354] Her husband had made that bead on the day Iven was born, polishing it while she labored and promising their family would outlast winter. [378] Mara threaded it onto the shuttle, although doing so meant risking her last complete memory of them together. [396]

At dusk the silence arrived early. [402] It poured between bamboo stalks without wind, bleaching crickets from the air and loosening stars from their constellations. [420] The loom stopped halfway through Mara's message. [427] Its shuttle hung above the amber bead, and the road in the cloth ended at a white cliff. [445]

To recommence, she needed a thread stronger than remembered joy. [455] She needed the memory that had taught joy its cost. [465] Mara closed her eyes and returned, through the hush of time's unraveling tapestry, to the night Iven departed. [483] He had been sixteen, frightened, and furious that she would not beg him to stay. [498] She had merely repaired his coat and said that love was not a locked gate. [513] Only after his lantern vanished had she collapsed among the pear blossoms. [525]

That hidden collapse, not her farewell, held the necessary strength: love choosing distance while bearing its wound in silence. [544] It was the latent sacrifice beneath every stoic year, waiting like a bright fiber under undyed wool. [561] She drew it from herself. [566] Pain became gold thread between her fingers, and the memory behind it went dark. [580]

Mara could no longer recall why a repaired blue coat lay in her cedar chest, yet she fed the thread through the puzzle frame. [604] The mismatched edges trembled, inventing center pieces from moonlight, sap, and everything the silence had erased. [620] The loom thundered once. [624]

Across the white cliff in the cloth, a road continued into a room of brass instruments, where a gray-haired stranger looked up. [646] Mara did not know his face, but her hands knew to tighten around the shuttle. [661] The stranger touched the woven words, and distance broke around his cry of Mother. [675] His answer returned along the road: I am coming; keep one pear for me. [689]

By dawn the observatory stood among the bamboo, rusted by seventeen missing years, and Iven knelt before her. [707] Mara greeted him kindly, as she would any exhausted traveler. [717] Then he placed the amber bead in her palm, and although his name found no echo, its trapped seed warmed her skin. [739] Behind them, the last pear tree opened one white blossom against the retreating silence, and Mara began teaching the stranger how to spin. [762]
