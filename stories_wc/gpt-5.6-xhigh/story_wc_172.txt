<story>During the lull of distant thunder, Cassian Rusk stepped onto the Orison Salt Flat and listened for a bird dead eighty years. [22]

Behind him, trucks from his Mercy Meridian Foundation idled beside crates of water, antibiotics, and rifles, each labeled RELIEF. [41] Rusk gave villages clinics after his companies ruined their wells, and he considered both transactions equally clean. [58] For six months, his cloud engines had salted approaching storms while private dams captured the runoff beyond the western ridge. [78] The resulting manufactured drought had emptied three provinces, enriched his grain exchanges, and bared the ancient riverbeds beneath Orison. [97] He had also funded every refugee camp, because suffering offended his taste only when it lacked competent administration. [115]

Biologist Elian Varo followed with a scanner, hating Rusk carefully enough to keep accepting his grants. [131] At their feet lay a map drawn by cracks in dried riverbeds, branching across the salt above vanished pressure and buried stone. [153] Varo had learned to read it: wide seams marked old channels, hooked seams marked nesting bars, and circles promised trapped air. [174] The pattern converged beneath a white mesa where the last Orison bellbird colony had been buried by a government reservoir. [194]

Officially, Rusk wanted the colony's final egg for the public genome library he had endowed in seven capitals. [212] Unofficially, he carried a little brass player under his coat and wound it each night when no servant could see. [232] On its wax loop, his mother whistled the bellbird's descending call, then laughed when the living bird corrected her. [251] She had died before the reservoir gates closed, believing her son gentle, a misunderstanding he had never paid to correct. [271] He did not miss her, he told himself; he merely intended to preserve the echo of extinct birds. [289]

Thunder muttered, then paused, and in that immense hush Varo found the circular fracture beneath a skin of salt. [308] Rusk's guards drilled where she pointed, opening a shaft that exhaled cold air and the odor of old reeds. [327] They descended into a stone counting room built by the first dam engineers, who had catalogued everything they drowned. [346] Inside a cedar box rested an extinct bird's egg, smoke blue, uncracked, and smaller than Rusk's closed fist. [364]

Varo's scanner found intact proteins, but no viable cells, no sleeping miracle for his laboratories to multiply. [381] Rusk stared as if arithmetic itself had betrayed him. [390] Then the storm spoke again, and dust trickled from the ceiling in thin gray threads. [405] Varo lifted the egg toward him, expecting rage, but his hands received it with fractured grace, careful and suddenly old. [425]

His radio cracked with the voice of Tern, the foundation commander, reporting that the western reservoirs were brimming under the storm. [446] If the dams stayed shut, their walls might fail; if opened, the first surge would strike camps still crowding the lower road. [468] Tern asked whether to release the water, and Rusk asked how many people had not boarded his evacuation trucks. [487] Four hundred twelve, Tern said, plus twenty guards and every drill crew on the flat. [502]

Varo understood then that the charity convoys had been positioned not from kindness, but because Rusk had priced this ending weeks ago. [524] The ordinary spillway would save the dams and drown the stragglers; waiting would scatter poisoned reservoir mud across the basin. [544] Varo looked at the crack map above them and saw an older answer: the eastern channel curved away from the road. [565] Opening it required blasting Rusk's buried diversion station, the machinery that had manufactured the drought and recorded who ordered it. [585] She told him, and for once offered no recommendation. [594]

Rusk brushed salt from the egg with his thumb. [603] His empire could rebuild machinery, courts, and reputations; it could not call a species back from the silence inside the blue shell. [625] He placed the brass player beside it, pressed the radio, and ordered Tern to evacuate the drill crews east. [644] Then he gave two commands: blow the diversion station, and release every gate when the fire rose. [661]

Tern warned that the flood would erase the drought ledgers, ruin Rusk's grain ports, and turn Orison into a lake. [681] "That is philanthropy," Rusk replied, "when accounting cannot improve the story." [692]

The blast struck during another lull, noiseless for a heartbeat, a red flower opening beyond the mesa. [709] Water entered the ancient channels, first as silver lines, then as muscular rivers that obeyed the fractures and spared the road. [730] Guards shouted, trucks fled, and the salt flat broke into a thousand trembling mirrors beneath the returning rain. [748]

Varo carried the egg uphill, but Rusk remained knee deep in the current, holding the player above his head. [767] At last he opened his private frequency to every relief tower and released his mother's whistle, followed by the bellbird answering across the reborn water. [792]</story>
