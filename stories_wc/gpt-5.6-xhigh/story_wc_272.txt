<story>At dusk, Mara Venn carried an unfinished restoration of her dead sister's portrait up the spiral stairs of Saint Orison Lighthouse, where a forgotten library slept behind the lantern room. [30] She had once painted faces so truthfully that mourners forgave the dead, but now every likeness split beneath her brush, leaving doubled eyes and mouths that remembered different griefs. [59] The crestfallen portrait artist had come because the lighthouse keeper's final letter claimed the library held a way to mend what her art had broken. [84] Outside, the sea shone like hammered tin; inside, shelves leaned inward, and Mara understood the old island warning that truth appears only when the walls close in. [111]

On the reading table lay a broken tarot card box, its lid crushed, its red lining teased into a nest of loose silk threads. [135] Only one card remained: the Tower, painted not with falling bodies but with tiny portraits of Mara at every window. [155] Who else ruins a world by trying to render it perfectly? [166] Who asks forgiveness from a canvas? [172] She favored questions, rhetorical questions sharpened by shame, yet the painted Maras turned their heads as if answering someone beyond the frame. [194]

The shelves groaned closer, squeezing aisles into seams, while the great lens overhead revolved without flame and swept pale radiance across the floor. [217] Wherever that beam passed, reality fragmented: rain climbed the windows, books whispered endings before beginnings, and Mara's shadow wore the face of her dead sister, Elian. [243] Years ago, Mara had redesigned Elian's portrait after the fever, smoothing away terror and adding a brave smile the girl had never managed. [266] That beautiful lie had pleased their mother, then spread like a crack through mirrors, memories, and every face Mara subsequently painted. [287]

The keeper's letter contained one instruction: redesign the Tower by untangling threads, and do not stop when the walls close in. [308] Mara pulled a scarlet strand from the ruined box, and a shelf jerked backward, revealing a stairway descending beneath the lighthouse. [329] She wanted daylight, rescue, any upward path, but restoring Elian required her to descend into necessary darkness rather than repaint it as light. [352] Taking the card, box, and portrait, she followed the strand downward while stone tightened behind her like a fist. [371]

Below waited a circular chamber filled with hanging canvases, each showing one possible Elian: smiling, screaming, ancient, unborn, accusing. [390] Blue light leaked from their painted wounds, bathing the chamber in a luminescent ache that made sorrow feel almost warm. [410] The scarlet strand divided among the canvases, knotting each possibility to the false smile on Mara's unfinished portrait. [428] If she chose the kindest past, would that not be love? [439] And if love demanded a lie, what had truth ever done except take Elian away? [454]

This time the shadows answered with Elian's plain voice, saying Mara kept asking because she disliked the answer. [472] The chamber narrowed, pressing canvases against Mara's shoulders, and the revolving beam began erasing whichever Elian it touched. [490] Mara worked by untangling threads, not cutting them, tracing every red crossing through the box's torn lining and the portraits' stitched edges. [512] Each loosened knot returned something painful: Elian's envy, her cruelty during fever, her fear of dying, Mara's relief when nursing finally ended. [534] Fragments converged, not into innocence, but into a sister complicated enough to be real. [548]

With no room left for another question, Mara scraped the smiling Tower from the card and redesigned it as an open door. [570] Then she painted Elian in the doorway exactly as memory offered her: furious, frightened, tender, and gone. [587] The walls met with a sound like a book slammed shut. [598] Darkness swallowed Mara, necessary and absolute, until the broken box clicked together in her hands. [613]

When light returned, dawn burned through the lighthouse lens, and the library stood wide again, every shelf upright. [631] Her unfinished portrait now showed Mara alone, crestfallen still, but whole, holding grief without arranging it into beauty. [649] Below, the sea resumed its direction, rain fell, and the island's windows reflected single rooms instead of branching lives. [668] Mara placed the mended card box beside the keeper's letter, though she left one red thread trailing from its corner. [688] Some truths, she understood, were not wounds to close, but paths that kept the dark connected to the light. [707]</story>
