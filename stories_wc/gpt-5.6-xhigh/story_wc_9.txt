Three nights after the birth of her daughter, Sera Vale, an onyx-ink cartomancer, climbed beyond the weather with a swaddled silence inside her. [23]

The child was safe below with her father, yet Sera carried the terror of loving someone while time remained hungry. [43]

At midnight she reached the glade above the clouds, where silver grass bowed around moonlit pools and every breath tasted of snow and roses. [67]

Its choking beauty made her want to kneel, but the cards stitched inside her coat tugged toward the glade's blackthorn heart. [88]

Sera had come to find where lost time accumulates, a place her guild dismissed as a merciful superstition for grieving fools. [109]

Unlike them, she was willingly perplexed, believing a mystery could be entered only after certainty had been left outside. [128]

That faith had made her readings famous, although it had not helped her understand why her mother, Anja, vanished eighteen years earlier. [150]

On Sera's ninth birthday, Anja traced her own hand beside Sera's, then departed before dawn without explanation. [190]

Only a saucer from Sera's dolls' tea set remained on the cloth, its crack joining the embroidered lifelines. [208]

Then the thorn trunk opened, and Sera entered a room furnished with every supper, fever, recital, and reconciliation her mother had missed. [334]

None had happened, yet all waited there, exquisitely preserved, their unreal candles smoking until Sera could barely breathe. [352]

Anja sat at the longest table, still young except for white eyes, her wrist fastened to a chain of translucent minutes. [373]

Her punishment was cloudbound repentance: she had fed the glade one possible life for each real hour she had abandoned. [415]
