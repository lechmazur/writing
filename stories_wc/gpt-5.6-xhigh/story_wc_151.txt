At dawn, the Golden Arcade woke one brass shutter at a time, its solar glass holding the pink light like an old postcard. [23]
Beneath its silent holograms, Mara Venn arranged pencils on an ebony clipboard and listened for the wheels of the surface mail cart. [45]
In a city where messages traveled directly from thought to thought, surface mail was reserved for grief, love, and treason. [65]
Mara was a pseudonym creator, licensed to invent names for citizens who needed to say one true thing without surrendering every other truth. [88]
She was carefully inventive, matching each client with a name that sounded remembered rather than manufactured. [104]
That morning, however, the name waiting on her clipboard was her own. [116]
Three nights earlier, a sealed envelope had appeared under her door, addressed to "The Girl Who Counts Edges" in her missing father's handwriting. [139]
Inside were two sheets of cream paper, empty except for tiny blue and yellow strokes along their margins. [157]
Her father had taught postal cryptography before vanishing into the municipal memory archive, and said machines read centers while humans survived at edges. [180]
Mara had spent the night trying to discover meaning in the margins, but the strokes resisted every cataloged cipher. [199]
Then sunrise touched the arcade tiles, revealing chalk drawings beneath the cleaning drones' transparent tracks. [214]
Children, playing an old postman's game, had covered the pavement with suns, rockets, hopscotch moons, and looping alphabets in blue and yellow. [236]
Mara knelt, laid the sheets beside them, and understood that the marks were codes in children's sidewalk chalk, transferred as short and long strokes. [260]
To read them, she had to juxtapose each paper margin with the matching border of a chalk square. [278]
The first pairing spelled NORTH LOCKER, and the second spelled BORROW A NAME. [291]
The north locker belonged to Ivo, the arcade's retired postman, who polished his red cart each morning despite monthly deliveries. [311]
When Mara showed him the pages, his weathered face tightened with recognition. [323]
"Your father gave me those pages and a letter eleven years ago," he said, unlocking the cart's false bottom, "and told me to slide the pages beneath your door when the archive began erasing names." [358]
The archive had begun that week, calling the deletions compassionate compression. [369]
Ivo produced a thin envelope bearing no sender, only a black wax seal shaped like a comma. [386]
Mara broke it and found her father's final request: mail the enclosed testimony beyond the city before noon, under a name no predictive engine could connect to him. [414]
The testimony proved that compression did not delete the dead; it deleted living dissenters, sealing their memories behind profitable simulations. [434]
Her father was among them, not missing but copied, muted, and sold as an advice ghost. [450]
Anger urged Mara to sign his real name in letters large enough for every scanner to see. [467]
Training warned her that truth without camouflage would be intercepted before Ivo reached the outer post. [483]
She studied the children, who were rebuilding a chalk rocket whenever a cleaning drone washed away its nose. [501]
They did not preserve one perfect drawing; each child added a line, and ownership dissolved into play. [518]
Borrow a name, her father had written, not invent one. [528]
Mara turned over the ebony clipboard, whose scratches recorded decades of clients pressing too hard while signing their safer selves. [548]
In those accidental margins she found fragments of old aliases: June, Copper, Bell, and Finch. [563]

Ivo smiled sadly and stamped the envelope with a manual date, an act so obsolete it sounded like a tiny door closing. [649]
A patrol drone entered the arcade, tasting ink and intention from the air, and Mara slid the envelope beneath her clipboard. [670]
The drone asked who had authored the outgoing material. [679]
Mara answered, "June Copperbell Finch," while the children crowded around her and shouted that June was all of them. [698]
Faced with one identity blooming from dozens of hands, it classified the author as a public game and floated away. [718]
Ivo tucked the envelope into his cart and waited, for surface mail required consent at every transfer. [735]
In the breath before a letter is mailed, silence made its final offer. [748]
Mara pressed two fingers to the coded margin, said goodbye to her father, and gave Ivo the nod. [766]
The red cart rolled toward the outer post, carrying an invented name made true by everyone who had borrowed it. [786]
