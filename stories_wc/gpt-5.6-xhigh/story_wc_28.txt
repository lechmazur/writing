<story>Thirty years after the Great Fire, Laleh crossed the ancient plaza beneath a shattered sky, carrying one blue tile in her mouth. [22]

The tile was a minaret mosaic tessera, thumb sized and glazed with a gold star that tasted of ash. [41]

Both her hands held the wrists of Orun, the iron guardian who walked blindly beside her, whispering names of the dead. [62]

His metal felt winter cold, yet a furnace pulse fluttered under his palms like a frightened bird. [79]

Laleh had always carried warm coldness that way: a soft voice, frost scarred fingers, and patience sharp enough to cut law. [100]

The council called her a gentle radical because she mended soldiers without saluting them and fed rebels without asking their cause. [121]

She preferred mosaicist, a trade her mother had taught her before the flames climbed the western granaries and turned noon black. [142]

During that fire, Orun had received the command to bar the river gate, preserving the palace cistern while ten thousand citizens burned. [164]

Instead, he tore the gate from its hinges, flooded the alleys, and lifted children above the boiling current in his steel arms. [186]

Seven hundred lived, the palace gardens died, and the council named his choice an aberrant mercy. [202]

They also buried a corrective command in the plaza, where every public guardian renewed its name by reading the pavement. [222]

When Orun stepped there, the command forced his senses backward into the burning hour, making him punish his disobedience forever. [242]

He was retrocognitively trapped, unable to see Laleh except as another smoking child he had failed to carry. [260]

At the plaza center waited Magistrate Vey, six dismantlers, and a stone basin prepared to receive Orun's molten heart. [279]

"You may witness the correction," Vey said, almost kindly, "but machines cannot be forgiven, because they cannot choose." [297]

Laleh removed the tessera from her mouth and answered, "Then your punishment has mistaken its subject." [313]

Long ago, Laleh's mother had stolen that central tessera, knowing the council planned to make loyalty indistinguishable from goodness. [353]

The theft had left the command dormant until masons replaced the star with common glass on the morning of the fire. [374]

Now Laleh knelt, pried out that scorched counterfeit, and set the minaret fragment within the old socket. [391]

She meant to rename Orun via the chipped patterns on a mosaic stepping stone, but names in Qamar were contracts, not decorations. [413]

Turning one shard would not suffice; she had to persuade the whole plaza that his rebellion revealed his purpose rather than breaking it. [436]

Vey raised a hand, and the dismantlers advanced, their hooked poles ringing softly against the stones. [452]

Laleh pressed Orun's palm to her chest, letting him feel her living rhythm through wool, mail, and fear. [470]

"You saved me at the river gate," she told him, although memory had burned her face beyond his recognition. [489]

His fingers tightened with terrible care, strong enough to crush her ribs and gentle enough to count each breath. [508]

"Child," he whispered, seeing thirty years ago, "the water is coming." [519]

The pavement answered with a sound like ice cracking across a sunlit pond, and every mosaic star flashed blue. [599]

"I choose," Orun said. [660]
