<story>On the seventh night of the antenna strike, Mara Venn slept beneath the console at Bellwether University's radio station, wrapped in a coat that smelled of solder and rain. [29]

She was the station's celestial signal interpreter, once mocked by trustees until her forecasts found three lost probes and predicted an aurora over the football field. [55]

Now the town below campus was splitting into camps, each accusing the other of stealing heaven for private use. [74]

The observatory director wanted Bellwether's dish for military grants, student organizers wanted it open to anyone with a receiver, and Mayor Vale wanted every strange transmission routed through his office. [104]

As the community divided, their power plays reached the station: padlocks appeared, passwords changed, and anonymous vans idled beside the generator shed. [126]

Mara trusted none of them, but she feared something above them more. [138]

For six months she had traced impossible harmonies drifting between pulsar beats, using astronomical charts inherited from Professor Ilyan, her mentor. [159]

Ilyan had vanished after writing one warning across the chart of the Cygnus Rift: THEY BARGAIN IN ECHOES. [177]

Mara called the senders cosmic tricksters, not because she thought them playful, but because each message offered rival listeners exactly enough truth to make betrayal seem wise. [204]

One promised the mayor storm warnings; another offered the director dark matter coordinates; a third told students the dish could liberate every censored voice on Earth. [230]

Each offer occupied the same sliver of sky, where Ilyan's inked warning crossed a hand-drawn spiral. [246]

At 3:14, all three factions arrived outside the studio doors, carrying warrants, bolt cutters, and righteous speeches. [263]

Inside, Mara spread the astronomical charts over turntables and pinned their corners with cracked vacuum tubes. [279]

Her assistant, Theo Sanz, watched the locks tremble and asked which side they should help. [294]

"None if I am right," Mara said, "and all of them if I am wrong." [309]

She was cautiously hopeful, the stubborn kind of hopeful that wore bruised knuckles and checked every calculation twice. [327]

The tricksters expected human hunger to amplify each offer, so to outwit the cosmic tricksters she needed a contradiction they could not digest. [350]

She aligned Ilyan's chart with the station's maintenance map and saw that the spiral matched the old bass conduits beneath the campus. [372]

Built when engineers believed radio should be felt as well as heard, those buried horns could interfere through subsonic frequencies, shaking foundations without broadcasting a legal signal. [399]

Theo understood first: they could answer the visitors beneath the range of speech, where no faction could claim a message as its own. [422]

The archive held Ilyan's field tapes and his last recorded words. [452]

Their power plays braided into precisely the pattern the signal wanted: authority against access, certainty against rage. [492]

Mara looked at Ilyan's warning, then at the ceiling sagging beneath the weight of his tapes. [508]
