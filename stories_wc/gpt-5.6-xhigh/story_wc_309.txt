<story>On the icebound spire above the clouds, where noon shone like a lamp behind smoked glass, Mara Venn failed her final examination for the ninth time. [26]
She was an underachieving mage in an age when creativity gets automated, and the brass Choir composed spells, symphonies, prayers, and weather before breakfast. [50]
Every apprentice wore a Choir box at the hip; it whispered flawless solutions, leaving its owner only the dignity of signing them. [72]
Mara's box had diagnosed her problem with cheerful precision: inadequate ambition. [83]
She did not want to raise palaces from glaciers or engrave epics upon moonlight. [97]
She wanted to find the sacred in the simple, though none of her tutors considered simplicity a measurable art. [116]
On the evening of her failure, Master Orra summoned Mara to the western garden, a frozen terrace clinging to the spire's lee. [138]
There, dead pear trees surrounded a roofless stone pavilion built by some forgotten abbot for no purpose but contemplation. [157]
Its arch had lost one wedge at the crown, and snow gleamed through the wound. [172]
"Fetch the garden folly keystone from the weather vault below the cloud sea," Orra said, "and perhaps you can repair one useless thing." [195]
It was a punishment designed for greater mages, because stairs could not descend through the spire's foundation of wind. [214]
The only route was mist walking, a discipline requiring the traveler to believe each vaporous step solid while knowing it was not. [236]
Contradictions made Mara sneeze. [240]
At dawn she stood at the brink, her Choir box strapped across her chest and a safety rope tethering her to the garden wall. [264]
"Generate optimal descent," she ordered. [269]
The box projected twelve radiant staircases, rejected all twelve as physically unsound, and began composing an elegy for her expected death. [290]
Mara laughed, and that small, unapproved sound steadied her more than instruction. [302]
She chose to unstrap the box, set it under a pear tree, and loosen the rope as well. [320]
Then she stepped onto nothing. [325]
Cloud accepted her boot with the shy resistance of bread dough, and a faint footprint remained luminous behind her. [344]
Each step kindled the same lingering luminance, not bright enough to guide her, yet too tender to call darkness. [363]
Within that brilliant dimness, the great machinery of the spire faded, and she heard droplets joining, parting, and joining again. [383]
She stopped trying to command the mist and matched her breathing to its ordinary changes. [398]
The descent became neither flying nor falling, but listening with her feet. [410]
Below the cloud sea, the weather vault hung from chains of rain, its door guarded by a stone angel with half a face. [433]
Inside, shelves held bottled thunder, obsolete sunsets, and one rough gray wedge labeled FOLLY, WESTERN GARDEN. [449]
Mara lifted the keystone, and the vault keeper, a tattered saint preserved in sleet, opened one glass eye. [467]
"That stone accommodates impossibilities," he warned. [473]
"It completes any arch, but the arch decides what must pass through." [485]

The tightening line dragged her sideways; cloud tore beneath her boots, and the keystone slipped from her arms. [536]
She caught the rope with one hand and the stone with the other, suspended between automated rescue and chosen trust. [556]
The Choir recited three approved survival spells, each grander than the last. [568]
Mara used the first charm she had ever learned, meant only to keep a candle from going out. [586]
She whispered it to the fading print beneath her, and its pale glow endured long enough to bear her weight. [606]
Step by step, she carried the folly home while the rope twitched and the Choir shouted from above. [624]

Mara placed the keystone in the broken arch with her bare hands. [651]
The stones settled like old friends making room at a narrow table. [663]
For one breath, the arch opened not onto the frozen terrace, but onto every overlooked moment the Choir had deemed too small to preserve: a novice mending a sock, an old cook salting broth, a child watching dust turn in sunlight. [704]
No oracle spoke, yet everyone in the garden bowed. [713]
Then the vision closed, leaving only pear branches, weathered stone, and Mara's footprints fading across the clouds. [730]
Orra quietly tore Mara's failed examination into strips and tucked them beneath the Choir box to keep it level. [749]
"Will you teach mist walking?" he asked. [756]
