After making first contact, Doctor Mara Venn returned alone to the rainwashed tower in spring, when the copper gutters sang softly and every field below shone like newly opened glass. [30]

The newspapers called her the luminous skeptic because contact with the visitors had dusted her skin with harmless blue radiance, while she continued to doubt every theory about why they had come. [62]

Her colleagues preferred prophet, victim, or ambassador, names that turned uncertainty into ceremony. [75]

Mara, academically rebellious since her first dissertation had disproved its own premise, chose uncertainty. [89]

She came to the tower to understand the chemistry of homecoming, not to worship the six silent figures waiting above the clouds. [111]

They had descended three weeks earlier as jointed shells of ceramic and mist, touched her brow, and then climbed back into their vessel without speaking. [136]

Only one thing had changed inside the tower: a sealed drawer now stood open, revealing her grandfather's lump of fly amber, stolen from the university museum decades ago. [164]

Within the honey-colored stone, an ancient fly held its wings as if arrested on the threshold of departure. [182]

Beside it lay a strip of visitor material that smelled, impossibly, of the kitchen where Mara had grown up. [201]

That odor made her distrust herself more than any miracle could. [212]

She carried both specimens to the old spectrometer in the lantern room and found the same pattern of terpenes, acids, and proteins in each. [236]

The pattern also marked her glowing skin. [243]

It was not a message but a recipe, assembled from the amber's preserved molecules and her own remembered scent. [262]

At dusk, the visitors sent a tone through the tower, low enough to tremble rain from the windows. [280]

Lights answered across the valley, not in a line toward the vessel, but around the abandoned estate where her grandfather had once taught her the names of trees. [308]

Mara understood that she was being invited on an artificial pilgrimage, a route constructed from pieces of her past rather than sacred history. [331]

She refused until the tone shaped itself into the three notes her mother used to call everyone home for supper. [351]

Then refusal became another untested hypothesis. [357]

She left the tower after midnight, guided via dusty footprints left on an overgrown sculpture path. [373]

Rain had rinsed every ordinary track away, yet these prints remained dry, pale, and six-toed beneath nettles and leaning bronze saints. [394]

Each sculpture had been altered to face backward, so that stone kings, iron children, and a faceless astronomer all watched her leave. [416]

The arrangement annoyed her with its crude symbolism, which steadied her. [427]

At the path's center, she found her former students, the museum director, two farmers, and her estranged brother Tomas approaching from different gates. [450]

All bore faint blue light, though none had touched the visitors. [461]

The same domestic odors had drawn them: soap, wet wool, burnt pears, library paste, and soil lifted by a childhood spade. [482]

They began to converge around a cracked bronze fountain, each following a private road to the same dry basin. [501]

In the fountain waited six visitor shells, no longer towering, but folded small as kneeling travelers. [517]

Tomas reached Mara first and asked whether she had arranged this as another experiment. [531]

His suspicion hurt because it sounded like hers, and because she had not spoken to him since selling their family house. [552]

"I thought home was a place that trapped us," she said, holding out the fly amber. [568]

"Perhaps it is only the compound that lets us recognize one another after change." [582]

When Tomas closed his hand around the stone beside hers, the visitors opened like white flowers. [598]

They released not words but molecular portraits, brief fragrances that carried impossible distances: salt from their vanished sea, metal from their birthing chambers, smoke from a sun they had outlived. [628]

Mara saw then that first contact had not been their arrival. [639]

It had been their question: could an alien mind rebuild belonging from chemistry, and could strangers answer without surrendering doubt? [659]

She looked around the basin as old grievances loosened, not erased, among those gathered there. [674]

Her grandfather's theft, her brother's anger, her students' defiance, and the visitors' exile remained facts, but no longer walls. [693]

Dawn filled the wet valley with a bronze quiet, neither hopeful nor mournful, while the tower rang behind them. [712]

Mara placed the amber in the fountain, returning it to no rightful owner and to everyone who had followed its scent. [733]

As the blue light faded from her skin, she remained luminous to Tomas for a simpler reason: she had come back. [754]
