After the last ship sailed from Orisel, Mara climbed from the empty harbor into the palace garden, where autumn had blackened the fountains and laid copper leaves along every path. [30]

She was a traveler who wrote obituaries for the living, a profession born from her belief that people vanished long before their hearts admitted it. [55]

In inns and sickrooms, she interviewed those left behind, then composed vividly descriptive accounts of the absent as if mapping countries erased by fog. [79]

Her clients called these pieces explained absences, and reading one aloud often released a grief that ordinary farewells could not touch. [100]

Yet Mara had never written about Tomas, her brother, who had returned from war smiling, slept beneath her roof for three months, and departed without a word. [127]

She had crossed seven provinces to understand the geography of healing: whether sorrow diminished with distance, followed rivers, or gathered wherever memory found shelter. [151]

Every road had answered differently, so she came to Orisel because its solitary queen was rumored to keep vanished courtiers alive by projecting presence into the shadowed garden. [179]

Beyond the yew arch, lanterns glowed among bare branches, illuminating marble benches occupied by coats, gloves, books, and cups arranged for owners who never came. [204]

The splendor was lonesome, each abandoned object casting a shadow more persuasive than a body. [219]

At the central pavilion, Queen Ilyra knelt over a magnificent carpet whose blue field showed islands, currents, and tiny silver ports. [240]

A narrow wound crossed the woven sea. [247]

"My husband cut his final route from it before he boarded the last ship," she said, offering Mara a loose blue weft thread from the palace carpet. [274]

"Tell me where grief goes when no road remains." [283]

Mara touched the thread, surprised by its warmth, and saw that the missing strand curved from the capital toward a blank western margin. [306]

Ilyra explained that each evening she set her husband's boots beside the fountain, ordered his favorite quince tea, and spoke until his silence seemed attentive. [331]

Projection had preserved his habits so perfectly that she could no longer remember his face without the garden supplying moonlight, perfume, and an empty chair. [356]

"That is not presence," Mara said gently. [363]

"It is an absence you have taught to perform." [372]

The queen stiffened, and guards shifted beneath the trees, but Mara threaded the blue filament through a bronze needle. [391]

"Help me mend the map, and I will write him truthfully." [402]

They sat knee to knee on the cold pavilion floor, passing the needle between them in an intimate rhythm, their hands meeting whenever the sea required a turn. [430]

For every stitch, Mara asked one small question: what the king feared, whom he disappointed, why he laughed during thunderstorms, and what farewell he withheld. [455]

Ilyra's ceremonial answers slowly broke open, revealing a man who hated ships, loved apricot jam, and had sailed because remaining king had become another kind of disappearance. [482]

The missing route was not his escape from her, Mara realized, but his desperate attempt to become visible to himself beyond crown and marriage. [506]

As the carpet closed under their fingers, the thread led not westward but inward, spiraling toward an island newly made from their joined stitches. [530]

Mara began the obituary aloud, describing the living king not as lost or faithless, but as a frightened man who chose uncertainty over a life already entombed. [557]

She described the queen, too, as a woman who had mistaken perfect remembrance for devotion and furnished emptiness until it resembled love. [579]

Then, because honesty demanded payment, Mara added Tomas: a brother who came home bodily yet remained stranded on some battlefield no sister could enter. [603]

Her voice failed. [606]

Ilyra took Mara's hand, not as queen or client, but as one abandoned shore recognizing another. [622]

Together they carried the boots, tea, coat, and empty chair from the garden and locked them in a cedar room before dawn. [644]

Nothing vanished when the key turned; instead, the leaves resumed their dry whisper, the fountain became merely stone, and the garden widened around two breathing women. [670]

Mara finally understood that healing had no geography measurable in miles. [681]

It was the intimate border where invention ended and another person's unknowable life began. [695]

At sunrise, she left Orisel with the mended carpet's pattern copied in her notebook, while Ilyra kept the single leftover thread. [716]

Behind her, the palace shone through amber trees, splendid and solitary, no longer pretending that those who left were present, only admitting they had mattered. [741]
