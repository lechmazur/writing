<story>At the coastal kayak launch, Jonah Vale watched rain erase the horizon and tried not to look like what everyone called him: the broken navigator. [25]
Once, he had guided rescue boats by stars; then a confident shortcut had wrecked one on Needle Reef, taking his wife, Elian, with it. [49]
Now his left hand trembled, his license remained suspended, and Elian's brass astronomical compass knocked against his chest like a second, accusing heart. [72]
Their daughter, Tessa, shoved a red kayak toward the tide while townspeople clustered beneath the shelter, waiting for the memorial crossing he had promised to lead. [98]
But the weather siren failed, the lighthouse vanished behind fog, and the forecasted calm became a gale precisely when expectations collapse into consequences. [121]
Then the harbor radio crackled: a school skiff was drifting beyond the drowned causeway, carrying six children and a teacher with a broken engine. [145]
The rescue crew could not launch its heavy boat across the sandbar, and every face turned toward Jonah before remembering why it should not. [169]
Tessa clipped a towline aboard and said, "I am going, with you or without you." [184]
Jonah wanted to forbid her, wanted even more to hide, but the backlash of regrets struck harder than the wind: every old hesitation returned as fresh harm. [211]
He had spent three years apologizing in rooms where apology cost nothing; here, danger offered the price he had claimed he wished to pay. [235]
His only honest motivation was to align heart and action, so he steadied the kayak and climbed into the stern. [255]
They launched into brown chop, Tessa paddling fiercely while Jonah studied the shoreline that appeared and disappeared like a doubtful memory. [276]
Clouds made the astronomical compass useless, buoys had torn free, and his ordinary compass spun near cargo washed from a wrecked battery barge. [299]
Yet the flooded coast road paralleled the old channel, and Jonah remembered Elian teaching Tessa to count the utility poles from their kitchen window. [323]

Tessa understood, angling from pole to pole while current and wind tried to make all loose things gravitate toward the reef. [367]

Jonah froze, seeing Elian's white sleeve vanish under green water, and his paddle slipped from his trembling hand. [403]
Tessa caught it, then laughed once, a merrily melancholic sound so like her mother's that grief briefly became guidance instead of weight. [425]
"She taught us the route," Tessa said, "not the wreck." [435]
Jonah breathed, reclaimed the paddle, and followed the final poles until the line ended where the causeway dropped into open water. [456]
Somewhere ahead, children shouted, but fog scattered their voices, making every direction equally urgent. [470]
Jonah opened Elian's astronomical compass with flickering faith, although its mirrored dial needed one clean glimpse of sun to establish a bearing. [492]
As the gale tore a narrow seam overhead, a pale disk flashed in the mirror, then disappeared. [509]
