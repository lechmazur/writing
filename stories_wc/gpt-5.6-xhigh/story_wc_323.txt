Nine years after discovering aliens, Mara Venn taught navigation in Pelagos, the benthic city buried beneath the lightless Sere Trench. [20]
Her classroom had once been a submarine dock, and black water leaned against its windows like a patient, listening night. [40]
Mara was a navigation instructor because maps had saved lives before the Visitors arrived, although now every familiar map seemed ashamed of its borders. [64]
The alien vessel had opened above the Pacific without warning, unfolding silver sails whose angles pointed toward stars invisible from Earth. [85]
It transmitted no greeting, only a moving diagram, then sank intact and settled three kilometers beyond Pelagos. [102]
Since then, governments had sent pilots into its corridors, and six had vanished, their final transmissions unable to distinguish north from home. [124]
The seventh candidate was Ilyan Rook, Mara's quietest student, who wanted to learn to navigate by starlight because his sister was among those six. [148]
Pelagos offered no stars, so he studied their archived positions while lamps trembled across the ceiling like weak constellations. [167]
He was balancedly curious, asking why each rule worked, yet never touching an unknown control until he understood what failure might cost. [189]
On the eve of his descent, he brought Mara a corroded boat anchor chain link recovered from the alien vessel's threshold. [210]
Its iron was human, stamped with the registry of the trawler that had carried Mara's husband when the first silver sail appeared. [232]
The trawler had vanished that day, and grief had made Mara distrust wonder even as wonder kept choosing her classroom. [252]
She almost withdrew Ilyan from the mission, fearing the ship had placed the link as bait, a precise hook for her consent. [274]
Instead, he laid it over the transmitted diagram, and its worn oval completed the one shape human analysts had dismissed as broken. [296]
Mara saw that the diagram was not a chart of places, but instructions for making direction from boundaries. [314]
She called the method edge geometry: measure the curve that confines you, compare it with distant light, and let each limit reveal scale. [337]
To test it, Mara and Ilyan entered a pressure sphere and crossed the seabed toward the waiting vessel. [355]
Pelagos dwindled behind them, a crown of amber domes above forests of tube worms, beautiful because every pane resisted an ocean eager to crush it. [380]
Inside the alien hull, corridors widened when approached and narrowed when remembered, while windows displayed stars from impossible viewpoints. [399]
Their compass spun, but Ilyan pressed the chain link against each luminous edge and calculated orientation from the changing intersections. [419]
Mara followed his reasoning until walls became meridians, corners became bearings, and the ship's terrible strangeness resolved into navigable form. [439]
At the central chamber, they found the missing trawler suspended inside a clear field, its crew preserved at the instant of disappearance. [461]
Mara's husband stood on deck, older only in sorrowful imagination, one hand still raised toward a sky no longer around him. [482]
Beside the field floated Ilyan's sister and the other pilots, awake but trapped within separate folds of space. [500]
A star map flared beneath them, and Mara understood the Visitors' purpose with a somber marvel that hurt worse than certainty. [521]
They had not invaded; they had gathered travelers lost at fatal boundaries, pausing death until another species could solve the route home. [543]
The chain link was proof, carried through the fold by her husband and returned so human memory could complete alien mathematics. [564]
Yet opening one field would collapse the others unless pilot and instructor could expand the diagram into a path with many exits. [586]
Mara anchored the link at the map's center while Ilyan used edge geometry on every surrounding star window. [604]
He traced not one destination but relationships among limits, holding grief and caution in balance as the luminous lines multiplied. [624]
Mara supplied the final bearing, the remembered angle of her husband's raised hand against the dawn on their last morning together. [645]
The chamber opened like a dark flower, revealing unfolded horizons: ocean floor, trawler deck, Pelagos dock, and a thousand remote suns sharing one rim. [669]
The pilots fell gently into the sphere, the trawler settled onto the seabed, and seven stolen years rushed through Mara's husband's eyes. [691]
He reached for her, but the vessel had begun folding shut, and Mara knew reunion would wait upon survival. [710]
She and Ilyan steered by starlight through the closing passages, not fleeing wonder now, but giving it boundaries they could trust. [731]
When they returned to the benthic city, its amber domes no longer seemed buried; they seemed poised beneath a larger sky. [752]
Mara resumed her lessons beside the recovered trawler, teaching that a horizon was not where knowing ended, but where responsibility began. [773]
