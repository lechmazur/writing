Upon the shimmer of twilight frost, Elian Vale entered the ruined orchard district, lost in centuries of thick fog, carrying an astronomer's broken sextant under one arm and three blank mirrors under the other. [34]
She was the last mirror maker in Vey, a craftswoman known for silvering glass so precisely that a face seemed less reflected than patiently remembered. [59]
Neighbors called her firmly grounded, because grief had not made her superstitious and because she wore boots with lead soles whenever dreams began leaking into the streets. [86]
Yet she had come to capture a runaway dream, and the dream belonged to her dead son, Orin. [104]
On his final fevered night, Orin had dreamed of glass mountains rising beyond the orchard, transparent summits ringing under stars, then drawn their jagged line before Elian watched that impossible range fade from his cooling eyes. [140]
Three nights later, the mountains appeared in every mirror she made, each time farther away. [155]
Then customers slept before the glass and never woke, while pale footprints wandered north into the fog. [172]
Elian understood that Orin's dream was running from reflection to reflection, searching for a horizon broad enough to become real. [192]
At the district's center, dead pear trees arched over a frozen observatory whose dome had split like an eggshell. [211]
Here, Astronomer Senn had once mapped stars by bouncing their light among orchard ponds, before the fog erased sky from earth and road from grave. [236]
The sextant she carried, salvaged from this rubble years ago, had one snapped arm and a cloudy sight, but seven notches on its brass arc matched Orin's seven summits. [265]
Elian set her mirrors between three trees and fitted shards from the smallest into the sextant, making its missing arm a corridor of reflections. [289]
To recreate the instrument's lost angle, she needed two observers, one fixed in waking and one moving in dream. [308]
She had brought no companion, but when frost silvered the roots, Orin appeared in the western mirror, barefoot and nine years old. [330]
"Do not cage me," he said, "because the sleepers are holding onto me; they fear their own mornings." [348]
Elian's hands shook, not because the apparition frightened her, but because his voice had forgotten the rasp of illness. [367]
She wanted to pull him through, shut him in polished glass, and keep one room in the world untouched by death. [388]
Instead, she planted her lead soles in the frozen soil and asked what the mountains needed. [404]
"A sunrise," Orin answered, "but there is no east here anymore." [415]
Fog pressed against the triangular mirrors, and within them the glass mountains leaned close, sharp, silent, blue as buried bells. [435]
Elian saw then that Senn's device had not measured the boundary between heaven and earth; it had maintained it, and the break had begun erasing boundaries among memory, sleep, and place. [466]
Repairing it would imprison every wandering dream again, including Orin's, but turning its geometry inside out might free the sleepers. [486]
It might also erase the boundary that kept Elian herself from dissolving into longing. [500]
"Hold my rhythm," she told her son, and through synchronized breathing they began: her breath clouding the real glass, his clearing the reflected pane. [524]
With each shared exhalation, she rotated the brass arc, aligning a star visible only above his mountains with frost shining only beneath her feet. [548]
The sextant hummed. [551]
Pear branches flashed transparent; roots showed constellations; the observatory opened downward into a lucid sky. [566]
All across Vey, sleepers gasped awake as their pale footprints rushed back and fitted themselves beneath warm blankets. [584]
Orin smiled, but the mountain behind him cracked, revealing morning like molten gold. [597]
"Come home," Elian whispered. [601]
"I am home," he said, and touched the place where her palm met his across the mirror. [618]
She could capture him by reversing the final angle, yet his eyes held neither fever nor fear, only the widening light of a dream completing itself. [644]
Elian let the sextant fall. [649]
"I cannot keep you by making the world smaller," she said. [660]
She breathed out once more, alone, and the mirrors shattered without falling, their pieces suspended like bright snow. [678]
The fog streamed through those countless openings, no longer a wall but a river, and east returned as a red line beyond the trees. [702]
By dawn, the orchard remained ruined, but every trunk cast a clean shadow, and frozen pears chimed in the thaw. [722]
Elian carried the broken sextant home, not to mend it, but to hang above her workbench as proof that some instruments succeed by opening what they were built to divide. [751]
In her next mirror, she silvered no border, and whoever looked into it saw both a firmly grounded face and, far behind it, glass mountains welcoming the sun. [779]
