After rain, the cobalt dock shone like a blade laid flat across the sleeping canal. [15]

At its farthest post stood Orin, the silent canal guardian, listening with one hand on the sluice wheel. [33]

Blue lamps trembled in puddles, warehouse shutters dripped, and no boatman dared disturb the stillwater tension beneath the pilings. [53]

Orin had not spoken since the canal took his son, but grief had sharpened his attention until another person's fear felt almost audible. [76]

That night, Lysa, the lockmaster's young apprentice, came running barefoot through the runoff, clutching a dental mirror. [93]

"My brother went below to open--" she began, then stopped when harbor constables appeared at the bridge. [110]

Everything that followed would happen within the edge of a half-spoken secret, before accusation could close around it. [128]

Orin saw mud under her nails, rope burns on her palms, and the terror of someone who had helped with forbidden work. [150]

He did not demand the rest; silent empathy was his only oath, and her unfinished confession was enough. [168]

He lowered the signal-house shutter twice, raised the eastern pair once, and left a narrow bar of lamplight between them. [188]

Lysa knew his language: by creating patterns with shutters, Orin could warn, question, forgive, or summon help without betraying anyone. [208]

She answered by angling the dental mirror toward the bar, flashing three quick sparks upon the wet boards: lower star chamber. [229]

The chamber was a sealed cistern beneath the canal, where old glass lenses once fed reflected constellations into the city's water. [250]

Since smoke had hidden the sky, something lightless had gathered there, pressing nightly against the gates and learning hunger from human dread. [272]

Lysa's brother Tavin had gone down to restore the lenses illegally, hoping stars might return clean water to their fevered district. [293]

Now a black thread curled through the dock seams, and from below came one measured knock, then two frantic ones. [313]

The constables heard it, drew their hooks, and ordered Orin to flood the lower chamber before the darkness reached open water. [334]

Obedience would drown Tavin; delay might loose the thing into every canal, where moonless windows already waited like mouths. [353]

Orin turned the wheel halfway, apparently complying, spiralingly calm as his free hand circled across the signal levers. [371]

One by one, the shutters opened, and their blue-lit rectangles began to weave the pattern his son had named the Fisher's Crown. [393]

Orin had kept the chamber not merely to contain darkness, but to teach darkness the value of stars. [411]

Each night he showed it order instead of threat, distance instead of abandonment, brightness that did not wound what it revealed. [432]

The darkness had never answered, yet Orin understood long silences and did not mistake them for emptiness. [449]

Tonight one point was missing because the central lens had cracked, so the Crown looked like a trap instead of a shelter. [471]

Orin held out his palm, and Lysa surrendered the dental mirror, the tiny tool Tavin always used for inspecting corroded gears. [492]

He fixed it in the final shutter's hinge, catching a cobalt lamp and sending its cold spark through the floor grating. [513]

Below, the black thread recoiled, then rose in a soundless column that made the constables grip their hooks and forget breathing. [534]

Inside it, dim as a thought nearly lost, Tavin floated against the locked hatch. [548]

Lysa stepped forward, but Orin barred her gently and closed two shutters, turning the Crown into the sign for grief shared. [569]

He touched his heart, pointed into the column, and opened those shutters again, offering his own loss without demanding repayment. [589]

The blackness loosened as if unclenching a fist, laying Tavin alive upon the dock while rainwater silvered his face. [608]

Then it folded beneath the pilings, not defeated, but carrying the reflected Crown into depths where no sky had ever reached. [629]

The constables lowered their hooks, unable to condemn a rescue they had witnessed but could not explain. [646]

Tavin woke coughing and whispered the rest of Lysa's secret: they had meant to steal light for people the city preferred unseen. [668]

Orin answered by opening every shutter, and dawn, pale behind the smoke, entered the signal house in long blue bands. [688]

Across the canal, other keepers copied his pattern, window after window, until the dark water held a sky made by human hands. [710]

Orin remained silent, but Lysa understood: some secrets survived not by staying hidden, but by becoming constellations everyone agreed to guard. [731]
