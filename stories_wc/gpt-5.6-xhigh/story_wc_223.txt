During the interregnum after Queen Sera died childless, Mara Venn earned six coppers a day pretending to survive disasters. [19]
She was a crisis actor in the royal Whispering Gallery, where curved walls carried a murmur completely around the marble dome. [40]
Each week, rival ministers rehearsed coronation bombings, poisonings, stampedes, and speeches of reassurance while the empty throne waited upstairs. [59]
Mara accepted the work because staged terror exposed unguarded kindness: a guard shielding a servant, or a cook offering water before orders. [81]
She meant to become the historian of small gestures, not another clerk embalming the victories of kings. [98]

On the morning the Council would choose a regent, she found a chessboard centered beneath the gallery's oculus, missing all the pawns. [120]
The remaining ivory nobles stood in an elegant siege around a black king, and thin silver cords ran from their bases into the board. [144]
Mara touched nothing; noticing before touching was another small gesture, and often the one that kept actors alive. [162]
Above her, funeral mirrors turned toward the sun, casting the nomadic penumbra across the chamber. [177]
This wandering belt of cool darkness had followed the queen's coffin through every province, letting mourners view its radiance without going blind. [199]
Now, with the mirrors halted over the capital, the shadow circled the dome like an orphan seeking a home. [218]

The ministers entered at noon, jeweled and suspicious, followed by servants forbidden to speak during deliberations. [234]
Chancellor Ors ordered Mara to perform the planned seizure, then smiled kindly as he buckled a leather gag around her mouth. [255]
"Authenticity," he explained, although no rehearsal had included a gag. [265]
Mara fell on cue, and at that instant every door slammed, the oculus blazed white, and the chess pieces began sinking. [286]
Silver cords tightened beneath the floor, folding away the mirror vanes that made the traveling shadow. [302]
A blade of concentrated sunlight swept after the shrinking penumbra, igniting robes and blistering marble. [317]
The rehearsal screams became real, and Mara, no longer an imitation victim, understood Ors's design: kill every rival and emerge as rescuer. [339]

He alone wore a mirrored cloak, but he had misjudged the servants, who dragged burning ministers into the narrow band of shade. [361]
Their forbidden hands, not princely speeches, held the realm together. [371]
Gagged, Mara pointed to the pawnless board, then traced letters with her finger on the dusty floor: WALK THE WORD. [391]
The gallery's deaf founder had devised a pattern of walking that spells words, using tiled paths to turn footfalls into commands. [412]
In old drills, UNBIND released the mirror rigging, but the command required six walkers moving at once. [429]

Mara rose into the moving shadow, benevolently ruthless now: she shoved dukes aside and chose the calmest servants with hard, precise gestures. [451]
She kicked Ors behind the knees when he blocked them, spared no glance for his rank, and used his mirrored cloak to wrap a burned maid. [477]
As the six walked the shapes of U, N, B, I, N, and D, their footfalls whispered the command around the dome. [499]
"Unbind," the walls answered in the queen's recorded voice. [508]

The chessboard split open, revealing sixteen pawn sockets, each attached to a knotted silver cord. [523]
Mara and the servants plunged their hands into the holes and hauled, filling the board with living pawns the killer had neglected to count. [547]
Great knots released overhead; mirror vanes flowered, and the fierce beam dissolved into ordinary afternoon. [562]
The nomadic penumbra loosened, crossed the ministers one final time, then wandered out through the western arch as softly as mourning. [583]

Ors tried to claim that his emergency plan had succeeded, but every whispering curve repeated the maid's quiet testimony. [602]
By sunset, the Council had chosen no regent; instead, cooks, guards, ministers, and sweepers formed a temporary chamber until an election. [623]
The interregnum endured, less elegant and more honest than any coronation rehearsal. [635]

Mara spent three weeks bandaged, learning that real pain had no cue, no applause, and no clean ending. [653]
When she returned to the gallery, she did not write first of Ors, the mirrors, or the council's decree. [672]
She recorded the servant who steadied another's elbow, the guard who opened his scorched hand, and the maid who shared the cloak. [694]
Last, she described the shadow departing, leaving brightness not as triumph, but as luminous absence around those small, saving gestures. [714]
