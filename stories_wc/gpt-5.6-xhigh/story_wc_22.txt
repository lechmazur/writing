<story>After the reef died, the sea withdrew from Gannet Strait as if ashamed, exposing cannon ribs, helmets, and a road no map remembered. [23]
At twilight, that road became a thread between walls of dark water, a narrow vastness crossed only by Mara Venn, the gloom-hill coachman. [46]
Her black coach carried neither passenger nor coffin, but a collection of dried seed pods cushioned in battlefield wool. [65]
Each pod had descended from a plant that once grew beside a soldier's last letter: poppy, vetch, iris, sea kale, or rose. [87]
Mara meant to sow them across Marrow Field, where two drowned armies still faced each other beneath a handspan of tide. [108]
She wanted no monument there; she wanted to let a garden reclaim a battlefield, root by rusted root. [126]
Yet the salt mud was sterile, poisoned by powder, copper, and the white skeleton dust of the reef. [144]
To fertilize it, Mara needed the silt sealed under Concord Jetty, and taking that silt was forbidden. [161]
The jetty had bloomed since spring, its old timbers furred with blue bindweed and moonflowers that opened at twilight. [180]
Beneath it lay the submerged concord, twelve lead tablets dropped into the strait when the kings ended the Salt War. [200]
Their dead languages promised that neither shore would dig the common seabed, lest hunger and war return together. [218]
Mara spoke dead languages, a talent learned from hauling scholars up Gloom Hill and ghosts down from it. [236]
She had read the tablets through clear water and knew the prohibition ended with a clause everyone else had forgotten. [256]
When the reef bears no color, the clause said, the seabed shall feed the peace, provided winged witnesses consent. [275]
The reef bore no color now, but Magistrate Harl, keeper of the jetty, laughed at witnesses with feathers. [293]
He barred Mara's shovel with his ceremonial spear and warned that one broken tablet would reopen every grave in the strait. [314]
Then the terns began crying above the blooming jetty, wheeling in the violet air as moonflowers unfastened their pale mouths. [334]
Most heard quarrels, but Mara had driven the coast so long that she understood bird calls as clearly as weather bells. [355]
The terns called of silver fry trapped inland, of nests abandoned on the dead reef, and of worms moving below the eastern piles. [378]
Their eldest bird repeated a three-note phrase that matched the concord's word for consent. [392]
Harl lowered his spear, not convinced, until hundreds of terns settled upon the twelve tablet markers and sang the same phrase. [413]
Mara translated aloud, first in the vanished court speech, then in the fisher tongue Harl's mother had used. [431]
"Open where the worms breathe, return what was buried, and give us islands." [444]
Harl's fear did not leave him, but it changed shape, becoming duty rather than refusal. [459]
Together they pried up the eastern planks, careful to leave the marked tablets sleeping under water. [475]
The worms had honeycombed a pocket above the concord, filling it with black silt, fish bones, gull droppings, and rotted weed. [496]
By understanding the bird calls, Mara knew which silt could be taken without cracking lead or stealing the terns' nesting ground. [517]
They loaded twelve barrows, one for each promise, and mixed the silt with reef dust in the coach's empty passenger box. [538]
Night gathered, compressing sea and sky around the lantern-lit road, while the horses stepped between drowned gun carriages like priests. [558]
At Marrow Field, Mara scattered the living compost over the poison and pressed every seed pod into its proper depth. [578]
Poppies went among muskets, vetch around boots, iris beside the officers, and thornless roses along the line where both ranks had fallen. [600]
Last, she planted sea kale on raised mounds, making the islands the terns had requested. [615]
Rain came before dawn, soft enough not to uncover bones, steady enough to carry nourishment down. [631]
Through winter, Harl guarded the field, and Mara drove refuse from both shores to fertilize it under the birds' sharp supervision. [652]
By spring, roots had netted the swords below, stems lifted helmets, and the first tern chicks hid within flowering kale. [672]
People from the former enemy shores walked the old road carrying water together, because flowers needed hands more than history needed hatred. [693]
Mara watched from her coach as Marrow Field vanished beneath red, blue, white, and green. [708]
Far behind her, the concord remained submerged, not broken but fulfilled, while the living reef began again in petals above the dead. [730]</story>
