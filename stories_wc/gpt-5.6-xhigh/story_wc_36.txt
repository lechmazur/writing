After the Guardianship ended, Mara remained its last relic keeper, driven less by loyalty than by an unanswered mistake. [19]
Seven years earlier, her order had tried weaving between worlds and lost a mountain village, three guardians, and Mara's brother. [39]
The official record called the disaster sabotage, sealed the surviving relics, and forbade questions beneath penalty of exile. [57]
Exile suited Mara because she wanted no pardon; she wanted to harvest insights from failure before someone repeated it. [76]
That purpose drew her into the Thunder-echo Range, where storms spoke for hours inside red canyons after clouds had gone. [96]
Yet on the morning she entered, the range was avalanchingly quiet, as though immense silence had buried every loose stone. [116]
Mara followed a dry river whose banks shifted whenever she blinked, one bank belonging to autumn and the other to spring. [137]
Maps failed in such seams, so she navigated by arranging driftwood as directional markers, pointing each bleached branch toward home. [157]
If a marker vanished, she knew the worlds had rewoven behind her, and she laid another without surrendering her pace. [177]
At noon, she found the drowned village suspended above a gorge, its houses wavering like reflections without water. [195]
Her brother Iven waited on an inverted roof, unchanged except for silver cracks running from his eyes to his collar. [215]
'You came late,' he said, though his lips moved a heartbeat after the words. [229]
'I came prepared,' Mara answered, refusing the hope that sharpened his familiar face. [242]
Beneath the roof hung the order's cedar case, chained to a door that opened upon a starless summer sky. [261]
Inside rested the gilded reed flute, the tool their founders had used to loosen reality and braid safe passages. [280]
Mara cut the chain, steadied the case across her knees, and began to uncase the instrument. [296]
Iven flinched, and far below, unseen boulders answered with a grinding that never became sound. [311]
'Do not play the closing measure,' he warned. [319]
It was the measure he had played during the disaster, after Mara abandoned her harmony to pull a child from the seam. [341]
For seven years she had blamed that rescue, believing her broken duet had trapped everyone between seasons. [358]
Now Iven pointed to the flute's blackened lowest hole, and the silver cracks widened along his hands. [375]
'I finished alone,' he said. [380]
'The passage resisted, so I forced it shut, exactly as the Guardianship taught us.' [394]
Mara heard what the official record had concealed: not sabotage, but obedience made desperate by shame. [410]
Their craft had failed because it treated worlds as ropes to be knotted, never as neighbors whose doors required consent. [430]
She raised the flute, but instead of sealing the seam, she breathed the unfinished harmony she had left years ago. [450]
One clear note entered the canyon hush and returned bearing rain, birdsong, funeral bells, and a child's delighted gasp. [469]
Mara did not command those voices; she paused between notes, allowing each world to answer and alter the melody. [488]
The hanging village descended grain by grain until its streets settled beside the river, wet with seven years of deferred weather. [509]
Iven's roof righted itself, but he remained translucent, held together by the last echo of his closing measure. [527]
'Can you bring me through?' he asked. [534]
Mara wanted to promise, yet the answering melody carried his truth: he was not a prisoner, but a bridge already spent. [555]
'No,' she whispered, 'but I can stop calling your sacrifice a crime.' [567]
Relief softened him more completely than rescue could have done, and he scattered into warm rain across the restored roofs. [587]
By dusk, villagers emerged from their homes carrying lamps, bewildered that grief had aged outside while they had not. [606]
Mara showed them the driftwood arrows, now rooted and budding, and told them the route would hold only if freely followed. [627]
Some chose the lower valley, some stayed, and their choosing kept both riverbanks in the same season. [644]
Mara wrapped the flute in her old keeper's sash, leaving the cedar case open beneath the ordinary sky. [662]
The Guardianship had guarded relics by hiding failures, but she would preserve this one by teaching it without excuse. [681]
When thunder finally returned, it moved softly from ridge to ridge, no longer an order, only one world answering another. [701]
