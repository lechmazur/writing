<story>In the canyon of endless echoes, towers of bone rose from the red floor like fingers testing an empty sky. [20]
Between them walked Sera, the copper-veined storyteller, whose skin showed bright threads wherever old mine dust had entered her blood. [40]
She spoke little, but she was quietly articulate, choosing each surviving word as if setting a cup beneath a final drop of rain. [63]
Her people had once named every grain, ridge, polish, crack, and temperature a hand could feel, then sung those names to map the canyon. [87]
Now that language was dying with its last elder, Oru, who lay fevered inside the lowest bone tower. [105]
Sera had spent years beside him, recording tales across the death of a language, yet marks on hide could preserve meaning, not touch. [128]
Her impossible purpose was to translate texture into sound, so children not yet born might hear how vanished things had felt. [149]
For that, Oru said, she needed the Mourning Star, a flawed diamond rumored to be cursed, locked beneath the tallest tower. [170]
Miners claimed its cloudy fracture trapped the voice of anyone who touched it, leaving the victim able to remember speech but unable to speak. [194]
Sera entered before dawn, pressing her palms along walls polished by generations, and found the diamond mounted above a throat-shaped shaft. [215]
The shaft was the tunnel of echoes, a natural passage winding under every tower and returning the smallest noise in a thousand altered voices. [239]
When she lifted the gem, silence closed around her mouth. [249]
The curse was real. [253]
Yet her copper veins warmed, and through her fingertips the diamond's broken inner planes felt like a storm trying to pronounce itself. [275]
Unable to call for help, Sera climbed outside and saw canyon swifts pouring between the bone towers. [292]
Their cries multiplied below, but their wheeling bodies did not follow the returning sounds; they followed invisible currents born from the tunnel's hidden mouths. [316]
She remembered Oru teaching that birds read air as fingers read cloth. [328]
Thus Sera began to chart the passage through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight. [345]
A tightening spiral marked rough stone, a sudden fan marked glassy stone, and paired dives revealed opposing grooves where air trembled like plucked wire. [369]
She spent the day moving clay shutters over the tunnel mouths, shaping each current until the swifts drew distinct signs above each tower. [392]
Then she descended with the diamond and fitted it into a narrow socket at the tunnel's heart. [409]
By sliding samples across its facets, she could harness the echoes: sandstone became a low rasp, salt a hiss, wool a warm hum, and bone a clear knock. [437]
The flaw was not damage but a prism for vibration, splitting each touch into notes the canyon could carry. [456]
But every test stole another word from Sera's mind as well as from her tongue. [471]
First she lost the word for diamond, then bird, then Oru. [482]
She ran to the elder while she could still remember why. [493]
Oru listened as she tapped his blanket, his wooden cup, the tower wall, and finally the skin over his failing heart against the gem. [517]
The tunnel answered with four textures, and above them the flock formed four remembered signs. [532]
Oru understood her design and smiled, though Sera no longer knew the name for smiling. [547]
He placed her hand on his throat, then on the diamond, offering his last voice willingly. [563]
His final breath entered the fracture, but instead of one man's speech, the tunnel released the whole old language stored in his memory. [586]
Words rolled through the canyon, struck the tuned shutters, and returned as rasps, chimes, hisses, and hums matched to the swifts' living script. [609]
The bone towers sang until sunset, while children laid hands on stone and cloth and learned each sound by watching the sky. [631]
At last Oru died, and the spoken language died with him, but its knowledge crossed into another form. [649]
Sera's voice never returned, nor did the words the diamond had taken, yet she remained the people's storyteller. [667]
She told histories by brushing surfaces across the Mourning Star while listeners watched birds braid meanings between the towers. [686]
Years later, no one called the diamond cursed, although every teller surrendered something to it, because translation, they knew, was not rescue without loss. [710]
And whenever Sera touched its flawed face, the endless echoes carried Oru's farewell in the sound of a heartbeat felt through skin. [732]</story>
