In Cinderwell, where furnaces warmed streets carved under a dead volcano and buried waterworks powered every public clock, Elian Voss repaired timepieces respectable makers declared beyond saving. [27]

Everyone called him the broken-clock tinkerer, partly for his trade and partly because, since his sister Nara vanished, he measured life by failures. [50]

He kept schedules precise enough to insult chance, yet secretly apprenticed himself to chance, determined to befriend uncertainty in planning rather than fear it. [74]

That resolve was tested when seven public clockworks, brought from seven districts, arrived stopped at 3:17, each packed with gray dust. [95]

"Ash," said Admiral Seln, laying an empty velvet case on Elian's bench, "and beneath the ashes, perhaps my lost compass rose." [116]

The rose was not a drawing but a brass star, once set inside the admiral's compass, whose needle had guided surveyors through Cinderwell's unmapped foundations. [141]

Seln had lost it three nights earlier, when Nara disappeared while investigating tremors beneath the abandoned botanical quarter. [159]

"Now, when coincidence becomes pattern," Elian thought, "it is either a warning or a machine." [174]

He opened the seven cases and found, tangled around every balance wheel, the serrated seed of a windfern that grew only in one neglected greenhouse. [199]

Seln proposed soldiers and a measured descent, but Elian noticed the ash smelled freshly wet, so he chose rain gear, wire, and no timetable. [223]

"If Nara had followed a rigid plan," he said, "someone expecting it could have trapped her." [239]

At dusk, he and Seln advanced through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse, though every pane was closed and no earthly wind moved. [263]

The ferns circled a floor grate in gusting spirals, then went still whenever the distant clocks chimed the quarter hour. [283]

Elian felt uncertainly certain: the air pulsed upward on a hidden mechanism's breath, and the stopped clocks had recorded its cycle. [304]

Below the grate, iron steps descended past root-webbed brick until the roar of a subterranean waterfall swallowed speech. [322]

Lantern light revealed black water plunging through a cavern and, on a ledge beneath the spray, a gear house buried in ash. [344]

The bridge to it had collapsed, leaving only a chain that whipped above the torrent. [359]

Seln ordered retreat, but Elian saw windfern leaves falling from his coat, watched their flight, and decided to improvise. [378]

He tied his wire to the chain, weighted it with Seln's lantern, and let the waterfall's breathing swing the hook onto the far railing. [402]

They crossed one at a time, trusting a rhythm Elian could predict only half a heartbeat ahead. [419]

The gear house door bore a star-shaped socket, and ash around it held two sets of fingerprints: Nara's small prints and another man's broad thumbs. [444]

Elian looked at Seln's hands, and the admiral stopped pretending surprise. [455]

He confessed that Nara had discovered his secret project to divert the falls toward poor Furnace Ward whenever upper tunnels flooded. [476]

She stole the compass rose that keyed the gates, entered the gear house, and vowed to block his diversion until he exposed the scheme. [500]

He wrenched at the rose in the outer socket; a tremor cracked it, sealed the door, and let shame persuade him she was dead. [524]

Rising ash in the clocks finally suggested that the machinery, and perhaps Nara, remained alive. [539]

Elian knelt at the socket and brushed aside the damp ash, not randomly, but along the twin arcs traced by the door's vibration. [562]

Beneath the ashes lay the admiral's lost compass rose, cracked at one point yet still bright. [578]

No careful repair was possible, so Elian splinted the crack with a clock spring, wrapped the star in wire, and pressed it home. [601]

The door opened, but so did three sluices, and the cavern shook as water climbed the ledge. [618]

Inside, pale but living, Nara hung from a brake lever, using her weight to keep the full torrent from Furnace Ward. [639]

"The teeth are stripped," she gasped, "so do not time it; listen to the falls." [654]

Elian heard no fixed tempo, only surges, pauses, and the flutter of leaves still clinging to his sleeves. [672]

He fed broken clock wheels into the stripped gear one by one, each a temporary tooth chosen for the next uncertain load. [694]

At his command, Seln took Nara's place on the lever, accepting that rescue and confession would share the same consequence. [714]

Together the siblings shifted the gate between surges until the waterfall turned toward an empty magma cistern and settled into thunder. [735]

By dawn, guards had arrested Seln on his own testimony, while physicians carried Nara upward through the greenhouse. [753]

Elian returned the repaired rose as evidence and placed the seven stopped clocks in his window without fixing their hands. [773]

They marked 3:17, the minute he learned that uncertainty was not failed certainty, but the living interval in which courage could choose. [795]
