<story>In the murmur of half-remembered dreams, Mara Venn returned to the abandoned spaceport repair bay where her brother had vanished. [20]
She was a sensory map maker, trained to chart places by taste, pressure, heat, echo, and the private colors that sounds painted behind her eyes. [45]
Ordinary cartographers had declared the bay empty, but Mara sensed a cold seam hanging between the dismantled engines. [63]
It smelled of rain on hot copper, exactly as Ilyan's jacket had smelled the night he disappeared. [80]
For seven years she had come whenever dreams loosened the walls of waking, carrying fresh paper and his last possession. [100]
The possession was a green glass shard, supposedly broken from a runway signal lamp, though no lamp in the port had ever used that shade. [125]
When she laid it against her tongue, the shard tasted not green but unfinished apologies. [140]

Mara wanted to discover the weight of a withheld truth, because Ilyan's final message had said only, "Do not follow me until you know what I hid." [167]
Her instruments could not weigh truth, so she built a scale from sensation: grief in one pan, memory in the other, and the shard as its needle. [194]
At midnight, the scale tipped toward the cold seam, and the dead repair bay unfolded like cloth. [211]
Beyond it stood the same bay, bright and staffed, under a sky with three small moons. [227]
A younger Ilyan worked beneath a shuttle, laughing with a Mara who had never lost him. [243]
The sight nearly pulled her across, but the shard burned her palm, warning that this was not her brother. [262]

She understood then that the seam was weaving between worlds, crossing possibilities as a loom crosses threads. [279]
Its rhythm was cyclically spontaneous: every thirteen heartbeats it opened somewhere new, yet no sequence ever repeated. [296]
Mara mapped each opening by sensation rather than sight: cinnamon meant a world without Earth, numb teeth meant war, and violet bells meant Ilyan had passed nearby. [323]
She waited through twelve false worlds before violet bells rang inside her bones. [336]
Then she stepped through. [340]

The bay on the other side lay flooded to her knees, and stars swam beneath the water as if space had leaked indoors. [363]
An old woman sat on a hoist above the flood, wearing Ilyan's jacket and Mara's face. [379]
"You are late," the woman said, "which is fortunate, because he hoped you would be." [394]
Mara raised the shard. [398]
"Where is Ilyan?" [401]
"Scattered," her older self replied. [406]

She explained that their brother had discovered the seams while repairing a navigation core and learned that every crossing stole mass from the traveler's home world. [432]
A footprint of dust here became a mountain elsewhere; a living body could unbalance suns. [447]
Ilyan had crossed once by accident, then spent years learning to redirect the damage through parallel universes already empty of life. [468]
Each rescue split him among destinations, until no single world held enough of him to return. [484]
"That was his withheld truth," the woman said. [492]
"He left you uncertainty because certainty would have made you chase him." [504]

Mara put grief on her inner scale and felt its familiar drag. [516]
Yet the woman's words possessed too little weight; they rang hollow beside the shard's taste of apology. [533]
"You are not me," Mara said. [539]
"You are the part of Ilyan still trying to frighten me home." [551]
The false Mara smiled sadly, and her face dissolved into water. [562]

Mara flung the green glass shard into the water, not as a weapon but as a coordinate. [579]
Its impact chimed violet, locating a thin remnant of Ilyan woven below the illusion. [593]
The world tore open, revealing hundreds of repair bays layered together, and in every one a different fragment of her brother reached toward her. [617]
Mara could not pull them home without burdening her world, but a map maker need not move what she could connect. [638]
She pressed both hands into the seam and charted Ilyan's fragments by their shared sensory memories: burnt sugar from childhood, engine oil under his nails, thunder tasted through a loose tooth. [669]
Using the shard as a shuttle, she wove those coordinates through the empty universes he had chosen. [686]
She did not restore his body; she gave his scattered mind a path along which its pieces could speak without crossing. [707]

His voice filled every bay at once. [714]
"Mara, the truth was never that I left you," he said. [725]
"It was that I trusted you to find a lighter way." [736]

The seams closed at dawn, returning Mara to the abandoned bay with the shard clear and warm in her hand. [756]
She marked the place on no public chart, but whenever forgotten dreams murmured, she listened there with Ilyan, keeping the worlds balanced by sharing truth instead of bodies. [784]</story>
