<story>
Liora Vale, natural history illustrator to the skyward menagerie, had spent three years drawing creatures that vanished whenever anyone named them. [21]
She worked in a city where a rooftop canal system wove between towers, carrying rainwater, messenger boats, and silver eels above the clouds. [44]
Once each season, the mountain giant beneath the foundations slept from dusk until dawn, and during that nap the canals flowed uphill. [66]
Liora would search across the giant's nap to find the place where echoes are born, which her missing brother Orren had sought before her. [90]
All he had left was his infinity symbol pendant, found hooked around the beak of a stone gull at the canal's highest lock. [113]
The pendant was no keepsake; when turned sideways against moonlight, its two loops showed the same moment approaching from opposite directions. [134]
At dusk, the giant yawned, windows rattled, and Liora launched her narrow skiff with Orren's field journal beneath her paints. [154]
His final sketch depicted a bird made of empty staves, its heartbeat written as an interrupted cadence that promised resolution yet refused to land. [178]
Under it, he had written, "Follow what cannot finish." [187]
As the canals reversed, every tower bell rang backward, swallowing its own note, and a pale feather appeared in Liora's wake. [208]
She painted it before it could vanish, mixing pearl with soot, and the wet image tugged her boat toward the eastern aqueduct. [230]
There, canal keepers had barred the channel with a brass cage whose bars trembled in a rhythm of almost, almost, never. [251]
Inside fluttered the stave bird, and each unfinished beat erased a second from the surrounding world. [267]
Drops hung above the water, a keeper's blink remained half closed, and Liora understood that the creature sculpts time itself with its frantic wings. [291]
Orren stood beyond the cage, older than memory and motionless except for his eyes, trapped inside a second the bird had stolen. [313]
"Liora," he mouthed without sound, and pointed first to the lock, then to the darkening sky. [329]
The cage's plaque warned that opening it would release all captured instants at once, aging the city by a century before sunrise. [351]
Yet leaving it shut would feed the bird's broken song until the giant woke, when every stolen second, including Orren's, would dissolve. [373]
Liora studied the pendant and saw that its crossing was not a knot but a narrow bridge between what had happened and what might happen. [398]
Orren's journal described the bird as an echo before its voice, forever hunting the final note that would make it real. [419]
She could not manufacture that note, but she could steal its arrival from the future. [434]
By borrowing time from the giant's coming wakefulness through one loop of the pendant, she drew dawn's first bell into the other. [456]
The bell sounded in miniature, bright and complete, answering the bird's interrupted cadence. [469]
For one dangerous instant, the bars became reeds, the canal became glass, and every painted creature in Liora's portfolio opened its eyes. [491]
She used the borrowed instant to uncage the bird, turning the key while ordinary time remained too astonished to object. [511]
The creature flew through her chest without harm, carrying the completed bell note into the chamber where Orren waited. [530]
The cadence shattered his prison into winged seconds, which settled on the canal as ripples instead of years. [548]
Orren stumbled forward, no older than on the day he vanished, but his joy faltered when the bird circled them and cried with his voice. [573]
"Every echo begins here," it said, and the eastern tower unfolded like an ear around a hidden well of darkness. [593]
From that well rose all sounds seeking second lives: dead children's laughter, vows forgotten by lovers, thunder from seas below the clouds. [615]
Liora understood why Orren had come; their mother's last song was among them, still missing the final phrase illness had taken. [636]
He reached toward it, but Liora caught his hand, for the well offered no sound without taking a living moment in exchange. [658]
They had crossed the city to recover what was lost, yet she now saw that an echo was not a return, only loss learning to travel. [684]
Together they let the song rise unfinished, and its open cadence guided every captive echo out across the canals. [703]
As dawn neared, the bird settled into Liora's blank page, no longer empty staves but a small brown creature with a red throat. [726]
The borrowed bell returned to morning exactly as the giant stirred, so the nap ended neither early nor late. [745]
Before the giant fully woke, Liora wrote Origin Bird beneath her illustration, and it did not vanish; Orren added: "What finishes is not always what heals." [771]
