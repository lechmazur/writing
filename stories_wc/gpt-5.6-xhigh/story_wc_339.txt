Six months after finding Amelia Earhart's plane beneath the Nikumaroro reef, Mara Venn descended toward the forgotten sunken ship library called Orison. [22]

Orison had vanished in 1937 with twelve thousand forbidden books, and the world remembered it only because Earhart's recovered log mentioned its last transmission. [46]

At three hundred meters, Mara's lamp found brass letters, green as old grief, above a doorway furred with anemones. [65]

She entered through the ruptured map room, carrying a snow globe in the padded pocket over her heart. [83]

It had belonged to her brother Julian, who had drowned five years earlier while pushing her toward the rescue line. [103]

Her own heart had stopped beside his, which was how Mara came to be on a first-name basis with Death. [123]

He called himself Silas, and now he swam beside her without disturbing the silt. [137]

"You took your time, Mara," he said through no channel her helmet could identify. [151]

Quietly analytic even when haunted, she checked oxygen, tether, depth, and pulse before answering. [165]

"I came to understand the physics of letting go," she said, "not to surrender." [179]

The library waited below, its galleries sealed behind thick crystal doors that the pressure had somehow spared. [196]

Inside, ranks of books floated in glass-fronted cases, their swollen pages opening and closing like pale gills. [213]

A wire song trembled through the hull: three descending notes, a pause, then a woman's exhausted voice naming coordinates. [232]

It was the signal Earhart had noted, copied, and tried to answer on the morning she disappeared. [249]

The coordinates had brought Mara here; the repeated phrase had brought Silas. [261]

"We have saved every ending," the transmission whispered. [269]

At the catalog desk, thousands of copper filaments emerged from index drawers and converged upon a black transmitter. [287]

Silk labels tied to them bore names of passengers, authors, sailors, and strangers whose radios had once crossed Orison's wake. [307]

Julian's name glimmered on a thread newer than the rest. [317]

Mara's hand tightened around the snow globe, and its white flakes rose against gravity. [331]

From the transmitter came Julian's laugh, followed by the unfinished sentence he had spoken before the wave closed over them. [351]

"If you get back, tell Mom..." [357]

Mara had spent five years believing those six words were a command she had failed to complete. [374]

Now she noted that each syllable arrived on a different frequency, stitched from recordings made years apart. [391]

"That is not Julian," she said. [397]

"No," Silas replied, almost kindly. [402]

"It is the shape your refusal gives the noise." [411]

Beneath the desk, Mara found the chief librarian's final note laminated between two title cards. [426]

The machine preserves information by braiding a dying signal with any listener who cannot release it, the note explained. [445]

To stop the broadcast, one must separate memory from demand by untangling threads, never cutting them. [461]

Cut wires would erase the archive, while knots kept the dead rehearsing whatever the living needed to hear. [479]

Mara followed Julian's filament through a snarled century of voices, loosening each crossing according to phase and harmonic decay. [498]

The work demanded the same patience he had shown when teasing sea grass from her hair as children. [516]

Silas shelved each loosened label inside an empty drawer. [539]

"Conservation is not captivity," Mara murmured, watching the globe's flakes begin to settle. [552]

Only the oldest knot remained, binding Orison's distress call to Earhart's unanswered reply and every later listener. [592]

Mara eased it open, and the last transmission finally continued beyond the phrase repeated for a century. [609]

"We have saved every ending," the librarian said, "but we forgot to save an end for ourselves." [626]

Then Earhart's young voice answered through static, not as an icon, but as one lost woman consoling another. [644]

"I hear you," she said. [649]

"You can rest now." [653]

Julian's thread lay free in Mara's palm, no longer glowing, only copper. [684]
