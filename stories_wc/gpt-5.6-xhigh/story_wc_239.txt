<story>At the end of history, when every empire had become a footnote without a page, Mara Venn remained as the appointed witness. [22]
Her title was end of history witness, a duty she had accepted for one reason: to unravel cosmic illusions before they hardened into eternity. [46]
History had not ended cleanly; it had folded into contradictory versions, each claiming to be the only past. [64]
Beyond the last observatory, she found an ancient temple library lost in time, its basalt doors opening onto eras instead of rooms. [86]
Within, a resounding hush pressed against her ears as if silence itself had learned to sing. [102]
On a lectern waited the one object her instruments said was real: a softcover dictionary, swollen by rain that had never fallen. [124]
Its cover bore no title, but its first page defined universe as a story frightened by its own alternatives. [143]
Mara read onward, and the shelves wheeled around her, arranging themselves in a vast corkscrew descending through extinct suns. [162]
A bronze librarian with seven sealed mouths stepped from the stacks and named the mechanism spiral arbitration. [179]
Whenever two histories disputed the same moment, the library wound them inward, heard their evidence, and erased the less coherent world. [200]
Yet the arbitrations had stalled because every history now possessed perfect evidence, manufactured by the illusions that fed on certainty. [220]
At the spiral's center, those illusions were preparing to unify all possible pasts into one immaculate lie. [237]
If they succeeded, existence would continue forever, but no choice would ever have been freely made. [253]
The librarian offered Mara three doors, each labeled EXIT, and warned that only the false ones led deeper. [271]
The true path, the dictionary added in fresh ink, required journeying inward while walking out. [286]
Mara understood: she must leave the temple physically while descending through the private memories that had made her its witness. [306]
She chose no door, turned toward the entrance, and began retracing her steps as the spiral uncoiled beneath her mind. [326]
Her progress was wanderingly exact, each wrong turn outside matching a truthful turn within. [340]
As she passed the astronomy shelves, she revisited childhood beneath a green moon and admitted that she had always remembered it as silver. [363]
At the gallery of wars, she surrendered the noble speech she had invented for her dying brother and heard his actual terrified plea. [386]
Each confession dimmed a shelf, because cosmic illusion was not falsehood alone; it was pain polished until nobody could question it. [407]
The dictionary grew warmer in her hands, its pliant spine bending where the stone tablets of official memory would have cracked. [428]
Halfway to the doors, Mara reached the memory she had guarded most fiercely: her appointment at history's end. [446]
She had believed the dying civilizations chose her for incorruptible judgment, but the scene dissolved when she spoke that belief aloud. [467]
Underneath lay a smaller truth: everyone else had fled, and Mara had stayed because she feared having nowhere meaningful to go. [488]
The admission rang through the resounding hush, and the seven mouths of the librarian opened somewhere behind her. [506]
All seven histories accused her at once: coward, saint, deserter, guardian, orphan, tyrant, witness. [520]
Spiral arbitration demanded that she select one verdict so the others could be destroyed. [534]
Mara instead opened the dictionary at witness and found two definitions: one who sees, and one who testifies. [552]
Between them, in her own handwriting, appeared a third: one who permits truths to stand beside each other without becoming the same. [574]
She tore out the page, though tearing the last real thing felt like breaking a bone, and fed it into the turning shelves. [597]
The soft paper divided the spiral without severing it, making space between histories where judgment had allowed only victory. [616]
Across a universe's hiccup, every star vanished, returned, and remembered having shone in several colors. [631]
The illusions starved, for uncertainty freely acknowledged gave them no certainty to counterfeit. [644]
Mara crossed the basalt threshold just as her inward journey reached the spiral's center. [658]
There she found not a throne of lies, but countless frightened selves asking for a single past that could absolve them. [679]
She could not absolve them, so she did the kinder thing and let them remain complicated. [695]
Behind her, the temple sank beyond time, preserving no final record, only arguments that could continue without annihilation. [713]
Before her, history resumed, unfinished and many-voiced, while the dictionary in her hands began quietly defining beginning. [730]</story>
