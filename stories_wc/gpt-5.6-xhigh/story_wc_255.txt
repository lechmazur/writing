<story>After the phoenix rose above Bellweather, half the city fell into cinder sleep and could awaken only when touched by captured firebird breath. [23]

Thus began the market of bartered awakenings, where families exchanged heirlooms, secrets, and years of service for breaths captured in glass. [44]

Tomas Vale, a colorblind painter, came to the glassblower's courtyard carrying his sleeping daughter Lark on his back. [62]

He also carried one unfinished canvas, his last thing of value. [73]

Tomas had painted Lark's fever as a storm of light and shadow, because crimson and green looked equally brown to him, and because he had always worked to find beauty in imperfection. [105]

Now the canvas showed the phoenix with one ragged wing, modeled on a pigeon Lark had once rescued. [123]

Furnaces breathed around the courtyard, glass fruit glowed on iron tables, and sellers shouted prices for morning, memory, and song. [143]

At the central arch sat Master Ysra, whose voice had burned away when the phoenix passed overhead. [160]

She communicated through plant arrangements: rosemary angled east meant refusal, three violets meant grief, and ivy around cracked pottery meant hope. [181]

Beside her stood Kit, her innocently cunning apprentice, with soot on his cheeks and the open expression of a choirboy. [201]

Kit studied Ysra's tray of leaves and told Tomas, "She will grant Lark an awakening if your painting makes the phoenix seem perfect." [224]

Tomas looked at the ragged wing and understood that perfection was the one color he could not counterfeit. [242]

Before he could answer, Ysra overturned the rosemary and placed a fern beneath a glass bell. [258]

"That means hurry," Kit said, too sweetly. [265]

Yet Tomas recognized the fern from Lark's bedside games: hidden truth. [276]

He lifted the bell and saw a concertina reed tongue, thin as a fish bone, taped beneath its rim. [295]

Ysra had tuned the courtyard's communal awakening vessels to that tongue, which had vanished from her concertina on the market's first morning. [317]

Without it, Broker Pell had claimed, only his costly phoenix breaths could awaken the sleepers. [332]

Pell now strode across the courtyard, silver tongs raised, offering Tomas one breath in exchange for the flawless portrait he wanted as his market seal. [357]

Kit smiled at him and innocently asked why a rich broker feared a little scrap of brass. [374]

While every face turned toward Pell's anger, the boy slipped the tongue into Tomas's hand. [389]

Then Ysra circled a scorched rose with seven mismatched seedlings, and Tomas finally read her request. [405]

"Do not paint what rose; paint what survived." [413]

He set Lark down among the ivy, fitted the reed tongue into Ysra's battered concertina, and pulled the bellows. [432]

The instrument coughed, wheezed, and released a note so imperfect that the glass fruit shivered in sympathy. [449]

Hairline cracks brightened through every vessel Pell had labeled empty. [459]

They were not empty; he had corked the communal phoenix breath inside flawed glass, knowing buyers trusted smooth bottles and his smooth lies. [482]

One by one, the cracked vessels sang back, and pale fire streamed over the courtyard walls toward houses, hospitals, and humble pallets. [504]

Pell lunged for the concertina, but Kit tripped him with a watering cord and apologized with such honest surprise that even Pell doubted the evidence. [529]

Tomas kept playing until his fingers bled, bartering no possession except the certainty that his painting would ever be bought. [549]

When Lark opened both eyes, she did not ask whether the phoenix on the canvas was red. [566]

She touched its crooked wing and whispered, "It looks as if flying hurt, but it flew anyway." [583]

Across Bellweather, other sleepers awakened to debts suddenly made worthless, while the market erupted into embraces, accusations, and astonished laughter. [603]

Ysra planted the scorched rose in the cracked pot, then gave Tomas the first smile he had seen from her. [623]

Kit translated without being asked: "She says the courtyard needs a sign, and perfection charges too much." [640]

Tomas finished the canvas there, preserving the torn feathers, the smoky sky, and one thumbprint Lark left beside the sun. [660]

He could not name its colors, but he knew their values by the way darkness made room for light. [679]

They hung it above the arch, where afterward anyone seeking an awakening first had to offer one freely to someone poorer. [700]

And beneath the ragged phoenix, Ysra arranged ivy around the frame, the city's new word for beauty that survives being true. [721]</story>
