At the hush just before the condemned library's scheduled demolition, Mara Vale entered its split oak door. [17]

The building was a colossal tree trunk, hollowed three centuries earlier by the Astral Scholars' Guild. [33] Yellow demolition engines waited beyond the roots, their sleeping blades wet with dawn. [46] Inside, dust hovered so motionlessly that each mote seemed pinned to the blue light. [60]

Mara was a portrait artist but face blind, recognizing patrons by posture, scent, and the rhythms of their silences. [79] For twenty years, families had praised her likenesses, although each completed face soon became a stranger. [95] She had come now not to preserve a face, but to fulfill Master Orin's impossible commission: to mirror the sky inside walls. [117]

Orin, the guild's last scholar, had died after claiming the oak's ceiling could open without breaking. [133] He had left Mara a centuries-old silver coin and a note: "What resembles heaven must also admit its edge." [152] Now the foreman's bell would ring in less than an hour. [163]

The circular chamber rose around her, shelved into heartwood, with ladders curled like ribs. [177] Above, a plaster dome bore constellations rendered with fractally precise branches, each star flowering into smaller versions of itself. [196] Mara unpacked pigments and charcoal, then began to craft narrow brushes from single sable hairs. [211] Yet paint alone could only imitate daylight, and imitation had become the prison of her trade. [227]

She examined Orin's coin beneath a lens. [234] Its emperor had worn smooth, but tiny dents traveled across the field behind him, too regular for damage. [252] By turning the silver toward the dome, Mara discovered a map through faint indentations on a centuries-old coin. [270] Each dent aligned with a painted star, except seven marks that pointed toward empty knots in the shelves. [288]

She pressed the knots in order. [294] The false atlases sighed outward as the iron door of the scholar's secret book safe. [309] Inside lay no manuscript, only seven mirrored plates and a small brass crank. [322] Orin had hidden machinery where thieves would seek knowledge. [331]

Fitting the plates into the seven knots, Mara turned the crank, and wooden shutters withdrew high overhead. [348] Real sky appeared through seven narrow apertures, pale and cold, casting moving shards down the inner bark. [365] At first she laughed, because Orin had solved the commission before asking her to begin. [380]

Then she saw the flaw. [385]

The openings showed pieces of heaven, while the mirrored plates multiplied them into glittering cages. [400] Every reflection ended at an oak curve; every painted branch betrayed the boundary it disguised. [415] Mara understood why Orin had chosen someone whose gift was inseparable from a limit. [429] She could measure an eyelid's shadow perfectly, yet could not recognize its owner across a crowded room. [446] His ceiling, likewise, could frame the sky but never contain it. [457]

Beyond the door, an engine coughed, then stopped; even the workers resisted disturbing the stillness. [472] The demolition order named the oak unsafe, warning that its hollow crown might crush the square. [488] Mara climbed a ladder and studied the dark beams until their angles joined Orin's branching constellations. [504]

The safe was not merely concealed inside the library; its closed iron door was a brace bearing the weakened trunk. [524] Opening it had shifted the load. [530] A hairline crack began descending the dome. [537]

She had minutes, perhaps less, and no painting could mend oak. [548] Mara reversed the crank, removed the mirrors, closed the safe, and felt the crack cease beneath her palm. [566] Saving the building meant sealing Orin's mechanism and surrendering the seven windows. [578] She finally discovered the commission's true limit: walls could welcome the sky only by remaining walls. [594]

Below, the foreman lifted his bell. [600] Mara called him inside and placed one mirror on the floor where a seam in the dome cast its thinnest light. [621] She showed him the stopped crack, the load-bearing safe, and the old guild mark stamped upon its hinge. [639] Demolition law protected any certified guild structure whose original supports remained functional. [651]

The foreman traced the mark, lowered his bell, and sent a worker running for the city surveyor. [668] While engines cooled outside, Mara worked within the reprieve. [677]

She blackened the dome, then set the mirrors in shallow sockets, each angled toward a different seam of daylight. [696] Upon that darkness, reflected blue gathered without pretending to be endless. [707] Clouds passed as quiet changes of brightness, entering the room yet belonging wholly beyond it. [722]

By noon, the surveyor had stayed the demolition, but Mara scarcely heard the announcement. [736] She stood under Orin's bounded heaven, unable to remember his face, certain at last that forgetting did not erase him. [756] Within the oak's great silence, what she could not hold had become what she could honor. [772]
