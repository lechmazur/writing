In the cliff town of Vey, Mara restored broken masks for families who feared that a cracked likeness gave the dead another mouth. [23]

She was the last broken mask restorer, trained to mend funeral faces with rice paste, shell dust, and a tapestry needle finer than a gull's bone. [49]

After years among those hollow eyes, she could hear the dead in the harbor fog, a lucid unrest of whispers that never blurred into dreams and never allowed sleep. [78]

They wanted names, blame, or simply the weight of earth, and Mara wanted only to ground the restless spirits before their unrest entered every child in Vey. [105]

On the autumn equinox, harbor keeper Senn brought her a mask split from brow to chin and a centuries-old fisherman's net stiff with salt and black knots. [132]

"My family says our first grandfather wore this when the White Shoal drowned," he said, naming a wreck two centuries old. [153]

Mara touched the net and heard no ghost; instead, it drank the scrape of chairs, the hiss of lamps, even Senn's frightened breathing. [176]

"It accumulates silence," she wrote on her slate, because speaking aloud near it felt like surrendering blood. [193]

Senn then shared the secret his family had guarded: the White Shoal's crew had returned alive, but the town priest had murdered them to hide stolen rescue silver. [221]

Their bodies lay beneath the Prism Sanctuary, sealed where colored glass turned starlight into bars across the floor. [239]

"Across the sharing of a secret, truth passes from one keeper to another," Senn said, repeating his family's oath, "but tonight it must pass into the ground." [266]

At midnight they climbed to the prism sanctuary under starlight, carrying the net, the broken mask, spades, and Mara's narrow case of tools. [289]

The sanctuary had no roof; seven crystal walls gathered the constellations and laid blue, red, and gold paths over a mosaic of sleeping sailors. [313]

Yet feet shuffled below the mosaic, measured and patient, while no wind moved above it. [328]

Mara understood that the net was both map and lock: each knot crossed like a street above the crypt, and each trapped sound kept one grave sealed. [355]

She threaded her tapestry needle with moon-pale cord, not to sew the mask yet, but to tease apart tarred loops too tight for fingernails. [379]

With the first loosened knot, a cough escaped and one mosaic tile lifted, breathing grave-cold air. [395]

The second released a shouted order; the third, an ax striking bone; the fourth, a prayer bitten short. [413]

These sounds did not break the quiet, but braided themselves into an interlaced hush, distinct strands of terror held in one terrible calm. [436]

As the net emptied, the crystal walls dimmed, for the silence it had accumulated was flowing downward and waking the murdered men. [458]

Hands pressed through dust between the tiles, and Senn recoiled, confessing that his ancestor had not been a witness, but the priest himself. [481]

The mask bore that ancestor's face, and Senn had broken it that morning, hoping to end an inheritance of guilt without speaking its cause. [505]

In old Vey speech, "across sharing a secret" meant the perilous hours between confession and absolution, when truth belonged fully to neither heart. [528]

They were inside those hours now, and the dead rose higher as if uncertainty were a tide. [545]

"No," Mara said when Senn reached for the spade, "because we must exhume them by unraveling knots in the centuries-old fisherman's net, or we will uncover bodies while leaving their deaths buried." [577]

So Senn dug only where each loosened loop opened a tile, while Mara worked the needle until her fingertips bled and twelve bundled skeletons emerged. [602]

One knot remained, hardened around a small silver coin, and beneath it the priest's voice still commanded the crew to kneel. [623]

Senn begged her to cut it, but a severed strand would free the command without the confession that answered it. [643]

Mara passed the needle through both halves of the broken mask, laced them loosely with the net's final cord, and placed the coin behind its clay tongue. [670]

"Speak your name, not his," she told Senn. [678]

He confessed his silence to the sailors, his neighbors, and himself; as his words entered the mended face, Mara drew out the last knot. [702]

The sanctuary rang with the sailors' voices and Senn's living answer, then the sound sank through the opened graves like rain into thirsty soil. [726]

Dawn found the bones laid beneath honest stones and the priest's mask buried face down, its repaired crack still visible. [746]

For the first time, Mara heard ordinary gulls in the harbor fog, and the silence around them held nothing it had stolen. [768]
