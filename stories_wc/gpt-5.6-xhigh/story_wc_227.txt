<story>The gears-and-cogs workshop called Mercy-of-Brass floated on an acid sea, its iron pontoons hissing while green waves gnawed them thinner. [20]

She doubted every formula she had inherited, especially the one written in her mother's hand: CERTAINTY IS THE FIRST POISON. [82]
Yet she descended, because she had spent her life trying to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion, where lost things left only stains. [106]
At the flood hatch, she tied her mother's key ring charm around her wrist, filled her lungs, and plunged. [125]
Everything that followed happened between breaths held underwater, although terror stretched that interval into a small, merciless eternity. [143]

Nera had expected a lock, a lever, some mercifully obvious wound to mend. [199]

The charm tugged at her wrist as if recognizing its maker. [233]
It was a brass disk no wider than a coin, battered with tiny dents that Nera had mistaken for age. [253]

The aligned marks flared gold, drawing memories from the stone into her. [288]

Neither choice resembled salvation. [457]
Then Orra's borrowed shame touched her mother's broken love, and Nera understood the lesson both women had failed to complete. [477]
Kintsugi intentions, her mother had called them: when a purpose breaks, do not disguise the fracture; fill it with something precious, and let the scar redirect its strength. [505]
