At dawn, Mara Vale rowed toward the abandoned carnival ship drifting in a foggy bay, though no tide moved beneath her. [21]
Mara was the last coastal chimera twin discoverer, trained to identify children whose bodies housed memories from siblings never born. [41]
She sought the vessel to find the garden of forgotten names, where legend promised each unborn twin had once been recorded. [62]
Around her neck hung an emerald swatch card, the final clue left by her brother Iven before everyone but Mara forgot him. [84]
The card bore no map, only six shades of green labeled with the warning, "Here, time passes as memories alter." [104]
She stepped onto the midway deck, where a rusted Ferris wheel turned slowly under its own ruined weight. [122]
Booths crowded the rails like bright cells, offering the trapped freedom of games that could be played but never won. [142]
Calliope music leaked from drowned pipes, jaunty, exhausted, and blissfully aberrant against the groan of the empty hull. [160]
At the ticket booth sat a boy with antlers, glossy seal eyes, and two mouths sharing one uncertain smile. [179]
"Name?" both mouths asked, one voice arriving an instant after the other. [191]
"Mara," she said. [194]
The boy shook his head and pointed to a sign: ADMISSION REQUIRES WHAT YOU CAME TO RECOVER. [211]
"Iven," she answered, and the deck lurched as if the ship had swallowed a wave. [226]
The boy handed her a ticket printed UNKINDLED SOLACE, the carnival term for comfort that exists before anyone can feel it. [247]
He warned that the garden could be reached only through narrative threads, stories told correctly enough to stitch broken rooms together. [268]
But each telling would steal one true memory and replace it with a kinder lie. [283]
Mara entered the hall of mirrors, whose reflections showed her childhood in mutually impossible versions. [298]
In one, Iven drowned at seven; in another, he captained the carnival; in a third, Mara had invented him. [317]
The palest stripe on the card now read, TELL THE DAY HE GAVE YOU YOUR NAME. [333]
Mara spoke of two children found nameless beside the salt marsh, and of Iven calling her Mara after a stubborn shorebird. [354]
Green thread unspooled from the card, crossed the mirrors, and pulled open a door into the carousel stable. [372]
There wooden animals breathed in chains, each bearing a brass plaque engraved with a name nobody ashore remembered. [390]
The second stripe commanded, CONFESS WHY YOU HUNT CHIMERAS. [399]
She had never admitted that every child she cataloged might prove Iven lived inside her, not beside her. [417]
Once the confession left her, she could no longer remember the marsh, only a warm house where their supposed parents waited. [438]
This kinder lie tempted her to leave, but his name burned fresh on the emerald card. [454]
She followed its thread beneath the carousel and reached the flooded ballroom below, where flowers of paper tickets grew from black water. [476]
At their center stood the antlered boy, older now, bearing the face Mara remembered as Iven. [492]
"You found the garden," he said. [498]
"But you did not come for its names." [506]
Mara understood that she had come to recapture not her brother, but the certainty that she had ever been only herself. [527]
"We were not twins," Iven said; both his mouths moved together now. [539]
"We were one abandoned child who survived by telling two lives." [550]
The carnival had harvested that second life from her years ago, giving Iven freedom inside a prison that could never dock. [571]
The darkest swatch turned nearly black and offered one final sentence: NAME WHAT MUST BE FORGOTTEN. [587]
Mara looked across the ticket flowers and saw thousands of names invented by lonely minds, each invention real enough to suffer. [608]
She would not erase Iven to make her past orderly, nor remain aboard to preserve him unchanged. [625]
"Certainty," she said, surrendering the only cage that had ever made her feel safe. [639]
Every green thread snapped taut, sewing ballroom to deck, deck to fog, and fog to the waiting shore. [657]
The ship groaned as paper flowers tore free, wheeling like birds toward families who would wake remembering whom they had lost. [678]
Iven touched her forehead, and his body unraveled into two memories: the marsh as it was and the warm house as it might have been. [703]
She kept both, not as proof, but as companions. [712]
At sunrise, Mara rowed away while the carnival ship finally sank, its calliope playing one clear note beneath the fog. [732]
The emerald swatch card lay blank in her palm, free of directions and therefore terrifying. [747]
For the first time, unkindled solace caught fire within her, not comfort exactly, but room enough for every true and invented name. [769]
