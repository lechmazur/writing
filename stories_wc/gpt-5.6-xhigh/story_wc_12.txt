At the center of Saint Orison Monastery stood the dimensional anchor, a black column pinning seven neighboring worlds apart. [19]

Its chamber swallowed echoes, so Brother Coda, the loop pedal priest, became known as one who decorates with silence. [38]

He placed rests between recorded breaths, blank measures around prayers, and pauses where ordinary clergy hung bells. [55]

Beside the column waited Mara Venn, an artist clutching a palette with one color: a gray made from anchor dust. [75]

She had come to paint the maintenance sigils, but something inside the column had entered her shadow first. [93]

Now the shadow stood when she knelt, smiled when she wept, and climbed her spine one cold inch at a time. [114]

The anchor keeper set Coda's deadline: intervene before the possession completes and makes every adjacent world collapse. [131]

Soldiers proposed cutting away Mara's shadow, while scholars proposed feeding it memories until it slept. [146]

Coda rejected both plans because each assumed the thing was foreign, hungry, and singular. [160]

His calling was not merely exorcism; it was to illuminate hidden assumptions before frightened people made them fatal. [178]

He connected three brass pedals, set a microphone beneath Mara's chin, and asked her name. [193]

"Mara," she whispered, but the pedal returned, "Mara, Mara," with the second answer lower than the first. [210]

Coda listened to the discrepancy, then painted a gray circle around her feet using one careful thumb. [227]

The color matched neither stone nor shadow; it marked the narrow border where certainty lost its authority. [244]

Within that border, the chamber developed an unraveled stillness, as if quiet itself were being pulled into thread. [262]

Coda began to plumb the invading mind, not through instruments or torture, but through rhetorical devices. [278]

"If you are a demon," he asked, "why do you need a woman's name to answer?" [294]

The pedal repeated the question backward in order: answer to name, name to woman, woman to demon. [311]

The shadow trembled, and Mara's left hand reached toward the single gray pigment as though recognizing home. [328]

"If Mara contains you, do you contain Mara?" Coda asked, folding antithesis over paradox and looping both. [345]

That breath widened across the room, muffling alarms and clarifying every scrape, heartbeat, and swallowed objection. [378]

In its muffled clarity, Coda heard two pulses inside Mara's shadow, alternating rather than competing. [393]

The scholars had called one pulse human and the other alien, yet both accelerated when Mara feared erasure. [411]

Coda saw the hidden assumption: everyone believed a self must be indivisible to be real. [426]

He turned to Mara and asked, "Which memories would prove you are alone inside yourself?" [441]

She remembered her dead sister teaching her color, and the gray on her palette suddenly shone blue in one world, gold in another. [464]

The anchor had not admitted a demon; it had caught the sister's unfinished possibility when Mara mixed dust into paint. [484]

The possession stopped a heartbeat before completion, transformed from conquest into consent. [625]

Mara answered with her own voice and her sister's cadence, "The one that refuses your question." [676]

Coda erased the outer circle, but left the inner gap unpainted as a doorway no doctrine could close. [694]

Afterward, pilgrims said the loop pedal priest had defeated a spirit, because victory was easier to repeat than understanding. [713]

Coda corrected none of them; he simply decorated their certainty with silence and let its unanswered echo illuminate the rest. [733]
