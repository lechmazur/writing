<story>Across the lull of a wounded season, when ash storms paused but spring refused to heal, Mara kept the locks beneath Bellweather. [22] She was the city's most reflective locksmith, given to questioning every click before trusting what any key claimed to open. [42] Her gaze was peripherally focused: blurred at the center, keen along the edges, where ordinary eyes misplaced small truths. [61] That gift led her through the marble cavern below the city, whose white walls folded lamplight into pale, wandering doubles. [81]

At its heart stood the Resonary, a brass orchard of funnels and glass jars built for the annual harvest of echoes. [102] When thawwater rang through marble channels, the machine gathered each returning note, sealed it, and labeled its hour. [120] Citizens later opened those jars during bleak months to hear vanished rain, market laughter, wedding bells, or a child's first song. [141] Mara loved the practice because she wanted to store wonder in the cellar of reason, for later, when certainty became hungry. [162]

This year, however, frost had split the orchards, ash had dimmed the sun, and the cavern's great timer had stopped. [182] Without its measured shutters, no echo could be bottled; opened together, the returning sounds might also crack the cavern roof. [202] The council gave Mara until the thaw's next surge, then ordered her to dismantle the Resonary and salvage its brass. [222]

Its broken timer mechanism had no maker's diagram, only seven warped gears, a mirror wheel, and one deliberately empty axle. [242] The signature inside belonged to Oren, Mara's father, who had vanished in the cavern during the previous harvest. [260] Grief suggested a missing gear, but reflection warned her that an obvious absence could be a lock rather than damage. [280] She resolved to reverse-engineer the device without filling that space until she understood why Oren had left it bare. [299]

For thirteen quiet days, Mara slept beside the machine and mapped its glints instead of merely counting its teeth. [318] She worked by studying reflection angles at different times: blue dawn, white noon, copper dusk, and the black shine before moonrise. [339] Because her sight favored margins, she noticed reflected scratches that disappeared whenever she looked straight at them. [356] The scratches formed instructions, not for clockwork, but for aiming each funnel toward an echo's second return. [373] A first return was loud but coarse; a second had traveled deeper through marble and carried the shape of its source. [394]

On the fourteenth dusk, Mara aligned the mirror wheel, and Oren's voice issued softly from the empty axle. [412] "If you hear me," it said, "the timer did not fail; I stopped it." [426] "The Resonary does not copy wonder, Mara; it removes the echo from whoever made it." [441]

Mara remembered last year's singers leaving the cavern unable to recall their own melodies, while listeners praised unusually vivid jars. [461] Oren had discovered the theft and pulled the governing gear, trapping himself beyond a closing shutter before he could return. [481] His preserved warning was his final echo, caught accidentally in the cavity he had made. [496] Mara could restore the old harvest, save the tradition, and perhaps hear more of him, but only by renewing its quiet robbery. [518]

Outside, a thaw tremor rolled overhead, shaking dust from the marble and starting the council's deadline bell. [535] Instead of replacing the absent gear, Mara cut a new key with twice the usual number of teeth. [553] One toothed edge advanced the shutters; the other returned them, making every captured vibration pass back through its source funnel. [573] The design might copy a second return while restoring the first, though no calculation could promise the marble would endure. [593]

Mara inserted the key as the thawwater struck, and the Resonary opened all seven throats in sequence. [610] Rain, bells, laughter, and forgotten songs rushed around her, so forceful that hairline cracks raced across the ceiling. [628] She turned the key backward before fear could make her grip it still. [641] The mirror wheel reversed, the jars flashed, and each sound poured toward the channel from which it had once been taken. [662]

Across Bellweather, sleepers woke remembering tunes, vows, jokes, and voices they had surrendered without knowing. [677] The ceiling cracks halted, and new echoes settled into the jars, fainter than before but clear, gifts rather than amputations. [697] One jar held Oren saying only her name, no longer a warning, simply the warm recognition she remembered. [715] Mara did not open it again; knowing it was there proved enough for that wounded spring. [731]

When the council descended, she showed them the paired teeth and required every future singer's consent. [747] Then she locked the Resonary with her new key, preserving both wonder and its owners, and carried reason upstairs. [766]</story>
