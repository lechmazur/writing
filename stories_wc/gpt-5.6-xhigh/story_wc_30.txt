After tomorrow disappears, the city of Vey wakes each morning beneath the same pale winter sun.[16]

Calendars retain yesterday, but their next pages are blank, and no clock dares promise another day.[32]

Only the moon continues changing, its silver face fluctuating through phases in no order anyone can predict.[49]

At the harbor's center, seawater enters a glass-roofed tidal atrium, lifting nine circular galleries on chains.[65]

Mara Venn, the titan-gear librarian, patrols those moving shelves inside an iron exoskeleton built for ancient dock giants.[83]

Its steps shake water from the tiles, yet Mara's hands remain delicate enough to mend a child's diary.[101]

She oversees the Registry of Selves, where migrating identities arrive as memories, gestures, trades, and desires seeking new keepers.[120]

Without a future to pull them straight, people's unfinished selves have begun drifting on moon-drawn currents from body to body.[140]

A baker wakes knowing knots; a sailor weeps over cakes she never baked.[153]

Mara records each passage because she believes careful routine can preserve wonder, as a frame preserves an open window.[172]

Every morning she hides one marvelous item among the ledgers, then lets a visiting child discover it.[189]

Today it is a blue feather that hums when the tide turns.[201]

Near noon, fisherman Iven Ro arrives carrying his fisherman's bamboo creel, although the harbor has yielded no fish since tomorrow vanished.[222]

He sets it before Mara, and two voices from its woven darkness say, "I am Iven."[238]

One is the old man's calm baritone; the other belongs to Lio, his apprentice, who drowned three winters earlier.[257]

The creel contains their bronze registry seals, tangled together by white roots and warm as living palms.[274]

Iven explains that he hauled it from the atrium's drainage gate, then remembered both drowning and hauling.[291]

"Choose which man survived," he asks, "before both of us wash away."[303]

The Index, a pearl mechanism that tests identity, fluctuates with moon phases, and tonight it points simultaneously to full and new.[324]

Its contradictory reading should be impossible, but impossibility has become Vey's most dependable weather.[338]

Under full moon force, the seals will fuse and trap both identities in bronze; under new moon silence, both will vanish.[359]

The Registry handbook orders Mara to separate any collision, preserving the identity with the longer documented life.[376]

That would save Iven and erase Lio, a neat answer suited to blank calendars.[390]

Mara locks her titan gear around the creel and descends with Iven as the black tide raises the galleries.[409]

At the furnace platform below, wintersun serenity pours through the glass roof, turning every ripple to quiet gold.[427]

Mara remembers Lio finding her humming feather years ago, and Iven pretending not to smile at his delight.[445]

Saving only the longer life would preserve information, she realizes, but destroy the wonder that made those years matter.[464]

The furnace can smelt registry bronze, though no librarian has melted seals still claimed by the living.[481]

Iven grips the creel and says in both voices, "I taught myself fishing, and I was taught by myself."[500]

Mara hears not an error, but a complete grief speaking from opposite shores.[513]

She understands that migrating identities cannot be anchored by choosing one truth and murdering its rival.[529]

They must be housed by accepting contradictions, as the atrium houses both architecture and sea.[544]

Against the handbook, she drops both seals into the furnace cup.[555]

Her titan gear braces against the tide while she pumps the bellows, and Iven feeds in strips cut from his creel.[576]

Bamboo smoke sweetens the salt air; bronze runs bright, carrying two names without blending their letters.[592]

Mara pours it around a strand of bamboo, making a slender shuttle like those used in mending nets.[610]

When the moon's double phase strikes the atrium, the shuttle rings once, low and young together.[626]

The voices leave the creel and settle into Iven, not as rivals, but as a tide with two directions.[645]

He remembers Lio's death without claiming it was merely his own, and remembers surviving without denying Lio's presence.[663]

Above them, hundreds of sealed identities begin knocking softly in sympathy, asking for room to be more than singular.[682]

Mara revises the Registry's first rule before the tide lowers the galleries: a self may contain every truth it can carry kindly.[704]

Thereafter, her routine remains exact, but each day she smelts one needless boundary and shelves one beautiful inconsistency.[722]

The city still has no tomorrow, yet its people cease treating sameness as an empty room.[738]

In the mild winter light, Iven teaches children to weave creels, sometimes with an apprentice's quick hands guiding his weathered ones.[759]

And Mara continues her rounds, hiding impossible feathers between ordinary records to preserve wonder in routine.[775]
