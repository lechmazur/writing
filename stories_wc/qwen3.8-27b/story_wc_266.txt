The tide had a name on this shore, and the wise ferry guides knew it best. [16]
Each dusk, they kept a vigil in twilight, standing where the water folded into stone. [31]
They wore wool the color of moonlight and sang a shoreline lullaby low enough to calm the gulls. [49]
In the center of their circle lay a ticket punch with star stamp, older than the harbor bell. [67]
Its teeth had cut safe passages for drowned songs, lost names, and all the things too afraid to arrive. [86]
Maren, a girl with a paper boat and a question in her chest, watched them from the seawall. [104]
Her name was not her courage, and her courage was not yet anywhere she could find. [120]
That evening, a gray fog rolled in and broke the ferry rope, so the old ferry guides lifted their lanterns. [140]
"After the leash breaks," said the eldest guide, "the veiled ridgeway opens for those who are gently persistent." [158]
Maren had come to the shore to find where unused courage waits, because her brother had been missing since the mist broke the ferry rope. [183]
The rope was a leash, the guides explained, because it kept the fog from running away with every small hope. [203]
When the leash breaks, hope must choose its own path. [213]
Maren thought of her brother, Pello, who had always carried his fear in his pocket like a stone. [231]
He had said that courage could wait in odd places, where the straight road gave up. [247]
The ferry guides gave Maren the ticket punch with star stamp and told her to follow the star marks. [266]
"You must not walk the direct line," the eldest warned, "for the direct line is where the fog learns your name." [287]
"Traverse the veiled ridgeway through indirect routes, and the stars will punch safe edges for you." [303]
Maren climbed, gently persistent, though her fingers trembled on the cold stone. [315]
The ridgeway was a breath between the sea and the sleeping hills, veiled by silver mist. [331]
She looked for the star stamp, but the punch was heavy and the fog seemed to swallow her footprints. [350]
Then she saw Pello, or maybe only the shape of him, standing where a broken ferry ladder leaned into black water. [371]
He did not run to her, because he was listening to something she could not hear. [387]
Maren remembered that to find where unused courage waits, one must sometimes let courage find you. [403]
She turned away and walked through indirect routes, around a stone arch, under a moss bridge, beside a pool that held a single moon. [427]
Each step left a faint mark, as if the mist were remembering her. [440]
At the narrowest point, the fog tightened like a collar, and the shoreline lullaby became a low pulse. [458]
Maren raised the ticket punch with star stamp and pressed it into the air, not to make a mark, but to make a promise. [482]
A pale star bloomed on the mist, then another, forming a crooked path that refused to point straight at Pello. [502]
She followed it, traversing the veiled ridgeway through indirect routes, past the place where her fear had been waiting with open hands. [524]
At last, the stars led her not to Pello himself, but to a small iron box half sunk in the reeds. [545]
Inside was a folded ticket, and on it a star stamp, pressed long before that night, beside a child's drawing of a ferry. [568]
On the back, in Pello's hurried hand, were the words: I left this for the part of you that was not ready to be brave. [593]
Maren opened the box, and the unused courage rose like smoke, thin and bright, until it touched her lips. [612]
The fog loosened, and Pello appeared on the near bank, shivering but safe, his leash of fear finally broken behind him. [633]
The wise ferry guides were waiting at the shore, still keeping their vigil in twilight, as if no hours had passed. [654]
Maren brought Pello to them, gently persistent in every step, and the eldest guide nodded once. [670]
"You found where unused courage waits," the guide said, "because you did not drag it by the leash." [685]
Maren smiled, realizing that the truest crossings are made through indirect routes, with a lullaby in the water and a star stamp in the dark. [710]
And after the leash breaks, she thought, even fear can be ferried home. [723]
