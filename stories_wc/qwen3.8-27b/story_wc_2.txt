Mara, the broken-clock tinkerer, had kept her shop above the river for nine years, learning that stopped things still remembered motion. [21]
She repaired pendulums, springs, and cracked faces, but she avoided the deep question of which hour deserved to continue. [40]
The admiral's lost compass rose arrived on a Tuesday wrapped in a child's sleeve, silver leaves cold as a winter promise. [61]
The child said it had fallen from a ship that was not a ship, and the needle always pointed downward. [81]

Beneath the ashes of her workbench fire lay a map of the city's hidden veins, drawn by hands that feared clocks. [127]
It showed a subterranean waterfall where water fell upward into roots, carrying the names of days that had been thrown away. [148]
Mara understood that they could not arrive by schedule, because the map only opened when coincidence becomes pattern. [166]
She had always planned in straight lines, but the old tinkerer before her had failed by refusing to befriend uncertainty in planning. [188]
Now Mara wanted to befriend uncertainty in planning, so she told the child they would improvise, one strange step at a time. [210]
They left before dawn, following not the road but the way rain preferred to enter the cracks in the cobbles. [230]
The city answered them with doors that opened by mistake, keys that fit nothing, and bells that rang without tongues. [250]
By noon they stood at the edge of a neglected greenhouse where broken glass had turned a garden into a cage of light. [273]

Mara followed it uncertainly certain, because the thread pulled only when her breathing slowed and her thoughts loosened. [312]
The path dropped through a root, then through a bell, then through a hollow where water sang in a voice like a name. [335]
The subterranean waterfall was not water at all but a curtain of moments, each one falling into a pool of black glass. [357]
There, beneath the ashes of a burned plan, lay the true rose, which had been hidden by an admiral who feared the sea's forgetting. [381]
The child lifted it, and the admiral's lost compass rose split into two, one part for memory, one part for the next step. [404]
Mara saw that uncertainty was not a gap in the plan but the room where the plan could breathe. [423]

The next-step half glowed beneath the child's palm, as if waiting to be carried back into a city that never stopped missing. [467]
Mara realized she had to improvise again, not because the way was lost, but because the right road had changed its mind. [489]
She placed her own stopped watch into the pool, and the pool accepted it like a promise made by a patient friend. [511]
The waterfall bent, and a staircase of white stone rose from the glass, each step marked with a day no calendar owned. [533]
They climbed while the garden answered through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse, as if counting every step. [552]
When they reached the surface, the shop's door stood open, and every broken clock inside had paused at the same moment. [573]
The child put the rose on Mara's bench, where it pointed not to north but to the space between one second and the next. [597]
Mara smiled uncertainly certain, knowing that a plan can be a river, and that rivers learn by finding new banks. [617]
Outside, a bell rang without a tongue, and the city continued, ready for whatever hour would choose to begin. [636]
