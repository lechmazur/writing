The suspended palace hung in a cavern of black mist, its bridges anchored to giant stalactites that swayed like the ribs of a buried god. [25]
Below, the world had become all pistons, and every song was a counted vibration, and no one remembered why the air used to smell of rain. [51]
The prince was bedraggled, his cloak stitched with copper scraps, his boots full of oil, his eyes full of questions he was afraid to finish. [76]
He had climbed through the throat of the palace, where the anchor chains passed through stalactites, because a small white shape had glittered in a crevice and called to him without sound. [108]
It was an extinct bird's egg, pale and cracked, older than the clockwork, older than the cavern's first name. [127]
The prince cupped it, and the shell was bitterly sweet against his palms, as if sorrow and honey had been ground together by time. [151]
He began hypothesizing about everything, the way he always did when the machines made him afraid: perhaps the palace had once been built to protect a garden, and the garden had been lost before the first gear was cut. [190]
Perhaps the birds had gone extinct so completely that even their names had been filed away as broken parts, and their eggs had been kept in the highest rooms where beauty was allowed only as a spare. [227]
He hypothesized that the world was a flawed genesis, a pattern begun from a broken mirror, where every living thing was copied as a mechanism and then left to wear itself thin. [259]
His purpose had never been rule or repair, but to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world, and the egg trembled in his hand like a promise he could not yet afford. [290]
With shaking fingers, he began to assemble a cradle from discarded springs, a thread of moss, a shard of glass, and the faint heat of his own breath. [318]
Around him, the palace held a quaking reverie, the stalactites humming as if the stone remembered a sky and was dreaming it back into being. [343]
Through the discovery, the cavern slowed, and the prince forgot his hunger, his fear, and the cold arithmetic of the city below. [365]
He hypothesized that the egg would not hatch into a bird but into a question, and the question would open the palace like a door long jammed by rust. [394]
He also hypothesized that beauty was not a thing that could be restored by force, but only invited, fed, and allowed to fail before it was allowed to live. [423]
The egg opened with a sound like a tiny bell struck in a wet cave. [438]
What emerged was not a creature but a note, a green light, a feather made of heat, a melody that hung in the air and made the gears weep. [467]
The note drifted down through the palace, entering engines that had known only order, and it made their motions strange, softer, and almost human. [491]
In the lower halls, workers paused, and for a moment their hands were not tools but leaves, and the city below seemed to breathe. [515]
The prince understood then that the flawed genesis had not been a mistake but a wound, and beauty could not be built from perfection only from what survives the break. [545]
He set the empty shell back into the cradle he had assembled, and the cradle held it with the tenderness of a machine learning to dream. [571]
For the first time in a long time, the palace was not only a machine holding up a city, but a heart remembering why it had ever wanted to beat. [601]
The stalactites swayed, the palace quaked, and the prince remained there, bitter, sweet, and awake, waiting to see what the note would become. [624]
And in that suspended hour, beauty began again. [632]
