<story>You, the lost wax caster, wake in an icebound sanctum under aurora. [12]

It is a transcribed memory, not of music alone, but of the syllables of children, soldiers, bakers, and river women whose names the city forgot. [85]
You want to preserve the sound of forgotten names before winter can chew them into silence. [101]
For you are technically gentle, trained to warm wax without blistering it, to lift a pin without bruising the hole beside it. [123]
The technique is not force; it is listening, a slow patience that lets brittle things reveal their shapes. [141]

By reconfiguring the braids of a centuries-old ceremonial rope, the sanctum turns the rope into a lens. [187]
Each strand carries a different old note, and when their order is changed, the rope learns a new song. [206]
You begin with the red braid, which the old texts call the braid of bread, and you loosen it only one twist at a time. [231]
The green braid is the braid of salt, and you feed it beneath the red until the rope hums like a kettle. [253]
The black braid is the braid of grief, and you do not pull it, because grief must be carried, not torn out. [275]
After forgiving yourself for the name you once burned to keep another warm, you let the black braid rest in your palm. [297]
Your forgiveness is not a grand mercy; it is the small thaw that lets your fingers trust the rope again. [317]
A snowmelt ardor rises in you, not the hot blaze of youth, but the patient heat of ice becoming river. [337]
You take the wax from your satchel, a pale disk cast in the shape of a tiny mouth. [355]
