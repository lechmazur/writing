Maren had always believed that endings were only doors that had not yet been knocked. [15]
Now, inside the whisper of closing pages, she felt every door press against her chest. [30]
She was a conflicted novelist, and the feral nexus in mist had chosen her for its last unfinished chapter. [49]

Every step she took against the current made her bones remember a slower, kinder sea. [76]

She had found it in the gutter after a rain that smelled of salt and old ink. [110]
Now the fragment hummed, asking her to collect the shapes of different hopes before the mist forgot them. [128]
That was her task, though no one had written it in any page. [141]
She was lucidly watchful, reading the fog the way a sailor reads a broken horizon. [156]

His hope was not for love, but for a single sentence that might make him brave enough. [255]
Maren took that shape gently, for she knew how fragile a hope becomes when it is named. [272]
The mist answered her with a low, swirling hush, as if all pages were closing at once. [289]
A third cameo rose from the grey, an old woman whose fingers traced the edge of a window. [307]
She hoped only to be remembered correctly, in a voice that did not flinch from her failures. [324]
Maren felt the weight of that hope settle into the fragment like a tiny, bright stone. [340]
The feral nexus tightened, and the mist began to pull at her words as though they were hair. [358]

She thought of the girl, the boy, and the old woman, each waiting in the mist's soft throat. [471]

She solemnized the nexus in that loss, and the mist grew still enough to be called a hush. [550]
Maren did not feel empty, because she had become a witness rather than an author. [565]
The ephemeral cameo appearances faded, but their shapes remained in her memory, lucidly watchful as small lamps. [582]
The swirling hush gathered around her, and she moved against the current one last time. [597]
She stepped out of the feral nexus in mist, carrying no story at all. [611]
Behind her, the closing pages whispered a new word, and she almost smiled. [624]
It was not the word she had hoped to find, but it was true enough to let her breathe. [643]
