Mira Vale is a natural history illustrator, known for her careful plates of beetles, shells, and strange coastal flowers. [19]
She has been sent to Sarn, a city that sleeps across a giant's nap, to find the place where echoes are born. [41]
The museum believes the echo is an old voice, shaped once by water and kept alive in stone. [59]
Sarn rises in towers, and a rooftop canal system weaving between towers carries dark water over iron ledges. [77]
The canals move through stone channels, and their patient flow has a soft, steady cadence that the citizens learn to trust. [98]
At the ninth bell, the cadence is interrupted, and every sound in Sarn stands still for one breath. [116]
That interrupted cadence is the only thing Mira can see with her illustrator's eye, a clean break in the world's line. [137]
Her mentor had pressed an infinity symbol pendant into her palm before the journey, and now it hangs warm against her chest. [159]
The pendant is not jewelry, for it sculpts time itself. [169]
By borrowing time from the giant's dream, it can hold a broken second open, like a flower held between pages. [189]
Mira follows the water where it hesitates, and the rooftop city narrows into a spiral of bridges. [206]
Each canal has a different voice, a thin whistle, a low drum, a dry laugh, but none of them is the echo. [228]
She knows the echo will not reveal itself while the cadence is whole, so she waits for the ninth bell again. [249]
When the interruption comes, she touches the infinity symbol pendant and feels the loop pull through the air. [267]
The pendant borrows time from the sleeping giant, and the city's broken cadence becomes a doorway. [283]
Mira chooses to uncage the echo by borrowing time, releasing a pale voice from the water's stopped mouth. [301]
The voice has no face, only the smell of rain and the blue weight of a page that has not been drawn yet. [324]
It asks her to draw it, because a place where echoes are born must be witnessed or it will fold back into the giant's sleep. [349]
Mira opens her sketchbook, but the paper refuses ink, as if the world is still deciding what the echo should be. [370]
She realizes that she cannot copy the echo; she must let the interrupted cadence draw itself through her hand. [389]
The pendant grows heavier as it sculpts time itself, stretching the broken second into a long, silver room. [407]
Around Mira, the rooftop canal system becomes a map of lines, and every tower holds a note in the giant's dream. [428]
She draws not a bird or a shell, but a waterline that turns into a mouth, and a mouth that turns into a bell. [452]
The cadence returns, but it is no longer the old song; it carries the echo's name in every ripple. [471]
When the giant stirs, the borrowed time pays its debt, and Sarn wakes with a faint laugh in its stones. [491]
Mira lowers her pencil, understanding at last that the place where echoes are born is not a spot but a pause. [512]
Her commission was never to trap the echo, but to show the museum how a city can keep a sound alive by letting it rest. [537]
She had thought the place where echoes are born would be a cavern, a mirror, or a machine, but it is a rhythm that can be interrupted. [564]
The infinity symbol pendant dims, having returned the borrowed time, and Mira feels lighter, as if a second has been paid back. [586]
The natural history illustrator closes her sketchbook and bows to the water, to the towers, and to the giant's waking breath. [607]
Below, the canals begin to sing, and each note is a line of her drawing moving through the city. [626]
Mira leaves Sarn with the pendant cold in her pocket, carrying the first true illustration of a sound she can hear. [647]
