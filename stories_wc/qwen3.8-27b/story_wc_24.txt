The hydraulic clock shepherd lived at the end of a narrow pier in fjord quiet. [15]
Her name was Inga, and she was eminently peculiar among the station workers. [28]
Every morning, after blowing the whistle, she opened the gauge doors and listened to water moving. [44]

Its main purpose was to record when the waterline met the sky. [72]
Inga believed the station measured not distance, but the patience of light. [84]
That belief led her to understand what clocks really measure. [94]

Inside, a brass hand trembled, pointing to a time that had not happened. [166]

Back three: the clock had been running, and the fjord had been still. [297]

Inga did not fear it; she had been looking for it all her life. [566]
