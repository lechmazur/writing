You wake in the lighthouse prison off a jagged reef, where the sea keeps its dead in iron cages and the lantern turns above your bed. [26]
You are the cry wolf boy, and you are the shepherd who cried wolf's son, marked by a lie your father told to save one sheep and lose a village's trust. [57]
The chapel beside the lamp room has no priest, only a lattice from confessional hanging in the beam, its wooden bars worn smooth by decades of whispered sins. [85]
It sustains impossibilities, so you press a story against it the way a sailor tests a rope: the false does not sink, but waits for your hand to give it weight. [116]
You are chasing hush, that low copper sound after a confession, when the storm forgets its own name and the reef lies flat beneath the light. [142]
At night the tower's copper panels glow in copperlight repose, and the prison seems less a cage than a cradle rocking on the dark water. [167]
The village says you are the wolf, because your father called a wolf when no wolf came, and because the sheep went missing on the night he died. [195]
But the night was not simple, and the tower's old doors remember more than the island's gossip, so you go down in the dark with the lattice under your arm. [225]
You find your way through the scuffs on a narrow door left ajar for decades, where a wolf scratch, a man drag, and your father's thumb mark the same low line. [256]
The scuffs are not marks of a hunt, but marks of a guide, as if something had been led through here, turned, and left with a promise. [283]
You came to thread a labyrinth with your own story, beginning at the false alarm and ending at the real one, because a lie told only once becomes a room with a locked door. [317]
Each loop of the lattice asks a question, and each answer lets you hover above the guilt, not free from it, but able to see the next turn. [345]
The first loop asks why your father spoke, and you answer that he heard the gulls scream and saw the light bend wrong through the fog. [371]
The second loop asks what followed, and you answer that the real wolf came later, not to kill, but to drink from the cistern where the children hid. [399]
The third loop asks why the tower kept its mouth shut, and you answer that the keeper had locked the sea gate to save a ship, and the ship's crew took the key. [432]
The lattice holds the impossible shape of your father's voice, the wolf's howl, the mother's scream, and the sea's quiet receipt for all three. [456]
You do not run, because the story already ran, and now you must follow the trail it left like a man walking through his own echo. [482]
The scuffs lead down to a door that was never opened in living memory, and beyond it the reef's hollows echo with breath that is not yours. [509]
You thread the thread through the last bar, and the false door in your mind clicks shut, while the real door, old and worn by salt, opens onto a cave. [539]
Inside, the bodies are not where the islanders fear they are, but they are not gone either, and before they find the bodies you understand what the wolf took and what the lie kept. [573]
The wolves are alive in the upper hollow, thin but breathing, and the sheep bones lie lower in the sand, kept from the reef by a scent your father laid down with his last breath. [608]
You knew this from the lattice, which had held the impossible shape of mercy, and you know it now from the live pulse under the wet rock. [635]
You take the lattice down, weave your story into its bars, and place it across the narrow doorway like a confession that can be read by sea and land. [664]
Then you hover on the gallery rail, one hand on the lantern, and let the light find the cave, the cave find the truth, and the truth find its way home. [695]
The village boats come at dawn, but they come to a prison that no longer needs a lock, and to a boy who has turned his name into a key. [725]
You are no longer the cry wolf boy, because you told the wolf a truer lie: the one that opens instead of closing, and asks to be forgiven by the sea. [756]
