After his shadow detached from his heels at dusk and rose upward like a kite cut loose, the crafty lantern maker found he could no longer walk the slate paths of his floating tea garden without an absence trailing behind him. [41]
The garden itself floated, moored among pale clouds by ropes of braided lightning, and each morning he climbed the terraced steps to prune the jade-leafed tea and listen to the small bells that grew among the branches. [78]
He had always been a careful man, deft with wire and beeswax, but grief had made him craftier than his own hands could account for. [103]
The night the shadow came loose had been no different from any other, except that a name he was not ready to speak had risen in his throat and refused to settle, and he had felt himself descend into a hush while something inside him climbed toward the stars. [152]
In the days that followed he carried that feeling with him the way other men carry lanterns: openly, carefully, with the wick turned down. [176]
He polished small river gems against his thumb until they sang, and the humming filled the hours his companion used to occupy. [198]
Each stone he finished he threaded with copper wire and hung in the tea bushes so that, in the early hours, light fell through them in patches of bruised gold. [228]
The garden became a chapel of his own making, luminous and full of torment, every polished cabochon a small unsaid word he had managed to set down without breaking. [257]
He was descending, he thought, into the slower rhythms of patience, and yet something in him was ascending too, mounting on the heat of his own attention, becoming larger than the grief that had hollowed him. [293]
It was during one such polishing hour that the dream first found him. [306]
He had placed a violet garnet against the whetstone until it began to glow, and when he closed his eyes the floating garden tilted, dissolved, and reassembled itself as a glass basilica drifting above an even higher sea of cloud. [346]
The basilica had no roof, only a crown of ribs that caught the wind and sang a long, single note, and inside it he could see other people sleeping on benches of pale ice, their breath curling into the rafters as if drawn upward in ink. [392]
A clerk in a long gray coat moved among them, dipping a quill into a basin that glimmered as if filled with the same garnet-light, and wherever the quill touched a sleeper's temple a small silver thread spooled out and was wound onto a wooden reel. [438]
He understood at once that he was watching his own dream being recorded, and the understanding made him feel neither seen nor unseeable, but both at once, like a bell rung in reverse. [471]
When he woke the violet garnet still glowed between his fingers and the garden had returned around him, faithful and floating. [492]
He lit a beeswax taper and began, then, the patient labor he had at last understood: he would transform grief into a thing with wings. [517]
From a sheet of mulberry paper he made a crane, then another, and another. [531]
With each fold he spoke a name: hers, the one his throat would not release; his mother's; his father's; the names of friends lost across years of gentle weather. [560]
The cranes, when finished, he set adrift on warm updrafts from the garden's hearth-vent, and they rose through the lanterns and the gem-strung tea so that the whole sky above the floating isle filled with a slow migration of paper grief, turning as they went, catching the light he had polished into them.
