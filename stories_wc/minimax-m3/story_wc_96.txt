The coffee tin clinked against my hip, its secret false bottom holding what no one in this city would suspect of a vagrant penny poet. [25] I had walked the cracked pavement from the prison gates to the rusted library under stars, and the moon had just cleared the skyline when I reached the broken doors. [55] A friendship had sparked sorting books at a prison library six weeks earlier, when Marisol and I had first spoken over a battered copy of Neruda and the slow alphabetizing of broken spines. [88] She had called me a coward when she learned I was a vagrant, then a saint when she learned I read to the other inmates, and by the second week she had called me neither and we were just friends. [128] I had warned her about my strangeness, the way I molted personalities monthly, shedding one skin for another like a snake that could not decide what it was. [156] She laughed and said everyone in this place was rebuilding themselves, that the difference was I had the honesty to admit it. [178] That night in the rusted library under stars, we sat among shelves that sagged like old ribs and read aloud by the thin light, and I told her about the coffee tin. [210] I pried it open and showed her the false bottom, where I kept a small cloth bag of wildflower seeds, the kind that would push through asphalt if you let them. [241] We went out into the courtyard, where the bricks had cracked open years ago and weeds had already begun their slow riot, and we scattered the seeds like a quiet conspiracy. [272] This was blooming resistance, I told her, the only kind of war I knew how to fight, the only kind worth a vagrant's time. [296] She asked what I wanted from it, and I said I wanted to forge beauty from anarchy, to make something the guards would have to walk around. [323] She said she would rebuild, when she got out, she would rebuild her mother's kitchen and the back porch and the dog she had lost, and that this garden was a beginning. [355] I said I rebuilt myself each month, that the tin stayed the same but the man who carried it always changed, and that change was the only prayer I trusted. [385] We worked in the courtyard until the moon cleared the skyline and the rusted library under stars was lit silver through its broken roof. [409] There was a tone to our silence that I can only call luminous fracture, the way a broken bottle catches streetlight and makes a cathedral out of a curb. [438] When the seeds sprout, I told her, the guards will pull them, but the roots will remain, and something will bloom in the cracks they leave behind. [465] She put her hand on the coffee tin and said that was the most vagrant thing I had ever said, and I told her that was the finest penny anyone had ever paid me. [499] The next month I was someone else, as I had promised, but I still carried the tin, and the seeds we had planted were already cracking the asphalt by the time I came back. [533] Marisol was gone by then, transferred, but the rusted library under stars still held what we had made, and the wildflowers were a small riot no one had yet bothered to pull. [565] I knelt and pressed my palm to the soil, and I could feel the roots reaching down through the broken brick, stubborn and luminous, and I knew we had built something the guards would have to walk around for years. [605] The tin was lighter now, emptied of seeds, but heavier with what we had done, and I walked back into the cracked streets as the moon cleared the skyline again, a vagrant penny poet still, carrying a small riot in my pocket.
