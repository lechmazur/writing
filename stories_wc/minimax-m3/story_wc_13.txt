Maren had been a natural history illustrator for nineteen years, and in all that time she had never once drawn a creature she could actually see. [26]
Instead, she sketched the intervals between them: the flicker where a moth decides to land, the held breath of a tide, the precise and trembling pause a leaf makes before it lets go of its branch. [62]
It was for this peculiar gift that she had come to the city of Roost, a vertical tangle of slate towers laced together by a rooftop canal system weaving between their spines like silver sinew. [97]
The canals, she had been told, carried not water but memory, and somewhere in their slow circulation lived the place where echoes are born. [121]
She had been searching for that place since her grandmother died, and the infinity symbol pendant she wore against her throat had been the woman's parting gift, forged from a sliver of frozen moonlight and a single tear of hers. [161]
It was no ornament. [165]
When Maren pressed the pendant to the soft hollow beneath her jaw, she could taste the hours ahead of her, and by borrowing time she could keep a moment alive beneath her brush as long as she needed. [203]
This was how she sculpted time itself, drawing its grain the way another artist might draw the curl of a fern or the barb of a feather. [230]
The canals of Roost flowed to the rhythm of something older than the city, an interrupted cadence that the oldest inhabitants still spoke of in whispers. [256]
Beneath the foundations of every tower slept a giant, curled around the bones of an even older world, and the giant's dreaming breath pushed the water up and down through the copper veins of the upper town. [293]
Across the giant's nap, Maren had climbed from spire to spire, following the rise and fall of that pulse, listening for the place where the rhythm faltered. [320]
She had come to uncage something, though she did not yet know what it was. [335]
Every drawing she had ever made had taught her that caged things leave traces, the way a trapped bird teaches a room the shape of silence, and so she followed the traces the city had left for her: a chip of stone here, a bent railing there, a single feather caught in a downspout. [389]
They all led to the tallest tower, where the canals narrowed into a single silver ribbon and the pulse of the giant was loudest. [413]
She climbed the last ladder as the sun set, and when she reached the rooftop pool, the water was still as a held breath. [437]
Maren knelt at the lip and listened. [444]
The cadence was unmistakable: a great slow inhale through the stones, a long exhale back into them, and between them a silence so heavy it bowed the surface of the canal into a dimple. [478]
It was inside that silence, she understood, that the first echo was folded, trapped since the moment the world had learned to throw its voice against itself. [505]
To free it she would have to interrupt the cadence, slip a silence into the middle of the giant's breath, and the only way she knew to do that was by borrowing time. [538]
She pressed the pendant to her tongue and drew the next minute out of tomorrow's dawn, swallowing it the way her grandmother had taught her, and the rooftop dimpled deeper as the pulse beneath her began to slow. [576]
She took a second, then a third, and the canal water thickened around her wrists like cold honey. [594]
Maren opened her sketchbook, charcoal in hand, and began to draw the cadence itself: the inhale rising, the exhale falling, the long quiet between them. [619]
On the next rise she lifted the charcoal and let the line break, splitting one continuous stroke into two with a gap no wider than a beetle's eyelash. [647]
The drawing stuttered. [650]
Far below the city, the giant's breath caught, and in the caught space the caged echo slipped loose from the rhythm that had held it for centuries. [677]
It rose through the copper veins and the slate spines and the silver canals, a single tone that was not a tone at all but the clean ringing memory of every sound that had ever bounced off stone and returned. [717]
Maren caught it on her page and held it there, borrowing one more second, then another, until the sound had dried into ink the color of dawn. [744]
She had crossed a giant's long dreaming to find this, and now the rooftop was quiet and the canals were beginning to move again. [768]
The pendant cooled against her throat, and Maren closed her sketchbook, knowing that somewhere in the world a bird she had never seen had just decided to land. [796]
