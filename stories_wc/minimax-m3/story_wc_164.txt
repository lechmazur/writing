Mira was a sea-foam recollectionist, which meant she collected the bright frothy memories that washed up from forgotten coastlines, and she worked inside the iridescent cave of a giant crystal geode. [31] The geode hummed softly around her, its walls throwing back colors that had no names in any spoken language. [50] She carried a shard of crater glass, smooth and ancient, which had been forged in a moment of impact long before the first tide. [74] Through it, she could see what others could not: the shapes of feeling, transliterated into curling glyphs of light. [93] This morning, however, the glyphs were dim, and one section of the cave wall was simply not there. [111] "Missing pieces," she murmured, and the word tasted of brine and old lullabies. [124] Mira had spent her whole life chasing such absences, trying to discover why some paths disappear from the world without warning. [145] A path was a kind of memory, she believed, and a memory was a kind of breadcrumb left by those who walked before. [168] If a path vanished, someone had stopped walking, or the breadcrumb had been swept away. [183] She knelt beside the empty place in the wall and pressed her crater glass against it. [199] The shard began to glow, painting the air with loose, jitterting sentences: a laugh that fell, a name half-said, a doorway closing. [221] Mira read the transliterated emotions aloud, and the cave answered with a soft, cheerful chime, as though glad to be heard. [242] She smiled, though the smile was a bit hollow, because she knew that joy and sorrow in such places were never far apart. [265] Outside, she had heard, a great migrating bird passed through the sky once every hundred years, and wherever it paused, it dropped a rain of seeds. [291] She had collected some of those seeds in a small felt pouch, and now she took them out, one by one, and set them along the edge of the missing wall. [322] It was her method, this strange planting, to conjecture where the lost path had gone by reading the way the seeds settled. [344] If a seed rolled to the left, the path had bent west; if it rolled right, east; if it balanced on its end, the path had climbed. [371] The first seed balanced. [375] The second tumbled. [378] The third, to her delight, sprouted a tiny silver leaf that pointed, unmistakably, downward into the geode floor. [395] "Downward," Mira whispered, and her crater glass pulsed with a pale rose hue, which she knew meant hope tangled with grief. [416] She knelt and pressed her ear to the crystalline floor and heard, very faintly, footsteps walking in a corridor that had once led somewhere bright. [441] The path had not been destroyed, only buried beneath the slow growth of the geode itself, and those who had walked it had simply forgotten the way back. [469] Mira felt the cheerful gloom of her profession settle warmly around her shoulders, the same feeling she always felt when she found a thing and lost a thing in the same breath. [501] She had to work quickly, before memory faded, before even the footsteps in the floor dissolved into the ordinary hum of crystal. [523] With steady hands, she carved a small door into the floor of the cave using her crater glass, and the glyphs around it spelled a single word: return. [551] Then she planted another seed in the new threshold, so that whoever came after her might have something to follow when the next migrating bird passed overhead. [578] She tucked her shard away, brushed the sea-foam dust from her knees, and stood beneath the iridescent arch with her felt pouch lighter and her heart a little heavier. [607] Outside the geode, the world was ordinary and grey, but Mira carried inside her a small, bright map of where the missing pieces had once been, and that, she thought, was enough to begin again tomorrow.
