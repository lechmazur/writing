The orchard had been her brother's idea, and now it was hers, which meant it was cursed and it was hers. [21]
Old Maren had written the word curse three times in the ledger and underlined it, and Maren was the last to walk out of the cliffside labyrinth breathing. [49]
The labyrinth ran between the apple rows like a dropped stitch, fenced on one side by the cliff and on the other by hawthorn, and at every junction a mechanical bird ticked on its perch and watched the gaps with a glass eye. [92]
She had inherited the title of caretaker the way she had inherited the boots in the mudroom: without asking, in the wrong size, after the funeral. [118]
She was reluctant in the way of a woman who did not want any of this and tended it anyway because someone had to, because the night frost was forming and anyone still inside the rows would not walk out. [158]
The blueprint had been Maren's, scribbled on the back of a seed packet and lost under the granary boards for thirty years, and she found it behind a husk of small nuts while sweeping the cider press. [195]
The paper had gone soft at the creases but the ink was stubborn, and the lines were offhandedly exact in the way of someone who had walked the rows so often she no longer counted her steps. [232]
She spread the paper flat on the press and stared at it until she understood her aunt's design. [250]
The labyrinth was not really a trap. [257]
It was a tongue. [261]
The mechanical birds were teeth, the hedges were gums, the cliff a throat, and the orchard was swallowing anyone who wandered in from the coastal road above. [288]
Maren, graceless in her beauty, had drawn the throat open in the blueprint and left a path marked with the impressions of her feet pressed into the soft clay at every corner. [320]
There was a traveler now, calling for his dog, and the dog had already been taken by a different row. [340]
She took up the blueprint and a small nut from the press and went out among the apple trees. [359]
The wind off the cliff smelled of iron and of something sweet she did not want to name. [377]
She did not touch him. [382]
That was the rule, set down in Maren's graceless, beautiful hand: you cannot guide a lost traveler by the hand, because the labyrinth reads your hand as another walker and takes you too. [415]
So she had to leave impressions without touching, the way her aunt had. [428]
She pressed her boot into the clay at the first turn and again at the second, and at the third turn she set the small nut into a knot of hawthorn so the brass bird would tick past it, because the birds counted things that gleamed and the nut, when the frost touched it, would gleam. [484]
She moved down the row and the impressions darkened behind her, and the traveler stumbled along them, and the brass birds tracked her but the birds were looking for hands, for warmth, not for bootprints in cold clay. [522]
She assembled the path the way Maren had assembled the blueprint, piece by piece, with a stone here and a pressed heel there, and the labyrinth opened around her like a held breath. [555]
By the time she reached the seventh turn, the traveler had stopped calling for his dog and had started following the trail of darker clay toward the gap in the hawthorn. [586]
He was a thin man with a salt-stiff coat. [595]
He stepped past her with his eyes on the ground, and she stood behind him with one hand on the bark and the other holding another small nut. [623]
She pressed the nut into a crack in a fencepost, and the bird above them ticked once and went back to counting, and the traveler walked on through the gap and out onto the coastal path. [659]
The frost came up the cliff like a white hand and crept through the labyrinth, sealing the clay and the cracks and the nuts, and the mechanical birds quieted as if they had always been waiting to be stilled. [698]
She walked home along the row with the blueprint folded against her chest, and the orchard behind her was graceless and beautiful and full of swallowed strangers who would, in the morning, be guided out by the gleam of small nuts in the frost and by the prints of boots that had not belonged to anyone they would meet.
