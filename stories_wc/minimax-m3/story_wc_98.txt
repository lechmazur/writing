<story>Mara climbed the ancient ridge at dawn, her satchel bumping against the gnarled staff that marked her as the island's only reality checker. [23] The path was not a path at all, but a memory of one, stitched from moss and the wishes of those who had walked it before. [49] Every violet and trembling orchid along the slope carried a fragment of the island's collective memory, and every flower she passed whispered a name she had to check against the ledger in her head. [83] She was hired to be empirically whimsical, a phrase her grandmother had invented and the council had written into law, meaning she must weigh each marvel with a hand that was half scholar and half child. [119] The ridge was in bloom because it was almost time, and almost time meant the monsoon was only three suns away. [140] When the rains came, the blooms would melt into the mud, and with them, the dreams that held the island's history would dissolve into a forgotten hum. [167] Mara had come to waken something older than herself, a sleeper buried in the heart of the ridge, whose breath was the reason the flowers had not yet fallen. [196] She had the knowledge fruit seed, a small nut no larger than a thimble, which the elders had pressed into her palm at the festival of the first star. [225] They told her that the seed would only sprout if it were planted in a dream that had been correctly interpreted, and only then would the sleeper stir. [253] Mara found the place where the ridge folded like a sleeping back, and there she ate the seed, for the seed was meant to be consumed, not buried, and the dream it carried would be a memory made solid inside her. [294] She lay down among the orchids, and the blossoms leaned toward her, curious and polite, and the air filled with a luminous doubt that smelled of pepper and rain. [323] She closed her eyes, and the dream came as a tide of names, each one a bell that rang in the marrow of her bones. [348] There was old Halia who had woven the first net, and the boy Ren who had taught the gulls to sing the harvest in, and the councilor Iru who had once argued with a star and lost, and Mara had to test each one, had to weigh them in her half-scholar hand to see if they were true. [406] Some names rang hollow, and she let them go, and the dream thinned in those places like a worn cloth. [426] Others rang so clear that her teeth ached with their sweetness, and these she gathered into the soft place behind her sternum where the seed had begun to unfurl. [455] When the dream was finished, the seed had become a small tree in her chest, its roots threaded through the bones of her ribs, its branches brushing the underside of her lungs. [487] The sleeper in the ridge felt the new growth and began to stir, and the ground beneath her hummed with a sound like a hundred softly struck gongs. [515] Mara opened her eyes and saw that the orchids had turned toward her like a crowd, and the petals of each one held a tiny face, and every face wore the expression of someone who had just remembered a thing they had almost forgotten. [559] The ridge itself began to breathe in earnest, and the earth cracked in long, even lines, and from each crack a small green shoot pushed upward, and each shoot unfurled into a leaf on which was written a name, a song, a recipe, a small forgiveness, an old joke that had kept an entire village alive through one lean winter. [619] The knowledge fruit seed, having been a dream and now become a tree inside her, was also becoming a grove across the ridge, and the collective memory of the island, which had been about to drown, was being planted instead in the soil, and Mara could feel the doubt in her chest glow like a coal that would not go out, warm and questioning and alive. [685] When the monsoon finally came, three suns later, the rain fell on the leaves and did not wash the words away, and the island remembered, and the rememberers went on remembering, and Mara, the reality checker, walked back down the ridge with a tree in her chest and a head full of half-checked, half-believed, wholly cherished wonders.
