<story>The old bloodhound named Ash lay stretched on a bed of coiled wire in the dim reach of what had been Highway 9 before the last resource war ended everything. [30] Maren crouched beside her, the tips of her fingers brushing the ridge of scarred muscle that ran down her shoulder, and thought of the long winters when her hound had led search parties through heaps of twisted metal. [68] Tinhaven, the scrapyard settlement, had spread itself across the cracked wasteland like something that no longer remembered it had once been a road. [91] Tents of patched truck tarp leaned against the carcasses of bulldozers, and chimneys built of stacked carburetors breathed thin grey up into a sky that never fully cleared. [119] Throughout the long quiet, the wild hush that had come after the fighting seemed to keep changing its shape across the hours. [141] Maren kept an autumn leaf pressed inside a slab of beeswax on the shelf above her cot. [158] It had fallen from a tree that no longer stood, the one her daughter had climbed the week before the treaties collapsed and the long road out had closed behind them. [189] The wax had yellowed but the leaf remained a sharp red against it, and sometimes in the afternoons the low sun would shine through the cracks in the wall and the leaf would bleed colour into the dust of the floor. [230] She had come to call those small hours the tinted revelations, because something true seemed to cross the room in those minutes and settle on the boards without a sound. [260] Ash lifted her head when the tin phonograph began to turn. [271] The machine had been pulled from a buried listening-post in the eastern junk, and Maren had spent two winters learning to read the tiny scratches etched into the grooves along its surface. [303] To her, the cylinder was a book written by a language that had chosen its books to be round, and only those who knew how to tilt an ear to the right angle could hear them. [339] She had come to learn, through patient listening, that the secret texts in those grooves were not music at all but instructions for how to translate distance into closeness, which was the only work she had left in the world. [380] Who am I supposed to become now, she asked the dog one morning, as if the question were a riddle and Ash were the keeper of its key. [408] Ash, who read Maren the way rain reads pavement, gave no answer at first, only settled her chin against the old woman's knee and sighed in a way that Maren recognized as a kind of knowing. [444] But then she whined softly, one short and two long, and Maren heard it as the intuitively descriptive sound of a mind that already knew, and she said, You are already becoming what I am. [481] The leaf in wax seemed, for a moment, to hold the colour of her daughter's red sweater, and Maren understood that the distance between them was not nearly as wide as the leaf was flat. [516] The phonograph whispered its small scratched wisdom in answer, and Ash leaned in closer, and the wild hush that filled Tinhaven did not feel empty anymore but felt instead like the held breath of something waiting. [552] On the hundredth morning after the autumn they had chosen to count from, Ash rose from her bed for the first time in a season. [577] Maren watched the old hound pick her way to the wax shelf, take the leaf between her teeth with the gentleness of one returning a borrowed thing, and lay it in the open palm of Maren's hand. [614] It was the closest translation she had ever known, a body that had spent its life finding others finally bringing something home to the one who had been waiting, and the wild hush of the settlement seemed to soften around them like the wax around the leaf. [661] The phonograph's grooves caught the morning as if they too had been waiting, and the coloured light that had been only a passing thing settled, just for a breath, on the wax and the leaf and the slow hand above them. [702] Outside, the wind off the wasteland carried the smell of sage and the low hum of a settlement that, at long last, no longer feared the war's return. [730] Maren closed her fingers around the leaf, and somewhere in the cracked wasteland behind her, a small red light in the wax bled across the boards and was, for once, exactly the colour of a child's voice calling from very far away.
