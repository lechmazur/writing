<story>After the last echo of laughter fades from the upstairs parlor, Mira descends. [13] She carries her case of vine charcoal and a single sheet of paper she has kept folded against her ribs for eleven years. [36] Her fingers are the darkest part of her, blackened to the second knuckle, and they leave small ghosts on every doorknob she touches. [59] The stairwell behind her is colder than the cellar ahead. [69] She does not look back. [74] The subterranean crystal cave breathes around her as she pushes through the low stone door. [89] Pale spires rise from the floor in clusters, each one humming faintly with the memory of light it has held and lost. [111] She has been here only once before, the night her sister went missing. [124] The cave remembers her too. [129] The crystals brighten a fraction, as if greeting her with the soft, mournful warmth of dawn breaking over snow. [148] This is auroral sorrow, she thinks, and the phrase fits so well she almost laughs. [163] Mira is mnemonically gifted. [167] She can summon a face she glimpsed once in passing forty years ago and reproduce it in exact charcoal before a candle wanes. [190] This blessing has become her heaviest thing. [197] Every portrait she draws sharpens the ache of the one face she cannot bring back whole. [213] Her sister's smile arrives in fragments - the left corner first, then the small mole near the ear, then the teeth, never assembled into a single expression. [239] She has filled sketchbooks trying to rectify this small injustice, to gather the pieces into one complete mouth, and every attempt ends in a smudge where the smile should be. [269] Mira knows she is chasing a ghost. [276] She draws anyway. [279] Tonight she has come because the calling has become inescapable. [289] It began as a pressure behind her eyes, weeks ago, when she finally unpacked the old wooden case her sister had pressed into her hands the morning she disappeared. [318] Inside was a small oil painting of a woman holding a key, and behind the canvas, tucked into the hollowed frame, a folded letter. [342] The letter was sealed with a broken combination lock, the kind that once opened a child's diary. [359] The dial would not turn. [364] Three numbers, scratched into the tarnished brass, hinted at a sequence Mira could not guess. [379] She tried every combination she could remember - birthdays, addresses, the year of the cave - and the lock would not give. [399] So she brought the painting here, to the only place that had ever answered her. [414] She sets her easel among the spires. [421] The crystals lean toward her as she works, drawn by the soft scraping of charcoal, the whisper of her breath. [441] She begins the portrait: her sister's face, but slower than before. [452] She lets the line of the jaw settle. [460] She lets the eyes find their true shape. [468] The cave hums, and as she works, the crystals begin to glow the color of apricots at the edge of morning, the color of grief that has begun, finally, to forgive itself. [500] Halfway through the left eyebrow, the broken lock on the painting's frame clicks. [513] She had not touched it. [518] The cave had. [521] The brass dial rotates, slowly, of its own accord - one number, two, three - and the seal pops open. [539] The letter uncurls on the floor like a sleeping bird. [549] Mira reads it with the patience of someone who has spent a lifetime decoding faces. [564] Her sister's handwriting, careful and slanted: I hid my paintings behind my paintings. [577] Look behind the one I gave you. [584] I am sorry I could not stay. [591] Mira lifts the canvas from its frame. [598] Taped to the back, a second, smaller painting: her sister's true self-portrait, drawn the morning she left. [615] In it, her sister is smiling. [621] Whole. [622] Mira weeps, but quietly, so as not to wake the cave. [633] Then she sits again. [637] Her charcoal finds the paper. [642] She begins, at last, to uncork the creativity she has kept stoppered inside her chest since she was twelve years old. [663] She draws, and the crystals listen, and the sorrow becomes a kind of light. [677] Long after her candle has burned to a stub, she is still drawing. [690] The cave does not let her go until she is ready, and even then it hums after her as she climbs the stairs, charcoal-fingered, lighter than she came. [718]</story>
