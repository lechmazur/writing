Vael stood at the mouth of the canyon of endless echoes, where the wind itself seemed to learn new words each morning. [22]
The towers of bone rose behind him in jagged white spires, and the stone walls of the canyon were carved with a thousand listening holes that made every whisper into a choir. [54]
He was a copper-veined storyteller, his skin threaded with the same reddish ore that ran through the cliffs, and he had watched across the death of a language as the Old Verdict faded from the tongues of his people. [93]
He pressed the flawed diamond against his palm, feeling the small flaw in its heart like a scar that refused to heal, and the stone had been passed down to him as a cursed thing, a focus for echoes that should never be summoned. [137]
The canyon had earned its name from the people who fled it, a tunnel of echoes where even the dead could be heard arguing with the living. [164]
Vael had learned to read the patterns in a flock of birds in flight as if they were punctuation in an unwritten scripture, and today he would harness those patterns to do what his grandfather had failed to complete. [203]
The Old Verdict was a tongue built not from grammar but from texture, a way of speaking that used the roughness of bark or the slickness of river stones as vowels, and his life's work had been to translate texture into sound before the last speakers forgot how to feel the words in their mouths. [258]
He walked deeper into the canyon, quietly articulate in his movements, each step placed so the echoes would carry his presence as a soft drumming instead of a shout. [287]
At the center of the canyon floor lay a pool of still water that reflected nothing but the sky, and above it, the towers of bone curved inward like the ribs of some colossal sleeper. [322]
He unwrapped the diamond from its silk and held it up, and the flaw inside it caught the light and threw a crooked line across the stones, a line that only he could read as the first syllable of a prayer. [363]
Birds came then, black wings against white bone, wheeling in formations that shifted as the wind shifted, and Vael watched them trace the grammar of a speech that no human throat could make. [396]
He threw his voice into the canyon, shaping the air with his hands the way a potter shapes clay, and the echoes returned the sounds to him doubled and redoubled, each repetition wearing away the silence that had settled into the Old Verdict over generations. [441]
The diamond trembled in his grip, the curse within it straining against his intent, but he pressed it to his chest and let the flaws in the stone drink the echoes like a sponge drinks water. [477]
The flock wheeled once, twice, three times, and Vael read their turning as the closing clause of a sentence his grandmother had begun. [500]
He spoke then, not in words but in the textures he had gathered all his life, the grit of the canyon floor, the smoothness of the river stones, the sharp bite of winter air, and the canyon answered him back in the dying tongue, each echo a breath drawn into a lung that had nearly stopped. [556]
By sunset, the Old Verdict had been spoken once more, complete and whole, carried into the waiting stone and woven into the patterns the birds would fly tomorrow. [584]
Vael closed his eyes and felt the diamond go cold in his hand, the curse within it finally satisfied, and he knew that the language would outlive him now, living on in the echoes of the canyon long after the last copper-veined storyteller had walked out into the silence beyond the bone towers.
