On the seventh night of the waxing moon, Mira climbed the rusted ladder to her rooftop greenhouse above the smog-choked city. [21]
The panes were clouded with soot, but she wiped a circle clean and let the pale light fall across her hands. [42]
She was an insomniac painter who had not slept in four days, and her eyes carried the bruised color of plum skins. [64]
Still, she moved through the narrow rows of pots with the careful patience of a woman who had decided to be methodically hopeful. [87]
Inside the greenhouse, the air tasted of wet earth and orange peel. [99]
She had hung dried saffron threads from the rafters to catch the moonlight, and they gave the quiet a warm, golden hum. [121]
Tonight she had brought something new with her, an old wooden box she had found in a thrift store. [140]
Inside, wrapped in black silk, lay a calligrapher's moon-tipped brush, its handle carved from pale bone and its bristles still holding a faint silver dust. [165]
She had bought it because the shopkeeper, a stranger with kind eyes, had told her that it once belonged to a scribe who copied the last words of dying languages. [195]
Mira did not believe in magic, but she believed in the kindness of strangers, and that was enough to bring her up the ladder night after night. [222]
She set the brush beside her easel and dipped it into a jar of water mixed with soot. [240]
Outside, the city coughed and murmured, but in here the only sound was the drip of condensation on the glass. [260]
She began to paint, not a picture but a series of small symbols that had been whispered to her by her grandmother before she forgot how to speak. [288]
Each curve was a word in a tongue that no one living could read, a lost language that had once carried the names of rivers and stars. [315]
She did not know if she was spelling anything true, but her hand moved with the slow certainty of someone practicing a forgotten prayer. [339]
As the waxing moon climbed higher, its light thickened into something almost tangible, pooling on the floorboards like spilled milk. [359]
Mira felt her eyes grow heavy, and the sleepless ache behind them loosened into something softer. [375]
She allowed herself to drift, and the daydreams came softly, sparked by the moonlight slanting through the saffron threads. [394]
In one dream, she stood in a market where people spoke in colors instead of words, and a child pressed a small clay bird into her palm. [421]
In another, she walked along a river whose water was made of whispered thank-yous from people she had never met. [441]
Each dream left her with a small, warm certainty that kindness was a thing that traveled, even when the mouths that gave it had gone silent. [467]
The visions roused her gently, like a hand on the shoulder, and she sat up with the brush still in her grip. [489]
She saw that she had drawn a symbol she did not recognize, a door opening or perhaps a mouth about to speak. [511]
Mira smiled, and the smile was methodically hopeful in the way only a tired person can manage. [528]
She decided then that she would take the painting down to the street below and pin it to the broken wall by the bus stop. [553]
She would not sign her name, because the point was not to be remembered but to prove that strangers remember kindness. [574]

The lost language did not need to be translated to be heard; it only needed to be offered. [618]

Tomorrow, perhaps, she would try to remember another word. [708]
Tonight, she had been enough. [713]
