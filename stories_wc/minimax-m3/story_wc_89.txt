<story>The orchard breathed silver under a moon that would never set. [11] After the phoenix had risen, the ashes still tasted of cinnamon and clove, and the trees remembered how to bloom. [31] Mira walked between the gnarled trunks, her hands bare to the night air, listening to the small hearts hidden under bark and leaf. [54] She had been a beast whisperer since she was old enough to hear the world chew its cud, but lately the beasts would not answer. [79] The orchard was a fairy-tale orchard blooming under permanent moonlight, every fruit a story half-remembered, the silver light laid like a quilt across the boughs. [104] Mira had come here after the phoenix had risen from the funeral pyre of her mentor, Old Vell, whose bones she had carried in a cedar box for three days through bramble and rain. [138] She did not know yet what the phoenix had meant by sending her here, but she knew she must listen. [158] The orchard smelled, that night, of cardamom and smoked pepper and the green sharpness of unripe quince, and Mira understood that the wind had begun to conjure. [185] The conjure was not a spell, exactly. [192] It was a scent-doctrine, an old recipe her mentor had taught her before he died, in which rare spices were ground and burned, for only via the scent of rare spices could one conjure the beasts who had forgotten their names. [233] Mira knelt in the loam, paradoxically steady, her palms flat to the earth, and let the perfume climb her arms. [253] She had been reluctant to learn this doctrine. [261] Reluctant metamorphosis was the price her line paid: to speak with beasts, she must unmake herself a little, season by season, until she was almost one of them. [289] She had always refused. [293] Until now. [295] She took the small paper twist from her pocket, the fortune from a cookie Old Vell had once broken with her at a winter market, and read again the cramped handwriting on the back: "When I am gone, walk where the moon forgets to wane." [340] She pressed the paper to her lips. [347] The spices smoked. [350] First came the hare that was no hare but a hare-shaped grief, leaping through the roots and weeping. [368] Then came the stag whose antlers were libraries, shaking his head to scatter the names of every animal he had eaten. [389] Then came the small fox who sat on her knee and was, Mira understood with a sob, her mentor in some new and unspeakable form. [414] "Why did you not tell me," she whispered, "that you would come back?" [427] The fox tilted his bright head. [433] "I did not come back," said the fox, in a voice that smelled of star anise. [449] "I was always here. [453] "You were the one who left." [459] Mira felt the reluctant metamorphosis begin in her sternum, a slow unfolding like a bud that knew it would never be a flower again, only something rarer. [486] Her skin remembered scales. [490] Her throat remembered song. [494] Her heart, paradoxically steady, beat with the rhythm of the orchard itself, and the moonlight leaned in to listen. [513] She was becoming a beast to speak with beasts. [522] She was becoming grief to speak with grief. [530] She was becoming the scent she had burned. [538] "I want," she said, and her voice came out in two registers, human and something older, "to reforge a bond beyond death." [560] The fox pressed his cool nose to her wrist. [569] "Then stop whispering," he said. [574] "Begin." [575] She breathed in the cinnamon, the clove, the cardamom, the smoked pepper, the star anise, and the orchard around her exhaled in answer, blossoms opening one by one like small pale bells. [607] Where her knees had knelt, roots began. [614] Where her palms had pressed, bark began. [621] Where her eyes had wept, two fruit hung heavy and sudden, each one glowing with the silver of the unmoving moon. [642] She was not lost. [646] She was, at last, the whisperer she had been afraid to be. [658] The fox lay down beside her new bole and closed his eyes, and somewhere in the orchard, the phoenix sang once more, not in fire but in the small, steady cracking of new wood. [692]</story>
