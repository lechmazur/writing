Vessa had not meant to reenter the grotto, not when the last attempt had cost her three winters of memory and a finger. [23]
But the manuscript margin doodle had surfaced again in the locked drawer, the same looping curl of ink she had once mistaken for a stain, and now she understood it as a map. [56]
The serpentine grotto in mist lay two ridges east of the abbey, coiled along the river's blind bend, a place that swallowed sound and gave back only the slow, breathing hush of moss. [89]
She arrived, as she always did, at the threshold hour, the one hour in which gravity inverts for an hour, when stones drift upward and the dew climbs the blades of grass instead of falling. [124]
As a prism-minded archivist, she had learned to read a place the way others read a page, splitting each shadow into its component lights, and so she saw the grotto not as stone but as a slow, patient lantern. [163]
The entrance was a mouth of ivy, half-choked, and stepping through it required the particular discipline of letting go, of making her body heavier than thought, so that the world would accept her as a falling thing and then, at the last possible instant, reverse her. [209]
This was threshold effects in their oldest form, the trick of the door that only opens for those willing to be heavier than air. [233]
Inside, the walls curled like sleeping serpents, and the mist hung in long, low ribbons that refused to settle even when the hour tipped. [257]
She had come for what she had failed to bring back before, the forgotten combinations, the locked grammars of a tongue that could name a thing and thereby summon its opposite. [288]
Vessa believed these combinations were still hidden somewhere in the grotto, perhaps in the bones of the rock, perhaps in the small, clandestine luminous patches that only appeared when the hour was at its deepest tilt. [324]
Now, standing again at the threshold, she felt the familiar pull of the place, the latent blaze that lived beneath its cool surface, as if the grotto itself were a banked fire waiting for someone to ask the right question. [364]
She had prepared, this time, with the seeds of vigilance sewn into the hem of her robe, a thread of watchful herbs that would alert her if her attention wandered. [394]
Vessa had learned, in the years since her last visit, that the grotto did not steal memories so much as borrow them, lending them out like library books, and the only way to reclaim what was hers was to remember, in vivid detail, what she had been before she entered. [444]
She took a breath, and stepped. [450]
For a long moment she fell upward through the ivy, and the world went dark, and then she was standing in the grotto again, her boots resting on the ceiling of the place she had just left. [487]
The hour of inverted gravity was already half spent. [496]
She moved quickly, her robe drifting behind her like a slow, dark fin, and found the place where the manuscripts had been kept, the hollowed stone shelf that smelled of cedar and old ink. [530]
There, scratched into the stone, was the doodle's twin, a curl that matched the one in the locked drawer, and beside it, a single word in a script she almost recognized. [561]
She pressed her palm to it, and the stone grew warm, clandestinely luminous from within, the glow leaking through cracks like the blush of an ember. [587]
The word on the stone was the first of the combinations she had come for, the name of the thing that holds the door open and the name of the thing that closes it, said together. [623]
She whispered them, both at once, and the grotto shuddered, and the mist drew back, and there, suspended in the air where the wall had been, were the rest. [652]
Each combination hung like a small, bright seed, a constellation of forgotten possibilities, and she gathered them with her hands, feeling them weightless and warm against her fingers. [680]
The hour was nearly ended. [685]
She felt the gravity begin to reassert itself, a slow, patient tug at her bones, and she hurried back through the threshold, the ivy parting for her like a curtain. [715]
When she stepped out, she was falling, properly, toward the earth, and the moss caught her, and the mist closed behind her like a book. [740]
Vessa walked home with her pockets full of light, and the seeds of vigilance stitched into her hem sang a small, quiet song against her ankle. [766]
