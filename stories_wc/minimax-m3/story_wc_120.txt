Soren Vael wrote fortunes that no one could quite read, cryptic slips of paper folded into pastries, because each one was really a small boat meant to ferry memory back across the years. [33]
He lived alone in a glass spire by a frozen lake, a tower so clear that from the shore it looked like a single upright breath waiting to be spoken. [30]
The silence around him had a strange tilt to it, an upturned hush that rose toward the rafters and hung there like dust caught in a slant of sun. [29]
Through the curved panes of the cupola he saw the dawn arriving, a thin rose seam stitching sky to water in the mirrored horizon that ruled his mornings. [28]
He had retreated here because he was chronologically homesick, his soul three decades behind his body, and quietly he meant to become the conductor of unheard symphonies no one yet believed in. [32]
In his pocket he kept a single nautilus shell segment, a curving fragment his grandmother had pressed into his palm the morning he left home for good. [27]
Tonight, or this morning rather, he unrolled butcher paper across the cupola floor to map his childhood by its rooms, his hallways, his forgotten tunes. [25]
He drew the kitchen first, mustard-yellow walls, his mother humming over bread, then the porch where the cat slept in a square of sunlight. [24]
He drew his father's study, the alley behind the bakery, the stairwell where his grandmother had warned him about exhaustion, that quiet fog that ate the edges of things. [29]
He had promised himself he would finish before exhaustion hit, and so he worked quickly, letting the nautilus segment serve as compass for every doorway. [25]
By midmorning the paper had grown into a city of soft rooms, and Soren stood among them with salt drying on his cheek, his palm warm against the spiral. [29]
He lifted the shell to his ear the way children do, expecting the hiss of a manufactured sea, but he heard only the lake humming beneath him. [27]
Outside, dawn cracked the eastern edge of the ice, and the mirrored horizon became a thin gold thread where two worlds pressed their lips together. [25]
He thought of the symphonies folded away in his boyhood, the small music of spoons and stairwells, and he understood they had been waiting for him. [26]
He straightened his hunched shoulders, raised the nautilus shell segment like a baton above his paper town, and drew a long breath through the spiral. [25]
The breath moved through the glass and rang against the ice below, and somewhere under the lake a slow note answered, rising like a bubble that had remembered which way was up. [32]
The upturned hush broke open, and in its place arrived something that sounded almost like a song, faint and patient, like a child calling from the far shore. [28]
Soren smiled, the first honest smile his face had made in many winters, and pressed his palm against the cold glass as if greeting a brother he had not seen in thirty years. [33]
He had mapped his childhood, he had carried his grandmother's spiral, he had kept his vigil until dawn, and now he understood that the conductor he had wanted to become had been waiting inside him all the while. [38]
The reflected Soren in the glass below also lifted his hand, and the mirrored horizon doubled him into a small chorus of men, each one beginning to remember his own first kitchen. [32]
He would write of this morning in his fortune slips later, each cryptic message carrying within it the small chord of a frozen lake and a shell curved like a spiral stair. [32]
By the time the sun had climbed the spire to its brightest seam, his shoulders had loosened, his paper town was finished, and somewhere far below the ice had begun to whisper the old song his mother hummed over bread. [40]
He folded the butcher paper carefully, slid it into the drawer beneath his writing desk, and tucked the nautilus segment beside it, where it would wait, faithful, until the next dawn. [31]
From the shore below, a stranger paused and looked up at the tower and thought he heard, very faintly, a melody rising out of the frozen lake and into the brightening sky. [32]
