Veris had never been surprised. [5]
That was the boast she carried into the Singing Grotto, where every crystal rang like a struck bell and the dark itself seemed to glow. [30]
A pilgrim had arrived the night before, a thin girl with a wooden flute and questions like coals, and the grotto had answered her with a long, low chord that Veris still felt humming in her back teeth. [68]
Veris came not for answers but for confirmation. [76]
She was a clairvoyant whose gift had made her famous in three counties; she could read the shapes of futures the way other women read embroidery patterns, and she had grown tired of the world refusing to surprise her. [115]
In the grotto, she planned to see what she had always seen, and to leave satisfied. [131]
But the grotto had its own way of seeing. [140]
The crystals did not merely ring; they listened. [148]
They listened the way a river listens to a thrown stone, gathering the ripples into a single, shifting note that hung in the air like a held breath. [176]
Unseen forces moved through the columns, bending the light into shapes that were never quite names. [192]
Veris recognized the trick. [196]
She had read it in the older grimoires, the ones whose pages tasted of iron. [211]
She smiled, and began to walk. [217]
The cave pearl waited at the grotto's heart. [225]
It was the size of a child's fist, pale as the underside of a moth, and it pulsed with a light that was not quite light. [251]
Pilgrims called it the Pearl of Listening. [258]
Veris had seen it before, in dreams, in the cracked mirror of a scryer's bowl, in the small moments before sleep when her third eye opened like a sore. [287]
She reached for it without hesitation. [293]
The grotto inhaled. [296]
Overlapping shadows slid down the columns and pooled around her wrist, cool as well water, and the pearl did not resist her. [318]
It simply moved into her, the way a song moves into a throat, the way a thought moves into a listener. [339]
Veris gasped, because the gift she had relied on for thirty years had been quietly, steadily unpredictable, and she had not, in all her seeing, seen this. [366]
Voices began to inhabit her. [371]
Not the dead, not the unborn, not the bright parade of futures she had grown used to watching like scenery from a moving cart. [395]
These were smaller voices. [399]
The first voice was a cook who could not remember her grandmother's recipe. [412]
The second was a child afraid of bees. [420]
The third was a man who had never told his wife he loved her, because he was sure she already knew. [441]
They did not speak in words. [447]
They pressed against the inside of her ribs like tired cats, and they hummed. [461]
Veris understood, with the slow clarity of a bell being struck, that the unseen forces of the grotto had not wanted her sight at all. [486]
They had wanted her attention. [491]
The pearl had magnified, not prophecy, but the small, ordinary magic that happened every morning when someone chose kindness over convenience, when a stranger held a door, when a song drifted up from a kitchen and made a passerby weep for reasons they could not name. [537]
She laughed, and the crystals answered. [543]
The sound was bright and dark at once, the way rain is bright on a window at night, the way a child's eye is dark with wonder. [570]
It was the tone of a place where every moment was a small miracle, and where miracles were never louder than a whisper. [593]
She had come to magnify everyday magic, and the grotto had taken her at her word. [609]
She left without the pearl, because the pearl had never been a thing. [622]
It had been a habit of listening. [629]
Behind her, the pilgrim with the wooden flute played a single note, and the crystals, for one held breath, did not ring. [651]
Then they rang, and the dark was bright, and the bright was dark, and Veris walked out into the morning with her third eye closed and her two ordinary eyes open, finally surprised. [684]
