Marlow catalogued the tides the way other performers counted tips, by feel and minute variations in the moon's pull on the pier. [22]
He stood between acts on the salt-warped boards of the Tidal Arcade, where the machines only worked when the water was at certain heights. [46]

The stars above were sharper than usual, the sharpness that comes only when the fog lifts and the air is full of brine. [88]
Marlow was a street performer of the analytical kind, watching crowds more than he played to them, noting who stopped and who kept walking, reading passersby by the angle of their shoulders. [120]
He was between acts, his viol stowed in its case, his hat placed upside down on the boards like a small boat waiting for rain. [145]
The hat was empty. [149]
It was always empty between acts. [155]
He had told himself, years ago, that the empty hat was part of the performance, the pause between two notes, the breath that other men called applause. [182]
He walked slowly along the row of arcade cabinets, the ones that hummed when the tide came in and clicked like mechanical starlings as it retreated. [208]
A woman in a green coat was trying to win a stuffed bear from a claw machine that was, at this hour, almost certainly rigged. [233]
The claw missed the bear by a finger's width, and the woman sighed and walked away, and Marlow noted the exact width of her sigh. [258]
He catalogued these things the way a chess player catalogues the squares. [270]
In his pocket he carried a bishop's chess piece, a small wooden thing with a mitre carved into its top, and he rolled it between his fingers as he walked. [300]
It was his instrument, in a way. [307]
Marlow believed in what he called orchestrated serendipity, the art of making chance look like fate. [323]
He was not a hustler. [328]
He was not a con man. [334]
He was, in his quiet way, a poet, and his poems were the small collisions of strangers. [351]
Between acts, when the tide was right, he would plant a coin in the claw machine, or hum three bars of a song someone across the arcade might recognize, then watch, lightly attentive, how the dominoes fell. [388]
He never forced anything. [392]
Forcing would have ruined it. [397]
He ordered randomness instead. [401]
He would, for instance, place the bishop's chess piece on a particular cabinet at a particular minute, and the woman who came along would happen to be the right woman, the one whose mother had taught her to play chess. [441]
Or he would tune his viol a quarter-tone sharp, and the man who heard it would happen to remember a quarter-tone sharpness from childhood, and the two of them would meet for a conversation neither had planned. [478]
These things happened, in Marlow's world, with the regularity of the tide. [490]
They were not accidents, but they were not quite planned, either. [501]
They were a third thing, something like music, something like weather. [512]

A wilderness, because it was wild. [556]
