Vesper had been the beacon-tower custodian for seventeen winters, ever since the sky went quiet and the rivers forgot their mouths. [21]

The glass humming pavilion rose from the rubble like a lung still learning to breathe, its crystal ribs catching the wind and singing in frequencies only the broken remembered. [50]

It had been the heart of the great beacon, a lens that drank the stars and poured their fire down into the valley so the lost could find their way home. [143]

Now, after the collapse of civilization, the valley held only thistle and the wandering bones of those who had not made it. [165]

But Vesper had never stopped believing that coincidences have meaning, that the crack in the prism and the pattern in the stars and the visions in her skull were threads of the same unraveling tapestry. [200]

It was a fragment no larger than a child finger, and when she lifted it the world fell into a hush so total that even her breathing became a rumor. [311]

This was the shard of hush, the seventh piece missing from her counting, the one the collapse had swallowed and the dust had hidden. [335]

In the sudden silence she saw what she had been waiting seventeen winters to see: the moment the prism broke, the hand that struck it, the reason why. [363]

The prism, broken and remade, drank the light and began to sing. [574]
