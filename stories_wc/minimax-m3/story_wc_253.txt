The wind came down from the turbines in long, deliberate sheets, and it never quite touched the farmhouse below. [19] Calla had been told, once, that the sound would become invisible to her. [32] The thrumming above was the anthem of the new era, the era after the Happiness Mandate, and she had stopped listening years ago. [55] She stood at the kitchen window and narrated her own movements, because the silence needed company. [71] "Now I wash the cup," she said, and the water ran over her knuckles, gray and then clear. [89] "Now I set the cup down." [95] The house accepted her voice without complaint. [102] Its walls were the color of old paper, and its floors remembered every footfall from before the Mandate, when grief had been a private craft rather than a regulated offense. [132] On the table, a dried flower bouquet leaned against a chipped saucer. [144] It had belonged to her brother. [150] She did not arrange it. [155] She only moved it when she needed the surface, and then she moved it back. [170] Whittling was her last allowed confession. [176] The knife was small and the shavings curled like sleeping creatures onto the boards. [190] "Now I sharpen," she announced, and the stone hissed. [199] "Now I begin." [202] What she made were not figures. [208] She made holes. [211] She carved tiny absences into blocks of apple wood and called them, with great seriousness, doors. [227] Her neighbors said she was a blank-faced performer, which was not entirely wrong. [240] She had trained for the theater in the years before the Mandate, and her face had learned to hold its shape through anything. [263] In the old days, audiences had mistaken her stillness for depth. [274] Now, after the Mandate, they mistook her stillness for compliance, which was why the wind authorities left her alone. [293] The turbines turned, and the world turned with them, and Calla felt nothing she was required to feel. [311] She had been declared happy twice, then declared suspicious once, then declared happy again. [325] Grief, however, had not been entirely legislated away. [333] It lived in the rooms where laughter used to be, and it hummed there like a moth in a jar. [353] She had decided, in the remade hush of her kitchen, that if grief would not leave, she would make it lighter. [374] This was her private heresy. [379] She picked up a new piece of wood and held it to the light. [393] The turbines threw their shadows across her hands, long and slow. [404] "Now I observe the grain," she said, and her voice was almost tender. [417] She thought of her brother, who had refused to perform happiness and had been taken for reconditioning. [434] He had come back, briefly, carrying a bouquet he had pressed from the last flowers in their mother's garden. [453] He had set it on this same table. [461] He had said, "Keep something that won't be improved." [470] The flowers were dry now, and the improvement had come. [480] Everything had been improved. [484] Calla began to carve. [488] "Now I make the first door," she said. [496] The knife slid along the grain and a pale curl fell. [507] "Now I make the second door." [513] Another curl. [515] A morally ambiguous idea was forming in her, the way knots form in old rope. [530] If she whittled enough doors into enough blocks of wood, she could fill the farmhouse with them, and the wind would whistle through. [553] The turbines would become an orchestra for a small, private music. [564] The Happiness Mandate had been repealed three seasons ago, but its echoes were still in the air, and people still walked with their faces arranged. [589] Calla, who had never lost the trick of arrangement, kept hers blank as a stage. [604] She was not sure what she was preparing for. [613] Perhaps for her brother to return again. [620] Perhaps for the wind to stop. [626] Perhaps for nothing at all, which was the most honest answer she could give. [640] "Now I count the doors," she said. [647] There were thirty-seven on the table, and she made the thirty-eighth. [658] "Now I set them by the window, where the shadow of the longest blade will pass through them at dusk." [678] The day thinned toward evening. [683] She narrated each small motion, and the house held her voice the way a shell holds the sea, distantly and with no opinion. [706] Outside, the turbines turned, indifferent and immense, and inside, the dried flowers leaned against the saucer, and Calla carved another door into another piece of wood, and the hush, which had been ruined once and remade by her own stubborn hands, was almost, almost light enough to bear.
