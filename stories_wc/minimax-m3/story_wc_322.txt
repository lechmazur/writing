<story>The gallery stretched in impossible directions, every corridor folding back on itself like a thought reconsidered. [16]
Mara had been hired to map it. [23]

Her commission came from a faceless patron whose name dissolved when she tried to recall it. [65]
The fee was simple: traverse the optical illusion gallery, document its impossible geometries, and return with a sample of whatever held it together. [88]
The walls were realistically imaginary, plaster and brick that flickered between ruin and pristine, remembered and invented, and her footsteps made no sound on marble that was also, somehow, wet earth. [119]
She carried a spool of sinew thread, pale and fibrous, wound from the tendons of forgotten dreams, and with it she intended to find the seams. [145]
The gallery was built on rational enchantment, a system of magic that obeyed its own laws with the patience of a careful equation, and Mara trusted it more than she trusted the shifting floor beneath her boots. [182]
As memories altered in the patrons who had walked here before, the gallery responded, its illusions redrawing themselves in real time, a slow, deliberate unfolding that felt less like magic and more like breathing. [216]
She entered the central atrium, lit by lanterns that burned with the color of regret, and there, suspended in the air, hung a tapestry made of nothing. [243]
Mara unspooled her thread. [247]

The first pattern was a spiral, and the wall behind her erupted into a door that had not been there before, its frame humming with a quiet, unsettling calm. [325]
She stepped through, and the room beyond was a library whose shelves rearranged themselves every time she blinked, books breathing in and out like sleeping ribs. [351]
