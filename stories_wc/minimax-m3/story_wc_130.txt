<story>The conjurer's name was never whispered in the upper towns, and this suited her, for she had chosen long ago to live among roots and dripping stone rather than among people. [31] She had made her home in a cavern orchard, where twisted fig and sour pomegranate trees climbed toward stalactites that wept mineral water into shallow, patient basins. [58] Tonight, after the orchard had grown silent beneath the moon, she knelt upon a mosaic stepping stone whose glaze had chipped away into wandering patterns of herons, rivers, and unfinished skies. [89] From her satchel she drew a taxidermist's glass eye pair, two pale spheres that had once belonged to a clockwork crow she had made, then loved, then lost in a way she could not name. [124] The crow had neither died in any clockwork sense nor flown into a sky she could point to, and that was the ambiguous loss she had spent seven seasons trying to understand. [156] It had simply stopped arriving at her wrist, where it liked to perch, and she had not known whether to mourn a death, a departure, or a slow forgetting. [185] She had decided that the only honest way to coax the truth loose was to understand the mathematics of emotion, her secret labor, conducted by candlelight while the stalactites kept their slow percussion. [218] The mosaic stepping stone, with its chipped patterns of birds and rivers, was her instrument, for she had learned to read the missing tesserae as a quiet grammar of what had slipped away. [251] Tonight she meant to dovetail the glass eyes into the pattern, to fit them into the empty places where the crow's image had once been, and to see what the mosaic answered. [283] She placed the left eye where a heron's wing had broken from the mosaic, and the right eye she set into a pool of blue tile that suggested a sky without the bird. [316] Then she whispered the gently inventive equations she had invented for this kind of surgery, the ones she trusted more than any spoken prayer. [340] One equation asked, quietly, if sorrow were a substance, what its density might be, and whether it weighed more at the moment of parting or in the long years after. [370] Another asked, with the same care, if love were a vector, where it might point when its object was no longer locatable, and if pointing at nothing still counted as pointing. [401] She murmured the answers into the chipped patterns, and the candle bent as if listening, throwing fruit-shadows across the cavern walls. [422] The orchard held its breath, and even the stalactites, those patient stone teardrops, seemed to pause in their slow descent. [442] Then, with a sound like two soft boards sliding into a joiner's groove, the glass eyes dovetailed cleanly into the mosaic, each chip and curve finding its quiet match. [471] The chipped patterns answered, and a new line of glaze spread outward from the eyes, a silver vein crossing the stone, and along it walked a small dark shape. [500] The shape was not quite the crow, and not quite not, and it tilted its head in the candlelight with the same curious angle the crow had always used. [529] It was made of a hundred small reflections of the orchard, of water and moon and stone, and its feet clicked softly as it moved toward the conjurer. [557] The conjurer held out her wrist, the small bare patch of leather where the familiar had once liked to perch, and she tried not to hope. [583] The shape hesitated, and the moment stretched long, ambiguous as any loss, for she could not tell if it remembered her or was only an echo shaped by her own wanting. [614] But it stepped onto her glove with a click of clockwork on leather, and its weight was cool as the mineral water that fed the pomegranates. [640] Something in the conjurer's chest unlocked, a small latch she had not known was fastened, and she understood then that the mathematics of emotion was not a sum that resolved to a clean number. [674] It was a sum that resolved, instead, to a permission, the quiet permission to hold what returned, even when one could not prove it was the same as what had gone. [705] She tucked the glass eyes back into her satchel, still faintly warm from the pattern they had joined, and she did not weep, for weeping would have been too simple an answer. [737] Tomorrow she would map the new line of glaze the mosaic had given her, but tonight she walked through the silent orchard, the shape riding her wrist, the stalactites beginning once more their slow applause. [772] Beneath the quiet, beneath the ache, something like hope stirred and settled into her bones, patient as a seed, stubbornly alive. [793]</story>
