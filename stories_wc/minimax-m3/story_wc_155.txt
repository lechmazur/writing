<story>The fog in the ruined orchard district remembered nothing, and that was its gift to Mirael. [16] She arrived each morning of the daydream the way a traveler arrives at a remembered inn, by letting the path lead, since no map of this place had been true for three hundred years. [50] Mirael, a vivid mural artist whose reputation had once filled the galleries of three ports, now worked only where the fog permitted her hands to wander. [76] She had come hunting the fresco fragment that old Luthien, before the long silence, had pressed into her palm as though it were a key for a door no one else could see. [109] Her lost joy, she suspected, was somewhere folded inside it. [119] The fragment, when she lifted it from the moss-eaten pedestal near the oldest orchard wall, was no larger than a child's palm. [141] It held a single face, half in pigment and half in the chalky ghost of pigment that had outlived its own maker. [163] Mirael set it against the cracked plaster and considered the second half of her task. [178] She paints with light, which is to say she lays silver salt and waits for a sun she cannot see. [198] The orchard walls become, in her hands, exposed film, a place where the day's brightness leaves itself behind like an honest signature. [220] Today the daylight was a pale rumor that the fog kept whispering to itself. [234] Mirael did not despair; she simply unfolded the knowledge network she had been quietly building across fourteen fogbound years. [253] It was a tapestry of references, half memorized and half imagined, tying her to every artist who had ever touched a wall and hoped to leave it happier. [281] From those threads she borrowed warmth and method, from the orchard she borrowed silence, and from the fog she borrowed patience. [302] She mixed her silver paste in a bowl whose glaze still bore, faintly, the green of pears her grandmother had once pressed into the wet clay. [328] The pears had grown here too, before the winters stretched and the fog thickened into something almost architectural. [346] Now gnarled limbs rose out of the mist like the ribs of sleeping cathedrals, and the only ripe fruit was the rust on forgotten tools. [371] Mirael laughed, a small and pensive sound, because the district had a way of making sorrow look like a curious animal rather than a tyrant. [396] She began to paint the half-empty face from the fresco fragment onto the plaster, but inverted, the way one sets a mirror against a window. [421] In that inversion lay the experiment: where the old artist had used pigment to hide loss, Mirael would use light to reveal that the loss had been imagined in the first place. [453] She hummed, and the fog, perhaps because it was a daydream and dream things enjoy cooperation, thinned at the precise height of her shoulder. [477] Through that pale window, a single shaft reached the wall and lingered, obedient as a well-trained cat. [494] Her silver salts darkened where the light touched them, and an image began to unfold on the plaster, soft as a memory of laughter. [518] It was the missing half of the fresco fragment, completed by an absence that had waited centuries to be addressed. [538] Mirael found, within the fold of the daydream and within the fold of the wall, a small grin on the painted mouth, the kind a child makes when a secret has been kept successfully for a long time. [576] She set the fragment and the new image side by side, and for a long moment considered how to display them so that the long sleep of the orchard could be politely interrupted. [609] She did not shout, did not announce, but simply left them propped where a returning wanderer might notice, and she walked deeper into the fog to let the place accept them on its own schedule. [644] As she went, she felt the missing joy reassemble itself in her chest, not loudly, the way a key reassembles a song when slid back into a piano. [672] She had rediscovered here, at the slow heart of the orchard district, that making was itself a kind of return, a small persistent pensive mirth, the laughter of someone who had remembered that she was allowed to be glad. [711] The fog closed gently behind her, like a curtain after a play that everyone, including the walls, had quietly enjoyed. [731]</story>
