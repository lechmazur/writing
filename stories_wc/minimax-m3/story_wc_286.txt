Hesper had been a potter for forty years, but only lately did she understand that the clay had been making her, not the other way around. [26] She walked into the scorched wasteland at the hour when chimney smoke flattens against the bruised sky, a time she had long favored for its honesty. [52] In her satchel she carried a square of faded lantern glass, given to her by a teacher who had died before she could ask why it mattered. [79] The glass remembered a kinder light, or so she had begun to suspect, and she kept it wrapped in linen for the same reason one keeps a quiet friend close. [109] The beekeeping oasis appeared like a sentence left unfinished in the sand, with its clay hives low and round and a stone trough still dark with water that no one had drawn in many seasons. [144] The date palms had gone to skeleton, and the wind moved through them like a finger along a comb. [163] Yet the place resisted the way a held breath resists the body that releases it, and Hesper had come to understand why some places resist change, because she was that kind of question in sandals. [198] She had learned long ago to be phototropic to kindness, bending toward any warmth offered, and the oasis had offered her the smallest of warmths: a shape that did not vanish. [229] She moved through the ruin by following sun patterns, the way the lost bee moves, the way prayer moves through a quiet room, the way a potter moves from the wheel to the kiln. [263] Her shadow was a thin and faithful animal that she did not try to command. [278] She set the lantern glass against a hive and watched the light bend through it, bent and unbent, and saw a small figure cross the pane: a child in a yellow coat, who was also her mother, who was also a stranger she had not yet met. [325] This was the property of the oasis, or her property, or the property of all places abandoned in earnest. [344] The timelines here were fractured, and the fractures did not leak. [355] They hummed, and they thrummed like a kettle set to boil by a hand that had gone out centuries ago. [375] Each shard of time held its own weather, and the weather spoke to her in the voice of a daughter who had not yet been born, who said only her name, and then again, and then a third time as if the name were a small door. [422] Hesper knelt on the warm sand. [428] She touched the trough and felt a cold so particular it had a name in a language she almost spoke. [448] The water was not water. [453] It was a clock that had forgotten to wind itself, and the dates on the palms were not dates but a counting the oasis did only when someone kind was watching. [484] She worked the clay of the abandoned hives, pinching and pressing, and the shape that came was not a pot but a question asked in a vessel. [511] The fractured timelines offered her their weather, and she offered back her attention, and this was the only commerce that had ever felt honest to her. [537] A bee, or the memory of a bee, landed on her wrist and stayed. [551] It was warm. [554] It was also the wind, and also her father, and also a note played on an instrument she would one day build, and Hesper did not ask it to choose, because she had been a potter long enough to know that a thing can be many vessels at once. [603] When the sun crossed its second pattern she packed the glass away in its linen, and the oasis seemed to fold gently into the sand like a letter slid into an envelope. [635] Hesper walked back across the wasteland, carrying the new vessel in her hands, and the chimney smoke in some distant village had not yet risen, or had already fallen, and she could not say which. [670] This was the sharp gentleness of the world: that it does not always tell you what time it is, only that you are allowed to be in it, and that the thrum of a fractured hour is sometimes indistinguishable from the thrum of a healed one. [716] She had not found an answer to her question about why some places resist change, but she had found the question itself, freshly shaped, sitting warm in her palms, and she carried it home the way one carries a sleeping child.
