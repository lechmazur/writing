<story>The queen had been warned, and the warnings had ended. [11] After the final warning, the heralds fell silent, their brass voices swallowed by the cobalt grotto above clouds where she had retreated. [34] The realm below was no longer hers to command, and so she made her home in the high places, in a palace of ice and vapor, where the wind sang through galleries of frozen blue. [71] She was the disenchanted queen--her crown no longer a weight but a memory, her scepter tucked behind a wardrobe mirror. [94] Power had soured in her mouth years ago. [104] She had ruled with a steady hand, then a trembling one, then no hand at all, and now she kept only the habits of authority. [130] Her voice remained softly commanding, even when she spoke to no one. [143] Even when she spoke to herself, the words came out as though she were addressing a room of ministers. [163] For three seasons she had been visited by the same dream. [174] A ridge. [176] A path of pale ash. [181] Something waiting at the summit that exhaled light. [190] She woke each time with the taste of iron and the smell of scorched sage. [206] To unravel the mystery of recurring dreams, she knew, she would have to leave the grotto and climb. [225] On the morning she chose to act, the air over the cobalt grotto was thin and bright as a struck bell. [246] Her attendants were few: a mute cartographer, a half-blind astronomer, and a page who carried her gloves and her silence. [269] She told them, in her softly commanding way, that they would journey to the ember grotto on the ridge, where, according to her dream, a fire had once burned and never truly gone out. [305] The climb was gentle at first, then treacherous. [313] They moved single file along a path that the cartographer insisted had not existed the day before. [331] The astronomer spoke of old maps that showed a beacon here, a hearth for sky-travelers. [347] The page walked behind the queen, breathing in time with her, as children do. [362] It was at the second switchback, just as the cobalt walls narrowed to a corridor of smoking stone, that the bleeding starlight began. [386] Above them, the sky was no longer sky. [394] A seam had opened, pale and wet, and through it poured a thin red radiance that soaked the rocks and the queen's white gown. [419] It was not dawn. [423] It was something older and more wounded, light that seemed to be in pain. [437] The astronomer whispered that stars did not bleed, and the queen replied, softly commanding even now, that they did, and she had seen them. [462] The lacy menace of the place settled over them like a veil. [475] Every ledge held a trembling fern. [481] Every pool reflected a face that was not quite their own. [492] The queen felt the air thicken around her ankles, felt the mountain itself inhale. [506] She did not slow. [510] She had been warned, and the warnings were over, and whatever came next was no longer a warning but a fact. [531] At the final bend, when the ridge opened into a hollow of charred stone and old coals, the queen stopped. [550] The ember grotto was there, exactly as her dream had shown it. [562] A low fire breathed in the center, red and gray, alive without fuel. [575] The bleeding starlight fell upon it and made the coals shimmer like wet garnets. [589] Then the queen and her three companions shared an exhale. [599] It was unplanned, involuntary--a single breath drawn together and released together, four chests moving as one. [616] And as that breath left them, spotting the first fireflies of summer lifting from the coals, she understood the dream. [638] The fireflies were not insects. [643] They were small, torn pieces of the bleeding star, falling to earth and remembering, here, how to glow. [663] The grotto was a wound. [668] The ridge was the lip of a wound. [677] The queen had been called, after the final warning, to stand at this place and decide whether the wound would close or keep bleeding open into her kingdom. [707] She lifted her hand. [711] Her softly commanding voice, which had not truly commanded anything in years, crossed the hollow and touched the coals. [732] The fireflies drifted upward, then returned to her palm, faint and luminous. [745] She would not climb down yet. [751] There was still the mystery to unravel, and the dream to finish, and the wound to bind. [769] The night above the cobalt grotto deepened. [777] The lacy menace of the mountain held its breath. [787] And the queen, disenchanted still, began at last to enchant. [798]</story>
