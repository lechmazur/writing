<story>The foghorn had stopped its mournful groaning three days ago, and the harbor below breathed only the lap of sluggish water. [21] On the seventy-third floor of the Meridian Trust Building, where no one came except by appointment, old Theo Vance kept his orchard. [43] He was a venerable oath scribe, the last in the city who could bind a promise into something that endured past the season of the speaker. [69] The gnarled apple trees climbed out of steel planters, blossoms sour with brine, roots drinking from pipes that wept condensation from the air-conditioning shafts. [93] Theo walked among them in his dust-colored robe, hands clasped behind his back, listening for the small sounds oaths make as they cool. [116] He spoke dead languages the way other men spoke weather: without thinking, in passing, with the easy certainty of long habit. [137] Old Dutch of the harbor pilots, the clipped Latin of the court clerks, the sighing Welsh the lighthouse keepers had used to call the fog home. [163] These were not parlor tricks; they were instruments, each one tuned to a vanished kind of feeling. [180] His task, since the city's harbor went quiet, was to archive emotional patterns before they too went silent. [198] Not the words of promises, but the colors behind them - the gray of resignation, the rose of reluctant surrender, the bright, terrified gold of a lover's vow. [225] He carried them in cylinders, the old way, the way his grandfather had taught him before the building, before the orchard, before anything but the trade. [251] Today he held a single shaving, no longer than his thumbnail, lifted from a cracked wax phonograph cylinder that had belonged to a schoolteacher of the old quarter. [279] It had come to him that morning in a tin box with no return address and no note, only the smell of cedar and old chalk. [305] He did not know yet what it would do. [314] He carried it to the rooftop cistern, set the shaving carefully upon the copper rim, and lowered himself into the water until his chin broke the surface. [341] This was how he worked: by immersion, by letting the dead tongue of the cylinder wash into him through his skin. [362] The shaving began to hum against the copper, a faint, sweet vibration. [374] A child's voice, piping and certain, said in a classroom of forty years ago: We are going up to see the roof garden, Mrs. Hale said, and Mrs. Hale said the trees on top of the building were older than the building, and Mrs. Hale said that if we listened very carefully we could hear the sea in the roots. [434] Theo, in the cistern, felt the words settle into his chest like small bright birds. [449] The third-graders had climbed the old freight elevator, had laughed at the gnarled branches, had pressed their palms to the bark and left their warmth there. [475] They had not known, he understood now, that the orchard was already ancient when the building's first steel beam went down into the mud. [499] They had not known that they would be remembered - every one of them - in a language no living child would ever speak aloud. [522] He realized, with the slow horror of a man who sees the river rise at the foot of his bed, that he had been prophesying. [547] Not in any voice, not to any audience, but in the way that the scribe's oath is prophecy: by the act of recording a feeling, he had sealed its future. [577] The gold of a terrified vow, once archived, must happen. [587] The children of that curious field trip, now gray-haired, had gone on to marry, to betray, to die in wars whose names had been mispronounced in the papers. [615] Theo, in the water, had only meant to listen. [624] But the shaving played on, and the orchard swayed in a wind that had no source on the rooftop, and the harbor foghorn stayed silent. [649] When he climbed out at last, shivering, the first apple of the season had ripened on a low branch above his head. [671] He did not pick it; he only wrote, in the clipped Latin of the court clerks, the shape of the feeling it would carry. [695] Somewhere below, a ship was leaving the harbor for the first time in months, its horn a small, uncertain bleat against the quiet. [718] Theo listened, and archived, and did not pray, though the orchard smelled suddenly of apples and of loss. [736] The orchard would outlast him. [741] It had outlasted the children who came to press their palms against its bark. [755] It would outlast whatever came after, and the prophecies he had written without meaning, and the small horn sounding in the harbor like a question. [780] He folded the shaving back into its tin box and walked, slowly, into the trees. [795]</story>
