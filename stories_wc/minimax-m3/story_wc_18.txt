<story>Every twilight in the ruined orchard district, when the frost began to shimmer on the blackthorn, the mirror maker Elara climbed the slope to her chapel. [26] For three centuries the orchards had rotted beneath fog so thick that even noon was a kind of dusk. [45] The trees had become glass mountains of ice each winter, brittle and singing in the wind. [61] Yet Elara was firmly grounded in this place, her boots planted in the same furrow her grandmother had once traced with a silver dibber. [85] Tonight she carried an astronomer's broken sextant, its brass arc cracked, its mirrors clouded by a hundred winters of breath. [105] The sextant had belonged to the astronomer Vesper, who had loved Elara's grandmother and tried, with this very instrument, to capture a runaway dream that wandered the orchard each dusk. [135] He had failed, and the dream had slipped between every reflection he angled toward it. [150] Each morning Vesper would wake to find a single iridescent feather on his pillow, and each evening he would hear laughter dissolving into the fog above the apple trees. [179] When he died, the sextant passed to Elara, and with it his unfinished errand. [193] Elara intended to recreate the moment Vesper had first glimpsed the dream, and she would do it through synchronized breathing, drawing the fog in and out of her lungs as if the air and she were a single glass instrument. [233] She climbed into the ruined chapel where she worked, set the broken sextant on the stone altar, and began. [252] First she aligned the sextant's cracked mirror with a shard of polished obsidian, then with a flask of river water, then with her own eye. [277] Then she breathed. [280] In, the fog rolled into her chest; out, it streamed back through her mouth in a slow silver ribbon. [299] With each breath she matched the rhythm of the orchard itself, the way it inhaled at dawn and exhaled at dusk, until there was no boundary left between her and the ruined trees, between her skin and the cold, between breath and silence. [342] She was erasing boundaries, the way her grandmother had taught her, with the patience of a tide working at a stone. [363] As the twilight frost shimmered brighter on the broken windows, she caught a flicker in the sextant's tilted glass. [382] The dream was returning. [386] It drifted through the fog in the shape of a young woman with hair like a comet's tail and laughter like bells under water. [410] Vesper had tried to trap it in a single mirror, but Elara understood what he had not: that a dream is not a reflection but a breath, and you cannot capture a breath in glass. [445] You can only match it. [450] She inhaled, and the dream drew nearer, curious. [458] She exhaled, and it settled onto the altar beside the sextant, a faint warmth against the stone. [475] For a long moment they breathed together, the dream and the mirror maker, and the sextant recorded their joined rhythm in a thread of light that ran along its cracked arc. [506] The boundary between waking and sleeping thinned, and Elara felt the dream pour into her like water into a vessel of glass. [528] She did not keep it. [533] She let it pass through her, and in doing so she recreated it, renewed, as Vesper's sextant had once tried and failed to recreate the constellations on a foggy night. [563] The dream rose from her lips on the next exhalation, brighter than before, and hovered a moment above the altar in the shape of a small luminous bird. [591] It sang once, a note like the ringing of glass mountains, and then was gone into the fog, leaving behind only a single feather on the cracked brass of the sextant. [622] Elara picked up the feather and tucked it beside the others she had gathered over thirty years of trying. [641] Outside, the fog folded itself around the orchard like a soft blanket of glass, and the ruined trees rose in the last light as glass mountains, and somewhere in their rows the dream went on singing, free. [678] She smiled, still firmly grounded in the furrow of her grandmother's garden, and began to pack the sextant away. [697] Tomorrow there would be another dream, and another after that, and the orchard would go on being lost in fog, and the sextant would go on measuring angles too wide for its cracked arc.
