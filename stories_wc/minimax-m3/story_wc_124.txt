Maren adjusted the wick of her smallest lantern, a glass globe meant for bedside tables, and watched the pale flame bend as if listening. [24] She was a lantern broker by trade, though her customers rarely wanted light. [37] They wanted the smell. [41] In her shop, each lantern held a different scent, carefully chosen, aromatically cautious arrangements of memory and mood. [59] Tonight, however, the shop was closed. [65] Maren walked the long glass corridor to the city's greenhouse for extinct plants, her crystal perfume vial tucked against her palm. [86] The greenhouse had been her mother's work, and her mother's mother's, three generations of women tending specimens that no longer existed anywhere else. [109] Now there were only seven plants left, and one was changing. [120] She paused beneath the arched door of the Sundew Wing, where the last specimen of a flower called the Weeping Veil hung in its terrarium. [145] The transformation had been happening for weeks. [152] Tonight, as the transformation completes, the petals would turn from white to a deep, bruised violet, and then they would fall. [173] She was here to grieve. [178] That was the part no one understood about her work. [188] People thought the lantern broker only sold pleasant scents, but the truth was that every meaningful fragrance was a memory, and every memory was, in some way, a small loss. [218] She had been aromatically cautious since she was a girl, ever since a drop of her grandmother's rose attar had made her weep for an hour over a woman she had never met. [251] She had spent her life trying to understand why some smells trigger tears when others bring nothing at all. [270] The Weeping Veil had always been her favorite. [278] When she was a child, her mother would lift her up to smell it, and the scent had been clean and cool, like rain falling on linen. [305] Now, beneath the ultraviolet lamps she had installed for her work, the petals glowed faintly. [320] The ultraviolet revealing trick was her own invention, a way to see the hidden oils inside a flower, the way scent actually moved through a petal. [346] She held the crystal perfume vial closer. [353] Inside it was a single drop from the very first Weeping Veil bloom, gathered by her grandmother sixty years ago. [373] It had been her inheritance, her burden, her question. [382] She uncapped the vial and let the scent rise into the warm greenhouse air. [396] It smelled of her grandmother's hands. [402] It smelled of the day her mother stopped speaking. [411] It smelled of every lantern she had ever sold, every tear she had ever explained to a customer, every quiet hour spent trying to understand why some smells trigger tears when others bring nothing at all. [447] The ultraviolet glow shifted as the transformation completed. [455] The petals released their oils in one long, slow sigh, and the air thickened with something that was almost grief itself. [476] Maren stood very still. [480] She had thought, when she began this work, that she would find a scientific answer, a chemical truth behind the way scent unlocked the locked doors of memory. [508] There was no answer. [512] There was only the door, and the moment of opening, and the willingness to walk through it. [529] She grieved, as she always grieved, for the woman who had lifted her up to smell a flower, for the flower itself, for the small girl who had first understood that a smell could break her open. [566] The crystal perfume vial caught the ultraviolet light and scattered it across the terrarium in tiny prismatic shadows, crystal shadows that fell across her hands like a blessing. [594] By morning, the Weeping Veil would be gone. [602] She would bottle the scent of this final bloom, and she would carry it to her shop, and she would place it in a lantern on a high shelf, and some night, years from now, a stranger would open the door and weep without knowing why. [648] That was her work. [652] That was her gift. [656] That was the impact of memories, passed quietly from one hand to another, a candle lit from a candle, the way grief becomes a lantern, and the lantern becomes a small light in a dark room. [692] She bowed her head. [696] The crystal perfume vial held its single drop. [704] The greenhouse breathed. [707] Somewhere, the transformation had completed, and the first petal was already falling. [719]
