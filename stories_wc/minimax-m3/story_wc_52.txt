Mireille set the kettle on the iron stove and listened for its first complaint. [14] The loft above answered with the rain-beaded skylight she had come to love, each droplet a slow bead of glass-gray light rolling across its panes. [39] For nine months she had been the lion tamer's widow, and the title still felt awkward in her mouth, like a coat that did not yet know her shoulders. [68] The ash wood carving blank lay on the worn bench beneath the sloping light, pale as a sleeping animal, neither shaped nor flattened, only present. [93] It had arrived in the spring from his old friend in the country, a piece of ash offered without explanation, the kind of gift one sends to a house where no other gift could possibly do. [129] It was, she thought, hauntingly practical; the blank itself a small promise, the work still to come. [146] The kettle climbed toward its boil, and she counted its minutes the way Henri had once counted the breaths between cage and chair in the old arena. [173] She had begun, in these slow afternoons, to harvest lessons from loss, though the harvest had not yet settled into any recognizable shape. [196] What was there to gather, when what was lost was so thoroughly a face, so thoroughly a name, so thoroughly the smell of sawdust and the long easy stride of a man who moved among lions as if their teeth were a tongue he knew the taste of? [244] The world had marked Henri as simply here and gone, as if a man could be replaced by the fact of his absence. [267] She had a method for keeping him closer than that. [277] She called it crystallized time. [282] When she held a memory long enough, with the patient pressure of attention, something in the moment slowed, thickened, and finally hardened into a shape she could carry up the stairs and lay beside the ash block on the bench. [322] It was not magic. [326] It was only the work of a woman learning to outlive her own undoing. [340] The rain ticked on the skylight in its small chorus, and she listened as to an instrument tuning itself to her grief. [362] In this loft, with this wood, she was learning at last to contain her sorrow instead of merely shedding it. [382] Containment, she was beginning to understand, was not a wall around what hurt. [395] It was a shape, carved gently into the days: the slow wearing of water into stone, the patient mark of a hand against grain. [419] Her husband's lessons were returning to her now, one by one, in fragments that glowed like embers in the dim. [439] He had loved her without ceremony, held her without instruction, seen her without a mirror. [454] She had not known, in the long years of their marriage, that this was what she would one day wish to gather, polish, and lay against the pale grain of ash. [485] The kettle began its thin, ascending keen, and Mireille did not rise to draw it. [500] She pressed her thumb, instead, into the heart of the block, and felt the wood receive her attention as if it had been waiting all along for exactly this pressure. [530] Years would pass before the carving was finished. [538] She was not in a hurry. [544] The wood would remember her touch the way she remembered his hands, and what it finally became would be, she thought, a small portrait of their shared quiet: perhaps the golden shoulder of a lion, perhaps only the curve of an ear, perhaps only the empty chair Henri had sat down behind, in the last minute of every performance, to become, briefly, ordinary again. [608] She would return to this loft again and again, in the rust-hued peace of these afternoons, to gather what she could. [629] The kettle would always boil. [634] The rain would always bead the glass. [641] The block would wait, patient as ash, patient as grief, patient as anything here and gone and somehow still, impossibly, close. [662]
