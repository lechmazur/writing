<story>The observatory perched on its floating isle like a brass-bound thought drifting above the cloud-sea, and it had been sleeping for a long time. [24] Long after the day spoons bent reality and the world remembered it was made of folded paper, the isle still rose and fell with the slow breaths of sleeping weather. [54] In the highest tower, a budding rune scholar named Vere worked at a desk cluttered with brass calipers and the soft ticking of a forgotten metronome. [80] She was quietly deliberate by nature, choosing each gesture as if she were laying down stones in a garden path that no one but she would walk. [107] Her obsession was a small and stubborn riddle: she had been trying for years to find where shadows go at noon. [128] The riddle mattered because on this floating isle, the sun was never permitted to arrive, and so noon was a kind of borrowed hour. [152] At noon there were no ordinary shadows anywhere on the isle, and yet every object still kept a soft patient gloom beneath itself. [175] She suspected the missing shadows had been gathered and sealed away inside the observatory's locked heart, alongside something else that had once woken and now slept again. [202] A clockwork automaton the size of a child stood frozen in the central chamber, where the great telescope pointed forever downward at the cloud-sea below. [227] It was the last clockwork automaton left on the isle, and its small iron chest was crossed with pale runes that Vere had begun to read by lamplight during her quiet shifts. [259] She carried in her pocket the clockwork automaton key, a slender object shaped almost like a question mark, and she was afraid of what would happen if she turned it. [289] Tonight something had changed: the moon had slipped behind the floating isle, and the sealed windows of the central chamber had begun to glow. [313] The runes on the automaton's chest were warm, and Vere could feel them pulsing against her palm when she pressed her hand flat there. [337] She understood she had to relearn the old sealing runes, the circular patterns that the first rune scholars had used to wake, bind, and reawaken their creations. [364] To relearn by closing loops meant to finish what her teachers had left half-done: the binding loops had been cut short, like a song that ends in the middle of a phrase. [396] So Vere took out her chalk and began to redraw the loops, slowly, in the patience of one who knows that haste dissolves magic faster than sunlight dissolves frost. [425] She began at the automaton's left shoulder, where a small seam of copper showed through the rust, and she drew a loop that reached around the iron collar she could feel only with her fingertips. [460] The chalk hissed faintly against the metal, as if the surface were remembering a tongue it had once spoken, and the glow in the seams deepened like water that had been waiting for ice to give way. [497] Then she turned the clockwork automaton key, and the automaton shuddered as if a long-held breath had slipped out of it. [518] Its eyes opened with two small green pinpricks that seemed almost embarrassed at being seen, and the sealed awakenings began to lift from its chest. [543] The lifting felt like a paper boat on a rising river lifted at last from a hidden rock, and Vere felt a sudden coolness across the floor as if a small window had opened and let in a quiet piece of noon. [585] She understood then what had been done: the first rune scholars, fearing the creature's awakening, had sewn the missing noon-shadows into its chest as a sort of cushion, because awakened things cast heavier shadows than sleeping ones, and the isle had not been able to spare even one at noon. [635] So the shadows had been sealed away inside the automaton's gentle ribs, where they had gone soft and grey, and the automaton had gone quiet, and the isle had gone still, and every noon had gone bare. [672] Vere lowered her hand, slowly, and she bowed once to the small blinking machine. [686] Then she sat on the cold floor beside it and closed her eyes as if she were listening to a song that had been finished for the first time in many years. [718] The morning would bring questions, and the floating isle would lift its slow shoulder toward the dawn, but in the chamber of the nocturnal observatory the sealed awakenings had begun to lift, and the shadows had begun, very softly, to find their way home.
