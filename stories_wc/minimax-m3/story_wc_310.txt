The last light of the contracting universe had dimmed to a thin seam along the horizon, and Maren stood on the ambered observatory deck watching it go. [27] She had been chosen in the old way, by casting lots from a leather cup that had belonged to her grandmother. [48] The others who had drawn had refused, or wept, or simply walked away into the thinning air. [65] Maren alone had been quietly enthusiastic about the strange commission: to distill, from the remnants of the world, a single coherent prophecy. [87] The ambered observatory deck had been built centuries before, its planks fossilized into resin deep enough to catch the dying starlight and hold it. [111] She kept her collection of foreign coins in a velvet pouch at her hip, each stamped with the sigil of a nation that no longer existed. [137] Tonight she would need them. [142] A prophecy, the elders had taught her, must be read backwards to be understood forwards. [157] She did not yet know what that meant. [165] She climbed the curved stair and waited, as she had been taught, for the static bursts. [181] The first came as a faint crackle in the inner ear, like a beetle walking across silk. [198] Maren closed her eyes and listened with the technique her grandmother had called understanding static bursts: parsing the meaningless noise for the meaning beneath it, the way one strains to hear a violin in a hurricane. [234] At first there was only hiss. [240] Then, very softly, she heard her own name. [248] It was spoken in a voice she did not recognize, in a language she had never learned, and yet she understood every word. [271] The voice said: When the last coin is given away, the deck will bloom. [285] Maren opened her eyes. [289] The seam of starlight along the horizon pulsed once, slowly, like the heartbeat of something vast and tired. [307] She reached for her pouch and poured the coins into her palm. [319] There were eleven of them. [324] She counted them again. [328] Eleven. [329] The prophecy had said the last. [335] So either she had miscounted before, or she had been wrong about which was last, or the prophecy was wrong - or, and this was the thought that caught her breath, the prophecy had been spoken backwards. [371] Stellar doubt, her grandmother had named it: the particular uncertainty that comes when the cosmos itself refuses to confirm your hopes. [392] Maren had felt it before. [397] She sat cross-legged on the ambered observatory deck and turned the coins over one by one. [413] Each was warm, as if some distant sun still lived inside it. [425] She chose one at random - or rather, she held the pouch and let her fingers fall where they would, the old way of the lots. [450] The coin she drew was small and dark, almost black, and bore on its face the outline of a flower blooming. [471] The deck, she realized, would bloom when the last coin was given away. [484] But what did it mean to give a coin away, after the universe had contracted? [499] There were no hands left to receive it. [507] And yet the voice had spoken her name. [515] Maren stood, her shadow stretching long across the ambered observatory deck. [526] She walked to the edge where the seam of starlight was brightest and held the coin out over the abyss. [546] She thought of her grandmother, who had first drawn the lots and then refused to follow them. [563] She thought of her own quietly enthusiastic acceptance, how it had felt less like courage than inevitability. [580] She wanted, simply, to wavelength align with purpose - to step into the beam of meaning she had been offered and let it carry her. [604] She let the coin fall. [609] It caught the starlight and spun, and as it spun it seemed to sing, and as it sang the ambered observatory deck began to glow beneath her feet. [637] Not with the dying light of the contracting universe, but with something older waiting in the resin all along. [656] The other ten coins in her pouch grew warm. [665] One by one, without her willing it, they lifted from the velvet and drifted into the dark. [682] Maren did not try to catch them. [689] She had understood, at last, what the backwards prophecy had meant: the giving must come before the receiving, and the receiving before the asking, and the asking before the listening. [719] She listened. [721] And the voice, which had spoken her name in a tongue she had never learned, spoke it once more in her own, and told her she had done well. [750] The ambered observatory deck bloomed beneath her, petal by petal, into a flower of light. [765] She closed her eyes. [769] She was, at last, aligned. [774]
