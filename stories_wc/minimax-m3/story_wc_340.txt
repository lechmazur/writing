<story>The ascending servant stepped into the brass-bound elevator at the base of the icebound spire. [15] Through the windows, only the roiling white of clouds was visible, far below. [29] He was bound for the tenth floor, where his master preferred his evening tea. [40] The elevator groaned upward with the patient sigh of long use. [55] Around his neck hung the airship captain's goggles he had inherited from his late uncle. [70] Their lenses were tinted the color of weak tea, faintly smelling of leather and sky. [87] He was, by nature and training, warmly inquisitive, with a quiet habit of noticing what others overlooked. [101] As the elevator climbed ten floors, the shaft doors slid past in slow procession. [114] Each level opened briefly onto a corridor of polished ice before closing again. [133] On the seventh floor, where the spire passed through a colder band, frost had gathered along the inner walls. [148] Through scattered light from a high clerestory window, something glinted behind a loose wooden panel. [160] He bent with unhurried patience and drew out a small leather-bound diary. [175] Its cover was warped by cold, but the pages were still dry and somehow expectant. [191] He unfolded the first leaf, and the binding cracked softly, as though the book were waking. [205] The first page bore a line of unfamiliar letters arranged beneath a faded date. [229] The text had been written in an encrypted hand, and he recognized, with a small thrill, the very cipher his uncle had taught him. [247] Through scattered light, the symbols resolved into sense, and the diary unfolded its confession page by careful page. [274] It had been kept, he read, by a former steward of the spire, a man dismissed without explanation by a master who could not bear his warmth. [292] The steward had pressed small sprigs of dried lavender in the margins, their scent faintly preserved even now. [305] The ascending servant felt the familiar pinch at the edges of his smile. [321] He had been taught to keep an affectionate detachment from the lives of those he served. [348] And yet here, in this diary, he found a man whose bitterness had been folded away with care, as though grief itself could be dusted each morning. [368] He resolved, before the elevator reached the tenth floor, to seek out the joy beneath anguish in this small record. [390] When the elevator sighed to a halt, he slipped the diary into his waistcoat and stepped out into the cold sweet air. [411] He pushed open the gilded door into a room of tall windows, where his master sat silhouetted against the falling snow. [434] A second cup had been laid out, which his master did only when there was something to discuss that could not be hurried. [456] He set down the tray and, with the warmly inquisitive quiet that was his small reputation, laid the diary on the table. [483] His master looked at the book, and his face, carved into careful neutrality by years of service, softened for the first time the ascending servant could remember. [513] It was, he said, the diary of his father, the very steward whose dismissal had been his first great sorrow, and whose code had defied every locksmith in the city. [545] The ascending servant spoke, gently, of the lavender in the margins, of the cipher he had learned from his uncle, and of the small benedictions the steward had pressed between the lines. [562] Through scattered light from the tall windows, the master read the last page, his eyes growing bright. [579] He folded the diary shut with the same care with which it had been unfolded for him. [601] The tea went cold and was reheated, and the two men spoke of the slow unfolding of a forgiveness neither had expected. [626] When the ascending servant descended again, the diary remained above, his master asking him to return each week with what new pages the cipher revealed. [640] He stepped into the elevator with a lighter heart than he had carried up. [659] Through the scattered light of the shaft, he watched the floors fall away, each door a small remembered kindness. [691] And the airship captain's goggles, still warm against his chest, seemed to hum with the small persistent joy of a man who had at last found the happiness that hides in grief. [691]</story>
