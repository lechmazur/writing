<story>The orchard planet of Veris was riddled with floating rock arches that hung in its skies like the ribs of some ancient leviathan. [23]
Among the terraces between them, a community of orchard keepers had learned, seasons ago, to call one another family. [42]
Some of them still remembered being strangers; others had only ever known the orchards as home. [58]
At the eastern edge of the village, where the apricot terraces met the cliffside, a faint figure drifted each morning before the sun. [81]
It was Mira, who had died in the great frost and returned, an altruistic ghost, to tend what she loved. [101]
She was strangely trustworthy in a way the living could not explain, and even the skeptics among the orchard keepers listened when she spoke. [125]
Her voice never carried further than a single ear, but her chalk did. [138]
Each dawn, she knelt on the deserted sidewalks of the village and wrote her messages in careful, looping script. [157]
She wrote: The watering bell has been silent for nine days. [168]
She wrote: The moth orchids are curling inward again. [177]
She wrote: We must reexamine what we have been doing to the eastern grove. [191]
The bell, an heirloom butterfly garden watering bell cast in tarnished copper, hung from a beam in the meeting arbor. [211]
Its ringing had once called the workers in from the terraces at midday, when the sun grew cruel and the fruit needed shade-cloths. [234]
Now its clapper had been stilled by grief. [242]
A blight had come to the orchards, and with it a strange dimming of the sky where the nebula of respite usually burned. [265]
That nebula, a soft violet cloud that drifted above the floating arches each summer, had long been the community's signal that the harvest would be kind. [291]
Without it, the orchards felt like a held breath. [300]
Her altruism bent toward a single aim: to orchestrate the collective effort she had seen them forget how to make. [320]
She had come back, she sometimes whispered, not because she was unkind in death but because there was still work to be done. [343]
She had been the first to greet the strangers who had wandered in from the broken arches years ago. [362]
She had taught them to prune the silverleaf trees and to read the weather in the lichen. [379]
She had eaten with them at the long table until their laughter and her own had become indistinguishable. [397]
Now she moved among them again, a translucent suggestion of kindness, asking only one thing: that they look once more at what they had refused to see. [424]
The blight, she had realized, was not a curse. [433]
It was the orchard's own tired sigh, a response to the way the community had begun to pull more than it gave back. [456]
They had grown complacent, taking the nebula's glow for granted. [466]
They had stopped tending the butterfly gardens that pollinated the upper canopy. [478]
They had let the watering bell go quiet because no one had time to ring it. [494]
On the third dawn after the chalk had begun appearing, a young orchard keeper named Tov found the words still fresh on the sidewalk. [518]
He stood there in his patched coat, holding his breath, and then he walked to the meeting arbor. [536]
He lifted the copper bell and rang it, and the sound rolled out over the terraces like a slow bell of rain. [558]
One by one, the others came. [564]
They came from the pruning sheds and the sorting tables and the sleeping lofts under the arches. [581]
They came because Mira had asked, and she was strangely trustworthy, and because the chalk on the stones had shown them what they had been hiding from themselves. [609]
Together they walked back to the eastern grove, where the moth orchids were indeed curling inward, brown at their edges. [629]
Together they began to work, to reexamine each terrace and each path, to mend the irrigation channels and to plant new seedlings in the shade of the arches. [657]
Mira drifted among them, her fingers brushing the leaves as if she could still feel their warmth. [674]
By the end of the week, the nebula of respite had returned to the sky, faint at first and then deepening into a violet bloom that hung above the floating arches like a promise. [708]
The orchard keepers stood in the cool of the evening and looked up, and for the first time in many days, they breathed easily. [732]
There was work still to do, and the blight had not vanished, but hope had returned to the terraces in a form that was tangled and thorned and very much alive. [763]
Mira smiled, or seemed to, and chalked one last message on the sidewalk at dawn. [778]
It read only: We are still becoming. [785]</story>
