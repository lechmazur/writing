Mira took the long way to the braided ravine, the path that forked around an old culvert and then split again into a dozen smaller tracks worn by deer and water alike. [32] She was a polymer chemist by trade, accustomed to thinking in chains and crosslinks, in the way a single broken bond could unravel a sheet of material into something soft and dissolving. [64] For two years she had carried the same question into her work, the same way a person carries a stone in their pocket, smooth and warm: whether the spaces between molecules could be tuned to conduct feeling the way a wire conducts current. [111] Today she carried the question out of the lab and into the hills, because her brother had died in a hospital in March, and she had not visited the place where they used to walk. [148] The ravine opened like a fist releasing. [155] Water moved through it in three channels at once, braiding around pale stones, rejoining, parting again, and Mira sat on a slab of slate and watched. [182] She was symphonically minded, her brother used to say, by which he meant she heard everything as score: the tick of a centrifuge, the creak of a door, the way a kettle's whistle resolved into the next phrase. [222] He had been the one who actually composed, sitting at an old upright that would not stay in tune, scribbling on staff paper until the small hours. [250] Mira had not been able to listen to his recordings since. [261] She set down her pack and uncapped a thermos. [270] Something pale caught her eye at the base of a basalt column: an empty chrysalis, papery, almost translucent, the seam where its occupant had emerged still damp-looking though weeks must have passed. [305] She lifted it with two fingers and held it to the light. [316] It was smaller than her thumb, and the hollow inside made her think of the way she had avoided her brother's studio, his half-finished manuscripts, the condolence letters she had never answered. [351] The river was loud enough to fill the silence, but not loud enough to fill the space he had been. [370] Then, slowly, she began to watch the foam. [378] The current had built a lattice of bubbles along the slower eddies, and as she traced the patterns she noticed they did not move at random. [406] A wavelet at the top would crest and travel down, splitting into two divergent paths that never quite merged the same way twice. [428] Where the foam tangled, it built airy knots that briefly held their shape before drifting apart. [445] And then, without deciding to, she understood. [453] The foam was not debris. [458] It was the surface expression of how separation could behave: as the necessary counterpoint to reunion, as the breath after a held note, as the negative space that gave a melody its meaning. [495] Tracing the patterns was tracing the structure of her own grief, which had been bracing itself for two years against the current, demanding that the water run the way she wanted it to. [532] If she could forgive the divergence, the channels might still braid into the same larger river by the canyon mouth. [553] She had come here to atone. [559] Not in any theological sense, and not for any specific wrong, but for the way she had kept her brother's memory behind glass, classified, indexed, refused to let it live and shed its old skin. [596] The empty chrysalis in her palm was, she thought, what he had left her. [609] Not his absence, but the proof that he had been, and moved through, and emerged. [623] Mira placed it back among the stones, gently, in the fork of two currents. [637] She would write the letters tomorrow. [643] She would call the violist who had played with him and ask to hear the unfinished quartet. [659] She would translate the separation into connection in the only language she truly spoke, in chains and crosslinks, by designing a polymer that could carry a signal the way his music had once carried her through a long Tuesday. [703] The foam kept moving. [707] The water kept braiding. [711] She sat on the slate as the pieces fell into place, one by one, like a motif coming home to its tonic chord, and the grief, loosened at last, ran on. [742]
