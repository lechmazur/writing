The wandering fireworks designer had walked three days to reach the fortress, and he arrived before the dusk had finished settling. [21]
He had heard rumors of a place where the walls bloomed in winter, where the stones wept a fragrant resin, where something precious was being lost. [47]
He carried only a satchel, a small iron brazier, and a fire spinner's kerosene rag knot, kept wound tight at his belt. [69]
His name was Corin, and he was a man who chanted while walking, not because he believed in the words but because silence felt, to him, like slow suffocation. [98]
The fortress rose from the valley like a clenched fist that had softened into a garden. [114]
Vines crawled up the parapets, roses tumbled from arrow slits, mosses crept along every flagstone as soft as the underside of a rabbit's ear. [138]
Corin paused at the gate and felt the hush of a night with no stars pressing in like wet velvet. [158]
The sky was a closed eye; the wind had stopped; even the crickets had agreed to whisper. [175]
He thought: somewhere in this place, a fire is supposed to burn. [187]
He knew this not because anyone had told him, but because he had spent his whole life filtering the shape of things through ambient awareness, the way some men read a river by its color and others by its sound. [227]
A note in mosaic had been left for him, though he did not yet know it. [243]
He began to chant as he crossed the courtyard, an old rhyme about sparks and the patience of seeds. [262]
"Hold the small flame long enough," he sang, "and the mountain will teach you its name." [278]
A servant met him at the stair, a tired woman in a gray dress that looked wrung out of the same weather as the sky. [303]
"My lord is sleeping," she said. [309]
"I am not here for your lord," Corin answered. [318]
"I am here for the gravity of his hopelessness, which I have been told is becoming a problem for the stones themselves." [340]
She stared at him, then whispered, "They say the walls are blooming because he has stopped hoping. Each petal is a piece of his courage falling off." [367]
Corin nodded and climbed the stairs. [373]
In the highest chamber lay the lord, a thin man whose eyes were open but who seemed to be looking at something behind the room. [398]
Pinned beside him was a note in mosaic, hundreds of tiny colored shards pressed into wax. [414]
Corin read it not with his eyes alone but by the warmth of the room, the direction of the dust, the faint tremor in the man's breath. [441]
The note said: the gravity of hopelessness is only a costume; take it off and you will find the dancing underneath. [462]
Corin laughed once, soft and serious, because he understood the message was true and also terribly sad. [479]
He took the rag knot from his belt and held its flame against the brazier until the fire caught. [498]
The flame was a small stubborn yellow. [505]
He lifted it and whispered, in his chanting voice, a verse his grandmother had taught him about the duty of small lights. [527]
"Now we filter the darkness back out of you, my lord, the way a river filters silt, the way a song filters grief from a throat." [553]
He held the brazier close to the man's face, and the light climbed into the chamber like a small animal. [573]
Slowly, the man on the bed began to smile. [582]
Outside, the fortress walls still bloomed, but now each flower released a faint glow, as if the stones remembered how to keep a flame alive. [607]
A petal floated down and landed in Corin's open palm. [617]
He bowed to the lord, who was no longer sleeping but humming, very softly, a melody he had not heard since boyhood. [639]
To the servant at the stair Corin said, "Carry the note. It is a compass now. The kerosene rag knot will stay with you too, in case the night grows dark again." [671]
He tucked the small flame into a bronze dish he had carried for this purpose, lifted his satchel, and began his descent, chanting once more about sparks and seeds. [700]
Behind him the hush of a night with no stars loosened just enough to let a breath of breeze pass through the high window, bringing the smell of roses and something that might, with time, be called hope. [738]
And the fortress, blooming from sorrow, kept blooming, because that is what gardens do once someone remembers to tend them. [758]
