<story>In the hush of a hidden sunrise, Mira stepped into Echo Gorge, where the cliffs held every sound and returned it changed. [22] She was a lost soul collector, though the title never fit her the way a coat fits a shoulder. [41] Around her neck, on braided silver grass, hung a crocodile's tooth she had taken from a riverbed years ago. [60] It warmed against her skin when the canyon dreamed of voices it had swallowed. [74] She carried, too, a small harp lashed to her back, its strings tuned a half-step apart on purpose. [92] The instrument had been misaligned by her own hand when she was young and angry, and she had never found the heart to mend it. [117] Yet she had learned its sour notes slipped past the ears of the living into the porous listening of the dead. [138] She was, by temperament, modestly fearless, the kind of brave that walks forward because it cannot think of anything else. [158] Today she had come to pursue a particular ghost, the one who had laughed last, the one who died with a smile still on his face. [184] He was rumored to drift between the canyon's walls when the sun was a copper coin cresting the eastern rim. [204] Mira believed his spectral confession might teach her to integrate laughter into healing, the lesson she had circled for seven years. [225] The gorge, sensing her, returned the soft click of her heel doubled into a small percussion of welcome. [243] She walked, the canyon walked with her, and morning light fell in long slats like the keys of a patient piano. [264] Halfway in, she drew the harp forward and played, not a melody but a question of bent intervals seeking a hidden ear. [286] The misaligned strings hummed their off-pitch song, and the rocks bent the sound into something almost human. [303] A shape detached from shadow, a man in a coat too large, his outline flickering like a candle in a draft. [324] He did not speak first, having forgotten how, and Mira played louder, coaxing the air into a shape that remembered words. [345] The ghost opened his mouth, and what emerged was not grief but the cracked echo of a joke he had once told his daughter. [369] The gorge caught the laugh and carried it, and Mira heard, briefly, her own lost sister's voice tangled in it. [389] She felt, then, the luminous ache that had become her truest companion, the glow of a wound no longer needing hiding. [410] The ghost drifted closer, steadying as the harp's sour notes gave him a scaffolding of almost-music to lean against. [429] He confessed, in stops and starts, that he had laughed at his death because he had finally understood he was loved. [450] Mira's hands trembled on the strings, the crocodile's tooth pulsed warm, and she knew with the certainty of stones this was the answer. [473] She had thought healing was a quiet thing, but it was also the jagged laughter given back to the world. [493] The ghost smiled, his smile no longer flickering, and the canyon held it gently as a palm holds a trembling flame. [514] When he faded, he did not vanish so much as release, his confession settling into the stones like rain into a waiting field. [537] Mira lowered the harp and stood in the new sun, which filled the gorge and turned every surface to quiet gold. [558] She thought of the lost souls she had gathered before, the solemn and weeping ones, and wondered how she had missed the ones who laughed. [583] The crocodile's tooth cooled against her chest, satisfied, and she understood it had been a compass pointing not toward the dead but toward healing. [607] She turned to walk out of Echo Gorge, and the cliffs returned her footfalls as gentle, almost tender applause. [626] Behind her, the misaligned strings stirred in a wind that should not have been there, humming the ghost's laugh into the brightening stone. [649] She carried the sound with her, folded into the luminous ache like a letter in a pocket, to be read when the morning felt too heavy. [675]</story>
