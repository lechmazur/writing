Maren adjusted the fiddle on her shoulder and watched the empty shoulder of the road where, moments ago, the hitchhiker had simply ceased to be. [25]

She had picked him up at the switchback below, his thumb out, his coat the color of wet slate, his eyes as if he were remembering a song he had never heard. [57]

Now the pullout on the mountain held only her, the wind, and the kind of silence that asks questions back. [77]

This was her calling: she played bridge-songs, those fiddle tunes built to span what could not be crossed, and she was, by every wandering instinct, dynamically observant of the small departures ordinary people miss. [111]

She set the case down on the low stone wall and lifted from her pocket the thing she had been carrying since the curve before the lookout. [138]

It was a wet-plate collodion glass flake, no larger than a fingernail, and it caught the late sun like a sliver of frozen weather. [162]

When she tilted it, she could see the ghost of an image trapped in its chemistry, a face half-pixelated, half-perfect, asking for some kind of pixelated amnesty. [189]

The image was the hitchhiker's, or had been, or wanted to be, and the asking inside it felt almost human. [207]

She counted the seconds since he had faded into the scrub oak, since his coat had fluttered against her window in a way that she now understood as a map. [235]

A secret mapped by scaffold wrap flutters, she thought, the phrase arriving unbidden and true as the cold air in her lungs. [257]

To recount him felt like the only honest work left, the only thing the mountain had not already taken. [277]

She had spent years sculpting possibility from the ashes of "never," pulling tunes out of the silence between notes where listeners expected nothing at all. [302]

She had learned that a song, like a road, is not a thing but a permission to keep moving across. [321]

She lifted the bow, and the first note left her like a small bright animal running from a brush fire. [341]

It was the melody she had been humming when the hitchhiker's thumb had first caught the corner of her eye, a wandering modal tune that climbed and climbed. [368]

The glass flake in her left palm warmed against her skin and began, impossibly, to hum in sympathy with the strings. [388]

Each time she drew a phrase from the wood, a different pixel of the trapped face resolved, then dissolved, then returned, a luminous disbelief that asked her to believe in the returning. [420]

She recounted him as she played, the way a fisherman recounts the line he has just lost. [436]

She recounted the way he had said "ahead" as if "ahead" were a country with its own weather and its own quiet laws. [457]

She recounted the way he had folded his hands when the road turned to gravel, as if he were a letter being put gently away. [479]

She recounted how at the pullout he had stepped out, thanked her, and then stepped further, into the chaparral, his coat the last thing to vanish, the flutter of its scaffold-wrapped hem against the broom sage a sentence she had only now translated. [526]

The translation was this: do not be afraid of the unfinished, do not be afraid of the road that asks you to remain incomplete. [549]

She kept playing, and the glass flake kept catching pieces of him, and the mountain kept being a place where a person could almost be remembered into existence. [577]

When the last note left the strings, she saw him again, whole, in the flake, smiling with the mouth of someone who has just been forgiven by a landscape. [606]

He raised one hand, a thumb out, an offer, a goodbye, a beginning, a question that had no answer and needed none. [627]

She slipped the flake into her fiddle case beside the rosin and the cloth, a relic she would carry past every bridge and into every town where songs could still be built across what had never been. [664]

Then she started the engine and drove on, into the long luminous question of the road, where the future is a place one has to be forgiven into, again and again, each mile a small amnesty. [700]
