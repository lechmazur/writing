Cael was a drifting roadside poet before the energy beings came, trading verses for bread and shelter beneath the apple trees of the western valleys. [25]
After the energy beings arrived, the roads emptied and the inns went quiet, and only the terra-cotta grottoes that the ancients had hollowed into the red hillsides still felt like home. [56]
He found one such hollow under the pale wash of starlight, where clay walls held the day's warmth like a memory refusing to fade. [80]
In his pack was a frayed coil of rope, the only thing he had taken from the night he slipped out of the prison yard, a night he could no longer fully remember but could not bring himself to forget. [120]
The rope had been his lifeline then; now it had become a question he had been slowly unraveling for years, thread by patient thread. [144]
He believed every ethical knot could be teased loose if approached with the right kind of laughter, and this was his way of uncovering layers until the trembling truth beneath finally breathed. [176]
In the grotto he worked at his pattern, arranging the coil in a spiral across the floor, patterning verses into the air with his hands as dust motes caught the starlight and turned into constellations of their own making. [215]
The beings had come not as conquerors but as visitors, asking the people a single question that no one could answer: what do you do with the hours you have left? [246]
For Cael the answer had always been poetry, but the beings wanted something sharper, something that could be weighed and measured and given back to the cosmos in exchange for the privilege of remaining. [280]
The impossible choice arrived one night when a young being, half light and half longing, descended into his sanctuary and offered him the texture of time itself, felt and known and held in the palm, in exchange for the rope that still carried the breath of the prison he had fled. [331]
Cael smiled, because he had always been ethically playful, and he asked the being what it would do with such a trade if it were the one who had escaped. [361]
The being tilted its head and said that freedom was the only currency it understood, and that without the rope's memory the poet would become a man without origin, drifting forever along roads that led nowhere. [397]
He lifted the rope and felt the fibers whisper of old bars and a moon that had watched him climb, and the layers of his own life trembled beneath his fingers like pages in a book waiting to be opened. [437]
For a long moment he held both possibilities: the texture of time, vast and luminous, or the rope, small and frayed and utterly his. [461]
Then he made his pattern, laying the rope upon the clay in a shape that echoed the spiral of the galaxy turning above, and he began to speak. [489]
He spoke of the prison yard, of the stars he had seen through the bars, of the night he had chosen to run because some kinds of captivity could not be borne even when the door stood open. [527]
He spoke of the beings, and how they had taught him that time was not a river but a fabric, and that every moment was a thread that could be woven into something larger than any single self. [565]
And as he spoke, the rope began to glow, not with the visitors' energy but with the soft, persistent light of a thing that had finally been allowed to tell its story. [597]
The being watched, and the grotto held its breath, and the poet discovered that the texture of time was not something one could hold but something one could only give away, thread by patient thread. [632]
When the being rose and returned to its kin, it carried the pattern of the rope and the poem, and Cael sat alone in the terra-cotta hollow, feeling the absence like a new kind of presence. [668]
He did not regret the choice, because he had learned that impossible choices were simply the world's way of asking whether you were willing to become more than you had been. [699]
And in the silence that followed, he began a new verse, one that would travel along whatever roads remained, drifting as he had always drifted, but now with the stars tucked safely into the lines of his palm. [737]
