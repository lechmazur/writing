Mira was the first of her kind, a pioneering cart glider who had taken the old salt-flat carts and given them wings of hammered brass and canvas. [27]
Her workshop floated on an acid sea, a patchwork raft of corroded copper plates and oiled gears that hissed softly whenever the green tides rolled beneath. [26] [53]
She had built it herself, of course, because no one else believed in the project, and she liked it that way. [21] [74]
The raft bore a low, domed cabin whose walls were nothing but nested cogs, each one turning at its own rate, driving tiny pistons that pumped perfumed air through copper veins. [30] [104]
It was here, on a long iron bench stained with the ghosts of a thousand experiments, that Mira kept her single obsession: a hunter's arrow tip, blackened and barbed, found in the belly of a long-dead whale three seasons ago. [38] [142]
The tip was not interesting for its age but for what it implied. [12] [154]
Something had killed something larger than anything should have been able to kill, and then had the decency to leave behind a single, beautiful point. [22] [176]
She was, by temperament, steadily curious, which is to say she approached every mystery the way one approaches a sleeping cat. [20] [196]
She wanted to know what the whale had been chasing, and what the arrow had been loosed from, and most of all what sound had rung out when the bowstring snapped. [28] [224]
She was convinced, in the marrow-deep way of all true inventors, that somewhere in that sound was the shape of a bell no one hears yet. [23] [247]
Her tool for listening was a kaleidoscope she had welded from the eyelet of a sextant and three shards of an old mirror. [21] [268]
When she turned it toward the arrow tip, the rust patterns bloomed into corridors, into rivers, into the long slow procession of a thousand whales surfacing through fog. [25] [293]
Through the images in a kaleidoscope, she could read the tip like an open book written in a script she did not yet know, so she did what any sensible mystic would do: she transliterated. [33] [326]
She gave the rust a name, and the barb a song, and traced the bevels into a small mechanical alphabet she set spinning on the bench. [24] [350]
That alphabet was the heart of the year's greatest joke on her, the yearning algorithm, a thing she had built in her twentieth year, half in jest and half in prayer. [30] [380]
It sat on the bench like a brass cat, with rotating copper eyelids and a tiny mouth that, when fed a question, would click and sigh until it found the shape of the answer it wished were true. [34] [414]
She fed it the whale, the arrow, the fog, and the little brass mouth opened, and out came, in soft clicks, the same chord she had been chasing since she was a girl. [33] [447]
She laughed, because the algorithm was not supposed to do that, and because the chord was not a chord at all but the suggestion of one, the way a name is the suggestion of a face. [35] [482]
She laughed the way a person laughs when they stand at the edge of something and feel, absurdly, that they are exactly where they ought to be, and she felt irreverent awe, the kind the old poets used to warn against, the kind that turns a fool into a witness. [49] [531]
Then she set to work, and the workshop hummed around her, the cogs turning as they always did, the acid sea hissing its slow green prayer below. [27] [558]
She spent a year, then three, then a decade, feeding the algorithm every fragment of the rust alphabet, watching through the kaleidoscope as the fragments grew into something like a language, the way a child's drawings grow into a map of a country no one has visited. [46] [604]
Throughout the evolution of her own small science, she learned the patience the sea requires, the patience of acid eating copper, of whales passing ships, of bells ringing in towers no one climbs. [33] [637]
In her forty-third year, she held the arrow tip to her ear, and the kaleidoscope to her eye, and asked the brass cat the question she had been saving for forty years. [30] [667]
What does it ring, she asked, this bell no one hears? [10] [677]
The algorithm clicked, the kaleidoscope bloomed, the cogs turned, and on the long iron bench, in the floating workshop, in the green hiss of the acid sea, the arrow tip began, very faintly, to sing. [34] [711]
