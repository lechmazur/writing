<story>Tollen wound the last spring of the evening and listened to the chamber breathe. [14] Below the city's forgotten floorboards, his resonance chamber held every orphaned melody the world had mislaid, cradled in brass and bone. [35] He was the last music box repairman of the lower wards, and the covenant of rust bound him to them all: a quiet oath sworn by his grandfather, signed in oxidation, never to let a song grow cold. [73] Tonight the oath felt heavier than usual, for three nights the chamber had hummed off-key and he had traced the wrongness to a single missing light. [99] He could feel the world hanging in the balance, the way a single detuned pin could unmake a waltz, and he knew something precious had been lifted from the world. [129] In his palm he kept a chip from a philosopher's stone, a sliver of the old transmuter's relic no bigger than a thumbnail, and it pulsed with a pale, knowing warmth. [160] Around lies it would dim; around truth it would shine. [170] He had learned this the hard way, watching his own hands wilt near dishonesty, the skin paling, the joints trembling, until the falsehood passed. [194] So he kept his workshop bare of customers he did not trust, and he did not touch the folded note until he had read it three times in the lamplight. [224] The note begged him to come to the belltower at the stroke of midnight, where a song had been stolen and the light with it. [249] He thought of his mother's saying: mending what isn't torn is the hardest work of all, for the tear lives in the ear, not the wood. [275] He gathered his tools: a soldering iron no hotter than a sparrow's heart, a coil of silver thread for tethering loose gears to the breath of older songs, and the small chip set in a locket of hammered tin. [314] At the eleventh bell he climbed the spiral stairs, each step humming faintly beneath his boots, as though the tower itself remembered the music it had lost. [341] The chamber at the top was dark, but Tollen's eyes were used to the small glories of dimness, to the way a single flame could make a whole room sing. [371] He set the chip upon the sill and watched its glow steady against the glass, a small steadfast hearth against the heavy air. [394] Then he saw her: a girl made of midnight, her cloak stitched from the hour that had stolen the light. [414] She did not speak, but her silence was full of machinery, of wheels that had stopped and springs that had forgotten how to uncoil. [438] Tollen knelt and offered her the silver thread without a word, and he did not ask what she had done or why. [460] He only asked if she wished the song to return, and the chip, lying true upon the sill, blazed in answer. [481] She nodded, almost imperceptibly, and he began to tether the lost melody back to its cradle, knotting silver between the tower's hidden pegs and the lullaby she had once known by heart. [513] Each knot was a tiny vow, a stitch in the covenant of rust, and as he worked he felt the chamber's balance right itself, the hum of the world growing warmer, fuller, less afraid. [547] When the twelfth bell rang, the light came rushing back, not as a flood but as a dawning, and the girl of midnight exhaled a single, long note that turned the dark to amber. [581] Tollen smiled, because his hands no longer wilted; the truth had stayed the air, and the chip pulsed once against his chest like quiet applause. [606] He climbed down before sunrise, his locket warm against his ribs, and the resonance chamber welcomed him with a chord he had never played before. [631] He wound it gently, listened, and understood that some songs are not repaired but remembered into being, and that the covenant of rust is simply this: to love the broken long enough for the light to return.
