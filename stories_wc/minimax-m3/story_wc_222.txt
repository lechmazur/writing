<story>Mara pressed her palm against the glass of the icebound spire above the clouds and watched the horizon bruise itself in colors no sky should ever wear. [27] Once an apprentice of wonder under the dreaming masters of the lowland academies, she had become something rarer, a chivalrous thief whose conscience still quivered at every lock she opened. [57] She had come here tonight to embrace the unknown with open arms, though her steps carried the hesitant grace of cautious abandon, each footfall testing the ice as though it might ring like a bell. [92] In her sleeve she carried her mentor's pocket sundial lid, a brass disc engraved with a single phrase that had launched her upon this skyward errand. [118] The lid was old, and she treated it with a reverence that was sacredly playful, polishing it with her thumb while humming a sailor's shanty she had learned in better seasons. [149] The order of the dreaming masters had sent her to amend a wrong that the spire itself had begun when its founder's petulant grief had broken the pact between heaven and earth. [181] Each dawn since, the sky above the spire had grown more wrong, running backward through dusk, raining feathers, then glass, then the soft unkept promises of men who never returned. [211] Mara meant to climb the spire, find the founder's frozen study, and lay the brass lid upon the chessboard there so that the broken pact might be mended. [239] The chess pieces themselves were no mere carvings but hand-carved reliquaries of ivory and ash, each one humming with the secrets inside that the founder had imprisoned long ago. [268] To amend the broken pact she must swap hand-carved chess pieces with their hidden doubles, each holding secrets inside, replacing each lie with its secret truth so that the spire might remember what it had sworn. [304] She reached the chamber door at last, its hinges of green ice weeping softly with the warmth of her breath. [324] Mara smiled with the quiet, sacredly playful air that had once earned her the master Tollen's grudging approval, and slipped inside as if she were a guest expected for tea. [354] There in the frozen study sat the great chessboard, its pieces standing in solemn rows like a congregation of sleeping priests. [375] Each hand-carved chess piece was a small masterpiece of pale wood, and each held its own secret whispered through a thin seam of resin. [399] Mara felt the strange hum of them in her teeth, a low choir that made her want both to weep and to laugh. [422] She moved with cautious abandon, never quite trusting the floor, never quite doubting her welcome. [437] From her sleeve she drew the pocket sundial lid, and its brass caught the sourceless light of the wrong sky and threw it back as gold. [463] The engraving on its face read only one phrase, and she spoke it now as she had been taught, in a voice that was both prayer and jest. [491] Word by word she began to amend the spire, swapping piece for piece: the pawn of forgotten names for the pawn of remembered ones, the knight of broken oaths for the knight of kept ones. [526] Each hand-carved chess piece surrendered its secret inside with a sigh like wind through old rafters, and Mara tucked each truth into her palm as a mother tucks a child. [556] The pocket sundial lid caught each secret too, its brass warming as though a tiny sun had been coaxed awake beneath her thumb. [579] Nineteen pieces she swapped, and the sky outside the window began, very slowly, to behave. [594] Only the king remained, the great carved figure that held the founder's own grief like a black pearl at its heart. [615] Mara held it for a long breath, understanding that this was the unknown she had sworn to embrace with open arms. [636] With the same chivalrous daring that had first set her upon the path of the apprentice of wonder, she lifted the king and whispered her own true name into its seam. [667] She laid the pocket sundial lid over the place where the king had stood, and the brass rang once like a small, perfect bell. [691] The pact was amended at last, and the sky, shocked into honesty, wept a single great tear of clean rain that fell through the spire and out beyond the clouds. [721] Mara left the way she had come, a chivalrous thief with empty hands and a heart full of every secret she had agreed to carry. [746] Behind her, the spire's ice cracked softly and began to thaw, and she laughed, sacredly playful even in the face of all she did not yet understand. [773] Somewhere far below, the masters of wonder would be waking to a sky that was, at last and at last, properly blue. [795]</story>
