<story>The morning had washed itself clean, and now a burnished hush lay over the cobalt lagoon after rain. [18] The quiet shoreline poet walked the marshy edge where reeds bowed under the retreating water. [33] Her name was Mirael, and she was known in the village not for songs but for the small verses she left pinned to fishermen's doors at dawn. [60] She carried nothing sharp and nothing loud. [67] Slipped into her satchel was a drake's tail feather, deep iridescent green tipped with a curl of bronze, salvaged weeks ago from a heron's kill. [92] She had been saving it for a kite she would never quite finish. [105] For months she had been tending the lagoon by harmonizing discord - the brassy gulls against the small coots, the rusted tin cans dragged in by storm against the silver minnows she counted at low tide. [140] She believed, with the strange devotion of her craft, that a place could be coaxed into harmony by the right attention. [161] Today the discord was fresh. [166] A child's kite string had snapped somewhere over the water, and the kite itself had come to rest against a clump of cattails twenty paces out. [192] The string trailed away to where it had broken, its frayed tip still holding the memory of the knot. [211] Mirael set her satchel down and waded ankle-deep into the shallows. [222] Her boots were soft with mud, and the lagoon brushed her hem like a cold sleeve. [238] Above the eastern reeds, the sun was beginning its slow work of weaving dawns - long gold threads laid across long blue threads, until the horizon became a loom. [266] She lifted the kite carefully, a small paper diamond of yellows and reds, soggy now but still holding its shape. [286] A dragonfly clung to its trailing tail. [293] She coaxed it onto her wrist and stood a moment, warmly attentive to the silence that had gathered after the wind. [314] The snapped string bothered her. [319] There was a finality to it that felt wrong, even though the kite itself was rescued. [335] She wanted to know what had broken first - the twine or the trust of the child holding it. [353] She thought of her own unwritten poems, the stanzas abandoned mid-meter, and understood that she had come to the lagoon partly to ask a question she had been carrying for years. [384] How does one find beauty in the incomplete? [392] She waded back to the shore and sat on a slab of driftwood that had been warming all morning. [411] She drew the drake's tail feather from her satchel, and with it, in the wet sand at the water's edge, she began to write. [435] She wrote slowly, in a hand that the lagoon had taught. [446] The feather caught the new sun and threw a small green light against her knuckles. [461] She did not try to make the writing pretty. [470] She set down what the day offered: the broken kite, the dragonfly patient on the paper, the cattails, the loom of dawn, the snapped sound that had carried across the water. [501] After a while the dragonfly lifted from the rescued kite and landed a breath away from her writing, tilting its head as if reading. [525] Mirael paused. [527] She had been, she realized, tending the morning itself - smoothing the place where wind had ended and stillness had begun, laying one image against another until they quieted each other. [557] Her method had always been this: to harmonize the discord between what had happened and what was left. [575] She was not trying to mend what had broken. [584] She was trying to discover beauty in the incomplete - in the kite without its string, in the dawn that was still being woven, in the feather's curl that did not point in any one direction, in the poem that would end before it had said everything she had meant it to say. [636] When she finished, the writing was already half-obscured by the rising tide, and she let it be. [653] The lagoon would receive the ink as it received everything: slowly, without complaint, in the same burnished hush that had greeted her at the shore. [678] She tucked the feather back into the satchel and stood. [688] The cobalt water caught her reflection and let it go. [698] She watched a heron flap low across the new reeds and thought of the original feather she had carried. [717] Somewhere inland, the child who had lost the kite was being told that losing things was part of having them. [737] Mirael walked along the shoreline, watching for the next broken thing to tend.
