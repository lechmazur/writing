<story>The pebble-shore oracle had crossed nine valleys and one mountain pass to reach the storm-tossed ridge fort, and she arrived at the hour when only one star remained in a dimming sky. [32] She had come because she was mildly obsessed with a story her grandmother had once told her at low tide, when the smooth stones clicked together like small teeth. [61] The story was of an endless night - a darkness that fell once and never lifted, and the people who had wandered through it, mapping its corridors by the sound of their own footsteps. [94] The oracle carried a flaking gold leaf booklet, its pages so delicate that she handled them only in still air. [114] Tonight, at the fort, she intended to retell the story - but she would do it her own way, by summarizing silence. [135] In her tradition, a true retelling did not describe events; it re-created the hush in which events had happened, holding it up like a glass so listeners could see themselves inside it. [167] She climbed the broken stairs of the watchtower as the wind rose, and the fort groaned around her. [185] When she reached the top, she opened the gold leaf booklet and let the first page fall open. [203] The page was blank, or seemed blank, but in the ambered hush of the storm's eye she could read the small marks pressed into the leaf - tiny circles, lines, and pauses. [234] Each pause represented a silence in the original telling. [243] Some were long enough to hold a year. [251] Others were as brief as a single held breath. [260] The oracle had spent her life learning to summarize these silences, to fold them into shapes that could be carried. [280] Now she would draw a map of the endless night itself - not of its roads or hills, but of the hushes between them. [303] The marbled hush of the ridge fort, with its layered winds and old stones, was the perfect medium. [321] She began, and the first silence she offered was the one her grandmother had used to open the tale - a long, amber pause in which the listener was meant to imagine the sky without stars. [356] The oracle whispered this silence into the storm. [364] The wind took it and broke it into smaller silences, which fell across the stones of the fort like rain. [384] She turned a page, and the gold leaf crumbled at the corner, releasing a faint dust that glittered in the last starlight. [406] This, too, was part of the telling. [413] Each flake that fell was a word that had once been spoken and was now only remembered as quiet. [432] She continued, moving through the booklet, summarizing silence after silence. [442] The fort, storm-tossed and old, leaned into its own age as if listening. [455] The oracle was mildly obsessed, yes, but her obsession was gentle - she did not wish to possess the endless night, only to draw its map so others might walk within it without fear. [488] As the single star began to fade, she reached the final page. [500] The last silence in the booklet was the largest, the one her grandmother had called the keeper of all the others. [521] The oracle stood very still and let it rise through her. [532] It filled the watchtower, then spilled down the stairs, then ran across the ridge like a slow, golden water. [551] The storm did not stop, but inside the marbled hush it made, the storm was only another kind of silence - the loud hush of weather, the soft hush of stone, the deep hush of a story that was, at last, complete. [592] Long after the star was gone, the oracle sat among the silent stones of the watchtower, listening to the map she had drawn humming in the dark. [619]</story>
