The chef Maren could taste memories in any dish she prepared, and that strange palate had driven her across the salt flats to a fortress half-swallowed by dunes. [28]
She arrived in the hour the rain stopped, the air still heavy with petrichor, every pitted stone of the fortress walls gleaming as if it had been freshly lacquered. [57]
Word had reached her in the bazaar of Karran that beneath the shifting sand lay a coral icebound pavilion, its walls frozen in a beauty no living coral should ever know. [88]
Stories said it held the remorse of an entire people, stored in the calcium lattice like salt in a jar, sealed away after a war none remembered beginning. [116]
Maren had no map, only an appetite for what others flinched from, so she dug where the runoff had carved its small rivers through the sand. [142]
By the third hour she struck the coral, and it rang beneath her spade like a struck bell, brittle yet sturdy enough to bear her weight when she climbed onto its dome. [174]
Rainwater had pooled in the natural basins around the pavilion, and she knelt at the largest of these, beginning the work of analyzing tidal pool formations as her grandmother once taught her. [206]
Every basin was a memory in miniature: the brine, the sediment, the bones of small creatures that had crawled in and never climbed out. [230]
She pressed her palm against the coral, and the stored remorse flooded her, for her gift made every emotion emotionally contagious through touch; she tasted the unsaid goodbye of an old woman who had outlived her only son. [268]
The taste was sturdy, brittle, a thing that could hold the weight of centuries and yet shatter if you spoke its name aloud. [291]
Maren swallowed it, because a chef does not waste ingredients, and because her own grief had long needed sanding into a smoother shape. [314]
She had come here, though she could not have said it plainly, to sandpaper rough memories until they no longer caught the throat on the way down. [341]
The pavilion seemed to know her purpose, for as she circled it her reflection grew older, then younger, then older again, a face she almost recognized from a dream she had refused to finish. [375]
It was at the close of that unspoken dream that the coral began to hum, and she understood that the place meant to subsume her whole, to fold her into its lattice of regret and never release her. [413]
She did not run, because she had walked too far and hurt too long, and because some hungers are best fed in the place where they were born. [441]
Instead she pulled from her pack a wooden mortar and the herbs she had gathered across the seven deserts, and began to grind them into a paste. [468]
She worked the paste into the coral with her thumbs, listening as each pore drank it up, analyzing the tidal pool formations one by one as the brine changed color from iron to amber to something almost sweet. [506]
Where the sandpaper of her will touched a memory, it softened; where it did not, the remorse hardened and spread, a stain she could not lift. [532]
An old man's apology for a child he never acknowledged rose into the air as vapor, and she breathed it in, forgiveness loosening in her own chest. [559]
At the close of an unspoken dream, the pavilion stopped humming, and Maren stood inside a coral chamber that had not existed a moment before, walls thin as eggshell. [588]
Inside her, the absorbed remorse had been gently sanded; what had been jagged was now smooth enough to swallow, what had been bitter was now only faintly sour, like a lemon left too long in salt. [624]
She understood then she had not been swallowed but had become a vessel, the pavilion lending her its grief as a chef lends a guest the sharpest knife. [652]
The next morning she walked out into the dunes, and wherever she paused, the sand beneath her feet held the faintest impression of a footprint made of coral. [680]
In every kitchen she entered afterward, the guests wept for reasons they could not name, because her touch still carried the contagious weight of what she had absorbed. [708]
Maren cooked with that weight on purpose, sandpapering each plate until its sharpest edge was smooth, her hands sturdy, her heart brittle as the coral she still dreamed of in the small hours. [741]
She never told anyone the full story of the pavilion beneath the dunes, for some recipes are meant to be tasted, never spoken, and the dream of the place is enough. [772]
