The dusk settled over the xenobiology research station like a cool hand over a fevered brow, and Vera climbed the last rungs to the magnetic nexus platform. [27]
She was an obliging sky mechanic, which meant she answered every call from the biologists below with the same patient nod. [48]
The station floated on its magnetic bones above the violet seas of Garran-IV, tethered by nothing but field and faith, and it groaned softly as twilight shifted. [75]
At this hour, the great-winged specimens of the upper canopy were returning to their roosts, their wingbeats slowing from thunder to whisper until the sky went quiet. [102]
Vera called that time "the hush of beating wings at rest," and she did her most delicate work inside it. [122]
Tonight the nexus needed her. [127]
It was a latticed ring of cobalt alloy strung between the station's twin spires, designed to gather the planet's faint magnetic breath and coax living tissue out of dormancy. [156]
To Vera, it was also mechanically warm, a contradiction she had stopped questioning: the metal hummed against her gloves like a sleeping animal, even as frost feathered its outer curves. [186]
She checked the readouts through her goggles, which painted the world in soft cyan through holographic principles that made every wire and cell glow. [210]
The chief xenobiologist, an earnest man named Solin, had asked her to perform a test. [225]
The specimens recovered from the deep thermal vents were beginning to stir in fragments, a process the lab called subdued awakenings: hearts that remembered how to beat without remembering why. [255]
Solin believed these awakenings were evidence of something larger he had been trying to articulate for years. [272]
He wanted, he said, to prove that time moves sideways on this world, that a creature could wake into the future by sliding along the present rather than marching through it. [303]
He could not do it alone. [309]
Vera, who had fixed his coffee dispenser seven times and once rewired his daughter's music box, was, as always, obliging. [329]
She swung along the gantry until she stood above the nexus's central cradle, where a single brass lever waited in its housing like a question. [354]
She laid her palm flat against it and felt the warmth pulse up through the alloy. [370]
"Hologram on," she murmured, and the air bloomed with the soft architecture of the specimen they were studying: a translucent, lunged thing with feathered cilia along its spine. [398]
It hung before her in the cobalt light, breathing in colors she had no names for. [414]
Vera pulled the lever. [418]
The nexus sang. [421]
Not loudly, but the way a kettle sings when the water is almost ready: a low, patient, mechanically warm note that traveled up through the station's bones. [448]
One by one, in the hush of the roost, the creatures answered. [460]
Their wings stirred into a kind of listening, and Vera watched the holographic readout of their metabolic fields bloom outward in concentric rings, each ring a moment that did not replace the last but joined it, side by side. [499]
Solin's theory, given form. [503]
Time, here, did not march. [508]
It sidled. [510]
Vera felt her own pulse settle into the rhythm of the rings, slower than her heartbeat, and she understood why Solin called her obliging: she did what was asked at the exact pace the world required. [546]
She eased the lever back, and the hologram dimmed, and the creatures' wings settled into rest. [562]
The frost on the nexus caught the last twilight, and for a moment the whole station was a single cold jewel with a warm heart. [587]
Vera climbed down, her gloves steaming faintly in the chill, and thought about her mother, who had once told her that love was the only kind of time that moved sideways. [618]
She did not write this down. [624]
She simply filed it beside the coffee dispensers and music boxes, in the quiet cabinet of things she kept without naming. [645]

"Well?" he asked. [681]
Vera took her mug, wrapped her cold fingers around its mechanically warm ceramic, and smiled. [696]
"It moves," she said. [700]
"And it waits," he said. [705]
The dusk deepened into the long blue hour of Garran-IV, and somewhere in the lab a specimen opened an eye it had forgotten it owned, and the sky mechanic, who had never needed to be more than obliging, felt the whole station breathe with her. [750]
