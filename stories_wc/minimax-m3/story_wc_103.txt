<story>The hedge maze designer kept her labyrinth on the bones of an old arched bridge, where each planter was a living rib of ivy and wisteria. [28] Such a garden - a living bridge garden - was an unusual commission; the city council had asked for beauty that walked you somewhere before you knew where you were going. [61] She had accepted the work because she herself was searching, in her own quieter way, to discover the texture of time. [83] Not its mathematics, not its ticking, but the grain of it beneath her fingernails, the way a hand remembers the rope it clung to in childhood long after the rope itself is gone. [118] For years she had been retrocognitively trapped, unable to stand in any present moment without first remembering a moment that had not yet arrived at her; the now was always a porch she reached through a hallway she had not walked, and she grew dizzy on its threshold, her hands full of futures that had already been. [181] The photographs she kept in the gardener's hut at the bridge's crown were her small consolation. [199] They were old prints of her own hands, of the first seedlings, of a wedding she had not attended but somehow mourned; each one was fading now, the silver halide dissolving in its bath, the faces turning into a soft sepia smudge of an afternoon nobody had recorded on purpose. [252] She watched them go with the patience of someone who had outlived their meaning but could not bring herself to throw the paper away. [278] Then one morning a halo fragment turned up in the mulch at the path's center, and her patience cracked into something brighter. [302] It was a sliver of something luminous, half-buried amongst the privet, casting a thin trembling ring onto the leaves above it, as though a saint had brushed against the garden in passing and dropped a chip of her aureole into the soil. [350] She knelt, and the moment she touched it, the world fell forward through her in a long, slow wave. [371] She saw gardeners older than the river, a hedge not yet dreamed, a sky in which no bridge stood at all, and she understood at last that this was no mineral she had found. [406] She was a designer of hedges by trade; she knew that ordinary Euclidean geometry made closed loops, returning the wanderer to her starting point. [432] But this fragment belonged to a different mathematics. [441] It unfolded space the way her mazes were not supposed to: every step she took away from its glow shortened the distance to its center, and every step she took toward it spiraled her outward into regions the bridge had never contained. [483] She understood then, with the hush of someone hearing a chord resolve, that she was holding something shaped by hyperbolic geometry, surfaces of constant negative curvature, saddle-shaped, where parallel lines bent apart from each other and triangles had angles whose sum was less than a whisper. [532] Time, in such a space, could not move forward the way water moves down a drain. [550] It moved outward, in skins, in peels, the way an onion remembers itself into the air. [567] This was the texture she had been seeking, all these years, this was the feel of time beneath her palms. [587] Behind her, in the hut, the photographs began to disintegrate without ceremony. [600] The silver in their emulsion, already half-spent by years of waiting, was the first to go; then the paper, curling each remembered face into a flake the bridge wind took up and scattered into the maze below, where it drifted through the leaves like a tiny, deliberate snow. [649] She felt no mourning. [653] The garden, in its quaking reverie, leaned toward her with its many green arms; the wisteria trembled on its trellises as if in prayer; the river clapped softly against the stone piers; and she stood at the center of the living maze she had built for strangers, haloed in a half-light that was not hers, and understood that to discover the texture of time she would have to let go of every flat picture of it she had ever kept. [737] That evening she redesigned her hedges so that they curved away from every entrance, so that no wanderer could find their way back, and every wanderer would have to travel, at last, the full strange skin of a single moment.
