The pier had been dead for forty years before the market breathed again beneath its bones. [16]
Lena Marrow knew the route by smell alone - salt-rot, diesel, the sweet cloy of cured tobacco - and slipped between the barnacled pilings as the dusk thickened into a velvet bruise of sky. [48]
She was the moonshine runner's daughter, and although her father had long since traded his copper still for a quiet grave, every lantern-lit dock still remembered her hands. [76]
Tonight she carried no jars. Tonight she carried a recording horn, a wax cylinder, and the patient hunger of a woman paid to listen. [100]
The buyer had called it a truce of hush, and paid her in gold to be there until sunrise for one specific sound: the moment trust broke between the two old captains who were set to meet below the rotting planks. [141]
She found the market in a cavern of tar and shadow, where lanterns swung from rusted chains and contraband glittered in the gloom. [164]
Pirates haggled over bolts of silk that had been kissed by every sea, over pocket watches with no faces, over bottles whose contents hummed when the wind shifted. [192]
Lena did not linger. She chose a beam, set her horn, and waited for the captains to arrive. [210]
They came in through separate doors, as if the truce required them to enter loneliness before they could meet in silence. [231]
Captain Vex wore a coat of sealskin and a frown that had curdled into something almost kind. [248]
Captain Morda carried, in his palm, the antique watch fob she had been told to watch - a tarnished silver disc engraved with a name she did not ask to read. [278]
He clicked it open. A tiny chime, brittle as moth wings, rang once and was swallowed by the dark. [297]
This was the language of their accord. The hush was not silence but the absence of betrayal, and she knew that when the chime no longer sounded between them, the accord would be over. [331]
But the night was not so generous. The first false start came as a boy stumbled into the captain's path with a tray of smuggled gin, glass shattering at Morda's boots. [362]
Lena cursed under her breath. Her horn caught the clatter and, worse, the gasp of surprise that escaped Vex's lips - a gasp that was, technically, a sound of trust straining. [392]
She had to thwart her own equipment's eagerness. She muffled the horn with a damp cloth and reset the cylinder, her fingers curiously steadfast despite the cold sweat on her knuckles. [423]
The second false start: a parrot, escaped from some trader's cage, screaming into the rafters a song that sounded almost like laughter. [445]
The third: a child, lost and weeping for a mother who had probably never existed on this pier. [463]
Each interruption, she recorded, discarded, and reset. The market pulsed around her like a sleeping beast, comforting and uneasy in equal measure, its breath a mix of woodsmoke and sea. [493]
Around midnight, the captains moved to the agreement's heart. They sat across a crate draped in black velvet, and began the slow ritual of their peace. [519]
Vex spoke first - a low murmur about a debt owed in 1898, a daughter, a drowned son. [536]
Morda answered with another chime of the antique watch fob, his thumb against the seam. [551]
The truce held. The hush deepened. [557]
Lena realized with a small shock that the sound she had been sent to capture was not a shout, a draw of steel, a curse. [582]
It would be softer than that. [588]
It would be the moment one of them simply stopped clicking the fob. [601]
She waited, her horn pressed to the beam, her breath shallow as a held note. [616]
Just before dawn, Vex reached across and placed his hand over Morda's knuckles, stilling the chime forever. [633]
There was a click - but it was the closing of the fob, the seal of an end. [650]
No chime. No hush renewed. The trust broke like a glass dropped from a great height, inaudibly at first, then completely. [671]
Lena's cylinder turned. Her hand was steady. [678]
She had caught it: the small, definitive stillness where two men stopped pretending. [691]
She packed up as the first pearl of sun threaded the boards above her, and slipped back through the pilings, the fob's last silence still humming in her horn. [720]
