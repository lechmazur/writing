Maren kept her notebook open even when her hands were too cold to write. [14]
She owned the only nursery on Vermilion Lane, a narrow strip of greenhouses tucked behind the river where the fog settled each morning like a held breath. [41]
Every storm season, she came back to the same mossy trellis after rain, notebook balanced on her knee, to study plant behaviors that no botanist she knew had bothered to describe. [72]
Her colleagues thought her method unscientific. [78]
She had a way of being circuitously sincere with them, agreeing with their critiques in long, patient paragraphs until they forgot they had criticized her at all. [105]
The trellis leaned against the south wall of the propagation house, where climbing roses and a wild grapevine had been allowed, over decades, to do almost as they pleased. [134]
After a hard rain, the moss along its wooden slats bloomed into a green so vivid it seemed lit from beneath. [155]
Maren liked it best then. [160]
She would crouch among the puddles and watch. [168]
Her motivation was never purely academic, though she told her grant officer it was. [182]
She wanted to study plant behaviors because plants were the only honest teachers she had ever known. [199]

It was turning. [234]

She lifted her pencil. [258]
The leaf was mid-metamorphosis, and it was, she could see, reluctant about it. [271]
Something in the curl of its edges suggested a creature being asked to leave a familiar skin. [288]
Maren recognized the posture. [292]
For three years she had been putting off a decision about the nursery itself. [306]
The land had been sold. [311]
A developer wanted the river frontage. [317]
She had been drafting a defense, then tearing it up, then drafting it again, circling the truth of what she wanted without quite landing on it. [343]
Being circuitously sincere with herself had become her most practiced skill. [354]
She set the notebook down and let her hands hang loose between her knees. [368]
The storm had passed, but another was forecast for evening. [378]

If she wanted to understand what the linden leaf was doing, she would have to wait, and she would have to watch without forcing a shape on it. [427]
That was the rule of her work, and it was the rule she could not apply to her own life. [447]

The plants, predictably, had resisted. [480]
