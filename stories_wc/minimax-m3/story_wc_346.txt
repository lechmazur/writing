The dusk came soft as a held breath to the hidden lighthouse, its beam a thin ribbon of gold laid over the darkening sea. [24] Inside the round chamber, Mira wound the great wheel of her craft, the doomsday clock adjuster of the Last Coast. [44] She had walked here through seven valleys and forgotten names, carrying in her cloth satchel a broken puppet head with one remaining eye of tarnished glass. [70] That eye caught the lamplight and scattered it into small, weeping stars that wandered the curved walls like restless ghosts. [90] Tonight the clock must be set one careful tick past the border of forever, and she had come, as she had come so many times before, to vouchsafe to the dying world a gentler crossing. [125] She set the puppet head upon the worn workbench, and the pane of hush that was the lighthouse itself seemed to lean closer, listening. [149] Mira was beautifully flawed, her hands scarred from a thousand prior adjustments, half her sight gone from staring too long at the gears of ending. [174] But those flaws had taught her where the seams of time lay thinnest, and tonight she would need every tender scar. [195] She laid out the brass keys of her trade, each one etched with the sigil of a forgotten kingdom, a forgotten hour, a forgotten lullaby. [220] The clock stood in the central well of the lighthouse, its face worn to a mother-of-pearl blur that seemed to breathe. [241] She wound it not by force but by song, threading her voice through the nested patterns of the mechanism as a needle might thread through old embroidered cloth. [269] Each note she sang was also a key, each key a memory, each memory a thousand small lives, each small life another note, and the pattern turned inward and outward at once, like the chambers of a nautilus, like grief, like prayer. [312] She thought of the children she had known in towns that no longer existed, of the lullabies she had once been paid for in bread and kind glances. [340] Across the hush of time's final ripple, she wished to whisper a single lullaby that would outlive the sea, outlive the dark, outlive every clock. [365] She lifted the broken puppet head and pressed it gently to the curved glass of the lighthouse, and the pane of hush sang back to her in a voice that was every voice she had ever loved. [402] There, in the meeting of glass and tarnished eye, the lullaby took shape, a beautifully flawed thing stitched from grief and small mercies, from the ache of mothers and the stubbornness of children. [435] She coaxed it outward through the nested patterns of her craft, vouchsafe by vouchsafe, breath by breath, until the air around her glowed with unheard music. [461] The doomsday clock stirred in its housing, and the dusk outside deepened into the indigo of a bruise. [479] Somewhere far away, a child who had never yet been born stirred in the long sleep of becoming. [497] Somewhere older, an old woman lifted her face from her pillow and felt the music pass through her like warm rain through a broken roof. [522] Mira sang until her throat was a husk, until the broken puppet head wept silver from its one good eye, until the pane of hush around her had absorbed every tremor of her voice. [556] Then she wound the clock one final, careful turn, and the mechanism whispered a word that meant forever, and the lullaby began its crossing. [580] Across the hush of time's final ripple, the melody walked outward in concentric rings, each ring a thousand lifetimes wide. [600] And Mira, the doomsday clock adjuster, the beautifully flawed singer of endings, sat down upon the cold floor and let the dusk hold her as gently as a tired mother. [630] She had vouchsafed what could not be kept. [638] The lighthouse lamp burned on, casting its thin gold ribbon across the darkening sea. [652] The puppet head watched the dark with its one good eye, and the pane of hush held them all in a bruised awe, and the clock hummed its soft, bruised hallelujah. [683]
