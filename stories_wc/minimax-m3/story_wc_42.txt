Iolan, the sage camera repairer, had spent forty years inside the lunar observatory that floated above the clouds on a spine of black glass. [24] He was a quiet man, more familiar with shutters and lenses than with sleep, and he kept his hair long to brush the dust from the smallest cogs. [52] The observatory was a relic from an age when the world still trusted its eyes, and he had been its only keeper since his teacher, Esma, closed her eyes for the final time. [85] Esma had been changelessly constant, a fixed star in his orbit, and he had shaped his life around her silence the way a river shapes its bed. [112] The day the foundations shook, the clouds below churned like milk. [123] Iolan was winding a brass lens when the tremor climbed the glass spire and kissed the dome. [140] It was not a violent thing; it was the polite announcement of a world rearranging itself. [156] Mountains shifted in their sleep, and the sea drew back a hand. [168] He set down his tool and listened, and in the hush he heard a single leaf fall somewhere in the garden far below, a sound so soft it barely existed, and that sound became his compass. [204] In the central chamber the great clock hand refused to rest. [215] It had been turning for as long as anyone could remember, marking not the hours but the slow rotation of the stars, and tonight it spun faster, a silver needle trembling in the moon's eye. [250] Iolan approached it with the reverence of a man greeting an old wound. [263] The hand was changelessly constant, and yet it seemed to mourn. [274] He understood then what the tremor had loosened: the world had grown tired of holding its shapes, and even the heavens were beginning to slip. [299] He had come to the observatory to harness the ephemeral, that fleeting flash of light that visits a camera's eye only once, the breath of a moment that is gone before the shutter closes. [333] He had spent his life building devices that could catch it, hold it, make it speak. [349] But the light he sought was not of the sun. [359] It was the light in Esma's last smile, the one she had given him as the breath left her, and he had never caught it. [384] The cameras had all failed, the plates had come back blank, and he had chased that brightness for forty years. [404] Now the foundations of the world were shaking, as if to say there was no more time to wait. [423] So Iolan began to restructure the great clock, laying out his tools on a cloth of white wool, each one polished to a quiet gleam. [448] He disassembled the mechanism with the care of a man unwrapping a memory. [461] He removed the gears that had kept the hand turning in its old, faithful circle. [476] He replaced them with new ones cut from the meteorite that had fallen the day Esma died, a stone he had kept in a velvet box for four decades. [505] He aligned the springs by the sound of a leaf falling, listening to the soft percussion of its landing, matching each tick of the mechanism to that whisper. [533] It was absurd, perhaps, to tune a cosmic instrument to a falling leaf, but the leaf knew the weight of patience, and patience was what he needed to break free of the gravity that kept him earthbound. [570] When the new gears were set, he aimed the clock's eye not at the stars but at the heart of the tremor itself, at the place where the world's foundations were shaking loose. [603] He wanted the image to achieve escape velocity, to rise from the shaking ground and climb into the silence above the clouds where nothing could pull it back. [631] He had calculated the angle by the moon, and the moon by the sound of the leaf, and now, with the clock hand still spinning, still refusing to stop, he let the shutter open for one breath. [668] The light that entered was not the light of the sun. [679] It was the color of Esma's last smile, and it was brief, and it was the only thing that had ever truly escaped. [702] Iolan felt it pass through the lens into the silvered plate, and the clock hand gave a single, luminous shudder, then went still. [725] The foundations groaned and settled, and the clouds below smoothed into a pale, aching sea. [740] Iolan, the sage camera repairer, sat down among his tools, and for the first time in forty years, he closed his eyes without feeling he had lost something. [768] The observatory held the image in its heart, a single point of light that would not fade, and Iolan knew the light had achieved the speed to outrun everything, even time. [799]
