Sera, an onyx-ink cartomancer, had kept her vigil in a midnight glade above the clouds since the night after the birth of her daughter. [24]
She had not climbed up here to read fortunes; she had climbed up to repent in a place suspended between earth and ether, where the air was thin enough that beauty itself became something one had to struggle to swallow. [64]
The clouds beneath her knees were not solid, only dense enough to bear a kneeling woman, and they carried the hush of all the apologies she had been too proud to speak aloud. [97]
This was her cloudbound repentance, and she had chosen it the way the drowning chooses one more breath. [115]
In her lap she held a deck of cards stained with onyx ink, the same cards she had used for years to read the futures of farmers and lovers and small frightened children. [148]
Beside her, set into a fold of her cloak as if it were a relic, lay a porcelain fragment from a dolls' tea set. [172]
It was the saucer, cracked clean through its little painted roses, and she had carried it up from the birthing room because her daughter's small fingers had broken it in that bewildered hour when Sera had held the child and felt nothing she could name. [217]
She had wanted, very much, to feel something she could name. [228]
The crack in the saucer was the first thing that looked back at her with a familiar shape, and so she had taken it and borne it with her on the long stair of cloud. [263]
She was, by her own quiet admission, willingly perplexed. [272]
She could have understood her daughter's first cry as simple hunger, and her own silence as familiar exhaustion, but she refused the easier readings. [296]
She shuffled her cards slowly, each blackened arc catching what little moonlight filtered through the higher clouds, and she laid them out not to divine but to delay the understanding she was afraid to reach. [331]
The cards, as they so often did, fell into the pattern she had been taught to read as childhood. [350]
Then the custom of tracing hands on birthday tablecloths surfaced in her, the way a drowned thing surfaces once and is gone. [372]
In her family, every birthday had meant pressing a small palm against wet paint and leaving its outline on linen that grew, year by year, into a thicket of hands, each one a small ghost of a year survived. [411]
She had been the child who traced; she had been the mother who did not, and her own daughter's hand was not on any tablecloth. [436]
She whispered to the cards the question she had carried up through the regions of ordinary weather and into the place where weather itself becomes a kind of memory. [465]
"Where does lost time accumulate?" [470]
Somewhere up here, she believed, there were cisterns in which the minutes one had failed to notice pooled and thickened, and if she found one, she could pour a little of it back over the morning she had missed, over the first cry she had heard without answering. [518]
She began to unfasten the braid of red silk she had tied around the broken saucer, the binding that had held the two halves together since the night she had stolen it from the cradle-side table. [554]
Her fingers were clumsy in the thin air, and she was weeping, though the tears, when they left her lashes, did not fall; they rose, jewel-bright, and hung about her face like a small constellation of regrets she had been carrying in lieu of prayers. [599]
When the last knot fell away, the two halves of the saucer drifted apart on her palms, and the crack between them began to glow with the soft, unbearable blue of a candle inside a paper lantern. [636]
The beauty of it was almost enough to stop her breath, and it did, for a long moment, stop something in her chest that she had been calling grief because she did not yet know its real name. [674]
This was the kind of beauty that chokes - that lifts you by the throat and will not put you down until you have agreed to see. [700]
In the glow between the porcelain halves, she saw the outline of a small hand pressed against light, as if waiting to be lifted into paint. [726]
She understood then that lost time accumulated in the spaces between a mother's hands and her child's, in the cracks of broken things, in the tablecloths never marked. [754]
She pressed her palm to the fracture, and the glade filled, briefly, with the sound of her daughter's first breath. [774]
The weight she had carried up settled into something almost forgivable, and Sera, the onyx-ink cartomancer, began the slow work of coming down. [797]
