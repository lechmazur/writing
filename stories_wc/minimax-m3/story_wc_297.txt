Maren had been called root-bound since she was small, a name the elders gave to mean her visions sprouted from the soil of the Cobalt Glade and could not be carried far. [32]
She accepted the title without complaint, though she was also purposefully curious, the sort who bent close to every whispering leaf and listened to the small stories mosses tell. [61]
Tonight the glade was drenched in its nightly dye, a deep mineral cobalt that pooled in the hollows, clung to the fronds of the ferns, and turned the path into a river of dusk. [95]
She knelt before the maze topiary, the ancient green labyrinth whose hedges had been clipped by her grandmother and her grandmother's grandmother, every cut a sentence in a long, patient grammar. [126]
With a small bronze blade her mother had forged in a dream, she took a single cutting, a tender shoot no longer than her finger, and slid it into the leather pouch at her belt. [161]
The topiary remembered. The clipping would remember too. [169]
That was the old law, the one written into the eclipsed covenant that bound her people to this place, a promise made in dim ink and older blood. [197]
The covenant had promised a century of quiet, a hundred years of roots and rain and soft dreaming, before the great alignment would test the glade's patient heart. [225]
But the future had arrived early. [231]
The sky had begun to bruise in the wrong places, a violet wound above the eastern hills, and the moss under Maren's knees hummed with a frequency she had only ever tasted in her deepest visions. [267]
She had spent her life tracing the threads of what might be, and now those threads had knotted into something she could almost hold, almost name, almost follow to its source. [298]
Her motivation was simple and impossible: to find the intersection of dreams, the single bright point where every sleeping mind in the glade met and remembered itself as one. [327]
The oak at the glade's heart was scarred in long spirals, each scar a record of a visionary who had tried before her, who had reached and returned or reached and not. [359]
She pressed her palm to the bark, linking through scars, letting the old failures warm against her skin, reading the warnings the way a sailor reads a fading map. [388]
Some had reached the intersection. [393]
Few had come back with their minds intact. [401]
Tonight she would heed the warnings written there, the ones only a root-bound visionary could read, and she would walk, though walking had never been her gift. [428]
The roots would have to loosen. [434]
The intersection of dreams was not a place you could find while your heels were buried in the moss, while your thoughts were still tethered to the loam that had raised you. [466]
She felt the anxious elation rise in her chest, that trembling joy the glade itself seemed to share, a gladness edged with the cold awareness of what was at stake. [496]
Because the future arriving early was not only a warning. [506]
It was also a gift. [511]
There was still time to choose what the tomorrow would become, before the eclipse sealed the covenant's last clause and locked the world into a shape no one could unmake. [541]
Maren stood, her bare feet pale against the darkening moss, and the glade leaned toward her in a way that was almost a bow, almost a blessing. [568]
She tucked the clipping deeper into the pouch, felt it curl like a small green animal against her hip, and began to walk. [591]
The path out of the glade was narrow and wound between hills that looked, in the failing light, like the shoulders of sleeping giants dreaming their own slow dreams. [620]
She walked with one hand on the clipping, the other trailing along the bracken, her eyes fixed on the bruise spreading across the eastern sky. [645]
Behind her, the cobalt lingered, holding its breath, unwilling to let her go. [658]
Ahead, the future waited, already knocking, already inside the walls of the world, already rearranging the furniture of the morning. [678]
She would heed it. [682]
She would find the intersection, or the intersection would find her, and either way the dreamers of the glade would wake into a world they had helped to choose, rather than one chosen for them. [717]
Anxious, elated, rooted only in purpose now, Maren walked into the dusk and did not look back, though the glade watched her until the last blue light failed. [745]
