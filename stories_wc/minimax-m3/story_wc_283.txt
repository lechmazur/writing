The amber warehouse beneath the cliffs exhaled in mineral dust, its rafters bowed under the weight of forgotten time, and the air inside carried the slow perfume of pine resin and old paper. [33]
Mira, the last hourglass turner of the eastern reach, walked the long aisles each dawn to listen for what the sand refused to say, her bare feet leaving prints in the gold that drifted from the upper bins. [71]
She was quietly inspired, her hands steady, her breath shallow, the way the cliffs themselves seemed to listen before any storm, and she carried this hush with her like a second garment. [103]
In her apron pocket rested a single jar, sealed with beeswax, holding the canyon's echo she had once caught at dusk: a low, patient hum that trembled the glass whenever truth leaned close, and that never failed to settle when a lie passed by. [147]
The prophecy was old, half-buried, spoken by a voice the cliffs no longer remembered, and it had been folded into a language that only objects could read. [174]
It promised nothing less than the shape of a new century, but it had been knotted into riddles, each word a bead lost from a broken string that once wound around the world. [207]
Mira believed that to spin a prophecy into raw possibility, she must first unscramble the lost threads, taking each tangle apart until only the original fiber remained. [234]
The method had come to her in a dream, delivered by an old button maker whose face she could not recall: by cataloging lost buttons, she would learn the alphabet of what had been forgotten. [269]
So she worked, kneeling on the cool stone floor of the amber warehouse, sorting every button she could find, from bone discs of shepherds to enamel rosettes of forgotten brides, from iron loops of soldiers to pearl studs of children who had grown old in the upper town. [317]
Each button was a syllable; each buttonhole, a place where the world had once closed and then opened, and Mira wrote their names in a ledger of moth-eaten vellum that smelled of cedar. [350]
When she threaded a button onto a string of moonlight pulled from the highest window, the prophecy spoke a single word into the jars on the shelf, and the jars answered in glass. [383]
The canyon echo in the jar grew louder with every word she released, until the sand in the nearest hourglass began to flow upward, against itself, as though time had decided to listen rather than measure. [419]
The hourglass she held was her own inheritance, turned by her mother, and her mother's mother, and now by her, a chain of turning that bound the family to the cliffs. [450]
Mira did not speak, for the warehouse was a place of unearthed silence, and even the cliffs had learned not to interrupt, and the echo in the jar kept its own counsel. [482]
She worked on, night after night, threading button after button, and slowly, as the truth unravels, the prophecy took the shape of a path that led from the warehouse floor to the heart of the canyon beyond. [519]
The path was not written in words but in objects: a brass button, a bone button, a glass button, each one a year, each one a turn of the glass, each one a breath held by the cliffs. [557]
On the ninety-seventh night, the jars on the shelves began to chime in sequence, a quiet music that rose from the lowest bin to the highest rafter, and Mira heard the prophecy say its final word: share. [594]
She understood, as the sand settled into its new direction, that shared wisdom was the only kind worth keeping, for a truth held by one was only a rumor, but a truth held by many became a country. [632]
The canyon echo softened then, returning to its first low hum, and Mira felt the cliffs exhale as if they had been holding their breath for a thousand years. [661]
She lifted the hourglass from its hook, and as she turned it once, the new century began to fall, grain by golden grain, into the dust of the warehouse, and the prophecy became possibility. [695]
The button-string she left behind on the highest shelf, a small bright line in the amber dark, so that whoever came after her would find the alphabet already begun. [724]
She had spun a prophecy into raw possibility, and the cliffs kept the echo long after she had gone, humming it softly into the stones for the next turner to hear. [755]
