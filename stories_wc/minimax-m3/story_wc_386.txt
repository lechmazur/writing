The wreck-diver arrived at the shrine before the aurora woke. [10]
She was a whisperer of ghosts, but tonight she came not to listen, but to ask. [26]
The shrine was carved into the heart of a glacier, and candles in hand-cut alcoves threw borrowed starlight across the blue walls. [48]
In her palm she cradled a melted snow globe, its village smeared, its storm stilled, its glass clouded with three winters of sorrow. [71]
She had pulled it from the freighter that broke its spine on the shoal three years ago, when Mira drowned reaching for it. [94]
Mira's ghost was quiet now, as most of them were quiet when the whisperer came, because they knew what she was and what she wanted. [119]
She wanted truth, and truth required windows of connection that most divers were too frightened to open. [136]
She had learned to weave truth carefully, knotting fact to feeling so the dead would not feel betrayed by the living. [157]
Tonight she needed to expose a thing she had hidden even from herself. [170]
She set the melted globe on the altar of ice and knelt, and the candles bent toward her like attentive ghosts. [191]
Outside, the glacier groaned as it always groaned at this hour, a sound like a sleeping god turning in his bed. [212]
She began the old work: reading the dead by their cameo appearances in strangers' dreams, glimpses in windows and doorways and crowded rooms. [235]
A girl in a fishing village two hundred miles south dreamed of a woman standing behind glass. [252]
The whisperer watched through her own window of connection, a window that smelled of salt and frost and the slow ticking of remembered grief. [276]
The woman behind the glass was Mira, younger, alive, holding a snow globe that had not yet melted. [294]
In the dream, Mira set the globe on a table and pressed her palm against a windowpane. [311]
On the other side, a small child reached up, fingers spread, and pressed back. [325]
The whisperer felt the contact in her own palm, and the melted globe in her hands grew warm. [343]
This was what she had come to expose: the truth she had mistaken for three winters. [359]
When she had dived the wreck and seen Mira's hands still reaching, she had thought Mira was reaching for the globe. [380]
But Mira had been reaching through the glass, through the memory of glass, toward a window where a child waited. [400]
A child who had not been on the ship, who had stood at that window every morning since, who dreamed of her every winter, cameo-silent, cameo-present, cameo-grieving. [427]
The whisperer had taken the globe because she wanted something pure from the wreckage, something that still held a piece of someone who had loved it. [453]
She had not understood, then, that the pure thing was not the globe itself. [467]
The pure thing was the love, and the love had been aimed at a child standing at a window, waiting for a mother who would not come home. [495]
She closed her eyes, and the borrowed starlight of the candles held her in place as she began to weave truth carefully, threading the dreamer's memory into Mira's unfinished story. [525]
She exposed the truth gently, the way her teacher had taught her: Mira had not been reaching for the globe. [545]
She had been reaching through it, through the glass, toward a window where a child waited. [561]
In the fishing village, the child woke with tears still drying on her cheeks, and the melted snow globe on her dresser was warm to the touch. [588]
On the glacier, the whisperer watched the cameo change in the dreamer's sleeping mind: no longer a ghost glimpsed in a window, but a mother, finally seen, finally recognized, finally allowed to pass. [621]
She bowed her head, and the candles dimmed one by one, and the glacier exhaled, and the borrowed starlight went back where it had always come from. [648]
Outside, the aurora woke at last, and the wreck-diver rose from the ice with empty hands and a full heart. [668]
