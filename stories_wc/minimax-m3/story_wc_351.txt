The canyon walls trembled under a moon so white it seemed to have been polished by the hands of forgotten gods. [21] Sera, a helix-minded engineer whose thoughts curved through problems like the spiraling rungs of DNA, stood at the lip of a ravine and felt the ground of her old life giving way. [53] She had come to this place during a period of profound transformation, when every certainty she had built was being disassembled and rebuilt in unfamiliar geometries. [79] In her satchel she carried only what she trusted most, and chief among these was a broken periscope mirror, a fractured oval of glass salvaged from the ruins of an abandoned observatory. [111] Its surface still caught the moonlight and threw it back in jagged, asymmetric shards, and she believed it could help her become the archeologist of abandoned dreams she had long wished to be. [144] Tonight was the night she would begin her true excavation, not of stone and bone, but of the entangled memories that tangled her own mind in knots she could no longer ignore. [176] Her method had always been chaotically precise: she would scatter her attention like thrown sand, then gather it in sudden sweeping gestures that left perfect patterns in the dust of thought. [207] She tilted the broken mirror toward the canyon walls, angling it so that the moonlight slid through the cracks, and in its crooked surface she saw not the rock but the shimmering overlay of every dream she had ever abandoned. [247] There, in the trembling reflections, floated the child who had wanted to fly, the lover she had let go, the city she had refused to build, the song she had never finished, the garden she had sworn to plant and never did. [289] Each fragment caught the light at a different angle, and each angle triggered a cascade of recollection, a soft detonation along the branching avenues of her neurons. [316] She understood, with a clarity that felt almost like vertigo, that to modify the architecture of her own remembering she would have to work through synaptic plasticity itself. [344] She closed her eyes and let the moonlight fall through her eyelids as a pale red glow that pulsed in rhythm with her breathing. [368] She began, slowly, to modify the connections between her recollections, not erasing them but weaving them into new helices of meaning that her mind could finally hold. [395] The process was not gentle; it came with the quaking reverie of a dreamer who has finally woken inside the dream and found it willing to bend to her will. [425] The canyon itself seemed to breathe with her as the silver light shifted on the stone, and she felt the old fears unspool and re-spool themselves around new centers of gravity. [456] The broken mirror acted as a kind of compass for the migration of her inner constellations, each fracture in the glass pointing toward a different buried chamber of the self. [486] A low wind rose through the canyon and sounded almost like a chorus of the abandoned, urging her on with voices that were both her own and entirely other. [515] She let those voices guide her hands as she traced invisible diagrams on the cold air, and as the hours deepened, the moon traced its slow helix across the sky. [545] Sera traced a matching helix in the dust at her feet with the toe of her boot, completing the circuit between the heavens and the ground on which she stood. [575] By the time the first pale suggestion of dawn touched the eastern cliffs, she had mapped more than seventy abandoned dreams, each one catalogued in the new neural lattices she had painstakingly grown. [608] She no longer wished to be an archeologist in the ordinary sense; she understood now that the excavation was not outward but inward, and the site was the living tissue of her own changing mind. [643] She placed the broken periscope mirror carefully into her satchel, knowing she would return to it again and again, for every fragment still held angles she had not yet learned to see. [675] When she finally rose to leave the canyon, her legs unsteady, her heart full, she turned once more toward the place where the moonlight pooled like spilled silver and whispered her new name for herself: keeper of the braided past. [715] The canyon did not answer, but the wind shifted, and somewhere in the rocks a small stone fell, and in its tiny clatter Sera heard the applause of the world she was in the slow, trembling process of becoming.
