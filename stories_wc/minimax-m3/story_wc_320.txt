<story>Mira was a celestial signal interpreter, and the work was mostly patience dressed in overalls. [15] The city of Wran-loft hung over a gorge on cables older than its memory, an abandoned radio tower rebuilt and rebuilt until it had become a forest of antennae, ladders, and small practical rooms. [49] Tonight, the moon rode at perigee, drawn so close it pressed its pale cheek against every westward window, and the whole city tilted a little, the way a sleeper tilts toward a dream. [82] Mira was paid to listen at hours like these. [91] She climbed past the hollow transmitter hall, where the last operator had left a thermos and a note that read simply GOOD LUCK, and she reached her own cabin three hundred rungs above the gorge. [126] On her workbench waited the object of the season: a fragment of a Hollywood star, a sliver of gilt plaster pried from a sidewalk and sent aloft by a client who believed that fame, like weather, could be tuned. [165] The fragment lay in a small glass dish, and around it the air had a quality Mira could only describe as a shard of hush, a silence so specific it had its own weather. [199] When she placed her copper rods against it, the rods hummed, serenely electric, as if something gentle were trying very hard to be heard. [223] The transmission she had been hired to clarify was old and sour. [235] A musician on the far side of the gorge had recorded a final song, then walked into the canyon mist, and the recording had been picked up by Wran-loft's largest dish and frozen into the static. [271] Her job, in plain terms, was to find the beginning inside the song's last note and play it back to the family. [293] She did not believe in ghosts; she believed in waveforms, and that was the more useful kind of belief. [312] She began, as she always did, by discarding assumptions. [321] She set aside the notion that endings were endings, set aside the idea that the dead had nothing left to say, set aside her own quiet conviction that the moon at perigee amplified sentiment rather than signal. [358] What remained, when she had cleared the bench of these habits, was the simple task of tuning. [375] She turned the dials. [379] She adjusted the impedance. [383] She coaxed the dish with small, patient words. [391] And slowly, the static thinned. [396] The song emerged not as a song but as a preparation for a song. [410] The musician's last note was in fact the opening phrase of a thing he had never finished, a kind of bridge he had been saving for a life he had meant to live. [443] Mira listened to him tune his own instrument, and the tuning was not performance but promise, the small bright sound a person makes when they expect to need their voice tomorrow. [474] The fragment on her bench caught the moment and held it, its gilt glittering under the perigee moon, and Mira felt the cabin fill with a hush that was not absence but arrival. [507] She recorded what she had found, wrapped the wax cylinder in cloth, and wrote her report in the steady hand of a person whose profession is attention. [534] Beginning in the ending, she noted, for the family, with the musician's own words. [548] Then she climbed down into the sleeping city, the gorge murmuring far below, and the moon followed her like a lantern carried by someone who had walked this road before. [578] She would keep the fragment, she decided, until the next perigee, when she would be paid to listen again, and again to find, beneath the static of any farewell, the small bright tuning of a life about to begin.
