At the edge of that luminous cliff stood Vael, a luminous skeptic whose faint luminescent veins pulsed like old neon, doubting every legend but never the ring of tin that hung on his left hand, a relic he called the tin compass ring. [87]
Vael had come to this remote promontory while forests hold elections, an unprecedented ritual where ancient oaks and shimmering birches cast ballots of pollen and light, and the air itself seemed to vote with ripples of scent and temperature. [126]
His purpose was to learn why some people carry weather with them, a mystery that had haunted him since childhood when a passing stranger had left a sudden, fragrant rain on his schoolyard, and now he hoped the aurora-lit bluff would reveal a clue. [170]
To investigate, Vael relied on a peculiar method: sensing through taste changes, an innate ability that turned each atmospheric shift into a flavor on his tongue--copper when a storm gathered, honeyed amber when a warm front approached, and a faint metallic tang when a rare magnetic field passed overhead. The ring's guidance pointed toward a small gathering where diverse electors--bioluminescent sprites, rust-clad engineers, and even a few wandering nomads--converge, each one emitting a subtle, terrestrially cosmic aura that Vael could taste as a strange, salty ozone on his palate. [306]
Among them, a tall figure in a coat of woven storm-cloud silk moved with an uncanny grace, and Vael's taste buds flared with the sharp, electric tang of impending rain, confirming that this person carried the very weather he sought. [346]
The ring's needle spun and settled, pointing at the figure's clenched fist, and the emergent compass's glow intensified, casting a soft amber light that seemed to map the invisible currents of moisture that clung to the carrier's fingertips. [384]
As the forest electors cast their luminous votes, a ripple of static electricity rose from the ground, and the tin compass ring vibrated, its tiny metal resonating like an ancient bell, reminding Vael of the days when his grandmother told stories of sky-bound travelers who could summon rain with a sigh.
