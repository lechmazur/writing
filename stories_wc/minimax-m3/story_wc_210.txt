The paper lanterns swayed without wind over the empty boardwalk of Paper-Lantern Cove. [13]
Each one, made of pale rice paper stitched with silver thread, hung from rusted iron hooks that groaned above the silent storefronts. [35]
Once the cove had been a port where fortunes were made and unmade in the same breath; now it was a husk of salt-eaten timber and silence. [62]
The last wish had been granted three nights ago, spoken by a child whose voice had carried across the bay like a bird finding its way home. [89]
After that, the magic had gone quiet, and the town with it. [101]

In a workshop wedged between two collapsed warehouses, Maelis knelt among broken vials. [114]
She had been the cove's alchemist in a life that felt borrowed, and when the townspeople drifted away one by one, she had stayed. [138]
Deserved the name or not, she was now the deserted alchemist of a place that no longer breathed. [156]
The others had called her stubborn; she called herself unfinished. [166]

Around her neck, suspended on a cord of braided flax, hung an amber bottle no larger than her thumb. [185]
She turned it often, the way other people turn worry stones, but the bottle never warmed. [201]
It had no opening, no seam, no lip to pour from. [212]
It was simply a drop of coniferous sunlight, holding whatever it held behind glass that would not yield. [230]
For thirty years she had carried it, since the day her mentor pressed it into her palm and whispered something she had never been able to recall. [257]

Tonight she was trying to recall. [263]

She had spent the decade since the town emptied searching for a way to revive it, and every path had been a dead end dressed in false comfort. [291]
The merchants from the inland cities had offered coin to rebuild the docks, but only on terms that would make the cove a lesser mirror of their own harbors. [320]
A traveling preacher had promised that faith alone would summon the souls of the departed back to their porches, and she had watched hopeful neighbors weep into empty cups until the preacher's tent collapsed in a storm. [357]
She had rejected each of these offerings as poison dressed as honey. [369]
The cove deserved better than imitation, and the dead deserved more than being summoned back to a place that no longer knew their names. [393]

So Maelis had begun to conceive in earnest. [401]
Her aim was nothing less than to reimagine an entire civilization, to lay down in working memory the bones of a cove that did not yet exist but could be brought forth. [433]
She worked through memory the way a mason works through clay, gathering the weight of what the cove had once been and pressing it into a new shape. [461]
She closed her eyes and walked the boardwalk in her mind, hearing the gulls, the merchants, the creak of rigging, the laughter of children chasing each other around the lantern poles. [492]
Each recollection was a brick; she laid them one atop another until a different town began to rise behind her eyelids. [513]

The amber bottle warmed against her collarbone. [520]

She had never considered that the sealed vessel might be the key, because she had always assumed that whatever was inside was meant to come out. [546]
But working through memory, hour after hour, she finally understood. [556]
The bottle had no opening because the opening was not in the bottle at all. [571]
The opening was in her, in the architecture of recall she had been building since the last wish was granted and the lights went out across the cove. [599]

She pressed her palm flat against the glass. [607]
Inside, something shifted, though there was no seam to show how. [618]
A soft gold moved like sediment in a sleeping river. [628]
She thought of the founding families, the shipwrights, the lantern-makers, the children who would grow up here if she could imagine them well enough. [652]
She thought of streets she had never walked and songs she had never heard, and she laid them beside the memories she already held. [676]

The bottle did not open. [681]
She had no need for it to open. [689]

Outside, a paper lantern flickered to life above the boardwalk, then another, then a dozen, as if the cove itself were remembering what it had always been. [716]
Maelis smiled, exhausted, and began to write down the names of people who had not yet been born. [734]
