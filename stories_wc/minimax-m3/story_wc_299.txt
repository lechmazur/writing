Cole had spent twenty years on the road, hauling amplifiers and crawling under stages, before he learned that the loudest moments of his life belonged to other people. [28] Now he sat in a quarry office trailer that smelled of diesel and chalk dust, his magnifying loupe catching the light from a single bulb. [53] The job had come after the failure, after the television segment where he miscalled a Chippendale as a Hepplewhite and watched the studio audience laugh in the polite, devastating way that strangers laugh when they have already decided you are finished. [94] He was still here, working for a granite company that occasionally asked him to evaluate old radios and amplifiers left behind by previous tenants, because nobody else in the valley knew what a grounded cathode looked like. [131] He tried to be confidently uncertain in his reports, the way he had learned to announce set lists into a microphone when the singer was late: certain about the facts, vague about the soul. [165] Today the foreman had set a box on his desk. [175] Inside, wrapped in tissue, was a speaker grille cloth scrap the size of a handkerchief, woven in a pattern Cole had not seen in decades. [200] He lifted it with tweezers, the way he used to lift microphone cables from coils, and he felt the old tug, the ghost weight of the crate. [227] He had been wrestling with the same question for three years. [238] It was not about money, though the money had run out twice. [250] It was about the purpose he had lost when the road ended, when the buses stopped coming, when the only applause that mattered was for someone else's song. [278] He told himself he was insulating emotionally with facts. [287] He told himself the grille cloth was from a 1964 Vox amplifier, that the weave matched a specific factory run, that the dye was aniline-based and would have a particular smell if he breathed on it. [323] He told himself these things the way a man in a valley tells himself the echo of his own voice is something other than his own voice returning. [351] The trailer had thin walls, and the quarry kept cutting in the distance, a slow chewing sound that reminded him of a long encore that no one had asked for. [381] He missed the applause. [385] He missed it the way a person misses a limb, in a place that should hurt but has forgotten how. [405] He laid the cloth flat under the lamp and began to write. [417] The report was dry, factual, certain. [423] He identified the cloth, dated it, valued it, and recommended it for a small auction lot. [439] Then he stopped writing and looked at the scrap again, and the confidently uncertain man he had become began to lose his footing. [462] He had failed publicly on that stage, and the failure had cracked something open, and through the crack had come a strange, persistent wanting that he could not file under any heading he knew. [496] He wanted to be needed again in a way that mattered. [507] He wanted to walk into a room and feel the hum of a hundred speakers pointed at him, the way the grille cloth had once caught the breath of an audience and given it back. [542] He thought of the singer he had worked for, the one who had died in a hotel room in Tulsa, and how he had carried his guitar to the bus and no one had noticed. [577] He thought of the quarry and how it kept eating the hill behind the trailer, and how one day the trailer would be gone too. [602] He picked up the cloth and held it against his cheek, the way he had once held a set list to his face in a green room. [629] It smelled like dust and old electricity. [636] He closed his eyes and let the facts fall away, just for a moment, and let the longing in. [655] Then he opened his eyes, and he began again. [664] He wrestled the report into shape, certain in the details, uncertain in the rest, and he carried the box to the foreman's door. [687] The foreman thanked him, and Cole walked back through the quarry yard, past the cutting machines and the waiting trucks, and he stood for a long time at the edge of the pit. [720] The echo came back to him from the far wall, his own voice returning changed, and he understood, finally, that the road had ended but the listening had not. [749] He went home to write down what he had heard. [759]
