<story>Maren knelt where the orchard floor gave way to stone, brushing loose loam from a cracked flagstone. [17] Above her, the apple branches bent low, their leaves whispering against each other in a language older than any tongue she had learned abroad. [41] Below, the roots she had come to study reached deeper, threading white between ribs and skulls in a pattern no cartographer had ever mapped. [65] She was, after fourteen years in coastal cities and colder countries, a diaspora returnee, and the soil smelled like a confession she had been postponing. [90] The villagers had welcomed her with measured politeness, the way one greets a letter long overdue, and they had pointed her here without ceremony. [114] "Find what we buried before we forgot why," the eldest had said, pressing a trowel into Maren's palm. [132] So she had come to excavate a tomb her family had tended for generations, the one she had refused to inherit when she was young and certain of escape. [161] Now certainty had thinned to a pale membrane, and the tomb waited with the patience of all abandoned things. [180] She worked with the warmly curious attention she had learned in museums, treating every find as both evidence and offering. [200] A child's tooth in a slip of cloth, a clay lamp still smelling faintly of oil, a brass clasp from a cloak no one remembered. [225] Each object she lifted felt like a sentence recovered from a book she had never finished reading, and she arranged them in careful rows on a bedsheet of clean muslin. [255] By the second hour, her knees ached and the shadows of the trees shifted, and she understood that excavation was itself a kind of slow, patient confession. [282] The catacomb orchard held its breath, and the roots moved imperceptibly, tightening their hold on what they guarded. [300] Near the third pillar, her trowel struck something flat and circular, and the sound rang like a small, clear bell. [320] She scraped soil with her fingers until the shape revealed itself: a sundial base, its bronze face gone green but its carved numerals still legible. [345] The gnomon was missing, broken off long ago, and the dial was set into a plinth of carved limestone, almost certainly a masterwork of her great-grandfather's hands. [372] Maren sat back on her heels, the metal cool against her palms, and felt something inside her chest turn over like a stone in a tide. [398] She remembered now the journeys that had been interrupted, hers and others', the crossings that had been left unfinished when the old wars began. [422] Her grandmother had been the last to read the sundial, and Maren had been the last child to hear her do it, before the funeral that scattered the family like seeds in a hard wind. [457] The sundial had been a measure of hours, but it had also been a measure of who returned and who did not, and it had been waiting in the dark for a hand that knew how to ask. [495] Maren closed her eyes, and the orchard settled into the cautious enchantment of places that have been believed in for a very long time. [519] She would rebuild the gnomon first, casting a new blade in the village smith's mold, and she would set the base where the noon light could find it. [547] Then, through purposeful wandering, she would walk the orchard again and again, mapping the roots that snaked between the bones, learning which kin lay where and which prayers had been spoken over them. [580] The villagers, she suspected, would watch her come and go with the same politely measuring eyes, and she would let them, because trust was a kind of slow excavation too. [610] Her faith had slipped its mooring years ago, when the diaspora had taught her to survive on portable, smaller devotions, and she had not dared to pray in a place that might answer. [643] But here, among the apples and the bones, she thought she could begin again, threading a new gnomon onto a broken dial and letting the hours mark her return. [672] She lifted the sundial base carefully, as if it were a sleeping child, and the roots shifted once around the bones, settling deeper into the long, patient dark. [700]</story>
