<story>The tide fell away from the rocks like a long, reluctant breath, leaving the base of the lighthouse exposed under a bruised November sky. [24] Inside the lantern room, Mira tightened the last gear of her newest mechanism with knowing fingers. [40] She was a counter-clockwise clock maker, and every piece she touched turned the world in the opposite direction. [58] The villagers on the mainland called it heresy, but she preferred the word inheritance. [72] Tonight she meant to step beyond fate's design, to push her craft past the limit the old engineers had drawn in iron. [94] On the brass table before her lay a hunger stone, black and faintly warm, the size of a clenched heart. [114] It had washed in with the last storm, purring against the shingles until she carried it upstairs. [131] Beside it sat the object that had haunted her since the museum basement: an ancient yoyo, its axle wound with silver thread. [153] No one alive remembered what the toy had been made to do, but its inner spool was a puzzle of nested weights. [175] Mira intended to solve it, to reverse-engineer its clockwork and let the answer spill into her own design. [193] She worked in the neatly inventive way her grandmother had taught her, each sketch pinned beside the last, every tool returned to its hook. [217] The lantern swung once, twice, then settled, casting slow shadows across the stone's wet surface. [232] She had learned, while cataloguing the lighthouse's forgotten logbooks, that the beacon was older than the island it stood on. [252] The first keeper, whose name had been scrubbed from every page, had built the lamp to summon something rather than warn. [273] The hunger stone, the keeper's notes whispered, was the key to that summoning, and only a clock built backward could turn it. [295] She believed it, because she had felt, in the small hours, a pulse in the stone that matched her reversed heartbeat. [316] Now, with the tide at its lowest and the world holding its breath, she began. [331] She traced the yoyo's grooves with a stylus of whalebone, mapping the path of the silver thread as it dipped and rose. [353] The pattern was a spiral that moved against the sun, the way a whirlpool moves against the current. [371] She set the hunger stone at the center of her drafting board, where its faint heat would not warp the paper. [392] Then she began to take the yoyo apart, neatly, the way a surgeon opens a chest, the way a thief opens a lock. [415] Inside the spool she found a tiny mirror, cracked but still bright, and beneath it a seed of glass that glowed when she breathed on it. [441] That glow was the latent radiance the old texts described, a light trapped in matter waiting for permission to wake. [461] Mira's hands, usually so sure, trembled as she set the seed into the heart of her unfinished clock. [479] She had built the clock to run backward, each tooth reversed, each spring coiled in the wrong direction, and now she would initiate it. [503] The first click of the mechanism was a small, dry sound, like a bone settling in a fire. [521] The hunger stone shivered on the table, and the lantern above her guttered as if the wind had changed. [540] Mira felt the room tilt, not in space but in time, as though the hour had slipped and caught itself again. [561] She understood, with a calm that tasted of iron, that she was not summoning a thing but undoing the summoning that had waited since the first keeper lit his lamp. [591] The latent radiance seeped from the seed into the gears, and the gears turned the other way, drawing the stone's warmth into the clock's case. [616] Outside, the tide, which had been falling, paused, then began to creep back toward the rocks, though the moon was still low. [638] Mira laughed, a short, bleak sound, because she had stepped beyond fate's design and found only a larger, darker room. [658] The lighthouse burned, the stone drank, the clock ran backward, and she stood in the lantern room with the wind combing her hair, exhilarated and afraid. [684] The ancient yoyo rolled once across the floor and rested against her boot, as if it had finally been allowed to stop.
