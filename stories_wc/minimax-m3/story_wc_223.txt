<story>In the weeks after the old queen fell and before the council could seat another, Maren found her way to the eastern gallery where the dome held sound like a cupped hand holds water. [34] The interregnum had hollowed the city into a luminous absence, each street bright with the ordinary light of noon and yet strangely emptied, as if the sun were shining on a stage without actors. [68] She herself had become a kind of nomadic penumbra, a wandering half-shadow who had outlived the woman she had been, and the whispering gallery, with its soft acoustic hush, was the one place where her in-betweenness did not feel like a failure. [110] She had been a crisis actor for the city's training drills, hired to weep and scream in the wake of simulated catastrophe, but the previous winter a real flood had taken her brother and left her quietly competent in a way that no rehearsal had prepared her for. [158] Now she walked the marble floor in slow, deliberate patterns, her footsteps spelling small words into the dust: here, gone, wait, bread. [180] The dome gathered each syllable and carried it along the curved wall to her ear, and she wrote the words down in a ledger of small gestures she meant to one day call a history. [215] One morning she discovered a chessboard set on a low table near the gallery's edge, a game left mid-play, and she stood over it for a long time without touching it. [246] The pawns were missing, all eight white and all eight black, and only the larger pieces remained in their squares, leaning faintly toward one another as though expecting orders from a hand that would never come. [282] It was, she thought, an exact image of the city itself, the foot soldiers swept away by the flood and the regents left to consider moves that no longer made sense. [313] She had been paid to play the survivor in dozens of drills, and afterward she had played the survivor again, this time without payment, and the two roles had become so entangled in her that she could no longer say which gestures were rehearsed and which were her own. [362] A shadow moved along the curved wall, a piece of the same nomadic penumbra that had been trailing her since the flood, and she knew before she turned that it was the old steward, Halden, who had served three sovereigns and now served none. [406] He was benevolently ruthless, the kind of man who would pull a splinter from a child's hand without asking and would not hesitate to break a treaty if keeping it would cost a single life more than necessary. [444] He watched her pattern of footsteps with the still attention of someone who had once been a different sort of man, and when the last letter faded into the marble he said, "You are writing a book no one will read." [485] "I am unbinding myself from the person I was hired to be," she answered. [499] "When the drills were over, I kept the face on, like a mask I had forgotten how to remove, and the gallery has become the place where I take it off, one spelled word at a time. [536] By the end of each day I am lighter than I was in the morning, and lighter, I think, is the only history worth keeping." [561] Halden crossed to the chessboard and lifted the white king, turning it in his thin fingers. [577] "A crisis actor survives by pretending, and a real survivor survives by remembering, but you are both, and that is why you are dangerous to the men who would like this interregnum to last forever." [612] He set the king back in its square with a small, careful click. [625] "There is a girl in the lower ward who has not spoken since the water rose, the daughter of a pawn in the old sense, and she is waiting for a crisis actor who knows how to be a survivor. [665] Help her, and your ledger will have its first true entry, and you will have begun to be the historian of small gestures you say you mean to become." [694] She walked to the lower ward at dusk, her feet spelling the girl's name into the dust of the gallery as she went, and when she found the child she did not sit beside her or speak to her but simply stood in the doorway and breathed. [741] The luminous absence of the room steadied around her like light from a source no one could name, and after a long time the girl looked up. [768] Maren, who had been paid to cry in a hundred different ways, let her face do nothing at all, and that, she knew, was the first gesture worth keeping. [797]</story>
