Under the hush of borrowed starlight, the astronomical clock tower kept its careful vigil over the sleeping town, its bronze gears whispering numbers into the dark. [26]

At the base of the tower, in a small room that smelled of dust and pressed paper, Mira, the town's only harmonious choral librarian, polished the brass of a cuckoo clock weight she had inherited from her grandmother. [64]

Each morning, Mira tuned the town with her voice, singing parts in counterpoint with herself as she shelved books in four different languages at once, a habit her colleagues called "Mira's mixing." [96]

Tonight, however, the silver quiet was broken by a low, grinding complaint that rose from the easternmost grain elevator, a tall iron silo that had stood beside the river for seventy years and had, in the last month, begun to speak. [137]

"Will no one," the silo rumbled, its voice like wheat shifting in a long bin, "tell me if I am still useful? My brothers and sisters have been emptied one by one, and my own belly is mostly dust." [176]

Mira set down the cuckoo clock weight and wrapped a shawl around her shoulders. [190]

She was, by training and temperament, a person who decided with feeling: empathetically decisive, she could hear the exact shape of an unspoken need and move toward it without flinching. [220]

The silos of the town had once formed a constellation around the clock tower, each one connected to the next by wooden walkways, by voices, by the small economies of grain and gossip that knit a community together. [258]

But the walkways had rotted, the gossip had thinned, and the silos, feeling themselves reduced to mere storage, had grown taciturn, then sullen, and finally, in the case of the easternmost one, articulate in its sorrow. [294]

Mira crossed the moonlit yard toward the iron tower, the cuckoo clock weight swinging gently in her hand, its pendulum a small bronze heart. [318]

"Good evening, friend," she called up the ladder rungs, interspersing languages the way her mother had taught her, slipping between the local tongue and the old farmers' dialect and the singing language of the river trade. [354]

"Me tonight, madam, also tired," she said in the farmer dialect, then continued in the river tongue, "but I have come to ask what you need." [380]

The silo, surprised to be addressed in voices it half-recognized, groaned a little softer. [394]

"I need to be rehabilitated," it confessed. "I have become afraid of my own emptiness. I would like to hold something again. I would like to be heard." [422]

Mira nodded. "Then we will fill you," she said, "not with grain, which is too heavy for the work I have in mind, but with resonance. We will make you a bridge between your brothers and sisters, so that they may speak to one another through you." [469]

She climbed the tower and hung the cuckoo clock weight from a beam inside the silo's iron throat, adjusting its pendulum until it swung in time with the astronomical clock far above. [501]

When the pendulum caught the starlight, it began to sing, a clear small note that the silo amplified into a soft, ongoing hum. [524]

Mira climbed to the second silo, then the third, interspersing languages as she went, singing in three tongues at once, her voice carrying across the yards and down the alleys. [554]

By dawn, she had strung seven silos together with new ropes of sound, each one tuned to a different pitch, each one a stepping-stone in an auditory bridge from the riverbank to the clock tower's foot. [590]

The townspeople woke to a symphony of grain elevators, humming in gentle conversation, and Mira, exhausted but satisfied, stood at the base of the astronomical clock tower and laughed. [619]

She had come to construct bridges from silos, and she had, with a cuckoo clock weight and a voice that wove four languages into one rope, rehabilitated not only the easternmost silo but the whole constellation of iron breasts that had forgotten how to sing. [664]

The astronomical clock chimed six, and the harmonies rose, vigorous and tender, under the last hush of borrowed starlight. [683]
