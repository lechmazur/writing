<story>The last amber light of the honey-thick dusk bled across the iron spire of the lightning collection tower, staining the clouds the color of warm nectar. [26] Maren, the toll keeper, leaned against the copper railing and watched the city below lie silent and dark. [44] It had been three days since the city-wide blackout, three days since the drone beehive that normally swarmed the tower's flanks had simply stopped. [68] The mechanical bees, each one no larger than a thumb, had been the city's pride: a cloud of them rising into storms, luring down the lightning, channeling it into the great copper funnels that fed the streets below. [106] Now they hung motionless in their hive cradles, antennae slack, wings folded, their small glass eyes dark. [123] Maren touched the lacquer box at her belt, the one she had carried since her first day on the tower. [143] It was her inheritance from her father, the previous toll keeper, and it held the only key to the tower's manual override, a brass lever wrapped in cloth. [171] She had been told, in his last breath, never to open it unless the city truly needed wonder again. [190] Being the toll keeper meant being many things, but most of all it meant being involuntarily truthful. [207] She had not chosen the affliction; it had chosen her, a quirk of birth that made every sentence she spoke land exactly as she meant it, stripped of all polite varnish. [238] Visitors had learned to be careful with their questions. [247] On the second night of the blackout, a stranger climbed the tower stairs and found Maren at her post, watching the dead drones. [270] He was small, soot-stained, and he carried nothing but a matchbox, which he set on the railing between them. [289] I was sent by the council to ask what you know. [300] They sent you because I cannot lie, Maren said, and the words tasted like cold iron. [316] I was told, the stranger said. [322] He pushed the matchbox toward her. [328] Inside, on a slip of paper no bigger than a fingernail, was a cryptic riddle written in her father's hand. [348] She read it twice, and the third time the answer rose in her like a bell struck in a quiet room. [369] The riddle spoke of the lever, the bees, and a single spark that had to be carried by a living hand. [390] The drone beehive failed, she said, because no one remembered that the tower was built for human hands first. [409] She opened the lacquer box. [414] Inside, the brass lever lay in its cloth, but beside it was a small glass vial of something that glowed faintly, like trapped sunset. [438] What is that, the stranger asked. [444] It is nectar from the first hive my father ever tended, Maren said, and she could not have lied if she had wanted to. [468] The drones were meant to carry it up the spire and pour it into the lightning channel, sweetening the strike so the energy ran clean and steady. [495] Without it, the current is too wild for the modern grid. [506] The drones forgot the taste. [511] She lifted the vial and, with the stranger watching, she climbed the inner ladder to the very top of the collection funnel. [533] The storm was gathering far out over the plain, a bruise of purple and gold. [548] She uncorked the vial and held it up as the first fork of lightning cracked down the sky. [566] The honey smell rose around her, impossibly sweet, and the bolt struck the funnel and was gentled by it, slowed into a slow, golden river that poured down the copper throat and into the city below. [602] In the streets, lamps flickered, then bloomed. [609] The drone beehive, sensing the proper current, stirred, and a thousand small wings began to hum again. [626] Maren descended the ladder, the empty vial still warm in her palm, and found the stranger waiting with the matchbox open. [647] He left you more than the riddle, the stranger said. [657] He left me the truth, Maren said, which I have been keeping in a lacquer box all these years because I was afraid of what it would do to the wonder. [688] She closed the matchbox and gave it back to him. [698] Wonder survives being told plainly, she said. [705] It only dies when we hide it. [712] She looked out over the city as the first streetlamps came on, one by one, like a string of amber beads being threaded by an unseen hand, and the dusk deepened into a sweetness that asked nothing of her but to be witnessed.
