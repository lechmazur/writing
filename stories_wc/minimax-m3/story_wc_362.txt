The quartz arbor rose from the hillside like a ribcage of pale crystal, half-swallowed by the mist that never quite lifted from this valley. [24]
Three winters had Vael kept vigil here, her feet bare against the frozen quartz even when the cold bit her toes to redness. [47]
She was grounded in the way stone is grounded, in the way roots are grounded, and she did not know how to be otherwise. [71]
Visitors sometimes mistook her stillness for absence, but Vael was the most attentive mystic in the province, more attentive than the wind and more attentive than the frost. [99]
What she sought was this: to prove that objects absorb emotions. [110]
Not metaphorically, but literally, demonstrably, in a way her grandmother's old teachings could rise from folklore into fact. [128]
Until then, her grandmother's stories were what Vael called truthful lies, true in the marrow and false in the letter. [148]
Tonight the mist had thickened until it pressed against the arbor's faceted spines like wet wool. [164]
At the edge of the clearing sat the old sedan, a relic of a neighbor who had gone south years ago and never returned. [188]
Its windows held the season's first hard frost, and Vael loved these windows the way other people love mirrors. [207]

She approached the sedan and laid her palm flat against the cold glass. [244]
The patterns in the frost bloomed under her touch, not melting but deepening, the way ink deepens in water. [263]
She bent her head, raised the prism to her eye, and began to commune with what the window remembered. [282]
A neighbor's daughter had wept here once, in the front seat, before driving away. [296]
A stranger had slept in the back after a long walk home from the bus stop. [312]
Each thread of frost, she had learned, was a sentence the car had not quite finished speaking. [329]

It deepened and widened and drew inward at the same time, like a bell being struck in slow motion. [369]
