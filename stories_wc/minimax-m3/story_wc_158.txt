<story>In the hollow curl of a gargantuan shell that had rested on the reef since the moon was young, Vespera the insomniac poet kept her vigil. [26] The shell was bigger than any cathedral, its inner chambers pearly and dim, and through its apertures the sea-light filtered in reef-shadow joy, dappling the walls with shifting patterns of indigo and rose. [59] For centuries of longing she had lived there, ensconced among the rustling pages of unfinished verses, her quill a spine of sea-glass, her ink drawn from squid-black waters. [87] She could not sleep. [91] Every night, when the tide exhaled, the wind that slipped through the shell's mouth came half-sung and off-key, as if the ocean itself were trying to remember a lullaby it had once known in another century. [127] Vespera would hum along, and her poems would grow in the cracks between the notes. [142] Her art, she had decided long ago, was to photosynthesize pain into beauty. [155] The ache of sleeplessness, the salt-sting of memory, the slow grief of loving someone across centuries she could not even name, all these she fed into her verses and watched them open like anemones in sunlight. [191] One morning, when the reef was especially quiet, a thing drifted in through the shell's largest aperture and settled at her bare feet. [214] It was a postcard. [218] Not of any sea she recognized, but of a galaxy: spiral arms of blue-white stars, a nebula blooming rose and saffron, and in tiny silver script, a single line: "Come home when the armistice blossoms." [253] Vespera picked it up with trembling fingers. [259] She did not know who had sent it, but she felt the words settle into her chest like a second heartbeat. [280] That night, she sang louder. [285] She sang through the wind, half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key breeze that spiraled up from the shell and out across the reef, brushing the polyps awake. [312] The corals listened. [315] Slowly, shyly unstoppable, they began to open. [322] White and gold and soft impossible pink, the reef blossomed in a tide of petals that were not petals at all but living, breathing peace. [347] The blossom armistice had begun. [352] Across centuries of longing, the reef had waited for this, the moment when the war between the deep and the shallows would finally cease, when the soldier-crabs would lay down their claws and the jellyfish would drift without stinging. [391] Vespera watched, and her sleepless eyes filled. [398] For so long she had been unable to stop her longing, shyly unstoppable in its own way, and now she saw that longing was the very engine of the bloom. [428] She wrote through the night, her quill scratching half-sung lullabies of her own onto the pearly walls. [445] When dawn came, the shell glowed from within, every chamber etched with verses that smelled of salt and jasmine. [464] And when the wind returned that evening, it came on-key for the first time in her memory, carrying her songs back to her from somewhere far beyond the reef, somewhere up among the spiral arms of that galaxy on the postcard. [505] She smiled, ensconced in her cathedral of shell, and let the reef-shadow joy wash over her like a tide. [524] The armistice would hold. [528] The poet would still not sleep. [534] But she would sing, and the reef would blossom, and somewhere, across the centuries, someone would find another postcard waiting on the sand. [557] She had been a poet before she was sleepless, born in a village that had long ago folded into the sea, where she had first scratched a verse upon a scrap of kelp and felt the world tilt toward her. [597] Her gift had always been shyly unstoppable, surfacing in the small hours when no one was watching. [614] Now, ensconced in her pearly chamber, she understood that the gift had been the armistice all along, the quiet treaty between her sorrow and her song. [640] She lifted her quill, and the wind outside lifted with her, off-key no longer but full, and somewhere a new poet opened her eyes to the dark and reached for ink. [671] And the gargantuan shell hummed softly, holding its insomniac poet and her reef-shadow joy the way a hand holds a candle against the centuries-long dark. [696]</story>
