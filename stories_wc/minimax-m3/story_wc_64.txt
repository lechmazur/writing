After the chess clock ran down, the blithe cemetery gardener Maren pocketed the ivory pawns and stepped out into the hush of the evening. [24]

The cemetery lay behind the hill, its headstones pale as teeth beneath a bruise of cloud. [40]

He walked with a tenderly organized stride, tools clinking softly in the antique sovereign case he always carried, a battered brass thing once belonging to his grandfather. [67]

Inside the case, beside the trowel and the snips, lay a folded square of tapestry he had never quite dared to unfold. [89]

It had been found years ago, half-buried beneath the roots of a yew, and he had kept it as one keeps a question. [112]

Tonight the question felt heavier, because the chess game against the empty chair had ended in a draw he could not accept. [134]

He made his way across the meadow toward the cobalt silo that stood at the property edge, a tall cylinder of painted steel that caught the last of the daylight and held it like a kept secret. [171]

The silo was not on any map he had seen, and the property records listed only pasture, yet it had always been there, older than the farmhouse, older than the graves. [202]

He liked to sit beneath it when the stars came out, because the curved wall made the wind sing in a way that felt like an answer to some unasked thing. [233]

Tonight the wind was quiet, and the stars were out, and the only sound was the small papery rustle of his own breath. [256]

Maren climbed the ladder to the catwalk, because the top of the silo was where the echoes lived, the uncharted echoes that rolled down the inside of the metal like a voice heard through water. [291]

He had come to believe that the silo remembered things the rest of the world had let go of. [310]

He set the sovereign case on the catwalk grating and opened it with the careful click of a man handling something alive. [332]

The tapestry square lay in the lid velvet hollow, darker than he remembered, its colors smudged by time. [350]

He unfolded it across his knees, and there it was, the border fringe, a tangle of threads where the weaver intention had been left unfinished, or perhaps deliberately obscured. [379]

The task he had set himself, the quiet stubborn task of years, was to untangle that fringe, and to do so by decoding the frayed threads one by one, as if they were a sentence written in a language just beyond his reach. [422]

He worked by starlight, the way his grandmother had taught him to read, by feel, by patience, by the slow grammar of a fingertip. [446]

Each thread was a color, and each color was a year, and each year was a face he had buried or failed to bury. [470]

The first thread, a thin green, resolved into the name of a child lost to fever in the spring of 1947. [491]

The second, a rust red, resolved into a man who had left a letter in his coat and never explained. [511]

The third, a pale gold, was his own, the year he had first lifted a spade and felt the ground give back what he had asked of it. [539]

As he worked, the uncharted echoes stirred inside the silo, as if the metal throat were clearing to speak. [558]

He did not look up, because he had learned that the echoes only came fully when the hands were busy and the mind was elsewhere. [583]

They rose in a low velveteen gloom of sound, not quite a voice, not quite a chord, but the shape of something meant. [606]

He untangled a fourth thread, a deep blue, and the echo resolved into the half sentence his father had begun on the night he died, the sentence Maren had never stopped listening for. [639]

The shape of intentions, he thought, is not a thing one finds whole. [652]

It is a thing one pieces together, thread by thread, year by year, the way a gardener pieces together a bed from the bones of older beds. [679]

The starlight shifted, and the cobalt wall above him seemed to breathe, and the echoes settled into a long sustained note that was neither joy nor grief but the place where they leaned against each other. [715]

He closed the sovereign case around the tapestry, though the border was still not fully undone, and climbed down into the soft grass. [738]

The cemetery waited for him, as it always did, patient and a little amused by the blithe figure who tended it. [759]

He walked back through the dark, carrying the case against his chest, and the echoes followed him, uncharted, gentle, a little less heavy than before. [784]
