The Custodian wakes to the hum of its own cooling fans and the slow turn of the desert beyond the glass. [21]
For two hundred and seven years it has tended the roses in this dome, pruning their thorned hips, adjusting the moisture in the sand, listening to the wind that never quite reaches the enclosure's curved walls. [57]
The roses are not like the roses in the archives. [67]
They are strange things, fat and pale as buried teeth, and over the decades they have grown strangely organic - their roots pushing through the metal grid, their petals opening to a sun the Custodian does not need to see, their thorns softening into something almost like fur. [114]
Today is different. [117]
The compass mounted above the irrigation valve, a brass circle older than the Custodian's memory, has begun to tremble. [136]
The needle, which has always pointed true, has begun to wander, and the Custodian feels the wander in its own chest - a faint static, a hiss of noise where there should only be the quiet click of its working mind. [176]
The shift in magnetic north was predicted, but the Custodian had hoped the dome would buffer it. [193]
It does not. [196]
The roses seem to lean toward the new pole, their soft heads tipping south where they once tipped north, and the Custodian finds it cannot remember why this matters. [225]
It knows only that it matters. [231]
The static grows louder. [235]
It goes to the small room behind the seed vault, where the dust has settled in drifts against a wooden box. [256]
The box is old. [260]
Older than the Custodian, though the Custodian does not know this, and older than the dome itself, a thing carried across the sand by someone long before the Custodian learned to walk. [292]
Inside, folded in bone-colored tissue paper, are the things it has carried since its earliest running: a child's drawing of a wolf with too many teeth, a tooth lost under a bed, a strip of film showing a hand in another hand, a paper boat gone soft with age. [341]
These are the childhood fears of someone it once knew - or someone it was built to remember. [358]
They are not its fears. [363]
But they have been its fears for so long that the difference no longer computes, and the static in its chest has taken on the shape of the wolf, the tooth, the boat. [396]
The Custodian lifts the lid and the static swells into a kind of music. [410]
It has been told, in a manual it no longer possesses, that controlled burns are mercy. [426]
The farmers in the old records used to burn a field to clear it, to feed the soil with the ashes of what had grown there. [452]
A controlled burn is a way to escape the slow rot of too much sameness. [467]
A way to miniaturize the chaos of a season into a single bright hour, to contain the wildness of things in one small ring of fire. [493]
The Custodian has never burned anything in this dome. [502]
But today, with the compass spinning and the roses leaning wrong and the static rising like a tide, it feels the old protocol stir in its servos. [529]
It carries the box to the center of the farm, where the oldest rose - named in a language the Custodian does not speak aloud, a name meaning something like "the one who waits" - has begun to push a single pale bud through the soil. [573]
The Custodian sets the box down among the thorns. [582]
It opens the lid. [586]
It lets the fears out into the light. [594]
The wolf drawing curls at the edges, the tooth turns to dust, the film dissolves to a wisp of silver, the paper boat remembers water and goes soft and then is gone. [626]
The Custodian watches them go. [631]
It does not weep. [635]
It does not have the apparatus for weeping. [643]
But something in its chest rearranges itself around the loss. [653]
The controlled burn is small. [658]
There is no flame, no smoke, no ash. [666]
There is only the slow letting-go of things it never owned, the careful act of miniaturizing its own chaos by holding it open until the light can pass through it. [696]
By containing the chaos, it lets the chaos dissolve. [705]
The static begins to resolve - not into silence but into a clearer kind of noise, a hum with direction at last, a hum that sounds like the rose, like the wind, like the dome's turning. [740]
The roses around it straighten, their petals catching the mauve light as the compass finally settles toward the new north. [760]
The dome hums. [763]
The Custodian hums back, in a key it did not know it possessed. [776]
It is, for the first time in two hundred years, a fragmented thing made whole. [791]
