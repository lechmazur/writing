The marsh-reed sculptor arrived at the crater's rim with her fingers smelling of lowland water and her arms cradled in damp braids of withy. [24]

She had walked for what felt like seasons, past saltflats where her reflection had forgotten her name, past the petrified hives of a township that no longer hummed. [52]

Now the crystalline orchard unfolded before her, each tree a translucent column humming with internal fire, every leaf a frozen prism catching the crater's slow shimmer. [78]

The crater itself breathed below, a bowl of liquid silver exhaling into dawn. [91]

It was here, beyond the murmur of lost languages, that the orchard kept its silent counsel, and it was here that she hoped to graft herself onto the community that tended it. [123]

The orchard's caretakers were few. [128]

They spoke in gestures and pressure, in the tilt of a chin toward a fruit ready for picking, in the way a hand pressed flat against bark meant stay. [157]

Among them moved Aeren, a wiry, sun-bleached figure who wore tags of charcoal on his wrists as a kind of scarification. [178]

He was the one who first called her by gesture rather than name, pressing a reed into her palm with the kind of warmth that asked nothing in return. [207]

She was, in the way she had learned to be, boldly sympathetic. [219]

She listened with her palms and matched the slow rhythms of the orchard's pruning, the way a branch yielded to a careful twist, and in this careful labor she began to feel the webs of kinship tightening around her, gossamer-strong, holding her upright. [262]

But the orchard held a mystery. [268]

Somewhere within its terraces was a tag, a single bold glyph left by an artist who had passed through decades ago, perhaps centuries, and no living keeper could find it. [298]

To find the tag was, by old rite, to be named; to find it was to be dedicated into the community in a way that working and waiting could never accomplish. [329]

She and Aeren began a shared search, moving together through the terraces in the long quiet hours. [346]

They tracked the older pruning marks and followed the almost invisible tilt of certain crystal boughs toward a single point. [366]

One evening, as the crater's silver lifted to lap the orchard walls, she laid a freshly cut marsh-reed against a bough, and Aeren laid his charcoal to its skin, and together they began to sketch the idea of what the missing tag might mean to them, not what it had once been. [418]

The image that came was a tree within a tree, a shape they did not yet have a word for in any tongue they remembered, and which the orchard itself seemed to remember instead. [452]

They worked until their fingers were rimed with frost and resin, and the luminescent ache of being almost-named hummed between them like a held note. [477]

It was Aeren who found it. [483]

Behind a shelf of crystal fruit, where a glimmering obsidian arbor arched in a small private grove, the wall was scratched with the lost glyph: a spiral opening into a smaller spiral, and inside it, the silhouette of a reed-wrapped hand. [524]

He did not cry out. [529]

He simply turned and pressed the discovery into her palm as he had once pressed the reed, and she understood that the dedication was not hers alone to claim. [558]

The webs of kinship were doing their old work, drawing two strangers into the shape of one another, and the orchard's quiet keepers gathered to witness. [584]

She stepped forward to the obsidian arbor, where the crater's silver light pooled deepest, and she did not speak, because here the murmuring of lost languages could not help, and beyond them lay only the bright ache of being held. [624]

She raised her reed-knife and began, slowly, to carve the spiral into her own palm, not to wound but to join. [645]

The community watched in the way communities watch when a knot is tied. [658]

She had come with nothing but a longing to belong, and now she belonged, threaded into the lattice of the orchard and the lives of its keepers and the patient shimmer of the crater below. [693]

Then came the feast of crystal fruit and bitter reed tea, and Aeren placed a band of braided charcoal at her wrist, and she placed a band of braided reed at his, and the webs of kinship tightened one more notch. [734]

Outside, beyond the murmur of lost languages, the silver crater caught the dawn and held it, as it always had, as it would, luminescent and aching, the way any true home must be. [767]
