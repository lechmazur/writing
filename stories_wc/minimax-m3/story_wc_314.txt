Beyond the kaleidoscopic waterfall lay an orchard no map had ever recorded, and within it the auburn-haired alchemist Selene kept her long apprenticeship. [23]
The trees were older than the cataract, their bark silvered into whispering runes, and their fruit rose in slow exhalations of perfume toward the hidden sky. [49]
She had come with a small flame in one hand and her grandmother's frozen dew crystal in the other, the first to warm the world, the second to remember it. [79]
By every guild report she was pure deduction, and no one suspected that she was secretly sentimental, that beneath her workbench she kept a locket of her mother's hair and three pressed blossoms. [112]
The crystal was her inheritance, a teardrop of morning sealed by her grandmother's art, and on the night she died her grandmother had told her that within it lay the touch of wonder. [145]
She had not believed her then, for she was a child of balance sheets, and wonder was a luxury or a lie. [167]
But the guild had sent her to the orchard because her final transformation was at hand, and there were preparations the city's stone corridors could not hold. [194]
She was to become a Master of the Higher Art, a working in which the body itself is the alembic, and her task was to harmonize logic and intuition into a single weave. [227]
It was this she feared, for her logic was a blade honed over decades, and her intuition a small shy thing she had always, in her secret tender way, loved. [257]
The first morning she set the crystal on a flat stone beneath the oldest tree, lit a careful flame beneath a copper dish, and began the slow distillation of her own attention. [289]
At noon a flock of starlings wheeled into the clearing, their shadows moving across her hands like the hands of a clock she had never learned to read. [317]
She watched them, because watching was a kind of listening, and they moved as one body through a dance obeying no law she could name. [342]
The touch of wonder, she realized, was not added to the world like sugar to a pear, but was already there, and the alchemist's task was to be present when it arrived. [374]
She let the flame gutter and brighten with her breath, let the crystal begin to sweat, and the starlings folded and unfolded their patterns like a great soft lung. [403]
For the first time in her apprenticeship she wept, and her tears fell upon the crystal, which opened by a single hairline fracture. [426]
Through that fracture she saw the orchard as it had been in her grandmother's time, the fruit rising, the waterfall singing in colors no prismatic vocabulary could contain. [454]
She understood that wonder was where the frost of understanding met the green pulse of the world, and to harmonize logic and intuition one must let them marry like two rivers in a single bed. [489]
The flame steadied to a blue so pure it seemed the color of the space between stars, the starlings wheeled once around the crystal, and the orchard was full of a quiet she had not known she was missing. [528]
She gathered up the crystal, now light in her palm, and put out the flame with her fingers, for some fires are not banked but simply closed, like a book one has at last understood. [563]
The final transformation, she knew, would be no transmutation of guild, but a second birth in which the sentimental girl and the rigorous woman would at last share one voice. [593]
She stepped from beneath the silvered tree, the waterfall parted for her, and the orchard glimmered in the ancient freshness of a world that had never quite agreed to grow old. [624]
The locket at her throat she let fall open, and the three blossoms inside were, impossibly, no longer dry but flushed with the dew the crystal had given back to her as a gift. [658]
As she walked toward the city, she felt the touch of wonder settle on her shoulders like a cloak of her own weaving, light as breath, old as the first orchard, and as new as the morning. [695]
