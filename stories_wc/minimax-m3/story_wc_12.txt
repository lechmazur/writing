The loop pedal priest stood at the altar of the dimensional anchor, where the walls breathed in rhythm and the floor remembered every footstep ever placed upon it. [28]
He wore a vestment of woven frequencies, each thread humming a tone he had long since learned to forget, and around his neck hung a small brass key that opened nothing he had ever found. [63]
Before him, on a slab of obsidian glass, lay his only instrument: an artist's palette with one color, a grey so absolute it seemed to drink the light and return only questions. [95]
This was the hue of unraveled stillness, the pigment of questions left hanging in empty chapels, and he stirred it with a brush of hollow bone, watching the grey pool and reform like weather. [129]
For thirty years he had been a sentinel here, for the dimensional anchor was not merely a place but a covenant, a knot in the fabric that kept a thousand neighboring realms from folding into one another like wet paper. [169]
Now, something pressed from the other side. [176]
He could feel the possession beginning in the lintels, in the soft complaint of the pews, in the way the stained glass had begun to sweat, and in the cold drafts that slipped under the door without ever opening it. [216]
He had until dawn, perhaps less, before the possession completed and the anchor became a door, and everything he had tended would become someone else's long unanswerable sentence. [244]
So he worked. [247]
He pressed the loop pedal beneath his foot, and the silence that followed was the first layer, the foundation upon which all his other silences would be built. [275]
He decorated with silence the way other men embroidered vestments with gold thread, layering absence upon absence until the air itself grew thick with intention, until the chapel resembled a reliquary filled not with bone but with breath. [313]
Each loop recorded what was not there: the hush after a confession, the pause before a bell is struck, the held breath of a congregation learning to be still. [342]
Then he began to plumb. [347]
He spoke, but his speech was a sounding line dropped into the well of his own congregation, which was, in truth, only himself repeated and reflected, the priest and his echo holding a quiet colloquy beneath the vault. [385]
"Tell me," he whispered into the loop, "what do you assume I am?" [398]
The recording answered in his own voice, a half-beat late, and the rhetorical question hung in the air like a censer swinging slow above the altar. [424]
"Is it a man, or the music of a man? Is it the priest, or the pedal, or the prayer, or the pause between them?" [449]
He let the loops multiply, each iteration a thread in a rope he was weaving down into the dark. [468]
"Whose hand is on the pedal? Whose breath makes the bell? Whose silence is being broken, and who, in the breaking, is finally heard?" [492]
Through these rhetorical devices, question and answer and question again, like a plumb line tapping the walls of a well, he illuminated hidden assumptions the way a surgeon illuminates a cavity: by making the shadows speak. [528]
He illuminated the assumption that the anchor was stone, when it was agreement. [541]
He illuminated the assumption that the possession was a foe, when it was only an invitation, half-heartedly extended by a neighbor grown tired of knocking. [566]
He illuminated the assumption that stillness was empty, when in fact stillness was the only vessel strong enough to hold what came next without cracking. [591]
The loop pedal pulsed beneath his foot, and the priest's voice began to thin, until he was speaking in a tone so muffled it was almost indistinguishable from the room's own breathing. [623]
This was his register, the muffled clarity in which the truest things are said, as if from beneath a great quilt, or from the bottom of a well, or from the back of the mind where older dreams are still active. [664]
He raised the single-color palette and, with a single brushstroke across the altar, drew a circle that was not a circle but a question, the kind a child draws in the dust. [696]
The grey settled into the stone like a sigh, like the last word of a man who has finally agreed to sleep. [718]
Outside, something vast turned its attention toward the anchor and, finding no purchase, withdrew, and the priest felt the lintels cool by a single degree. [743]
The possession did not complete that night, nor the next, and the priest looped his silence again, decorating the hour with the soft machinery of his own unraveled attention, and waited, patient as a held note, for the next asking. [783]
