The abandoned spaceport repair bay hummed with the quiet static of dormant machinery, its catwalks strung with severed cables that swayed like wind chimes in a breeze no one could feel. [31] Here, among the stripped consoles and dry-rotted pilot seats, Irena wove her maps. [44] She was a sensory map maker, though the title meant little to anyone left to hear it. [61] Her craft was the charting of textures, scents, the temperature of light in places that existed only when the shard in her palm caught the right angle. [88] The green glass shard was old - older than the spaceport, older than the worlds it pried open. [105] She had found it years ago in a drift of meteoric dust, and since then it had become the hinge on which her perception turned. [130] Each time she lifted it, the bay would thin, and she would fall into the murmur of half-remembered dreams - the collective whisper of selves she had almost been. [158] She worked in cycles, her attention cyclically spontaneous, darting from one strand of reality to another as a spider leaps between silk threads. [181] One moment she would be tracing the salt-rust smell of a desert world where the suns rose in pairs; the next she would lean into the ozone tang of a sky-faring city whose streets folded over themselves like origami. [220] The abandoned bay served as her loom, the empty repair cradles her shuttles, the glass shard her needle. [238] Through parallel universes she traveled, not by ship but by careful redirection of her own attention. [254] The shard caught the slant of a thought and bent it sideways, slipping her into another version of the bay - one where the consoles still glowed, where a younger version of herself waited with a cup of tea and a warning she would never quite speak. [300] Irena would nod, pour herself tea from a kettle that was not there, and carry the warning back like a stone in her pocket. [324] She had been mapping for so long that the worlds had begun to bleed into one another. [341] The dream-murmur was louder now, and the truth she sought was heavier for being unspoken. [356] There was something she had been told - or had told herself - in a life she could not quite locate. [375] It concerned the shard, perhaps, or the bay, or the selves that branched from her like cracks in a frozen lake. [396] In one cycle she found a version of the bay half-flooded with amber light, its ceiling open to a sky full of unfamiliar stars. [420] A figure stood by the main console - her own face, older, eyes like polished flint. [435] The older Irena held out a second shard, identical to the one in the younger hand, and said only: "It was never about the mapping." [460] The words landed like a dropped tool. [467] Irena reached out, her fingers brushing the offered glass, and felt the truth's weight for the first time. [485] It was the weight of a life lived in parallel, of choices not made, of doors closed in universes she had only ever brushed against. [510] The shard in her palm pulsed once, then went still. [520] She had spent decades weaving between worlds, redirecting her attention through parallel universes, believing she was gathering data for a map no one would ever read. [546] But the map had been a veil. [553] Beneath it lay the simple, terrible question she had been avoiding: what had she done, in the world she could not return to, that made all the others necessary? [582] The green glass shard dimmed, and the murmur faded. [591] The repair bay settled back into its long silence, and Irena stood alone with the weight of the withheld truth at last in her hands.
