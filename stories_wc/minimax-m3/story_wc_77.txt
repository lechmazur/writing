Mira opened her eyes to a lingering luminance that should not exist. [12]
The paramedics had shouted her back from death, but the place they carried her was the abandoned carnival funhouse on the edge of town, its bulbs still glowing like spoiled fruit in the dark. [46]
She tried to speak, and a whisper crawled into her ear that was not her own. [62]
"Only the one who advised you before," the whisper answered. [72]
"The one who told you to eat the apple, to climb the ladder, to put your hand through the curtain." [92]
Mira remembered the apple, the ladder, the curtain, and how her reflection in the funhouse glass had smiled back with a smile that was not hers. [118]
She tried to sit up, and the clasp of an opera cloak dug into her collarbone, bright as a beetle, cold as a coin. [142]
She did not own an opera cloak, and yet the fabric pooled around her shoulders like an inherited memory. [161]
"You are photographically cursed," the whisper said. [168]
"Every picture of you is a warning. [175]
The camera loves you the way a trap loves a rabbit." [186]
Mira thought of her school portrait, of how the face in the frame had begun to look away from the lens as if it had somewhere better to be. [215]
"Why am I here?" she asked the dark, and the dark answered in the whisper's voice. [231]
"Because fear has a weakness, and you are stubborn enough to make it show itself." [246]
"In the funhouse," Mira said, and the words tasted of candy floss and rot. [260]
"Where the mirrors still remember every face that ever screamed in them," the whispering advisor agreed. [276]
Mira stood, and the floor tilted like a deck of cards, and the lingering luminance followed her like a dog made of light. [299]
She walked into the hall of bent mirrors, and each reflection lagged behind as if the glass had to be reminded of her. [322]
In the third mirror, her reflection wore the opera cloak, and the clasp at its throat was the same beetle-cold thing against her skin. [347]
"It suits you," the whisper said from inside the glass, and she realized the voice had always come from there, not from beside her. [371]
Mira touched the clasp, and the metal opened, and inside was a folded scrap of paper no bigger than a moth's wing. [393]
The paper was a page torn from a dream dictionary, and the entry read: PARADOX. [408]
To dream of a paradox is to be told a lie by the truth, or a truth by the lie. [428]
The cure is to unbosom the contradiction aloud. [436]
Mira understood, then, what she had to do. [444]
Fear was hiding in the funhouse, in the lingering luminance, in the clasp, in her photographically cursed reflection, and it could only be tricked into showing its weakness if she said the unsayable thing. [478]
She opened her mouth, and the whisper tried to stop her, and the reflection tried to stop her, and the bulbs tried to dim into a forgiving dark. [506]
"I am alive," Mira said, "and I am not afraid of being alive, and I am not afraid of not being alive, and the camera loves me, and I do not love the camera, and I am ugly, and I am beautiful, and I am the rabbit, and I am the trap." [558]
The whisper shrieked, and the reflection bent double and crawled out of the glass on fingers of mercury. [576]
The opera cloak pooled at her feet like a spill of black water, and the clasp lay on it, still shining, still waiting. [599]
From the place where her reflection had been, a small pale thing crawled toward her, faceless, holding out the one secret fear had kept. [623]
Mira took it, and the repulsive allure of it was almost sweet, like a candy you know is poisoned, and she understood that fear's weakness had always been that it wanted to be seen, wanted to be unbosomed of its name. [664]
The whispering advisor in the mirror went quiet, and the lingering luminance began, at last, to fade. [681]
Mira walked out of the funhouse into a dawn of rain and iron, and the clasp in her hand was warm now, ordinary, slipped into her pocket like a coin returned from a country she had almost visited. [719]
She did not look back. [724]
The photographically cursed girl had tricked fear, and fear, in its repulsive allure, had given her back her name. [743]
