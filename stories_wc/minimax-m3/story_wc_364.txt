The sky over Vellanth hung in pale blue folds, the same color as the magician's silk folded in Orin's pocket. [20]
Orin was a meek blacksmith, small of frame and soft of voice, who kept his hammer wrapped in cloth to soften its ring. [43]
He lived on an orchard planet where fruit trees grew in terraced rings beneath arches of stone that drifted, weightless, above the hills. [66]
The arches were old, older than the mines, and they caught the dawn light like bruises against the morning. [85]
People said they were the bones of a sleeping giant, but Orin thought they looked more like questions left hanging in the air. [108]
He preferred the mines, the abandoned ones, where the air tasted of iron and forgotten things. [124]
He went there deliberately, choosing each tunnel the way a jeweler chooses a facet to cut. [140]
In the deepest shaft, where the lanterns guttered and the walls wept moisture, he had built a small forge from rubble. [161]
His only ambition was to coax resilience from rubble, to make something that would not break when the seasons turned. [181]
The silk had been a gift from a traveling conjurer who had stopped at his forge one autumn and traded three words of power for a horseshoe. [208]
The words were stitched along the hem in ink that glowed faintly at dusk. [222]
Orin had never read them aloud. [228]
He only smoothed the fabric over the cracked stones of his mine-forge, and the stones held fast through every tremor. [248]
The trade, a bartered awakening, was this: he had given away the only piece of work he had ever been proud of, and in return he had been given a question. [279]
The question was how to make metal remember the orchard. [289]
Orin carried that question between understanding and confusion, in the long hours when the mine was dark and the arches outside glowed faintly through the shaft mouth. [316]
He was not a scholar. [321]
He was only a blacksmith, and a meek one, and his hands were calloused in the way of people who do not ask for much from the world. [349]
But he was quietly determined, and the silk in his pocket grew warmer as the seasons changed. [366]
Each morning he walked the orchard terraces, gathering fruit that no one else wanted, the bruised pears and the windfall apples, and he carried them down into the mine. [395]
He pressed the fruit against the metal he had forged, and he whispered the words on the silk, though he could not read them. [419]
The metal did not change. [424]
But the fruit did. [428]
It grew heavy, dense as stone, and when he struck it with his cloth-wrapped hammer, it rang like a bell. [448]
One evening, as the floating arches drifted low over the shaft mouth, Orin felt the air shift around him. [467]
He climbed to the surface and found that the orchards had begun to bloom out of season. [484]
The blossoms were the color of the silk, a pale and stubborn blue. [497]
Azure defiance, the old orchard-keepers might have called it, the color of a sky that refuses to fall. [515]
Orin stood among the trees, the silk loose in his hand, and understood at last that the bartered awakening had not been his alone. [539]
The question had been for the planet, not for him. [549]
He had traded his pride, and the planet had traded its winter. [561]
The metal in the mine began to sing, a low hum that traveled up through the stone and into the floating arches, which began, very slowly, to descend. [589]
The arches settled onto the hills like sleeping animals, and the orchards grew quiet beneath them. [605]
Orin returned to his forge, his hammer, his silence, and laid the silk upon the anvil like a prayer. [624]
He had not become a hero. [630]
He had only learned to coax resilience from rubble, and to work with deliberate hands, and to be, in the soft blue light of his quiet world, entirely himself. [659]
