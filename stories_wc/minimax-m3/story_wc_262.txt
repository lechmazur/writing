<story>The projection booth of the Royal Alhambra smelled of dust and mothballed velvet, and Mira had made it her cathedral. [20] She was a crystal gazer by trade, though the velvet seats below had not needed a fortune teller for decades. [40] The booth itself was a small iron room, stacked high with reels whose titles no living audience could remember. [59] On a cracked leather stool sat a brass-bound box, and inside the box rested a single film strip. [77] She had found it the previous night while sweeping beneath the carbon arc of the projector. [93] The strip was the palest amber, and its sprocket holes were punched by a hand that was no longer alive. [113] Mira's fingers itched to touch it, though she had been raised to fear such a thing. [129] Her teacher in the old Quarter had called it psychometry, the dangerous reading of what objects still remember. [147] He had warned her that the past would not politely leave when she asked it to. [163] Tonight she did not want it to leave; she wanted it to confess. [176] She had written three goodbye letters that morning, ink still drying on the fourth. [190] Each one was addressed to a man who had loved her once and then loved a country more. [208] The letters sat folded inside the cigar box on the shelf, waiting like small, patient doors. [224] Mira unwound the film strip along the table, counting frames by lamplight as a doctor counts a pulse. [242] The first frames were nothing but black, then gray, then a kitchen she did not recognize. [258] She pressed the celluloid between her palms and let her mind open the way her teacher had taught. [276] The booth seemed to breathe around her, the projector humming its long-familiar hum. [289] Images came in pieces, like forgotten lyrics searching for the tune that had once carried them. [305] She saw a woman's hand pressing dough, a child's laugh over a tin radio, a soldier kissing a palm. [324] The film strip had been made by someone trying to keep a love from being forgotten. [340] Mira realized with a small shock that the soldier and the dough and the child were all the same man. [360] He had recorded his tenderness the way other men record their sins, frame by careful frame. [376] Somewhere in this reel, then, was the place where his unused love had gone to hide. [392] She was spatially imaginative, able to walk the inside of a memory as if it were a building with rooms. [412] So she walked into the strip's first frame, then through it, following the hum of the projector deeper. [430] The kitchen smelled of onions, the radio of a song half-remembered, the kiss of river water. [446] Somewhere a melody drifted, its words lost but its shape still reaching for an accompaniment. [461] Mira let the vision incubate inside her, the way one lets a broth simmer without lifting the lid. [479] She did not steer it; she only kept the flame low and even, the way her grandmother had taught. [498] Slowly the room in her mind brightened, and at its center stood a small oak chest. [514] The chest was the place, the exact place, where unused love is stored in those who cannot say it aloud. [534] She understood then that her goodbye letters were the wrong shape, ink shaped like an apology. [550] The soldier's reel was not an apology; it was a confession, made carefully, frame by frame. [566] Mira pulled her hands from the celluloid and sat very still as the projector ticked its cooling tick. [584] Outside the booth's porthole, the great empty hall sat in a hushed thunder of its own making. [601] A thousand phantom patrons seemed to lean forward in their seats, waiting for the picture to begin. [618] She took out the fifth sheet of paper and began again, this time describing what she had touched. [636] She described the kitchen, the dough, the kiss, the soldier's tender and trembling hand. [650] She described the small oak chest inside every heart that had loved and had not been allowed to speak. [669] Then she folded the letter, slid it beneath the spool of the unused reel, and let the projector warm again. [689] She did not know if he would ever read it, somewhere across the sea, in whatever country now held him. [709] But the film strip had given her a place to send the words, and that was almost the same as a song. [731] Above her, the bulb flickered, and the screen below caught the first pale frame of his kitchen once more. [750] The forgotten lyrics of his life found their tune at last, humming softly against the velvet dark. [767] Mira, the crystal gazer of the empty palace, smiled, and the booth held her like a held breath. [785]</story>
