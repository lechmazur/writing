<story>Ezra had been the late-shift pier lamplighter for nineteen years, and on the night of the sky lantern release, the harbor fell under a meteor hush so complete that even the waves seemed to hold their breath. [37] The pier stretched out over black water like a wooden spine, and Ezra moved along it with a long-handled taper, lighting each gas lamp in turn until the boardwalk glowed like a procession of small moons. [73] Above, townsfolk gathered at the pavilion, their paper wishes cupped in careful hands. [86] Ezra had been promised one, once, by a woman whose voice had sounded like rain on canvas. [103] The promise had been written on a hotel skeleton key tag, brass, small, numbered 217, and Ezra had carried it in a coat pocket ever since, though the coat had long since been given away, and the promise, too, had gone quiet. [145] That had been the luminous vow, glowing faintly across nineteen years, but it had always been hiding behind truth. [164] Tonight the lanterns drifted up like a second Milky Way, and Ezra set the taper down and walked to the end of the pier, where at low tide the wreck of the Marien Celeste could be entered through a door in her barnacled hull. [208] The ship had been there since Ezra was a child of seven, watching her father dive for coins in the shallows, and inside her ribs lay a forgotten library, its shelves bowed with books no one had read in eighty years. [249] Ezra had heard, in the way harbor stories are heard, that somewhere in that library one could find the color of sound. [271] That was why Ezra went. [276] The descent was easy at this tide. [283] Ezra dropped through the door into the dry-rot belly of the ship, and the water that had drowned her had left behind a smell of brine and old ink. [312] Lantern light from above filtered down through the deck planks in pale shafts, and Ezra moved between the shelves wandering purposefully, fingers trailing over cracked leather and foxed pages. [341] The library was a graveyard of stories, but Ezra was looking for a different kind. [356] Then, in a glass case at the heart of the hold, Ezra found it. [370] A small spinning disc of light, no larger than a coin, hovered above an open book. [386] It shifted hue as Ezra watched, sometimes amber, sometimes a violet so deep it seemed almost black, sometimes the pink of a healed wound. [410] And as it shifted, Ezra heard it. [417] Not with the ears, but somewhere behind them. [425] The disc was the color of the woman's voice. [434] The color of the promise. [439] The color of the luminous vow that had been made and broken and remade across nineteen years of lighting lamps on a dark pier. [463] The vow had been hiding behind truth, Ezra understood now. [473] The truth was that the woman had left. [481] The truth was that Ezra had stayed. [488] The truth was that the sound of a voice saying I will return was not the same color as the sound of a key tag spinning against a wooden floor in an empty hotel hallway. [523] But the disc showed both, and in showing them, it signified. [534] Ezra reached out a hand, and the disc settled into the palm like a fallen petal. [550] It was warm. [553] It was the color of every lantern rising over the harbor tonight, and the color of every lamp Ezra had ever lit, and the color of the meteor hush that had fallen over the pier when the first paper flame lifted into the sky. [597] Above, the sky lantern release reached its peak. [605] Hundreds of paper moons drifted upward, each carrying a wish that no one would read. [620] Below, in the library of the sunken ship, Ezra closed a hand around the color of sound and understood, at last, what the woman had meant. [646] The vow had not been a promise to return. [655] It had been a promise to be remembered in color, and now, finally, Ezra could see it. [672] Ezra climbed back out of the ship and lit the last lamp of the shift. [687] The pier glowed. [690] The lanterns glowed. [693] And somewhere in a coat pocket that no longer existed, a hotel skeleton key tag spun slowly, signifying nothing and everything at once. [716]</story>
