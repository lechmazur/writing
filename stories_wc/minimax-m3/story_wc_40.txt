He carried nothing but a leather satchel and the small fragment of crystal he had pried, years ago, from the library's central chandelier. [22]

This was the morning after the last dictionary had been printed, and the shelves behind the boy held only the memory of words. [22]

The scholar lowered himself onto a bench near the long windows and let the early light fall across his hands, which were weathered in a way that suggested a long apprenticeship to patience. [31]

When he was young, he had believed that the growth of a language could be measured by the weight of its tomes, and he had tried, in vain, to count the new words added each decade. [34]

Now, older, he understood that growth was not a matter of accumulation but of fracture, of the way a single sentence could shatter into a thousand meanings and still hold together. [31]

He had come to testify, in his own quiet way, that the silence of these rooms was not emptiness but a kind of listening. [22]

He rose, walked to the great central chandelier, and looked up at the place where the crystal had once hung among its brothers. [22]

Then he began to speak, though his voice was so soft that only the library itself could hear. [16]
