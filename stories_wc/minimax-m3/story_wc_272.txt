The portrait artist climbed the spiral stairs for the last time. [11]
She had come to this forgotten library inside a lighthouse because every other room in the world had failed her. [31]
Each portrait she painted came back to her unrecognizable, the sitters' eyes forever asking questions only silence could answer. [50]
She had asked herself: what was the point of likeness when the likeness itself refused to stay? [67]
Up in the lighthouse, the walls had begun their slow creep inward, and the books on the shelves pressed against her shoulders like tired friends. [92]
There was no one else here. [98]
The keeper had died decades ago, and the library had been left to gather dust and silence and the particular kind of hush that settles in places forgotten even by their own keepers. [131]
His portrait still hung above the reading desk, the oils so old they had begun to soften at the edges. [151]
She opened the broken tarot card box she had carried for years, and the cards spilled across the reading desk in a cascade of moth-eaten corners. [177]
Some were missing. [180]
Some had been torn. [184]
The Moon card had lost its face entirely, and The Tower had cracked down its middle as if cleaved by the very lightning it depicted. [209]
In this place, she understood her craft had always been a kind of divination. [223]
Each portrait was a fragment of the sitter, a fragment of herself, and together they composed a fragmented reality that refused to hold its shape. [248]
The library was a model of her own splintered mind, every shelf a memory she could not quite reach. [267]
She had come here to descend into necessary darkness, to feel the weight of the depths she had spent her life avoiding. [289]
Below the reading desk, a drawer held a tangle of threads. [300]
They were not ordinary threads. [305]
They were the colored silks she had used in her earliest portraits, the ones she had stitched into canvas before she learned to paint. [329]
The threads were knotted in ways that suggested someone - perhaps herself - had been trying to hold a thousand small repairs together at once. [352]
She pulled one thread free. [357]
It led to nothing. [361]
She pulled another. [364]
It led back into the tangle. [370]
The work of redesigning anything, she realized, was the work of untangling threads. [383]
She began to pull. [387]
The thread that came loose was the color of her mother's kitchen light. [400]
Another was the gray of her first sitter's eyes. [409]
A third was the black of a window she had looked through for years without seeing. [425]
As she worked, the library walls seemed to stop their inward drift, and the keeper's portrait gave a small sigh as its oils dried. [449]
The light from the beacon above poured down through the cupola and fell upon her hands. [465]
It was the luminescence of an ache she had carried since childhood, the quiet glow that had always lit her from the inside while dimming everything else. [492]
She had been a crestfallen portrait artist for so long that she had forgotten what the falling had been for. [512]
The threads, she understood now, were not the source of the fragmentation but the remedy. [527]
Every knot was a question she had refused to ask herself. [538]
Every unraveled strand was a small act of redesigning the self she had thought was fixed. [554]
She worked until the lamp above her dimmed, and the room grew soft with the dark she had chosen. [573]
In the necessary darkness, she found clarity. [580]
The tarot cards on the desk rearranged themselves, or she rearranged them, she could no longer tell which. [598]
The Tower fell upward. [602]
The Moon returned its face, but it was her face, and it was not the same as before. [620]
She took up a blank canvas she had brought with her. [631]
She began a portrait that was no portrait, an image stitched from the threads she had untangled, a self-portrait rendered in the colors of every sitter she had failed to render. [662]
In the forgotten library inside a lighthouse, when the walls had closed in, she had at last become the keeper. [682]
