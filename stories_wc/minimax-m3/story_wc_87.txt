<story>The kayak launch smelled of salt and old rope, and the broken navigator stood at its edge as if waiting for a tide that had already turned. [27] He had once charted the tradewinds of the South Pacific with an astronomical compass whose brass casing gleamed like a small sun, and his logs had been studied by admirals. [57] Now that compass sat silent in his coat pocket, its needle swinging without conviction, and he could not bring himself to repair it. [80] He had let pride do the welding, and the backlash of regrets had fused every gear of his career into something jagged and useless. [104] A gull laughed overhead, and he found himself whistling along, a sound merrily melancholic in the grey morning. [122] He had come down to the coastal kayak launch because his wife had finally said the quiet thing aloud: that she no longer recognized the man who came home each night. [153] He had told her he was a navigator, and she had laughed, and the laughing had been the loneliest sound of his life. [176] The launch ramp slid into the bay like a tongue of concrete, and he counted the utility poles along the shoreline as a substitute for stars. [202] Each pole was a waypoint, a small terrestrial constellation strung between the dunes, and he navigated by them the way old sailors navigate by the smell of land. [230] The kayak he had rented was a cheerful yellow, and he pulled it toward the water, feeling his boots gravitate toward the slick of seaweed and sand. [257] He did not know how to swim, which was a fact he had hidden from everyone for thirty years, and now it seemed the most honest thing about him. [286] Flickering faith moved through him like a lantern carried into a cellar, sometimes bright enough to read by, sometimes barely a coal. [308] He had promised himself, the night before, that paddling beyond the breakers might realign his heart with his hands. [327] To align heart and action was the only prayer he knew now, though he had forgotten which gods to address it to. [349] The water slapped the hull, and he paused with one knee in the kayak, listening to the small arithmetic of waves. [370] He thought of the charts he had once drawn, the patient latitudes, and how each had promised a destination he had never reached. [393] The expectations had collapsed years ago, in a courtroom in Manila, when a freighter under his instruction had run aground on a reef that was not on any of his maps. [424] He had been cleared of blame, technically, but the clearances of courts do not reach the deeper courts of the self. [445] The compass in his pocket ticked softly against his ribs, as if reminding him that the stars were still up there, waiting for someone who would look. [472] He was not that someone, and he knew it, and he set the compass on the ramp instead of taking it with him, because a tool you cannot trust is a heavier burden than a tool you do not own. [512] He pushed the kayak out, and the bay received him with a shrug of small waves, and he began to paddle, and he began, very quietly, to cry. [540] The tears were merrily melancholic, the kind that come when you realize you have been grieving a life you never actually lived. [562] He counted utility poles as he went past them, one, two, three, and the rhythm of the counting steadied him the way a rosary steadies some, the way a song steadies others. [594] Halfway across the bay, the kayak began to drift, and he let it drift, because he had spent a lifetime insisting on direction, and direction, he was learning, was sometimes the enemy of arrival. [628] A cormorant surfaced nearby with a silver fish in its beak, and the fish still shone, and the shine was a kind of answer he had not expected. [656] He paddled back when the wind changed, and he returned the kayak to the rental shack, and he walked up the ramp with empty hands and a lighter coat. [685] At the place where he had left the compass, he found only a small tide pool, and he understood that even broken instruments return, eventually, to the waters that made them. [716] He stood at the coastal kayak launch as the sun began to break through, and he felt his boots gravitate once more, this time toward the parking lot, where his wife was waiting in the car. [752] He had no destination now, only a direction, and the direction was toward her, and his heart, for the first time in years, was doing the same. [779]</story>
