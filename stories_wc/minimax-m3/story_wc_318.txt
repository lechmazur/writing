The hollow had not always been silent. [7] Once it breathed with the family's laughter, the clatter of cutlery, the small arguments over breakfast, the goodnight kisses traded in doorways. [29] Now, after the final grain slipped through the hourglass, the house had become a museum of unfinished sentences. [47] Every morning the leaf blower symphony conductor descended the basement stairs, the pull cord handle of his battered machine dangling like a rosary at his hip. [73] He was a strange figure: a man in a threadbare tailcoat, a dust-lined cravat, his hair combed back as if for a concert no one would hear. [100] The townsfolk whispered that he had been a music teacher once, before the accident, before the silence settled over the family's porch like snow. [124] They called him mournfully eager, because he arrived at every doorstep with the same patient, almost hungry smile, asking to be permitted inside. [147] He had come to this house the way he came to all of them: drawn by the unspoken, and by the slow hope of restoring lost laughter to a household that had forgotten how to make any. [184] The father, hollow-eyed at the kitchen table, the daughter who practiced scales on the piano but no longer sang, the mother who kept her sister's photograph facedown on the mantel, each carried a grief too soft to speak aloud. [223] Tending to the unspoken was his peculiar trade, the only work left to him after his own voice had been taken. [244] Tonight the wind through the eaves was off-key, sighing through the gutters in a way that was almost tuneful, and the conductor listened for a long moment before tightening his grip on the pull cord handle. [280] The basement door opened onto a tunnel that no one else in the household had ever explored, though the blueprints clearly showed a passage running beneath the property and out toward the old ravine. [314] The walls of the tunnel were curved and smooth, like the inside of a seashell, and when he stepped inside, the smallest sound returned to him in a long, breathing hush. [345] An echo chamber tunnel, his father would have called it, the kind old miners carved to amplify the sound of a distant pickaxe. [368] Here, the children had once played at being giants. [377] He pulled the cord. [381] The leaf blower coughed, sputtered, and then settled into a low, steady drone, and he raised his arms as if raising a baton. [404] The machine's roar was not a roar at all, in his hands, but a chorus, a sustained, sighing chord that pressed the air into a thousand small currents. [432] He began to conduct, swaying, his palms open, his fingers curling, and the dust motes in the tunnel began to move in long, deliberate arcs. [457] He was not cleaning the basement. [463] He was macerating the grief that had settled into the bones of the house, breaking it into something that could be carried. [485] And then he sang, or half-sang, the way a person hums to a child who is just past sleep. [504] The lullabies were old ones, fragments his own mother had crooned, the words long since lost, only the rise and fall of the melody remaining, the vowels soft and rounded, the consonants softened to almost nothing. [540] The off-key wind caught them and lifted them, sliding them along the tunnel's curve, sending them up through the floorboards, into the parlor, into the bedrooms, where they settled on the sleeping like a hand on a brow. [578] The father woke first. [582] He sat up in the dark, listening. [589] There was something in the air, something almost familiar, and he could not say why his eyes were wet. [608] The daughter came down the stairs in her nightgown, drawn by a sound she had not heard since she was very small, and she stood at the basement door and listened, her throat working. [642] The mother arrived last, her hand on the frame, the photograph of her sister now lifted to the light. [661] In the tunnel, the conductor did not stop. [669] The leaf blower droned on, a low brass section beneath the breath of his voice, and the wind outside wove the half-sung lullabies into a single, almost-rhythmic pulse. [697] The echoes returned, softer and softer, as if the tunnel itself were learning to let the sound pass through. [716] By morning the house was not healed, grief of that depth does not lift so quickly, but the silence had cracked, just a little, along its seam. [743] Somewhere a kettle whistled. [747] Somewhere, a child laughed in her sleep. [754] The conductor, mournfully eager still, coiled the pull cord handle neatly around his wrist, nodded to the empty basement, and climbed the stairs into the new day. [781]
