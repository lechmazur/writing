At the edge of a suspended platform, where the roots of a tidal arbor curled into mist and the sea churned far below the clouds, the wistful station pianist Maren set down her velvet case. [35]

The station she had played for thirty years had already become a memory, its benches empty, its schedules dissolved into wind, and she had climbed here seeking refuge from the weight of ordinary time. [69]

She had brought with her the only things that still mattered: a spliced black-and-white film reel, brittle with age, and a broken anniversary clock that no longer kept any hour but her own. [102]

The compasses she carried flickered against her palm, six of them, no two pointing the same direction, their needles jumping like startled fish. [125]

They were instruments she had made herself, strategically creative little engines of brass and steely longing, each one tuned to a different year of her marriage. [151]

When she turned them, they whispered coordinates toward moments she could not revisit any other way. [167]

The arbor accepted her without judgment, its branches bending in slow tides, lifting and settling with the breathing of the sky. [188]

Above her, a rail of cloud-traffic passed in silence, and below the ocean wore its colors out against the sun. [208]

Maren walked until she found the grove she had seen in dreams, where the leaves were silver and the bark held the texture of old piano keys. [235]

She had told no one she was coming, because her motivation was simpler and more dangerous than explanation: to surprise fate with an unplanned encore, to finish the piece her husband had been playing when his heart stopped mid-concerto. [274]

The reel in her pocket held the rest of his melody, captured in a single afternoon and never shown to her until the lawyers had finished with his desk. [303]

She threaded the film through a small projector she had carried up the winding stairs, then routed it through the hidden sections of a black-and-white film reel she had modified herself, gaps cut into the celluloid and lined with her own scores, so that image and music would finally meet on the same frame. [357]

This was her method, the kind of method only a strategically creative woman would dare, a method that stitched one medium into another until the seam disappeared. [384]

The picture on the screen would be his hands on the keys; the sound would be her hands completing the phrase he had left suspended in air. [411]

The broken anniversary clock, set on the moss beside her, refused to move, its hands frozen at 3:17, the minute of his last breath, and she had carried it across three countries because she did not trust herself to leave it behind. [453]

It would not tick; it only watched. [460]

She sat at the piano the arbor had grown for her, an instrument of dark wood and pearl, and waited until the horizon invited night, until the gold drained out of the clouds and the first violet arrived in the west. [501]

The compasses spun and settled, and one of them, at last, pointed true toward a memory she had almost abandoned. [521]

She began to play, and the film began to roll, and the hidden sections aligned, and for the first time in seven years she heard the duet she had always imagined, his melody answering hers, his hands in monochrome lifting at the exact moments hers fell. [567]

The arbor swayed around her, and the branches caught the notes and passed them down through the roots into the sleeping sea below the clouds. [592]

Somewhere beneath her, a train she had once played for whistled at a junction she could not see, and she did not look. [615]

The encore was for him, and for the part of herself that had refused to finish the song when it mattered, and she let it finish now in a place no audience would ever reach. [650]

The reel ran out, the screen went white, and the clock still read 3:17 and would read it forever, and she let it. [673]

When the night was fully drawn, she closed the lid of the piano and stood; the compasses were quiet in her pocket; the arbor, generous as it had been, began to fold its tides inward, preparing her descent. [711]

She carried the clock, the reel, the projector, and the completed silence back to the stairs, and she went down lighter than she had climbed. [736]
