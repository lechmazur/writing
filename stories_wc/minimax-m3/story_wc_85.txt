The cobalt terrace caught the last amber light like a bruise just beginning to fade. [15]
Mara set her satchel on the cracked blue tile and listened to the sieved hush the sea made through the breakwater stones. [37]
She had come here to quell the thing that had lived behind her ribs for eleven years, ever since the night she sold a family line to a man who used it to destroy a daughter. [73]
Eleven years of carrying the weight of unspoken words she had swallowed like river stones. [88]
She was a black market genealogist, and she had made a careful fortune digging up the buried branches of bloodlines for people who did not want to be known. [117]
Some of her clients were rightful heirs. [124]
Most were not. [127]
She worked from a back room in a printer's shop on Rosario Street, behind a curtain printed with parrots, where the air smelled of ink and old paper and the particular desperation of people who could not ask their questions in the light. [170]
Her guilt had not arrived all at once. [178]
It had been a slow accretion, like barnacles on a hull, and by the time she understood what she was, the barnacles had become the hull. [204]
Tonight she had brought with her a small wooden box, and inside the box a single watch hand, brass and slightly bent, that she had pried from a movement in a pawnshop in San Ildefonso. [239]
It was not her father's, though she had told herself for a long time that it was. [256]
It was not anyone's. [260]
She had manufactured the memory that it had belonged to him, because the alternative was that she had never had a father at all, only a name on a manifest and a mother who would not speak of him. [299]
The lie had been useful for a season. [307]
It had become a kind of cradle. [314]
Now, on the cobalt terrace, with the dusk settling like ash into the cracks of the tiles, she meant to lay it down. [337]
Below the terrace, a forgotten shore curved away into the mist. [348]
The tide had long since stopped reaching it, and the sand was littered with driftwood, each piece carved by the water into something almost like a face, almost like a letter, almost like an answer. [383]
She descended the broken steps and began to sift through the driftwood carvings, turning each one over in her hands. [403]
Some were smooth as a confession. [409]
Some were jagged as a refusal. [415]
The hush here was different from the one on the terrace. [426]
It had been sieved through reeds and rock, and what came through was a quiet so thin it almost rang. [446]
She thought of all the families she had unwoven. [455]
The heir in Cordoba who turned out not to be an heir at all, the widow in Lisbon whose lost nephew was a forger, the boy in Veracruz who had not spoken since. [488]
Each name a watch hand, she thought, a small piece of a mechanism that, once removed, would not tick again. [508]
She had told herself, in the early years, that she was only a scribe, that the truth, even when it cut, was a kind of mercy. [534]
But the truth, she understood now, was not a single thing but a thing that could be shaped, like a key, to fit a lock the buyer had chosen. [563]
And she had learned, in her trade, exactly how to shape it. [575]
The sieved hush deepened as the dusk thickened. [583]
A gull passed over without crying. [589]
Mara found, half-buried in the gray sand, a driftwood carving in the shape of a closed hand, and inside the closed hand, a small glass eye that caught the last light. [620]
She held it up and saw her own face inside it, very small, very tired, and she understood that the work of atoning was not a single act but a daily sifting, a daily choosing of what to pick up and what to leave for the tide. [667]
She put the watch hand into the hollow of the wooden hand, and she set the wooden hand into the surf where the forgotten shore met the water. [695]
She did not pray. [699]
She did not weep. [703]
She only stood under the weight of the unspoken words she had carried, and she let them be heavy, and she let them be hers, and the cobalt terrace behind her went dark, and the tide came in as it had always come in, patient and impartial, taking what she had finally offered, one small brass hand at a time.
