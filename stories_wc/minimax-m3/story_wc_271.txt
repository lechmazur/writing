The cliff path narrowed where the sea spray had eaten the soil, and beyond it the arboretum held its breath against the wind. [23] Maren walked it every morning, her boots finding the same cracked stone, her hands carrying the same secateurs. [41] She had arrived at the garden a season after the last lighthouse keeper's grandson padlocked the lantern room and walked away. [62] The light had been automated for years before that, but the keeper had stayed on, tending the flame as if it were still his own. [87] The arboretum, founded by his great-aunt on the same granite shelf, had outlived the light it was meant to complement. [107] Maren was its quietest volunteer. [112] She was, by any honest measure, quietly talented with a blade and a page. [126] Her pressed-flower illustrations had been exhibited once in the coastal town below, in a gallery that smelled of salt and turpentine, and the curator had asked for more. [154] Maren had promised and not delivered. [160] She was busy holding herself together in the way survivors are busy, with a thousand small tasks that keep the body's machinery from noticing what the mind has learned to outrun. [191] In the pocket of her canvas apron she kept a crumpled photograph. [203] It was her wedding day. [208] She had been twenty-three. [212] The bride's dress had been borrowed from an aunt, and the groom had worn his brother's jacket, and the music had been a single guitar, and the drone had come in over the almond trees at the moment the rice was thrown. [254] The photograph showed none of that. [260] It showed her laughing, his hand on her waist, a sky that was whole and blue and had not yet broken. [281] She kept it because she wanted to remember the before, and because she wanted to remember the after, and because the paper, once smooth, now held the shape of her fear in its creases. [316] This morning, thinning the sea-holly along the eastern terrace, she heard a small sound above the wind. [333] It was the thin electric whine of a rotor. [342] Maren's body knew before her mind did. [349] Her fingers tightened on the secateurs. [355] Her shoulders drew up toward her ears. [362] Her breath stopped in the cage of her ribs, and for a long suspended second she could not move. [381] It was not a thought. [386] It was the somatic markers of a terror written into her nervous system on a particular afternoon, and now, in this garden perched above the water, those markers demanded the same obedience they had demanded then: freeze. [423] She froze. [425] She became a statue of herself, scissors still open, eyes fixed on the cypresses where the sound seemed to hover. [445] The rotor passed. [448] It was a delivery courier, a yellow quadcopter trundling on to the next cliff-top house. [463] Her breath came back in a ragged pull. [471] Her shoulders did not drop. [476] She stood inside the freeze for another ten heartbeats, letting the alarm run its course, because she had learned, in the months since she had come here, not to fight it but to let it pass through her like weather. [516] In the stillness that followed, in the pearl of stillness that formed in the aftermath of the alarm, she felt the cliff and the garden and the wind and her own standing body all at once, as if she had been given a second to be wholly present in the world that had, against all odds, continued to hold her. [575] She had come to the arboretum to do this work. [585] Not to forget, but to anchor optimism in realism: to believe that a garden could be planted and tended on the same cliff where a light had once warned ships away from the rocks, and to believe that beauty and warning could share a shelf of granite. [632] She did not want to be a person who pretended the strike had not happened, and she did not want to be a person who let the strike take the rest of her life. [666] The arboretum asked only that she show up and cut and press and water. [680] In return, it gave her back the shape of her days. [691] She finished the sea-holly, wiped the secateurs, slid the crumpled photograph back into her pocket, and walked on to the succulents. [712] Behind her, the lighthouse stood with its dark lantern. [721] Somewhere out on the water, a freighter moved along the horizon, and the sound of its engine was nothing like a rotor, and she kept walking anyway. [748]
