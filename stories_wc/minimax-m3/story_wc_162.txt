The terrace was a drift of glass and slow light, prisms turning in currents no one could name, and the child stood at its edge with a borrowed stillness. [29]

The book was not theirs. [5]

It had been left behind by someone who had flown above the mazes and recorded, in faded code, the weather of every entrance and exit. [25]

They had been intuiting for weeks that the codes were not about temperature or pressure but about thresholds, about the small meteorological events that occur when one space decides to become another. [32]

Inside the logbook, the numbers read like a hymn: 27015, 44003, 01812, each a confession of distance. [17]

The child believed their father had understood this too, that the mazes he built were not puzzles but quiet arguments for necessary distances, the kind of space a heart needs to breathe between one love and the next. [38]

On the terrace, a single chair waited, its wooden arms ridged with a grain the child could read the way other children read faces. [24]

They sat and began their slow method, analyzing the grain in wooden furniture as if it were a coastline, as if the swirls and knots were the turning paths of a maze long since grown over. [36]

Here, the hush was fractured by the soft click of prisms meeting in the air, a sound like teeth on a comb, like the inside of a thought. [28]

It was the year their father had drawn his last maze, the year he had walked into it at dawn and never walked out by the same door. [28]

A relative with a gentle face had told them that some thresholds are not crossings but invitations, and that to enter one is to accept a distance you cannot yet name. [31]

Beneath it, in their father's hand, was a single word: stay. [11]

The child understood then that the sacredness of a threshold was not the threshold itself but the pause it demanded, the necessary distance between the foot lifted and the foot set down. [32]

Outside the railings, the world was a maze of fields, each row a corridor of light, and the child, who had once been lost within such corridors, knew at last that being lost was only a way of being held at a respectful remove. [44]
