The medium would not speak, so she breathed on the ferry's cabin window and wrote in the fog.  ASK FOR THE MOMENT, NOT THE ANSWER.  The letters faded before I could answer, which was her whole grammar. 

Outside, the mangroves stood knee-deep in black water, and the spirits in them were singing.  They sing what nobody sends.  Nine years of my letters to my sister were out there in those roots, sung in my own voice by mouths that had never owned a body.  That is why the labyrinth eats people: you follow your own words in, and they never stop.  Everything she told anyone had to vanish, or the labyrinth would learn it and sing it forever. 

She put a stone in my hand: a shard of a gargoyle's rainspout, the throat-piece, broken from the drowned chapel upstream.  Water had gone through that mouth for two hundred years, and nothing had ever stayed in it.  She fogged the glass again: HOLD IT TO YOUR THROAT AND EVOKE.  Then, under it, smaller: BEFORE MOONSET. 

Her palm was split open from the last crossing, and I watched the moonlight lie down in the cut and knit it shut.  She healed only in moonlight; by day she came apart again, which is what guiding costs.  I asked her what I would find.  THE SECOND BEFORE YOU DECIDED.  It was what I had come for. 

Not to undo the leaving; I would leave again, I was nineteen and the town was a fist.  I wanted the breath before it, where the apology still sat unspoken, waiting nine years for my mouth to open. 

The medium pushed the ferry into the roots and killed the engine.  We drifted.  The singing came up through the hull.  Dear Junie, I am not angry, I am only busy.  Forty-one letters, and every one of them a small lie told gently.  I loved her ferociously and from a distance, which is the coward's way of loving.  They sang the fire that took the skin from her hands, so she could never write back, and my silence doing the rest.  Roots closed behind us. 

Moonlight came through the canopy in coins, and the medium's face went smooth and young in it, and she pointed at my throat.  I held the gargoyle's shard against my voice and evoked.  The singing stopped, the way a room stops when someone stands to speak.  And there it was: the pier, the gulls, Junie's burned hands wrapped in grocery-store gauze, her chin up, daring me to say the kind thing.  I saw my own mouth closed around the apology.  I had been living in that second for nine years, paying rent in it, keeping my letters folded in it like clothes.  It was not a door.  It was a room I had furnished so well I never had to leave it and speak. 

On the cabin window, fog appeared with no breath behind it: YOU CANNOT REDECIDE.  YOU CAN ONLY ARRIVE.  So I did the thing the stone was made for.  I put the rainspout shard to my throat and said it out loud, into the water, into the roots, into every mouth that had been singing my cowardice back at me.  Junie, I left you alone with your hands, and I am sorry, and I am coming home.  The shard did what rainspouts do: it carried the thing through and kept none of it.  The spirits lost that letter.  They cannot sing what is no longer unsent.  One by one they went quiet, and the labyrinth, which is only made of withheld words, opened a channel of straight black water and let us out. 

The medium's palm had opened again on the way back, and she held it up into the last of the moon and let it close.  She fogged the glass one last time.  AN APOLOGY IS A DIRECTION, NOT A ROOM.  The words vanished as the glass warmed, the way all her words did, and I did not need them to stay. 

In my bag were forty-one letters, and I would not mail them; I would read them aloud at her door, with my own mouth, in the daylight, where nothing heals for free.  Junie would hit me, probably, with those ruined hands, and I would let her, and then I would say it again.
