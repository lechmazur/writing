The bunker remembered better than she did.  Vines had swallowed its concrete throat, and the forest above had gone feral with forty years of nobody's permission.  They had named her Foresight as a joke, because she had hindsight only.  Yesterday lay open to her like a ledger; tomorrow was a wall; the present was a room she could hear but never enter.  So she slept, and read her dreams, and interpreted them like weather.  Dreams were the only tense she had that meant now. 

Tonight's dream: a girl at the blast door, one foot bleeding, a map inked on her palm.  Tonight's dream, repeated: the girl knocking, and the knocking getting softer.  Three taps, a pause, two taps: precisely playful, a rhythm that wanted in but would not beg.  She woke into the smell of ozone and remembered the last storm exactly: the same pressure in the teeth, the same silence of birds.  Hindsight told her what the storm would do, because it had done it before.  Light through the seam, then the drum memory wiped to white. 

The directive was already dying.  Its letters had been flaking for a decade, whole clauses lost to rust, so that ADMIT ONLY THE CHOSEN now read ADMIT ONLY THE.  Beside the door, the definition lived under the wafer seal: pressed ceramic thin as a communion host, laid over the directive's core.  Break the seal and the directive could never be verified again; it would be only a story people told.  So she had kept it whole, and turned away nine walkers in thirty years, and each refusal was perfect in her memory, which was the worst room in the bunker. 

She had one question, and she had asked it of every record she owned: why do some paths choose their walkers?  The trail appeared on no map, yet some people found it and some never did.  The oldest entry said the path had chosen her at fourteen, barefoot, following a dream she had interpreted wrongly and gone anyway.  Thirty years ago they had made her keeper and told her to press the wafer seal and never read beneath it.  A keeper guards the definition; a keeper does not use it.  She had obeyed with her whole life, which is a way of not having one. 

Thunder came down through the vines like something dropping through water.  When the storm arrives, she thought, the last copy goes white, and the rule dies with or without me.  A decaying directive gave her this much: obedience had an expiration date, and it was tonight.  She went along the corridor by memory, counting the eleven steps, the lip of the hatch, the cold wheel.  Hindsight was good for that much: a map of a room that had not changed. 

She could not see the girl.  She could hear her, which was not seeing, which was the whole condition of her life.  Through the steel came three taps, a pause, two taps, exactly as the dream had knocked.  She had no test for chosenness; she had only nine refusals and a rotting sentence.  The old keepers had a word for going past the record: transcend, and they had used it only as a warning.  So she did the thing no keeper had done.  She put her thumb against the wafer seal and broke it.  It gave like the skin on cooling milk. 

Beneath, in the old pressed letters, unrotted, the definition: CHOSEN MEANS ANY WHO KNOCK.  Her own thumbprint sat in the clay beside it, thirty years old, exactly the size of the answer she had covered.  Nine walkers had knocked.  The path chose the ones who came anyway, on a badly interpreted dream, bleeding, without proof.  That was why it had chosen her, and why she had spent thirty years unchoosing everyone else. 

She turned the wheel.  Light came through the seam and the drum went white behind her, the whole ledger of yesterday burning off like fog.  She knelt into the now she could not see and found the girl by heat, by the smell of iron, by a hand closing on her wrist.  No record would hold this; the drum was blank, and she would not write it down.  She lifted the girl through the hatch and carried her down the eleven steps she knew, toward an ending she did not.  For once she did not look back to check.  The storm took the rule, the forest took the bunker, and the path, unbolted at last, went on ahead of them, choosing.
