The archive had been under siege for nine days, and the Syndics had begun to ram the door.  They wanted the relic at her throat and the route they were sure she had memorized.  Sefa Oro, last lore keeper of the ark Anthem, kept peeling the wall.  Every generation had painted the corridor a new color, and every generation had painted over the map.  Nine layers came away under her knife like dry skin: teal, oxide, cream, the gray of the plague years.  Beneath them lay the first crew's mural, drawn in pigment ground from hull rust.  Wren held the lamp and did not ask her to hurry, which was why Sefa kept her.  The mural was a tangle of blue flourishes, meaningless until Sefa took the ruby flake from her collar.  It was a chip of the command chair's canopy, broken off by scavengers four centuries ago, back when the throne was still worth breaking.  Through the red glass the blue lines went black and vanished, and a red line surfaced: a stair, a tree, a falling ribbon of water.  The stairway of ascension.  Anthem's throne had stood unclaimed since the landing, because the ship would open its command deck only to someone who could find the way without being told.  That was the ancestors' joke and their test: a captain who could not read had no business commanding.  At sunrise the Syndics would cut the deck open with torches and sell the chair for its metal.  Sefa had read the wall three times before she moved, which was her whole philosophy: read three times, leap once.  She rolled the flake into her collar, took the lamp, and told Wren to open the coal chute.  Their sally was a small one, two women and a lamp against eleven men, but it went out through the north breach while the ram was still swinging at the front.  By the time the Syndics understood, the lore keeper was already downhill in the dark, past the dead turbines she loved like grandparents.  Anthem had landed so long ago that its engines were furniture, its hymns were lullabies, and nobody remembered the taste of the world it had come from.  The reclamation cascade thundered ahead, falling through the prism panels the first crew had salvaged from the solar sails.  Even by lamplight the water broke into wheels of color that turned and turned, green to rose to a blue nobody had a name for anymore.  The pumps idled at night; at sunrise they would wake and drown the gap behind the falls.  She timed the pulse of the runoff twice, then stepped through on the third.  The orchard was there, exactly as the mural promised, hidden in the steam.  Forty apple trees, grown from seed the ancestors had carried out of a world that no longer answered calls.  Every trunk wore a copper tag with a planter's name, and Sefa read them as she passed, because that was her office.  Wren laughed and put her palm on a trunk, and Sefa let her have three heartbeats of wonder.  The oldest tree stood at the center, its roots wrapped around a hatch.  A reader plate glowed beside it, dusty, patient, still waiting for the token it had been built to know.  Sefa set the ruby flake into the slot, and four hundred years of silence ended with a click.  The hatch opened on rungs going up into the spine of the ship, and the plate returned her flake.  They climbed until the rungs became a stair, and the stair became a corridor, and the corridor let them out on the command deck.  The throne sat under a canopy of ruby glass with one flake missing, like a mouth with a chipped tooth.  Four centuries of strong men had tried to force the deck; none had thought to give anything back.  Below, torchlight was already crawling toward the hull.  Sefa pressed the flake back into its scar.  The chair warmed, recognized the whole of itself, and asked her, in the voice of a woman four centuries dead, to state her name and her business.  "Sefa Oro, lore keeper," she said. "My business is remembering."  Sunrise came through the canopy and turned the deck the color of the orchard's fruit.  The locks sealed; the torches below went small and stupid.  Wren asked whether they were queens now.  "No," Sefa said, sitting down on the unclaimed throne, which was claimed at last. "We are the people who know the way."  Then she opened the log, and began to write the tenth layer.
