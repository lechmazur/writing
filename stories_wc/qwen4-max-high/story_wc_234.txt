Nell leaned across the barrel and whispered her secret into my ear, here at the ember nexus where the four forge roads meet and the day's coals are carried out to cool.  Twilight came down the color of a struck match.  She said it slowly, twice, the way you say a thing you have practiced for years, so I had time.  I am the guild assayer.  I price light: lumens per grain, brightness per copper, a ledger of small honest numbers.  Nell is a pocket-sun alchemist.  She grows suns the size of plums and sells them warm to men who work the night shift, and by my own scale I have always underpriced her.  The secret was this: the suns are not made of ember.  They are made of her.  Every plum she handed over the counter carried a measured spoon of her own presence, and she had been spending it for eleven years.  That is why her hands look like paper.  That is why the ash-cats no longer sleep on her stall.  She told me once, scientifically romantic to the bone, that love was a growth equation and she could prove it.  She keeps a nautilus shell beside the scales for that reason.  Not for the pearly face of it: for the columella curl, the small axis at the shell's heart where every chamber pivots.  The animal grows by returning to the same angle at a wider radius, again and again, never repeating, never leaving.  That is her whole method: wind a moment around the columella curl, then come back to it differently, one chamber out, until the moment thickens instead of fading.  Not brightness.  Density: how much of a person is actually in the room.  She wanted that number, and she wanted it before she ran out.  Her mother had gone thin and unremembered in a room two streets from here, and Nell swore she would leave a measurement instead of a rumor.  Her scale is a palette of resolve, twelve hues on a strip of tin.  Ochre for stubbornness.  Cold green for patience.  A thin rose at the far end for the resolve it takes to be told the truth and keep standing.  She never mixes them the same way twice.  She snatches whatever the twilight offers, a coal, a moth, a laugh from the noodle stall, and improvises; the aim never wavers.  The aim is the density of a life.  Her breath is still in my ear.  In my coat pocket, the sun she gave me on my thirtieth birthday is warm as a held hand, and I understand now whose hand.  Six years I have carried a spoon of Nell and called it a lamp.  The ledger is open on the barrel between us.  Column one, lumens.  Column two, price.  Four coppers a plum, because that is what a plum of light does in a dark stairwell.  So, inside the length of her whisper, I revalue.  I strike the lumens.  In the margin I write the unit she always wanted: presence per grain.  I lift the shell, turn the columella curl to the last coal-light, and match the sun in my pocket against her strip of tin.  Rose.  She was told six years ago how little of her was left, and she stood, and that same week she made me a sun.  Four coppers is the price of what it does in a stairwell.  The density is unpriceable, which is a coward's answer, so I write a number: one Nell, entire, and the debt runs the wrong way.  The guild will call that fraud; I will call it calibration.  Then I whisper back, because a secret traded is a secret halved.  I never lit a room with it.  I only held it, every night, and holding is not spending.  Her breath stops.  Something in her face comes back a little, the way color returns to a coal you blow on.  The nexus darkens; the coal-cart bells go on toward the river.  Tomorrow I will come down the same road at the same angle, one chamber wider, and I will not bring the ledger.  That is how the shell grows, and that is how I intend to keep her.
