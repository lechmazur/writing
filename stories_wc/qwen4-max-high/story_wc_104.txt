The chain ran from Vera's ankle to the iron post of the prize counter, and in eleven years she had worn a pale groove into the floorboards with it.  Above her, the abandoned carnival's redemption center still held its shelves of unclaimed plush bears, their glass eyes gone cloudy with dust.  The Authority had chained her here where everyone could see the woman who gave seed away.  Beyond the ticket booths, the wall stood across the basin, eighty feet of poured gray, holding the dry country apart from the water.  At dusk an arrow came through the broken skylight and stuck quivering in the counter beside her hand.  Its fletching was strange: not feathers but strips of red piano felt and folded seed paper, the old courier's signature.  One vane of it was a sorghum packet, and someone had written across it in charcoal: the wall comes down at dawn.  Under the floor of this room lay the vault her teacher had built, a clandestine library of jars: millet, amaranth, mesquite, buffalo gourd, every one of them a thing that could live on almost nothing.  He had tuned pianos for the carnival and hidden the vault's combination where no guard would look, in the bench, among the keys he pulled from dead instruments.  The hatch lay under her own counter, close enough to touch.  The chain gave her nine feet of floor, and the bench sat at eleven.  So for two winters she had taught a scavenger boy named Tobin a counting game, tapping the boards, singing numbers, letting him think he was learning music.  Patiently, the way you plant a tree you will not sit under.  She had never sent him to the bench, because seed opened in a closed country is only a rumor, and rumors get burned.  She whistled, and Tobin came in through the turnstile, and behind him came Sarno with a rifle.  Sarno had impeded her for years, blocking the couriers, turning back the women with empty sacks, doing his job slowly and well.  He put his boot on the bench lid.  Vera took from behind the register the one jar she had never spent, and held it up so the label caught the last light.  Beans from his mother's field, the ones the Authority had burned out of every other storehouse.  She had waited eleven years to buy exactly this much with them: one boot lifted off a lid.  Sarno took the jar in both hands like a man taking communion, and stepped back.  Tobin lifted the bench lid and laid the ivory keys out in the row the tuner had left them, black and white, out of every proper order.  Count the whites between the blacks, she said.  He counted the way she had taught him, tapping, singing, and the numbers came: four, nine, two, six.  She turned the dial under the counter, and the floor exhaled cold air that smelled of paper and beans and old rain.  Then the charges went, and the wall came down.  It did not fall so much as kneel, section by section, and the dust rolled through the carnival doors and turned the morning the color of millet.  Through the gap came the people of the dry side, walking into the ruin with their sacks open.  Vera knelt at the hatch inside her nine feet of floor and passed the jars up, one after another, her chain dragging a slow bright arc through the dust.  Her hands moved as if over keys, wrist to wrist, and the chain kept time.  Sorghum, amaranth, mesquite, gourd; a woman weeping over a handful of gray seed as if it were water.  Tobin found a hacksaw in the ticket booth and came back with it raised.  Cut me and you lose the morning, she told him.  The flats are six miles out, and seed goes into dry soil best while the night dew is still on it.  She put the last crate in his arms and turned him toward the gap, and he went out running, and the sight of a boy carrying a country in a box nearly undid her.  Sarno stayed.  He set the rifle against a shelf of bears and sat down on the boards with his mother's beans in his lap, and when the paving trucks came he told them the floor was undermined, unsafe, a season's work to survey.  Vera let her hand rest on the arrow, on its felt and its paper, and thought of green rows eleven miles away, standing up out of the dust.  The chain held; she had never needed to walk through the wall.
