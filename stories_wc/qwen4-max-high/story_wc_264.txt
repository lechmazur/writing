After the last forest burns, the sand learns to glow.  It glows because nothing is left to look at it, and unlooked-at things spread.  Hush, says the rail, hush, says the rail.  Ilya guards the freight that carries the divergent archive, eleven cars of everything that almost happened.  The forest that did not burn is in car four, breathing.  His mother's lullaby, whole, all four verses, rides in car eleven.  Ilya remembers two.  Freight guards are made dreamless for this work.  A dream is a measurement; it picks one version and lets the others go dark.  So they took his dreaming, and he took the night shift, and the archive kept its thousands of maybes.  His instrument is a chess clock frozen at midnight, both flags up, neither side running.  Midnight is the hour the last forest caught.  While no one's time runs, no one's turn comes, and every branch stays possible.  The archive's entries are occasionally everywhere: smeared thin across the desert, in the car and in the dune and in his hands at once.  Never always everywhere.  Only occasionally, and only until someone looks.  The auditors are coming to look.  Certainty is cargo too, and it pays better than maybe.  They will read car four, and the unburned forest will become a fact or a nothing, and either way it will stop being able to grow.  So Ilya stops the freight where the dune is in bloom.  The dune opens light like small green flowers, one for every spark that has no one to tend it.  He has wanted this since the taking: to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners, the dim branches nobody bothers to hope for.  Nine years of nights he has listened to other people's almosts, and none of them ever got to be a morning.  He gardens through wave functions, the way his mother raised bread: by singing.  The old word for it is charm.  Charm is a flavor the physicists count, and also what a woman does to a loaf.  He sings the two verses he has, and leaves the gaps where the other two go, and the gaps are the door.  Hush now, hush, the sand says back to him, in his mother's voice, almost.  Amplitude gathers where he sings; the dim branches lean toward the broken song.  He sings until the unburned forest is louder than the fire, and he cannot know whether loud is loud enough.  But a charm can only make a maybe likely.  To make it true, someone must spend a look on it, and a look is a dream, and he has none left.  In car eleven there is also a version of Ilya who was never taken, who still dreams.  He has guarded that man for nine years and never opened the door.  The auditors' lamps come over the rise, three of them, steady as arithmetic.  He puts his hand on the chess clock.  One spark can be cultivated into a world, but only if the rest go dark.  He presses his flag.  Midnight lets go; the other side begins to tick; time, after nine years, takes its turn.  He opens car eleven and lies down in the unlit corner beside his dreaming self and the whole song.  He charms one last time, out loud, with the gaps in it, and then he does the forbidden thing.  He dreams.  He dreams the unburned forest, and dreaming is a look, and the look spends every other maybe to pay for this one.  Car four goes quiet.  Car nine, car ten, the thousands, all of them, hush.  The auditors find ten empty cars, a dune in bloom, and one green shoot standing in the glass, ordinary as a weed.  They write it down: one seedling, no archive, nothing to sell.  They do not write down the guard asleep in car eleven, smiling, his lips moving through a fourth verse.  Hush now, the leaves say, in his mother's voice, and this time not almost.  The spark is not occasionally everywhere anymore.  It is here, one place, one small unlit corner lit, and it will need water.  Sleep, says the freight, going nowhere now, sleep, sleep.
