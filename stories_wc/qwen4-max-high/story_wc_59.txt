The palace hung in the dark like a chandelier nobody had lit in three hundred years.  Its chains ran up into the throats of nine stalactites, each one thicker than a cathedral, and when the pumps below changed shift the whole floor trembled under Ivo's knees.  He had learned to love that trembling; it was the closest thing his world had to a shiver. 

He was Prince Ivo, though the title had gone threadbare along with his coat: ink at the cuffs, one boot resoled with wire, a beard he trimmed only when it interfered with the lens.  His mother the Regent kept the Engine indexed; Ivo kept hypothesizing.  He could not stop.  Why did the stalactites drip faster in winter, why did the pumps stutter at the third hour, why had no one alive ever heard a bird?  Nothing in the cavern was born: rain fell on a schedule, the brass gardens opened on cams, and even grief had a form to fill out.  Hypothesizing was his method and his illness: guess, test, guess again, and let the guesses keep him company. 

In the Reliquary of Defects, drawer nine, he had found the egg of a cavern lark, a species struck from the index two centuries dead.  The label said: PRODUCED IN ERROR, RETAINED AS EVIDENCE. 

Everyone knew the Genesis was flawed.  The Founders had cut their master cam by hand, and the hand had wobbled, so everything the Engine stamped came out a hair off true, and every copy of that thing a hair off again.  Drift, the regulators called it, and they culled whatever drifted farthest: the larks first, whose songs would not hold still long enough to be written down. 

Ivo's hypothesis, the dangerous one: the wobble was not the wound.  It was the only door through which anything new had ever entered the world.  He wanted beauty back, and he was ragged enough to say so out loud, to the pumps, to the chains. 

So he began to assemble a lark.  He stole bellows-brass for the lungs, a music-box comb for the throat, watch jewels for the eyes, and the egg itself, cold as a plumb bob, for the heart.  He had candled it in lamplight and found no shadow inside, no bird, only chalk and silence; he built around it anyway.  He hypothesized about every part.  If the comb were silver, would the note be colder; if he set the wings a degree asymmetric, would the flight look like choosing?  Forty nights, and the palace quaked its patient quake above him, and his hands shook so badly he had to brace his wrists on the bench to solder. 

Then he cut the bird's cam.  He could have filed it perfect.  Instead he copied the Founders' error, the same hair's wobble, and set the drift free inside a body the size of his fist.  If his hypothesis was wrong, he would build a broken toy and be flogged for theft.  If it was right, the regulators had spent two hundred years killing the world's only mouth. 

He wound it at the hour the pumps changed shift, when the floor trembled.  The lark opened its beak and gave him one note, then a second that no comb in the world had been cut to give.  It was thin.  It was wrong.  It wandered off the index and kept wandering, and Ivo wept, because the sound was bitterly sweet: the lark itself was gone forever, no craft would resurrect it, and still the dark had made a noise it had never made before. 

Here was the discovery, arriving through the floor and up his wrists: the flaw had never spoiled the genesis, it had been the genesis, the one part of the design that could still be surprised.  Beauty was not the opposite of the error.  Beauty was its offspring, and they had been culling the children. 

Ivo carried the singing thing to the rail, where the nine great chains went up into the stone, and he thought, hypotheses first, always: if he hung it there, the wobble would travel down the chains into the Engine's bed, and the Engine would begin, slowly, to make mistakes again.  If he was wrong, they would take his title, which was already threadbare. 

He hung it on the chain.  The palace quaked.  Somewhere below, a pump missed its beat, and the whole suspended world leaned into the gap, listening.  Ivo let his hands shake and did not brace them.  Let the whole flawed world shake with him; something in it was finally awake and did not know what it would say next.
