Nine weeks I hoed Odile's beds under grow-lights strung with frost, and nine weeks I lied about why.  She thought I was a runaway with cold hands; the town thought I was a runaway too.  On a cord under my shirt I kept a flint arrowhead from the riverbed, and in my notebook I kept numbers.  I was a cryptid researcher, and this was fieldwork. 

Everything had changed after the missing returned.  Eleven of them walked out of the November woods in February wearing their own faces slightly wrong, and my brother Tobin was the fourth.  He hummed a chord that never resolved, three notes leaning on each other, and spoons drifted off the table toward him like curious fish.  The returned were gravitationally playful; they made the world forget its weight for a moment, then remembered, and dropped things.  Only the arrowhead stayed honest.  Near Tobin it hung plumb and heavy on its cord, a small brown tooth of certainty, and I used it as my instrument.  The Institute paid by the specimen, and I had already mailed them Tobin's weight in grams.  I meant to find the thing that had taken him and name it, then kill it, then laugh.  That was the whole ambition: to turn nine weeks of bitterness into a cosmic joke with a punchline I could survive. 

Odile grew bitter things on purpose: wormwood, gentian, angelica, dandelion crowns fat as fists.  Bitterness is a medicine, she said, if you give it somewhere to go.  The garden was the herbalist's prescription, three rooms of borrowed root cellar under a dead grange, warmth from a compost heap, light from stolen bulbs.  Every night she gave the returned an old cordless phone.  The base was unplugged; the battery had swollen years ago; the handset should have been a brick of plastic.  It hissed anyway.  They lay in the wormwood and confessed into it, small ugly truths, and the static confessed back.  No root did it, no ritual; the change came via the static-laden confessions on that old cordless phone.  I watched Perrin metamorphose on a Tuesday, her freckles spreading into lichen, her voice splitting into that same untamed chord.  It was not a melody anyone could conduct.  It was a harmony untamed, wild and unresolved, eleven throats refusing to land on one note and somehow not fighting.  I wrote: subjects lose mass, gain consonance.  I wrote: vector unknown.  I did not write: I am so angry I cannot sleep. 

Odile put the handset in my hand on the ninth week's last night and went to turn the compost.  She knew.  She had known since the first day, when I flinched at Tobin's humming instead of weeping.  The static waited like a held breath.  I said the bitter thing, the true one: I wanted him to stay missing.  I said, a missing man is a door, and a returned man is a wall, and I have been leaning on the wall for nine weeks wanting my door back.  The static laughed.  Not cruelly; it laughed the way Tobin used to at his own bad jokes, and then it said, in my voice, you were missing too.  You just never left town. 

The arrowhead lifted off my chest and began to orbit my wrist, slow and delighted, a stone learning to be a moon.  My plumb line had been waiting nine weeks for permission.  There was the joke: I had come to measure monsters with the one thing that wanted to fly.  I laughed until the frost on the bulbs cracked, and something in my chest went soft and green and rearranged.  I did not become lichen, not yet.  I became a woman who could hold two notes at once. 

Tobin came down at dawn and put his cold hand on my cold neck and hummed, and I found the third note, the one that leans.  We still do not resolve.  That is the point, Odile says; a chord that resolves is finished, and we are not finished.  My last report to the Institute was two lines, and the joke is kind.  Species: us.  Habitat: a cellar, in winter, under a frost that will not lift until April.  I hoe the gentian with hands that ache from cold and are somehow lighter than they were, and I feed the bitterness to the roots, and the roots give it back as medicine.  Upstairs the grange is empty; out in the woods something is still missing; the arrowhead rides my wrist like a small patient planet.  Hope here is frostbitten: black at the edges, alive at the center, humming.
