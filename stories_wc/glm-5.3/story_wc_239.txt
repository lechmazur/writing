The universe ended on a Tuesday, briefly, the way a held breath ends. [13] It hiccuped. [15] In the pause between that ending and whatever came next, the ancient temple library stood exactly where it had always been lost: nowhere in particular, deep in everywhen. [43] Ilvane, the last witness of history, kept her post among its spiral stacks. [56] She had watched the final page of everything turn, and she had signed it, in the margin, because witnessing means lending your name to what actually happened. [83] But endings, she suspected, were cosmic illusions, and she had spent the hiccup unraveling one. [98]

Two claimants came to her at the heart of the library, where the shelves coiled like a frozen galaxy. [117] One called itself Conclusion, and it argued that the universe was finished, sealed, complete. [131] The other called itself Continuation, and it argued that the hiccup proved nothing had truly stopped, only stumbled. [149] Between them the silence was so vast it echoed, a resounding hush, the sound a temple makes when it is listening. [170] "You will arbitrate," they said together, in a voice like dust settling on dust. [184]

Ilvane was wanderingly exact, which is the only kind of exactness that survives an apocalypse. [199] Her mind moved in loops and yet arrived, every time, at the precise truth, the way water wanders and still finds the sea. [222] So she chose the old form of spiral arbitration: each claim would wind inward, circle by circle, until it either reached bedrock or collapsed into opinion. [248] She opened the only book she trusted, a softcover dictionary, its spine cracked from ten thousand consultations, its corners soft as cloth. [270] All the grand tomes had burned or lied at the end; only the dictionary kept its promises, word by word. [290] "We proceed," she said. [294]

Conclusion spoke first, and its argument spiraled inward: every cause had found its last effect, every question its terminal answer, and a finished thing does not hiccup; it rests. [323] Ilvane circled it with questions, each one closer to the center, until at the innermost ring she asked, "Who signed the ending finished?" [346] Silence. [347] Conclusion had no answer, and an argument without bedrock ends where it stands. [360]

Continuation spoke next: the hiccup was breath, history was merely resting between chapters, and the library itself was proof, since a lost library keeps being found. [386] Ilvane wound inward again, question after question, and at the center asked, "Resting between which chapters, when you claim the book has none left?" [410] The hush deepened, resounding like a bell of nothing. [419] Both spirals had collapsed, and two truths lay in the dust, still breathing. [432]

This was the illusion she had come to unravel: that ending and continuing were enemies at all, that history was a line with a wall at the end of it. [462] Lines were the lie. [466] She showed them the dictionary and read the entry for "end": the point where something ceases, and also its purpose, its aim. [488] "One word," she said, "carrying both." [494] Conclusion and Continuation leaned over the soft pages together, and the page, being soft, bent toward both of them equally. [514] "To unify you," Ilvane said, "I only have to define you correctly." [526] So she wrote in the margin, beneath her old signature: the end of history is its purpose continuing. [544] The two claimants flickered, wavered, and folded into each other like two meanings of one word. [560] Where they had stood, the air was simply air again. [570]

The hiccup, which had held its breath for the whole arbitration, let go. [583] Time resumed, but not as a line; it resumed as a spiral, each turn containing every turn before. [601]

Ilvane tucked the dictionary under her arm and began to walk. [612] The library's only exit stood at its center, and its center stood, somehow, at the world's edge; she went journeying inward while walking out, each aisle she descended leading deeper into memory, each step toward the door a step further into herself. [654] She passed shelves of histories she had witnessed and histories she had been; she passed the shelf where her own name would one day be filed, and nodded to it politely. [685] At the last turning of the spiral she looked back once. [696] The temple library was already lost again, folded back into everywhen, patient as a definition waiting to be needed. [715] She stepped through the doorway into a universe still slightly stunned from its stumble, and behind her the hush followed her out, resounding, the way meaning follows a word. [744]

Somewhere ahead, history was beginning again, and this time it knew itself, and this time she would witness not the end of it but the endlessness, which is a different word, and she had the dictionary to prove it. [783]
