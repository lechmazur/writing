Ridge House perched above the clouds, a hospice on a granite shoulder where mornings arrived as slow gold floods over the cloud sea below. [24] Its halls were quiet but never without rhythm; monitors kept small patient time, and breath moved through the rooms like tide. [45] The nurses called it the radiant haven; Mara, the music therapist, called it stillness with a pulse. [62]

"What do you still want?" she asked, decisively kind, never wasting a dying man's minutes on pity. [127] Alden reached into his nightstand drawer and laid a shard of broken vinyl on the blanket, black as a beetle's wing, one grooved edge catching the light. [154] "My brother kept the rest," he said. [161]

[189] She had hymned the mundane all her life, and the record had caught it. [203] Then came the fight over selling the family house; Alden smashed the disk on the kitchen floor, Sam gathered the pieces and drove away, and this one shard had slid beneath the stove, where Alden found it later and kept it like a splinter of his own rib. Mara's own method had always been hymning the mundane; she sang to water pitchers and folded towels until patients remembered that ordinary things deserved music too. [277] She hummed the dishwashing hymn's first line, which Alden had taught her months ago without knowing he was teaching, and he wept at how small and complete it sounded. [306]

"Then you want a flawless goodbye," she said. [314] "We won't improvise one; we'll finish an old song." Sam had not lost the middle. [503] His voice cracked on the second verse, and Mara's rose beneath it, low and steady, the pulse under the stillness.
