The canyon of endless echoes kept everything it was given. [10] Every word spoken among the towers of bone stayed, layering over older words until the air hummed with a thousand years of voices. [33] The people who lived in the shadow of those ribs learned to speak softly, and to weigh each word before releasing it forever. [56] Of them all, none was so quietly articulate as Maren, the copper-veined storyteller. [69] The mines had seeded copper into her family's blood, and in her it surfaced: fine green-gold lines mapped her wrists and throat, and because copper carries vibration she felt the canyon's echoes in her body as others felt rain. [108]

Her gift was texture, for the old tongue was a language of surfaces, holding a thousand words for how things felt: the grit of a wet stone, the nap of woven wool, the particular smoothness of a grandmother's knuckle. [147] Long before she had a word for it, what she wanted was to translate texture into sound, so that a feeling could be kept the way a name is kept. [177] But the mines brought the trade tongue with them, and children raised on its plain merchant words never learned the old ones, so the language began to die by inches, one unremembered texture at a time. [213] When her grandmother died in the spring, Maren became its last living speaker. [226]

She inherited two things: a shawl that still held the shape of the shoulders it had warmed, and the flawed diamond the miners called cursed. [251] They had pried it from the copper seam a hundred years before, crazed at the heart with inclusions, and every owner had gone quiet within a year, enough for rumor to harden into fact. [285] Only her grandmother had kept it anyway, wrapped in wool, waiting. [296] Alone in her first winter, Maren learned its truth: pressed against any surface, the flaw turned that surface's grain into tone, so that wet stone and dry bone, wool and knuckle, each sang its own held note. [333] The owners had not been silenced by any curse; the diamond had simply taught them to hear, and once a person hears texture as tone, ordinary talk feels like theft. [363] But tones released into the canyon scattered, tangled with the hoard of older sounds, and dissolved. [379]

It was the starlings that showed her the way. [388] They nested by the thousand in the hollows of the towers, and when they rose together the flock folded and opened like a hand, each turn carving the air and bending echoes the way a lens bends light. [426] Certain patterns gathered stray sound into standing knots; certain patterns unwove them. [438] Over three winters, with scattered seed and near-silent whistles pitched below anyone's notice, she taught herself to read the flock, and then to lead it, until she could harness the canyon's echoes through the patterns the birds made in flight, folding one chosen sound at a time into braids that held. [489]

Only one place was deep enough to keep such braids safe: the tunnel of echoes that ran through the greatest tower, a throat of bone the founders feared so much they sealed its mouth with river stones. [526] Sounds that entered it never came out; they only circled, and grew more purely themselves. [541]

In the spring of the fourth year, when the last child who could even greet her in the old tongue left for the mining towns, Maren opened the tunnel stone by stone. [573] She carried the diamond through the dark on a cord, and behind her the starlings poured in, ten thousand wings turning the sealed air into a slow instrument. [601] All that night she walked the tunnel's length, pressing the diamond to surface after surface: the granular bone of the walls, the wool of the shawl, a clay cup her grandmother's thumbs had worn smooth, and for each note the flock bent itself into the holding pattern, so that one by one the textures of her people entered the bone and stayed. [663] Near dawn she pressed the diamond to her own copper-veined wrist, and the flock made one long turning, and the last word of the old tongue, spoken so quietly that only bone could hear it, went in after all the rest. [704]

Across those years the language finished dying, in the way scholars count such things, though the tunnel still carried it, circulating. [725] She left the diamond where the tunnel curved deepest, and walked out into an ordinary morning. [741] Travelers say the towers sing at dusk now, and that the diamond sleeping inside is cursed, for no one who listens can say what they heard. [767] They are right to fear it and wrong about why: the diamond takes nothing away, it keeps, and it is patient. [788]
