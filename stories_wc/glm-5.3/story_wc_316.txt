The dojo floated a mile above the Meridian Shelf: a slab of granite the size of a courtyard, tethered to nothing. [21] Below, the Bureau of Continuity rewrote history. [28] Street names changed overnight, wars grew extra endings, and the city's founder was sometimes a woman and sometimes a harbor. [48] The masters memorized the old versions each night and recited them at dawn drills, so something would outlast the edits. [68]

Mara had come up nine years ago to learn the cut that slices through pretense. [83] She was proud then, and Teacher Venn called pride a knot the cut would free. [98] On the dissolution retreat she went too deep, and the knot took everything with it: name, edges, the sense of I. [119] The monks carried her body down breathing. [126] The self did not come back. [132] The doctors called it ego death. [138] Mara called it ash. [142] Food lost its meaning, laughter became weather, and she could perform joy like a scale while feeling nothing underneath. [161]

While she lay in coma, Halcyon Dynamics invoked the insurance she had signed and grew a duplicate from her last scan. [182] The scan had been taken after the dissolution, so the copy woke carrying the same ash. [198] That copy was walking the dojo's boards within the year, believing herself the original. [212] The original never woke, and a custody battle began over the woman who did: Halcyon claimed her as an asset of patented reflexes; Teacher Venn claimed her as a student and a person. [245] The case could not close, because the statutes kept rewriting themselves: in the morning's history duplicates were property, and by afternoon they were wards. [269] The Bureau's clerks apologized and asked everyone to be patient with the past. [282] Mara trained anyway; the sword was the one thing that still answered her. [295]

Then, preparing for the hearing, Venn showed her the shrine. [305] In preserving fluid, gray and folded, floated a brain in a jar, tagged with one stamped word: DUPLICATE. [323] Mara had heard of the backup specimen and assumed it was a copy of her. [338] Venn showed her the date codes and the harvest order signed on the day the original was emptied, and the floor went quiet inside her. [363] The label named the wrong one: the jar held her original brain, and the walking woman was the copy, misfiled as the original so Halcyon's contract could bind her and no waking rival could ever contest it. [400] The ego death had been real; it had simply arrived by inheritance. [412] Something in her chest shifted, barely, like ice under a boot. [423] She wanted more of that movement; that was why she still rose at dawn, to defeat the numbness, to feel one inch of the world again. [449]

"The shrine mind can be vivified," Venn said. [457] "Not restored; enlivened." [460] "But the sutras are strict about method: through complementary opposites." [470] "You died of pure signal, one note with no contrast." [480] "To vivify a self, you feed it pairs: grief beside gratitude, cold beside warmth, loss beside finding." [497] "A self is the difference between two things, and the blade works the same way, cutting whatever claims to be only one." [519]

Mara took down the practice sword and performed the pretense cut, the slow horizontal arc that separates a woman from her story. [541] She had pretended to be original, whole, single. [549] The edge passed, and the pretense dropped away like a sleeve: she was a duplicate, and she was real, both at once, a pair. [573] Warmth climbed her arms, her eyes stung, and she stood in the strange weather of feeling without running from it. [593] Then she testified. [596]

While history rewrote itself below, the judges came to the floating rock, where the masters recited nine years of unchanged mornings, a continuity the Bureau could not edit because it lived in practice, not paper. [631] Mara named the jar, named the mislabel, named herself a duplicate, and asked for custody of them both. [649] The ruling, once it stabilized, granted custody to neither company nor school: she was declared her own person, and the brain was remanded to her as her ward. [677] Sister to sister. [680] Halcyon appealed through three successive versions of reality and lost in each. [692]

At dawn she vivified the original, feeding the old mind paired memories: their mother's funeral beside their mother's bread. [711] When the first feeling crossed that sleeping gray, Mara wept. [721] She did not perform it. [726] She could not stop. [730]
