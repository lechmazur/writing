Mother built bunkers down, and Wren's lungs hated her for it. [11] Every family in Hollow Ridge paid for steel doors and canned air, because twice a year, for one hour, gravity inverted and everything not nailed down fell up. [39] The neighbors called the sky a thief; Mother called it a client. [51] Wren, claustrophobic since the crib, called it the only door she could breathe through. [65] Underground, her chest contracted like a fist; under open sky, it stretched. [77]

While other children hoarded marbles, Wren collected silences. [85] She wanted the flavors of them all: the iron taste of a bunker at midnight, the cool mineral hush of a snowed-in morning, the papery quiet of an empty church. [115] For this she needed a vessel, and she had the perfect one: a tiny porcelain doll, cracked at the shoulder, that had tumbled out of a bunker wall while Mother poured the concrete. [148] Her painted head was hollow; tap it, and it answered with a small ceramic echo. [163]

Experiment taught her the doll's one rule: a silence could be caught only by limiting exposure. [179] So the doll lived wrapped in waxed cloth inside a matchbox, starved of sound for days, until the moment it was unsealed somewhere quiet and drank like a thing dying of thirst. [211] One silence at a time; each faded within days, so Wren memorized every flavor and let them go. [229]

But all her flavors were lowland flavors, and the old stories told of one that lived above the clouds. [248] They named it the gilded hush, and said it pooled on the xenial trellis, a vast lattice of cable and living vine woven generations ago to host whatever the inverted hour sent skyward. [281] Xenial, the stories explained, because it received strangers like guests. [291] Nobody in Hollow Ridge admitted to believing in it, because a kind sky was bad for the bunker business. [310]

On the morning of the autumn inversion, Mother welded the last hinge on the Combs family's new cellar and ordered Wren below with the sandwiches. [335] Wren went down three steps, waited for the torch to swing away, and came back up holding her breath. [354] The matchbox had been sealed for nine days. [362] When the hour struck and the world's weight forgot which way to fall, she let go of the fence post and let the sky take her. [388]

Rising felt like the first stretch after a long cramp: fields shrinking, hawks below her now, cold going silver at the edges. [410] She punched through the cloud deck into gold. [418] The trellis was real. [422] It ribbed the sky like a ruined cathedral, cables and vines knotted into platforms, hammocks, and empty chairs facing the sun, everything gilded and growing. [447] Vines reached out as the stories promised, wound around her ankles, and set her down on a platform as gently as a host settling a guest. [473] Then she understood what the stories had failed to say: during the inversion, above the clouds, sound itself fell upward and away, and what remained pooled everywhere was the gilded hush. [504] It was sunlight poured into the absence of noise, and Wren had never tasted anything like it: honey, cold metal, the first page of a new book. [531]

Her hands shook as she unwrapped the matchbox, nine days of stored quiet breaking open at once. [548] The doll drank until her painted hair seemed warm. [557] The hush became intangibly tangible inside the porcelain: nothing Wren could grip, yet the doll grew heavier in her palm, warm as a held bird. [582] Then, from the crack in her shoulder, a sprout pushed up, pale green, unfurling two leaves the size of eyelashes. [602] Every silence Wren had ever caught had faded; this one had roots. [614]

Behind her, a vine lowered another guest onto the platform: Mother, welding mask pushed up, rope-burned, furious, crying. [632] She had seen Wren slip the fence and jumped after her, twenty years of professional suspicion of the sky abandoned in one step. [655] She opened her mouth to shout, and the hush lifted the shout away like a leaf. [671] For the first time in her life she had nothing to seal, nothing to survive, nothing to build against. [690] Wren pressed the doll into her mother's concrete-roughed hands and watched the tight place between her eyebrows stretch open. [709] The sprout leaned toward the sun. [715]

When the hour ended and gravity remembered itself, the vines lowered them like guests being seen out, and the fall home was slow as an apology. [741] All winter, Mother sketched structures that opened instead of closing. [751] By spring she was pouring her last bunker, and this one had a skylight. [765] The doll sat on the kitchen sill, the sprout grown into a seedling, flavoring the whole house with the one silence that never faded. [789]
