Vess had ridden guard on the night freight for thirty years, and in all that time she had never dreamed. [20] Hush, said the rails under her boots. [27] Hush and haul. [30] The last forest had burned eleven winters ago, and the world after it was a gray song sung slowly. [49] What survived of the woods survived as probability, folded into the Divergent Archive: every tree that might have grown, every spring that might have sung, each one a ghost of branching light. [81] The archive was occasionally everywhere. [87] It flickered at the corner of the eye: green shimmer over ash flats, rain-smell inside a dry boxcar, gone the moment you turned to look. [112] Most people called it haunting. [117] Vess called it inventory. [121] Dreamers who hauled such freight began to fork, living three lives at once and none of them well, so the runs went to her. [145]

Her father had been the archive's last librarian, and he had played chess with her between the stacks while the fires came north. [168] Every seed is a cloud of might-be trees, he told her; the archive keeps the cloud, and only the soil can choose. [190] At midnight, when the sealing crews locked the vault, his heart stopped, and the chess clock on the table stopped with it, both faces frozen at twelve. [217] "It's still your move," he used to tell her, "little guard." [228] She had carried the clock ever since, wrapped in freight cloth, keeping his move. [242]

Now she rode the night freight with a crate of seeds bolted to the floor and a purpose stitched under her ribs: to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners. [272] Burned basements. [274] Flooded mines. [276] The dark undersides of bridges where even the moon forgot to look. [288] Wherever the world had gone unlit, she planted. [296] But the seeds were running out, and the archive, they said, would not open for locks or crowbars or prayer. [316]

Then came the rumor, carried by a switchman with dust in his teeth: a glowing dune in bloom, out past the ash line, flowers shining where nothing had shone since the fire. [348] One seed, he swore, had collapsed out of the archive and back into the world. [363] Vess uncoupled from her run at the next siding and walked. [374] Three nights she walked, dreamless under dreamless stars, the frozen clock a small cold weight against her spine. [392]

On the fourth night the dune rose to meet her, burning soft blue and gold, a hill of lanterns swaying on no wind at all. [417] At its crown stood a shimmer, man-shaped, occasionally everywhere and now, kindly, here. [430] The archive wore her father's shoulders. [436] "Little guard," it said, in a voice like pages turning. [446] "You did not come all this way for a flower." [456] "No locks," it told her, "and no crowbars." [464] The vault was sealed with wave functions, ten thousand forests held in soft suspension, each one undecided, each one waiting to be asked. [487] Asked, not forced. [490] You cannot break a probability, her father had said; you can only charm it. [504]

Vess knelt in the blooming sand and unwrapped the clock. [514] Both faces still said midnight. [519] She set it between herself and the shimmer, and she began the lullaby he had sung over the seed trays, the one with the broken refrain, half a rhyme and half a hush. [552] "Sleep, sleep, you might-be forest; sleep, sleep, you barely-there." [561] The song was a charm worked through wave functions, a coaxing, a mother-tone laid over ten thousand branching futures, asking each one to fall, gently, like a tired child, into this single bed of world. [596] The dune brightened as she sang. [602] The flowers turned toward her voice like small moons finding a planet. [614] Somewhere beneath the sand, she felt the great held breath of the archive, ten thousand might-be forests leaning toward the song. [635] Her voice cracked on the refrain. [641] It cracked and she kept singing, because a fractured lullaby is still a lullaby, and a broken world is still a world, and it was still, as her father liked to say, her move. [675]

She reached out and pressed the clock's plunger. [683] Click. [684] The frozen midnight let go. [689] One thin hand began to tick. [695] And the archive, charmed, opened like a seed. [703] All across the gray country, in ash flats and flooded mines and the dark undersides of bridges, seeds of light pushed up through the unlit corners, small and stubborn, occasionally everywhere, blooming. [735]

Vess walked home slowly, carrying an empty crate and a ticking clock. [747] That night, for the first time in thirty years, she slept soft and deep, and the world, which had needed her dreamless so long, kept the watch for her, and dreamed. [778] Hush now. [780] Hush, little forest. [783] It is your move. [787]
