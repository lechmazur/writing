Nobody had ever seen the Logistician, but everyone in the flooded districts had eaten her biscuits. [16] She moved through disasters the way weather did: announced by rumor, verified by results. [30] When the levee broke at Marrow Creek, someone whispered she'd already landed, and by dusk every roof wore a tarp. [50] Nobody knew her name, which was how she liked it, because names were favors, and favors were debts, and debts were chains. [72] Her whole life ran on one law: reciprocity, exact and immediate. [83] Take a cup of tea, owe a cup of tea, repay a cup of tea, and keep moving. [101] She accepted nothing she couldn't return by Tuesday. [109] This made her efficient, elusive, and profoundly alone. [117]

She told herself the cure for loneliness was momentum, and she had been administering it for years. [134] In her coat pocket she carried the one debt she could never settle: a fragment of moon rock, thumb-sized and grey, given to her by an astronaut whose evacuation she'd orchestrated out of a collapsed launch town. [171] "You can't repay the moon," the astronaut had said, pressing it into her hand, "so consider it a binding." [190] She'd laughed it off (she laughs at everything, that's her tell) and left before the sentence could settle. [208] For six years the rock rode in her pocket, the only weight she permitted herself. [223]

Here is how the story changes. [229] It changes at a disused railway station on the edge of the flood plain, an ivy-strangled platform where the vines have swallowed the timetable and the clock reads some tenacious, obsolete noon. [261] Relief workers used the station as a rumor exchange, the only postbox the Logistician trusted, since rumor cannot be audited. [281] You whispered what you needed into the ivy, and it traveled, hand to hand, mouth to ear, improbably and faithfully, and supplies appeared. [304] The chain was absurd: a ferryman told a baker, the baker told a beekeeper, the beekeeper told nobody but wrote it in pollen. [327] It worked because rumors, unlike officials, are impossible to intercept. [337] A single whisper could catalyze a hundred rescues; she had built her whole career on that chemistry. [354]

That autumn a cyclone scattered the chain, and the whispers had nowhere to land, so the Logistician came in person, which she never did. [378] She stood on the platform in a waxed jacket, joking desperately at the dark. [392] "Trains still run here," she told the ivy, "they're just very, very behind." [405] She was tallying what she owed the region (4,000 blankets, 11 generators, one apology) when the whisper turned around. [424] Here's the thing about rumor chains: they carry traffic both ways. [435] Someone had seen her on the night ferry, and the whisper had traveled the same improbable network backwards. [453] The message was: she is coming, and we owe her. [463] And they came to repay her. [469]

The ferryman arrived first, with a thermos. [476] Then the baker with bread, the beekeeper with a jar of honey labeled "for the moon lady," a nurse with a crate of penicillin, the astronaut herself, gray now, carried up the ivy steps in a chair. [513] They queued on the platform, politely and anarchically, a line nobody had organized and everybody had joined, obeying no regulation and every courtesy. [536] "Don't," said the Logistician, backing away, hands up, still grinning. [546] "Stop this immediately, it's an ambush, it's communism, it's... is that shortbread?" [558] The ferryman poured tea. [562] She hadn't accepted a cup of tea in eleven years. [572] You take a cup of tea, you owe a cup of tea, and then someone adds shortbread, and the debt compounds, and before long you are chained to a place, to people, to a platform at tenacious noon forever. [611]

She ran the math out loud, joking all the while ("do you know what tea does to a woman in my position"), and the astronaut rose from her chair and took the moon rock from the Logistician's pocket. [649] She pressed it into the hand of the ferryman. [658] "There," she said. [661] "Now you owe her, and she owes you, and the chain is closed." [674] The moon rock would pass from hand to hand along the rumor network for years, never belonging to anyone, binding everyone, a debt that circulated like currency. [701]

The Logistician drank the tea. [706] It was too sweet. [710] She stayed through the flood season, coordinating from the ticket booth, joking at a rate that finally slowed, then steadied, then turned into something almost like ease. [737] The cure had not arrived in a crate, auditable and exact. [748] It was an unauditable debt, an ambush of shortbread, a chain of people politely, catastrophically bound to one another. [767] The trains never ran again. [772] But every Tuesday, the ivy whispered. [778]
