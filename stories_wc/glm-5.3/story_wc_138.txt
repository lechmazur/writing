The solar storm arrived at dusk, and the prismatic escondite answered it with light. [14] Vesper had hidden in that crystal canyon for four hundred years and had never seen the sky bleed green like this. [35] Auroras crawled where auroras had no right to be, and her only companion, a compass, spun in slow, drunken circles. [55] She was bored, the way only an immortal can be bored, and boredom had made her attentive. [72] So she noticed, long before she should have, that space was plotting. [84] Nine thousand years of living had worn her thin at the edges until she was dimensionally aware, the way old cloth grows translucent. [107] She could see the seams between directions, and tonight those seams were whispering. [120]

The dimensions had chafed under her curse for a very long time. [132] The amulet that granted her unending life had shattered centuries ago, but one fragment remained, and that fragment was an anchor. [153] While it existed, every dimension was obligated to hold her, to keep her thread unbroken, to refuse her the courtesy of an ending. [176] Space, it turned out, resented carrying anyone forever. [184] And now the solar storm had come, strangling the world's magnetic field, and the fragment's grip, which ran through that field like a root, was loosening. [210] The dimensions had chosen their moment, and their intrigue ran through magnetic fields; she could read it in the compass needle, in the humming of the crystal, in the charged smell of the air. [244] The whispers said mutiny. [248]

Vesper sat very still, the fragment warm against her sternum, and considered what a mutiny of space might actually mean. [268] It meant distance refusing orders. [273] It meant up and down conspiring, rooms folding their walls like wings, the road home deciding to be longer than grief. [294] It meant that the rules which had imprisoned her might, for one storm's length, be breakable. [310] She had said goodbye to emperors and midwives, to languages, to her own children's children's children, and she had never once learned what happened after. [335] That was her last hunger, the one appetite nine thousand years had failed to starve: to discover what happens after goodbye. [356] The mutiny could teach her. [361]

So she began her own intrigue, conducted the only way that worked, through magnetic fields. [376] She wound copper wire from a dead explorer's radio around the fragment and let the storm's currents speak for her, spelling her offer in pulses of induced voltage. [404] The offer was simple: give me my ending, and I will give you the anchor. [419] The dimensions did not trust her; she felt their suspicion as pressure behind her eyes, like weather in a sealed room. [440] They had been promised endings before, by other cursed things, and lied to by every one. [456] But the storm was peaking, and mutinies do not wait. [466] When the aurora came down into the canyon itself and the crystals split it into seven colors of ghost, the answer arrived: now, or never. [491] Vesper stood, an old woman in a young woman's body, and unclasped the chain. [505] The fragment fought her. [509] It had been a curse for so long that cursing was the only shape it remembered, and it burned her fingers with every year it had ever lent her. [538] She held it up into the auroral light anyway. [547] The storm's magnetic fingers closed around it, induction heating the metal white, while the dimensions pulled from their side, each taking a color, prying the fragment apart along the seams of the world. [580] It came apart like a held breath. [587]

Then the mutiny reached her. [592] Space stopped holding her the way a mother stops holding a child who has grown. [607] The canyon opened like a door it had always secretly been. [618] Her body, which had been a fortress, was suddenly a farewell. [629] She felt the goodbye coming the way you feel thunder before you hear it, hushed and enormous, rolling up through the floor of everything. [653] She did not fight it. [658] Nine thousand years of boredom had sharpened into one clean point of curiosity, and she leaned into the ending, attentive to the last. [681]

What happens after goodbye? [685] The dimensions folded her out through a seam of green light, and the prismatic escondite kept its secret. [703] By morning the storm was over, and the compass lay still, pointing at the place where she had been. [722] But beyond the seams, in the hush after thunder, Vesper learned her answer, and it was kind: after goodbye, the world keeps your shape for a while, the way a bell keeps sound. [755] Somewhere, faint and lasting, a bell made of her kept ringing. [766]
