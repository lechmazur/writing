The fever broke at dawn, and by noon Elena was climbing the ridge with her dead brother's coat folded under her arm. [22] For three weeks the sickness had cooked her dreams down to one image: a slip of paper going past her teeth. [43] She had swallowed that slip, and now she needed to know what it weighed. [57]

The mountain weather station clung to the summit like a barnacle, anemometers ticking in the thin bright air. [75] Inside sat the oracle: brass drums, a paper tape, built sixty years ago to answer questions without pretending to be perfect. [96] Its engineers called it fault-tolerant, which the valley translated as humble. [107] It never said yes or no. [113] It printed probabilities, each with an error bar that swayed like a stem in wind, so the valley called its answers swaying certainties. [136] The oracle did not speak from the middle of anything. [146] It occupied margins, printing its verdicts along the edges of logbooks, maps, and slips left near it, because it perceived only peripheries: the rim of a gauge, the corner of an eye, the last line of a page. [184]

Elena found the old logbook where she knew it would be. [195] Anselm, the keeper who had trained Tomas, had filled the margins of every page with botanical illustrations, saxifrage, gentian, mountain avens, drawn in graphite so fine they seemed to breathe. [225] He said the oracle liked company out there. [233]

Two months ago, on the page dated before the storm, he had drawn a stem of avens with its petals open. [254] Beside it, tucked into the margin, lay the slip it had printed when she and Tomas asked about the high pass: it would close, seventy-nine percent, give or take. [283] Tomas had been watching her face, not the page. [292] She tore the slip free and swallowed it whole, believing that a truth no one else read might forget how to be true. [315] Tomas took the pass in fair weather that afternoon. [324] The storm arrived early, outside the error bar, the kind of fault the machine was built to forgive and she was not. [346] Searchers found his coat at the lip of the pass; they never found him; the fever found her while she was still calling his name into the snow. [374]

Now, with the fever broken and the sickness gone, she laid the coat across the oracle's desk and asked her question aloud. [396] "What is the weight of a swallowed truth?" [404]

The drums turned. [407] The tape crawled out blank down its center; the machine waited, peripherally aware of her the way it was aware of all things, at the edge of her patience, at the border of her breath. [442] Then she understood and leaned close to the logbook's margin, close to the open-petaled avens. [457]

Letters appeared beside the stem, small as seeds: THE PAPER WEIGHED TWO GRAMS. [470] She waited for more. [474] The oracle gave it, unhurried: EVERYTHING ELSE YOU CARRY, YOU ADDED YOURSELF. [486]

Elena pressed her thumb against the illustration, not meaning to, and the graphite smudged, petals blurring into gray weather. [505] She almost apologized to Anselm's ghost. [511] Then she saw what the smudge had done. [519] The drawn flower, blurred at its edges, looked suddenly alive, as if wind had finally reached it, as if every certainty in the book had begun to sway. [547] The margin filled again: A TRUTH THAT SWAYS CAN STILL BE HELD. [559] A truth nailed down, she thought, is only a pressed flower. [570]

She asked her second question, the one underneath: had her swallowing changed anything, had the number been certain enough to save him if he had read it? [597] The drums turned a long time. [603] The answer came in the smallest letters yet: SEVENTY-NINE IS NOT ONE HUNDRED, AND TWENTY-ONE IS NOT ZERO, AND I CANNOT TELL YOU WHICH FAULT WAS YOURS. [630] That was mercy, the kind with teeth. [637]

Outside, the sky had gone green at its edges. [646] The aurora rose the way it always did above the station, in ribbons of cold light, beautiful the way a confession is beautiful, sorrow made visible and finally harmless. [675] Elena carried the coat to the window and let the light cross it. [688] Two grams. [690] She said it aloud, weighing it, and found that naming a weight is how you finally set it down. [709]

Before leaving, she took Anselm's pencil and, in the margin of the storm page, beside the smudged avens, drew Tomas's coat. [730] She drew the collar turned up against weather, the fabric slightly blurred, so that whoever found the page would see a coat that had begun, gently, to move. [758] The oracle, watching from the edge of everything, said nothing, which was its way of saying yes. [775] Down in the dark, the valley's small lights were swaying too, or her eyes were, and she let them. [794]
