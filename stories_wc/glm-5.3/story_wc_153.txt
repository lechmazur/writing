Maren kept the shard because it was the only part of her sister's death that still cut her. [18] It had snapped from the chapel gargoyle's rainspout the night Ilse vanished into the mangrove labyrinth, and it had opened Maren's palm when she reached for her sister's sleeve and caught only stone. [51] The wound healed at moonrise and split again each dawn, as if the day itself refused to let it close. [71] She was the town's medium, and the town called her cryptic because she never explained, only delivered. [88] The dead wrote to her in condensation on windows; she carried their words on her breath to whoever grieved. [107] A mother would wake to find her drowned son's handwriting blooming in the fog on the kitchen glass, and Maren would be gone before the crying started. [134] Everyone received their messages except Maren. [140]

Nine years of letters sat unsent in her sea chest, one for every year since the quarrel, each beginning the same way: "I did not mean it, Ilse." [168] She had called her sister a coward for staying, and Ilse had gone out at high tide to prove otherwise, and the mangroves had closed behind her like a mouth. [198] The spirits there sang, the fisherfolk said, but their song had a hole in it. [213] Maren heard it every night through her shutters: a hundred voices braided together, and one voice always stopping short, waiting for a phrase no one would give it. [241]

What she wanted was not forgiveness, not yet. [249] She wanted the moment before deciding: the instant before she opened her mouth, when she had still been the sister who loved Ilse and not the one who broke her. [279] If she could find that moment, hold it, understand what had lived in her before the cruelty chose her, then perhaps the apology waiting nine years in her throat could finally move. [311]

On the night the moon came up full, she took the shard and the newest unsent letter and walked into the labyrinth. [333] Roots knotted overhead into black arches, mud pulled at her boots, and the singing spirits parted their voices like reeds to let her through. [357] They knew her. [360] They had been waiting, as everything in that drowned forest waited, inside the space where an apology waits unspoken, the held breath between injury and repair. [386] At the labyrinth's heart, where the tide pooled silver, Maren knelt and held the shard up to the moon until its scarred face gleamed. [410] Then she began to evoke her sister, not the way other mediums called with candles and blood, but with the cleaner craft that was hers: she breathed onto the cold stone. [441] Her breath fogged the shard's flat face, a window no bigger than a palm, and in the condensation, letter by letter, a hand began to write. [467] It was Ilse's hand, round and hurried, the same hand that had labeled jam jars and signed school permissions. [486] "I kept my letters too," the mist said. [494] Maren's whole body shook. [498] "You never came back to the window, Ilse." [506] The condensation bloomed again, patient, precise: "I was turning back when the channel turned." [520] There it was, the moment before deciding, given back to her from the other side of it: her sister had already chosen to forgive her, had already turned around, and the water had simply been faster. [556] Maren spoke the apology out loud at last, every unsent letter folded inside the words, her voice so fierce the singing stopped to listen. [580] Then the labyrinth sang the phrase it had waited nine years for, the hole in the song closed over, and Ilse's voice was simply part of the music now, near and unafraid. [612] The shard's ancient crack knitted shut in the moonlight, and the wound in Maren's palm sealed without a scar for the first time in nine years. [638]

Maren walked home with the shard warm in her healed hand. [649] She keeps it on her sill now, whole in the moonlight, and once a month she breathes on it and waits. [670] The letters are no longer unsent; they go out one per moon, dissolving off the glass like dew, and every month the mist answers, round and hurried, with news of tides. [701] Some in town say the medium is less cryptic these days, softer. [713] They are wrong only about the softness. [720] Her love was always fierce; it simply has somewhere to live now. [732]
