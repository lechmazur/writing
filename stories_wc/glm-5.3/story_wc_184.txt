Marta Voss catalogued the clock tower the way she catalogued everything: warm tea first, then labels, then patience. [18] Aldersgate University had sent its archivist up a hundred and forty stairs because the mechanism room needed inventorying before restoration, dust included. [40] She was adaptable; when the pigeons claimed crate four, she simply numbered the pigeons too. [55] Crate nine held the clockmaker's treasures: a diplomat's wax seal impression, taken in 1752 and labeled in faded copperplate, and beside it a singed codex titled Fables of Absence. [84] The impression showed a lark above two crossed keys, the arms of Envoy Lark, who had signed the accord that moved Aldersgate onto the Gregorian calendar. [110] Marta knew the name, because the fire that took the East Stack three years ago had also eaten the treaty the impression once verified. [134] The catalog still listed that treaty, entry 4417, a ghost she could not bring herself to delete. [151] Her whole trade, she liked to say, was translating confusion into clarity, and grief was only confusion with nowhere to sit. [172]

That night she read the fables by lamplight while the stopped clock's small calendar wheel, driven by its own stubborn spring, ticked quietly on. [196] Each fable described a missing thing with suspicious precision: the bell that rang only in unhearing rooms, the librarian who shelved unwritten books, the eleven days the Crown deleted from September. [227] Fables, she realized, were finding aids; every impossible story was a record of something an archive had lost. [245] The last entry was titled The Door That Should Not Exist. [256] It said the university's clockmaker, grieving the eleven abolished days, had wound them into his calendar wheel, where once a year they still turned, an impossible September inside the ordinary one. [287] During those days that officially did not exist, a door stood on the west wall of the mechanism room, sealed with a blank boss of wax. [313] Beyond it waited everything the university had lost, shelved inside the days the world refused to count. [330] But a door only shows, the fable cautioned; to keep what it shows, you must merge. [346] The method was given plainly: unbraid the ceremonial rope the clockmaker had woven from nine strands into three plaits, and rebraid all nine into one, going over wherever he had gone under. [378] Tonight was the night: the wheel read the second of September. [389]

She did not sleep; she prepared, warmly methodical even in absurdity, brewing a second thermos and finding the rope coiled on a bronze peg behind the winding drum. [417] Nine strands, three plaits, the fibers gone soft as an old dog's ear after two and a half centuries. [436] At midnight the disengaged striking train sighed, and the wheel crawled from the second of September to the third, the first of the days the world had deleted. [464] On the west wall, where bare stone had been, stood a door. [476] It had no hinges and a boss of dark wax at its center. [489] She had expected a lock; finding wax, she adapted, and pressed the diplomat's impression into it, lark to lark, keys to keys. [511] The wax took it like a lock taking its only key, and the door swung inward onto the East Stack, whole and unburned. [534] She saw the treaty of entry 4417 on its shelf, the fables' intact twin, and a hundred other absences she had grieved in ghost entries. [559] But the door was already thinning at its edges, days draining out of it. [573]

Every archival instinct said never alter an artifact; the fable said artifacts remember what they are for. [590] She unbraided the three plaits strand by strand, keeping their order in her head the way she kept catalog numbers, then rebraided all nine into a single plait, over where the clockmaker had gone under. [625] It was slow, reversible, patient work, which is to say archival work. [637] As the ninth strand tucked home, the door did not close. [648] It dissolved, and its two sides flowed together like warm wax meeting warm wax, the lost Stack folding into the real one until shelf stood seamlessly upon shelf. [676] Smoke-eaten pages met their unburned twins, empty slots filled, and the burned East Stack became, shelf by shelf, complete. [695]

The wheel gave one last tick and settled on a proper, legal, existing date. [709] The eleven stolen days had been spent, and spent on paper. [720] She carried the rope down a hundred and forty stairs and across the quad, and hung it beside the East Stack's door, where visitors would admire it without understanding. [749] Then she went to shelf 4417 and pressed a fresh impression of Lark's seal into soft wax beside the treaty, so that if absence ever came back, clarity would know its shape. [781]
