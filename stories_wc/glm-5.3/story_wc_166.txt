Sela was the finest tide dancer the Meridian Court had ever shaped, and on the night the nostalgia ban fell, she was dancing. [23] Nostalgia, the Court declared, made the water heavy with longing; remembering made the tides late; the sea must never look back. [44] So came the Grinding Night, when every moonstone cabochon in which the dancers had stored their memories was pulverized, a thousand years of polished recollection reduced to dust and scattered across the black water. [78] Sela danced while it fell, because obedience was the shape her skill had always taken. [93]

One speck of that dust did not scatter. [101] It touched the hollow of her collarbone and held. [110] Dust cannot hold memory; only a cut cabochon can, yet this speck spontaneously anchored itself to her skin, fusing like a driven nail, and inside it the Doyenne's last dance burned on. [142] The Doyenne had been unspooled years before, her memories unpicked thread by thread for humming the old tide-verses, and her last words to Sela had been these: skin remembers, water only repeats. [174] Sela had not understood her then. [180] She understood her now, with an emptied woman's dance glimmering at her collarbone like a caged star: if memory refused to die, it deserved a body that was allowed to keep it. [212]

The wardens of the Unremembering heard the speck humming within the month. [224] They came for her on the neap tide and found the water empty. [237] She had fledged at dawn, leaving the sea for the first time in three hundred years, and she had done it the only way she knew: by observing gaps. [266] A tide dancer's whole art is the reading of intervals: the rest between waves, the pause between the wardens' circuits, the seam in the star-watch where two watch-stars set before their relief climbs the sky. [301] Sela moved through those silences the way she had always moved through water, and no one saw a ripple. [320] Her exile was elective, and she had signed it herself; any night that month she could have surrendered the speck and kept her place in the tide. [347] She wanted, instead, to pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin. [358]

The land received her at twilight, in a garden above the tideline where mortals walk when the light cannot decide. [378] Sea-holly and jasmine grew wild in it, and lunaria gone to seed, its pods hanging like small green moons, and above everything the stars kept their distances and kept their gaps. [409] The garden's keeper was a retired sailor named Ivor, an old tattooist with salt-cracked hands, who had inked shipmates for fifty years and never once been told what he might not remember. [441] She told him what she was and what she carried, and he bared his forearm without a word, because sailors know that some cargo must go on skin or be lost to the sea. [475] The verse was the thing the Court had feared without knowing it: the truth she had learned from three hundred years of intervals, that the world is mostly gaps, and the gaps are where the music lives. [512] She wrote it with the speck itself, drawing the glimmering grain across Ivor's skin, and where it touched, lines rose pale as moonlight on water: the names of unspooled dancers, the Doyenne's final turn, the exact blue of a drowned century. [553] It did not hurt him. [558] It felt, he said, like being remembered. [565]

Word travels fast among people still allowed to keep it, and by autumn the garden filled each dusk: a fisherwoman's shoulder took the line about slack water; a gravedigger's palm took the line about the pause between heartbeats; a bride took the line about the space stars leave so light can pass. [617] Each carried one line and no more, so that no single skin could be seized, and together they formed an archive the Unremembering could never pulverize, because you cannot grind a people. [649] On clear nights Sela stands at the garden's edge and dances the tide she can no longer touch, and the verse moves through the town below her like weather. [678] The speck has faded now to a faint star at her collarbone, nearly spent, and she does not grieve it; memory, she has learned, is not a stone to be kept but a light to be handed on. [716] Somewhere in the black water the Doyenne dances on, remembering nothing, but she is remembered. [731] The sea's gates are still open behind her, patient and black as obsidian, and every dusk she chooses again the garden, the stars, and the gaps between. [758]
