<story>
Perle was made of sea foam, which meant she held together by opinion more than matter. [16] Every evening she climbed into her cave, a giant hollow geode lined with crystal teeth, and set to work before memory faded. [38] Memories arrived on the tide as foam, and foam bursts within a day, so the work was urgent and the work was endless. [61] Perle called her profession cheerful gloom: saving things that were already leaving. [73] The iridescent walls threw rainbows at nothing in particular, which she found companionable. [86]

Her tools were simple: shards of crater glass, born when a falling star struck the desert and fused its sand. [106] Emotion, pressed against that glass, transliterated. [112] Grief became small gray notation; delight became a spiral of dots; longing wrote itself as a long line that refused to end. [134] That was her gift and her title: recollectionist, transliterator of feelings, keeper of the not-yet-lost. [149]

Tonight, though, a memory arrived with a hole in it. [159] It belonged to a walker named Odell, who had followed one path his whole life, a path his mother had shown him, a path that now simply stopped. [187] Perle recognized the missing pieces by their weight: places where his recollection went soft, like bread left out in rain. [207] He remembered setting out; he remembered arriving; the middle was gone. [218] This was her oldest puzzle and her private hunger: to discover why some paths disappear. [233] The tide had brought her someone else's vanishing, and she intended to catch it before it faded. [250]

A commotion of feathers interrupted her. [256] A migrating bird, storm-tossed and late, had taken shelter in the geode's mouth, and as it rested it dropped seeds from some far province, seeds that scattered across the crystal floor and rolled into the hollows. [292] Perle was about to sweep them politely away when she saw the pattern. [305] The seeds had fallen in a line, and the line broke exactly where Odell's memory broke. [321] She knelt, which for foam is more of a courteous ripple. [332] A gap here, a gap there: the missing pieces of the path, mapped in seeds. [347]

"Ah," she said, doing what recollectionists do second-best: conjecture. [356] Suppose paths are not structures but organisms. [363] Suppose they seed. [366] Suppose every path that disappears has not died but gone to flower somewhere it is needed more, and the reason nobody remembers the middle is that the middle has migrated, the way birds migrate, carrying its pieces and dropping them over new country to sprout. [411] It fit the evidence, which is the most a conjecture can hope for. [424]

But hope is not proof, and Perle needed the glass. [434] She carried a shard of crater glass to the seed line and pressed Odell's fading memory against it, transliterating his feelings as fast as they dimmed. [460] Where the path still existed, his contentment wrote itself as steady script, patient as handwriting. [475] Where the path was missing, she had expected grief, small and gray. [487] Instead, the glass bloomed. [491] The missing stretches transliterated not as loss but as restlessness and pollen-bright anticipation: the feelings of something in transit. [510] Odell's vanished road had not been taken from him; it had left of its own accord, hurrying somewhere, glad to be going. [532]

The bird chirped once, as if confirming, or begging crumbs. [542] By then the foam memory was thinning, so she transliterated the final emotion, which was not sorrow but wonder, and the crater glass held it, permanent as a star's scar. [572] Just in time; a moment later the last bubble popped, spent, and Perle was alone with crystals, seeds, and a beautiful suspicion. [594] She laughed, because the answer was both sad and lovely, and she had never seen the point of choosing between those. [615]

Paths disappear because they are needed elsewhere; they shed their middles like seeds behind migrating wings; and somewhere, in a province Odell would never visit, his road is already growing, and a stranger is walking it without knowing whom to thank. [656] Perle filed the glass with the others, where the geode's iridescence made every saved feeling gleam like weather. [674] The bird slept, the seeds settled, and the sea outside went on delivering its doomed, luminous cargo. [691] When the tide next carried word to Odell, she would tell him the truth she had transliterated: not everything missing is lost; some things are merely ahead of you. [720]
