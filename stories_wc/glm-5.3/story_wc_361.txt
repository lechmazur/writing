Pell's hands shook as she walked the aurora-lit ridgeway, and the relic crate clicked against her hip like a second heartbeat. [21] Above her, the aurora burned green and violet, which the old couriers called memory on its way to becoming myth. [41] Every light up there had been a story first, told aloud somewhere below. [54] That was the fade: first the face went, then the voice, then the faults, and what rose into the sky was too bright to be true. [80] Tonight Pell carried the last cargo of a woman being faded: Corvey of the Long Road, courier, mentor, lately Blessed. [100] The Sanctum had claimed everything for the vault at Cold Harrow, sealed and catalogued. [114]

Pell had signed for one road-worn satchel, one tin whistle, and one dragonfly-wing pendant no bigger than a thumbnail, which she carried in her own pocket against every rule of custody. [145] The pendant had been Corvey's whole trick. [152] Laid on a waystation table, it vibrated sympathetically with whatever stood near, a kettle, a laugh, a homesick voice, and whatever it touched, it lengthened. [177] Five minutes of her company stretched like warm honey, until strangers who shared one cup of tea spoke of Corvey for the rest of their lives like a lifelong friend. [207] So the legend was an artifact of the artifact, ten thousand extended encounters mistaken for intimacy. [223] Pell alone had known the unextended woman, and that was the whole trouble with tonight. [238] Corvey had been three winters dead, and the absence still sat in Pell like a stone. [254]

"Hold it when you're in a hurry," Corvey had told her once, "and watch what a hurry is worth." [273] The whole trade, she said, was coaxing wonder from the routine. [284] Pell had never learned it; she was the jittery one, the relic courier who counted her heartbeats to keep from counting disasters. [306] Now she walked the last run of a forty-year road, and her hands would not settle. [322]

At Harrow Gate, a new girl of maybe fourteen was tending the waystation lamp, oiling hinges nobody had asked her to oil. [344] "You'll be wanting tea," the girl said. [351] Pell meant to refuse; she had a vault to reach and a shake to outwalk. [366] Then the pendant woke in her pocket, trembling at something near, the kettle, the girl's chatter, her own quick pulse. [386] The moment stretched. [389] Steam climbed the lamp flame slowly enough to watch, and Pell found herself sitting down. [404]

"You rode with the Blessed Corvey," the girl said, not a question, because everyone knew. [419] "They say she once talked a bandit into weeping." [428] "She once talked a bee out of her sleeve," Pell said. [439] "Took an hour, because she was terrified of them." [448] The girl laughed, and the lengthened moment gave the laugh room to linger. [461] Pell told about the thunder-fright, and the honey cakes stolen from the Prior of Marrowdale, and how Corvey sang flat and refused all evidence. [485] The girl asked questions no litany would ever ask: what Corvey's boots smelled like, whether she snored, what she called her mule. [507] Each answer settled somewhere behind the girl's eyes the way seeds settle into turned earth. [522] Pell could almost watch them germinate. [528] When the pendant's trembling stilled, the encounter closed like a hand, and Pell paid for her tea and walked on into the green light. [552]

At Cold Harrow, the archivist logged the satchel, the whistle, the pendant, item by item, for the vault of the Blessed Corvey of the Long Road. [578] "Properties of the pendant?" the archivist asked. [585] "It extends brief encounters," Pell said, "by vibrating sympathetically." [594] The archivist wrote resonance, possible timekeeping application, and reached to close the drawer. [607] "It's not for keeping time," Pell said. [614] "It's for making five minutes enough." [620] When she said her mentor's name for the ledger, the pendant hummed from the closing drawer, and the hum stretched the moment until someone absent seemed almost to laugh. [649]

On the way home she stopped where she could see Harrow Gate's lamp. [662] The girl was on the porch talking to two drovers, arms flying, telling the story of the bee. [680] One drover would tell it at Marrowdale; the Prior's granddaughter would hear about the honey cakes; somewhere inside the bright myth a true fault had germinated and would grow. [709] Across the fade from memory to myth, Pell realized, seeds could travel in both directions. [724] A legend needed a flaw to be believed, and being believed was a kind of being alive. [741] In her ledger, in the courier's remarks column, she wrote: carried wonder, routine cargo. [755] Corvey would have corrected the grammar. [761] Corvey would have been right. [766] And Pell's hands, for the first time all night, were steady. [777]
