Mirren kept the smallest island's library, a stone lantern of a building open only from midnight to first light. [19] The whispering archipelago held her there, forty isles where wind poured through sea caves and came out carrying voices, every one of them the voice of something missing. [47] That was the arrangement on these islands: beauty made out of absence, luminance made of loss. [63] The tide delivered what people lost, and Mirren catalogued it. [73] Bottled letters, false teeth, wedding rings, and, on certain luminous nights, runaway dreams, which came ashore as pale moths of light that fled any eye that looked at them straight. [103] Fishermen's wives arrived at three in the morning to claim the dreams they had abandoned in youth, and Mirren would carry the fragile glow out to them in a lantern. [133]

One dream never let itself be caught: hers. [141] As a girl she had dreamed nightly of painting watercolors beside her mother, and when her mother sailed away and never returned, Mirren woke one dusk to find the dream gone, slipped out through the window like heat from a cooling stove. [183] Years later it drifted back into the archipelago with the other runaways, but it would not come to her. [202] It haunted the stacks instead, a soft radiance behind the shelving, always one aisle over, and wherever it passed the island's whispers changed. [225] Mirren, who catalogued everything, began to transcribe them. [233] For eleven years she filled notebooks, and for eleven years the whispers refused to mean anything, until the night she stopped reading them as words and started reading them as a cipher. [265] The dream's longing, it turned out, was spelled in fragments: one whisper per island, in the order the tide ran through the chain. [288] Set end to end, the fragments gave a place and an hour: the eastern skerry, at the moment before sunrise. [308] Not a demand and not a farewell, she realized, but an invitation. [320]

She closed the library early and rowed east with her mother's watercolor palette, its little pans emptied and dried to chalk years ago. [343] She had never refilled them; pigment felt like a forgery. [353] Instead she painted with light: long exposures at sunrise, the shutter open while the dawn moved, color pouring into the frame the way water pours into a held cup. [382] Sunrise photography, the fishermen called it, though it was less a picture-taking than a collaboration; she supplied the stillness, the sky supplied everything else. [406] On the skerry she set the palette on the rock before the camera and waited in the hush of improbable beginnings, that gray hour when the world seems to be deciding whether to exist. [440] The dream came up out of the water like a slow lamp. [452] Her hands ached to seize it, jar it, shelve it, keep it. [464] Eleven years of wanting had built that ache, and she understood, standing in the deepening pink, that capture was the one thing that would kill it. [490] A caged light is only light. [496] So she did the other thing, the harder thing: she opened the shutter and let it go. [513] The dream poured itself across the rock, into the empty pans, through the lens, onto the waiting film, and the sunrise followed it in, gold into rose into a blue that had no name in her notebooks. [550] For as long as the exposure lasted, the dream was not fleeing her and she was not chasing it; they were making something together. [574] When the shutter closed, the skerry was empty. [582] The dream had not been captured; it had been vitalized, poured from one life into a larger one. [600]

She developed the film in the library's darkroom between loans, and when the print swam up out of the developer tray she had to set down the tongs. [628] There was the dream, unmistakable, her mother's palette at its center with every pan brimming, and around it the light moved, actually moved, a slow wheel of pink and gold turning inside the paper. [662] It was a photograph the way the sea is a glass of water. [675] She hung it in the reading room, where the whispers pooled deepest at night. [689] Word crossed the water, as it does, and now the lost come to her at improbable hours, fishermen and brides and lighthouse keepers, each asking after some light that left them. [720] She photographs their longing at sunrise, paints it with light, and vitalizes what capture would only have embalmed. [738] The absences do not close; she never promises that. [747] But on the whispering archipelago, absence has learned to shine, and every dawn, in the hush before the world commits itself, Mirren rows east with an empty palette and an open shutter, in case her mother's voice is the next whisper the wind decides to spell.
