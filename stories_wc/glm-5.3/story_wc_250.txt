The sentence had hung unfinished in Marit for three days: "The night the boat came in, I..." [17] She had stopped right there, at her grandmother's table, and the rest of the half-spoken secret had followed her instead of leaving. [39] Now she stood on the cobalt dock after rain, where sky and canal held the same deep blue, and it was hard to say where one stopped being the other. [69] The water was so still it seemed to be listening. [79]

The keeper was already there, as she had known he would be. [91] He had been the canal's guardian longer than anyone could remember, and in all that time no one had heard him speak. [113] Children said he had given his voice to the water. [123] Marit did not believe it, but she believed this: whatever you could not say, he could hear anyway. [141] He stood before the dockhouse wall and its long bank of wooden shutters, and he was spiralingly calm. [159] His was not the flat calm of a still night; it turned, slowly, the way water turns above a deep place. [180] If you stood near him long enough, your own noise was drawn down into it. [195] Marit felt it happening now, the unfinished sentence settling in her chest like silt. [209] Everything that followed happened within the edge of that half-spoken secret, while the rest of the sentence waited. [227]

The keeper lifted his hands to the shutters and began to work. [239] He tilted the slats, opened some, closed others, and the lamp behind them broke into bars of gold that fell across the canal. [262] He was weaving. [265] That was the only word for it. [272] Open and closed, light and dark, the slats made a pattern that moved along the water like fabric drawn through a loom. [294] Where he closed a shutter, the weave went black. [303] Where he opened one, a thread of gold ran through the dark. [315] Marit watched, and the stillwater tension got inside her, taut as a rope holding something heavy just under the surface. [335]

She came to stand beside him. [341] "I have to tell you something," she said. [349] The keeper did not look at her. [356] He reached into his coat and took out a small thing on an angled stem: a dental mirror, its glass no bigger than a coin. [381] He had found it in the canal mud years ago, among the lost things the water gives back, and kept it because it showed what the eye cannot reach straight on. [412] Dentists use such mirrors to look behind teeth. [420] The keeper used his to look behind the dark. [429] He turned the little glass until it caught the lamp and held one small, round point of light, steady as a held breath. [452] Then, with a tilt of his wrist, he set that point into a gap where the pattern had gone all the way black. [475] A single star, placed exactly in the darkness. [483] Marit understood that he did this every night, alone, through all the years he had never spoken: teaching the darkness the value of stars. [507] Not fighting the dark. [511] Giving it something to hold. [516]

"I can't say it," she whispered. [522] The keeper opened three shutters at once, and a broad band of gold crossed the water to her feet and stopped there, waiting, the way an open hand waits, saying nothing. [547] It was the kindest thing anyone had done for her since the wreck, and it was done entirely without words. [567] She looked at the weave, at the dark places and the lit ones, and saw that it was made of both, that gold went nowhere without black to define it. [597] If her secret was a dark gap, then a gap was exactly where a star could be set. [615] The keeper tilted the dental mirror toward her, handle first. [625] Her hand was shaking; his spiraling calm moved through the space between them and slowly turned her shaking down, drew it under, let it settle. [650] She took the little mirror. [655] She caught the lamplight in it; her own eye looked back at her, wide and dark, before she turned the glass to the water. [679] She found a black place in the weave, where no shutter stood open, and set her light there beside his star, the way you stand beside someone at a grave. [709] The two lights held together on the still water. [718]

"The night the boat came in," she said, and the canal did not move, and the keeper did not move, and nothing in the world rushed her, "I left the lantern on the dock." [752] The words fell into the dark like the small round light, and stayed. [765] She wondered what sentence had once stopped in him and never gone on. [778] Beside her, gently, shutter by shutter, the keeper began to weave again. [790]
