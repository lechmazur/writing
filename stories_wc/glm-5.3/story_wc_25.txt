Mira had kept the Stack for eleven years, and she knew its ten thousand towering shipping containers the way mothers know bedrooms: by the footsteps inside them. [27] A maintenance worker in a port city learns such things, because a labyrinth forgives everything except not being known. [46] She could identify a person's walk through three walls of steel: the crane driver's flat stomp, the foreman's dragging shuffle, the tally clerk's skipping step. [71] On Tuesday she had heard a new one inside the deep rows: quick, toe-first, uneven, a boy's rhythm, counting steps as he went. [94] The manifests said the Stack held only cargo. [102] Manifests, in Mira's experience, were a kind of wish. [111]

On Friday the geomagnetic storm arrived, and the sky over the harbor turned the green of deep water. [129] Satellites wobbled, GPS screens went blank, and radios spat static like gravel. [141] Kellan, the night supervisor, stood at the west gate with a megaphone and a search party, bellowing for a stowaway the crane crews had spotted at dusk. [168] The shouts shattered against the containers and came back from everywhere, so the boy, wherever he was, only pressed deeper into the maze. [191]

"Your shouting is feeding the labyrinth," Mira said. [199] "Then find a better way," Kellan said, "before the storm grounds every crane and we lose him till morning." [218] She felt the old heat rise, the need she had carried for years: to prove that whispers travel farther than shouts. [239] "A challenge, then," she said. [244] "Your megaphones against my whisper until midnight." [251] He asked what she would stake, and she produced the zodiac coin, worn smooth, twelve signs circling a small sun, minted the year the first container was stacked. [279] If she lost, he could keep it, and she would retire her theory forever. [293] If she won, the megaphones would be banned from the Stack for good. [306] Kellan laughed and shook on it. [312]

Mira flipped the coin to choose her starting gate, because she liked her decisions to look like accidents. [330] The coin was slightly bent, and a left-thumb flip always answered Sagittarius, the archer, pointing her west. [347] Kellan called it luck. [351] She called it maintenance. [355]

At the west gate she unfolded her map of the Stack, creased soft as cloth from years of service. [374] Folded maps were the whole trick of the place. [383] Bring two distant corners together, and the crease told you what the builders had hidden: the labyrinth had folds in it, seams where far points lay one step apart. [412] She folded the deep rows, where the boy's counting walk had faded on Tuesday, onto the west gate. [430] The crease ran through a single container in row K, one with a welded-looking face and no door code. [449] From her pocket she drew the foreman's key, his parting gift and his standing joke, a key with no apparent lock in all the Stack. [474] "Locks are only locks you haven't folded yet," he used to say. [486] At the container she scraped paint and rust until a keyhole surfaced like a coin in river mud. [504] The key turned, the far wall swung inward on greased hinges, and the fold opened: row K's darkness looked out, impossibly, onto the deep rows half a mile away. [533]

She leaned her lips to the corrugated steel and whispered. [543] Shouts scatter; a whisper hugs the metal, riding the corrugations from container to container like a bead of water down a wire. [565] "Boy who counts his steps," she breathed, "walk toward the cold draft." [577] Kellan's megaphone wailed somewhere far off, its echoes tangling into nonsense, while her whisper traveled on, half a mile of steel carrying a voice no louder than breath. [605] The storm could scream all it wanted; the sky itself was shouting tonight, and everything loud had died with it. [625] Only the quiet things still worked. [631]

Ten minutes later she heard footsteps in the fold, and she knew him before the light did: quick, toe-first, uneven, counting. [652] The boy stepped out blinking, green fire burning above the cranes. [663] He had followed the walls that spoke, he said, because the walls that shouted frightened him. [679] Kellan arrived at a run, megaphone dead in his hand. [689] He set it on the ground between them like a surrendered sword. [701] Mira pressed the zodiac coin into the boy's palm. [710] "The maze takes a toll," she told him. [718] "Now you have paid it." [723] To Kellan she said only, "The storm shouted down every satellite in the hemisphere, and still one whisper crossed half a mile of steel." [747]

That winter the charter of the Stack was quietly amended: no shouting in the labyrinth. [762] The port still calls Mira lucky. [768] The key rides in her pocket, patient as the next fold. [779] Luck, she knows, is only a plan that arrives looking like an accident. [792]
