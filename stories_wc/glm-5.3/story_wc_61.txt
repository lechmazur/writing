I am the fourth keeper of the Nursery, a garden suspended in the ribs of a dying nebula. [18] My mother called me an otherworldly sentimentalist because I kept what keepers are taught to burn. [34] The exception was the calculating machine gear I carried: brass, small as a thumbnail, its teeth cut in a ratio that never resolves. [57] My grandmother called it infinity approximated: two gears chasing each other forever, never meeting. [71] She meant our family. [75]

Each petal fall, the nebula dims, and the keeper walks to the engine at the garden's heart. [92] The engine computes the burn; the keeper sets fire to a third of the blooms so the rest can open, seed, and hold another generation. [117] My mother burned the orchard where I was born. [126] She watched it go with the calm of someone lighting a lamp, then handed me the gear, and said the pattern holds us together. [150]

This petal fall the dimming came early, and it was my turn to make the transit. [166] I crossed the Nursery by sonar, the keeper's way: a ping sent into the gas, an echo returned off the buoy flowers, a path read in silence. [193] The transit lasted the whole season, across the quiet sigh of falling petals, each one letting go like a held breath. [214] In the deep dark, where even a keeper's eyes fail, I pinged harder than protocol allows. [230] A petal caught the pulse and flared, a soft gold blush that faded in a heartbeat. [246] I pinged again; a hundred petals answered, lighting themselves, unburned. [256] I stood in the dark wanting one thing only: to share light without using fire. [271] Wanting it did not change the engine. [278]

At the heart of the Nursery stood the calculating machine, tall as a chapel, its brass ribs green with age, waiting for the gear that every keeper carries to her own burn. [310] We carry it between burnings so the machine can never run without a keeper's hand. [325] I fitted it home. [329] The engine woke and did what it has always done: it counted. [341] Its ledger unrolled: first keeper, burn one field in nine; second keeper, one in six; my mother, one in three. [361] Because our ratio never resolves, the projection never stopped; the machine, patient as its gears, approximated infinity, an endless procession of burns, each larger, each dimmer. [387] Infinity, I understood, is not a promise. [394] It is a threat delivered gently. [400] Then the machine asked the question it asks every keeper: name the field to burn. [415] My mother had answered with the orchard. [422] My grandmother, she said, had answered without hesitation. [431] I am a sentimentalist; I hesitate. [437] While I hesitated, I touched the gear, and it rang. [447] The ring rolled through the machine's hollow body, gathered in the brass, and went out into the nebula as the largest ping ever sounded here. [472]

The garden answered. [475] Petal by petal, field by field, the Nursery lit from within, gold spreading along the dust threads like dawn remembered, and nothing burned. [498] The engine, which had only ever computed fire, recorded the anomaly in its patient ledger: keeper four, zero fields burned. [518] The light had been ours to give all along; we had simply been giving it the way our grandparents taught us, hand to hand, burn to burn. [545] I should tell you it was joy. [552] It was serenity of a troubling kind. [559] The light showed me the garden entire for the first time in my life, and the garden was smaller than any keeper admitted: gaps where fields had been, black scars where my mother's fires ran, whole terraces gone to ash. [599] The petals kept falling through the glow, sighing as they fell, serene. [611]

I will teach my daughter to ping. [618] She will grow up in a lit garden and learn the burns as history, and one day she will ask why four generations needed a machine to tell them what one sound could say. [652] I will not have an answer that spares us. [661] Patterns are like that gear's teeth: they turn forever unless someone refuses to let them mesh. [677] Each dimming now, I climb to the heart and strike the gear, and the garden answers whole, and nothing is asked of the fire. [701] It is a new pattern, I know. [708] My daughter will inherit it the way I inherited fire: hand to hand, without a choice in the matter. [727] Generations are gears; all I changed was the direction. [736] The petals fall, the ping goes out, the light comes back, and somewhere in that quiet sigh I can almost hear my mother, calm as a lamp, asking if I am sure. [768] I am. [770]
