The man in the gray coat had followed her from the records office, keeping half a block behind for eleven streets. [21] Nell did not run. [25] She noted his boots, his pauses, the hour. [33] Noticing was a storm recorder's trade, and Nell was meticulous. [43] At home she took her tea in the quiet courtyard at dusk, where the light lay in slow, layered bands across the wall. [66] This was the hour she guarded most, the small unhurried ritual by which she nurtured integrity in solitude. [84] The pigeon dropped onto the wall like a leaf. [93] She knew its band at once: Kestrel Row, the loft that carried weather readings between towns when wires failed. [112] Tied to its leg hung a brass message tube. [121] Nell was credibly curious, never feverishly so; she wanted to know things the way an auditor does, with receipts. [140] So before unscrewing the tube she examined the bird. [149] Its breast was spent, its feathers ruffled by hard flying. [159] Yet her ledger said the wind had blown from the east all afternoon, gentle and steady. [175] A bird flying east, into the teeth of that wind, had begun its journey somewhere west of her. [193] The capital and its Ministry lay east. [200] She unscrewed the tube and read. [206] The message bore the Ministry's seal and was brief: amend your storm log for the fourteenth; record the hail beginning at dusk, not at noon; burn this note. [234] Nell sat very still. [238] She remembered the fourteenth perfectly, because hail had fallen at noon and stopped by three, and she had weighed the largest stone herself: thirty-one grams. [263] A true ministry bird, released in the capital, would have arrived with the wind behind it, easy and dry. [282] This one had fought the wind, coming from the west, from the direction of Millward. [297] Millward was the district sealed last month as a quarantine zone. [308] A direct question would earn her a direct lie, so she progressed laterally instead, sideways through the week's small facts, as she always worked. [332] The wire office had closed early on the fourteenth, conveniently. [342] The quarantine posters on Millward's gates were dated the fifteenth, yet they curled with a week's worth of rain she alone had measured. [365] And the zone appeared in no gazette, no minister's dispatch, only on barriers nailed up overnight by men in unmarked coats. [386] That was the shape of it: a rogue quarantine zone, declared by no ministry, needing the weather rearranged. [404] At dusk on the fourteenth, Millward's granary had burned; with hail officially falling at dusk, lightning would explain it, and no one would ask what the fire was hiding. [433] She could not prove a word of it. [441] She did not need to. [446] Intuiting, she had long believed, was only noticing carried to its end, and she let hers finish: the gray coat had not followed her to frighten her. [473] He had followed her home to learn where the ledger slept. [484] The tube was a courtesy; whatever followed a refusal would not be. [496] Nell fed the pigeon from her palm and let the dusk deepen, layer over layer, into blue. [513] Then she worked, unhurried and exact. [519] She copied the falsified entry into a decoy ledger, dated it, blotted it, and left the decoy in plain sight. [539] From the true ledger she cut the page for the fourteenth, which still said what the fourteenth had actually done. [559] She rolled it tight, slid it into the empty tube, and wired the tube back onto the pigeon's leg. [578] Back to Kestrel Row it would fly, into the wind again, a short hard mile, carrying the day's real weather to the one loft that kept honest birds. [606] Let the gray coat come. [611] He would find a quiet woman in a quiet courtyard, tea gone cold, records in perfect order, nothing anywhere worth burning. [632] Integrity, she had learned, needed no audience; it needed only one keeper who would not rewrite the rain. [650] The first star showed. [654] Nell opened a fresh page and recorded the evening as it truly was: wind east, light failing, one small storm of feathers climbing eastward, carrying the truth home. [682]
