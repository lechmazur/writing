The lift cage rose through Solreign Spire with the patience of a servant who had made this climb a thousand times. [21] Wren counted floors the way other people counted blessings. [30] One: the ice cellar, blue and murmuring. [37] Two: the kitchens, still warm from yesterday's ovens. [45] Above the clouds the spire wore winter like a uniform, hoarfrost feathering every window, so that sunrise entered not as sun but as a scatter of colors thrown across the walls. [76] Wren had learned to love that light from a polite distance, the way one loves weather. [92]

On the tray rested the captain's goggles, retrieved from the study where the old man had hurled them a week ago. [113] Sixty years of sky were scratched into the lenses, and something was wedged behind the left one: a strip of paper folded into eighths. [137] Wren had noticed it while polishing the brass, because Wren noticed everything, warmly, and asked almost nothing. [154] Curiosity in a servant is permitted if it is quiet. [164]

Between the third and fourth floors, Wren unfolded it. [173] The writing was a cipher: columns of tidy glyphs marching in ranks, meaningless as moth wings. [189] A diary, then, or a log, locked against everyone. [198] Four floors gone; six remained. [203]

Wren held the page against the window on a whim. [213] The frosted glass broke the sunrise into ribbons, and the ribbons fell across the glyphs, and the glyphs began to bloom. [234] It was not magic, only optics: the ink was doubled, one script over another, and scattered light pulled them apart the way a prism pulls a beam. [261] Even better: held to the eye, the captain's own scratched lenses did the same, each old abrasion a tiny diffraction grating. [282] He had written in ink that only his goggles could read, which meant he had wanted it found by no one, or by exactly one. [307] Wren put the goggles on and read through their scratched gold. [318]

Fifth floor. [320] The surface script was what anyone would expect from Captain Aurelio Vance after the disaster at Karst Reach: grief in inventory form, names of the lost, wind readings at the moment each man went over. [355] Anguish, filed neatly. [358] But the underwriting, unfolding word by word in the scattered colors, was something else entirely. [373] "Ollie laughed so hard at four thousand feet that he cracked a rivet." [386] "Tomas taught the cabin boy constellations wrong on purpose, and the boy navigated us home by a sky that does not exist." [408] "We ate the emergency chocolate on the equator and none of us was sorry." [422] Joy, hidden under grief like a second script, encrypted exactly because it seemed unbearable next to the names. [440]

Sixth floor. [442] Wren had served the captain for nine years, through the telegram, the funerals, the winter he stopped speaking, and had come to believe that beneath the old man's anguish there was something worth digging for, the way ore sits under ice. [483] Not to fix him. [487] Servants who try to fix their masters become cruel or saints, and Wren intended to be neither. [504] Only to find it, the joy, and leave it where he could reach it. [518]

Seventh floor, eighth; the light swung from rose to gold to white. [530] Near the end of the strip, one entry sat in the underwriting alone, no surface grief above it at all. [550] "If I am lost," it went, "let whoever finds this know that I loved the sky more than I feared it, and my crew more than I loved the sky, and that I would sign on with those same fools again in any weather." [594]

Ninth floor. [596] Wren folded the diary back into eighths, warm as bread, and tucked it behind the scratched lens where its author had kept it. [619] The cage slowed. [622]

Tenth floor: the study, the silence, the old man at the window like a figurehead washed ashore. [639] Wren set down the tea, placed the goggles on the desk without a word, and angled them, with great casualness, toward the morning ice. [663] Then, in the tone reserved for weather, remarked that a cabin boy once steered three hundred miles by a constellation his navigator invented, and wasn't that something. [690] The captain did not turn. [695] But his hand, the one with all the old rope scars, drifted across the desk and settled over the goggles, thumb finding the folded edge. [720]

The lift waited, doors open, already half in love with what it would carry down tomorrow. [736]
