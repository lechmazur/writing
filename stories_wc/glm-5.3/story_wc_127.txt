The amber grotto held its charge best at dusk, when cooling stone drew sparks from everything it touched. [18] Sera worked here for the same reason a luthier chooses spruce. [29] By trade she was a counterfeit emotion dealer, and professionally thorough. [40] Every batch she sold was a half truth, distilled from real feeling and padded with chemistry, true enough to convince a body but never a soul. [66] Her clients never asked which half was missing. [74]

Tonight a man named Vane sat at her bench with an empty face and a funeral at dawn. [92] "I need grief," he said, "by morning, and it has to be mine." [105] He told her it had been a good marriage, and Sera heard the shape of the half he was leaving out. [126] Three decades of tending a sick wife, then years of suppressants, and now nothing in him answered when he tried to mourn. [148] Her synthetics would not take. [153] Counterfeit feeling keys itself to the residue of feeling a client already carries, and Vane carried none. [170] Sera told him this gently, the way she told everyone everything, with affection held carefully away from her. [188]

One possibility remained, sitting in her tray for eleven years. [198] Ilse had been a fragrance chemist before the emotion trade lured her across, and she taught Sera to test blends on a blotter petal, a paper flower that held scent and charge better than skin. [233] It was Ilse's last, still saturated with the unfinished emotion she had been compounding when her heart stopped. [251] A grief she had mixed for her own dying, Sera believed, and never got to feel. [267] She had never dared use it, because she was thorough, and thoroughness had always been her substitute for feeling. [286] She could read any emotion on earth through static electricity, the crackle of it moving across the amber into her fingertips, grief a low hum, joy a bright sting. [315] Insight, flawless and complete. [319] Empathy, none. [321] What she had wanted, all her working life, was to fuse the two. [334] Tonight the wanting outweighed the caution. [340]

She lifted the petal from its paper sleeve and let the grotto's last light lean through it, honey-thick. [358] Then she began the blend, base and carriers and fixatives, every step logged twice, because half measures were for amateurs. [378] Genuine grief needs no key. [383] It roots in anyone. [387] But a stored charge turns real only by passing through a living body, Ilse taught, and hers was the only one at the bench. [411] She warned him it might hurt her. [418] Vane looked at her with something almost like envy, and she liked him for it, distantly, the way one likes weather. [439] Outside, dusk was draining from the grotto mouth like the last of a tide. [453]

She dipped the petal into the crucible, laid her palm flat against the amber wall, and closed the circuit. [472] The static rose at once, and it did not behave. [482] Grief was supposed to arrive as a low hum. [491] This came in as a flood, Ilse's blend braided with the grief Sera had never let herself feel, and she understood that the petal had held two charges all along. [521] The half truth of her trade had been hiding a whole one in plain sight. [536] In the crucible the blend turned from counterfeit to true as the transformation completed, dusk giving its final inch, and the charge kept climbing because it had found somewhere to go. [567] Into her. [569] She could have broken contact. [574] Instead she opened her hands and let it subsume her, grief and insight fusing into something she had no assay for, no label, no price. [599] She cried without design, which was new, and noted the quality of it even as it drowned her notes, thorough to the end. [622]

When it settled, the grotto was dark except for the faint gold of the walls, and Vane was watching her with his empty face cracked open. [648] "It worked," he whispered, meaning the vial, though his eyes stayed on her face. [662] She sealed the vial and, for the first time in her career, told a client the whole truth. [680] It was real. [683] Most of it was not his. [689] It would hurt exactly as much as grief should. [698] He paid her double, which was also, she supposed, a kind of truth. [711]

At the grotto mouth she touched his sleeve, a static spark passing between them, and felt his future sorrow the way she now felt everything, warmly, precisely, from a slight distance. [742] She had wanted to fuse insight with empathy. [750] It had cost her the petal, and it was worth more than everything else in the tray. [767] Sera banked the amber's charge for the night, grieving pleasantly, and opened her ledger to log the loss. [785]
