The eclipse ended the way a wound closes: slowly, then all at once, light flooding back over the wind-carved encampment. [20] Maren pulled her hood lower over her white hair, though the sting behind her pink-white eyes had already settled in for the day. [43] Albino trackers work the hours of shadow; an eclipse is the one sky they can watch unhooded, and the vanishers had spent this one leaving. [68] Eleven years ago, in another eclipse, her brother Corin poured ink across a stone slab, stepped in, and time slowed enough to show her every fold of the pattern he vanished through. [100] That slowing was the question she carried like a stone: why does time stretch only when the heart breaks? [119]

The register-keepers called her consciously wild, a woman who could return behind walls and refused, and they were right; she had chosen the open country as one chooses a religion. [149] Wild lives run on crisis, settled lives run on schedule, and only crisis, she had come to suspect, was currency where Corin had gone. [173]

She unhooded the hummingbird from its felt sling: a brass and glass drone no bigger than her thumb, wings shivering the air into a blur. [198] The vanishers' sentries never troubled themselves over hummingbirds, which was the point. [210] It rose, caught the light like a jewel, and went ahead of her into the hollows. [226]

The place was a city carved by wind: tents of sandstone, windows bored by ten thousand years of grit, doorways like flutes that moaned when the weather turned. [254] The braziers were still warm. [259] The congregation had gone through at totality, forty souls pouring forty pools of ink, and the wind was already sanding the patterns away. [282] All but one. [285] In the deepest hollow an old man sat beside a basin of wet ink, waiting, as if he had known she would come. [308]

"You have his white eyes," the old man said. [317]

"You're the ink-wright who taught my brother to leave," Maren said, and her voice was level, without heat; her anger had gone serene years ago, worn smooth as water wears glass. [348] The hummingbird drifted down between them and hovered, its lens drinking the basin. [361]

"Ask your question," he said. [366]

"You were there the day Corin went through." [374]

"I poured the ink he vanished through," he said. [383]

"Then tell me why time slowed." [389]

The old man smiled at the ink, not at her. [399] "Time does not slow to be kind, tracker. [407] It slows because something is drinking it. [414] A mind facing its ending spills its seconds like a knocked-over cup, and the pattern is woven from what spills. [434] We call it disappearing ink, for the lines and for the people. [446] The door costs a crisis, child. [452] That is why we gather at eclipses: the whole sky is a held breath, awe comes cheap, and the ink is hungry." [474]

Maren felt the truth of it settle over her, quiet as ash. [486] Eleven years of asking, and the answer was a ledger: Corin's leaving had been paid for with her slowed seconds, drawn from her when she needed them most. [514] Her anger did not rise; it simply confirmed itself, the way a compass confirms north. [529]

"Where did he go?" [533]

"Wherever the ink remembers. [537] Every vanished person is written in it, in lines that fade even as they are read. [553] Your brother's page is nearly gone. [559] You have carried a crisis for eleven years, tracker. [568] You could buy a door with it." [575]

He tipped the basin with one finger. [582] Ink sheeted across the stone and bloomed: spirals unrolling into fern fronds, fern fronds into letters of no alphabet she knew, the ephemeral pattern assembling and fading within four heartbeats. [612] The old man stepped into the deepest coil and was gone. [623] The pattern faded to a grey ghost, then to nothing, and the wind carved on. [638]

Maren stood a long moment in the returned light, letting her eyes burn. [651] The hummingbird settled on her wrist, its memory full of him. [662] The footage would hold him, fold by fold, proof for every register-keeper who called her mad. [678] Then she drew Corin's vial from her coat, recovered from that first slab and never opened, and poured it out. [698]

The ink ran black, then blue, then began to bloom. [708] Time slowed, and this time she understood it: not mercy, not magic, a tithe, her stretched seconds paying the toll. [728] She let them be taken. [733]

The pattern opened like a written door; she sent the hummingbird up to film it; and Maren, angry, serene, wild on purpose, stepped into the ephemeral lines of ink and disappeared. [764]

After the eclipse, the encampment held only wind, a cooling basin, and a hummingbird circling above the place where the pattern had been. [787]
