Vesh was the smallest titan ever born, which is to say she stood a head shorter than a door. [19] She kept the ancient temple library that time had misplaced, a sprawl of shelves that drifted between centuries like a boat slipping between moorings. [43] Though the Titan Throne was hers by blood, she wore no crown, mended her own sandals, and answered to Librarian, which is one way of being regally unpretentious. [71] Her only regalia were pellets of iron gall ink, hard as beetle shells, which she dissolved one at a time in a clay dish of rain. [97] A single pellet outlasted a decade of writing. [104]

With that patient ink she had spent four hundred years unmasking a pretender, leaving cryptic messages in library books. [123] The pretender called himself Emperor Corvane the Titanborn, and he had built his throne on a forged genealogy and a crown looted from a dead titan's barrow. [150] He could not rule comfortably while the true bloodline breathed, so he declared the temple library a myth and burned every genealogy he could find. [175] Vesh answered the only way a drifting library can: she planted evidence in books that would not be shelved for centuries. [196] In a bestiary she wrote the succession whole, name by name, in a hand scholars would come to call the Margin. [217] In a book of crowns she noted where the barrow-crown's engraving had been filed away and re-stamped with Corvane's imaginary crest. [238] In a hymnal she hid the recipe for iron gall ink itself, so that someday an honest chemist could date her letters and swear the margins were older than the empire's lies. [270] Each book left the library on the drift and landed in some future reading room, waiting for its reader. [289]

The drift itself came from a rune cut into the library's keystone, older than any alphabet still living: a rune of drift, twin spirals that taught solid things to wander. [319] While the rune held, the library rode the pauses of the world, present everywhere and reachable nowhere. [336] But once in three thousand years, the constellations pause in their slow wheeling, a held breath between one sky and the next. [358] In that pause, Vesh learned, a rune could be read backwards, unmade, sundered. [371]

On the night Corvane chose for his coronation -- his astrologers had promised him the pause of shifting constellations as a backdrop -- the drifting library slid into the capital's sky. [400] It came down the way snow comes down, unhurried, entire, and settled in the cathedral square while ten thousand witnesses held their breath. [423] Doors that had not opened for an age opened. [432] Vesh walked out, small and barefoot, carrying the bestiary, the book of crowns, and the hymnal. [448] She read the margins aloud in a voice that carried without being raised. [461] She read the true succession down to its last living branch, which ended in a librarian. [477] She read where the crown's engraving had been filed, and a stepladder was fetched, and the crowd saw the older name beneath the new one. [502] She gave the chemist her recipe, her dish, and a pellet of iron gall ink, and the ink under his lens told the truth about its own age. [530] The pretender's claim came apart the way wet paper comes apart, gracefully, fiber by fiber, with no one needing to shout. [551] Corvane knelt, not because anyone commanded it, but because a lie is heavy and he was tired. [568]

Then Vesh climbed to the keystone and, across the pause of shifting constellations -- a pause that lasted a heartbeat and a lifetime -- she sundered the rune of drift. [596] She struck it once with the mallet she used for mending shelves, which is one way of being regally unpretentious. [616] The twin spirals released, and the library, its long errand finished, began its graceful collapse. [631] Shelves bowed to one another like courtiers at the end of a reign. [644] Dust rose in slow columns and hung there, undecided, then lay down. [656] Books slid from their places and opened flat, showing their margins to the sky. [670]

Vesh carried out only the clay dish and the mallet, and stood aside while the walls folded down like a mantle being shrugged off. [694] Above her the stars resumed their wheeling, sliding into new houses, and the world moved on. [710] The council offered her the Titan Throne that night. [719] She asked instead for a small library in the capital, a rain barrel, and a supply of paper. [737] Rumor says she is still there, annotating. [744] Somewhere in a book not yet written, in a margin not yet inked, the next lie is already drifting toward its unmasking. [766]
