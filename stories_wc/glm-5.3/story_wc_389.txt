Mara kept the last observatory on Thornel Spire, a brass barnacle stuck above a sea of storms. [17] Below the clouds, the world forgot her; above them, she forgot to mind. [30] She was a lonely visionary in the honest sense: she saw things that were not yet true, and she wrote them down anyway. [53] Her great project was the portico: a doorway of cobalt glass, salvaged from a drowned cathedral, mortared with lightning-fused sand. [73] At dawn the light came through it blue before it came through it gold, as if the sky were being born twice. [95] Behind the portico she stacked her inheritance: ten thousand photographic plates, the shadows of ancient starlight pressed onto glass by astronomers a century dead. [119] Her plan was simple and probably mad: on the right morning she would project those ancient shadows through the cobalt glass and shape a new dawn out of them. [148]

The radio interrupted. [151] It had been a dead thing, good for weather and static, until the night the storms parted and a voice fell through in pieces. [175] The men in the valley call me Prophet, the voice said, but the mountain chewed it into fragments, and she caught what she could. [199] Across that season the prophet rose. [205] Every night his sermons climbed the spire in fragments, and every night the fragments disagreed. [220] On one frequency he was iron: burn the archives, the past is a landlord, evict it. [236] On another he was glass: carry the old light forward, a dawn is only a door. [252]

Mara began to talk back, because loneliness makes a contrarian of anyone. [264] She read her plates aloud to the transmitter, whispering the dead astronomers' notes, warming the batteries with her tea. [283] The stars of 1849 will arrive tomorrow, she told the static; they are late but faithful. [299] It sounded like prophecy because it was: starlight is old, and every forecast of it comes true years late. [318] In spring, the glass variant of the prophet quoted her. [328] Her own words came back down the mountain wearing his voice, softened by static into scripture. [344] So the prophet had two faces, and one of them was hers. [356] She understood then that allegiance came in variants, like signal, like weather, like dawn. [370] You could kneel to a man, or to the best sentence he ever stole. [384] She chose the sentence. [388]

By midsummer the pilgrims came, a slow beard of torches growing up the scree, because rumor said the observatory held the prophet's other voice. [412] Mara, who had wanted an audience for thirty years, panicked and swept the floor. [426] On the chosen morning, storms ringed the horizon like spectators who had paid to see weather. [442] She set the arc lamp behind the plate stack, aimed it at the cobalt portico, and let the whole archive unfold. [463] The dead sky spread across the living one. [471] Old constellations unmade themselves into new ones; a nova that had died before her grandmother's grandmother burned politely through the blue glass. [493] The pilgrims went quiet the way crowds do when something better than a sermon happens to them. [510] Then the prophet himself climbed through the door, smaller than his frequencies, rain-beaten, ordinary. [524] He looked at the shadowed sky, then at her, and asked which of his voices had kept her company. [543]

The iron one, she admitted, was louder. [550] That one is mine, he said. [556] The soft one began the night my antenna caught a woman on a mountain, reading the stars their letters. [575] I have been preaching you back to yourself for a year, and the crowds like you better than me. [594] Mara laughed, a rusty sound, and poured him tea warmed on transmitter batteries. [607] Allegiance, she told him, is not a cage; it is a door, and doors want opening. [623]

They rigged a microphone beside the portico and broadcast the dawn as it unfolded, two variants of one voice arguing cheerfully about light. [646] Below the clouds, ten thousand radios spat static and scripture in equal measure. [659] The storm grumbled off eastward, unpaid and unimpressed. [667] And the new dawn came through the cobalt portico blue first, then gold, then something older than either: the patient shadows of dead stars, lending their light to a morning that would owe them everything. [702] Somewhere below, the world began, cautiously, to look up. [711]
