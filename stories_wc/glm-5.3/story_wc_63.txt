Joss wound the tower clock the way other men prayed: on his knees, by small increments, at the same hour each day. [22] He was the precise winder of Glassreach, a town nailed to a tidal obsidian ridge, where the sea drew back twice a day and left the black glass burning. [51] The tower was the town's shared balance: its strikes told the saltern keepers when to open the flood gates, the boats when the tide would turn on the razor shore. [81] Joss was ethically diligent to the point of fever; he checked the escapement twice, logged every lost second, and forgave himself nothing. [103]

Seven years ago, fevered himself, he had turned the pendulum's rating nut one turn the wrong way, and for a single tide the clock ran slow. [129] The sea turned eleven minutes before the tower promised, and his brother Tam, raking the salt flats by that promise, was caught and cut to death climbing the glass. [158] The sea gave back his rake, its handle snapped, and Joss kept it wrapped in oilcloth at the top of the stairs, an unopened wound. [183] Glassreach forgave him by the second winter; he refused the pardon. [194]

Winding is a trance of tiny motions, and in that trance he had learned to live inside the pauses, the held fractions of a second at each end of the pendulum's swing when the brass bob hangs weightless and time holds its breath. [237] One night, deep in this reverie, his hands found the oilcloth bundle and unwrapped it before his mind could object. [257] In the pause between two ticks he tilted the broken rake handle toward the lamp and saw that it was written over. [279] Tiny inscriptions ran along the whole length of the wood, cut with a raker's blade, each mark smaller than a grain of salt. [302] Tam had kept a ledger. [307] Joss read it in the pauses, one line per held breath, and every line burned. [322]

Snapped at the ferryman, and beneath it, mended his oarlock. [332] Shorted the widow a scoop of salt, and beneath that, left firewood at her door. [347] Each small wrong set down, each small right set against it, a life balancing itself by increments. [364] And the last entry, deepest cut, nearest the break: mocked Joss about his clockwork prayers, never said sorry; and beside it, no balancing mark at all, only raw unfinished wood. [394] Tam had died owing his brother one small kindness. [403]

Through those tiny inscriptions, seven years overdue, came the lesson: absolution is not a door that opens; it is a mark cut beside a mark, an act set against an act, however small. [436] So he took up the unfinished entry as his inheritance. [446] In the pause of every swing, one held breath at a time, he set about restoring the shared balance his eleven minutes had broken. [470] Each balancing deed earned its own tiny inscription carved into the broken handle beside Tam's, in a graver's patient hand. [490] He rehung the ferry gate that had shrieked for a decade. [501] He taught the gatekeeper's daughter the escapement, so the town would never again lean on a single pair of hands. [521] He salted the widow's icy step before dawn, mended the saltern doors, carried Tam's tools to men who needed them. [541] Year by year the marks climbed the handle, and year by year the shared balance restored itself, gates and boats and grieving neighbors drawing level, click by click, like a movement coming true. [574]

Tonight is the annual cleaning, the one sanctioned stoppage, and Joss stands in the longest pause of all. [592] He has caught the swing and cradles the great brass pendulum bob against his chest, a held sun, and the whole town below holds its breath with it. [620] The handle is crowded now, his marks running beside Tam's, and one last space waits at the unfinished line. [639] For it there is only one debt left, the first and oldest: eleven minutes, one brother, one night. [657] He has spent seven years balancing everyone's account but his own. [668] The graver moves, and the last tiny inscription goes in beside Tam's: for eleven minutes, the rest of a life, given back to the town. [693] He releases the bob. [697] The escapement ticks, the town exhales, and below the withdrawing tide lets the obsidian ridge emerge from the sea like a spine of black fire, every edge blazing with low light. [728] Something emerges in Joss too, in that same held moment: not a pardon, nothing so whole, only the certainty of the next small right, and that, at last, is enough. [758] He climbs down to wind the clock again, the way other men pray, and the sea returns to the burning glass. [779]
