Three winters after Mira forgave herself for the foundry fire, a letter came out of the icebound north. [18] The wardens of the Aurora Sanctum wanted a lost wax caster, someone whose hands knew how to empty a thing without ruining it. [41] The fire had taken her aunt's street organ and its library of punched cards, and every card had been a child's name rendered as melody for a christening. [69] Only a scrap survived, eleven holes that ended Aunt Rusa's own name-tune, and Mira had carried it through her years of guilt like a coal. [94] Forgiving herself had not erased the loss; it had only opened her hands, and open hands could be gentle when it mattered. [116] So she rebuilt the organ and went north. [124]

The sanctum was a vault of blue ice roofed with clear panes, and above it the aurora moved like slow green water. [146] On a bed of snow lay the ceremonial rope of drowned Karsfjord, braided four centuries ago from the hair of its last mothers. [169] Its knots held the names of everyone the sea had taken, the wardens said, but no one living knew how those names had sounded. [193] "The wool is going brittle," said Warden Ester. [201] "In ten years this rope will remember nothing but silence." [211] Mira touched one knot and felt a throat about to speak. [222] She had crossed the ice for exactly this, to preserve the sound of forgotten names before the cold finished its patient erasure. [244]

When a warden traced a knot's spacing on frosted glass, Mira's breath caught, for she had seen that rhythm before, in holes punched down a card. [270] At midnight she laid the punched card scrap on the snow beside the rope, under the panes. [287] The aurora flared. [290] Green light poured through the eleven holes and illuminated the braids with eleven small coins. [305] Every coin of light landed on a knot. [313] She understood then that the card punchers and the rope braiders had been one people, exiles who carried a single code from fjord to fairground, transcribing memory into holes in cardboard and knots in hair. [348] The scrap was not a relic; it was a key. [358]

An organ reads a card with spring pins, she thought, and pins could read knots too, if she reconfigured the ancient braids into a ladder where every knot stood in a rank like a hole. [393] But the hair was old, and one rough pull would snap a name in half. [408] Luckily, a lost wax caster is schooled in technically gentle ruin, since she must burn wax out of a mold so slowly the plaster never cracks. [434] Mira warmed snow water over a lantern and steamed each braid until it softened. [448] She unpicked over from under a strand at a time, murmuring to the wool as if it were something injured. [468] Then she rebraided, knot to rung and rung to rank, building a ladder of names as wide as her two hands, cutting nothing, so the rope could always be combed back into its old holiness. [503] The work took four days. [507] Ester wept on the second day, brought tea on the third, and by the fourth had stopped pretending she was only watching. [529]

On the fifth night the aurora stood directly overhead, and they carried the ladder to the organ. [546] Mira threaded the rope into the keyframe, set the first rank against the pins, and turned the crank. [564] The bellows sighed, the pipes caught, and the icebound sanctum filled with names. [577] Karsfjord's names had been tunes all along, three rising notes for a fisherman, a falling fifth for a child lost young, the whole rope a hymn unplayed for four hundred years. [608] The wardens stood in the moving light and received their ancestors the way snow receives warmth. [624] Then the organ played eleven notes that Mira knew by heart: Aunt Rusa's ending, braided here by some grandmother whose grandchild had wandered south to punch cards. [651]

Her scrap had been an orphan of this rope, and now it had found its family. [667] Mira laughed and cried at once, and the sound she made was thaw. [680] Heat gathered from lanterns and busy bellows until a bead of meltwater let go of the ceiling and struck the snow like a small bell. [705] All winter the sanctum had held its trust frozen; now, under the swaying green fire, it loosened name by name, drop by drop. [728] Before she left, Mira would cast the code in bronze, a mold shaped around absence the way her craft had always been, but first she cranked the organ again, and again, and let every forgotten name ring out of the ice while the aurora burned and the vault, warmed by such ardor, wept clean snowmelt.
