The sycamores kept their hush even when the rock lifted, even when Dr. Vane stepped aboard and the whole floating platform swayed beneath her, gentle as breath. [27] She was fifty-one that evening, though she had been seventy when she recruited me, and would be a child by spring. [48] Her whole research existed because of that arithmetic: she studied what vanishing leaves behind. [62]

The meditation platform, a slab of iron-shot stone the size of a chapel floor, hung above the pond because of what lay beneath the water: the magnetic grotto her instruments had found forty years before, a cave of lodestone that starlight stirred. [104] On clear nights the field strengthened and the rock rose, and the same starlight that stirred the grotto ran her years backwards faster, so that on the strongest nights she lost whole years by the hour. [140] She could have hidden from the sky and kept her years, but she had chosen the strongest night of autumn, because a lesson withheld too long is a lesson lost. [170]

"Tomorrow," she said, "I will be too young for the work, and the work will have to be yours." [189] I asked her, then, for the finding itself, the conclusion of forty years, and she only smiled, because Dr. Vane never lied and never simply told; she wove truth carefully, the way sycamores let light down in pieces. [227] "Withholding is not hiding," she said. [233] "It is timing." [236]

Then she chose a stone from the platform's rim, round and dark, and held it flat on her palm for me to see. [259] "Watch what the water does with an ending," she said, and dropped it over the edge. [275] The stone fell into the pond and vanished at once. [285] The rings began. [288]

She bade me lie back, breathe with the leaves, and follow the circles out until the trance took me, because the lesson, she said, lived through the trance and nowhere else. [319] I went under the way she had taught me, into the sycamore hush, and the night grew long and slow. [339] Through the trance, the rings did not fade; they widened, each circle keeping its whole history as it traveled, so that the pond became a book written outward. [367] I saw that the first ring was gone before the last ring was born, and still the last ring carried the first inside it, the way her seventies were folded inside her fifties, the way my own infancy waits inside my age. [409] The stone was nowhere in the pond. [416] The pattern was the stone. [421]

Deeper in the trance, I saw beneath the water and beneath the mud to the grotto itself, its lodestones strung with cold light, holding our rock aloft without touching it. [451] Starlight poured into the cave mouth, the field brightened, and I felt the platform rise another hand's width under us, as if the sky were reeling us in to hear something. [482] I thought that was the finding: that nothing the magnet holds is touching it. [496] But the night withheld me a while longer, because Dr. Vane had taught me that revelations arrive late or they do not arrive at all. [521]

Hours on, a small weight leaned against my shoulder, and I found a girl of eight beside me, wrapped in her own outgrown coat, watching the water with the same patient eyes. [553] "The rings reach the shore before the stone finishes sinking," she said, in a child's voice, with a scientist's patience. [573] "That is the finding, and I could not tell you before, because a told thing is only a sentence." [592] Then the trance took her under too, and she slept against my arm, shrinking toward the beginning of herself. [611]

In the trance's last hour, the final ring touched the far bank, and I understood what she had withheld, and why her whole method had been the message. [639] Vanishing is not the end of appearing; it is the slowest way of appearing, the outermost ring, the pattern a stone becomes when it stops being a stone. [667] She had spent forty years learning to teach vanishing to appear, and she had done it at last by vanishing, ring by ring, out of adulthood and into me. [696] Dawn came up through the sycamores in the same hush, gold and slow. [709] The platform settled as the stars went out, and the girl who had been my teacher slept on, smaller than memory. [730] I picked a stone from the rim, round and dark, and threw it as far as I could. [748] The water opened, and opened, and I settled into her lesson: to watch, to wait, and to let the truth arrive as slowly as it must. [774]
