Rain beaded the skylight of Odile's loft, each drop a lens bending gray light over a city still numbed after the crashes. [22] Below her, the tower that had housed the last cryptocurrency exchange stood dark, its screens sold for scrap. [40] Only the night guard, Marek, still climbed the stairs, sharing her kettle because the vending machines had gone with everything else. [61] Odile was a cartomancer, third of her line, and she worked exclusively in onyx ink, grinding the stone herself until the black ran like liquid midnight. [87] Her cards were maps of nothing on earth: hills of appetite, rivers of rumor, coastlines shaped like crowds. [105]

She had inherited them from Zofia, her grandmother, along with one instruction written in Polish on the lid of a wooden box: start at the wreckage and walk backward. [134] That was the family method, and its logic was reverse engineering. [145] You took the disaster, the thing everyone could see, and worked backward card by card until you found the first small spark that people had mistaken for a sunrise. [174] Tonight the wreckage was the crash itself. [181]

Odile knelt on the wide floor beneath the skylight and laid the final card first: a black square she called Ruin. [202] Then the card of panic, then leverage, then belief, then boredom, then the oldest card of all, Hunger. [220] From her box she took Zofia's measuring tape, a tailor's ribbon gone soft as cloth from decades of thumbs. [239] Distance was meaning in this discipline. [245] She measured from Ruin to Panic: eleven inches, close, terror sprinting behind disaster. [258] From Belief to Ruin: four feet, a generation's length, far enough that no one believes the bridge will ever fall. [278] Then came the method Zofia had guarded most jealously: multiplying paths. [289] Odile pinned black thread between cards, not one route but every route a mind might take from boredom to belief to leverage to panic, and counted them, and multiplied. [318] Where the threads thickened into a knot, there the crowd had gathered, and where they thinned, there the careful few had walked out early, rich. [343]

Marek came up at midnight with two chipped cups and watched her work without asking, the way he had watched the exchange's green candles fall without being allowed to say a word. [375] She was steadily curious, never in a hurry, asking the cards one small question at a time, and this patience was why she let him see. [401]

"You think I read futures," she said. [408] "I don't. [410] I map cultural patterns. [414]

She confided in him then, which she had done for no one living. [427] Her line had read tulips in 1637, railway shares in 1847, and dot-coms in 2000, always in some borrowed room, always with this same tape. [452] The pattern never changed, only its costume. [459] Every crash rearranged itself into the same constellation of want. [469] "And this deck predicts nothing," she said. [476] "It remembers. [478]

Marek looked at the web of thread, then at the last card in the box, which was blank, waiting. [497] Zofia had died before inking it. [503] Her note said only: the blank card is the next hunger, and you will know its face when the wreckage comes. [524] Odile dipped her needle in onyx ink. [531]

This was the inherited mystery that kept her awake at night: not whether the method worked, but why the women before her had trusted it without proof, as if they had once seen the ledger of crowds and never spoken of it. [573] She drew slowly, watching her own hand as if it belonged to the lineage and not to her. [591] On the blank card a shape emerged: not a coin, not a rocket, but a door standing open in a dark field. [613]

She measured its distance from Ruin, from Belief, from Hunger, and laid thread along every path that might lead a bored world through it. [637] Four feet, again. [640] A generation. [642]

Marek touched the card's edge with one careful finger. [651] "What's through the door?" he asked. [657] "Whatever we decide is free," she said, and rolled the tape back into its coil. [672]

Above them the rain stopped, and the skylight cleared bead by bead, as if the loft itself were blinking awake. [692] Odile placed the new card in the box beside Zofia's note, face down, a mystery folded inside an inheritance. [711] When the next hunger came, someone would start at the wreckage and walk backward, measuring, multiplying, remembering. [728] Marek refilled the kettle. [732] Outside, the ruined tower caught the first light and, for one moment, looked like something worth believing in. [750]
