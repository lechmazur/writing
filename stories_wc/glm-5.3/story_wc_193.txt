Maren had volunteered for the deep shift on Material Night, alone in the gutted submarine where the co-op farmed its bioluminescent mushrooms, because a decade underground had taught her that pressure was unfinished work. [34] The navy had sold the hull cheap; the co-op buried it in a hillside and seeded the racks with oyster mushrooms that glowed blue along their gills, a seam of light stitched through the dark. [69] Out in the world, once, she had believed the Lattice, a pyramid that promised her wealth while it drank her savings and her sister Thea's, and she had learned the only alchemy that never lied: to translate pressure into diamonds, you must survive the press. [114] Once a decade, on Material Night, concepts became physical: kept promises hardened into glowing stones, broken ones collapsed to ash, and the undecided ones, the quantum promises, flickered between both states until somebody kept or broke them for real. [153] Hers arrived at midnight, settling on the rack beside her like a struck match that refused to go out: I'll make us whole, Thea, I swear it. [180] Then her phone buzzed with glitchy text from parallel dimensions, letters sliding like the far side was trying to remember how English worked. [203] G0T OUT IN MY W0RLD, read the first, from a Maren who had never joined the Lattice; then a second, from a Maren who had climbed to its crown: C0ME THRU THE P0RTH0LE, BR1NG UR PR0MISE, I'LL PAY UR S1STER, U CAN SK1P THE PRESSURE. [248] The old porthole in the forward hull had gone soft at the edges, a seam in the world exactly porthole-sized, held shut by a single brass submarine porthole bolt the size of her fist. [282] Since midnight the bolt had been turning itself out, thread by patient thread, and the messages leaked through the widening gap. [303] That was the Lattice's old offer in new clothes, wealth without weight, and she almost laughed at how every dimension kept the same con. [327] But Maren had survived the pyramid by learning its tricks, and she had spent years since spending them only on predators, which is what virtuously mischievous means when you have been burned and decide to become useful fire. [365] She texted back, pretending to be a third Maren, a regulator from a dimension further along the seam, offering licensing rights to a fresh world of recruits. [392] She fed the crown-Maren the exact script that had once hooked her, flattery first, scarcity second, the chance to be first through a door that was closing. [419] By three in the morning the rich Maren, greedy for a downline of entire worlds, had paid the price the fake regulator demanded, her whole fortune wired to a restitution fund for the people the Lattice had flattened, and was still thanking her for the opportunity. [465] Which left the harder work, her own. [472] The quantum promise weighed nothing and everything, ash one blink, sapphire the next, and she carried it to the ballast chamber where the earth leaned hardest on the hull. [501] There she did the unglamorous thing no scheme ever sells: she called Thea, said the true numbers out loud, admitted every loss, and promised, without ornament, twelve years of monthly payments. [532] Kept promises are the only ones that get heavy, she had told the new hires, and as she spoke the words the flickering thing stopped choosing and condensed, pressure finally doing what pressure does to anything that holds. [570] In her palm sat a diamond, small and flawed and real, worth maybe three months of payments, which was exactly the point. [592] At four she screwed the great brass bolt home until the porthole closed and the seam between worlds sealed with a sound like a held breath released. [619] The phone buzzed once more, glitching: G00D, the never-joined Maren wrote, NOW WATER UR MUSHR00MS. [634] At shift's end she tried the bolt again and it spun out freely, its seam gone, so she threaded it onto a cord beside the diamond, one weight she had earned and one she had held. [670] Dawn never reached the farm, but the mushrooms brightened anyway when the surface world woke, ten thousand small blue lamps along the shadowseam, and she watered them, humming, a survivor keeping her small lights fed.
