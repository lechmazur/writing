The dome had been standing for forty years, and the desert had spent every one of them writing on it. [20]
Wind-driven grit had etched the glass in fine white runes, so that noon came through softened, a blaze made tranquil, light like warm water on the skin. [47]
Wren Vane called it engraved quiet, and she trusted it more than any treaty ever signed in the capital. [66]
Inside, she farmed desert roses, row upon row of them, in beds of sand her father's airship had carried down from the northern wastes. [90]
Her grandmother had taught her the trade, and one rule above all: roses learn what they hear. [107]
So each morning Wren knelt at the seedling bench and gave them their lessons, patience spoken slowly, mercy pronounced with care, telling them that thorns were for holding on, never for opening wounds. [140]
The town below called her senseless for it, senseless and worse, the bounty hunter's daughter who would not carry a blade, who knew things she had no way of knowing. [170]
That was the other thing about her. [177]
She was senselessly perceptive, feeling weather in her teeth and grief in the wind two days before either arrived. [196]
Nine days ago the roses had all leaned east at once, in still air, and she had known something enormous was coming. [218]
Yesterday the horizon confirmed it: a smear of smoke where the capital was, and riders coming up the wash with the news. [240]
The Guild Tower had fallen. [245]
It had stood a thousand feet over the harbor, holding every bounty ledger, every contract, every warrant in the known world, and now it was rubble, and the warrants were ash, and the trade her father had given his life to was scattered on the wind like ballast. [293]
Wren did not grieve for the tower. [300]
She grieved for the chaos it would leave, for the hunters with no law to answer to, and she began, that same hour, to weave. [325]
She took cuttings from every mother rose in the dome, wrapped their stems in damp cloth, and set them in the cool room, so that whatever broke, the line would hold. [356]
"Chaos takes what is loose," her grandmother used to say. [366]
"Weave what you want to keep." [372]
Two mornings after the news, she woke before dawn with the taste of engine oil in her mouth and knew her father was coming. [396]
The Kestrel came over the rim of the basin at first light, riding low, and dropped ballast to make the dome's landing shelf. [419]
One sandbag burst on the gutter and split, spilling its northern sand down the glass in a long hiss. [438]
Wren climbed the ladder and gathered the torn canvas, a rough scrap stamped with the Guild's anchor and her father's number, and folded it into her apron without knowing yet why. [469]
Marek Vane stepped off the gangplank looking ten years older than the man who had left in spring. [487]
His arm was bound badly; the warrant bag on his shoulder was empty. [500]
"The tower fell on the archive floor," he said. [509]
"Thirty years of contracts, gone in an hour." [517]
"There is no Guild, Wren." [522]
"There are men who did the work with me, and no one left to tell us we are done." [541]
She sat him on the bench among the seedlings and cleaned the wound while he talked, and the roses leaned toward his voice the way they leaned toward her lessons. [571]
"Then it is your turn to learn something," she said. [581]
That evening she took the scrap of ballast canvas from her apron, cut it clean, and sewed it into a sheath, double-stitched, the Guild's anchor showing on the face. [610]
Her father watched from the doorway, understanding her better than he wanted to. [623]
He drew the long knife he had carried since before her birth, the one she had never once seen drawn in anger, and held it out. [649]
Wren sheathed it. [652]
Then she carried the sheathed blade to the empty bed at the dome's center, pushed it down into the sand until only the pommel showed, and planted a young climber beside it. [684]
"A rose needs something to hold," she told him. [693]
"Hold this." [695]
In the morning he was at the seedling bench, awkward and careful, pronouncing patience to a row of cuttings. [714]
The sun came up full flame off the dunes, and the engraved glass took it all and made it gentle, quiet written in fire, the whole dome glowing like a coal that had decided to be kind. [751]
Chaos had taken the tower, the trade, the warrant law of the world. [764]
Here, it found a line already woven, root to graft to cutting, and could not find a loose end anywhere. [784]
