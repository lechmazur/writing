After revealing the secret, the Warden had expected the sea to feel heavier. [13] Instead it felt lighter, and the diver Isla proved it by returning at dawn, sinking through the green dark toward the forgotten gardens of drowned Vesper. [39] Her lamplight found the dome's broken crown, then the rows of pale figures standing among the coral like guests interrupted mid-festival. [60] The secret was this: when fever came down from the hills four hundred years ago, the city's gardener had given everyone the foxglove tincture, slowing three hundred hearts to a whisper, then opened the floodgates so the cold sea could hold them safe until the fever passed. [107] No one came back. [111] Forgetting settled over the water like silt, and the gardener stood watch among her sleepers until stone climbed her too, though her mind stayed awake inside it. [138]

"You came back," said a voice that used no sound at all, pressing itself directly into her mind. [156] The Warden's stone face had not moved in four centuries, but her attention was alive, and it was kind the way a scalpel is kind, cutting clean so wounds close. [186]

"Tell me the rest," Isla said. [192] So the cascade began, each truth knocking loose the next. [202] Yes, the sleepers dreamed; the Warden heard them, hundreds of small bright minds circling the same festival night. [220] Yes, the dreams were dimming, one candle each year, and past a certain dimness a sleeper could not be reached. [240] And yes, waking them was possible, the truth the Warden had never dared say to herself. [256]

Isla's hand went to the pouch at her hip. [265] Inside lay the family keepsake: a foxglove-stained glass ampoule, violet ghosts of old doses pressed into its base. [283] The Warden's attention sharpened to a point of pure recognition. [292] "Where did you get that?" [297] "My grandmother," Isla said. [301] The next truth fell: that grandmother had been the Warden's apprentice, up on the hills gathering foxglove when the gates opened, spared the sleep by an accident of errands. [330] She never found the way back, but the ampoule and her maps came down the family like a held breath, and Isla finished the journey. [355]

"She did not abandon us," the Warden said, incisively kind, cutting the old shame out of Isla in one clean stroke. [376] "She was fetching the other half of the cure." [385] "Then they can wake," Isla whispered. [391] "A drop of the tincture stopped each heart," the Warden said. [402] "A smaller drop, placed rightly, can start one." [410] That was the revelation under all the others: the poison and the bell were the same liquid, and only the dose decided. [432] The Warden had been certain for four hundred years that her tincture only ever ended things. [448] Tonight she was trying, for the first time, to welcome nuance into that certainty, and her stone hands trembled, mineral grinding softly. [470]

She turned her gaze toward the youngest sleeper, a girl no older than twelve, frozen with a half-eaten honeycake raised to her lips. [493] "I built a flaw into each of them," the Warden said. [504] "At the breastbone, a channel, a weakness." [511] "I could not tell you why I did it then." [521] "I can tell you now," Isla said, understanding even as she spoke: doubt had been the mercy all along. [540] The method would be through structural weakness, the Warden's own shameful craft turned into a door. [556]

Kneeling in the silt, Isla drew the ampoule, thumbed loose the stopper, and threaded its slim glass neck down through the channel in the girl's chest until it rested against the stone shell of a sleeping heart. [593] One drop, the Warden thought into her, smaller than a grain of salt. [606] The drop fell, violet unwinding in the dark like a ribbon of ink. [619] For a long moment the garden held still, three hundred statues and one living one, all listening. [636] Then the stone ticked. [640] Then it ticked again, and again, finding a rhythm, a heartbeat learning to keep time. [655]

The girl's fingers tightened around the honeycake; her lashes moved; a bubble slipped from her lips and rose like a small bright thought. [678] Her eyes opened, and the whole drowned garden seemed to inhale, coral swaying, kelp lifting, silt rising in slow golden veils where no current moved. [703] The lifeless had only been the paused, and now everything animated: light swimming, the Warden laughing without lungs, one child blinking up through four hundred years of water, breathing it like an old song. [737] "Grandmother sent me," Isla told her, watching her oldest certainty dissolve: her family had not simply been gone, they had been on their way. [761] Above them, in the cracked crown of the dome where air still pooled, the last foxglove of Vesper stirred its purple bells, as if it had always known the flaw was the door. [794]
