The medication gone, my hands remembered how to shake. [9] Six weeks without the little white pills, and the river came back loud, every stone arguing under the current. [28] I am the county's river chronicler, which means I record the truth of water: where it has moved, where the maps still lie. [51] Since stopping the medication I have been daringly methodical about it, wading the cold edges, checking each grid reference twice, climbing the weir wall while the spray tried to peel my notebook away. [84] Feeling everything is supposed to be the reward. [91] Nobody warns you that everything includes the night I still owe a correction for. [105] I stopped the pills because grief medicated is grief deferred, and I wanted, at last, to deserve the truth I chase for a living. [129]

My brother Sam believed maps the way other people believe priests. [140] Four Aprils ago, reference 5107 still showed a ford where the river had quietly moved a half-mile east, and I had known that for eleven days when he drove out to check my survey markers. [175] The pills made eleven days feel like a reasonable pause. [185] I will not write down what the river did with the truck; the truth of water has chapters I refuse to chronicle. [207]

My survey line ends where the railway used to, at a disused station overgrown with ivy, green swallowing the platform numbers one tendril at a time. [233] The last train ran before I was born; Sam and I played here as children, and later he busked summer evenings under a glass roof that let the light through like cider. [265] His accordion still waits on the bench in the booking hall where I left it after I dried it out, bellows patched with silver tape from an old repair of his. [296] I could not keep it at the house, and I could not throw it in the water, so I gave it to the ivy. [320]

That evening the rain came sideways, and I sheltered in the gents' room off the platform, whose walls hold forty years of what the idle and the stranded know. [349] Bathroom graffiti is the most honest archive in the county. [359] Under a rusted mirror someone had carved: WRONG MAPS STILL GET YOU SOMEWHERE. [372] Beneath it, in green marker: THE RIVER KEEPS NO SCORE. [382] And low on the tile, in handwriting I would know anywhere, because I had read it on birthday cards my whole life: SORRY IS A DIRECTION, NOT A DESTINATION. [411] Sam had written it the summer before, between trains that no longer came. [424] He used to say these walls were the only newspaper that admitted it was written by drunks and angels. [443] The rain hammered the roof, and the words over the sink argued back: FORGIVE YOUR GPS; YOU STILL CHOSE THE ROAD. [464]

I stood at the mirror and alluded to the thing out loud, the way you test ice. [481] Eleven days, I told my reflection. [487] A form I meant to file. [493] A pause, I said, that outlived its reason. [501] The words never named what the pause cost, and the walls did not need them to. [517]

In the morning I did the methodical thing first, because daring is mostly sequence: reference 5107, ford deleted, new channel surveyed, my name signed under the correction like a confession with coordinates. [549] Then I carried the accordion to the platform and worked the taped bellows until they hissed and held. [567] The tape had survived the water once; tape is stubborn the way surviving brothers have to be. [584] I played the only three chords he ever taught me, badly, and the ivy shivered as if the station remembered being a place where sound was expected. [611]

Self-forgiveness, I am learning, is not a verdict. [619] It is a path, and like the river it moves. [629] Some mornings it runs a half-mile east of where I need it. [641] So I chronicle it the way I chronicle the water: reference by reference, lie by lie, correction by correction. [660]

The station keeps its ivy. [665] The accordion keeps its tape. [670] I keep the notebook, the shaking hands, and the eleven days, which I no longer carry as a reference to a place I refuse to visit, but as the first line of a longer survey. [705] The river, at least, tells the truth now. [713]
