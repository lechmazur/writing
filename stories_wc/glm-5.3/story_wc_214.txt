The shooting star tracking station sat high on the ridge, its copper dome gone green, forgotten by the era that built it. [22] The astronomers called those overlooked decades the Hush: the long gap between comet schedules, when the academies moved their great lenses to brighter mountains and the mail came twice a year. [53] Venna stayed. [55]

The Ministry's official title for her position was cosmic janitor, a phrase that had begun as a clerk's joke, though Venna came to love it for its honesty: she tidied up after the universe. [89] Each morning she swept the observation decks with a broom worn to the shape of her hands. [106] Each night she traced the path of every falling star the old instruments caught, then walked the ridgelines to gather whatever had come down. [130]

The stars fell whether or not anyone watched. [138] That was the first lesson of the work, and it hollowed her at first, the way indifference hollows. [156] Then, one autumn night, a star came down burning, and when she followed its arc across the ledger page, something in the geometry of the fall made her chest ache. [186] It had burned brightest at the very end. [194] Venna had always been spiritually curious, the child who asked priests questions they could not answer, and now the question rose in her again: where do the falling ones go? [224] The question was not really about stars. [231]

Over the years she traced their paths the way a physician traces a pulse, and she began to find the fallen: small beads of cooled starlight, dark and plain as frozen tears, scattered through the heather. [267] One fragment was different: a flake of yellow lantern glass, warm as a held hand, that glowed faintly whenever the moon rose. [289] Its light was the color of kitchens, of windows seen from a cold road. [303] She kept it on the sill and spoke to it sometimes. [314] "You fell," she would tell it. [320] "Everything that falls was first held up by something." [329]

The Hush deepened around her, years stacking like snowfall. [338] The Ministry's letters ceased, and no visitor climbed the ridge for eleven winters. [351] And here is what the busy academies would never have believed: the station flourished in that stillness. [368] Moss took the north walls soft and deep. [376] Owls raised their young in the empty instrument housings. [385] The silence was not an absence but a soil, and things grew in it, including Venna. [401]

She had come to the mountain grieving and armored, having lost her brother to a war whose name kept changing. [421] At first she had swept the floors like someone punishing them. [432] But the falling stars taught her otherwise, each in its own small grammar of descent. [447] A star, she came to understand, is most naked at the end: stripped of its orbit, its history, its company, burning in the open where anyone may watch. [475] And it is in that nakedness that it blazes. [484]

"A star in its orbit is a rumor," she wrote in the ledger's margin. [498] "A falling star is a fact you can feel." [507] Tracing their paths night after night, she learned to empathize with the fallen, because she was fallen herself, and the learning was slow and unglamorous, the way true things are. [537] Some nights she carried the yellow glass to the dome, set it on the rail, and spoke the grief she had never permitted herself, and the glass glowed brighter, as if listening, as if grief shared were grief halved. [576]

She was not strong in the way the Ministry meant strength. [587] She wept at the smallest falls, sparks that lasted half a heartbeat. [599] But she stopped hiding it, and that stopping was the strongest act of her life. [614] Here was strength in vulnerability, learned not from scripture but from falling light. [627]

Decades later, when the comet schedules swung back, young astronomers climbed the ridge and found a spotless station, a ledger of ten thousand falling stars traced in a steady hand, and an old woman who had grown as calm as the mountain itself. [670] In the margin of the last page, they read what she had written: [683]

"I feared the quiet, so I listened until it sang. [693] I feared falling, so I followed those who fell, and found they were not ending but arriving. [710] Light does not die when it lands. [717] It becomes other things: glass, warmth, seed. [724] The stars never asked to be caught. [731] They asked to be accompanied. [736] So I went with them, every one, all the way down." [747]

On the sill, the yellow lantern glass glowed on, warm as a small sun that had decided, at last, to stay. [768]
