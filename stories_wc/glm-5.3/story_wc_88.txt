They told me the Sweep takes only what you ask it to take. [13] After the procedure, I woke in a rented room under a name, Elias Marsh, that fit like borrowed shoes. [32] In my pocket was the one thing I did not remember owning: a baseball, worn smooth, signed in green ink, FOR THE MAN I WILL BE, THE LAKE REMEMBERS, E., 2049. [63] My own initial, dated thirty years ahead of me: a forger's joke or a time traveler's receipt. [80] The clinic said my dirty past was my business, already erased. [91] Clean slate, they promised. [95] What they never mentioned is that empathy goes out with the wreckage; the Sweep cannot tell sediment from water. [114] Afterward I held everything at arm's length: dogs, funerals, the smell of bread. [127]

Except newspapers. [129] I hoarded them, an old habit with its memory amputated, and read them at night. [144] One evening, reading between the lines, literally between them, in the pale gutters where ink should not live, I found letters: BENEATH ASHTON STATION, THE STAIRS THAT BREATHE. [172] The next night a different paper whispered: LEFT AT THE DROWNED BELL. [184] Someone was writing to me in the whitespace. [192] I followed. [194]

The journey down took eleven days, through dead subway tunnels and older things beneath them, the papers in my satchel decoding one junction after another. [219] Then the walls began to glow. [225] The lake lay at the bottom of an underground cavern so vast the light bent around it, blue and cold and alive. [247] Bioluminescent algae traced every ripple, a delicately robust clockwork of creatures living on almost nothing, ancient and breakable as breath. [267] Between the lines of a water-stained sports page, the lake's secret: this is where the Sweep puts what it takes. [287] Not destroyed, displaced. [290] Every memory scrubbed from every client drains down here and feeds the light. [303]

The tunnels around the lake split and re-split, a labyrinth of choices, water rising and falling so that yesterday's path drowned overnight. [325] Every junction asked the only question I had left: turn away, or go through. [339] I went through. [342] The papers steered me still; a line about harbor seals, read sideways, said THE BELL RINGS WHEN THE LIGHT DIMS. [362] At the center of the labyrinth (I refuse to believe that was an accident) stood a dock, a lantern, and an old man wearing my face. [388]

"You're me," I said. [392] "Older," he agreed. [395] "I came back the long way, from 2049, from a city where nobody has empathy left to lose, because we sold the Sweep to everyone." [420] He told me the rest, since one of us had to remember it. [433] I had been a technician, not a client; I had administered the Sweep eight hundred and forty times, to debtors, to deserters, to mothers who couldn't stop grieving loudly enough for their landlords. [466] My dirty past was eight hundred broken empathies, and I had paid to forget I was the knife. [484] "I can't fix that future," he said. [491] "I can only leave a trail for the one person innocent enough to follow it and guilty enough to matter." [511] "The ball is my receipt; I signed it in 2049 and planted it on you the night before your procedure." [531] "You can't get your past back," he said. [539] "It's down there, diluted in everyone else's." [546] "But you can carry up a cup and start kindling." [556]

Kindling empathy where none remains, in a city scrubbed numb: that was the job, and the job was mine. [575] I knelt at the waterline and drank. [582] It was not forgiveness. [586] It was ten thousand ordinary mornings arriving at once; I wept at a stranger's wedding I had never attended and mourned a dead dog I had never owned. [614] The lake brightened where my tears landed, and I am too old and too new to pretend that wasn't beautiful. [634] The old man was already gone, the way travelers go, between one blink and the next line of newsprint. [653]

I filled a canteen, pocketed the ball, and started back through the labyrinth toward the city of the numbed. [672] The last junction offered the drowned bell or the breathing stairs. [683] Both would flood by morning; the lake gives each path only one night. [696] I chose the stairs, of course. [702] Wonder is a jaded animal; it bites the hand that starved it. [714] But it followed me up anyway, muzzle pressed to the canteen. [725] Somewhere above, a thousand swept-clean faces were waiting to feel something, and I had eleven days of climbing to learn how to share what I carried. [751] The procedure gave me a clean slate. [758] I intend to dirty it, carefully, with everyone I can reach. [769]
