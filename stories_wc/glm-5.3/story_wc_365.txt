Mira Voss made sensory maps of things no instrument could measure. [11] She charted the texture of silence on empty moons, the taste of foreign rain, the way light leaned in a station built by homesick engineers. [36] Her workshop was the repair bay of a spaceport abandoned for thirty years, its gantries draped in dust, its tool racks stocked with instruments nobody alive remembered how to name. [66]

She worked here because the bay remembered. [73] Its walls had hummed when the jump gates were alive, and they hummed still, faintly, in the register of half-remembered dreams. [94] That murmur was her medium. [99] When she slept beneath the cracked cradle, the murmur rose, and in it doors opened into worlds that ran beside this one, parallel as train tracks, each a breath different from the last. [132]

Her key to those doors was a green glass shard no longer than her thumb, split from the great focusing lens the night the gates failed. [158] Through it, adjacent universes glimmered like sunlight on moving water. [168] The weavings came on their own schedule, cyclically spontaneous, as regular in their returns and as impossible to predict as weather. [189] Every ninth sleep or so, the murmur deepened, and she went where it took her. [204]

Other map makers charted for trade companies and survey offices. [214] Mira charted for one reason: her mother. [221] Elka Voss had been the lens-wright here, the keeper of the gates, and she had died in the collapse that shut the port forever. [245] That was the official record, and it weighed nothing. [254] But her mother's last letter weighed everything, and it had a hole in it. [268] Half of one page had been cut away with scissors, cleanly, deliberately, by a steady hand. [284] Mira wanted to know what a dying woman had chosen not to say; withheld truths have mass, and she could feel this one pulling, like a planet felt through the sole of a boot. [318]

On the night the cycle returned, she lay down with the shard on her sternum and let the murmur take her. [339] She wove sideways through parallel universes, threading between worlds the way her mother had taught her to thread a needle: gently, and expecting resistance. [363] In one world the port thrived and torches rang; in another it was rubble under a green sky; in a third she stood in a meadow where the gantries should have been. [395] Each version she pressed into the map, sense by sense, the way you take a cast of a key. [414] And in every one of them, faint as a pulse in the wrist, the murmur said a word that was almost her name. [437]

She followed the word down through a thousand worlds, faster than breath, until the murmur resolved into a voice, and the voice was Elka Voss. [462] "Mira," it said, from everywhere at once. [469] The truth arrived not in sentences but in sensation, the only language a map maker trusts: the night the gates failed, thirty thousand refugees had stood in the departures ring while the lens cracked under the weight of so many crossings. [510] The gates could be held open for one more minute, but only if something became the lens. [527] Elka had stepped into the focusing cradle and let the light take her apart, thread by thread, and had woven herself through the doors so that every refugee could be redirected into parallel universes where their wars had never happened. [567] The cut page had held this: not a death, but a scattering. [579] The murmur had always been her: dispersed across a thousand worlds, too thin to hold a body, thick enough to hold a word. [602]

Her mother had not withheld the truth to punish her; she had withheld it so that a child would not spend her life listening to walls, trying to hear her. [632] The weight of it settled into Mira like ballast, and she understood at last why secrets sink: they are carried, not lost. [654] "Stay," the voice asked, "or go." [660] Mira understood the offer: she could dissolve into the murmur and keep the doors open forever, or she could carry the truth home. [683] She chose a third thing, because map makers live in the spaces between choices. [697] Each night when the cycle rose, she took up the shard and redirected, through parallel universes, whatever needed moving: a fever out of a child on a dying colony, a storm off a settling fleet's path, her own grief sideways into a world built to hold it. [744] She never dissolved, and she never left. [751] And every ninth sleep, in the murmur of half-remembered dreams, a map maker and her mother kept the doors of an abandoned repair bay open together, weaving between worlds, one thread at a time. [785]
