Wren wrote poems for strangers at the roadside, three lines for a sandwich, a sonnet for a tank of gas. [20] She had been a prisoner when the energy beings arrived, and she was still one, in the way that mattered. [40] The night the sky changed, the walls of Ironvale flickered like candle flames, and she went over them on a rope that was already coming apart. [66] She kept the frayed coil in her pack, knots still tied, one for every decision made on the way down. [86] Seven knots. [88] The sixth was the one she could not look at, the one where Dax climbed below her and the rope began to sing. [111] She had cut it. [115] Dax told her to, and the telling did not make the doing lighter. [128]

After the arrival, time stopped being a road and became weather. [139] It pooled in hollows, ran fast along the interstates, thickened in old rooms. [152] People blamed the beings, though nobody had proof, only the sense of something vast and delighted paying attention. [170] Wren drifted because drifting let her feel the grain of hours move against her skin, and she wanted, with a want that embarrassed her, to know the texture of time itself. [201]

On a night in autumn she sheltered in a terra-cotta grotto off Route 9, an arched hollow of baked orange clay where truckers once left candles for a saint nobody remembered. [232] Starlight came through a split in the roof and lay across the floor like a spilled thread. [249] A being was already there, folded into the light the way heat folds into metal. [264] It had no face and yet seemed about to grin. [274] "You carry your history in knots," it said, pleased as a child who has found a beetle. [291] "Show me yours," Wren said, "and I'll show you mine." [301] The being laughed in colors. [306] They loved games, everyone knew; they were ethically playful the way rivers are physically wet, without malice, without mercy, only appetite. [327]

"A trade," it said. [331] "I will show you the texture of time, all of it, the thing you beg for in your sleep. [350] In exchange, untie one knot, and the choice it holds never happened. [362] Any knot," it said, glowing with anticipation. [369] "Untie the sixth and Dax falls differently, or does not fall, or you fall together; the pattern will decide. [388] Untie the first and you never leave your cell at all." [399]

Wren set the coil on the clay floor between them. [409] "Show me first," she said. [414] "Greedy," the being said, delighted. [419]

It reached into the grotto wall and peeled it like an orange skin, and under the terra-cotta was another terra-cotta, older, water-marked, and under that another, and another, layers laid down by centuries of floods. [454] This, it said without saying, was time's true shape, not a line but a sediment, every moment resting on every other. [475] Wren touched the innermost layer and felt her own hand, small and sunburned, thirty years before, pressing wet clay at a riverbank. [497] She wept without noise, the only way she had ever wept. [508]

The being peeled deeper, and the layers began to hold choices. [519] Here was a woman at a door with two keys and one turn. [532] Here was a doctor with two patients and one vial. [542] Here was a boy choosing which parent to stand beside. [552] Every layer held one, and every choosing had a grain, a nap, a direction, like fabric stroked with or against. [572] That was the texture, she understood at last. [580] Time felt like choices. [584] Its smooth parts were hours when nothing asked anything of anyone; its burrs, its frays, its beautiful roughened patches were every moment someone had to cut or hold. [612] The rope on the floor was suddenly the most honest thing she had ever owned. [627]

"Choose," the being said, glowing like a struck match. [636]

Wren knelt and took the coil, and instead of untying the sixth knot she untied nothing at all. [654] She tied an eighth. [658] Her fingers knew the work; they had been learning it all her drifting life. [672] Not a door but a pattern, a way of answering the game by playing past it. [688] "The choice stands," she said. [693] "I'm keeping the rough patch." [698]

"You were supposed to pick a door," the being said, its light flickering, uncertain for the first time in its long delighted existence. [721] "I know," Wren said. [725] "That was my choice." [729] The being considered the eighth knot, laughed a color she had no word for, and folded itself back into the starlight. [750]

In the morning she walked the shoulder of Route 9, hands rough on the pack straps, feeling time rasp gently against her like rope through a careful grip. [778]
