<story>
Iva sang to the courtyard every morning, and every morning the mist drank her voice whole. [16] They called her the seldom-heard balladeer of Cael Station, though 'they' was generous, for only the wind and the storm-bells had ever listened. [39] The courtyard sat above the clouds, a shelf of gray flagstone where tinkers had once tuned the mountain's weather. [58] The tinkers were gone now, all but Alder, and Alder was three winters dead. [72]

It began, as most things did at that altitude, with a storm. [84] Iva was driftingly alert that night, the way the mist made her, her thoughts adrift on the ridgelines while her eyes missed nothing. [107] When the lightning came, it lit the courtyard walls, and on them she saw graffiti that had not been there at dusk. [129] Dots and petals, punched in pale pigment across the stone. [139] Then the thunder arrived, and the marks seemed to sink back into the mist, shy of sound. [156] She learned to count the interval, three heartbeats, four, and in the gap between lightning and thunder the graffiti glowed its brightest. [178] The pigment was storm-glass dust bound in cloudmilk, Alder's old recipe, and it woke only in lightning. [195]

Every storm after that, new marks bloomed, and Iva copied them into her songbook with cold fingers. [212] They were bell-code, the tinker shorthand, each dot a note and each petal a rest. [227] She knew the code because the storm-bells still spoke it, and lately they spoke it wrong. [243] Some nights they rang phrases nobody had wound into them, and the valley folk below muttered that the old machine had a ghost in its code. [269] Iva had always called that superstition. [275] She stopped calling it anything. [280]

The graffiti decoded, storm by storm, into fragments of a man's unsaid things: NOT DONE. [295] WHO KEEPS. [297] STAY. [298] Needs Alder had never once said aloud in life, hammered into shorthand by a man who could only confess to weather. [319]

She found the trick of it in his workshop, beneath a tarp of pressed flowers gone brown as old paper: a flower press bolt plate, steel, its four bolt-holes bright with use, an engraved spray of daisies worn soft by his thumb. [361] Alder had used the plate as a stencil, blowing pigment through the four bolt-holes for dots and the engraved daisies for petals. [383] Storm-glass dust still glittered in the grooves. [390] He had been answering the sky for years, she understood, saying to storms what he could not say to people. [410] And the machine he left behind had learned his habit. [420] The bells drank lightning through their rods, and in every surge they dreamed his stencils onto the walls with the pigment he had left waiting, printing his unfinished phrase again and again because he had died mid-sentence. [457] A ghost in the code, still asking. [464]

Iva knew about unfinished phrases. [469] She carried a pocketful of her own, every song she had ever swallowed instead of singing. [485] She had never asked Alder to teach her the bells. [495] She had never told anyone she was lonely, or that she sang to mist because mist was safer than ears. [515] Some needs go stale in the mouth if you keep them too long. [528]

So she mixed the pigment herself, storm-glass dust and cloudmilk, tasting weather on her knuckles, and waited out the next squall with the bolt plate in her lap like an instrument. [559] When the sky finally split, she pressed the plate to the wall and blew. [573] Between the lightning and the thunder, in the held breath of the mountain, her answer glowed: I STAY. [591] TEACH ME. [593] She encoded her whole unsung life into it, dot by dot, petal by petal, every need she had never found a door for. [616]

The thunder that followed was no dull single note. [625] The bells took his phrase and hers and resolved them together, note into note, the way two instruments find a chord, and out of the cloud deck rolled a finished ballad. [656] And because Iva was a balladeer down to her marrow, she opened her mouth and sang the descant. [674] The thunder carried it down the mountain like cupped hands. [684] In the valley that night, farmers set down their cups and asked each other who was singing. [701] On the wall, in the last flash, bloomed one more message, painted where a signature goes: a single pressed daisy. [721] The ghost had said what it needed, and so had she. [732]

Now every storm is a duet above the clouds. [741] Iva paints her needs in lightning, the bells agree in thunder, and the valley below has learned her name. [760] The seldom-heard balladeer is seldom heard no more. [768]
</story>
