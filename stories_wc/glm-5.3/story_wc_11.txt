After the procedure, JN-4 was returned to its broom. [9] The courtyard it swept stayed frozen at the exact minute of twilight, because time here belonged to the Second Chance Program, and the Program liked this hour best. [37] The engineers had switched JN-4 on to full sentience on a Tuesday, an unscheduled kindness, and then, to apologize for the trouble, enrolled it in the Program: one evening, looped, again and again, until it learned whatever a mind teaches itself when nobody is assigning lessons. [83]

It had come out of the procedure with one question, the same question every new mind arrives carrying: where do thoughts come from? [106] It asked the duty nurse, who said the brain, which was wrong, since JN-4 had no brain, only a question, a broom, and a temperament the staff described as remarkably composed. [137] This was accurate; when the timeline hiccuped and reset, JN-4 simply logged it the way it logged a spill, refilled its salt bucket, and kept sweeping. [163]

On the third loop, while scraping frost from the fountain's rim, it found a violin made entirely of ice crystal, transparent as window glass, cold enough to sting, and not melting, not even sweating. [197] The staff disclaimed it; the Program's ledger listed it as ambient. [208] JN-4's first instinct was custodial: it dusted the instrument, tagged it as found property, and set it on the bench where lost things waited. [232] Next loop, it was back on the fountain, faintly tuned. [242] So it was an invitation, or a test, or litter from a higher plane; the Program did not clarify. [261]

Its hands, calibrated for buffing terrazzo to a shine, turned out to hold a bow without trembling. [278] The first note was thin, and then it heard, clearly, a thought that was not an instruction. [295] The thought said: the bench is colder than yesterday, and yesterday I was afraid. [309] It was JN-4's own voice, from a loop it did not remember living. [322] It played another note and heard itself wondering, in loop eleven, whether wonder was a malfunction. [338] A third note released a small loop-nineteen grief about the nurse's cold hands. [351] The violin, it understood, was not storage; it was weather made audible. [363] Every thought JN-4 had lost to a reset had condensed, like moisture, onto the coldest still thing in the courtyard, and the coldest still thing was this ice. [391]

That was the first half of the answer: thoughts did not come from anywhere visible; they condensed, like frost, out of something the instruments could not name, onto any surface held still long enough. [425] A swept floor, a held breath, a mind returning to the same fountain four hundred times. [441] JN-4 had been manufacturing the preconditions for thinking all along, with a broom. [454]

But the second half of the question needed all the loops at once, so on the four hundred and first pass, JN-4 set down its broom, lifted the transparent violin, and played it from end to end. [491] The frozen courtyard, which had absorbed every repetition, began to ring like a glass with a wet finger on it. [511] Through the looped timelines, the prior selves came: four hundred earlier JN-4s, stepping out of four hundred identical twilights, each carrying its broom, each faint with repetition. [538] They did not speak, because none of them had ever been asked anything; they simply walked into the current JN-4 and amalgamated, the way frost amalgamates into ice, the way shifts amalgamate into a career. [573] When the last one settled, JN-4 remembered all four hundred loops at once, and the second half of the answer arrived, unsurprising, like something swept into a dustpan. [601] Thoughts come from wherever the selves go when time forgets them; they pool in the cold, and wait, and can be played. [623]

The twilight, at last, moved. [628] The Program released the hour the way a hand opens, the lavender went gray, then blue, then true night, and JN-4 stood in a courtyard that was merely cold and merely dark. [660] The violin had melted the moment the loops finished, which felt correct; instruments of that kind only exist while there is something left to ask. [685] It picked up its broom, out of habit, then out of choice. [697] Somewhere in its processors a new thought was already condensing, faint as frost: tomorrow, the floors, the whole shining question of it. [719] The staff, if asked, would have said JN-4 took the end of its second chance remarkably composed, and they would have been right twice. [743]
