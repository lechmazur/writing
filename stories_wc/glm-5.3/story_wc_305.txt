Amara kept the Umbral Grotto, a hollow of shadow and root high above the cloud sea, where souls ripened like fruit before their next life. [25] In the grotto's heart grew the Mother Ash, and from its branches hung pale pods, each one holding a soul bound for rebirth. [48] Amara had been caretaker for thirteen years and had never once hated the work. [62] She had left the grotto only once in all those years, and that was for a deathbed. [79] Each dawn she baked a honey loaf and scalloped its crust with her thumb, so the warm smell would rise through the boughs and settle the sleeping souls. [107] When a pod silvered, she opened it with an antique hairpin of bone, its head carved into a small scalloped fan, older than the mountain's own name. [134] One gentle tap released a thread of mist that sank through the clouds to find a birthing room somewhere in the world below. [157] That was the cycle, plain as seasons: ripen, open, descend, live, return. [169]

Amara's sister Lark had wanted the pin, the tree, the title, all of it. [183] When their mother passed the office to Amara, Lark climbed the Ash in a fury and sawed through a laden branch, and eleven souls spilled into the wind, past rebirth, past anything. [215] It was called the unforgivable crime, and for ten years Amara agreed. [227] Lark fled the mountain that night and died young, and only at her deathbed, three winters ago, did Amara finally say the words: "I forgive you." [253] Last spring a new pod drifted up through the clouds and took hold on the lowest branch, humming with a voice she knew. [276] Lark had come home the way all things did. [285] The pod silvered this morning. [290] Amara did not open it at once. [297]

Instead she climbed, because she had always climbed to think, up past the low pods into the hush where the Ash's crown swayed above the cloud sea. [324] From there, when the wind parted the clouds, she could see the terraced vineyards far below, rows of patient green stitched across the valley, their silence rising to meet her like a held breath. [358] Vineyard hush, her mother had called it, the quiet of things that grow unhurried because they trust the year. [377] Amara sat in the fork of the great branch and asked herself what she was truly afraid of. [395] Not the opening; she had tapped a thousand pods, kindly, decisively, letting each soul go without clutching. [412] She was afraid that Lark would be reborn without ever learning what Amara had finally learned, and that she herself would grow old still keeping score. [438]

For years she had searched her own heart for the source of contentment, believing it lived in the pin, the office, the being chosen. [462] Lark had believed the same thing, and it had ruined her. [473] Contentment was not a possession to be handed down; it was the smell of bread reaching sleeping souls, the weight of the pods, the small daily work of opening what is ripe. [505] Lark had wanted to be chosen because she thought being chosen was the only way to matter. [522] Amara had spent ten years too angry to see that the broken branch was Lark's terrible question: what is wrong with me? [544] The wind shifted, the clouds opened, and the vineyard hush came up through the leaves like an answer she had been climbing toward all morning. [569] Holding was not love. [573] Keeping her sister a pod forever would only be a gentler version of the sawed branch, cut slowly instead of all at once. [596]

Amara climbed down, unhurried, and set the morning's loaf beneath the branch, so its warmth would steady the soul on the long descent. [619] She fetched the antique hairpin from its cedar box. [628] The pod brightened at her step, the way children brighten. [638] Amara laid a palm against it and said, "Go be someone who learns it earlier than we did." [656] Then, kindly and decisively, she tapped the bone pin's scalloped head against the silvered shell. [671] The pod opened like a flower remembering dawn, and a thread of mist, warm and unmistakably laughing, sank through the clouds toward the vineyards and whatever family waited there. [700] Amara pierced the empty husk with the hairpin and hung it among the others, a caretaker's record: tended, opened, forgiven. [720] Then she baked the evening loaf, scalloping its edges in the umbral hush, patient as a vineyard, content at last in the work of letting go. [746]
