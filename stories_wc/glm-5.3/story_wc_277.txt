The call came at dusk, and by full dark Marlowe Vane was climbing the observatory stairs, as she always did when the past demanded an appointment. [26] Mr. Reyes, the journalist, had questions about Hollow Creek, about 1962, about her father; she had promised him an answer by morning, which was generous, since she had been failing to answer for sixty years. [61] The observatory sat above the cloud line, a brass dome her grandfather had bolted to the mountain like a medal he had awarded himself. [85] Inside, her voice came back to her off the dome, twice, which she appreciated; every conversation about the family deserved a witness. [107]

She was the heiress to the Vane Courier and to the newsprint mills that fed it, which meant she had inherited both the paper and the reason nobody trusted it. [137] She had spent her life discovering hidden truths the way other heiresses collected thoroughbreds: as a habit too expensive to explain at parties. [160] In 1962 she had been nineteen, a copygirl with a devotedly observant eye, and she had watched a story go onto the spike - solvents in the creek, children in the clinic - and had said nothing. [195] Regret, she had decided somewhere in her seventies, was not a line; it was an orbit, and she the faithful satellite, sweeping around and around the same small dark mass. [225] Tonight the orbit carried Reyes's question into view: had her father, then the Courier's editor, killed the story to protect the mills? [247]

She would not answer on faith; faith was for churches and weather. [259] She would answer the way her telescope answered questions: by contradiction. [270] Assume, then, that her father was guilty. [277] Guilty men leave fingerprints, and her father's fingerprints were made of ink. [289] She fetched the box from beneath the star charts and lifted out his pen nib, rosewood, tines worn to a silk sheen, the only nib he had used since 1948 and the only one he trusted with anything that mattered. [329] Wood, unlike steel, forgives nothing; every downstroke split slightly along the grain, leaving a doubled hairline her observant eye had catalogued across four decades of birthday cards and marginalia. [358] She set the facsimile under the lamp - the kill order, unsigned, pulled by Reyes from the paper's morgue and laid at her father's feet - and looked. [384] The strokes were clean, single, unbroken: steel. [391] Contradiction; therefore her father had not written it. [399] The dome echoed, as if the stars were clearing their throats. [410]

But proof by contradiction is a cruel instrument; it executes one truth and promotes whatever survives. [426] Assume, then, that her family had been innocent. [434] She opened her father's ledgers, the ones she had never had the stomach to audit, and found, beginning in 1963, small quarterly payments to eleven families in Hollow Creek, disguised as paper deliveries, running for thirty years until the mill closed and took the disguise with it. [481] Contradiction; therefore the family had known. [487] Not her father's guilt, then, but her grandfather's - the looping letters matched his recipe file, of all things, which she had read at nine and never once forgotten. [515]

Hidden truths, she thought, are like the far side of a comet: they exist, they are cold, and they come around. [536] And what had come around now was worse than the old accusation; it was a half-truth with a deadline. [555] Left alone, Reyes would print that Edmund Vane spiked the story, and the valley would learn to hate a dead man for the one sin he had refused to commit. [585] Unless she intervened. [588] Intervention, for an heiress, is mostly paperwork, which is why it is so rarely forgiven. [603] Before dawn she wrote out her authorization: Reyes could open the archive, the ledgers, the correspondence, all of it, on one condition - print the whole orbit, the spike and the payments and the silence, or print nothing. [640] Then she fitted the rosewood nib into a pen for the first time in forty years and signed. [658] The doubled hairline in her signature, the little ghost split in every downstroke, looked exactly like his. [675] Her father had carried his apology in a checkbook; she would carry hers in newsprint, which was at least the family's native language. [698]

Her orbit of regret had not broken - orbits never break - but for the first time in sixty years it was carrying something other than regret. [723] Below her the clouds began to silver and come apart, the way secrets do when somebody finally looks down on them from a height. [747] Marlowe Vane went down the stairs to wake the fax machine, whistling - which is the sound solemn people make when they are finally, quietly, unburdened. [772]
