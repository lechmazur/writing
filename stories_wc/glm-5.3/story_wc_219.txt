The pilgrims called it the Way of the Signal, though the route was only eleven years old, designed by a marketing firm and poured in concrete. [26] Dr. Mara Voss called it an artificial pilgrimage, and she walked it anyway, because a false road can still arrive somewhere true. [48] Eleven years had passed since first contact, since the tower received the signal and the three night operators walked out at dawn with faint light rising under their skin like lamps behind paper. [81] Mara was the last of them, and the only one who refused to call the glow a blessing. [99] She was, the newspapers liked to say, a luminous skeptic: a woman shining with the miracle and doubting it in the same breath. [122] The academy had doubted her too, from the other direction. [132] When she published her paper arguing the signal was not language but a structure, a molecular diagram dressed in radio, they revoked her laboratory and her title in the same semester. [163] Academically rebellious, her dean had written, as if rebellion were a stain. [175] She had kept the rebellion and lost everything else, which felt, on the whole, like a fair trade. [193]

In spring the sculpture path was at its worst and its best. [205] The gardeners had been let go years ago, and the overgrown path wound between bronzes half-swallowed by honeysuckle and young maple, their faces green with pollen and rain. [233] Along the ground ran the footprints: casts of the operators' shoes, set in bronze by the pilgrimage company, a reliquary you could walk on. [257] Pollen dusted them gold; every spring storm rinsed the tower clean and left the sheltered path powdered, so the footprints looked freshly made, as if someone had just gone ahead. [287] One of those footprints was hers. [293] It was strange to walk behind your own shoes. [302]

In her pocket she carried the fly amber, a rough yellow bead with an insect folded inside it, forty million years dead and perfectly preserved. [327] Her mentor had pressed it into her hand the day before the academy let her go. [343] Resin, he said, is a tree's wound becoming a home for whatever it catches. [357] She had thought it was only sentiment. [364] Then, three winters later, in a borrowed lab, she matched the signal's decoded numbers against a spectral library and found her structure inside Cretaceous resin: a molecule ancient trees made when hurt, and that someone had transmitted across the dark. [404] Not a greeting, she had realized. [410] A recipe for homesickness. [414] Whoever they were, they had sent the chemistry of their own homecoming, hoping someone would synthesize it and, for one breath, feel the warmth of a world they had left. [444] No one had synthesized it. [449] The pilgrims came anyway. [453]

They came by the thousand every May, converging on the tower by way of the dusty footprints, carrying candles they would not need. [476] Mara came for a colder reason: to understand the chemistry of homecoming, to learn once and finally why they wept. [496] Homesickness had a chemistry; grief had a chemistry; she did not believe any feeling was beyond a spectrometer. [514] She reached the tower at dusk, rainwashed and steaming faintly, its old dome dripping spring. [529] Bronze and wet stone; petrichor; the green smell of cut honeysuckle where pilgrims had pushed through. [545] And under it all the sharp ghost of resin, rising from a thousand wounded maples as the rain warmed off them. [566]

Inside the fence the crowd stood in a bronze quiet, hushed and warm-toned, faces turned up. [582] Their weeping had a shape to it, she noticed, almost a frequency. [594] She took out her vials and then did not open them. [605] A girl beside her was crying the way people cry at a homecoming, at a familiar coat on a familiar hook. [626] Mara held the amber up to the last light. [635] The fly hung in its gold, wing veins fine as radio static, forty million years from the tree it had been flying toward. [658] A wound that became a home. [664]

She understood, then, that the pilgrims were not wrong, the academy was not wrong, and she had been asking the wrong question. [686] The molecule was not the message. [692] The homecoming was. [695] Every spring the rain rinsed the tower, the wounded maples breathed out their bright chemistry, and the pilgrims breathed it in. [716] The message, or its near cousin, loose in the air, free of any spectrometer. [730] Somewhere across the dark, the senders must have known that grief and resin and rain are the same grammar everywhere. [750] Her skin brightened, unbidden, the way it did at dusk. [760] For once she did not reach for an explanation. [769] She closed her fist around the amber, took the girl's hand, and stood with the crowd in the bronze quiet, shining, doubtful, finally home.
