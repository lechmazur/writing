Wren woke in a canopy of copper leaves, three hundred miles from where she had fallen asleep, and knew better than to be surprised. [24] This was simply what her body did now. [32] When the sixth sense awakened in everyone, on the night the mechanical rainforest first hummed, most people received modest gifts: a whisper of weather, an itch of premonition. [60] Wren received travel. [63] Every sleep, a translocation; every dawn, a new grove. [72] After that night, miracles were mostly a matter of probability, and probability was mostly a matter of listening. [90] The Academy of Systematists had tried to chart her for a year before conceding that some things are not for maps, and she had been a stray ever since. [119] Stray philosopher, they called her, half insult, half job description. [129]

She learned to read the forest by ear, because the wind through different trees said everything the eye could not. [149] Wind through cogwood clicked like a room full of polite applause; through spring-vines it hissed one long vowel; through capacitor fronds it crackled, dry and electric, like the edge of an idea. [181] By dawn of any day she could name the district she had been deposited in, simply by listening. [199]

Her purpose had grown stranger than her sleep. [207] The Systematists believed the mind could be fully catalogued, every thought given a shelf and a number, and after the awakening they had planted map-trees: brass trunks wound tight with engraved wire, each coil a catalogue of some mapped psyche, each leaf stamped with a conclusion. [253] Wren wanted the opposite country: to rediscover the wild frontier of the mind, the unshelved thoughts, the interior every child owns before the cataloguing begins. [278] In her pocket she carried one small gear, salvaged from the first clock she had ever repaired, before the awakening, before everything. [300] It had begun to hum. [305] Gears hummed before they fit, the old clockmakers said; the hum of insight was the sound of two things almost becoming one machine. [328] Hers hummed louder each night, and it hummed in a direction, and she had learned to trust directions. [346]

On the morning that matters, she woke beneath a tree so vast that its shadow had its own climate. [365] The wind through it made no sound she knew. [374] Not cogwood, not spring-vine, not frond: a low, patient tone, like the forest clearing its throat before speaking. [392] This was the Master Map-Tree, the Systematists' crown: one trunk wound with the wire of ten thousand catalogued minds, the great index of everything a person was permitted to think they were. [424] Her small gear grew so hot with humming that she had to switch pockets. [438]

A keeper stood at the trunk, oiling the coils, and asked if she had come to be catalogued. [456] "I sleep in a different country every night," she said. [466] "Your index will need longer shelves." [472] He smiled the way keepers smile at strays, and warned her that the wire could never be unwound: the coils held the forest's dreams in place, and to unravel them would let the wilderness back into thought. [509] "Exactly," said Wren. [512]

When he left at dusk, she set her fingers to the outermost coil. [525] The wire was warm and wanted to move; the hum of insight climbed her arms like a chord being decided. [545] She began to unravel it. [550] The coil came away in long singing loops, and where it fell the ground forgot its grid, and around the loosened trunk the air changed taste. [576] Wind moved through the bared branches and made a sound with no precedent, a sound like wind through trees that had never existed and were considering it. [603] The whole rainforest took it up, grove by grove, the hum passing outward like news of a birth. [621] Somewhere far off, she was sure, ten thousand catalogued minds were dreaming past their final page for the first time in years. [643]

Wren knelt and pressed her small gear into the loosened soil at the trunk's base, because a frontier needs a seed, and every seed in this forest had always been mechanical. [674] Then, being tired in the ordinary human way, she lay down in the strange new wind and let sleep take her wherever it liked. [698] She woke in the same place. [704] That had never happened, not once since the awakening, and she lay still a long while, listening for a mistake. [724] There was none, because you cannot be moved away from a frontier that travels with you. [740] The gear had sprouted overnight into a sapling of soft brass, its first leaves shaped like small questions. [758] The wind through it made a sound she had never heard in any district, because it was not a district yet: it was a frontier, and it hummed, and it was hers. [790]
