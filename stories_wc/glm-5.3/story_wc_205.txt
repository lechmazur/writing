<story>
After the Clarification, when time became visible and hung about every object like gauze remembering fire, the asteroid of Santa Luce turned from palace to reliquary. [26] Its baroque chapels, gilt and spiraling, now sheltered the braided light of every past a thing had worn. [44] Archaeology became a matter of reading strands, and reading strands became a matter of dispute. [59] Odile Varr had once been a revolutionary; now she was a defunct one, which on Santa Luce counted as a kind of office. [82] Her uprising had cracked the Doge's power for eleven bright months before the curators bought it back and gentled her into a pension. [105] She had been poetically inclined even then, and children still recited her manifestos without knowing she was alive. [123] What she wanted now was larger than any palace: she wanted to reimagine an entire civilization, beginning with its memory of a vase. [146]

The Vase of Concord had been the old regime's holiest object, a crystal vessel the founding Doge supposedly filled with water from Earth. [169] Odile had shattered it herself, forty years ago, on the palace steps, calling it a lie with beautiful manners. [188] She had kept one shard, small as a tooth, and carried it through every cell and amnesty since. [206] Now the Academy of Strand-Readers had published their finding: the vase's visible past confirmed the founding gift, proving the old concord real and the uprising a vandalism. [233] The city, craving absolution, began to love its dead Doge again. [244] Odile read the finding aloud in her rented chapel, then wrote a one-line rebuttal that took her three days: "They read the strand in stormlight, and stormlight flatters whatever it touches." [275]

Everyone on Santa Luce knew the custom of covering mirrors during storms. [287] When solar wind struck the asteroid, visible time went volatile, and any reflective surface stitched false pasts from stray light, gorgeous and fraudulent. [310] Covered mirrors meant honest readings; that was the grammar of the place. [322] The Academy's master reading had taken place during the great storm two winters past, in the Hall of Mirrors, every glass uncovered. [344] Vanity or oversight, it had contaminated the strand, and the strand lied to them graciously, as mirrors do. [362] No one wanted to hear it. [368]

So Odile devised a demonstration instead of an argument, because she was old enough to know that people believe their eyes quicker than their neighbors. [393] She petitioned the Academy to convene during the equinox storm, in the Hall of Mirrors, before the whole city. [412] The Academicians consented, certain she meant only to martyr herself. [422]

When the storm arrived, light crawled over the asteroid like spilled honey, and the crowd pressed into the hall. [441] Odile stood beneath the tall glasses and held up an ordinary glass bead. [454] She let the stormlight strike it. [460] In the mirrors the bead wore a past it had never had: a shimmering provenance, a blessing by cardinals, a history gold as envy. [484] The crowd gasped at the beauty of the lie. [493] Then her helpers drew the mourning cloths, black and heavy, and covered every mirror in the hall, as families did when storms and death came together. [519]

In the sudden honest dimness, Odile set the bead aside and produced the shard. [533] Here is the true strand, she said; read it, or admit your finding was written in flattering weather. [551] The master reader, an old rival with kind, defeated eyes, bent over the crystal with his lenses. [568] The strand that rose was thin and steady, and it showed what the mirrors would not. [584] The vase had been blown not by blessed artisans but by convicts, chained to the furnaces, their lungs full of crystal dust. [606] One of them, a woman with burn-scarred wrists, had etched words into the mold, hidden inside the crystal where no eye could find them. [630] Only visible time had ever given them back. [638] The master reader translated the old dialect slowly, and the hall grew quiet enough to hear the storm. [656] It was a poem, four lines long, about making beautiful things for ugly masters, and about the water of Earth, which the prisoners were promised and never given. [684]

Odile had not known the verse existed, and she held the shard high, letting the whole city watch two griefs cross in the light. [708] Then, because she was poetically inclined and had nothing left to seize, she performed the only revolutionary act remaining: she answered it. [730] Four lines, in the old dialect, giving the maker the name history had refused her. [745]

The Academy withdrew its finding the next morning, and a burnished sorrow settled over Santa Luce, the shine of grief polished by long handling. [769] Now, every storm, the asteroid covers its mirrors and reads two poems to the dark, a civilization reimagined one grief at a time. [792]
