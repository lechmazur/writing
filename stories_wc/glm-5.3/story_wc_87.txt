Dawn found Mara at the coastal kayak launch, where the tide's retreat left the mud studded with forgotten anchors. [19] She had gravitated here for eleven months, the way iron filings find a magnet she no longer believed in. [38] People at the marina called her the broken navigator, half fondness, half verdict, and she had stopped correcting them months ago. [59] Twelve years she had charted blue water for the merchant fleet, until the company dissolved, her marriage followed, and every future she had plotted evaporated with them, in the season her expectations collapsed like a hull breaching. [96]

Afterward she lived two hundred miles inland and navigated by utility poles: sixty poles to the office, a hundred and forty to the therapist, nine to the bar she pretended was a lighthouse. [129] She delivered this method with a laugh, merrily melancholic, the way only the truly lost can joke about being lost. [149] In her dry bag, wrapped in a wool sock, lay her father's astronomical compass, the kind that ignores magnets entirely and takes its bearing from stars. [175] He had taught her to shoot Polaris through its sighting vanes on night crossings, saying a magnetic needle points at what attracts it, but the sky tells you where you actually are. [207] That was the lesson she had failed, wasn't it: her needle had pointed at safety, promotions, a sensible life, while her heart stayed out here on the water. [235]

The backlash of regrets had come for her the way winter storms come: not all at once, but in successive waves, each one rearranging the wreckage. [261] She regretted the channel she hadn't taken, the charter business she'd talked herself out of, the night she'd called her father's charts sentimental and let them mildew in a garage. [291] Every regret, her therapist said, was proof she still wanted something; Mara had replied that this was like calling a rip current a swimming lesson. [316] And yet this morning she had driven to the coast before work, kayak lashed to the roof like a promise she was tired of breaking. [341] Her motivation was simple and humiliating in its simplicity: to align heart and action, to make her days point the same direction as her wanting. [366] Faith, she had learned, was not a bonfire; it flickered, guttered, and if you wanted it at dawn you had to carry matches. [389] Her match, this morning, was the compass. [396]

She slid the kayak down the barnacled ramp, the hull hissing over grit, and settled into the seat like a woman returning to her own name. [422] The fog was lifting in rags, and the utility poles along the shore road stood in a line like the masts of a sunken fleet, her old landmarks still offering themselves. [453] She laughed out loud at that, alone on the gray water, because the world had followed her after all. [472] She would navigate by them one last time, then let them go: three poles to the red buoy, then stars, then whatever came. [495]

The regret hit her mid-channel, exactly as she had known it would, the backlash rising with the tide: her father's voice, the mildewed charts, the decade of counting poles. [524] Her paddle stuttered; the kayak yawed; the flicker of her faith guttered nearly out. [538] Then she remembered the compass in its wool sock, and the remembering itself was a bearing. [554] Afloat, she unpacked it, thumbed the alidade toward the pale ghost of Polaris still hanging above the horizon like a lantern someone had left on for her. [581] The astronomical compass gave her true north the way her father had promised: not what she was drawn to, but where she actually was. [605] Regrets still circled her, but circling was just orbiting, and orbiting was just navigation by a different geometry. [623] She dug the paddle in and held the bearing. [632]

When the sun finally cleared the headland, the poles along the shore had become ordinary again, just wood and wire, unburdened. [653] She was still broken, probably; navigators who lose their fleets usually are. [665] But a broken compass still points at something, and a broken navigator still knows that the difference between drifting and traveling is a heading you choose on purpose. [693] Mara laughed again, merrily, melancholically, faithfully, and took the next stroke, and the next, out past the buoy where the deep water began. [716]
