The forgotten library kept its exiles in fragments, one shattered mirror for every soul who had ever cast away a grief too heavy to carry. [25] Marisol, the shard-mirror archivist, dusted them each evening, and each evening she argued with them as though they could argue back. [46] Tonight one fragment refused to stay quiet. [53] Inside it, caught like a moth, an afternoon shadow leaned against the glass from the inside, palm flat, patient. [72] It belonged to Juna, who had exiled the last good afternoon of her life three winters ago and now knocked on the library door every Thursday, demanding it back. [101]

"No," Marisol said, before the girl even sat down. [110] "You'll get your shadow when your shadow won't kill you, and not a minute sooner." [125] "You don't know what it's like," Juna said. [133] "I know exactly what it's like," Marisol said, and turned her sleeve so the lamplight caught the place where her own reflection should have been and wasn't. [160]

Exile, she had learned, is always reversible; reversal is what costs. [171] Any coward can push a thing away. [178] Only the stubborn can pull it home again and keep walking. [189]

She brewed tea, made Juna drink it to the bitter dregs, then tilted the cup and read the arrangement like a map. [211] This was how she annotated every shard in her care, tag by tag, by analyzing the geometry of the tea leaves. [232] Acute angles meant anger still hot; obtuse meant the grief had widened into something a body could live inside. [251] A tight spiral meant the exiled one was circling home. [261] Juna's leaves tonight made a spiral. [267]

Marisol set the cup down too hard and walked backwards the length of the study, heels first, the way she always did when something frightened her badly. [294] "Stop doing that," Juna said. [299] "It's how I keep from running," Marisol said. [307] "If I must retreat, I retreat facing the thing." [316] "Tonight, then," she said. [320] "The leaves say tonight." [324]

Beyond the library's cracked window, the old playground sat dark, the school long closed, the children long grown. [342] One swing still hung from the rusted frame, and the evening wind moved it without hurry, back and forth, back and forth, in a hush so complete you could hear the chains. [374]

"Do you know what an exile is made of?" Marisol asked, fetching the shard down from its shelf with both hands. [395] "Absence," Juna said. [398] "Weight," Marisol corrected. [401] "It is grief you refused to carry, and taking it back means shouldering all of it at once, at the hush when the swing moves and nothing else does." [430] "Shadows can only cross back at that hush; it is the one moment the world forgets to watch." [448]

Juna had been twelve the afternoon her brother pushed her higher and higher on that same swing, laughing, two days before the fever took him. [473] She had exiled the whole afternoon, shadow and all, so its sweetness would stop ambushing her in doorways. [491] "If I take it back," she said, "I take back the laughing too, and then the hospital, and then everything after." [512] "Yes," Marisol said, and did not soften it. [520] "That is the price and the gift, and I will not insult you by pretending otherwise." [536]

The wind fell quiet. [540] The swing drifted once, twice, empty and unhurried, chains whispering. [550] "Now," Marisol said, and her voice shook, and she stepped backwards to the window so the girl would not see her face. [572] Juna pressed her bare heel to the glass. [580] The shadow uncurled from the fragment like smoke finding a draft, slid across her ankle, up her spine, and settled into the hollows where it had always belonged. [608] Juna made a sound like laughing and like breaking, both at once. [620] "He pushed me so high," she whispered. [627] "I screamed at him to stop and he wouldn't." [636]

"Good," said Marisol, still facing the window. [643] "Go on and cry; grief shrinks when you stare it down." [654] When the worst of it had passed, she poured more tea, and this time she drank with the girl, and read her own leaves, and said nothing about the perfect spiral they made. [687] On the highest shelf, wrapped in cloth, sat the one shard she never dusted, the one that held her own reflection, annotated in her smallest hand: not yet. [715]

Juna blew her nose, glared, and asked, "That's your plan for me then: tea and insults?" [731] "It worked," Marisol said, "and you'll come back Thursdays anyway, because you're stubborn, which is the beginning of resilience and the end of exile." [755] Outside, the swing drifted on, keeping the hush for whoever needed it next. [768]
