Nautical twilight came, and with it the mist, and Colm climbed down to the opal dock because the sea owed him an explanation. [23] The dock planks, cut from shell-stone, held the last of the drowned light and gave it back green and blue, like the sea dreaming in color. [49] He was an eccentric keeper; he knew it, kept a log of the tide's moods, named the gulls, argued politely with the lamp. [72] Eccentric men notice what ordinary men step over. [80] So when the rowboat appeared without oars or sound, he was already watching. [93] The stranger who tied it to the cleat wore a city suit gone gray with salt, and on his lapel, a dried Edelweiss boutonniere, white as a held breath. [122] Colm raised his lantern. [126] "You're him," the stranger said. [131] "I'm the keeper," Colm said. [136] "Same thing, at this hour." [141] Nautical twilight is the hour between the sun's leaving and its light's leaving, and things that should not choose must choose then. [163] The stranger walked the dock, and Colm, softly persistent as low tide, walked with him, matching steps, saying nothing. [182] At the third plank from the end, the one that had rotted and been replaced with new wood that never matched, the stranger stopped. [206] He unpinned the boutonniere. [210] He touched it to his brow. [215] He touched it to his lips. [220] He held it out over the gap between planks where the dark water moved. [234] Then he stepped backward off the dock into the mist, and the mist closed like a ledger. [251] The rowboat was gone. [255] The boutonniere lay on the new plank, which had never held anything before. [268]

Colm did what he always did with the sea's nonsense: he investigated. [280] Not with questions, for there was no one to ask. [290] He investigated by observing gaps. [295] In the logbook: a missing week, seven pages cut out cleanly, the entry before and the entry after in his own hand. [317] In his memory: a mountain morning worn thin as onionskin, and a name, Tomas, with no face attached to it. [337] In the boathouse: two oilskins on one hook. [345] Gaps, he had learned across thirty years of lightkeeping, are not absences. [357] They are the shape of something carried away. [365] And he knew the old story the mainland grandmothers told, that the drowned cannot keep their own memories, that water dissolves them like sugar, and so the drowned borrow. [394] Borrowed memories leave holes in the lender. [401] A keeper with a missing week and a faceless name was not a man forgetting. [416] He was a man being read. [422] Tomas had been reading him for thirty years. [430]

He sat up that night with the boutonniere in his palm, working at the stranger's final gesture the way he worked at fouled knots, patiently, by lamplight. [457] Brow, lips, the gap. [461] Think, he told the lamp. [466] The lamp said nothing, being a lamp, but burned steadier, which Colm chose to take as encouragement. [483] Brow: what is held in the head. [490] Lips: what is held back, unsaid. [496] The gap: where things are returned. [502] It was not a farewell. [507] It was a giving-back. [511]

When he pressed the dried flower to his own brow, the borrowed memories came home, and they came softly persistent, like water finding the low place, years of them: two brothers on a mountainside, Tomas picking the Edelweiss that grows only where fools climb, pinning one blossom to Colm's coat and one to his own, saying, so we cannot forget each other, saying, one more season, then the lighthouse money, saying, the sea is calm, she is calm. [589] The wave that took the boat, the cold, Tomas's hand letting go, the guilt that had walked Colm to this rock and nailed him to the light. [616] And the last morning, given back whole: Tomas at the rail in that same suit, Edelweiss fresh on his lapel, waving Colm aboard. [639] Colm wept the way isolated men weep, briefly and completely, and then he did his work. [655] He climbed to the lantern room and turned the light through its rounds, because twilight was coming again, and things that should not choose must choose. [681] In the logbook he filled the missing week with everything returned, every detail, no gaps, and signed both names where both names belonged. [704] No one would ever corroborate one word of it. [713] That is the keeper's whole inheritance: to be the only witness, and to keep the light anyway. [730] He pinned the dried Edelweiss boutonniere above the logbook, where it could watch. [743] Below in the mist the opal dock shone through its own hour, empty now, and the sea, patient, went on asking to be forgiven. [767]
