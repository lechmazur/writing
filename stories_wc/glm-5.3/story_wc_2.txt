<story>
Elka mended broken clocks in a harbor town where everything else had given up. [14] The town called her its broken-clock tinkerer, and she liked the title well enough. [28] Her workshop ran on order: gears labeled, tools ranked, every repair logged twice. [41] People said she was certain about everything. [48] In truth she was uncertainly certain: sure of her hands, never of what tomorrow would ask of them. [66]
When Admiral Kest died, his estate sold its junk by the crate. [78] Elka bought three crates of his clocks, all of them stopped. [89] The first one she revived ran sweetly for a day, then halted at 4:17. [103] She opened the case, found nothing wrong, and touched the minute hand; it walked on without complaint. [120] The next day it halted at 4:17 again. [128] So did the second clock, and the third. [136] By the sixth, coincidence had become pattern, and patterns were Elka's favorite kind of trouble. [151]
The estate's caretaker remembered the hour dimly: 4:17 was when the old greenhouse burned, forty years back. [168] The admiral had walked out of the flames carrying only a smoking compass case, they said. [184] Its rose, the brass star a compass turns on, had been lost for decades. [198]
Elka did what planners do: made a list, then a map, then a small lie to herself about needing either. [218] The greenhouse still stood on the bluff, neglected, its panes fogged gray with ash no rain had ever fully washed. [238] Inside, dead leaves lay a foot deep. [245] No wind was blowing, yet the leaves lifted, swirling in slow spirals that all drifted toward the far corner. [264] Another pattern. [266] She waded through the swirling leaves and swept a patch clear, and there, beneath the ashes, an iron grate showed through the brick floor. [290] Air poured down into it, cool and mineral, pulled by something far below. [303]
Her plans said go home. [308] Her hands were already loosening the grate. [315] Rusted rungs dropped into the dark, and she climbed down them, lantern first. [328] Halfway she heard it before she saw it: water, falling, everywhere. [339] The rungs ended in a cavern where a subterranean waterfall dropped silver from the rock's black throat into a pool without any bottom she could see. [365] The draft that stirred the dead leaves above was born here; the falls exhale cold air up the shaft all day and all night. [389] Spray had laid a gray apron of old ash across the stones, ash drawn down from the burned greenhouse by that same patient breath. [413]
Through the veil of falling water, something glinted on a shelf of stone. [426] Her plans offered nothing; no list covers a black pool. [436] So she improvised, hauling a rotted seed bench down the rungs and poling it across on a length of copper pipe, trusting weights she could not calculate. [463] The bench held. [466] The world carried her the moment she stopped insisting on guarantees. [477]
On the shelf lay a brass star, eight points, palm-wide, lacquer worn to soft gold: the admiral's lost compass rose. [498] Above it, letters had been cut into the stone, worn shallow by spray. [511] 4:17. LOST HERE AS A BOY. FOUND HERE THE NIGHT OF THE FIRE. LET EVERY PLAN KEEP ONE MINUTE OF NOT KNOWING. [533]
Elka sat on cold stone and understood the clocks at last. [544] They were not broken; they were disciplined. [551] Each movement held a hidden cam that halted the train at 4:17 and held it until a human hand touched the dial and chose to let time continue. [579] A minute of stillness a day, built into thirty machines by an old man who had learned to befriend uncertainty in planning. [601] He had put to sea two days after the fire and won the battle that made him famous, fond of telling his officers that a man who cannot stand one minute of not knowing has no business commanding an uncertain sea. [642]
Elka carried the rose home. [647] She mounted it on a pin above her bench, where it spins at the smallest breath, pointing everywhere and therefore anywhere. [668] Her logbooks now carry wide margins. [674] Every plan she writes holds one blank hour, unlabeled, for whatever the day insists on being. [690] And every afternoon at 4:17, thirty clocks pause in her shop, and Elka sits still among them, uncertainly certain, keeping the admiral's minute of not knowing. [716]
