The Duke died without an heir, and the city entered its interregnum the way a widow enters a cold bed: carefully, out of habit. [24] The palace petition dome still opened at dawn, and its famous acoustics still worked: a whisper pressed to the north wall arrived, intact, at the south. [50] They called it the whispering gallery. [56] Its floor was a chessboard of black and white marble, each square once inlaid with brass studs: the whole court, king to pawn. [79] In the hungry years after the flood, scavengers pried out the little brass pawns and sold them; the heavy stone majors were too big to carry. [105] The board remained, missing all its pawns. [112]

Mara had been in the lower town when the weir broke. [123] She survived; her sister did not. [129] At the inquest, the Commission's lawyers called the public mourning staged: crisis actors, they said, hired to deepen the case against the Duke's engineers. [153] There is no answering such an accusation, so Mara took the only work left to a proven liar: playing casualties for the city's emergency drills. [178] On dry riverbanks, wearing grave makeup, she taught young rescuers what a real drowning looks like, and never once told them how she knew. [202]

Now, in the first winter of the interregnum, the same Commission of Nine wanted an official history of the flood, something with heroes in it, something that would look like legitimacy. [233] They hired the crisis actor. [238] Mara accepted, then went to see the Registrar, an old archivist named Pell who had lost a son to the same water. [260] "Write the gestures, not the names," he told her. [269] "Names summon mobs; ask your sister's accusers." [276] He was, she decided, benevolently ruthless: a man who would burn a page to save a person, and had. [295]

So Mara set up her history in the whispering gallery and named her office honestly: she would be the historian of small gestures. [318] Not the moment the weir failed; the engineers' histories would keep that. [330] She wanted the jacket tied over a child's eyes, the butcher who lowered a lantern down a flooded well, the woman who passed bread along a windowsill. [357] A gesture, she had learned, is the one thing no commission can call fake. [371] Survivors came at dusk and whispered into the north wall, and the dome carried their words to her table at the south. [393] But a ledger can be forged; a crisis actor's whole life was proof. [406] So she built a public method: each evening she walked the marble squares in a pattern of walking that spells words, tracing each letter across the grid while watchers on the gallery's rim called them out as her feet laid them down. [448] JACKET. [449] LANTERN. [450] BREAD. [451] The words went into the record spoken by a hundred mouths at once, impossible to fake because everyone had watched them being made. [474]

That spring the almanac promised an eclipse whose shadow would cross the whole province: a nomadic penumbra, the astronomers wrote, a darkness that travels homeless while the throne itself stands empty. [505] On the appointed morning the penumbra arrived like a slow animal, dimming the marble until the squares where the pawns had been seemed to glow, a luminous absence, light where the little brass should have been. [541] Half the city crowded the gallery's rim. [548] In the strange half-light a woman leaned into the north wall and whispered what Mara had waited years to hear: a girl in a flooded stairwell, counting the stairs aloud so the ones below her would know the way up. [588] Eleven, twelve, thirteen, until the water reached her chin. [597] Mara walked COUNT across the board as totality came on, one letter at a time, and the watchers said each letter aloud as her feet spelled it, and the dome gave the word back in a hundred voices. [635] Her sister's voice was in all of them and none of them. [647]

Afterward, at her table, she took a razor and unbound the ledger. [659] The first pages held her private drafts, written while the mud was still drying, every gesture tagged with a name. [679] She cut them free and handed the pages back to their owners as the crowd filed out, one name at a time, keeping the gestures for the city. [707] "The history keeps the gesture," she told each of them, "but you keep your name." [722] Her sister's page she folded and kept in her pocket. [732]

When the penumbra had moved on and the light came back, the Nine chose a new Duke and asked Mara to record the coronation. [756] She went, and recorded one gesture: a footman steadying the new Duke's trembling hand. [770] In the evenings she still walks the board; the pawns never come back, but the words do. [787]
