The sun rose in the west, and Master Ilvane did not look up from his chessboard. [16] That was how I knew the sky was truly wrong. [26] I was the apprentice of wonder at the Spire of Hours, an icebound needle above the clouds where the astronomers wound the sky each night like a clockmaker. [54] Each of the master's chess pieces was hand-carved from cloud-pine, hollowed, and sealed around one secret: a star's true name, the smell of rain, the hour of noon. [82] His nightly solitaire was not a game but a rite; the pieces held the sky in its grooves. [100] Now the sun climbed backward, and green light lay along the frost. [112]
She came at dusk, when the wrong sky bruised purple, and she knocked. [125] Sera Vint had climbed four hundred iced steps for the pleasure of being polite about it, which tells you what kind of thief she was. [150] "Chivalry," she said, hanging her axes by the door, "is thievery with manners, and I have excellent manners." [168] She bowed to the chessboard the way one bows to an altar, sacredly playful, apologetic and entirely unrepentant. [186] For a year she had robbed us by swapping hand-carved chess pieces with secrets inside: she left flawless replicas on the board and let the freed secrets drift down the mountain to whoever needed them most. [222]
Master Ilvane went very still. [227] "Which piece did you take last?" he asked. [235] "The white king," she said. [240] Inside the white king rode the hour of noon. [249] Without it the sun could not find its slot, and the whole day wandered west like a lost dog. [268] "I have come to amend it," she said. [276] She set the unopened king on the board; a king is crowned shut with wax, and she had never brought herself to crack one. [300] Beside it she laid the thing that made my master flinch: a battered pocket sundial lid, brass, hinged, older than both of them. [323]
"Your father's," Ilvane said. [327] "Yours first," she said. [331] "You stole it from his workshop the year I was born, and I stole it back, and I have been taking back what you people hoard ever since." [359] I watched my master become an old man in the space of a breath. [373] "I was jealous of him," Ilvane admitted. [380] "Keep the lid, then; it is yours." [387] "No," she said, "it belongs to the sky now; a sundial without a lid is a man without a confession." [407]
Then he told us what amending would require. [415] "We do not repair the sky," he said; "we amend it, as one amends a ledger, by paying the debt in person." [437] The king must be carried out to the balustrade, held high with open arms above the clouds, and unsealed at the exact moment of true noon. [463] "And under a wrong sky," he said, "only this lid can find noon; the shadow inside a lid remembers every light it has ever covered." [488] "It must be an apprentice who makes the offering," he added, "someone whose trade is wonder, who can embrace the unknown with open arms and mean it." [515]
Every instinct I owned said no. [521] I have always been cautious the way ice is cautious, and yet some hungers in me were larger than my carefulness. [542] Sera tied the rope herself and checked my knots twice, and then I leaned out over the void anyway, hands open, the king burning cold in my palms. [570] The wrong sun hung behind me like a mistake. [579] Sera opened the lid, and its small shadow swung, wavered, and settled, pointing not at the sun that was but at the sun that should be. [605] "Now," she said, and I cracked the wax. [613] Noon came out of the little king like a struck bell. [624] Light poured down my open arms, found its slot, and the day turned over gently, like a page. [642] The sun set in the west for the first time in a month, correctly, obediently, gratefully. [658]
That night Master Ilvane and Sera Vint played a real game, laughing like conspirators, and no one watched the board more closely than the man losing. [684] She left before dawn, taking nothing but her axes. [693] She left the lid on the board, over the white king's square, where its shadow could go on finding noon for both of them. [717] I stayed, still cautious, still hungry, an apprentice of wonder learning the only lesson that matters: check your knots twice, and then, with open arms, leap. [743]
