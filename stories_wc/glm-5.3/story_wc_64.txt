Wren kept her blitheness the way other people keep grief: privately, and with practice. [14]
For eleven years she had been the cemetery gardener at Ashfen, singing to the chrysanthemums, whistling past the angels, unbothered by the congregation beneath her knees. [40]
Every Thursday she played chess with Aldous, the caretaker, in the gatehouse where the lamp burned amber and the board sat between them like a small fenced field. [68]
He played slowly that last winter, and she let her clock tick while she watched his hands instead of the board. [89]
Over the years he had fed her fragments between moves: a silo painted cobalt for reasons nobody recorded, a weaver nobody thanked, coins that went into the ground. [117]
On the final Thursday his flag fell before her knight could land; the chess clock ran down, and so, three nights later, did he. [141]
His lawyer handed her an antique sovereign case, silver gone soft with thumbing, its hinge worn to a whisper. [160]
Inside lay no coins, only a note in Aldous's slanted hand: The silo, after dark; knot for a grave, doubled knot for a child, cut thread for a name he was never given, loop for a row; I could never make myself count them. [204]
The cobalt silo stood on the hill above the paupers' field, a blue finger pointed at the stars, unused since the farm failed. [227]
She climbed its ladder that night with a lantern, the case cold in her pocket, and found the gloom inside deeper and softer than any she had known, a velveteen gloom, the dark itself worn smooth like the case's silver. [267]
A tapestry hung from the roof ring to the floor, taller than a man, its border a long darkness of frayed threads. [289]
The parish register held one line about Josiah Verre, sexton in 1877, the man paid to bury the workhouse fever dead at night, quietly, in rows no map remembered. [318]
Two hundred souls: unmarked, uncharted echoes with no address. [327]
The fever carts had come by night, unloading in the silo's shadow before he dug; afterward, in the same blue dark, he had woven this. [352]
What the workhouse paid for was silence, and silence was the one thing Verre could not weave. [369]
The sovereigns had been his wage for keeping quiet; instead he told it in thread, and the border's fraying was not damage but notation, a ledger kept where no ledger was allowed. [401]
Wren understood then what she had come for: not coins, but the shape of intentions, the outline of why people bury what they cannot say. [426]
All that spring she worked by lamplight, learning to untangle the border strand by strand. [441]
Decoding the frayed threads took her until the plum trees bloomed. [452]
Slowly the uncharted echoes found their places: two hundred knots, fourteen rows, a geometry of grief spread across the hillside she had mowed for eleven years without knowing. [480]
Aldous's unfinished story finished itself in her hands. [488]
Verre had returned his wage coin by coin, laying a sovereign on the eyes of each body before the earth closed, until the case swung empty, a receipt for what he refused to keep. [522]
It had passed from sexton to caretaker ever since, each keeper waiting for hands that would neither sell it nor burn it. [544]
Through summer she reorganized the paupers' field, tenderly, row by knot: a rose at every doubled knot, lavender tracing the rows, boxwood for the long loops, so the pattern could be read by anyone who walked slowly. [581]
The dead returned to the landscape as garden: two hundred green names where stone had never been allowed. [599]
Only later did she grasp the shape of Aldous's intention: he had needed a player who would watch a board for years without complaint, hands patient enough for roots and knots alike. [631]
The chess had been his slow test, the clock his way of choosing a moment he might not live to see. [652]
On the autumn equinox she carried the sovereign case up to the silo and set it on the gallery rail, filled with two hundred knots of blue thread, one for every echo she had freed. [687]
Stars filled the silo's open mouth like coins in a cup. [698]
Below, the hillside lay tenderly organized in the dark, its lavender breathing. [710]
She stayed until the velveteen gloom softened toward morning, whistling to nobody in particular, blithe as ever, now that nobody was lost. [732]
She had come for the shape of intentions and found it everywhere: in a weaver's knots, an old man's slow pawns, a garden that had been listening all along. [761]
