Opening day, and the library is quiet at last. [9] The morning browsers have gone. [14] The evening readers have not come. [20] This is the lull. [24] Around me the glass orchard library hums, branches like windowpanes heavy with fruit-shaped books. [38] All of it floats on a swirling sea of clouds. [48]

I am a clean slate vendor. [54] This is the first shop I have ever owned. [63] Everything I sell is blankness, weighed and priced. [71] Glass cut from trees that never bore a story. [80] Blank tablets, blank pages, blank anything you need. [88] People buy them to stop being their old tale. [97] Mindfully scattered, the masters call my condition. [104] I sold my own story off over the years, piece by deliberate piece, until no whole memory of me remains anywhere, not even in me. [129]

Because I am looking for something. [135] The place where stories begin. [140] Scholars insist it is the first word. [147] I have doubted this for twenty years. [154] My method is quantifying absence. [159] I weigh what is missing. [164] Every slate in this shop has been measured for the room it holds, the words it lacks, the story it has not had. [187]

On the top shelf sits my one bottle of moonlight. [197] The orchard grants every new shopkeeper a first bottle, with one rule attached. [210] Never pour your first bottle. [215] Moonlight is the only blade that can incise a clean slate, and a slate must be incised before any story can take hold of it. [240]

A woman comes in during the lull, and she glimmers. [250] She does not shine. [254] She glimmers, at the edges, the way unfinished things do. [264] She is out on parole from a story. [272] The library grants it to readers who wander into the fruit-books and cannot find their way out. [289] Glimmering parole, the librarians call it. [295] The terms are exact. [299] You may live outside while your sentence remains unfinished, but every night you must return to the shelf and be read back in. [322] Hers has been unfinished for nine years. [329]

"I want a clean slate," she says. [336] "Then I must measure you first," I say. [344] This is how blankness is sold. [349] I set her beneath the scale I keep for absences and begin counting what she does not have. [367] An ending is missing. [371] Eleven words, by my count. [376] The gap where they should be glows faintly, a dark little lantern under her ribs. [391]

"There is no clean slate for you," I tell her. [401] "Your blankness is already shaped like an ending." [409] "Can you cut it?" she asks. [415] "Only moonlight cuts clean glass," I admit. [422] She looks at my first bottle, the one I was told never to pour. [436] "It is opening day," she says. [442] "Everything begins once." [445]

I take the bottle down. [450] The bottled moonlight moves like a slow animal. [458] I choose the slate with the deepest absence I have ever measured, her eleven missing words already nesting in its room. [479] I pour the light along my chisel's edge. [487] The blade drinks it and begins to shine. [495] Somewhere in the orchard, a rule breaks quietly. [503] Then I incise, one word at a time, and the glass takes each word with a sound like a held breath ending. [525] The eleven words are these: "She walked out of the story and into her own life." [541] When the last word closes, her sentence is complete, and the glimmering stops. [554] She stands solid in my shop, a finished woman, free in the plain way finished things are free. [572] She thanks me with a whole voice and goes out over the clouds. [585]

The lull holds. [588] The evening readers have still not come. [595] I look at the spent bottle, then at the scale, and I finally understand what twenty years of measuring was for. [616] Stories do not begin at the first word. [624] They begin in the absence the first word is cut from. [635] In the gap. [638] In the lull. [641]

I set a fresh slate on the counter. [649] I weigh my own absence, and there is room in it, more room than I expected, whole unwritten fields of it. [670] Somewhere out there, my scattered pieces are living as other people's beginnings. [682] The orchard hums. [685] The clouds swirl beneath us like a page being turned. [695] I dip the chisel in the last drop of moonlight and make my first cut, right here, in the quiet before the evening readers come, in the place where stories begin. [726]
