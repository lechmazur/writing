The tide went out at dusk, and Maren went in. [10] She was a spelunker who feared tight places, a contradiction she kept like a stone under the tongue. [28] Caves had been her grandmother's country; grief made her brave for exactly as long as the grief lasted. [46] Four hours of low water lay before her, four hours before the sea came back to drown the passage, and that was all the invitation the place ever gave. [75] The mouth of the cave was a throat of rock, a squeeze no wider than her shoulders, and beyond it the crashing surf arrived strangely as hush. [102] The sea broke itself against the cliffs outside; inside, the breaking arrived as pressure on the eardrums, a roaring silence, a crashing hush. [125] She counted her breaths the way her doctor had taught her and pushed through. [139]

The grotto opened like a held breath. [146] Halfway across its black pool, sheathed in barnacled steel, ran the cable: the old transatlantic line, the reason anyone had ever come to this hole in the world. [174] Station Vela had been built around it in 1959, a forgotten research facility of damp concrete and humming transformers, mothballed, struck from the maps, left to the anemones. [202] Its crew had one job: listen. [208] The undersea cables carried every voice that mattered and a few that shouldn't have, and the listeners at Vela logged the ones that shouldn't. [232] Ghost voices, the official reports called them, and then the official reports were lost, and then the listeners were lost with them. [254] Cable layers, mostly: the men and women who died sewing the ocean floor together, whose names were never entered in any ledger but this one. [279] The ledger was a codex, hand-bound, three hundred pages of signal logs kept by her grandmother and the other listeners before her. [301] Maren had found it nine years ago, on her first and only descent, and had fled in a blind panic, leaving it behind. [324] The last pages held a roster of the dead, every entry struck through by some auditor's tidy hand, as if striking ink could strike the world. [350] At the bottom, in a smaller hand she knew like her own, her grandmother had written her name. [368] Tonight she returned, because she was the last person alive who knew there had ever been names at all. [387] Somebody has to stand by the unremembered, her grandmother used to say, even if the only place left to stand is underwater. [409]

In the control room, salt had eaten the consoles, but the codex lay where she remembered, in a steel drawer, dry as a promise. [433] The margins were illuminated with gold. [439] Her grandmother had mixed illuminant fleck into the ink, gold dust she called it, because gold, alone of everything in the station, tracked invisible things. [464] Where a ghost voice passed through the wire, the flecks drifted after it and gathered in the margins, mapping everything the ear could not keep. [489] Maren opened the codex on her knees. [496] The gold was moving. [500] Not much; dust barely dares; but the flecks were leaning, all of them, toward the cable, the way grass leans before rain. [522] The line had been dead for forty years, and still something invisible was traveling it, and still the gold knew. [542]

Then the water began to come back. [549] It rose without drama, a black mirror lifting up the walls, and the old panic closed its hands around her chest, because the way out was the squeeze, and the squeeze would be underwater. [583] She wrapped the codex in her dry bag, said her grandmother's name once into the crashing hush, and dove. [602] The return was a passage through phases: gas in her lungs turning held and precious, air giving way to cold liquid, her solid body pressed through a solid throat of stone. [633] In the squeeze the panic flared white, and she answered it the only way that ever worked: she thought of the unremembered waiting in the dark margins, and she kept moving. [664] Then the stone let go of her, and she broke the surface into the roar and spray of the flood tide. [685]

On the shingle, shaking, she unwrapped the codex. [693] A wave had reached one page, and where the water touched it the gold underwent its own quiet change: the flecks loosened, ran liquid along the fibers, and settled, line after line, the way words gather. [729] Names. [730] Struck-through, seawater-legible, returning to readability the way she herself had just returned, through every phase water knows. [747] Maren stood in the crashing hush, salt-heavy and unafraid, and read them aloud, one name to each wave, standing by the unremembered while the tide climbed to take the cave again. [778]
