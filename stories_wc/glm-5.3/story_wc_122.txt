Verna had been deaf for six autumns, and numb for five of them. [13] She still taught music to the town's children, pressing their small palms against the old piano's ribs, showing them where sound lives when the ears go quiet. [40] But she felt nothing anymore, and the numbness frightened her more than the silence ever had. [56] In her coat pocket she carried a broken phonograph needle, snapped the morning her hearing died, kept like a splinter of the life before. [80]

When a letter came from Ilse, her old student, now a drum maker in a limestone cavern, saying only, "Come," Verna went to defeat the numbness, because during the faint rustle of turning seasons even the frozen need somewhere to kneel. [121] Leaves whispered down the hillside as she walked, and she felt their falling as a dry static through the soles of her boots. [144]

The cavern mouth breathed cool and smelled of hide glue and pinewood. [156] Inside, drums hung everywhere like sleeping moons, their skins curing pale in the stone's patient cold. [172] Ilse met her with ink-stained hands and a lantern and led her down to the deep chamber, where the walls returned your own stillness to you, silence answering silence, a mirrored hush. [204] There stood a phonograph, absurd and beloved, beside a wall of ashwood drum shells. [218]

Ilse had learned her letters from Verna, and her sign language too, and now her hands moved easily as she explained that she played her records backward. [245] A reversed song cannot be hummed ahead of itself, she signed; it arrives the way weather arrives, and the body has no choice but to answer it raw. [273] "I tune the drums this way," her hands said, "because a reversed song swells where the original died away, and endings must be heard becoming beginnings." [299]

She set a record on the turntable and lowered the arm, and the steel needle snapped against the groove with a small dry click Verna felt in her teeth. [328] Ilse searched her drawers and crates, but the cavern was a week's walk from anywhere that sold steel. [346] Verna took the broken needle from her pocket and held it out on her open palm. [362] Ilse lifted it into the lantern light: a shard of steel with a hairline crack through its tip, ruined for clean sound, perfect for something else. [388] She wedged the phonograph atop a bass drum so its shell would carry the song down into the stone. [407] Then she seated the cracked shard in the arm and started the record spinning backward, and the music that rose was audibly muted, half swallowed, as if sung through a wall of felt. [440] The crack rounded every edge and softened every attack until the song was less a sound than a breathing. [459]

Verna knelt and laid both palms flat against the cavern floor. [470] The muted notes came up through the limestone smooth and long, waves without spikes, and for the first time in six autumns her body could hold them. [497] Played backward, the song gave back its grief before its joy, the resolved chord opening like a wound that had learned to close itself, and Verna could neither predict nor armor against it, only receive. [532] The music enthralled her, and the numbness, that old frost, began to thaw in her chest. [548] She wept without sound, and the mirrored walls held her silence the way they held all silences, gently, giving it back whole. [570]

When the record ended, the cracked needle kept circling the run-out groove, and through the floor Verna felt it: a small repeating pulse inside the silence, faint as a heartbeat under a blanket, a filament of hush. [607] It was not the absence of music, she understood at last; it was music's smallest bone, the rhythm that silence keeps when everything else is spent. [633] Ilse knelt beside her and pressed her own hand to the stone, and the two of them stayed while the needle circled, hush answering hush. [658]

Above ground the season was still turning, frost stiffening the last leaves, and Verna felt that too now, the great slow rustle passing through the earth like a chord. [687] Before she left, Ilse gave her a small drum with a skin thin as paper, and pressed the cracked needle back into her hand. [711] That winter, in the schoolroom, Verna seated the old needle in her own phonograph and taught the children to play the songs backward, and the muted music rose through the floorboards into their waiting hands. [746] When each record ended, the children kept their palms down, enthralled, listening with their bones to the circling pulse, learning what their teacher had learned in the drum maker's cavern: that silence is never empty, and a broken thing can still find the groove. [790]
