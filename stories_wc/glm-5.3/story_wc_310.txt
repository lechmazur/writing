On the ambered observatory deck, where the resin of ten thousand years had slowly hardened into the windows, the last Decider of the Sorter Guild listened to the universe end. [30] The great contraction had swept through long ago, and now every surviving star was being pulled into a huddled, blueshifted core, their light bleeding through the deck's amber panes like the inside of a struck bell. [66] She was quietly enthusiastic about endings; most people found this trait unbearable, which was one reason she was alone. [85] The other reason was that everyone she had ever known was already part of the core light. [102] Before her, on a cloth worn soft as conscience, lay her collection of foreign coins: stamped copper from drowned Tellurin, hexagonal silver from the Cloud Markets, a dull oval that had been currency among people who farmed lightning. [140] Each coin was a lot, and each lot was a dead world's vote. [153] When a decision outweighed her own small wisdom, the Sorters had always cast lots, on the theory that chance is simply the universe refusing to flatter you. [180] Tonight the decision outweighed everything. [185] The receiver had caught static bursts from the contracting edge, and when she slowed them and read them backwards, as the contraction's reversed entropy demanded, the static became prophecy. [214] That was the cruel physics of it: in a universe falling inward, causality ran the other way, and prophecy arrived wearing the past tense like a borrowed coat. [242] This one said: You have already sent the seed. [251] The seed was a crystallized archive of every solved problem her civilization had ever produced, compressed to a single wavelength, waiting in the transmitter beneath the deck for a decision she had apparently already made. [286] Prophecies that remember forward are worse than none at all, she thought, because they rob you of the choosing and leave you the doubt. [310] The doubt was stellar in scale. [316] It was doubt aimed at the stars themselves, which had huddled so close that their light warmed the deck, watching her the way invalids watch a doctor. [343] Perhaps the static was only the crush of matter, and the prophecy only her loneliness finding grammar in noise. [362] Perhaps the next universe, if a bounce came, would be nothing but a repetition, and the seed nothing but a fossil of arrogance. [385] She had spent her whole vocation trying to wavelength align with purpose, tuning herself the way she tuned the receiver, hunting the narrow band where meaning ran clean. [413] Tonight the band was full of hiss. [420] So she did what a Decider does. [427] She cupped the coins, shook them the way you shake a thought loose, and cast them across the cloth. [446] The dead worlds voted. [450] Heads, faces, glyphs, and edges scattered through the amber glow, and she leaned in with that quiet enthusiasm that had once made her colleagues wince, reading the fall the way she read the sky: patiently, expecting nothing, hoping for everything. [490] Twenty coins said send. [494] Twenty said stay silent. [498] One coin, the dull oval of the lightning farmers, stood on its edge, wobbling, refusing. [513] A tie, then, and a refusal, which the old guild rules called the loudest answer of all: the universe declines to choose for you. [537] She laughed once, a small dry sound, and the standing coin fell flat. [550] Face up. [552] It was the lightning farmers' rune for yes, and she remembered, suddenly, what they had farmed lightning for: not power, but listening, because they believed creation spoke in bursts and deserved an ear. [585] Understanding static bursts, she realized, had never been about decoding. [595] It was about trust. [599] She went below and began the long work, letting the receiver pour its noise through her filters, coaxing the bursts apart, distilling them drop by drop until the hiss thinned into tone and the tone into something almost like a voice saying, in the past tense, over and over: It went out. [651] The prophecy remembered it happening. [656] She aligned the transmitter to that remembered wavelength, matched herself to it, and pressed the key. [672] The seed went out into the falling sky, aimed at a bounce no instrument could promise, and the doubt did not leave her, because doubt is the shadow cast by any real hope. [705] But when she climbed back to the ambered deck, the huddled stars had turned, all together, like a jury softening, and she felt their stellar doubt meet hers halfway. [734] She gathered the coins one by one, thanking each world by name, quietly enthusiastic, as ever, about whatever came next. [754] Above her the universe kept contracting, patient as a held breath, and somewhere ahead of time, something had already heard her. [775]
