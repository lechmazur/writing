Curator Pell tended the astral orchard of inherited things, which blossomed on a lonely asteroid that no map admitted to. [20] The trees bore pistachio shells instead of fruit, and inside each shell waited a cameo, already carved with the face of someone who would, had, or was currently inheriting. [49] Pell cataloged them all, bewildered, because inheritance here ran in no order anyone could hold. [64] Yesterday she had filed a bequest from a woman born in 2140; this morning, a pocketwatch left by a granddaughter to a grandmother who died before the granddaughter was ever born. [95] The rules were simply complicated: harvest before planting, inherit before birth, carve only what has already been received. [113]

Every bequest was recorded in the Ledger, a book as wide as the orchard, and the Ledger kept one law printed on its spine: do not inquire beyond the first page or the last. [147] All things, the old curators said, lived between those two pages, and nothing needed to live anywhere else. [165] Pell had never minded the law, until the morning the oldest tree split open a shell and offered her a blank. [186] A pistachio shell cameo blank: pale, curved, uncarved, with no face in it at all. [201] Blanks were not supposed to exist. [207] Every shell held a face; every face named an inheritance; every inheritance had a giver and a taker, even when the taker had died a century before the giver drew breath. [238] A blank meant an inheritance with neither. [245] It meant an inheritance from outside the pages. [253]

Pell lifted it into the blossom-light and felt the hunger she had hidden from every curator before her: the desire to pursue forbidden knowledge. [277] She went to the Ledger and did what curators never did. [288] She inquired. [290] "Who gives this, and who receives it?" she asked, pressing the blank against the first page. [306] The Ledger turned its own pages, backward and forward at once, and answered the only way it could: it bloomed. [326] Ink unfurled into white petals across the columns. [334]

Bewildered, Pell retreated to the only room on the asteroid with a door that locked: the bathroom. [351] This was her method, and she had stopped being embarrassed about it years ago; all her philosophical breakthroughs happened in bathrooms, where the plumbing hummed like a patient tutor and the mirror made her look herself in the eye. [390] She sat on the edge of the tub, turned the blank over, and thought. [404] Inheritance, she realized, was nonlinear: not a line but a shape. [415] A bequest could travel forward, backward, sideways through time; the orchard had been proving that since before her grandmother's grandmother was a seed. [438] But a blank, a bequest with no face, could only come from somewhere the Ledger did not reach. [456] Which meant there was an outside. [462] Which meant the first page was not the beginning, and the last page was not the end. [479] Which meant, the mirror suggested with terrible gentleness, that everything Pell loved existed between the first and last page of something, and that the something was, at this very moment, being read. [511] The plumbing hummed approvingly. [515]

She came out of the bathroom changed, the way curators before her must have come out of bathrooms, carrying small enormous thoughts. [537] The orchard had brightened while she sat thinking; every tree had gone from pearl to lamp, and the blossom-light now fell upward as well as down, as if the orchard were trying to be seen. [572] That was the disquiet inside the brightness: being lit and being watched are not so different, from within a book. [592]

Pell carried the blank to the Ledger's final page, where the record of inheritances ended mid-column, and there, against every rule, she wrote a line of her own. [620] "Bequeathed: one pistachio shell cameo, blank, from Curator Pell to whoever is holding the pages: carve it yourself." [638] It was an inheritance running the wrong way, out of the book entirely, and the orchard rustled with something that was either applause or wind. [663] She pressed the blank cameo into the last page, where the paper took it the way soil takes a seed. [683] Somewhere outside the covers, she hoped, a hand would find it, and a face, not hers, never hers, would rise pale and curved out of the pistachio shell, inheriting the orchard, the asteroid, the light, and every bewildered question she had ever asked. [726] The blossoms brightened again, obligingly, and Pell shivered in all that warmth, because the last page had begun, very slowly, to turn. [748]
