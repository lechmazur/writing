The mist came off the river after midnight, silvering the workshop's glass roof until the stars above it seemed to swim. [21] Maren had locked the gates against the reporters, but she could not lock out the quiet. [37] Three days had passed since the con was revealed, and the sanctuary's name had burned through the news like a struck match. [59] Doctor Voss, the beloved founder, had never rediscovered a lost species at all. [72] He had bought common chameleons by the crate and lacquered their skins in colors no honest animal wore, then collected donations for a breeding program that existed only in his brochures. [103] The world called it fraud, and the world was right. [113]

Maren, the sanctuary's wildlife handler, who had fed these animals for six years, who had told schoolchildren they were witnessing a miracle, sat on an upturned bucket and felt her convictions turn to ash. [147] She had not known. [151] That was the truth she kept circling. [158] But not knowing had not kept the lies from moving through her mouth. [171] The chameleons waited in their misted cages, patient as small planets. [182] Voss had fled, the volunteers had scattered, and the donors wanted their money, but nobody had wanted the animals. [201] So Maren stayed, because staying was the only act left that needed no audience. [215]

All evening she had turned the choice over like a stone in her pocket. [229] She could walk out and let the whole rotten enterprise be seized, keeping her hands and her dignity clean. [248] Or she could stay, and be gentle, and be mocked for it. [260] Somewhere between the two, she suspected, lived whatever kind of person she actually was. [274]

She lit the work lamp, warmed a dish of sweet oil, and lifted the first cage lid. [291] The lacquer would have to come off. [298] Not peeled in a single triumphant strip, the way the news had peeled Voss's story apart, but dissolved the way the animals deserved: slowly, by shedding layers, each one softened and wiped away while the creature beneath breathed. [336] She began with the oldest female, the one the brochures had named the Ghost of Analamazaotra. [352] Under the false violet, her skin was green. [360] Plain green, river-weed green, and so entirely itself that Maren's throat closed. [372] She worked with two fingers and a cotton cloth, following the animal's willingness, stopping when it flinched, resuming when it settled. [393] Gentleness, she had learned long ago, was not softness; it was attention carried to the point of devotion. [411]

Near midnight she reached the last cage, and the last animal, and the reason she had waited for this one. [431] He was the young male Voss had painted too hastily, gripping him by the tail, so that the final inch had stayed free of lacquer. [456] All these months, copper from head to flank, he had carried one honest chameleon's tail tip, green and quietly vivid, wrapped around his own hind leg like a kept promise. [486] Maren had noticed it years ago and thought nothing of it. [497] Now it struck her as the thread of truth she might have pulled, if her convictions had been less fragile and her hunger for miracles less deep. [524] She did not look away from that thought. [532] Looking away was how she had gotten here. [540]

She set the male on her wrist, and he gripped her thumb with that green tip as she worked the oil into the copper, layer by misty layer. [568] The false color came away in cloudy swirls on the cloth. [579] Beneath it he was the same green as his tail, whole and unpartitioned, an animal reconstructed rather than invented. [598] Through the glass roof the stars shifted; the mist thickened and thinned; the lamp held its small circle of gold. [618] A shimmering stillness held the room. [624]

When the last of the copper was gone, she sat a long time with him breathing on her hand, and understood what she would do. [649] In the morning she would sign the sworn statement naming every talk she had given, every brochure she had distributed, every dollar she had raised for a lie. [677] Her name would appear beside Voss's in the reports, and she would not contest it. [692] That was the self-respect half. [697] The other half was this: she would stay through the seizure and argue, patiently, for homes for every animal, driving the van herself to whichever zoo or classroom would take them. [728] Compassion without accounting was how the con had flourished; accounting without compassion was how the world would answer it. [747] She meant to live in the seam between. [755]

The chameleon climbed to her shoulder and turned, without ceremony, the color of her coat. [770] Only his tail tip kept its green, quietly vivid against the gray wool, a small lamp she could follow out. [790]
