The fallow period began on the first hard frost of November, when the ground beneath Veilmark went quiet enough to hear itself think. [23] Wren Calloway counted her breaths at the lip of the shaft, because she was claustrophobic and had chosen, of all lives, the explorer's. [46] She was prudent about it: chalk in one pocket, string in the other, and a promise that fear, once it reached her teeth, meant turn around. [72] Beneath the church lay the nexus, five tunnels the founders had drafted like spokes, and for sixty years those tunnels had been mutinying. [95] Walls drifted a handspan a night, angles forgot themselves, and a corridor that had run true for a century might wake up curved, or elsewhere, or gone. [122] The mutiny had already swallowed two cellars and was working on the churchyard wall. [136] The town blamed damp, or the devil, but Wren had read the founders' blueprints: the tunnels had been drawn with a drafting compass, and its point, seated in the hub socket at the nexus heart, was the vital key that held every angle true. [180] In the margin, the surveyor had warned: should the point ever leave its socket, the space itself will mutiny. [199] Sixty years ago the point vanished, lost to time; her grandfather went down during the fallow to find it and never came back. [222] Tonight his granddaughter had an organ. [228]

Old Marta, too frightened to play for her grandfather, had agreed at last to play for her at midnight, alone, so the church organ's reverberation would pour down the bell shaft and into the bones of the nexus. [266] Miners' lore said drifting stone holds still for a held chord, transfixed the way a snake is transfixed, and Marta could hold a chord a very long time. [294] Wren went down the rope with her eyes on her hands, not the dark, and tied off in the shadowed nexus, where starlight fell through a rusted grate, thin and silver, far above. [327] The tunnels should have fanned out around her in five true spokes; instead the walls leaned and wandered, and her chalk marks from two days before had drifted three feet along the stone. [360] The low ceiling pressed on her like a palm; she counted four in, six out, and told herself that fear was only the body doing arithmetic about exits. [388]

She followed the fourth tunnel and found, fifty yards in, a rotted string tied to an iron ring, and chalk arrows in a hand she knew from birthday cards. [417] Her grandfather had made it this far. [424] The arrows led to a niche where the wall had cracked open, and inside, cradled in sixty years of dust, lay his canvas satchel. [448] Wren opened it with cold fingers, and there it was: the drafting compass point, the steel thorn that had scribed the founders' blueprints, wrapped in oilcloth beneath his last chalked message. [479] FOUND IT. [481] THE WALLS WERE HIDING IT. [486] No bones, no further chalk. [491] The mutiny had hidden its own key, and kept the man who found it. [505]

Then, far above, the organ began. [511] It came down not as music but as weather, a pressure of held sound, and when Marta leaned into her chord, the note thickened into something stone could not ignore. [541] The nexus went still, transfixed, pinned through by the lonely church organ's reverberation, and for the first time in sixty years every tunnel stood where it had been drawn. [570] Wren ran, four in, six out, following corridors suddenly straight as ruled lines, to the hub where a socket no bigger than a coin waited in the floor of a stone dais. [602] The chord above her was already thinning. [609]

She knelt, point in hand, and understood what her grandfather had understood: the mutiny was not malice; it was space refusing to be a drawing forever, longing to be a cave again. [641] Full seating meant full obedience: every wall nailed to its blueprint for good. [654] One turn loose, and the tunnels would swing about their pivot like a compass drawing circles, free to drift, but never again to swallow anyone. [679] So she set the point in the socket, turned it three times, felt the stone sigh into alignment all around her, and stopped one turn short of forever. [707] The chord broke; the last note climbed away like a bird. [718]

Wren climbed back into starlight and cold air, and stood a long while on the churchyard grass, listening. [736] Beneath her feet, faint as a turning page, the underground began, slowly and without malice, to redraw itself. [754] Some fallow nights, she thought, a key is not the thing you lock but the thing you let turn. [773]
