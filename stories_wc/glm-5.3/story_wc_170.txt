Spring rain had just left the echoing terrace, and the prism parapet caught the last light, breaking it into slow ribbons of violet and rose. [25] Nara came up the wet stairs the way she always did, unseen, for the mirage painter was famous precisely for being impossible to find. [49] She was perceptively calm, the kind of calm that notices everything, and what she noticed tonight was the hush before bells ring. [71]

For three years the town's bells had kept silence, and the silence had a name: Ilse, the ringer's daughter, who died in the fever spring and left her heart to a stranger. [103] The stranger was Nara. [107] Her own heart had quit that same season, and the surgeons gave her a borrowed heartbeat she felt but did not know, a rhythm that arrived already used, already mourning. [137] At night it beat in patterns that were almost melody, and it took her a year of listening to admit the truth: the heartbeat kept time to a song. [166] Ilse had written lullabies for the town's children, and one of them lived on inside Nara's chest, note for note. [186]

A mirage painter works in what is almost there, and Nara had spent her whole craft on one problem: the town's mirrors showed only what was. [212] Since the fever, glass in every hallway showed people their losses with perfect, merciless accuracy, and the grieving had begun to hang cloth over their reflections. [238] Nara wanted to persuade the mirrors to show possibilities instead, to make each pane a window rather than a wound, but mirage paint fades from glass within an hour. [267] What did not fade was song. [273]

So she chose a subtler method, working through coded lullabies in children's songs, seeding Ilse's old tune with intervals that glass remembers the way it remembers light. [300] For weeks she left the verses where children would find them, folded into songbooks, chalked onto hopscotch squares, hummed once into the ear of a sleeping choirboy who woke certain he had dreamed it. [334] Now, on the rain-rinsed terrace, the children gathered at dusk, and their voices rose off the stone in echoes that layered the melody into harmony, low and violet as the sky. [365] The borrowed heartbeat in Nara's chest kept perfect time. [374]

Below, in the bell tower's shadow, the old ringer heard the song his daughter had written, and he came to the base of the stairs with his lamp, not climbing, only listening. [406] Nara knew him from the letters the donor office never should have forwarded and always did. [422] Every month he wrote to the stranger who carried his daughter's heart, and every month he asked the same question: is she still in there, somewhere, beating? [449] Nara never answered, because a mirage painter's answer would be an illusion, and she refused to lie to grief. [468] Tonight she could offer something truer than an answer. [477]

The last verse ended, and the code inside it finished its slow work. [490] Across the town, cloth coverings slipped from panes, and the glass began to show not what stood before it but what might stand there: a widow seeing her garden in full summer, a lame cobbler dancing, a shuttered bakery warm with bread. [532] Possibilities, rendered in dusky harmony, each reflection a small, credible future. [543] In the mirror by the tower door, the old ringer saw his bell ropes moving, though his hands hung still. [563] He looked up. [566]

Here was the moment Nara had dreaded and planned for, and she met it with grace, which is the only courage available to the almost-invisible. [591] She stepped down into the lamplight, letting herself be seen at last, and pressed his last unanswered letter to her chest, over the place where the borrowed heartbeat kept the rhythm of the song. [625] 'She is here,' Nara said, 'and she is also there,' and she nodded at the mirror, where the ropes swung and the bells existed in the future tense. [653]

The old man climbed. [657] He rang the bells into the hush that had waited three years, and the sound moved over the terrace like weather, and the prism parapet split it, bell by bell, into colors the town had no names for. [695] In every open window, mirrors held their possibilities steady, as if the ringing had ratified them. [711] The children, who never knew what they had carried, only knew the song felt finished, and went home singing it softly. [732]

Nara slipped back into the dusk before anyone could thank her, because gratitude pins a mirage down, and she meant to stay unpinned. [755] But at the stair she paused, one hand on the wet prism parapet, and let the borrowed heartbeat settle, at last, into a rhythm she recognized as her own. [784]
