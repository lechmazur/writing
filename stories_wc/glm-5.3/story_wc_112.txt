On the ice moon of Veyra, the xenobiology research station clung to the pole like a brass tick. [18] Mira Sela worked its upper gantries, the station's obliging sky mechanic, forever saying yes. [32] Patch the weather drones, Mira. [37] Soothe the humming coolant lines. [42] She always answered of course, and meant it. [50] But her private yes belonged to Dr. Ansel Raye, who believed the lumen moths proved something impossible. [67] The moths were the reason the station existed, vast translucent-winged natives that beat through the methane dusk in murmuring rivers. [87] Except at twilight. [90] At twilight they settled on the station's crown, folding around the magnetic nexus, and the whole world went quiet within the hush of beating wings at rest. [117] Ansel's heresy was simple: the moths did not sleep. [126] They moved sideways. [129] Their wing scales, he claimed, held interference patterns like frozen light, each twilight layered over the last, all of them still happening somewhere beside us. [154] Time, he said, moves sideways. [159] Mira had lost a brother to a drone crash three winters back, and sideways sounded like a door. [177] So she meant to prove it. [183] The proof required the nexus. [188] The magnetic nexus anchored the station's field lattice, and at twilight the resting moths damped its flux into strange, patient standing waves. [210] Ansel's theory: the scales modulated those waves, etching every twilight into the field the way light etches a holographic plate. [230] If he was right, the pattern could be decoded through holographic principles: split the flux into a reference wave and an object wave, recombine them, and the stored twilights would rise as images. [263] The administration called it moth mysticism and refused funding. [272] Mira built the decoder anyway, out of drone parts and patience, on hours she stole from sleep. [289] For six evenings she failed. [294] On the seventh, Ansel found her on the crown gantry in her heated gauntlets, hands resting against the nexus housing the way a nurse rests a palm on a fevered brow. [325] "You'll freeze," he said. [329] "The housing runs mechanically warm," she said, and it did, a small animal heat inside all that ice. [347] That was Veyra's whole trick, she thought: icy warmth, cold air around warm metal, warmth you had to work for. [367] Ansel watched the moths settle, ten thousand wings folding shut like a field closing its eyes. [383] "If this doesn't work," he said, "we publish nothing and I look like a fool." [396] "Wear the title proudly," Mira said. [402] "It comes with colleagues who fix things." [409] The hush deepened. [412] When the last wing went still, the standing waves steadied on her scope like held breath. [428] Mira threw the brass lever she had salvaged from a decommissioned skimmer, and the decoder sang. [444] The flux split, bent, recombined. [449] Light gathered above the gantry in a slow column, and the column filled with twilight. [464] Not one twilight. [467] Many, layered edge to edge like pages fanned sideways across the air: the moths settling yesterday, settling last winter, settling the night her brother's drone fell burning through the dark. [497] Every resting moment, side by side, none of them gone. [507] Ansel made a sound like a man being forgiven. [516] Mira watched the fanned twilights and understood what she had actually built. [528] Not a resurrection. [531] A guarantee. [533] Her brother's last evening still existed, whole, breathing faintly, one page over from her own. [548] She reached toward that page, and her warm hand passed through cold light. [561] She could not cross. [565] She had never expected to. [570] It was enough that the page was there. [578] Then, across the crown, the moths began to stir. [587] Not the violent waking of startled animals, but something gentler: subdued awakenings rolling through the crowd like a tide deciding to come in, wings opening one folded pair at a time inside the fading image of every twilight they had ever rested through. [630] Ansel was already recording, whispering figures to himself. [638] Mira eased the lever back, and the column of light folded itself away, page by patient page. [655] "Say it," she told Ansel. [660] He laughed, breath fogging in the icy air. [668] "Time moves sideways." [671] She looked at the dark where the hologram had been, at the living wings rising around her, warm machine against her palms, cold moon in her lungs. [698] Sideways would do. [701] Some doors you do not walk through; some you only need to know are unlocked. [716] Below, the station lights came on one by one, small warm squares in the ice, and Mira went down to fix whatever needed fixing next, saying yes to all of it. [747]
