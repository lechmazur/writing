The moon clears the skyline at nine, and Wren is already waiting in the rusted library. [16] She calls herself Wren this month; last month she was Ash, and before that, Mercy. [31] Every month she molts her personality like skin, because a vagrant penny poet who stays one person too long gets remembered, and remembered gets removed. [56] Pennies buy her soup; poems cost nothing to carry. [65] The library is an old Carnegie branch the city condemned, its steel shelves rusted orange, its roof holed so the stars come through like scattered change. [91] Elias is inside already, wiring a salvaged lamp. [99] They met eleven years ago, sorting mildewed donations in the Halloway prison library, two prisoners building a friendship out of alphabetical order. [121] She was in for burning a slumlord's rotting building, an act she still calls urban poetry; the judge called it arson. [142] Elias had thirty months for a car that wasn't his. [152] In a box of church donations they found a coffee tin, and inside the tin, a false bottom. [170] Underneath lay seed packets--marigold, cosmos, poppy--folded by somebody's dead grandmother. [182] Anything can hide a second chance, Elias said, if you build it a hiding place. [197] She has carried the tin through every molt since. [206]
On the streets again, she sold poems for pennies--a cent a line, sorrow cheerfully negotiable--and dropped the coins in the tin's honest top half. [232] The seeds stayed below, illegal as hope in a city that paves first and asks questions never. [249] When Elias walked out, they began the work they never named out loud. [262] They scattered seeds into sidewalk cracks and vacant lots at four in the morning. [276] By June, sunflowers had shouldered through the asphalt on Delancey, and the demolition crews arrived to find a street the neighborhood had suddenly, fiercely decided it loved. [303] Bulldozers pause for flowers; lawyers do not. [310] The pause bought them a summer, and a summer was enough to pick a target: the condemned library, scheduled for wrecking in October. [333] They decided to rebuild it instead of letting it die. [343] So the molting poet and the ex-car thief spent the season hauling rust out by the wheelbarrow, straightening shelves with borrowed tools, patching glass with salvaged windows that never quite matched. [374] Every month she changed her name and he did not blink. [385] "Still you under there," he would say, and it was the only sentence that survived every skin. [402] That is what the molts taught her: identity is a coat, but some people know your shoulders. [419]
Tonight is October's first cold night, and the moon is clearing the skyline through the broken skylight, pouring silver down the fractured glass so the whole ceiling shines like a wound that learned to bloom. [454] Four hundred books stand straight on clean shelves, every one of them donated, stolen, or rescued from the dumpster behind the university. [476] A hand-lettered sign waits by the door: Open When the Stars Are. [488] Elias lights the lamp and the rusted dark turns amber. [498] "Penny for a poem," he says, because tonight the poems are free, and the penny is just to keep the ritual honest. [520] She writes one for the first visitor, a girl in a coat too thin, who trades a penny for four lines about sunflowers and jackhammers. [545] The girl takes a book on stars and vanishes into the cold, and that is the whole revolution, right there. [565]
The demolition notice is still taped to the door; the flowers outside are still illegal; the city will come with machines eventually. [587] But the moon has cleared the skyline now, and under it a rusted library is open, and the tin sits on the desk with its false bottom full of next spring. [618] Anarchy gave her ashes; she forged them into verses, and the verses into seeds, and the seeds into this: beauty, blooming in resistance, fractured and lit from inside. [646] When the wreckers come, they will have to tear down something alive. [658] Next month she will be someone new--Sol, maybe, or Verity--and Elias will look up from the desk and know her anyway. [681] The moon climbs; the pennies gleam; the seeds wait under the false bottom for the next broken ground. [699]
