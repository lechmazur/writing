After the resuscitation, I began to glow in photographs. [9] Not a flattering sheen, but a lingering luminance: the flash refusing to fade, pooling beneath my skin in every print like milk under ice. [33] Each new picture burned a little brighter, as though the light were accruing interest. [47] My sister called me photographically cursed; the doctors called it double exposure and blamed the camera. [63]

The abandoned funhouse pulled at me the way a scab pulls a thumb. [76] It was there, in the spinning-barrel corridor, that my heart had stopped; and it was there the smell of wet rot and caramelized sugar still hung, sweet and spoiled at once, like a wound dressed in perfume. [113] I hated it and could not stay away. [121]

On my third visit, moonlight snagged on something in the mildewed straw by the ticket booth: an opera cloak clasp, silver gone soft with tarnish, shaped like a moth with spread wings. [153] It had belonged to Madame Vesper, the fortune teller who owned the mirror maze until the carnival closed. [171] The moment my fingers closed around it, the whispering began. [181] "You glow because you are hiding something," the advisor breathed, a voice like dust settling on a warm lamp. [200] "Hidden things leak." [203]

The whisper guided me into Madame Vesper's booth, where her dream dictionary still lay open, its spine swollen with decades of marginalia. [225] The notes she had added were paradoxes, every one. [234] Beside Drowning she had written, "To breathe, exhale." [242] Beside Falling: "You land by letting go." [249] Beside Death, in a hand pressed so hard the nib had torn the page: "What is unbosomed cannot be dreamed again." [270] "Fear is a parasite of silence," the whispering advisor murmured, its breath warm against my ear though the booth was freezing. [291] "It grows fat on everything you refuse to say." [300] "So we will neither fight it nor flee it; we will trick fear into revealing its weakness, which is this: it cannot survive being named completely." [326]

I carried the dictionary into the mirror maze, the clasp biting cold into my palm like a coin of ice. [346] Distorted reflections multiplied around me, a congregation of grotesques: too tall, too wide, sagging, stretched, each wearing my frightened face. [366] They were hideous, and I could not look away. [375] That was the place's repulsive allure: it showed you the worst of yourself and made you crave a longer look. [395] There, before that warped congregation, I unbosomed myself. [403] I confessed everything the doctors, my sister, and my own diary had never heard: how the spinning barrel had thrown me into the mirrors, how the pain arrived like a struck bell, how in the final second I had felt relieved. [444] That was the secret eating me alive: dying had felt like being set down. [458] I said it aloud, every shameful syllable, and the mirrors drank it in. [471]

Then the glow left me. [476] I watched it go: the luminance slid out of my chest and pooled across the mirrored glass, lingering there as a soft, greasy sheen, beautiful the way spilled mercury is beautiful. [507] In the cleared mirrors my reflection straightened into an ordinary woman, and standing behind her, translucent and moth-winged, was the advisor: Madame Vesper herself, or whatever the funhouse had kept of her. [539] "Fear's weakness," she whispered, already fading, "was that it could live only in hiding, and you have left it nowhere to hide." [561]

I pinned the clasp to my coat on the way out, a small tarnished moth over my sternum, where the light used to leak. [585] At home I photographed myself in the bathroom mirror, hands shaking, half sick with the need to see. [603] The flash flared and died the way flashes do: obedient, ordinary, gone. [615] I was only a woman in a dim room, unlit, unlovely, alive. [627] And still, some nights, I drive out to that reeking funhouse and stand in the spoiled-sugar dark, staring at the soft glow trapped in the mirror maze, wanting it back the way you want back a wound you cannot stop touching.
