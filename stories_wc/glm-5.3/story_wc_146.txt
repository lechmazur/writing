The bell tuner arrived in Embervale on the last market day anyone bothered to hold, carrying nothing but a leather roll and an ebony tuning fork. [26] He did not say his name. [32] When the innkeeper asked what business a bell tuner had in a town whose bells had been silent for eleven years, he only smiled and asked which street walked heaviest. [62] He slept beneath the ancient archway at the town's east gate, where the stones were carved with dancers whose faces had been worn smooth. [86] In the morning he struck the fork against the archway's keystone and held it to the ground. [103] The cobblestones answered, faint and wrong, like a choir humming through clenched teeth. [116]

The mountain above Embervale had been shifting for years. [125] The surveyors gave the town two winters before the slope came down. [137] Half the families had already left; the other half stayed because leaving is harder than it sounds when your dead are in the ground. [161] The tuner began walking. [165] All day, every day, up one street and down another, striking his fork, listening, placing his boot heel on individual stones in slow deliberate beats. [190] He was infinitely patient. [194] Where another man would have cursed the tedium, he treated each crooked stone like a student who simply needed more time. [215]

On the ninth day the schoolteacher, Marta, cornered him under the archway. [227] "You're fixing nothing," she said. [232] "The bells are rusted, the young are gone, and the mountain is coming down on all of us." [250] He held up the ebony fork, still humming. [258] "The founders did not pave these streets for beauty," he said. [269] "They built an instrument." [273] He tapped a cobblestone with his heel, three slow knocks. [283] "Every footstep seats a stone deeper, if the rhythm is right." [294] "A thousand walkers, in time, held this hill in balance for four hundred years." [308] "Then the bells went flat, the dancers forgot the measure, and the balance began to die." [324] "You want to rebuild a town with footsteps?" [332] "I want to restore what it stood on," he said. [342] "Balance is a shared thing, and Embervale stopped sharing it." [352]

He started with the bells, because bells are the town's metronome. [363] Climbing the tower in the ruined church, he filed rust, replaced pins, and brought each bronze voice back to its old pitch, one grain of correction at a time. [392] When the noon bell rang true for the first time in eleven years, the sound moved down the streets like weather. [413] People came out of houses with their heads tilted. [422] That evening he taught them the measure: heel, pause, heel, a walking beat older than their surnames, the same step the carved dancers on the archway had been keeping all along. [453] It felt foolish, at first, forty people walking in step down an empty street like a procession for a holiday nobody remembered. [475] But under their feet the cobblestones began to sing. [484] Each step struck a small clean note, and the notes braided into chords, and the chords passed from street to street until the whole valley hummed like a struck string. [514]

Word travels strangely in the hills. [520] Exiles heard it in market towns, the story of a dying place where the streets had begun to ring, and they came home the way birds return to a light left burning. [552] They arrived under the ancient archway and were met with a heel, a pause, a heel. [568] Within a season, Embervale's footsteps numbered in the hundreds again. [578] Shutters reopened. [580] The bakery hired a second baker. [586] Children who had never seen the town full learned its streets by their music. [600]

On a windless night in the second winter, the valley went utterly silent, the wild hush of a held breath. [620] The mountain shifted. [623] From the archway, the tuner watched the dark slope lean toward the town, and he struck the ebony fork one last time, letting its note run down through the stones like a summons. [656] Below him, four hundred sleepless people poured into the streets and began to walk. [670] Heel, pause, heel. [673] The cobblestones answered in a great slow chord, and the hillside, finding the town holding its old balance, settled back into its long sleep like a body rolling from the edge of a bed. [707] In the hush after, the bell tuner stood beneath the archway, listening to a town that had been rebuilt one step before ruin. [730] Then he rolled up his tools and walked out through the gate, his footsteps fading down the road in perfect time. [751]
