The first letter arrived on a Tuesday, announcing my father's death and enclosing his watch. [15] He had been a confidence man his whole life, and the watch was his final instrument of persuasion. [33] Later I would understand that everything that mattered happened between that letter and one still to come. [50] The lawyers called the watch a trinket; I wore my veil to the reading and said nothing, as I had said nothing for eleven years. [75] I am a dreamer by nature and veiled by vow, for when my father stood trial, I could neither lie for him nor speak against him, and I have said nothing since. [107]

Since then, every word I refused to speak came back to me in dreams, always the same dream: a library glowing inside a wall of mist. [133] I believed it was the library of unspoken words, where every withheld sentence is shelved, and finding it had become the one desire I permitted myself. [159] The watch ran strangely: an hour forward, an hour backward, cyclically reversed, as if time could not decide what to make of my father. [183] Late one night I noticed that the pauses between its reversals were never the same length. [199] Three seconds, then eleven, then three again: the delays were not flaws but letters, an unfolding cipher hidden in the mechanism, a message propagating toward me one deliberate pause at a time. [231] So I began to document it. [237] For nine nights I sat with a candle and a notebook, logging every delay, and the numbers became letters, and the letters became words: north, hill, river, dawn. [265]

With each decoded word I felt a joy I did not trust. [277] Con men leave no gifts, only hooks; I knew the message might be bait, the glowing library might be his final performance, and still my hands trembled with something dangerously close to hope. [310] Cautious exuberance, I told myself, is still exuberance. [318] At dawn, when the hands swung backward, I walked north out of the town, over the hill, across the river, and into the mist. [342] Even light suffered propagation delays in that mist, arriving late, so that I walked always toward where the glow had been, never where it was. [367] Then I was through it, standing in a reading room that glowed like a held breath. [383]

Shelves climbed into fog, and every book was thin, because a book of unspoken words is only ever one sentence long. [404] A keeper with mist for a face asked whether I had come to deposit or to withdraw. [421] I answered in my notebook, because I did not yet trust my voice: withdraw. [435] She led me to a shelf marked with my father's alias, and there it was, one slim volume: the sentence he never said. [458] My heart pounded like a mark's as I opened it. [468] It read: You were the only true thing I never conned, and I could never say so, because truth was the one language I never learned to speak. [496]

Then the keeper brought me to my own shelf. [505] Eleven years of silence sat there in a single slim book. [516] A withdrawn sentence must be spoken, the keeper said, or it returns to the shelf by morning. [533] I took my book, and the watch in my pocket ran backward the whole way home, as if counting down to something. [555]

The second letter was waiting on my table, posted by my father before he died and timed, the lawyer's note said, to arrive exactly now. [580] Even the postal service was one more channel of delays he had measured perfectly; his lies had always arrived instantly, but his love could only travel slowly, reaching me after everything else. [612] The letter read: If this has found you, the delays did their work, and you found the library. [630] One last confidence, my darling: everything I never said was true. [641] I lifted the veil. [645] In the empty room, aloud, at last, I spoke my withdrawn sentence: I forgive you, Papa. [661] The watch ticked forward then, and kept going, no longer reversed, no longer carrying messages, just an honest clock measuring out the rest of my life. [687]
