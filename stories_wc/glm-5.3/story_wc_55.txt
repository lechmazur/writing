The sanctuary kept no sound, and Merren had kept it that way for thirty years. [15] She was its clock scribe, the one who wrote down the last hours of machines that would never tick again, and she preferred ink to voices because ink never asked her anything. [47] Behind the eastern ridge, a hidden sunrise was already at work, sliding grey light along the floors before the sun itself dared to appear. [71] The hush in those halls was glacial, old and slow, a silence that had crept down over centuries the way ice creeps down a valley, grinding everything soft. [99]

This morning she carried something that was not a catalogue. [109] Under a length of grey cloth she bore the clock she had begun at nineteen and abandoned at twenty-three: the regulator she had meant to build for the sanctuary tower, the maker's dream that had broken the day her master died and the guild ruled her hands too uncertain for fine work. [161] For thirty years it had waited in a chest beneath her desk, unfinished, unwound, unspoken of. [177] She had told herself she kept it for parts. [186] She had lied with the fluency of someone who lies only to herself. [199]

To reach the Hall of Stilled Clocks she had to cross the forecourt, which the founders had paved with slabs of clouded crystal. [222] It had rained in the night, and rain was the one thing the crystal did not forget. [239] Each slab drank whatever fell on it and held it, and for hours afterward the whole court whispered back its rain, thin and silver, a thousand small voices reciting weather that had already gone. [273] That was the sanctuary's one mercy: silence, yes, but silence with a memory in it. [288] The stone smelled of petrichor, wet dust and old rain, and the smell undid her a little, because it was the smell of the morning she had last touched the clock. [319]

She stepped out under the eaves, and the whispering rose around her ankles like shallow water. [335] The crystals were playing back the night's storm in their broken, tumbling way, and somewhere in the wash of it Merren heard something she had no reason to hear: a tick. [366] Then another, spaced like a heartbeat, spaced like the escapement she had designed at nineteen and never heard run. [385] She stood very still in the glacial hush and let the soundless court imagine her clock aloud. [402] It was not the dream, she knew that. [410] It was the dream's echo, arriving thirty years late, the way light arrives from a star already gone. [428]

Inside the Hall of Stilled Clocks, a thousand dead machines hung from iron hooks, their pendulums resting like folded wings. [448] Merren found the empty hook she had measured weeks ago, the lowest one, near the floor, where a small unfinished thing would not look ridiculous. [473] She unwrapped the clock with excruciatingly gentle hands, tilting it so the light found the brass wheels she had cut herself at nineteen, the half-finished face, the empty place where the hands should have gone. [508] Her fingers had steadied years ago, but by then the dream had been under the desk so long that digging it out felt like grave robbery. [534] Fear had been the reason, not her fingers, and this morning, in the grey light, she saw it whole and did not look away. [558] She hung the clock on its hook, and the hall accepted it without comment, the way it accepted everything. [577] Then she took out her ink and her ledger, because she was still a scribe, and scribes give things their true names before letting them go. [603] Her hand did not shake. [608] One regulator, movement incomplete, she wrote, maker unknown to the guild, known to me. [622] The last three words cost her more than the first thirty years had. [635] She closed the ledger and let out a breath she could not remember taking. [649]

When she crossed the forecourt again the crystal had nearly finished its rain, the whisper thinning to a seam of sound, then to nothing, then to the hush that was always underneath. [681] The petrichor was fading too, and she found, to her surprise, that she could bear its going. [698] That was the whole gift, she decided: not the dream returned, but the weight of it set down. [716] Behind the ridge the hidden sunrise finally broke, and light walked up the forecourt slab by slab, filling each crystal with a brief white fire, and Merren walked out through it, empty-handed, unhurried, keeping the sanctuary's silence as she had always kept it, but lighter now, like ice at the end of its long creep, arriving at last at the sea.
