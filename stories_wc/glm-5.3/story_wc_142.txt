Mira meets the landborn newcomers at Garboard Gate, where the raft city of Ponte Vivo rocks beneath their unaccustomed feet. [20] Ponte Vivo is a perpetual carnival afloat: ninety lashed-together rafts of calliopes, carousel horses, fried-dough smoke, and netted rooftop gardens. [40] Mira is the carnival's culture shock absorber, hired to steady people whose stomachs and certainties tilt with every swell. [59] For nine years she has taught arrivals the tide tables, the haggling songs, the etiquette of Ferris wheels at dawn. [79] The Meridian Group, which holds the harbor lease, pays her salary and requires her tidy monthly reports. [96] The neighbors who raised her after Old Harrow drowned call her theirs. [108] She serves both, and lately both have begun to notice. [118]

Privatization comes in spring, when the lease converts into private deeds and the carnival becomes a gated resort. [136] Until then everything is technically common water, still negotiable, still savable, and that technicality is all the rafters have. [155] Gentrification is already lapping at the edges: ticketed funiculars, a wine flotilla, eviction notices written in polite ink. [173] Families who hammered these decks together are being priced off them, deck by deck. [187] Absorbing shocks was her trade, but no training covered gentrification, so she would learn to navigate it instead. [205]

Mira wears a chip of Old Harrow's foundation stone on a cord beneath her shirt. [220] When the town drowned, the mayor split the cornerstone, giving one half to those who fled inland and one to those who took to the water. [246] Her mother fled inland carrying that other half, and last month a parcel arrived holding a chip snapped from it with a jeweler's care. [270] The note read: Meridian is buying the drowned county too. [280] Same signatures, same polite ink. [285] So the two halves of Mira press at her from both directions: the company that feeds her and the people who made her, the shore and the water, the deed and the dance. [318]

Her mind has always run in two registers, orderly and intuitive, like the raft itself. [333] She files reports in flawless columns; she also reads the current beneath a conversation the way a pilot reads undertow. [353] Intuition showed her the graffiti first; order let her decode it. [364] On Ponte Vivo, graffiti art is not vandalism but a postal system: carp in certain colors mark safe moorings, spirals mark Meridian surveyors, a green lantern outline means a family needs volunteers by morning. [398] Meridian's clerks photograph the walls and file them as noise. [408]

When the eviction schedule leaked, Mira knew she would have to reorganize the entire alphabet of walls. [425] The symbols had grown up neighborhood by neighborhood, mutually incomprehensible, useless for relocating twelve hundred households in eleven weeks. [444] So she rebuilt them into one grammar, one legend, teachable to a child in an afternoon. [460] The trouble was accuracy: the new code was worthless without Meridian's own survey maps. [474] Only she could borrow them, because only she was trusted, because she had spent nine years being trustworthy for the other side. [496] She sat with that choice for three nights, the stone chip cold against her sternum, one broken town pressing on her from both sides. [520]

Then she thought of her Tuesday orientation tour, the mural workshop where landborn visitors painted welcome banners to feel like part of the carnival. [544] Let the buyers spread the code themselves. [551] She copied the survey data into innocuous paint-by-number patterns, and in eleven Tuesdays the reorganized grammar bloomed across every hull and breakwater, applied by the very visitors whose money was displacing her neighbors. [584] Meridian photographed cheerful noise. [588] The families read the same walls and found mooring assignments, pooled rents, and a schedule for chaining their rafts into a cooperative flotilla that could simply row away before any deed was signed. [621]

On the last night before privatization, she filed her final report: honest, complete, decoding every symbol, dated for release after the flotilla cleared the harbor mouth. [647] Let them read it when reading no longer mattered. [656] Dawn came up gray and confetti-bright, calliopes wheezing, ninety rafts straining at their cables. [670] Mira cut them loose in order, deck by numbered deck, her columns and her hunches finally pulling the same direction. [690] Behind them the resort would open on empty water, selling views of a memory. [704] She wrote one line inland: bring the other half, we are starting a new cornerstone. [719] Desperation had cut the cables; hope would have to make the crossing. [731] Ahead the water belonged to no one yet, and the carnival, as always, went on. [746]
