At twilight, the ember nexus kept its usual hush: a vast stone bowl sunk behind the cathedral, banked with red coals that gave light but were never permitted to flare. [30] Brother Wren knelt at its rim with his ledger, recording attempt one hundred and nine in a careful hand. [49] He was a hopeless alchemist, and no one knew it better than he did. [63] For eleven years he had carried ash from this place, mixed it with rainwater, wine, blood, and song, and coaxed it toward life. [86] One hundred and eight small failures lay described in his pages, each mourned in gentle ink, for Wren felt everything and complained of nothing. [110]

The Order had long since decided what he was for. [120] His mother had been Bishop of the Preserved Flame, and her crozier still leaned in his cell, its silver crook snapped clean from the shaft on the day of her funeral, when he refused consecration in her place. [158] At the next equinox, they said, the refusal would be forgiven and forgotten. [171] He would take the vows, tend the nexus in its lawful twilight, and keep the fire from changing until he died. [192] It was a kind destiny, warm and smothering as ash. [202]

Every handful of nexus ash cost a grave-penny, paid into the Offering Mouth before he was allowed to gather. [221] Tonight his fingers were stiff with cold; the penny slipped, rang on the stone lip, rolled, and fell into the coals. [242] The embers closed over it like a mouth. [250] Where it sank, the red deepened, and a thread of true flame, the first Wren had ever seen there, stood up briefly and was gone. [275]

That was when the penny dropped twice: once into the fire, and once inside him. [290] Staring at the bright scar among the coals, he understood, all at once and completely, what eleven years of failure had been trying to tell him. [316] The nexus was starving. [320] Nothing had been fed to this fire in a century; the Order only took ash out, and the law forbade anything to burn here, so the embers lay in a state that was neither death nor life, preserved exactly as he was preserved. [363] Fire does not live on keeping. [369] Fire lives on eating. [373] Nothing could be vivified from ash that had never been allowed to consume. [386] Creation ran on destruction the way flame runs on fuel, and every one of his mixtures had only ever been a drawing-back, a keeping, a small politeness offered to a fire that wanted to be fed. [422]

Wren rose, went to his cell, and carried the broken crozier back in both arms, crook and severed shaft together. [442] His hands shook, and he let them; stoic, he had learned, was not the same thing as stone. [460] His mother's face was carved along the crook, and he loved her, and he laid her on the coals anyway. [480] The nexus took the wood the way hunger takes bread. [490] Flame climbed in a soft roar; twilight became honest day for the first time in a hundred years, and behind him the cathedral bells rang at nothing, alarmed. [518]

Then there was full burning, and after full burning, for the first time in his life, genuine dark: coals consumed to nothing, the great bowl going cold. [545] Wren waited in that darkness breath by breath, making the only correction that had ever mattered. [561] One hundred and nine attempts, each a little truer than the last, and this had been the final approximation: destroy completely, and let what comes next come. [588] Toward morning, in the deepest of the ash, something moved. [598] A pale shoot stood up and opened two leaves. [607] Then, from its crown, a flower unfolded, white with a heart of live red, its petals edged like frost on a windowpane, cool even to look at, opening out of perfect blackness. [639] It breathed, unmistakably, and the whole dead bowl of the nexus breathed around it. [653]

The Order would call this ruin; they would arrive at dawn with new laws, and perhaps with chains. [671] Wren opened his ledger and wrote, beneath one hundred and nine, the entry for attempt one hundred and ten: vivified. [691] His destiny had been to stand at this rim forever, keeping a fire from ever changing. [707] He had interrupted it the only way interruption is possible, by letting the fire finish. [722] Out of that ending, the ash had done what ash has always wanted to do. [737] He knelt in the cold, feeling every ache in his old knees, and watched the frozen bloom shine. [755]
