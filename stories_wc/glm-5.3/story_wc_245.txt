Mara had been blind since seven, a mapmaker since nine, and lost since the fever took her sister Ila last spring. [21] Her maps were famous in the valley: raised lines carved in cedar, rivers a fingertip could trace, mountains that rose under the thumb. [44] She could chart any terrain on earth, but the country inside her own chest stayed blank, and that unmapped dark frightened her more than any cliff. [70] She had one strange gift to guide her: language entered her through skin. [83] Press a palm to a page and words soaked in like rain into sand; hold a stranger's hand and you learned its history of calluses, its dialect of scars. [112] When Ila died, Mara held her wrist and absorbed the last of her, a language with no words at all, only grief's grammar. [135]

So when old traders spoke of the Sirenwood, an ultrasonic forest whose trees sang above the range of ears, Mara understood at once that the forest was speaking. [163] Its song could not be heard, only felt against the skin, and hidden deep inside lay a stillpoint, one place where the chorus stopped and left a silence so complete you could finally hear yourself. [198] Traders who stumbled into it came out changed, quieter, scoured clean. [209] It opened only across the pause of shifting constellations, one night each autumn when the stars hung between arrangements, holding their breath. [231] This year, the pause came tomorrow. [237] Mara could refuse her own grief no longer; she would go and navigate its uncharted depths, or be lost with Ila forever. [259]

She went in before dawn wearing her mother's platinum compass ring. [270] The ring was her instrument: she had used it for years to dowse for whatever was unresolved in whoever wore it, and all morning it tugged her wrist toward the heart of the wood. [304] She let it lead, and it led by music. [313] The first tree hummed when she touched it, one high note above hearing, felt in her palm like a struck tuning fork. [335] She pressed her cheek to the bark and the note soaked through her skin, and she knew it was a direction, not a sound. [359] East, it meant, and a little sorrow. [366] The next tree gave another note, and the next another, a trail of tones she absorbed one by one, and slowly the trail became a hidden melody, assembling itself phrase by phrase as she walked. [401] By noon she recognized it, and her knees nearly gave. [411] It was the song Ila used to tap against her palm when they were girls, their private code for when the dark got too loud. [436] The forest had no way to know it. [444] Unless grief, absorbed through skin, leaks back into the world and is answered. [457] Mara stopped arguing with the mystery and kept walking, following her sister's song. [470]

The chorus thickened as afternoon fell, hundreds of trees singing inaudibly, and she felt the whole forest as one vast slow voice repeating the only sentence grief ever says. [499] Stay, it hummed. [502] You cannot. [504] The ring pulled harder. [508] Night came, and with it the sky's great pause: constellations hung mid-shift, the Hunter half-risen, the Swan refusing to set, all of heaven still between one shape and the next. [538] The trees fell silent all at once, the way a held breath is silent. [552] Mara had found the stillpoint. [557] It was not emptiness. [561] It was a room. [565] In that absolute quiet she heard, plainly, at last, the second half of the melody, the answer phrase Ila never got to tap out. [589] It did not say stay. [594] It said go; it said I was never lost, and neither are you. [607] Mara wept there in the dark, and the weeping was a quiet blaze, low and steady, burning without destroying anything. [627]

When the stars resumed and the Hunter finished rising, she knelt and worked by touch, as she always had. [646] From her pack she took a cedar board and her graver and carved the stillpoint itself: no rivers, no ridgelines, only the melody written in raised notes, a map of the exact shape of the hole Ila left, and the path that led through it and out the other side. [696] It was the first chart of uncharted depths she had ever finished, and it was of no place on earth at all. [718] She walked out at dawn with the ring cool on her finger, pointing at nothing, resolved at last. [736] In the valley they asked what she had found, and she held out the board and let them run their fingers along the notes, one by one, all the way home. [767]
