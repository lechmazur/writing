They were cutting the orchard down at dawn, and the whole town held its breath like a choir before the downbeat. [21] In that hush I climbed the steps of the Hollis Sheet Music Archive with an antique perfume bottle sweating in my coat pocket. [44] The bottle had been my sister Wren's. [51]

So, people said, had the ghost haunting the stacks, though they only said it in the back pages, in a column called Dust and Echoes. [76] The column's author was Ottoline Vane, our town's only exorcist, who worked out of the newsroom the way other exorcists work out of churches. [100] She found her hauntings the way she found her scandals: by listening. [112] A ghost, like a secret, loses its grip once it is read aloud. [125]

She was waiting for me between the shelves, gray and small as a pencil shaving, a hand-cranked induction coil cradled in one arm. [148] "You came on the last quiet morning," she said. [157] "The saws arrive at sunup, and ghosts get restless when their roots are threatened." [171] She talked like that always, in metaphors, as if plain speech were a sin she couldn't afford. [188] "An apology," she told me, "is a letter you keep writing after the address burns down." [204] I had been writing mine for eleven years. [212]

Wren and I fought in this archive the summer we were nineteen, over a borrowed dress, over a duet we never finished, over the small change sisters fight with when the real currency is fear. [247] She drove off through the orchard in October rain and never came out the other side of the curve. [266] Everything I meant to say calcified into a stone I carried instead. [278]

"Piano after hours in the Hollis stacks," Ottoline had written, "playing the second voice of a duet that has no first." [299] That missing first voice was me; even dead, Wren waited for my part. [312] "Can you bring her back?" I asked. [319] "I traffic in imperfect miracles," Ottoline said. [326] "Whole ones are above my circulation." [332]

She led me down the aisles, past boxed symphonies and spider-legged metronomes, to the old upright in the reading room, where orchard air drifted through a cracked window. [360] "Spirits are ferrous," she said, setting the coil on the piano lid. [372] "Spin a current through a wire and you spin the dead along with it." [386] She clamped leads to the strings and cranked, and the piano began to hum, every wire buzzing at once, a hive of wrong notes. [410] The induced currents moved through the archive like weather. [419] Pages turned themselves on the shelves, and in the static-heavy air my sister assembled, note by note, out of dust and disbelief. [441] She was imperfect from the first second, flickering like film through a broken projector. [455]

"Open the bottle," Ottoline said. [460] "Scent is the oldest rope we have; memory ties itself to the nose before it ties itself to anything else." [480] The antique stopper fought me, then gave. [487] Out came the orchard in blossom, bottled years ago, when we were still girls who thought there would be more springs. [508] Wren's outline thickened wherever the sweetness touched it, the way a photograph develops. [521] I said it then, the overdue apology, all of it: the dress, the duet, the eleven silent years. [539] I was sorry for the words I had used and sorrier for the ones I had withheld. [556]

Ghosts cannot cry, I learned, but they can hold a chord. [567] The piano sustained one low tone under her flickering hands, and I understood it as well as I ever understood anything she played. [590] Eleven years of silence, and we had reconnected for the length of one held note. [605] Then the humming thinned, because miracles burn whatever fuel they find, and ours was perfume, current, and the orchard's last quiet hour. [627] Wren faded the way the column had promised she eventually must: not banished, only finished. [642]

Ottoline unclamped the leads and closed her notebook. [650] "Will she come back?" I asked. [656] "Miracles are loaves, not ledger entries," she said. [664] "You get one warm, and you don't get to choose the shape." [676] Outside, the eastern sky had gone the color of piano wire. [687] We stood in the doorway while the last hush held, breathing blossom and ozone, waiting for the saws. [705]

"Imperfect," Ottoline said, reading my face. [711] "But a cracked bell still tells the hour." [719] I held the empty bottle to my chest, warm as a struck chord, full of nothing, which is to say full of her. [742]
