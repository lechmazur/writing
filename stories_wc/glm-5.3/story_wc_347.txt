<story>
Odell had kept the cursed orchard for eleven years and hated every crooked tree in it. [16] The apples grew bruise-colored, the pears sideways, the nuts bitter as regret, yet the whole grove smelled like honey left too long in the sun. [41] It was a graceless beauty, and it was hers, because she had eaten one green apple at seven years old and the orchard never forgot a debt. [68] She was a caretaker the way a prisoner is a resident. [79] The bargain was simple: eat the fruit, tend the trees, forever. [90] Nobody had ever told her what the orchard was for. [100]

Below the grove, the cliff folded into a labyrinth, and through its passages patrolled mechanical birds on ticking brass wings. [120] They were beautiful the way traps are beautiful, all polished pinions and dead glass eyes. [135] Tonight, three lanterns bobbed among the turning walls, and lanterns meant travelers, and travelers meant people dead before the night frost finished forming. [158] The walls swallowed every sound, so shouting directions was useless. [168] Odell knew frost, though; she counted things without meaning to, offhandedly exact the way other people are tone-deaf. [186] Moonrise in ninety-one minutes, she judged, give or take nothing. [196]

The birds should have guided wanderers to safety; that was their whole purpose, back when the orchard's first keeper built them. [217] The labyrinth's soil claimed whatever touched it, and whatever it claimed, the orchard's roots drank. [232] The birds were meant to starve that hunger by walking the lost back out. [246] They read grooves pressed into the passage dust, a great spiral leading always upward, toward the gate. [263] But the blueprint of that spiral was forgotten a hundred years ago, and every clumsy repair since had pressed the grooves backward, so the birds now herd travelers deeper in. [293] Her grandmother had told her so, and had left her one small nut from the orchard's single straight tree, iron-gray, shut tight, with one instruction: never open it. [321] Odell had obeyed for eleven years. [327]

Tonight she set the nut on a flat stone and struck it with her pruning iron, once, twice, until the shell split into two perfect halves. [353] Etched inside each half, fine as a hair, lay the forgotten blueprint: the spiral's true map, every passage drawn in dotted lines, every groove's beginning and end. [380] Grandmother, you impossible woman, she thought, and almost smiled. [389]

The trouble was the ground. [394] She could not touch the labyrinth's soil; what touched it, it kept. [406] She could not carve the grooves, then; she could only leave impressions without touching and assemble the true pattern out of small dents before the frost set the dust like stone. [437]

So she worked the cliff path above the passages with the shell halves tied to her belt, measuring by eye, because her eye refused to be wrong. [464] "Thirty-two paces to the third turning, near enough," she muttered, which meant exactly. [477] Her tools were the orchard's own bitter nuts, shaken down by the basketful from the crooked branches. [494] She threw them over the edge one at a time, and every little strike in the dust below was one dot of the drawing. [518] A nut fell true or she threw again; the pattern admitted no guessing. [531] Dot by dot, line by line, the spiral assembled itself beneath her, graceless and exact, ugly little dents adding up to mercy. [553] Her shoulders burned, her hands cramped, and the lanterns below kept circling the wrong way, closer to the cliff's black heart. [574]

Then the first bird noticed. [579] It hung above a fresh line of dents, ticking, recalibrating, its glass eyes catching the rising moon like a struck match. [600] It banked, sang out in a voice like a kettle, and every bird in the labyrinth answered. [617] The flock turned as one body and went to work, herding the lanterns along the new groove, upward, always upward, toward the gate. [640]

Odell stood on the cliff path with an empty basket and watched three stumbling strangers walk out of the maze alive. [661] The frost found the dust just then, freezing her thousand small impressions into something permanent, a pattern pressed into the cliff without one human fingerprint on it. [688] The travelers would say they had been saved by birds. [698]

Odell climbed back up to her ugly, honey-sweet trees, aching and ungraceful and not done with them yet. [716] She nailed the two shell halves above the orchard gate, blueprint outward, so no future keeper would ever have to guess again. [738] Then she picked one small nut from the straight tree, closed her fist around it, and went back to hating her beautiful, graceless orchard. [762]
