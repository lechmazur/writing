The night train sighed into Wrenhaven rail depot, and the mist closed over it like a held breath. [18] Mira heard it all from her repair shop between the sidings: brakes easing, crates of ship's provisions sliding down ramps, and under everything the harbor, waiting for morning. [46] She had been blind for eleven years and had overseen this shop for ten of them. [62] Her calm was the sort that made the depot men quiet without knowing why. [76] Two apprentices slept upstairs; she could correct their soldering by ear alone. [88]

Her workbench held shallow glass trays and a thin channel of circulating water, angled to catch the depot lamp. [107] When light passed through, it bent, and it fell on her palms as fine threads of warmth. [124] A true surface bent it one way; a warped one another. [135] She read refraction the way other artisans read blueprints. [144]

The knock came before the last crate settled. [152] "It's the Meridian's bird," the harbor keeper said, breathless from the platform. [164] "Flew the channel lights to test them for the morning and clipped a signal wire. [179] Her wing's torn. [182] She lifts anchor at first light, Mira. [189] Maiden voyage." [191]

Every ship leaving Wrenhaven on its first voyage was led out by an artificial hummingbird drone, a thing of brass and glass that read the channel markers' refractions through mist and water and sang the safe channel to the pilot. [231] Mira had built this one the year after the depot fire took her sight. [245] She had poured her remaining horizons into it. [253] What she had wanted, more than she could name, was to turn the world's scattered feedback into direction, and the bird was how she did it. [279] She repaired as some people pray, unhurried, wholehearted, keeping a ledger of every healed thing. [294]

Now she lifted the bird and found the wound by touch, a ragged seam in the left wing where the iridescent panels had sheared from the rib. [321] Patching it was not the difficulty. [327] Matching it was. [330] Every panel had to bend light exactly as its neighbors did, or the bird would read the channel crooked and sing the Meridian onto the shoals. [356] She laid the wing over a tray and let lamplight pour through the water onto it. [372] Light bent by a sound panel warmed her left palm; light bent by the wounded one warmed her right. [391] Wherever the warmth in her right hand strayed, she patched: filed, soldered, pressed new glass into place. [408] The bird hummed against her fingers as she worked, a low interrogative tone wherever the fit sat wrong, a rising note wherever it sat true. [433] She decoded each hum into direction: a hair left, a hair down, again. [446]

The mist coated the glass thickly. [452] Down the water, the Meridian's crew was waking. [460] The keeper lingered at the door. [466] "You could come to the quay," he said. [474] "We would describe it all to you." [481] She smiled without lifting her hands. [487] "Send the bird," she said. [492] "It sees better for me out there than my eyes ever did." [504]

The anchor chain began at first light. [511] She heard it come up across the harbor, link by patient link, the anchor lifting for the Meridian's maiden voyage, and her hands did not hurry. [537] Hurry bent light as surely as warping did. [545] She set the last panel, then held the finished wing above the channel of circulating water and let the lamp pour through. [567] Both palms warmed identically, two threads of light bent into the same argument. [580] The bird's note climbed and steadied. [586]

She carried it to the platform door, mist curling in around her boots, and opened her hands. [603] It rose, a small brass weather of wings, and struck its slow surveying circle. [617] Its chimes came back across the water, each one a bearing, and she stood translating: marker one, the sandbar's elbow, the deep green water beyond. [642] A whistle answered from the Meridian, low and glad. [651] The bird sang the channel out, and the ship went onto its first morning, and Mira listened until the feedback thinned into open sea. [675] Then she turned back to her benches, her trays of still water, the repairs waiting under cloth. [692] The room did not feel small. [698] She knew exactly where she stood, and which way the water ran. [710]
