Inside the dormant volcano of Kel Sareen, the old amphitheater breathed. [11] Every hour the caldera lake swelled with geothermal heat, the cracked dome widened, and the floodgates of the lower tier groaned open to spill the flood into channels that warmed the terraces above. [44] Inhale, exhale: the building had kept this rhythm for two hundred years, and Orenna Vail had kept its gates for twelve. [65] She was the settlement's ingenious floodgate machinist, the only hands the mountain still trusted. [79] Tonight the rhythm was failing. [84] Pressure gathered too long in the dome; hairline cracks ran through the crumbling amphitheater's tiers like veins on a leaf. [104]

The two councils had argued for weeks and reached nothing. [114] The Ventwardens demanded the gates be thrown wide, bleeding heat away before the dome burst. [129] The Hearthkeepers demanded the gates be sealed, hoarding every measure of warmth against the cold season coming. [146] Both were right; both were cruel in their rightness. [155] Orenna wanted neither victory. [159] She wanted harmony, a timing that spared the dome and the orphan terraces both. [173]

The founder's calibration tables, which gave the gate timings, had burned in the archive fire of ninety years ago. [192] Only one instrument survived: the antique surveyor's chain of Maslin Kell, a hundred brass links on a walnut board, hung where the founder had hung it, above the master gate. [222] Kell had measured every channel and vent with that chain; the whole city was built to its length. [240] And the chain trembled. [244] Whenever the building breathed unevenly, the links shivered against their board, a sound like rain on tin. [261] The old machinists claimed Kell had tuned it as a bell is tuned, to fall silent only when the mountain and the city were in accord. [287] Orenna believed it, because she had never once heard it still. [298]

At midnight she climbed to the archive tier, where heat from the vents made the air swim, and searched the founder's correspondence for anything the fire had missed. [326] She found a bundle of letters and one envelope long since emptied, its flap torn open, the seal broken by impatient hands a century dead. [351] It was addressed to the second machinist of Kel Sareen, and it had never been delivered. [367] The letter was gone, but the flap still carried its leftover adhesive, gum laid down in a careful pattern of dots and strokes. [390] Orenna held the flap over a warm vent, and the heat woke the gum. [404] The wet ink of the vanished letter had pressed into the adhesive while the envelope cured, and now, warmed, the ghost of the writing surfaced in pale brown lines. [433] She read by dappled light falling through the cracked roof, sun and shadow moving together across the old paper. [452]

"The councils will fight," Kell had written, "and whichever wins will rewrite my gates to serve its virtue alone." [471] "So I have hidden mercy inside the arithmetic." [479] Nineteen links of the surveyor's chain bore a maker's stamp, nineteen marks in a sequence. [494] Read as numbers, they gave the gate timings for the deep exhale, the great venting that came with the season's turn. [515] The pattern spared the dome, as the Ventwardens wished, yet it skimmed a quiet share of the flood toward the infirmary terraces and the nurseries, where the sick and the small slept close together for warmth. [551] It was compassion, encrypted in brass so that no council could ever vote it away. [566] "Kindness hides where only the patient look," the letter closed, and Orenna laughed at the slyly heartfelt old man, dead two hundred years and still smuggling care past his enemies. [596]

The mountain began to inhale. [601] She ran down through the tiers as the dome swelled and dust sifted from the carvings. [617] At the master gate she lifted the antique chain from its board and walked it link by link, counting stamps, setting the great brass cams the way the numbers told her. [648] Her hands trembled; she let them, since fear keeps a machinist careful. [660] Members of both councils had gathered on the tiers above, lit in patches of sun and shadow, watching with folded arms. [681]

The exhale came at dawn. [686] The dome gave its long sigh, the gates opened on Kell's stolen arithmetic, and the flood ran silver through the channels, easing the pressure, warming the terraces, generous and precise at once. [718] And the chain, hanging from her fist, went still. [727] Not one link trembled. [731] Orenna hung it back on its board and turned to the two councils, dappled light crossing her face, half shadowed and half bright. [754] "The founder left you both a compromise," she said, "and he welded it shut so you could not argue it open." [775] Above her, the amphitheater breathed, and the mountain, for the first time in her twelve years, kept no secrets from her. [796]
