The blood lab called it chimerism; Mara called it a discovery. [11] Two genomes, one body: a twin absorbed before birth, still living in her marrow, in the green of her left eye. [32] Her mother had named that twin once, and then, they said, sailed out and planted the name somewhere it could never hurt anyone again. [56] The S.S. Lethe still drifted where her mother had left it, a carnival ship loosened from its pier, turning slow circles in a foggy bay. [81] Mara rowed out at dusk to find the garden of forgotten names, climbing the rusted gangway with an emerald swatch card folded in her pocket, the one her mother had kept from painting a nursery meant for two. [119] Sixteen greens on the card; only one had gone on the walls; only one matched her eye. [136]

The deck creaked under fog, carousel horses standing shoulder to shoulder, their painted eyes patient. [151] She felt free here and knew she was not; the bay held the ship the way her body held her twin, a freedom with walls around it. [178]

Down amid the funhouse mirrors she found the ticket booth, and in the booth, the Namewright. [194] He looked stitched together himself, seams of lamplight running up his wrists, and when he spoke, his voice came from several directions at once. [218] "You could still trade," he said. [224] A name you could not bear to keep, planted in his garden behind the funhouse, and you walked away lighter. [244] That was the promise, anyway. [249] Mara had watched that promise fail for thirty years in her mother's face; the forgetting had never once caught fire, a solace that stayed unkindled, cold as the bay. [278]

"I'm not here to plant," she said. [285] "I'm here to recapture." [289] The Namewright studied her mismatched eyes and smiled at what he found there, that blissfully aberrant gleam, cells from a vanished girl humming happily in a borrowed sister. [317] "Then you'll need the threads," he said. [324] Names in his garden were not kept in soil but in story; each one grew as a thread on a great loom, alive only while its narrative held. [352] To take a name back, you followed its narrative threads hand over hand, telling the story again from the far end. [373] "But hurry," he added, "because the Lethe does not hold still for anyone." [386]

She understood soon enough what he meant: aboard this ship, memories altered. [398] Walking the corridor to the garden, she lost her mother's face and found it changed, gentler, then crueler, then gone. [418] She forgot the color of her own kitchen and remembered a kitchen she'd never had. [433] The fog was editing her, line by line. [441] She pressed on, holding the swatch card like a compass. [451]

The garden of forgotten names was real: a moonlit plot behind the shattered funhouse, no flowers, only the loom, vast as a carousel, strung with thousands of silver narrative threads, each one trembling with a small dry whisper of story. [491] She raised the emerald card to the threads, and near the loom's heart one strand brightened, drinking the same green. [511] She took hold and began to recapture the story through its threads, hand over hand, the way the Namewright had said: a young woman paints a nursery emerald; a doctor hears two heartbeats, then one; a name is spoken aloud exactly four times, once at a christening that never happens. [561] The thread warmed. [563] The name rose up through her palms with the story's last knot. [575] It was Ivy. [578] Ivy, her mother had called her, and Mara said it out loud for the fifth time in history, and the fog recoiled from the sound. [603]

The loom loosened that thread and let it go. [612] No fire kindled in her chest; no warmth spread; the solace stayed unkindled, exactly as it had stayed unkindled for her mother. [634] But she had the name now, and a name is not a flame; it is a seed. [651] Seeds don't need to burn; they need carrying. [659]

On the row back, the ship turned its slow circles behind her, free and trapped, adrift and anchored in fog, and Mara understood that she was the same. [687] Her memories would keep altering now, small edits the Lethe had made; the kitchen would stay wrong forever. [705] It didn't matter. [708] She carried her twin in her blood, a garden in her chest, an emerald card gone silver at the edges, and one green name, planted at last in the only soil that had ever been hers. [744]
