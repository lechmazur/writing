Wren was the Watcher of the Halden Arbor, a black pine canopy above a permanent sea of cloud, where the world below was rumor and the night was pattern. [29] She was a midnight pattern seeker: she read the moths' spirals, the breathing gaps in the cloud sea, and the small wrongnesses in harness webbing that other eyes let pass. [59] Each dawn, couriers leapt from the platforms and glided down through the clouds, trusting one thing: Wren's hands had found the flaw their own never would. [85] That was the watcher's burden: not the watching, but the being trusted. [97] Seven years ago her brother Tam leapt with a parachute buckle she had passed, its tongue fatigue-cracked, missed by lamplight, and the canopy answered with silence. [123] No scream reached her; only the soundless echo of it, which never stopped arriving. [137] She had not flown since. [142]

For six nights, storm static had sealed the radio. [151] On the seventh night, the static cleared like a held breath released. [163] The voice was thin: a supply glider was down on the mountain's shoulder, below the clouds, and someone was alive, signaling. [184] The lift cables were iced, the stairs buried; the only way down was the old jump, off-pattern, in the dark. [204] The young couriers gathered at her platform with lamps and said nothing, because saying it made it real: the Watcher must jump. [226]

Wren fetched her rig from the chest where it had lain seven years. [239] The pack smelled of pine and dust. [246] She unpicked the flaps, found Tam's handwriting on the inspection card, and beneath it the buckle: same model, same bright tongue. [267] Her eyes wanted to pass it. [273] Her hands did not. [277] Under her thumb, where lamplight forgave, the tongue carried a hairline chill of fatigue, a flaw fine as a moth's vein. [298] So she worked, attentively inventive, the way fear had taught her. [309] She took a second buckle from a training rig and revised the assembly so the load crossed both tongues at opposing angles, each guarding the other's weakness, a lattice first read in moth wings. [343] There was no time to test it; there was only the older test, the thousand drills of her first years, when Tam stood behind her, silent, until her hands found the sequence alone. [376] She let her fingers go ahead of her fear. [385] They remembered through muscle memory what her mind refused: fold, stow, thread, double, cinch, check by touch, check again. [404] Somewhere in the sequence her hands stopped trembling, because Tam's drills lived in them still, an echo made useful. [423]

The couriers watched her dress the harness. [430] She understood why she could not hesitate: to preserve trust under pressure, the Watcher must be the one thing on the mountain that never wavered. [455] If her hands doubted, theirs would freeze mid-air. [463] So she buckled the doubled buckle before them, slowly, like a rite, and let every eye see it hold. [482] Then she read the sky, because blind jumps were death and she was, before anything, a pattern seeker. [500] The clouds breathed in long swells; every ninth swell opened a seam of starlight above the shoulder, held for forty heartbeats. [521] She counted three cycles to be sure, then stepped to the edge with the rhythm in her blood. [539] The couriers lifted their lamps. [544] She leapt into the ninth swell. [550]

The fall was soundless, the whole world an echo of falling. [561] The cloud deck parted like cold smoke, and below lay the wreck's small fire, burning faithfully in the dark. [580] She opened the parachute at the right height, and the doubled buckle took her weight: two tongues holding where one had failed. [602] She landed hard on the scree, gathered the silk, and ran. [613] The fallen courier was seventeen, ankle broken, alive because he had kept his beacon on schedule, schedules being patterns too. [633] "The Watcher came," he kept saying, as if the words were warmth. [645] She splinted him, wrapped him in the silk, and winched him up before dawn. [659]

At sunrise the young couriers stood at the platforms with their rigs on, waiting for her word. [676] She checked every buckle by touch that morning, thumb on every tongue, and revised the old checklist: never trust the eye alone. [698] One by one they leapt, every buckle held, and the arbor kept its trust the way it kept the night: completely, without sound. [721] The burden did not lift, Wren knew; burdens like hers never did. [733] But that night, when the moths rose, she felt Tam's drills moving in her hands and understood the soundless echo was not emptiness. [756] It was instruction. [759] Some watchers are given burdens; the attentive ones are given, too, the patterns that let them carry the weight. [778]
