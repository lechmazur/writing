Wren returned to the observation deck on the anniversary, as her family had always done, though no one was left to notice. [22] The station had been dark for thirty years, ever since the shutdown. [34] Her boots rang against the grating, and the sound came back to her twice, three times, stretched thin, as though the silence were remembering her footsteps and repeating them. [63] That was the Fold's doing. [68] The observation deck hung in the time-dilation well of the dead star, deep enough in its grip that time crawled here. [89] A day on the deck was five months outside. [98] During the shutdown, Wren had volunteered to run the decommission sequence from inside the slow zone, because the sequence took weeks within it and decades beyond it. [125] She had walked out eleven weeks older and thirty years late. [136] The world called her the blessed pariah. [143] Blessed, because she alone had witnessed the Fold bloom and carried its final data; pariah, because she was a woman out of her own time, mourning people who had finished mourning her long ago. [177] Reporters wanted her grief; scientists wanted her readings; nobody wanted her company. [189] For eleven weeks and thirty years, the confusion had sat in her chest like a swallowed stone: why her, why stretched, why alone. [212]

Tonight she meant to answer it the only way her family had ever answered anything: by looking up. [230] She wore her old coat, the one her mother had sewn a gilded compass rose applique onto when Wren was seven, the gold thread catching the emergency lighting even now. [260] "So you always know which way is home," her mother had said. [272] Home was gone; the rose remained. [278] Every anniversary of the station's founding, the whole family had climbed to this deck to sketch the night sky with paper and charcoal, each claiming a quadrant, arguing gently over magnitudes. [309] Grandmother Amma had started the custom the year the deck was built, insisting that a family which draws the sky together cannot drift apart. [333] Wren spread her paper on the cold sill and picked up the charcoal stub she had carried ever since the evacuation. [354]

She had done the arithmetic on the voyage out: light that fled the deck on that last anniversary, the night before the evacuation, should be completing its first slow orbit of the Fold tonight. [388] The star field was not the one she remembered. [397] Stars had shifted, dimmed, died, been born. [404] Only the Fold itself stood exactly where it had always been, patient, bending everything that passed it. [421] Then, in the Fold's curved dark, she saw light that did not belong to now. [436] The arithmetic held true; light bent into a slow orbit returns like a voice down a long hall. [454] In that returning light, she glimpsed them. [461] Her mother, not yet gray, kneeling at the sill with charcoal; her father pointing out a variable star; Amma's white head bent over the quadrant she always claimed. [489] The image ran slow, temporally stretched the way everything near the Fold was stretched, so that her mother's laugh unfolded over whole seconds, a ribbon of sound that arrived as silence. [520] Wren did not weep. [524] She sketched. [526] She drew the slow ghosts quadrant by quadrant, arguing gently, under her breath, over magnitudes. [541] The custom held. [544] The family had not drifted apart; it had only been bent, like the light, and was arriving home now, in order, thirty years late. [568]

The confusion in her chest loosened. [574] Being stretched was not exile; it was a vantage. [583] What confused her could be explored. [589] The Fold had been returning the station's light for thirty years; more echoes were out there, curving home, decades of them, and she alone knew how to read the slow sky. [620] She was the only cartographer alive for a country made of delayed light. [633]

Wren pressed her palm flat over the gilded compass rose on her sleeve, felt the raised gold thread like a ridge of certainty. [656] True north, Amma used to say, is not a direction on a map; it is whatever does not move while everything else does. [679] Stars drifted; the station shut down; her family scattered into years; Wren herself was pulled out of true and warped by gravity. [701] But the custom did not move, and love did not move, and north, she saw now, had been pointing at them the whole time. [725]

In the morning she sent one message to the world that had made her a curiosity: she was staying. [744] Let the pariah keep her blessing. [750] The Fold's echoes would arrive for decades, and she would be here to glimpse each one, sketching the sky every anniversary, adding each returning light to the map, drawing her family home line by line, until the silence rang with all of them.
