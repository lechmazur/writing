The heart arrived in Maren's chest like a stranger moving into a lit house, and she -- a dye master of thirty watchful years -- could not stop listening at its door. [30] In the months after receiving the donor heart, strangers on the street had begun stopping her -- to confess things, to tell her about their fathers, their debts, their drowned dogs. [60] This became her reason for traveling, plainly stated in the leave application she never filed: to learn why some people magnetize stories, and why she had lately become one. [89]

So she closed the dye shop, packed the blue morpho wing that had come folded in the donor's last letter like a signature made of light, and climbed toward the floating rock tea house above the valley. [126] The letter mentioned the tea house once, without explanation. [135]

The rocks there hung above the mist like unlit lanterns, tethered by rope bridges, and the tea house sat on the largest, its windows open to the drifting white below. [165] Mist breathed in and out of the valley all day, and everything -- wood, brass, the backs of hands -- wore a soft gray bloom, as if the world were being gently forgotten and remembered again. [199]

On her first evening the tea keeper, a stooped man named Osen, gave her the house's only rule: stories belong to whoever the light lands on. [225] Through the day, sun trails crawled across the floorboards, sliding from table to table as the mist bent the light, and wherever a bright patch settled, voices gathered -- travelers turning toward the illuminated stranger, telling him about a shipwreck, a marriage, a debt. [268] For three days Maren watched, dye-master careful, chalking a small ring wherever the light lingered longest, until the floor wore a constellation of circles and the day's bright itinerary lay open to her. [301] But the stories did not follow her chalk. [309] They followed a young ferryman who sat wherever he liked and was found by light anyway; strangers leaned toward him the way plants lean toward a window. [336]

At night, in her narrow room upstairs, she held the blue morpho wing to the lamp and did what dye masters do best: she studied what cannot be dyed. [365] The wing held no blue at all, she knew; ground to powder it would turn dust-brown, because its color was not pigment but architecture -- ridges that caught light and answered it at an angle. [399] Bright but never still, visible but never graspable -- this was the caution she had kept all her life in the dye trade, worn like the wing wore blue: an iridescent caution, one facet tilted forward, the others kept dark. [438]

That night the donor heart woke her with its quick, quietly explosive knocking -- a small applause beneath her ribs -- and she lay still while it hurried toward the sound of the ferryman's laughter below and slowed when he did. [477] It had been hurrying toward voices for months; she had thought the change was in the world, and now she began to suspect it was in the angle of her own chest. [509]

On the fourth morning she stopped watching the light and watched the ferryman instead, and understood: he turned toward every speaker like a dish toward a signal, catching each small lamp of a face and handing it back brighter. [548] He was not a lantern; he was a wing -- ridged, angled, answering at an angle -- and stories came to him the way blue comes to the morpho: not made, only returned. [579]

By tracking sun trails along her chalked map, she knew that at twelve minutes past noon the brightest patch would reach the ferryman's table, and when it came she set the morpho wing in it, tilting the borrowed beam so that shifting blue light began to illuminate every face in the room. [631] For one held breath nobody spoke; then the whole tea house began talking at once, and half of them were turned toward her, spilling shipwrecks, marriages, debts. [658] In her pocket the donor's letter made sudden sense, ending with a line she had first taken for poetry: "I never keep them; I only catch them at the right angle and pass them on brighter." [694]

Her heart answered with its small, quietly explosive applause, and Maren -- watchful still, but finally angled -- listened. [711] Outside, the mist breathed over the valley, and the floating rocks held their places, patient as wings, waiting for whatever light would come. [734]
