The sea had been exhaling for nineteen days. [8] It drew back from the rocks of the island a little more each dawn, and the lighthouse stood above the widening wound of sand like a struck match that refused to burn. [40] No chart named the island; nobody remembered who raised the tower. [51] Vess built clocks that ran counter-clockwise. [57] She had wound them that way since the night the sea took her brother, believing that if a mechanism could be made to turn against its nature, then maybe time itself could be argued with. [92] The mainlanders called her work unnatural and bought it anyway, out of the same hunger. [107]

On the nineteenth day, the water uncovered the hunger stone. [117] It rose from the slick black sand like a molar of the drowned, carved over with warnings in dead alphabets, and the oldest legible line read: if you can see me, weep. [149] Everyone on the island wept, because everyone knew fate's design: when the stone bared itself, the fish left, the nets came up bleeding rust, and the exhale of the sea never turned to breath again. [184] The night the stone surfaced, the lighthouse lamp went dark. [194] Wick, oil, glass, all intact, all useless; the radiance in it had not died but gone latent, like a word held behind the teeth. [218]

In the keeper's chest at the top of the tower, Vess found the ancient yoyo. [233] Two discs of grey stone, a bone axle, a string gone to silk. [246] The previous keeper had left a note: it never comes back up. [258] For three nights Vess sat with it on the gallery floor, spinning it down, watching it sleep at the bottom of its arc and refuse the climb, and she felt something obscene in that refusal, something that matched her own. [298] So she did what she was made for, being neatly inventive, and took it apart. [313] The puzzle was not in the discs but in the string's end: no knot, only a loose loop around the axle. [334] A wheel on such a loop is never chained; it returns only when the loop catches, and it can be made not to. [357]

And when she held a disc against the dark, its heart carried a faint warmth, a dim ember-glow buried in the stone: latent radiance, banked like a coal. [385] The toy was a hunger stone. [391] Small, ancient, and full: she understood at last, with a chill that felt almost like joy. [407] Somebody centuries ago had fed this little stone until its hunger closed over into warmth, then carved it into a plaything, a wheel that goes down and comes back, teaching children the shape of the bargain. [443] The great stone on the sand was the same creature, starving. [454] It was not an omen; it was an appetite. [463] Fate's design was a loop, and a loop could be fed until it caught. [477]

On the twentieth night, with the tide at its farthest and slowest, the whole sea holding its long, slow exhale, Vess climbed to the lamp room with the ancient yoyo in her fist. [510] She mounted the fed disc at the heart of the great lens and wound the tower's clockwork drive to spin it counter-clockwise, against the sun's own way. [537] Then she threw the string exactly as a child throws a yoyo: she released the drive and let the wheel spin. [558] Spin woke the banked radiance; the ember opened into a thread of light, and the lens gathered it, thickened it, and laid it down the flats to the hunger stone like a rope thrown to a drowning man. [596] Every keeper before her had tried to starve the hunger or smash it; she was here to initiate the opposite. [616] The stone drank. [619] She felt the beam thin as the appetite pulled, felt the little disc cooling in the lens, felt the year run backward through her teeth, and still she poured, laughing at the arrogance of the gift, her voice ugly and bright in all that dark. [664] Then the stone's heart caught. [669] Latent radiance kindled at its core and rose through the carved warnings until the whole boulder shone from within, every famine-word lit like a scar remembering warmth, and the appetite closed over into fullness. [703]

The sea inhaled. [706] It came back up the flats slowly, hugely, a wheel returning along its string, and the first wave broke around the glowing stone with a sound like a held breath released. [737] Vess stumbled down into water already around her boots, and she stood in it shaking, grinning, hollowed out; she had stepped beyond fate's design once, and she knew the loop had not been cut, only caught, this once, her way. [777] Somewhere up the stair, one counter-clockwise clock began, softly, to tick. [788]
