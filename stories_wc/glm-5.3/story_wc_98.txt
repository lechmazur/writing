On the island of Ka, Wren held the only office nobody envied: Reality Checker. [14] When fishermen reported a kraken, she rowed out with her brass pendulum, her salt tin, and her ledger, and returned with a verdict. [37] Mostly the verdict was no. [42] Her ledger read like a bestiary of disproven wonders: false kraken, drifting kelp; false ghost, goat in a curtain; false prophecy, indigestion. [64] But lately she had been recording a different kind of report, one no stamp could settle: the songs were getting shorter. [85] Every year she measured the harvest song with a knotted cord, and every year it lost a verse, like a tide going out and never returning. [111] Grandmothers contradicted each other on the founding of the harbor; children drew maps with the old names spelled wrong; and the memory tree, which had once held the island's collective memory on the ancient ridge, had thinned to a rumor of a rumor. [154]

In her archive, between false relics, sat the last knowledge fruit seed, filed under Unverified: a wrinkled amber pip that had never sprouted under any test she could devise. [183] According to the one surviving fragment of verse, the tree had borne knowledge fruit: bite one, and a lost memory returned whole, with its smells and its sorrows. [211] The fragment ended with a warning: the seed sleeps, and will waken only through interpreted dreams, never through rain. [230] That mattered; the monsoon was coming. [236] Fishermen read the sky the way she read ledgers: ten days, they said, before the first storm buried the ridge in water. [258] A sleeping seed would rot in the flooded soil; a wakened one could grip the ridge and drink the monsoon itself. [279] So Wren, whose trade was doubt, set out to do the one thing doubt had never asked of her: interpret a dream. [301]

She climbed the ancient ridge in bloom, wild iris and white fire-lilies combing the old terraces, and planted the pip at the crown where the tree used to stand. [330] That night she pressed her ear to the soil and heard it dreaming. [343] The seed's dream came up through the ground like warmth through a floor: a thousand islanders standing in a circle, singing the harvest song, each one forgetting a different word, until the silence was enormous. [378] For six nights she listened, wrote the dream down, tested it against every instrument, and found nothing she could verify. [398] The pendulum hung still over the seed: no verdict. [407] On the seventh night, the islanders began dreaming it too. [417] They came up the blooming ridge in ones and twos, sheepish, holding their hats, describing the same circle of singers and the same growing silence. [442] Wren interviewed them all, because that was her nature, and every testimony matched, which frightened her more than any kraken: a fact built entirely of dreams. [468]

Then she understood what the seed asked. [475] It was not dreaming of forgetting; it was dreaming of waking into a people who would forget it. [493] It needed proof that memory on Ka was alive, and it needed it before the monsoon. [509] Wren could not offer proof. [514] She had spent thirty years proving that most of what people remembered was wrong. [528] But standing on the ridge at dusk, with fire-lilies opening like small lamps, she felt her doubt turn strangely luminous: what if memory was not a record to be verified but a thing a village did together, gaps and all? [568]

So she gave the seed her interpretation, out loud, as an offering. [580] "Here is what your dream means," she said. [588] "We will forget you, and remember you, and forget you again, and argue about you, and sing you wrong on purpose, and that is how you will stay ours." [617] Then she asked the crowd to sing the harvest song with every hole left open, loudly, proudly. [634] They did. [636] The gaps made a rhythm of their own, claps and hums where the verses used to be, and the sound went down the blooming ridge like water finding its old channel. [667] The soil shivered. [670]

The amber pip split along a seam no instrument had ever found, and a pale green shoot rose into the last dry light before the monsoon. [696] Thunder walked in over the sea that same hour, and the first rain came down warm and enormous, and the shoot drank it and held. [721] Wren took out her ledger, and for a long moment her pen hovered, because she could not yet verify what the tree would hold, or whether a fruit could carry a people's past, or whether any of it was more than a lucky pip. [765] At last she wrote: Verified by continuation. [772] Behind her, the islanders were already arguing about how the song used to go, voices glowing under the rain, keeping the tree awake. [795]
