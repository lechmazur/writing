On the third evening after the memory surfaced, Noor carried her tent up the old viaduct, past wisteria hanging in purple storms from the arches. [25] Twilight came down the river valley with the fog, and the blooms nodded in it like sleepers. [42] She pitched the black canvas at the viaduct's midpoint and set a brass lens into its wall, a hole no bigger than a lentil. [66] Inside, the drowned world appeared on her drawing board, upside down and impossibly clear: fog, arches, flowers, every lamp of every walker crossing high above the river. [93] This was her trade, camera obscura artist, tracer of inverted light, a woman who made fog confess its shapes. [112]

Three nights ago, mid-tracing, her breathing had changed without her permission, and for the length of that altered breath she stopped being Noor. [135] She had been a nurse on this same bridge when it carried plague trains, counting the breaths of the dying, and one of them, a ferryman with a musician's hands, had asked her not to look away. [172] She hadn't looked away. [176] But she hadn't said the word that rose in her like water either, and he died at dawn not knowing, and the love went on pulsing with nowhere to arrive. [206]

Since then, one question owned her: where is unused love stored? [217] Her grandmother had kept a tear-shaped pearl in a matchbox and called it Kwan Yin's tear, wept by the goddess at their village temple for all the love that could not be delivered in time. [252] Noor had inherited the pearl and the question both. [261] She was, everyone agreed, spiritually curious to a fault; she had sat with monks, mediums, and beekeepers, collecting breathing practices the way other artists collected brushes. [287]

Now she sharpened a pencil and watched the walkers cross the projected sky, each lamp a small fire on pilgrimage. [307] That was how she thought of people this foggy season: pilgrims of sparks, carrying their little fires across the dark. [327]

A knock came at the tent pole. [334] The man who ducked inside was older, gray-coated, holding his cap like an apology. [348] "I heard in town there's a room where you can see the fog clearly," he said. [364] "Sit," she said, "but quietly; in here, breath is visible." [374] It was true in her way of counting: the projected image trembled with every exhale, so the whole inverted viaduct shivered like a reflection when he sighed. [401]

He sat, and she watched the world instead of him. [411] His breathing told her everything before he did. [419] It was shallow and guarded, the breath of a man carrying something heavy up stairs. [434] Then two moths tangled in a passing walker's lantern, two sparks meeting and parting in the projected sky, and his breath caught hard; the arches rippled. [460] "My wife," he said to the dark, because it was that kind of dark, a confessional dark. [477] "Forty years, and the last five I couldn't say it, not once, not out loud." [492] Then she died, and the word was left in him without an address. [505]

Noor did what the monks had taught her, what she had once done for the ferryman without knowing why. [524] She breathed so he could hear it. [531] In for four counts, out for six, the long release like a hand on the back of the neck. [550] His breath reached for hers the way a tired swimmer reaches for a rope. [564] Slowly the image steadied; the arches stood; the fog went on being fog, lucid and impenetrable at once. [582] When his breathing finally broke open into weeping, a tear fell onto her drawing board and struck the traced arches, puckering the paper like a wound that wants to heal. [612]

There was her answer, arriving the way everything arrived in the tent: upside down and undeniable. [628] Unused love is stored in the body, in the held breath and the unwept tear, kept soundless in the dark of us like an image in a lightless box, waiting for an aperture. [661] And it heals the way light heals, by being let through a hole. [674]

She took the matchbox from her coat, opened it, and pressed the tear-shaped pearl into his wet palm. [692] "Kwan Yin's tear," she said. [697] "My grandmother said it's where the undelivered prayers go." [706] "Then it has been waiting for me," he said, and his breath had changed into something with a floor under it. [727]

When he left, his lamp joined the others in the fog, one more spark on the pilgrimage, crossing the blooming viaduct toward the far shore. [752] Noor breathed with the empty tent, in for four, out for six, and somewhere a ferryman's grip loosened on a wrist two lifetimes gone. [776]
