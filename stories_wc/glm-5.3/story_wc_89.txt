Since the phoenix rose above the fairy-tale orchard, the moon had refused to set, and the apple trees bloomed and bloomed beneath its silver eye. [25] Mara, the beast whisperer, walked the rows at the hour that used to be dawn, speaking low to foxes and owls who had forgotten how to be afraid of her voice. [56] Under the oldest pear tree, where the moonlight pooled like spilled milk, her hound Ash was buried. [73] She had buried him three winters before the firebird came, and she still knelt there each night the way other people prayed. [95]

In her pocket she carried a paper fortune from a cookie, the one Ash had cracked out of a peddler's pack on the last fine day of his life. [124] He had eaten the shell and left her the slip, as if he knew. [138] "You will meet again," it read, "but not as you were." [149] She had hated that sentence for years. [156]

Now the risen phoenix had burned away the wall between the living orchard and whatever lay beneath it, and the old beasts said a soul could be conjured back across, for a price. [189] The price was the self. [194] Whoever called the dead must be remade, because the dead do not answer to a shape they left behind. [213] Mara had refused for a long time. [220] She was a whisperer, not a wolf; her whole craft lived in her human throat, in the softness of her flat hands. [242] To change was to lose the only self Ash had loved. [253] But the moon hung patient over the pear tree night after night after night, and grief is also a kind of unending light. [276]

On the anniversary of his death, she laid out what the spice caravan had carried her: star anise like wooden stars, black cardamom smoked over cedar, a thimble of saffron worth a month of eggs. [311] Scent was the oldest door, the foxes had told her; a smell could open a grave the way a word could open a cage. [335] Ash had loved these spices. [340] Once, he had pushed his whole face into her satchel of cardamom and sneezed for an hour, delighted. [358] Her hands shook as she measured them onto the coals. [368] They were paradoxically steady too, the way a plumb line trembles and still finds the floor; fear ran through her and she poured anyway. [392]

The smoke rose straight as a bell rope in the windless moonlight, and the orchard inhaled. [408] Anise, smoke, saffron, sweetness and char together: the smell of every good morning they had ever shared. [425] She called him the way she had called frightened things all her life, low and certain, not commanding but inviting. [445] "Ash," she whispered. [448]

The change came before the answer. [454] It began at the edges of her, in the fine hairs of her arms that thickened to silver fur, in her nails that darkened into honest claws. [481] She wanted to pull back. [486] Her human throat, her flat hands, her name--all of it going, going. [498] But the fortune had promised it: not as you were. [508] She let the moonlight finish its work, and the pain was oddly like joy, like being unknotted. [525] When it was done, a beast stood on four legs in the orchard, wearing a coat of moon-colored fur, and the whisperer's voice inside it had become something older than words. [556]

The ground by the pear tree softened, and breathed, and opened. [567] Ash came up through the roots the way water comes up through sand, without hurry, because souls that are called by love are never lost, only deep. [594] He was thinner than memory and smelled of loam and anise. [605] He circled her once, reading the new shape of her with his nose, and she understood that he had never once loved her hands or her throat or her name. [635] He had loved the warmth that lived behind all of those, and warmth keeps. [649]

They pressed their foreheads together under the unbudging moon, two small warm things in an enormous cold and beautiful sky, and the bond that death had broken closed again, like a bone setting straight. [683] Far off, above the phoenix's old ashes, something bright stirred in its sleep. [696] The orchard bloomed on, patient as the moon, and the two beasts ran together down the silver rows, home. [715]
