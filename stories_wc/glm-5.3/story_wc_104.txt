The wall came down at dawn, and by noon the whole city tasted of chalk. [15] Vesna went the other way, toward the fairgrounds, her broken shackle chain wound twice around her waist like a belt she refused to remove. [39] Twelve years in the quarry had taught her that freedom was not a door but a habit, and she had practiced it the way the patient practice anything: slowly, in order, without applause. [72]

They had called her the chained liberator even before her arrest, because she freed seed. [87] In a dry county where the Bureau licensed every kernel and the licensed corn withered on schedule, Vesna moved heirloom grain through the midway like contraband joy. [114] Her vault was the prize redemption center, a building the city had forgotten to map, shuttered since the last carnival left, its counter still hung with dusty ribbons. [142] Behind the plywood and the plastic trophies sat nine hundred jars labeled in faded ticket-red: winter wheat, sand cherries, a plum that laughed at frost, sorghum that drank like a camel. [173] Resilience, shelved where the teddy bears used to be, waiting for dry soil. [186]

Today, while the city sang, the Bureau's men were coming with diesel and torches to raze the fairgrounds before the fallen wall brought cameras and questions. [212] She had known they would; fires are tidy, and falling walls make everyone want to tidy. [228] Her task was not to defeat them. [235] It was to impede them for one hour, and she had spent twelve years learning that an hour, correctly spent, is a fortress. [258]

The old player piano stood where the funhouse met the ticket booth, and in its bench, exactly as Marta had left them, lay the keys. [283] Brass, iron, one of silver, each tagged with a tiny painted star. [295] Anyone else would have seen junk. [301] Vesna, patiently strategic, read them as music: their arrangement was the closing-time calliope song, note for note, which meant the order of the midway's storm gates, and the final key in the melody was no note at all: it was the hidden room's door. [345] Marta had died without telling; she had told only in the way liberators tell, in codes and arrangements, trusting time to deliver the message. [369]

Vesna climbed the rusted Ferris wheel with a bow she had not strung since her arrest. [385] The arrow she loosed carried strange fletching, dyed ostrich feathers cut from a prize boa, bright as a lie and unmistakable to anyone who had ever redeemed a ticket at that counter. [417] It arced over the wall's smoke and the streaming, singing crowds, and it said what it always said: come now, bring everything that holds. [441]

Then she went down the ladder and began to close the fairgrounds in the order of the song. [459] Gate, gate, chain, spike, chain. [464] She threaded her old prison chain through the last two bars herself, locked it, and dropped the key through a knothole into the dark beneath the midway. [491] By the time the diesel trucks found the narrow lane, they met iron singing shut all down the midway, gates crossbarred in a sequence no blueprint explained, and men with torches stood cursing a dead woman's melody. [528]

The people the arrow summoned came over the wall's rubble with wagons, aprons, coat linings, guitar cases. [545] She turned the silver key, the hidden room exhaled dust, and she dealt out jars like prizes. [562] "Take two," she said. [566] "Plant one, save one, and teach your neighbor which is which." [577] A girl with dust to her eyebrows asked what the seeds were for, and Vesna looked at the dry, broken ground where the wall's shadow had lain all her life, then at the crowd carrying tomorrow through the gap in the world, and could not find a small enough answer. [627]

When the shelves stood empty but for one jar, she unscrewed it, took out a single wheat seed, and pressed it into the crack beside the redemption counter, covering it with a palmful of chalky rubble. [663] Impeded, the Bureau's trucks finally breached the midway at dusk and found nothing but ribbons, a silent bench, and nine hundred empty jars glinting like spent fireworks. [690] By then the seed was already an inch underground, doing the patient, strategic, staggeringly graceful work of coming up through dry soil toward a sky it had never seen. [719]
