They called Marek the morning fog's saxophonist, though no one had hired him and no one could fire him. [19] Every dawn he climbed to the radiant grotto above the harbor, where mist rose off the water and pooled in the bowl of stone like spilled milk. [46] At the grotto's heart stood the prism atrium, a dome of faceted glass that Ilse had built with her own cracked hands, and the mist wrapped it as if afraid to touch it. [79] That was the trouble with Ilse's work. [86] Nothing wanted to touch it, and everything wanted to read it. [97]

As dewdrops formed on the facets, each drop bent the first light into a glyph, and the glyphs chained themselves into lines, and the lines were a prophecy she had spoken in code before she walked into the sea. [136] Speak it aloud, the old order warned, and it would stop being a prophecy and start being an itinerary. [155] So they had vested the hush in Marek, the way you vest a coat on a cold man, and he had worn it for forty years. [181]

The hush was not silence. [186] It lived in his saxophone, in the rests between notes, and it traveled the way a whisper travels, through negative space. [207] When he played, the notes themselves did nothing but ache. [217] It was the gaps that worked, sliding between the dewdrops and smearing the glyphs before any eye could hold them. [237] He played her lullaby backward, because that was the tune that hushed her best. [251] Nostalgia was the wrong word for what he felt, he told himself, and then he felt it anyway: the eerie certainty that she was standing just behind him, humming the forward version. [283]

This morning the fog came in wrong. [290] It was too still, too clean, and the dew formed too fast, fat and perfect, and the glyphs came up quietly vivid, each line legible as handwriting on a fogged window. [321] For the first time in forty years, Marek could almost read it. [333] He wet the reed and began, but his lungs were an old bellows, and the rests came out shallow, and through the thin gaps he saw the code steadying, holding, waiting. [364] The third line of the prophecy glowed like a signature. [374] He knew the shape of it from her letters: it meant, as near as he could tell, I did not go willingly. [396] His hands shook and the lullaby broke. [403] In the broken bar he heard the grotto listening. [412]

Here was the thing the old order never understood: he did not hush her prophecy to save the harbor. [431] He hushed it because it was her voice, and if it ever finished speaking, she would be gone in a new way, a last way, and there would be nothing left to have almost heard. [466] Selfish, yes. [468] But forty years is a long time to love a code. [479]

The dew kept forming. [483] The fourth line began to shine, and he felt its meaning arrive in him the way a name arrives on the tongue. [505] It said: the last to hush it will be the first to speak it. [519] Marek lowered the saxophone. [523] He could play, smear, survive, come back tomorrow to a grotto that would remember nothing. [538] Or he could answer her at last. [545]

He leaned close to the atrium, close enough to fog a facet with his breath, and he put his lips to the gap between two notes, the true negative space, the room she had left for a reply. [583] He whispered her name. [587] The whisper went through the prism like light through water, and the glyphs shivered, and the dew slid, and the lines ran and blurred and gave themselves up, all but one drop at the dome's crown that would not fall. [627] It held the final word of the prophecy, quietly vivid, patient. [638] Marek shouldered the saxophone and started down through the mist, and behind him the grotto glowed like a held breath, keeping her answer until tomorrow. [663]
