In the city of forgotten fountains, the basins stood dry and nobody asked why. [14] The Wardens of Stillness had sealed the water long ago, and the forgetting had followed like a tide. [32] People simply stopped remembering what they had lost, because what they had lost was the remembering itself. [49]

Vess knew better, though she doubted even that. [57] She glowed. [59] A faint gold seeped through her coat at the sternum, the color of lamplight through a hand, and she distrusted it completely. [81] Phosphorescence, she told herself, or a fever of the skin, never mind that it brightened each autumn when the geese paused overhead. [103] Every year the migrating flocks circled the city for three nights, refusing to land, held there by a scatter of breadcrumbs no one admitted to laying. [129]

This year Vess decided to follow the crumbs, because a skeptic tests things, and because the trail was the only question the city had left. [154] The crumbs led her at dawn, rim to dry rim, fountain to forgotten fountain, until they stopped at the Meridian, the grandest ruin of all. [179] There, in the old pump-house beneath the basin, she found a woman baking hard bread from rationed flour. [197] "You came," the woman said, as if Vess had passed an exam. [209] "Eleven autumns I have laid the trail, and only the curious follow it." [222]

Her name was Ilse, last of the fountain-keepers, and she spoke in the city's true register, a defiant hush, the voice of people who resist by whispering. [249] She showed Vess the thing atop the dry basin, where the statue of the river-giant used to stand: a box of stained glass, tall as a child, every pane smoldering with slow color. [282] When Vess stepped close, the panes brightened, and her stubborn glow brightened with them, answering like a struck bell. [301] "Proof," Ilse said. [304] "That is your kept memory in your chest, one that slipped the sealing." [317] "When the Wardens stopped the water, every memory made at the fountains stuck fast in that glass: the wishes, the weddings, the names of the drowned, the whole common past of this city." [350]

A people with no shared memories cannot mourn together, and what cannot mourn together cannot gather at all. [368] That, Vess thought, was exactly the kind of story a skeptic should strangle. [381] But she was realistically imaginative, which meant she doubted a thing and then imagined how it might still be true, and then she checked. [405] She touched a pane and saw her brother, six years old, launching paper boats in the Meridian spray. [423] Her glow had been him all along, his laugh kept lit under her ribs, and she had spent a decade calling it a rash. [447] Checking done, she believed, and asked the harder question: how to free the rest. [461]

Ilse unfolded a star chart soft with handling. [469] "Sidereal patience," she said. [473] "The box is a lantern, and the panes are stuck with the Wardens' wax." [487] "Only when the winter star crosses the brass meridian line, while the geese still pause, will its light pass clean through every pane at once." [512] "I have waited eleven years for the sky to agree with the geese." [525] Patience measured in star-turns, not in moods. [532] Vess, who trusted nothing, found she could trust a clockwork of stars, because stars did not care what anyone believed. [552]

On the third night of the pause, the whole city stood in doorways watching the geese turn, and the Wardens watched the sky too, which was the point of the birds. [583] No one watched the fountain. [588] Vess and Ilse climbed the basin with the last bucket of true fountain water, saved eleven years under a floor, and warmed it between their hands. [614] Vess pressed the warm cloth to the seals, and one by one she began to unstick them, each wax mark softening, each pane tilting loose, the whole city's past going faintly liquid in her fingers. [649] It took as long as it took. [656] That was sidereal patience learned in the body: not hoping, just holding. [668]

The winter star crossed the line. [674] Light went through the glass the way a note goes through a choir, and the panes poured their colors out over the basin. [697] Geese broke their circle and dropped through the beams, wingbeats churning the light into mist, and the memories came unstuck, drifting off the glass in bright motes, sliding through open windows, settling on sleepers like warm snow. [734]

By morning the fountains were still dry, but the city was not. [746] People came carrying cups, saying names they had not said in eleven years, and the Wardens could not arrest a memory. [767] Vess stood among them, her glow finally dim, her brother's laugh gone home with the rest. [783] She was a skeptic still. [788] But now she doubted the silence, not the light. [797]
