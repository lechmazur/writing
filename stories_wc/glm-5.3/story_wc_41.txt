The junkyard prophet lived in the ticket booth of the fog sculpture garden, among four hundred unwatched videotapes. [18] He had salvaged them from the junkyard when the visitors stopped coming, and now the garden kept only one weather: fog. [39] His curse worked forward instead of backward. [46] He forgot the past almost as it happened but remembered the future; he spent his days inside things that had not yet come, and his nights missing things he could no longer name. [79] He knew what was coming the way other men know the tide. [91]

The tapes were his memory. [96] Every winter he recorded his own voice reading the names of the sculptures onto a blank tape, one a year, so that someday, played in order, they would hold nothing less than the sound of a man growing older. [135] It was the only project that made sense to a man who could not keep a yesterday. [152] Each tape was an echo he could cling to, and he clung. [164]

This year the future had finally grown crowded. [172] Tomorrow, when the fog thinned toward noon, a young woman would come up the drowned coast road carrying a tape deck on her hip. [196] He did not remember whether he had ever met her. [206] She would sit on the counter and play every tape from first to last, and the hush of all those unwatched years would end. [230] He had been waiting for her his whole remembered life. [240]

There was a problem, and it smelled of salt. [249] His last blank tape was not in the booth; the losing of it was his to forget. [266] He remembered finding it, because the finding was still future, but future memories arrive like tide-wrack: shapeless, salted, far from where they fell. [289] He saw himself kneeling in the dunes past the whale-shaped sculpture, lifting something pale out of the sand, and he saw the water coming back for it. [316] The sea gives a thing and then wants it again. [326] So he had until the tide turned. [333]

A man who forgets backwards must be an engineer of signs, and the prophet had spent the morning at it. [353] Through the fog he arranged driftwood as directional markers, a line of bleached arms leading in from the drowned road to the booth, then on past the whale to the wrack line. [385] With a piece of plain chalk, salvaged years ago from a drowned schoolhouse, he wrote on each marker. [403] The fog was slower than rain, and chalk was honest: it promised to vanish. [417] THIS WAY, OLD MAN, said the first marker. [425] YOU ARE GOING FOR THE TAPE, said the second. [434] DO NOT STOP AT THE WHALE, said the third, though he knew he would. [448] Beside the booth he planted a fourth arm and wrote: THE TAPES ARE INSIDE, AND THEY ARE ALL ONE MAN. [468]

He went out into the gray. [474] The sculptures rose like drowned gods, iron shoulders beaded with fog, and he touched each one, because touching was a kind of remembering even he was allowed. [501] An hour out he forgot why he was walking; the driftwood told him, and he blessed the man who had set it, not knowing it was himself. [528] At the whale he stopped anyway, as foreseen. [536] He laid a hand on its rusted flank and longed, in a way that had no object, the way the tide longs. [558]

The last marker pointed down to the wrack line, where kelp lay in scripts nobody could read. [575] The water was already sliding back over its own footprints. [585] He dug where the sand looked disturbed, where the future said he had dug, and his fingers found the edge of a videotape, pale as a fingernail. [612] He located it, and the locating felt like being forgiven. [622] A wave reached for his boots and missed. [630]

That night, with the fog pressing at the window like a listener, he recorded the last entry. [647] He read the sculpture names in a voice gone thin and tidal, and when he played it back he heard an old man, the only yesterday he had ever kept. [677] The sound of growing older, caught at last. [685]

Near noon the fog thinned, just as promised. [693] A young woman came up the drowned road with a tape deck on her hip, and followed the driftwood arrows he had chalked. [716] He did not know her, but then he did not know himself either, except on tape. [732] She sat on the counter and put in the first tape, and somewhere in the fog a voice that was young began reading names. [756] The prophet closed his eyes and clung to the echo, and let it carry him out and back, out and back, like the tide. [780]
