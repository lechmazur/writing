On the evening Ilse Braddock died, the archivist carried her walnut ink down to the jetty. [16] The old planks were blooming again, sea thrift crowding between the boards, every flower descended from seeds that had ridden in the pockets of drowned sailors. [42] Maren had steeped the ink herself, walnut hulls simmered in rainwater until it turned the brown of old harbors, because a covenant deserved nothing cheaper. [67]

Ilse had been the last person alive who remembered the night the fleet went down, which meant, by the terms Maren kept, that the context had changed. [94] When context changes, the covenant must be regenerated. [102] That rule was older than the archive, written in a hand so faded that only the walnut-ink archivists, trained to read what light has half erased, could follow it. [131] Maren was gently thorough about such work, as her mother had been, and her mother's mother, checking tide tables twice, sealing the ink bottle three times, walking the jetty end to end before setting down a single word. [169] Twilight came on slow and gold, and the flowers seemed to hold their breath. [183]

The locket arrived in Ilse's effects, carried down the planks by the vicar's boy, who handed it over like something warm. [204] Inside was a photograph small as a stamp: two fishermen on this same jetty, laughing, one shoving the other's shoulder, sleeves rolled against a summer that no longer existed. [233] The younger man was Ilse's brother, lost with the fleet; the older was her husband, lost the same hour. [252] Ilse had kept the locket shut for sixty years so that the moment inside it would stay exactly the size it had been. [275] That was the whole of her calling: to preserve the weight of small moments, because small moments were the only ballast the town had ever had. [301]

The first covenant had been made the morning after the drowning, when the survivors stood on this jetty and promised the sea nothing grand. [325] No bargains, no blood: only that they would keep naming the dead, keep tending the flowers, keep recording each small kindness done at the harbor's edge. [351] The covenant regenerated through proximate causes, the old ledger said, never through miracles. [364] A named fisherman causes a tended grave; a tended grave causes a child who asks; a child who asks causes an archivist; and an archivist causes the writing down that causes the town to hold. [399] Each cause stood close enough to touch the next, and that nearness was the whole mechanism. [415]

Maren knelt at the jetty's end, laid out the ledger, and unstoppered the ink. [429] The twilight steeped everything, gold into the grain of the wood, gold into the petals, until the whole jetty looked lit from underneath. [452] It was the hour she loved and dreaded, when the boundary between the living town and the drowned one wore thin as paper. [475]

She wrote the names of the dead first, all forty-one, in her smallest and steadiest hand. [491] Then she wrote Ilse's name at the bottom of the witnesses' page, and beneath it: regenerated this night, the context having changed. [513] Her hand was shaking, and she let it. [521] The shaking was part of the record. [528]

Last came the locket. [532] Maren opened it flat on the ledger, so that the two laughing men faced the page, and wrote them in: the shove, the rolled sleeves, the joke already lost but not its laughter. [565] This was why the covenant renewed here and not in a church: the photograph needed flowers at the edge of its frame and gold in the air, needed the world to lean close and confirm it. [601]

As she wrote, the sea went flat and bright, and Maren felt them, forty-one, then a forty-second, Ilse arriving among her people like a guest at a table already set. [631] She was not afraid. [635] Grief and gladness came up in her at once, braided, and she understood the first keeper's meaning: you carry the small moments precisely because they press down, and pressing down is how a thing holds. [670]

When she finished, the first star was out and the flowers were folding shut for the night, each one a small hand closing around what it knew. [697] Maren closed the locket gently, the way you close a door on someone sleeping, and hung it around her own neck. [718] Tomorrow the boy would ask what she had written, and she would tell him, and his asking would be the next cause. [740]
