Mara had kept the vigil fire below the tower all night, as the rite demanded, and when its last spark died at dawn she climbed the lighthouse stairs cold, unslept, certain. [31] For twenty years she had carried one clean sentence: my lamp went out, the Wren went down, and my brother drowned. [52] Last night she had finally paid three coppers for the only trade that might lift it from her: a conscience for hire. [74] Vess lived in the decommissioned lighthouse and sold doubt the way the old keeper had once sold light. [92]

"House rules first," she said. [97] "Everything I feel up here is rented. [104] My guilt, my forgiveness, my judgment: none of it is real, and I say so plainly, because if I meant any of it I would be one more certainty on your pile, and look how that pile is treating you." [144] She recited the disclaimer gently, the way you would warn someone their blanket is rough, and Mara felt safer for it. [165] That was the gift of her: the insincerity was announced, guaranteed, sincere, so you could lean your whole weight on it. [186]

"The full service, then," Vess said. [192] "Cloak, shard, chimes. [195] You are not paying me to absolve you. [203] You are paying me to make room in you: to welcome nuance into certainty before the certainty finishes the job." [223]

Vess unhooked the cloak of doubt from its nail by the door. [235] It was grey, moth-eaten, salt-stiff, stitched by the old keeper from the sail of a wreck. [251] Every thread, the lore said, was a certainty that had failed to hold. [264] She draped it over Mara's shoulders. [270] "Now say your sentence," she said, "and the cloak will weigh it." [282]

"My lamp went out and they died." [289] The cold flooded the shoulders, cold as the sea that took them. [301] "It is built from parts," Vess said. [308] "Wear the parts." [311] "My lamp went out." [315] The cold eased. [318] "They died." [320] Cooler, but bearable. [323] "They died in a storm no lamp on this coast could have cut." [336] The cloak went almost warm, the warmth of grief, which is at least true weather. [351]

Then Vess lifted the hollow mirror shard from a box lined with old sailcloth. [365] It had been cut from the lighthouse's first mirror, glass blown thin as a bubble and silvered on the inside of its hollow. [388] "Full mirrors flatter," she said. [393] "Hollow ones make room." [397] Mara looked, and her reflection was not flat. [405] The hollow held depth, a second face behind the first. [415] Behind the woman who had let the lamp die stood the girl who kept the watch alone, because the village would not fund a second keeper. [441] The shard made room for both and took no sides. [451]

Last came the gallery, where nine chimes hung from the rail, each cut by the old keeper from keel-iron, bell-brass, and broken tools, each tuned to a word of the cove's fog-code, so that when fog hid the light the wind could still speak to the boats. [498] Dawn came up breezy, as it always did there, and the wind moved through the tubes and made its selection. [518] Not all of them; a selection. [524] Vess shut her eyes and marked which tubes spoke and in what order, deciphering wind chime selections by the keeper's log the way other people read letters. [551] "Storm," she said at last, "and then home." [559] "It rang the storm-word, then the come-home." [566] "The same wind that took the Wren just signed its own confession, and left you an invitation." [583]

Mara understood, and wept, but the weeping was grief now, not a verdict, and grief is survivable weather. [601] On the stairs going down she rewrote the sentence: my lamp went out, the storm took them, I could not stop it, I loved them. [626] There was room in it now; there was even room for her. [638] At the door she pressed the three coppers into Vess's palm and tried to thank her. [654] "Don't," said Vess. [657] "I meant none of it." [662] Which was true, and was therefore, Mara thought, the kindest arrangement on the coast. [676]

When the tower was empty, Vess held the shard a moment and let it show her what she kept off the clock: the ache of every client, all the borrowed guilt she took home so it could not drown anyone else. [717] Then she packed it away, told the chimes she felt nothing, and meant the telling, sincerely. [733] The chimes, being only wind, kept her secret. [741]
