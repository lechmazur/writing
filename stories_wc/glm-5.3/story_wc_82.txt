On the floating island of Solmara, the tea garden falls in silver terraces around a glass basilica, and in that basilica lives Wren, the last of the lantern makers. [29] Her craft is dream recording. [34] Each night the island drifts low over sleeping villages, and in the basilica's nave her lanterns hang like a congregation, sipping the dreams of sleepers into polished gems. [62] In the morning the dreams are poured out as sheets of light and folded into paper cranes, because grief, creased small and given wings, becomes light enough to lose. [91] A thousand released cranes lift the island higher; the basilica's oldest law adds a promise: a thousand folded by one pair of hands will buy one wish. [118]

Wren no longer casts a shadow. [124] It detached on the night her daughter Imey died, tearing loose to follow the smaller shadow that had slipped away first, and it took with it every dream Wren had ever owned. [156] In the months after the shadow detached, she slept like a stone, and a lantern maker who cannot dream is a lantern maker with nothing to pay. [183] She had one motive now: fold her grief into origami cranes, a thousand of them, and spend the wish. [202] But Wren had always been crafty. [208]

A gem polished thin enough, she reasoned, cannot tell a dream from a memory. [222] So each dusk she climbed to the bench beneath the basilica's rose window, took up the gem Imey had last touched, and worked. [245] Gem polishing is patience ground to powder: coarse emery, then rouge, then tea-seed oil, then, when nothing else would clear the stone, tears. [268] Night after night the gem thinned until her memories showed through it like fish under ice. [284] She chose the bright ones: Imey at seven, folding cranes at this same bench, tongue between her teeth; Imey singing wrong words to the pickers' songs; Imey asleep in a row of tea. [317] Each memory she pressed into the stone, and the recording burned as it took, luminous and tormenting at once, a light with teeth in it. [342] The lantern swallowed the gem, lit, and spilled a single sheet of light thin as paper. [358] Wren folded it, corner to corner, wing from wing, until a crane of bright grief stood trembling on her palm. [378]

One crane is nothing, so she began the long ledger of them. [390] And every dawn, as the island climbed away from the villages, Wren walked down through the tea terraces to the rim-gate, so that she was always descending while everything she stood on ascended, a lacquered box of finished cranes under her arm. [432] At the gate she loosed them, and they flew downward like slow bright snow. [446] The sleepers below dreamed of a girl folding cranes and woke lighter, not knowing why. [461] This was her theft and her gift: grief recorded, creased, given away. [473] Through the winter she folded, ninety a week, her fingertips scarring with light. [486] By the last frost she had nine hundred ninety-nine. [495]

Wren knew her wish by heart: to hold Imey's last dream, the one her shadow had chased into the dark. [515] On the thousandth night the gem would not clear. [524] She polished until her thumbs split, and still the stone stayed fogged, because the only memory left inside her was Imey dying, and no gem should be made to carry that. [555] Then the rose window darkened, and her shadow climbed back over the sill. [568] It had gone where dead daughters' shadows go, following the smaller one that had slipped loose first. [585] From its flat gray palm it laid down a chip of night, and Wren, hands shaking, set it to the wheel. [606] She polished shadow the way she polished gems, emery and rouge and tears, until the fog lifted and Imey's last dream showed through the stone. [631] The lantern lit itself. [635]

Imey had dreamed of this: the tea terraces, the glass nave, her mother's hands at the wheel, a thousand cranes rising. [656] She had been dreaming Wren the whole time. [664] Grief cannot be unmade, but it can transform, and it did, all at once, into light with no teeth in it. [685] Then the lantern poured out a final sheet, and the shadow spread itself beneath it, and together they folded into the thousandth crane. [708] At dawn the island rose hard and fast, and Wren went down through the terraces, descending as her world ascended, and opened her hands at the rim-gate. [735] A thousand cranes fell together toward the waking villages, bright and aching, hers and not hers anymore. [752] Below, every sleeper dreamed one dream: a lantern maker in a glass basilica, tea silver under her feet, folding her sorrow into wings until nothing heavy remained but her, standing luminous in the torment of so much light.
