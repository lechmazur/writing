Every evening at twilight, Marek climbed the ridge where the mountain's magnetism gathered, the place the valley folk called the nexus. [21] Compasses failed here; the iron filings he poured from his pouch rose and hung in the air like dark pollen, tracing slow geometries against the fading sky. [48] The exiled chess tutor came to watch the silence move. [58]

In his pocket he carried a polished basalt worry stone, its small dent pressed there by a thumb that was not his. [80] Princess Lira had rubbed it through three years of lessons, turning the black rock whenever a position grew difficult, until her thumb had worn a shallow hollow into its face. [110] She had not spoken since the eclipse, the terrible noon dark in which her mother died, and silence had become her country. [132] Chess was the only language she still consented to speak. [142] He taught her openings and endings, and she answered in tempo: a capture played gently meant forgiveness; a hesitation over the knight meant fear. [166] Once, after long thought, she advanced her pawn to the final rank and refused to choose a queen, and he understood that she was asking what she would become. [195] He kept notebooks of these games, thoughtfully precise, translating silence into meaning one notation at a time. [212]

When the king was dying, he had drawn Marek close and bound him to a covenant: stay with her until her silence speaks, whatever the court decides. [239] The court decided quickly: her uncle the regent declared that a servant who claimed to translate a mute princess spoke only for himself, and Marek was banished beyond the mountains before the frost. [272] They took him from a game in progress, her attack still hanging over his king. [287] So the covenant was eclipsed, its oath still true, its keeping lost in shadow. [301] As the guards led him through the gates, she had run the length of the courtyard, pressed the worry stone into his palm, and closed his fingers over the dent. [331] It was the longest sentence she ever spoke, and he had spent seven years translating it. [347]

Exile gave him no one to teach, so he taught the mountain, which never spoke. [362] Each twilight he scattered filings across a slate and recorded the shapes the nexus drew in the failing light. [381] Then he would correlate each shape with the positions from her old games, lining pattern against gambit like a scholar of two alphabets. [404] It was his way, he told himself, to discover beauty in the difficult, as one does in a cramped middlegame where every move hurts and one move, exactly one, sings. [434] Tonight the pattern refused every memory: the filings gathered along one edge of the slate, then thrust a single line of dust toward the northwest, toward the pass, toward home. [464]

Hooves sounded on the pass side of the ridge, and a mud-caked courier dismounted, gasping out the news. [482] The regent was dead; Lira would be crowned at the coming eclipse; he had ridden day and night to reach her old tutor before the next phase of the moon. [512] She had chosen the hour herself: she would take the crown beneath the same dark that had taken everything else. [532] Then the courier held out a letter bearing no seal, only a single line of chess notation in a small determined hand. [554] It was her next move in the game they had never finished. [566] He translated it the way he had translated everything she never said. [578] It was her queen, offered across the length of the board: your move, teacher; the game is still ours; come finish it. [600] Or perhaps it meant only: I remember. [607]

He was weeping as he packed, and the tears did not slow his hands. [621] Later, on the road, he would laugh at himself for the awe in his chest, because the filings had not been prophetic, only precise: the courier's horse wore iron shoes, and the mountain had merely announced a guest. [659] The covenant had never broken, he understood; it had only been eclipsed, and an eclipse is a phase, not an ending. [680] Some light, once promised, comes back on schedule. [688] He pocketed the slate, settled the worry stone against his thumb, felt her old dent receive him like a word, and started down through the last of the twilight toward the pass, one move ahead of the dark. [726]
