The search party disbanded on the ninth day, and Sera returned to the foundry alone. [15] Rain fell through the broken roof, washing the old crucibles, pooling in the molds Tam had left behind. [33] She was an alchemist of balance, sworn to equivalence: nothing transmuted, nothing mended, without a cost paid in equal measure. [53] Tonight she set her scales on the anvil and weighed the zodiac coin. [66] Tam's coin bore the Water-Bearer, struck the day she named him, when he was an unnamed orphan of seven. [85] In her craft, name-weight was no metaphor; it was fact, as measurable as heat. [99] A name held what its bearer carried of himself, and the coin weighed it. [113]

The searchers had dragged the river for him and found nothing, and gone home. [127] But the coin was not weightless, and dead men's coins were always weightless; she had proved that on graves. [146] He lived. [148] He simply did not wish to be found. [156] Sera stayed to understand the weight of names, because she had made his heavier than he could carry. [174] She had named him Tam, a small, steady name, and then she had loaded it: apprentice, successor, the son of her old age. [197] Every expectation added grams. [201] When he poured a transmutation past her safe threshold, she called his work reckless, and he dropped the bowl he was holding, and left into the rain. [228] The bowl lay in two pieces ever since, on the shelf where it fell. [242]

All winter she explored the limits of the fact instead of grieving, because limits were the only language she trusted. [262] She weighed the coins of brides and runaways, of convicts and pilgrims, mapping how weight rose and fell with what a person kept of himself. [287] One edge she could not test: whether a name wholly set down went weightless too, indistinguishable from a dead man's. [307] Tam's coin dimmed and lightened each month, and she finally understood: he was setting his name down somewhere far off, gently, the way one sets down a heavy bag. [336] On the first clear night she began the mending. [345] Her master had taught her the kintsugi of intentions: a broken intention could be joined with gold, but never hidden. [365] The seam remembers the break, and the gold remembers the care. [376] She mixed gold dust with rainwater and bound the two halves of the bowl, and into the glowing seam she spoke everything a balanced woman had never said: that his recklessness had been alive, that her caution had been cold, that she was sorry. [420] Gold holds what is spoken into it. [427] That was the apology, done as only she could do it. [438]

Then came the harder work. [443] The coin still carried her claim, a base metal smelted into the striking, and claims, like lead, can be redeemed out of gold, but only at a heat beyond her sworn threshold, where the balanced way fails and the metal stands at the edge of ruin. [489] To redeem it, she would have to do the reckless thing, his thing, and pour past every limit she had ever set. [511] She lit the crucible. [515] The rain came again while it heated, hissing on the old iron, and the foundry filled with steam and gold light. [536] She held the coin in the flame's white heart, past the point her training called death, past the safe margin, watching the tarnish of her ownership rise off it like breath. [568] Her hands, steady for forty years, shook. [575] She held on anyway. [579] At the limit, where the gold began to soften and the Water-Bearer's rim began to blur, the last of the lead burned away, and the coin flared clean. [607] She withdrew it, gasping, and set it on the anvil to cool in the rain. [622] When she weighed it at dawn, it was lighter than any coin she had ever measured, and not weightless. [641] Alive, then, and free, and carrying only what he chose. [651]

She understood the weight of names at last, which had been the whole of her reason for staying: a name is a gift that becomes a chain the moment the giver stops holding it open-handed. [686] She had loved him with a closed fist. [694] Now she set the redeemed coin on the anvil beside the mended bowl, whose gold seam took the morning light and gave it back doubled. [719] If he ever came back, he would find his name waiting, holding nothing but itself, and a bowl more beautiful for having broken. [742] If he never came back, the regret would remain, but it would shine, the way kintsugi shines: not despite the break, but through it. [766] The rain kept washing the foundry, and the gold kept its light, and Sera opened her hands. [783]
