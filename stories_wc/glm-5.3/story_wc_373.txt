At dusk the tide slid out, and the pirate market hidden beneath the rotting pier lit its lamps. [18] Nell came down the ladder with the last two jars of her father's shine on her shoulder, a moonshine runner's daughter to the bone. [42] On her waistcoat hung his antique watch fob, a brass stag worn smooth by forty years of thumbing, and beneath the pier it was the only name she had. [71] The market ran on a truce of hush: no names, no questions, nothing seen repeated above, and it held from ebb until sunrise, when the water came back and the bazaar folded into shadow. [105] It was frightening the way home is frightening: tarpaulins against the drip, a brazier of coals, shrimp stew in dented cups, lantern light through the plank gaps like a tame constellation. [136]

Mag, who kept the market's dish and its silence, caught her elbow at the ladder foot. [152] "There's a man here tonight," she breathed, "smelling of ink under the tar, asking after your father's route." [170] If he buys it true, the revenue men will wait at the cove by the next dark moon, and the families who run that water will hang like ornaments. [199] But rumor has no face, and Mag could not point him out. [211]

Her father had told her once, thumb on the brass stag, that trust breaking makes a sound, a yes too quick and too smooth; she had come tonight to capture the sound of trust breaking, because a thing heard can be a thing answered. [255] The traders found her curiously steadfast in this, a girl who stood listening in the lamplight instead of hiding, but her father had been steadfast about worse. [282] So she set out to thwart him through false starts: she would sell her father's route three times, let every sale die, and listen to how each buyer let it die. [313]

The first buyer was a wrecker woman with hands like dock rope, and Nell began the route prettily and put Hadder's Sluice where the true landing should be. [341] The woman spat into the tide pool. [348] "Hadder's dries to mud by ten, girl," she said, "your father taught you better," and she walked off offended, which was the sound of trust holding, gruff and warm as a stove. [380]

The second buyer haggled like a man wrestling an eel, and when Nell stumbled over her terms and changed them twice, he cursed her for a time-waster and stalked off, and his anger was honest, a coin with weight to it. [421]

The third man wore grey gloves and smelled of ink under a smear of tar, and his eyes went to the brass stag before they went to her face. [450] She laid the route out slow, braiding lies through it like rot through a plank, and he said yes. [469] Yes to the Sluice, yes to the drowned church spire torn down before she was born, yes, yes. [487] The yes came quick and smooth as poured cream, and Nell closed her eyes and captured it, the exact sound of trust breaking, every note. [512] He asked for earnest, then asked for more: the stag itself, the fob off her waistcoat, proof they were friends, and no honest trader had ever asked for a family's token. [543] She unhooked it and laid the warm brass in his gloved palm, and then, because the night required one more false start, she broke the deal off entirely. [571] "Tide's wrong," she said. [575] "Sleep on it," he said, and folded the fob away, "sunrise changes minds, and I keep this against yours." [594]

Toward morning the water came back, cold over the boots, and the market folded itself away, tarps down, until only Mag's brazier and lantern stayed lit at the ladder. [623] The grey-gloved man came to climb out with the route in his head and the brass stag swinging at his watch chain, and in a market with no names, wearing a stolen name was the one crime everyone could see. [663] Nobody said a word. [667] The truce of hush closed around him like water, forty traders going silent in a ring, and silence, Nell had learned, was the only sentence that market passed. [695] Mag lifted her lantern, Nell held out her hand, and the fob came back warm, as if it had never left. [716] The man climbed out into the dawn with a head full of false starts, and off the Weepstone bar a revenue cutter would find every one, come the next dark moon. [747]

Nell hung the stag on her waistcoat, took the last cup of stew from Mag's brazier, and sat at the ladder foot, listening to the hush, which was, she now knew, the sound of trust holding. [783]
