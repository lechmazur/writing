The frozen library hung above the clouds like a held breath. [11] Inside, the Orderly Records Chief, Wren Solari, walked the frost-silvered stacks on soundless feet. [25] Above her, the last illusions of the world were fading to memory, and she meant to measure every one before it went out. [48]

Her instruments were simple: a quartz prism, a humming fork, a ledger bound in blue. [63] By sampling frequencies, she could take a dying wonder's pulse. [73] Each illusion sang a note as it thinned. [81] Phantom bridges hummed low and amber; false summers shimmered at a high silver whine. [95] Wren noted each frequency in a hand so gently meticulous that her columns looked like rows of sleeping birds. [114] The work was her vow, though she had never called it that. [126]

The Age of Glow was ending, and with it every borrowed marvel: cloud roads, singing statues, lantern fish swimming through the night sky. [149] What no one could explain was why. [156] Wren had a theory, the kind a person keeps folded small: if the illusions were only ever light, then somewhere in them must be the thing light had promised. [185] She was trying to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion, one measurement at a time. [201]

On the ninth night of the fade, deep in the unprocessed trays, her prism caught a note that did not belong to any illusion on record. [227] It came from a single magic lantern slide, glass and pearl, cool as a spoon. [242] The catalogue said nothing about it; the catalogue, in fact, ended three trays before this one. [258] Wren set her fork humming and sampled the slide's frequency, expecting the tired arc of a dying glamour. [276] Instead the fork held steady, a clean middle note, patient as a heartbeat. [289] It was not fading. [293]

She carried the slide to the great reading lantern, the only warm thing left in the library, and set it in the frame with both hands. [319] Light poured across the frost, and the quartz hush of the stacks broke open into a picture. [336] Two figures stood on a cloud road at dawn, hands clasped, speaking words Wren could not hear but could see, because the words were made of light. [363] Luminous vows: the old rite, older than the library, in which promises spoken under aurora were fixed into light itself. [383] The founders of the library had made such a vow, the story said, though the story had faded with everything else. [404]

Wren's hands trembled, then stilled themselves. [410] She understood, all at once, what the fading meant. [419] The world was not losing its light; it was being sorted by it. [432] Illusions were light wearing borrowed shapes, and they dissolved back into memory as the shapes were forgotten. [449] But vows were light given a debt to carry, and debts do not fade. [463]

All night she searched, sampling frequencies shelf by shelf. [472] Beneath the dying whine of every glamour, if one listened closely, a few notes endured. [487] A nurse's promise stitched into a bandage charm. [495] A fisherman's oath sealed in the last lantern fish. [504] A child's word to a dying dog, glowing faintly inside a toy soldier. [517] The illusions fell away from these small stubborn lights like frost sliding off glass. [531] Wren worked until her ink froze twice and her lamp burned low, cataloguing each survivor with the same gently meticulous care she had given the dying. [557]

By dawn the sky below the library was ordinary cloud, white and slow. [570] The singing statues were stone. [575] The cloud roads were mist. [580] But on the long table by the reading lantern, thirty-one slides and keepsakes glowed with a low, even note, and Wren wrote beside each one the word that mattered: held. [610] The first slide she propped upright before the window, facing the empty sky. [623] When travelers finally climbed this high again, they would find a library with nothing illusory in it, and a records chief who could prove, frequency by frequency, what the age of marvels had actually been made of. [660] Not wonders, she would tell them. [666] Promises. [667] The illusions had only ever been the light vows make when they travel far from home. [683] And in the quartz hush above the clouds, Wren Solari dipped her pen and began the ledger's final page, where the true frequencies were kept, the ones that would not fade to memory, no matter how long the world forgot how to listen.
