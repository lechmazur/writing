The library had been left to the town by a silk merchant who loved quiet, and in autumn its velvet chairs drank the last of the light. [27] Every Thursday, Nadia unlocked it for the choir. [35] She was a choir teacher of the old harmonious kind, unable to hear a disagreement without reaching for the note underneath it. [57] She was also the only teacher willing to take children from both banks of the river. [73] For six years the banks had exchanged gunfire instead of songs; for twenty more, silence. [88] The children came to her carrying grudges like coats inherited from large, angry relatives, too big for them, warming nobody. [108]

On the reading table, between the atlas and the candle stubs, lay Josip's zither. [122] It was the last one ever built on the east bank, where the craft had quietly stopped breathing. [140] Around its soundhole bloomed a rosette Josip had carved with a bent needle: eleven petals, each petal a note of the old river round. [164] The petals denoted the whole melody, he had told her, if a person knew how to read them. [182] She had hated the east bank once, the way you hate an entire river because one wave drowned your brother. [202] Josip had hated the west bank for reasons of his own, older and duller than the war. [219] Unlearning hatred took the two of them twenty years, one shared bench outside the courthouse, and a thousand arguments conducted at the volume of confession. [244] By the end, what remained was two old voices, one zither, and a habit of meeting on Thursdays. [262] When he died, the instrument came to her with a note that said only: For the children, both banks. [281]

Nadia taught the way Josip had tuned: by subtracting noise. [291] She never added anything to a child's voice. [299] She asked them only to notice what did not belong: the sneer buried under a consonant, the held breath of two east-bank boys standing nearer the door than the others, the flat mutter of a word that used to be an insult. [341] Subtract that, she would say, and what is left is already harmony, waiting. [354] Her motive was never a secret: she meant to braid their diverse voices, the west's bright vowels and the east's dark ones, into one sound no bullet could tell apart. [384] She was not sentimental about the odds. [391] The vanishing arts had been vanishing all her life: rosette carvers, round-singers, the men who knew which tree gave its wood gladly. [413] She had grown expectantly resigned to this, the way one grows resigned to autumn while still lifting a face toward whatever light arrives. [436]

That Thursday, with the autumn deep as spilled wine, she set the zither on her knees. [452] Eighteen children settled among the velvet chairs like birds on a wire that did not quite trust itself. [470] "I cannot play it," she told them, which was true; Josip had tried to teach her, and she had laughed too much to learn. [494] "But the rosette can." [498] She let them gather close and trace the petals, one finger per note, the way Josip's needle had once traveled. [518]

Then she had them sing the round in halves, east voice against west voice, wrong at first and full of noise. [539] A west-bank girl sang sharp on purpose to spite an east-bank boy; the boy answered by dragging the tempo like a stone in a river. [564] Nadia subtracted. [566] She took away the sneer, then the drag, then the fear beneath both, naming each one gently and laying it outside the music like a coat handed over at the door. [597] What remained, on the fourth try, was the braid. [606] The east voice entered late, as the carving said it should, and the west voice rose to meet it, and for eight bars there was no river in the room at all, only one water. [641] Even the boy by the door had wandered three steps nearer. [652] A leaf struck the window and slid, and nobody turned to look, which was the highest praise the room knew how to give. [675]

Afterward the girl who had sung sharp asked, in a voice trying not to hope, whether the zither would be here next Thursday, and whether she could learn the petals. [705] Nadia regarded her for a long moment, resignation and expectation passing through her like two currents that had finally stopped fighting. [726] "It will be here," she said, "for exactly as long as someone is learning it." [741] Then she watched the children go out into the yellow air, both banks of them walking down the same street, and sat a while in the traveled stillness with her palm resting on the rosette. [776] Through the soundhole under her hand came a faint ring, the shape of eleven voices holding one note. [794] The vanishing could wait. [798]
