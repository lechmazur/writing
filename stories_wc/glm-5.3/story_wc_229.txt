The heat wave broke at three in the morning, and by dawn the first fog in forty days came off the water like a breath finally let go. [28] Wren, assistant lighthouse keeper of the cobalt tower at Cormorant Point, felt the change in her teeth before she saw it. [49] She had grown up in the harbor below, where everything worth knowing arrived as a whisper. [65] Old Silas, the head keeper, knew only one volume. [74] Forty years beside the foghorn had taken most of his hearing, so he shouted his kindnesses the way other men whispered them: eat, girl, until you drop; coat, girl, before October kills you. [107] Screaming tenderness, Wren called it, the only kind he had left. [118]

His wife May had been the other kind of voice. [128] She kept a garden on the seaward ledge and let a girl named Wren deadhead jasmine by moonlight, teaching her that everything vibrates with the intention behind it. [156] The horn vibrates with fear, May said, and fear dies in fog. [168] Love moves through anything. [172] May drowned eleven summers ago, swimming after a dinghy that had slipped its mooring, and her voice went with her. [192] This summer the heat wave finished what grief started; the jasmine burned brown, and the ledge garden stood dead above a sea too warm to make fog. [219] All that remained of May's nights was a small vial on the kitchen shelf, sealed with a cork she had carried from her mother's country, a cork said to purify whatever intention was sealed inside it. [255] Inside was the scent of the garden at night, the last summer of it, gathered before she died. [273]

Wren had been assistant keeper for two years now, and for two years she had been trying to prove to Silas that whispers travel farther than shouts. [300] Silas answered with the horn, always the horn. [308] Now the fog came down like judgment, and the horn coughed once and died. [322] Silas hammered the compressor with a wrench, screaming words Wren could not hear over the surf and did not need to. [343] The radio said two boats were running for harbor, blind, and the lamp's beam bounced uselessly off the fog's white wall. [364]

Wren ran down the cobalt spiral, took the vial from the shelf, and climbed out onto the ledge where the dead garden dripped in the new cold. [391] She shouted to Silas, once, that she loved him, because if she was wrong about everything, she wanted that to be loud. [413] Then she knelt in the ruined jasmine and pulled the cork. [424] The scent of the garden at night came out of the glass the way a voice comes out of a sleeping house, unhurried, certain of being heard. [451] Jasmine, rain on warm stone, the green breath of crushed leaves. [462] Because the cork had purified May's intention before it was ever sealed, only the true part rode the wind now, the wanting-them-safe part, and fog cannot stop what it cannot frighten. [493] Wren held the open vial to the east wind and whispered into it, come home, come home, a whisper with no fear in it at all. [519] A scent can beckon where a shout cannot, May used to say; a garden at night calls you in without one word. [541] The scent went out across the water in a slow ribbon, and the fog did not swallow it. [559]

Far out, engines changed pitch, turning. [565] The radio crackled: a skipper saying he could smell flowers, that his chart showed no flowers out here, that he was following them anyway. [589] A second voice joined the first. [595] Silas came out of the horn house with the wrench still in his fist and stopped at the top of the steps, his ruined ears straining. [621] He could not hear the whisper, but he felt it in his chest, the way you feel a held breath. [641] Then he was shouting her name into the fog, May, May, all his loud love pouring out at once, and it carried nowhere, and that was somehow right. [669] The whisper carrying his wife's garden went on without him, out and out, and the boats came in one by one, noses lifting over the swell toward the smell of home. [700] They found the harbor by it, tied up shaking, alive. [710]

Dawn came up behind the tower, cobalt melting to gold, the fog thinning to silk. [725] The last boat slid in on the smell of jasmine. [735] Silas sat down hard beside her, threw his arm around her shoulders like a falling beam, and shouted the tenderest thing he had ever said: that May's garden had saved them all. [767] Wren pressed the cork back into the empty vial and whispered, told you. [780]
