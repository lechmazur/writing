The letter from the mainland clinic gave Mara six months, and she filed it between pressed fern samples in her botanical diary, where it seemed most at home. [28] Forty years as caretaker of Farrow Isle had taught her that deadlines worked on people and seasons alike. [46] She spent that evening, as she spent every evening, walking the temple orchard where eleven oaks stood inside a ring of living stone pillars. [70] The pillars were not carved; they had grown, grain by patient grain, and the temple roof rested on them like a hand on a shoulder. [95] Most visitors saw ruins. [99] Mara saw correspondents. [102] The architectural secrets were hers to keep and, now, hers to finish translating. [115] Decades of entries in the diary had mapped them: load paths that followed root paths, hidden cisterns that mirrored the oaks' thirst, doorways placed exactly where shade fell at noon. [145] Whoever had raised the temple had not built over the land; they had negotiated with it, and the building was the treaty's signature. [168] The treaty had a clause still unfulfilled, and her six months were just long enough to fulfill it. [186] The original builders had left the pillars' inner faces unfinished, waiting for the day the oaks would be invited to touch them. [208] Mara had found the invitation carved in the lintel: a branch, a pillar, and a wire joining them, worn almost to nothing. [230] So she would remodel the orchard, not as an owner redecorates but as a host rearranges seats so shy guests finally meet. [252] Her method was gently systematic, the way all her work had been: one tree, one branch, one entry, one dawn. [272] Each morning she chose the oak whose mood ran most toward patience. [284] She coiled copper wire around its longest branches, loose enough never to bite, firm enough to suggest. [301] Copper, the diary's oldest pages insisted, was the interpreter both kingdoms trusted: soft as sap, patient as stone, carrying the faint current that trees and pillars traded underground and could not trade through air. [335] Week by week she bent the wired branches upward and outward, training the crowns toward the pillars' waiting faces. [354] She recorded every adjustment in the diary, sketches beside measurements, measurements beside guesses honestly labeled as guesses. [371] The mainland doctor called in her fourth month, surprised she was still booking appointments. [385] She thanked him and said she was mid-conversation and could not be interrupted. [398] Her calm unnerved people more than tears would have, and she had stopped apologizing for that years ago. [416] What was grief, next to an orchard mid-sentence? [424] If serenity this late looked like audacity, fine; brazen calm was still calm. [437] The purpose underneath the wire and the wiring of her days was older than the diagnosis. [453] She had come to the island at nineteen, furious at a world that treated forests as lumber and stone as gravel. [474] She stayed to foster interspecies respect, one careful page at a time, proving that the island's old arrangement, builder and tree and pillar and keeper, was not myth but maintenance. [504] In her fifth month the first oak branch touched its pillar, copper glinting where bark met stone. [521] That night the pillar hummed, a note too low for ears, felt in the teeth. [536] By the sixth month, ten of eleven oaks were wired and leaning inward, and the ring had become something new: not pillars holding a roof over trees, but one congregation breathing together under it. [570] The eleventh oak was young and stubborn, and Mara left it unwired on purpose; a treaty that allowed no refusal was just a fence. [594] On the last morning she could reliably stand, she carried the diary to the temple's threshold and weighed it open with a copper coil. [618] The final entry was addressed to two readers: whoever came after her, and whatever had been reading over her shoulder all along. [640] It gave instructions for the remodel's next hundred years, then closed: "I kept you forty years; you kept me whole for all of them; we are even, and I was never alone here." [673] She walked into the orchard, sat against the young oak's trunk, and did not find the ending remarkable. [691] The ferryman found her three days later, and after him came a successor, who found the diary first, as intended. [711] Within a season, visitors stopped calling the place ruins. [720] They came to see the temple where the stones had faces, the trees wore copper, and the newest pillar, small, mica-bright, unmistakably fresh, had begun, grain by patient grain, to grow. [751]
