The Arkwright was built from what Earth had left: hull plates of scrapped ferries, a cathedral's rose window, the pressed bones of ten thousand cars. [25] It recycled everything: water, air, and the dead, who went into the recycler's blue flame and came out as wall panels, as thread, as warmth in the corridors. [53] Nothing was wasted, not even grief. [59]

Yura was the last human ever born. [66] The Quiet Fever had ended births three generations from Earth, and she was its final note, old now, drifting through hallways that murmured with everyone the ship had ever swallowed. [96] The dead did not haunt the Arkwright; they were the Arkwright, and they were patient. [111]

At the ship's heart waited the Fold, a pocket dimension sealed shut by the first builders, locked behind a gravimetric door that no torch, code, or plea had ever opened. [141] Inside, the elders swore, lay the seed of a living world, soil, rain, the embryos of birds, kept for whoever grew light enough to carry it. [167] For joy shed mass; that was the ship's one strange law. [178] Humans grew gravitationally lighter when happy, and the door would open only for a heart that carried almost nothing. [197] Yura had stood on the brass plate before it every year of her life, and every year the needle mocked her heavy. [219]

She carried a relic: Kwan Yin's tear, a drop of hardened resin her grandmother had smuggled aboard in a coat seam, the tear the goddess of mercy wept upon hearing all the cries of the world at once. [257] Its lesson was simple and terrible: compassion means not looking away. [268] Yura had practiced it all her life. [275] She was the Arkwright's steadfast pacifist, the one who refused, during the Raider years, to let the recycler forge guns, and who talked three boarding parties back into their airlocks with only her voice and open hands. [312] But talk had not saved the Hendricks deck. [320] Forty people went into the blue flame in a single night, and afterward Yura found, in the cellar of herself, a shadow wearing her face, a self that had wanted to answer fire with fire, that still wanted to, that whispered of torpedoes whenever she passed the recycler. [368] She had never once looked at it directly. [376] Her grandmother had a name for the task: shadow work, the slow and dangerous art of turning toward what you have buried. [398]

Her method was already in the archive. [405] The ship kept a vault of faded photographs from Earth, and the dying had wept over them for generations, leaving long-dried tear stains that caught the light with a faint shimmer, like salt maps of grief. [441] Yura had learned to read that shimmer. [447] When she tilted a photograph beneath her lamp, the sorrow that made each old tear rose up through her fingers and became briefly hers: a mother's last look at a garden, a boy's goodbye to a dog, and, though she had avoided it for fifty years, the Hendricks deck on fire, streaked with tears she had shed at thirty. [506] So she worked. [509] Night after night she sat among the photographs, and each shimmer she absorbed made the shadow in her cellar louder, until at last she lifted the photograph of the burning deck and looked at what she had buried. [547] There it was: her rage, whole and hot; beneath it, the guilt; and beneath the guilt, astonishingly, grief for the raiders too, children of the same dying species, scared as anyone. [578] Kwan Yin had heard all the cries of the world, Yura thought, and hers must have been among them. [597] She did not forgive herself by deciding to; she forgave the way a wound closes: slowly, and by feeling everything. [617]

Then she stitched. [620] She sewed the photographs together into a banner, joining tear stain to tear stain with thread the recycler had spun from the dead, and at its center she stitched Kwan Yin's tear, so that mercy sat down among the sorrows instead of floating above them. [665] When it was finished she pressed the banner to her chest, and the walls hummed with approval, or grief, or both; she could not tell. [690]

She walked to the Fold old and unhurried, ghostly with resolve, carrying the banner, nothing in her heart she had not already faced. [713] She stepped onto the brass plate. [719] The needle slid down the scale as her heart, at last, lifted, and settled below the line. [736] The seal let go with a sound like a held breath released, and Yura set her palms against the seam of light and pushed. [760] The locked dimension opened like an eye, spilling green: soil, rain, the small breathing of birds; and the ship of the dead leaned close, weightless, to watch the world begin again. [791]
