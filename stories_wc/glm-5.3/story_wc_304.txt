The grotto hung a mile above the sea, a bowl of pale stone open to the sky, and all night the aurora poured down into it. [26] Maren kept its radiant hush the way priests keep candle flames, tending it, never breaking it. [42] She was the grotto's beautician mortician, the only trade that had ever fit her hands. [57] She had painted brides below once; now she painted the dead, gentler clients who never fidgeted. [73]

Here the dead did not rot. [79] Laid on the slate shelves, they entered a threshold of stillness: nine days in which the body held its shape while whatever remained hung behind the eyes, watching the aurora, waiting. [110] On the ninth night the stillness broke and the dead dissolved into light, rising to join the aurora. [128] That shimmer was static, the scholars said: ten thousand forgotten souls smeared together into noise, their sparks lost. [146] Oblivion, they called it, and they called it kind. [155] Maren had never agreed. [159] Her gift was finding patterns in static. [166] Where others heard noise, she read handwriting: ten thousand scripts crossed and recrossed, and she had learned to isolate a single hand. [188] Before the ninth night, she could read a person's pattern in that light, catch the spark still theirs, and infuse it into a stone for the family. [215] It was the whole of her calling: to capture one last spark before oblivion. [229]

Late that autumn, the winch creaked and the basket rose from the clouds carrying a body wrapped in gray linen. [249] Maren unwound the cloth and stopped breathing. [256] It was her sister, Nell. [261] Drowned, the note said, though the sea had returned her calm, as though she had only lain down to rest. [281] Nell's eyes were open, lucidly watchful, tracking the slow green rivers of light overhead. [295] Maren had made a thousand strangers beautiful for their last nine days; now she would make her sister beautiful, and her hands shook. [318] The work asked for patience and love, and she had both in terrible amounts. [332] She warmed pomade between her palms as she had when they were girls sharing one washbasin, smoothed the salt from Nell's hair, and the hush held them like cupped hands. [362]

The emerald came out on the fourth night. [370] It was an emerald chip, really, no bigger than a fingernail paring, broken off their mother's earring when Nell was six and had borrowed the pair to be a queen. [400] Nell had kept it in a locket all her life, and the locket came up with her; Maren waited three days to open it, knowing what it was for. [429] Now she set the green shard on Nell's sternum and began the reading. [442] Finding the pattern was slow, patient work. [449] She sat beside the shelf, lucidly watchful, and let the aurora pour through her, searching the static for the one rhythm that did not belong to the ten thousand. [478] Every soul flickered differently: some pulsed like surf, some stuttered like a bad lamp, some beat in waltz time. [497] Nell's, when it finally separated from the noise, was laughter. [507] Three quick flashes and a long one, over and over, the exact meter of the laugh that had gotten them both thrown out of chapel as children. [534] Maren wept without noise, because the hush was part of the craft. [546] Within the laughter were smaller patterns, memories folded into the flicker like seeds in a fruit, and she had to choose one. [568] A wedding, a storm, a kitchen, a song. [576] This was the cruelty of the gift: one spark only, and the rest went to the noise. [593] She chose the morning the queen went wading in the tide pools, struck an earring on the rocks, and came home roaring that the sea owed her a treasure. [622]

The infusing itself was simple. [627] On the ninth night, as the stillness thinned, she pressed the chip to Nell's lips and tapped the rhythm against it, three quick and one long, until the green stone drank the light and began to shine. [664] That was all infusing ever was: giving the spark somewhere small and lasting to live. [679]

At moonset the threshold broke. [684] Nell's face loosened into threads of light that rose in a slow corkscrew, and Maren stood in the radiant hush and watched her sister join the ten thousand. [712] Oblivion took everything it was owed, but not everything. [721] In the morning she threaded the chip onto a wire and wore it at her throat. [737] Sometimes, in the quiet after a reading, it flickered: three quick, one long. [750] A last spark, kept out of the noise, laughing at her from inside the green. [765] And on clear nights she could still find, somewhere in the crossed and recrossed light, everything else her sister ever was. [786]
