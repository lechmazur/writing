The opal library hung above the clouds, its walls veined with milk-blue fire, and no visitor had climbed to it in ninety years. [23] Inside, the museum of extinct feelings kept its marvels, hall after deserted hall. [36] The Gallery of Being Forgiven. [41] The Corridor of Missing Someone on Purpose. [48] Marvels, every one, and not a living soul to wonder at them but Mereth, the caretaker of the glass orchids. [68]
Each orchid grew from its pedestal of pale stone and held, behind translucent petals, the final echo of a feeling the world below had stopped feeling. [94] Touch a petal and it whispered a name, the name of the last person to carry that feeling before it left the world. [117] Mereth had one purpose left, to preserve the sound of forgotten names. [129]
Her care was gently systematic: west halls on the first day, east halls on the second, the dome on the third. [150] She watered the orchids with cloudmelt, wiped each petal with lambswool, and wrote in her ledger every name that still had sound. [172]
Over the entrance arch hung a wind chime of bamboo tubes, carried up from the drowned valleys before the clouds closed. [193] Each tube was tuned to one feeling, and when an orchid began to fail, a wind with no source found its tube and rang it. [218] The eighth tube was cracked. [223] It had cracked forty years ago, on the night Mereth chose to stop feeling the one feeling she had left, and every strike since had rung sour. [250]
The feeling was called foxglove love, because it was a medicine at the right dose and a poison beyond it. [270] Her teacher Verity had named it, laughing, the day she taught Mereth the rounds and the song. [287] The song was the whole method of the work: when an orchid dimmed, you could light it again, but only by the echo of a long-forgotten song, the one its last bearer had loved, hummed back into the glass until the name remembered itself. [331]
On the morning of the third day, in the dome at the top of the library, the foxglove orchid began to fail. [353] An orchid fades when nothing living feeds it, and this one had been starving for forty years, because Mereth had never once dared to sing to it. [380] Its petals went gray at the edges, and down through nine floors of deserted halls the sourceless wind rose and struck the cracked tube, once, twice, a wrong bright clatter. [410]
Mereth climbed toward the dome with her ledger and her lambswool and her steady system, and her heart going wrong in her chest. [433] She knew whose name the orchid held. [440] Verity had loved her at exactly the dose that keeps a heart beating, and when the fever took her, Mereth had chosen numbness over the risk of one more measure. [470] That made her the last bearer, and the last name she planted that year was her own. [487]
In the dome she knelt at the gray orchid and tried to sing Verity's song back into the glass. [506] She had not hummed it in forty years, and it came out in pieces, wrong intervals, a handful of dust where a melody should be. [531] The petals dimmed further. [535] The wind struck the cracked tube again, its broken note ringing up the stairwell, flat and sour, and Mereth finally heard what she had been hearing for forty years. [564]
Verity had never sung the song true. [571] Age had bent her voice, and she had laughed at her own flat notes, and she had sung that way every morning, cracked and cheerful, across the museum of extinct feelings. [602] The echo Mereth carried was the broken version, had always been the broken version, because the broken version was the one her teacher had loved. [627]
So she stopped trying to mend the melody and sang it as it had truly come to her, cracked, flat, and full of laughter, an echo of an echo. [656] The glass warmed, and light rose through the petals like dawn through opal, and the orchid spoke her name back to her in Verity's voice, and the eighth tube rang, cracked and true at last. [691]
The regret did not leave her. [697] Regret, she had learned, is the same herb as the feeling it mourns: medicine at the daily dose, poison beyond it. [718] So each morning she took her measure, walking her rounds, humming a broken song through a deserted marvel of a museum, keeping one more forgotten name in the world by sound alone. [750]
