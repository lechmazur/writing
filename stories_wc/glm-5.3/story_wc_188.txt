On the last road of a bad year, Wren came walking up to the fortress at dusk, chanting her inventory the way other people hum. [25] "Saltpeter, sorrow, sulfur, sky," she sang, because a wandering fireworks designer who forgets her ingredients is a funeral waiting for weather. [46] The fortress bloomed above her, absurd and lovely, its cannon-slots stuffed with moonflowers that opened as the light failed. [65] Roses had colonized the battlements; ivy strangled the flagpole. [74] Above it all, the sky offered nothing: a starless night settling in like a held breath. [90]

The gatekeeper was a boy with a lantern he never lit. [101] "Nobody lights anything anymore," he said, not unkindly. [109] "The stars went out three winters back, and people decided that was the sky telling us to stop." [127] "Stop what?" she asked. [131] "Everything," said the boy, as if it were obvious. [140] Wren nodded gravely, the way she did at bad news, while privately noting that cannon-slots made excellent mortar tubes. [159] Serious work, this; but there was no law saying it couldn't be fun. [172]

In her pack, wrapped in oilcloth, she carried her whole remaining fortune: a fire spinner's kerosene rag knot, tied by hands she missed. [195] Tamsin had spun fire in a hundred squares before the starless winter took the crowds and the joy; then she vanished with a satchel of mosaic glass and no goodbye. [225] The knot still smelled of her: kerosene, cut grass, stubbornness. [235]

Wren followed the boy across the courtyard, where an old garrison mosaic lay half-buried in petals. [251] It was a map of the night sky, thousands of glass stars set by some homesick soldier. [268] Wren let her chant drop to a murmur and opened her awareness instead, the way her mother had taught her: no hunting, just receiving. [292] Ambient details drifted in and arranged themselves. [299] Petals falling on the mosaic sounded different on glass than on stone. [311] One patch near the center rang faintly hollow. [319] And there, tucked among the clear stars, a scatter of amber: nine tiles, deep as honey, where every other star was plain glass. [342] Someone had wanted those found. [347]

"Bring your lantern," Wren told the boy. [354] She struck her flint, soaked a corner of the kerosene rag, and lit the boy's lantern: the first honest flame the courtyard had held in three winters. [381] Then she filtered the lantern light through the amber tiles, holding each up like a jeweler asking questions of a stone. [402] The amber drank the light and answered in color, throwing words onto the courtyard wall, a note in mosaic that only fire could read. [426] Nine words in a looping hand she knew like her own: WREN, THE SKY IS ONLY HIDING, MAKE IT BLUSH. [446] Wren laughed once, a short broken bark, because grief and delight had always shared a room in her chest. [465] "The fire spinner was here," she said. [472] "A woman with burning ropes," the boy agreed, wide-eyed. [481] "She stayed one night, and the flowers have never stopped blooming since." [493]

Beneath the hollow tile, Wren found a soldier's tin packed with Tamsin's genius: star shells, ready-fused, wrapped against the damp, and a pouch of amber glass. [519] Enough for one honest sky. [524] "Your town made a mistake," Wren told the boy, rolling up her sleeves. [537] "The sky didn't tell you to stop; it dared you to answer." [549] They worked through the last of dusk chanting together, loading shells into cannon-slots while moonflowers opened like a slow audience. [569]

When full dark came, it came completely: no stars, no moon, a hush so total the town stopped breathing. [588] Perfect conditions, Wren decided, for a first light. [596] She touched the burning rag to the fuses. [604] The first shell climbed the dark like a question. [613] Then the fortress bloomed again, upward this time: gold chrysanthemums, silver willows, a crimson peony opening over battlements where roses gaped. [634] Light rained down through the moonflowers and arrived changed, filtered to rose and honey, as if the flowers themselves were learning to shine. [657] Doors opened along the walls. [662] Faces appeared, upturned. [665] Somebody wept; somebody was already begging the boy to ask her to stay. [678] Wren watched her handmade stars fall and fade and answered every one with more. [692] This was the whole trade: every shell an argument against gravity, and in towns like this, gravity went by the name of hopelessness. [715] Despair was only weight; her job was escape velocity, with sequins. [726]

Between finales she took four shards from the pouch of amber and set them into the mosaic's empty corner, a note for whoever wandered here next: IT BLUSHED, COME SEE. [756] Then she retied the kerosene rag into its knot, tucked it away still warm, and started down the road, chanting. [776] "Saltpeter, sorrow, sulfur, sky," she sang to the dark, which was only hiding. [789]
