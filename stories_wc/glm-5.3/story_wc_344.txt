Vera had been born under a lunar eclipse, and the elders of her coastal town swore that such children could only become locksmiths of a particular kind. [27] They were called lunar-eclipse locksmiths, and they worked only when the moon crossed into shadow, one night every few years, no more. [49] The moon chose the hour; the locks chose her. [58]

Tonight the eclipse had chosen the frozen library above the clouds, a granite whale of a building drifting at the top of a long stair, its windows iced over like the eyes of something sleeping. [93] She carried her grandfather's clockmaker's gear assortment in a satchel worn soft as driftwood, brass and steel wheels nested in fading velvet, and she carried his last words too: learn the rhythm of repair. [127] That was why she had climbed. [133]

Inside, the cold had stopped everything mid-gesture. [140] Books stood open on lecterns, readers' gloves abandoned at the turn of a page, dust hung in the air unmoving as if even time had been asked to wait. [169] The great clock at the library's heart had jammed decades ago, and without its beat the whole place had frozen, then lifted, then drifted up among the clouds like grief seeking altitude. [201] No ordinary locksmith could touch it. [208] The elders had told her, as they told all eclipse-born children, that such locks were found through scraps of prophecy hidden in a library's corner, tucked into books no one borrowed twice. [240]

So she went to the corner where the castoff shelves leaned together, where tide-smelling atlases and almanacs of forgotten harbors sat gray with cold. [264] The first scrap found her fingers more than she found it: a curl of paper between pages 88 and 89 of a pilot's log, inked in a hand she half-recognized as her grandfather's. [297] Where the mirrors forget their faces, the key remembers, it read. [308] Another scrap, tucked in a hymnal: The eclipse shines backward; turn the work toward it. [323] A third, folded into a manual of tides: The rhythm is not in the hands but in the listening. [342]

Each fragment pointed deeper, past the reading rooms, to a door of clouded glass she had mistaken for a window. [362] Beyond it lay the hall of mirrors. [369] It was a corridor the length of a held breath, walls of old silvered glass, and in each mirror stood the clock, whole and running, reflected at angles that no single room could hold. [403] The mirrors remembered what the library had lost. [411] At the hall's end, the real mechanism slumped in its casing, gears seized, pendulum stiff, a heart that had forgotten it was ever a heart. [436]

Above her, through a skylight of ancient ice, the eclipse reached totality, and the darkness that fell was not darkness at all but a hauntingly bright bronze, moonlight strained through the earth's own shadow, the color of old bells. [475] The mirrors drank it and gave it back a thousandfold. [485] In that light Vera finally saw the truth the scraps had been circling: the lock was not on the door, and the repair was not of the clock alone. [514] Every mirror held a figure at a workbench, hands bent over a small broken thing, and every figure was her grandfather at a different age, and the youngest of them was her. [546] The rhythm of repair, he had said, and she understood at last that rhythm is inherited, passed hand to hand like a tool. [569]

She knelt at the casing and opened the satchel. [578] From the clockmaker's gear assortment she chose a wheel whose teeth matched the seized ones, and from the velvet's corner she took the small stoppered vial of pivot oil that had ridden there for forty years. [614] Her thumb trembled only a little as she uncapped it, and the smell that rose was her grandfather's workshop, salt and brass and patience. [638] She oiled the pivots, set the new wheel, and then did nothing. [650] She listened. [652] That was the rhythm: to move, then listen; to listen, then move. [664]

Under the soft hush of a distant morning that was not quite arriving, not quite refusing to arrive, she worked by eclipse-light and by echo, and somewhere in the third listening the pendulum shivered, remembered its weight, and swung. [703] The first tick went through the hall of mirrors like a pebble through still water, ring after ring, and in every glass the clock struck once, then together, then the library itself exhaled. [736] Frost loosened from the windows. [741] The dust began, very slowly, to settle instead of hang. [751]

Somewhere below the clouds, the coastal town would wake to the sound of a building descending, patient as driftwood returning to shore, carrying one locksmith who had learned, at last, the rhythm of repair. [785]
