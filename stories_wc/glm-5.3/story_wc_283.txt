Mara, the hourglass turner, worked at dusk, when the amber warehouse beneath the cliffs went the color of held breath. [20] Twelve glasses stood on shelves cut into the rock, each filled with amber dust ground from the cliff face, each marking one age of the town above. [47] Her mother had turned them, and her grandmother before her, back to the first turner, who sealed something in a jar on the highest shelf with one instruction: do not open until the prophecy fails. [82] The prophecy had never failed. [87] It had simply arrived scrambled. [92] Each generation sent a speaker to the canyon mouth, and the canyon returned a voice folded into itself, words stacked on their own reversals, and the council assembled the prophecy from whatever sense they could make of the ruin. [131] This year the canyon returned nothing at all. [139] A silence, unearthed from some depth the town had never plumbed, rolled down through the rock and stilled the amber dust in all twelve glasses. [164] The council read the silence as a verdict and told the town to prepare for its ending. [181] That, Mara decided, was a kind of failing. [189] She climbed the shelf ladder and took down the jar. [199]

Inside, coiled like smoke, hung the first echo, caught by the first turner on the night the canyon first spoke. [219] She knew it would be scrambled, because canyons fold whatever they return, and a jarred echo keeps the folding. [238] To unscramble it she would need what no one else had kept: the buttons. [252] The floor had been collecting them since the night of the sealing. [264] Amber, rubbed by the passage of coats and sleeves, keeps a small hunger for light things, and everyone in the town had come down that night, and the echo had passed through them all. [298] It passed eleven times, folding tighter as it went, and each pass shook loose buttons that held single words, one word to a button, in the order they were heard. [328] Mara's grandmother had begun cataloging the lost buttons; Mara finished the work: four thousand and more, sorted by depth in the amber, by thread, by the wear of that year on their faces. [361] The order of the buttons was the order of the words. [372]

She laid them all out across the floor in a long line, unstoppered the jar, and let the echo wander down the row like a finger reading a shelf. [401] Word by word, the fold came undone. [408] She worked through the night inside the new silence, and near dawn the sentence lay flat and plain on the floor before her. [431] It was not a prophecy. [436] It was a question. [440] The first speaker, two hundred years ago, had stood at the canyon mouth and asked, "What should we become?" [459] The canyon had only ever returned what it was given, and this year the town had given it nothing but fear, and fear makes no sound a canyon can carry. [489] Every prophecy since was the town hearing its own fear folded into shapes it called fate. [505] This year's ending was no verdict at all; it was the council's own dread, spoken aloud in the silence and mistaken for the canyon's answer. [530]

As the truth unraveled, Mara felt something she had no name for, and then found one: not grief, not triumph, but the feeling of being quietly inspired, as if a door she had polished all her life had turned out to be a door. [574] A prophecy is a closed shape. [580] A question is raw possibility, and she meant to spin the prophecy into it before anyone could fold it shut again. [601] Anyone can answer a question. [606] No one can answer a fate. [612] So Mara did what a turner does: she spun it. [622] She poured the unscrambled echo into the twelfth glass, the one whose age was ending, and turned it over, so that question and amber dust fell together through the narrow waist and mixed into something that was neither. [660] When the speaker came down at dawn, Mara gave her the glass and told her to let it run at the canyon mouth. [683] The question spilled over the cliffs, unhurried and ordinary: what should we become? [696] Because answers spoken near the canyon came back folded but recognizable, each reply layered over the others, a shared wisdom assembling itself out of returned words. [722] A farmer asked it and heard the weaver's answer inside his own. [734] A child asked it and heard both. [741] Mara went back inside and stood among her glasses in the unearthed silence, which was no longer empty, and turned the first hour of the new age herself. [769] It sounded like a beginning. [774]
