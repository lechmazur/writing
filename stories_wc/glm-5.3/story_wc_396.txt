The orchard grew in a wind-hollowed ring around the lighthouse, its apple trees bent inland as if flinching from the sea. [21] Below the cliff, shifting ice floes ground together with a sound like slow applause. [35] Marah walked the rows at dusk, shadow-laced hands folded, listening to the ice argue. [49] Dark veins ran beneath her skin like roots, the mark of an anomaly no one could explain. [66] The village feared it politely and still brought her every quarrel, because she was calmly intuitive where others were loud. [86] Peacekeeper, they called her, and she kept the title the way she kept the shard in her pocket: carefully, and with guilt. [108] The shard was the largest fragment of the old crystal ball, the glass peacekeepers once used to show two quarreling sides one shared truth. [132] It had shattered forty years ago, the elders said, the night the village's boats were crushed and the floe-riders, begged for rescue, sent back nothing. [157] Marah had been born that same night, and the shadows had come with her. [171]

Tonight the ice had turned the ledger: a floe-shelf split beneath the riders' winter camp, and eleven survivors clung to a drifting plate bound for the grind-line below the cliff. [201] In the lantern room, the council argued about boats. [210] "Forty years of silence," Councilor Breck said, "and now they want our boats." [223] Marah stood among them feeling the shape of their fear, and it was her private grief that empathy came to her so easily and did so little. [250] The shadows under her skin moved with other people's feelings, mapping them perfectly, and a map is not a road. [270] Tonight she meant to map empathy onto action. [278]

While Breck marshaled his grudge, she slipped out and ran down to Halloran's vintage store, wedged between the boatworks and the sea stairs. [301] Mirrors crowded its shelves, salvaged from wrecks and estate sales, and old Halloran let her in without a word. [320] The assembly hall where the ball broke had long since been stripped, and its mirrors had ended up here. [339] Mirrors remember, her grandmother had told her. [346] Marah pressed the fragment to the first mirror and caught a glimpse of the shattering: the orb falling, light bursting, a shard spinning toward the glass. [372] The fragment in her hand warmed and drank the image. [382] Each mirror held one glimpse of that night, one angle, one lost shard; she moved along the shelves, pressing and gathering, her palms burning. [406] In a shipwrecked cheval glass she glimpsed what the elders' story had left out: a line of lanterns on the far ice, burning through the whiteout while the hall argued. [436] The riders had answered after all; the storm had simply swallowed the light of their reply. [452] When the last mirror gave up its glimpse, the fragment in her hand hung heavy as a fist. [470] Glass only remembers its shape, her grandmother had also said, in the weighted breath before a reply. [487]

She ran back up the sea stairs. [494] In the lantern room the argument had gone quiet and ugly. [505] Old Vess, chair of the council, looked around the table and asked the only question that mattered: "Do we launch the boats, yes or no?" [530] Silence gathered, and every person at the table drew the weighted breath that comes before an answer, the breath in which the real reply is still molten. [557] Marah stepped into it. [561] She held up the fragment, shadows streaming off her wrists like smoke, and the glimpses poured out of it: every mirror's memory of the shattering, and among them the lanterns crossing the ice, unseen for forty years. [598] In the held breath the glimpses found each other's edges and clicked. [610] The crystal ball reconstituted itself in her hands, whole and cold and turning the lantern light into daylight. [628] Inside it the council saw the floe as it was now: eleven riders, two of them children, packed against a dwindling plate of ice. [652] Beneath that, layered like sediment, ran the lanterns of forty years ago, burning toward a shore that never saw them. [672] Breck let his breath out. [677] "Boats," he said, and it did not sound like losing. [687]

They went down through the orchard in the dark, carrying lights, while the lighthouse turned above them. [704] Marah walked last with the ball under her arm, feeling the council's courage move through her shadows like weather. [723] Anomaly, they had called her all her life; tonight the anomaly had been the courageous one, and the difference had been a matter of breath. [748] A map, she thought, becomes a road the moment someone walks it. [760] Behind her, in the dark of the vintage store, the empty mirrors waited to remember something kinder. [777]
