Between kings, the palace of alabaster stairways and brass lenses kept only one law: heaven must be cloaked. [18] So gray linen hung over the great mosaic of stars in the throne hall, because no omen could be read for a throne that sat empty. [44] King Marduk-apla had died at dusk two evenings past; his son would be crowned at dawn. [60] Between those regimes, Iddin the astronomer remained, as frugal a man as Babylon had ever ignored. [76] It was a Babylon no tablet described: gardens of hanging glass, corridors of polished brass, a throne hall roofed in hammered silver sky. [99] His account tablets showed nine years without a single purchase for himself: he mended sandals with scraps of star-chart, rationed lamp oil by the drop, and ate dates and salt. [129] The palace called him a miser, and he never corrected them, because the truth was larger and more private: he had simply stopped replacing anything, since nothing of hers could be replaced. [161] Aya had been a scribe of plans, a woman who thought in schematic representations: the garden as a grid, the loom as a grid, the sky as a grid, and she had died in the ninth spring. [198] She left him one thing she had made herself: a fired clay tile, four cells by four, every cell stamped with a number. [221] Every row, every column, both long diagonals summed to thirty-four. [231] The courtiers had called it a child's game; Iddin had called it a magic square tile, and for nine years he had carried it without understanding it. [258] On this last night of the interregnum, with the guards withdrawn to the coronation tents and no king existing to forbid him, he climbed the hall's scaffold alone. [286] The mosaic beneath the shroud was Aya's masterwork, a schematic of heaven: sixteen hundred star-cells, each a hammered silver point, arranged in the grid her mind had lived in. [315] He lifted a corner of the linen, set the tile against the sky, and began, empirically, the way he had measured everything for forty years. [340] Cell by cell, the tile's numbers chose stars. [348] Sixteen above three, two beside thirteen; he inked the selections onto wax, joined them in row order, and stopped breathing. [368] The joined points drew cuneiform, clean as her handwriting: her devotion, encrypted nine years in arithmetic, reading I AM YOUR CONSTANT. [389] Thirty-four, the sum every line returned to no matter how the square was turned. [403] On the tile's underside, small as an apology, a last instruction waited: when you find it, answer aloud, and the covenant holds across whatever comes. [428] A covenant with the unknown: it was exactly the sort of clause he had spent a lifetime striking from contracts. [448] He had always maintained that only the measurable was real, and here the measurement itself, the arithmetic, the grid, delivered the one thing he had declared unmeasurable. [475] It was empirically ironic, and he laughed once, quietly, the sound swallowed by linen. [489] His frugality, too, stood revealed as devotion: had he ever bought a new tile, replaced her cracked pens, let the garden bench weather past use, the evidence would have been lost. [520] The miser had been an archive all along. [528] At the dark turning of the night he took the hem of the great shroud in both hands and drew it back, an uncloaking no living king had witnessed. [557] Silver stars emerged from gray wool the way the first planets emerge from dusk, one patience at a time. [576] In the exposed grid her message lay hidden among the stars, legible only to someone who both loved a scribe and trusted arithmetic. [599] Her instruction gave the night its purpose, and he meant to keep it before the new reign began. [617] He spoke his answer to the uncloaked sky, to the unknown on the far side of every star-chart he would ever draw. [639] "I kept the sum," he said. [645] "Every line still comes to you." [651] Something in the hall settled, the way dust settles when a door is finally closed with gentleness; he chose to call that the covenant sealing, and he was, for once, content not to verify it. [686] He did not re-cloak the sky. [692] Let the new king inherit heaven already open, already carrying a love letter in plain silver, already holding one covenant no regime could dissolve. [716] He climbed down, folded the gray linen into a careful square, and sat on the steps with the tile warm in his pocket. [739] A dusky harmony still held the western windows when the first light of dawn touched the eastern ones, and it seemed fitting that Aya had been named for that hour. [769] Iddin unrolled his frugal breakfast, one date, one pinch of salt, and watched the light cross her stars. [787] The covenant held; he felt no need to measure it. [797]
