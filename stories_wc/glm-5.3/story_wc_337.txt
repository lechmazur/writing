The prism sanctuary stood above the harbor, its walls of cut glass so thin that starlight passed through and broke into threads. [22] Maren worked at the altar by that light, mending masks. [32] Every man the sea had taken left a carved storm-mask behind, and every mask came ashore split. [49] The village held that a drowned fisherman could not settle until his mask was whole again, so the broken pieces came to her. [72] She was the restorer; she had been for forty years; her hands knew splint, gesso, and paint better than they knew anything living. [95] Tonight only one mask remained, halved clean along the jaw: her father's. [107] Glue would not close such a wound. [114] It needed thread drawn from the old net. [122]
The net lay coiled in the crypt beneath the altar, centuries old and heavy as wet rope, knotted by drowning men. [143] Custom said a sinking man ties one knot to hold his last secret from the water, and the knot keeps it until someone exhumes the words. [169] There was a method, and the method had rules. [178] The knot must be worked loose with a tapestry needle, blunt-tipped so it cuts nothing, long-eyed so it can carry what it finds. [201] And it will only open across the sharing of a secret: one true confession, spoken while the hands unpick. [220] A knot hides from a silent room. [227]
So Maren knelt with the net across her knees and the needle in her fingers, and the words rose in her like brine. [250] She began the way she always began. [257] "I am not afraid of the dark," she said, easing the first loop. [270] That was not the secret; that was the tuning of the voice. [282] The knot gave, and a man's last word left it, and the sanctuary grew quieter by exactly one voice. [301]
This was the strangeness of the place: it accumulated silence. [311] Each loosed knot added its hush to the hush already there, thread over thread, until quiet hung in the glass the way weave hangs in cloth, an interlaced hush that starlight caught and carried. [345] She could hear the prisms working in it, a ringing below sound. [357]
She had loosed eleven knots in eleven nights. [365] Tonight's knot was the twelfth, the master's knot, her father's, tied the night the Merrow foundered on the reef with nineteen men aboard. [388] Her needle found the loop and hesitated. [395] She had restored eighteen masks to buy the right to this one. [407]
Now the confession. [410] "The beacon went dark that night because of me," she said. [421] "I was thirteen." [424] "The oil was low, and I siphoned a cupful for my workshop lamp, because I wanted to carve masks by night like the old restorers, and I told myself one cupful meant nothing." [457] The knot tightened, testing her. [462] "The flame guttered at the hour the fleet came home," she said, working the needle under the strand, "and the Merrow read the dark as a straight channel." [490] "Nineteen men." [492] "My father among them." [496] Starlight bent through the prisms and lay along the net in threads of violet and cold. [512] "I never said it," she said. [518] "Not to the priest, not to the widows, not to the sea." [530] "I let the sea take the blame, and I let it take me in when the broken masks began washing ashore, and I have spent my whole life paying a debt no one ever billed me for, because unbilled debts are the ones that keep you awake." [577]
The knot gave. [580] Not slowly, the way the others had, but all at once, and her father's last word left the fiber like a breath: her name, only her name, spoken without anger. [610] The silence that followed was the largest the sanctuary had ever held, a completed weave, warp and weft of hush pressing down through the glass. [635] Maren threaded the freed line through the needle's long eye and drew the two halves of the mask together, stitching the jaw, the cheek, the brow, binding each splinter with a fisherman's knot turned gentle. [670]
Below the floor, where the crypt breathed cold, she felt them settle. [682] Nineteen small groundings, like anchors finding bottom; the restless ones letting go of the surface at last. [699] That had always been the reason, though she had never dared say it plainly: to ground the restless spirits, not to punish herself. [722]
The prisms dimmed toward dawn, and the hush stayed. [731] She set the finished mask on the altar, whole at last, and it caught the last starlight the way a face catches it. [754] Maren stayed awake beside it, clear-eyed and unquiet, listening to the enormous accumulated silence, and inside that silence, small but hers, one secret gone. [778] She found she missed it already. [784]
