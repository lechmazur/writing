The greenhouse city of Verdessa learned to whisper after the oasis dried. [12] Beneath its thousand sprawling glass corridors, where ferns pressed against panes dulled by the toxic amber sky, people traded pleasantries like water tickets, and pleasantries never wilted anything. [40]

Marn was a gilded-leaf calligrapher, and gilded leaf wilts near dishonesty, which made her trade a quiet catastrophe. [58] Her letters could carry only truth, so after the springs failed, neighbors stopped climbing to her shop in the old postal annex. [80] Nobody could afford candor anymore; a wilting leaf above a dictated letter was evidence. [94] Last spring a bride had dictated three vows, the leaf browned at every one, the wedding was canceled, and Marn was blamed twice. [117]

She kept one heirloom on the counter: a zinc-etched postal plate fragment, salvaged by her mother from the presses that had printed the oasis speaking-slips, the small notes strangers left floating on the water for anyone to read. [155] When the oasis dried, the slips were declared lost with the water itself, gone wherever lost conversations go. [173] Her mother, a sorter to her last day, had called that a lie, then gone silent in a way Marn could not transcribe. [196] Finding where lost conversations go had become her only unpaid commission, the question her mother's silence left behind. [214]

The answer appeared the night a floorboard in the sorting room split. [226] Beneath it lay a shredded treasure map, hundreds of ribbons, deliberate as confetti. [239] She spent nine evenings piecing it together with tweezers and wax until the ribbons resolved into the city understructure, the dried oasis basin, and one blank gap at the center. [269] The gap matched, exactly, the zinc-etched fragment: the registry stamp of the Dead Slip Vault. [284] Her mother had shredded the map but kept the key. [294]

Marn pinned a fresh gilded leaf inside her collar and went down through the fern halls, past mist condensers sweating in the sulfur light, into the undercity. [321] At the basin checkpoint, a warden in a sulfur mask said the vault had flooded and been emptied years ago. [341] Against her throat, the leaf curled. [347] She thanked him for his honesty and walked on across the cracked silt, certain now that something remained. [365]

The air below the glass smelled of old water and paper, the vanished oasis preserved like a pressed flower. [384] The vault door still bore the postal seal. [392] Inside, crates climbed into the dark: every speaking-slip ever fished from the water, catalogued by date, tens of thousands of confessions, jokes, apologies, and unsigned love. [418] This was where lost conversations had gone: not into the air, but into boxes, waiting. [433]

She found her mother's registry year and searched by hand until her fingers turned gray. [448] One slip, in a sorter's careful print. [455] She reread it once, and then she reread it until she could say it without the paper. [472]

"Marn. [473] The map under the floor is shredded because I was afraid of what you would find down here, which was only me. [495] I liked the water because it made strangers honest, and I never managed that face to face. [512] Come anyway. [514] Be honest in small moments; it is all anyone ever remembers. [525]"

The gilded leaf at her throat had never once wilted inside the vault, and she understood why: a room of ten thousand candors leaves no room for lies. [553]

She came up carrying two crates, and the story moved through Verdessa faster than any poison wind. [570] Within a month the annex counter had become a counter of lost conversations: anyone could come, read a stranger's slip, and write a true one in gold. [597] The leaves wilted sometimes, and people learned to say it again, and say it true. [612]

Above the glass, the sky stayed poison; below it, the city spoke carefully, then plainly, then well. [629] On quiet nights Marn reread her mother's slip, set it among the others, and listened to a greenhouse full of small honest things growing. [653]
