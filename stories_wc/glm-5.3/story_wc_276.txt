On the night the patterns break themselves, I take my rail spike down to the sound wave garden and fish. [20] The garden does not grow flowers. [26] It grows waves in long terraced rows, each one humming a different note, and fishers like me wade between them with iron hooks, because iron remembers a tune. [54] My spike came off the dead railway that used to run behind the hedges, and it still holds the low moan of trains in its rust. [80] I am called cool-headed, and it is true. [88] Fear reaches me only as temperature, grief only as pitch; I transliterate whatever I feel into notes I can use. [108] Tonight the sky unfastens. [112] The constellations sleepwalk. [115] That is what the breaking of patterns means here: the Bear forgets being a bear, the Hunter forgets his belt, and the stars come down the dark like drowsy animals, wading into the garden to drink the notes. [153] They trail zigzag wakes across the rows, crooked as cracks, because broken patterns cannot walk straight. [169] Other fishers chase them with nets and bells. [177] I am here for one catch only: the echo of the goddess. [189] She sang the first lullaby, the one that tucked the constellations into their shapes and kept them there. [207] Then, one breaking-night long ago, her own pattern broke itself first, and she scattered, and the world has slept badly ever since. [229] They say her last note sank into this garden and hid inside a silence. [243] Every note in the garden is loud, so the silence is easy to find: a row of waves with their mouths shut, black water making no sound at all. [272] I wade to it and stand at its edge, and the quiet there has teeth. [287] Nets cannot lift a silence. [292] Hooks cannot hold one. [296] The old fishers taught me the only method: you catch a silence by voicing it, by giving its emptiness a throat. [317] So I begin to hum, low and rough, the way you would hum to a dangerous child. [334] The silence stirs. [337] I hum the rail spike's rust-moan into it, the old train-grief, and the silence takes the sound the way dark takes a lamp. [360] Somewhere under the hedges, a zigzag of drowned starlight begins to move. [372] It is her broken constellation, sleepwalking after all these years, walking the crack of the line where she tore. [391] The stars of her come on crooked, each one wet, each one remembering a fraction of the song. [409] My heart wants to bolt, but I am cool-headed; I transliterate the fear into a steady low note and keep humming. [430] The zigzag walks straight for my silence, because silences are the only beds a broken goddess can lie down in. [450] When her starlight touches the water, the silence finally answers me, and the answer is not a note. [468] It is a feeling, huge and untranslatable, loneliness the size of a sky. [481] It floods me, and any other fisher would drown in it. [492] But I transliterate as fast as it pours: I turn her loneliness into a drone, her love into a shimmer, her breaking into three descending tones, and I feed every one of them through the rail spike and down into the black row. [535] The spike takes it all without bending. [542] That is the trick of iron. [547] That is why we fish with railway bones. [554] The silence fills up with voiced feeling until it can hold no more, and then it opens. [571] Up rises the echo, the goddess's last note, wearing her loneliness like a pelt. [585] It does not want a net. [591] It circles me, feral, testing. [596] I do not grab. [600] I keep humming. [603] I hold the spike low in the water like a mother's arm, and the echo rubs against the rust, and the rust hums back her own lost lullaby, the one she sang to the constellations before she broke. [641] Then it settles into the crook of the iron, gentle as anything with teeth can be. [657] I lift, slow, slow, and the garden goes quiet around us, every loud row listening. [672] On the bank, her broken constellation gathers itself along a zigzag, star by star, trying the old shapes on. [691] The echo sings, and the stars lie down in it. [701] I will not keep her. [706] A catch like this is only borrowed. [713] I carry the spike to the tallest terrace, where the waves are grown for sky-songs, and I raise the echo up so the whole dark can hear the first lullaby again, feral and low, a song with claws that holds you anyway. [755] The sleepwalking constellations lift their heads. [761] One by one they walk back into their patterns, tucking themselves in. [773] And I stay till dawn, humming the silence empty again, because every wild thing deserves someone cool-headed standing guard while it sleeps. [795]
