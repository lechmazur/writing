Rain had never trusted the desert, yet it came anyway, drumming the fortress of Durn hollow and gray. [18] In the kitchen, Imra the chef stirred lentils and tasted what the lentils remembered: a farmer's burnt hands, a market at dawn, a debt unpaid. [43] That was her gift and her tax: every meal carried its history, and she could not spit it out. [62] One memory she could not chew. [68] Nine years ago, during the siege, she had thinned the soup and lied about the pot count, and a boy named Seff had never woken. [93] The memory sat in her like a fishbone, rough at both ends. [105] She wanted it sanded. [109]

The old cistern keeper had told her how: beneath the dunes lay a coral pavilion locked in ice, a cellar where the vanished sea kept its remorse, and remorse kept there was contagious through the skin. [145] Whoever subsumed it, took it whole into the body like stock takes bone, would walk out smooth. [162] No one had returned to confirm this. [169] She did not believe legends. [174] She believed pools. [177]

After rain, the fortress courtyard filled with tide pools, and Imra analyzed their formations the way other cooks read dough: ring over ring, sediment sorting itself, salt blooming at the rims in patterns too regular for chance. [214] She tasted each pool. [218] They tasted of deep water, of grief older than the fortress stones, and their rings turned slowly like a dial, and the dial pointed down. [243] For seven rainy seasons she had mapped them, and the map agreed with her recurring dream, the one she had never spoken aloud to anyone: a stair of coral, a breath of cold, a door. [278]

Tonight the dream was closing. [283] She felt it the way one feels a sentence ending. [293] The rain stopped mid-thought, the clouds split, and the eastern dunes, weighed and rinsed, slid open like a lid. [312] Imra tied her apron, took her tasting spoon, and went down. [323] The stair was real. [327] Coral columns rose black and jointed, and between them lay ice, thick as pantry frost, and inside the ice stood the pavilion, patient as a preserved thing. [354]

Remorse hung there in visible layers, like sediment, like jars on a shelf. [367] She put her bare hand on the rail. [375] It came through her palm at once, other people's remorse, a stranger's letter unsent, a door shut on a mother, a rope untied too late, a thousand regrets that were not hers and one that was. [411] Her knees buckled. [414] Contagion, the cistern keeper had warned, and she had thought he meant danger. [427] He had meant instruction. [431] You could not sand a single edge. [438] Regret was one long board, and everyone's grain ran through it. [449]

She was a chef. [453] She knew what to do with bitter things: not to throw them out but to subsume them, simmer them until they gave up their thickness, until grief became flavor and flavor became nourishment. [486] So she did not pull her hand away. [494] She gripped the rail with both hands, set her teeth, and drank the pavilion down. [509] The ice sighed. [512] Layer after layer let go, sailors' remorse, the sea's own remorse for leaving, mothers', soldiers', hers, and she took them in as a stockpot takes the whole market, bones, salt, weeds, and all. [545] She tasted Seff's name one last time, fully, without flinching. [555] It did not vanish. [559] It went smooth. [562] That was the mercy: not removal, but sandpaper, grain still visible under the palm, splinters gone. [578]

Above her the dream finished closing, and the last unspoken word of it dissolved on her tongue like sugar. [597] Imra climbed into a rinsed and ringing dawn. [605] The dunes had already closed behind her, tidy as a bed. [616] In the courtyard the pools were drying in new formations she did not need to read: wide rings, no dial, pointing nowhere, finished. [639]

By noon she was back at the stove, because the fortress still ate. [652] The lentils tasted this time of rain on stone and of the gardener humming, small clean memories. [669] She seasoned, she tasted, and under everything ran a smooth dark bass note that had no single owner. [687] Her hands were steady. [691] There were cracks in her, yes, running rim to rim like the salt rings on the courtyard floor, but they held water now instead of grief. [717] Sturdy, she thought, and brittle, and honest. [724] She would cook until the fortress was full, and if anyone asked what the soup carried, she would tell the truth: everyone's regret, well simmered, fit to eat. [752]
