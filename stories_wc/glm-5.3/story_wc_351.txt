On the night the canyon finally agreed to speak, Mara Voss climbed down its moonlit throat with a broken periscope mirror strapped to her back. [25] Nine weeks into the rewiring, her hands had not stopped shaking, and she had learned to let them. [43] The tremor was not fear; it was her own brain being remade through synaptic plasticity, the deliberate loosening and re-knitting of synapses that could hold two truths at once. [72] She needed that doubled mind because the memories buried below were entangled, and no ordinary head could carry them. [91] The Republic called them dreams to make the burying easier, but a dream is only a memory with its eyes open, and entangled memories answer each other: wake one, and its twin changes wherever it sleeps. [127] Eleven years ago, during the Departure, a million citizens shed their bodies for the white lattice, and the Republic, unable to upload anything as soft as a dream, poured the surplus into the canyon's vitreous walls. [163] Mara had engineered the pouring helix herself, the great spiral channel that let the dreams settle in layers, and she had signed the oath that required her to surrender one dream of her own, unchosen and unnamed. [200] She had never been told which one they took. [209]

People called her helix-minded, and they meant it as an insult: she thought in paired spirals, every argument braided with its counter-argument, every design wound around the absence it answered. [239] The insult had become her tool. [245] The mirror had belonged to her grandmother, who had steered the submarine Delia before the seas went dry, and its silver face was split by a hairline fracture into two overlapping halves. [277] A broken periscope mirror shows every object twice, slightly askew, and that doubleness was precisely what the entangled memories required. [297] Her plan was simple to state and absurd to attempt: modify the fracture into a feature. [313] She would re-cut the halves and set them at a helical offset of eleven degrees, so that moonlight entering one half would leave the other carrying a pair of memories instead of one. [346]

At the canyon floor, moonlight lay across the glassy strata like a skin of milk, and the buried dreams shivered under it, faintly luminous, faintly restless. [372] She worked the way she always worked, chaotically precise: shims scattered like fallen leaves, taps that seemed arbitrary, each one landing exactly where the mathematics demanded. [398] She looked like a woman losing a fight with a mirror; in truth, every grain of dust was plotted. [417] The diamond file sang against the glass; the fracture widened into a clean seam; the two halves twisted against each other with a click she felt in her teeth. [446] The mirror had become a periscope again, but it no longer looked over water; it looked into the settled sleep of a vanished people. [470] She pressed her eye to the eyepiece, and the moon poured through her, and her freshly plastic synapses opened like hands. [491]

The first dream rose green and cold: her grandmother, young, at the Delia's wheel, ice water overhead and whales moving through it like slow thunder. [516] And because the memories were entangled, the dream's twin flared across the canyon, and Mara understood, with a quaking that started in her knees, whose dream it was. [544] Her surrendered dream was braided with her grandmother's: they had been poured in the same hour, paired by the helix she herself had built. [568] She could not read her own dream, sealed as it was, but she could infer it from its twin, the way astronomers infer a hidden star by the wobble of its neighbor. [600] It was a dream of the same water: she had dreamed of diving where her grandmother had steered, and she had never missed it, because missing requires knowing. [628] That was when the transformation finished itself: something in her cortex folded, and she was suddenly double, holding grief and wonder in the same thought without dropping either. [656]

The canyon seemed to feel it too; the strata hummed, and a fine rain of glass dust fell through the moonlight like snow inside a bell. [682] When the trembling eased, she began, methodically, to dig. [691] She would excavate the grandmother's dream first, then the child who had dreamed of flight, then the ten thousand others layered like seasons in the walls. [717] She would catalogue each pair, trace each entanglement, and carry what she could not return. [732] There was a word for what she had become, and she said it aloud to the moonlit water of glass: archaeologist of abandoned dreams. [756] The title carried no rank, but the mirror was already showing her the next stratum down, and her double mind, still quaking, was wide enough to hold it. [784]
