At twilight the arcade went cobalt, glass roof drinking the last clean light, shutters down along both walls. [18] This was the disputed territory's one neutral artery, and Anton was its mailman. [31] Neither republic claimed him; both needed him. [38] The day's lone mail drop happened at the brass slot beneath the broken clock, once every evening, no exceptions. [57] Letters in, letters out, in a hush so brittle the whole street seemed about to crack. [73]

Anton was respectfully rebellious. [77] He bowed to the drone operators' booths at either end of the arcade, stamped every form in triplicate, and recited postal treaties at anyone who questioned his sack. [105] Then he crossed the line anyway, because mail was older than the border and would outlast it. [122] The drones owned the sky in between. [129] They hung along the boundary like patient wasps, filming every crossing, and thunder made them fall, which was why both armies had outlawed weather. [153] Rain was rationed; storms were confiscated at checkpoints and poured down drains. [165]

Old Mrs. Vale had disobeyed magnificently. [171] On the night the valley was halved, she caught the last true thunderstorm in a wine bottle, corked it, and told no one. [194] At tonight's drop she pressed it into his sack between two bundles of bills. [208] A customs slip was already tied on, in her copperplate: one weather, personal effects. [222] Deliver it, she whispered, to where broken promises heal. [231] Anton did not argue. [235]

He had spent eleven years hunting that address, by linking absences. [246] His sack filled with the undeliverable: letters to streets renamed twice, to villages erased from both maps at the partition. [266] One watcher, a girl named Mira, came daily to the slot: her mother had crossed the line for work, sworn to write weekly, and sent one postcard stamped from a town that soon vanished from every map. [303] Anton had fostered her since the silence began, rationing her mother's postcard the way the skies rationed rain. [321] He kept every returned envelope. [326] Spread across the arcade floor after closing, they made a constellation of gone people, and its lines pointed at the same blank. [348] Each republic's map ended a little shy of the river's third bend, leaving an unpainted square where neither flag was sewn. [369] The absences clustered there: the erased villages, the vanished postcard town, all lay inside it, and no drone flew over it, because a place that did not exist could not be disputed. [401] Somewhere in that blank, the promises the partition had swallowed might still be kept. [415]

Tonight, the operators told him, the treaty would be signed at midnight and the line sealed forever. [432] This was the last drop. [437] Anton tucked Mira's forty-one letters, written to no address, inside his coat. [449] At the northern booth he presented the customs slip with both hands, as etiquette required. [464] One weather, the sergeant read, personal effects, and, finding no clause against it, waved him through. [480] Halfway across the drone border, the drones found him, lenses whirring like startled moths. [494] Anton stopped, uncorked the bottle, and apologized to the sky for the delay. [507] The storm came out as a smell first, then a growl, then rain that had waited eleven years. [525] Static crawled up the boundary; the drones shivered and dropped into the wet grass, filming clouds. [541] Anton walked on through weather he was technically smuggling, rain flattening his cap, the dead letters dry as a reliquary inside his coat. [564]

Past the third bend, the blank square resolved into roofs. [574] Salka, the envelopes called it. [579] Its people came out slowly, the way the displaced do, checking his face for a flag. [595] He was only a mailman. [600] He delivered eleven years of undeliverable mail under the first thunder either side had heard since the partition, and the queue formed in silence, each person holding a mended absence. [630] Near the end stood a woman with Mira's chin, gripping nine years of letters written weekly and never sent, because mail from a place that does not exist cannot be posted. [661] Her promises had all been written; only the map had broken them. [673] Anton gave her Mira's forty-one letters and took her arm. [683]

The rain would hold until dawn, long enough to walk home, long enough for the drones to stay blind, long enough for the arcade's hush to be broken by one child's voice. [715] He had never once broken a rule he could not quote. [726] He was simply carrying the mail, and the sky, home. [736]
