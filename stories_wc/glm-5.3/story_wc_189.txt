Wren's sentence broke off halfway: "Gran, I can't be." [9] Then the pause opened, wide as the grotto itself. [18] Nell let it open. [22] Forty-one years of tide ledgers had taught her that pauses, like tides, have a texture, and she meant to learn this one by heart. [46]

They sat in the sea cave she had chronicled since she was seventeen, the ancient grotto where a crack in the roof let the stars down to lie across the water. [77] Slack tide. [79] Not ebb, not flood; the pool hung at the hinge of itself. [91]

Nell was a tidal-pool chronicler, warmly observant by trade and by temperament, and her eyes paid the small things the courtesy of going to them first. [117] A hermit crab had stalled mid-paddle, antennae tasting the stillness. [127] Two anemones were folding shut, petals closing like a decision made gently. [139] The star reflections lay on the water without a tremor. [149]

From her coat she took the jade cicada and set it on her knee. [163] She had found it here at seventeen, wedged in a rock shelf far above any tide line, a land creature's token carried down to the sea by a hand she would never know. [196] A museum curator had once told her the old custom: a jade cicada laid on the tongue of the dead, a promise that the long dark was only a waiting. [226] The living insect did it too: seventeen years underground, then one summer of song. [240] Seventeen, like her, that first year. [246]

The difficult thing Wren was not saying filled the grotto like held breath: the city job, the leaving, the chronicle nobody wanted. [268] Nell could not look at it head-on. [275] She never had. [278] Her whole method, if forty-one years of ledgers could be called a method, was tangential: you did not stare at time, you watched what it did at the edges of things. [309]

So she watched the crab resume its paddling, having decided the stillness was over. [323] She watched the star reflections begin, very faintly, to drift; the tide had turned, flood was starting, and the pause would end whether anyone consented. [348]

By the light of what the pool was doing, she began to foresee. [361] Not prophecy; texture. [364] She foresaw that Wren would go, which had been true for a year already, the way molting is true before the shell splits. [387] She foresaw the empty cottage, the ledgers continuing anyway in her own slow hand, and the day another hand would find them, the way she had once found the cicada: a token left for a future its maker could not meet. [428] She foresaw Wren at a city window at thirty, smelling rockweed in rain and not knowing why. [445] She foresaw a child of Wren's, restless, brought here some summer, bending over the pool with the exact tilt of head that ran in the family. [471] None of it arrived as pictures; it came the way tide knowledge always came, sideways, in the corner of the eye. [492]

She had written in a ledger margin once: every transition is a creature that is neither before nor after. [511] The shore between tides is neither sea nor land and holds more life than either. [526] The pause in a sentence is not speech or silence. [536] The cicada's emergence is not ending or beginning but the door between them. [549] That was what she had come hunting at seventeen, and what had kept her here: not to stop time, only to learn its texture, and this pause, she thought, had been a fine specimen. [583]

The water stirred against the rock; the flood had the matter now. [595] Wren was watching her with wet eyes, waiting for a verdict. [606] Nell closed her hand around the cicada, warm from her knee, and smiled. [619]

"Then go," she said. [623] "The tide doesn't ask the shore's permission. [630] Leave the ledgers where the light can find them, and I'll keep the pool, and the pool will keep the time." [651]

She pressed the jade cicada into her granddaughter's palm and folded the fingers shut over it. [667] "It waited seventeen years in the dark to sing," she said. [678] "So have you." [681]

Wren's breath went out of her like a tide. [690] Above the grotto's broken roof the stars went on keeping their own long ledger, and the water rose to meet them. [711]
