The floating observatory hung three kilometers above the drowned coast, its gravity arrays humming like a struck bell that would not finish ringing. [23] Mara Voss had used the government settlement to buy the last research berth, money paid for a wedding that ended eleven minutes after it began. [48] The drone had not cared that she was the bride. [58] Of two hundred guests, she alone kept breathing under the collapsed canopy, her white dress drinking blood that was mostly not hers. [80]

On her desk sat the only object she had carried out: a fist-sized geode, gray and dull as a potato, her groom's wedding gift, given that morning with the words that the best of it was sealed where no one could spend it. [123] Seven years later, she had made it her calibration mass, because grief, unlike crystal, never settles into a stable lattice. [143]

Her work was officially fringe: mapping where unused potential pools. [153] Every life cut short is an unspent budget of futures, she wrote in the proposal no committee approved, and conservation laws do not forgive debt. [178] Somewhere, she believed, all that interrupted possibility had to drain and pool, like water finding the low ground beneath a broken dam. [200]

The hunt required the arrays at full burn, and at full burn the circuit breakers tripped. [216] So she worked in the gaps: while circuit breakers cooled in their housings, the noise floor sank so low that the universe forgot to pretend it was smooth. [244] Her method was perturbation theory, the oldest trick in physics: nudge the world slightly and watch what pushes back. [263] She would fire a gram of engineered gravity into the dark, a ripple meant for nothing, and log every flicker that answered. [285] Empty vacuum answered the way textbooks promised, small and boring. [295] But under the observatory, three hundred meters down and drifting, something answered wrong: the nudges bent, slowed, and gathered, as if the dark there were a basin. [322] It drifted beneath them wherever the observatory moved, as if the pool preferred to be hunted. [338]

For six cooling cycles she mapped the anomaly, and the shape that emerged was a shallow bowl of stalled possibility, intangibly tangible: the gravimeters weighed it, yet when she reached toward the place they swore it occupied, her hands closed on nothing at all. [382] It registered on every instrument and on none of her senses, a mass no finger could find, a pressure no palm could feel. [405] Two hundred unspent lives, she calculated, plus the marriage, plus the children never conceived, plus every ordinary Tuesday the dead had coming. [427] The arithmetic matched the mass. [432] She had expected triumph and instead felt something colder and brighter, a bleak exhilaration like standing on a rooftop in a storm, thrilled by the lightning that could kill her. [462] Proof is not reunion. [466]

On the seventh cycle she did what the safety protocols existed to prevent: she killed the filters. [483] The arrays could feed her senses directly through the interface shunt, unmediated, and the awakening, when it came, was unfiltered: not data but perception, the pool pouring into her awareness whole. [514] She saw the wedding still running, eleven minutes long and eternal, two hundred guests mid-laugh under a canopy of latent light, the groom turning toward her with the toast unfinished in his hand. [547] She saw the unlived children learning to walk across possibilities that would never harden into days. [563] It was the happiest thing she had ever witnessed, and it was made entirely of loss, and she understood then that those were not two feelings. [589] The breakers finished cooling; the arrays recharged; the filters slammed home; and the vision folded shut like a wound closing clean. [610]

Mara sat alone on the instrument floor, laughing the way people laugh when the grief finally finds a shape it can be held in. [634] Then she took the geode to the bench and, for the first time in seven years, cracked it open with a single clean blow. [658] Inside, a cathedral of violet crystal, every surface grown in secret and never once touched by light. [675] He had been right about sealed things. [682] She set it beside the gravimeter readout and began recharging the arrays, because one thing in the pool had answered differently from the rest, one signature wearing her own face. [712] The Mara who would have married him was in there too, laughing and whole, an unused self pooling with the rest. [733] The hunt was not over; it had finally found its first door. [745] While the circuit breakers cooled, humming their small patient song, she plotted the next nudge, exhilarated and bereft in the same breath, unable to tell which of the two Maras was the survivor. [778]
