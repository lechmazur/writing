You could hear the turbines before you saw the farmhouse, a low churn that never ended, and after the Happiness Mandate that was the whole point. [26] Silence, the Bureau said, was the sound of sadness, and sadness had been ruled a public hazard. [43] So the state built the turbines, a thousand giant fans to blow an engineered joy across the valleys. [61]

I came to the Pell farm on a Tuesday, blank-faced as always, which was both my crime and my trade. [81] I had been a mime before the mandate; my face learned stillness and never unlearned it. [97] Inspectors read blankness as grief and grief as treason, so I traveled on a performer's license that listed me as entertainment. [118] In a way it was true. [124]

Mrs. Pell met me at the door with her jaw set in the required smile, the kind that never reached anything. [145] Her husband had been dead eleven months, which meant eleven months of mandated brightness she could no longer afford to fake. [166] She had heard, the way the grieving hear everything, that I performed inside farmhouses and that afterward people carried their dead a little lighter. [190] That was the work: not comfort, which was illegal, but lightness, which nobody had thought to outlaw. [207] I took her money first, because grief pays anything, and I have stopped pretending that shames me. [224]

Then I took out my knife and a length of ash and began to whittle, because every performance needs an opening act. [246] The method was simple: I narrated ordinary things as if they were sacred. [259] She boiled water and I gave it a voice: behold, the kettle, guardian of the last honest sound in this county. [280] She swept the floor and I pronounced it a rite: see how the broom argues with the dust, and how the dust wins. [303] In the theater of chores, tears became applause, and applause is only entertainment. [316]

The turbines helped. [319] Their churn drowned the sound of weeping, so the microphones on the poles heard only weather. [335] We timed everything to the wind. [341]

On the third visit she brought out a shoebox, and inside it her wedding bouquet, dried to paper and shorn of half its stems. [365] Her husband had carved the original stems himself, she said, from the ash tree that stood where the first turbine now stood. [387] The stems had crumbled; the flowers had held. [395] So I whittled her new ones, ash again, thin as fingers, and I narrated the carving as I worked, because the work alone would have been unbearable. [422] Witness, I said, this knife, which takes away only what the wood was pretending to be. [438] Observe the shavings, which fall like everything else this government has thinned. [450] She laughed then, wet and illegal, and the turbine blades took the blame. [463]

Here is what I have not told her. [471] My license renews each spring, and it renews because I file a report listing every farmhouse I visit, every name, every date. [493] The Bureau believes it is tracking sadness. [500] Mostly they are right. [503] Some names I write and some I smudge, and I could not tell you the rule I use, because there isn't one. [525] Mrs. Pell's name I wrote clearly. [531] Her husband had helped survey the turbine routes. [539] I do not call it justice. [545] I do not call it anything. [551]

The last night, the wind died. [557] The turbines slowed to a standstill, and the valley lost its manufactured cheer all at once. [573] Silence like that is dangerous; the microphones get hungry. [582] So we remade the hush ourselves, quietly, out of what the mandate had left us: the tick of the cooling stove, the rasp of my knife, her breathing, mine. [611] A hush is not silence; it is silence you chose, silence with hands on it. [626] In that remade hush she held the finished bouquet, dried flowers on new ash stems, old grief on new wood. [646] She said it weighed nothing now. [652]

That is the sentence I perform for myself on bad nights, dramatically, in her kitchen voice, and nothing in me answers it. [674] You should know that I feel nothing during any of this. [685] My own dead went into the ground years ago while I stood at the graveside, mandated smiling, and whatever the mandate wanted from me it got. [711] The void where the feeling was is the blank face the inspectors keep flagging, and it is also, I think, my instrument. [733] I whittle it smoother every year. [739] In spring I will file the report, renew the license, and walk the turbine roads to the next farmhouse where the kettle is waiting to be announced. [766] Someone there is holding grief wrong, and lightness, as nobody has yet outlawed, is still for sale. [783]
