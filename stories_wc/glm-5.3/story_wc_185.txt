The energy beings arrived in the spring, and the harmonic caves have not been quiet since. [16] Vell, the last ripple-mark mapkeeper, stayed when everyone else fled aboveground. [27] Her maps were made of sound; her ink was the ripples that song left in silt. [43] Every cave had a voice, and every voice had a pattern, and she had spent forty years reading them. [62] The caves had been shaped for music; every chamber was an instrument. [74] Her mother had died mapping the deep chambers, and Vell had inherited the hammer and the habit. [91]

The beings meant no harm; she was sure of that. [101] They had come burning out of some distant grief, and their pain rang through the chambers until the ice itself began to sing. [124] Now the storm of their voices widened every hour, and the cracks in the great lake room crawled toward the pillars that held the roof. [149] Her mother had taught her that every storm hides its own opposite, and Vell wanted nothing else now: to find the quiet in the storm. [174]

For this she would need the echo chamber stone, which lay in the deepest chamber like a sleeping heart. [193] The texts said it drank every echo the caves had ever made. [205] For three months it had been drinking the storm. [214] Whatever it held, it now held the beings' whole roaring grief, folded in with forty centuries of ordinary songs. [233] Echoes return to their source, her mother had said. [242] If the stone gave the storm back to itself at the right place and in the right phase, the two voices would meet and end each other. [269] The right place was the hard part. [276]

So Vell went out onto the lake with her lantern and began examining the crack patterns in the ice, the way she had once examined ripple marks in silt. [305] Cracks, she had long suspected, were ripple marks made by force instead of sound; both were handwriting. [322] Where the storm pressed hardest, the cracks ran straight and eager. [333] Where it weakened, they branched, wandered, and starved. [341] For two days she mapped them, and the map spoke plainly: every crack in the ice bent around one small circle at the lake's center, as if afraid of it. [371] There, and only there, the ice was unbroken, milk-smooth, silent under her boots. [384] The node: the still floor of the storm, where its waves crossed and canceled. [398] At that still point, if her mother's teaching held, she could conjure the storm's opposite. [413]

It took half a night of levering and cursing to haul the echo chamber stone up from the deep chambers where her mother had died, and drag it onto the lake. [444] The storm rose to meet it, delighted, terrible. [452] She set the stone in the center of the unbroken circle, knelt, and listened until she found the storm's pulse. [472] Then she raised her mother's brass ice hammer and struck. [482] The stone answered with everything. [487] Every echo of every century came out together: dripping water, wedding songs, funeral keens, children's calls, and beneath them all, inverted and immense, the storm's own voice returning home. [516] The two storms met, and where they met, they stopped. [526] What bloomed from that stopping was not a sound but a silence, a bloom of hush unfolding outward across the ice like a pale flower opening. [552] It was gently luminous, that silence, glowing the way moonlight glows through ice, and everywhere it touched, the roar simply ended. [573] Vell knelt in the middle of it, hammered into stillness, and let the quiet wash through her. [590]

Inside the fading storm, the energy beings became visible for the first time: small, tired, flickering things, no larger than sparrows. [611] They had not been screaming to break the caves; they had been screaming because no one could hear them over their own noise. [634] In the hush they settled, one by one, into the crystal of the walls, gently luminous now themselves, like lamps sunk in amber. [657] The caves kept their voices, as the old texts had promised, but kept them softly. [672]

When the settlers came back down, wary, they found the harmonic caves singing again, deeper than before, with a new low note of light threaded through every chord. [700] They found Vell asleep against the echo chamber stone, hammer still in her hand, her face carrying the particular repose of something that has been struck and struck and finally come true. [732] And pinned under a pebble beside her lay the first sheet of a new map: the ripple marks that silence leaves, which she would spend the rest of her life learning to read. [765]
