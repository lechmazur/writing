Wren was the last gilded-leaf stationer on the mountain, and she had stayed after the town went silent because paper, unlike people, cannot be abandoned. [25] Below, the streets kept their shapes but lost their sounds when the last buses groaned away. [41] What remained was the mountain, and on its shoulder a blooming labyrinth of bonsai forests planted by some patient keeper a century before. [64] Wren walked its turning paths each morning, miniature chestnuts and pines brushing her sleeves, whole woods small enough to hold an hour. [86] At the labyrinth's center stood her workshop, a glass hut where one fluorescent lamp ran off a power line no one had bothered to cut. [111] In dawn light that lamp hummed, faint as a held breath, and the hum was never twice the same. [130]

Her mother had taught her, as her grandmother taught her mother: every hour of every day has a texture, the way paper does, and the lamp's hum carries it. [159] So Wren had made it her work to collect the textures of different times, the way another woman might collect stamps or griefs. [182] Her instrument was a battered notepad, cover soft as cloth, its grid pages dense with dates and the small stubborn curves of hums charted at first light. [209] She was earnestly studious about it, standing in the cold with a pencil and a wristwatch, refusing to round a frequency to make it prettier. [234] Precision mattered, because the charts were not records. [242] They were prisms. [245] Here is what the old stationers knew and the town never asked: a charted hum, angled through a sheet of gilded leaf, will refract. [269] Sound bends in gold as light bends in water, and what lands on blank paper is time itself, split into strands. [290]

It was this craft that gave Wren her terrible and beautiful idea. [302] The town's histories had gone silent before the town did. [312] The archive burned in seventy-one, the mill's ledgers went for scrap, and the old families carried their stories away in boxes marked kitchen. [335] A place without a past has no texture, and Wren had watched the town thin and empty like paper held to a flame. [358] So she began to write histories for it, honest enough to call them what they were: invented. [375]

A fisherman who never existed hauling nets through a frost no record mentioned. [388] A schoolmistress who taught the mountain's weather to forty children. [398] Two brothers who quarreled over a pear orchard and reconciled, in Wren's version, exactly where the labyrinth now turned. [417] Each dawn she charted the hum, angled the notepad against a sheet of gold, and read her inventions into the refraction. [438] If a story was false to the hour, the strands tangled like wet thread. [452] If it was true enough, the strands lay flat, parallel as laid paper, and the gold took the words. [471] She hung the finished leaves in the bonsai branches, and by midsummer the labyrinth glittered with a town that had never quite existed and could no longer be lost. [500]

Then came the dawn when the hum changed. [508] Wren heard it before the door was open: lower, slower, a grain she had never charted, and her pencil hovered with no curve to guide it. [534] This was not the texture of the old town's hours. [544] It was the texture of the silence itself, time arriving with no one in it, and none of her histories would take. [566] The fisherman tangled. [569] The schoolmistress tangled. [572] Even the reconciled brothers knotted into a fist of light. [582]

She sat with the problem all day, the way she sat with every problem, and near dusk she understood. [601] She had been inventing the town's past, but the new time had no past yet. [616] It needed a history that had not happened, and there was no one to write it but her. [634] So she wrote the only true sentence she had: "After the town goes silent, one stationer stays, and gilds the hours so they will not blow away." [661] At the next dawn she charted the slow hum, held the notepad to the gold, and read the sentence into the refracted light. [684] The strands lay down flat and shining, one beside another, like the first lines of a new book. [702] The lamp steadied. [705]

She gilded the sentence and hung it in the tallest miniature tree, where dawn struck it and scattered gold down the turning paths. [728] It was not a rescue; she did not believe in those. [739] It was stationery: a place for time to stand. [748] Below, the town kept its silence. [754] Above, every invented history caught the light at once, and the mountain held all the town's times together, the way a page holds ink that has not decided whether to be a lie. [787] Wren turned to a fresh grid page and waited for tomorrow's hum. [799]
