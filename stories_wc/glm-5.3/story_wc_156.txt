The keeper of the sound wave garden asked his question, and in the pause before Marisol could answer, everything happened. [20] She had spent three seasons restoring the mural that curved along the garden's western wall, a painting of a garden inside the garden, its hedges cut from malachite green, its listeners rendered mid-listen. [53] In that painted garden stood another wall bearing another mural of another garden, and inside that one another, receding like rooms behind a keyhole. [77] Marisol worked the way she did everything, methodically playful: she named each paint layer like a pet, whistled major thirds against the wall to hear what the wall whistled back, and dared the pigments to misbehave. [113] She dressed like her palette, oxblood boots and a cobalt bandanna, as though she too might someday need restoring. [132] Her talisman was a hobo nickel, a buffalo coin some drifter had carved generations ago into a winking stranger's face and traded to her for a cup of paint water. [162] She burnished gold leaf with its worn edge and kept it in her pocket like a secret. [179] The keeper's question had been simple: was the restoration authentic, original, finished, certifiable, yes or no? [195]

In the pause between his question and her reply, the garden did what it had been built to do. [214] Parabolic hedges caught her absent humming and folded it back on itself, and the curved wall returned the sound harmonized and delayed, every reflected note landing like a hand on the small of her back, steering her toward the wall. [254] The reflected sound bewitched her the way reflected light bewitches water, and the mural's surface forgot how to be a surface. [275] She fell into the first painted garden and woke jade-limbed, mid-listen, a listener rendered listening. [290]

Here the acoustics ran truer than truth: rain arrived before clouds, bells before hands, every answer arriving before its question. [310] Being methodically playful had trained her for exactly this, so she did not scream; she inventoried. [326] Greens: eleven kinds. [329] Questions the painted garden could not answer: also eleven. [338] And in her painted pocket, the hobo nickel, warm as a held hand. [351]

She pressed her ear to the second wall, heard her own humming coming from inside it, stepped through, and woke again. [372] This time she was the drifter himself, sitting on a crate in 1934, carving a winking stranger's face into five cents because he could not bear to leave a thing unaltered. [403] His hands were her hands; his restlessness was her restlessness, renamed. [414] He was, she understood, painting this very mural, and painting himself out of certainty and into curiosity with every stroke. [434] She pressed the nickel into his wet plaster; it sank without a ripple, and she woke a third time. [453]

Now she stood in a garden that was neither painted nor real, where sounds hung like visible weather, and a keeper who was also her, only older, was asking a question she could not quite hear. [489] It was the same question, arriving from every layer at once. [500] Yes or no, the garden insisted; certifiable or not; authentic or counterfeit; finished or unfinished. [515] And Marisol, who had built a whole career on giving people the certainty they paid for, felt how heavy certainty becomes once you notice you are carrying it. [543] The painted listeners around her were not waiting for answers; they were waiting for better questions, and they had been waiting beautifully for decades. [567]

So she climbed out the way she had fallen, hand over hand along her own whistled thirds, each reflected note a rung. [589] The wall remembered it was a wall just before she crossed, and she arrived on the scaffold with sawdust on her knees and the taste of 1934 in her mouth. [619] The pause was still holding, wide as a held breath. [629] The keeper stood below with his clipboard, his question held aloft by the hedges, folded back and back and back. [649]

Marisol descended, took the hobo nickel from her pocket, and pressed it into his palm, closing his fingers around the winking stranger. [671] "Certainty for curiosity," she said, the only exchange she had ever trusted. [683] Then, instead of answering, she asked him what he heard when the wall gave his own voice back to him, changed, and the pause bloomed into a garden of its own. [714] Behind his eyes something stirred, a listener waking inside a listener, and the mural, unfinished forever now, gleamed. [732]
