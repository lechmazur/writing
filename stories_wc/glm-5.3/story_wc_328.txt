Mara Voss took the job at Fenn's Herbary because the creature she hunted lived beneath it. [16] Officially she was an apprentice to the herbalist; unofficially she was a cryptid researcher with three years of fieldwork and one changed brother. [39] Danny had gone missing in March and come home in June lighter by something no scale could name; he laughed in his sleep, wept at breakfast, and could not say why. [70] The whole town was full of such people now, in the season after the missing returned changed, and nobody joked about it except in whispers. [95]

The herbary sold tinctures upstairs, but the growing happened below, in an underground garden tunneled out of the root cellar: mint and feverfew in terraced beds, lamps burning like low suns. [126] Mara noticed the wrongness in her first week: dropped shears drifted down like leaves, and once a watering can hung a moment before remembering to fall. [152] Gravity in that garden was not broken, merely playful. [161] And on a shelf between the chamomile and the wall sat an old cordless phone, off its cradle, its static moving at night like breath. [186] The returned told stories about phones like that: lost in some gray country, they had found dead lines humming and confessed into the static whatever was heaviest in them. [215] A voice like juggled china had answered, they said, and they had come home lighter. [230]

In her coat pocket rode a flint arrowhead she had found by the sinkhole where Danny vanished, gray as a tooth, still sharp: her one piece of evidence, and a thing to press her thumb against when the bitterness rose. [270] She was bitter, and she knew it. [277] She had wanted Danny back broken so she could mend him herself; instead something nameless had mended him first, leaving her grief nowhere useful to go. [303] Her plan was to catch the catcher: exposure, capture, blame finally landed somewhere. [316]

On the ninth night the arrowhead slipped from her coat as she knelt at the feverfew, and old Mrs. Fenn picked it up before it finished falling. [343] "Where did you get this?" [348] Mara told the truth, because lying needed more breath than she had. [360] The sinkhole; her brother; the spring he was gone. [369] "Ah," said Mrs. Fenn. [373] "One of the angry ones, undercover as one of the curious ones." [385] She led Mara past the last beds to a hollow in the cellar wall, where a stone shelf held flint arrowheads, centuries of them, arranged like a joke being told very slowly. [417] "Every generation shoots at it once," she said. [425] "It keeps the points the way you'd keep love letters." [435]

"What is it?" Mara asked, though the word she meant was who. [447] "The Juggler," said Mrs. Fenn. [452] "It lives in the static between channels and in the soil of this garden, and what it does, mostly, is turn bitterness into a cosmic joke." [478] "Your brother confessed into a dead line out there, and the Juggler caught what he dropped." [494] "It has been playing with his burden ever since; that is why he came home changed, and lighter." [512] "And the garden?" [515] "A harmony," said Mrs. Fenn, "but an untamed one; it lightens the soil, we keep the lamps lit, and neither of us signed anything." [539] She set the arrowhead on the shelf with the others and went upstairs, leaving Mara alone with the phone. [558]

The static-laden line was loud that night, like snow on a dead channel. [571] Mara picked it up the way you pick up something that might be sleeping. [585] She meant to interrogate it, demand terms, win. [593] What came out instead was the confession: the search parties, the guilt, the relief she hated herself for feeling when Danny came back mended, the wish that his grief had been hers to carry. [627] The static received it without comment. [633] Then the hollow rang with china juggled in the dark, and the garden's gravity turned inside out. [650] Mint leaves rose in slow spirals, the arrowheads lifted and spun in glittering orbits, and Mara felt her bitterness leave her like a stone remembering how to fly. [678] That was how the confession metamorphosed you: not into something else, but into less of what you could not carry. [698] Mara laughed, once, at the punchline of herself. [706] She had come to catch the Juggler, and it had caught her instead, gently, mid-fall, the way it caught everything. [726]

When she climbed the stairs, frost had sealed the town's windows, and the stars looked juggled. [742] She walked home lighter. [746] Danny was awake, and she told him everything, and he laughed until he cried, and so did she. [764] It was a joke the universe had taken years to tell, and outside the frost held, but under it, everywhere, things were growing. [787]
