The last dictionary had been printed eleven years before Mira stopped speaking. [12] Not from grief, but economy: she had learned that words spent aloud could not be spent on paper. [30] She was the Institute's resident etymologist, a reticent scholar of sixty-one, famous for answering committees in nods. [47] Each dawn she crossed the shadowed library where the vocabulary now stood legally complete, ninety-one volumes of finished language, and she crossed it without greeting anyone, because there was no one to greet. [80] The Print Closure, the commissioners had said, would settle the language the way frost settles a field. [97] Mira suspected the frost was lying. [102]

Her private work was therefore quiet heresy: to decode the pattern of growth, to prove that words, like teeth and rivers, keep moving after the paperwork stops. [129] Her only witness was the chandelier. [135] It hung forty feet above the reading floor, a century of leaded crystal, and its lowest pendant had cracked in 1934, which was why the conservators eventually scheduled it for removal. [166] Mira had asked to keep the broken piece, one crystal from the chandelier, small as a molar, and nobody questioned her, because nobody ever questioned the silent. [193] Her grandmother, a lamp-lighter here before electrification, had told her once that the crystals were sonically reflective, that glass remembers what is said beneath it the way a bell remembers being struck. [225] Mira had spent three silent years testing that claim, and the claim held. [238]

The method demanded a suspended hush, the library at dawn before the boilers woke, before pigeons, before the street; only in that total stillness, sound held like a coin under water, could the crystal be coaxed to testify. [276] It never testified cleanly. [280] What it gave were fractured sequences, splinters of sound shed out of order: a sneeze from 1961, then a quarrel from 1889, then a page torn in fury sometime between the wars. [312] Mira did not mind; she had spent her whole life assembling sense from fragments, and broken glass, like broken sentences, still carries light. [335]

This morning she unrolled the velvet pad on the catalogue desk, laid the shard upon it, and waited for the first gray light to find its facets. [362] Warming, the crystal loosened its hoard. [368] A child's voice, thin as thread: "Aunty, is fedorable a real word?" [380] A clerk from 1907 pronounced telephone as if naming a disease. [391] Two students quarreled over whether contact could ever be a verb, and lost. [404] A woman said hopefully at the end of a sentence in 1968, and a man near her hissed. [422]

Mira recorded each fragment in her ledgers, dating them by accent and allusion, and slowly the pattern of growth emerged, plain as rings in wood. [447] A word was misused, mocked, repeated, resisted, and finally printed; every coinage traveled from mouth to margin to page at a speed she could measure. [472] Growth needed error, and error needed living voices, and the volumes had stopped recording both. [487] But the mouths had not stopped. [493] The shard held one splinter more, and it was recent. [503] A night-guard, whispering to himself last winter among the closed stacks: "Four more hours, then home, then hammock, then hushfall." [523] Hushfall: a word for exactly this hour, the stillness between the streetlamps dying and the sun arriving, and it appeared nowhere in the ninety-one volumes that had finished the language. [553] The language had been declared complete, and it had already grown a new limb. [567]

Mira sat very still. [571] Her knees ached; her ledger hand had gone crooked as a root; her face had weathered sixty hard winters. [590] But something young turned over in her chest, the same something that had turned when she was eight and first understood that ink was once speech. [616] The weathering had never reached it. [622] She would not publish the word; the commissions would only snip it and press it flat behind glass. [640] Better to let it grow where growth actually happened, in warm dark mouths. [653] She would simply tend the evidence. [659]

The sun's first true ray crossed the desk and struck the crystal, and the suspended hush of the reading floor deepened, as if the building itself were listening. [687] Then Mira did the thing she had been saving herself for. [698] She leaned over the shard and spent a word, her voice rusty with three years of disuse, one syllable-weight given back to the air: hushfall. [723] The crystal drank it in, the way it had drunk every voice for a hundred years, and held it, patient, for whoever came needing testimony. [748] Outside, the street woke; a pigeon landed; a boiler shuddered. [758] Mira pocketed the shard, squared her ledgers, and smiled the way only old things and children smile, entirely, without protection. [778]
