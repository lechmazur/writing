Wren was the station's celestial signal interpreter, which meant, in practice, that she interpreted the past. [16] The gravitational anomaly they called the Bend hung off the port side, a slow bright knot that caught old light and curved it into loops. [41] Signals arrived out of order: a freighter's log from thirty years back, a dead moon's hiss, sometimes a voice. [60] Her work was to thresh meaning from all of it, separating the grain of intent from the chaff of noise. [80]

Eleven years ago, she failed at that threshing once, badly. [90] She misread a harmonic in a survey signal and certified the world Meridian as arable, and the colony ship Perdita trusted her reading. [113] Four hundred people planted their seed vault in soil that tasted like a wound. [127] Nothing came up except one stubborn barley, and the letters that looped out of the Bend afterward were full of hungry winters. [149] Wren read every one. [153]

A farmer named Marisol wrote most of them, and she never once called Wren a murderer, which was somehow worse. [173] She wrote about the barley that tolerated the salt, about her daughter's first tooth, about how the light bent strangely at dusk on a world nobody had chosen. [201] Wren answered none of them. [206]

She kept her grandmother's fountain pen in the desk drawer instead of a reply, dried up for eleven years, its nib bent where she had thrown it the day she signed the survey. [239] The nib was the exotic part, ground from a sliver of iron a salvager swore had been sheared off a neutron star. [261] Inside the Bend's steep field, the dry pen wrote anyway, dragging lines of shifted phase across the signal holograms like a finger through water. [285] And the lines stayed. [289]

That was the discovery she sat with for a month: the anomaly archived light, and the archive accepted marginalia. [308] She was editing history, one faint stroke at a time. [318] She tried dictating outcomes first, scrawling "the soil is sweet" across the old survey signal, and nothing happened at all. [338] The Bend ignored commands. [342] But when she traced what a signal depended on, the relay post that carried it, the junior analyst who double-checked it, the captain who trusted the check, and nudged only one dependency, the change rippled. [377] History was not a text but a web, and it could only be edited through contingent relationships, one thread of trust or habit at a time. [403]

She could unmake the whole error that way, carefully, over years. [414] She told the night engineer she meant to try. [423] "Are you sure it will work?" he asked. [431] "No," Wren said, with complete confidence, "and that is the truest reading I have ever made of anything." [449]

But erasing Marisol's hungry winters would erase Marisol's letters, and the daughter's first tooth, and the barley that learned to drink salt. [471] Wren had spent eleven years threshing meaning from that experience, and she refused to burn the grain to spite the chaff. [492] So she chose the smallest edit she could love. [501] First she took the bent nib between two careful fingers and unbent it, slowly, the way you straighten a seedling rather than a wire. [525] Then she wrote on the one signal still trapped in transit inside the Bend, a resupply inquiry from Meridian that would not reach the shipping lanes for another year, and she gave it a new overtone: "the salt, the salt, send salt-tolerant stock." [568] Not a command, just a nudge riding the web of who owed whom a careful reading. [584] When it was done she unbent the signal itself, easing its path out of the loop and onto a heading, which was the old interpreters' term for setting light free. [614]

Whether it would arrive, whether anyone would read it rightly this time, whether the young analyst at the relay would double-check, all of that was contingent on other people, and Wren found a strange comfort in that. [651] The Bend hummed against the hull like a hearth. [660] In the drawer the pen lay warm, dry and somehow full, and Wren finally answered Marisol's last letter, four years late and one word long: "soon." [686]
