On the nights when the universe hums its secret song, Vessa sings to the dead. [15] She is a canyon echo singer, the last of a thin-blooded line, and the grieving pay her in bread and salt to stand at the rim and call back the names of their dead. [49] Some names return. [52] The canyon gives them back warm and whole, saying whatever they loved to say, and the mourners weep and go home lighter. [74] Most do not. [77] Most fall like stones, and the silence after is the only grave some families get. [92]

For nine years Vessa has wanted to know why some people echo and some go quiet, ever since her sister Lark died and the canyon gave back nothing at all. [122] What the canyon gave her instead was a seashell, half of one, split clean along its spiral, found on Lark's grave a week after the burying. [148] On humming nights it whispers. [153] It whispers in Lark's voice. [158] So tonight, when the hum rises into what the old songs call the Long Hum, Vessa climbs. [175]

Above the switchbacks the gorges braid into the serpentine nexus, a winding throat of rock where every canyon in the country meets under starlight, and it is there the whisper in her pocket pulls hardest. [210] Her trade has made her richly perceptive to sound, alert to pitch and decay and the small lie inside a voice. [231] She notices, three switchbacks from the top, that the whisper is not begging her upward. [246] It is answering something. [250] The hum deepens, and threaded through it, faint as breath on a cold windowpane, she hears other whispers, dozens of them, layered. [272] She rounds the last bend and stops. [279]

Under starlight, strung across the narrows, hang webs. [287] Not spiderwork, but wire and silk, lattice after lattice, sagging under hundreds of split seashells, every one whispering. [305] The rock here was seabed once; someone has been gathering the sea's old ears by the thousand. [322] The first understanding comes slowly, the way betrayal does. [331] Nothing in these canyons echoes by nature. [338] The dead sing back only when a shell has been seeded into the stone, and someone up here decides whose. [358]

Voices drift up the path, and she presses into a hollow: two keepers making rounds with shaded lanterns, talking webs and deliveries. [380] A salt-city merchant paid four hundred crowns so his son would echo. [392] A widow was refused because her coin was short. [401] Then, laughing, they recall a harvest nine years back, a little singer dead of fever, her voice tearing as they took it, the ruined half left in the grave dirt. [431] Vessa's hand closes over her shell. [437] Nine years of loving a thing that was the waste of a theft. [450]

One keeper leans toward the webs, greedy. [457] "Loudest hum in sixty years," he says. [464] "The universe grieving, and every shell fat with it." [473] "Good night for harvest." [477] They mean to take more voices while the world hums, and every family below will keep paying, and keep not knowing. [498]

Vessa came here to learn why some people echo. [507] Now she knows, and knowing obligates her. [514] She steps out of the hollow into the starlight and begins to sing. [527] Not a name this time. [532] She sings what she heard, the whole crooked scheme of it, in the plain words the lowlands use, and her trained voice pours down every gorge at once. [560] Then she pitches her song to the whorls of the shells, the way she would pitch it to a canyon, and the shells answer. [584] One by one they tear loose and ride the cold current down through the nexus, and every freed voice takes up the song. [607] The disclosure unrolls village by village the way echoes travel: first her voice carrying the scheme, then the dead singing their own names over the rooftops, then the keepers shouting theirs, because in the nexus everything echoes now, the guilty along with the mourned, and no one will be able to unhear it. [660] She stays until the webs hang empty, to ensure that every voice is carried home on the wind. [678]

One shell falls last into her open hand and fits against her own half, clean along the spiral. [696] It whispers once, very close. [701] "Sing it again, Vess," says Lark, and then the little voice thins into the great hum, not gone exactly, but joined to it, part of the secret song the universe is always singing. [734] Vessa walks down through the switchbacks with the empty shell against her palm. [747] Far below, the canyons are still passing the song along, gorge to gorge, and she can no longer tell her own voice from her sister's from the wind's, which is the way mourning is supposed to sound. [784] She sings anyway, soft and reed-blown, all the way down. [794]
