The hush in the canyon was not born; it was forged. [11] In the days before the ritual, the priests of the pale spire struck their iron gong at dawn, hammering silence into the towers of bone until even the echoes flinched. [41]

Vey slipped between the femur-columns anyway, a defiant artist with charcoal-stained fingers and a satchel of unsanctioned poems. [59] Tomorrow the pale spire in the mist would devour the echoes of the forgiven dead, and with them the last of her brother. [82] That was the bargain the valley called mercy: speak your grief to the mist, forgive the dead who wronged you, and the spire took your beloved's echo as payment. [111] Forgiveness, Vey had learned, was the only coin the valley minted, and it cost you the voice of the person you loved. [133] Her brother Tam had been hanged for the granary fire, and his echo still wandered here, repeating the confession the magistrate had beaten into him: I lit it, I lit it, I lit it. [167] Only Vey knew the truth: she had watched the Hierophant leave the granary with a lantern, smiling, the night the town's winter burned. [190] If she spoke that truth at the ritual, the spire would swallow it whole, the way it swallowed every grievance, and hand her back a tidy, hollow peace. [218] So she had come the night before, to commit a different kind of crime. [232]

A boy's echo found her first, thin as a whistle: Fen, dead sixty years of fever, still innocently mischievous, still stealing shiny words from travelers the way living boys steal plums. [263] He plucked the word lantern from her muttered rehearsal and skipped it around the canyon, giggling from six towers at once. [284] Vey laughed despite herself, and the laugh came back wearing twelve different voices. [297] That was when she understood what this canyon of endless echoes could do. [310] The bone walls refracted sound the way a prism refracts light: one word entering, many words leaving. [327] And a single truth, split into a thousand echoes, could never be swallowed whole. [341] That was all she had wanted since the rope went taut: to refract one truth into many, too many to silence. [362]

So she began her impromptu poetry sessions, right there beneath the forged hush, calling invitations into the dark. [380] The dead came curious, echoes clustering at the mouths of the bone towers like moths at glass. [397] "A poem," she offered, "passed mouth to mouth." [405] "Each of you takes one line and carries it somewhere the priests will never look." [420] It broke every canon the spire had ever written, which was precisely why she smiled. [435] "Tam of the valley carried no lantern. [442] The fire was fed by a holy hand. [450] The innocent swung while the holy walked free. [458] Ask the bone, ask the mist, ask them all." [467]

The first echo took a line and fled, and the canyon split it into forty. [482] Fen turned the whole poem into a skipping game, innocent of the treason he was committing, delighted by the weight of the words in his borrowed voice. [509] He taught it to drowned sailors and fevered grandmothers and a murderer who had waited two centuries for someone to say something true. [532] By midnight the canyon was a choir, one truth refracted into ten thousand glinting pieces, far too many for any spire to swallow. [555]

Then, from the deepest tower, a familiar voice. [563] "I lit it," Tam said, dutiful even in death. [572] "No," Vey said. [575] "You carried no lantern, little brother." [581] She spoke the poem's last line straight into his tower, and the bone walls took it up and softened it, over and over: ask them all, ask them all, ask them all. [613] For the first time since the rope, Tam's echo fell quiet, listening. [625] "It was never mine to forgive you for," she told him. [636] "The valley owes you an apology, and come morning it will hear itself give it." [651]

Dawn arrived gray, and the priests came processing toward the pale spire with their iron gong. [667] The Hierophant raised his striker to forge the ritual hush. [677] The first blow fell, and silence tried to settle over the canyon like a lid. [692] But a canyon of endless echoes does not forget on command. [703] From every tower, in ten thousand voices, the dead kept singing. [714] Fen's giggle skipped the poem up the spire itself, until even the mist began, softly, to recite. [731] Vey stood among them, charcoal-stained fingers folded, no longer begging the valley for peace. [745] Forgiveness had a price, and she had finally learned its true shape: it could not be minted by a spire or hammered by a gong, only paid slowly, in truth, by every voice at once. [780] The ritual had not yet begun, and already it belonged to the dead. [793]
