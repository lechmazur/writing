The murmuring pavilion stood where the dunes gathered, its veiled walls carrying every word ever spoken beneath them in a soft, endless hush. [23] Nights there felt like standing inside a held breath. [32] I came to it after the prophet arrived, walking the thin silver line I had strung between the ridges years before. [53] I am a line walker, half-masked since the sandstorm that took my cheek and taught me better ways of seeing. [73] The mask blocks the center of my vision; truth, I learned, lives at the edges. [88] What drifts in from the side cannot prepare a lie in time. [100] This is why the desert calls me strangely honest; I only report what arrives at my edges. [117]

The prophet had spoken for three days inside the pavilion, and the walls had drunk every sentence. [134] But the murmur was thinning. [138] Veils fray, the pilgrims told me, and what the walls forget is forgotten everywhere. [152] The prophet had already gone east, refusing disciples the way rain refuses a command. [166] I had heard the last hour of the teaching from the doorway, sideways, as I hear everything. [183] It was not poetry; it was instruction about water, patience, and the duty of carrying another's thirst. [200] Wisdom like that does not deserve evaporation. [207] So I came to preserve the echo of wisdom, and I knew of only one vessel deep enough. [225]

Beneath the dunes slept the kinetic parapet, a rampart built by the first walkers, which drinks motion and keeps it. [245] Every step I had ever walked had gone somewhere; the old doctrine says no stride is wasted, only banked. [264] My lifetime of lines had been falling into that dark wall like coins into a well. [280] At midnight I slid through the pavilion's undergate and descended the spiral of cooled sand. [295] The parapet was exactly as doctrine promised: a black breastwork running deeper than lamplight, trembling faintly with every footstep stored inside it. [317] When I laid my bare palm against it, I felt my own ten thousandth step shiver home. [334] Stored motion, the walkers say, is only waiting speech. [343] A parapet full of decades could feed a murmur for centuries, if something taught it the words. [360]

Catching the murmur required the old craft: conjured clearings, circles of commanded emptiness where nothing may enter uninvited. [378] I climbed back up and stood in the pavilion's center at first light. [391] I did not look at the walls. [397] Looking straight at a murmur scatters it, the way staring scatters a shy animal. [411] Instead I widened my attention sideways, through peripheral awareness, letting the drifting syllables graze the edge of me like wind-carried seed. [432] The prophet's words gathered there, hesitant, then certain: the duty of carrying another's thirst. [446] I spoke the syllable of clearing, and around me the dust, the braziers, even the pillars bowed out of existence, leaving a perfect ring of bare silence. [473] In a conjured clearing, sound has nowhere to hide; it must choose you. [486] The whole murmur turned and poured itself into the ring, into me, a field of whispers heavier than sand. [505] I carried it down the spiral on legs gone stiff as stilts. [517]

At the parapet I set both palms on the black stone and gave the murmur somewhere to live. [535] Motion met meaning; the wall began, softly, to speak. [544] But speaking is not keeping. [549] To make the echo permanent, a walker must consecrate what she has built, and consecration cannot be faked. [567] The rite requires the plain truth of the one who performs it. [579] So I told the wall the whole truth, sideways but unsoftened: that I had loved the sound of my own balance more than any person; that I had strung lines across the desert so I would never have to stay; that the prophet's words about carrying another's thirst had caught me at the edge of hearing and would not let go. [640] The parapet drank the confession as hungrily as it had drunk my steps. [653] Then it stilled, and the murmur inside it steadied into something that will outlast the dunes. [669]

I climbed back to the surface as dawn flattened the shadows. [680] The pavilion above has gone quiet now, and the pilgrims grieve it. [692] I let them grieve; honesty does not require cruelty. [701] When they stand barefoot on the dune above the parapet, they feel a faint tremor of footsteps beneath them, and out of it the voice comes, patient and low, teaching water, patience, and the carrying of another's thirst. [739] I will keep walking my lines. [745] Every step still banks in the deep wall, so the voice never runs dry. [759] But I walk slower now, and sometimes I stop altogether, listening the way I trust: sideways, at the edge, where nothing can lie. [782]
