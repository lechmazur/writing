Wren saw the bunker first in memory, which for her meant the future: a concrete jaw under ivy, breathing. [19] She had been built, or broken, so that only foresight and hindsight reached her; the present was a smear she walked through like fog. [43] So she traveled by remembered tomorrows, stepping where she had already seen her feet fall. [58] The rewilded forest had swallowed the old access road, and vines draped the bunker door like green braid over a dead woman's eyes. [81] Thunder walked the horizon. [85] The storm she had foreseen for eleven years was finally approaching, and it had her name folded inside it, or so the dreams insisted. [109] She had come to learn why. [115] Why some paths choose their walkers, and never the other way around. [127]

At the door hung the wafer seal, a disc of ceramic and gold circuitry, older than the trees around it. [147] It pulsed faintly, like a slow heart, and when she pressed her palm to it, it read her dreams the way a lock reads a key. [173] The door ground open on roots and rust. [181]

Inside, a voice like dust on a lens said, 'You are four minutes early, or four years late, depending on which way I am facing.' [206] The Warden had waited sixty years with a decaying directive. [216] Preserve what sleeps; open when the storm arrives; deliver the sleeper to the one who cannot see the now. [235] But each decade had eaten a word. [242] 'Deliver' had gone first, then 'arrives', and the Warden admitted it no longer knew whether the storm was weather or the world ending politely. [266] It was precisely playful about its ruin. [273] 'I have lost the beginning of my sentence,' it said, 'so I have decided to enjoy the end of it.' [293] Cryo-cradles lined the walls, all empty except one, and in that one slept something the Warden would not name. [312] 'Name it,' Wren said, 'and I will tell you if you are right.' [325] The Warden laughed in static. [330] 'Better,' it said. [333] 'I will show you mine if you show me yours.' [343]

It led her to the dream chair, a web of sensors grown over with pale fungus, and explained the bargain. [363] She would sleep; it would interpret; together they would read what the storm wanted. [377] Dreams were the only present she had ever owned, the Warden said, because in dreams the three tenses braid into one river. [399] She lay down and the fungus hummed against her skull like a struck bell remembering being ore. [416] She dreamed the forest walking uphill, carrying the bunker like a seed. [428] She dreamed herself old, standing at a crossroads, weeping with gratitude. [439] She dreamed the wafer seal cracking like an egg and a road unspooling from it, choosing her feet the way rivers choose valleys, by patient drowning. [465] The Warden read each image aloud with exact, gentle mischief, revising itself whenever a reading made her flinch. [483]

Then it found the dream it had been waiting sixty years for. [495] She dreamed of the sleeper in the cradle, and the sleeper had her face, and it opened its eyes, which were not eyes but two small storms. [522] 'Ah,' said the Warden, softly, 'there you are.' [530] The directive, it explained, had never decayed at all; it had been composting. [543] The word 'deliver' had not been eaten; it had gone to seed. [555] The sleeper was the message, and the storm was the reader, and Wren was the road between them. [573] 'You wanted to know why paths choose their walkers,' the Warden said. [585] 'They do not choose.' [589] 'A path is simply a walker looking backward at herself and mistaking the shape for the world.' [606]

Above them the storm arrived like a decision. [614] Rain struck the vine-draped concrete; lightning made the corridor a negative of itself. [627] The wafer seal heated under its moss and sang one clean note. [639] 'Now,' said the Warden, 'the only part I never lost.' [649] Wren understood that to finish the path she had to transcend it, had to step out of foresight and hindsight into the burn of the instant. [675] Through interpreted dreams the Warden built her a ladder of images, each one a rung closer to now. [693] She climbed: the forest, the crossroads, the egg, the sleeper's eyes. [704] At the top rung she stopped seeing what would be and what had been. [718] The seal broke. [721] The cradle opened. [724] And for one white second, Wren stood inside the present, and it was the storm, and the storm was her, choosing nothing and everything at once. [750]

When the rains thinned, the bunker stood empty, door wide, vines already reaching inside. [764] The Warden hummed a half-remembered sentence and, for the first time, enjoyed its end. [778]
