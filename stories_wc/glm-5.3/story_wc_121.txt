The rain had stopped, and the viaduct answered it with light. [11] Wren walked the underside of the arch, boots kissing stone above her head, because gravity had never consented to hold her. [32] People below called her a gravity defier; she preferred trespasser of directions. [44] Below her, the flooded canal held the viaduct's glow like a bowl of green fire, and one last raindrop hung at the lip of the arch, falling, not yet fallen. [74] She had learned to live inside the pause between that drop and its ripple, a country no clock had ever visited. [95] Time fractured there the way light fractured in wet stone. [105] Every choice she had ever made still ran somewhere, side by side, timelines splitting off the main road like tributaries. [125] In the suspended instant she was periodically eternal: she lived forever, but only in loops, each one ending where the ripple finally touched the bank. [150] Then the drop fell again, the light reset, and so did she. [162]

Against her chest, wrapped in oilcloth, she carried the clay tablet she had pried from the viaduct's keystone. [180] Its runes were older than the city, older than the word for city, and when rainwater touched them they ran like gears, like instructions, like a loom deciding what to weave next. [212] The tablet did not tell fortunes. [218] It generated labyrinths. [221] Read it once and it built a maze from the first rule; walk the maze and every junction rolled a new corridor into being, procedurally, endlessly, no two halls alike. [251] The maze was not of stone. [257] It was of her. [261] Every corridor was a corridor of self: the summer she lied to her brother, the door she never opened, the apology she swallowed like a stone. [287] She had entered it to conquer it, which is to say, to conquer herself, and she had been entering it for what felt like ten thousand ripples. [314]

Tonight, on the ten-thousandth loop, she understood the trick at last: a generator cannot bear repetition. [330] The labyrinth minted a new self for every fork she took, but if she retraced her steps exactly, through procedural generation itself, walking the same halls in the same order, the machinery would stutter, seize, and show its one hidden door. [371] The way out of a maze that reads you is to become legible. [384] She uncorked her canteen and let the canal-fed water crawl across the runes. [397] The viaduct's glow brightened, and the arches folded inward like ribs, and the underside of the world became a hallway. [417] She began to retrace. [421]

Left at the corridor of the lie, where her brother's voice asked the question she had dodged for thirty years. [441] Right at the gallery of her mother's hands. [449] Straight through the hall of small mercies she had refused. [459] Each hall hurt in its own bright way, because the maze was kind in its cruelty: it lit everything, hid nothing, made her look. [483] That was the darkening brightness of the place: the deeper she walked, the more light it gave her, and the more light it gave her, the less she could pretend not to see. [516] In loop after loop she had taken different forks, hungry for the exit, and in loop after loop the labyrinth had happily built her a brand-new stranger to be. [545] Now she took the same forks, and the walls began to complain. [557] The glow strained, whitened, went strange. [563] At the final junction the corridor of the lie appeared again, but this time she stopped inside it and answered her brother's question, out loud, in the dark, decades too late. [594] "Yes," she said. [597] "I knew, and I said nothing, and you drowned." [606]

The machinery shrieked like a nail drawn from green wood. [616] Every corridor repeated at once, the generator grinding against its own rules, and in the seam between two identical walls a door opened that had never been generated before. [645] Beyond it: the viaduct, right side up, rain-fresh, glowing lower now, the way candles glow when they mean to go out. [666] The raindrop was still falling. [671] She stepped through, onto the top of the arch this time, feet down, gravity finally allowed to hold her, and the tablet crumbled in her hands into plain wet clay, its runes spent. [704] The drop touched the canal. [709] The ripple began. [712] And Wren, no longer eternal, no longer looped, let time carry her forward with everyone else, into whatever came next, lighter by one secret, darker by none. [739]
