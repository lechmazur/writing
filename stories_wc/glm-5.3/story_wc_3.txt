The morning after the spirit left, the arbor began to sink. [11] Naia watched the cloud deck rise against the branches, a finger's width an hour, and did what she always did with frightening things: she measured them. [37]

For eleven years she had translated cuneiform up here, in a grove of magnetic trees floating above the clouds, because iron was forbidden in the arbor and clay tablets were the only archives the orchards trusted. [73] Her teacher, Vess, had been the orchardist. [80] His ambition had been the seed. [86]

One knowledge fruit had ever ripened here, a lantern-colored sphere whose flesh, the tablets promised, would teach whoever tasted it; he had refused to plant its seed. [113] He held it at touching distance for forty years, willing it to confess itself, and when he died the ambition stayed behind, pacing the boardwalks, bending over the magnetometers, murmuring germination ratios at dawn. [147] A ghost, the old hymns said, is an ambition that has not finished its sentence. [162] She had translated that line years before he died, and understood it only when he began haunting her. [180] She had loved him, so she stayed. [187]

Then, last night, he had finished. [193] She never learned what satisfied him - a dream, an apology, a word of hers that closed his clause - but at moonrise the air went quiet, every needle swung wide and settled at zero, and the trees began, slowly, to lose their sky. [235]

That was the part she could not stop turning over. [245] The trees were magnetic; their iron-dark sap held its orientation against the world's field, and the grove rode that stubbornness the way a compass needle rides north. [272] But at the instant of Vess's leaving, the field had collapsed. [283] A transition, then, was physical. [288] Departure had weight, or removed it. [294] Naia sat with the seed in her palm and admitted to the empty air her true motive. [311] She had not stayed only for Vess. [318] She had stayed because she wanted to understand the physics of transition - how a man becomes a ghost, a ghost becomes an absence, a seed becomes a tree - and whether the passage leaves a mark you can read. [356]

The arbor sank another finger's width. [362] Below the clouds, the wet would eat the clay archive in a season, and the fruit, if another ever came, would rot on the bough. [387] She had to reestablish the field. [393]

Here is what a translator knows and no orchardist thought to try. [405] A wedge means nothing alone. [410] It means by relation - this stroke against that angle, this sign in this position, sound leaning on sound until sense stands up. [432] Twenty years of mapping one system onto another had taught her to think in analogies the way other people think in words. [454] If a sign can be realigned by its neighbors, she thought, so can a tree. [469] If a departure can collapse a field, an arrival might raise one. [481]

So she worked by her kindly, methodical habit. [489] She numbered the trees - sixty-one - and walked the grove twice, reading their lean the way she read a damaged tablet, not for what was lost but for what remained legible. [519] Thirty held some orientation; thirty-one had gone slack, their sap's magnetism scattered by the departure the way letters scatter when a tablet is dropped. [543] Scattered, she reminded herself, not destroyed. [549] Domains remember, the Seed Hymns said, what a patient hand reminds them of. [562]

Then, at the center of the grove, she did the thing Vess had never dared. [577] She planted the seed. [581] Not at touching distance - she placed it in the dark of the soil and gave it the distance that touching requires. [602] You cannot feel what you are gripping, she told the place where his voice used to be. [619]

Germination took nine days. [623] On the ninth, a green hook broke the soil, every needle swung at once, and thirty-one slack trees shivered and leaned toward the seedling like readers toward a first line. [653] The arbor rose. [656] Slowly, the sinking run in reverse, but it rose, and the grove reestablished itself around a beginning instead of an absence. [677]

That evening she pressed a fresh tablet. [684] Transition has physics, she wrote in wedges Vess had taught her. [695] A departure scatters; an arrival gathers. [701] What leaves disorients us; what comes makes us lean toward it. [712] She could not prove where he had gone. [720] But the seedling stood at the center of sixty-one leaning trees, and she understood that she had written the oldest sentence in the world in a language of iron and green - the closest she would ever come to touching him again. [761] So she kept her own ambition kindly and at a distance, where it could grow. [776] Above the clouds, in the reestablished hush, the haunting settled into a garden. [789]
