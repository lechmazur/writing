In the hush of a hidden sunrise, when light touched only the rim of Echo Gorge and never its floor, Wren, collector of lost souls, tuned her harp out of alignment. [31] Perfect notes, she had learned, were swallowed whole by the gorge; a note bent sour survived. [47] Her trade was the collecting of spectral confessions, and lost souls hid from that work inside the gorge's thousand echoes. [67] Somewhere in the stone's throat, a dead boatman had been repeating the same unsaid words for forty years. [85]

Wren was modestly fearless: she did not flinch at what people could not say aloud, and she never thought to brag of it. [108] She struck a chord, and the distorted notes ran ahead of her like a lantern of wrong-colored light. [126] Somewhere in the returning echoes, faint as breath on glass, a confession flickered. [139] She followed it down a switchback of shale, playing as she went, because bent frequencies snag on soul-stuff the way hooks snag on wool. [163]

The soul surfaced at the bend, an old man made of river-colored light, and when he saw her he fled, folding himself into an echo of wind. [190] She pursued. [192] Through the distorted notes of the misaligned harp she pursued him, for sour chords made every echo betray its hiding place, and a soul cannot bend an echo silently. [221] He slid into the sound of dripping water; she bent the chord, and the drip came back wrong. [239] He tried the whistle of night wind; the night wind returned with a limp. [253] At a dead-end wall seamed with mica, he ran out of echoes. [265]

"I am not finished," he said. [271]

"Nobody who repeats himself is finished," Wren said. [279] "Say it to me instead of to the stone. [288] A confession is only a secret aimed at someone." [297]

The boatman opened his fist. [302] In it lay a crocodile's tooth, yellowed and heavy as regret. [313] "My brother and I poled ferries across the Savuti," he said. [324] "The river took him. [328] I pried this tooth from the hull of his boat, from wood his hands had polished." [344]

Wren waited, because the true confession always arrives one sentence after the one a soul has rehearsed. [361]

"Three years later," he said, and his light guttered, "I laughed. [372] At a wedding. [375] A cousin danced badly, and I laughed until I wept, and it was the first time since the river that I felt alive. [398] Then I touched the tooth in my pocket and understood what I was: a crocodile, weeping false tears over my brother while my mouth opened for joy. [425] I never laughed again. [429] Forty years, and I could not even die honest." [438]

Wren leaned the harp against the wall. [445] "Do you know why I keep the strings misaligned?" she asked. [456] "Grief played in tune is only grief repeated. [464] Bend the note and you hear grief's neighbor, which is laughter; my work is to fold that laughter back into healing. [485] Your brother laughed on that river, didn't he?" [493]

The old man's light shuddered. [498] "Every day," he whispered. [502] "He laughed at his own terrible dancing." [509]

"Then when you laughed at that wedding," Wren said, "he was in the room. [523] That was not betrayal; that was healing arriving in the only shape you would let through the door. [541] Laughter is not the opposite of mourning; it is mourning, allowed to move." [554]

Above them the hidden sun found a notch in the rim, and one thin blade of light dropped a thousand feet to strike the mica, and the whole dead-end wall lit up like the inside of a bell. [592] The boatman looked at the light, then at his own remembered joy, and he laughed. [607] It began as a rusty creak and broke open into something enormous, the laugh of two brothers on a morning river, and it carried the luminous ache of every good thing that ever ended. [641] Every echo in the gorge took it up, multiplied it, and gave it back brighter. [656] He came apart inside that sound, gently, the way light comes apart in water. [670]

The crocodile's tooth dropped to the shale with a small, definite click. [682] Wren picked it up; it was warm. [689] She slid it into her pocket beside the loose tuning peg she had unwound years ago, when she first learned that anything held perfectly in tune can neither grieve nor heal. [720] She shouldered the harp and climbed toward the rim, carrying the tooth of an animal famous for false tears, now her truest relic of laughter. [745] Behind her, Echo Gorge rang on in the hush of the hidden sunrise, laughing in a hundred smaller voices, the way the living do when they are finally allowed to. [775]
