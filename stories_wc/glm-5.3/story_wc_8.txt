Dr. Ilse Barrow waited on the platform of Aldermoor station, watching the old rosebush surrender its last bloom. [18] Ivy had swallowed the ticket hall, the benches, the great arched roof, so that the place resembled a green and silent whale. [40] Only the rosebush still flowered, planted seventy years ago by the stationmaster for the daughter the fire had taken. [59] Ilse had written two books about that fire; the second had won prizes. [72] The Miracle of Aldermoor told how stationmaster Elias Crane carried nineteen souls out of the burning express, though his own child burned with it. [96] The town had rebuilt itself on that sentence. [104] Margaret Sedge, the last living survivor, had called for her in her final week, gripped her wrist, and said: after the final petal drops, look under the third bench, where my mother sat. [137] Then she said the words that had followed Ilse for a month: "What we told was kind, and it was killing us." [159]

The petal trembled in the wind and let go. [168] Ilse crossed the platform, tore ivy from the third bench, and pried up a loose slate beneath it. [186] There lay a biscuit tin, and inside it, wrapped in oilcloth, a single sheet of vellum. [202] A letter of confession, in Elias Crane's cramped hand, beginning with an illuminated I. [216] Before the railways he had trained as an illuminator, and it showed: gold leaf laid over gesso, a tiny rose vine climbing the capital, every whorl of it grieving. [245] Ilse's hands shook, and her mind, as it always did when her hands shook, went calmly to work checking the ink, the foxing, the date. [270] It was genuine. [273]

"I, Elias Crane, gave the express the wrong lamp," the letter ran. [285] "Nineteen souls were lost by my hand, among them my Lily." [296] "The vicar and the survivors made of me a hero so the town would not die of grief, and I let them, and I have watered her rose every year in payment." [328] When the bush could bloom no longer, he wrote, let the truth come up out of the ground with it. [348]

Ilse sat down hard on the ivy. [355] Her beautiful book, her lectures, the wreath-layings she had attended with wet eyes: all of it a nursery for a noble lie. [377] Part of her wanted to press the slate back down. [387] The lie had been kind; kindness was real; but a town that could not check its own story was a town that would sign anything, believe anything, burn again. [416] She had not spent thirty years in archives to leave truth lying in a pit of lies. [433]

That evening the parish hall was full, because rumors travel faster than historians. [446] Mayor Kett had heard about the tin. [453] "Lock it away," he said, not unkindly. [460] "The Miracle is the only wealth we have left; the lie fed us, and you would starve us." [478] Murmurs; the hall smelled of rain and damp wool. [487] Ilse felt the crowd's fear as if it were her own, and used the feeling as data, the way she always did. [509] Then she stood and did what historians do: she drew parallels. [520]

"In 1907 the river took six of our men, and we counted them aloud, and we're still here," she said. [540] "Next valley over, the pit owners lied about firedamp for thirty years, and that town is a name on a map and nothing else." [564] "The plaque says nineteen saved; the letter says nineteen lost; the same number, turned like a coin." [581] "A kind lie and a cruel one run on the same rails, and eventually they arrive at the same station." [601]

She held up the vellum so the gold caught the light. [612] "He painted his guilt beautiful because he meant us to keep it, not to bury it." [628] The hall was quiet in a different key now. [637] Margaret's grandson stood, and then the vicar's granddaughter, and then the vote was not close. [652] They amended the record by lamplight; truth, recaptured, went into the parish book beside the old legend, the two laid out in parallel columns. [676]

In spring they cleared the ivy from the station instead of pulling it down, and set the letter where the timetable used to hang. [700] Ilse planted a new rosebush at the edge of the platform, and the town came to see it done. [719] The old lie still stood in the guidebooks, now with a footnote; the footnote was hers, and she was proud of exactly one sentence of it. [745] "What we honor and what is true may sit in the same hall, if we never again confuse them." [764] The first petal of the new bush opened in June, and it looked, Ilse thought, like a small door ajar. [784]
