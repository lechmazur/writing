Juna read the fates of places, not people, and no place had ever kept her. [15] As a landscape astrologer, she had charted a hundred deserts and harbors, each one a stranger by morning. [33] Tonight a dive bell lowered her toward the Sarn Institute, a research facility gripping the seabed beside the drowned city of Vell, forgotten by every map but this one contract. [63] The institute had hired her to cast the city's horoscope before sunrise, to say whether Vell slept soundly beneath the silt or still dreamed of surfacing. [89] Her own motive she kept folded smaller: after thirty years of reading other places' rhythms, she wanted, once, to learn the rhythm of belonging. [113]

Toma met her at the airlock, the last keeper, gray to the wrists. [126] He fed her tea and ghost stories in the galley, then led her along the observation corridor, where portholes opened like eyelids onto the streets of Vell. [153] Lamplight from the drowned windows still hung in the water like held breath. [166] On the desk in the chart room lay a single burnt feather, curled black at its edges, still faintly smelling of smoke. [188] "The night Vell drowned, the generators caught fire," Toma said. [198] "A swan flew through the smoke above my rooftops, the flame took her wing, and I could save only this." [218] He had kept it forty years, proof that something had been alive here, proof that grief could be held in one hand. [240]

Juna worked through the deep hours, and she worked the way she always had, softly ingenious, coaxing the dead instruments back with wax and copper thread instead of forcing them. [270] She measured tide against stars and silt against moon, drawing Vell's chart in strokes as light as current. [288] Near midnight the water bloomed: a slow snowfall of lantern-jellyfish rose through the drowned streets, and for one hour the whole city glowed, a luminous respite, Vell breathing light instead of grief. [320] Even Toma stood silent, a man watching his city dream. [330] When her pencil failed at the city's dimmer gradients, she asked to borrow the burnt feather, trimmed it to a nib, and found that its charred edge laid down shadow in the exact gray of the deep. [367] "It writes what the city remembers," she said, and Toma looked away, undone. [380]

Toward four, when the jellyfish thinned, he touched her sleeve. [390] "One more thing before your sunrise," he said, and led her up the mast ladder to the crow's nest, the only part of the institute that still touched open air. [420] There, in a crate of soil his late wife had carried up forty springs ago, a small garden had outlived its gardener. [442] "Every first warm night, she and I would stand here and breathe out slowly together, and where the breath ended, the light began," he said. [467] It was the first night of summer, and he had never once done it alone. [482] So they stood shoulder to shoulder above the drowned world and exhaled, one long shared breath into the dark, and where the breath faded, the first fireflies of summer lifted from the garden like sparks remembering they had once been stars. [523]

The sight captivated her more than any conjunction she had ever computed: small lights, brief as breath, insisting on shining anyway. [544] She understood then that belonging was not a coordinate on a chart but a rhythm kept with someone: two lungs agreeing on the same slow tempo, season after season. [573] At sunrise she finished Vell's horoscope with the burnt quill, writing beneath the final house: this city rests; let no one wake it. [596] The jellyfish still glowed far below, faint as the seam where shadow meets light. [610]

Toma watched her pack, then said the only thing that could have kept her: "The fireflies will rise again next summer, and I am getting old at breathing alone." [639] Juna set her cases back down, unpacked the burnt feather, and inked a fresh heading on a new sheet: Observations, Volume Two. [661] She would stay until the next sunrise, and the one after that, learning the rhythm, keeping time with a keeper, a garden, and a city that glowed in its sleep. [691]
