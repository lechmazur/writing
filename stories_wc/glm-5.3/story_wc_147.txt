Mara sent the distress signal with the last battery of her dying submersible, four words of static: Submersible down, position attached. [21] Then she swam toward a darker dark on the ridge, toward a roofline no chart she had ever corrected had promised. [42] Columns, a doorway, a darkness older than any survey she had ever run. [55] She passed through and surfaced, gasping, inside a forgotten underwater temple. [66] The air there tasted of old stone, breathable, barely. [75]

And the hush was not mere silence. [82] It was a gathered hush, the quiet of centuries pooling in stone: every drowned procession, every stopped chant, settled like silt into one held breath. [107] Cold blue light bloomed through the dome, and the pillars wore their ruin like ceremonial dress. [123] It was the loneliest splendor she had ever stood inside, and she had known plenty of both. [140] Her wrist gauge did the arithmetic: rescue at the earliest in three hours, her rebreather in fifty minutes. [158] Time ran short, then shorter. [163]

So she waded the flooded nave toward the altar, because a dying surveyor may as well die curious. [181] On the altar lay a crystal perfume vial, small as a sparrow's heart, stoppered with gold gone soft with age. [201] The moment her fingers closed around it, the vial touched memories that were not hers. [216] Priests in kelp-green robes; lanterns of carved pearl; a fragrance poured out for a god of passing things. [234] The vial held no perfume at all, she understood, watching the visions turn. [247] It held moments, distilled; the temple had been a distillery for the ephemeral, bottling what the world would otherwise misplace. [267] That was why the hush felt gathered: it was the residue of ten thousand bottlings, the silence left behind when fleeting things were preserved. [291]

Mara had spent twenty years mapping wrecks, cataloguing everything the ocean erased, and nobody had ever asked her why. [310] She had never said the answer aloud: she was trying to beat the water at its own game. [328] Now, with the gauge ticking her remaining breaths, one motive surfaced clean and whole: to harness the ephemeral, the way the priests once had. [352] If she died here, something of her would not be lost; it would be kept, deliberately, in crystal. [370] She unstoppered the vial with both thumbs. [377] It touched her memories this time, and it chose. [386] Her mother's kitchen; rain needling the glass; cinnamon; a laugh Mara had almost forgotten she was rich enough to own. [406] The scent of it flooded the gathered hush like light. [416] She breathed her moment into the crystal mouth, slowly, the way you give away anything you hope to keep. [435]

Then the seafloor shrugged. [439] Silt thundered down the columns, the dome groaned like a hull, and the guideline she had laid from the doorway snarled itself into the fallen lattice of a carved screen. [469] The open vial stayed clamped in her fist, silver threads of her mother's kitchen still rising from it. [487] Instinct howled at her, simple and ancient: yank, tear, swim, now. [498] She felt her hands begin to obey, felt the old animal in her spine reach for the door. [516] Overriding instinct was the hardest thing she had ever done underwater, and she had once cut her own dive partner free of a net. [540] She stopped, knelt in the settling silt, and began to unsnarl the line, loop by loop, while the gauge counted her breaths like a debt collector. [566] The knots were coral-fused and mean, but the temple had already taught her patience. [580] Unsnarl one loop; breathe; unsnarl another. [586] The dome groaned again, and she kept working, because panic was only instinct with better advertising. [602] At last the line ran clean through her hands, and only then did she stopper the vial and set it back on the altar, beside the memories of priests who had shown her, without a word, how the thing was done. [643]

Through the water came a sound she nearly wept at: the rising ping of a rescue pinger, closer than her arithmetic had dared to promise. [668] Her signal had been heard. [673] Mara pulled her mask down, took one last look at the lonesome splendor around her, and sank out through the drowned doorway into the ascending light. [699] The temple stayed where it had always stayed, forgotten and splendid and alone, keeping its gathered hush. [716] But the hush held one new thing now, harnessed at last from the ephemeral: a kitchen, rain on the glass, cinnamon, a laugh, waiting in crystal for whoever came next. [746]
