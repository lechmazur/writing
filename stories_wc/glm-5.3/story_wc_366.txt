Marit ran supplies to the island lights for thirty years and never needed many words to do it. [18] Her trade was watching: the set of a keeper's shoulders, coal smoke leaning the wrong way, a kettle gone cold. [38] She was warmly observant, which people mistook for silence, and she let them. [51] Words wore out like rope, she figured; noticing never did. [61]

She and the keepers kept a language older than any of them, stacked on the landing beaches. [78] One flat stone meant safe crossing; a tilted stone meant wait for slack water; a white pebble crowning the pile meant thank you. [101] In thirty years nobody under a light ever had to shout. [112]

Then there were the letters. [117] The first turned up in a crate of wicks at Cold Point, unsigned: "To whoever keeps this light tonight, you are not alone, and you are doing it well." [146] The keeper there answered on the back of a tide table and left the pair in the oil store for whoever came next. [169] It became a chain, passed hand to hand, station to station, Marit ferrying the growing bundle up and down the coast like tide in a bottle. [195] Nobody signed, and that was the rule nobody ever spoke. [205] Letters arrived for bad nights: for fog that talked, for the winter a keeper's wife coughed through March, for the plain dark at three in the morning. [232] Marit read them by lamp in her wheelhouse and never could have said why they steadied her, only that they did. [253]

Keeper Ansel at Gray Skerry was the quietest of the lot. [264] He sent no letters ever, only stones, and one autumn he capped his cairn with a piece of green sea glass rubbed round as a thumbnail. [290] Marit held it to the lamp and understood without translation: all's well, don't you worry. [305] She kept it afterward in a small bottle on her wheelhouse sill, and on bad nights she shook it once, the way another sailor shakes a compass, and the night behaved. [336] She was, though she would have denied it, trying to bottle the feeling of safety: Ansel's calm, the lights burning, the sea kept to its lanes. [362]

Then the Board began automating the coast. [369] Gray Skerry's lamp flickered out one black March night and would not take flame again, and Ansel followed it before the daffodils came. [392] The Board's orders were plain: strip the tower, burn the paper, take the brass. [406] Marit took the run because it was hers, and because nobody else knew the landing. [421]

The stair up smelled of pipe smoke that no one had lit in a year. [436] At the turn where a man could rest his oil can, the cold gathered like someone stepping aside to let her pass. [458] Inside, the great lens sat blind, and the room felt occupied the way a pew feels occupied after the congregation has gone. [480] Marit was not a woman who frightened easily, but she stood very still. [493] The floorboard behind her gave its old creak, the one Ansel's boots had made a thousand times. [510] She said nothing, being Marit, and got to work, because grief and ghosts both respect work. [526] Under the burner, wedged where only a hand that knew the lamp would look, lay one more letter. [544] No signature, no greeting. [548] "A light is only a bottle for the feeling of safety," it read, and beneath that, "Cork it well and carry it somewhere dark." [572]

The salvage men came in the afternoon with sledges and a handcart for the brass. [587] Marit put the letters into her oilskin, all of them, before anyone asked. [600] When the foreman asked about the paper, she looked at him the way she looked at weather, and he found something to do on the jetty. [626] Some things you protect by keeping quiet about them. [635]

That evening she landed at Cold Point, where a young keeper in his first winter alone was trying hard to look unafraid. [657] She left the bundle in his oil store without a word, the way the first letter had been left, and on the beach below she built a cairn. [685] Flat stone for safe crossing, tilted stone for slack water. [695] On top she set the green sea glass, out of its bottle for the first time in years. [713] All's well, don't you worry. [718]

Out on the black water behind her, Gray Skerry showed one green flash where no lamp was wired to live. [738] Marit watched it from her wheelhouse and felt the old steadiness settle over her like light. [754]
