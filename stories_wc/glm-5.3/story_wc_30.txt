After tomorrow disappeared, the world kept its appointments anyway. [9] The calendars simply stopped at the column marked with a small rising sun, and people learned to live inside one long, low winter day. [33] Mira was the titan-gear librarian, keeper of the stacks built into the ribs of a sleeping stone colossus whose great gears still turned, slow as weather. [59] At the library's heart lay the tidal atrium, a hall of glass and shallow water that filled and emptied with the pull of the moon. [84]

Her self migrated on the same schedule. [91] At the new moon she was Mira, meticulous and gray-eyed; as the moon fattened she became Marin, reckless with laughter; at the full she was only "the Librarian," a calm presence who signed the ledger in a hand no one recognized; in the waning dark she answered to nothing at all. [142] The townspeople called it a curse. [148] She called it a staff of one, stretched thin across a month. [160] She kept a ledger of notes passed hand to hand between her selves: dust the astrolabe shelf; the fisherman takes honey in his tea. [184] Routine, she had decided, was the last safe vessel for wonder, once tomorrow was cancelled. [199]

Every day, at the same pale hour, Old Brann the fisherman waded the atrium tide with his bamboo creel on his back. [221] He fished where no river was, casting into water that arrived from nowhere whenever the moon pulled, and every cast brought up silver smelt, thumb-length fish that gleamed like filings shed from the colossus's gears. [256] "Where do they swim from," Mira asked once, "when there is no tomorrow for them to swim toward?" [274] Brann shrugged, wet to the knees in wintersun light that lay along the water like warm butter. [291] "Fish don't need a destination," he said. [298] "They need a tide." [302] She copied that into the ledger under all of her names. [313] In the waning dark, when she was no one, she read it and remembered how to be someone. [331]

Then came the day Brann did not wade in. [340] His granddaughter brought the creel instead, setting it on the atrium steps with both hands, the way you set down something still breathing. [363] "He is not gone," the girl said. [370] "Only finished." [372] Without tomorrow, no one in town died anymore; they simply completed, like a sentence finding its last word. [390] Inside the creel lay a coil of line, a note folded around a fistful of silver gear-filings Brann had swept from the atrium floor over the years. [417] "The filings are moon-stuff," the note read. [424] "They only hold together under a full moon." [432] "Smelt them before the dark comes back." [439]

She read it aloud to the empty hall, and the tide seemed to lean in and listen. [456] The trouble was that none of her selves knew how. [466] Mira distrusted fire; Marin would burn the stacks for a joke; the full-moon Librarian knew everything but could explain none of it; the nameless one did not believe in tools. [496] The contradiction sat in her like a swallowed stone. [505] She tried to stay one self, whole and unmoving, and that week the tide slowed, the great gears ticked off-center, and no smelt came in with the water. [533] The atrium, she finally understood, ran on contradiction: a sea kept indoors, a giant asleep but working, a woman who was four and one. [557]

On the night of the full moon, she gave up wholeness. [568] She set the filings in a clay crucible on the flooded steps, opened the ledger to all her names at once, and let every self stand there together: Mira's precision, Marin's laughter, the Librarian's calm, the nameless dark that simply watched. [609] Accepting contradictions was the only method; there was no other, and she stopped hunting for one. [625] Moonlight poured through the glass roof, and the filings softened, ran together, and refused to choose a shape. [643] Each self wanted something different: a key, a bell, a hinge, nothing at all. [657] She let the silver hold all four wants at once, and it cooled into a small bell shaped like a smelt, its fish-mouth the bell's mouth. [683]

She hung it above the atrium door. [690] When the tide returned, the smelt returned with it, thousands strong, turning the shallows to mercury under the low gold sun, and every day after, which was the same day endlessly, she met them at her steps with the creel on her back, one person or four, entirely herself. [739] On windy evenings the little bell rang, one clear note for each of her names, and the sound was not confusion at all. [762] It was a chord. [766]
