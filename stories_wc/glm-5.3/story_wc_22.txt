When the reef died, the sea went quiet first. [9] Then the boats came home empty, and then the towns went to war over the last of the fish. [28] That was the year Maren became the gloom-hill coachman, driving the dead up the black road because no one else would touch the reins. [52]

The battle itself lasted one afternoon, on the flats below the gloom-hill, and Maren carried the fallen of both sides in the same cart, learning their curses as they died. [82] Her brother was the third body she carried, and she memorized the weight of him. [97] A dying man from the other town taught her six words of his language before he finished. [114] She kept collecting after that, trading words with the wounded the way other people traded bread. [130] Now she speaks four dead languages fluently and one dying one, which is a way of saying she talks mostly to herself. [152]

Peace came strangely. [154] Neither town could bear to meet on the flats where their children lay, so the delegates dove instead, down to the white bones of the reef, and signed the treaty among the coral skeletons, each name pressed into a lead tablet and hammered to the stone. [200] They called it the Submerged Concord, and Maren drove every delegate to the water in her coach. [217] She remembered what the divers said when they surfaced: that the dead reef looked like a burned city, and that it deserved better than to be the only monument both towns had. [249]

So she made herself a promise: the flats would not stay a battlefield. [262] Let a garden reclaim it, she thought, let green have it the way the sea once had the reef. [281] She began a collection of dried seed pods from every abandoned garden on both sides of the gloom-hill, labeling each tin in whatever dead language the flower's name survived in. [311] By the tenth spring the coach's boot held more pods than the harbor had held hope: sea campion, thrift, wild carrot, mallow, poppies of a kind recorded in no living book. [342]

But the soil was salted and burned, and nothing took. [352] It was the birds who taught her what the ground needed. [363] Dead languages, she discovered, were good training for bird calls, because both reward you only if you stop expecting sentences. [383] Flocks of knot and turnstone cried over the places where the blood had soaked deepest, where the salt of it still locked the ground. [407] Gulls marked the tides of dead fish that rolled in from the empty reef, and curlews told her, in their descending grief-note, when the worms returned, meaning the soil was breathing again. [439] Listening, she learned to fertilize: fishbone and kelp ash ground into the worst places the knots named, pod chaff and rotted wrack worked into the rest, the whole bitter pharmacy of a drowned sea returned to the land it had been stolen from. [482] As she worked she recited the names of the dead in their own tongues, one name to each handful, so that even the fertilizer was a kind of translation. [511]

Tonight, in her sixty-third year, she drives the empty coach down to the jetty at twilight. [527] The jetty blooms. [530] Thrift and sea campion crowd every seam of its old planks, purple and pale, so that walking out feels like wading through a narrow meadow suspended over a vast gray sea. [561] She has come to understand that this is the true shape of the world after the reef dies: a narrow strip of tended ground, a plank's width of color, hanging above an immensity that no longer answers. [598]

Behind her, on the flats, the garden is winning. [607] Poppies stand where the fighting was thickest, and the children of both towns play there now, picking them, unaware. [626] Somewhere below the gray water, the lead tablets of the Concord have gone green with weed, which is its own kind of signature. [649] A curlew calls once from the shallows, and Maren answers it in a language no human has spoken in forty years. [670] It means, more or less: the ground is rich, the pods are sleeping, come spring we plant again. [688] The sea is vast and does not answer; the jetty is narrow and does; and on the whole, she prefers it that way. [711]
