Snow fell on the silver courtyard, and Veya crossed it without leaving footprints. [13] The flakes did not melt when they touched her, because snow here was not weather. [28] It was the lullaby made visible, the Hush the Choir sang nightly, falling silver until every difference lay buried under the same white quiet. [52] At the courtyard's heart stood the prism sanctuary, a spire of faceted glass taller than the bell tower, and starlight passed through it in slow ribbons. [78] Every echo harvested in the city lived inside it. [87]

Veya was a courtesan of the Metered House, trained until her body had become a poem. [103] Her footsteps kept time like lines of verse; her hands moved in assonance; when she bowed, clients wept without knowing why and began, helplessly, to speak. [129] Speaking was the beginning of the harvest. [136] A courtesan's trade was to gather what a voice shed: every confession, every laugh, every unguarded word, wound onto grey silk and carried to the prism. [162] People emptied of echoes slept beautifully and smiled the same smile. [173]

Veya had never taken a single echo into herself, and in the Metered House this was her scandal, her vow, her chastity. [195] The other courtesans swallowed their clients' voices whole and grew heavy with other people's lives. [210] She let nothing past her lips. [216] She wound each echo onto silk, because the Matron watched, but she memorized the face behind every thread. [234] Her mother's face was the first. [240] Eleven years ago the combers took her mother's laugh, her grief, everything but the silver smile the lullaby left behind. [260] Her mother had worn it every day since, even at the funeral for the self she used to be. [279] Tomorrow the Choir would sing the Final Verse and finish conformity's work for good: no more intervals, no gaps, no one left who remembered what a gap felt like. [308]

So tonight she had come to unlock the sanctuary. [317] The lock was not metal. [322] At the prism's base, in a silver basin, lay a knot of grey threads, thousands of them, one for every harvested citizen, snarled by the combers so that no single voice could ever be found again. [358] The founders had built the sanctuary as a vault, meant to keep echoes safe until they could be returned, and their law still held. [382] The knot yielded to no scissors and no key, only to patient fingers that had never taken anything at all. [402] For a hundred years the Choir had combed the Metered House for such fingers, and every girl who might have qualified learned quickly to swallow. [427] Her chastity had never been a virtue, exactly. [435] It was a siege. [439]

She knelt at the basin as the Choir began the night's verse, and across the city ten thousand sleepers sighed together, one long communal inhale. [464] The smile came when the chord resolved, a few heartbeats later, and in the interval between sigh and smile the doors of every emptied self stood ajar. [491] Only in that interval could an echo find its way home. [502] She worked. [504] Thread by thread she read the knot the way she had once read clients, following each silk line by touch, learning where it had been bent against its nature, easing it loose. [536] Her hands kept their meter even when her breath would not. [547] The knot fought her the way knots do, politely, with the serene insistence of things that believe they are already perfect. [568]

Sigh. [569] Her fingers moved. [572] A thread slid free, and somewhere an old man frowned in his sleep for the first time in years. [591] Smile. [592] The window closed, and she waited, snow gathering on her shoulders. [603] Sigh. [604] Two threads, then five. [608] Smile. [609] Sigh: a child three streets away woke crying and was soothed, awake, by a mother who remembered how. [627] Smile: the lullaby pulled the interval shut. [634]

There were thousands of threads and not enough sighs, so she stopped reading the knot and trusted it, as a dancer trusts the count, until her whole body was the untangling and the knot could not tell her fingers from its threads. [676] Somewhere in the silk her fingers found a thread that hummed with a laugh she had loved all her life, and she freed it first. [701] Then the knot came apart all at once, gently, like snow deciding to be snow again. [717] The prism opened with no sound at all, which was the most frightening thing Veya had ever heard, and starlight poured the echoes home through every window in the city. [747]

By morning the snow fell as weather only, and her mother woke, frowned at the white courtyard, then smiled, and the smile was her own. [772] Veya stood in the silver hush, chaste as ever, and her stillness was no longer a siege but a rest. [792]
