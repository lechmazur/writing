Midsummer arrived the way it always did in Otto's shop, with ants. [12] For thirty years he had been the town's only antique map dealer, working alone, and he had learned to read small disturbances the way other men read headlines. [40] Loneliness, he found, was mostly an inventory problem: so much world, and no one to show it to. [58] He was empirically intuitive, his wife Mara used to say, a man who trusted a thousand small observations over any grand theory. [80] So when the ants crossed his counter carrying pale petals from no flower in town, he did not sweep them away. [101] He laid Mara's last map beside their trail and compared. [111] The petals matched, exactly, the small white bloom she had drawn in the margin above the creek fork. [129] Evidence, then: petals at midsummer, a bearing southeast, and her creek fork lying southeast of town. [145]

Every line of that map was finished except its legend, one empty cartouche she had refused to fill. [163] "When the creek rises at midsummer," she had promised, "the cavern blooms, the doorway of decisions opens, and we cast the last word together." [187] A lingering promise is a heavy thing to keep in a quiet shop. [200] Mara had grown up above a type foundry, and for the legend she had cut her own matrix chip, a single brass ampersand, her small joke about their partnership. [229] She died in the spring, three years back, and the chip had lived in his vest pocket ever since, going gray like everything else he touched. [255] He had one errand left in him: to find the doorway of decisions and finish her sentence. [272]

He took it now, took his boots, and went out to follow the ants home. [287] Following ants home, it turned out, was honest work. [296] They marched along the creek, which was rising with snowmelt, brown and loud, swallowing its banks a foot at a time. [317] Her map said the cavern flooded completely by nightfall; she had drawn the waterline as a dotted threat. [335] The ants climbed a limestone bluff, slipped behind a curtain of roots, and poured into the dark. [352]

He followed, and stopped, because the cavern was blooming. [361] White flowers opened from shelves of stone, hundreds of them, petals like little lanterns, and gypsum blossoms glittered on the walls between. [383] A seam in the ceiling let down thin noon light, and the wet stone caught it and shone as if on purpose. [405] The ants streamed across the floor to the far wall, where the passage forked into two black doorways. [423] Between them, set into the rock, hung a lead strip of raised letters: STAY on the left, GO on the right, and at the center one empty socket, a matrix seat the size of a brass ampersand. [460] She had built the choice years ago and left the joining word uncast, because they were to cast it together. [480] The doorway of decisions was not two doors, he understood at last; it was the socket. [496]

Creek water had already reached the threshold, laying a skin of fine clay across the stone. [512] Kneeling, shaking, he pressed her matrix chip into that clay, and the ampersand bit deep and clean. [529] Rising water slid over it and filled the impression, and the small joined shape caught the light and began to glimmer, faint, then steady. [553] STAY ampersand GO, cast in water and light. [561] The promise had never been to choose, he saw; it had been to carry both. [576] The flood reached the two doorways at the same moment and poured into each passage equally, linking them somewhere in the dark until the cavern held one rising, joined pool. [606] Even the mountain, it seemed, kept her sense of humor. [616]

Otto stood in the bloom and the noise of water, no longer deciding anything. [630] He spread her map against his chest, and in the empty cartouche, in his careful dealer's hand, he inked the finished legend: Otto ampersand Mara. [655] Then he waded into the joined water, petals riding past his knees, and went to see what she had wanted him to see. [678] Outside, the creek kept rising, doing its patient work, folding every fork it found into one bright, unfinished river. [697]
