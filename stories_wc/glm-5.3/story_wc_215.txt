After the Unveiling, when the dead became visible and stayed silent, Marek moved to the edge of the scorched lands, where an old beekeeping oasis stood gray and hiveless under the sun. [32] He was a recluse by trade and temperament, and he had chosen the ruin precisely because no one, living or otherwise, had ever troubled it. [57] Then, on the first cold evening of autumn, two ghosts came walking out of the north between the empty hive stands, and Marek, who had not been astonished in years, stood astonished at his door. [92] The dead of the new age did not speak. [101] They appeared, they lingered, and whatever they needed they had to show, so Marek, being tranquilly perceptive, began to read them the way he read weather. [127]

The ghosts were two women in beekeeper's veils. [135] Every evening they stopped before the third hive stand, and every evening they did the same thing: one gazed at the empty wood while the other gazed at her. [164] Their silence had grammar, and Marek studied it. [172] Through contextual clues, he rebuilt the oasis. [179] In the cellar lay a ledger kept in two hands, and the two hands argued in the margins the way sisters argue, warmly, and then not. [205] The last entry broke off mid-sentence, in the season of the great burn. [218] On the third hive stand, the wood held two initials inside a carved ring, and beneath the ring a line of words had been started and then gouged away. [247] Half a fence crossed the old clover field, built and abandoned in the same summer. [262] Piece by piece, the story assembled itself in him without a single word. [275]

Two sisters had kept this place when it was green, and they had bound themselves with a covenant: every quarrel settled by dusk, everything spoken, nothing left to rot inside the mouth. [307] It was an old beekeeper's law dressed up as a promise: you tell the hive everything, deaths and weddings and griefs, or the bees sicken and go. [334] The clues said there had been one quarrel too large for dusk. [346] One sister had begun to carve her half of it into the hive and could not finish. [363] Then the burn came down out of the north, and the elder sister died in the smoke of the clover field she was trying to save, and no one told the bees, because there were no bees left to tell. [403] The younger survived, lived on alone among the dead hives, and when her time came there was no hive left to tell. [425] The covenant was tangled now: sworn to speech, delivered to silence, and the silence had outlived the bees, the clover, and the world's kindness. [449]

What held Marek was not the quarrel. [456] It was the box. [460] Beneath the third hive stand, wrapped in waxcloth, he had found a stained glass box the size of a loaf, its small panes amber, honey-gold, and a green like drowned clover. [491] Inside lay folded papers, many of them, tied with string gone brittle. [503] Letters, he understood from the shape of the folds and the wear of the corners: unsent. [519] Every word the surviving sister had sworn by covenant to speak aloud, she had written instead, year after year, and sealed away where no ear and no hive could ever reach it. [551]

On the fourth evening, Marek carried the box out and lifted it into the last light. [567] The panes caught the sun and threw color across the ash: amber bees, gold bees, green bees, swarming soundlessly over the dead hives. [590] The ghosts turned, and their gazes met his, and Marek understood what the silence had been asking all along. [609] Not to be opened. [613] Not read, not spoken, not untangled. [619] The words had weight only because they had never been said, and that weight was the last thing still holding two souls to the earth they had loved. [647] To speak them would be to spend them, and spent words weigh nothing. [660]

So Marek did the only work left. [667] He rewrapped the letters exactly as he had found them, set the stained glass box on the third hive stand, and stepped back without a word. [693] The elder sister looked at the younger, and the younger at the elder, and for the first time neither gazed at the empty wood. [717] What passed between them then had no sound and needed none. [728] They faded the way dusk fades, unhurried, together, and the colored bees faded with them. [743]

Marek stayed on at the oasis, its keeper now, though there was nothing left to keep but one box and its freight. [765] He never read the letters. [770] Some weights exist to be carried, not understood, and each evening he carried the box into the light and gazed, and kept it. [793]
