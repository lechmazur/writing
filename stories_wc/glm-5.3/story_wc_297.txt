The eclipse came forty years early, and Wren felt it in her roots before she saw it in the sky. [20] Her ankles had grown into the garden twelve winters ago, seed to soil, so the wrongness arrived through the ground first: a shiver, a tug, the whole estate leaning toward a darkness that should not arrive for decades. [58] The future had come early, and with it everything the charter feared. [70] Out in the cobalt glade, dusk had arrived early too, the light gone the color of deep water. [88]

Wren dragged her roots through the hedgerows, bark rasping on gravel, her heart hammering with something she was ashamed to call joy. [110] Fear, yes, fear was proper. [115] But under it ran a current of elation, because an eclipse was the one hour her whole life had been pointing toward, and it had waited long enough. [143] She was the estate's visionary, root-bound by choice and accident both. [154] At nine she had fallen asleep in the heart of the hedge maze, and the garden had taken root in her, and she in it; ever since, she dreamed the garden's tomorrows. [186] Those dreams always crossed another dream at a single bright point, a crossing she could never reach, and finding that intersection had become the quiet engine of her days. [215] She had been purposefully curious about it all her life, not idle wondering but deliberate mapping, sketching each night's vision in a ledger fat with ink. [241] Tonight the ledger said the crossing would be open. [250]

The estate stood on a covenant signed under an eclipse three centuries ago: the wild forest had lent this clearing to the gardeners, and the debt fell due when the moon's shadow next crossed this ground. [286] But the charter's final clause had been eclipsed, its ink swallowed by the shadow that fell as it was written, and no copy survived. [310] An eclipsed covenant, her grandmother called it, a promise with its face turned away. [324] The astronomers had sworn the shadow would not return for forty more years. [337] The astronomers were wrong, and the wild edges of the garden were already creeping inward, roses going feral, the maze's walls bristling. [359]

Her grandmother's last words had been plain: when the debt comes due, heed the scars. [374] Wren had almost dismissed it as fever talk. [382] Now she heeded it, because she had nothing else. [391] At the maze's mouth she pressed her scarred palms flat against the hedge. [404] Her hands were a ladder of old marks, shears and thorns and burns, and the yew wore its own scars, centuries of cut wood healed into pale whorls. [432] Wound to wound, the linking opened like a door. [441] She walked the turns with her palms trailing, and the hedge's memory poured through her scars: every clipping ever taken, every shape the topiary had been forced to hold, and under it all a loneliness shaped like a question. [480] It wanted to be dreamed, not only trimmed. [488]

Trembling, Wren took her shears and cut one fresh sprig from the maze's heart, a clipping shaped like a turning path. [509] In eclipse light, sap beaded on the cut stem and ran into letters. [522] There was the hidden clause, written in the forest's own ink, readable only under a shadowed sun: the debt is not land. [544] The forest had only ever asked for one shared dream, a dream that gardener and garden both carried, delivered at the crossing. [566] Three centuries of gardeners had assumed ruin and prepared for war. [577] Wren laughed, a cracked, terrified sound, because she was elated, actually elated, standing at the end of the world with the answer in her fist. [602]

The eclipse deepened. [605] She rooted herself in the glade's center, set the clipping in the soil, and laid her scarred hands over the earth's older scars, frost cracks and root lifts, linking everything she had ever tended to everything that had grown untended. [645] Her dream rose on one side, all rows and patience and the wish to be seen. [661] The wild dream rose on the other, all climb and green riot and the wish to be dreamed at all. [681] They crossed directly above her, and for one held breath she stood inside the intersection of dreams, and it was not an ending. [704] The covenant turned its face toward her at last. [713]

When the shadow passed, the glade stayed cobalt a moment longer, gently, like a hand not yet ready to lift. [733] The feral roses folded back into their beds. [741] Her roots loosened, not free, never free, but flexible now, long enough to carry her into the wild edges and home again. [763] The little clipping had already rooted, one green turn of path reaching toward a wilderness it had never been allowed to want. [785] Wren was still shaking. [789] She was still, she realized, glad. [795]
