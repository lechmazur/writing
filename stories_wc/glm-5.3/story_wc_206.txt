Corvin Pell, music box repairman of Bellharbor, lived by the covenant of rust: rust may claim everything in time, but it must wait its turn, and tinkers must mend all broken things before rust collects them. [36] It was a fair bargain, and it kept the town's music singing. [48] Every music box in Bellharbor held at its heart a chip of the philosopher's stone, too small for gold, just large enough to hold light. [73] When a lid was lifted, a small dawn rose out of the gears. [86] Then Midnight came, and the dawns went out. [94]

He slipped into the tower on the last night of autumn and stole the balance wheel from the great clock, and with it every lamp, every hearth, every stone chip's gleam, gathered into his coat like moths. [131] Without the wheel the hour could not turn; the town hung at one minute to midnight, in the balance, and stayed there. [153] Days that never dawned make liars of honest people, and Corvin, watching neighbors swear the dark was fine, pinned his one treasure to his coat: his grandmother's forget-me-not, carved from a chip of the philosopher's stone, which wilts near dishonesty. [193] All week it drooped. [197]

So Corvin did what his family had always done when something loved was lost: he went to mend it. [216] Not with pliers, though he carried them, and not with solder; his grandmother had taught him the family's true method, mending what isn't torn. [240] Hearts, hours, promises; these have no seams, but they split. [250] Midnight kept his hoard in the resonance chamber under the tower, the hollow where every song ever played in Bellharbor still echoed, layered a thousand deep. [276] Corvin climbed down inside the dark, and the walls hummed his grandmother's lullabies back at him, and his fear forgot itself. [297]

Midnight sat in the middle of the chamber on a stool of stillness, wearing all the light in town like a heavy coat, and it did not suit him; it sagged. [328] "Nobody up there is missing anything," Midnight said. [336] The forget-me-not wilted flat against Corvin's lapel. [343] "That's a lie, then," Corvin said gently. [350] Midnight flinched, and the stolen glow flinched with him. [359] "I take only what morning abandons," he tried. [367] The flower drooped lower. [371]

Corvin set down his toolbox and looked at the great tangled coat of light, at the balance wheel clutched in one pale fist, and saw nothing torn, nothing cracked, nothing broken. [402] "There it is," he said. [407] "Nothing here needs repair, thief." [412] "Then why have you come?" Midnight asked, and for once his voice was only curious, and the forget-me-not lifted its head. [433] "Because I mend what isn't torn," said Corvin. [441] "You are not broken, friend; you are out of covenant." [451] He told him then, above the murmur of a thousand stored songs, how rust had promised to deliver all things in its own patient hour, and tinkers had promised to keep mending until then, so that nothing was ever stolen, only lent. [493] "Every dawn will dim to you honestly, in time," he said. [504] "You never needed to take anything." [510]

Midnight sat with that. [514] The chamber's thousand songs thickened, listening. [520] "It is very quiet, being midnight," he said at last, and the flower, testing the words, stood straight and even brightened, for it was the truest sentence ever spoken in that dark. [552] So Corvin opened his toolbox and did his gentlest work. [562] He drew a thread of brass from an old song string and made a tether, fine as spider silk, and tied one end to the balance wheel and one end to the town's oldest music box, the one in the tower window that had played dawn awake for two hundred years. [613] "Light on a tether," he said. [619] "It can visit you, but it must come home." [628] And because a mended thing should sing, he set the wheel back into the great clock with his own hands, and it turned. [651]

The minute gave up its hovering; the hour tipped out of the balance; midnight struck twelve soft strokes, like a music box clearing its throat. [676] Light poured upward out of the heavy coat and ran home along the tether, lamp to lamp, chip to chip, and Bellharbor's windows lit like a struck match. [704] Dawn followed the light the way a song follows its first note. [716] Midnight stood lighter, almost translucent, wearing only his own proper dark. [727] Corvin pressed into his hands a small music box with a chip of stone at its heart, tuned, of course, to play only at twelve. [752] "So midnight won't be quiet anymore," he said. [760] And every night after, when the hour hung in the balance before tipping, the tower played one small song into the dark, and rust, patient as promised, waited its turn. [790]
