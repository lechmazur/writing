Pell was the meekest blacksmith on Ciderhold, an orchard planet where rock arches floated above the apple rows like the ribs of a sky that had lain down to rest. [30] He had hammered horseshoes and hinge plates for twenty years without once raising his voice, and the town loved him the way it loved rain, useful and easy to forget. [60] Then the Deepvein mine collapsed, taking eleven men and loosening the anchor stone that held the eastern arch in place. [80] The arch began to drift, and the orchard beneath it yellowed, fruit falling early, branches curling like burned paper. [99] The town council voted to abandon the shafts and replant west, and Pell, who never argued, said nothing, and hated himself for it. [122] That was the year the magician Ysolde returned to Ciderhold, trading awakenings from the back of a wagon drawn by a patient gray mule. [146] She did not sell them; she bartered. [153] A widow once traded three years of grief for an awakening that let her taste her husband's cooking again without weeping. [174] A soldier traded his nightmares and woke for a week unable to remember why he had ever flinched. [192] Pell waited in line all morning with his cap in his hands, and when his turn came he said, quietly, that he wanted to buy courage. [218] Ysolde laughed, not unkindly. [222] "Courage is not for sale," she said, "because it is not a thing you can hold." [238] "But your meekness is, and it is worth more than you think." [250] Pell considered the twenty years of yielded arguments, the orders taken, the apologies offered for faults that were never his. [270] "Take it," he said. [274] She drew a square of azure silk from her sleeve, fine as smoke, and held it out. [291] When Pell breathed on it, the silk drank something out of him, a heaviness he had carried so long he had mistaken it for his spine. [317] In exchange, an awakening entered him like cold water: he suddenly understood the Deepvein shafts, every gallery and fault line, the anchor keystone that had slipped, and the exact timbers that would hold if a man drove them true. [356] "Understand," Ysolde said, "that clarity fades." [362] "You have the space between understanding and confusion, no more than that." [374] "What the silk takes, the silk keeps, unless you ask me kindly." [386] Pell did not go home to think. [393] He went to the forge, deliberately, and loaded his wagon with timber, iron wedges, a sledge, and lamp oil, working with a quiet determination that surprised his own hands. [422] The abandoned mine shafts stood behind a fence of warning ribbons, and he cut through them at dusk. [440] Inside, the dark was total, but the silk, folded in his breast pocket, gave off a faint azure glow that defied it. [462] He walked the galleries by that light, past the rubble of the collapse, past a boot, past a hammer someone had dropped and never reclaimed. [487] The understanding still burned in him, but he could feel confusion waiting at its edges, patient as rust. [505] At the failed keystone he set his timbers with a blacksmith's precision, drove the wedges until the mountain groaned and settled, and packed the brace tight with fallen stone, coaxing strength out of the rubble itself. [541] He did not leave when the understanding began to blur. [551] He left when the last timber held, and not one breath before. [563] Above ground, dawn was coming up azure over the drifting arch, and as Pell watched, the great rock shivered, righted itself, and hung steady for the first time in three years. [594] By midsummer the eastern orchard had greened, and children who had never eaten a ripe Ciderhold apple ate them with juice running down their wrists. [619] Ysolde came back with the spring thaw, found Pell at his forge, and asked whether he wanted his meekness returned. [639] "No," he said, hammering, and it was not rudeness, only fact. [650] The silk still hung by his anvil, faded but still azure, still defiant, holding the shape of the man he had been. [672] Ysolde studied it, and him, and the orchards green beneath their patient arches. [685] "What did you buy with all that yielding, blacksmith?" [694] "Resilience," he said, turning a finished shoe in the coals. [704] "It was sitting in the rubble all along, waiting for somebody meek enough to go down and get it." [723]
