Vessa Oru was the last lore keeper of the Spindle, and for sixty years the throne of the Meridian had stood unclaimed above the city's head. [26] The stairway of ascension, which every claimant once had to climb, was lost the night the Spindle cracked, and the council of stewards preferred it lost. [52] But Vessa was wisely adventurous, which is a rarer thing than either half of it alone. [68]

In the archive vault sat a ruby flake no bigger than a fingernail, catalogued for sixty years as provenance unknown. [88] Her grandmother had matched it at last to the founding drawings, which showed the door-star of the claimants' mural cut from the heart-stone of the throne, so that the throne's heart would light the way up. [124] The star had been pried out during the Fracture to end the age of claimants, and had been hiding in a mislabeled drawer ever since. [149] Grandmother found the drawings but never the door; she died leaving Vessa one instruction: find the stairway of ascension. [168]

Her sally began at dusk, when the coolant pumps switched to night duty and the old cascade woke in color. [188] Behind the kaleidoscopic waterfall, where prismatic filters turned falling water into slow-turning stained glass, lay a hidden orchard of seed-vault apples planted by the first colonists and forgotten by everyone after. [219] She had until sunrise, when the pumps changed over, the color died, and the orchard gate sealed under tons of plain gray water. [242] So she counted her water, checked her lamp, tested the vine ladder twice, and went through the falling light anyway. [262] The apples hung like paper lanterns, red going gold going green, and the orchard smelled of a future her grandparents had been promised. [285]

At the far end stood the claimants' hall, a maintenance shed really, painted over by every generation that had wanted to forget the ascent. [309] Vessa knelt with a conservator's scalpel and began to peel. [319] The first layer came away in curls of corporate teal, the confidence of the second expansion. [335] Beneath it lay the mourning gray of the plague decade, then the chrome of the boom years, then paint so old it had gone to powder. [361] Through the peel of paint layers, hidden murals emerged: a procession of claimants winding up a spiral stair beneath a sky of small red stars. [386] Every star was a dot of ruby pigment except one, high in the mural's corner, where the wall showed a clean empty socket. [409]

Vessa's hands were shaking as she pressed the ruby flake into the door-star's place. [423] It fit the way a word fits the end of a sentence you have carried for years. [440] The wall sighed along the procession's painted feet, and a seam of gold light opened. [455] Beyond it, the stairway of ascension rose through the dark of the anchor tower, worn silver at the edges by ten thousand climbing feet, as the drawings promised. [483] Vessa laughed once, quietly, the way you laugh when your grandmother turns out to be right about everything. [501] Then she propped the seam open with her water flask, because a wisely adventurous woman leaves herself a way home, and began to climb. [525]

The stair was old but honest, and she tested every third step, slowly, all the way up. [542] Through the tower's windows the night rolled over, stars thinning into a rim of warm steel, and the cascade's hum below changed pitch as the pumps readied themselves for morning. [572] Sunrise was coming, and it was coming for her. [581] Her thighs burned and her lamp died and she rationed her courage like the rest of her supplies, in small sensible amounts. [603] The last door was stiff with sixty years of rust; the sky behind her had gone from black to bruise to rose. [625] She put her shoulder to it, the dawn put its weight behind hers, and the door gave. [642]

The throne room at the crown of the Spindle was small and east-facing, and the unclaimed throne stood in the first light like a chair waiting for someone expected. [671] Its heart-stone, the great ruby of the Meridian, wore the wound where the door-star had been cut at the founding, and Vessa understood the architects at last. [698] They had made the throne incomplete on purpose, and set its missing heart down among the orchards and the archives, so that no one could rule without being handed a piece of the throne by the people who kept it. [738] She did not sit. [742] A lore keeper keeps. [746] Instead she threw open the shutters, and sixty years of dust rose gold in the sun, and far below the city stopped to stare at the light burning in the crown windows. [778] The throne stood unclaimed, but no longer lost. [786]
