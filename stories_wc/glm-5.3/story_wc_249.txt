The clinic called them pioneers; the consent form called them subjects; Mara called herself what she was, a guinea pig with a port in her spine. [26] The experimental treatment was meant to defeat forgetting: entangle the memories of twelve patients into one shared lattice, so that whatever one mind lost, eleven others could return. [54] It worked, mostly. [57] It also worked on things the consent form never mentioned, like the night Ivo, subject seven, described a lagoon he had dreamed of since boyhood, gold as struck brass, cradled under starlight, and by morning every one of them remembered dreaming it too. [100] Then Ivo's heart failed, and the lattice did what it was built to do: it kept him. [117] His memories lived on in eleven heads, braided with theirs. [127] The clinic called it contamination. [132] They issued suppression pills to quiet the dead man's echoes and scheduled a pruning, a flush of the lattice, for the first week of autumn. [157]

Mara nodded at every appointment, pocketed every pill, and poured every pill down the drain. [172] She had never been loud about anything in her life, but she could be quiet the way a fuse is quiet. [193] Her rebellion had a method: drumming on everything. [201] She had learned it by accident, drumming her fingers on the clinic's waiting-room table on the day of Ivo's funeral, when his memory of a mountain childhood bloomed behind her eyes so vividly she wept in public. [238] Rhythm steadied the entangled memories, she found, let her hold one at a time like a card held to lamplight. [258] She drummed on bus windows, on countertops, on the rail of the footbridge, on her own sternum, sorting his life from hers, learning him. [282]

The sound wave garden sat on the reclaimed flood plain at the city's edge, an acre of hollow reeds, tuned stones, and copper bells planted in patterns that turned touch into visible waves, ripples of light chasing every strike. [321] Children came to bang the bells and acousticians came to chart the standing waves, but Mara came because it was the one place loud enough, and kind enough, to hold what she was planning. [355]

The other subjects met her there in the last hour before dawn, the hour the old caretaker named the hush of the universe inhaling, when even the wind seems to draw breath and the whole garden goes still enough to hear a heartbeat at fifty paces. [401] They had all stopped taking the pills. [408] They had all kept pieces of Ivo. [415] The eldest, a retired ferryman, carried his fear of deep water; the engineer carried his laugh; Mara carried the dream, the auric lagoon under starlight, so bright behind her eyes some nights that she mistook it for her own. [454] That was the cruelty of it: grief replaced by a stranger's homesickness for a place none of them had ever stood. [475] So Mara had proposed the plan, and they had agreed, because the only thing worse than carrying Ivo's dream would be letting the clinic prune it out like a fault. [505] Before autumn, before the flush, they would breathe life into the shared dream: give it one night of being real. [525]

At the hush, when the universe inhaled, they took their places. [536] Mara perched on the rim of the great stone basin at the garden's center, closed her eyes, and waited until she could see the lagoon perfectly: brass-gold water, stars floating whole on its surface, Ivo's boyhood joy hanging over it like an aurora. [579] Then she began to drum. [584] Palms on stone, heels on the basin's lip, knuckles on the copper bells the others held up to her one by one; she drummed the water's hush, the slap of oars Ivo had never owned, the heartbeat under the dream, and the garden answered. [628] Waves of gold light rolled outward through the tuned reeds, lapped at the stones, gathered in the basin until the whole acre shimmered like water at night, and ten others stood ankle-deep in light, inside a dead man's dearest dream, breathing it into being. [672] The ferryman wept; the engineer laughed Ivo's laugh; Mara kept drumming, her sorrow lit from beneath like the northern sky, because this was the bargain she had made with herself: one night, one whole night, and then she would let them prune. [714]

At first light the universe exhaled, the gold drained out of the reeds, and the lagoon went back to being a dream in eleven heads, borrowed, mortal, and soon to be pruned. [746] Mara stayed perched on the rim until the bells went cold. [757] It was enough, she told herself, and almost believed: a dream does not have to live forever to have lived. [777]
