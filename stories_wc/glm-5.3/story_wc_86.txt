The parapet had been storm-worn for three centuries, and the mist tonight made it look like something drowned. [18] Wind combed the stones with a sound like held breath. [28] Marek Voss climbed the last stair into that furrowed hush and felt, as always, the crossing of a threshold. [47] Martan Tower was the only neutral territory two warring kingdoms trusted, a ruin that belonged to neither because the sea had claimed it first. [71] He had negotiated eleven prisoner exchanges on this windblown parapet, and he was still alive because he was uncommonly thoughtful, a trait quicker men mistook for slowness. [98]

He kept a ledger of every failure: the miscounted exchange that left a man chained an extra winter, the rain-slick night a runner bolted, the handshake offered too soon. [127] Each mistake he ground down and reforged into a rule; that was his whole craft, recycling error into wisdom. [146] Rule twelve read: never trust a clock, trust the moon. [156] The kingdoms kept different hours and different bells, but the moon belonged to no one, like the tower. [174]

Every exchange was synchronized through the moon's phases: tonight, when the crescent cleared the eastern cleft, both sides would cut their ropes in the same breath, and no one could cheat the dark. [207] Twelve prisoners came up the southern stair, wrists tethered, and twelve came up the northern. [222] Across the parapet stood Ives, the other kingdom's negotiator, gray as the mist, patient as stone. [238] Neither spoke. [240]

The armistice signed three springs ago was an apology, carefully worded, but forgiveness was still being drafted in rooms far from here, and everyone on the parapet stood inside that gap, between the sorrow said aloud and the sorrow finally believed. [281] Marek walked the line of his own prisoners slowly, checking knots, checking faces. [294] At the end of the row he stopped. [302] Tam Brann. [304] Exchange seven; the miscount; the extra winter in the pit at Dren. [316] Marek's own arithmetic, Marek's own mistake, and the man had never once cursed him for it, which was worse. [335] Tonight Brann would walk free last, and Marek had spent a year deciding what that meant. [351]

In his coat pocket, worn smooth as a river stone, he carried an antique drawer pull, brass scrolled into the shape of a small oak leaf. [377] It had come off his mother's desk, the drawer where she kept the unfinished treaty, the forgiveness she drafted and died before she could sign. [402] The drawer had stuck for years; the pull had come away in her hand the week before the fever took her; she had laughed and called it a lesson. [431] "What sticks is not locked," she said. [438] "A drawer that resists still means to open." [446] He had carried it ever since, the way other men carried charms, though it was not luck he was hoarding. [466]

The moon climbed. [469] Mist thinned around it, and the crescent slid toward the cleft like a blade being drawn slow. [486] When it cleared the rock, twenty-four knives moved at once, and twenty-four tethers fell into the wind. [503] The freed prisoners crossed each other in the mist, north-bound and south-bound, none of them running, all of them glancing back. [524] Marek untethered the last rope himself, Brann's rope, and pressed the brass pull into the man's cold palm. [542] "For the winter I owe you," he said. [550] "My mother pulled it off a drawer that stuck for ten years." [562] "What sticks is not locked." [567]

Brann turned the pull over in the moonlight, brow furrowed, and did not answer. [581] That was right, Marek thought. [586] Forgiveness was not a thing to be handed over on a parapet like a token; it was a thing that came later, or never, in its own weather. [614] Brann only nodded once, closed his fist, and walked north into the mist. [627]

When the parapet was empty, Ives crossed the stones and stood beside him at the low wall. [644] "The treaty commission convenes next month," Ives said. [652] "They want you at the table." [658] Marek looked out at the moon, which owed nothing to either kingdom and lit both of them anyway. [676] He thought of the ledger in his coat, every error annealed into a rule, and of his mother's stubborn drawer. [696] "Send them my mistakes," he said. [702] "There's wisdom in them yet." [707] Ives almost smiled, and the two men stood a while longer in the hush, on ground that belonged to no one, halfway between the apology signed and the forgiveness still being written. [739]
