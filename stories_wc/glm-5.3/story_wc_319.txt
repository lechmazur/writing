The garden had been frozen so long that the guardian no longer remembered green, only the idea of it, kept safe behind its ribs. [24] It prowled the library's rim each night, feral and patient, ink-black fur stiff with frost, eyes like two reading lamps in the mist. [47] The books called it the Warden, when they bothered to speak at all. [60] Long ago, cultists had pulled it through a door of light to guard the library they had stolen, and then the cultists had died, as thieves do. [87] They had frozen the garden as a seal, so nothing could follow them back through. [102] Only the Warden stayed, and its homesickness, which had teeth. [112] It was homesick the way a severed hand is homesick, dimensionally, for the body of a world it could not touch. [133] In its home dimension there had been no boundaries of light, only light without shores. [148] Here, light had edges, dawn and dusk, and the Warden had learned that edges could be doors, if you held the right key. [171]

So it waited. [174] Waiting was one craft. [178] The other was this: a library is the outside world folded inward, page by page, and the Warden had continued that work on itself. [202] It drank the mist and kept it. [209] It memorized the frost patterns on every dead rose and held the garden's lost summer in its chest, where no cold could reach. [232] The exterior world froze; the interior one did not. [241] In the first years the homesickness had nearly made it mad, and it had chosen the only cure it could find: it set out to make friends with uncertainty. [270] It learned to sit beside the question of ever returning without flinching, the way one sits beside a strange dog until it stops growling. [294]

On the night the mist moved wrong, the Warden was already crouched at the garden's edge, breath silvering, patient as punctuation. [315] A figure came walking through the veil of sleep, the way the old cultists had, one foot in a dream and one in the frost. [340] He was the last of them, old and gray, and at his throat hung a pendant of tarnished silver, an eye inside a sunburst, the very key that had opened the door. [372] The Warden growled, and the growl sounded like pages turning. [382] "Easy," the old man said, raising both hands, though his voice shook with cold or guilt. [398] "I came to give it back." [404]

He lifted the pendant over his head and held it out, and it drank the fog's gray light and returned a thin gold thread of something older. [431] Through that thread the Warden smelled home: unbounded light, warm as a mother, and every lonely decade rose up in its throat at once. [455] "At dawn the boundary thins," the old man said. [464] "The pendant can open the way home for the one who was pulled through. [478] As for me, I will lie down in the garden I killed, which is fair." [493] The Warden circled him slowly, and the truth circled with it: an open door swings both ways. [510] Whatever came through behind it, into the mist, into the library with its ten thousand sleeping doors of paper, would not smell like home. [534]

The Warden looked at the pendant, at the old man, at the east, where the sky was thinning from black to iron. [556] Certainty stood right there in the frost, holding out its key. [567] But uncertainty had been its friend for a hundred winters, and you do not trade a friend for a door. [587] Dawn came the way it always did here, sideways, a gray line leaking gold along the garden's eastern wall. [606] The boundary thinned; the pendant hummed like a struck glass; the air smelled of shores. [621] The Warden stepped forward, and the old man closed his eyes to hear the door open at last. [639] Instead, gently, teeth careful as a scholar's hands, it took the pendant from him and swallowed it whole. [657] A door that exists inside a body can only open inward. [668]

The old man laughed once, softly, at the trick or the mercy of it, and lay down among the frozen roses. [689] The Warden waited beside him through the last of the night, until the dream that had carried him returned and drew him back, deeper, through the veil of sleep. [718] Only frost remained, and the pendant's faint warmth under the Warden's fur, a swallowed key. [733] That night it slept, and through the veil of sleep it walked on light without shores, and woke with the taste of home still gold on its tongue. [761] Now it waits at every dusk and dawn, the two edges of the light, key inside, door inside, home inside, letting the mist teach it, one evening at a time, everything it does not yet know. [797]
