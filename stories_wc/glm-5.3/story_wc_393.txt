Rain hadn't started yet, though the sky over Halstead Street had gone the color of wet slate, and the only light left fell into Kessler's Repairs in long, slanted bands. [30] The shop smelled of lemon oil and old spruce, and beneath the glass counters lay cylinders and combs and tiny brass flywheels, each one labeled in faded copperplate. [58] Somewhere in the back, a disc music box played two bars of something waltz-like and wound down, and the quiet afterward felt colorful, almost loud. [83] Chaplain Amos Price stood inside the door, hat in both hands, a shoebox held to his chest like something breathing. [103] A woman worked at the far bench, loupe at her eye, and everything about her was unhurried, aptly deliberate, as if haste were a kind of dishonesty. [130] "You're Kessler," Amos said. [134] "Ruth Kessler. [136] Nobody else winds a thing in here." [143] He set the shoebox on her bench and lifted the lid. [154] Inside, on a bed of torn toilet paper, lay a heart of brass and steel no bigger than a plum, its surfaces filed bright, its gears exposed like ribs. [183] "It's a clockwork heart," he said. [189] "It beats when you wind it." [195] "So I see. [198] Whoever made this had twelve years of patience and one good file." [210] "Fourteen years. [212] His name was Eldon Marsh. [217] He built this in the penitentiary workshop where I'm the chaplain." [228] Ruth's hands went still for the length of one breath, then resumed. [240] He told it plainly, because plainness was all he had. [250] Eldon Marsh had walked into a gas station with a shaking pistol at forty-one and left behind a daughter who returned every letter he sent, unopened, for eleven years. [279] In the workshop he went back to lathes and escapements, and the letters she never answered became gears instead. [298] Amos had his own second opinion to account for: the parole board saw a number, the guards saw a lifer, and for his first year Amos himself had seen the same thing. [330] Then he'd watched Eldon spend a month filing a single cam so a cylinder would play three notes true, and revised his opinion of the man entirely. [357] Then came the diagnosis, six weeks, and a last request. [367] "He asked me to bring her this," Amos said. [376] "Not to explain it. [380] Just this." [382] Eldon had left no address, only the trade he'd taught her at his bench before the robbery, music box repair, and so Amos was working down a list of every such shop in three counties. [417] "A jeweler on Fifth told me it was ruined, mainspring kinked, escapement bent, and frankly, Reverend, not worth a repair." [437] "So you came for a second opinion," Ruth said. [446] "I came because an apology that never leaves the mouth is just wind," he said. [461] "I want to turn his into action before it dies with him. [473] She deserves at least that." [478] Ruth lifted the heart and weighed it, thumb against the winding post. [490] Outside, thunder rolled somewhere over the river, and the light dimmed to brown, and she waited. [506] "You check it in this?" Amos asked. [513] "By the way light breaks through storm clouds," she said. [523] "Flat light lies less. [527] It shows every file mark, every crack. [534] When the sun tears through after thunder, I hold the work in it and the metal tells me what's true." [554] The storm arrived while she worked, drumming the awning, silvering the window. [565] Amos watched her smooth the kinked spring with a horn stick and true the escapement a hair at a time, in no hurry whatsoever, the shop around her humming its vibrant hush. [597] She turned the key three half-turns. [603] Then the clouds split, and low gold light broke across the bench, and she tilted the little heart into it. [623] The gear train caught. [627] The heart ticked once, then found its rhythm, a small brass pulse, sixty beats to the minute, steady as a man keeping his word at last. [653] "It runs," she said, and her voice had gone rough. [663] It was still, for another breath, the calm before realization. [673] When she lifted the back plate at last, she found engraving on the inner frame, filed letter by letter where only a repairer would ever look: FOR R., WHO DESERVED BETTER THAN ME. [706] Amos cleared his throat and reached for his hat. [715] "Where do I find her?" he asked. [722] "The daughter." [724] Ruth set the clockwork heart down in the band of breaking light, between them, still beating. [740] "Reverend," she said, "you've found her." [746]
