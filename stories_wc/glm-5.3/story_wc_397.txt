I went abroad to die the way other people go for wine or surgery. [14] They call us euthanasia tourists, though we come for the opposite of sightseeing. [27] The clinic gave me a white room and a bitter syrup, and I drank it gratefully. [43] Three days later I woke up changed. [50] The doctors called it rare, a reprieve with no return address. [61] Something in me had loosened where it used to hold on tight. [73] I grip reality loosely now, the way you hold a hand you no longer need to keep. [90] Sounds pass through me instead of stopping. [97]

So when the northern coast flooded and the disaster relief agencies asked for volunteers, I gave my hands, because I wanted to learn the sound of approaching endings. [125] Mine had come for me once, and I had slept straight through it. [138] I did not intend to miss it twice. [146]

They stationed me at the drift-ice marina, where the relief boats wintered over. [159] In March the floes ride down with the current, and the whole harbor clinks like a wind chime the size of a town. [182] That was the year the surge took the lower streets and thirty-one people with them. [197] Our work was simple and unspeakable: we trawled. [205] Each morning we dragged the gray water for what the flood had kept: roof tiles, outboard motors, a child's sled still strung with bells. [229] Some days the nets brought up things we wrapped in canvas and carried in silence, silence being the only ceremony we had left. [252]

I listened while I worked. [257] That was what I was really there for, under the volunteer tabard and the rubber gloves. [273] Endings, I discovered, are not silent. [279] They have weather. [282] A marriage ends days before anyone speaks, in the sound of a door not quite closed. [298] A town ends in the creak of pilings a week before the water rises. [312] Because I grip the world loosely, these sounds pass through me with less friction than through the others, who brace against everything. [334] Grief is loud; gone is very quiet. [341] I could hear which drowned houses were grieving and which were simply gone, and where the gone were resting. [360] Twice I asked the crews to turn the nets, and twice the canvas bundles came up, and no one asked me how I knew. [384]

The old woman at slip nine waited every day at the rail, patient as a mooring post. [401] Her name was Ingrid. [405] Her sister Marta had been swept out of the gift shop they ran together, the one with lace in the window. [426] "I can wait," Ingrid told me. [432] "Our mother waited through the war for our father. [441] Patience runs in us like a grain in wood." [450] But I could hear her patience folding, crease by crease, the way cloth folds and refolds until it thins at the seams. [472] A fold in patience makes no sound you could record, but it changes the shape of the person waiting. [491] By April, Ingrid stood crooked, like a letter folded once too often. [503]

On the ninth of April I woke before the klaxons with the sea talking in my teeth, and I told the boats to trawl east of the breakwater. [531] The nets came up heavy. [536] Wrapped in canvas was Marta, at rest, at last. [545] Tangled beside her, silk gone to pulp, was the heirloom lace parasol their grandmother had carried across the sea a century ago, its bone tip still carved with a rose, still wearing its knot of lace like a small white thought. [586] I carried the tip back on the tender and put it into Ingrid's hands at slip nine. [603] She closed her fingers around it, and I heard the last crease settle, not the sound of an ending but of a fold coming finally to rest, which is a different thing. [635]

"She kept it for the christenings," Ingrid said. [643] "Every child in this family was shaded by it once. [653] It tipped toward every beginning we ever had." [661] Then she looked at me with eyes gone clear as shelf ice. [673] "You're the one who hears things," she said. [681] "Is my waiting over?" [685] "The boats are bringing her in now," I said, "gently, with the ice to carry her." [701]

Out past the breakwater the wind moved through the turning floes, carrying warmth from the open sea, and the whole harbor rang. [723] That was when I understood what I had come to learn. [734] Endings do not sound like doors or drums. [742] They sound like a change of wind, solace carried a long way and set down softly at your back. [761] Mine is still out there somewhere, patient as Ingrid, folding itself for the journey. [775] When it comes, I will be listening. [782]
