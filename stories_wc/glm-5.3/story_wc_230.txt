The storm arrived sideways, as storms did in the ravine, and the hanging orchard swayed on its cables like a congregation arguing with the wind. [25] Mara counted anchor wires from her ladder, thirty-one, all taut, because counting was the one prayer she still trusted. [44] She was a truffle forager, a rational one, which after the empathy virus was nearly a contradiction. [61] The virus had swept the valley years ago, leaving survivors who felt their neighbors' grief through plaster walls. [79] It had also left Mara involuntarily truthful: the day the fever broke, her ability to lie broke with it. [98] Ask her a question and her mouth answered honestly before her judgment could intervene. [112] So she lived among oak roots and truffles, where nothing asked her anything. [125]

Her sister Lark lived in the cellar, because the virus had hit her hardest; other people's sadness poured into her like water into a cracked jug. [151] Mara's whole motive, the quiet architecture of every careful day, was to cultivate a patch of psychological safety, an acre where Lark could climb into the light without drowning. [180]

The smuggler arrived at dusk, half-carried by the flood-swollen stream, clutching a book to his chest. [196] It was a plague-era manual of rumored cures, one of the discredited textbooks the Ministry of Calm had ordered burned: snow baths, fox-grease salves, sleeping overnight in empty churches. [225] The Ministry called such books false hope and collected them anyway, which was why they traveled hidden. [242] "Take it to the orchard keeper," the smuggler said, and then, because he too had survived the virus and could soften nothing, "I'm dying, and I'm afraid, and I've already failed twice." [274] Inside the book's carved-out pages lay an antique glass eye, hand-blown, its iris a storm-blue spiral. [290] It had belonged to the Weeper, a nurse who pulled her own infected eye during the plague and held the dying with the one she had left. [317] Rumor said whoever wore it felt only their own heart, a shield against everyone else's. [332] The Ministry called that rumor heresy. [338] Either way it was a smuggled relic, and it was hers to hide. [351] The smuggler died before dawn, and she let the stream take him back, because a grave was a question she could not answer untruthfully. [375]

Here rationality earned its keep. [380] Mara could not lie to an inspector; the words would betray her mid-sentence. [393] But lying was not the only tool. [400] One could also occlude. [404] She had read the surviving textbooks years ago, and they all agreed on one rumored cure: bury the sick beneath the roots of an old oak, where the ground keeps what it is given and tells nothing. [441] Nonsense as medicine. [444] Sound engineering. [446] Truffles already proved the principle: the oak's root web held scent, moisture, and secrets with equal patience. [463] The storm had loosened the soil beneath her oldest tree, and when the flood withdrew it left a root-hollow the size of a fist. [487] She sealed the eye in beeswax, wrapped it in spore-dark cloth, and pressed it into the hollow, packing soil and leaf-rot over it until the ground occluded it completely. [516] Then she waited for the inspectors with clean hands and an empty mind. [529]

They came that morning, gray coats dark with rain, following the stream because floods carried smugglers like timber. [548] "What are you hiding?" the senior one asked. [556] The truth rose in her like floodwater. [563] "Grief," she said. [566] "My sister's, mostly." [569] "It's all I keep." [573] He flinched, because even Ministry men had caught the virus, however loudly they denied it. [588] His detector swept the orchard and found fungus, rain, wire, oak, nothing that had ever been rumored to cure anything. [608] He left her a citation for unregulated nostalgia, and she thanked him honestly, because his leaving was a genuine relief. [628]

That night she buried the emptied textbook beneath the oldest root and set the eye in a hollow knot of the old oak, at head height, a single blue window that kept out every heart but the wearer's own. [667] She carried Lark up the ladder at dawn. [675] "It will occlude the noise," Mara said, and no correction rose in her throat, which was the nearest thing to certainty the virus had left her. [701] Lark pressed the glass eye to her own and, for the first time in six years, looked at her sister without weeping for strangers. [725]

The orchard hung on. [729] The storms kept battering the ravine, wires singing, trees swaying their slow argument with the wind. [745] And beneath the oldest root the ground kept what it was given and told nothing, which is one rumored cure the discredited books got right. [770]
