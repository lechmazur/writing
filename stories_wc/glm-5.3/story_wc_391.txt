The night the lake froze from shore to shore, Mara climbed the basalt sanctuary's stair, lantern low, breath ragged with wanting. [21] Twelve winters she had mapped this horizon, and impatience still gnawed her like water gnaws stone. [37] That was the bargain Old Sef had set her: sculpt patience from impatience, or be useless to the work. [56] So each night she chiseled her urgency into watchfulness, plotting lanterns on far hills, patroller schedules, the moods of ice. [76] From the sanctuary's columned mouth she could see everything: the frozen lake laid out like hammered tin, and beyond it the viaduct. [98] The Company had raised it of black iron to carry ore across the water, and its girders held a strange magnetism. [119] After rain the thing woke; needles spun, chains swung toward it, iron tools crept across planks as if called. [138] Near it no compass could be trusted, which was why the shore people had learned another way. [155] They steered by moon paths: the ribbon of light the moon laid on water, and on ice, a road no iron could bend. [178] Mara had charted those paths for years, each arc inked in her ledgers, a scripture of silver lines. [196] Tonight the moon rose full, and the path it threw across the frozen lake ran straight as a rail to the far shore. [219] Behind her, boots crunched on basalt. [225] Old Sef came up behind her, moving like a man who had all the time in the world and meant to spend it well. [249] "It's time," he said. [253] She had waited two years for those two words, and they landed in her like a swallowed coal. [271] Below, at the viaduct's near footing, the ore drays stood in the cold. [284] Forty teams of horses were hitched to wagons of black stone, waiting for a morning train that would drag the mountain's heart away forever. [308] The drivers slept till dawn in the Company barracks. [317] The plan was Sef's, and it was patient the way glaciers are patient. [330] No fires, no broken windows, no shouted oaths. [338] For a year the shore families had quietly moved grain, tools, and grandmothers to the far side. [355] All they lacked was the horses, and the one night the ice would carry them. [370] "A revolution you can win by waiting," Sef called it. [380] Mara had hated the waiting. [385] In autumn she had wanted to cut the tack lines and scatter the herds in one reckless night. [403] Sef had only asked what the wild would gain by being spent all at once. [418] "Restrained," he said, "is not the same as tamed." [427] She had turned that over every night since, like a stone in a river. [441] Now she went down the stair ahead of him, softly purposeful, her knife already loose in its sheath. [459] The viaduct was still slick from three days of rain, its iron humming faintly, drinking the moon. [476] The blade felt small in her hand as she cut the first harness trace. [490] Unyoking forty teams was slow, respectful work: unbuckle, lift, set each wooden yoke down easy so nothing rang. [508] The horses breathed steam and leaned into her shoulder, trusting her the way the shore had learned to trust the moon. [529] Others arrived out of the dark, sixty quiet people, and the work went quickly. [543] When the last yoke was lifted clear, Sef nodded once, and they led the horses onto the lake. [561] The moon path lay before them, a pale road running from their boots to the far shore, and Mara walked first because she knew it best. [587] She counted her paces, watched the ice flex, kept the ribbon of light centered under her heart. [604] Behind her came horses, whispers, and the creak of a hundred careful steps. [617] Halfway across, a shout rose from the barracks side, then another, and lanterns stamped along the viaduct. [634] The patrollers ran out onto the iron with their compasses out. [645] She could imagine the needles trembling, ticking, swinging toward the girders' wet magnetism, insisting north was inside the bridge. [664] The men stopped at the far span, arguing with their own instruments, while the moon path led her people on, unhurried and unseen. [687] They reached the far shore as the moon slid behind a ridge. [699] No one cheered. [702] Sef took her hand, and they stood listening to the horses crop frost grass, wildness banked in every one of them like coals under ash. [727] In the morning there would be questions, searches, and eventually a new horizon to map. [742] Tonight Mara looked back only once, at the viaduct standing dark over its bright trapped lake, then turned toward the unknown line where earth met sky. [768] Her impatience was still there. [773] It simply had somewhere to walk. [779]
