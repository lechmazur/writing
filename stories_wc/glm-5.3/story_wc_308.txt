The canyon remembered every sound, and the pulse had been one of them for three hundred years. [17] It had been a heartbeat once, a girl's, drumming in her ribs as she ran the canyon floor with a message no one would ever receive. [43] The arrow took her below the shoulder blade. [51] Her heart stuttered, and one beat tore loose and kept going, because that is what pulses do; they cannot stop. [71] A pulse that stops is a pulse that dies. [80] So it wandered. [83]

The towers of bone rose on either side, ribs of something older than names, and between them the echoes never settled. [104] A cough from the year of the black flood still ricocheted faintly off the sixth rib. [120] The pulse had learned reverence for such histories: every echo was a document, and it had appointed itself their keeper. [140] It never added noise to the archive. [147]

It could not touch anything, being only rhythm, but it could strike, and over its first century it taught itself the striking of stones. [171] A pulse driven through a flat stone at the right frequency sent it skipping across the dust, each skip a note, the notes a chord. [196] The chord went out, the canyon answered, and the answer told the pulse where the walls were, where the hollows were, where the dead lay. [221] There was no one left to name what it had become, so it named itself after its work: harmonic stone skimmer. [242]

The birds helped. [245] Flocks nested in the bone towers, and when a chord passed through them they rearranged, closing ranks over solid ground, scattering over hollows, threading single file above buried walls. [274] The pulse read the flock the way the living once read maps, and by reading, it traveled. [291] This was the whole of its ambition, three centuries deep: to learn to navigate without moving. [307] It was a pulse; movement was its body, and it was tired. [319] If it could know a place without walking to it, maybe it could finally stay somewhere, and staying, for a pulse, was the only dream left. [345]

In its fourth century the birds found something under the dust that the pulse had missed for three hundred years. [365] It skimmed until a skip struck the thing loose: a stone arrowhead, point-up in a scar of older, darker sediment. [385] The pulse circled it with the care of a struck bell circling its own note. [400] Arrowheads meant hunters, and hunters meant the girl had not been alone, which the pulse had never known. [418]

To appraise it properly, to learn whose hands had knapped it and why, it would have to read the layered dust, which held centuries the way cliffs hold strata. [447] So it began the long appraisal. [453] It skimmed chord after chord across the flats, and each chord passed through the bones and came back changed, and the flocks, scattering and reforming, wrote the changes in flight. [483] Where the arrowhead lay, the birds bent their line into a shallow arc, and the arc was the shape of a trajectory. [505] It followed stone by stone until the flock's pattern resolved: two hunters on the high rim, a bow drawn in bad light, a deer that was not a deer. [534] The arrow had been meant for meat, not for the girl. [545] It had gone astray in a wind the birds still remembered, a west wind that gusted and died and gusted again. [566] And when the pulse followed the arc to its end, the end was a scatter of small bones it had been circling for three hundred years without knowing why. [595] The arrowhead, then, was the one that had torn it loose. [606] Something sharp at the center of the pulse loosened at last and blew away like dust. [622] There was no murderer in the canyon, only an accident grown old, and the hunters had likely mourned her in a language still bouncing between the ribs. [649]

It skimmed one last stone, a long low chord, an offering, and let the echo carry the arrowhead's story into the archive of the bones. [674] Then it did the thing it had practiced for centuries. [684] It stopped. [686] The echoes did not. [690] Every chord it had ever thrown was still traveling, still skipping, still reporting back, and as long as they moved, the pulse could be still. [715] It settled beside the small bones and let the flock's slow wheeling overhead tell it where everything was: the walls, the hollows, the far rim, the whole world, arriving. [744] A pulse that stops is a pulse that dies, it had always believed, but it had been wrong about the stopping. [765] Instead, stopped, it became what it had been before the arrow: a heartbeat, at rest, in exactly the place a heartbeat belongs. [787]
