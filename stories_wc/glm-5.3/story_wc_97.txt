Odessa played the departures hall of Arbor Station, built high in a tidal arbor where great trees rooted themselves in open air above the clouds. [25] Beneath the platform, twice a day, the cloud sea rose and fell like a slow gray tide. [42] When the tide turned, every compass on the brass railing flickered, because the iron mist swallowed north and left the needles spinning like men looking for a lost thought. [71] Eleven years ago, on such a turn, the airship Halcyon followed a flickering needle out over the cloud sea and never came back. [94] Her navigator had been Elias, who gave Odessa an anniversary clock on their last night together, wound with his promise to be home before it struck seven. [121] It stopped at 6:47, the minute the Halcyon's lamp vanished, and it had not ticked since; she kept it on the piano anyway, its hands frozen mid-welcome. [148] Old pilots swore the deep mist kept its lost ships, drifting slow and half-forgotten beyond any map; Odessa had chosen to believe them. [171]

Grief ran on schedule at Arbor Station; the departures board listed her pieces like trains, and fate, Odessa believed, kept the timetable. [193] But she had one unscheduled hour, taken at night in the projection booth, where the station ran an old black-and-white travelogue for waiting passengers. [217] Into its reel she had spliced hidden sections, forty frames of her wedding, gray and grainy, buried between shots of the arbor. [239] Legend said the first projectionist had folded herself into the film to survive a winter; Odessa had learned the trick, and each night she stepped through those hidden sections into refuge. [270] For six seconds Elias turned toward the lens and laughed at something she had said, and the laughing was enough, most nights, to keep her hands steady until morning. [299]

Wistful, the night porter called her, but Odessa was also strategically creative, and patience had made her a scientist of the flicker. [321] Eleven years of logbooks had taught her that a compass drowning in iron mist does not point at nothing; it leans toward whatever holds a steady, low vibration. [349] The generator hum tugged the needles; the station bell tugged harder; and once, in a storm, a sustained bass note from her own piano had pulled every flickering compass in the hall three full degrees. [384] For three years she had waited for the right evening to misuse it. [397]

Tonight the tide would turn exactly as the horizon invited night, the first anniversary hour in eleven years to coincide with dusk, and the last ferries would sit at their moorings with useless needles. [431] The evening passengers gathered in the hall, checking their flickering compasses, arguing about false norths; the travelogue chattered on the screen; Odessa did not go up to the booth. [460] Not tonight, when fate had kept so careless an appointment. [470] She had decided to surprise it with an unplanned encore. [480]

When the mist began to rise through the boards and every needle started to shudder, she laid her hands on the keys and played a piece that appeared on no timetable. [511] Her left hand held a low C the way a lighthouse holds its beam. [525] Above it her right hand unwound the wedding waltz, transposed into something steely and unashamed, and the passengers stopped arguing because the needles had stopped spinning. [551] One by one the flickering compasses swung, steadied, and pointed at the piano. [564] She played until the horizon invited night fully in, until the windows went black, until every needle in the hall held like a compass convinced at last of a north. [594] If needles leaned toward low vibrations, then somewhere on the cloud sea, if the Halcyon still drifted, its compass leaned toward a sustained C and a woman who refused to close the piece. [627] Her arms ached; the low C burned; she held it anyway. [638]

Far out where the mist was thickest, a light rose through the gray, then a second, then the slow lantern of an airship climbing the tide. [664] Someone in the hall said the Halcyon, and someone else said impossible, and Odessa let the chord go. [682] In the ringing silence, the anniversary clock on the piano ticked, once, and its frozen hands began, very slowly, to move. [703] She glanced toward the booth, where the hidden frames of the wedding still waited, her old refuge, and decided they could stay spliced into the past where they belonged. [732] Fate had scheduled a loss eleven years ago and never checked the timetable again; she had played an encore it did not see coming. [756] When the Halcyon's horn sounded over the arbor, deep and rusted and real, Odessa put her hand on the ticking clock and reached for the keys, in case the sky wanted a second. [789]
