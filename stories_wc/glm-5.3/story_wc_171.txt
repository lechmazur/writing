The ancient Terrace of the Twin Altars had outlasted the war that both armies refused to end. [17] Three hundred years of it, and Sera Odan had carried its weight for forty. [31] She was the negotiator, the only name both sides still spoke without spitting. [44] On midsummer night she stood at the terrace's center while delegates mustered along the worn steps, silver banners on the left, ash-gray on the right. [69] Above them the two wandering stars began their slow dance, drifting toward a conjunction that came once a century, when the old rite could be spoken. [95] The treaty was ready except for one line. [103] Neither nation would sign until the other confessed the Burning of the Lower City, the atrocity that had turned the forever war from grief into theology. [129]

Sera knew the truth, because she had carried the torch. [139] Not as a negotiator. [143] As a soldier of nineteen, doing what her captain swore would end the siege by morning. [159] The Lower City had sheltered four thousand civilians, and the fire had not stopped at the walls. [176] She had spent the forty years since bargaining pieces of herself away for truces that lasted a season, then a year, then two, because she believed ideals meant nothing until they were anchored in practice. [211] Tonight she meant to anchor the last one. [219]

In her hands she carried the ebony clipboard on which every treaty of the long war had been signed, its surface dark with three centuries of ink. [246] Its wood had come from the sacred grove below the terrace, and her fire had been the last thing that grove ever felt. [269] The board had survived in the shrine, the grove had not, and so the last piece of that holy wood was the one object Sera Odan could not put down. [299] She had learned early that you do not end a forever war with arguments; you end it through ritual significance, with objects and hours men are afraid to defile. [328] When the wandering stars touched, the rite of the Common Hour opened, and any oath sworn on the sacred wood in that hour bound both nations. [354]

The delegates argued while the sky turned. [361] Silver demanded confession; ash-gray demanded confession; both promised signatures and neither believed the other. [375] Sera listened with that operatively gentle attention of hers, the kind that did quiet work on a man's anger the way water works on stone. [400] It was not kindness. [404] It was technique, sharpened by four decades of men screaming across her table. [417] As the stars closed to a hand's width, she raised the clipboard, and the terrace went silent. [434]

"The clause requires a confession," she said. [441] "Here is mine." [444] She clipped the treaty to the dark wood, clipped a second page beneath it, and read her own name into the record as the hand that burned the Lower City. [474] The words came out level and unhurried, her gentleness turned on the wound itself, opening it so it could finally be cleaned. [496] Both delegations stared. [499] The rite allowed this. [503] An oath sworn on the sacred wood must be accepted, but every oath had a price, and the price of absolution was exile without a name: her signature struck from every treaty she had ever won, her person barred from both nations forever. [546]

It was, the old laws said, the only redemption a fire could purchase. [559] She signed her confession as the stars touched, their joined light lying across the ancient stones like a seam of silver. [580] Then, one by one, they came, silver first and ash-gray after, each delegate bending to the ebony wood because none of them, past that hour, dared refuse. [607] Some signed in grief, some in shame, one woman weeping for a grandmother she had finally stopped hating. [625] By dawn the treaty held forty signatures and Sera Odan held nothing. [637] She left before sunrise, taking the road down from the terrace with no banner, no escort, no country. [655] The clipboard stayed on the altar, treaty and confession bound together under glass, the holiest object two nations now shared. [675]

They say the peace has held nine years. [683] They say her name is chiseled off the rolls of both capitals, and that farmers on the border roads leave bread and lamp oil at shrines to a woman no one is permitted to name. [718] It is a flawed kind of beautiful, like a mended bowl, like a scar that closes clean. [735] Sera would have accepted that. [740] She never asked the world to be perfect, only to be practiced. [752] Somewhere on a road between two countries that no longer burn, an old woman walks at midsummer, and the slow stars keep her appointment. [776]
