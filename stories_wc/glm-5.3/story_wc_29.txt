Mara Kessler had interpreted the sky for eleven years, and the sky had never once lied to her until March. [20] The lie arrived at the university radio station on a Tuesday, buried in the hum between two pulsars. [38] As the station's celestial signal interpreter, she was the one everyone called when the arrays picked up something that shouldn't exist. [59] This time the something was a voice wearing a pulsar's clothes. [70] It ticked like J0437, spun like J0437, but its period drifted in ways no dead star could manage, sliding toward a rhythm that felt almost conversational. [96] Something out there was imitating the sky. [103]

She spread her predecessor's astronomical charts across the console, paper gone soft as moth wings, margins black with forty years of corrections. [125] Old Halloran had charted every stable source by hand, and Mara trusted his ink more than any database. [143] Against his maps the imposter stood naked: its harmonics stacked wrong, its Doppler shift pretending to belong to a proper star. [164] And beneath the signal's bright surface, down in the subsonic range where ears gave way to bones, ran a second voice, patient and low, whispering in frequencies that made listeners feel watched. [196] She played it for the department on Thursday. [204] By Friday the community had cracked in two. [212]

The dean wanted an announcement, first contact on the evening news, the university's name in every headline. [229] A defense contractor wanted the raw data sealed and sold. [239] The students split into camps with names and banners, Believers and Skeptics, shouting across the quad while the signal pulsed overhead. [260] Mara watched the power plays unfold from her booth, and she noticed the thing nobody else seemed to notice: the angrier the campus grew, the stronger the subsonic whisper became. [290] The imposter wasn't just mimicking stars. [296] It was farming discord. [300]

Halloran's marginalia confirmed it. [304] In fading pencil beside his chart of the Pleiades he had written a warning: mimics that sing like rescuers and feed on answers. [327] Cosmic tricksters, luring young civilizations into shouting their locations into the dark, or better still into tearing themselves apart over the meaning of the song. [352] Every broadcast of certainty was a flare lit for the hunters. [363]

Mara was not a bold woman. [369] She checked her findings three times, backed them up twice, and even then believed only about eighty percent. [387] But underneath the caution, stubborn as a seed in pavement, grew a small hope: that people, given clean information, could still choose each other. [411] If she could interfere with the whisper itself, the campus might hear the sky without the venom in it. [430]

The plan was simple, which is why it terrified her. [440] The tricksters' venom lived below twenty hertz, under the threshold of hearing but not under the threshold of the body. [460] So she would answer in their own register: a phase-inverted copy of their whisper, generated in the booth and pushed out through the station's transmitter on the same subsonic frequencies, cancellation against aggression, silence against song. [496] Every radio in town would carry it, every speaker and cheap dorm stereo, a lullaby too low to hear. [515] The charts gave her the clean reference she needed, Halloran's honest J0437, the signal the way the sky actually sang it. [536] She worked through the night while the factions screamed at each other in an emergency forum across campus, headphones on, hands steady, hope held carefully like water in a cracked cup. [567] At three in the morning she flipped the switch. [576] In her headphones the whisper stuttered, thickened, and flattened into silence. [587] For one long minute the only sound on Earth's side of the console was the true pulsar, ticking away as it had for a billion indifferent years. [614]

The imposter tried on another mask an hour later, then another, each one checked against Halloran's charts and stripped away in turn. [636] She could outwit them once and they would simply move; she understood that she had not won, only learned the shape of the game. [660] That was enough for tonight. [665]

At dawn she crossed the quad, where the banners hung slack and two Believers were sharing a thermos with three Skeptics, none of them sure anymore why the arguing had felt so urgent. [698] The venom was leaving them slowly, the way static leaves a room. [710] She rolled the charts under her arm and kept walking, eighty percent sure, give or take, that the tricksters had heard her and would be waiting patiently for whatever she dared to try next. [744] She intended to disappoint them. [749]
