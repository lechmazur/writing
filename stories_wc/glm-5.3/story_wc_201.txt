Anesthesia wore off the way her interrogators had scheduled everything else: on a curve she could feel. [17] She counted her recovery in proprioceptive data, because they had sharpened her body-awareness to impossible precision: pulse mapped capillary by capillary, breath weighted, the tremor in her left ring finger logged at 4.1 hertz. [51] When her eyes opened, the forest was violet. [59] Not violet-lit but violet-made: a black-light canopy, leaves fluorescing in blue veins, the ground glowing faintly where roots surfaced like scars. [80]

They had told her, before transfer, that the sanctuary biome grew under ultraviolet, that Earth-stock trees had been grafted and altered until they drank light her eyes had no name for. [111] Displaced trees, rooted anyway. [115] She understood the tone of the place before anyone spoke: this was a world for things moved against their will and told to grow. [139] A woman stood at the edge of her cot, a penitent, bare-armed, her skin written in glowing script that showed only under this light. [163] Ultraviolet penance, the tribunal called it: survivors could always see what a penitent had done, and a penitent could never again take her history off. [188]

"Session one," the penitent said. [193] "You will remember through the body, or not at all." [203]

She braced. [205] Her shoulders locked, her jaw set, thumbs curling in, the old defensive geometry, ten years deep. [221] But her enhanced proprioception read the bracing not as failure but as data: a flinch was a coordinate, a lock was a landmark. [244] The interrogators had heightened her sensorium so that no part of her could hide from their instruments, so that pain could be administered with precision. [269] Now she used their enhancement for cartography, mapping her own trauma responses, letting the body's alarms chart where memory had been buried under the anesthesia they dosed her with between sessions. [300]

"Brace," the penitent said softly, when her shoulder blades drew together. [311] "In structural terms, a brace is not cowardice; it is the point where load is met and distributed." [329]

She had been a mathematician, before. [335] She held onto that the way she held onto the pewter mask strap, the only object she had carried out of the facility: the soft metal band that had once cinched a breathing mask to her skull through months of controlled drowning and controlled breath. [380] Pewter takes the shape of whatever holds it down. [389] Here, in the ultraviolet forest, the light itself burned, so survivors wore veils, and every morning she braced, then buckled the old strap around her head, feeling its familiar dents, her own skull's shape pressed into metal, now fastening a mask that protected her instead of one that hurt her. [439] That was the closest thing she had to a proof: an instrument of pain, refitted to preserve. [456]

"Why keep it?" the penitent asked once. [463]

"Because I need the mathematics of mercy," she said. [472] "The interrogators had an equation: escalation curves, thresholds, computed increments, everything optimized, no term for stopping. [488] They would anesthetize me, and after the anesthesia wore off, the next session began on schedule. [504] It was mathematically complete except for one variable: mercy carried no value in it. [518] I intend to derive that value, not to forgive them, but to formalize stopping, so that no machine and no tribunal ever mistakes mercy for error again." [545]

The sessions worked as promised. [550] When the penitent reached toward her, her body braced, and inside the bracing a memory surfaced: the mask, the water, the pewter warming against her temple as she strained. [579] Each trauma response was a door, and her enhanced proprioception was the hand on the handle. [595] She plotted her flinches like points on a plane and watched, week by week, a function emerge: not linear, not clean, but converging. [618] Recovery had a slope. [622] Mercy, she began to suspect, was a boundary condition, the line you draw and then defend. [638]

By the third month she could stand under the fluorescent canopy and feel rooted, which was absurd, since she was light-years from any soil that had ever known her name. [668] But the trees were the same: displaced, grafted, drinking impossible light, putting roots down anyway. [683] When the penitent asked what she would do with the finished proof, she touched the pewter strap, warm now from her own skull, shaped by survival instead of surrender, and gave the only answer her mathematics allowed. [720]

"Mercy is where the load is met," she said. [729] "I was braced for ten years against what people did to me; the proof is me, standing here, rooted in the place they sent me to break, braced like a structure, bearing weight." [762]

Behind her veil, in the violet glow, the forest kept growing. [773]
