The orchard district had drowned in fog for three hundred years, and no one alive remembered stars. [17] Mira, the last mirror maker, worked in the astronomer's ruined tower, where apple roots had cracked the foundation and glass from the old observatory dome lay everywhere under the moss. [47] On her bench sat the astronomer's broken sextant, brass gone green, its graduated arc snapped in two. [64]

The district's one surviving story said the fog had come the night that arc broke. [79] The astronomer, the story went, had dreamed too large: a sky without a horizon, stars without edges, nothing to divide heaven from earth. [102] Her sextant, which measured the angle between star and horizon, was the last boundary the dream could not cross. [121] When it fell, the runaway dream escaped her and spilled over the orchards as fog, erasing every line it touched: sky from soil, tree from tree, neighbor from neighbor. [150] The astronomer herself walked into it and never returned; only the sextant was found, cold at the tower's base. [169]

Now the dream wanted to come home, and it used Mira's sleep to say so. [184] Night after night she dreamed of drowning gently in light, endless and edgeless, and woke each morning grieving for a sky she had never seen. [209] A dream that size could not be captured with hands or nets; it needed a place shaped like itself, boundless inside, walled outside. [232] Only a mirror was both at once, and only a sextant could give it the sky's own geometry. [250] So Mira set out to recreate the broken instrument. [259]

She was a firmly grounded woman, mistrustful of anything she could not weigh, and that was her one advantage. [278] Dreams dissolved people who floated; they could not dissolve a woman who counted her own heartbeat like coin. [296] She salvaged the observatory's fallen dome glass, melted it down, and blew a new arc by hand, curving it degree by degree to match the broken brass. [323] But glass alone would hold nothing. [329] The dream had to be silvered into it, the way an image is silvered into a mirror, and no furnace in the world could heat a dream. [356] In her dreams, though, the fog breathed; she had felt it, a slow tide swelling and emptying through the ruined streets. [377] Breath was the only language they shared: if she breathed in perfect time with the dream, the boundary between them would thin to nothing, and she could draw it into the cooling glass as gently as breath fills a bottle. [417]

Such work could happen only upon the shimmer of twilight frost, the hour when the fog's breath condensed on black branches and glittered, making the invisible exhalation briefly visible. [446] On the first cold evening of the season, twilight came down and the whole dead orchard turned to glass mountains, every tree a glittering ridge, silence stacked like crystal. [475] Mira carried the half-sextant to the tower roof, set the glass arc in its brass cradle, and stood barefoot on the frozen slates so the cold could anchor her. [504]

Then she began to synchronize. [509] In went the fog; she waited. [515] Out it rolled; she released. [520] Seven breaths, then eleven, then a hundred, her rhythm creeping toward the dream's tide while her heart kept time underneath, a drum under a river. [545] At last her lungs and the fog moved as one animal, and the boundary between them simply was not. [564] She saw, for one held breath, what the astronomer had seen: stars beyond number, no horizon anywhere, an ocean of light with no shore. [588] It was beautiful, and it was drowning her, because she was forgetting where her breath ended and the world began. [608] The frost bit her feet; her heart counted on, stubborn; she stayed firmly grounded even as her edges dissolved, and that was the trick of it. [634] Holding the vision, she bent and breathed her whole synchronized lungful across the glass arc, and the dream poured into it, silvering as it went. [659] The frost shimmer flared white and went out. [667]

When Mira came back to herself, alone in her one body, the fog was draining from the streets like a tide finding its moon. [691] Above the ruined orchard, in a clean violet dark, the first stars in three centuries came out over the district, sharp and cold and edged. [716] The recreated sextant lay in her hands, its curved mirror holding an entire boundless sky, small as a cup and endless as ever, a dream with a wall around it at last. [748] She carried it up to the astronomer's old room and set it in the window, face to the stars, horizon and no-horizon finally at peace with each other. [776] Below her, the frozen trees stood glittering like glass mountains under an open sky, boundaries drawn gently back over the world. [797]
