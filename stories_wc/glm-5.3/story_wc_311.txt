The reaper found the old recordings three days ago, seventeen wax cylinders in a crate beneath the ruined stair, each labeled in Maren's hand. [24] Tonight, at last, she carried them up into the dome. [34] The ancient observatory stood on its cliff above the gray sea, and through the dome's slit the sky was burning down toward dusk. [57] She had not dared play a single one until now. [67] Some fears are like that; they wait to be faced. [77]

The reaper had walked this coast for four hundred years, gathering the dying, and carried an affliction no living soul would believe. [99] She echoed forgotten phrases. [103] Every farewell she collected stayed in her mouth, surfacing decades later so that strangers heard a tall gray figure murmur other people's goodbyes. [126] Mortals feared her as they feared the water beyond the maps, namelessly, and so the fear grew as large as it wished. [148]

Maren had been the observatory's last keeper, a cartographer's daughter with an atlas page pinned over her desk, torn from a book older than the dome. [174] Along its margins coiled the sea monsters of the old mapmakers, leviathans and krakens drawn wherever the known world gave out. [195] "They named what they could not chart," Maren told her once, "because a nameless fear is infinite, and a named one fits inside a word." [220] In time it became the reaper's creed as well: to give names to nameless fears. [235] Maren had even named the reaper, whom every living heart dreaded without knowing why. [249] "Vesper," she called her, after the first star of evening, the one that appears exactly when the light is dying. [269] For thirty years the reaper had visited, and Maren had never once been afraid. [283]

Maren died in the storm of a century. [290] The observatory served as a seamark, and by the old rule its great lamp had to keep turning no matter what the sky did. [314] So Maren, with her failing heart, climbed and turned the mechanism through the whole black night, and seven crews came home because of her. [338] At dawn the reaper came for her, as duty required, and found her at the desk with the atlas page before her, a new monster sketched in the margin, the storm itself, a name written underneath. [374] "Vesper, look," Maren said, which was all the breath she had left. [386] "The light held." [389] The reaper carried her out through the wrecked morning, heartbroken in the particular way of those who may never say so, and for eleven years her echo had murmured it to strangers. [421]

Now the cylinders waited on the meridian table beside the atlas page and the brass phonograph, and the sun was nearly gone. [443] The reaper set the first cylinder on the spindle. [452] Maren's voice filled the dome, low and amused, reading the monsters of the margins like a litany: the Coil of the Sargasso, the Whale of the False Shore, the Serpent of the Ninth Fathom. [486] Other cylinders held ordinary phrases, the kettle boiling, the mist lifting, nothings recorded in her failing winters for the reaper's affliction alone, so that one voice would keep surfacing in her mouth forever. [519] The last light came through the slit and struck the old prism the astronomers had used to split starlight, and the scattered bands fell across the page, red to violet, over the coils and fins. [554] She could not have stopped her echo if she had wanted to. [566] She followed each phrase a heartbeat behind, so that two versions of one dead woman moved through the room together. [586] Where the scattered light crossed the ink, the sound began to thicken. [598] It crystallized. [600] Fine crystals grew along the monsters like frost, word by word, Maren's voice turning mineral in the colors, and the echo of her sacrifice, all those recorded winters, all that love laid down in advance, took shape as something she could hold. [642] On the final cylinder, Maren spoke plainly, the way she always had. [654]

"If you are hearing this, Vesper, then you found them, and you are afraid. [668] Only nameless things can devour us. [674] So I have named your last fear for you: it is not losing my voice. [689] It is keeping it, which is a kind of touch." [699]

The dusk finished. [702] The reaper lifted the page, heavier with its glittering weights, and left it in the dome where evening light would find it, so that every clear dusk the scattered colors would cross the crystals and sailors below would hear the cliff singing. [744] She pocketed one crystal, the one grown along the storm's new name. [756] Then she went down the cliff road into the dark, doing her work, echoing the forgotten phrases of the dead, and among them, always, the kettle boiling, the mist lifting, the light holding, the light holding on. [793]
