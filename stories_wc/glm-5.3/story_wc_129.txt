Sena arrived in the crater town with a bundle of marsh-reeds on her back and a grief the new tongue had no word for. [24] It was a time beyond the murmur of lost languages; the old words had gone quiet everywhere, and the young speech of the rim was all anyone had left. [53] She was a sculptor of marsh-reeds, bending dried stems into herons, lanterns, hands reaching for hands, and she had come wanting one impossible thing: to graft herself onto a community, the way a gardener presses new wood into old bark and prays it takes. [97]

A crystalline orchard perched at the edge of the shimmering crater, its trees ringing faintly in the wind and fruiting small moons of light, and the town's crooked streets braided around it. [129] At the orchard's heart stood the obsidian arbor, a trellis of black volcanic glass that seemed to hold stars even at noon. [151]

Sena was boldly sympathetic, which had embarrassed her in every town before this one, and the first thing she noticed was not the marvels but old Marta, whom everyone had agreed not to see. [185] Every autumn the town held the Searching, a dawn-to-dusk hunt for the tag of the Moth, an elusive street artist who left exactly one mark a year somewhere new, always in the script of some language already lost. [223] Finding it was the nearest thing the town had to a holiday, and the search itself was the web of kinship made visible: finders shouting from rooftops, grandmothers comparing notes across fences, children trading rumors like coins. [260] Sena wanted into that web more than she wanted bread. [270]

On the morning of her first Searching, while neighbors whispered that she was wasting the day, Sena climbed to Marta's crooked door and knocked louder than the whispering. [298] "They say the Moth never tags the same district twice," Marta said, eyes narrow, testing her. [314] "Then we will have to think like someone who wants to be found," Sena said, "and like someone who does not." [335] Marta's laugh was rusty as a hinge, but she took down her lantern. [348] All day they searched, Sena carrying Marta's stool when the old woman's hips failed, telling her about the marsh without being asked: the reeds, the herons, the language that had gone down with the water. [383]

Marta listened the way the crater listens, and near dusk she led Sena down the long aisle of the crystalline orchard. [404] The trees chimed; light pooled in the crater below like something spilled from heaven and healing. [420] The obsidian arbor rose at the aisle's end, glimmering, throwing back the last sun in long violet seams. [438] "There," Marta said, pointing her lantern at the underside of the lowest beam, where no one had ever thought to look, and Sena knelt in the cold glass shade and forgot how to breathe. [472] Etched fine as a hair into the obsidian, catching the dusk, was the tag. [486] It was not the looping script of years past. [495] It was marsh-script, the writing that had drowned with her village, and the word it spelled was 'selda,' marsh-tongue for 'the one the web holds,' in a language Sena had believed dead with everyone who spoke it. [532]

Sena shouted, and the shout ran back through the streets and gathered the whole town like a net gathers fish. [552] They came with lanterns, bakers and tinkers and half-dressed children, and stood in the arbor's black shine while she read the dead word aloud, and her voice cracked, because nine years had passed since she last heard her own language, and it took a stranger's hand to give it back to her. [604] Then she did the thing she had carried across three valleys to do. [617] From her bundle she took the young reeds she had kept alive in wet cloth, and grafted them to the obsidian arbor, weaving living green into dead glass, a lattice the next spring would finish. [652] "I dedicate this to the ones who search together," she said, which was her way of saying: take me, I am yours. [674] The town took her at her word: hands steadied the reeds, someone brought twine, a child held a lantern close, and the web closed over her so gently she never felt the moment she stopped falling. [710]

Only Marta lingered when the others drifted home, and her fingernails, in the lantern light, were dusted with the fine black grit of glass newly etched. [736] She pressed Sena's hand to the warm obsidian beneath the graft and whispered one word in the marsh-tongue, the answer to the tag, the word for stay. [763] Below them the crater shimmered on, holding its old ache of light, and the arbor, for the first time, held something alive. [785]
