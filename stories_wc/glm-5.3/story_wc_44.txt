The Nocturne Observatory drifted above the sea on its floating isle, and Maren kept its lamps lit against a sky of patient stars. [23] She was a budding rune scholar, eighteen and quietly deliberate, the kind of student who read a line seven times before daring to underline it. [48] Below her feet, through the observatory's glass floor, the world turned green and sleeping, bent forever by the Bending. [67] A generation ago, spoons had bent reality. [74] No one knew why the humblest utensils in every kitchen began twisting the laws around them, only that when a spoon stirred, gravity forgot which way was down, and stirred memories surfaced in the tea. [109] Worse, things began waking up. [114] Doorknobs learned names, clocks learned regret, and so the rune scholars of that era learned sealing. [130] Every awakening was pressed into a circle of runes and closed, because a sealed awakening could not spread. [148] Maren's mentor, Ostrow, had sealed hundreds. [154] Then, on the night the isle rose from the sea to escape the bent lands, he had sealed one more, wound the observatory's clockwork automaton down to stillness, and gone. [184] He left her three things: the lamps, a drawer of unfinished rune loops, and the automaton's brass key. [202] The key was longer than her hand, toothed like a tiny city skyline, warm no matter the cold. [220] For two years she had refused to touch it. [229]

Her refusal was not grief but method. [236] Ostrow had taught her that a rune scholar relearns by closing loops; you never close the last one first. [255] So each night she chose an unfinished loop from the drawer, an open circle of runes with a single arc missing, and drew the final stroke, and the loop's memory opened inside her like a held breath released. [293] Closing one, she relearned the grammar of seals. [301] Closing another, she relearned Ostrow's lecturing walk, the way he circled a table while he thought. [317] Each closed loop was a small noon, he had said once, and she had asked the question that had carried her here: where do shadows go at noon? [345] He had only smiled and said she would have to follow one. [357] Because at noon shadows do not die; they slip beneath things, beneath stones and soles and the world itself. [376] She had watched from the observatory at midday, the one hour its shutters opened to the hated sun, and seen the isle's shadow shrivel to a coin and dive under the rock. [408] Everything sealed, everything hidden, went down there. [415] She wanted to know where, and why, and whether her mentor was among the hidden. [430]

Tonight only one loop remained in the drawer, and it was drawn not on slate but etched around the automaton's brass heart, a circle of runes broken at the top like a mouth about to speak. [466] Maren knelt before the still machine, key in hand, quietly deliberate even now, and checked her reasoning one last time. [486] Ostrow had begun to wake, near the end. [494] She had seen him arguing gently with a teacup, seen his shadow stand up a half-second after he did. [513] Rather than be sealed by strangers, he had pressed his own awakening into the automaton's heart and left the loop open, unfinished, for a student steadier than grief. [541] She slid the clockwork key into the slot beneath the broken arc. [553] It turned with a sound like a question finding its answer. [564] Gears woke through the automaton's brass body, and as they turned, they themselves completed the rune circle, closing the last loop from the inside. [588] The automaton's eyes lit like two small noons. [596] It did not speak with Ostrow's voice, and she had not truly expected it to. [611] Instead it stood, took her wrist with careful fingers, and pointed the great telescope straight down, through the glass floor, beneath the isle. [634] She looked. [636] Under the rock, where midnight met the world's underside, drifted every shadow that had ever slipped away at noon: the isle's, the bent lands', teacups', spoons', a slow dark ocean of everything sealed and everything hiding, turning gently like a second night sky beneath the first. [682] There, among them, a shadow that walked with a lecturing circle, patient as a rune awaiting its closing stroke. [701] He had gone where shadows go at noon, and left her the lamps. [714]

Maren closed the shutters against the coming day and, quietly deliberate to the last, began to teach the automaton runes. [734] A scholar relearns by closing loops, after all; and some loops, she now understood, are doors. [750] One day, when she had closed enough of them, she would follow her mentor down into that patient dark, and bring the sun with her. [775]
