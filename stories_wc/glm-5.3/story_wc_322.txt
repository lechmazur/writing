The Gallery of Rational Enchantment admitted the public only by fever. [11] Doctors prescribed it the way they prescribed rest: a delirium, properly housed, does less damage. [26] Mara had been the gallery's fever dream architect for eleven years, and in that time she had never once been allowed a fever of her own. [52] Architects, the board insisted, must remain rational. [59] Her medium was sinew thread, donated by each patient from a small incision below the collarbone, because the body trusts its own fiber above all else. [85] Woven into the gallery's patterns, a patient's own sinew made an illusion realistically imaginary: it had their weight, their warmth, their stubborn logic. [108] The delirious walked the Ames room and felt themselves shrink gently instead of shatter. [122] In the corridor of facing mirrors, a nightmare could be walked to its end and dismissed. [138] Every result was logged, replicated, peer reviewed; this was enchantment with a methodology, and Mara believed in it the way she believed in stairs. [162]

What she did not believe in, anymore, was touching. [171] She had designed eleven hundred dreams and felt none of them arrive; she wanted, with a flat professional hunger, to discover the texture of connection. [196] She kept one thread that was not on the books. [206] Her sister Ada had donated it before the surgery that did not work, and Mara had promised to weave her something warm. [228] The promise was eight years old. [234]

She had begun to keep a ledger against herself, because as memories alter, even architects need measurements. [251] In the first alteration, Ada's laugh went from bright to low. [262] In the second, her hands, which Mara remembered as pianist's hands, turned broad and burnt from ovens. [279] In the third, Ada began to look like their mother, and Mara could no longer say which of them she was grieving. [301] The gallery's literature called this drift ordinary; memory, they wrote, is an illusion gallery too, and no railing holds. [320] Mara found the sentence comforting the way a full waiting room is comforting. [333]

In the twelfth winter, the fever came for her, politely, like a colleague. [346] The board advised her to hire an architect and rest. [356] She filed the paperwork saying she had, and closed the gallery for one night. [370] Then she sat at the great loom in the gallery's center, where the corridor of mirrors doubled everything into a soft infinity, and she began to weave. [397] She worked by weaving patterns she had no names for yet, letting the fever choose the crossings, because Ada had never once done anything Mara designed. [423] The sinew went through her fingers warm. [430]

Around hour three, the pattern erupted. [436] There was no sound; the thread simply lifted off the loom the way frost lifts off glass, and kept weaving itself in the air above the corridor. [463] The mirrors multiplied it into a braiding crowd. [471] Down the hall, the painted snakes began to rotate without moving, and the checkerboard wall's level lines lay down quietly and refused to stay straight. [496] Every illusion in the building was erupting at once, calmly, the way a sentence erupts into meaning. [513]

In the corridor stood a figure woven from Ada's thread, and it was not an illusion in any way Mara could measure. [535] It cast a shadow. [539] It breathed at the rate of someone who had just been laughing. [551] Its hands were broad and oven burnt, the altered memory made flesh, or thread, and Mara understood with terrible calm that she would never learn which came first, the sister or the remembering. [584] The figure held out one woven hand. [591] Mara took it. [594] The texture was nothing she had designed: coarse, warm, ridged with old burns, ordinary past all argument. [611] Connection, it turned out, felt like a hand you had dried dishes with. [624] She stood in the corridor for what the mirrors reported as both one minute and a thousand, and the fever burned itself out politely around them. [650]

At dawn the thread lay flat on the loom again, just thread. [662] The mirrors showed one woman, straightening her collar. [670] Mara opened the incident ledger, because rational enchantment requires documentation, and recorded the night under CLASSIFICATION: eruption, benign, unreproducible. [689] Under CAUSE she wrote her sister's name, then crossed it out and wrote it again, because the memory had altered twice while she wrote, and both versions were ones she loved. [720] She filed the ledger, unlocked the doors, and admitted the first fever of the morning. [735]
