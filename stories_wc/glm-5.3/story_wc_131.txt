The city's lights died at 4:51 a.m., and the library exhaled into a sapphire hush. [15] Between the second and third floors, in a crawl space ribbed with joists, Mira, the dweller, woke to the emergency lights' low blue throb. [39] She had lived inside the walls for six years, unseen, and made herself useful the way water is useful, quietly and everywhere. [61] Her caches ran the building's spine: batteries behind the atlas case, mended books reshelved before opening, a wool blanket folded in the map room's dead flue. [87] She was kindly, the staff agreed in notes left for each other, and methodical; someone kept straightening the crooked stacks, and someone oiled the door that squealed. [114] But usefulness, she had learned, is not the same as being seen. [126]

Three weeks ago, kneeling at a floor grate, she overheard a girl at the reference desk, sixteen maybe, asking for a recording. [148] "My grandmother told her stories into the Harbor Voices project," the girl said, "and the reel with her voice was lost in the flood." [172] The archivist was sorry; the catalog showed it destroyed, waterlogged, decades ago. [184] "She's losing her words now," the girl said, "and I wanted to bring her own voice back to her." [203]

Mira had searched the building's hidden veins ever since. [212] She worked the pipe chases methodically, one chase per night, each marked off in chalk. [227] Old janitors cached treasures in them, and she had uncovered spectacles, a 1946 pay stub, a child's tooth in an envelope labeled sweetly. [250] Tonight the unexpected outage had stopped the fans and pumps, and the silence let her hear a knock she had never caught, faint and metallic behind the northeast stair. [279] Not a knock, she realized, sliding the access panel aside; a loose strap of metal was ticking against a cooling steam pipe. [301] She inched along the chase on elbows and breath. [309] Wedged where two pipes crossed, swaddled in oilcloth, sat a biscuit tin. [321] Inside, on a bed of silica, lay a ferromagnetic tape reel fragment, a broken coil of brown ribbon still shining with oxide. [343] A penciled label read: Harbor Voices, session 12, saved what I could, signed Hennessey. [357] Hennessey, the night janitor dead thirty years, whose peppermints still surfaced in odd corners; a keeper before her, hiding rescued memory in the walls. [381]

The moment her fingers closed on it, sense memory took her: hot dust and machine oil, her grandmother's radiators ticking at dusk, a voice she had thought she was finished losing. [412] That is what tape is, she thought: memory you can hold, rust and plastic keeping the warm weather of a throat. [433] She could leave it on the desk, another anonymous mercy. [443] Instead the old want rose in her, the one that had no cache: to be visible, at least once, to someone who needed her. [467]

Dawn was thinning the tall windows from sapphire toward gray-gold, and through the glass she watched the city's lights flicker, fail, flicker, whole blocks breathing back on. [494] The girl would come at opening; she came every day now, but this morning, after the blackout, she was already on the steps, as though grief mistrusted clocks. [522] Mira climbed down inside the wall, took a steadying breath of chalk and copper, and let herself out through the atlas case. [544] In the shadowed reading room, the emergency glow gave everything a drowned, patient blue. [558] She crossed it for the first time in six years, upright, in the open, floorboards announcing her. [575] Beyond the glass, the girl sat hunched in her coat, watching traffic lights blink undecided. [590] Mira unlocked the deadbolt with the key she had copied years ago and never once used. [606] The door swung; the girl looked up. [613]

"Harbor Voices, session twelve," Mira said, holding out the coil. [623] "Your grandmother's voice is in here." [629] "The librarians have a machine that plays this, and I can show you the chase where it hid." [647] The girl stared, took it, stared at her. [655] "Who are you?" [658] "The one who mends the books," Mira said, and then, more honestly, "I live here." [673] "I've been here the whole time." [679]

Above them the power surged back street by street, and the chandeliers woke in a soft ringing bloom, no longer sapphire but warm. [702] The girl's eyes went from the tape to Mira's face and stayed there. [715] "Thank you," she whispered, meaning both the voice and the woman, and Mira, visible at last, let herself be seen. [735]
