The Vanishing Point Gallery earned its name honestly: its central corridor appeared to recede forever, though Wren knew the far wall stood thirty paces away. [25] The trick was done with mirrors and patience, and it was the kindest lie she knew. [41] For thirty years she had served as the gallery's ash scatterer. [52] Grieving visitors sat in the old rocking chair at the corridor's head, and Wren drew their lost ones in charcoal, asking gentle questions, taking down only what each sitter offered. [82] Then she burned each portrait in the copper basin and carried the warm ash into the corridor, scattering it into the slanted light. [105] There, at the unreachable vanishing point, the carbon would gather for a single breath into a gray figure, vivid and alive, before dissolving. [128] A mother's particular laugh, a soldier's unfinished smile: crystallized for one instant, then gone. [142] That instant was the gallery's entire promise, and Wren guarded it the way other institutions guard gold. [159]

Her own face she kept hidden behind a mask of shattered porcelain shards, salvaged from the doll that had broken on the night her mother died and wired back together with silver. [191] Through its broken lattice, every face came to her in fragments, and fragments were what she trusted. [208] Whole truths frightened people, and whole faces had once frightened her. [219]

So she wove truth carefully. [224] To every griever she said that nothing given to the gallery was ever lost, and this was true, for the ash settled forever into the floor's gray powder. [252] What she withheld was that the living figure lasted only one breath, that the endless corridor was a mirror trick, and that its light stayed sweet and shimmering only because she crept in at midnight to push the old rocking chair, whose patient motion kept the air trembling. [300] The chair her visitors wept in had been her mother's. [310] Safety, in the Vanishing Point Gallery, was a mirage, and Wren its quiet weather-maker. [324] The mirage worked through incomplete information: no visitor ever walked far enough to touch the far wall, and no one asked what the soft powder beneath their shoes was. [353]

People assumed grief had made her strange, and grief had, but the gallery served a purpose older than her strangeness. [373] On the night her mother died, Wren had been drawing her portrait in charcoal, and had stepped away to sharpen the stick, and had missed the last expression entirely. [402] Thirty years of other people's lost ones had been thirty years of practice, so that when the moment came again she would not blink. [426] Her motive had never once changed: to crystallize that fleeting emotion, the look she had failed to catch. [444]

Tonight her hands ached too badly to push the old chair again, and she let it run down. [462] From the locked drawer beneath it she took the only portrait she had never burned: her mother's, unfinished at the mouth. [483] She fed it to the copper flame, ground what remained in her mortar, and lifted a fistful of warm powder. [503] Behind her, the old rocking chair finally slowed to a stop. [514] The trembling went out of the light. [521] The far wall stepped forward like a curtain being drawn, and thirty paces were suddenly only thirty paces. [539] Wren scattered the ash anyway. [544] With no shimmer to carry it outward, the carbon did not flee toward any vanishing point; it hung, then began to powder down through the still air, sifting past her shoulders like slow snow. [578] And in that falling gray, a face assembled itself nearer than any vanishing point, close enough to touch. [596] Her mother's eyes were following someone just out of frame, following a daughter who had stepped away to sharpen her charcoal, and they held no reproach at all. [624] The look Wren had missed for thirty years was tenderness: a woman memorizing her child while there was still light to see by. [647] The safety had never been for the visitors; it had been hers, a careful weaving of half-truths to keep one portrait unfinished and one question unanswered. [673] The figure held as long as the powder fell, which was longer than a breath, longer than any trick of light had ever allowed. [697] While it was still falling, Wren lifted the porcelain mask away and let her whole face feel the gray come down. [718] The gallery was only a room now, short and true, its floor soft with everyone she had ever scattered. [737] She lowered herself into the still chair, folded her charcoal-blackened hands, and found that she could see all the way to the far wall, where floor met wall in an ordinary line. [769] Nothing here was lost, she had always said, and now, at last, it was wholly true. [785]
