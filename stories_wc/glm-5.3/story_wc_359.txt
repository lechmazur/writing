The Council of Necessities had ruled the aurora nonessential, and the sky had gone the gray of dishwater ever since. [20] Beneath the city, in an ice cavern where the great bells hung dark, Yssa waited with an amber music sheet pressed against her chest. [44] She was the last prism-song bell ringer, and she was synesthetically blessed: every sound she heard arrived as color, every silence as a held breath of white. [71]

The Council's hush-wrights had carved silence into the walls themselves, a sculpted hush of dampening grooves and sound-eating flutes, so no bell could sing and no song could rise. [100] To Yssa the hush glowed a flat, expectant gray, like a canvas before the first stroke. [116] The amber sheet held the Unfettering, a score sealed in resin by the first ringers, its notes invisible in ordinary light. [137] One instruction came down with it, passed like a heartbeat: read it through the colors of the aurora, and only at the moment a sculptor's chisel first strikes marble. [166] The hush ate echoes; it ate songs; but a first strike was not a repetition of anything. [183] It was making, and making passed through. [190]

Above, in the frostbitten square, old Master Harl had broken the ten-year ban that morning, setting his chisel to a block of white marble. [214] Yssa closed her eyes and listened with her whole skin. [224] The strike came up through the mountain like a silver seed. [235] Tock. [236] The ice shivered, the sculpted hush flinched, and along the ceiling a single filament of green light unfurled, thin as a first hair of dawn. [261]

Yssa raised the amber sheet into that thread of aurora, and the first measure woke: three notes pressed in gold, plain as footprints. [284] She climbed the bell scaffold and swung her mallet. [293] The first bell rang, and the tone bloomed crimson, spilling across the ice like wine across a tablecloth. [311] The hush rushed in to swallow it, and for a moment the cavern held nothing but that gray contest, tone against silence, red against fog. [336] Then the second bell, and the crimson deepened to ember orange, and the hush recoiled a step. [353] Third bell: gold, and the gold did not fade. [362]

Each ringing unfettered another band of the bound aurora, and each band lit more of the amber score, until reading and ringing became one motion, breath and color braided together. [392] Green came next, then a blue like deep water, then a violet that made her teeth hum. [409] The hush fought in sculpted whorls, sucking color into its grooves, and Yssa rested between measures, swaying, letting stillness gather before each swing. [432] This was the way of prism-song: not a march but a tide, effort and release, toll and hush, toll and hush. [453]

Far above, the aurora cracked its gray shackle and poured. [463] The whole city stopped to look up, and looking up, they began to hear. [477] Green hissed like rain on a hot pan; violet chimed. [487] Wonder, Yssa had always argued before the Council, was not decoration. [498] It was the lining of the lungs. [505] They had struck it from the Ledger of Essentials anyway and begun unmaking whatever could not justify itself in calories or coin. [527] Tonight she meant to unfetter the sky and certify wonder as essential, not with ink but with evidence no ledger could refuse. [549]

The final measure glowed into view: one low note on the eldest bell, held until the ringer's arms gave out. [569] Yssa set her shoulders and struck. [575] The tone rose through every color at once, a white pillar of sound, and the sculpted hush did not fight it. [596] It softened, loosened, and lay down like a dog that had only ever wanted a kind master. [613] Unfettered, the aurora flooded the cavern, and the ice itself began to ring in sympathy, every frozen wall a bell. [633]

When Yssa climbed back into the winter night, the magistrate was waiting with the Ledger under his arm and constables at his back. [656] Overhead the sky churned green and violet, and he could not keep his eyes off it. [672] "Do you hear it?" Yssa asked. [678] He listened, and his face did what faces do when a door opens in them. [693] "Green," he whispered, "sounds like rain." [699] He opened the Ledger, and his pen moved. [707] In the column for essential things he wrote one word, and the word was light. [722]

Below the square, Harl's chisel struck marble again, and each strike rang up through the mountain like a small bell answering. [743] Yssa stood in the falling colors and let the tide move through her, toll and hush, toll and hush, certain now that wonder needs no certificate except an open eye, an unstopped ear, a sky unbound.
