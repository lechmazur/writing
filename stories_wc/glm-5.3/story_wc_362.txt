I am a mystic by trade, which is to say I am a professional liar, and everything I lie about turns out to be true, which is the most inconvenient thing about me. [33] The attentive kind, I should say: I notice everything, the tremor under a widow's wedding ring, the exact geometry of frost on a car window, the temperature at which grief begins to look like patience. [68] My clients meet me at dawn in the quartz arbor behind my house, twelve milky pillars rising out of the mist like the pipes of an organ nobody is playing. [98] They believe the quartz gathers spirits; I believe it gathers condensation, but I have never once corrected them, because a lie that comforts is still a lie, and still comforts. [128] I am, you should know, firmly grounded: I trained as a lab chemist before the money ran out, and I keep two ledgers, one for the lies I sell and one for the humidity and dew point that make them possible. [169]

My method is simple: my car sits at the arbor's mouth overnight, and by morning the frost has ferned across the windshield in patterns I claim are messages, and I commune with them on the client's behalf, tracing each crystal like a word in a language I invented. [217] For this work I use a glass prism, my brother Theo's, which he hung from the arbor's lintel as a boy so the light would shatter into colors on the quartz. [248] He died in the car three winters ago, on an ordinary Tuesday, of an ordinary aneurysm, and I inherited both the prism and the vehicle. [273] Here is my motive, plainly: I wanted to prove that objects absorb emotions, because if the windshield could hold what Theo felt in his last minute, then something of him was still here, still trapped like light in glass, and death would be a matter of geography rather than erasure. [323] For three winters I read frost for strangers and never once, until this morning, read it for him. [341]

The mist was dense enough to lean on, and the arbor had gone the color of drowned milk, and the frost on the windshield had grown thick, dendritic, feverish. [370] I held the prism against the glass and asked, in a voice I kept deliberately steady, whether he had been afraid. [391] That is when the hush shifted. [397] You will think I mean silence, but silence is absence; this was a presence of quiet, a hush that moved from the arbor into the car the way weight moves across a floor, from outside me to inside me, where it settled, attentive, waiting to be read. [444] My breath, I noticed, was fogging the glass; my questions, spoken an inch from the windshield, were warm, and warmth is an author of frost. [469]

The assumption shattered the way assumptions do, all at once and in a direction I hadn't braced for: the patterns were not his; they were mine. [495] Every fern and feather of ice had been steered by my exhale, my questions, my heat; the windshield was a mirror, not a letter. [519] I checked my second ledger later, because I am firmly grounded even in ruin: the dendrites branched at exactly the angles that physics predicts, sixty degrees, obedient, indifferent, empty of anyone. [550] And still the hush sat inside me, and still it answered, in my own voice, which is the only voice the dead are ever given: he was not afraid; he was listening to the radio; he was thinking, absurdly, of the prism, of how he'd meant to rehang it. [599] This is a lie, of course; I cannot know it. [609]

But notice that I have been telling the truth all along, just in the wrong body: the object that absorbed Theo's emotions was never the glass. [635] Objects do absorb emotions; I simply had the objects wrong: not quartz, not windshield, not the prism splitting dawn into its seven patient colors, but me, this stubborn, firmly grounded, attentively lying prism of a woman, who has been refracting him since the day he died. [681] So I keep the arbor, and the mist, and the two ledgers, and I go on communing for strangers through frost I know is only physics, because it is only physics plus a woman full of everyone she has ever loved. [722] A widow arrived an hour later and asked whether her husband still existed somewhere; I looked at the frost, which was saying nothing, and told her yes, with total confidence and no evidence, which is the definition of my profession, and she wept with relief, and it was true, or will be, because I have decided to make it so, one truthful lie at a time. [788]
