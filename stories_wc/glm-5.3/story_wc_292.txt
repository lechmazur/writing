Nine years after the jet stream shifted, the fog over Gannet Rock had learned to stay. [16] It was spongey stuff, gray as wet wool, and it soaked into everything the station owned. [32] Wren Odell had come to the experimental weather station as a map librarian, sent to reconcile the old charts with a sky that no longer matched them. [59] She believed, idealistically and against all evidence, that a map was a promise. [72] In her coat pocket she carried a copper nail taken from a coffin. [85]

When the rising sea took Saltmere's churchyard, her grandmother walked out with the relocation crews and pulled one nail from Marta Odell's coffin, because iron forgets and copper remembers. [114] The coffin had been empty; the sea never gave Marta back. [125] Marta had been Saltmere's last pilot. [131] On the night the brig Lucida struck the Teeth, the inquiry wrote that she had put about and left them drowning, and the harbor master signed it. [158] The same man had written the night orders that put the Lucida on the Teeth in the first place. [177] They struck her name from the pilot roster, and the Pilotage Code being what it was, exile from the record could be reversed, but only if someone stood and questioned it with evidence in hand. [212] Nobody had. [214]

The roster board itself had come to Gannet Rock when the harbor died, a black beam hung in the instrument room, every pilot's name pinned with a copper nail except one empty hole. [247] Wren passed it every morning and felt the hole pass through her. [259] Then the new storms, walking crooked paths since the jet stream shifted, dredged the Lucida up from the Teeth, and with her a pilot cutter the divers said had gone down lashed to her side. [294] In the cutter's locker lay Marta's shipwrecked journals, their pages waterlogged and swollen spongey as bread. [310]

Wren dried them herself, page by page, in the instrument room where the barometers ticked like patient clocks. [328] Marta had kept her true log in verse, because the harbor master read every plain entry and struck out whatever contradicted him. [350] Eleven coded stanzas, and every one of them was a question put to the sea. [365] The first asked, Is the wind still owed a debt, and the count of its beats, read as degrees, gave a bearing. [387] Wren plotted each answer on the old Saltmere chart, her hands nervously confident, steady enough, almost steady, and the points drew a course. [410] The course went out into the storm, curved hard at the Teeth, held station in the worst of it, and ended where the divers had found her, lashed to the Lucida's side. [442] The inquiry had said she fled; the course said she went in and stayed until the sea took both boats. [462] And when Wren wrote the first letter of each question down her notepad, in order, they spelled I TURNED BACK. [482]

The dread that had soaked into Wren since childhood, the spongey certainty that her family carried a coward's name, began to wring itself out. [506] What remained was procedure. [510] She went to Supervisor Ashe, who kept the Code and the roster board, and asked leave to question the record of the night the Lucida struck the Teeth. [538] Ashe set the hearing for the next fog, which was to say the next morning. [553] The station gathered in the instrument room, meteorologists and salvage crew, the barometers ticking, the fog pressing on the glass like something that wanted in. [578] Wren stood beneath the empty nail hole, and her voice shook, and she let it. [593] She read the eleven questions aloud, and one by one the room read the answers out of them, the meteorologist taking the wind, the diver taking the tide, until the course lay inked on the chart for everyone to see. [633] Then she read the first letters, and nobody in the room breathed. [645]

I TURNED BACK. [648] Ashe said the Code was plain: exile from the record is reversible when a standing question is answered with evidence. [668] Wren took the copper nail from her pocket, the one her grandmother had pulled from a coffin, and set its point in the empty hole. [693] It went in on the second blow. [700] Afterward the fog did not lift, because the jet stream had shifted and that was the world now, but it seemed to Wren less like a weight and more like weather. [731] She went back to the charts with a new rule for her catalog: a map records not only what was seen, but who was believed. [756] Somewhere on the Teeth the sea kept the rest of Marta's courage, and the record kept the part that mattered: a name, a nail, and a course corrected. [784]
