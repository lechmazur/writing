Mist braided through the serpentine grotto at dawn, curling along the winding dark as though the sea were learning to breathe. [21] Maren lived where the tunnel crooked deepest, an earthbound star watcher with salt in her hair and old light in her eyes. [43] Once she had swung among the constellations; now she watched her sisters glimmer from the wrong side of the sky. [63]

Her fall was an old story the waves still told, and she had stopped minding it. [79] Each morning she gathered sea glass from the cove, frost-white, bottle-green, the rare cobalt that made her breath catch. [98] Fishermen had tried torches in the grotto once; the smoke stained the walls, damp swallowed the flames, and boats still broke on the night rocks. [123] Maren wanted another way to share light without using fire, something patient that the mist would not fear. [141] Glass, she had discovered, remembered light. [147]

In her workshop she weighed every piece on a driftwood balance, glass on one pan, gray gull feathers on the other. [168] The heavier shards went to the outer tunnel, where moonlight could find them; the lighter ones, almost weightless, she kept for the deep. [191] Weight mattered, for only glass light enough to float on stillness would carry the glow inward. [207] Then she polished them as the sea did, only faster: crushed shell to grind, fine sand to smooth, ash and leather in slow circles until the frost lifted and each gem turned reflectively vibrant, returning twice whatever light it was given. [248]

The work took days, and in its rhythm memories resurfaced like bubbles from a sunken hull. [264] As memories resurfaced, her hands moved without her, and she was falling again through cold silver air, the sea rising to catch her like a mother cupping a dropped ring. [294] The water had tumbled her gently for a hundred years, she remembered, the way she now tumbled glass. [312] When it set her down at last, most of her starlight had washed away, and only one seed remained, cooled and rounded into a small white bead she carried in her pocket. [344] A pearl of stillness, the waves had called it, the calm left over when a star is done shining. [363]

By day she fitted the polished gems into niches along the walls, and at night the mist held their borrowed moonlight like breath held in glass. [389] Travelers began to pass safely through the winding dark, and the fishermen blessed the cold lantern that used no flame. [409] But the innermost chamber, the great vault where the grotto gathered itself, stayed black. [423] No moonlight reached that deep; the gems sat dull as teeth. [434] Only still light, light that carried its own quiet, could seed that darkness. [447] Maren knew what would do it. [453] She had known for years, and had never once weighed it. [464]

On the last night of winter she set the pearl on the driftwood balance, opposite the feathers. [481] The pearl outweighed every feather she owned, though it was hardly bigger than a raindrop; some things weigh exactly what they cost. [503] To give it away was to give up the last door back to the sky. [518] As she hesitated, the last memory surfaced: her sisters, the night she fell, leaning out of their constellations to look for her. [540] They had not been sorrowing. [545] They had been shining at her, hard, the way you wave a lamp for someone walking home. [562] Maren laughed, one soft syllable in the dark. [570] Sharing light was the whole of what stars were for, and she had been hoarding hers like a dragon of the tideline. [592]

She polished the pearl one final time, ash and leather, unhurried circles, until it was smooth enough to drink her fingerprints. [613] Then she walked to the deepest chamber and set it on a plinth where a thread of mineral in the ceiling pointed down like an aiming star. [640] For a breath, nothing. [644] Then the pearl woke, its stillness spreading outward in rings, and every gem caught the glow and gave it back doubled and redoubled, until the whole vault bloomed with light that had never once needed flame. [680] The mist, far from fearing it, leaned in and wore the light like silver on water. [696]

Word traveled up the coast of a grotto that glowed cold and clear from within, and travelers came to wonder through its serpentine dark. [720] Maren walked past them all to the tideline, where the first warm wind of the year was shaking loose the blossoms. [741] Above the mist her sisters glimmered down, faint in the daylight, and she glimmered back, no longer earthbound in any way that mattered. [764]
