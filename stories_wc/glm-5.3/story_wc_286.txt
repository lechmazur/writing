The wasteland had been scorched for so long that Moro no longer remembered green; she remembered only the color of ash settling on her kiln cart. [26] She was a potter, and zen-minded in the way of people who have watched clay fail a thousand times and stopped taking it personally. [50] When the timelines fractured thirty years ago, splintering the world into overlapping versions of itself, most places came apart quietly, like bad glaze. [73] But a few held. [77] That was the question that had pulled her across the waste for eleven years: why do some places resist change? [97] She navigated by following sun patterns, because each broken timeline kept its own sun, and the light crossed at angles no whole sky would allow. [122] Where the shadows agreed, the ground was sound. [130] Where they argued, she walked carefully, or not at all. [140]

The oasis appeared at midday as a smudge of order: date palms still standing, clay hives in rows, a low house with a chimney. [164] Abandoned, the maps said. [168] Maps were mostly fiction now. [173] Bees flickered at the hive mouths like bad memory, there in one sun and gone in another. [190] Moro tethered the cart and waited, because a potter who rushes a kiln gets cracks, and a traveler who rushes a fracture gets worse. [214]

Smoke still rose from the dead chimney, from no fire she could find, and as it flattened against the evening air, spilling sideways like a held breath let go, the overlapping suns set in rough agreement and the oasis steadied into one place. [257] In the house she found a hearth still warm in three timelines at once, a stone basin for watering strangers, and, hanging from a nail, a lantern with faded glass. [287] She knew it instantly. [292] Her sister had made that lantern before the fracture; the glass had gone milky with decades, but Moro would have known its chip anywhere. [316] Ila had walked into a disputed noon eleven years ago and never come out. [330] Moro's question had a sister's face on it. [338]

When she lifted the lantern down, the faded glass warmed against her palms and began to thrum, a low, insistent note, like a struck bowl refusing to finish ringing. [367] It pulled, gently, toward the water basin. [374] Moro had seen glass do many things, but never this; the pane tilted in its frame like a stem toward light, and she understood that it was phototropic to kindness, drawn to wherever care had been practiced, the way true glass gathers sun. [417] Wherever her sister had been kind, the lantern leaned. [426] The oasis, she realized, was Ila's ledger. [433] Every hive a stranger's hands had helped lift, every basin filled for travelers who could not pay, every winter the hearth was left banked so the lost could find it warm, all of it had soaked into the clay the way heat soaks into fired ware, and the fracture, meeting that density of care, had gone around. [490] Places resist change, she thought, when people keep answering them with kindness; the world will not break what keeps mending it. [511] It was not a comfortable answer. [517] Comfortable answers do not survive a wasteland. [524]

That night she did what potters do: she worked the oasis like clay. [537] She hauled water from the deep well and filled the strangers' basin to the brim. [552] She righted the fallen hives and sealed their cracks with slip mixed from oasis clay. [567] She banked the hearth as the old marks on the mantel said to, and set the lantern in the doorway, its faded glass flush with new flame. [594] The glass thrummed harder with each small act, pitching up like a hive deciding to swarm. [610]

At the hour when chimney smoke flattens, the seam between the two nearest timelines thinned, and Moro saw her sister through it, kneeling at a basin on the other side, filling water for travelers Moro could not see. [648] Ila had not fallen out of the world; she had landed in another one, and had simply kept working. [667] Their eyes met across the fracture, two women at two basins. [678] Neither of them wept, which was its own kind of weeping. [689] Moro thought about reaching through and pulling her sister into a single, agreed-upon world. [703] But you cannot drag a live thing from a kiln and expect it to survive the shock. [720] She raised her clay-blackened hand instead, and Ila raised hers, and between their two palms the lantern glass sang one clean note. [742]

Bees poured through the closing seam like sparks finding a draft and settled into the mended hives, so that by morning this timeline had honey again. [768] Moro stayed a season, then took the question onward, reading sun patterns, following the glass wherever it leaned. [786] Some places resist change because someone keeps choosing them. [795]
