The ridge path lay silver under a moon three-quarters full, and Vara Reyes walked it with the cracked porcelain box riding in her chest pocket, over her heartbeat. [28] Below, the fire camp slept; above, the stars wheeled their slow dance, the Dipper dragging itself around the pole like a stubborn relative. [51] She had not come for the view. [58]

June Okonkwo, the youngest on the crew, sat at the lookout rail, silent for four days, ever since she carried a boy out of the Cedar burn and his lungs quit before the helicopter came. [93] Vara sat down beside her and said nothing, because her own scars had taught her that silence, offered correctly, is a door. [115] The burn tissue ran from her left ear down her neck to the collarbone, old rope now, twelve years weathered. [135] Her hands had survived better: gently skilled hands that could rig a rappel harness, thread an IV, split kindling, and hold a bird without closing. [160] On the mountain she was known for those hands, and for using them on people the same way: firm enough to matter, soft enough to be borne. [187] Sixteen seasons as a forest firefighter had taught her that much. [198]

After a while she stood and held her hand out, and June, after longer, took it. [214] They walked the ridge path as the sky turned its slow degrees. [226]

Tell me what the fire said, Vara asked, when it had you. [238] June's throat worked; nothing came. [243] That's fine, Vara said. [247] You'll speak it when you can. [253] She had learned not to counsel but to reflect deeply, because that was the only honest way to enlighten anyone: hold a memory up until the light gets through it. [283] In the years after her own fire she had decided that pain is not an event but a language, one the whole world speaks in dialects, and that her work now, the second half of it, was translation. [321]

She stopped where the old burn scarred the far slope, black bones of lodgepole standing in moonlight, and pointed to the green fuzz already lifting from the roots. [349] Fire is honest, she said. [354] It takes, and it tells you exactly what it took. [364] We're the ones who lie about the ledger. [372]

June looked at her, and Vara took the box from her pocket and turned it in the moonlight so the crack caught a white seam. [397] It had been painted once, blue flowers gone gray with smoke. [408] Winter Creek Fire, she said. [413] A girl named Ana went back inside for this box, because her grandmother's ring was in it, and the floor of the world fell in. [438] I found her under a wet blanket, this box clutched in both arms. [451] The ceiling was coming down, and I made her a bargain, the only deal I had: eyes shut, arms around my neck, and the box goes in my fist. [480] Innocence for skin, I didn't say out loud. [488] She kept the shut eyes; I took the ember fall on this side of my neck and shoulder, and the box cracked between my fingers like something being born. [517]

June's breath caught, and Vara kept her voice level, easy, a hard thing held soft. [532] At the hospital, Ana informed me the tooth fairy owed me now, because I had traded for a box with her tooth in it, and that arithmetic let her sleep. [562] She translated what happened to her into something a child could carry. [574] That's all I've done since. [579] Every scar, every boy in a blanket, every name on the memorial: turning it from smoke into words somebody can hold. [600]

She pressed the box into June's hands. [607] June turned it over twice, the way you test whether something is true, and asked the first words she'd spoken in four days: What was inside? [633] A tooth, Vara said, and a little gold ring, and a note in crayon that says the fairy pays for skin too. [655]

June laughed, which broke into something else, and Vara held her on the moonlit path while the celestial wheel swung its slow degrees overhead. [679] That's the whole teaching, she said later, when the Dipper had dragged itself low and gray touched the east: pain is a language. [702] Every soul on this crew is fluent. [709] Learn to say it out loud, so it never learns to say you. [722]

They walked down as the slow dance of the stars kept turning, June carrying the box like a small lantern, the crack full of moonlight. [747] At the bottom, June handed it back. [754] Keep it, Vara said. [758] It's easier to carry than the other thing, and it holds together anyway. [771] June held it to her chest and, walking into camp, began quietly to say the boy's name.
