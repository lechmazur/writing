They graduated Vess last in her class, forty years after the engines learned to dream. [15] The Chorus composed spells now, ten thousand an hour, each one flawless, each one finished. [30] Creativity was an industry now; every poem, spell, and symphony came stamped from thinking mills. [45] Machine magic always ended cleanly; a conjured flame guttered out on schedule, a summoned bird dissolved at dusk. [63] Nothing lingered. [65] That was the flaw Vess had spent her underachieving life circling, the question she never tested well enough to answer. [85] What does light owe, when its maker lets it go? [95] The archive where she shelved other people's triumphs kept one legend in its coldest drawer: a garden folly, built by hand-mages on an icebound spire above the clouds, whose keystone was said to hold a glow that had outlasted its casters by three hundred winters. [140] She requested leave; the Chorus approved it in a microsecond and wished her productive rest. [155] The climb took nine days, crampons biting blue corridors of rime, the sky below her like a second, colder sea. [175] Above the clouds there was no wind, only hush, and the dimness there was not empty but lit from somewhere below, a brilliance banked low like a coal bed under ash. [206] On the tenth morning she found the last bridge collapsed, a gap of forty feet between her ledge and the folly's hanging garden, and beneath the gap, only mist. [235] The old manuals called the crossing technique mist walking, a craft so unprofitable the engines had never bothered to learn it. [256] Mist walking asked for bare feet and an unhurried heart; you did not conquer the vapor, you were taken in by it. [278] Vess sat on the ledge and unstrapped her crampons, then her harness, peeling away every engineered certainty she owned. [297] The cold found her skin and, strangely, honored it. [306] She stepped out. [309] The mist took her weight the way snow takes a falling leaf, and she walked, slowly, across nothing. [327] Halfway over, she understood the lesson the manuals never wrote: mist walking works only if you believe the world accommodates impossibilities, and belief of that grade cannot be automated, only practiced. [358] The folly was a small ruin, deliberately ruined, its columns cracked on purpose by builders who understood that beauty needs a little grief. [381] Vines of actual ivy, green and stubborn, grew through the ice. [392] At the center of the arch stood the keystone, a plain wedge of granite with no sigil, no script, nothing but a thumbprint pressed into the mortar while it was still soft. [424] It glowed. [426] Not blazing; glowing, the way a held breath glows in the body, a dimness so dense with patience it had its own light. [449] Three hundred winters of luminance, lingering because no one had told it to stop; the stone accommodated impossibilities the way a doorway accommodates guests. [473] Vess pressed her palm beside the ancient thumbprint and felt the stone remember warmth the way a cup remembers a mouth. [494] She had come expecting a relic, a proof, a weapon against the engines. [507] What she found was simpler and harder: a mage long dead had made one modest light and loved it enough not to bind it shut. [532] The sacred, she realized, was never in the great works the Chorus composed by the thousand; it was in the small work a hand chose to finish slowly, and then let go. [564] The engines could out-perform her; they could not out-linger her. [574] Anything finished on schedule ends on schedule. [581] Only love leaves residue. [585] She stayed three days in the hanging garden, tending ivy, learning the ruin's ration of sun. [601] On the third evening she cupped her bare palms and cast the only spell she had ever invented, a beginner's charm so minor the academy had graded it insufficient: a seed of light, no brighter than a match. [639] She set it on the keystone beside the old thumbprint, beside the ancient glow. [653] Then she took her hands away. [659] The seed did not gutter. [664] It held, dim and stubborn, her first lingering luminance, and in its patient shine the whole dim garden seemed to lean closer and approve. [688] Descending, she walked the mist barefoot again, unstrapped and unhurried, carrying nothing but the knowledge that she was, at last, the right kind of mage: last in her class, first in her light. [721] Below the clouds, the engines hummed on, composing their flawless, finished things. [733] Vess went home and began teaching, slowly, one student at a time: anyone willing to climb, and stay, until something simple refused to end. [757]
