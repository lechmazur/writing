The oracle came up the ridge road with the storm at her heels, her boots full of pebble dust from the shore she had left behind. [26] They called her kind pebble-shore oracles, women who read the world not in voices but in what fell quiet between them. [47] The fort above her was storm-tossed even on calm days, its stones bruised by weather, its garrison thin as the sky. [68] There was one star left now, hanging over the western wall like a lamp no one remembered to put out. [88] The others had gone out one by one for eleven years, and the dark that replaced them had a texture the oracle thought of as an ambered hush, as though the whole world had been sealed in resin while it was still trying to speak. [133]

She was mildly obsessed, and would have admitted it to anyone who asked. [146] For eleven years she had walked from silence to silence with a booklet of flaking gold leaf under her arm, writing down every quiet she met: the hush of emptied towns, the hush of birds deciding not to sing, the hush of a mother listening for a child who would not call back. [199] The gold had been laid on the pages by some dead craftsman, and it lifted off now in small bright scabs that she caught and kept in a matchbox, because she did not believe in wasting light. [236]

The gatekeeper, a boy with a storm-rusted voice, asked her business. [247] "To draw a map," she said. [253] "Of what?" [255] "Of the endless night." [259] He let her in, because in a dimming sky even a strange purpose is company. [274]

Inside, the last commander of the fort sat over a cold stove with eleven soldiers who no longer counted the hours. [295] They had stopped telling stories, which was, to the oracle, the worst symptom of all. [310] So she offered them a trade: their silences for her map. [321] "I retell things," she explained, unrolling her paper on the stove's flat top. [334] "Other people retell by summarizing what was said; I retell by summarizing silence." [347] "Speak your memories, then stop, and I will map the stopping." [358]

It began as a game and became a liturgy. [367] The old gunner spoke of his village, then went quiet where the orchard should have been, and the oracle drew a hollow there, a coast of absence, and marked it: here the dark is deeper, avoid. [403] The commander described the night the fourth star went out over the sea, then stopped for a long time, and that long time she rendered as a ridge of hatching, a mountain range of held breath. [439] Every silence had a shape and a weight; she had spent a lifetime learning their grammar, and she apologized, mildly obsessed with getting each pause exactly right, whenever she made someone repeat one. [472]

Outside, the storm that gave the ridge fort its temper came up from the west, and the one remaining star shivered in the torn clouds like a coal refusing the dark. [503] "It will go out tonight," the boy said from the window. [514] "Then we should hurry," said the oracle, and opened her booklet. [525] The pages were dark with her handwriting, and every flake that had peeled away had left behind a small pale ghost of where it had been. [551] She shook the matchbox out over the map and began, with a needle's tip, to set the saved flakes down where the stars had been, one grain for each dead sun, fixing them with breath and patience. [588]

The soldiers gathered close, and their silence as they watched was the warmest she had ever recorded, a marbled hush, dark and quiet veined through with something gold. [616] She drew it into the map as the border of everything. [627]

Late in the night, when only that one star remained in a sky dimming like a closing eye, she pressed the final flake into place and sat back. [655] Then the star went out. [660] Nobody in the fort spoke; the whole ridge held still; even the storm seemed to pause and consider. [678] In that absolute ambered hush the oracle rose, turned the map toward the soldiers, and waited for the storm's next far-off lightning, and when it came the gold flakes caught it and gave it back. [713] "There," she said softly, retelling the sky by summarizing its silence. [724] "It is all still here." [729] "Darkness is only a country nobody has mapped yet." [738]

She left the map on the wall of the great hall, where the garrison learned to read it, coast by coast, hollow by hollow, range by range of held breath. [768] Travelers who climbed the ridge afterward were shown the gold and told no stories of stars, only silences, which was, the oracle always said, the truer retelling. [795]
