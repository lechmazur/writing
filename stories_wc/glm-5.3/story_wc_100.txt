Merrick had buried forty-one drowned sailors in his thirty years as undertaker of Greywater Bay, and he could recite every name like a psalm. [24] He was a weary man, quiet but never still; there was always another storm, another bell, another knock on the embalming room door after midnight. [49] The package arrived on a Tuesday, wrapped in tarred rope with no sender's mark, addressed only to The Undertaker, as though the sea knew his trade and nothing else of him. [80] Inside, bedded in dried kelp, lay the broken lighthouse lens. [90] The Greywater light had failed forty years ago, the same night the city of Lorelei slid beneath the bay, and every wreck since had been laid at the reef's door. [120]

When Merrick lifted the largest shard, the room filled with voices. [131] Mayday, they said, in dozens of registers, from ships he had buried and ships the sea had never given back. [151] The lens had drifted all those years, gathering every distress call it could not answer, and now the whole catalogue of unanswered cries sat humming on his table. [179] He set the shard down and the voices faded, but not before the youngest one asked, very politely, where he was supposed to go now. [204] That night they followed him into his dreams, and he woke at three with salt on his lips. [222]

Merrick had spent his life giving the dead a place to be: a plot, a stone, an address grief could find. [243] These callers had none of that, nothing but the calling, and calling, he knew, is not rest. [260] He meant to give them what he gave everyone: rest with a permanent address. [274] The old charts showed Lorelei's library still standing on the hill that had become a shoal, dome intact, its great catalogue built to hold every name that ever mattered to anyone. [305]

The fishermen said the only way down was a fissure in the reef too narrow for anything living, that divers had turned back at the first bend, that it could not be done. [338] They had said the same things when he took the undertaker's post, and again when he refused to leave after the third great storm, and Merrick had never once dug in his heels; he simply failed to notice, each time, that he was allowed to quit. [384] The town called it stubbornness, though it never felt like a decision. [396] He thanked the fishermen, packed the shards in oilcloth, and went down the cliff stairs at low tide. [414] The fissure did not look possible, and he occupied it anyway. [425] He folded his shoulders through turns that had no room for shoulders; he breathed where there was nothing to breathe; and each time the passage narrowed to nothing, he found a nothing-shaped gap waiting, as if the rock had been holding his place. [468]

Lorelei rose around him in the green dark: empty squares, furred bells, streets still pointing home. [484] On the hill at the city's heart stood the library, domed and unbroken, its shelves climbing like ribs. [502] He climbed to the reading room, set the broken lens beneath the great glass oculus, and angled it to catch what light the surface let through. [528] Sun came down, struck the fracture, and split the way light does, into threads. [542]

Each thread found a page, and the library, which had been holding its breath for forty years, exhaled. [560] Lamps kindled along the stacks without hands; ink darkened; the drowned catalogue opened to the first empty leaf. [578] The voices poured down through the fractured glass, one thread for each call, and where every voice landed, words rose on the paper: names, ports, last coordinates, the youngest caller's question answered at last with a shelf number and a reading lamp. [620] Calling is motion, and the calls slowed as they were written, and stopped altogether when each was shelved between two neighbors. [641]

Merrick stayed to finish the work properly, assigning every soul what he had given his forty-one on the hill: a location, a marker, an address that grief could always find. [671] That winter no ships broke on the reef, as if the sea had stopped testing a light it now knew was answered elsewhere. [694] He had chased stillness all his life, one grave at a time, never once catching it. [710] Only here, lamp-lit and shelved and quiet, did he understand that it had been chasing him too, patient as any undertaker, and that in the library of answered calls it had finally caught him. [744]
