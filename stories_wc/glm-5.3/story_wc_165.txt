Between her acts, the tidal arcade belonged to Mara, and she walked it like a field biologist walking unmarked bush. [20] The pier's old machines flickered under a roof broken open to the stars, and beneath the floorboards the tide came in with its patient, animal arithmetic. [46] She was, by trade, a street performer; by nature, an analyst; and between the fire-eating of her first act and the knife-throwing of her second, she practiced her true discipline, which was making luck. [80] Not faking it, which any conjuror could do; making it, so that fortune genuinely happened to people who had stopped expecting anything at all. [104] She called the work orchestrated serendipity, in her own head, where all the best jargon lives. [120] The method was ordering randomness: weighting one die in the boardwalk dice game a hair heavier, seeding the jukebox with a song two estranged friends each loved, tuning the claw machine's grip so it would hold exactly once tonight. [159] Chance, she had learned, is just order you have not yet arranged. [171] Her instrument tonight was a bishop's chess piece, bone-white and thumb-worn, which she carried in her coat pocket the way other performers carry lucky coins. [196] She loved the bishop for its law: it travels only on the diagonal, never the straight line, and so does every good intervention. [219] You do not hand a lonely man happiness; you tilt the board until it slides into him sideways. [237]

Tonight's board was Efram, the caretaker, who for eleven years had fed the same mechanical horse-race machine the same four coins at the same hour, losing to the same painted mare. [268] Monotony like that hurt her the way a flat note hurts a musician, and her whole motive, if anyone had demanded one, was to serenade monotony with creativity until it sang back. [300] So she watched him, lightly attentive, the way you watch a heron: no staring, no pressure, just a soft perimeter of notice. [322] At 11:40 he would insert his first coin; at 11:52 the painted mare would beat his horse by a nose; that was the shape of his nights. [349] Her plan ran on rails of small nudges. [357] She had loosened the coin return on the fortune-teller machine so it would spill two coins at Efram's feet, and calibrated the jukebox to play a fiddle tune from the coast town where he had grown up, and set the claw machine, on its single strong grab, to hover over a plush gull. [410] The gull mattered because a woman named Dree, who sold mussels two stalls down, had mentioned wanting one for her granddaughter, and because Efram had never once spoken to her in eleven years. [443] Everything was arranged to happen in a chain, and every link looked like accident. [457]

Then the wilderness part of the evening raised its head. [467] The tide came in faster than the almanac had promised, swelling under the boards until salt water licked the horse-race machine's legs. [489] Salt crept up its guts, and an hour before Efram's ritual the machine coughed, shorted, and died. [506] Mara felt the plan tearing along its seams, and she felt, too, the old familiar intoxication of improvising inside chaos, which is what calculated wilderness is actually for. [534] She took the bishop from her pocket and set it on the dead machine's coin shelf, one diagonal move ahead of where Efram's hand would fall. [560] Then she went to work on the fly, re-routing the whole chain around the drowned race: jukebox two minutes early, claw grip tightened to grab-now, coin return timed to Dree's walk home instead of Efram's ritual. [596]

At 11:40 Efram's first coin dropped into a dead slot, and his fingers, groping for another, found the bishop instead. [616] He picked it up, puzzled and pleased, holding it the way you hold a question. [631] The retimed return spilled two coins at Dree's feet; she scooped them up, fed one to the claw, and the claw closed hard on the plush gull, delivering it like a verdict. [663] She laughed, called across the stalls to ask what a man does with a chess piece in a half-drowned arcade, and Efram, holding the bishop up to the stars, supposed aloud that you move it sideways. [699] Diagonally, Mara thought, but let him be wrong. [707] They talked until the tide turned, the gull promised to the granddaughter. [719]

Back on her little stage, the second act about to begin, she tallied the night the way she tallied everything: rigged die, seeded song, timed claw, one bishop spent. [748] Nothing in the ledger proved serendipity can be manufactured, and nothing needed to; it only had to happen, and tonight it had. [770] Under the broken roof the stars kept their own appointments, random to anyone not paying attention, and Mara, lightly attentive to the last, suspected somebody was. [796]
