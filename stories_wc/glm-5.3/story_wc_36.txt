Ten years after the guardianship ended, Veylan climbed back into the Thunder-echo range with a lacquered case on her back. [20] The canyon where the worlds were woven had gone avalanchingly quiet, a silence that gathered like snowpack above the treeline and let go all at once, burying every sound before it could become an echo. [55] She had not come to atone. [61] Atonement was for people who believed their hands had done the wrong. [73] Veylan, the range's last relic keeper, came to harvest what her failure still held. [87] What others called obsession, she called method. [94]

Below the abandoned watch station, an ancient lakebed held centuries of driftwood, bleached bone-white and light as secrets. [112] All morning she worked, arranging driftwood as directional markers, laying each branch so it pointed toward the seam in the cliff where the weave had frayed. [138] Her old training held that echoes respected order; a listener who gave sound a direction could tell a true voice from its reflection. [161] She needed that distinction now more than she ever had while wearing the gray sigil of office. [178]

At dusk she reached the anchor stone, a flat slab the size of a door, and knelt beside it. [197] Her hands shook as she set the case down. [206] Inside, swaddled in wool, lay the gilded reed flute, the instrument that had threaded nine worlds together every ninth autumn, note binding to note until the boundaries thinned and the weave held for another cycle. [241] It had been cased for ten years, sealed like evidence. [251] "Uncase it," the old masters used to say, "only when your intention outweighs your grief." [266] She weighed hers, found it heavier, and lifted the lid. [276] The gold leaf caught the last light. [283]

Everyone knew the story of her failure, or thought they did. [294] On the ninth autumn of her guardianship, at the twelfth note of the binding song, she had faltered, and the ninth world, Serel, had slipped its thread and gone dark beyond the reach of any crossing. [330] The council had unstrapped the gray sigil from her shoulder and spoken the word release. [345] Ten years of silence had followed, and in that silence she had replayed the moment so often it had worn smooth, featureless, useless. [368] Memory polished smooth tells you nothing. [374] So she had returned to the source, to play it raw. [385]

She raised the flute, and the canyon hush leaned in. [395] The first notes unwove the air. [401] Threads of sound moved along the driftwood markers, following them like water following a cut channel, and at the seam in the cliff the boundaries began to thin. [429] Weaving between worlds felt the way she remembered, like walking a rope bridge in fog, each note a plank laid an instant before the foot. [454] She played toward the twelfth note, the one that had broken her. [466] Nine, ten, eleven. [469] Then she stopped, deliberately, exactly where the falter had lived, and held the silence open like a wound. [487] In that gap, the truth finally had room to speak. [497] Serel had been singing before she ever lifted the flute. [507] She heard it now, carried down the driftwood channel, a thin voice braided into the old weave, singing not a greeting but a farewell. [531]

The world had not been torn away by her weakness. [541] It had asked to leave, and keepers before her, deaf to the request, had bound it tighter every cycle for a hundred years; the thread was fraying long before her breath touched the reed. [575] Her falter had not been a failure of nerve. [584] It had been the first honest hearing the world ever received. [595] Veylan lowered the flute, and the avalanchingly quiet dark came down over the lakebed, and she wept inside it where no echo could follow. [619] This was the harvest: failure opened and examined, yielding not absolution alone but a lesson with edges. [636] Listen first. [638] A binding that cannot hear a farewell is a cage, not a bridge. [651]

In the morning she did not leave the way keepers leave, with the case latched and the silence intact. [670] She left the flute uncased on the anchor stone, gold leaf turned toward the seam. [685] Beside it she pressed a page of her own hand into the stone's shadow: the farewell voice, the fraying thread, the hundred years of deafness, the withheld note and why. [715] The driftwood markers she left standing, arrows of bone-white wood pointing the next listener toward the gap where truth pools. [735] Whoever came after would inherit not a flawless weave but a map of the one mistake that mattered. [753] She climbed out before sunrise, and behind her the canyon held its hush, no longer a burial of sound but a listening, patient and vast, waiting for the next breath to cross the reed. [787]
