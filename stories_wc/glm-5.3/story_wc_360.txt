The stars were going out one by one, and between vanishings, Vela set type. [14] Her workshop was carved from topaz, and the surviving starlight came through the walls in slow amber sheets. [32] In that lucid light she printed letters for the bereaved, and the bereaved left changed, or at least disoriented, which she considered a kind of change. [58] Vela was a cryptic letterpress operator; nobody understood her methods, and she preferred it that way. [74]

When a client brought her grief, she took the grief home inside herself first. [88] This was her habit, borrowing personalities: she would speak in the widow's cadence, eat what the soldier had eaten, weep at the specific pitch of the drowned man's sister. [117] Only wearing a feeling could she set it correctly in lead. [128] She called the practice transposed empathy, after the mirror-writing of her trade: what she felt arrived backward, like type, and read true only on the printed page. [155] Everything else she did was deliberate ambiguity. [162]

On the press sat a fragment of halo, fallen the night the first star went dark; it was warm, curved, and faintly singing. [185] She used it to weight the galley, though she suspected it was weighing her. [199] Her motive was simple and monstrous: she wanted the mathematics of emotion. [211] Every borrowed personality was a data point; every disoriented client was a solved variable. [225] In her ledgers, grief ran to seven decimal places. [234]

The night the six hundredth star vanished, a boy came up the amber stairs carrying an empty envelope. [252] "My mother went with the light," he said, meaning she had died in the instant of the vanishing, as some did. [273] "I want a letter that says what I can't." [282] Vela borrowed him whole. [286] For an hour she was sixteen and hollow, his mother's laugh caught in her throat like a fishhook. [304] Then she set his grief in type, and she set it wrong on purpose. [318] She transposed the words the way a storm transposes a shore, so that the sentence read as goodbye and as promise, as accusation and as pardon, depending on where the reader stood. [350] Ambiguity was her scalpel. [354] A precise letter would have told the boy what to feel; an ambiguous one made him feel it, then doubt it, then feel it differently, until his grief loosened its grip out of sheer confusion. [389] He left the topaz workshop disoriented, and somewhere on the stairs the disorientation turned into breathing, which is what she sold, really. [411]

That night she entered his numbers and saw, for the first time, the equation falter. [426] The ledger insisted his sorrow equaled four others she had measured, but it did not; it kept changing while she measured it, the way starlight changes between the vanishings of stars. [457] She sat under the last few constellations, holding the halo fragment, and admitted what she had avoided for years: the stars were not vanishing. [481] They were being transposed. [485] Every emotion she borrowed and set and printed had to go somewhere, and it went up, wearing light the way ink wears a page. [509] When a feeling was fully transposed, felt backward by someone else and released, its star burned out, finished, a proof completed. [530] She had been solving the sky. [536]

The halo fragment sang louder, warm as a struck bell. [546] Three stars remained. [549] Vela understood then that the mathematics of emotion had one axiom she had refused to test, because it required borrowing the only personality she had never borrowed: her own. [578] She set type for herself, hands steady in the turbulent amber, and wrote the most ambiguous sentence of her life: "I am the operator of losses, and I have lost nothing, until now, which may be everything, or nothing at all." [619] Reading it back, she felt her own grief arrive backward, familiar and monstrous, and she could not solve it, and that was the answer. [643] Emotion was not an equation but an ambiguity you survived; the turbulence was the lucidity. [658] She pulled the press lever. [663] The halo fragment flared white, then went quiet, and one of the three remaining stars thinned into the dark like breath off glass. [686]

Two stars remained, and Vela, operator of losses, kept the shop open. [698] The bereaved still climbed the amber stairs, because even at the end of the sky, people needed letters that disoriented them into living. [721] She printed for them between the vanishings, borrowing, transposing, and the topaz walls glowed on, turbulent and clear. [739]
