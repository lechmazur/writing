On the night the forests voted, Mira climbed the lavender bluff wearing her grandmother's tin compass ring. [17] The ring had never pointed north in its life, and Mira, whose skin glowed faintly whenever she doubted something, doubted it constantly. [39] She was a luminous skeptic by trade, hired to audit the old sciences, and she had come to the bluff for one reason: to learn why some people carry weather with them. [71] Clients hired her glow as much as her mind; it made her a human lie detector. [87] Her grandmother had been one of them, a woman whose grief once gave a valley eleven years of rain. [106] Mira wanted the mechanism, not the poetry. [113] Her tongue held an augment, a legacy of the sensor wars, so she read the world through taste: ozone meant strangers approaching, copper meant sorrow, and a cold sweetness like snowmelt meant joy too large for one body. [151] All week the bluff had tasted of ozone. [159] The forests had held citizenship since the Green Accords, and tonight their fungal networks were casting ballots, flavors riding the wind that only augmented tongues could read: pine for yes, rot for no. [192] Democracy had become something you could taste. [199] Above the lavender rows, the aurora unspooled in green ribbons, terrestrially cosmic, the way all things were now: vast as galaxies, but grown from soil and statute and human feeling. [229] Scientists had explained it once, something about charged pollen and the magnetosphere, but the explanation never survived contact with the sky. [250] Other figures were climbing the bluff paths. [257] Each wore a tin compass ring, and the needle inside each, a sliver of magnetized pin, trembled toward the others. [277] Alone, one ring was almost nothing; tin barely holds magnetism, and a lone needle only shivered. [293] Together they made an emergent compass, a working instrument that existed only when its parts converged, pointing at nothing on any map. [315] Her grandmother had stamped five hundred of them in a shed before the rain years, and mailed them to strangers. [335] The strangers were converging now. [340] Mira's augment caught their flavors as they gathered: copper, salt, anise, iron, each person preceded by their weather. [358] An old man arrived inside a personal heat shimmer. [367] A girl of maybe nine walked in a hush of fog that swallowed her footprints. [382] The carriers had been summoned, the way they were every forest-election year, because the forests counted weather as testimony. [401] What the sky felt, the ballots remembered. [408] Mira held up her ring among theirs, and for the first time the needle steadied, all five hundred needles steadying together, and swung to point at the center of the crowd. [439] There stood a tall woman tasting, Mira's augment reported, of rain on hot stone. [453] You knew my mother, Mira said, not asked, because her skin had gone dark. [467] Doubt, for her, had always been luminous; certainty felt like this, an unlit quiet. [481] The woman nodded. [484] She carried a drizzle under her collarbones like a held breath. [495] Your mother didn't make the rain, the woman said. [504] She let it happen to her, and it happened to us. [515] Weather isn't carried, it accrues; one sorrow is a mood, ten thousand are a climate, and some people simply become the place where feelings converge. [540] That was the answer, then, and it arrived the way the aurora did: terrestrially cosmic, ordinary material lifted until it glowed. [561] Her grandmother had not been a storm engine. [569] She had been a compass needle, one trembling sliver among many, and the eleven years of rain had been the valley's grief finding her, converging on her, because she was the point where it could all agree. [606] Mira tasted pine. [609] Pine, everywhere, rolling down from the forests in cold green waves: yes. [621] The forests had voted, and the taste of the verdict was resin and approval, and somewhere in the rooting dark the old trees were counting humanity's weather and finding it bearable, even beautiful. [654] Around her the carriers lifted their ring hands, five hundred tin needles agreeing at last, and the aurora bent lower, as if to read them. [679] Mira's own glow did not return that night. [687] She had come for a mechanism and left with an inheritance: the knowledge that no one carries weather alone, that a person can be a compass only when others converge, and that doubt, however luminous, was never meant to be permanent. [728] She mailed her audit report the next morning, one line long. [739] Some people carry weather, she wrote, because the rest of us need somewhere to point. [754]
