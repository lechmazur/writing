Queen Maren had stopped believing in her own reign the way one stops noticing a stain on a favorite tapestry: gradually, then entirely. [23] For eleven years she had signed, pardoned, married, and buried, and every act felt like rehearsal for a life happening elsewhere. [44] Then came the dream, always the same: a hollow of blue stone above a sea of cloud, with light bleeding down the walls in slow threads, gold thinning to red, like a wound learning to weep. [80] Three times it had spoken in a voice like tearing silk: "This is a warning." [95] On the third night it added, "There will be no fourth." [106]

Her council had warnings of its own. [113] That evening they stood in a row, the physician's letter folded in the chancellor's hands, and told her that one more midnight walk, one more word of dreams, and they would discuss her comfort with her cousin the archduke. [152] It was, the chancellor said gently, a final warning. [161] Maren thanked them in a voice that never rose above a whisper and never once failed to be obeyed. [180] After the final warning, therefore, she went to find Nara. [190]

Nara lit the palace lamps, a ridge-village girl with coal-dusted cuffs and the only eyes at court that never flinched at the queen's. [213] "There is a cave on your mountain," Maren said. [222] "The ember grotto," Nara said, and hesitated. [229] "My grandmother said never go up after dark." [237] "About the grotto, or about everything?" [243] Nara's smile was brief and unhappy. [249] "Everything," she admitted. [252] Maren had dreamed that cave for a hundred nights, and she meant to unravel the dream or be unraveled by it. [273]

They climbed at moonset. [277] The path left the orchards, became switchbacks, became handholds, until the world below vanished into a sea of cloud glowing faintly, like the inside of a shell. [304] Above the cloud line the air tasted of struck flint and cold iron. [317] The grotto opened in the ridge's shoulder like a socket, and inside everything was cobalt, veined where the stone had split and glowing at the seams like banked embers. [346] And from the splits, starlight was bleeding. [353] Not flowing, but bleeding: bright beads that swelled, trembled, and fell, and lay quivering where they landed like small live coals. [374] It was exactly the dream. [379] Maren knelt. [381]

"Grandmother's other rule," Nara whispered, "was that if you must see what falls from the sky, you both breathe out at the same moment, because what you exhale together, you see together." [413] So they stood shoulder to shoulder in the cobalt dark and let out one long breath. [429] It smoked in the cold, hung, thinned, and as it vanished, lights rose around the grotto's mouth, dozens of them, gold-green, drifting up the ridge. [454] "Fireflies," Nara breathed. [457] "The first fireflies of summer," said Maren, "only by courtesy." [467] One drifted close and settled on her wrist, weighing nothing, burning faintly, and she understood. [482] They were drops of bleeding starlight, fallen seed-fine, come looking for somewhere living to land. [497]

The dream resolved like a lock when the right key turns. [508] A star had fallen here long ago, and the first queen of her line had climbed this ridge and sworn to keep it with breath and oath. [535] The oath had lapsed; kings found it economical to forget. [545] For a century the star had bled quietly, and everything gone gray, the wheat, the winters, the court, her own dull heart, had been draining through this cut in the world. [576] "I dreamed you," Maren told the light, softly, the way she commanded, without volume and without doubt. [593] The bleeding quickened. [596] A bead swelled at the lip of the vein and fell toward her open hand, and there was menace in it, lacy and precise: it fell like a kiss and struck like a debt. [630] It entered through her palm, and her pulse changed to match something older, slower, and farther away. [647] "The oath was blood and breath," Nara whispered, appalled, clutching at old lore. [660] "Then it shall have breath," said Maren. [667] She leaned into the split stone and exhaled, long and slow, and the starlight drank the fog of her breath and, drinking, slowed. [690] The bleeding eased from a flow to a seep. [699]

Somewhere below the clouds, the lamps of her dark palace were burning a little warmer. [714] They climbed down at dawn. [719] "You will have to come back," Nara said. [727] "Every month," said Maren, "for the rest of my reign." [737] The council had given its final warning; in the morning she would issue her own, in the soft voice no one ever disobeyed. [760] She had climbed up disenchanted and would climb down enchanted, and the difference, she knew now, was only that the enchantment was hungry, and it had chosen her. [788]
