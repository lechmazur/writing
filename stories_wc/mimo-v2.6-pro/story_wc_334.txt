The orchard greenhouse had sunk the year Ilyra turned thirty, and the sea kept it like a secret. [18]
Decades later, she came back with salt-stiff boots and a notebook whose spine she had broken in eleven places. [37]
She was a hands-on folklorist; she dug with her fingers, tasted soil, and pressed her ear to rotting wood to hear what stories had settled inside. [63]
Chronically curious, she had never once in her life let a question sleep overnight. [77]
The greenhouse lay in a shallow lagoon, ringed by shipwrecks whose ribs rose out of the water like the keels of whales. [99]
At low tide you could walk from wreck to wreck, stepping on anchors and drowned railings, until the glasshouse appeared under a skin of barnacles. [124]
Inside, the fruit trees still stood in their rows, pale and slow, drinking brackish light. [139]
Beneath them the floor was a black mirror of water, and on that mirror floated a single fairy ring mushroom, pale as a coin left on a grave. [167]
It was the last one; the ring had closed decades earlier when the flood came. [182]
Ilyra had come for her memories, the ones stolen from her on the night the water rose. [199]
She could remember the shape of the theft but not its contents, the way a word sits on the tongue unnamed. [220]
Her method was an old one, taught to her by a woman who kept bees in a beached trawler: detect what is missing by studying broken constellations. [247]
Every stolen memory, the woman said, leaves a hole where a star used to be, and if you map enough holes the missing figure will show itself. [274]
So Ilyra mapped. [277]
She knelt at the water's edge and pried a shard of greenhouse glass from the silt, holding it up so the sun broke into sharp cold stars. [304]
Where the light scattered, the drowned trees cast constellations across the ceiling panes, and the gaps between them made a figure she almost knew. [328]
Her habits began to break then, the way ice breaks in warm rain. [341]
She had come with a schedule, a tide chart, a ration of dried plums; by the second hour she had abandoned all three. [364]
When habits break, the old knowledge rises like silt, and the room begins to smell of the orchard as it was. [385]
She remembered her mother's apron stained with plum juice, and the sound of her father calling her in from the dark. [406]
She remembered the ring of mushrooms under the pear tree, how the children danced in it and came out older. [426]

Below the surface she saw the broken constellations whole: the scattered lights of the greenhouse lamps, long dead, arranged in a pattern her body knew before her mind did. [491]
They made the shape of a house, and inside the house a table, and on the table a dish of plums. [512]
The missing figure was her own outline, small, seated, waiting to be called in from the dark. [529]
