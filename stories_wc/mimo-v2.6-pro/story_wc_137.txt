Maren did not believe in memory that could be held in stone. [12] She believed in mortar, in angles that carried weight, in the arithmetic of a well set course. [29] The magic square tile her master had carried for forty years was, to her mind, a very old coaster. [48] Then the stroke took his words, and the coaster began to speak. [60] She was at the third tile already. [67] The tower was a stargazer's tower, open to the weather on all sides, and the wind never stopped working. [86] It was the season of whale migration, and below, in the black water of the bay, the whales were passing south, their blows hanging in the cold air like punctuation. [116] She touched the third tile and the stone gave up a memory that was not hers. [132] The memory showed her master, young, wet to the knees, laughing at a girl in a red coat on a ferry deck, the whole scene scored by an unheard symphony that only he could hear. [167] She wrote it down in the ledger, transcribing what the stone offered, word for word where there were words. [186] It was slow work, and each memory cost her something to carry, but she was courageously kind about it with him. [207] She told him what she saw, every scene, in a steady voice, even when the scenes were about loss. [226] The tile was nine stones set in a square, and the memories were entangled, each one bonded to a moment in his life so tightly that the stones could not be read at random. [260] That was the trouble. [264] Touched in the wrong order, the stones answered with noise, a smear of unrelated years. [279] She had wasted two afternoons that way before she found the pattern. [291] It was on the back, cut shallow so the wind had nearly finished it: a counting ring, nine strokes circling a center, the old way of tallying nets or stars or debts. [323] Her master had never explained it. [329] Tracing it with her thumb, she understood that the order lay in the forgotten pattern, and that it had been buried on purpose. [352] The fourth tile was the river. [358] The fifth, the argument in the doorway that neither of them had ever named again. [373] The sixth, the first whale he had ever seen, a gray back rolling under a sky full of stars he was there to chart. [397] The seventh held only a sound, his wife humming at a window, and the eighth was the argument's undoing, the two of them laughing on the same ferry deck years later. [428] She transcribed each one before she went on, because the ledger was for him and he could not hold them anymore. [449] Her hands ached and the cold came up through the stone floor of the tower, but she kept her voice level for him. [472] By late afternoon the alcove was done. [479] She had cut it into the tower's south wall, a small arched niche deep enough for the tile and shielded from the wind, and she set the last stone while the migration passed below. [513] It was the space she had been trying to sculpt for months: a place for reflection, small and quiet, where the thing could stop traveling. [538] The ninth tile waited until the stars came out. [547] She touched it and the tower went very still, the wind dropping the way it does when something important is about to be said. [571] The memory was not his. [576] It was hers. [579] It showed a girl of six on a wide stone shoulder, a man's hand steadying her boot, the sea below full of whales, and the whole world arranged for one perfect hour of looking. [613] The tile had been entangled with her before he ever gave it to her. [627] Belief had never been part of the transaction. [635] She set the tile into the niche and stepped back. [645] Below, a whale surfaced and blew, and the sound rose through the open tower like a low note held too long. [666] The ledger was full. [670] The symphony had been unheard all along, and now that it was written down it would remain unheard, which is the only way such a thing can be kept. [699] Maren still did not believe in magic. [706] She believed in the tile, in the niche, in the migration below, and in the man upstairs who was, for the first time in a year, smiling at a page he could not read. [740] That was enough arithmetic for anyone. [746]</story>
