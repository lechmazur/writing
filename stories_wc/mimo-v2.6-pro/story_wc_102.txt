The camera obscura tent stood at the far end of the twilight viaduct in bloom, its black cloth breathing with the river wind. [23] Inside, Sora ground her lenses, a camera obscura artist who had learned to paint with light before she learned to speak about grief. [46] She was spiritually curious in the stubborn way of the sick, asking every passing bell what it meant. [64]

Three nights earlier a past life memory had surfaced while she slept, uninvited and exact. [79] She remembered kneeling on wet stone, binding a stranger's wound with silk she had saved for her own wedding. [98] The stranger died anyway, and the silk stayed in her hands, unused. [110] That was the ache she carried now, the practical shape of a question she could not stop asking. [128] Where is unused love stored? [133] It had to be somewhere, because nothing that real could simply vanish into the fog the viaduct wore each evening like a shawl. [156] So she had become a pilgrim of sparks, walking the long spans to find the depot of the unlived. [175]

On the third night she set up the camera obscura facing the river and propped her treasure on the tripod leg. [196] It was a bead of glass the color of stormlight, no bigger than a lentil, and the old nun had pressed it into her palm without a word. [224] They called it Kwan Yin's tear. [230] Sora had stopped asking what it was for, since asking had never once made the river answer her. [248]

Tonight the viaduct was truly in bloom. [255] Wisteria had climbed the iron ribs all week, and in the violet hour the blossoms gave off a faint sweet static, as if the flowers were quietly burning. [283] Sora breathed the way the nun had taught her, long on the exhale, and the lens began to turn the world inside out. [306] This was her method, the only honest one she had left: revealing through breathing changes, letting each shift of her chest alter what the light allowed. [332] A held breath showed the city solid and indifferent. [341] A slow release showed it gauze-thin, every person lit from within by a low blue flame. [357]

Above the arches, far out over the water, a shadow gathered that no picture could contain. [373] It moved like someone searching a coat pocket. [381] Sora exhaled, and the bead on the tripod warmed, throwing a single drop of light onto the ground cloth. [400] The drop ran toward the shadow and disappeared into it, and the shadow stopped searching and stood still. [418] Then the fog lifted for one lucid second, the way a fever sometimes breaks without curing anything, and Sora saw the depot she had been walking toward. [445] It was not a place. [450] It was the space between two people who never quite met, the pause before a door someone decided not to knock on. [472] She had walked past it a thousand times, and it had held all of it, patiently, like water held in a jar no one opened. [497] Unused love was stored there, kept warm by absence, waiting to be spent on anyone at all. [514]

The shadow knelt in that space and opened its hands. [524] In them lay the wedding silk, still folded, still uncut. [534] Sora felt the old wound in her chest loosen, not from the memory but from understanding that the silk had been used at last, on a stranger, in a life she had already buried. [568] That was the healing, and it was small and it was complete. [580]

She breathed out a long, slow breath, and the viaduct settled back into its ordinary violet, and the beads of wisteria stopped their quiet burning. [605] Sora packed the lens, slipped Kwan Yin's tear into her shirt pocket where her heartbeat could keep it, and stayed a while longer. [628] There were other shadows out over the water, she knew now, other pockets being searched. [643] So she would keep walking the spans, a pilgrim of sparks with fog in her eyes and lucidity in her lungs. [664] The viaduct held her like an open hand, and the night went on blooming without needing her to understand it. [684] By dawn the cloth was damp with fog, and she left the tent standing so the next seeker would find the light already warm. [708]
