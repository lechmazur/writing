Ilya was the shard-mirror archivist, the last keeper of a forgotten library where every cracked pane held someone's exile. [19] The exile could be reversed, but only one way: the shard remembered the road home while the exile forgot it entirely. [40] So each night he walked the aisles backwards, because reversed motion was the only language the glass still trusted. [59] He walked backwards when he was nervous too, and these days he was nervous in every hour but one. [78] His work was to annotate: a line for the cut, a line for the return, a small fierce X for the ones he could not yet solve. [105] He annotated in a hand so small the pages looked printed, and he dated every line in both directions. [124] The shard he could not leave alone held a girl of seven and the hush of an empty playground swing drifting in the breeze. [148] Her name was Sera, scratched into the sill by a hand that had since learned to stop asking for help. [168] Beside her in the glass, an afternoon shadow lay caught across the floorboards like a second child waiting for the first to stand up. [192] That shadow was the key, and it turned only once a day, at the hush, when the courtyard swing outside began its slow drift. [216] He read the shard by analyzing the geometry of tea leaves. [227] A cup had dried in the trapped afternoon, and the leaves had fanned into straws, and the straws ruled a chart across the porcelain. [251] Where the straws converged, that was the place the shard would cut deepest on the way back through. [269] The chart told him to mark his own left palm, and he did, because a mentor who hides the cost teaches cowardice. [291] Nothing here was catalogued anymore, and the dust had learned to lie about how long anything had waited. [309] He had not told Sera he was teaching her at all; that was the quiet part of the arrangement. [328] She was seven when the glass took her, and she was still seven in it, and he was old in both directions now. [351] "Stop shuffling," she said from behind the pane, which was the first combative thing she had said in a year. [371] "Stop slouching," he answered, and set the ledger down where she could see the X he had drawn for her. [391] "That is mine," she said, and he said, "That is yours, and it is going to hurt, and you will hold it anyway." [414] Tenderness in his mouth always came out as a dare; he had never learned the softer grammar. [431] Resilience, he had decided long ago, was not a speech but a scar kept in view. [447] On the day of the turning he climbed to the north window, where a crack let the courtyard weather in. [467] The hush arrived the way it always did, the way a room stops breathing when someone in it decides something. [487] He was one breath late. [492] The shadow in the glass turned anyway and faced outward, and the seam he had mapped closed over his voice instead of her name. [516] Reversal has a price and it is never the one you bargain for; the ledger had warned him in his own handwriting. [538] Sera stepped out onto the sill of the world with the afternoon shadow walking at her side like a dog that had finally been called. [563] She looked at the cut on his left palm, which had not stopped bleeding, and she did not cry, which was the whole lesson. [587] "You are the archivist now," he wrote on the sill, since the price had taken the softer grammar too. [606] "Then I will walk it backwards," she said, and began, and got it wrong the first three times, and did not stop. [628] He stood in the aisle he could no longer name out loud and felt the combative tenderness in his chest go quiet, satisfied. [651] Outside, the empty swing drifted once more and settled, and the hush closed over the library like a page turned by a careful hand. [675]
