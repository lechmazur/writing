In the year after Sela stopped answering letters, Oren carried the courier satchel alone. [14] He was a jittery thing on the road, quick to flinch at wind. [27] Sela had trained him to hold still and listen, and then she had gone where no post ran. [45] What she left him was a dragonfly-wing pendant, thin as rumor, strung on a thread of copper. [62] It worked by vibrating sympathetically, waking against anything that trembled the same way he did. [77] His trade was the road between Nihl Ridge and Kesh Valley, where the Way of Ashes climbs to a black lake. [98] Under the aurora the ridgeway lit up in green arches, and the parcels he ferried were always dull. [116] A mason's invoice, a widow's winter salt, seeds for a farm that had failed twice. [131] Sela's rule was to coax wonder from the routine, to let the smallest errand germinate until it filled the whole hour. [152] So he learned to open each long walk into something larger than its errand. [166] In the year after she vanished, that practice was the only inheritance he trusted. [180] The pendant extended brief encounters; a two-minute rest against a stone could open into a wide, slow hour. [198] Time lengthened, and in its length a parcel could germinate into a story, a stranger into a friend. [216] Once a shepherd on the ridge gave him water that lasted a whole evening, and by dawn they had traded the names of everyone they loved. [242] He left that encounter lighter and heavier at once, which was the price of the extra hours. [259] He used the pendant sparingly, since the gift was a loan against his own rest. [274] That spring a clerk pressed a sealed letter into his hand and read him the address in dead ink. [293] The name was Sela's, and the road it named was the Way of Ashes. [307] He refused the contract and put the letter in his own satchel instead. [320] He told himself he carried it only so no one else would carry it wrong. [335] By the second day he had not opened it, though his fingers itched at the wax. [351] The ridge climbed, the aurora pulled its twin arches over him like the ribs of a great lantern. [369] The wind up there was thin and cold, and every loose strap on the satchel ticked like a nervous animal. [389] He stopped where the trail bent and pressed the pendant flat to the cold stone. [404] It answered at once, vibrating sympathetically, and the world slowed into a held breath. [418] In that lengthened hour he laid the letter on his knee and broke the seal. [433] Sela's hand was small and slanting, and the first line was not an instruction but a question. [450] She asked whether the road had begun to astonish him yet. [461] The letter was no order at all; it was an invitation to a place past the black lake, where she meant to germinate a school of couriers who would learn her rule. [493] She had not stopped answering; she had gone ahead to build the road he was already walking. [510] For two years he had mourned a woman who was not gone so much as ahead. [526] The ache in him swelled and germinated into want, the way a seed splits in the dark. [543] He read the letter twice, then a third time, until the words thinned and the story under them grew. [562] The pendant had been vibrating sympathetically the whole while, and he understood at last that it was not the stone that stretched the hours. [586] It was the wanting; the pendant only answered what he already trembled for. [599] That was the slow fade he had been warned about, memory wearing down into myth: the woman he knew becoming a figure on a trail she had drawn herself. [628] He rose with the aurora dying over Kesh Valley and walked the rest of the Way of Ashes without flinching. [648] The pendant hung warm at his throat, humming whenever the road trembled. [660] He carried the letter the whole way, and by the time the black lake opened at the horizon, he had told its story to two strangers who had never heard Sela's name. [692] Each telling thinned the woman and thickened the story, and he let it happen gladly. [707] In the year after that, his parcels were no less dull, and he could make any of them germinate.
