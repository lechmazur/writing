Ilse was a sharp-eyed dusk noticer, the archivist who alone kept the hour when the light gives up its argument with the day. [23] Each evening at seven she pressed her palm to the west windows of the blooming library above clouds and felt, under the glass, the long luminous regret of a world that had learned to share its dreams and lost the private shape of them. [67] In her coat pocket she carried a river stone, pale as a knuckle, worn flat by a current she had not stood in for forty years. [93] The stone had been in her hand the last afternoon she saw the house by the water, and she kept it to preserve the shape of hope, which erodes faster than stone. [125]

That spring the Vault called her down to the diff room, where two silent video recordings of the same river bend hung side by side, one filmed decades before dreams became shareable and one filmed this year. [162] No sound had ever been kept; the river spoke only in light, and Ilse was trained to read it. [181] She ran the comparison herself, softly deliberate, dragging the frames against one another until the library's lamps dimmed to match her patience. [203] Where the newer film grew darker than the older one, pixels shading where they should not, the system marked a shape. [224] It was a shadow unbound, a thing that had slipped out of the old footage and would not stay inside the wall. [246] It moved across the shelves and the marble, keeping the outline of a person who had once stood at that bend at dusk. [269] Ilse felt it come over her hands, and where it touched, her skin remembered cold river water. [286]

It did not speak; dreams had been shareable long enough that nothing needed to. [300] But she understood its request the way she understood dusk, as a change asking to be let finish. [318] It wanted to metamorphose. [321] Ilse set the river stone on the diff table, and the shadow went into it the way water goes into a crack, filling every worn hollow it found. [349] The stone darkened, then lightened, then took on the shape it had carried in the old film: the outline of a house with one lit window and a child's shadow standing in it. [382] For a moment the diff room held both of them, the woman and the house, as clearly as the river had once held the sky. [407] Then the shadow lifted out of the stone and went back to the wall, and the house's shape stayed behind in her palm, warm as a kept promise. [435]

Ilse did not trap it. [440] She had learned, in the years since the dreams came pourable and public, that hope is not kept by holding. [460] So she let the shadow go wherever unbound things go at dusk, and she kept only the warmth. [524] Nothing in the Vault made a sound, and that felt right. [535]

Years later, on the evenings when the ache climbed into her knees and the west windows went violet, Ilse still pressed her palm to the glass at seven. [563] She would take out the stone, which had never quite cooled, and hold it up against the last of the light. [584] The house was gone from it now, worn away by her thumb, but the warmth stayed where the window had been. [605] That, she thought, was the shape she had set out to preserve: not the house, not the child, but the lit thing that made a person look up from the water and believe the day might come back. [643] It had cost her the outline, and it had cost her the shadow, and she would pay both again. [662] Under all that cloud the river went on with its long argument, and the dusk, as always, noticed her first. [682]
