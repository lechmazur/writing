The whispering gallery ran the full circle of the dome, and Maren walked it the way she had learned to walk anything: at the focal point, where a cough at the north wall arrived intact at the south. [38]
She was a crisis actor for the port authority's drills, which meant she played the drowned and the stranded and the hysterical for crews in orange vests. [65]
She was also a real crisis survivor, the one person on the payroll who had actually been pulled out of the floodwater on Dock Nine, and she could not stop surviving. [96]
It was the interregnum then, the city with no governor to file to and no order to obey, so the drills kept running on their own momentum and Maren kept walking. [127]
In the center of the gallery floor sat a chessboard missing all the pawns, sixteen pieces and a double line of empty squares, a game abandoned mid-opening by someone who had never come back. [161]
Nobody dusted it. [164]
Nobody claimed it. [167]
The gallery was lit but emptied, and the light that fell through the high glass moved across the boards like something looking for a seat. [192]
Maren wanted to become the historian of small gestures. [201]
Not the flood. [204]
The flood had been written down already. [211]
She wanted the record of the hand that hesitates over a rook, the foot that turns at the wrong step, the way a person says nothing at the exact moment nothing is called for. [245]
So she walked to spell. [250]
Half-steps made the serifs; a long stride at the focal line made the stem of a letter; a pause made the space between words, and the dome carried each word around to where Halvard stood with his back to the wall, reading her ankles. [294]
Halvard had run the gallery for thirty years and he was benevolently ruthless, which meant he kept her account exact and refused every offer of pity she made to herself. [324]
"Again," he said, when she spelled UNBIND and then stumbled. [334]
He did not help her up. [340]
He wrote down the stumble. [345]
The nomadic penumbra came at four, the shadow of the traveling dusk that lived on the outer gallery with the tour groups and left with them, and the dome took on its long gray tone. [380]
Maren's heel had broken open inside her shoe and she did not stop walking, because stopping was the one gesture she refused to record. [404]
Halvard came down off the wall with a bandage and did not ask, and she let him, and neither of them said the word mercy. [429]
When the light thinned to nothing she walked the last word and stood still at the board. [446]

"You keep counting them," Halvard said. [481]
"Someone has to," she said. [486]

Then she unbound the record she had carried, the floodwater account she had wrapped around herself for four years, and set it down on the rim of the board where the pawns should have been. [527]
