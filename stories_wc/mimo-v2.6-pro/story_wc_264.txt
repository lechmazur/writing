Ilya Sorn does not dream, and that is why the archive cannot hold her. [14]
She guards the Bramble Wake, a freight cart with twelve sealed jars of salvage lashed to its ribs, and she walks it alone after the last forest burns. [42]
Ash still falls like grey snow from the west, and the ground remembers heat it can no longer name. [61]
Bolted above the driver's bench is a chess clock, a two-dial relay stasis unit from the burnt forest line, its faces frozen at midnight, 00:00, the hour the fire finished eating everything. [93]
It is the only thing on the cart that has not moved since she took the route. [110]
It is a poor joke of the forest line that the instrument built to keep watch is the one thing that will not keep time. [135]
She hums against the silence, a fractured lullaby her mother used to half-sing over the grinder, *hush now, little cinder, the dark is only unlit*. [160]
She never finishes the song, because the ending is where the archive waits. [173]
Twice a night it taps at the edge of her hearing, a ticking that is not there. [190]
The divergent archive is not a place; it is a lattice of wave functions that collapse into possible selves whenever something stops moving long enough to be read. [218]
It wants the jars, and it wants the woman who will not sleep, because a waking mind makes the finest index. [239]
It comes to her the way weather comes, occasionally everywhere at once. [251]
She turns and sees the glowing dune in bloom standing behind her as well as ahead, the same hill doubled in the ash-light, petals of ember-seed opening blue along its spine. [282]
That is the lure, she knows, and still she unhitches the Wake and walks toward it, because the dune is a hive of dormant sparks and she has not come all this way to leave them sleeping. [319]
Her job was never the hauling. [325]
It was to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners of the burnt world, to find what the fire thought it had finished and set it growing again. [353]
Twelve jars, twelve corners, twelve small arguments against an ending. [363]
Halfway up, the archive opens her. [369]
The air folds, the dune doubles again, and a version of Ilya steps out of the bloom with her mother's face and the archive's patience, smiling, holding out a hand to be taken. [402]
*Sleep now, little cinder, and I will carry the jars,* the other Ilya says through wave functions, her voice a set of probabilities collapsing into comfort. [428]
Ilya does not take the hand. [434]
Instead she charms it, the way she charms rusted latches and stubborn mules, with a low hum and a truth told at the wrong moment: *you are not my mother, and I am not tired, and the clock is not yours.* [475]
The charm works because charm is only attention paid on purpose, and the archive has never been looked at before, only used. [497]
The lattice shivers; the probabilities wobble; the frozen chess clock above the cart takes a single breath of time and its dials jump from 00:00 to 00:01. [524]
Once it moves, the archive can no longer pin her to one still image, and every version of Ilya it has built goes soft at the edges. [551]
In the folds she sees other routes she might have walked, other guards, other fires, and none of them end here. [572]
The false mother sighs into a thousand maybes and blows away like ash. [585]
Ilya walks the last of the slope and puts her palms flat into the warm bloom. [601]
The ember-seeds open under her hands, blue and then gold, and the whole dune answers like a choir given its note at last. [624]
*Hush now, little cinder,* she sings to them, *the dark is only unlit.* [637]
She leaves the jars at the crest, unsealed, so the seeds have something to root in. [653]
Then she walks back down to the Wake, where the chess clock is ticking softly at one minute past the end of the world, and she takes up the reins. [683]
There is ash for miles and no road, and behind her a hundred small fires she has not seen yet are about to sprout. [707]
