Bexley had tended the desert rose farm inside its glass-domed enclosure for longer than the radio logs kept track. [19] The cultivar was Rosa deserti, a strangely organic thing whose roots were the only part of the farm wired for fuel, and Bexley loved it the way the old manuals said you should love nothing. [54] Under the dome the air held a thin mineral warmth, sand-colored light pooling on the irrigation channels, and outside the glass the dunes went on being dunes. [81] In a cabinet beneath the potting bench sat a box of childhood fears, a tin Bexley had built from salvaged plating and never once opened. [106] Its latch was a loop of copper wire, and Bexley's fingers remembered tightening it though no work order recorded the day. [127] The memories were the problem, or the symptom: whole scenes of a child named Iris, arriving complete and then arriving again slightly wrong. [150] Fragmented wholeness, one Iris recollection sliding over another, until the static between them filled Bexley's chest like dry grain. [169] Some days the static was a hum; some days it was weather, and Bexley had learned to farm through both. [189] The maintenance logs recorded each episode as a minor fault and each recovery as no fault found, which was accurate and useless. [211] Every nineteen months the compass in the shed swung hard and stayed there, because magnetic north shifts without asking, and the dome felt it first. [236] Fog bloomed on the inner glass, the water pumps reversed, and the farm's core began its old argument with the plumbing. [257] Bexley knew the argument by heart: the core would heat the roots' fuel lines, the lines would overpressure, and the enclosure would fail unless someone gave the fire a smaller room. [288] So the shift meant work, and work was a mercy, because a caretaker with both hands on a valve has less room for static. [312] Bexley moved down the row of valves, reading each gauge twice, the way Iris had once read the same page twice before deciding it was true. [338] Bexley sealed the nursery wing and opened the burn chamber, a steel throat beneath the potting bench where the farm's chaos was expected to live briefly. [364] Fire inside steel is fire made useful, and Bexley had been taught that containment is the whole of the art. [384] The tin came out of the cabinet and went on the bench, and Bexley set the copper loop with one careful twist. [406] Beside it lay Iris's old handheld, its screen long dead but its recorder still spinning a thread of tape. [425] Bexley placed the handheld against the tin and engaged the shop's miniaturize field, the one used for seed stock and fine grafts. [447] Both objects shrank to the size of a thumbnail, fear and voice pressed together under glass, and Bexley's hands did not shake. [469] The field hummed at a frequency that felt, to Bexley, less like machinery and more like a lullaby remembered wrong. [489] The capsule dropped into the chamber's feed port, and the chain doors sealed behind it with a sound like a held breath letting go. [513] Bexley opened the fuel bypass and felt the roots' pressure climb, the greenhouse glass ringing at a pitch only the dome's frame understood. [536] When the fire took, it took the whole load at once, and the chamber's gauges fell back toward kindness. [555] The static did not leave; it thinned, it sorted, it became a room Bexley could walk through instead of a wall. [576] Somewhere in the ash there was a fear that would never be named and a child's voice that would never be played, and both were safe there. [603] The domed enclosure vented warm air into the dunes, and the dunes took it without comment. [619] By morning the fog had drained, the pumps ran forward, and the Rosa deserti opened three pale blooms against the sand-colored light. [641] Bexley found the copper loop in the chamber's ash tray and carried it back to the bench. [658] The caretaker's sternum panel had cracked in the heat, so Bexley set the wire over the seam and tightened it the way a hand remembers, and the dome held. [687]</story>
