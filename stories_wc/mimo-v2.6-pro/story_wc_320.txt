Mara had been a celestial signal interpreter for eleven years before she understood that the moon spoke mostly in pauses. [20] Above the gorge, the towers of Vesspera held their old catwalks like the ribs of a lantern nobody lit anymore. [40] Forty years earlier a broadcast premiere had gone out over these frequencies and a piece of its marquee light had fallen from the sky, cooling into what the scavengers called a hollywood star fragment. [74] Someone had set the fragment into a tuning fork, and the fork had passed to Mara when her teacher died. [94] She slept at her console with the fork across her knees, listening to the dead carrier hum of the suspended city. [115]

The notice arrived at the fourth hour: lunar perigee, the moon at its nearest, the one night in a year when the towers could be made to sing. [143] Mara climbed. [145] The ladder to the master mast was salt-slick and the wind off the gorge was cold and practical, and she took both as ordinary weather. [170] At the top she opened the console and saw the dial drifting, as it always did, in the direction nobody had ever named. [193] She tuned by the book first, because the book was what she had been given. [208] She set the fork against the receiver plate and listened for the long tone the manuals promised, the one that would open the frequencies and let the city sing. [237] Nothing came but the wind, and beneath the wind a smaller sound, like a held breath with edges. [255] Below her the gorge answered with nothing at all, and the catwalk cables sang their thin wind-song, and the city kept its silence like a debt. [281]

Mara began to discard her assumptions, one at a time, because they were the only instruments she had left. [300] She let go of the idea that the moon's signal was a voice; a voice needs words, and this had none. [321] She let go of the idea that the towers needed to broadcast; they had always been built to receive. [340] She let go of the idea that an ending must be finished with, that her teacher's death had closed a door rather than opened a frequency. [366] When the last assumption went, the console's hum fell away and the tower went serenely electric, the whole mast lit with a pale, even charge that hurt nothing. [394]

Mara understood then what the fork was for, and she struck it once against the rim of the plate. [413] The hollywood star fragment rang, and where its note should have been there was a shard of hush. [431] It was not empty silence; it had weight and shape, the pause that stands in front of every first word the moon will ever say. [456] Through the receivers below, the city answered: each tower took the hush and gave it back a note lower, a slow decrescendo rolling out over the gorge like a tide going out. [488] Mara recorded it all, naming each band by what it was not, which is how a celestial signal interpreter works when the sky refuses to be literal. [515]

The perigee passed and the light on the mast thinned to its old rust and rope. [531] Mara climbed down with the fork warm in her hand, and at the console she wrote the last line of the night's report before she wrote anything else. [559] She wrote that the beginning of the signal was the silence before it, and that this was the beginning she had come to find, the one that had been sitting inside her teacher's ending the whole time. [596] Outside, the gorge kept the hush like a held note, and Vesspera waited above it, all its towers tuned at last to the same patient frequency. [622] Mara slept then, and did not dream of the moon, which she took for a good sign. [639] In the morning the interpreters would come asking what she had heard, and she would hand them the silence first, and let them begin there. [664]
