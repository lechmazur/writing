Snow had sealed the outer windows of the annex hours ago, and the library beneath it had grown so quiet that Ilse Marek could hear frost feathering along the duct glass. [31]
He was the cryogenic archivist of Ward Nine, which meant he kept the register of the sleeping and the dust of the shelves in the same steady hand. [59]
Tonight he was moving frozen case files from the deep stacks to the thaw bay when his lamp caught something wedged between two steel binding rings. [85]
It was an envelope of heavy bond paper, cracked at the fold, and it had no catalogue number, which in this building was the same as having no name. [114]
He turned it over and read the endorsement line in a faded violet hand: to be delivered by hand, and never by ledger. [137]
Inside lay a single sheet that named the sleepers of Ward Nine by number and argued, in cold administrative language, that their forgetting had been arranged. [163]
It was a dangerous liaison letter between the council archive and the hospital wing, a confession dressed as correspondence, and it was thirty-one years late. [188]
Ilse read it twice, and the surprise of it detonated inside the pause of one breath, the blink that follows surprise, when the room tilts and time goes long. [217]
The dizzying quiet of the stacks pressed against his ears, and the building seemed to hold its breath with him. [237]
He stood bravely conflicted, one hand on the letter and one on the register, custodian of two truths that could not share a shelf. [261]
The register said the sleepers rested in care. [269]
The letter said they had been misplaced on purpose, their numbers retired to keep the budget of the ward in balance. [290]
Contraband truths, he thought, are the only cargo a building like this cannot hold. [304]
He wanted to provoke an unspoken conversation, the kind that begins between two people who both know and neither speaks. [324]
So he folded the sheet into the envelope and carried it to the wall where the old pneumatic tubes still ran behind the shelving. [348]
These were the dormant networks of the annex, silent since the flooding of the lower vaults, and no one living had charted their routes. [372]
Ilse had charted them anyway, in his first winter here, and he knew that line four terminated in the counting room of the neighboring ward. [397]
He oiled the brass gasket, seated the canister, and felt the mechanism take the load with a vibration that traveled through the floor. [420]
The distant motor coughed once and the canister went away into the dark, carrying the letter toward the doyen of that ward, Nils Aurvang. [444]
What he had done would scandalize both archives if the sheet reached a ledger, and Ilse knew he had just insured that it would. [468]
He waited in the stacks with his lamp turned low, counting his own pulse against the tick of the cooling plant. [489]
Twenty minutes later a single graphite stroke appeared on the case tag of sleeper 447, scratched into the frost by a hand that had reached the receiving slot. [517]
It was not a signature and not a refusal. [526]
It was the beginning of the unspoken conversation Ilse had wanted, the first mark in a margin where nothing had been written for thirty-one years. [551]
By morning the graphite would be copied, and the copied line would be copied again, and the annex would no longer be able to keep the quiet it kept. [580]
Ilse walked back to the register and set his lamp on the desk beside it. [595]
The snow above the windows had thickened until no daylight reached the stacks at all. [610]
He opened the book to the page for sleeper 447 and read the line he had maintained for nine years. [630]
Resting in care. [633]
He wrote nothing new beside it, because the conversation belonged to others now, and because his custody was still his to answer for. [656]
Then he returned to the wall, opened the service panel, and set each gasket back in its numbered seat. [675]
He cleaned the oil from the brass and brushed the graphite dust from the case tag until only the frost remained. [696]
Bravely conflicted, he chose for the second time that night, and he chose differently. [710]
The contraband truths were out through the dormant networks and could not be called back, and the register would be tended by a hand that had seen them leave. [739]
In the dizzying quiet of the echoing library he closed the book, and the click of it carried to the far wall and came back to him. [766]
