After the foghorn fell silent, Vashen climbed to the orchard garden atop the Hesper Tower and laid out his instruments. [20]
For sixty years he had served as the city's oath scribe, the last man licensed to bind a promise in law, ritual, and language. [44]
He was venerable in the truest sense: stooped, walnut-dark, and trusted the way a well-worn seal is trusted. [62]
His hands shook now only when he handled the shavings. [72]
Each shaving was a pale curl of wax pared from a phonograph cylinder, thin as a petal and smelling faintly of honey and iron. [96]
Vashen's project, begun after his wife's death, was to archive emotional patterns: the exact texture of grief, the pitch of relief, the tonal drift of a vow decaying into indifference. [126]
He had no science for it, only the shavings, the phonograph horn, and the dead languages he could speak without thinking. [147]
Sumerian, Etruscan, Gothic, the liturgical syllables of a faith that had outlived its gods by a thousand years. [165]
Old words held emotion the way riverbeds hold water, and Vashen knew where to dig. [180]
The orchard garden was itself an anachronism, four hundred ancient grafted apple trees in raised beds of black soil, irrigated by cisterns, ninety floors above the harbor. [207]
A philanthropist had built it during the last century so that children would never forget that fruit once grew on trees. [228]
Vashen remembered, without meaning to, the curious field trip he had taken here in the third grade, thirty small children in yellow caps. [251]
Their guide had been a young oath scribe, the same office he would one day hold, and the man had let the class record their names on a wax cylinder. [281]
Each child spoke; the cylinder turned; the words sank into wax and slept there. [295]
He had never heard the playback, only the shaving the guide trimmed afterward and handed him as a souvenir. [314]
Tonight Vashen did what he always did: he poured warm water into a shallow basin and immersed the shaving until it softened and turned translucent. [339]
Then he laid it across the diaphragm of the phonograph, where a needle of bone would ride its faint grooves and speak what had been spoken over it. [367]
Usually the machine gave him murmurs, the emotional residue he catalogued in ledger after ledger. [382]
But the shaving from his childhood held something else. [391]
The horn exhaled, and a chorus of small voices poured out into the fog-still air of the orchard: thirty children speaking their names in a dead language. [418]
Vashen went cold, because he understood every word. [426]
They were not names, not anymore. [432]
They were instructions, each child reciting in Sumerian the manner of a death that had not yet happened. [450]
One would drown in the harbor; one would be taken by fire in the tower's lower floors; one would simply vanish, leaving a coat folded on a bench. [478]
Last of all came the voice he remembered as his own, saying only: keeper. [492]
It was an accidental prophecy, unwilled and unasked, a prophecy made by children who had no idea what they were saying. [513]
Vashen stood in the orchard with the phonograph still turning and felt a velvet disquiet settle over him, soft as the fog that had swallowed the foghorn. [540]
He did not know whether to stop the needle or let it finish. [553]
Silence, he had learned, was the only vow no one ever broke. [565]
He let it finish. [569]
When the last syllable faded, the garden seemed to lean toward him, all four hundred trees listening. [586]
Below, the city resumed its work of keeping promises it would not honor. [599]
Vashen sat down among the roots and opened his ledger to a fresh page. [613]
He would archive this too: the emotional pattern of knowing, before anyone else, exactly how the end would come. [632]
He wrote in Sumerian, then in the language of the first vow he had ever sworn, which had no name left to carry it. [656]
Above him the fog pressed down again, and the silence of the harbor held like a drawn breath. [674]
Not a single horn answered from the water. [682]
Only the phonograph, winding down, gave its long low note, and it carried every name that had never been true. [702]
Vashen closed the ledger and waited for morning with the patience of a man who has already heard the whole of it. [724]
