Lino had been drifting for eleven years when the woman with the rope found him at the roadside shrine. [19]
He wrote epitaphs for strangers in exchange for supper, and he was, by the reckoning of every village between the coast and the salt flats, the only honest trade left on the road. [52]
Her name was Serai, and she set the coil down in front of him like a purse. [69]
It was a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape, salt-stiff in the fibers, one end hardened where it had burned against a wall. [94]
Three months under my mattress, she said, and now I want words for it. [108]
Lino picked the rope up and began his method, which was uncovering layers. [121]
First the salt, which meant the sea wall of the prison at Verra. [134]
Then the char, which meant she had not gone down the wall but up and over, past the fires. [153]
Then the fraying, which meant the rope had been used twice before her. [166]
He asked what she had done to earn the sentence. [176]
Serai told him, and the story was the other half of the payment. [189]
The guards had offered her an impossible choice: trade her half of the bridge at Corren for her brother's release, or keep the bridge and keep the sentence. [217]
She had refused, the ethical way, and so they had handed her the rope themselves to make her choice cost less. [238]
That was how they were, she said, always building a softer door behind the hard one. [254]
She watched his face while she said it, ethically playful, as though the whole matter were a poem she had already finished. [276]
Lino led her down to the terra-cotta grotto under starlight, where he did his serious work. [292]
The cave was alive with formulae, every hand-sized piece of stone traced in lines that the water had laid down. [312]
Every hand-sized piece had been set in place by those same arrivals, twenty-two years ago, when the energy beings came and began lifting stones out of the earth. [340]
Lino had spent those years making rubbings of the stone, because his real motive was to discover the texture of time, and time left its texture in what it touched. [370]
He told her all of this while the stars turned, and she listened the way a person listens who has already made her hardest decision. [395]
What he wanted was a pattern, one wording he could say for anyone, a line that would hold whether the dead had died well or badly. [421]
He had been close to it three times and lost it each time, which is why he took the rope as payment and not coins. [446]
By midnight he had uncovered the last layer. [454]
The rope had not saved her. [460]
Her courage had. [463]
She had climbed out on a thing that two others had already failed on, and she had done it knowing what waited above. [486]
So he wrote the first draft on the cave floor with a wet finger: One / free hand / for the cliff. [506]
Serai read it and laughed, a short sound that the grotto threw back at her. [521]
That is for a man, she said. [528]
Give me mine. [531]
He looked at the rope again, at the three months of it, at the choice she had made and then unmade by leaving. [554]
The pattern came then, not in a rush but the way a rubbing comes up off the stone, line by line, and he saw that the texture of time is what a person leaves behind on the things they handle. [594]
He stripped his words down to three, because a poem for a woman who had refused a soft door should not have four. [617]
Three months / under the mattress. / She left. [624]
Serai did not cry. [628]
She put her hand flat on the wet letters and held it there until the stone took the warmth out of her palm. [651]
Then she pressed the coil of rope into his hands and told him he had found the texture of time, and that she would not need the last layer of it where she was going. [686]
Lino stayed in the grotto until the starlight thinned, rubbing the rope between his fingers the way he rubbed the stone. [707]
It had the same feel as the cave wall and the same feel as her voice, and he understood at last that he had been reading one material all along. [737]
When the energy beings lifted the next stone out of the earth, at the far end of the valley, he was already walking toward them with the rope over his shoulder, ready to ask what their epitaphs should say. [776]
