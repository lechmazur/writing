Resa had played the sky-bridge since before she remembered choosing to. [11] Each dawn she crossed the span of fused iron and glass, violin tucked beneath her chin, and sent her music down into the orchard that nobody else was permitted to enter. [42] The orchard lay under the charge of the Court of Clockwork, and its trees bore fruit of hammered gold. [61] Living statues walked its rows, pale guardians whose blank faces turned toward any deliberate sound. [76] Resa was tolerated because she was useful. [83] The Court measured beauty the way it measured torque, and her playing kept the orchard's enchantment turning. [100] It was, everyone agreed, a fine arrangement, except that lately Resa had begun to feel like one more cog among the golden fruit. [123] The thing she could not stop thinking about was the locust shell. [135] It sat on a marble plinth at the orchard's heart, empty and brown and thin as a dried leaf. [154] The Court had found it in the first spring and set it there as a relic of the old wild magic that the machines had replaced. [180] Resa knew exactly what it was. [186] It was the proof that something had once wriggled out of its own armor and flown. [202] Understanding that, and knowing what it asked of her, were two separate distances, and she stood in the gap between them every morning while she tuned her strings. [230] This dawn she did not tune. [236] She laid her bow across the rail of the bridge and pressed two fingers to the hollow of her throat. [256] Signaling through heart rhythms was a thing the old musicians had done before the Court tuned the world. [274] A pulse carried along the vein is soundless, and the statues cannot hear it. [288] Resa sent one beat down through the glass and iron, slow and deliberate, the rhythm the orchard had once known. [308] Under the hammered fruit, something resonantly soft answered. [316] Roots that had not moved in a century began to loosen their grip on the old stones. [333] Then Resa climbed down. [337] She had never set foot in the orchard, and the moment her boots touched the soil the first statue turned its blank face toward her. [362] It heard nothing. [365] It heard only her, and it began to walk. [374] Resa ran, and her blood hammered out a rhythm of terror that the statues could not parse. [391] She reached the plinth and closed her hand around the locust shell. [403] It weighed almost nothing. [407] She meant to break it free of the marble, to uproot it the way the orchard was uprooting itself, but the pin that held it was fused and the plinth would not yield. [440] The first statue reached her. [445] Its stone fingers closed on her shoulder, and the contact sent a sound through her that was not a sound at all but the Court's whole tuning, cold and perfect and finished. [477] In that gap between knowing and understanding, Resa finally stepped across. [488] She stopped trying to save the relic and began to play. [499] With the statue's grip on her she lifted her violin, and she played the rhythm her heart had been carrying, the one the orchard had answered, resonantly soft and unmechanical. [529] The statues froze. [532] Not because they heard it, but because for one instant the Court's tuning failed, and what the guardians enforced simply was no longer there. [556] The locust shell cracked. [560] Not because she had broken it, but because she had stopped holding it together with her wanting. [577] It fell away in pieces, and underneath there was nothing at all, which was the whole point of a shell. [597] Resa stood in the orchard with her violin and her unguarded heartbeat and felt the trees begin to fruit in colors that had no name in the Court's ledger. [626] The statues stood where they were, patient and tarnished, waiting for an order that would not come. [643] Some of them, she thought, looked relieved. [650] By evening the bridge above was quiet, and Resa played for no one. [663] The music that came out of her was tarnished wonder, the sound of a beautiful machine learning it could stop. [683] Nothing in the orchard was engineered anymore, and nothing was safe, and for the first time in her life Resa did not know what the next note would be. [712] That was where beauty lived, she understood at last, not in the answer but in the space just before it. [732] Behind her the orchard breathed, wild and unfinished, and somewhere in its rows a locust began to sing. [750]
