<story>Mara called herself a myth buster, and she meant it literally. [11]
Since her father's death she had made a living dismantling coastal legends for a regional folklore journal, proving each one a trick of tide, light, or grief. [38]
The cliff observatory had been her first assignment and her only failure. [50]
It stood abandoned now, its brass telescope rusted at the swivel, the dome's shutter frozen half open as if the building itself were mid-sentence. [74]
She came back anyway, inside the whisper of closing pages, because the city had posted demolition notices and the observatory had five days left. [98]
Her father had brought her here as a girl to watch the winter swells. [112]
He had also left her three things: a half-finished cello, a painted seashell, and a riddle copied into the back of his field book. [136]
The riddle was the last numbered question in a ledger of numbered questions, and it read: What points everywhere and nowhere until it is told? [161]
She had spent two years trying to decode a universal riddle from a dead instrument maker, and she was no closer than the day she started. [187]
What she had instead was the cello, unfinished, its spruce top planed thin as a fingernail, its scroll still blank. [207]
And the shell, which he had painted in bands of grey and stubborn yellow. [221]
She pressed the shell against the cello's belly, the way he had once shown her, and held the phone he had recorded his calls on. [246]
Then she began the work of retelling. [253]
She hummed through the bridge of the half-finished cello, low and even, until the wood answered her with resonant hums of its own. [276]
The seashell took those hums and turned them, and for a moment the air between them held a direction. [295]
She called it the ephemeral compass: a heading that existed only while the sound lasted, and scattered the instant she stopped. [316]
That was where the phone came in. [323]
She played his recordings and spoke back to them, reimagining what he had said until the words she gave him were the words she wished he had used. [351]
Each retelling bent the past a little, and each retelling bent the compass with it. [366]
It always pointed somewhere new, and never anywhere true. [375]
Tonight the compass pulled her toward the dome's frozen shutter instead of the sea. [389]
She laughed at that, a thin and cheerful sound in a very gloomy room. [403]
Being a myth buster had taught her to be cheerful about disappointment; it arrived so reliably. [419]
She set the phone on the observing ledge and let the call play out to its end, her father's voice thin against the wind coming through the shutter. [447]
In the recording he was telling her about the tide charts, and in her answer he was telling her he was proud. [469]
Both were true, or neither; that was the trouble with reimagined conversation. [481]
The shell hummed its heading again, and this time it held steady longer than it ever had. [498]
Mara followed it, cello under one arm like a wooden child, and the direction took her up the observatory's iron stair to the roof. [522]
There the compass did something it had never done. [531]
It stopped pointing at the land or the water and tipped upward, into the grey, as though the riddle had been waiting above her the whole time. [558]
What points everywhere and nowhere until it is told? [567]
The sky, she thought: visible from every shore, belonging to none, fixed only when someone stands and names what they see. [588]
She spoke the answer aloud into the wind, and the ephemeral compass held one beat longer, agreeing, before it dispersed. [608]
Below her the demolition notices fluttered on the fence like pale birds that had not yet learned to leave. [627]
Mara sat on the cold metal with the painted seashell warm in her palm and the half-finished cello across her knees, and she played her father's call one last time. [657]
She hummed an answer she had never given him. [666]
For one bright gloomy moment the compass came back and pointed at the sky, and the old observatory kept her secret inside its closing pages. [691]</story>
