The gloom-hill coachman drove the last of his passengers down to the blooming jetty at twilight. [16] The reef had died the year before, and the harbor water, no longer colored, carried a flat metallic shine. [35] His name was Vessel, though nobody used it. [43] They called him the coachman because he ferried goods and grief between the hill and the water, and because the hill was called Gloom. [67] Beneath the jetty lay the old battlefield, drowned and quiet, the place where two nations had finally signed their submerged concord. [88] The treaty's ink had been swept out to sea long ago, and nobody living could read the terms. [106] The war had lasted nine days and taken four thousand, and the peace that followed took the rest. [124]

Vessel kept a collection of dried seed pods in a tin box under the driver's bench. [140] He had gathered them for eleven years, one pod for every voice he could still remember. [156] For Vessel spoke dead languages. [161] Not one, but the four buried under this coast: the salt tongue of the reef-folk, the whistled court speech of the drowned palace, the ledger cant of the harbor guilds, and the hymn language of the battlefield's dead. [199] He spoke them the way other men breathed, without effort and without hope of reply. [214] The jetty was narrow, a single plank arm reaching into the vast gray of the water, and standing on it felt like standing inside a held breath. [241] That was the shape of the world now: narrow vastness, a thin place where everything distant pressed close. [259]

He left the coach where the planks began and walked out alone, the tin under his arm. [276] The pods were dry and hollow, each one a small dark bell. [288] He had come to fertilize the drowned ground, to let a garden reclaim the battlefield. [303] Grass and silt had already begun the work, but grass and silt were slow, and Vessel was not a patient man. [324] His method was older than patience. [330] He listened to the birds and understood what they said. [340] The gulls cried in a fractured reef-tongue, a word that meant both grief and salt. [355] The marsh wren piped the court whistles, asking permission for nothing, asking it endlessly. [369] A single cormorant on the piling spoke hymn language, the only voice left above water that still prayed. [387] So the birds told him where the ground was ready, and where it was still bitter with iron and bone. [407] He crushed the pods over the water at the spots they named. [419] The seed meal drifted down through the pale twilight and settled on the drowned field like a second snow. [438] Where it touched, the silt darkened and something in it turned toward the surface. [452] With each pod he spoke a name in a dead language, and each name was a door. [469]

Ossua of the Reef-Folk, whose nets once held the whole bay. [480] Marda of the Court Whistles, who signed the concord with a broken wrist. [493] Guild-voice Tern, hymn-keeper Allo, and the eleven unnamed who had held the jetty when it was a wall. [511] The birds answered each name with a single clear note, and Vessel marked the water with his thumb. [529] He understood them because the languages were dead only to the living. [541] In the throat of a bird, an old word went on being said. [554] When the last pod was empty, he set the tin on the plank and stood. [569] Below him the jetty bloomed, that strange flowering of barnacle and weed that had taken the wood since the reef died and left the water strange. [595]

Vessel had not come to mourn the reef. [603] He had come to make the drowned ground answer for itself. [614] The concord had been an agreement between men; the garden would be an agreement between everything else. [631] His coach had carried the mourners up the hill for a decade; tonight he carried nothing but the empty tin and the certainty of green. [656] He climbed back onto the bench, took up the reins, and turned the coach toward Gloom-hill. [672] Behind him the last light held on the water, narrow as a blade and wide as a world. [690] He did not look back, because looking back was the only language he refused to speak. [706] By spring, the birds would say the names again, and the drowned field would answer green. [722]
