The basement chapel of the rented house smelled of cold laundry and hot wire. [14] One bare bulb hung over the loop pedal, and the congregation tonight was a woman strapped to a folding chair and a tone. [37] The tone was the dimensional anchor, a single perfect note held in a speaker cone, and it did not waver. [57] Everything else in the room leaned toward it, including the loop pedal priest. [68] He knelt at his pedal board with his collar open and his thumb on the record switch, and he waited for the stillness to start unraveling. [94] It was already starting. [98] The woman's head had been level for an hour; now it tilted, degree by degree, as if something behind her eyes was adjusting a mirror. [123]

[149] His voice went out clean and came back as a question. [159] He tapped the pedal: one pulse, recorded, played, layered. [168] Then again, so the pulse had a shadow. [176] Then a third time, and the shadow had a shadow, and the room filled with a small crowd of his own breathing. [198]
This was his method, and he had learned it from rhetoric, not from scripture. [212] He fed the thing its own assertion: you are comfortable here. [223] Then he looped it, rose an octave, and let the phrase hang until the grammar gave up and it heard the question inside it. [247] The woman's mouth opened on the wrong beat. [254] Behind her teeth something answered in a voice that was mostly his. [266] He was glad of that. It meant the loop had gotten inside. [278]
The priest knew the anchor had to be plumbed, not shaken. [289] The note held the door; the thing held the woman; only the stillness between them could be made to fail. [309] So he would plumb it with silence. [316] He decorated the loop with gaps, whole measures of nothing, dropped in like counterweight. [330] Each gap was an anaphora of absence; each time the silence returned it was the same silence, said again, and it accumulated. [352]

[375] He had not chosen it; it had been left in the house, and he had adopted it because he could not afford a choice. [399] One color was honest. It said: this is the only thing I intend to mark.
