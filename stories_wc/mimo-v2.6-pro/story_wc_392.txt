The rain had not stopped for two days, and the subway tunnels were drinking back what four years of drought had withheld. [22]
Inez Vautrin, preventable death investigator for the transit authority, walked the maintenance corridor with a tape measure on her belt and a lantern in her left hand. [49]
She moved watchfully calm, the way she had been taught to move through any place where a body was waiting. [69]
The lantern was old, a railroad lantern, black iron with a chimney of green glass, and it had been her grandmother's before it was taken from her at her induction. [99]
Aldric Pane had called it a teaching object and kept it on his shelf for eleven years. [116]

She found him forty meters in, propped against the conduit wall with his coat buttoned to the throat. [134]
His own maintenance lamp still burned beside him, untrimmed and steady. [145]
Everything about the scene was deliberate, staged by a man who had always believed the corridor should narrate itself. [164]
She set the grandmother's lantern down and began, through empirical observation, to make the corridor tell her what had happened. [184]
She measured the tread depth at his heels: four millimeters, the same worn geometry she had documented in her third case, the one Aldric had refused to let her close. [214]
She leveled the conduit: a two degree bow toward the pump room, which she had also recorded, in a corridor that should not have existed. [239]
In his breast pocket she found three unfinished memos, all of them citing her case numbers. [255]
Cite a case, bend a life, he used to say; she had thought it was a joke. [272]
Their memories were entangled here, his past and hers braided around the same masonry, and the closer she measured, the less she could say whose recollection she was standing inside. [302]

The tunnel did its work on the sound. [310]
Her tread echoed three ways at once: her own step, the drip of new rain on the conduit, and the hum of the generator that had run for four years without stopping. [342]
Each sound came back altered, and the alterations seemed to reverberate through her until the corridor breathed with two sets of lungs. [364]
She wrote it down anyway. [369]
The drought had taught the city that a failure to see is not the same as a failure to be, and the tunnel had been here the whole time, dry and patient. [401]
Now the water found it, and with the water, her. [411]

Aldric had spent his last morning here, she decided, not investigating but revising the record. [426]
His memos corrected her findings the way his voice had corrected her for eleven years: not loudly, not cruelly, but with the calm certainty of a man who believed stewardship meant holding a person in place. [462]
That was the controlling influence she had never named out loud. [473]
It kept her at her desk when she wanted to leave and at his side when she wanted to speak. [493]
She had been fortified all her adult life in the shape of his instructions, and she had called it character. [513]
Here was the vulnerability under the fortification, a small and shaking thing that wanted her grandmother's light back. [531]

She picked up the railroad lantern. [537]
The wick caught on the third strike, and the green glass threw a low circle over the conduit bow. [556]
It had crossed a border once under a floorboard like this one, she remembered, and the memory was not his to correct. [578]
She took the measurements again by its light because the body would not stay and the numbers would. [596]
The tread was deeper than four millimeters at the toe. [606]
The conduit had not bowed toward the pump room but toward a blind spur, bricked off before the drought. [625]
Aldric's memos were wrong in two places, and he had never known it. [638]
The relief she felt was physical, like a joint going back into its socket. [652]
She could break free now, because the method had answered the doctrine: a measure is not an opinion. [670]

She wrote her findings in her own hand, in her own notebook, and left his memos open on his chest where the rain would come for them. [697]
The transit authority would call it a preventable death and file it, and she would write the reason herself, because no one was left to write it for her. [726]
Then she lifted the lantern and walked out the way she had come. [739]
Behind her the generator hummed, the new rain dripped, and her tread faded into the corridor that had waited four years to be heard. [763]
