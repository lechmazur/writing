Oren Vale was a music box repairman who kept his workshop in the old resonance chamber beneath the clocktower. [19]
The chamber's vaulted walls curved like a violin's belly, so every tick, every chime, every whispered gear-song gathered there and multiplied, and Oren loved that tinkersong wonder more than money. [49]
On his bench lay the prize he had chased for three winters: a chip from the philosopher's stone, no bigger than a thumbnail, warm as a coal in a sock. [79]
It held one grain of the stone's old light, and Oren meant to set it in a broken lullaby box so the light would sing instead of hide. [107]
For the chip, like every honest metal in that room, wilts near dishonesty; when a lie is spoken, its glow folds inward like a flower closing at dusk. [135]
That rule was no mere trinket of magic but the covenant of rust, the old accord the tinker-guilds swore at their first forge: that no mend made on a lie would hold. [167]
Rust would creep the seams of any cheated repair, and the covenant's witnesses were the hammers and the hinges and the patient grey dust. [191]
Oren's customer that morning was a widow named Sela who brought him a box that had not played since the midnight of her wedding night. [216]
She said the lid was warped, the cylinder cracked, the comb teeth bent. [229]
Oren opened it and found nothing broken at all; only a hollow where the lullaby's light had been. [247]
Some thief of hours had taken it at midnight, and what remained was a wound in tune. [264]
He understood then that his true work was mending what isn't torn. [276]
The box was whole; the song was not, and repair had to reach past brass and into the balance of things. [297]
For that he would need the chip, and he would need a tether. [310]
He drew a silver hair from Sela's comb, twisted it into a thread, and set one end in the chip and the other in the empty light-cradle of the box. [340]
The tether would not bind metal to metal but grief to hope, which is the only repair the covenant of rust ever honored. [363]
Then Sela leaned over the bench and lied. [371]
She said the box had never mattered to her, that she was only clearing out her house, that she did not care whether it sang again. [397]
The chip on the bench wrinkled and darkened, its glow folding shut like a fist. [412]
Oren stayed the tools and looked at her with a tinker's quiet, waiting for the truth to come back into tune. [433]
At last she said that the box held the only recording of her husband's voice, that midnight had stolen it while she slept, and that she had lied to keep from weeping in a stranger's workshop. [469]
Her honesty unfurled the chip again, brighter than before, and the resonance chamber answered with a low warm chord. [488]
Oren pressed the tether home and turned the crank. [497]
Out of the silence bloomed a man's humming lullaby, thin and bright as a candle in a well. [515]
It rose through the vault of the chamber and came back multiplied, so that the room rang like a choir of small gold bells. [539]
Sela wept then, freely, and the chip burned clean in her lap. [551]
When she left, Oren opened the window to the cold hour before dawn and felt the world still hung in the balance. [573]
He knew his work was not finished, because somewhere in the tower the thief of midnight still carried stolen light. [593]
So the repairman wound a fresh tether of silver and song, mending what isn't torn. [608]
His own lullaby box, waiting on the shelf, needed the light that midnight stole from him the night his daughter died. [629]
In the covenant of rust he had sworn to mend, and he would not break that oath before the coming dark. [650]
He set the chip in his pocket, where it glowed against his heart like a small stubborn dawn. [668]
Then he climbed the clocktower stairs, to reclaim the light stolen by midnight and hear the whole world sing. [687]
