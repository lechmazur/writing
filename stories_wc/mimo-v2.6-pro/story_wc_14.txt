The gilded-leaf stationer kept her shop at the foot of the mountain, and after the town went silent she had no one left to sell to. [26]
So she went up before dawn instead, into the blooming labyrinth of bonsai forests that had overtaken the summit terraces. [46]
She was earnestly studious about the work, which was this: to collect the textures of different times. [63]
Her method she had worked out herself, by charting the faint hum in a fluorescent lamp at dawn. [81]
The hum was almost nothing. [86]
It was the only sound left in the building, and it changed a little each morning, and she believed those changes were the times rubbing against one another. [114]
She wrote them down in a battered notepad she had carried since the shop opened, its cover soft as cloth from handling. [136]
Each page held one time. [141]
She gave them dates she did not verify, because verification was no longer available, and she preferred the pages to stay quiet. [163]
On the path up she carried a brass rule, and when the mist thinned she would refract the dawn along its edge and measure how far the gold fell. [192]
That was the second half of the method. [200]
The first half was the hum; the second was the light. [211]
Together they were the texture of a morning, and a morning was a time. [225]
The bonsai forests had been ordinary nursery stock once. [234]
Left untended on the terraces they had bloomed into a labyrinth, hundreds of miniature pines and maples and olives in their pots, each no taller than her knee, all of them now walled into corridors by years of unchecked growth. [274]
She walked those corridors with the notepad open in one hand. [285]
On the fourth morning she found a tree larger than the rest, a pine in a cracked ceramic pot, and it carried a wire tag with a name and a date on it. [318]
The date was older than the town. [325]
It did not belong to anything she could remember, and she did not know the name, Aurelia Voss. [343]
So she decided it was true. [349]
She wrote the history of the pine that day, and the history of the schoolteacher who had planted it before the terraces were cut, and the history of the terraces themselves. [380]
None of it was true. [385]
All of it fit. [389]
She filled eleven pages with invented histories that week, each one cross-referenced against the lamp's hum and the fall of the gold, each one set in a different wood. [418]
Pine for the planting, olive for the silence, maple for the leaving. [430]
The notepad grew heavy with them. [436]
On the eighth morning, at the drainage channel where the terraces emptied, the notepad slipped from her hand and went into the water. [459]
The water was cold and fast and brown with the mountain's own clay. [472]
She got it out, but the ink ran. [480]
Numbers and history lines and the whole invented past bled together into one long wash, and she could not tell which date belonged to which tree, or whether any of it had been charted at all. [516]
The artifact had become fiction, and she had made it herself. [527]
She sat down beside the channel and did not cry, because crying would have been a texture she had not earned. [548]
She held herself in incandescent restraint, and went back up at dawn the next day anyway. [564]
Without the notepad she could only listen, and she listened with the whole of her. [579]
The hum was still almost nothing. [585]
The gold still fell along the brass rule the same distance every morning. [598]
She began to scratch her histories into the pots instead, one line per pot, in the clay rim where it was still rough. [621]
The labyrinth could hold more than a notepad ever had. [631]
By the end of the season she had named every tree in the upper terraces, and most of them had been named by nobody. [655]
The pine with the wire tag she left alone. [664]
It had a name already, true or not. [672]
In the second winter the channel froze and then thawed and gave up what it had taken. [689]
The notepad came back to her out of a nursery pot, pressed flat, with pale roots running through the pages where the water had fed something. [715]
She could read fragments only. [720]
Aurelia. Gold falls. Pine, twelve, do not verify. [728]
The rest was texture now, and texture was what she had wanted. [740]
She planted the notepad in the largest pot she owned, in the corridor nearest the summit, where the light came down cleanest. [762]
By spring a small pine had rooted in it, and she read it every dawn, and it said nothing, which was the truest thing she had charted. [789]
