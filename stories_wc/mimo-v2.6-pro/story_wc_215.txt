The beekeeping oasis had been abandoned for nine years before the ghosts became visible, and the recluse had already forgotten the sound of a swarm. [25]
He kept the place anyway: forty-two clay hives beneath a scorched canopy of reed, a windbreak of dead oleander, and the cracked basin where water once pooled and now held only baked salt and the rings of old thirst. [64]
Beyond the windbreak the wasteland ran flat and ash-colored to every horizon, and nothing crossed it except heat and the occasional bone-colored bird. [87]
At the center of the compound, on a plinth of fused sand, sat the stained glass box he could not restore. [108]
Its panels were dense with color the wasteland had outlawed, and where the light passed through it, the ground showed a small cathedral of red and amber that moved with the sun. [140]
He had spent years trying to read it, not the words in it, for there were none, but the arrangement of its leaded seams, which behaved like grammar. [168]
Some seams brailed and tight, like whispered obligations; some loose, like breath held in a chest. [184]
When the ghosts became visible they came as the dead come in places that have been left alone: not suddenly, but as if the air had been thin all along and he had only now learned to see through it. [224]
The first stood at the far hive door with its back to him, and stayed there the length of one afternoon, and did not turn. [249]
The second arrived at dusk and looked only at the box. [260]
The sight of it astonished him more than any mirage had. [271]
They never spoke, and the astonished recluse, who had not spoken aloud in six years, was grateful for the courtesy. [291]
He read them through contextual clues, the way he read the glass. [303]
Where they paused, the covenant had been made; where they turned away, it had been broken. [319]
A woman in a grey smock stood each evening at the fourth hive, the one still packed with fossilized comb, and held her hand out, palm up, and closed it slowly. [350]
A tall figure sat cross-legged before the plinth and traced the box's seams in the air without touching them. [369]
Reading them together, he understood the tangled covenant as a knot rather than a line: the keepers of the oasis had promised the bees their seasons, and the bees had promised honey to the keepers, and the people of the wasteland had promised to let the whole arrangement continue. [418]
Each strand had been cut, and each cut had made the others worth less, and the ghosts were the remainder. [438]
What the covenant demanded now, he could infer from where the dead arranged themselves: they wanted the words that had never been said over the arrangement to be said at last, and weighed. [471]
This was the one thing the recluse could not do. [481]
He had come to the oasis to preserve the weight of unspoken words, his own and everyone else's, the way one preserves a seed: sealed, dry, untested. [508]
Speech was the spending of that weight, and he refused to be bankrupted. [521]
Yet the box had been waiting all these years to be opened, and the dead had come to see it opened, and they looked at him without grief. [549]
He was a tranquilly perceptive man, and he had read the wasteland for years the way other men read weather. [569]
So he looked back at the dead, and held their gaze, and let the silence carry what his voice would not. [590]
Then he set his hands on the lid, which gave at once, as if it had been unlocked for a long time and only closed. [615]
Inside there was no relic and no honey and no ash. [626]
There was only a single fused comb, thin as a page, in which every cell had been sealed with something that was not wax and not glass and had the color of held breath. [660]
Weight for weight, it was the exact mass of everything he had never said. [674]
The dead inclined their heads, and the woman closed her hand, and the tall figure stopped tracing, and the oasis was quiet in a way it had not been quiet in nine years. [707]
The covenant would not be repaid in speech after all, but in keeping: he closed the lid, and set the box back upon its plinth, and did not open it again. [738]
The hives stayed empty and the salt stayed white and the wasteland kept its silence, and he kept the weight of it. [760]
In the small cathedral of red and amber, the last ghost waited by the fourth hive with its palm upturned, and did not turn. [784]
He met that gaze with his own, and neither of them blinked. [796]
