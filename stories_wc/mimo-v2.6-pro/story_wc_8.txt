Casimir Veld arrived at the disused railway station as the ivy took the last of the evening light. [18] The platform had not held a train in forty years, and the glass roof was a lattice of missing panes. [38] He was a historian by trade and a stubborn man by temperament, and he had come to recapture something. [57] For ten years he had pursued a single illuminated letter, the gold-leafed capital that opened the founding charter of the municipal archive of Ghent. [81] The letter had been stolen, and with it the only proof that the city's celebrated origin story was a fabrication. [101] His purpose was plain: to rescue truth from a pit of lies. [113] He checked the clock on the ruined station house. [122] The old rosebush in the freight yard below had shed its petals all week, and he had come only after the final petal dropped. [146] That was the signal they had agreed upon years ago. [156] A crate waited under the brambles, half-sunk in the gravel. [166] Marguerite Ossen stepped out of the shadows of the ticket hall. [177] She was the archive's greatest patron and its most careful liar. [188] "You came," she said. [192] "I always come," said Casimir. [197] "When the last petal falls, I come." [204] Marguerite had buried the letter here herself, in the crate, in the year the theft was discovered and quietly unreported. [224] She had told herself a noble lie: that the charter's true text would break the city's faith in itself, and that a nation sustained on a beautiful falsehood was better than a nation divided by an ugly fact. [262] The lie had cost her sleep for a decade. [271] She was emotionally rational, and she knew exactly what that meant. [282] She had reasoned her way into concealment, and she had felt each step of the reasoning as if it were a physical weight. [305] "You will tell me I am a thief," she said. [315] "I will tell you a parallel," said Casimir, and he sat on the ruined bench where passengers had once waited for the coast train. [339] "In 1619 the magistrates of Leiden altered a charter to justify a tax." [352] "The alteration held for two centuries." [358] "When it was found out, the tax was resented, and the whole civic settlement collapsed in a summer." [376] "That is one parallel." [380] He drew another. [383] "In 1784 a bishop suppressed a marriage record to protect a family name." [396] "The name survived." [399] "The family did not." [403] "Truth withheld becomes a second and worse crime." [411] Marguerite listened with her hands folded, the way she listened to auditors. [423] "Your parallels are old," she said. [429] "All parallels are old," said Casimir. [435] "That is their power." [439] "They are the same story wearing different coats." [447] "So I give you the last one." [454] "A city that hides its own first document does not preserve its myth; it poisons it." [470] "Every year the lie needs a new lie to hold it up." [482] "You have been holding it up alone." [489] The ivy moved in the wind where the roof had been. [500] Marguerite went to the crate and knelt in the gravel. [510] She was emotional in the way of someone who has chosen reason as a discipline and then found it insufficient. [530] "The founding myth says our first burghers freed themselves in a single night," she said. [545] "The letter says they sold the harbour to a foreign count and lied about it for a year." [563] "That is the pit of lies you would rescue truth from." [574] "It is," said Casimir. [578] She pried the lid with the blade of a railway spike. [589] Inside, wrapped in oilcloth, lay the illuminated letter, its vellum eaten at one edge. [603] The gold still caught the failing light. [610] It was a capital T, for Trouwe, for fidelity, and the irony was not lost on either of them. [629] Casimir took it with both hands. [635] "I will put it back in the archive under my own name," he said, "and I will publish what it says." [656] "And the city?" [659] "It will argue with itself, and that is the health of a city." [672] Marguerite stood and brushed the gravel from her knees. [681] She had expected to feel defeated. [687] Instead she felt the weight leave her shoulders, and the feeling was so like relief that she laughed once, quietly. [707] "You have recaptured more than a letter, historian." [715] "I have recaptured the right to be doubted," said Casimir. [725] He wrapped the letter again and put it inside his coat. [736] Below them the rosebush stood bare, its last petal already gone to the wind. [750] The two of them left by the old footbridge, and the station took back its silence. [766]
