<story>The mountain had been a bookstore for so long that its pines grew in alphabetical order. [16] I came to it after remembering became painful, when every closed door in me opened onto the same unfinished room. [36] Inside, the shelves went up in terraces and down in wells, and each aisle curved until it forgot where it began. [57] Staircases braided themselves between floors of stitched spines, and a person could walk for a day and still be among the B's. [79]

I was looking for the lost language stone. [87] My grandmother had spoken of it in the tongue she buried when she left the valley, and buried again inside me as a talent I was not allowed to use. [117] Buried talents rot into weather. [122] Mine had become the storms that gathered behind my ribs, thunder I could feel but never hear. [139] There were talents buried all over the mountain: a librarian who had interred her singing in the footnotes, a porter who had folded his painting into shelf order. [167]

At the summit, where the map room should have been, there was a pool of quicksilver standing perfectly still in a glass cradle. [190] This was Vesper, the mercury-glass seer. [196] She wore no face, only the reflection of whatever you had most recently lost. [210] It had been ten years since I let the buried tongue speak, and in those years the mountain had grown like a slow thought. [234] In her surface I saw the doorway of my grandmother's house, which had not existed for forty years. [252] "The shape of home," I said. [258] "Tell me what it is, and I will stop walking." [268]

Vesper spoke the way mercury falls: in a single, heavy, glittering line. [280] "You cannot understand the shape of home while something is substituting it." [292] "Every night the stone does its work, and every night it swaps the valley's true memory for a cleaner one." [312] "That is its gift and its theft: it substitutes reality nightly, and the people below wake up lighter and lost." [332] "Then seed it," she said. [337] "A stone seeded with a living word will grow the language it was made to hold." [353] "But not by hand; glass cuts words into pieces." [362] "You must do it via the texture of a feather, the way a word first lands on a child's tongue." [382] She lifted one from the quicksilver, and it was not white but the grey of rain that has already fallen. [402]

Beneath the cradle, hidden under a false shelf of atlases, the stone lay like a closed mouth. [419] It was black, and small, and warm, and it remembered the taste of my grandmother's name. [435] I set the feather's down against it and breathed out the word she had buried in me. [452] It was not a name and not a prayer; it was the sound a house makes when someone is expected home. [473] The stone took it the way soil takes seed. [482] Something green and furious began inside the black. [490] Then I understood that the seed was not only for the stone; the seed was for me too, and something in my ribs began to loosen. [516] For a moment nothing happened, which is how thunder works. [526]

Then the mountain's shelves exhaled, every title on every spine changed at once, and the valley below woke up remembering. [546] Vesper's faceless surface went still as glass, then as mercury, then as something that could look back. [563] "You have seeded it," she said. [569] "Now you may hear what has been under your ribs." [579] And I did: the hidden thunder came into sound at last, slow and enormous and patient as weather over a roof. [600] It sounded like my grandmother setting two bowls down on a table. [612] The shape of home, it turned out, was not a place at all but an arrangement of listening. [630] Vesper sank back into stillness; mercury keeps what it is given and asks nothing further. [645]

I walked down through the B's and the C's and out into a valley that finally matched its own memory. [665] The mountain stayed a bookstore behind me, patient, half in shadow, full of words that had learned to grow. [684] Above the pines, in a glass cradle at the summit, mercury kept watch with no face and every face. [703] Below, inside the seed of the stone, the lost language put out its first green shoot, and the remembering no longer hurt. [725]</story>
