I held her first recipe in my bark before she held it in her hands. [15] That is my right as the oldest oak in the hidden orchard behind the kaleidoscopic waterfall, and I will tell this straight. [37] The water falls in sheets that bend the light into every color at once, so no traveler finds the clearing, and no traveler finds Maren. [62] She came here an auburn-haired alchemist with vellum sleeves and ash under her nails, and she came here before the final transformation. [84] I know the hour exactly, because she knelt at my roots and pulled three folded notes from the slit in my trunk where she has hidden every hard decision since she was nine. [117] The notes were pure logic, ratios and timings scratched tight enough to cut the page. [132] Then she pressed a locket flat against her palm, and did not look at it. [147] Nobody in her order knew she kept it. [155] That is the second thing I will tell straight. [164] She was secretly sentimental, and she had built a whole science to hide it. [178] In the clearing she set a stone bowl on the fire pit and coaxed a flame out of apple wood and paper. [200] The flame made a knot of heat that held steady without rising, which is the first mark of her art. [220] Into that knot she laid the frozen dew crystal she had carried for eleven winters. [235] It was the size of a walnut and it held a whole morning inside it, and it never once thawed. [255] Heat touched it and the frost inside rearranged itself, and the crystal went clear as spring water. [272] Maren stopped working and simply looked at it, and that was the touch of wonder she had spent twelve years earning. [293] I have watched her earn it the slow way. [302] Then the starlings came over the waterfall in a black ribbon, and she chose to read them instead of calculating. [322] This is how alchemists of her line have always harmonized logic and intuition: they let the living world finish the sentence. [343] The flock made a figure eight above the fall, then threw itself into a chain, then drew a spiral that tightened toward my crown. [367] I felt every bird of it, the way you feel a thought arrive. [380] Maren wrote nothing down. [384] When the spiral closed, she opened the locket. [392] Inside was a bead of dew she had gathered from this orchard at dawn when she was nine years old, sealed in so long it had gone sweet. [420] She poured it into the bowl with the clear crystal, and the two made one thing without her hands moving. [440] That is the whole of the work: the ratio held in the vellum, the heat held in the flame, the memory held in the locket, and the pattern held in the birds. [472] The final transformation began where she knelt. [479] Her hair darkened at the temples first, not with age but with certainty, and then the color ran back out to the ends and settled. [504] The orchard answered her. [508] Blossoms opened on every branch I own, out of season and all at once, and the water behind the fall turned the color of morning. [533] Maren looked at her own hands as if they belonged to someone older and kinder. [548] She said one word, which was enough, and it was the old word for a thing that is both measured and loved. [570] Then she folded the three notes and put them back in my slit, because the ratios no longer needed guarding. [590] The locket she wore out into the world where anyone could see it, and that is the bravest thing I have watched her do. [614] I am an old tree and I am secretly sentimental too, so I will say the ending plainly. [632] Logic and intuition are the same sense at different ages. [642] She proved it before the final transformation by listening to starlings over a hidden waterfall and remembering a morning from her childhood. [664] The orchard still smells of apple smoke and thawing frost, and the water still bends every color at once. [683] If you ever find the fall, put your hand in it and keep walking. [697] You will know the place by the quiet, and you will know me by the recipes I am still holding. [717]
