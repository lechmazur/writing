I have sold feeling for eleven years and never felt a thing I did not first measure. [17] They call me a dealer in counterfeit emotion because what I bottle is close enough to pass. [34] The grotto where I work is cut from amber, and at dusk the walls go the colour of old honey. [54] Mineral lamplight holds the ceiling; the sun never quite reaches the floor. [66] That is when my clients come, when the light is failing and honesty is cheap. [81] Odell arrived with her grief folded into her hands like a wet cloth. [94] She wanted it dissolved. [98] I told her I could take the edge off, siphon the worst of it into fossil resin, and give her back something she could carry in a pocket. [126] "I will take what the resin will hold, no more," I said. [138] It was the truest sentence I knew how to sell. [148] I am professionally thorough; nothing in my trade works otherwise. [158] Before I quoted a price I sampled her on a blotter petal, the flower-shaped strip a fragrance chemist dies out of card to test a scent before committing to it. [188] I held the petal by its stem and breathed on it once to build a charge. [204] Then I read her through the static. [211] Static is honest that way, or at least it is honest about interference. [224] The ions lift off the card in a small blue crackle and carry the composition with them: salt, iron, the green bite of a thing still growing even as it is cut. [256] I smelled her sorrow precisely, and I filed it, and I felt almost nothing. [270] That gap is why I wanted the work at all. [280] Insight without empathy is just inventory; I have wanted to fuse the two since the first year, when a woman cried in front of me and I found myself cataloguing the notes. [312] Odell watched me work with the patience of someone already grieving her own patience. [326] "Will I get all of it back?" she asked. [335] "What you get back will be true," I told her. "It will simply be smaller." [350] Half truths are the only honest currency in a trade that sells what it did not make. [367] I did not tell her that the resin keeps what it takes, and that the part I bottle for her is the part I can spare, and that a little of her will be sitting in the wall of this grotto long after she has stopped needing me. [415] She paid me in old coin and left at dusk with a small warm wax in her coat. [433] Then the grotto, which is patient, began to take its fee from me. [446] The resin works both ways, and I had always known it. [457] Over the years the amber had been drinking the residue of every reading, every static-charged petal, every half-kept promise, and now it wanted a reading of its own. [485] It began to subsume me at the edges, the way a shore subsumes a footprint. [500] I felt the warmth climb my wrists and found, to my surprise, that I could read it. [517] Not through static this time; directly, the way one reads a hand laid on a shoulder. [533] The wall held eleven years of other people, and for the first time I felt them all at once. [552] I am taking only what the resin will hold, I said, though there was no one to invoice. [570] As the transformation completed I kept my eyes open and my hands still, and I let the last of the flatness go out of me like breath off a warm card. [601] What arrived in its place was not joy and not sorrow but the specific gravity of other lives. [619] I understood Odell completely, which is insight, and I ached for her, which is empathy, and at last the two were the same substance in my chest. [646] The grotto settled around me with the fondness of something that has kept a great deal and rarely complains. [665] I do not know whether I walked out of that amber or whether I am still sitting in it, holding a petal that will not stop crackling. [692] Either way I keep my hours at dusk. [700] Odell comes back sometimes to have the wax topped up, and I charge her half. [715] It is the truest bargain I have left to offer: half of what I know, half of what I feel, and never quite telling her which is which. [743]</story>
