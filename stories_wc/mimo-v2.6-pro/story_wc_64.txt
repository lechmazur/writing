Ansel kept the graves green the way other men kept promises, silently and mostly for himself. [16] He was a blithe cemetery gardener, and his blitheness was a trowel he turned on anyone who got too close to the dirt. [39] The gloom of the place did not trouble him; he wore it like a velvet coat he had been issued at hiring and never thought to question. [66] Each evening the night deposit came in, and each evening he sorted it. [79] What the night deposit brought, he arranged in the cobalt silo at the back field, under stars. [96] Boxes, a stepladder, a folding card table: everything was tenderly organized there, as if tidiness could argue with loss. [115] Tonight the deposit held one thing only: an antique sovereign case, black leather gone brown at the corners, its clasp a small brass mouth. [139] Inside lay no coins but a folded tapestry, heavy as a hand. [151] The donor was the man in plot nineteen, buried that morning, who had signed everything away in the pawnbox downstairs except this. [173] Ansel checked the pawnbox once more, out of habit, and found its tray empty where the man's watch had been. [193] Nothing left to collect from him. [199] Only the tapestry, and its border. [205] Ansel carried it up the silo steps. [212] By the light of the stars, and the single bulb he had rigged to the rafter, he spread it across the card table and saw at once that the border was not ornament. [245] It was a sentence, worked in knots and frayed thread, and the fraying was the message's own undoing. [263] He understood then what he had always been hired to do without knowing it. [277] To untangle. [279] He sat down with the needle case and began, strand by strand, to untangle the border's knots and lay the frayed threads in order on the table's edge. [307] Decoding frayed threads in a tapestry's border is slow work, and it is not guessing. [322] Each color was a letter, each broken loop a space, each place where the wool had worn away a word the man had decided not to say. [349] The chess clock on the rafter shelf was running, as it always ran in the silo, as if some game up there were being played to a finish. [377] Ansel did not look up. [382] When the border spoke at last, it said: I did not leave you the money because money is a shape and not the shape I meant. [408] Go to the pit. [412] There was a pit in the silo floor, a dropped sump where the old auger had gone through, and Ansel had covered it with the stepladder laid flat and had never looked. [444] He lifted the ladder away. [449] Out of the dark came the uncharted echoes, the voices of everyone the cemetery had taken and no one had ever written down. [472] They were not loud. [476] They were velveteen, almost: that soft, that heavy, that impossible to brush off a sleeve. [491] The gloom in the silo thickened and took on a nap, and Ansel felt it settle into the weave of his coat. [513] Then the chess clock began to run down. [521] It was not an hour left but seconds, and Ansel understood the game had been waiting for him to finish the border before it could end. [547] After the chess clock runs down, the dead have no more moves. [559] The final chime unstoppered the pit like a cork. [568] Every echo rose at once, and in the rising Ansel heard what the man in plot nineteen had truly been doing all those years in his upstairs room. [596] Not hoarding. [599] Braiding. [602] He had been braiding the dead into a shape, and the shape of intentions was a knotwork net wide enough to hold a whole town's goodbye. [628] Ansel laid the last thread down and said, to the pit and the stars both, "All right." [645] He had discovered it. [649] In the morning he would close the silo, carry the tapestry down to the office, and record every name the border gave him in the cemetery book, which no one had kept for forty years. [684] But that was work, and this was the hour of it. [695] Ansel stayed at the pit's edge, blithe as he had ever been, and listened to the velveteen gloom take back its own. [717]
