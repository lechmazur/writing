The mountain ranger cabin smelled of pitch and cold stone, and the birch bark scroll lay unrolled across the pine table like a shed skin. [25] A pine-charcoal stub rested in the chronicler's right hand, already smudged to the second knuckle. [40] Below the window the valley was a dark throat full of wind, and above it the ridgeline held the last of the snow like something it refused to give back. [70] The chronicler had walked up here from the graveyard an hour ago and had not yet taken off the coat. [90] The scroll was for the chaos. [96] Everything else could stay disordered, but the entries had to hold. [107] So the chronicle ran backwards and sideways, a death recorded before the departure that caused it, a childhood winter pressed after a battlefield spring. [131]

The granite slab stood where the trail bent, and the name on it had been eaten by lichen down to a few honest strokes. [155] The chronicler had knelt there long enough for the cold to come up through the knees. [171] Seeing your own grave is not a metaphor when the stone is under your palm; it is a fact with weather on it. [194] The date was wrong, or the date was early, or the date was the one honest thing in a life that had lost its sequence. [219] Chronologically confused did not begin to describe it. [227] The chronicler had buried a stranger there in a dream once and had woken up weeping for someone who never existed. [248] That was the trouble with lucid dreaming: you could direct the storm, but you could not stop it from wetting you. [269]

Back in the cabin, the first entry on the scroll was the boy at the river, skipping shale and missing every skip. [291] The chronicler wrote under it, not knowing which year it belonged to. [303] Then the soldier in the mud, laughing at nothing. [312] Then the woman on the stair who had not yet decided to leave. [325] The entries kept arriving out of order because memory is feral nostalgia, a thing with teeth that eats the timeline and leaves the feelings whole. [350] Feral nostalgia was the only honest term for it. [359] It did not want the past back; it wanted the past's hunger. [371]

Reading the entries aloud, the chronicler felt the old dream-habit take hold at the base of the skull. [389] The lantern smoke coiled toward the rafters, and the cabin floor let go without a sound. [405] The chronicler began to soar. [410] Not far, not heroically, just a foot and then a yard above the table, the way a hawk hangs on a thermal it has learned to trust. [437] From up there the scroll looked like a map of a country that had been demolished and rebuilt in the wrong shape. [459] Jaded wonder was the only weather the chronicler trusted now. [469] Everything up here was so old and so bright that it stopped being sad. [483]

Then the jealousy came, as it always did, and it came from below. [496] The boy at the river still had every morning ahead of him, and did not know it, and that not-knowing was a wealth the chronicler could not earn twice. [525] The soldier in the mud still got to be afraid of dying. [537] The woman on the stair still got to decide. [546] Getting jealous of past selves is a craft, not a sickness, if you write it down before it bites. [565] So the chronicler wrote, floating, charcoal scraping the birch bark while the wind went over the roof like something enormous dragging a coat. [588] Each line took a piece of the disorder and pinned it flat. [600]

The scroll swayed once under the rafters, and the cabin took the chronicler back. [614] Boots on boards, coat still on, the smell of pitch again. [625] On the floor lay one loose strip of birch bark that had torn free during the flight. [642] The chronicler turned it to the lantern and read the single line the chaos had written without help. [660] It said: I was here, and I was many, and none of me stayed. [674] That was the voice the whole chronicle had been trying to distill. [686] The chronicler set the strip down beside the scroll and did not write another word.
