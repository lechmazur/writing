Maren climbed with the onyx ink drying black under her nails and the saucer wrapped in a birthday tablecloth against her ribs. [22]
The tablecloth was the birthday one Agatha had stained with coffee in April, at the last observance of the family custom of tracing hands at its edge, the small hands in pencil and the old hands in wine. [60]
Agatha's outline was still there, a violet hand between the cups, and Maren carried the cloth because it was the only proof left that the missing months had once been minutes. [91]
Above the treeline the ground went soft and silver, and she walked out into the midnight glade that hangs over the cloud sea like a held breath. [118]
This is where lost time accumulates. [124]
Maren had come to find it herself, because eleven years of reading futures in onyx ink on pastless card had not shown her the nine months that vanished after the birth. [155]
Orla had come on 12 November, three weeks ago to the night, and Cassel had taken the baby from her arms while Maren stared at the wall and could not say what had become of the year. [192]
She could not say. [196]
The glade held no trees, only the silver grass and, at its centre, a shallow basin where spent minutes fell like ash and lay in drifts that smelled of rain on stone. [228]
Kneeling, Maren unfastened the ink-glass from her belt and set it on the rim, where it could take what the basin would give. [251]
She unwrapped the saucer. [255]
It was the smallest piece of Agatha's dolls' tea set, the one kept in the loft, and it had cracked in the night the old woman died, though nothing had touched it. [287]
The crack ran from the gilt rim to the little painted rose, fine as a hair and twice as stubborn. [307]
Cassel had wanted to throw the set away in September. [317]
Maren had carried it instead, the way she carried the tablecloth, because a dead woman's hands are hard to put down. [338]
In the basin the ash of spent time shifted, and the drifts made the shape of a room she knew: the kitchen in April, the low table, Agatha laughing with the pencil behind her ear. [373]
Nine months of it lay there, whole and warm, and Maren understood with a choking sweetness that she had never once been afraid of losing it, because she had never once known she had it. [408]
She had carried a child and lost the carrying. [417]
The wind came down off the cloud sea and turned the grass flat, and it spoke in the voice Agatha used for scolding, which was also the voice she used for love. [449]
It said that she had come up here to repent of not knowing. [462]
It said that her not knowing had been the whole of her knowing, and that the sky had forgiven her hours ago for a thing she never did. [490]
This was the cloudbound repentance: not a payment but a return, grief handed back to the one who had been holding it without hands. [514]
Maren, who was willingly perplexed by her own life and had stopped apologising for it, knelt in the silver grass and let the time come. [539]
The ash rose in a thin bright column and poured into the ink-glass, which took nine months without complaint and without growing heavier. [562]
Then she set the saucer beneath the last of the falling and watched the crack unfasten, slowly, the way a knot comes undone when someone finally stops pulling. [590]
The two halves lay open like a small pale mouth, and what the saucer had held came out. [608]
Agatha's handprint was in it, in coffee and wine, and Orla's future was in it, and Maren's own nine months, and none of it was lost now; it was only spent, which is what time is for. [645]
She wrapped the halves in the tablecloth and started down. [655]
Below the clouds, in a warm room that smelled of milk and iron, Cassel was asleep with the baby on his chest, and the coffee stain on the cloth would grow one more hand this year, small, in pencil, when Orla's birthday came round. [699]
Maren went down to it with the ink still under her nails. [711]
The sky behind her closed without a sound. [719]
