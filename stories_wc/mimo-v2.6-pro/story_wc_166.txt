The last tide dancer kept her garden where the law could not see it. [14] Twilight gathered under the first stars, and the salt wind came up from the harbor and turned the gravel to silver. [35] Three years earlier the nostalgia ban had passed, and remembering had become a taxable offense. [50] The tide itself, being the moon's long memory of the earth, had been listed among the forbidden things. [68] She had not been exiled. [73] She had exiled herself, which is the only kind that holds, and she had chosen the garden because it faced the water and the water never stopped remembering for her. [103]

Now she danced at twilight, and she did it for free. [114] She was the most skilled tide dancer left alive, and her dance was a sequence of gaps. [131] She had learned it as a girl by observing gaps: the space between two swells, the pause in a wave before it breaks, the hole a full moon leaves in a constellation. [163] Her body read those absences and translated them, and the rhythm she made had no punctuation in it at all. [183] There was nowhere for a court to insert a sentence. [193]

In the low shed behind the garden she kept a tattoo house. [205] That was her trade, and it answered the only question she still asked of strangers: whether they would let her pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin. [232] She worked without stencils. [236] By observing gaps in the face before her, she found the places where something had gone missing, and she inked there. [257] A lobe of the ear. [262] The hollow of a wrist. [267] The span of a collarbone where a name used to sit. [278] Into the black ink she mixed moonstone cabochon dust, one speck to a verse. [292] The dust was spontaneously anchored: the instant it left the brush it settled, and once settled it would not be moved by wind or hand or grief. [319] It fell to the floor of the shed and lay there like a second kind of star, and there was nothing anyone could do about it. [345]

The verse could not survive its reader. [352] When a client carried it away, the words went out of them within a season, and all that remained was the speck, which held no words at all. [380] The law had no word for a thing with no words in it, so the law let it lie. [399] She wrote four verses that autumn. [405] Four specks fell. [408]

The inspector came on a night of high water. [417] He wore the ban on his coat like a medal, and he did not knock. [432] Through the floorboards he heard the tide dance in the garden above, unpunctuated, and he climbed the stairs and arrested her for it. [455] She did not contest the charge. [461] At the tribunal she argued that a memory without stops could not be entered as evidence, and the magistrates agreed. [481] They could not prove a nostalgia, so they sentenced her to elective exile, which is to say the exile she had already chosen. [504] She signed the order herself. [509] She had been practicing her signature for three years in case they ever asked. [523]

That night she danced in the garden one last time, and the stars came down far enough to graze the trellis. [544] In the shed she found the four specks she had written, and she set them on her tongue like salt. [564] She had been moving toward the edge of this all along. [575] She would fledge now: not to fall, but to accept the air that had been holding her since the beginning. [595] She went down to the water. [601] The fifth speck, the one she had made without a reader, had rolled behind the kiln of its own accord, and she chased it to the surf, and the sea took it. [633] That was what they called her movement when they spoke of it later, if they spoke of it at all: obsidian grace. [655]

By observing gaps in the surf she found the opening and walked into it and did not come back. [674] The garden stood empty at twilight, and the gravel stayed silver. [685] Under the stars the tide remembered everything it had ever remembered, which is only to say that it went on. [705] It had no word for what it lost, and it needed none; that was the whole of the verse, and the whole of the exile. [730]
