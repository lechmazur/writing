Asha had polished the yellow lantern glass at the shooting star tracking station longer than the station had a name. [20]
She was a cosmic janitor, which meant she kept the corridors swept, the panels dusted, and the small rituals of the place intact. [43]
The station sat on a shelf of black rock where the sky came down close enough to hear. [61]
It had always been the hush of an overlooked era here, a long quiet stretch that no chronicle bothered to number. [82]
Asha liked that. [85]
Unnumbered time gave her room to be spiritually curious without an audience, and room to do the one thing she had come for: to find strength in vulnerability. [113]
She kept a lamp burning behind the glass, and when the filament settled she watched it the way other people watched weather. [135]
Vess worked the transit table across the room. [143]
She logged every crossing on a long strip of paper and trusted nothing she could not measure. [160]
Asha's job was to wipe the glass and empty the trays. [171]
The trays caught what the sky dropped, and they were always full. [183]
Still, she did her real work by tracing the path of falling stars, the old method, drawing each line back to where it began. [207]
She had learned it from the janitor before her, who had learned it from a janitor before that. [225]
The stars taught it best in fragments. [232]
When one came apart in the upper dark, the pieces spoke for a moment in a language Asha had no name for, <em>all breath and direction</em>. [258]
She heard in it a kind of <em>primal elegance</em>, the shape a thing keeps when it stops holding itself together. [278]
That was the lesson she carried to the trays and the mop and the long quiet hours. [295]
Flourishing stillness, she called it: the way a fall can end in a settling that is not defeat. [313]
Vess called it noise. [317]
One night the clouds rolled in low and thick and sat on the station for hours. [333]
The transits kept coming but Vess could not see to log them, and her strip of paper lay blank under her hand. [355]
She sat very still, and Asha saw that the stillness was not calm at all. [370]
It was the stillness of a person braced against something. [380]
Asha put down the cloth and came to the table and did not speak right away. [396]
She had always believed you could learn more from a quiet room than a loud one, so she let the quiet do its work. [420]
Then she said, "You are afraid the sky will do something tonight and you will miss it." [437]
Vess did not deny it. [442]
"I have watched this table for nine years," she said. [452]
"If I miss a crossing, what was I doing all that time?" [464]
Asha thought about that, because it deserved thinking about. [473]
Then she took Vess's hand and pressed it flat against the yellow lantern glass, which was warm from the lamp. [493]
"Feel that," she said. [497]
"It is warm because I keep it clean." [505]
"It is clean because I let it be fragile." [514]
"The glass cracks if I press too hard, so I don't." [525]
"I learned to hold it without holding on." [533]
"That is where the strength is." [539]
She was telling Vess the truth, and she was also telling her, without saying so, that it was all right to be unbraced. [562]
That is what it means to empathize: to lend someone your own unguardedness until they find their own. [580]
Vess's shoulders came down a little. [586]
"Then help me look," she said. [592]
So Asha lifted the glass and held it up, clouded as the night was, and they watched the sky through it together. [614]
Something crossed behind the weather, far up, and Asha traced its path backward by the old method, the way her hands knew. [636]
It began out past the edge of everything and it fell toward the tray without hurrying. [652]
Vess wrote it down, and her line was steady, and neither of them spoke for a long while after. [671]
By first light the clouds had gone, the lamp was quenched, and the trays were full again, and the era went on being overlooked, which suited it. [698]
Asha looked up from the tray at last and let her shoulders answer the sky, a small quiet rise, and the doorway beside the unlit lantern glass waited, as doorways do, for the next slow hour of the hush. [737]
