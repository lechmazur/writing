He lingered. [2] That was the whole of his method, though he would not have said so. [16] Where others hurried past the rusted factory ruins, he stayed. [26] Tucked behind them lay a lavender field, and there he set up his work. [40] Everything he owned fit into boxes except one thing: a slim dove gray map case of soft leather with a brass button at its throat. [65] Inside it he carried the maps he drew from black-and-white photographs. [76] Each photograph he held a moment longer than it wanted to be held. [89] From that patience, arcs rose out of the grain. [98] The arcs were the routes grief takes before it becomes light. [109] His maps traced them. [113]

The shed had no roof on the west side, and he liked that; the rain gave each print a second exposure. [134] The bees came and went through the open side and he trusted them to do the sorting. [151] He was perceptively calm in the way of people who have looked at a lot of ruins, and the painter Vala called him a subtle puzzle archivist, a name he did not correct. [184] She was the first who came. [190] She arrived at the edge of the lavender with a shoebox of her brother's photographs and a face like a held breath. [212] He did not ask what happened. [218] He only lingered, sorting the prints by the light left in them, and when he looked up the field had gone violet with dusk. [242] The map he gave her was thin as a leaf. [252] She pressed it to her chest and left without a word. [263]

By midsummer the lavender was high enough to hide in and the vines were climbing the factory walls in earnest. [283] In the heat the ruins gave off a smell of iron and rain, and the lavender made it sweet. [302] That was how he measured time now, as vines climb. [312] Others followed Vala. [315] A boy with his mother's ring. [321] Two old men who had stopped speaking years ago and could not say why. [335] A woman who brought a whole box of a marriage. [345] He gave each of them a map, and each map was a little truer than the last. [362] What he wanted, he admitted to no one, was to transmute pain into a masterpiece. [377] Not to make a thing that shone, but to make a thing that could be walked. [393] His own wound he kept in the case with everything else: a photograph of a doorway, and nothing after it. [413] When the arcs ran thin he would hold that print for an hour, waiting for its curve to appear. [432]

The youngest arc belonged to the boy. [439] It began at the ring and it bent hard and it did not stop. [453] He watched the boy trace it with one finger and then trace it again, slower. [468] In the quiet afterward the boy asked if the map was the whole story. [482] It is the part that can be carried, he said. [492] Then he taught the boy how to read the rest, and the boy stayed until the light went. [510]

By the time the vines had swallowed the chimneys the maps were everywhere in the valley. [526] People hung them in kitchens. [531] Some had been folded so often the creases shone. [540] He understood at last that what he had been making was collective healing, not a monument. [556] A monument holds still. [560] This moved through people the way lavender moves through a room, which is to say without asking. [577]

In the garden behind the shed he kept the master set. [588] Every arc was there, hundreds of them, layered until they read as weather. [601] The old men came on Thursdays now and spoke a little. [612] The woman brought the box again, thinner. [619] She asked for a map of a beginning this time, and he drew one, which was harder. [636] Vala painted the field in white and gray and said it was the only honest color she knew. [654] He laughed at that, which surprised them both. [662]

On certain evenings he walked out through the broken factory gates with the dove gray map case slung at his hip. [683] The vines climbed higher behind him. [689] That was the only clock he kept. [696] He would stay a while longer, because someone always came with a shoebox and a face like a held breath. [716] He lingered. [718]
