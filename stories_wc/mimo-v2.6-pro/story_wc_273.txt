The eclipse let go of the desert at 3:41, and the light came back like a held breath released. [19] The tracker stood in the wind-carved encampment and did not flinch at it, though his pale lashes were useless against glare and his ruined eyes had learned to hate the sun. [50] He was albino, born without the pigment the desert demands, and so he tracked the way the blind do: by fingertip, by breath, by the small complaints the ground makes under weight. [82] At his shoulder, riding the still air, the artificial hummingbird drone held station on wings that beat too fast to see. [103] Its belly carried a vial of ink so fine it dried the moment it touched anything, leaving nothing behind but a rumour of itself. [127] Disappearing ink was the point. [132] The tracker had spent nine years asking why time slows in crisis, and the ink was how he meant to prove it. [154] He had watched a man fall from a dune and seen the fall last a minute. [170] He had felt a bullet spend an afternoon crossing twenty feet of air. [183] Something in the world loosens when the stakes rise, and he wanted the measurement. [197] The drone's quirk was the instrument for it: the faster it flew, the slower its wings beat. [214] At speed it became a stone with a shadow, and at the end its wings stopped entirely and it fell out of time altogether. [238] He gave it the command to flee and knelt to read what it left. [252] The vial pressed a bead of black into the sand and the bead bloomed, and in the wet the tracker set his fingers down and read the strokes the way a scholar reads a burned page. [288] Hovering, the drone dropped dots, round and soft-edged; flying hard, it dragged fine hairlines into the spreading pool. [306] Those ephemeral patterns in the spilled ink told him everything: altitude, direction, the exact instant its wings had begun to slow. [327] He followed the trail by touch, moving with a consciously wild gait, all loose elbows and long stride, the motion he had taught himself so the body would never hesitate before the mind agreed. [361] The encampment's dust, polished smooth by wind, held every grain etched in outlines that blurred together like wet ink at the edges. [383] Out past the last shelter the ink thinned to hairlines, then to nothing. [396] The drone had gone to full speed, its wings stilled, its ink spent, and so it left no trace at all. [417] It had disappeared exactly the way people do in crisis: all at once, and by the very thing that makes them fast. [439] He stood in the blank sand and let the anger arrive the way he always let it arrive, level and quiet, a held temperature rather than a fire. [467] Serenity was not peace in him; it was discipline, and it had a use. [481] He sat down where the trace ended and waited, because the one thing slower than time could not be caught by anything faster than it. [506] Hours passed and the shadows of the wind-carved shelters lengthened and softened, and the drone's wings began to beat again, slowly at first, then quickly, then ordinary. [533] It came back at hover speed and set a fresh bead of black onto the sand in front of him. [553] He read it with his fingertips: a single dot, fat and unhurried, the mark of something that had chosen to be found. [575] "You vanish when you hurry," he told it, in the flat courteous voice he used for things that could not answer. "So do we all. That is what I wanted written down." [607] The ink was already fading from the sand, and by the time he finished the sentence the bead had dried to a rumour. [630] He laid his palm flat over the place it had been and felt, for one stretched instant, the ground slow under his hand. [653] The drone hovered at his shoulder, its wings a soft blur, waiting out the answer with him in the restored glare. [674] That was the measurement he had wanted: not a number, but the proof that crisis borrows its extra seconds from somewhere, and pays them back in stillness. [701] He rose, shook the dust from his gloves, and walked back toward the encampment with the drone settling onto his shoulder like a hawk. [725] Behind him the last hairlines of ink turned to nothing, and the wind-carved sand took back its smoothness, and nothing at all remained to say that anything had passed that way. [756]</story>
