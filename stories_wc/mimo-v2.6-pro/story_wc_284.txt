<story>At the tilt of the earth toward spring, Marguerite Vance walked out to the zephyr bluff with the mist coming in off the water like breath held too long. [29] She had been burned eleven months, disavowed after the rig collapse at Suvla that killed everyone she had run. [48] The blame followed her like a debt no one would sign. [59] In her coat pocket she carried an engraved pillbox her husband had given her before the war. [76] Its lid carried her initials and the words For the quieter hour. [88] Her husband had died of the same fever that took half the harbor, and she had kept the box ever since. [109] Tonight it held no pills at all. [116] Tonight it held the names. [121] She was prudently daring, which meant she had mapped three ways off this bluff before she took the first step. [141] The mist gave her the silver hush she needed; sound died a few feet out and no one could see a hand raised in warning. [166] The zephyr itself was only a small wind off the land, but at this height it combed the mist flat and turned the world to pewter. [192] Behind her, somewhere below the path, two men followed the orders of Konrad Holl, who ran the German ear on this coast and wanted the new codes badly enough to send them uphill in fog. [227] Ehren went first with a torch he did not light, and Slavko carried the length of rope they would need if she ran. [250] Neither man knew the bluff; Marguerite had walked it as a girl and knew every place the ground gave way. [270] Marguerite had come out here to find where unused courage waits, in the hour before a thing is done, and she had not expected to find it alone. [298] The keeper's hut sat low against the slope with its window shuttered and one lamp burning at the sill. [317] Peter Ashford stood inside it, still inside Section Nine, the only man left who could vouchsafe her face to the people who had cut her off. [343] He had watched her burn and done nothing, and he had spent eleven months trying to find a way to undo it. [365] She tapped once, low, the way they had agreed in a room that no longer existed. [381] He opened the door a hand's width and looked past her into the mist before he looked at her. [400] You are dead on paper, he said. [407] Then let me fix it, she said. [414] What is broken can be mended if one person will carry the truth back. [428] The lamp on the sill threw their two shadows onto the snow, and neither shadow moved. [444] The codes were not in her hand or her mouth but in the accumulated snow along the north sill, where she had pressed a sealed paper into the drift the night before. [476] Cold held the ink; melt would release it, and the melt was three days off. [491] Holl's men would dig and find only water. [499] She set the engraved pillbox on the sill for Peter to take in, and she wrote nothing new in it. [519] The lid's promise was the only message she wanted read aloud. [530] A stone went down the far path and Ehren turned after it, the way a man turns toward a sound he expects. [552] Marguerite went the other way, along the ridge where the zephyr pushed the fog into a wall. [569] Slavko caught her sleeve at the turn and she gave him the second box, a plain tin with thirty pounds in it, and told him the codes were under the north drift. [601] He took the money because a man in his trade always takes the money. [615] That was her prudence: the lie was cheaper than the truth and it bought her twenty minutes. [632] By the time they dug the north drift and found nothing but slush, she was down at the harbor road with the Section Nine car waiting. [658] Peter had already spoken for her over the wire; he would vouchsafe her name to the board himself. [676] At four the church bell struck below the fog and the lighthouse turned once every twelve seconds, and she sat in the back of the car with her hands quiet in her lap. [709] By morning the paper in the drift would be ink again, and by spring her name would be clear. [728] The question she had carried up the bluff was where unused courage waits, and she had found it in the same place it always waits: in the last hour before you stop being afraid. [762] The hush held, silver and unbroken, until the light came up. [773]</story>
