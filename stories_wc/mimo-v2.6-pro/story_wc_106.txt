Ilex was a golden-hour portrait thief: she had never stolen a painting, only the light inside one. [17] Portraits were easy; the hard part was the illuminated moment, that thin seam of a minute the painter had caught and sealed into varnish. [41] She kept those minutes in jars. [47] Each jar of captured moonbeams held one stolen second of someone else's sunrise. [60] Tonight she carried nine of them in a lemonwood barrel-case, and they glowed like lemons ripened underground. [77]

The emerald station by moonlight was iron and malachite, all arched platforms and green-tiled vaults that threw the lamplight back in small wet coins. [101] Overgrown flowerbeds ran along the platform edges, and Ilex read them the way other people read timetables. [118] White ranunculus, cut short: the platform would be empty. [127] Three red dahlias, counted from the left: three minutes to the guard's round. [140] She was quietly savvy about all of it, and even savvier about what she left behind. [156] Tonight's arrangement was a single sprig of night-blooming jasmine, set in a chipped urn by the ticket window. [174] It meant: I am listening. [179]

The stationmaster, a broad woman with soil under her nails, kept her own code in the same beds. [197] When Ilex passed the second flowerbed she stopped, because the bouquet there was not one she had arranged. [215] Pale hellebore, angled toward the tunnel: someone knew the route. [225] She had to reinterpret it, and quickly. [232] Maybe the flowers were not a warning but an invitation, a door left purposefully ajar. [247]

Under a fleeting golden sunset, the sky thinning to amber, the jars in her case began to shine with their own stolen moon. [270] Sunset was the only hour she could see the seams in light, and she used it now. [287] She lifted the barrel-case onto the platform bench and opened it. [298] Nine little suns spilled their glow across the malachite tiles, and the whole station looked like the inside of a lemon. [319]

That was when the portrait hung above the ticket window began to weep its light. [334] It was a railway commissioner's wife, painted in a dawn she had never actually lived. [349] The illuminated moment inside her was still warm, still unlived, still waiting to be pressed into a jar. [367] Ilex worked quickly and without fuss. [374] She cupped her hands around the varnish and drew the seam out like thread. [388] The jar took it with a soft click, like a lock turning. [400]

She had been doing this since she was nineteen, and the reason had never changed. [415] She wanted to press her ear against destiny's door and hear what kind of house stood on the other side. [435] Every jar she filled was another second of someone's luck or ruin to listen to. [450] The ten jars now were ten different fates, glowing on the bench in a patient row. [466]

Footsteps came down the green vault. [472] The stationmaster arrived holding a spade in one hand and the sprig of jasmine in the other. [489] She looked at the jars, then at the weeping portrait, then at Ilex, with something like luminous disbelief on her weathered face. [511] "The hellebore meant listen," Ilex said. [517] The stationmaster set the jasmine back in the urn. [526] "It meant come to the tunnel door," she said, "and bring the lemon case." [540] "There is a portrait down there that has been crying its dawn into the dark for forty years." [558]

Ilex did not ask how the woman knew the phrase for captured moonbeams. [571] She had learned long ago that some doors open for exactly the ear that leans on them. [588] Together they walked the tunnel with the jars between them, nine small suns and one freshly filled, and the light on the walls moved like water. [614]

At the tunnel's end an unframed canvas leaned against the damp brick, and in it a woman stood in a dawn no calendar had recorded. [639] Ilex knelt and put her ear to the varnish. [648] What she heard was not a minute but a whole weather of them, rising like heat off a lemon grove. [668] The stationmaster watched her face change and said nothing at all. [679]

Under the last of the golden light, with the jars humming on the brick, Ilex finally understood the arrangement she had spent her whole life decoding. [705] It was not a code at all. [711] It was a garden, planted for one thief who only ever wanted to listen. [725] She filled one more jar from the weeping canvas and left it open on the ledge. [741] The night came through like a door unlatched. [749] Somewhere a lemon tree dropped its fruit into the dark. [759] She stood in that glow and felt, for the first time, what the light had been carrying all along: it had been carrying her, and she was alive. [787]
