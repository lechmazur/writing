Anton Vey had the hands of a copyist and the manners of a diplomat, an urbane calligrapher who kept a jar of sumi ink behind the bar at the far edge of the city. [34] Three nights a week he climbed into the decommissioned radar dome and played to drunks. [49] The dome had been a listening post once; now the field of empty glass was a ceiling of frost and the bar's only light came from a string of bulbs roped around the console. [83] He had learned the craft in worse rooms. [91] The city's censor board had barred him from every licensed stage after the wheat subsidy piece, so he had taken his marionettes into seedy bars, where nobody filed complaints and the owners only wanted the customers to stay for one more round. [133] Subversive puppet shows in seedy bars: it was a method, not a fallback. [146] The controller in his fist was a campfire story stick, a charred branch he had carried since his army years, passed hand to hand whenever someone in the circle had the floor. [178] In the dome it held the strings. [185] That night, before he had lit the first bulb, two men in the front row stood up and tore into each other over the wheat case. [211] One said a story could not be told without lying; the other said a lie told in service of the truth was still a lie, and then the first one threw his beer and the room went quiet. [249] What was left was the hush, a meteor hush, the kind that follows something bright burning through. [266] Anton was uncommonly serene in it; he set the stick across his knees and let the silence finish the argument. [286] "Good," he said at last. "Now we know what's at stake." [297] He never told a story he had not first rehearsed for serenity, because a nervous hand on the strings makes a puppet into a liar. [322] So he began in the hush the argument had made, with the whole room leaning forward and nobody willing to be the first to speak. [347] The play was the wheat case again, told small: a farmer, a clerk, a signed paper. [363] Malja, the bar's owner, leaned in the doorway with her arms crossed and her mouth thin, and she watched the stick, not the puppets. [387] Halfway through, when the clerk puppet leaned over the paper to sign, Malja walked out and took the stick off the table behind Anton, the spare he kept there in case a string snapped. [421] She meant to reverse the show herself, to seize the floor the way the stick said you could. [439] Anton did not turn around. [444] He only raised the charred branch in his fist and reversed the whole marionette stage, pivoting the puppets away from the audience and facing them into the concave wall of the dome. [476] The half-lit bulbs threw the shadows onto the curve and the curve threw them back ten times their size, so the little farmer stood thirty feet tall and the signed paper was a door. [510] These were the half-lit epiphanies he had been chasing all year: a story told from the far side does not shrink its subject, it enlarges it. [536] Malja stood in the doorway with the spare stick in her hand and understood that the floor had never been hers to take. [559] She set it back on the table and walked into the room and sat down. [574] The shadows played out the clerk's arrest and then his pardon and then the farmer's silence, which took the longest, because silence has to be held. [600] When the last string went slack, nobody moved for a long moment, and the hush that came afterward was different from the one the argument had left. [627] It was wider, and it had room in it now. [637] The two men who had fought shook hands badly, the way men do when they are ashamed of being right. [657] Anton packed the puppets into their canvas roll, wiped the sumi from the stick's grip, and wrote the night's heading on the back of a receipt in the fine copyist's hand he was known for. [692] He was exploring one question only, and had been for years: whether a story can be told without making the person inside it smaller. [716] On the receipt he wrote, in the smallest script he could manage, the words he always closed with: TURN THE STORY AROUND. [738] Then he climbed down out of the dome into the cold, carrying the stick like a man carrying a small, steady fire. [760]</story>
