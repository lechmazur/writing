Del Warren had hated small places since he was nine, when his brother locked him in a cedar chest and sat on the lid until their mother came. [28] Now he was forty-one, prudently adventurous, and he crawled into earth for a living. [42] The fallow period had closed the fields and the quarry alike, three slack weeks each November when nothing grew and nothing was dug, so he took the only work left to him. [74] Reverend Asha Pell had hired him to survey the void beneath St. Cassian's before the frost cracked the floor open. [94] What she did not tell him, and what he found in the parish ledger, was that the void had once been a cistern, and the cistern had once held the key. [125] The vital key lost to time was not a key at all, but a point. [140] St. Cassian's blueprints, drawn in 1840 by a master who signed himself only Ossian, were drafted with a single unblackened steel compass point, and the sexton's old instrument in Del's satchel still carried a sliver of that original tip. [179] He had come to unify the two: the drafted origin and the surviving shard, the way one reunites a broken seal. [200] The hatch behind the altar dropped him nine feet into a shadowed nexus under starlight, for the ceiling had long since fallen away to the sky. [226] Four passages met there, and not one of them obeyed the plan he carried. [240] The hall's cant shifted through twelve degrees, reversing orientation: north ran into east, and the nave he had measured from above lay folded back on itself like a hand closing. [270] Del called it a spatial mutiny in his field book, and then he stopped writing, because the walls were listening. [290] The blueprint drafting compass point slipped from the calipers when he knelt at the fourth passage's mouth, and it went down the joint with a sound too small for so large a loss. [323] He pushed the whole compass after it, losing the tool to retrieve the point, which is the trade every honest surveyor eventually makes. [346] Above him, Reverend Asha Pell climbed into the loft and put her hands on the organ, because she had promised him the only thing the church could still give: sound. [376] A dry cistern during the fallow period gives no reverberation at all; only the organ's pipes, worked by her foot on the bellows, could drive the rose window's armature and ring the brass hidden in the floor. [413] She played once at eleven, when the nave emptied, a lonely chord held long past courtesy. [429] Down in the dark it became a body of sound, and Del felt it in his teeth before he heard it, a reverberation that folded the passages back toward their first arrangement. [461] The final chord seemed to transfix him where he knelt, and he could not have moved if the ceiling had come down. [483] In that stillness the brass point rose out of the joint on a thread of its own ringing, standing upright in its socket like a compass set to draw a new map. [515] He took it between finger and thumb, and the mutiny ended: the cant unwound through twelve degrees, and north remembered itself. [536] The fourth passage, which had been a lie an hour before, was now a true and very narrow throat of stone, and Del Warren had to go down it on his belly with the point in his teeth. [574] His torch was dead and his pride would not let him climb back to the hatch, so he navigated by touch, which is to say he navigated by fear. [603] The stone pressed his shoulders and the dark pressed his mind, and for a hundred feet the two were the same pressure. [625] What carried him was not courage but the sound that was still inside him, that lonely organ still working its bellows somewhere behind his ribs. [650] He came out into the sanctuary on his knees, bleeding at both palms, and laid the point in Asha's hand without a word. [673] She set it against the 1840 plan and it matched the drafting mark exactly, a small bright wound in the paper where the master's hand had first touched down. [702] Then the floor gave a soft click, and the two of them looked at each other the way people do when a building has finally said what it meant. [731] Del wrote one line in the ledger that night and left the rest blank: he had gone down prudently adventurous and come up in a wistful rebellion against every plan that had ever been drawn for him. [768] The point stayed in the church; he carried the sound out with him. [781]
