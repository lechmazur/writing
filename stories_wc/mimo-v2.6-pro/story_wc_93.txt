After friction disappeared, the sea stopped tearing at the shore and simply slid over it like breath over glass. [19]
Salt no longer clung; it hung in the air, a shimmering grammar of things that would not grip. [37]
In that unresisting world lived an elusive eel herder named Tovin, who could not be found when he wished to be found and never wished to be. [64]
He kept his eels in tide-cut channels above the bluff, coaxing them with low calls that traveled farther now that nothing slowed a voice. [88]
Tovin was precognitively cursed. [92]
Every dawn he woke knowing what the next hour would ask of him, and so every hour arrived already used. [112]
He had watched his own griefs in advance and learned to stand politely beside them, waiting, hands empty. [130]

What drove him up the tidal bluff each night was a lingering promise, made to his daughter Sela on the morning the friction went out of the world. [158]
She had asked him why the aurora wrote in lines he could not name, and he had answered that he would find out. [181]
She had died before he could, and the promise did not die with her. [195]

Above the tideline he laid out his work: painted rocks, each one flat and chalk-bright, marked with signs he had copied from eel scales and star charts. [222]
He placed them in mathematical sequences, prime counts and spirals and the slow ratios that bees and nautilus shells agree upon. [243]
His motive was simple and impossible: to understand sacred patterns, the grammar beneath the aurora, the same grammar that ordered a shoal of eels into a single turning body. [272]
When the sequence was right, the stones hummed, and the humming drew what the sky had dropped. [289]
It came as collapsed star dust, a fine grey drift that fell upward and downward at once, too heavy to be light and too patient to be weather. [317]
Tovin meant to harness it. [322]
Not for power, but for proof: if the dust obeyed a sequence of painted rocks, then the sequence was the pattern, and the pattern could be read. [349]

Tonight the aurora stood over the bluff in borrowed dawn, that pale green morning light that is only lent to the dark and taken back before anyone can live in it. [380]
Under it he moved his rocks by three and by five, correcting with a knuckle pressed to the stone, tasting the numbers the way eels taste current. [407]
His curse had shown him this hour three days ago: the dust rising in a column, the bluff ringing like a struck bell, and a door of light opening where the tide had been. [441]
What the vision had not shown him was what the door would say. [454]
That gap, the one thing withheld from his foreknowledge, was the only place left where he could still be surprised, and he guarded it like a fire. [481]
He set the last rock, a blue one, at the mouth of a sequence that had taken him nine years to assemble from eel migrations and starfall dates. [509]
The dust answered. [512]
It poured from the aurora in a thin grey torrent and hung above the stones, turning slowly, the way a shoal turns when it hears the herder's call. [540]
Tovin opened his hands and let the numbers do what hands could not. [553]

The bluff rang. [556]
A door of light stood where the tidewater had slid away without resistance, and in it the aurora was not green but the color of a morning no one had lent out yet. [589]
It spoke in the language he had been trying to learn since Sela asked her question, and the language was not words but ratios: three to five to eight to thirteen, the sequence the rocks had been spelling all along. [629]
The sacred pattern was not hidden; it was patient, and it had been waiting for a herder who would stand still long enough in a world with no friction to notice that nothing was holding anything back. [666]
Tovin understood, and understanding released him from the curse as a key releases a lock that was never actually locked. [686]
For the first time in nine years he did not know what the next hour would hold. [703]
The borrowed dawn began to give itself back, thinning along the bluff, and Tovin gathered his painted rocks one by one. [724]
He laid them in the channel where the eels waited, each stone in its place, a promise kept and a pattern finally named. [747]
Below him the sea slid, without friction, toward a shore it would never need to fight again. [764]
