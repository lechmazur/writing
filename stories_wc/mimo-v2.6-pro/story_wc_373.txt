The market beneath the rotting pier breathed like something alive, and Etta had been listening to its breath since midnight. [20]
Salt rot and lamp smoke tangled in the low air, and the lanterns swung from barnacled beams as if each flame were counting down to something. [46]
She was a moonshine runner's daughter, raised on diesel and secret roads, and tonight she had come to the pirate market to do a thing that felt almost like prayer. [76]
She wanted to capture the sound of trust breaking. [85]
In her pocket she carried an antique watch fob, gold and heavy, its face gone dark with tarnish but its chain still strong. [108]
Her father had pressed it into her palm before the last run and said that trust was a currency you could spend only once. [132]
He had spent his on a man named Corr. [141]
Her father had not spoken the name since, and Etta had watched the silence between them grow teeth. [159]
Now Corr was at the heart of the market, selling the moonshine route to the Ossian syndicate for a price that would drown her family. [184]
Etta had until sunrise to thwart the trade. [192]
Until then the market operated under a truce of hush: no violence, no shouting, no blades drawn until the light returned. [213]
It was an old rule, older than the pier itself, and even the angriest men obeyed it because the alternative was losing the one safe place left to trade contraband. [243]
So Etta worked not with weapons but with false starts. [253]
The first was a boy named Pell, who owed her father a life. [266]
She found him near the seaweed racks and gave him a sealed envelope of counterfeit route papers. [283]
"Take these to Corr," she said, "and tell him you represent the eastern families." [297]
Pell nodded, his face pale, and vanished into the press of smugglers and collectors. [311]
Etta watched Corr open the envelope at his table, watched his eyes flicker with the first spark of greed and confusion. [332]
He would spend twenty minutes verifying papers that did not exist, and then he would look for Pell again and find only shadows. [355]
The second false start was a whisper sent to the Ossian broker, a woman in a gray coat, telling her the route had been compromised. [380]
"Someone is watching," the note said, and nothing more. [389]
The gray coat left her seat and went looking for whoever might be listening, which meant searching the stalls, which meant losing the minutes she needed to close the deal before the truce lifted. [423]
Etta pressed the watch fob against her palm until she could feel its edges, cold and certain. [440]
Each false start bought her time, but time was the only thing the market would not sell. [457]
Etta was curiously steadfast for a girl whose knees wanted to fold, her hands quiet, her voice low, every step deliberate. [478]
At last, near the seaweed racks again, she found Corr alone, pacing, the counterfeit papers crumpled in his fist. [497]
"You," he said, and in that one word she heard what she had come to hear. [513]
"The route is not yours to sell," she said. [522]
He lunged toward her, but the truce held him like a chain, and he could not strike or shout, and so he only shook with fury. [548]
Etta held up the antique watch fob between them like a verdict. [560]
"This is worth more than your deal," she said. [569]
Corr looked at the fob, then at the Ossian broker, who had returned and was watching from the shadows. [588]
The trust between them broke with a sound like a crack in ice, not loud, but final. [605]
Corr had been playing both sides, and now both sides could see it. [618]
The gray coat stepped forward and spoke a single word that ended the deal. [632]
Corr lunged for Etta just as the first pale light crept under the pier and the truce lifted. [650]
The market erupted in noise and motion, but Etta was already moving toward the stairs. [665]
She had captured what she came for: the sound of trust breaking, sharp and clean, when a man's greed turned against him before everyone he needed. [691]
The sun rose behind the pier, and the pirate market dissolved into the morning like a dream at first light. [711]
Etta walked away with the watch fob warm in her pocket, the sound still ringing in her ears, and for the first time in weeks she felt almost calm. [740]
