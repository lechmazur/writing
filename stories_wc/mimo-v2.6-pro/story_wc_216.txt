The emerald silo rose from the county road like a promise nobody had kept. [14] It had been sealed in 1974 as a time capsule for the town's centennial, and when the demolition crew finally cut the concrete lid, the vault inside was empty -- no letter, no coin, no photograph, only water. [51] The damage was the story now. [57] I am Kael, a timekeeper on borrowed time. [65] The specialist gave me eighteen months in the same flat voice he used for weather. [80] I stopped wearing a watch the day I signed the paperwork, and I took the county's salvage job because the silo was the only building I knew of that had been built to hold time and had failed at it. [120] I wanted to understand the texture of time passing, not its quantity. [132] Inside, stacked to the ribbed ceiling, were the boxes. [141] Every one was labeled with an anticipated mystery: MISSING MOTHER'S RING, LETTER NEVER SENT, THINGS FROM THE FIRE. [158] Nobody had catalogued them, and nobody was left to claim them. [169] The first thing the auger pulled from the concrete was a cremation urn fragment, no bigger than my palm, glazed green as the silo itself. [194] Its edge was sharp, and when I turned it over, it cut me. [207] The blood came out slow and dark, and I stood there holding the fragment and stopped counting anything at all. [227] For one long breath I was suddenly timeless. [235] Then the blood dried on the glaze, and the numbers came back. [247] That was the lesson of that year. [254] After the numbers changed meaning, the town stopped keeping time in any way I recognized. [269] The clock on the courthouse read one thing and the radio read another, and people simply went about their work and let the disagreement pass. [294] So I kept my own measure, by hand. [302] I carried a notebook, and I catalogued the boxes by feel -- weight, grain, the sound a lid made when it gave way. [324] The urn fragment rode in my breast pocket, from the fallen and of the fallen. [339] I had no way to keep what I found, so I sold it. [352] Every evening I posted the salvage on a garage sale board, one listing per item, and I learned to bury the truth inside the description. [377] A chipped platter: good for Sunday dinner, though it was the plate her husband broke before he left. [395] A wool coat: warm, size large, smells faintly of the train station where she waited three winters. [412] The buyers came for the coat and carried the waiting home without knowing it. [426] That was how the archive survived: hidden in plain commerce. [436] On the ninth night I found the message. [444] I had been reading filenames on the county's old server -- the boxes had been digitized once, badly, before the flood -- and there it was, wedged between two corrupted jpgs: URN_FRAG_0001.PNG. [474] I opened it and the screen gave me nothing but gray, so I held the fragment up to the monitor's light and rediscovered what the auger had missed: a name, scratched under the glaze, and a date. [511] The name was my grandmother's. [516] She had been the one to seal the silo, and she had put a piece of her brother in the concrete and left the rest to the fire. [544] That was the time capsule's true content: not an object, but a withholding. [557] The vault had been empty on purpose, because she wanted whoever opened it to go looking. [573] The county condemned the silo the next morning. [581] I stood on the road with my notebook and the fragment while the wrecking arm swung, and the green came down in slabs, and the boxes spilled and split open across the mud. [614] Letters dissolved where they lay. [619] I could not save the vault, and I could not save the silo, and the eighteen months were still mine to spend. [641] So I went home and began to write the last of the listings. [654] Emerald silo, parts of, now rubble. [660] Includes one cremation urn fragment, glazed, edge sharp. [668] Good weight. [670] Holds what you cannot say out loud. [677] Will not ship; must be carried, from the fallen, by whoever is still counting. [691] I posted it and left the price blank, the way my grandmother had left the vault. [707] Someone would come looking. [711] In the dark I stopped measuring altogether, and let the time pass over me like water finding its level. [730] It was not quantity I had wanted. [737] It was texture, and I finally had it, rough as glaze, sharp at the edge, and mine for as long as I held it. [761]
