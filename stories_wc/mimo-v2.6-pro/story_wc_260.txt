The mist hung in the workshop low and silver, and the gaps in the roof let the stars fall onto the bench where the chameleon's tail tip lay waiting. [29] Elias had kept it since the morning he found out, the morning the con was revealed and the whole market of collectors went quiet with shame. [55] His apprentice had been selling forged parts for two years, cheap bone dressed in pigment and sold as something a living animal had surrendered. [79] Only the tail tip was real. [85] A green one, shed honestly in a terrarium, and quietly vivid even now, the way chameleon skin holds its color long after the animal has walked away. [112] In the lamplight the tail tip glowed the deep green of wet leaves, and Elias thought of the terrarium it had fallen into, and the small life that had outgrown it. [143] Elias worked by shedding layers. [148] He lifted the varnish off the forged pieces one film at a time, the way you peel old linen from a specimen, and under each false shine he found the lie printed clear. [181] Bone from a chicken yard. Resin poured over bark. A spine assembled from five different animals that had never met. [201] He knew every forgery in the crate by touch, because he had taught the boy the techniques himself, in this very room, for the repair of genuine specimens. [229] He had known for a month and said nothing, walking past the drawer each morning with his jaw set, which was its own kind of dishonesty. [255] He could have turned the boy in and gone to bed with his name clean. [270] Instead he sat in the shimmering stillness of the night and tried to balance what he owed his own self-respect against what he owed the boy who had slept on his floor for three winters. [305] So he did the only thing that served both. [314] He decided to reconstruct the animal the tail tip had come from, so that at least one honest record survived the wreckage of the trade. [339] He measured the tip against the lamp light and wrote the width in a ledger he had bound himself in green cloth. [361] He peeled back the paper wrapping the apprentice had used and found the tiny scars where the tail had gripped a branch. [383] Layer by layer he built the creature back in ink: the grip, the color, the slow swaying walk a chameleon uses to look like wind. [408] He worked slowly, because slow work was the only kind that told the truth. [422] When he heard the door he did not turn. [431] The boy stood in the mist with his cap in both hands, and Elias could smell the resin still on his fingers. [453] "You kept one real thing," the boy said. [461] The boy's voice was steady, which frightened Elias more than tears would have. [474] "You kept one real thing too," Elias said. "You stayed." [484] He gave the forgeries to the inspectors at dawn without naming the boy, and told the collectors that their specimens were paint and barnyard bone. [509] His own standing went down with them, and he let it. [520] Self-respect, he had learned, was not the same as being thought well of. [533] The boy left at first light with a notebook of honest measurements under his arm and nothing he could sell. [553] Elias watched him go, then sat a long while in the thinning mist, the ledger open beside him. [571] On the page the animal stood complete and small, gripping a drawn branch, looking like wind. [587] That was the mercy and the honesty of it: the animal had already let the tail go, and he would not pretend it still belonged to anyone. [614] His convictions had never felt strong to him, only fragile things he had to keep choosing, and tonight he had chosen them again. [637] He wrapped the tail tip in soft tissue and set it in the drawer with the ledger. [654] Above the workshop the stars thinned toward morning, and the mist began to lift from the boards. [671] Somewhere a chameleon, the real one, was still climbing. [680]
