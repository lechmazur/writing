<story>Perrin's workshop hung above the acid sea on twelve chains of black iron, and every gear in it turned whether he willed it or not. [25] Below, the water smoked where the chains dipped, and the smoke came back up green and sweet-smelling, which was the enchantment of the place and also its threat. [53] He had been an alchemist once, uncertain of every measure in the way that a man is a physician to a corpse: with full knowledge, with the right hands, and with no result at all. [88] Three times he had tried to bring someone back from the edge, and three times the elixirs had done nothing but stain the flagstones. [112] So he had stopped trying to transmute and taken up the older craft: he narrated. [127] He narrated now, aloud, because the sound of his own voice was the only thing that kept the acid out of his throat. [150] His workshop was all gears and cogs, a clock escapement the size of a lung beating at the center of the floor, and he had hung every flask on a hook above it so the fumes would be caught in the mechanism and driven out. [195] Nothing in that room did what it was meant to do except the clock. [209] On his little finger he wore a key ring charm, a small armillary of tarnished silver whose bezel had cracked through in the shape of a lightning strike. [237] It had belonged to the statue in the lower chamber, and he had taken it, and that theft was the guilt he wore openly now, the way one wears a wedding band. [269] His intentions were kintsugi intentions: he would not hide the crack in the ring, he would inlay it with gold, and the repair would announce the break instead of erasing it. [300] That was the conjuring he had left: not to restore, but to make the damage mean something at the edge of oblivion. [322] He took his first held breath at the top of the ladder and did not release it until his boots touched the lower floor. [346] The chamber down there had been flooded to the knee and then drained by the same clock that kept the workshop breathing, so the stone was slick and the air tasted of copper. [379] The statue stood where it had stood for four centuries: a figure with its right hand open and empty, the fingers curled around a shape that was no longer there. [409] Perrin put his palm against its chest and closed his eyes and began to narrate what his fingers found. [428] His method was the only honest one he had left: he matched the pockmarks on the centuries-old statue, each pit and blister in the stone answering a flaw in the ring's bezel. [460] There, at the hollow of the collarbone, a shallow crater the size of a lentil; the ring's crack began in exactly that place. [483] He pressed the silver to the stone and felt the two scars agree, and the agreement was the closest thing to a miracle he had been granted in years. [512] His memory quilted itself together patch by patch as he stood there, the seams left showing on purpose. [530] He remembered the woman he had failed to bring back, and the second, and the boy, and each name stitched itself into the last until he could not tell where one grief ended. [563] That was the point of the quilt, he told himself aloud; a quilt does not apologize for its patches. [582] He climbed back with his second breath gone and his third still held, and the acid had risen while he was below. [604] The green water lapped at the lowest gear and began, very softly, to eat the iron. [620] Perrin sat at the bench, took up the gold wire he had drawn himself from a coin, and inlaid the crack of the ring while the floor beneath him ticked toward its own ending. [654] The gold ran into the lightning-shaped break like a river into a canyon, and where it cooled it shone brighter than the silver around it. [679] He held the finished thing up to the acid light and narrated it one last time: broken, repaired, legible. [698] The workshop groaned, the escapement skipped a beat, and the sea took one of the twelve chains. [715] He did not try to transmute the ending. [723] He only meant it. [727]</story>
