The vault door swung inward, and Maren had measured that inward swing a hundred times with her fingertips, because it was the reason she could not open it from inside. [30] Her father built bunkers for a living, and this one sat under the clouds like a fist. [47] Above it, lashed to nothing anyone could see, ran the xenial trellis: a lattice of green that fed strangers and asked no name in return. [72] Maren was claustrophobic, which in her father's house was a kind of disobedience. [85] She kept a tiny porcelain doll in her pocket, no bigger than a thumb. [99] Each year she glazed one cheek of it with a single flavor of silence she had caught, six flavors so far: the cellar's chalk, the storm's held breath, the last second before her father spoke. [134] Her motivation was simple and odd: to collect the flavors of different silences, one of each, before she grew too old to hear them. [158] The seventh was the gilded hush, the quiet at the trellis when the sky does not strain. [175] She could hear it through the bunker's meters of stone, faint and intangibly tangible, the way a smell is a weight you cannot set down. [200] Once a year, for exactly one hour, gravity inverted. [209] Everything loose rose. [212] Her father sealed the vault, set the wheel, and waited out the hour in a chair bolted to the floor. [232] He was a good man who loved her by limiting exposure. [243] Sun, wind, strangers, sky: rationed. [248] So Maren had spent four years feeding one seedling through the bunker's air duct. [262] Only one. [264] Light and air, doled out in pinches, so the vine would stay hungry and thin and strong. [281] A trellis plant, grown in the dark toward a promised sky. [292] The hour came. [295] The house hummed, then went the other way. [303] Her father gripped the bolted chair and said her name once. [314] The vine sprouted. [317] Not slowly. [319] Four years of held hunger let go in a rush, and green came boiling up the duct like water finding a crack. [341] It threaded the wheel of the vault door. [349] It turned. [351] The inward door gaped, and the inverted sky pulled at Maren's collar. [363] She rose. [365] The gilded hush broke over her like warm bathwater, and for once the quiet was not a wall but a field. [386] She rose through the open door and up the vine, and the xenial trellis caught her the way it caught everyone, without a question. [410] She lay in the lattice with her arms out and her lungs open. [423] The clouds were below her and the sun was a coin she could almost spend. [438] She took out the doll and glazed its seventh cheek with the gilded hush, her brush steady even as the wind tried to make her laugh. [464] Below, in the doorway of the bunker, her father had unbolted the chair and stood on the ceiling of his own house, looking up at her. [490] He did not shout. [494] He knew what a silence was worth to her. [503] The hour stretched and contracted the way her lungs were learning to, and Maren held the doll up so the gilded hush could see the sky that made it. [532] When the hour ended she fell, of course. [540] The trellis lowered her gently, because that is what a xenial thing does for a stranger. [556] Her father caught her at the door, and the vault closed behind them with a sound like a held breath let go. [578] He looked at the doll's seven glazed cheeks for a long time. [590] Then he unlatched the air duct and left it open, one finger of sky at a time. [607] Next year Maren will climb the vine herself, and she will leave the eighth cheek empty for whatever silence she finds up there. [630] But that is later. [634] Tonight the bunker is one degree wider than it was, and for a claustrophobic child one degree is enough to sleep. [655]
