After the tide pools emptied, Mara Quill carried her grandmother's storybook illustration plate into the red canyon. [17]
The labyrinth there was centuries old, carved into canyon walls by hands nobody named, and she had spent her whole life pretending not to love it. [43]
Mara was a wharf weaver, a poet with a shuttle, and the canyon was the only inheritance her family had not yet lost. [66]
Six generations of Quills had carved record books in these chambers, a precedent of stone after stone. [83]
Then the tide had taken her brother on the drowned ship, and the record stopped. [98]
She would not let the canyon swallow the plate too. [108]
Her thread was tarred black, salvaged from the ship's rigging, and she sewed with it because grief and repair had become the same gesture. [132]
Tonight she meant to sew a path where no path had been walked. [145]
She would do it by laying down the overlapping sleepwalking paths the canyon had recorded for centuries. [162]
Every night-walker's route had been scratched on the walls in thin silver lines, and no two of them ever agreed. [182]
But where the lines crossed and crossed again, a new route appeared that no single walker had ever made. [201]
Mara had been sleepwalking these corridors since she was nine. [211]
Her grandmother's routes were there too, ghostly silver under the torchlight, the deepest lines in the labyrinth. [228]
Tonight she walked them awake, following the overlaps like a needle following the weft. [242]
At the first crossing she stopped and knotted her thread into the wall's grooves. [256]
At the second she strung a cord that hummed when the canyon breathed. [269]
By the fifth she was sewing in earnest, the black thread dark against centuries of silver. [285]
The path led her to the chamber of ruined record books, where the Quill history lay in broken slabs. [304]
She had grown up here, tracing names that ended before hers. [315]
For a long year she had believed the precedent itself was the wound: do what was done, and the doing would take again. [338]
So the record books were allowed to rot, and every Quill after her would find nothing to follow. [356]
Broken precedents were all she had left to give them. [366]
Beyond the ruin the labyrinth opened into a narrow chamber misted with mineral water, and the walls bloomed with orchids. [386]
The solace they offered was orchid solace, delicate and improbable, blooming in a place that had never once seen the sun. [407]
She tied one bloom to her thread and kept walking, and it was the first thing she had carried forward instead of back. [430]
The last crossing carried her to the first chamber, the oldest room in the labyrinth. [445]
A shaft of late moonlight came down through a crack in the red canyon wall and fell on a bare stone altar. [467]
Here the first Quill had carved the first line, and every Quill since had carved beneath it. [484]
Mara set down the storybook illustration plate where the moonlight could reach it. [497]
Her grandmother had painted it by hand: a small figure carving a doorway out of a collapsed canyon, and behind the figure an orchid in bloom. [523]
Her grandmother painted it in the wreck year, and she painted it smiling. [536]
It had hung above the wharf loom all her childhood, and after the wreck it was the only light in the room. [558]
She painted its edges now with the ghostly silver thread-sap the walls were scratched in, so the plate would belong to the labyrinth and not to the ruin. [586]
Then she sewed the plate to the altar's stone lip with the tarred black thread, one stitch for each name that had ended. [609]
Twenty-two stitches. [611]
Mara was hauntingly optimistic in the way of someone who keeps looking at the death and chooses anyway, and she chose now. [633]
She took up the first Quill's own chisel from the altar and set it to the stone beneath the plate. [653]
This was the path she had come to carve: from ruin to reverence, one line that would outlast her walking. [673]
She wrote no name, only the route: the seven crossings, and where the orchids bloomed. [688]
Under her hands the carved line matched the illustration exactly: a figure making a doorway out of a collapsed canyon. [708]
What had been broken became the beginning of the next thing. [719]
Outside, the tide would return before dawn, and the empty pools would fill with small bright fish again. [737]
Mara gathered her needle and walked the stitched path back through the orchid chamber. [751]
The orchids were still there, which felt like an answer to a question she had stopped asking. [768]
Behind her the plate glowed faintly in the dark, and the carved line held. [782]
That was enough reverence for one night, and it was not the last. [795]
