Mara restored masks that could no longer hold a face. [10]
In her workshop beneath the prism sanctuary, cracked lacquer drifted across the benches like fallen comets. [26]
She was a broken mask restorer, and she knew the difference between a wound and a seam. [43]

The net accumulated silence the way a well accumulates water, slowly, without ever seeming to fill. [90]
Every knot held a hush that had never been spoken aloud. [101]
The sanctuary kept its own hours; starlight came through the glass in slow wheels. [115]

She began to exhume the silence. [150]
Method mattered to Mara; she worked by unraveling knots in the centuries-old fisherman's net, one at a time, coaxing each loop loose until the thread remembered it had once been straight. [181]
As each knot loosened, the hush it carried spilled upward and settled on her, an interlaced hush, layered and audible only in its refusal to be heard. [208]
Her hands shook, and she stopped only to breathe in that lucid unrest that had become her second climate. [227]
She did this to ground restless spirits. [234]
They came to her at night, the drowned and the unnamed, pressing their weight against the glass ribs of the sanctuary until the whole structure hummed. [260]
A spirit without a secret has nowhere to stand, so Mara gave them one. [274]
Across the hours of sharing that secret, she worked the net thread by thread and whispered the same truth each time. [295]
The secret was simple: the sea had not swallowed them whole; it had folded them into its salt and kept them. [316]

They lay still as anchors, and the room grew lighter with their absence. [351]
By the twelfth hour the net lay thin and ordinary on her knees, its three hundred years of hush released into the starlit air. [375]

He had kept his silence wrapped in the last knot so that no one would find what he had refused to say. [472]
Mara knew then what the night demanded. [479]
She did not undo the knot. [485]

The knot loosened itself. [533]

At dawn the glass ribs took back their color and the starlight thinned. [594]

But the floor beneath her held its hush like a held breath, grounded at last, and the night would not come back screaming. [673]
