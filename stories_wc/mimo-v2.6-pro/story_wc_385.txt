In the serpentine nexus under starlight, the canyon echo singer walked with his whispering seashell half tucked against his chest. [20]
He sang once into the winding gorge, and the rock answered twice, which was the trouble with echoes and with people. [41]
Some folk gave back only what they were given, but others replied with words that had never been spoken at all. [62]
He was richly perceptive, and that gift had become a burden he carried the way he carried the shell. [81]
The shell was only half of what it had been, cracked clean down its whorl, yet it still whispered. [100]
It whispered the way the universe hums its secret song, low and continuous, and never quite to him alone. [119]
He had come here to learn why some people echo, and the nexus had already begun to tell him. [138]
First, the ferrymen at the lower crossing sang his own verse back to him one night late and half-drunk. [157]
It was a verse he had only ever sung to the water, and the shock of it dried his throat. [177]
Second, the scribe in the chalk alcove wrote down a line of his that he had never finished aloud. [196]
Third, a child sleeping under a bridge hummed his mother's lullaby, though his mother had died three winters past. [215]
Each disclosure came alone, in its own hour, so the pattern could not be seen until it was nearly complete. [235]
That was the way of it here: the truth arrived through progressive disclosure, the way a river reveals its stones. [255]
By the fourth disclosure he understood that the echoes were not accidents but arrangements. [269]
Someone in the nexus was trading in voices, and the trade had grown into webs of intrigue that caught even the honest. [291]
A broker called the Verse-Keeper sold what singers left behind, unspoken words and unfinished songs and half-remembered griefs. [309]
She kept them in shells like his, and his was the pair-mate to the one she wore at her throat. [329]
The whisper he had carried for years was her inventory, being read aloud to buyers he would never meet. [348]
He found her at the serpent's mouth, where the nexus coiled under a sky wide as a held breath. [367]
"You sing," she said, "and the canyon keeps you. It has kept you longer than you know." [384]
She laid her shell beside his on the stone, and the two halves touched and grew quiet for the first time. [405]
Quiet, he realized, was the sound of a voice returned to its owner rather than sold onward. [422]
The other echoers were not broken people; they were people who had been written into someone else's book of songs. [442]
They repeated themselves because the first telling had been taken from them before they could finish it. [459]
He had to ensure the shells would not open again for her, and he could not do it with a voice. [480]
So he sang one last song into the gorge, a song with no words at all, only reeds and the reed-blown melancholy of water over stone. [506]
The canyon answered, and kept answering, and the answer filled the shells until they could hold nothing else. [524]
The Verse-Keeper listened for a stolen word and heard only water, and the hearing emptied her hands. [541]
Her buyers went home with echoes that meant nothing, and the webs of intrigue loosened thread by thread across the nexus. [562]
The ferrymen sang their own verse again and were surprised to find it theirs. [576]
The scribe crossed out the line she had written of him and began a page in her own hand. [595]
The child under the bridge hummed a new tune of her own making, and it did not sound like loss. [615]
He left his shell half in the crack of the gorge where the echo begins and walked out toward the dunes. [636]
He kept nothing of what he had learned except the question, which was quieter now and asked about himself. [655]
Because he had never once echoed back what he was given, and he did not know why he had been spared. [676]
