The velvet library in autumn kept its own hours, and Mara had learned to arrive when the light came in low enough to make the dust look like something worth keeping. [31] Her choir came in from the wet street with their coats still on, breathing out the cold, and she did not hurry them, because hurry was noise too. [59] She was a harmonious choir teacher, which meant she spent her evenings teaching fifteen stubborn throats to want the same chord. [80] The room had been a reading room once, and the shelves still held their ranks of dark spines, though the books had gone to the county depot years ago. [109] Velvet curtains, the color of dried cherries, absorbed every footfall, so the place had a traveled stillness to it, the kind a room earns from having been full and quiet at once. [141] She set her case on the long table and lifted out the zither. [154] The old man had given it to her in a spring she still refused to name, and for years she had hated him for the way the gift came attached to a lesson she did not ask for. [192] What she touched now, before anything else, was the zither soundhole rosette, a ring of inlaid wood the size of a plum, its pattern worn smooth where fingers had rested for a century before hers. [227] It was the only ornament the instrument allowed, and it was enough to denote that someone, somewhere, had thought sound deserved a doorway. [250] She had been expectantly resigned to the whole arrangement for years: the school would stop renting the library, the velvet would come down, the vanishing arts would keep vanishing, and she would still tune on Friday nights. [287] This autumn the notice had come: demolition in January, and the choir had eight more Wednesdays in this room before it was scaffolding and dust. [312] They sang, and the sound went soft against the velvet, and the boy named Eli held his note a quarter flat the way he had all semester. [339] She stopped them by lifting one hand, not by speaking, because speaking would have added to what was already there. [359] The method she trusted was always the same: by subtracting noise, you find the note underneath. [375] So she did not add force to Eli; she took things away from him. [389] She took the locked jaw and the lifted shoulder and the small proud scowl he wore like a coat. [408] Then she had him come to the table and put his fingertip on the rosette, and she told him the old thing about doorways and sound, in the flat way she had of saying true things. [444] The wood was warm from the room, and he stood there a moment longer than he needed to. [462] When they sang again the chord came up clean, and she felt the old resentment toward the old man loosen by one degree, the way a knot loosens when you stop pulling the wrong end. [497] That was the whole of her unlearning hatred: not a speech, not forgiveness, just one degree and then another, across rehearsals and winters and the slow subtraction of everything she had piled on top of the original gift. [535] After they had gone she stayed, as she always did, and went to the carved rosette above the mantel. [554] A century of candle smoke had blackened it until the pattern was only a suggestion, and she polished it with a dry cloth every Wednesday night, working by subtracting noise, wiping the blackness away until the thing denoted something again. [594] It was the same work as the choir, done with the same hands: to braid diverse voices into harmony until no one could say which thread was whose. [622] Outside, the autumn rain came down on the empty street, and the library kept its hush, and the zither lay in its case with its doorway waiting. [649] Eight more Wednesdays, she thought, and then scaffolding, and then whatever came after, and she would still be teaching someone to want the same chord as the person beside them. [679] That was not hope exactly; it was something quieter, the traveled stillness of a room that had heard ten thousand pages turn and would hear them again somewhere else. [708] She locked the case, switched off the low lamp, and left the velvet to its dark. [724] The door closed behind her with the soft sound of an instrument finishing. [737]
