Maren was a celestial signal interpreter at the Institute, and the stars spoke in single lines she read one at a time. [22]
She had taken the post to thresh meaning from experience, which is a poor way to earn a wage and an excellent way to survive a loss. [49]

It was the summer after planting the wrong seeds. [89]
Nobody said so aloud, but everyone walked through the ruined rows with the careful posture of people editing history in their heads. [111]

She kept it in a tin beside the telescope, and she was confidently uncertain about everything except that the instrument worked. [166]
Certain it worked; certain she could never know what it would yield. [178]

Maren uncapped the pen and saw that the nib was still bent, still refusing to sit true against the page. [214]

The line was her father's voice, the last one he left before he died, and he had gotten it wrong. [265]
He had said the wrong words in the wrong order, the way people do when the tide is going out of them. [287]

The pen knew it. [319]
Editing history was not a metaphor at the Institute; it was a narrow craft, permitted only through contingent relationships. [338]

The Institute kept a ledger of these ties, as ordinary as a parish register. [376]
Maren's tie to her father was the message itself, and she had spent three years trying to hear it correctly. [396]
