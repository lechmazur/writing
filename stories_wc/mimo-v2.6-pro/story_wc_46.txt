Iven called himself an earthbound star watcher, and the grotto never argued the point. [14]
Its serpentine walls coiled around him in long green ribs, and mist moved through the coil like slow breathing. [33]
Here, far under the cliff path, the light came in sideways and pale, so he learned to work by touch and patience. [55]
Above him the sky kept its fires, and he kept the floor of tide sifted sea glass that the water had finished with. [78]
Each morning he set the wheel on its stone and began the slow craft of gem polishing, one piece at a time. [100]
He never used fire, not for heat and not for shine. [111]
His mother had kept a coal oil lamp burning over the bottle of medicine that did not save her, and the memory of that small hot star had taught him what he refused to repeat. [146]
Light, he believed, could be shared without being consumed. [155]
So he polished. [158]
The grit warmed under his palm, and the warmth opened the memories the way a tide opens a shell. [177]
This was the timeframe he lived in, not hours but surfaces, each one raising what lay beneath it. [195]
The first piece he weighed that morning was the green bottle shard he had carried since he was nine. [214]
He set it on the small brass scale and weighed it again as he had a thousand times, then held it to the mist light and watched it go reflectively vibrant, green turning to gold and back to green. [253]
He felt the room tilt toward her voice. [261]
She had whispered that the lamp would guide the doctor up the cliff path. [275]
The doctor never came, but the lamp burned until dawn, and that was the fire he would not carry. [294]
He polished until the shard went quiet in his hand and set it beside the wheel. [310]
The second piece was the hull of a toy boat, cracked down the middle, left at the tideline the summer his wife sailed north and did not return. [338]
His daughter had pushed it into the water and said nothing at all. [351]
When he weighed the hull the scale barely moved, and he understood that some things are lighter than the silence around them. [373]
The mist thickened, then loosened, as if the grotto itself were breathing out. [386]
He polished the hull until the crack became a bright seam, a river of light running the length of the little keel. [408]
He had raised her alone after that summer, and she had grown into a woman who sailed, which he forgave because the sea is not a fire either. [436]
Then he set it down and reached for the smallest piece on the floor. [450]
It was clear, nearly round, worn by nothing but time and sand. [462]
He weighed it in his palm, and it gave back no warmth, no voice, no weather. [478]
What it held instead was a pearl of stillness, the kind of quiet that does not need to be filled. [498]
For a long moment he stopped working, and the grotto stopped with him, and even the mist held its shape. [518]
He thought of the stars he had never touched and found that he did not envy them. [535]
The wheel turned one last time under his hands and the clear piece began to glow from within, not the flare of a lamp but the zephyr bloom of an opening flower. [567]
He carried the three pieces out to the serpentine wall and set them along its curve at the height of a child's eye. [590]
Together they made a small trail of light, green and gold and clear, which brightened and faded like a comet that has decided to be kind. [616]
This was how he meant to share light without using fire, by leaving it where hands would find it rather than where eyes must be blinded. [642]
He hoped his daughter would come down the cliff path one evening and find the trail, and understand without being told. [663]
Then Iven walked back through the mist toward the cliff path, and the grotto kept what he had given it. [683]
