The funeral home prep room smelled of formalin and cold iron, and on the stainless table beside the drain sat a vintage ink pot, no bigger than a plum. [29] It was the only object in the room that was not dead. [39] Mr. Kim lay under a sheet on the second table, sixty-one years old, killed mid-crossing at the checkpoint with a bullet in his back and a passport that belonged to nobody. [70] He had asked for political asylum and been given a wall. [80]

His estate had come to me, as small civic problems always did, because I am the one who settles things for people who cannot be believed. [106] My mother was a con artist of genuine talent, and I am her honest child, which in our family counts as a birth defect. [130] She taught me every angle of a deal and every way to tilt a room before a mark knew he was standing on a slope. [155] What she could not teach me was how to want the tilt. [165] She died owing eleven people and forgiving none of them, and she left me her hands and her suspicion. [184] So I fix drains and estates and I tell people the price before I name the service. [201]

I waited, and waiting actively is its own profession. [210] It means you listen to the pipes and you count the drips and you keep your hands occupied, because a man who stands still in a room like this will be told what is under the sheet. [247] So I disassembled the clogged drain beneath the table with the tools I keep in my coat, and I laid the parts in a row on a clean tray, in the order they would go back. [283] Around me the prep room kept its own ledger: two tables, three drains, one barred window, a hose coiled like a sleeping animal. [306] That was the mathematics of a fair trade: every part accounted for, every part returned. [321] I have looked for that equation in everything my mother touched and never once found it in her hand. [340]

Mr. Kim's manifesto lay folded in his breast pocket, forty pages of argument for a borderless republic, written in a hand so rigid that no one at the crossing had been able to read it. [375] I opened the ink pot. [379] The ink inside moved like water that had decided to think. [388] When the corner of the first page touched the rim, the rigid strokes softened and ran, and the argument became a single fluid sentence I could finally understand. [416] The pot liquefies rigid thinking, that is all it does, and it is enough. [430]

The dead man was the only thing in the prep room with vitality; the fluorescent light buzzed, the drains stank, but Mr. Kim had died reaching, which is more than I have managed. [463] The corners of his mouth had dried in an expression my mother would have sold as peace; I did not sell it. [485] His gift to the new country sat on the steel table beside his head, and his last instruction, translated for me by the reformed forger who ran the crossing, was one line in the margin of page forty. [523] Find the mathematics of a fair trade and you will not need a border. [537]

Below the barred window the guards changed, and I counted their voices, and I moved only when the count said the gap was open. [561] That is the second lesson my mother left me, and the only honest one: you get the breach by waiting actively. [582] I carried the ink pot and the drained parts and the man's dissolved manifesto through the wall after dark, and I set all three down on the soil of the country that had refused him. [617] The checkpoint wall stands behind me now. [623] Asylum is a word for people who have already done the crossing; I am not asking for it. [641] I came to settle an estate and I have settled it, and the price I name is the price I will charge: nothing, since every part has been returned. [670] The pot is open on my desk and the ink is thinking. [679] I am waiting, and my hands are busy. [686]
