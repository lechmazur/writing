In the vanishing point gallery the halls recede until the far wall never arrives. [14]
He is the charcoal portrait ash scatterer, and he works after closing, when the lights burn low and gold. [33]
On his face he wears a mask made from shattered porcelain shards, wired together with copper thread. [50]
It was cut from the doll his daughter held the night the fire took everything else from him. [68]
The mask keeps the patrons from flinching, though he knows it cannot keep them safe. [83]
Safety is the mirage the gallery sells, and he has walked far enough into it to see the shimmer bend. [103]
Tonight he carries a portrait that burned mid-sitting, and the girl in it is almost gone. [119]
He must finish her through incomplete information, working from scorched corners and the memory of a witness who left the city. [140]
He weaves truth carefully, the way a spinner draws thin thread from a tangled fleece. [155]
Where the evidence fails he does not invent; he lets the silence stand and works around its edges. [173]
In the corner of the studio sits an old rocking chair, its runner worn into the shape of a long habit. [194]
Every night for two years he has rocked there while the ash settled on the drying portraits. [211]
His daughter rocked that way before she slept, and the motion is the last grammar he has left of her. [231]
He grinds charcoal to a fine powder on the marble slab, then mixes in the dust the fire left of her bones. [253]
The powder is grey as morning fog, and it clings to his fingers like something that refuses to leave. [272]
He presses it into the burned portrait where her cheek should be, and a face begins to gather. [290]
He works all night, scattering ash with his fingertips, and by dawn the girl has a jaw, a brow, a shadow where her laugh lived. [315]
But the mouth will not come, because no one remembers it well enough to give it to him. [333]
He stops his hands above the paper and understands that the missing piece is the truth. [349]
To fabricate a smile would be to build another mirage of safety in a hall full of them. [367]
So he leaves the mouth unfinished and signs the work with a small mark of ash. [383]
He has done this only to crystallize a fleeting emotion, the one that lived between her silence and her grin. [403]
Now it is fixed in charcoal, and it will not move again, and that is its own kind of mercy. [423]
He sets the portrait among the others at the far end of the vanishing point gallery. [439]
The halls swallow it gently, the way water swallows a stone, and he feels something loosen in his chest. [458]
He goes to the chair and sits, and for the first time he does not rock at once. [476]
He lets the silence do the rest of the work. [486]
Outside the gallery, the city keeps pretending, and he forgives it for that. [499]
Then the chair takes up its old rhythm one last time, slow and sure, while the ash on his coat drifts to the floor. [523]
He watches the dust settle and knows that he has finished what he could. [537]
As the old rocking chair finally slows to a stop, the last grain of powder comes to rest beside the runner. [558]
He sits very still, listening to the building breathe, and he does not reach for the charcoal again. [576]
The gallery holds its long perspectives open, and the girl in the portrait keeps her unfinished smile for anyone who can bear to look. [600]
In the morning the staff will find him sleeping in the chair, the mask beside him on the floor, all its shards gleaming like a broken moon. [627]
They will carry him out through the halls that never arrive, and he will go willingly, lighter than he has been in years. [650]
Nothing is safe, he thinks, and nothing is lost, and between those two facts lies the whole of his craft. [670]
