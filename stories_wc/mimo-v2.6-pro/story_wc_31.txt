Alder worked the late shift along the pier, lighting the harbor lamps one by one as the tide went out. [20] He was looking for the color of sound. [28] No one at the harbor office knew what he meant by that, and he never explained it twice. [46] He said only that a man should be able to see a bell ring, and he said it plainly, which was how he hid. [70] The truth was the best cover he had ever found. [80] That evening the wedding barges came out early. [88] Couples stood at the rails making luminous vows to each other while the water held their reflections like a second set of witnesses. [111] A bride in a pale coat asked him what he was doing down at the ladder with a coil of rope. [132] He told her the truth, all of it, that he meant to walk down to the Meridian Trust and read the drowned shelves until he found the color he was after. [163] She laughed and let him go. [169] Behind her the sky lantern release began, and the harbor went quiet under it, a meteor hush, as if something falling were listening. [192] He went under during that hush. [198] On his ring hung a hotel skeleton key tag, brass, stamped with the word ELEVEN. [213] He had never checked into that hotel. [220] He had kept the key anyway, the way some men keep a wound they will not name, and it had grown useful. [242] The Meridian Trust had gone down forty years before with her whole library intact, and no salvage crew had ever bothered with her. [265] He wandered purposefully along the silted deck, past the capstan, down the broken companionway, and he did not once turn aside. [286] Every step was a signal, though there was no one above the water to read it. [302] The library held. [305] Shelves stood in their rows under a skin of green silt, and the water inside them was perfectly still. [324] He lifted his lamp and set it on the reading table. [335] The light did not spread. [340] It gathered, the way sound gathers in a shell, and it made a low ring in the water that he could see. [362] The ring was the color of nothing he had words for. [373] That was the answer then, and he stood a long while letting it signify what it wanted to. [391] On the table lay a guest register from a hotel he had never stayed at. [406] The ink had not run. [411] His own name sat in it, in a hand he recognized, next to a date forty years gone. [429] He had been lying to himself for a long time in the most honest way available. [445] He remembered the vow then, the one he had made on a barge like the ones above him now, and the one he had broken before the year was out. [475] It had been a luminous vow, the kind that burns so bright no one checks whether it lasts. [493] The color he had come for was the same color as that promise. [506] He understood it then and stopped looking. [513] He wound the rope around the capstan and took the tag from his ring. [527] The brass key fit the deck locker, and the locker had not been opened since the sinking. [544] Inside lay a flare pistol and a sealed jar of oil for the lamps. [558] He took the oil and left the pistol. [566] Light was worth more to him than rescue. [574] He climbed the rope into the hush. [581] Above, the last lanterns were going out one by one, and the couples had gone home to keep what they had promised. [603] Alder finished his rounds with the new oil and lit the last lamp at the end of the pier. [622] He hung the brass tag back on his ring, where it would keep its secret a while longer. [640] When the harbor office asked him in the morning where he had been, he told them, in plain words, and they wrote nothing down. [664] The truth was doing its work again. [671]
