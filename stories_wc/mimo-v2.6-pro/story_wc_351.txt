In the moonlit canyon, the wind moved like a slow instrument being tuned. [13] Tamsin Kraye, a helix-minded engineer, crouched beside the firepit she had coaxed from damp sage and stubborn hope. [31] Her hands were chaotically precise, scattering rivets like seeds yet drawing every wire to its exact and necessary place. [50]

Before her, mounted on a tripod of welded scrap, hung the broken periscope mirror she had carried for eleven years. [70] A hairline fracture split its silver face into two futures that refused to agree. [84] She had found it in the wreck of her father's survey vessel, and the fracture had been there since the day the sea took him. [109] The mirror had always been honest about that. [117]

Tonight she meant to modify it. [123] Not the glass; the glass was beyond her, and beside the point. [135] She meant to modify the way she saw through it, and the modification would happen through synaptic plasticity, the slow law by which a mind redraws its own wiring in answer to what it insists on imagining. [172] She had spent a decade studying perception as an engineer studies load-bearing walls. [185] Now she would use that knowledge against herself, gently, chaotically, with the exactness of someone disarming a device she loved. [205]

This was her period of profound transformation, and she could feel it the way canyon stone feels water: slowly, and then all at once. [229] She had quit the observatory that morning, leaving her badge face-down on a desk still warm from the night shift. [249] She wanted to become the archeologist of abandoned dreams, the one who digs beneath the sleepers' castles and catalogs the rooms no one visits anymore. [274] Every person she had ever loved had a dream they had set down like a tool, and each dream still hummed where it lay. [298] She would learn to hear those hums, and to name them. [309]

The fire threw her shadow against the canyon wall, and the shadow shook with a quaking reverie she did not try to still. [332] She had already begun the work by recalling, in deliberate loops, the dreams of the dead. [348] Her mother had wanted to build bridges; her father had wanted to map the underside of the world; her brother had wanted to sing in a language that had not been invented yet. [381] Each memory she rehearsed caused a little rewiring, and she could feel the synapses yielding like rope under a measured pull. [402] This was the mechanism: entangled memories, braided together until one could not be touched without the others trembling. [420] Her father's maps braided with her mother's bridges braided with her brother's unfinished song, and all three braided with the silver fracture in the periscope mirror. [446]

She lifted the instrument and pointed it down the moonlit canyon, and the two futures in the fracture began, faintly, to converge. [468] What she saw was not the canyon alone but the canyon as it had been dreamed into being: a river that was really a bridge, a wall that was really a voice, an exit that was really a song no one had finished. [511] The vision quaked and held, and her own hands did not tremble, which surprised her. [526] She recorded what she could with the chaotically precise habits of her trade, scrawling coordinates and half-poems in the margins of the same notebook. [550] Engineers did not usually do this kind of arithmetic, but tonight arithmetic was only one dialect among several. [568]

By the time the moon stood directly overhead, the mirror's two futures had become one future with a scar, which is what honest futures do. [593] She whispered her brother's unfinished line into the glass and felt a new branch of circuitry bloom behind her eyes, warm as a small animal. [618] That was the synaptic plasticity at work, not magic, though the distinction had stopped mattering to her somewhere around the second hour. [640]

When dawn threatened at the canyon's rim, she packed the periscope mirror in its felt case and left the tripod standing as a marker. [664] She would return to dig; the canyon held strata of sleeping thought she had only begun to survey. [682] For now she walked out carrying an instrument that showed one future, and a mind that had just agreed to be modified. [704] Above her, the moon set like a lens being closed, and the last light in the broken mirror trembled once, then steadied. [726]
