[128] Everyone knew someone had been hurt. [134] No one knew it was Ines. [140] That anonymity was a shelter she had not asked for and could not quite bring herself to leave. [158]

[292] She had handed it to him herself, near the end, in the way you hand someone a weapon and call it a kindness. [315] Inside the amber a small fly was suspended, wings half-open, stopped mid-sound. [327]

[358] Ines had begun to keep track of the marks, the way another person might keep a diary. [477] He did not speak, and she did not fill the silence, and the water went on dripping into its own careful script. [499] At last he crossed the moss and held out his hand and proffered the amber pendant, its chain pooled in his palm like something sleeping. [524] She looked at the fly in its honeyed hush, the sound it had been making stopped and kept and made sweet by the keeping. [548] She did not take it. [553] Instead she pointed to the floor, where the drips had worn a pale constellation into the stone, and showed him the name she had given the mark nearest the door. [583] "Today," she said. [646] Ines stayed until the blue lights came on, and the hush settled over the racks like something poured, and the next drip found its mark. [671] Nothing was healed.
