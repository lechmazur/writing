Oren designed hedge mazes for a living, and in all his years he had never found the texture of time. [20] He kept a tin box of hard-edged photographs on his workbench, and a phone full of fading ones he could not bear to delete. [44] Every photograph was a doorway he could not step through, and that was the whole cruelty of it. [62] He wanted to hold a moment in his hand the way a mason holds a brick. [78] So when the letter came from the Living Bridge Garden, offering him a commission and a shard of pale glass, he went. [100] The letter said the garden grew on memory and had begun to grow wrong. [114] The shard was a halo fragment, broken from something that had once been whole and holy. [130] A grinning man met him at the gate and told him the garden would pay him in visions. [148] No name, no signature, only a diagram of roots that looked like folded limbs. [162] "Design us a maze that remembers," the man said. "One that grieves." [174] Verusha, the garden's client, stood under the braided limbs of the living bridge and pressed the fragment into Oren's palm. [194] "Everything here is retrocognitively trapped," she said, and her voice was a quaking reverie. [208] The leaves changed color around them with nothing to measure the change by. [221] Oren raised the halo fragment to the light and saw the last breath of every soul who had ever fallen here. [242] He could walk into those breaths and re-feel them; the garden fed on the feeling, and it grew. [260] Each soul he entered left something of itself in him, and left with something of his. [276] That was the trap, and it was also the only way to reach what he wanted. [292] He set his paths through hyperbolic geometry, so that the hedges multiplied faster than the ground could hold them. [311] In such a maze there is no far corner; every route curves back into the body of the maze itself. [331] He measured angles that should not have existed and set yew against them in long curving runs. [348] A canary landed on a leaf and ate one perfect square out of it, then vanished without going anywhere. [367] The sound it made while eating was the sound a candle makes when it gives up. [383] Oren walked the loops in a trembling half-dream, watching the visions gather and then fail. [398] He wrote his plan on tracing paper, but the lines blurred wherever the visions passed through them. [415] They failed the way his photographs failed: the face of his mother softening into colored mist, the edges going first. [435] He reached for each one and felt the grain of it slip through his fingers like dry sand. [453] He understood then that memory here was not stored but eaten, and slowly, and with joy. [469] The garden kept growing, the loops folding tighter, and the ground beneath him began to thin. [485] Verusha's voice came from somewhere inside the hedge. "It remembers too much," she whispered. "It has always remembered too much." [505] The hedge was a mouth by then, and the paths were its throat, and the sky was barely permitted. [524] Oren knew then what the garden wanted of him, and he knew he would give it. [540] He pressed the halo fragment to his eye and looked for the texture of time. [555] He found it: a fine cold grain, like the dust that rises from an old photograph when it finally lets go. [576] It tasted of dust and warm stone and of the last afternoon of everything. [590] The ground gave way beneath him, but he did not fall. [601] He disintegrated into points too small to see, drifting upward through the braided limbs of the living bridge garden. [620] Among the misted photographs he joined the others, each one still looking like itself. [634] They drifted there among the fading photographs, patient as pollen, waiting for the garden to be hungry again. [652] There was a photograph of Oren there too, and it was fading. [664] The hedge maze he had designed kept growing past the edges of the ground, and the leaves changed color with nothing to measure the change by. [690] Time, at last, had a texture, and he was made of it. [702]
