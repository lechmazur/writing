Orin had been gone three days when the cloud-sea climbed past the ninth platform's moss line. [16] Thessaly noted the new water height in the margin of her ledger, then allowed herself the small unclenching of the shoulders that was all the grief she could afford on a working morning. [49] The magnetic arbor had been built to hold things up: nine copper platforms on branches that reached into open sky, a field running through every seam that kept the knowledge fruit hanging and the white sea beneath from swallowing the place. [90] Without Orin the field was failing, slowly, the way a held breath fails. [103] She had come here as a cuneiform translator, and she had stayed after the spirit left because his tablets were unfinished and no one else could read the hand that had damaged them. [136] Her method had always been kindly; she worked wet clay with a wooden stylus, never a blade, because a gouge destroyed what it uncovered. [160] She supposed that was why he had trusted her with the ninth platform, and why she was still standing on it. [181] Every evening she climbed to the ninth platform the slow way, testing each rung, because a fall from this height meant falling into cloud and never being found. [209] His ambition haunted the place more than his absence did. [219] He had wanted to reverse the transition, to take the physics of spirit-departure and run it backward like a rope pulled through a pulley. [243] He had said so on the last morning, in that practical voice he used for impossible things, and then he had walked out onto the branch-tip and not come back. [273] Thessaly's ambition was smaller and, she thought, more honest: she wanted to understand the transition itself, not undo it. [292] His notebooks filled three copper chests, and in the margins he had written the same word over and over in Sumerian, kurun, kurun, kurun, as if repetition could teach the clay to obey. [325] That was why she had spent eleven weeks on a single damaged tablet that appeared to be a recipe for beer. [346] Kurun, sipa, kunin: beer, sheep, the long grass. [354] The signs did not resolve. [359] Fermentation and husbandry shared a column, the grain measures added up to nothing, and the last sign she read as a door, then a threshold, then neither. [386] It was the field failure that gave her the key, or rather the failure gave her the analogy, and the analogy gave her the key. [411] Beer rises, she wrote: the gas lifts the cap of grain and holds it above the liquid. [428] The field lifts; the arbor holds the fruit above the cloud. [439] The tablet was not a recipe but a description of a principle, written in the only vocabulary a herdsman civilization had for it, and the grain measures were not measures but a ratio. [472] She worked through the afternoon with the stylus in one hand and the ledger in the other, translating by analogy now, holding the two systems against each other the way a translator holds two languages and listens for where they agree. [513] What she built from it was a set of instructions, and she reestablished the field by writing them into the copper at the ninth platform's root, where Orin had always said the seam was thinnest. [548] The branches hummed, the white sea drew back below the moss line, and somewhere overhead a dry husk split and the fruit began to swell. [573] The seed had been inside the tablet all along, pressed into the clay the way a herdsman presses a tally, and now it was doing what seeds do: transforming one thing into another without asking permission. [609] That is the physics, she thought: not a door, not a threshold, but a direction. [624] The knowledge fruit was said to hold a single proposition each, ripened from whatever principle the arbor could gather out of the passing clouds, and the last fruit Orin had picked was still drying on the bench, unopened, because she had not been ready to read it. [671] She was still writing when the spirit came back along the branch, at touching distance, close enough that the hairs on her forearm lifted in the field it carried. [700] She did not reach. [704] Orin had reached, and Orin was gone, and she understood now that understanding is the only thing a person can actually keep. [726] The fruit hung above her, full and humming, and the cloud-sea settled into a long white quiet. [743]</story>
