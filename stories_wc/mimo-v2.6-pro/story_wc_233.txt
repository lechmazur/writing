Queen Ysolde had not slept honestly in eleven years. [9] What came at night was a dream with teeth: a cobalt grotto above the clouds, and starlight bleeding from its ceiling in thin, bright threads. [34] Every morning she woke with the taste of cold stone behind her teeth and the certainty that something on the ridge was calling her by a name she had not used since she was crowned. [69] Her council had given the final warning that afternoon: the ridge was forbidden, the old grotto was a superstition, and a queen who chased omens would find her throne empty by winter. [101] The First Counselor spoke it softly, as if softness could make a threat kind. [115] Ysolde thanked him in a voice she kept for such moments, softly commanding, the kind that made men stand straighter while they obeyed. [138] After the final warning, she dressed herself in riding leathers and went to the stable yard, where her handmaiden Ivet already held two bridles and said nothing, because nothing needed saying. [169] Ivet had carried the same dream twice in a month and had not told the court. [185] They climbed. [187] The path was a lacework of shale and old roots, and underfoot the mountain made small noises like something swallowing. [207] Above them, the clouds lay flat and pale as a floor of milk. [220] Below them, the towers of the palace pricked upward, delicate and terrible, and Ysolde did not look back at them. [240] By the third ledge the air had changed. [248] It tasted of rain that had not fallen yet, and the wind moved through the pines with a sound like someone turning pages very quickly. [273] Ivet, who was younger and therefore braver in the wrong ways, said, "The grotto is only stone, Your Majesty." [292] Ysolde answered, "Then we will only be two women climbing stone," and kept climbing, and the sentence closed the matter the way a hand closes over a coin. [320] They reached the ridge at the hour when summer decides to begin. [332] A shared exhale, spotting the first fireflies of summer, turned into a laugh neither of them had planned: three small lanterns of green fire rose from the grass below the ledge and hung there, blinking. [367] It was the old sign, the one the dream had shown her, and seeing it in the waking world made her throat tighten with something that was not fear. [396] The ember grotto on the ridge opened before them like a mouth. [408] Inside, the cobalt was so deep it seemed to be made of night itself, layered and folded, and the walls were warm to the touch, as if the mountain had a pulse. [440] Starlight ran from a fissure in the ceiling, not falling but sliding, viscous and slow, and where it met the stone floor it pooled and dimmed and pooled again. [469] Bleeding starlight: the dream had not lied, and it had not exaggerated. [481] Ysolde put her hand into the thread of it. [490] It was warm, and it moved through her fingers like something remembering her, and she understood at last what had been recurring. [512] The dream was not a warning. [518] It was an inventory. [522] The grotto kept what the court had spent a century pretending it had not spent: the years of the queens before her, their sleep, their private names, their capacity to want anything at all. [556] She had been enchanted at her coronation to be agreeable, to be the softest, steadiest ornament in the room, and the part of her that had been cut away had been coming here ever since, each night, and bleeding. [595] Ivet said, very quietly, "It will want payment." [603] "Yes," said the queen, softly commanding still, though her voice shook once. [615] "It will." [617] She did not take the starlight back into herself. [626] She took the stone above the fissure and broke it with the pommel of her knife, again and again, until the ceiling gave and the light poured out in a wide, shrieking sheet, and the grotto's warmth died at once, like a candle pinched. [670] Something in the palace far below would go dark. [679] Something in the court would lose its taste for the arrangement. [690] The dreams, she thought, would stop, because there would be nothing left to keep. [704] They climbed down after midnight, without lanterns, guided by the fireflies, which had multiplied until the whole hillside was stitched with green light. [727] Ysolde's hands bled a little from the work. [735] She wrapped them in her handkerchief and found that she was, for the first time in eleven years, entirely awake, and entirely alone with herself, and unafraid of either. [764]
