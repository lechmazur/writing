<story>Before the light changed, before the doors of the glassworks were chained and the counting began, I sat down to write this. [22]
My name is Havel, and I have been the glazier of Pearl Bay for thirty-one years, which means I have spent my life putting clear things between people and the weather. [53]
This is a ledger of my reckonings, kept in the margins of my work orders, and I write it now because by noon the doors will be chained and a man from the city will stand in the atrium with a clipboard and say the word value and mean something smaller than I do. [107]
The atrium is my best work and nobody's monument. [116]
Four hundred panes rise from the harbor wall to a glass crown, and at dawn, in the sea mist that Pearl Bay breathes out before it wakes, the crown splits the low sun into bands that fall across the floor like the bars of a slow instrument. [163]
I call it the prism atrium in mist because that is what it is when the world is still deciding what kind of day it will be. [190]
Noctilucent reckonings: I keep them at the hour when the night still glows on the water and the day has not yet claimed it. [214]
They are my account of what is worth keeping in work that other men call ordinary, and I have kept them since Seda died, because she was the one who first asked me to look at a pane and tell her what I saw. [258]
My method is an old one and it takes only a stone. [270]
At the edge of the atrium there is a pond the masons cut for drainage, and I kneel there at dawn and throw a stone into it, and I commune with whatever the rings bring back to me. [308]
The rings do not lie and they do not hurry; they carry a small event outward until it has touched the whole surface, which is more than most of us manage in a lifetime. [342]
I throw once for the work of my hands. [351]
The first ring this morning ran out and returned me to my father's shop, to the thumbscar he earned cutting the cheapest glass in the district, and to the window I set for a fishwife who paid me in herring and told me it was the finest thing on the street. [402]
So the plainest pane carried the deepest shine, and the city's grades cannot say that. [417]
I throw again for the ones I loved. [425]
The second ring gave me Seda's chapel of colored bottles, the way she set them in lead so the light had to travel through seams, and how she told me to read between the lines, meaning the dark lead was what made the colors mean anything at all. [473]
Our best hours had no witnesses; they were ordinary evenings with the kettle and the smell of cut glass on my hands, and I have learned that ordinary is where the value hides when no one is grading it. [512]
I throw a third time for the bay itself. [521]
The third ring widened and widened until it was the whole pond, and I saw the drowned village under these waters, the families who handed up a tin ring and a name when the tide took their houses, and asked only to be remembered by someone whose job was to keep things clear. [574]
I have booked their names in the margins of the last work order I will ever file, and they will go to the city's man with the clipboard whether he wants them or not. [608]
The mist is lifting now and the bands of color are climbing the floor toward the crown. [625]
Let them take the building; the prism atrium was never glass alone, it was a way of standing inside the dawn and refusing to call it ordinary. [652]
Tomorrow I will set panes in a warehouse for men who measure light by the square foot, and I will kneel at whatever pond I find and throw my stone and keep the reckonings. [686]
Before everything shifts, before the chain falls and the clipboard speaks, I want it written plainly in the record. [705]
Value is not what a thing fetches. [712]
Value is what it splits open in the light when someone takes the trouble to look. [728]
The rings are settling, and the stone is at the bottom where stones belong. [742]
I have thrown what I had to throw. [750]
What comes back now is up to whoever reads this, and I hope they read between the lines, where the lead is, where the color lives. [776]
This is my last entry in the glassworks ledger, and the book I am writing in my head has no margins at all. [799]</story>
