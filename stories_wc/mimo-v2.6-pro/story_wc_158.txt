<story>Inside the gargantuan shell the sea had forgotten to reclaim, the insomniac poet kept her lamp awake against the tide of her own thinking. [24] Sleep never came, so she wrote, and writing came easier than breathing in that nacreous dark. [40] She had found the galaxy postcard wedged in a fissure of the shell's inner whorl, a rectangle of impossible sky with a nebula printed like a bruise. [67] On its back, in a handwriting so faint it seemed embarrassed, someone had written: I am still waiting where the stars break. [89] The poet, who was shyly unstoppable in all her small enterprises, set about answering a message that had crossed centuries of longing to find her. [114] She could not sleep anyway, and the wind that combed the shell mouth came off-key, a wandering troubadour of a breeze that could not hold a pitch. [141] Into that wind she sang half-sung lullabies, the ones her grandmother had crooned for whales, and the off-key wind carried them outward like stray notes of a horn. [169] This was her method: to photosynthesize pain into beauty, the way the anemones in the shallows turned grief-light into glow. [189] Every verse she made was a blossom, and every blossom a small flag of truce between her and the dark she lived inside. [212] She called this the blossom armistice, though no one else was there to sign it with her. [229] Still, the agreement held. [233] She meant to ensconce the postcard in the shell's safest chamber, where the light pooled like spilled milk and nothing salted could reach it. [257] But first she wrote her reply on the back of a receipt, then a leaf, then the smooth inner wall itself, because paper kept running out and the longing did not. [288] The wind took each answer and whistled it out of tune, and she loved the wind for its bad manners. [308] Centuries passed in the way only poems and shells measure them, by the accretion of thin bright layers. [326] Her hair silvered; the lamp was replaced; the shell grew around her like a second sentence she had agreed to live inside. [348] And always the reef-shadow joy of that place: the happiness one finds only beneath the dark edge of things, where light is a rumor and its arrival a festival. [377] On the night the wind finally changed pitch and held a note for a whole breath, the poet looked up and found the shell's mouth full of pale drifts. [406] Petals, sea-borne and star-fed, had come in on her lullabies the way sailors come in on a chorus. [424] They settled on the floor of the shell and on her shoulders and on the galaxy postcard she had finally ensconced in its chamber of pooled light. [451] For one long hour the blossoms and the bruises of the printed sky were the same color. [468] The poet did not sleep, of course; she had not slept in longer than the sea had memory. [486] But she wept, and the weeping sounded like the off-key wind learning, at last, its song. [502] When dawn reached the whorl, she set down her pen and listened to the silence that had a shape now, a shape like a signature. [527] Someone across the centuries had written to her from where the stars break, and someone across the centuries had answered. [547] She was shy, and that was her secret advantage; the shyness never once crossed the line into stopping. [565] What she grew from that darkness was never for anyone in particular, and yet it fed everyone who stumbled into it. [586] In the chamber where the postcard waits, the light still pools like spilled milk, and the petals have gone to powder and to pearl. [610] The blossom armistice held. [614] It holds still, in the shell, in the dark, in the joy that grows where no one thinks to look. [634] Read it in the dark sometime, if the dark will have you; it has been expecting company all along. [653]</story>
