The canyon kept its dead standing in towers of bone. [10] He sat at the foot of the tallest one and pressed the stone arrowhead flat against the rib below his palm. [31] It was a grave-gift, older than the dust that had buried the people who gave it, and he held it the way a son holds a father's name. [59]

They called his trade harmonic stone-skimming, though he had never skimmed anything in the ordinary sense. [75] He struck stone with stone and listened to what traveled. [85] Each canyon wall returned the sound late, layered and re-cut, and each layer was a different century answering. [103] He had learned to appraise a place the way a jeweler appraises a stone, not by its shine but by its faults. [125] His master had died with her ear against a wall, and he had taken her seat in the dust without complaint. [146]

There was a pulse left wandering in the canyon. [155] No one had set it, no one had claimed it, and it had been here before the bone towers began to rise. [177] It moved without a body through the walls, through the dust, through the hollow chambers of the dead. [195] He wanted to learn to navigate without moving, to find his way by listening alone, and the wandering pulse was the only teacher that never stopped speaking. [222]

So he watched the birds. [227] Twice a day a flock came down the canyon from the north, and it never took the same line twice. [247] They bent around the pulse the way water bends around a stone, and their patterns in flight were the only map he had of a thing that cast no shadow. [277] He read the gaps between the wings, the widenings and the sudden drawn-in spirals, and turned each reading into a path he could walk without ever standing up. [305] The birds were not his to command and not his to follow; they owed him nothing and gave him everything. [325]

Centuries layered themselves over him the way dust layers bone. [335] Dust crept up the towers one rib at a time. [345] His hair whitened, his hands roughened, and the arrowhead's edge wore smooth where his thumb had rested. [362] He did not grieve for the years, because grief is a kind of motion, and he had sworn off motion. [382]

He was historically reverent in all things. [389] He named the bone towers as a pilgrim names shrines, and he spoke to the arrowhead's makers each time he set it down. [412] One season, long after he had stopped counting seasons, a young wanderer came down the canyon carrying almost nothing. [431] The wanderer asked him what he was doing. [439] He said, listening, and showed her how the flock bent around the pulse. [452] She stayed a season, then she stayed a life. [461] When she grew old she lay down in the dust and did not rise, and the dust took that body too and folded it into the canyon's layered record. [490] The skimmer sat on. [494]

Then one morning the flock came high and slow and closed into a single turning shape. [510] In its center the air bent the way water bends, and the wandering pulse rose to meet it like a struck note answering a struck note. [536] Everything he had spent centuries learning arrived at once. [545] He knew every wall, every echo, every fault in the stone without moving a hand to touch any of them. [565] He had not moved in so long that the dust had made a small dune of him, and from inside the dune he saw the whole canyon laid out like a chart. [597]

He pressed the arrowhead once more to the bone and gave thanks to the people who had left it for him. [618] Then he let his hands go still and let the dust finish its slow work over his knees, his shoulders, his brow. [640] He had learned to navigate without moving at all. [649] The canyon kept him, one more layer in the record, as the flock turned overhead and the pulse went on wandering. [670]
