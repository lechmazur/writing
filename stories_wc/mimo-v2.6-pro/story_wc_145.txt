The rusted outpost at Vorhalm stood eleven weeks empty of anyone but Teren Voss, and that suited him. [18] He was an atlas editor, dry-witted by trade and by necessity, and he had come to the frozen lake to prove that the bureau had printed a lie. [47] The lie was small, four letters on a copper plate: that the rivers began where the map said they did. [67] He did not believe it. [72] The mast on the roof carried a leather tether, and after the falcon returned that morning the whole arrangement of his winter made sense. [96] The bird came down out of a white sky with a brass tube under one wing. [112] Inside was a single page, and the page was an order: proceed south to Korrenfell, read the upper waters with his own eyes, report before the spring plates closed. [141] He read it twice, then folded it into his coat, subtly confident that he would be right and quietly aware that being right at the end of winter is an expensive comfort. [173] His only other possession was a phrasebook, four languages, dog-eared at one question. [187] In the second language it read: Where does this river begin? [198] He had never trusted a translator and he did not intend to start with the watershed. [214] The lake in front of the outpost was a mile of grey ice, and the ice was still, and the stillness was not the kind that reassures. [241] It was the kind that waits. [247] Worrying tranquility, he wrote in the margin of his field sheet, and underlined it. [261] He shouldered his pack at noon and walked south across the ice, and each step rang off into the trees like a small bell rung once and never answered. [290] Four days later the ice thinned under him and the rivers began to speak. [304] Korrenfell was a survey town of dark timber and derricks, where the bureau kept a gauging hut on the bluff above the gorge. [327] Teren climbed to it at dusk and found the glass cold, the stove unlit, and nobody within a mile who cared what he had to say. [353] That suited him too. [357] He lit the stove, set his notebook on the sill, and leaned close to the window to see what the water was doing in the dark. [383] He could not see it, but he could read the pattern of condensation on the glass, the way his own breath fogged and then ran. [408] Where the pane held cold from the gorge the beads gathered in filaments, and the filaments drew together, and every one of them went the same way. [435] That was the singular convergence he had been sent to find: four named rivers on the atlas, and one throat below. [456] The Kess, the Vorh, the Auld, the Marren were not four springs but one water, divided by rock and by habit and by four languages with four words for it. [486] He opened the phrasebook and dog-eared the page flat. [496] Then he shut his notebook once, hard, and the sound carried out over the gorge. [511] "Found," he said. [514] It was not a shout and it was not relief; it was a herald, the way a single stone heralds the fall of the rest. [539] By morning the fog had lifted and he could see the four channels braiding into the gorge, and he could see they were wrong on the copper plate. [567] He drew the correction in pencil, the line of one source and one mouth, and he drew it the way he always drew a fix: thinly, so the truth could be read through it. [601] The atlas would have to be re-cut, three counties would have to be renamed or admitted to being the same county, and the spring plates would close late. [630] He minded the last and not the rest. [638] That afternoon he packed the hut, left the stove open for whoever came next, and started north across the thawing ice with the falcon's tube in his pack and the phrasebook flat at his side. [673] Behind him the water found its one channel and ran south, and the lake began, very quietly, to give up its ice. [695] There was nothing left to edit now but the ending of winter, and Teren Voss had never trusted an ending he had not marked himself. [720]
