The escondite had held Aurel for three hundred years, and he had grown bored of it. [16]
It was a prismatic vault beneath the salt flats, a hidden room where light broke into slabs of color and the walls forgot which angle they were supposed to keep. [46]
Aurel had loved it once, the way only an immortal can love anything: absolutely, and then not at all. [65]
What he wanted now was a single thing he had never been given: to discover what happens after goodbye. [84]
He had said a thousand of them and stayed to hear none of them end. [99]

Around his neck hung the fragment of a cursed amulet, a broken tooth of black metal that had never wanted to be whole. [122]
Its curse was dimensional awareness; Aurel was no longer certain where his edges stopped. [136]
He would reach for a wall and feel it a room away. [148]
The fragment tugged at him through magnetic fields, a patient pull that never quite let go, and had been teaching him for two centuries that gift and disease are the same thing. [180]

That evening the sky began to work. [187]

It was a spatial mutiny. [257]
The rooms of the escondite were no longer obeying their floor plan; north had become a kind of distance, and the gallery had grown a second door. [284]

He walked the way the fragment pulled him, through a gallery where the ceiling had traded places with the stair. [304]
Intrigue had been his only companion for a century, and it served him now. [318]
He watched, he catalogued, he did not call out. [327]
Each room he passed was mid-argument with its own geometry, and he took notes the way he always had, because boredom had made him a scholar of small impossible things. [357]

At the fold he found Drevot waiting. [364]
They had parted badly a lifetime ago, when Drevot walked out of the vault and Aurel stayed, and Drevot had come back to see the wall come down. [392]
"The geometry is honest," Drevot said. "It only ever does what the fields tell it." [407]

"They never lie," Drevot answers, the tense shifting with the room, because the mutiny has left the floor plan. [447]
"Geometry never does. It is the only honest thing left." [457]
Drevot's aim was only to walk away upright, and Aurel's aim was the answer, and neither of them said the other's name again. [480]

Aurel stepped through first, and the fold closed on the room he had known for three centuries. [497]
He did not look back at it; looking back was how he had kept his goodbyes from ending. [515]
What greeted him was not a conquest but a rearrangement. [525]

He felt, for the first time in three hundred years, that his edges might belong to him again. [587]

That was the goodbye. [623]
This was after it. [627]

Aurel, who had been bored for three hundred years, was not bored now. [675]
He walked into the hush where the thunder keeps its voice low, and for the first time he did not know what was next. [699]
