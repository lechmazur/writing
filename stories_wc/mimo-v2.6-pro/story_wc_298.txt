Neme went down for a boy who had stopped dreaming. [10]
Lio was nine, and for three weeks he had woken with nothing behind his eyes, as if something had been scooped out of him in the night. [37]
She was a reckless idealist, and everyone in the parish said so, which was their way of saying she would go where sense forbade. [61]
The seamstress told her the truth outright: holes in the soul cannot be stitched shut from the outside. [79]
But the parish books named a place beneath the orchard where the dead were planted like rootstock, and where destiny could be bargained for. [103]
She went at dusk. [107]
The stair was cut into the hill, and the glow of half-lit corridors held her the whole way down, a weak amber from niches where fat still burned. [135]
Below, the catacomb orchard opened, and its trees grew out of the stacked dead. [149]
Roots snaked between the bones, threading ribs and jaws into the trunks, so that the wood carried the grain of somebody's femur. [171]
The orchard inlaid meaning everywhere; every apple bore a word set into its flesh like a chip of gold in old book letters, and the roots drank whatever was written above them. [203]
That was how the trade worked. [209]
You ate a fruit with a life written in it, and you got that life's worth of years, and the orchard kept your own name for its stock. [237]
Bartered destinies, the old book called it, and it warned that the honest price was always the piece of yourself you could least spare. [261]
Neme had seen what that meant in the stock-book: rows of names in a clerk's hand, each one traded away, each one now a branch. [286]
She found the tree she wanted by the letters set into the root flare: seven of them, one for each of Lio's years before the hole. [312]
She cut one apple down with her eating knife and bit into it in the dark, before she had read the word. [334]
Reckless, the parish would say. [339]
The fruit tasted of iron and rain, and something loosened in her chest and poured out of her like water from a cracked cup. [363]
When she lit her stub of candle to read it, the word was FORGIVE. [377]
She would have traded for a harsher one, but the orchard does not ask. [391]
She climbed back through the corridors within their glow, and at the last grate she wadded her wool scarf into the hinge and trammelled the gate shut behind her, so that nothing below could follow a bargain home. [429]
Then she went up to the boy, fiercely gentle, which was the only way she knew with people. [447]
Her hands were rough on the stone and soft when she pressed his cheek. [461]
Lio's dreams came back on the third night, thin and ordinary, and he did not notice how his aunt now stopped at doorways to listen to nothing. [488]
He did not notice that she could no longer say the word written on the apple that had bought him. [508]
It was FORGIVE, and the orchard had charged her the memory of who it was for. [524]
There was one thing left, and it was not hers to begin with. [537]
Before the descent she had laid the Star card over the boy's chest to scry the way, and the candle had taken its edge before she could pull it back. [567]
The figure that poured water from its two pitchers was gone at the shoulder now, and the smoke had gone into the weave. [590]
The Star is the card of what is replenished, and burning it was the orchard's receipt. [606]
That was the last of the trade, and the price of patching one hole was to leave another. [624]
She keeps it in her apron, the burned tarot card, where she can feel its charred corner whenever she is tempted to bargain again. [648]
