Mara had come back to the Thunder-echo range one season after the guardianship ended, carrying the failure like a stone in her chest. [23]
The canyons held their breath around her, a canyon hush so complete that each footstep felt like an intrusion. [42]
Snow had sealed the upper shelves, and the silence that poured down the slopes was avalanchingly quiet, the kind of quiet that buries sound the way white buries stone. [71]
She set down her pack on a ledge of driftwood-strewn sand and knelt, because the work ahead asked for humility she had not yet earned. [96]
The relic keeper's trade was not worship; it was maintenance of the seams between worlds, and the seams had torn under her watch. [119]
A village on the far shelf had gone silent in the crossing, and though the council had forgiven her, she had not come here for forgiveness. [145]
She had come to harvest insights from failure, to sift the wreckage until it yielded something usable, the way a river silt yields gold. [169]
First she gathered driftwood, bleached and salted by storms that no longer came this high, and arranged it as directional markers along the canyon floor. [194]
Each branch pointed toward a place where the worlds leaned close: one toward the mirror-bright realm of the Drowned Choir, one toward the ash realm of the Second Silence, and one back toward the ordinary shelves she had sworn to protect. [235]
The markers kept her from drifting sideways when the flute began its work, for weaving between worlds was less like walking and more like being pulled through a needle. [264]
Then she uncased the gilded reed flute, and the canyon hush deepened as if the instrument had drawn the last stray noise into itself. [288]
Its body was hollow and thin as breath, its gold leaf flaking in the cold, and the tuning holes wore smooth dimples from three generations of keepers before her. [317]
She did not play it, not yet; she held it to the wind and listened for the fault line she remembered. [338]
It came as a change in pressure, a thinning of the air above the second marker, where the driftwood lay at an angle that meant the worlds were about to kiss. [369]
Last time she had forced the crossing at the wrong moment, and the seam had snapped back like a jaw, taking forty voices with it. [394]
This time she played soft, a single held note that rose and fell in the narrow band the old manuals called the threading tone. [418]
The worlds opened along the driftwood lines, and she stepped through not as a conqueror but as a witness, cataloguing every fracture. [440]
She found the Drowned Choir singing in reverse, their music running backward toward the moment before the loss, and she heard exactly where her own breath had been too eager. [470]
In the ash realm she wrote the correction into the gilded body with a needle of bone, a small notch that would warn the next keeper against haste. [498]
When she returned to the canyon floor the driftwood had shifted, and she spent the last hour arranging it again, because directions decay when no one is watching. [526]
The Thunder-echo range gave up its name in a single low roll of sound that came from nowhere and everywhere, then folded back into silence. [551]
She uncased nothing further that day, only packed the flute against her ribs where the heartbeat could keep it warm. [571]
By dusk the wind had erased one of her markers, the one pointing home, and she let it go. [590]
Home was a word for people who had not stood between worlds; for a relic keeper there was only the hush and the work that the hush made possible. [619]
She wrote the night's findings on oilcloth in the small cramped hand that future keepers would have to learn to read by lamplight. [642]
Failure, she wrote, is only an unsorted harvest; the grain is there if you are willing to kneel in the chaff. [663]
Below her the canyon opened its long gray mouth and said nothing, and she took that for agreement. [681]
At dawn she rose, shoulders aching, and walked out along the driftwood line that pointed toward the ordinary shelves, leaving the other two markers standing like quiet priests at their posts. [712]
Behind her the Thunder-echo range kept its name in reserve, saving it for the next storm, the next keeper, the next slow work of mending what cannot be held. [741]
