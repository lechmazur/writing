The lighthouse orchard clung to the black basalt shelf above the shifting ice floes, its apple trees bent seaward by a hundred winters of wind. [25]
Down there the floes ground and parted like slow thoughts, and the water between them held a light that belonged to no hour. [48]
Kesh stood among the trees as the delegation waited below, and the question they had asked him still hung in the air, unanswered. [71]
He was a shadow-laced peacekeeper, the dark threads in his coat stitched from the very silences he had been sworn to keep, and they moved when he moved, slower than he did, like a second weather. [107]
In his left hand he held the broken crystal ball fragment, a thumb-sized shard of old glass with a frozen storm trapped inside its curve. [132]
The rest of the ball lay scattered somewhere in the world, and until it was whole again, no answer he gave could be honest. [156]
So he stood in the weighted breath before reply, that held pause in which a person decides what kind of person they are. [179]
The orchard's keeper, an old woman named Vess, had told him years ago that the lighthouse had once been a shop. [200]
Its cellar still held the vintage store, its shelves crowded with mirrors that nobody had polished since the ice came. [220]
Kesh went down the winding stair, each step sounding like a coin dropped into deep water. [236]
The mirrors knew him before he entered; each glass showed the room slightly wrong, and in the wrongness were the pieces he needed. [259]
In a foxed oval above the coat hooks, he glimpsed a shard lodged in the hull of a fishing boat two hundred miles north. [283]
In a leaning pier glass he saw one pressed into the mud of a river that ran under a city he had never visited. [307]
A shaving mirror no bigger than his palm showed him a third, carried in the pocket of a child who was walking toward the floes with a lantern. [335]
He raised the fragment, and the mirrors answered one another, silver to silver, passing the shard's cold light from glass to glass until the cellar rang with it. [363]
The shard in the boat loosened and slid through its own reflection; the shard in the river rose out of the mud like a bubble of clear weather. [391]
The child's shard leapt from the pocket and flew, because some things in the world still answer to courage. [410]
They came through the mirrors like light through a sentence, and Kesh, calmly intuitive, knew without counting that the sphere would close where the storm was thickest. [437]
He caught each piece in his shadow-laced hand and turned it once against the glass, and the seams began to reconstitute themselves. [459]
The crystal ball whole again showed him what a fragment never could: the delegation on the ice, not as enemies but as people whose fear had been taught to them by the cold. [492]
He saw, too, the child with the lantern, and understood that the courageous anomaly was not the flying glass but the small body walking out onto the floes to say what the adults would not. [527]
Kesh climbed back through the lighthouse with the sphere warm in his coat, and the orchard's wind had not moved at all. [549]
He had left on one breath and returned on the same one; the weighted breath before reply still held, unspent, over the ice. [572]
Below, the delegation shifted their weight from foot to foot, and the floes shifted with them, and the child's lantern was a small star on the moving white. [600]
Kesh answered then, and his answer was a map: he named the crossings where the ice was safe, the well each family could draw from, the road the child should take home. [632]
Empathy, he had learned, is only a rumor until it is drawn in ink and handed across the water. [651]
That is how you map empathy onto action: you show people the road before you ask them to walk it, and you walk it first. [676]
The delegation took the map, and the floes, as if they had heard, arranged themselves into the shapes he had drawn. [697]
The child came up the shelf with the lantern unlit, because the light had gone out of its purpose and into the world. [720]
Kesh knelt and gave the child the crystal ball, which was not a fortune now but a way of seeing the person in front of you. [746]
Above them the lighthouse turned its slow beam over the orchard and the water, and the shadow laced through Kesh's coat lay quiet at last on the roots of an apple tree. [778]
Nothing on the ice moved for a while, which in that place was the same as peace. [795]
