Velin had been a lost soul collector for eleven years, and she was modestly fearless about it, which meant the fear never left her but sat politely in the corner of her chest while she worked. [36] Her harp was a wooden frame strung with one crocodile's tooth, the great yellow fang of a river beast traded to her mother by a fisherman who had owed a debt to the dead, and because the tooth was never meant to sit under string tension every note the harp gave was misaligned, flat in the bass and screaming in the treble. [98] Those distorted notes were the only sound a wandering soul could hear in Echo Gorge, where the stone walls folded sound back on itself until a whisper became a choir and a choir became nothing at all. [135] She had come in the hush of a hidden sunrise, the one hour when the sun cleared the eastern rim but its light had not yet found the floor of the gorge, so the world glowed overhead and stayed blue below, like a wound seen through clean water. [183] Velin strummed, and the tooth answered wrong as it always did, and from the third bend upriver a shape began to follow the noise. [207] Souls were shy of everything except their own confessions. [216] Tell a ghost what it did and it would walk toward you forever. [229] So Velin pursued the way she always pursued, not by running but by remembering aloud. [244] "You were the ferryman's daughter," she called to the shape, "and you let the boat go out with one seat empty." [265] The soul stopped. [268] A spectral confession came back along the wall, doubled and redoubled by the stone until it arrived as a whole village agreeing. [290] "I kept the seat for my brother," the soul said, "and he never came, and the water took the crossing anyway." [311] Velin folded the confession into the harp's tuning pegs, which was where she kept every confession she had ever gathered, each peg wound tight with a grief too heavy to carry in the air. [345] Then came the part her mother had taught her, the part that had nothing to do with collecting. [363] Velin hummed the funeral song her mother had sung over river burials, and then she sang it wrong on purpose, up a third, with a hiccup in the middle, turning the dirge into something a little foolish and a little sweet. [404] Laughter was the only medicine that reached the dead, because the dead had been robbed of it and had not yet noticed. [426] When a soul laughed, the gorge took the sound and bent it warmer, and the ache in the stone turned luminous for a moment, gold running down the west wall where no gold had ever been. [462] The ferryman's daughter laughed once, twice, and stepped into the light that was not there. [477] Three more souls came before the sunrise thinned. [485] A drowned tax collector who confessed to counting the wrong dead. [496] A child who confessed to being afraid of the dark and then, in a smaller voice, to being afraid of the light too. [519] A stonecutter who confessed that he had carved his own name on a stranger's grave because he wanted the town to have spelled him right at least once. [547] Velin gave each of them the wrong funeral song and each of them laughed and each of them went. [566] But the last sound in the gorge was not a ghost's. [577] It was her own voice, running the wall back to her, saying the thing she had not said into the harp. [598] Velin had one confession the crocodile's tooth could not hold, and it was this: she wanted to hear her mother's voice again, badly enough to keep a few souls close so the gorge would have something to echo. [636] That was the ache under the luminous morning, the reason she kept collecting when she could have stopped. [654] "I kept the seat empty too," she said to the gorge. [665] The harp answered with its crooked tooth-note, flat and then sharp, and somewhere below the water a mother laughed, once and then not at all. [690] Velin pursued that laugh down the last bend of Echo Gorge, modestly fearless, in the last of the hidden sunrise. [710] And the harp kept singing wrong, which was how she knew she was getting closer. [725]</story>
