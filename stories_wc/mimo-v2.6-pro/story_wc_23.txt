The hydraulics failed on a Tuesday, and by Friday the channel was a mirror. [14] The floodgates hung half-raised at the mouth, useless as broken teeth, and the pumps under the tide house had gone silent for good. [37] With nothing left to push against, every boat that once ran inland turned its bow seaward, and the traffic on our charts reversed itself overnight. [62] We called it reverse migration, and it was my job to plot it. [75] I worked the starlit signal station on the breakwater, a shack of salt-eaten plank and candle tin where the lanterns stayed lit because the grid died with the pumps. [104] Mara was the patchwork navigator, and she had sewn her coat from signal flags and blanket squares so that she wore the whole coastline in scraps. [130] She balanced every chart against a patina bronze censer foot she had taken from a coastal chapel two years before. [150] It was a small heavy thing, the severed base of an incense stand, and its surface fluctuates with moon phases. [170] At full moon the patina bloomed white and the ships ran true; at the wane it went copper and green, and the routes she drew bent like old hoses. [199] Tonight the moon was a coin rubbed thin, and the censer foot had gone the color of a healing bruise. [219] At midnight the satphone rang in the instrument shed, and Mara answered it alone. [233] The callers reached her through cryptic phone calls at midnight, never the other way, and their voices came out of the encrypted line as coordinates and weather and the names of the dead. [266] She would write it all on the back of a tide table, then tell me at breakfast which way the fleet had gone. [289] Because the callers could only reach us, Mara decided to build. [300] Over the next three nights she raised a relay tower of scrap antennae and copper wire and the insides of dead radios. [322] I held the ladder and hauled the lengths of pipe, and she climbed with a hammer in her teeth like a figurehead that had learned to move. [349] It is not superstition to say the tower mattered; it is geometry. [361] A signal thrown from the flat coast falls into the sea; a signal thrown from height clears the water and reaches the boats turning back. [386] What she was really charting was the geography of loss. [396] Her brother had drowned in the channel in the last week the hydraulics still worked, and the boats that now fled inland water were running the same way he had. [426] She did not want to stop them. [433] She wanted to know where they were going so she could follow the shape of it. [449] On the fourth night the tower stood tall enough, and the line rang at midnight as it always did. [468] This time when Mara answered, her voice went out across the water through our own antennae, and the whole reverse fleet heard her read the safe channel back to them. [498] For an hour the lantern room was only her talking to the dark and the dark answering in engines. [517] I have heard people call that sound lonely. [525] It was not. [528] It was the closest thing to solace I know, raw as an unbandaged hand, a room full of stars and one woman telling strangers how to get home. [556] When the last boat cleared the shoals she set the censer foot down on the chart and pressed her thumb into the bruised green of it. [582] Then she began to pack. [587] Reverse migration meant running away from where you belonged, and now that the traffic was charted she meant to join it. [608] She left at dawn with the coat on her back and the censer foot wrapped in a rag in her pocket, and she took nothing else. [634] I kept the tower. [638] It stands on the breakwater still, throwing signal past the coast, and on the nights the moon is thin the line rings at midnight for me. [664] I answer, and I do not know whose voice guides the fleets now, only that the geography has changed and someone is still reading it aloud to the boats that turn back toward the sea. [699] That is enough. [702]
