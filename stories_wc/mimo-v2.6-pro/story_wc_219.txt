Sera had spent eleven years proving that light could lie, and the field had rewarded her with a reputation she now carried like a bruise. [25] She was, in the polite shorthand of her colleagues, a luminous skeptic: the woman who had dismantled four lumen-ghost cults and published the arithmetic of each collapse. [52] Then the signal came, and three months later her hands would not stop glowing. [66] It was not a metaphor and it was not a fraud; it was a faint gold phosphorescence that no solvent washed away, and it deepened whenever she was homesick for a place she had never lived. [102] So she booked passage on an artificial pilgrimage, the way other people book a train. [117] Twice a year the freight consortium strung its carriers with cheap paper garlands and sold the empty berths to anyone who wanted to arrive somewhere holy without walking there first. [147] The destination was always the same rainwashed tower in spring, the relay station where humanity had made first contact and then, with characteristic haste, turned the parking lot into a shrine. [178] Sera did not go to believe. [184] She went to understand the chemistry of homecoming, because homesickness with a measurable spectrum was the only kind she was willing to trust. [207] In her coat pocket she carried the fly amber. [216] It was a bead of Cretaceous resin no larger than a thumbnail, with a single gnat suspended inside it, wings still half-open as if the resin had interrupted a decision. [246] She had carried it into every lecture hall for a decade, a reminder that preservation is just chemistry catching something in the act. [269] The tower rose from a hillside of sculpture that the weather had been quietly reclaiming for forty years. [287] Ferns had opened the seams of the plinths, and moss had softened every hard bronze edge into the color of old instruments. [309] The path up was a gallery of overgrown figures, and the rain that morning had left the dust between them soft and unbroken. [332] That was how she found her way, and how she found him: by dusty footprints left on an overgrown sculpture path. [353] Her own prints came up from the pilgrim gate; his came down from the tower road, and at the base of the last statue the two sets converged into one muddy trample. [385] He was already standing there, older than her by half a lifetime, with the same faint light under his fingernails. [405] "You came by the prints," he said, as though confirming a step in a procedure. [420] "Everyone does." [422] "The dust keeps better records than the institute," Sera said. [432] "I am not here for a miracle." [439] "Good," he said. "The last one brought a chisel." [448] His name was Adem, and he had been measuring the glow for two years without publishing a single line. [467] He had been too academically rebellious for the institute that funded him, so he kept his data in a field notebook the color of the moss. [493] They sat on the plinth, and Sera took out the fly amber, and the afternoon went bronze quiet around them. [513] "Look at the gnat," she said. "It has been caught mid-wingbeat for a hundred million years and it never glowed." [533] Adem held the bead up to the rainlight and went very still. [545] "It is glowing now," he said. [550] Sera looked, and the resin had taken on the same faint gold that lived under her skin, and her hands answered it like a struck tuning fork. [577] It was not a haunting and it was not a god; it was resonance. [591] Whatever the signal had tuned human tissue to, the same lattice of carbon and hydrogen sat in that bead, and the two of them were speaking the only language chemistry permits. [622] Homecoming, she understood at last, was not a place. [631] It was a reaction that began when the body recognized a molecule it had always been made of, and it did not care whether that happened in a cathedral or in a bead of resin older than grass. [669] She wrote it in Adem's notebook in her worst handwriting, because the institute would have to wait for the proof and she was no longer willing to. [696] Below them the pilgrims were coming up the sculpture path in a thin, ordinary line, their prints darkening the dust one after another. [719] None of them knew what they were carrying, and all of them were carrying it. [734] Sera put the fly amber back in her pocket, where it kept its small gold weather against her ribs. [753] She did not glow any less, but for the first time in three months the glow felt like chemistry instead of a claim. [776]
