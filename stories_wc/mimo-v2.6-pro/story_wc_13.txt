Nia was a natural history illustrator by trade, though her real medium was harder to name. [16] The truth of it: she sculpts time itself, working minutes the way other illustrators worked ink. [32] Around her throat hung an infinity symbol pendant, a sideways figure-eight of blackened silver. [46] Borrowed time poured into its upper loop and spent time drained from its lower one. [61] She got the currency by borrowing time from the giant who was sleeping beneath the city. [77] The rooftop canal system weaving between towers was in truth the water that ran along his knuckles. [94] Each tower was a finger, and the towers went up out of sight. [107]

Her commission came from the Cartographers' Guild, who wanted her to find the place where echoes are born. [125] They wanted it on paper, with shading and a scale bar, before the giant woke. [140] When he woke, the canals flooded and the whole commission went under. [152] So she worked fast and she worked on borrowed hours. [162]

But there was a problem with her hand, and the problem had a name: interrupted cadence. [178] Every stroke she set down came out a half beat off the one before it. [193] Her breathing broke against her ribs the same way. [202] She would start a hatch of fine lines and the lines would stutter and refuse their rhythm, and she would have to stop, wipe the plate, and begin again. [231] Three times that morning she lost an hour to it. [241] She tried borrowing time from a gull hunched on the parapet and pressed an extra nine minutes into her wrist, but the cadence only skipped harder. [267] The gull lifted off resentful and two minutes younger. [276]

The canal took her best plate at noon. [284] A gust off the giant's slow exhale tipped the skiff, and her sketch of the knuckle-locks slid into the green water and went down. [308] She went after it without deciding to. [315] The cold closed over her head and the light came apart into long green bars. [330] Down there she heard it: the echo, or the making of one. [342] It was not a sound the water made; it was the giant's snore striking the pendant at her throat and ringing back through the silver. [367] The place where echoes are born was not a place at all; it was a piece of metal on a chain. [388] That was what the Guild would never have accepted, and it was the only true thing she had found down there besides her plate. [412] She surfaced with both. [416]

The giant's nap was ending; she could feel it in the water. [428] His breathing had turned, and the canals were already climbing the tower stairs. [441] All of this had happened across a giant's nap, and now the nap was a minute long. [458] Nia understood then what the interrupted cadence was telling her. [468] She had been taking minutes from a heart that was keeping time for an entire city, and every stroke she stole put the city a half beat off. [496] Her hand stuttered because the world was stuttering under it. [506]

So she did the only thing that would put the rhythm back. [518] She held the pendant over the water and uncaged every hour she had ever borrowed. [533] It came out as light, and the light went down into the giant like water into sand. [550] The pendant's loops went slack and the silver went dull and warm. [562] His breathing steadied. [565] The canals stopped climbing and settled back between the towers, and somewhere below a hundred doors opened at once. [584] Her cadence came back with it: even strokes, even breath, one line after another in their proper measure. [602]

She had no time left to sculpt and no interest in getting more. [615] The Guild would refuse the work anyway, since she had drawn them a sound instead of a coordinate. [633] She finished the plate on the parapet while the giant dreamed his last minute and the canals ran clear. [652] It showed the blackened silver of the pendant, the loops open, and the rings of sound coming off it into the water. [674] Under it, in her smallest hand, she wrote the caption the Guild had asked for. [689] "The place where echoes are born," it read. [697] "Ask me again when he wakes." [703] Then she went down the tower stairs with empty pockets and a finished drawing, and the water went with her, step for step. [726]
