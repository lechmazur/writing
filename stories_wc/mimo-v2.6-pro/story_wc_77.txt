The resuscitation left Rennard with a hairline crack in his memory and a voice sewn to the underside of his hearing. [21]
He came back to himself on the cracked asphalt of the midway, and above him the abandoned carnival funhouse stood with its mirrors blind and its doors held shut by rust. [52]
The voice, which he later named Sourquil, whispered that he had died of fright and been sent back to learn what fright was. [75]
Around his throat lay an opera cloak clasp he did not remember fastening: a tarnished sunburst mask whose tiny eyes were set with mirrored glass, warm as a fingertip, and it was from that clasp that the whispering came. [114]
Rennard's aim was narrow and merciless: to trick fear into revealing its weakness, so that never again could it stop his heart mid-step in some black mirrored hall. [142]
Sourquil approved, and the clasp grew a shade warmer, the way a bruise rewards a pressing thumb with that sickly-sweet throb of pleasure. [165]
Inside, the funhouse gave him lingering luminance: fluorescent tubes dying behind glass panels, each one bleeding a pallid glow that never quite finished fading, so the halls stayed lit by ghosts of light. [198]
The mirrors threw his reflection back at wrong angles, and when he raised the borrowed camera to his face, the flash fired and left a plate that held only the room behind him. [231]
That was the curse the resuscitation had hung on him: he was photographically cursed, his face fogging silver in every exposure like breath on a cold window that refuses to clear. [262]
Sourquil told him that a man who cannot be pictured is a man fear cannot frame, and that the missing face was an advantage to be spent. [289]
In the coat he had worn since the ambulance, Rennard found a water-stained dream dictionary, its margins crowded with somebody's small bitter handwriting. [312]
Every entry in it was written backwards: teeth meant safety, falling meant landing, and the dark meant company. [330]
Sourquil had him read the paradoxical notes aloud until the funhouse answered in the same inverted grammar, because a dream dictionary that lies in the right direction is a map to the truth behind the lie. [366]
Under the entry for mirror the margin read: the thing you show it is the thing it takes, so Rennard stopped showing it his face and let the plate go empty. [397]
Under the entry for fear the margin read: look at it and it hides, so he turned the flashlight on himself instead of on the dark. [423]
The light went into his chest and opened there like a black flower, and in the hollow behind it something began to unspool, a coiled thing with a name he already knew. [455]
It showed him the night this same funhouse had stopped his heart mid-step while the queue outside laughed at some stranger's joke, and it showed him how fear had done it by borrowing his own voice to scream. [493]
That was the weakness, and it was hideous and simple: fear needs a voice that is not its own, and so it can be tricked out of the one it borrows. [524]
Sourquil's whisper rose to something almost tender, as if a mouth pressed close to the dictionary's margin were leaning in to kiss the ear it was ruining. [551]
Rennard understood then that the voice itself was the borrowed scream, twenty-six years old, the same one that had killed him and been handed back on the resuscitation table. [580]
To give it away he had to unbosom: to stand in the mirror hall with the lamps dying in their glass and say the thing he had never once said aloud. [611]
Under the entry for breath the margin gave him its last inversion: speak and you keep it, so he opened the clasp at his throat instead. [637]
The funhouse answered by going finally, luxuriously dark, and in that dark the mirrors began to keep their images instead of handing them back. [661]
He pinned the opera cloak clasp through the fogged plate of the last photograph, and the mirrored eyes of the little mask drank the pallid luminance down to nothing. [690]
Sourquil tried to speak and found the throat it lived in emptied, because what Rennard had to unbosom was not a confession but a sentence: the voice was not his, and he was giving it back. [726]
Fear went with it the way a dog goes when you stop pretending to be afraid of it, and Rennard laughed at the sound of his own silence. [754]
Twenty-six years after the funhouse had killed him, he walked out onto the midway, and the photograph he left behind showed his face at last: plain, and washed in a light that stayed. [787]
