Beyond the murmur of lost languages, where no tongue alive could name a single tree, a crystalline orchard perched at the edge of a shimmering crater. [26]
There the marsh-reed sculptor came each morning with a bundle of green stems and a splint of tendon, and there she worked until the light bent. [52]
Her name was Ivri, and she had come to the orchard to graft herself onto community, because a life bent over reeds alone leaves the hands clever and the heart stupid. [83]
At the orchard's center stood the glimmering obsidian arbor, a black lattice that the old growers said had sprouted from the crater in a single night and had never once been pruned. [115]
Its branches threw a web of black shadows across the ice-glass fruit, and the children called those shadows webs of kinship, because wherever two shadows crossed, two families had once clasped hands. [147]
She had carved marsh birds, river masks, and funeral crowns for strangers who praised her hands and never learned her name. [168]
Ivri wanted her own shadow to cross another's under that arbor, and she waited for the hour when the orchard would give her a reason to stay. [195]
She did not want to be praised; she wanted to be recognized as someone who would show up when the roof leaked. [217]
The reason came as a boy named Halke, who burst through the sugar-frost rows with his apron full of chalk and his eyes full of fury. [243]
Someone had tagged the far wall of the orchard with a mark none of them knew, and the elders had ordered it scrubbed away before the harvest judges arrived. [272]
"It is not a scar," Halke said. "It is a signature. You do not erase a person because you cannot read him." [294]
Ivri, boldly sympathetic to any hand the world had taught to flinch, set down her reeds and knelt beside him. [314]
"Then we find the artist," she said, "and we ask them to write it where the elders cannot be ashamed of it." [336]
So began their shared search for an elusive street artist's tag, a search that carried them out of the crystalline orchard and down the switchback trail toward the shimmering crater. [366]
Halke ran ahead and Ivri followed, and though she was older and slower, she did not once tell him to wait. [387]
At every rusted milestone a fresh mark appeared, always the same three strokes, always just past the reach of morning. [407]
Ivri read them the way she read reeds: by their flex, their nerve, the small hesitation where a line gives up and admits it is lonely. [433]
The marks led them to the crater's rim at dusk, where the light rose up out of the pit like steam and made their faces shine without warmth. [461]
There, on a shelf of crystal no wider than a pallet, they found the artist: a woman with paint under her nails and old lost syllables on her tongue. [490]
She had been speaking in a language nobody had heard for two hundred years, which is why no one had answered her when she called for help. [517]
Halke, who could not read the words she sprayed, understood the hunger in them perfectly. [532]
"You have been writing your name on the world because the world kept forgetting it," he said. [549]
"Come up to the orchard and write it where we can all say it back." [564]
The artist wept a little and agreed, and Ivri carried her on her back along the switchbacks, the paint drying in stripes on both of them. [590]
That night, under the glimmering obsidian arbor, the whole orchard gathered to dedicate the new wall. [606]
Ivri set her finest sculpture at the base: seven reeds braided into a ladder, so that anyone who wanted to rise could put a hand on it. [633]
Then she stepped back and let the artist write her name in three strokes across the ice-glass, in letters large enough to be seen from the crater's other rim. [662]
The shadows of the arbor fell across the fresh paint and crossed, and crossed again. [677]
Ivri felt her own shadow join the lattice at last, and the ache in her chest shone so brightly she had to close her eyes. [702]
Beyond the murmur of lost languages, the orchard had found a word for her, and it sounded like every hand clapping at once. [725]
