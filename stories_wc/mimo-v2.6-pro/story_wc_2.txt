Orlen had spent thirty years rebuilding clocks in Ashfell, a town that handed him its broken things and trusted him to find the tick again. [25]
What he was bad at was planning, so that winter he decided to befriend uncertainty rather than master it. [44]
He kept no schedule beyond the sunrise and no ledger beyond what the bench demanded, and he told anyone who asked that the work taught him. [70]

In February the mayor brought him a clock that had stopped dead under a coat of ash, and inside the case Orlen found a folded chart. [96]
The chart showed the coast before the harbor was built, with signal stones marked where the lighthouse now stands. [115]
Beneath the ashes of each drawn signal fire the cartographer had written a small x, and beneath the third x the ink read that the admiral's lost compass rose had last been seen in the greenhouse. [151]
Orlen did not trust the chart, but he dressed for rain and walked out past the mill to the ruined conservatory at the edge of the ashfields. [178]
Its glass was gone and its iron ribs held only wind, and he waded through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse toward the far potting bench. [205]
Under a cracked seed tray lay the admiral's lost compass rose, brass and green with age, its pivot stiff and its needle long gone. [229]
He stood there uncertainly certain that the thing mattered, though not how, and put it in his coat. [247]

At the crossroads below Ashfell he met a farmer named Brann who was mending a fence post with tar and old rope. [269]
**Follow the dry creek to the limestone steps, then down until the sound of water takes over your hearing, and come back before dark, because those caves have taken two men and one of my dogs.** [305]
Orlen asked whether the creek had a plan for where it ran, and Brann shrugged: **Plans are weather.** [323]
That suited Orlen, who had stopped making plans and started making room for whatever arrived. [338]

He found the dry creek, then the limestone steps, and climbed down into the dark with a shuttered lantern held before him. [360]
Fifty steps in, the ceiling opened and the air turned cold and loud. [373]
He raised the shutter onto a subterranean waterfall that fell the height of the Ashfell church tower into a black basin. [394]
Spray hung in the lantern beam like dust, and behind the curtain of water he saw the arch of a low passage. [416]
Beneath the ashes of a collapsed ceiling inside that passage lay a small brass box, and in the box lay a magnetized sliver of steel no longer than his thumbnail. [446]
Now the coincidence hardened into pattern: the chart, the box beneath the ashes, the signal stones beneath the ashes of three drawn fires. [469]
Orlen took it, because the pattern was the only map he had, and the pattern wanted him to fix a compass. [490]
At the basin he improvised the repair, since to improvise was what remained when planning failed and the water was loud enough to drown doubt. [515]
He unscrewed the compass rose's broken pivot with the needle file from his vest, set the steel sliver into the empty mount, and balanced it on a flat stone in the spray. [547]
He was certain the pivot would still swing and uncertain that the needle would hold true, and he let that doubled feeling sit in his chest like a companion rather than a flaw. [580]
The needle turned once, twice, and steadied on the black mouth of the passage. [594]
Orlen followed it through the falls, his boots filling with cold water, and came out on the hill above the harbor as the light went grey. [620]

He worked the compass rose's broken pivot one more time on the greenhouse bench, where the leaves still spun, and set it in the mayor's hands that evening. [648]
The needle pointed at the door and the mayor called it a fine piece of work, and Orlen did not correct him. [670]

That night he sat in the public house with the fogged window behind him and no plan for the morning. [690]
Brann came in with tar still on his hands and sat across from him without asking. [706]
**You found your water, then.** [711]
Orlen said he had found the waterfall, and the box beneath the ashes, and something in himself he had been calling a fault until that afternoon. [737]
He said he had gone down uncertainly certain and come up only certain, and that the difference was the whole of what the admiral had lost. [763]
**Then keep it, because a compass that only tells you where to stand is a heavy coin.** [780]
Orlen folded no plans that night, and the coin in his pocket felt light enough to spend. [797]
