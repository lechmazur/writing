When the sky was wrong, Orrin climbed. [7] He was a chivalrous thief, which meant he robbed only men who could spare it and left a receipt. [26] Tonight his mark was Talin the glasswright, who had carved forty years into the icebound spire above the clouds and sold every secret but one. [51] Orrin kept a pocket sundial in his coat, and the first thing he did on the fifth landing was open its lid and check the light. [77] The gnomon cast no shadow at all, which was the wrongest thing a sundial could do. [93] He shut the lid and went up. [100] Talin's staircase was an argument the old man had been having with himself for decades. [115] It rose in thirds and then in fourths, and every fourth tread was carved like a chessboard. [132] Four locks stood on it, and each had been a chess piece until Talin had found the secret inside it and hollowed it out. [156] Orrin had carved four pieces of his own to replace them. [167] Swapping was the work of a careful man: hand-carved chess pieces with secrets inside, traded for the ones already there. [187] He did not steal. [191] He amended. [193] The first lock was a porphyry rook whose hollow held a vow written in Talin's own hand: to serve the known, and never to reach for what had not been measured. [224] Orrin set his own rook beside it, a porphyry twin with the same vow inside, but with the last clause scratched through and a new one carved beneath: the door is yours to open when you choose. [261] The second lock was an iron knight holding a promise: never to surrender to the unknown. [277] Inside his own iron knight Orrin left a map of the same unknown, drawn badly and honestly, with a note at the edge: the surrender is mutual. [304] The third was a lapis bishop that recorded every atelier Talin had ruined pursuing a glass that would hold the sky. [325] Orrin wrote inside his bishop: not one of them was empty when you left it. [340] The fourth and last was a felted pawn with a poem in its hollow, Talin's midnight confession that he had built the spire out of fear and called it craft. [370] Orrin matched it with a felted pawn of his own, and inside he wrote only: it is also a castle, and wonder is a tenant you may evict. [398] Then he climbed the last flight, with the abandon of a man who had done his careful work and could now afford to be careless. [423] At the top there was a workshop of black glass and hoarfrost, and no lamps, because the glass was the sky. [444] Talin sat inside it, an old man with chipped hands, watching a wrongness he had spent his life failing to capture. [465] The vault overhead was painted midnight at noon and dawn at midnight; the constellations had wandered, and the sun had a shadow that pointed the wrong way. [492] Orrin checked his pocket sundial once more and saw the gnomon now cast a shadow, and that it cast it backwards. [513] You swapped my pieces, Talin said without turning. [521] I amend, said Orrin. [525] The proof of it is that you may go down and read them all again and find your argument improved. [545] Then the thief reached into his coat and drew out the pocket sundial and cracked its lid like a small dawn. [566] The lid's underside was painted with the wrong sky itself: midnight ground into brass, gnomon fitted as the key that turned it. [588] When he set it on the bench, the wrongness in the vault above leaned down and poured itself into that small bright mouth. [611] The constellations shuffled home, the sun straightened its shadow, and Orrin found himself what he had never been: an apprentice of wonder, still learning. [635] Talin looked at the four carved pieces in the thief's hand, and at the four he had left behind on the stair, and laughed at the thinness of the lock he had trusted most: the habit of caution. [673] They ate bread at the bench and argued about nothing for an hour, which is the sacredly playful part of any trade worth doing. [697] When Orrin left, the sky was right again and he felt cheated of the strangeness he had climbed for. [716] So he stood on the fifth landing a while, the sundial still ticking in his coat, and decided he would climb again the next time the sky went wrong, with open arms. [748]
