Ilsa was a sensory map maker, the last one still working out of the old port. [16] She worked in an abandoned spaceport repair bay where the gantry arms hung rust-stiff over an empty service pit. [35] Her instrument was a green glass shard, flat on one side and curved on the other, warm no matter the hour. [56] She pressed it to her palm and the world rendered itself as texture and residual sound, a way of charting what a place felt like rather than what it measured. [86] Tonight the shard showed her a seam running down the middle of the repair bay. [101] It was the murmur of half-remembered dreams, the hour when parallel universes sat close enough to touch and memory ran thin. [122]

Weaving between worlds was a craft she had learned from her mother's charts, thin sheets of vellum marked with fingerprints instead of lines. [145] The crossings were cyclically spontaneous, arriving on no schedule she could predict and yet returning again and again, which was why she could chase them and why she was never ready. [176] She stepped through the seam and came out in a repair bay where the work had never stopped. [194] Overhead lamps burned, a shuttle cooled on its cradle, and someone had left a mug of tea on the bench with steam still curling off it. [220] The shard in her palm read the room as salt, copper, and a low vibration that might have been a voice. [241] On the bench her mother's toolkit sat open, and Ilsa felt the lie settle into its shape. [258] She had been told her mother died in this bay, crushed under a gantry arm on a night of bad weather. [279] But the toolkit was oiled and sorted, and this world's repair bay hummed on without grief. [295]

The seam pulled at her before she was done, cyclically and without warning, and dragged her back through the bay door. [316] She landed in a version of the port stripped to its bones, every plate sold, every gantry arm gone. [335] The green glass shard had a hairline crack now, and the texture it gave her was thin and reedy. [354] She had come here many times chasing the weight of a withheld truth, and found nothing, because the crossings did not go where she wanted. [379] They went where the fabric was thin, and the fabric was thin wherever the truth had been left out of the record. [401] So she stopped waiting for the crossings to carry her and began to redirect them. [416]

She used the shard like a blade, pressing its curved edge to the seam and dragging it sideways through the parallel universes until the pull bent toward her. [444] It worked, and it cost her: the crack in the glass lengthened and the bay's floor tilted under her feet. [464] The door opened onto the night of the bad weather, and the gantry arm was up, and her mother stood under it with a bag packed. [490] Rain came in sideways through the broken roof, and she could hear the shuttle that would not come back. [509] Her mother looked at the seam in the air where Ilsa stood and did not blink. [525] She had done this before, and she knew what it cost to leave a daughter with a story instead of a fact. [547] The shard gave Ilsa the weight of it, heavier than the accident, heavier than grief: she had chosen to go. [567] The withheld truth had a mass all its own, and she was holding it. [581]

She did not cross the threshold to her mother's side. [591] Instead she redirected the seam one last time and let it fold her back into her own repair bay, where the lamps were dead and the gantry arms hung rust-stiff. [621] The green glass shard was only a shard now, cracked through, and it read nothing. [636] Ilsa set it on the bench beside her own toolkit and found she did not need it. [653] Her hands knew the bay by touch: the cold of the pit wall, the grain of the floor, the way sound died near the service hatch. [679] She began the map she had come here to make, not of the worlds but of this one, and its weight was nothing like the one she had been carrying. [709] Here, in the bay that is hers, the map is light enough to carry at last. [725]
