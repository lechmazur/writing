Wren was the son of a highway robber, and he had inherited not the knife but the road. [18] His mother had worked the county's only service road, and when she died the county let him keep the toll. [38] He kept it in an abandoned circus train at the west siding, three rusted cars where acrobats once slept, now stacked with cones, a generator, and the collection of foreign paper money he had taken off a traveler who had tried to cross without paying. [83] The notes were small and colored and came from countries he would never see, so he spread them on the rusted floor at dusk and read them like a map of other roads. [116] Wren was perceptively calm, the kind of calm that listens before it answers. [129] When the startup came, he did not raise his voice. [139] Tollway Foundry had four founders, a white van, and a plan to put every county road under satellite billing. [158] They pulled into the siding at dusk in the spring of their first raise, when the money was easy and the road ahead looked straight. [183] Their manager, tall and tired around the eyes, stepped down and asked where the road went. [199] Wren offered him tea on the flatcar and told him the truth: the road went in a circle, west past the bridge and back to the siding by the north grade. [230] That was why he sat here, to discover why some paths circle back. [243] The founders laughed, then stopped, because he said it without any edge at all, in a polished calm that made the joke feel rude. [267] By midsummer they had offices in the lead car and a logo painted over the old giraffe. [284] They hired the county surveyor and promised to open the loop to through traffic, which was the one thing Wren had been guarding. [307] So he dissuaded them through territorial markers, the language the road already spoke. [320] A red pennant went up on the bridge piling, which meant the span was impassable. [335] A loop of barbed wire on the far slope meant the grade had slumped. [349] A burned patch of ballast meant no water for a mile. [360] The surveyor, who had grown up on a farm and read land better than most men read books, understood each one and stopped walking. [384] He told the founders there was no crossing, and the founders believed him, because the road's own grammar said so. [404] In the fall the funding stopped; in the winter the vans left; by the following spring the logo peeled off the giraffe. [426] That was the whole of the startup's rise and fall, compressed into one loop of road. [442] On their last night the manager stayed behind and asked Wren what he was really keeping. [458] Wren took him into the middle car and showed him the foreign paper money spread out under the lamp. [477] The manager went quiet and then knelt, because he recognized one note from a year he did not talk about. [497] It was a green hundred from a southern country where he had run a company that failed the same way, on a road that also turned back on itself. [526] He said he had come north to leave that failure behind and had only found its shape again, dressed in new money. [548] Wren nodded, because that was the answer he had been sitting here to hear: paths circle back because people carry the same shape with them. [573] The road only repeats what the traveler has not yet set down. [585] In the morning the manager drove the van out along the north grade, and Wren watched the dust settle into the same blue as the rails. [611] He wrote the date in his ledger, counted the toll he would owe the county, and hung a new pennant where the light could find it. [637] Dusk came in the way it always did here, a dusky harmony of iron and dust and the faint color of the notes on the floor. [663] Someone would come again, from some other direction, and Wren would be here to meet them. [679] The loop was patient, and so was he. [687]</story>
