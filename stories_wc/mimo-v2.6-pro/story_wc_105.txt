In the sunken orchard greenhouse, ringed by shipwrecks standing like the ribs of drowned whales in the tidal basin, Siv works at the junction desk and tries to hear the color of iron cooling. [34]
He is a hearing aid designer, and thirty years of work have taught him that every sound leaves a wear pattern on the world. [58]
But it is not the sound he works from; it is what the sound leaves behind, the absence. [76]
So he collects absences: the door that closed on half a sentence, the footstep that did not return, the name a widow stops saying after the grave is filled. [105]
He is hunting one absence in particular: the sound of trust breaking. [117]
Seven winters ago his brother Enno broke off a sentence in the Rift Hall and walked out into the storm, and Siv has been trying to isolate that event ever since. [148]
Above the glass the aurora runs in pale sheets, and by that light the ash reads as faint color, which is the only reason he can hear colors faintly at all. [179]
The ashen forge under the aurora stands at the heart of the greenhouse, older than the wrecks, its brick held together with tendon and old solder. [205]
Every mold he presses comes from its residue, and every repair he has ever made is a battered marvel, held together by nothing but residue and stubbornness. [232]
On the bench sit the molt plugs, the hardened residue he sintered and ground down with bone files and coarse sand, each pair shaped to sit like a second jaw in a patient's ear canal. [267]
They are what he cleans cement and debris out of before he fits anyone, and they taught him that patience is a material, not a mood. [293]
Now, while a seedling root divides at the junction desk, splitting cell by cell in its dish of black water, he lays the seven winters out in rows of ash. [323]
He digs the event out the way he digs out a damaged ear canal, one grain at a time, holding the tool at the angle where the wall stops crumbling. [353]
The Rift Hall that night: grey gutter iron overhead, the storm hammering the wrecks outside, Enno's coat already wet at the shoulders. [375]
Siv remembers the impact of the memory itself, which is not the words but the weight that left him the moment they stopped coming. [399]
That lightness is the absence, and it has a shape, and the shape has a pitch, and the pitch is the thing he has been listening for. [426]
He carries the mold of coarse ash to the forge and presses the silence into it, holding the pressure until the silence gives up its outline. [452]
This is the imprint, the oldest craft he knows: a sound pressed into iron until the iron agrees to carry it. [473]
The mold takes, and what it holds is the exact moment his brother stopped trusting him with the truth. [492]
He lifts it to his ear and hears colors faintly, the way he always does: rust as a low hum, wet glass as a thin high note, the aurora as something close to a chord. [527]
And underneath all of it, at last, the sound of trust breaking: not a crack but a soft loosening, like a hand letting go of a rope it had held too long. [559]
It is smaller than he feared and worse than he hoped, and it has been sitting inside him for seven winters doing the work of a beam. [586]
So he presses it again, deeper, until the memory stops being a wound and becomes part of the structure that holds the greenhouse up. [610]
When the mold cools he fits it into a hearing aid, the smallest he has ever made, and sets it on the bench beside the molt plugs. [637]
It will not fix what Enno said. [650]
It will let him hear it clearly, which is the only repair a person can honestly make to the past. [677]
Outside, the shipwrecks groan as the tide turns, the aurora dims, and the ash goes grey again. [694]
He has not found his brother. [701]
He has found the shape of the thing that parted them, and a shape can be carried, and carried shapes weigh less than carried guesses. [721]
In its dish, the seedling root finishes dividing and reaches toward the dark. [734]
Siv, a battered man in a battered place, listens to the quiet and finds it full. [750]
