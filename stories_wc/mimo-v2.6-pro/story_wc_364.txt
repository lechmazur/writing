Marrow the blacksmith was meek in the way of men who spend their lives listening to metal. [17]
He did not raise his voice at the market, at the guild, or at the sky that hung its floating rock arches over the orchard planet's rows like a promise he had stopped trusting. [51]
What he had was quiet determination, and a tower that had fallen. [63]
It had collapsed on the eastern ridge three winters back, and the rubble had taken his wife's voice and half the town's wakefulness with it. [88]
Since then the town bought its consciousness back an hour at a time, from the blue market under the second arch. [109]
Marrow went there too, but not to buy. [117]
He wanted to coax resilience from the rubble itself. [126]
Three nights he worked underground in the abandoned mine shafts beneath the orchard, where the roots of the fruit trees had grown down and found stone instead of water. [155]
His method was simple and deliberate: find the seam, heat it, fold it, and let the metal remember what shape it wanted. [177]
On the second night his pick rang true and split open a vein of blue glass no one had named yet. [198]
He took only what he could carry. [205]
At the market the dealers sat in their stalls with tattered magicians' silk thrown over their crates, and the silk shifted like something breathing. [229]
The blue market smelled of bruised fruit and hot iron. [239]
That silk was the only fabric that would hold a borrowed hour without leaking it back into the air. [258]
Marrow kept a strip of it bunched at his throat like a scarf, traded to him for a horseshoe and a promise he had not yet kept. [285]
The dealers sold awakenings in vials of that same blue glass, and the town drank them and went to work with bright, borrowed eyes. [309]
Marrow watched and said nothing, quietly determined the way a hearthstone is determined, by holding heat long after the fire is gone. [331]
On the third night he climbed the underside of the fallen arch with the blue glass wrapped in silk against his chest. [353]
Above him the other arches drifted, and the orchard trees bloomed and dropped their fruit into the dark for no one at all. [376]
He wedged himself into a cleft and began to forge. [386]
There was no anvil, so he used the tower's own broken stone, and the hammer rang out across the valley in a voice he had never owned. [413]
The dealers came up the shaft with their lanterns, confused, because he was doing something the market had no price for. [434]
He stood between understanding and confusion, exactly where he had meant to stand. [447]
Marrow wove the glass into the silk, thread by deliberate thread, until the fabric held something no vial could. [466]
Then he cut it into strips and gave one to every person the rubble had emptied. [482]
Not for coin. [485]
The bartered awakenings he had watched for three years were over, and what he offered instead was a shared waking that would not wear off by noon. [512]
He did not shout it. [517]
But when the dealers argued, Marrow found his voice at last, and it came out the color of the sky between the arches, an azure defiance that made the orchard seem, for one moment, like home. [553]
The market held its breath, then folded, because a man who will not sell what he gives cannot be bargained down. [574]
By dawn the rubble-strewn ridge was full of people digging, and Marrow walked the mine shafts again with his pick over his shoulder. [597]

He had meant to coax resilience from the stone; he had not expected the stone to hand it back. [646]
