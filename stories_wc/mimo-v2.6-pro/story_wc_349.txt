On the store's very first opening day, the lull settled early, and the clean slate vendor found herself alone among glass orchard trees whose branches held bottles of moonlight like fruit over a swirling sea of clouds. [37] The library had arrived that morning by raft of vapour, and the glass orchard grew straight through its floors, so the shelves and the trees were the same crystal structure, tapped by wind. [70] She set out her stock for the first time: slates scraped clean, edges pale, waiting. [85] Then she noticed a stack she had not ordered, delivered under the counter with a note she could not read, only feel, faintly raised. [109] Each slate held the white remnants of scraped-out words, ghosts of sentences someone had paid to forget. [126] Her job was clean, but her method had always been by quantifying absence. [139] She took the diamond stylus she keeps for labeling and began to incise the slate edges, one thin line for each missing word. [162] The counting was the only honest way she knew to find the place where stories begin. [178] She marked the slates 1 and 2 and 3 as she incised, her hand mindfully scattered, moving across three panes at once so no two grooves fell in rhythm. [207] Where the grooves crossed the old ghosts of words, the stone answered with a faint line of light. [225] She knew that light. [229] It was a glimmering parole, the quiet release a story gives up when you finally admit it was never really yours. [250] The parole climbed the air and pointed toward the library's upper galleries, where the light of bottled moonlight thinned to almost nothing. [272] Up there the shelves were empty, which was strange, because the day's customers had not yet come and nothing had been sold. [294] Absence, quantified, told her otherwise: the emptiness was not vacancy but subtraction, and someone had already cut a path through it. [315] Her stylus paused. [318] She breathed. [320] The orchard rang with a note too low to hear and too deep to ignore. [335] She followed the glimmering parole between the trees of glass, and the clouds below swirled like milk stirred by a slow thumb. [357] The shelves there held catalogues of what had been taken, each entry an outline, a shape cut out of the page that held it. [381] At the end of the gallery stood one book that had been left unwritten, its cover a blank slate the exact color of her own stock. [407] She opened it, and the pages were not blank but unfinished, a map drawn in the same raised script as the note under her counter. [432] The map had one destination, marked only by the absence of marks, a place so clearly unmarked that it had to be the beginning. [456] This, she thought, is the place where stories begin, and I have been selling its edges my whole life. [475] The bottled moonlight in the nearest tree flared once, as if the shop had heard her, and the light fell onto the map's blank heart. [500] In that glow the unwritten page filled, not with words, but with the shadow of every slate she had ever scraped, arranged like seeds in an orchard. [527] She understood then that the store was not a shop at all, but a threshold, and the lull was the only hour it ever really opened. [553] So she incised one last line on the book's blank spine, counted it, and let the absence stay where it was. [574] The first customer would arrive soon, wanting a clean slate, wanting to forget. [587] She would sell them one, and she would know exactly what they were handing over, because she had counted it. [607] Below the glass orchard the sea of clouds turned, and the library held its breath. [622] Somewhere above her, in the racks of bottled moonlight, one bottle dimmed, and she did not refill it. [640] That was the price of beginning. [646] Everything that starts leaves a clean slate behind it. [655] She straightened the slates on the counter, mindfully scattered, no two aligned, and waited for the bell over the door. [675] The lull held. [678]
