Liana Vos was a contact tracer for hearts. [8] She traced the residue the dead left in their belongings, and she was persistent enough about it that the Stiltehuis kept her on staff. [32] That evening she carried one task into the mist-clad atrium at twilight. [44] The atrium belonged to the Stiltehuis, the old hearing-aid library on Raampoort, where returned devices sat in paper trays until someone new needed them. [68] Glass held the fog; the lamps came on one by one; the radiators ticked. [82] The reeling hush of the place settled on her like damp wool, and she let it. [98] The task was trivial: log a 1962 Radioear, left ear, and close the file of its owner, Jan Vos, no relation, dead eleven years. [122] She was persistently curious about every device she logged, so she sat on the bench under the fig tree and put the shell to her ear. [148] The method was old and quiet: she worked by tracking faint pulses in a decades-old hearing aid. [165] A heartbeat under a blanket, a radio left on in a kitchen, a tram braking outside, all of it amplified once and then gone. [189] This is when the dead call. [195] They do not speak; they leave behind the room they loved, and if you hold still you can stand in it. [216] Liana heard a kettle, a cough, a woman humming three notes over and over, and the harbor bell that had not rung since 1971. [240] Then the pulses thinned, the way a room empties. [249] Something else crept in instead, faint and regular: the ticking of the pedestrian signal at the corner of Raampoort, sharp as a bird's foot. [273] Under it she heard a child singing, a bicycle bell, and the low roll of a delivery van that did not slow. [295] The singing kept moving toward the corner. [302] Liana was not thinking about heroism; she was thinking about a file she had not closed, and she was already walking, then running, out through the atrium doors. [330] The mist took her at the curb, and there was the girl, seven or eight, singing, stepping off after the signal had already turned. [354] Liana caught the back of her coat and pulled her onto the pavement as the van's mirror went past with a sound like tearing paper. [379] Nobody in the atrium had seen it; the glass was fogged, and the girl's grandmother was inside at the counter, filling out forms with a pen that had gone dry. [409] That was the whole of it: one grab, in the fog, on an ordinary Tuesday. [424] Back inside, the girl shook on the bench, and Liana knelt and assured her, in the flat voice she kept for forms, that she had done nothing wrong and that no one hears everything in time. [460] Then she went back to the fig tree and the file, because the file was still open. [477] Inside the paper tray lay the last photograph, and it was what closed the book: Jan Vos on the quay in 1958, his back to the camera, the Kever fleet behind him. [509] The fleet went to scrap in 1971 and the harbor bell with it; the photograph outlived both. [526] Nell Vos, Jan's sister, had come in at five and sat by the wall of forms with her own returned aid in her lap, too proud to ask for it to be fitted again. [560] She could no longer hear anything at all; Liana had learned that from the intake card. [576] So Liana wrote on the back of a form and held it where the lamplight was, and she assured Nell that the humming in her brother's ear was Nell's own voice, three notes over and over, and that it was still hers. [618] Nell read it twice and did not look up, and the atrium went on ticking around them. [635] Outside, the girl's grandmother finally found a pen that worked. [645] Liana logged the Radioear as returned, fit for reuse, and closed the file of Jan Vos. [661] No one would record the grab; the Stiltehuis has no form for that. [674] What she had was the shell, the quiet, and the small duty that had brought her: to protect the vulnerable voices the living kept talking over. [700] She put the 1962 Radioear in its paper tray and carried the tray to the shelf where the re-fittings were done. [721] In the morning a stranger would hear a kettle, a cough, and a woman humming three notes, and would not know whose room they were standing in. [748] Liana turned off the lamps one by one and left the mist-clad atrium to the dead, who called softly, and to the quiet that answered them. [774]
