The rain stopped an hour before Orren climbed the ridge, and the breezy islet breathed wet basalt and crushed saltgrass. [20] Below him the tide pulled away from the black rocks in long silver threads, rhythmically placid, as if the sea had decided to be gentle one afternoon at last. [49] Orren had come for the tutor, the slow-spoken man who kept a bonsai grove in the lee of the old lighthouse and who was said to know the tides of remembrance better than any navigator. [84] Since the severance, when the causeway was cut and the island was left to its wind and its waiting, Orren had carried an apology he could not say aloud. [113] He wanted the color of it, the true color of unspoken apologies, because he had lost the words somewhere and could only feel the shape they left behind. [141] The tutor's name was Halvek, and he met Orren at the gate with pruning shears in one hand and a cup of smoky tea in the other. [168] His voice moved like his hands, slow and certain. [177] You have come about a compass, he said, though Orren had not mentioned one. [191] Inside the lighthouse, on a cloth of antique lace, lay the ghost ship's compass. [205] Its brass was green with age and its needle never held still; it swung toward whatever the room remembered. [224] Halvek told Orren that the instrument had been lifted from a wreck off the southern shoals, from a ship whose crew had all died owing one another forgiveness. [252] Because they never spoke it, the compass kept searching, and the sea kept answering with the same slow pull. [271] The lace beneath it was Halvek's real lesson. [279] He explained that his grandmother had stitched instructions into the pattern, the way letters might be hidden in a song. [299] Each loop and knot was a direction: where to cut, where to wait, where to let the wind move through the leaves rather than the shears. [325] So they worked together through instructions woven into an antique lace pattern, Halvek tracing the thread with one finger while Orren bent a juniper branch that had grown wild for years. [356] The air inside was cerulean regret, a blue so particular it seemed to have been left behind by the last person who had stood here weeping. [382] Orren felt it settle on his skin like mist. [391] Halvek asked him to speak to the tree. [399] Orren tried, but the words he needed would not come; only the color came, a wash of dull sea-green that darkened as he breathed. [423] The tutor nodded slowly and said that guilt first appears green, the color of a thing still living that wishes it were not. [446] He set the compass on the workbench and told Orren to follow the needle while the light diffused through the rain-clouds into a pale and steady glow. [473] Orren walked out into the grove, and the needle swung toward the small pine he had been afraid to touch since his brother's last visit. [498] That was where the severance had begun, in an argument over their father's empty chair, and it had ended with a boat leaving in weather no one should sail. [527] Orren knelt and put his hands in the wet moss and let the tides of remembrance move through him the way Halvek had said they would, not a flood but a pull. [559] Green darkened to grey, and grey to a deep and quiet blue, and in that blue he found the color of unspoken apologies at last. [584] It was not sorrow exactly; it was the particular blue of a person standing at a shore, holding out a hand to someone who has already gone. [611] Halvek came and stood beside him without speaking, and the two of them watched the needle settle for the first time in years. [634] It pointed west, toward the open water where the wreck had gone down and where the causeway used to run. [654] Orren understood then that an apology does not need to reach its object to be real; it needs only to be spoken into the wind and left there, diffuse and certain. [685] He said the words at last, plainly and without ornament, while the islet steamed gently in the last of the rain-light. [706] Halvek poured more tea and went back to his pruning, because that is what one does with grief: one tends it, one cuts it back, one lets it grow toward the light. [738] When Orren left at dusk the compass lay quiet on its lace, and the tide came back to the black rocks in long silver threads, taking the color with it. [768]
