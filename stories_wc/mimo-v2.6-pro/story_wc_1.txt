Nera stood in the quiet canyon under stars, and let the cold finish what the fire had started. [18]
The attempt on her life had come three nights earlier, and she had lived through it the way glass lives through the annealing, slowly, and on purpose. [45]
She was sixty-four. [48]
Furnace years had taken most of her hearing, so she had learned to read the world with her hands and her patience. [70]
Her glassworks sat half-cut into the canyon wall below the town of Halden, cedar roof, stone flue, a place of steady heat in a country of wind. [97]
Below her boots the canyon floor kept its silence, and above her the stars moved like something cooling in a mold. [118]
She was a luminous glass artist, known in three provinces for vessels that held light the way a well holds water. [139]
Now she was known for having survived. [146]
In her breast pocket she carried the charcoal smuggler's ledger strip. [157]

The fire had made it a scorched testament, a document that testified against its own keepers. [198]
Pencil tally on one face, a charcoal mark on the other, and on the seventh slat a number that was one digit short because the flame had eaten it. [227]
She had read it a hundred times. [234]
It recorded seven crossings of the canyon route, each with a weight, an hour, and a count of carriers. [253]
The last number was the missing one, and that was the thing she meant to retrace. [269]
Retrace was the only word that fit, because the ledger gave a line, and a line can be walked. [288]
Nera had not become steady by being lucky. [296]
She was steadily adaptive, a woman who rebuilt her hearing, her hands, and her shop after each thing that broke them. [317]
The attempt had only confirmed the habit, and that was why she wanted it broken. [332]
She had come down here to discover purpose in the pauses, the empty hours between firing and cooling when nothing can be hurried and nothing can be helped. [360]
The canyon understood pauses. [364]
So she waited for morning the way she waited for glass to stiffen. [377]
Frost came with the first light, and the canyon floor turned silver, and Nera knelt at the east shelf where the smugglers had camped. [401]
This was her method, patterns in morning frost, because cold keeps a record that stone does not. [418]
She found eight boot shadows melted into the same frost blade at the same hour, each one pointing along the east route. [440]
Eight, for a ledger that had recorded seven. [448]
One carrier had gone uncounted, and the missing digit on the seventh slat was the smugglers hiding him from their own books. [470]
The scorched testament had told the truth after all; the fire had simply burned away the lie first. [488]
Nera walked the line the frost showed her, and the eight shadows became eight miles of canyon path. [506]
She found the second cache at the north bend, buried under a cairn of furnace stone, and she did not touch it. [528]

Afterward was where purpose lived, if it lived anywhere. [566]
She carried the ledger back to the glassworks and set it on the bench where her tools kept their order. [586]
By noon the furnace was up, and the charcoaler's mark on the strip looked, in that light, like any other maker's mark. [608]
Nera did not send for the magistrate. [615]
She melted the seven slats down with the batch, and pulled a single long cane of black glass with the tally running through it like a spine. [642]
Then she set the cane in the window, where the canyon light would read it twice a day, at dawn and at dusk. [665]
