Maren had been an apprentice mapmaker aboard the antique airship Celestine for eleven months when the starling murmurations began. [19]
Starlings filled the sky each dusk in spiraling rivers of feather and shadow, and the whole crew crowded the rails to watch. [41]
Maren stood among them, smiling, nodding at their exclamations, yet her mind kept drifting downward to the deck beneath her boots. [62]
She carried the peculiar, sociable isolation of every diligent mapmaker, surrounded by warm conversation but forever charting inner landscapes no one else could see. [86]
Her captain, Delphine, clapped her shoulder and laughed at nothing in particular, and Maren laughed too, feeling the gulf of her private ambition even as the crew's voices wrapped around her like wool. [119]
Belowdecks, in the airship's belly of sagging ribs and oil-dark timber, she kept her workspace: a tilted drafting table, a brass compass, and a shelf of riddled charts she had drawn from scraps of oral testimony. [155]
Every chart she drew was an act of faith, a wager that the world had shapes yet to be named. [175]
It was here, amid the murmuring of the plumbing pipes and the faint groaning of the gas envelope overhead, that she meant to chart the next great discovery. [203]
The Celestine drifted over a region of fog-shrouded valleys where no reliable map existed, and Maren believed the unmapped country hid something worth naming. [227]
One evening she found it, or rather, it found her: a polyglot's phrasebook tucked inside a barrel of dried apples, its cover swollen with damp. [252]
The phrasebook held words in a dozen tongues, worn smooth by fingers that had once crossed borders Maren could only dream of charting. [275]
She thumbed through pages dense with words in languages she could not read, and at page two hundred and seven a dog-ear caught her eye. [300]
The fold pointed not to any word but to the pattern of lines on the opposite page, faint ruled marks that looked less like grammar and more like geometry. [329]
The polyglot had folded that corner long ago, and the crease still held the secret they had not been able to speak in words. [353]
Maren, diligently curious, traced the pattern onto scrap vellum and held it against the starlight that bled through the galley porthole. [374]
What she saw there was stranded luminance, a thin thread of light that seemed to snag and pool in the shape of the marks, as if the page itself had trapped a glow and could not release it. [412]
The airship's old electric lamps flickered in sympathy, their dimness giving the moment an eerie, wondering hush. [429]
Excitement made her almost call for Delphine, but she bit the thought back; discoveries half-shouted were discoveries half-lost, and she was not yet ready to share. [455]
Instead she carried the vellum trace up to the upper deck, where the crew had painted a dusty hopscotch board for the children among the passengers. [481]
The game board was faded, cracked with age, its numbered squares filled with grit, but when Maren laid her traced pattern across it, the lines aligned with the squares in a sequence she had not expected. [517]
She pulled out her charcoal and began to note the secret patterns in the dusty hopscotch board, following the way each numbered cell corresponded to a mark in the phrasebook's geometry. [548]
The pattern was a map, she realized, or at least the skeleton of one: coordinates encoded in the shape of a game. [570]
Maren worked through the murmuration-hour, the starlings wheeling overhead in their enormous silent conversation, and she felt part of their pattern too. [592]
She copied each cell onto her vellum grid, her hand steady, her breath shallow with the excitement of what she was building. [614]
By the time the last starlings folded into the dusk, she had a chart that led downward, into the fog valleys, toward a point of terrain where light gathered and refused to move. [647]
Stranded luminance, she wrote at the top of the sheet, then underlined it twice. [661]
The crew sang shanties behind her, voices braided together, and Maren smiled at them over her shoulder before turning back to the blank space she was filling in. [689]
She was alone and not alone, a mapmaker at work amid friends who would never fully understand the shape of her longing. [711]
But tomorrow, she would tell them where to fly. [720]
