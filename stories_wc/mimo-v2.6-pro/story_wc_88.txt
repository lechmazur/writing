After the procedure, Renn woke with a head like a wiped whiteboard and one name in it. [17] The name was his own. [21] He found the paper in the clinic's waiting-room drawer, folded twice and soft as cloth. [36] It was six years old and it had been saved for him by somebody who no longer worked there. [55] The masthead said the Harbor Ledger. [61] The subhead said: Memory Trial Halted, Patients Unaccounted For. [70] Below that the column ran thin and wrong, full of skipped lines and ellipses where redaction had eaten the detail. [90] Renn read between the newspaper lines the way other men read tide tables. [103] He had been trained for it once, though he could not remember the training. [117] The gaps told him more than the print. [125] A name appeared three times in the gaps, always mid-sentence, always shaved to an initial. [140] It was his. [143] The photo at the bottom showed a man crouched over a lake that glowed from underneath, and the caption was a lie about tourism. [167] Renn knew the lake the way you know a room you were born in. [181] He began the journey that night. [187] The stairs under the old cannery took him down through ninety feet of wet granite and the air changed twice. [207] Then the dark opened and the water was lit by nothing he needed. [220] Blue-green algae sheeted the shallows and strung the far wall in slow ropes of light, aglow for no one. [239] It was delicately robust, that light, the way a soap bubble can hold a whole sky and then hold nothing. [259] Renn photographed it because he did not know what else to do with his hands. [274] Along the shoreline stood cairns of flat stone, each one marking a fork in the silt corridor that ran the length of the cavern. [298] He had read of this place in the missing columns: a labyrinth of choices, laid out for patients who had refused to come back up. [323] The thin red lines he had pieced from the newspaper ran under the silt like the maze plan he was walking backward. [345] At the third cairn he found the baseball. [353] It sat in a cradle of stone, chalked with algae, and the signature on the sweet spot belonged to a man who had told a twelve-year-old Renn he had come from another time and would be gone before the year was out. [395] Renn had lost that ball at the waterfront twenty years ago and had never once believed its story. [413] He picked this one up anyway. [419] It was the same ball, or its twin, and it held its shape under his thumb in a way that made him careful. [442] The trail past the cairns took him deeper, and the light got thinner and better. [457] At the far end of the corridor a woman sat on a ledge with her feet in the water, and she did not turn. [481] She was one of the unaccounted for; Renn knew it from the file he had rebuilt out of ellipses. [500] He sat down beside her and waited for the feeling the procedure was supposed to leave him with. [518] He had paid to have his guilt burned out so he could come here and kindle empathy where none remained. [538] The doctor had promised a clean slate and delivered one, and the slate was so clean nothing would write on it. [559] He looked at the glowing water, at the fragile thing in his hand, at the woman's still shoulders. [577] He felt nothing, and he said so out loud. [585] "You will," she said, not unkindly, "in about a year, when the paper starts to come back." [602] Renn laughed, because that was the jaded thing to do, and because he was already wondering which cairn marked the way out. [624] He left her there and walked the labyrinth back, choosing at every fork exactly as the red lines told him to. [645] The climb out took two hours, and the algae light faded behind him like a thought he was not yet allowed to have. [668] On the pier above, dawn was going pink over the harbor and somebody's radio was calling the weather. [686] Renn set the baseball on the rail and read the signature one more time. [700] Whatever the man had been, he had kept his promise about leaving. [712] The story the papers had not printed: Renn had been the orderly who signed the release forms that sent eleven people down these stairs. [736] He folded the newspaper and put it in his pocket, still soft as cloth, still mostly gaps. [753] It was the only account of himself he had left, and he meant to read it again tomorrow.
