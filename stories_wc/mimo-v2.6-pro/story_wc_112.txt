Mara Venn was the station's obliging sky mechanic, the one who climbed the spire girdles to tune the atmospheric pumps whenever a xenobiologist complained of a draft. [27] She had been hired for her patience, kept for her hands, and tolerated for her theory. [43] The theory was simple and unprintable in any journal: time moves sideways. [55] Dr. Osei, who ran Xenobiology Station Kestrel above the cloud deck of Pelagos, called it a charming delusion and assigned her to the wing-house anyway. [80]

The wing-house held the Somnules, six iridescent flyers the size of small sails, each with a body like a folded lantern. [101] Their wings were catalogued in Osei's files as primary locomotive membranes, but Mara thought of them as sails that remembered wind. [122] They slept in the hush of beating wings at rest, their pinions shivering only when dreams moved through them. [141] Mara loved that hush. [145] It was mechanically warm down there, a warmth that came not from bodies but from the great circulator throbbing in the floor, and she had grown fond of it. [174]

Tonight the Somnules would wake. [179] She had checked every bolt in the ceiling rig twice, because waking flyers were gentle only until they were not. [199] Every forty days the magnetic nexus at twilight flared, a slow knot of field lines braiding itself above the station dome, and the flyers rose to meet it. [227] Mara intended to be ready, and to prove her sideways theorem while the whole station watched. [243] Her notebooks were full of sideways diagrams, arrows bending at right angles where every other physicist drew straight lines. [262] She had spent three seasons building the Array out of salvaged holographic principles: projected wavefronts, reference beams, interference patterns etched in light. [284] If time truly moved sideways, then the past and future of any object lay adjacent in the lateral coordinate, and a hologram, which stored a whole scene in every fragment, should be able to reach across. [320]

She set the final plate beneath the central strut and reached for the lever. [334] The lever was old brass, cold under her fingers, and it engaged the Array with a sound like a held breath let go. [357] Light spilled upward in sheets, and in those sheets something stood that was not standing there. [373] A second Mara, ten minutes older, was already walking toward the far vent, wrench in hand. [389] Ten minutes. [391] That was all the lateral reach would give her, but ten minutes was enough to change a decision. [409] Mara's chest tightened with icy warmth, the joy of being right and the terror of it arriving together. [427] Then the Somnules woke, all six at once, a wash of pinions and low harmonic calls, and Osei's voice crackled over the comm demanding to know what she had done to the field. [460] "Proof," Mara said, and pulled the lever again to fold the Array away before the lateral echo collapsed and took her hand with it. [484] The duplicate faded like breath on cold glass, but not before it turned and looked at her, a look of plain recognition, tired and kind. [509] For a moment she wanted to ask it something, but the hush was returning and there was no time that ran in the asking direction. [534]

Osei arrived in the doorway in his stocking feet, staring at the settling light and the waking flyers. [552] He looked at her the way the station looked at weather, as a condition to be measured rather than believed. [572] "Time moves sideways," Mara told him, and set the lever's brass pin on the console as evidence. [589] Above them the nexus finished its slow braiding and unbraided, and the Somnules lifted toward it through the dome's open iris. [610] Mara watched them go with her hands still humming from the field. [622] She had not wanted glory; she only wanted the quiet vindication of a mechanic who has found the loose joint in the universe and tightened it. [648] Osei knelt by the Array, already recalculating, already afraid and eager in the way of men confronted with an unlabeled door. [669] "Show me again," he said. [674] "Tomorrow at twilight," Mara said. [679] "It only opens in the hush, when the wings are still enough to hear the sideways tick." [696] She wiped the brass lever with her sleeve and went to check the pumps, obliging as ever, while the station filled with the subdued awakenings of everything that had been waiting to open. [729]
