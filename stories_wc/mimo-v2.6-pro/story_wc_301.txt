The old railway station had been swallowed by ivy years before Marren stopped taking her medication. [16]
She came here on the first clean week, when the world felt both unbearably loud and unbearably kind, carrying the accordion bellows she had patched with gray tape the night her father left. [49]
It was a river chronicler's habit, this pilgrimage: she mapped the waterways that cities forgot and wrote their true names in a notebook that never lied. [75]
Her map references were careful and daring at once, each riverbank annotated with the honesty of a witness who knew she had once been unreliable. [100]
That is the whole of her method: daringly methodical, a woman who tests the ice with both feet before she admits she is frightened. [124]
The station had one working tap and one stall whose door hung on a single hinge. [140]
Inside, the walls were dense with bathroom graffiti wisdom, and Marren read it the way she read riverbeds. [158]
Someone had scratched in blue pencil: FORGIVE THE WOMAN YOU WERE WHEN YOU WERE SICK. [173]
Beneath it, another hand had answered: THE RIVER DOES NOT APOLOGIZE FOR FLOODING, IT JUST KEEPS FLOWING. [190]
Marren set the bellows on the sink and squeezed one empty note out of them, and the sound surprised her with its tenderness. [213]
She would not pretend the medication had been a lie, nor that stopping it had made her brave. [231]
It had only given her a floor to stand on while she learned, at thirty-four, to be her own witness. [251]
On the platform, ivy had cracked the timetable glass, and beneath the shards a printed route still alluded to a coastal line that had closed before she was born. [280]
The word alluded sat strangely in her head, and she wrote it down, because she was a chronicler and words were the only honest weight she trusted. [307]
She unfolded her map and traced the river that ran behind the station, a thin blue line she had drawn herself after walking it in April. [333]
Every map reference in the margin was a kind of apology for the years she had misremembered her own life. [353]
There was the bend where she had told her brother a cruel truth too quickly, and the shallows where she had kept a kind one too long. [380]
Truth-seeking had cost her a marriage and a reputation, and she was done being punished twice for the same hunger. [400]
In the bathroom again, she added her own line in the same blue pencil she used for contours. [418]
FORGIVENESS IS DOWNSTREAM OF HONESTY, she wrote, AND YOU HAVE TO WALK THERE. [431]
Then she sat on the platform's cold bench, put the taped bellows to her chest, and played three rough chords while the ivy listened without judgment. [457]
The sound was raw solace, the kind that does not comfort you so much as it sits beside you and refuses to leave. [480]
She thought of her father, who had taught her the accordion on a back porch in a town no map bothered to name. [503]
When the bellows tore that winter, he had patched them with tape and said that mended things sing a lower, truer note. [525]
She had hated him for leaving and then hated herself for hating him, and the medication had flattened both hatreds into one gray hush. [549]
Now the hush was lifting, and with it came the flood of every feeling she had delayed. [566]
She was terrified and careful and glad all at once, which is the only weather a truth-seeker ever really travels in. [587]
At dusk she left the station the way she had entered, by the gap in the ivy where the platform steps had crumbled. [610]
On her map she marked the station in red and wrote beside it: place where I forgave the girl who kept surviving. [632]
It was a map reference no atlas would ever print, and yet it was the truest coordinate she had ever recorded. [653]
The river ran on behind her, indifferent and generous, and Marren walked downstream toward the city that would never quite deserve her. [675]
Behind her the accordion sang one last hollow note through the ivy, and she let it go unanswered. [693]
Some questions do not need replies; they need a person willing to carry them, and she had become that person at last. [715]
Raw solace is not a cure; it is a hand on the shoulder in the dark, and it is enough for tonight. [737]
