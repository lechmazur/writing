Mara Enden had spent her fortune on other people until the only thing she owned outright was a bottled lightning bug collection and the coat on her back. [28] She called it her last endowment. [34] She had come to the black sand beach at the foot of the active volcano because the tide and the stars both kept a schedule that no board of trustees could rescind. [66] The sky was doing its slow dance overhead. [74] A whispering hush moved over the shore; the surf drew a fine bright line of foam across the dark grains while the mountain breathed its warm sulfur breath above her. [104] Nothing here hurried, and neither did she. [111] In her pocket she carried a torn atlas page on which some cartographer had printed a coastal legend about a coffer the sea returns every century or so, whose arrival ends the arrangement of age, storms and empire. [149] She had never turned it over. [155] Mara's great unspoken talent was that she orchestrated coincidences, arranging meetings and rearrivals so precisely that strangers assumed the world had meant to throw them together. [181] She never meant to predict anything. [187] Still the prophecies kept arriving in her hands first, and she kept fulfilling them accidentally, mistaking her own arrangements for luck. [208] That was the whole of her method. [215] There was a rim trail that would have taken her back to the village in an hour, but a fissure had opened across it that morning and sent everyone inland. [245] So she took the detour along the water. [253] The detour put her at the tideline just as the surf coughed up a small basalt-dark coffer, wedged in the rocks with its lid split and its hinges furred with salt. [284] Inside lay a coil of something luminous. [291] It was neither eel nor ribbon nor thread, and it moved the way heat moves over a road at noon, which is to say it moved like transformation itself. [320] She had wanted its texture her whole life. [328] She knelt in the whispering hush and opened her bottled lightning bug collection beside her, and the tiny lamps flickered over the dark sand like a row of patient donors lighting an examination. [361] Then a stranger came down the beach. [368] He was a young collector of glow-glass who had heard that a destitute philanthropist was giving wonders away on the shore, and he had come specifically to find her. [397] She found that neither surprising nor remarkable. [404] It was only the coincidence she had already arranged in some other life, another prophecy she was fulfilling by accident, though she would have sworn she had chosen this beach alone. [435] The coil would not be owned. [441] It had already unwound itself into the light of the bottles and lay there on the sand, so she pressed the coffer, the relic his collection lacked, into the young man's hands and asked nothing for it. [478] He paid her anyway, a single coin. [485] It was the last money she would ever have, and it kept her walking instead of sending her home, which was precisely the sort of arrangement she had spent a fortune learning to make. [519] Her philanthropy still worked at zero. [525] Above them the slow dance of celestial bodies turned the vault from violet to iron, and the volcano took the night air back into its throat with a long sound like a held breath. [559] The young man went home with his relic. [567] Mara stayed, because her whole life had been an attempt to understand the texture of transformation, and she was not going to learn it from a distance or a ledger or the gratitude of people she had ruined herself for. [607] She put her hand to the living thing in the light. [618] It was warm and grainy and restless, exactly like black sand cooling, and she knelt there feeling it change under her palm while the bottles burned their small patient fire and the mountain kept its vibrant hush. [655] In her pocket the atlas page waited, unturned. [663] On the reverse she would read at dawn, in letters smaller than a whisper, that whoever gave the coffer away would end the arrangement of age, storms and empire, and she had done exactly that without ever meaning to, the way she had done everything.
