Maris bought the jigsaw box at a rummage sale beside the village hall. [13]
It was cardboard gone soft as old bread, and its lid showed a forest of tall green culms stitched through with light. [35]
She paid the woman a coin and carried it home under one arm, the way she carried everything now. [54]
Maris was a wistful loom spinner, and her loom had not moved in three years, but the house still smelled of sized thread. [77]
At the bottom of the box, beneath the false tray, she found the jigsaw puzzle edges, and beneath those, the bead. [98]
It was tree sap amber, a bead the color of honey, with a black seed suspended in its heart like a held word. [121]
Maris knew the seed; it came from a pear tree that stood in a forgotten orchard deep in the bamboo forest, where her daughter Senn had walked seven winters ago and not come back. [155]
Senn had given her the bead on the morning she left, and Maris had kept it in a pocket ever since, a small stoical weight. [180]
Now she understood that it had been waiting to be carried back. [192]
That night she sat at the table and pushed the puzzle edges together until the frame closed. [209]
The artwork inside the frame was not the forest on the lid; it was the orchard, four leaning pear trees under a bruised sky. [233]
Every piece fit, which meant the box had held a complete map of the place she had stopped searching for. [253]
In the morning she laid the frame in the bamboo, flat on the leaf litter, and waited. [270]
Light found it the way water finds a crack. [279]
Where the frame touched ground a seam opened, pale and thin, and the seam ran off between the culms like a trail of unpicked thread. [304]
She took the bead in her fist and walked it. [314]
The forest closed behind her with a raveling silence, the sound of a long cloth coming undone at its edge. [334]
At the seam's end the bamboo thinned, and the orchard stood in the late light, stoically sparkling, each pear tree rimed and calm. [357]
Maris walked the row she knew by heart and stopped at the fourth tree. [371]
Its trunk bore the cut Senn had made at nine years old, a small ladder of scars. [388]
She set the bead in the hollow of that cut and put her mouth near it, and she spoke the message she had carried across impossible distance. [415]
It was not long; it was four words, and one of them was a name. [430]
Then she waited, the way a spinner waits for the thread to take. [443]
Nothing answered. [445]
The orchard held its breath; the orchard is patient in a way that people cannot be. [461]
Maris thought of her loom, of the cloth she had woven for thirty years, and of the one length she never finished, the one that was to have been Senn's wedding cloth. [493]
It still lay on the beam, warp threads slack, gathering dust the way abandoned things do. [509]
She understood then what the silence wanted. [516]
It would not take the words alone; it would take the weaver with them, and the bead would do the carrying after. [538]
Maris had known this since the rummage sale, perhaps since the morning Senn left. [552]
She did not weep. [556]
She said the old cantation she had spoken at every warp, the one her mother taught her through the hush of time's unraveling tapestry. [580]
Then she bent to the bead and made to recommence the weaving she had abandoned, binding the message into the sap of the tree. [604]
The bead cracked warm in its cut, and the amber ran, and the four words ran with it into the wood. [625]
The orchard lit from inside, stoically, the way a hearth does when nobody is watching. [640]
Maris felt the thread in her hands go slack, then straighten, then go slack again. [655]
She sat down with her back against the fourth tree and closed her eyes. [669]
Somewhere out past the bamboo, a loom began to move again. [680]
It was her loom, and it was weaving a road of green light through the culms. [696]
On the other side of impossible distance, a woman who had been walking a long time stopped and turned, because she heard her name arrive. [721]
The bead lay in the bark, empty and shining. [730]
The orchard kept what the orchard keeps, and the silence stopped raveling at last. [744]
Maris did not rise to go home. [751]
Some threads are cut so that others may be carried. [761]
This was hers. [764]
