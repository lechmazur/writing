Elowen walked the northern road with the brass astrolabe wrapped in her coat against the wind. [16] It was the only astronomical instrument she owned, and it had belonged to her grandmother. [31] Ahead of her the aurora-lit archway stood over the road like a doorway cut into the sky. [48] She had come to it at last, across an unwritten calendar of moon-counts and aurora-seasons. [63] No months named the time since her verses had stopped arriving; only the sky kept count. [79] Elowen was a moonstruck dreamer, and she knew it the way she knew her own breathing. [95] She leaned against the stone of the arch and let herself rest at last. [109] The cold found her slowly, a bone-deep chill that felt like honesty. [121] She set the astrolabe on the sill of the arch and opened its graduated rings. [136] Green light moved across the brass like water across a drowned bell. [148] When a star fell, she lifted the alidade and traced its path. [160] This was her method and her devotion: by tracing the path of falling stars she measured what could not be held. [181] Her grandmother had called the falling light remembered stardust. [190] Every soul, she had said, is made of the ash of stars that burned out before the first word was spoken. [211] To remember it was to remember a light you had never seen. [223] Her grandmother had kept the astrolabe for the same reason, tracing from the roof of a house long since fallen. [243] What she measured was never the star itself but the leaving of it. [256] Elowen closed her eyes and felt the rapture rise in her, shadowed the way a well is shadowed at noon. [276] She had been born hearing unspoken words, the things people hold behind their teeth. [290] In every market and doorway they crowded her: hunger, envy, the small cruelties no mouth would say. [307] Too many other voices had filled the channel where her own lines once came. [321] Her creativity had not vanished; it was trapped, pressed down under the weight of everything she had overheard. [339] She had walked here to free it, and she had walked a long while. [353] Three winters of walking had worn the road into her bones. [364] Under the arch, for the first time in three winters, nothing spoke. [376] The aurora held the silence open like a hand. [385] Another star fell, and she raised the alidade to trace it. [396] Then she stopped, because tracing was the loosening and rest was the opening. [409] She let her hands fall to her sides and simply stood. [420] No one had ever taught her how to stop listening, so she taught herself in the cold. [437] The first line came like a fish from dark water. [447] Then a second, and a third, until the verses rose out of her in a long unbroken breath. [465] They were made of remembered stardust and they did not sound like anyone else. [479] Elowen laughed once, softly, afraid to break it. [487] She spoke the lines aloud so the arch could hear them and the sky could keep them. [504] When the last of the rapture thinned to ordinary air, she took up the astrolabe again. [520] On the rim of the brass she cut a small mark with her knife. [534] It was one notch in an unwritten calendar, and it meant the channel was open. [549] She would cut another each time the words came back. [559] The road would be long, and she would be a moonstruck dreamer the whole of it. [575] So the sky would keep her count for her, since she had no month to name. [591] Elowen wrapped the instrument in her coat and turned south again, toward the towns and their unspoken noise. [609] This time she was not afraid of what she would hear there. [621] The verses were hers now, and the other voices were only weather. [633] Behind her the aurora guttered low over the archway, keeping the doorway lit for whoever came next. [650] A last star fell through the green, and she did not trace it. [663] She only watched it go, a thin bright thread of remembered stardust, and walked on. [678]</story>
