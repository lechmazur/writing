Corin is a lonely antique map dealer, and he keeps the door of the promise unlocked even in winter. [22]
Outside, the creek rises as the first of the midsummer rain comes down, climbing the third stone step of his shop. [42]
He works by lamplight over a slab of black basalt, filing a brass plate for a client who will never collect it. [63]
On the shelf behind him sits a type foundry matrix chip, wrapped in oiled cloth, which arrived nine years ago with no letter and only Marta's hand on the parcel string. [92]
He does not know what it is for, only that it is hers, and that she left it the way other people leave a debt. [117]
The chip is small, leaden at the core, with a punched socket like a mouth. [136]
He has tried it on locks. It opens nothing. [150]
He has tried it as a compass weight. It points nowhere. [167]
Tonight he sits back on his heels and watches a column of ants come out of the mortar, because when he cannot think he looks at whatever is moving. [197]
His eye for terrain is empirically intuitive: he reads waterlines on brass and brass-filings on wet stone and he draws the conclusion before he knows he has drawn it. [232]
The ants go down the wall and under the threshold and out toward the bank. [253]
He follows ants home, the way he has followed traders' routes across a faded gores-map of the world, and the trail leads him into the dark behind the tannery. [292]
There the ground opens. [301]
Below, at midsummer, the cavern is blooming: blind white flowers the size of saucers open along the wet ledges, feeding on minerals seeping from the rock, and the air smells of iron and pollen. [348]
The ants descend into it in a single unwavering thread. [367]
At the base of the cavern, where the thread ends, there is a seam in the limestone the shape of a tall door, and beside it, cut into the rock in a foundryman's hand, four words he has read before. [412]
He has read them on the docket that came wrapped with the chip: the doorway of decisions. [438]
That was what Marta said they were building, he remembers now, not a place but a habit, and he promised her he would find it before the water took the lower road. [478]
The creek has taken the lower road twice since. [494]
He kneels at the seam and does what a mapmaker does: he takes out his calipers and his chalk and he measures the tolerance of the crack, and when the plate of the door will not shift he sets the matrix chip against it as a key. [546]
Nothing turns. [552]
He presses harder and the chip's socket fills with grit and water, and he understands, kneeling there in the wet, that the chip is not a key but the negative of one, a mold from which letters are cast, and that he has been carrying a name and trying to use it as a tool. [601]
He speaks the name to the door. [612]
The door opens inward on more falling water, and on a choice he has put off for nine years, and a faint glimmer runs along the wet brass of the chip as it warms in his palm. [663]
Behind him the creek is past the third step and reaching for the fourth. [685]
He thinks of the shop, the lamplight, the uncollected plate, the parcels with her hand on the string. [712]
He thinks of the promise, which was never to arrive anywhere but to choose something and say so out loud. [741]
He steps through the doorway of decisions and says Marta's name into the dark, and in the water at his feet something catches a glimmer and holds it, like a map of where he has been. [791]
