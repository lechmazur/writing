Marek kept two ledgers at the celestial mapping observatory: one for variable stars, one for dreams. [16]
The second was not sanctioned, but the director had stopped asking about the handwriting he heard at three in the morning. [32]
He had come here to decode temple inscriptions, and the observatory had taken him on because the inscriptions were stellar notations, and nobody else wanted the night shift. [55]
The telegram that started it all he kept in the top drawer, pressed flat with a daylily his sister Inez had folded inside before she mailed it. [77]
It was one sheet, and the flower had fused with the paper so that reading it meant reading through the bloom. [90]
"We found the east wall," she had written. "The glyphs count something. I think it is people." [102]
Then the dig moved upriver and the letters stopped. [108]
The observatory at night was a good place for a man who could not sleep. [117]
Its dome breathed as the shutter turned, and the red darkroom lamp made everything the color of an old wound. [130]
Marek read the inscriptions the only way he could: by laying them over the sky. [141]
Through variable stars, he had learned, the temple builders kept their records, because a star that swells and fades can hold a number the way a drum holds a beat. [162]
Each glyph had a period, and each period had a meaning, and the sky was the code book. [174]
At night he worked the plate camera and wrote letters to Inez he never sent. [185]
He would read them aloud to the empty dome, because he had found that he healed with words only when he heard them leave his mouth. [202]
"Dear Inez," he would say, "today the delta Cephei numbers refused me again, and I have started to think the wall is a list of names." [219]
He always paused after that sentence. [224]
His dreams, which he chronicled with the same care as the stars, had begun to show him the same thing: a long corridor of stone where figures stood shoulder to shoulder and did not move. [248]
In the dreams he was allowed to walk the corridor, but only to witness, never to touch. [259]
He wrote that down too, in the other ledger, in the same ink. [267]
The plate he took on the fourth night changed everything. [274]
A new variable star had gone out of tolerance near the eastern horizon, exactly where the east wall inscription pointed, and its period matched the glyph he had failed to read for eleven weeks. [298]
When understanding dawned, it did not feel like triumph. [306]
It felt like the dome had stopped breathing and was listening with him. [315]
The glyph was not a name and not a command. [322]
It was an account. [325]
Each line of the east wall recorded a burden one person had carried for another: a debt taken on, a debt forgiven, a weight moved from one pair of shoulders to a second. [347]
That was what the numbers counted. [352]
The temple builders had written down their shared burdens and set them in stone beneath the stars so that the sky would hold the record after the bodies were gone. [374]
Marek sat back, and the red lamp swung, and he understood why the corridor in his dreams was full of people who did not move. [390]
They were holding something still for one another. [397]
He opened the drawer and took out the telegram and read it through the daylily one more time. [409]
"I think it is people," Inez had written, and she had been right, and she had not been there to hear him say so. [424]
This time he did not pause. [429]
He folded the letter around the plate exposure, addressed it upriver to the dig's forwarding post, and put a stamp on it. [443]
"Dear Inez," it said. "You were right, and I have needed to tell you that for eleven weeks." [455]
Then he wrote the second part, which was the part he had never been able to say aloud to the empty dome. [469]
"I have been carrying this alone and it has not made either of us lighter." [479]
The shutter turned overhead, and somewhere above the dome a variable star swelled and faded and swelled again, keeping time for someone who would not see it for ten thousand years. [502]
Marek recorded the period in the sanctioned ledger, and in the unsanctioned one he wrote: "Burden, shared, entered into the record this night." [521]
He stayed until dawn put gray on the slit of the dome, which was earlier than he expected, and he went out into it. [537]
The daylily stayed in the drawer, but the telegram had done its second job now, having carried a message twice, once across a continent and once out of a man's chest. [562]
He thought about the temple builders who had never learned to keep such things to themselves, who had carved their accounts into a wall so the sky could testify for them. [584]
He wanted to be that kind of person, and the first act of becoming one had been to stamp a letter. [597]
When the reply came, eleven days later, it was three words and a pressed flower, and he read it standing in the dome's shadow with the lamps off, because midnight solace had taught him that some words want the dark. [626]
"Send more," Inez had written. [631]
He did, and he has ever since, and the two ledgers at the celestial mapping observatory have been filling at the same steady rate, one period at a time. [652]
