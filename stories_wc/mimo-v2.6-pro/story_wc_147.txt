<story>The relay on the altar had been dead for a thousand years, but Lio pressed her palm to it anyway and spoke the words the salvage handbook gave her for a distress signal. [33] Her voice left her helmet and went nowhere in particular, which was the trouble with sending a distress signal from inside a forgotten underwater temple: the water kept everything. [62] She had come down with the rest of the Petrel's survey crew at dawn, six of them riding the cable into a dome of stone that the charts called a reef and that the sonar insisted was hollow. [100] Now the cable was cut, the dome had closed behind them in a fall of silt, and the gauge on her wrist read six minutes of breathable water left. [129] In the center of the altar, where the relay's last light still guttered, stood the thing they had come for: a crystal perfume vial, stoppered in gold, filled with a slow drift of luminescence. [163] Lio reached for it, and the moment her glove brushed the crystal, the temple touched her back with every memory it had gathered since the sea took it. [191] She was a priestess binding her hair in oil-light; she was a boy learning the hymn that kept the reef from breaking; she was an old woman alone at the end, listening to the flood come over the steps. [230] The vial, she understood, was not a perfume at all but a jar for the ephemeral, and the altar around her was thick with a gathered hush that pressed against her ears like deep water. [265] It was the silence of all those centuries, stored and still, and it had weight: the crew could not hear each other, could not signal, could not remember which way the current ran toward open sea. [301] That was why Lio had gone back to the altar alone, and why the five others were somewhere behind her in the dark, drifting toward the wrong archway. [329] Her lungs told her to swim for the ceiling, where the air had to be; the ceiling was stone, and there was no air anywhere in this place. [357] She knew the exit, because the priestess had known it: a narrow throat in the temple floor where the reef's own current pulled seaward. [381] To reach it she had to cross the flooded nave, and the nave was choked with the cut anchor line of the survey cable, knotted into itself like a bad thought. [412] Lio took hold of the nearest strand and began to unsnarl it, fingers slow, because the hush had taught her hands to panic and she had decided not to. [440] Every instinct she owned said pull hard, pull now, get free; the line only bit deeper when she did, and the gauge on her wrist said four minutes. [468] So she did the other thing, the thing that went against the animal in her: she fed the knot slack, let the current choose for her, worked the loop that felt like the trap instead of the one that felt like the door. [511] Overriding instinct was the only grammar this temple understood, the priestess had learned, and the memory of that lesson steadied Lio's hands until the last strand came free. [539] The nave opened. She swam the throat of the exit and felt the reef's current take her, cold and certain, dragging her toward a brightness she had not seen since morning. [570] She had one purpose left before the air ran out, and it was not salvage: it was to harness the ephemeral, to bottle one clean hour of sunlight in the crystal vial so the hush would not have the last word. [611] At the mouth of the reef she stopped, shook the vial, and the luminescence inside it answered with the priestess's first morning, gulls, oil-smoke, the hymn being taught badly and then well, and Lio wept inside her helmet where the water could not reach her. [656] Two minutes. She tipped the vial to stopper it and found that it would hold one thing only, and what it held now was the temple's gathered hush, poured out of the altar and into the glass like still wine. [696] The silence was gone from the water around her; she heard the cable groan far overhead, heard the others calling her name in the wreck, heard the sea doing what the sea does. [729] The salvage was worthless and the haul would never clear the debt on the Petrel, but Lio held up the crystal perfume vial against the last light, lonesome and splendid, and the six of them broke the surface holding it like a candle.
