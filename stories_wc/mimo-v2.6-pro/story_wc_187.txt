<story>I am the summer-quill archivist of the stormlit observatory above the clouds, and my work is to copy what the dead quills remember. [23] Collective memory is the only harvest this height allows, and it arrives as a fine gray ash inside every shaft. [43] I have been preemptively nostalgic since the first thunder, mourning the present while it is still falling on me. [62] The fog has been solid overhead since the third thunder, a pane of old glass pressed against the dome, and the observatory keeps its deepening hush beneath it. [90] I work by the stormlight alone, because the lamps would bleach the ash before I can read it. [108] My method is older than the building: I examine shadow lengths at the monuments and let the numbers speak first. [128] The long figure cut across the north plinth is the face of a drowned harbour, though no harbour has stood here in nine centuries. [152] I can only conjecture which faces belong to which stones, and conjecture is the honest tool of my trade. [171] So I measure, I transcribe, and I keep the summer quills warm in my sleeve so the ash does not freeze to them. [194] Below the dome, where the storm drains away, the crystal whispering forecourt after rain lies open like a second archive. [214] Every step across it returns a voice I did not ask for, a stranger clearing their throat three hundred years before my birth. [237] That is what collective memory does when it is kept in crystal; it refuses to stay in the past tense. [257] Or so the archive would have me believe. [265] I am no longer certain that the voices are older than I am, only that they are louder. [283] My motive for staying at this height has always been to sculpt identity from experience, to carve something whole out of what was felt. [307] The archive offers experience ready-made and asks nothing of me but a steady hand. [321] Yet a borrowed feeling makes a borrowed face, and I would rather be a stranger to myself than a copy of a copy. [344] So tonight I read the shadows again, slower than the method requires. [356] The west monument casts a length of eleven spans, which is the length of a mourning procession walking toward water. [376] The east monument casts seven, which is the length of a child's arm reaching for a door that closes. [395] I write both down in the ledger, then I write the loss itself, because the number without the grief is only weather. [417] The quills in my sleeve are cooling fast; the summer in them is nearly spent. [432] When they go cold the copying stops, and whatever the ash held tonight is lost with it. [449] I think of the forecourt then, and of the throat-clearing stranger, and I decide that one voice is enough to carry. [470] I choose the drowned harbour. [475] I press the warmest quill to the north plinth and let the ash of that one street settle into the shaft. [496] The observatory grows quieter around me, as if the dome above the fog is listening. [511] This is the deepening hush I have waited for, the hush that comes after the last useful question. [529] In the morning the crystal will dry and the forecourt will keep its whisper to itself. [545] What I have taken will be only mine, a single street and its water, cut down to a shape I can wear. [567] That is the identity I am making: not the whole harvest of a people, but one length of shadow understood. [587] The quills go cold at last, and the last ash falls from the dome without a sound. [604] I close the ledger and sit with the harbour until the stormlight thins. [617] Above me the fog stays solid, a ceiling of glass that will not lift for days. [633] Below, the crystal holds the rest of them, the processions and the doorways and the throat-clearing stranger, waiting for someone with a sleeve of warm quills. [659] They will wait a long time; I am not coming back down tonight. [672] Tomorrow I will measure again, and the shadows will be shorter, and the loss will be smaller, and I will be a little more myself. [697]</story>
