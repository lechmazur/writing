Above the salt flats, the prism terrace drifted on its slow current of air. [14] Everything in that country was in transit, and nothing stayed long enough to be called home. [30] Aran had been its gondola operator for eleven years, which meant he was the only one who still knew how to slow down. [53] He kept a storm glass vial on the brass rail, its crystal fogging and clearing as the terrace changed altitude. [73] Travelers read it as weather, but Aran read it as temperament. [84] When the clouds inside the glass stood up in feathers, the guests grew restless. [98] When they lay down flat, they wept for no reason anyone could name. [111] His job, as he understood it, was to teach silence how to comfort. [124] Noise was easy to manage; a gondola operator could shout an engine into submission, could steer around the roar of the canyon mills. [147] Silence was the harder craft. [152] So he had built his method from the only honest instrument he owned: monitoring noise levels. [168] A small brass meter hung beside the vial, its needle recording every cough, every whisper, every splinter of wind against the railing. [190] Aran worked fluidly, logically, the way water works, adjusting the terrace's pitch a hand's width whenever the reading climbed. [209] Too much sound and people begin to perform; too little and they begin to lie to each other about how they feel. [231] He wanted a middle register, a hush wide enough to stand under, like a vault in a cathedral. [249] The passengers called it, without knowing why, the vaulting hush. [259] It came on schedule each evening, in the quiet that follows a train whistle's echo. [274] Far below, the night express crossed the flats on its way to somewhere better, and the horn rolled out across the distance in one long held note. [301] Then the world folded the sound away, and for perhaps ninety seconds the air itself seemed to arch overhead. [320] That was the window he worked for. [327] Tonight the meter rose before the whistle even came. [336] A child in a red coat was counting the stars out loud, and a man at the rail was telling the woman beside him a story he had told before. [366] The vial's crystals stood up in feathers. [373] Aran did not hush them; hushing was a kind of violence, and he had sworn off it. [390] Instead he tilted the terrace until the far edge caught the last light, and the counting child stopped because the color was too beautiful to talk through. [417] He let the gondola's cable hum at its lowest pitch, a sound under hearing, so that the man's story simply ran out of fuel. [441] Noise fell; the needle dropped into the green band he had marked with a strip of tape years ago. [460] Then the whistle came. [464] It catalyzed everything. [467] The horn's echo climbed the terrace and broke against the prism panels, and for one long breath the travelers heard their own lives from very far away. [494] When it drained out across the flats, the silence that followed was enormous and vaulted and warm. [511] The vial's clouds lay down flat. [517] The child in red slipped her hand into the man's hand without looking, and the woman beside him put her head down and closed her eyes. [543] Nobody said anything, and nobody needed to. [550] Aran recorded the number in his ledger: nine decibels, sustained, uncoerced. [561] That was the fourth time this season he had coaxed comfort out of the quiet instead of order. [579] He let the terrace drift on. [585] Below, the express was already a line of small warm windows moving west, carrying its passengers toward whatever they believed was waiting. [607] Everyone is in transit, Aran thought; the trick is to make the passage bearable. [621] He wiped the storm glass with his sleeve and set it back on the rail, where it would fog and clear all night, telling him what the sky felt before the sky knew. [654] Tomorrow there would be more noise to measure and more silence to shape. [667] For now the needle held steady in the green, and the hush rose around him like a ceiling he could trust.
