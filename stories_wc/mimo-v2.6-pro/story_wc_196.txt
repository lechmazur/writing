Mara Venn kept the photograph of Theo in the bottom drawer, face down, and never once turned it over. [19] She had taken the archivist's post in the foster care file room six weeks after he stopped answering, on the theory that a basement full of other people's abandoned beginnings was the one job that would not ask her to imagine a future. [62] After the emancipation the state had dissolved the placement program and cleared out the records office upstairs, and someone had signed the whole accumulation over to her. [89] So the files stayed in the basement, thousands of them, and the room became the place where unknown histories went to be examined rather than filed. [115] Mara worked through accumulated dust patterns first. [122] The one high window threw raking light across the sorting table at two in the afternoon, and in that light the dust was a document. [147] She read the drifts to learn what had been carried in, what had been set down and forgotten, what had been lifted out again in a hurry. [174] Only after the dust had spoken did she open anything. [184] On Tuesday a seam of gray lifted along the top of a shelf where nothing had been touched in nine years, and she climbed the stool and brought down a sealed manila envelope with no name on it. [222] The seal was brittle and she worked it with her thumbnail until the flap gave, and she coaxed the paper out and unfurled it flat across the table under the window light. [254] Inside was a placement sheet so old it had gone soft at the fold, a carbon of a school transfer, and a button. [277] The button was brass, an infantryman's button, the kind that came off a dress tunic, and it had been taped to the inside of the folder with two strips of yellowed tape. [309] There was no name anywhere in the folder. [317] On the shelf below she found a sticky note from some earlier clerk that read only "Calloway," which was probably, perhaps, the child's surname. [341] Mara wrote it on a findings slip and set it beside the button, tactfully hopeful, and went back to the dust. [362] The dust around that envelope had been disturbed twice: once when it was stored, and once, much later, when someone stood a long time in front of the shelf and then left it exactly where it was. [399] That was the shape of the whole room, she thought; she had been hired to magnify small kindnesses into evidence. [419] Someone had taped a soldier's button inside a stranger's file and left no signature. [433] Someone had stood at the shelf and changed their mind about taking it home. [447] She opened the drawer and looked at the photograph for the first time in a year, and then she put Theo back face down. [471] What she could prove about Calloway was almost nothing: a transfer, a fold, a button, and a pattern in dust that said a hand had hovered. [497] In the spring she built the exhibit on the landing outside the file room, six shelves and a printed card, around what she could prove. [522] The card said: brass infantryman's button, found taped in an unlabelled folder, presumably Calloway, presumably a kindness, date unknown. [541] People read it on their way to the elevators and stopped, the way you stop at a grave with no name on it. [564] Mara kept the exhibit honest and kept the file room open on Thursday afternoons, and when visitors asked what had happened to the children, she said that the record did not say, and that this was itself the finding. [603] One afternoon the light came through the window at a new angle and laid a fresh gray seam across the shelf that held the Calloway folder. [629] Under it, in the drift, she read a second, fainter outline parallel to the first, and she understood that someone had come back. [652] They had put the button in their pocket and then, perhaps, put it back. [666] Mara set the brass on the findings slip under the window, where the dust would keep the record if the paper would not, and she went on reading. [694] The emancipation had emptied the foster care file room of its purpose but not of its weight. [711] Every unknown history in it was somebody's whole life, and she would keep them in the light as long as the building let her. [735] Then she turned to the next folder and began again with the dust. [748]
