The ninth scar on Mother Vael's knuckles began to tickle just as the sun thinned behind the glade. [18]
She was root-bound and knew it; her legs had been grafted into the cobalt floor forty seasons ago, and the iron root holding her did not negotiate. [45]
So she watched the dusk come in the way she always watched it, purposefully curious, naming the shades of blue the glade offered her as they arrived. [72]
The future had arrived early, two days ahead of every prophecy the hedge-priests had carved, and no one had told a woman who could not walk. [98]
The annular eclipse was coming that evening, a ring of dark iron hung in the sky, and with it came the collapse of the Vow of Nine Nine Names, the eclipsed covenant that had forbidden dream-contact since the glade was planted. [139]
Under the Vow she had been safe from other people's sleep; now their sleep would find her, and she would have to heed what it asked. [165]
The first dream came at the colour the children call thunder-blue, and it came with a thing she had never been given. [187]
A maze topiary clipping, cut from the great hedges that keep the root-bound apart from the walking, lay in her palm, still warm, still green, smelling of shears. [215]
The dreamer was Ilex, a hedge-cutter with broken shears and a debt he could not name, walking toward the glade as he slept. [238]
His dream showed her a corridor of clipped yew that ended in nothing, and then in a door she recognised from a childhood nobody had allowed her. [265]
The second dream came before she had finished holding the first. [276]
It belonged to Thorn, who had married into the walking and regretted it, and it showed her a field of the same blue grass as her floor, flat and endless, crossed by nine scars of trampled earth. [313]
Thorn's arms were scarred from the wedding tattoos she had been made to take, and in the dream those scars opened like mouths and pointed. [338]
The third dream was Quill's; Quill was nine and already keeping a book of things the adults refused to remember. [358]
In the dream the book's words blurred except for one line: the intersection of dreams is not a place you can plot on any map. [383]
Mother Vael, a visionary who had spent forty seasons trying to find the intersection of dreams, had assumed it would be a location. [406]
Now three strangers' sleep had crossed inside her skull, and she understood it was a moment, and it was coming at dusk. [428]
She was purposefully curious about what the crossing would cost her, because curiosity was the only thing the iron root had never held down. [452]
So she called the two hundred and eleven children of the cobalt glade to the rim and told them to listen with their scars. [476]
Every one of them carried at least one, and every one of them had been told the scars were shameful. [496]
The eclipse arrived exactly on time, which is to say early, and the ring of iron closed around the sun, and the glade went the colour of bruises. [524]
Mother Vael felt all nine of her knuckle-scars open at once, not bleeding but listening, nine channels where the dreams of Ilex and Thorn and Quill could pour through her into the grass. [557]
She had been afraid of that, and told to hide from the eclipse, to curl over her root and wait, but she chose to heed the dreams instead. [585]
Linking through scars worked the way the old song said it would: not by magic but by agreeing to be marked in the same places. [610]
The children pressed their arms to the cobalt grass, and the grass remembered four thousand and one dreams forbidden to it, every one arriving at the same instant. [638]
That was the intersection of dreams, and Mother Vael laughed, because it was not a place at all, but the moment three strangers, a root-bound woman, and two hundred and eleven children all wanted the same thing badly enough to be scarred for it. [682]
When the ring broke and ordinary dusk came back, Ilex stood at the hedge line with his broken shears raised, Thorn beside him with her arms uncovered, Quill behind them with the book open. [716]
Mother Vael could not go to them, so they came to her, and the clipping took root beside her iron, growing into a hedge already shaped like a door. [745]
She set the children a rule that night: whatever marks you carry, keep them open, because the next future is already on its way early and will need every channel it can find. [778]
