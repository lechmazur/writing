Vael was the chronicle keeper, and she had long ago sworn to witness change without judgment. [16] Her domain was an overgrown library hidden in the catacombs of lost knowledge, three floors beneath a city that had forgotten it ever owned a stair. [42] Kudzu had come up through the floor tiles decades past, and moss muffled every shelf, so that even the loudest collapse arrived softened. [65] The library had risen from a burning. [72] The city set fire to its own records in the year of the broken treaty, and the keepers who survived carried the ashes down here and taught the ash to read. [103] She liked that softness. [107] It suited a peace that was crumbling at every edge and never once complained about it. [123]

In her lap sat the origami swan she had carried since the flood year. [137] Its wings were covered in cryptic folds, creases that held writing where no writing appeared. [152] She read them by touch, pressing a thumbnail into each crease until a sentence surfaced in the paper's memory. [171] The swan was the only archive in the library that could not burn, could not mildew, could not be filed and so be lost. [195] It was why her hands had learned to grip reality loosely, the way one holds a bird one intends to set down. [217] Some nights she unfolded nothing at all. [223] The method demanded patience: a fold opened only so far, and never the whole bird at once. [240]

Each evening she left the stacks and burrowed down the riverbank to the obsidian refuge that stood along the water. [260] It had been carved in the season the river ran black, a single glassy room with a ledge wide enough for a keeper and her bird. [286] From there she watched the water carry whatever the city was finished with: fish, rope, a chair, once a wedding banner still bright. [309] She wrote none of it down. [315] Writing was for those who meant to argue with the past. [326]

Once a delegation climbed down to ask her which of their two histories was true. [341] Vael showed them the river and let them decide. [350] They left angry, and the swan remembered their anger in a fold she never opened again. [366]

Across the drift of untold seasons the catacombs changed around her like a slow animal turning in sleep. [384] The crypt at the far end of the second gallery filled with builders' lime and came out again as a transit hub. [406] The well that watered the lower vaults went sour, then sweet, then went entirely. [420] A generation of apprentices learned to read by the green light of the shelves and then stopped sending apprentices at all. [441] Through all of it Vael burrowed deeper, her corridors narrowing, her shelves leaning inward like tired men. [458]

The rain came first too little and then too much. [468] In the dry years the river fell away from the obsidian refuge and left it hanging above a bed of pale bones. [490] In the wet years the water rose to the lintel and the refuge became a mouth. [506] Vael sat inside it either way, unhurried, reading the swan by touch. [518] Judgment would have meant choosing a season. [525] She chose none. [528]

What she did not expect was that the silence would begin to work on her. [543] It was a regenerative silence, the kind that does not empty a room but fills it with whatever was left unfinished. [564] Her own earliest sentences returned to her out of the creases, and she found she no longer agreed with the woman who had written them. [589] She held both women loosely, as she held the bird, as she held the river. [604]

One morning she found the second gallery gone, replaced by a stairwell she did not remember and a sky the color of wet slate. [628] The swan sat warm in her hands. [635] She creased one more fold, small as a held breath, and set the sentence loose on the water. [653] Then she burrowed back into the stacks to witness whatever came next. [665] Behind her the refuge along the river filled with light and then with nothing at all. [681] Peace went on crumbling there, patient as paper, and the silence made her new again. [696]
