The tide gave nothing, so Maren fished it anyway. [9] She worked the sound wave garden at the shore's edge, where standing waves rooted like rows of barley and frequency bloomed instead of seed. [33] At night the sleepwalking constellations came down and walked the rows, bending every stalk with their cold gravity. [51] When they drifted wrong, the rows zigzagged instead of rippling. [61] That was the window. [65] When patterns break themselves, an echo loosens. [72] Maren wanted one echo. [76] The echo of a goddess who had drowned in the first tide and sung it afterward. [92]

In her hand she carried a rail spike. [100] It was forged iron, flattened at the head, and it held the memory of every blow that had ever driven it. [121] Pressure that had already happened. [126] That was all an echo was. [132] So the spike was a hook of a kind. [141] The garden was voice-worked. [145] Nothing in it registered feeling until the feeling was transliterated into sound. [157] So Maren transliterated first, always. [162] She could not say grief; she had to say what grief was shaped like in the mouth. [179] Then she spoke it. [254] Halfway across the garden they turned and began to zigzag, and every frequency in the ground snapped into broken teeth. [274] The pattern was breaking itself. [279] Maren did not shout.
