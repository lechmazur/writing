The fog had kept the orchard district folded shut for three centuries, and Emmer liked to say it was the only custodian that never took a day off. [28] He was a mural artist of the vivid school, the kind who signed nothing and finished everything. [45] Where other painters ground their colours, Emmer painted with light. [55] He found the fresco fragment half-buried in silt where the east wall of the old packing house had given up. [75] The plaster still carried a painted pear tree, heavy with fruit, and pasted over its face was a strip of exposed film gone milky with damp. [101] Someone had once set the whole district out under a camera and trusted chemistry to keep it. [118] Chemistry had been as forgetful as everyone else. [126] Cael found him at midday, though midday in the fog was only a paler shade of the same grey. [145] Cael was an urban explorer and the district's reluctant archivist, and he kept a knowledge network of every wall, ledger, and trade in the rows. [170] He knelt at the fragment and read its grid out loud the way other people read weather. [187] Row nine, row eleven, the pear row that yielded one bright fruit worth the whole season's keeping. [204] Emmer listened, and something in him that had been locked up tight began to ache pleasantly. [220] He had not come here to document the ruins. [229] He had come to rediscover lost joy, and he suspected it was buried in exactly the place that had forgotten how to be cheerful. [253] They worked within the fold of a daydream, that slack hour when the fog thinned to breath and the morning replayed itself inside its own dusk. [279] Emmer unrolled his sheets of old lantern glass and set the fragment flat against the packing house wall. [297] He began to paint with light, laying a cold blue along the trunk and a thin gold up the branches, so that the pear tree stood out of the plaster and into the open air. [332] Where the film had gone milky, the light bloomed hottest, and the lost exposure showed through at last. [350] It was not a pear tree after all, or not only one. [362] It was the whole orchard in leaf, a thousand lit trees standing in their rows and waving like people at a parade nobody had ever thought to throw. [390] Cael made a sound like a man recognising an old friend's laugh in a crowded room. [406] On the far side of the wall, the fog carried the noise of the orchard back to them, and it sounded like a radio voice of trees left on in an empty house. [439] They made the display before the fold closed. [447] Emmer hung the fragment at eye height and fired every lamp he owned at it, and the ruined packing house became a window onto the season the district had lost. [477] For twenty minutes the fog outside went gold at the edges. [488] People came, the way they always do when something glows. [498] They stood in the wet and laughed at nothing and cried at less, and Emmer understood that the joy he had come looking for had never been stored in the film. [529] It had been waiting in the part of the district that could still be roused. [544] Then the fold let go. [549] The lamps cooled, the milky film went opaque again, and the fog folded the packing house back into itself like a page being shut. [573] The mural kept nothing of the light. [580] That is the cost of painting with radiance; the wall forgets it the moment you stop. [596] Emmer did not mind. [600] He had seen the pear row, the one bright fruit worth the whole yield, hanging where the fog could not argue with it. [623] Cael wrote the whole thing into his network before the light was even gone, and by evening three other explorers had promised to clear the windows of the packing house so the next display could breathe. [659] Emmer walked out through the rows with his lamp under his arm, grinning at the fog like a man sharing a joke only he was in on. [686] Behind him the district settled back into its long grey sleep, holding one warm hour it would not admit to. [706]
