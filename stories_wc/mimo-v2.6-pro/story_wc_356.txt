At the turning of the tide's thought, when the estuary neither filled nor drained, Maren planted the corpse flower seed. [20]
She knelt on a plank of the Lareth Quay banyans, three hundred feet above the mudflats, and pressed the seed into a pot of peat she had carried up nine ladders. [51]
The village hung in the canopy like held breath. [60]
Bridges of braided vine, roofs of stitched leaf, smoke from the drying racks of Aroma Aevum drifting between trunks. [79]
Maren was an ambitious herbalist, and ambition in a place this quiet had to be handled like a hot pan. [99]
She was also olfactorily sensitive past the point of courtesy. [109]
Every bruised leaf, every spice shed from the racks, every low tide's sulfur rose in her at full voice. [128]
To raise the foulest bloom on earth was not vanity for her. [140]
It was a way of standing still inside a storm she could not close her nose against. [157]
Her grandfather had taught her to read the estuary. [166]
He called the slack water the undercurrent of wisdom, the hour when the water held no opinion. [183]
Nothing moves, he said, so everything can be heard. [192]
He was gone four years now, and what she had left of him was a family custom. [209]
He had doodled on the interiors of wrapping paper his whole life. [221]
Small ferns, tide charts, a woman's profile, a seed cross-hatched like a walnut. [234]
Maren kept the habit. [238]
Her herbalist's paper, the same sheet she wrapped tinctures in for the people of the Quay, carried her journal inside the fold. [260]
She journalized there in small pencil, between doodles of the seed's imagined anatomy. [273]
Day one. The seed is heavier than it should be. [283]
It sits in the peat like a closed ear. [292]
Weeks passed in the hush of the canopy. [300]
She misted the pot at slack water each day and said nothing to anyone. [314]
The other herbalists of Aroma Aevum thought she was raising ginger. [325]
She let them. [328]
Ambition kept in the dark grows a better root. [337]
A spear of leaf broke the peat on a morning when the tide ran thin and gray. [354]
It climbed fast, a green column with a ruffled collar, taller than Maren inside a month. [370]
She wrote: the bud is a promise I am not sure I want kept. [384]
Then she doodled the spadix, the hollow spike at the flower's center, and shaded it until the pencil tore the paper. [405]
The bud swelled for nine days. [411]
On the tenth evening the wind dropped and the estuary went to glass below the banyans. [427]
The village went quiet in the way villages do before something they have heard of but never seen. [445]
Maren carried the pot to the highest platform and sat down cross-legged with her paper in her lap. [463]

Then the smell came. [480]
Carrion, sweet rot, old fish, hot copper, every foul note her gifted nose had ever flinched from, arriving all at once and staying. [503]

This was the storm she had planted, and she meant to sit inside it until it became weather. [525]
She wrote: the stench is a door, and I am holding the handle. [538]
Around her the blooms lasted ten hours, and she used all of them. [551]
She breathed through the foulest air of her life and found, somewhere past the seventh hour, that the smell had a shape she could rest in. [577]
Her grandfather's slack water, arriving in the one place she had expected no stillness at all. [593]
The undercurrent of wisdom, she wrote, is not calm. [602]

The great leaf folded in on itself and the smell thinned to river mud and then to nothing. [643]
Below her the tide turned, one direction giving itself to the other without a sound. [658]
Maren sat a while longer with the pencil idle in her hand. [670]
She had wanted to prove she could grow anything. [679]

When the next slack water came, she would be up on the plank again, misting a pot of peat. [737]
