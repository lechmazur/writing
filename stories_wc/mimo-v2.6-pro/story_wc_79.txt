The gate rusted shut behind me in the spring, and no one has lifted it since, and no one tries. [20]
I keep this record now for whoever reads it after the last of us has gone down into the silence. [40]
Our city rides upon the shells of gargantuan turtles, whose backs are wider than any harbor I have ever charted. [60]
I am the spiral-shell cartographer, which is an old title for the person who reads where the animals will walk. [80]
For forty years the turtles bore us across the Silt Sea, and the careful maps I made were always correct. [100]
Then one night they stopped at the eastern shoals, and they have not moved again, and the sun-washed silence began. [120]
That was the summer I took the reed whistle, a tin of water, and walked south alone to find them. [140]
I went because I am quietly driven, and because the question would not leave me in the heat of noon. [160]
The silent watchers on the horizon had stood there since before my grandmother's birth, and no one had questioned them. [180]
I meant to walk to them and ask what they wanted, and to learn why the turtles had stopped walking. [200]
Beyond the last shell the sea turns to black silt, and my boots broke through its crust to the ankles. [220]
There I found the first marker of the lost expedition: a splintered mast, a boot, and a wax-sealed chart case. [240]
Three years earlier the Council had sent nine surveyors out with that chart case, and not one had come back. [260]
They had gone to read the watchers as one reads a written wall, but the watchers never once answered them. [280]
Inside the chart case their work was soaked but legible, and I knelt in the silt and read it there. [300]
They had written nothing at all about the watchers, only rows of numbers and the old grammar of the shells. [320]
I read that grammar through pattern languages, the way any spiral-shell cartographer is taught to read it from early youth. [340]
Growth rings of a shell repeat in sets of seven, and each set answers a question asked of the animal. [360]
The surveyors had found a ninth set where an eighth should be, and they had marked it again and again. [380]
Then I found the reed whistle lying half-buried in the silt, and it still held the shape of a mouth. [400]
Our people use such whistles to sing the turtles toward water, and a silent whistle means the singer is gone. [420]
I put the reed whistle to my lips and blew, and the sound went out flat across the dead shoals. [440]
Then I heard it far out across the water: an answering note, thin and high as a crack in glass. [460]
It was another whistle, and the person who held it had been calling for three years without food or rest. [480]
I walked toward it all that day and half the next, and I found her on a rise of shell. [500]
She was the ninth surveyor, and she did not look up, for her eyes were fixed upon the distant horizon. [520]
Beside her lay eight bundles wrapped in sailcloth, and I understood then that the others had not simply run away. [540]
The work of the world is like this: someone must stay and keep asking, even when no answer comes back. [560]
We sat through the white afternoon, and I gave her water, and she gave me the pattern they had found. [580]
It was not a map but a sequence of shell patterns, each one breaking the grammar of the one before. [600]
Then the shell beneath us let out a splitting sound, and a crack ran eastward across the turtle's great back. [620]
Light came up through the fissure, not sunlight but a whiter glare, and the silent watchers all turned at once. [640]
For the first time in any memory, they gaped, and the sound of it crossed the Silt Sea like weather. [660]
That was the answer, or the beginning of one, and it cost us the great shell beneath our trembling knees. [680]
The turtles felt the fracture and began to move again, and our city rode south with them at first light. [700]
I brought the ninth surveyor home, and I brought the reed whistle, and I bring the crack in the shells. [720]
Some day I will ask the watchers myself, and until then I keep the gate shut and the maps open. [740]
