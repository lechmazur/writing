Maren had been the archive's arcane linguist for eleven years, and the door at the end of Hall C had never once been opened by anyone else. [27]
Beyond it lay the extinct language archive, a cathedral of dead syllables where vowel-shifts hung in glass and consonant clusters slept in cedar drawers. [51]
Maren worked there now to unsnarl what the previous archivist, her mentor Solenne, had left twisted and half-burned at the end of their last argument. [76]
The silence between them had lasted two years, and every week of it had settled like dust on the glass ink well on Maren's desk. [101]
It was an old instrument, blown green and thin, with a stopper carved from the last branch of a tree whose name no one living could pronounce. [128]
Maren uncapped it now with hands that were carefully reckless, the way she did everything that mattered. [145]
The task before her was the Song of the Meridian Dead, the longest surviving symphony of truth in any extinct tongue. [166]
Every archive swore by symphonies of truth: texts so woven that to read them honestly was to change what you believed. [187]
Solenne had once read the Meridian Song aloud and wept for three days, and after that she would never say a word she did not mean. [213]
That was how the silence began: a promise kept too long, until keeping it felt like an accusation. [231]
Maren blamed herself for every quiet month of it. [240]
Now Solenne was dying in a house at the edge of the marsh, and the letters she sent were only dates and the sound of her breathing. [267]
There was no time left to waste on carefulness, and none left to spend on recklessness either. [284]
The Meridian Song had to be read before paths diverge, the archivists' old phrase for a life ending its last unbranching road. [306]
But the text was snarled: three stanzas folded into one, vows tangled with curses, and one entire column erased by Solenne herself. [328]
To unsnarl it, Maren needed the method the Meridian singers themselves used: via interconnected promises. [343]
Each line of the song bound the reader to a vow, and each vow unlocked the next line, so that no one could finish the poem without becoming someone who kept their word. [376]
Maren made the first promise in a whisper and dipped the glass ink well's stopper into nothing at all, because the ink was made of speech. [402]
Green light climbed the walls as the stanza unfolded, a cautious exuberance, like a bird testing a healed wing. [421]
She promised to speak plainly to Solenne before the week was out; the second stanza uncurled. [437]
She promised to stop hoarding the translations that should have been shared; the curses loosened and drifted up like smoke. [457]
She promised to forgive the erased column even without knowing what it said; the folded stanzas separated, humming. [475]
At the center of the song was one line in a language older than the archive, and Maren's careful recklessness told her to read it aloud with the glass ink well raised to catch whatever came out. [512]
She spoke it, and the well filled with green light that was almost ink, and in it she saw Solenne's face as it had been the day of the argument. [542]
It was a symphony of truth, and it did not flatter either of them. [556]
It showed Maren the promise she had broken first, the one she had made as a girl to never let anyone grow lonely in her care. [582]
Solenne had grown lonely; the silence had been her way of keeping Maren's vow alive so it would not have to be broken again. [606]
Understanding that, Maren closed the well and laughed a little, tearfully, with a cautious exuberance that surprised her. [624]
She wrote Solenne a letter that night, not dates but everything, and promised to visit before the paths diverge. [643]
The Song of the Meridian Dead lay unsnarled at last, its promises interlocked like fingers, its truth sounding clearly through the dead halls. [666]
Maren did not sleep that night; she listened to the archive hum, and she dared to believe she could restore trust after silence. [689]
In the morning she would walk the marsh road with the glass ink well wrapped in her coat, and she would knock, and this time the door would open. [718]
Until then the symphonies of truth kept playing in the dark, patient and new. [732]
