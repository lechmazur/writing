The port city had no streets above ground level, only the corridors of a towering labyrinth of shipping containers stacked forty high, and Wren worked its middle registers. [28]
He was a labyrinth maintenance worker, which meant he knew which hinges screamed and which gangways bowed under winter rain, but his real duty was narrower. [54]
He identified people's walks. [58]
One glance down a steel corridor and he could tell a customs clerk from a courier, a mother hurrying for the last ferry from a man following someone. [86]
It was identification work, and the harbormaster paid him for it, though not enough to advertise. [102]
In his coat pocket he carried a zodiac coin his mother had given him before she shipped out, a brass disc worn smooth on the rim. [128]
He did not think of it as a good-luck piece. [138]
He thought of it as proof that someone had once known his walk from a hundred others and been right about it. [160]
Months before, he had whispered a single sentence into a dead corridor and let the tower carry it, because he wanted to prove that whispers travel farther than shouts. [189]
Rook arrived at Register Nine with folded maps fanned in one gloved hand. [202]
She never unfolded them. [206]
She navigated by crease lines, by the softness of a fold that had been made and remade in the same place, and she had crossed three ports to reach the tower without once opening a sheet. [242]
She had come to challenge the reader of walks. [251]
The terms were old harbor law: he would name her route through the tower before she walked it, and if he was right she would leave her maps on his bench; if he was wrong he would give up the coin. [292]
Wren felt the weight of that and said nothing, because saying nothing is how a whisper starts. [309]
Then she put a key on the table. [317]
It was iron, toothed, and had no apparent lock anywhere in the port, though half the tower had tried it. [337]
She called it the second stake, and told him the contest would be decided on it as well. [355]
Outside, the geomagnetic storm that had been forecast all week came down over the port like a hand closing on a lamp. [377]
Every compass in the tower went soft and useless, the aurora threw green light between the container stacks, and Wren watched Rook's hands the way he watched feet. [405]
She palmed the key when she thought he was not looking. [416]
He let her. [419]
Calculated spontaneity was the only method he trusted: you plan the shape of a thing and let the details arrive on their own. [442]
He named her route in one breath. [449]
North stair to Register Nineteen, west across the load bridge, down the service ladder she would refuse to use because she never changed level where a sightline ran open, then the long catwalk to the dead end at Container 12. [489]
She laughed once, low, and walked it. [496]
Wren watched from the register rail and counted her tread, the slight drag on the third step of every ladder, the way she braced left before a turn. [524]
She was exactly where he had said she would be, and she knew it. [538]
At the dead end she raised the key and the question that had crossed three ports with her: what does it open? [560]
Wren came down to her, unhurried, and told her the truth the towers had taught him: the lock is a person's walk, and the key is the pattern of steps that fits it. [593]
That is why he could name her route before she took it, and why the iron key in her hand has no apparent lock of iron anywhere in the port. [623]
It opens the one thing it fits, and nothing else. [633]
Rook set the key on the catwalk grating and looked at him for a long moment. [649]
Then she took her folded maps from her coat and laid them on the bench, creases up, and did not pick them up again. [673]
The geomagnetic storm took the port's power out for exactly sixty seconds at 21:40. [687]
In that dark Wren felt for the zodiac coin in his pocket and held it, the only fixed thing in the tower. [709]
When the lights came back the nav wall was still down, so the crew threw the projected night map onto the container faces themselves: every corridor and gangway in white lines forty stories tall. [743]
For the rest of the shift the tower showed its own face to the sky. [758]
Wren walked home the long way, in the same pattern he had named to Rook, so that anyone who cared to read a walk would know it for a reply. [788]
No one shouted anything that night. [794]
