The palace hung from the cavern ceiling like a chandelier of iron and crystal, its towers and balconies fastened to giant stalactites that dripped with mineral light. [27]
Prince Aldren stood at the eastern gallery, his coat torn at the seam, his hair matted with dust and engine grease, a bedraggled figure against a world that gleamed with polished gears. [59]
He was remembering the songbirds that had once nested in the palace gardens before the mechanists replaced every living thing with brass and clockwork. [83]
Now even the wind arrived through calibrated vents, and the only music was the tick and hum of ten thousand springs. [104]
In his trembling hands he held the egg. [112]
It was pale as milk glass, faintly speckled with copper, and impossibly light -- the last egg of the Duskfinch, a bird that had vanished from the sky three generations ago. [142]
Aldren had hypothesized about everything: the temperature the egg required, the mineral composition of its shell, the frequency of vibration that might coax it toward hatching. [168]
He had read crumbling manuals and consulted orrery-keepers and signal-smiths, anyone who might hold a fragment of forgotten biology. [187]
Each hypothesis led to another, a lattice of maybe and what-if that he assembled nightly on sheets of vellum pinned across his laboratory walls. [211]
He needed the bird not for ornament or sport but for something deeper -- to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world that had forgotten how to ache. [237]
Beauty was the one rebellion his kingdom had not yet dismantled. [248]
Through the discovery of the egg in a sealed reliquary beneath the eastern stalactite, he had felt a tremor pass through him, a quaking reverie that blurred the line between dream and desperate hope. [282]
The discovery itself had been accidental: while repairing a corroded anchor chain, he had cracked open a panel and found the reliquary nestled among fossilized roots. [308]
Inside, the egg lay on a bed of spun copper thread, preserved as though the world above had not learned to forget. [330]
Now he sat at his worktable and assembled a cradle for the egg -- a suspension frame of silver wire and warm stone, calibrated to mimic the Duskfinch's ancient nest. [359]
His fingers moved with the precision of a watchmaker but the uncertainty of a child, each knot tied twice, each wire tested and retied. [383]
He hypothesized about the past, about whether the Duskfinch had sung at dawn or dusk, about whether its plumage held violet or rust, about whether the flaw in the world had begun the day the mechanists declared that nothing living need be beautiful ever again. [428]
He hypothesized about the future, about whether the egg would ever crack, about whether one small creature could undo an empire of gears, about whether beauty could survive a flawed genesis -- for surely the mechanists' world was born wrong, a creation that prized function so absolutely it devoured wonder. Aldren shielded the egg with his body until the machine hummed away. [512]
Bitterly sweet was the silence that followed, sweet because the egg still rested safe, bitter because safety in this place meant concealment and fear. [536]
He thought of the gardens that had been paved with cog-shaped tiles, of the fountains replaced by steam vents, of the sky he had never seen except through filtered glass. [566]
Every lost thing stacked upon every other until the weight pressed against his ribs like a second heartbeat. [584]

"I do not know if you will wake," he murmured. [619]
"But I have assembled everything I have left to give." [629]
Outside the gallery, the suspended palace swayed in its mineral cradle, anchored to giant stalactites that had held it for centuries, and the world ticked on in its flawless, joyless precision. [660]
Aldren closed his eyes and imagined a wingbeat -- the first in a hundred years -- and in that imagined moment the mechanical world seemed, for just an instant, to tremble with something ancient and alive. [694]
It was only a reverie, but it was enough to keep his hands from shaking as he worked through the night, hypothesizing about everything, assembling hope from wire and stone and the pale promise of an egg that should not exist. [735]
