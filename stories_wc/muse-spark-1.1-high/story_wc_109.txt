<story>I had come three days to find the pebble-shore oracle, but the sea had taken her beach. [17] The fishers said she now kept watch in the storm-tossed ridge fort. [29] I found her in the gatehouse, thin and soot-marked, turning a cold slate over in her hands. [46] She was not ancient, only tired, and mildly obsessed with how stones remember heat. [60] When I entered she spoke in a marbled hush that made the rain feel thinner. [75] In her lap lay a flaking gold leaf booklet I had carried from the drowned library. [91] I told her the pages showed no words, only bright wounds where gold had lifted. [106] She nodded as if expecting it and lifted the cover with a careful thumb. [120] You want to know why you kept it, she said. [130] I did, but more I needed to draw a map of an endless night that had settled over me after my brother died. [153] It had no stars and no roads, only a weight at the edge of sight. [168] She opened the booklet and the gold caught the poor light and threw it back. [183] Each page held an ambered hush, silence pressed until it hardened. [194] The monks who made it did not write, she told me, they listened and sealed quiet between breaths. [212] To understand it you would need to retell what was not written. [224] We waited while the storm-tossed ridge fort shuddered and loose shingles fell to the yard. [239] Hours passed and the dark outside grew complete. [247] Then, when only one star remains in a dimming sky, the wind fell as if cut. [263] That star hung red and low, a nail holding the dark to the sea. [277] The oracle set the open booklet on the stones and hovered her hands above it. [292] Then she began by summarizing silence. [298] She would study a page, close her eyes, and speak what lay at its edge. [313] This is the quiet after oars lift and before the first drip. [325] This is the quiet between a name called and no answer. [336] Each naming was a point, and I began to see she was charting distance with absence. [352] A night without light still has length, measured by how long quiet can hold. [366] I had thought a map needed lines, but hers needed pauses. [377] Her voice stayed in that marbled hush and the gold dust on the pages seemed to warm. [394] My brother's endless night rose again and filled the gatehouse, cold and without border. [408] The oracle did not pull away but turned another page whose gold was flaking. [422] Here, she said, is where you are, by summarizing silence that has lasted three winters. [437] I wanted to weep but the ambered hush inside the booklet held even that still. [452] She asked what I would do with a map that showed no exit. [465] I said I did not need an exit, only proof that the night was a place and not everything. [484] She closed the flaking gold leaf booklet and pressed her palm to its cover. [498] Gold stayed on her skin, a faint constellation in her lifeline. [509] Then she took up her slate and drew with a wet stone, dark on dark. [524] It was a spiral that never closed, marked with small crosses where each quiet had been. [540] You are here, she said, touching the outer turn, and the endless night is around, but see it has edges. [560] Outside the storm had spent itself and the fort dripped quietly. [571] The last star was gone and the sky was simply black. [582] I took the booklet and the slate, feeling the night still present but now with a shape I could carry. [602] On the path down I heard her retell my footsteps to remember them. [615]</story>
