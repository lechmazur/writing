<story>The light returned wrong after the eclipse ends, thin and buzzing at the edges of the canyon. [17] Mira stood in the middle of the wind-carved encampment and watched the shadows snap back into place like elastic. [36] She was the albino tracker the desert crews hired when their maps failed, and her skin seemed to hold its own eclipse beneath it. [60] Two years ago a flash flood had taken her brother, and she had watched the water take forever to close over his hand. [83] Since then she needed to discover why time slows in crisis, not as a scientist but as a witness. [102] The crews thought she tracked footprints, but she tracked moments that refused to disappear. [116] She was consciously wild by choice, trimming her hair with a knife and refusing the canvas goggles that would dim the world. [138] Her only instrument was an old field kit and an artificial hummingbird drone that hovered when she breathed out. [157] The drone had been her brother's, and she kept its solar wings polished despite the dust. [173] That morning the encampment's water clerk spilled his ledger ink across the sand, black blood spreading fast. [190] It was not ordinary ink but disappearing ink, designed to fade in sun so that ration debts could be argued away. [211] Mira felt a serene anger rise in her throat when she saw him shrug at the loss. [228] She knelt before the stain could vanish and released the drone low over the spill. [243] Through the drone's eye she did not see random blotches; she saw time trying to write itself. [260] She understood then that she could read crisis through ephemeral patterns in spilled ink, the way others read smoke. [279] The ink thinned, and in its shrinking edges she saw the flood again, not as memory but as diagram. [298] The drone shivered, magnifying the moment where seconds stretched like salt-water taffy over her brother's reaching fingers. [315] Mira had always thought time slowed because the brain filmed faster, but the ink showed something else. [332] In crisis the world itself hesitated, offering a narrow ledge before the fall. [345] That hesitation was mercy, and also cruelty, because you could see every choice and still fail. [361] The wind cut through the canyon and lifted the tent flaps of the encampment like lungs. [377] The ink was almost gone, pale veins in the sand, and the drone recorded its last curls. [394] Mira closed her eyes and let the pattern burn onto her retina, understanding that she had to let her brother go. [415] When she opened them, the sand was clean, the ledger empty, the clerk already forgetting what he had lost. [434] She called the drone back to her palm, feeling the heat of its frantic wings against her scarred skin. [453] The encampment would move on by dusk, following water that only trackers could smell after an eclipse. [470] Mira would go with them, carrying not a map of the desert but a map of that stretched second. [489] She no longer needed to chase the flood, because she finally knew what the slowness had been for. [507] It was not to save anyone, but to remember them completely before they disappeared from the light. [524] She tucked the drone into her chest pocket, where it ticked like a second heart against her ribs. [542] The wind carved another thin line into the sandstone above, and she read it as a promise. [559] Behind her, the children of the encampment laughed, their voices clear and unburdened by eclipses. [574] Mira smiled, and the smile held all her serene anger, calm as a stone and sharp as its edge. [593] She began to walk toward the canyon mouth, where the light was no longer wrong. [608]</story>
