<story>Mara had not asked to become the reluctant caretaker of a cursed orchard, but the valley left her no other inheritance. [21] The trees grew sideways from salt and sorrow, bearing glassy fruit that shattered instead of falling. [37] Beyond the cliffside labyrinth patrolled by mechanical birds dropped into white mist, and that was where travelers vanished. [55] She had watched them from her ridge for two lonely seasons, and finally decided she needed to guide lost travelers to safety. [77] In the stone shed her grandmother left, she found a forgotten blueprint pressed between seed catalogs and rot. [95] The blueprint showed not the orchard but the labyrinth, with safe lines drawn offhandedly exact, as if the draftsman had memorized the wind. [118] It was a graceless beauty, all sharp corrections and coffee stains, but it held. [132] Mara could not enter the labyrinth herself, because the birds knew her heat and would dive. [148] They were old guardians, copper-feathered and blind, listening for footsteps that pressed too hard. [162] So she studied the blueprint and learned a different way, leaving impressions without touching. [176] The orchard gave her the means, as cursed things sometimes do. [187] At its heart grew a single tree that never fruited, only dropped one small nut each dusk onto the cold soil. [208] The nut was warm and heavy, humming faintly, and when held it tugged toward the cliff. [224] Mara cracked it open with a river stone and found not meat but dust that glittered like iron filings. [243] According to the margin notes, the dust would answer intention if scattered from above. [257] She climbed to the outer wall of the labyrinth before the night frost forms, because after that the stones become treacherous and silent. [280] Below her, three travelers huddled in a dead end, their lantern throwing weak circles. [294] The mechanical birds circled higher, their wings ticking like a hundred clocks. [306] Mara breathed and poured the dust, thinking only of a clear line to the exit. [321] The dust fell slowly, then settled into shallow hollows in the rock, leaving impressions without touching it twice, confirming the method still worked. [344] From above they looked like nothing, but at ground level they caught light like footprints made of frost. [362] The travelers saw them and stared, unsure. [369] Then the youngest knelt and understood, and they began to assemble their courage to follow. [384] Mara scattered more dust, marking each true turn, each false wall to ignore. [397] She had to move quickly along the rim, knees scraping, hands raw, because the blueprint's hour was narrow. [415] One bird dipped low, buzzing near her, and she froze, holding her breath until it lifted. [431] Below, the travelers followed the shining impressions through the gray corridors, their steps light. [445] The birds did not hear them, for dust makes no sound. [456] At last the labyrinth opened and the travelers spilled onto the lower road that led to the orchard gate. [475] They knocked, cold and bewildered, and Mara let them in, pretending she had only been pruning. [491] In spring she would try again, even though she hated the orchard, because reluctance was not the same as refusal. [511] That night she placed another small nut on the sill, waiting for tomorrow's dusk to ripen it. [528] Outside, the frost finally formed, sealing the labyrinth paths till morning, keeping everyone safe for a little while. [546] She slept with the blueprint folded under her pillow, its lines already fading again. [560] Dreams came of birds rewired to carry seeds instead of warnings. [571] When she woke, the orchard was quiet, the cursed branches bowed low as if listening for footsteps that would finally return.
