<story>Mara had not expected to be called back to the station after the shutdown. [39] Her assignment was the time dilation observation deck, where minutes inside stretched into months for the world below. [57] The deck was built for a dozen scientists, but now it held only her, and it held her in an echoed silence that made her own pulse sound intrusive. [86] Command said she was there to monitor drift, but she had accepted the post to turn confusion into exploration, because she no longer understood what home meant. [113] The planet below turned slowly, its storms temporally stretched into smooth marble swirls that never seemed to break. [131] She marked her days by the old calendar, though time here hardly aligned with any clock she had known. [150] Keeping a family custom of sketching the night sky on anniversaries was the only ritual that kept her hands steady. [170] Today was the fifth anniversary of the flare, and she laid out paper on the scuffed console and drew what she could not photograph. [194] Tucked into her sleeve for luck was a patch from her mother's field coat, a gilded compass rose applique that had survived wash after wash. [219] Her mother used to say the rose did not point to what you wanted, but to where you were. [238] Her mother always insisted true north was not a place on a map but a decision you renewed each night. [258] Mara had laughed at that as a teenager, but now the words felt less like poetry and more like procedure. [278] Midway through her sketch, the sensors pinged, telling her she might glimpse the anomaly before it folded again. [296] She pressed her forehead to the cold viewport and watched the dark between stars thin like wet paper. [370] She scrambled to add the new light to her drawing, lines shaking, not because she feared being wrong but because she wanted to be understood by whatever was on the other side. [402] When the seam sealed, the deck clocks stuttered and resumed, and she felt time settle back into its familiar weight. [422] On the paper two skies overlapped, the familiar field of stars and the single bright axis she had just seen. [442] She lifted the applique from her sleeve and set it beside the sketch, its threads catching the panel glow like a tiny sun. [491] She realized she was no longer waiting for rescue or for forgiveness, she was waiting for the moment when confusion finally tipped and became curiosity again. [517] Carefully she logged the entry, not as an anomaly report but as an anniversary note, so whoever came after would know she had been here and had looked outward. [546] Outside, the planet kept turning, indifferent and beautiful, and inside the deck held steady in its quiet orbit. [564] She touched the compass and smiled, because for the first time since the shutdown she knew which way was forward, even without knowing where it led.
