<story>Silas had not wound a clock forward in thirty years. [10] The villages called him the counter clockwise clock maker because his hands ran backward to give drowning men more time. [30] He lived alone in the mysterious lighthouse on a rocky island that the charts pretended did not exist. [48] He only left his tower under the slow exhale of low tide when the causeway of black slate briefly rose from the sea. [71] At the lowest point the hunger stone emerged, a pitted slab carved by his grandfather to mark famine. [89] The stone was supposed to warn people, but Silas read it as a promise that everything must eventually return. [108] He was neatly inventive in the way of men who have too much time and too few listeners. [126] For decades he collected wreckage that washed against the rocks and cataloged it by weight and sorrow. [143] Last winter a fishing trawler broke apart and left him a sealed chest containing an ancient yoyo of bone and lead. [164] The toy did not spin properly, its axle wobbled like a planet refusing orbit. [178] Inside the lighthouse he spent months listening to it, convinced its failure was intentional. [192] He learned its secret through reverse-engineering the puzzle of an ancient yoyo rather than playing with it. [209] The halves unscrewed to reveal a chamber of mirrored glass that caught no light but seemed to store it. [228] Silas understood it as latent radiance, light that had waited centuries for a reason to return. [244] According to the scrap of parchment inside, the monks who made it believed time could be unspooled and rewound by hand. [265] He kept reading because he wanted to step beyond fate's design, not merely measure it with his backward clocks. [284] The hunger stone outside was now fully exposed, and the wind keened through its carved letters. [300] He felt bleak exhilaration as he realized the tide would not return for hours and he was completely alone with the choice. [322] If the yoyo could release its stored light, he could feed that light into the lighthouse lamp and turn the beam backward. [344] A backward beam would not warn ships away but call the lost ones home. [358] Silas decided to initiate the sequence that night, while the sea was still withdrawn. [372] He polished the mirrored chamber, set it into the lamp housing, and aligned the gears of his largest counter clock. [392] The tower shuddered as the low tide held its breath longer than it ever had. [407] The hunger stone sang, a low hum from deep in the rock. [419] Darkness pooled at the base of the island while the lamp above began to drink rather than burn. [437] For a terrible second nothing happened, and Silas thought he had built only another beautiful failure. [453] Then the latent radiance woke and spilled out, not as light but as remembered daylight from every ship that had ever passed. [475] The beam struck the clouds and bent backward, pouring yesterday over the water. [488] Out on the exposed seabed, figures formed from spray and light, crews and children who had drowned within sight of his tower. [510] They did not look angry, only surprised to be remembered. [520] Silas wept, and his neatly inventive hands finally stopped trying to fix time and simply let it flood. [538] The yoyo in the lamp housing spun perfectly now, rising and falling without a string. [553] The tide began to return, but slowly, as if reluctant to erase what had been written in light. [571] When the water covered the hunger stone again, the figures faded, not vanished but folded into the radiance itself. [590] Morning found Silas asleep against the lamp, his backward clocks all stopped at the same impossible hour. [607] He had not cheated fate, yet he had made it stutter, and that stutter was enough. [623] The mysterious lighthouse on a rocky island still stood, its light now steady, waiting for the next low tide to remember. [644]</story>
