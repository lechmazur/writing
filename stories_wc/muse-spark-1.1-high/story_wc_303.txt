<story>Elara Voss had spent twelve years as a provenance researcher for the Imperial Archive, tracing stolen things back to their stolen homes. [22]
They sent her to the edge of the empire because no one else wanted to certify the Ashen Collection, a vault of beautiful impossibilities. [46]
It was housed in the velvet observatory at midsummer, where the black dome was lined with drapes to soften the sun that never fully set. [71]
Inside, the air smelled of old paper and living moss, and because she was accidentally telepathic with plants she could hear the collection breathing. [95]
Every vine curled around the shelves whispered keep us, keep us, which made her job harder than usual. [113]

Its label claimed it was grown, not built, by the mountain nuns of Karst, who sang winter into glass. [209]
The provenance felt wrong, like a story with holes, a perforated destiny stitched to hide the punctures. [226]
She did not use imperial instruments to test it, because she had a different way of knowing things. [244]

The patron tore his promise in half and left, and she was left jobless but not without work. [682]
Outside, the summer world was waiting, full of small true things that needed a patient hand to bring them home. [702]
