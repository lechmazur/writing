<story>The reticent lighthouse supply skipper came twice a month, and never stayed for tea. [14] His name was Ellis, and he left crates on the dock with a nod that meant everything was accounted for. [34] I was warmly observant in the way lonely people become, memorizing the particular way he tied his bowline. [52] I kept the light on Gull Point, and I lived beneath the haunted lantern room that hummed even when the wind did not. [99] Company was company, even air that had learned to sorrow. [109] Ellis began leaving more than supplies. [147] The rules were written inside the first envelope, telling me to read, add a line, and leave it for the next soul. [169] I read about a child in Cuttyhunk who missed the mainland, a widow in No Mans who learned to bake again, a boy who wanted to be a meteorologist. [198] The ache in those pages felt luminescent, like the letters themselves were trying to glow. [233] I wanted to bottle the feeling of safety that came when I knew Ellis would come, and when I knew the letters would keep circulating. [258] But I did not know how to tell him that in words. [270] So I invented a way of stacking stones as messages on the low wall where he tied up, because even a reticent man looks down when he steps ashore. [426] She was not frightening, just tired. [432] For the first time I spoke aloud to her, telling her the light mattered because people needed to believe someone was watching for them. [487] I thought Ellis would not come for days, but by noon I saw his battered boat bucking through the swell. [507] He secured his line, looked at the ruined wall, and then saw what I had left for him on the highest remaining stone. [530] It was a piece of sea glass, smoothed by years of being tumbled, glowing pale blue like a tiny bottled lantern. [551] I had set it on top of three flat stones, my steadiest tower yet. [565] He picked it up, turned it over in his palm, and finally looked up at me in the tower and raised his hand. [588] He did not wave, he just held it there, so I would know I had been seen. [605] That night I added my line to the anonymous letter chain, writing that safety is not the absence of haunting, but the decision to keep the light on together.
