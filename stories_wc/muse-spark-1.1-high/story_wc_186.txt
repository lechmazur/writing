<story>Mara had kept the light before there was anyone left to see it, and after the collapse of civilization she kept it still. [23] On the coast the old maps still called her the beacon-tower custodian, though the tower itself was gone and only its lamp remained. [44] The lamp was not oil nor electric but a lattice of quiet light held inside a glass humming pavilion that sang when the wind passed through its seams. [72] Inside the lattice, at its heart, hung a broken crystal prism, dark since the night the sky fell. [90] Everyone said it could not be mended. [97] Mara had not been born retrocognitively blessed, she had become so when the prism shattered and cut her palm. [116] With blood on glass she had seen not the future but the past that had clung to objects, the echo of their making. [139] In the prism she saw the hands of the engineers who had grown it from sand and starlight. [157] They had called its purpose a shard of hush, a piece of engineered silence that could calm storms and clear static from minds. [180] When the world broke, the noise came back louder than ever. [191] Mara remembered that on the same night the prism went dark, a child in the valley had drawn an identical fracture pattern in the dirt without ever seeing the tower. [221] She remembered another coincidence, a gull that had landed on the pavilion roof at the exact hour the lattice failed, staring at her with knowing eyes. [247] Most people would call these chances empty. [254] Mara held them like prayers, to prove that coincidences have meaning and that the world still rhymed even after breaking. [274] If the fracture in the dirt and the prism shared a shape for a reason, then there was a pattern underneath grief. [296] So she decided she would rekindle the light, not with fuel but with memory. [310] She would do it by tracking star paths etched on the pavilion floor, the old navigation guide the engineers left for when machines forgot. [334] Each night she turned the dark prism under the heavens, aligning its broken edge with a different constellation. [352] The pavilion hummed louder when she found a true line, as if the glass remembered its purpose. [369] On the third night she saw it, not with her eyes but with her retrocognition, the day the prism was sealed. [390] The engineers had sung to it while it cooled, three notes low and clear, and Mara sang them now though her voice cracked. [413] The broken crystal prism shivered. [418] Light did not return as flame but as a thinning of dread, a hush that spilled from the crack and brushed the sea. [441] Mara felt the hill behind her hold its breath, the scrub and thistle holding it with her. [458] Far down in the valley a faint answer glowed, where that child, now old, had kept a lantern from dirt and spit and stubbornness. [482] Two lights looking for each other across darkness made a line between them that had not existed before. [500] That was proof enough. [504] She laughed, and the sound caught in the glass and came back softer. [517] She had always thought hope would be brass and loud, but this was thistle-blown hope, light and stubborn and catching on everything. [539] The pavilion sang now with a single clear tone, and the hush inside the prism deepened instead of fading. [558] Mara placed her scarred palm against the fracture and felt all the coincidences click like gears. [574] She would tend this low light not because ships needed guiding but because someone needed to prove that meaning could be rebuilt. [596] And as long as she kept her watch, the world would remain less broken than it had been. [614]</story>
