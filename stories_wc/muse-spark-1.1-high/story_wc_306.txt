<story>Mara had worked for ten years as a conscience for hire, renting out her judgment to captains who did not want to carry their own. [25] They came to her breezy lighthouse at dawn, because that was when the sea was honest and men were too tired to lie well. [49] Tonight the client was different, arriving not by boat but on foot, after the campfire's last spark dies on the rocks below. [71] He was a warden from the inland city, a man famous for being sincerely insincere, who could smile while signing a death warrant and mean both gestures. [98] He climbed the iron stairs without knocking, and found Mara oiling the lamp mechanism with the patience of someone who has stopped waiting for rescue. [123] I need to borrow your certainty, he said, because I have to decide whether to open the dam and flood the lower quarter. [146] Mara did not answer at once, because her work was to welcome nuance into certainty, not to hand certainty out like bread. [168] She gestured to the table where a hollow mirror shard lay in a bowl of salt water, humming faintly whenever the wind hit the tower. [193] The shard had been her teacher's, a tool that showed not a face but the space behind a decision. [212] Years ago, when she first put on the job like a coat, she had believed doubt was a weakness to be cut away. [235] Now she understood it as the cloak of doubt, something you wore to keep yourself from burning others with your bright convictions. [257] The warden stared at the shard as if it might accuse him. [269] Tell me what you hear in the chimes, Mara said, pointing to the cluster of copper tubes she had hung from the lamp rail. [293] For three years she had practiced deciphering wind chime selections, learning which notes meant a coming squall and which meant a drowned memory rising. [317] This morning the wind was restless, picking out a pattern she recognized as grief trying to disguise itself as policy. [337] The warden closed his eyes and listened, and his shoulders dropped for the first time since the climb. [355] I am afraid, he whispered, that if I do nothing, the city starves, and if I act, I become a murderer for the sake of engineering. [381] Mara felt the old familiar ache of protected vulnerability, the feeling of holding another person's trembling truth without trying to fix it. [403] She reached up to unhook the smaller chime, the one that sang only when the wind came directly from the north. [424] She pressed it into his palm, cold and vibrating. [433] Hold the hollow mirror shard over this, she said, and tell me what you do not see. [450] He obeyed, and the light from the lamp passed through the empty center, throwing a perfect circle onto the whitewashed wall, empty of any reflection. [475] That is the choice, Mara said softly. [482] It is not starvation or murder, it is your belief that you must decide alone and cleanly. [499] Certainty is a lighthouse that never moves, but doubt is the wind that proves the light is still needed. [518] If you flood the quarter, you must also stay to carry water to the people you displaced. [535] If you keep the dam closed, you must stand in the market and tell the farmers why their grain is short this winter. [558] The warden looked at the circle of light and for the first time his smile matched his eyes. [576] He slipped the chime into his coat and stood, lighter not because his burden was gone but because he had learned how to carry it with both hands. [604] Mara watched him descend, and then turned back to the lamp, already turning the wick up against the coming day. [624] Outside, the chimes kept playing, and she listened, no longer for answers, but for the questions the wind was brave enough to ask aloud. [648]</story>
