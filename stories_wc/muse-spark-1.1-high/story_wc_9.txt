<story>After the birth, Mara could not remember the hours between feeding and dusk. [13] She had been an onyx-ink cartomancer before, reading futures in cards stained black at the edges. [29] Now every future folded into the same small cry, and the past leaked like water from her palms. [47] She began to walk the night house for proof that she still existed outside the nursery. [63] Her mother said it was normal to lose time, but Mara needed to find where lost time accumulates. [81] The old decks showed a card she had never drawn, a meadow floating in starlight. [96] She took it as a summons rather than a prediction. [106] Behind the linen closet where she had hidden during labor, the air shivered and opened onto a midnight glade above clouds. [127] The grass was silver and did not bend, and the sky was a low, bruised violet that pressed on her throat with choking beauty. [151] In the center of the glade stood a table set for a child who had not yet learned to sit. [171] On it lay her cards, blank, and a tiny chipped saucer with a dolls' tea set saucer crack running through its painted roses. [194] Mara understood then that this was her cloudbound repentance. [203] Years ago she had read fortunes and told new mothers they would never be lonely, and charged them for the lie. [224] She had not known how time could be stolen without malice, simply by love being too heavy. [241] Now the glade demanded a truer reading, one she could not perform with ink alone. [256] She remembered a family custom of tracing hands on birthday tablecloths, the outlines overlapping year after year until the cloth became a map. [279] Her mother had cut out the oldest hands when they frayed, saving only the smallest. [294] Mara knelt and placed her own hand on the blank card, feeling the paper drink her warmth. [311] She tried to trace its edge, but her fingers shook so badly she became willingly perplexed, letting confusion soften her grip. [332] To be willingly perplexed meant not forcing the line to close. [343] She unfasten the locket at her throat where she kept a curl of her newborn's hair, and laid the hair across the card. [366] The hair curled on its own, drawing a trembling outline that was not her hand at all. [383] It was the shape of all the minutes she thought she had lost, piled like soft snow at the edge of the glade. [406] She saw them clearly now, not empty but full, each holding a breath, a rocking, a whispered name. [424] Repentance was not punishment here, but accounting. [431] She lifted the cracked saucer and poured the collected minutes into it, and the saucer did not overflow. [449] Instead the crack sealed, thread by thread, with light. [458] The cards around it filled with faint ink, not futures but records, tiny hands traced over and over on invisible cloth. [479] When she returned, dawn was just touching the nursery curtains, and the baby was still asleep, his fist curled exactly where her own hand had been. [505] She no longer felt hollowed by forgetting. [512] She took a clean muslin tablecloth from the drawer and laid it across the kitchen table for later. [530] For the first birthday, and all the others, she would trace his hand and her own, letting time accumulate where it could be seen. [554] The glade had taught her that nothing is truly lost, only set aside until we are ready to read it. [574] She placed the mended saucer on the shelf above the crib, where its rose pattern caught the morning light. [593] Now when the hours blurred, she did not panic, but breathed and looked for the small, shining pile at the edge of ordinary things. [617]</story>
