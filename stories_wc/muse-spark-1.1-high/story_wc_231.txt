<story>I had not seen the eccentric lighthouse keeper leave his tower in three winters. [14] At nautical twilight the world loses its edge, and that was when his lantern always failed. [81] The keeper descended his iron stairs faster than I thought possible for a man of eighty. [97] Inside the skiff lay a young man in a wool coat, too still, his skin the color of wet paper. [117] The stranger was alive for maybe a minute after we reached him. [129] He did not speak, but lifted a shaking hand to his chest and then let it fall. [146] Later I would understand this was his final gesture, and I needed to decode a stranger's final gesture. [164] On his lapel, pinned crooked despite the journey, was a dried Edelweiss boutonniere. [177] The white petals were brittle and yellowed at the edges, nothing a sailor would wear. [192] The keeper lifted the man ashore and sat with his own borrowed memories flickering behind his eyes. [209] He had a habit of collecting other people's shipwrecks and retelling them as his own. [224] The police boat would not come until morning, so we kept vigil in the tower's base. [240] The lamp above us turned and turned, its beam softly persistent against the fog. [254] I began to investigate, not the death itself but the careful placement of that flower. [269] The keeper insisted on silence and asked me to help by observing gaps. [346] We spread the boat's contents on the table: a tin cup, a soaked letter, and no identification. [363] The letter ink had bled into a blue bruise, leaving only an address in the mountains and the word forgive. [383] That is when the keeper removed the dried Edelweiss boutonniere and placed it in my palm. [442] He told me how his own brother had left the valley at seventeen wearing the same flower and never returned. [462] The stranger's gesture had not been pointing to the flower but pressing it down, as if returning it. [480] He was not asking to be remembered; he was asking to be released from a promise he could not keep. [500] That understanding settled over us, quiet and complete, in the small circle of lamplight. [514] At dawn the mist thinned but the dock still glowed faintly opal where the water caught the sun. [532] We buried the young man above the tideline with the boutonniere still folded in his coat pocket. [549] The keeper did not add him to the official log, because some arrivals belong only to the sea.
