<story>The wise orchard pruner named Silas had tended the border trees for forty years. [14] He was insatiably creative in the quiet way of old craftsmen, always finding a new graft or a kinder cut. [34] Upon the hush of budding hope, when winter had loosened its grip but spring had not yet declared itself, he climbed to the high garden. [59] The high garden was a verdant parapet beside a waterfall, built by his grandmother to catch mist. [76] There the trees grew half in this world and half in another, their roots drinking memory as well as water. [96] Silas understood this was not magic but weaving between worlds, a natural craft like his pruning. [112] For years the veil had been thinning, and the colors on the far side were bleeding out of sight. [131] The apple trees that once bore fruit like small lanterns now held only pale, tired leaves. [147] Silas knew he was called to succor what remained before wonder faded entirely. [160] His motivation was simple and stubborn, to preserve the color of wonder for those who would come after. [178] In his pocket he carried an heirloom seed, saved from the first tree his grandmother had planted on the parapet. [198] It was small, dark, and warm to the touch, as if it remembered sunlight from both sides. [215] The old man knelt where moss met stone and listened, and the world held a radiant hush. [232] The waterfall spoke in low syllables, and the other world answered through the leaves. [246] Silas realized he could not save the orchard by watering or cutting alone. [259] The trees were losing their story, not their sap. [268] He must help them remember by capturing essence, not by forcing growth. [280] From his tool roll he took not shears but a shallow bowl of polished river stone. [296] He set the bowl beneath the oldest branch where mist collected thickest. [308] Drop by drop, the waterfall's breath condensed, carrying with it flecks of light that had slipped from the other side. [328] The light was amber and violet, the exact shade of wonder he had seen as a boy. [345] Silas lifted the heirloom seed and lowered it into the gathered essence. [357] The seed drank, swelled, and gave off a soft chime. [367] In that moment Silas felt the two worlds align like grain in a well-made joint. [382] He pressed the seed into the damp crevice between stones where soil had gathered over decades. [398] He did not prune the dying branches that day. [407] Instead he trimmed back the veil itself, his hands moving as if tying a graft between weather and memory. [426] The work was slow, patient, and strangely joyful. [434] When he finished, the parapet glowed faintly, as though sunrise was caught under the moss. [449] Days passed, and the hush of budding hope deepened into true spring. [461] A green shoot pushed from the crevice, but it was not an ordinary apple sprout. [476] Its leaves shivered with both wind and elsewhere, catching light that had no source in this sky. [493] Silas sat nearby, his pruning knife resting, and watched the new tree learn to be in two places at once. [513] Bees came that had never been seen before, their wings humming with colors. [526] Children from the village followed, drawn by the sound of the waterfall made bright. [540] They asked what he had planted, and he told them it was wonder, kept alive by attention. [557] In time the tree bore fruit that held within each skin a small, shifting sunset. [572] Whoever ate it remembered something beautiful they had not yet lived, and smiled. [585] Silas, the wise orchard pruner, had not restored an orchard but had tended a threshold. [600]</story>
