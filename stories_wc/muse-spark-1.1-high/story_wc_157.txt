<story>In 2047, three years after thoughts become taxable, Mara made her living by forgetting for other people. [17] She was a charming rogue scholar who had been expelled from the university for teaching unlicensed semantics. [34] Her office was the back bench of an entomologist's greenhouse that the city had forgotten to audit. [51] The greenhouse hummed with leaf-cutter ants and glasswing butterflies, and among them grew a single trellis of bottle gourds. [70] Inside one dried fruit was the last gourd rattle seed from the old library, a smooth black bead she used to seal bargains. [93] The Revenue Mind Bureau could tax any declared intention, but they could not tax a pattern they could not read. [113] That was why Mara founded a secret language club in the soil itself. [126] She was candidly cryptic by nature, telling the truth in a way that sounded like a lie to machines. [145] When new clients arrived, she would say, pay me not to remember your rebellion. [159] They laughed, then understood, then paid in compost and cutting rights. [170] Her method was simple and invisible: joining community gardens to plant message patterns that only the unaudited could harvest. [189] Rows of carrots became Morse, beans became binary, marigolds marked dead drops. [201] The city inspectors saw only urban beautification, but the members saw instructions, warnings, poems. [215] The problem began when the Bureau announced a Thought Yield Audit for all greenhouse districts. [230] They would bring resonance scanners that could warp private recollections into taxable confessions. [243] Mara knew she could not hide the garden network from the new machines, only make it meaningless. [260] The converging chaos of city sirens, pollinators, dripping irrigation, and whispered translations rose in the greenhouse until the glass shook. [280] Her students arrived early that night, twelve gardeners with dirty hands and bright eyes. [294] We have to burn it, said Jun, the youngest. [303] Mara shook the gourd rattle seed between her fingers and smiled. [314] No, she said, we have to make them look harder and find nothing worth taxing. [329] To stand guard over your own spark of defiance, you cannot hide it, you must share it until it is everywhere. [350] She gave the order then, not in words but by replanting the central bed. [364] Instead of coded resistance, she planted nonsense, lunch, love letters, terrible jokes in perfect, beautiful spirals. [380] Every secret language club needs a moment when it chooses to become just a club. [395] At dawn the Bureau agents entered the entomologist's greenhouse with their scanners humming like angry mosquitoes. [411] They scanned the gourds, the ants, the twelve trembling gardeners, and Mara herself. [424] The machines tried to warp the signal into profit, but found only open, generous thinking. [439] Thoughts of tomato yield, of neighbor birthdays, of how light looks through a wing. [453] You are in violation of nothing, the chief auditor said, confused. [464] Mara bowed, candidly cryptic to the end, and offered him a gourd. [476] He declined, but Jun laughed and the laugh spread through the converging chaos like pollen. [491] That spring the gardens overproduced, and so did the untaxed ideas. [502] People came from other districts not to join a conspiracy, but to learn how to plant carrots that spelled hello. [522] Mara stayed in the entomologist's greenhouse, guarding her own spark by tending everyone else's. [536] She kept the gourd rattle seed in her pocket, no longer as a key, but as a reminder that some things grow best when undeclared. [561] The Bureau never returned, because abundance is harder to audit than secrecy. [573] On quiet nights she still teaches her charming rogue scholar trick, how to forget on purpose. [589] You hold the thought lightly, she says, and you plant it outside your head. [603] Then it belongs to everyone, and no one can tax it. [614] The ants carry crumbs of the message, the butterflies carry color, and the gardeners carry the rest. [631] In this way the secret language club survived not as a secret, but as a language. [647] Mara watches the new seedlings warp slightly toward the glass, not from coercion but from wanting the sun, and she understands. [668] Defiance, after all, is not a thought to be guarded alone, but a spark passed hand to dirty hand. [687] So she waters, waits, and answers every scanner with a louder rattle of seeds.
