<story>Mara had not been born a Pioneering cart glider, she had become one by bolting canvas wings to a salvage cart and driving it off cliffs for science. [28] Now she lived in a Gears-and-cogs workshop floating on an acid sea, which is exactly as terrible for property values as it sounds. [51] The workshop groaned, clicked, and occasionally tried to evolve its own better lungs Throughout the evolution of what the old manuals called Platform Nine. [75] Mara loved it with irreverent awe, the kind of love you reserve for a god that leaks oil on your boots. [96] She was Steadily curious, which according to her aunt was a polite way of saying incapable of leaving any broken thing alone. [118] In the center of the shop, under a dome of pitted glass, hung the Bell, and her whole life's absurd project was To ring a bell no one hears yet. [148] Not a metaphorical bell, but a three-ton alloy monstrosity that was supposed to resonate in a frequency only future atmosphere would carry. [170] The problem was power, translation, and that the Bell's clapper had been lost before she was born. [187] Mara found its replacement lodged in the workshop's gullet, a blackened Hunter's arrow tip fused to a spar that had fallen from orbit. [210] The workshop had swallowed it years ago and was using it as a toothpick, which tells you everything about the workshop's table manners. [233] The arrow tip sang when touched, not with sound but with intention. [245] Mara's hand-computer tried to analyze the song and instead displayed a single line of code it labeled Yearning algorithm. [264] The algorithm was not searching, it was missing, and it missed with such specificity that it created gravity around the absent thing. [286] Mara understood then that the clapper did not need to strike hard, it needed to want loudly. [303] She set to building a translator between wanting and ringing, because apparently she had time between bailing acid off the deck. [324] First she tried to hammer sensor grids directly into the arrow tip, which resulted in a lot of sparks and a firm personal rebuke. [348] Then she remembered the children's toy she had salvaged, a brass kaleidoscope whose mirrors were clouded with sea salt. [367] When she looked Through the images in a kaleidoscope, the Yearning algorithm stopped being code and became weather. [385] She saw the arrow tip's entire lineage, not as a weapon but as a promise that had never learned how to land. [407] Every hunter that had touched it had aimed at something beyond meat, at a future where hunger did not exist. [427] The yearning had stacked Throughout the evolution of that simple tool like sediment turning to mountain. [443] Mara, who had spent her childhood trying to Transliterate seabird cries into engine timings, finally had a language worth learning. [463] She did not write the translation, she grew it, wiring the kaleidoscope optics into the computer and letting the Bell's hunger read itself. [486] For three days the workshop drifted while its gears turned her new code into torque, into potential, into please. [505] On the fourth morning, when the acid sea was flat and reflective as a failed mirror, she hoisted the arrow tip clapper. [527] She had mounted her cart glider rig to the Bell's frame so that when the wind caught, she would ride and ring at once. [551] The sail snapped, the cart lurched, and for one holy irresponsible second she was exactly what the old stories meant by Pioneering cart glider. [575] The Hunter's arrow tip kissed the Bell, not with force, but with the accumulated yearning of every hand that had ever aimed it forward. [599] The Bell did not clang. [604] It exhaled a note so low and so wide that Mara felt it leave the acid sea entirely and go looking for air that had not yet been made. [633] No instrument on the platform registered the sound, but a hundred miles downwind, a boy building a kite from trash paused because he suddenly wanted to look up. [661] Mara hung in her harness above the hissing planet, laughing with reverent blasphemy at the beautiful useless perfect thing she had done. [683] The workshop, steadily embarrassed by her emotions, clicked louder to pretend it had done all the work. [700]</story>
