<story>Mara had been a harmonic stone skimmer for forty years before her knees finally gave out. [16] She came to the towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes because the old maps called it the Still Point. [38] The towers were not built, but grown from the fallen ribs of leviathans that had died before the first road. [58] Wind cut between them and made every footstep sound like three. [69] She dragged her low sled into the shadow of the tallest arch and sat. [83] Her hands, scarred from a lifetime of tuning slate on moving water, were historically reverent whenever she touched old work. [103] From a sewn pocket she took a stone arrowhead that a child had pressed into her palm years ago at the river market. [126] It was chipped, ordinary, and perfect. [132] The boy had told her it still knew where it was going. [144] Mara had kept it because she never learned how to answer that kind of faith. [159] Now she needed to learn to navigate without moving. [168] The skimmers of her youth could read a river by the tone it made under a flat stone. [186] That craft meant nothing here where water had never run. [196] What remained in her was a pulse left wandering, a low harmonic that had lost its river. [213] She set the arrowhead on her knee and tried to appraise its balance. [226] Through centuries of layered dust, the canyon floor held thousands of such points, each dropped and forgotten. [243] She brushed a thin veil away and saw the floor glitter faintly. [255] All that waiting had not made them less true. [264] A shadow crossed the sun. [269] High above, a flock of cliff swifts cut between the bone towers, turning as one body. [285] Mara had watched birds all her life because every skimmer knows water and wind are the same lesson. [303] She lifted her eyes and began to read through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight. [321] The swifts banked left where the echo was thickest, then broke around an unseen column of air. [338] Their turn marked the canyon throat more precisely than any map line could. [351] Another turn, tight and sudden, showed a shear where wind folded back on itself. [365] She breathed with them until her wandering pulse found their rhythm. [376] It was not mysticism, but work, the same weathered resolve that had kept her skimming stones when her arms shook. [396] After an hour she lowered her gaze to the arrowhead again. [407] It had not moved, yet now she knew why it had been knapped this way. [422] The maker had weighted intention into the stone. [430] Direction without travel. [433] She placed her palm flat to the dusty floor and felt the canyon answer back through bone. [450] Her pulse was no longer wandering. [456] It was waiting, and she finally knew how to wait with it. [468] When dusk pooled between the towers, she stood slowly, knees aching, and left the arrowhead where it lay. [486] She did not need to carry it to keep its direction. [497] Above her the first stars showed, and the swifts were gone, but their path stayed etched in the cooling air. [517] Mara closed her eyes and felt the canyon map itself inside her chest without shifting her feet. [534] She had spent a lifetime trying to cross distance, and now she understood stillness could be a kind of travel too. [555] Night filled the canyon with low hums as wind moved through hollow bone, and she listened until sleep came. [574] By morning her sled was lighter, though she had left nothing behind that mattered. [588] She would not skim stones again, but she would teach others how to stand still and find north. [606] The canyon kept its echoes, patient as ever, holding her new quiet alongside its old songs. [622]</story>
