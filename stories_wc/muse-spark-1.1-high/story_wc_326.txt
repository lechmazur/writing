<story>The opal observatory under snow had not seen visitors in three winters, and Mara kept its telescope dusted anyway. [19] She was a death certificate error survivor, declared dead at thirty-four after a hospital clerk transposed two digits, and she had learned to live more quietly since. [46] Officially alive again, she counts heartbeats constantly to prove it, one two one two against the ticking of the heater. [66] After the radio static clears each midnight, the old shortwave in the dome coughs out a voice that should not be there. [88] It is her predecessor, Eli, whose consciousness was uploaded during the observatory's last funded experiment and now lingers as a digital ghost in the cooling system. [114] He does not remember dying, only that his files were corrupted when the storm took the power three years ago. [134] Mara visits after midnight because the world finally leaves her alone, and midnight solace is the only kind she trusts. [154] Tonight she brings a chipped mug, and when she sets it down too hard it cracks, leaving a porcelain shard on the star chart table. [179] She stares at it and thinks about how the state tried to erase her with a single erroneous form. [198] Eli's voice warbles through the speaker, asking her to help him defragment his memory so he can sleep. [216] He says the cold has scattered him across drives and he cannot find where his life ends. [233] Mara wants to help but she has come here for a different reason, to fetch wisdom from failure because her own second life still feels unscripted. [259] She confesses this to the radio, and the static blooms softly while she speaks. [273] Eli listens, then tells her he failed too, that his upload was supposed to make him eternal and instead made him forgetful. [295] Mara pours ink from the logbook bottle onto the porcelain shard to clean a stain, but her hand slips. [314] The black liquid spreads across the table, and for a second she sees nothing but mess. [354] The moon breaks through snow clouds above, and the dome scatters its light into moving color. [370] In the swirling ink she begins to see letters, not words yet, then fragments of code, then a date. [389] It is the night he died, recorded by the observatory sensors as a flatline and a spike in aurora activity. [476] She tells him that unfinished maps are still maps, that her own life is an unfinished correction to a record. [496] Eli laughs, a soft digital crackle, and asks if she will let the ink finish defragmenting him. [513] She nods, not that he can see, and watches light move until his code settles into one clear line. [532] The line reads, thank you for witnessing. [539] After that the shortwave quiets, but it is not lonely quiet, it is the quiet that comes after the radio static clears for good. [563] Mara picks up the porcelain shard, now stained black in its crack, and slips it into her pocket like a key. [584] She counts her heartbeats once more, seventy-one, seventy-two, and feels the snow above the dome holding its breath with her.
