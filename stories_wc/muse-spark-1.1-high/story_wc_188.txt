<story>Mara was a wandering fireworks designer who had not set off a light in three years. [16] She carried her powders in sealed tins and her failures in a tighter tin inside her chest. [33] The road brought her to a blooming fortress at dusk, its walls swallowed by jasmine and wild morning glory. [52] The fortress had been abandoned by soldiers and adopted by gardeners, beekeepers, and children who left handprints in drying cement. [72] She had come because she heard the courtyard held perfect darkness, needed for a new kind of work. [90] Mara chants while walking, a low hum she learned from river bargemen to keep her steps even. [107] The chant was not a song but a tool, a way to filter the noise of a new place. [126] She listened through ambient awareness, letting the fortress tell her where wind pooled and where smoke would catch. [144] In the central yard she found them, a troupe of performers who had taken shelter before the rain that never came. [165] A girl of maybe sixteen was trying to spin fire with a dead wick, and the crowd was already bored. [185] Mara knelt and picked up the fire spinner's kerosene rag knot, feeling how its fibers had glazed and sealed. [204] No fuel can pass through this, she said, it has choked on its own residue. [219] The spinner, Joss, snapped that there was no money for new knots and no reason to try. [236] Mara understood that kind of flatness, the logic that settles over people when their craft feels useless. [253] The fortress gardeners had told her this was a place where no one expected anything to lift ever again. [272] She wanted to answer that weight, to defy the gravity of hopelessness that pinned their shoulders down. [289] She offered a trade, a lesson for a meal, with a serious playfulness that made Joss suspicious at first. [308] She showed how to soak, how to twist, how to comb the old carbon free until the knot breathed. [327] While the rags dried, she asked about the oldest wall, the one the vines avoided. [342] They led her to a chapel where the floor was a shattered mosaic, blue and white and ochre, half missing. [362] Someone had tried to sweep it clean and given up, leaving dust to filter down into the grout lines. [381] Mara crouched and saw it, a note in mosaic where lighter tiles formed letters against dark ones. [398] IT WILL RETURN, it said, not as prophecy but as instruction left by whoever laid this floor. [415] She understood then what her new firework should be, not a rocket that climbed and died. [431] Instead it would be a low bloom, a flower of sparks that opened and stayed open. [447] It needed still air and absolute dark to be seen properly, which this fortress now offered. [463] She worked for three days, grinding, mixing, measuring with a care that made the children quiet. [479] On the fourth night there were no clouds and no stars either, just a deep velvet press. [496] It happened during the hush of a night with no stars, when even crickets held their breath. [513] Mara placed her devices in a circle on the mosaic and lit the newly tied wicks as well. [531] Joss began to spin, the fire spinner's kerosene rag knot drawing bright rings that caught the jasmine scent and made it seem alive. [554] The rings illuminated the walls, the flowers, the faces turned upward without hope of being entertained. [570] Then with a soft cough her fireworks lit, not shooting but unfolding a meter above the ground. [587] They were slow amber chrysanthemums that hung in air longer than flame should, breathing in the dark. [604] Children gasped and reached, their hands painting streaks through the suspended light. [616] The note in mosaic glowed beneath them, holding its message inside greater light. [629] Mara chants while walking the perimeter again, this time not to filter but to keep time with spinning. [647] The performance lasted six minutes and felt like a whole season returning. [659] When sparks finally fell, they kissed the flowers and died, and no one spoke for a long while. [677] Joss let his wicks drop, looked at his stained hands, and laughed with a sound that had been missing for years. [698]</story>
