<story>Elias had not lifted a brush in three months, not since the commission that broke him. [16] He was a crestfallen portrait artist who could no longer see a face without seeing its failure. [33] When the letter came offering him shelter in a lighthouse on the outer coast, he took it without reading the second page. [55] The tower was decommissioned, its lamp long dark, but inside its stone drum was a forgotten library inside a lighthouse that no chart remembered. [79] The books were swollen with salt and years, their spines turned inward as if hiding. [94] On the central table sat a broken tarot card box, its lid split, its cards spilled like small, frightened birds. [114] Elias touched it and felt the room shiver. [122] For weeks his world had been a fragmented reality, where a nose slid off a cheek and eyes refused to match. [143] He thought the box was to blame, or perhaps a mirror. [154] What sane man keeps a library in a place built for warning people away, he muttered, and then answered himself, as he always did. [178] He questions rhetorical questions because silence feels like an accusation to him. [190] Is not every portrait just a beautiful lie, he had once asked his students, and now the question hung in the damp air waiting again. [215] The library breathed. [218] At night the circular shelves groaned, and Elias understood the keeper's final note: do not stay when the walls close in. [239] But he stayed, because he had promised himself to descend into necessary darkness rather than skim its surface forever. [258] The other word for healing, is it not just honest seeing. [269] He decided to work again, not to paint faces but to redesign what a face could be. [286] He would redesign by untangling threads, literally, because each portrait that had failed him was still in his trunk, stitched together from canvas strips he had torn in frustration. [315] He pulled the fragments out and laid them across the stone floor, eyes beside mouths, light beside shadow. [333] From the broken tarot card box he drew the cards without looking, using their random symbols as guides for placement. [353] The Tower, the Moon, the Thread that no deck ever had but here was, hand drawn and frayed. [371] As he worked, the library did what the note promised. [381] The shelves inched inward, stone grinding on stone, when the walls close in for true at midnight. [398] Air grew thin and damp and heavy, and the lamp room above remained dark because his reason for being there was not to be seen. [423] Why pretend we want light all the time, he whispered to the closing dark, and why pretend darkness is empty. [443] The pressure made his ears ring with a luminescent ache, a pain that also glowed, as if every unlived portrait inside him was finally turning on its own small lamp. [473] He did not stop untangling. [478] He separated weft from warp, shame from line, flattery from likeness, until the floor was not fragments but possibilities. [497] When there was no room left to stand, when his back touched cold stone and old books pressed his knees, he finally saw it. [521] The fragmented reality had never been a failure of his hands but a commission he had given himself to keep everything broken apart so nothing could be judged whole. [550] In the final inch of space, with dust in his lungs, he began to stitch. [565] Not faces, but a single vast face made of all faces, luminous and unashamed. [579] The box gave one last soft crack and lay still. [589] At dawn the tide found the walls returned to their width, the library open again like an eye after blinking, and in its center Elias slept beside a canvas that had never been there before. [624] It was a portrait of the lighthouse keeper, though he had never seen her, and she was smiling as if someone had finally answered. [648] On the back, in his own handwriting, he had written: why not stay. [661]</story>
