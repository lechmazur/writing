<story>Ell was an otherworldly sentimentalist who collected moments the way humans collected shells. [13] He had lived for three generations of stars, and yet he wept each time a seed failed to open. [32] His home was a nebula garden strung between dead satellites, where ionized roses bloomed for centuries and then folded. [51] Each rose remembered the light that made it, and Ell remembered each rose. [64] This was not memory but generational patterns repeating in his glass blood. [76] His mothers had tended, his mothers' mothers had tended, and their sorrow at loss had become his love of tending. [96] Now the garden was dying because the old sun that fed it had cooled. [110] Ell wanted to share light without using fire, for fire scarred the petals he loved. [125] He owned nothing bright enough except a calculating machine gear left by the first gardeners. [140] The gear was dull bronze, warm to the touch, and it approximates infinity when spun correctly. [156] His mothers had warned that spinning it would call the dark between stars. [169] Ell decided to transit the void anyway, carrying the gear in his chest cavity where his heart should have been. [189] He left his garden across the quiet sigh of falling petals, as every blossom loosened one leaf to mark his departure. [210] The silence that followed had a troubling serenity, as if the garden approved of being abandoned. [226] Outside, light was thin and space was larger than any mind could hold. [239] Ell could not see, so he navigated by sonar pinging, sending soft notes from his throat and listening for them to return. [261] The pings came back bent by dust, by gravity, by grief. [272] After a long fall that felt like centuries, he reached the cold sun. [285] It was a sphere of iron ash, barely warm, turning slowly in its own memory. [300] Ell placed the gear against it and pushed. [308] The gear caught, ground, and began to spin, drawing not energy but attention. [321] Light without fire requires a witness, his mothers had written, and Ell finally understood. [335] The gear sang, and the cold sun remembered it had once been bright. [348] A filament kindled deep inside the ash, pale blue and steady. [359] Ell felt his own glass blood warm for the first time. [370] He opened his chest and let the new light enter him, refract, and scatter. [384] From his back, borrowed light spilled in ribbons toward the nebula garden he had left. [399] The garden would not see it for a hundred years, but the roses would still be waiting, because some things wait longer than dying. [423] Ell stayed with the cold sun, pinging softly now not to navigate but to keep it company while it relearned how to burn. [446] His mothers would have called him foolish, and they would have been proud. [459] In the garden, far behind, a single bud opened in darkness, having felt his light before it arrived. [477] That is how generational patterns finally break, not with a shout but with a quiet debt repaid forward. [495] Ell knew he would never return to walk among the ionized roses. [507] Yet the troubling serenity remained, because he had learned that love does not need to hold what it keeps. [526] The gear turned slower now, its edge worn thin by doing what it was never designed to do. [544] It had been built to calculate, but calculation is only longing given numbers. [557] And longing, Ell thought, approximates infinity better than any machine ever could. [569] So he held the singing gear, and let his light go on traveling without him. [584] Across the nebula garden, across the quiet sigh of falling petals, his mothers would feel warmer without knowing why. [603]</story>
