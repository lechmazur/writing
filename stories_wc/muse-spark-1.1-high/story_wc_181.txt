<story>The archive did not have walls, only rows of light. [10] Elias was its bewildered curator, seventy years old and cataloging seeds that remembered being trees. [25] His post was the astral orchard blossoming on a lonely asteroid, where each plant grew in vacuum and sang faintly when touched. [47] The orchard was simply complicated, obeying no calendar yet blooming exactly when it was needed. [62] Elias had come here to pursue forbidden knowledge, the kind his order forbade because it rewrote the reader. [80] Between the first and last page of the Codex Lumen, there was a gap where no ink held. [98] The gap was not empty. [103] It was a field that displayed whatever future gardener would one day try to grow there. [119] Last cycle, the field showed Elias a small disc, pale green and perfectly oval. [133] He learned to call it the pistachio shell cameo blank, because it waited for a face that had not yet been carved. [155] Its provenance made no sense, for he had not planted it and the orchard had no import manifests. [173] That was when the orchard began exhibiting nonlinear inheritance, where cuttings bore fruit before their roots existed. [190] One morning he found blossoms that smelled exactly like his childhood kitchen, though that kitchen had not yet been built when the seeds were stored. [215] Elias tried to log the anomaly, but the catalog kept erasing his notes. [228] He would need to inquire of the Codex itself, not its indices. [240] The Codex, however, only answered in the small sanitary cubicle behind the observatory, the one place where echo failed and thought sharpened. [262] There, Elias had developed a method of having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms, where the plumbing hummed like distant stars and nobody expected holiness. [285] He sat on the cold seat and stared at the blank. [296] The pistachio shell cameo blank warmed in his palm, though it had no metabolism. [310] What if I am inheriting this from myself, he thought. [320] The thought arranged everything into a strange, brightened disquiet, as if sunrise had arrived at midnight and apologized for being early. [341] In that quiet, the Codex finally displayed text inside the gap, not written but remembered backward. [357] Line one read: you carve what you find. [365] Line two read: you find what you have already carved and sent back. [378] Elias understood then that the orchard survived only because its curators looped their knowledge, each planting a memory for the previous one to discover. [402] His forbidden knowledge was not a secret to steal but a responsibility to transmit across an impossible direction. [420] Hands shaking, he took the tiny etching tool from his pocket and began to carve his own profile into the pistachio shell cameo blank, not as vanity but as a marker. [451] The shell resisted, then gave, each curl falling upward as if returning to the tree it never had. [469] When he finished, his face stared back at him from an object older than his hands. [485] He walked out to the orchard and pressed the cameo into the soft vacuum-soil beneath the central light-tree. [503] The orchard pulsed. [506] In the gap of the Codex, a new entry fixed itself permanently, a record of a curator who had learned how to belong to a circle instead of a line. [536] Elias was no longer bewildered. [541] He felt the old bright disquiet settle into a calm luminosity, the knowledge that he would be grown before he was born and remembered after he would be gone. [570] He returned to cataloging, humming the song the vacuum plants taught him. [582] Somewhere in the bathroom, the plumbing still hummed, waiting for the next impossible question. [596] Above him, the astral orchard kept blossoming, patient and endless, on its lonely asteroid. [610]</story>
