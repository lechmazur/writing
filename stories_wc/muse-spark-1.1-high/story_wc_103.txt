<story>I did not retire, I rooted, trading the country estates that hired a hedge maze designer for the damp hush of the living bridge garden in the hills. [28] The bridges here are not built but coaxed, ficus roots braided over decades into arches that breathe and thicken each monsoon. [49] I came here to discover the texture of time, because a maze is not about hedges but about how long a minute feels when you are lost. [76] My clients had wanted puzzles for their children, follies for their lovers, but what I was really selling was a thicker stretch of afternoon. [100] I kept fading photographs of every maze I cut, Polaroids taped in a ledger, and now the green bleeds out of them even in the dark. [126] The boxwood is going white, the yew is going silver, and my memory is going the same way, retrocognitively trapped in the summer I finished my last and largest maze. [156] I can still see the boy who ran the ash spiral in Kent, his mother calling, but the sound has no source and the garden here does not have an ash spiral. [188] It is a quaking reverie, this living, this remembering, as if my heart beats out of rhythm with the slow thrum of roots growing over stone. [214] Yesterday, or a day that calls itself yesterday, I found something cold in the lattice where two bridges kiss, a halo fragment chipped from plaster and old paint. [242] I had placed a cheap saint in the center of a Warwickshire maze for a widow who wanted absolution, and the statue wore exactly this cracked gold rim. [270] Touching it brought the past louder, the hedges I had shaped roaring up around my knees here where no hedges should ever grow. [293] The garden keeper, a boy with feet dark as bark, told me the fragment was a seed that trees mistake for light. [315] I held it against my palm until the sweat made the gilt weep, wanting to know whether time was rough or smooth, looped or frayed. [340] I began to map the living bridge garden not as it looks but through hyperbolic geometry, where parallel lines diverge and space grows hungry at its edges. [367] It was the same math I had hidden in my mazes, the secret that a small entrance could contain impossible distance if the angles bent wrong. [393] Only here the geometry was alive, the roots obeying it without being taught, and walking a bridge could take longer returning than going. [416] The photographs sensed it and began to disintegrate faster, the paper curling when I laid the halo fragment upon them like a paperweight. [439] I watched a yew labyrinth I built long ago lift and scatter, green dust becoming green memory becoming nothing, and I did not weep because I finally understood. [467] Time does not scrape or polish, it branches, and the texture I had chased all my life was the bark of ever-widening choice. [490] I am retrocognitively trapped because every turn I ever cut for strangers is still growing somewhere in me, each dead end a kindness I failed to give. [517] Tonight the monsoon drummed the canopy and the bridges swelled and trembled, and I walked them with the halo fragment cupped like a coal. [541] My ledger is now a pile of pale squares, the fading photographs gone, but the trembling in my chest has steadied into listening. [564] I no longer need to see the past, only to feel how it widens, and that is enough, even in this quaking reverie that still shakes my small hands. [593] I returned the halo fragment to the root lattice, where green wood is already growing around its curve, forgiving the plaster with life. [616] The garden will keep it, and keep me, and the mazes will keep disintegrating until only the way they felt remains. [637] That is the texture I wanted. [643]</story>
