<story>The canyon was not of stone, but of silence stacked upon silence. [12] Within it rose towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes, the bleached spines of a leviathan species no one remembered. [34] Among those towers lived Joren, a copper-veined storyteller whose forearms showed metallic filigree beneath translucent skin. [50] He was quietly articulate, never raising his voice because the canyon would raise it for him. [66] Joren was the last fluent speaker of Avarn, and his life would unfold across the death of a language. [85] When he was young, his grandmother pressed a flawed diamond rumored to be cursed into his palm and told him to listen. [107] The stone was cloudy at its heart, and it caught no light, only sound. [121] The canyon elders said the diamond drank voices and gave them back wrong. [134] Joren did not fear it, for his motivation was singular: to translate texture into sound before Avarn faded. [152] Avarn was a tactile tongue, where words for smooth were sung differently if the surface was wet or warm. [171] No recording device could capture it, because the language needed resonance, not just hearing. [185] In the deepest part of the bone towers lay a hollow, a tunnel of echoes where a whisper could live for days. [207] Joren discovered that the flawed diamond, placed on the tunnel floor, would vibrate to the textures he pressed against it. [227] When he dragged river silk across the diamond, the tunnel sang a high, clear note that meant silk-wet-moonlight in Avarn. [247] When he pressed sun-baked bone against it, the echo came back low and granular. [261] To preserve these echoes, he needed to harness the canyon itself as an amplifier. [275] The bone towers were unstable, and a single mistranslated vibration could collapse them. [288] He learned to read the wind through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight, as they threaded between towers. [309] The birds, called avra, only flew when the air pressure was perfect for the tunnel to hold a note. [328] For twenty years, as the last elders died and Avarn thinned from marketplaces, Joren worked. [343] Children who once mocked his copper veins now brought him objects to hear their true names. [359] He would place a child's woolen coat, a shard of salt, a smooth river stone upon the diamond. [377] The tunnel would answer, and Joren would speak the ancient word, though no living ear but his understood. [395] The supposed curse of the diamond was truth: each time he used it, his copper veins dimmed a fraction. [414] Still, he continued, because when Avarn finally died in other mouths, it would live in resonance. [430] On the last day, the sky filled with avra, turning in a perfect spiral above the bone towers. [448] Joren understood the pattern meant a storm that would bury the canyon forever by morning. [463] He carried every texture he had hoarded, silk, ash, basalt, bone, water, into the tunnel of echoes one final time. [483] He set the flawed diamond in the center and laid his own copper-veined hands upon it. [499] The tunnel answered not with one note, but with all of them at once, a language becoming music. [517] Outside, the bone towers trembled, yet held long enough for the avra to hear and remember. [533] When travelers find that canyon now, filled with sand, they swear the wind speaks textures against their skin. [551] And if they close their eyes, they sometimes understand it, though they never learned the words. [567] Joren's copper faded to brown that day, but the diamond, freed from curse, turned clear. [582] It lies still in the buried tunnel, humming whenever a hand brushes sand. [595] The avra still fly there each year, and their patterns write his story again and again. [611] No one now remembers that he was quietly articulate, only that the canyon learned how to speak. [628] And in that speaking, texture finally became sound forever. [637]</story>
