<story>Joren had been officially classified as an underachieving mage for three years running. [13] This was back when creativity gets automated, and even apprentices could commission a cantrip from a promptbox. [30] His spells worked, but they were always a little tired, a little derivative, like secondhand light. [46] So he left the lowland academies to find the sacred in the simple, which no one had bothered to map because it was not profitable. [71] The monks of the white library told him about an icebound spire above the clouds that predated the automata. [90] It was not a fortress or temple, but an old weather observatory built around a garden that accommodates impossibilities. [109] At its center, they said, a single rose still bloomed in midwinter, lit by its own lingering luminance. [127] Joren wanted to see that light, not because it was powerful, but because it persisted without audience. [144] He could not afford a skyship, so he learned mist walking from a shepherd who guided goats between peaks. [163] The technique was not in the curriculum, because it demanded attention to each breath rather than scale. [180] For three days he climbed until the air thinned, and on the fourth morning the clouds rose like a second sea beneath him. [203] Then he began to walk. [208] The mist held only if he did not try to impress it, which was the hardest lesson for an underachiever who still wanted applause. [232] Step by slow step he crossed the veil, and by noon the icebound spire above the clouds resolved out of white into white. [255] It was smaller than legend, pitted by wind, its observatory dome collapsed like a broken eggshell. [271] Inside, the garden was indeed there, impossible in the thin air, soil warm to the touch, bordered by low stone walls. [292] At its heart a single glass rose grew, not made of glass but lit from within, and that was the source of the glow. [316] The air around it showed a brilliant dimness, bright enough to read by yet soft enough to make him squint not from glare but reverence. [341] He understood then that this was not stored sunlight, but a lingering luminance that had learned to stay. [359] Around the rose, the garden was not manicured but true, weeds and thyme equally welcomed, and that was the point. [379] Joren knelt and saw that the low walls were not walls at all, but the remains of a garden folly built for contemplation not defense. [404] Each arch had fallen except one, whose center stone, the garden folly keystone, still hung impossibly without mortar. [422] The keystone thrummed faintly, holding the arch, holding the garden, holding the quiet against the wind that should have scoured it away. [444] He realized the rose was not generating the garden; the keystone was, by remembering what warmth felt like. [462] His pack straps cut into his shoulders, so he had to unstrap the heavy promptbox he had carried from the academy as proof of competence. [487] The device was dead in this air anyway, its runes dulled by cold. [500] He set it down outside the arch and stepped through emptyhanded. [511] Passing beneath the keystone, his own derivative spells fell quiet, and for the first time his mind was silent enough to notice small things. [535] The way frost feathered on the rose stem. [543] The way thyme smelled sharper here, as if cold clarified it. [554] The way light did not need to be grand to be holy. [566] He sat, and instead of casting, he tended. [574] He cupped loose soil around the rose with his hands, which were clumsy but warm. [589] The lingering luminance caught his palm lines and made them look, briefly, like rivers of light. [605] In that moment he stopped measuring himself against archmages and automata alike. [617] He understood that being underachieving in one system only meant he had been training for another, slower one. [635] Night came above the clouds, and the stars turned, and the little garden kept its heat. [651] Joren did not build a new spell; he simply learned how to stay, like the light. [667] When his food ran low, he walked back down through mist, with the memory of the keystone held not in his bag but in his hands. [693] He would tell no one how to find it, for fear they would automate that too, but he would teach anyone how to unstrap and look low. [720]</story>
