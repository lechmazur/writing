Joren had always been known as a Meek blacksmith on Varnhollow, an Orchard planet riddled with floating rock arches that held the old appleworlds aloft like tired ribs. [28]
He was Quietly determined in the way old iron is, not loud, not bright, just unwilling to break. [46]
When the Harrowwind collapsed the central arch and buried the low orchards, his forge became useless and his purpose narrowed To coax resilience from rubble. [71]
The planetary agronomists had left years ago, declaring the soil salt-sour and dead. [84]
Joren did not argue because meek men rarely argue with data. [95]
Instead he lived Between understanding and confusion, where he understood that trees need water and was confused why no one tried to bring it. [119]
His answer became Deliberate and simple: he would rehydrate the orchard roots Using abandoned mine shafts that still wept with deep aquifer seep. [142]
The shafts ran like veins under the arches, dug by lithium crews and left to flood when the market fell. [162]
In his dead mother's trunk he found a folded square of Magician's silk, impossibly light and cold to the touch, that never tore and never let water through. [190]
The traveling magicians who once bartered tricks for peaches had left it as payment before the collapse. [207]
Joren discovered its true use during the first sleep-experiment the old wellmonks taught, a ritual of Bartered awakenings where you trade an hour of deep sleep to keep a root alive. [238]
He draped the silk over his face, breathed through it, and felt his dreams siphoned downward like cool smoke into the mine shafts. [261]
Below, where the silk veins now lined the shafts, the water shivered awake and began to climb. [278]
It was Azure defiance to see that first thin spring crack the salt crust under a dead orchard, a blue so stubborn it hurt his eyes. [304]
Day after day he worked, small hammer in hand, shaping lost channel gates from scrap because his anvil was still his language. [326]
He was too meek to claim hope, so he measured progress in green. [339]
One green bud on a skeleton limb was a sermon. [349]
Two buds were a rebellion. [354]
Each Bartered awakening cost him more, leaving dark rings under his eyes, but the water kept rising through the silk-lined shafts into the root chambers. [379]
He learned to trade only the lightest dreams, the ones where he floated among the arches as a boy. [398]
The orchard did not explode back to life, because real resilience never does. [411]
Instead it sighed, then flexed, then pushed small white roots through rubble that agronomists had called dead. [428]
Children from the cliff-habs came down to watch, at first mocking the old smith who talked to dirt. [446]
Then they knelt and helped him lay the last silk sheets into the deepest shaft. [461]
When the first full tree flowered, its blossoms reflected the arch-sky with an almost defiant blue light. [478]
Joren stood under it, exhausted, unremarkable, and let the petals fall on his rusted apron. [493]
He had no speech to give, because meek blacksmiths seldom have speeches. [505]
He only touched the silk where it now veined the earth like a cool second skin and felt the planet breathe awake. [527]
Between understanding and confusion he finally understood that waking something else can also wake you. [542]
The orchards would need tending for years, and his trade would never be fully repaid. [557]
But blue had returned to the horizon, not as empty sky but as labor. [571]
And that, for Joren, was enough to keep hammering quietly against the coming dust. [585]
Above him the floating arches caught evening light and turned the color of hot forge scale before cooling dark. [604]
