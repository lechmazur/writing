<story>In the northern quarter where the city paid for beauty but feared its consequence, lived Lira, a chaste courtesan whose company was bought not for touch but for stillness. [29] She moved with a bodily poetic grace, as if each limb remembered a poem the mind had forgotten. [47] The House of Veils that owned her contract taught its daughters to sing the city's lullaby, a low tune that made everyone want the same life. [73] Lira had sung it for seven years, until the winter the government built the vault. [88] The vault was not a prison but a prism sanctuary under starlight, a dome of angled glass in the old quarter where sound went to be kept. [115] Officially it was for archiving music, but everyone knew it held the harvest of echoes, every forbidden song and unsung prayer the city had tried to bury. [142] Lira had never been inside, because courtesans were not scholars and scholars were not allowed to want. [159] Yet on the night the snow finally held, she left the House without permission, to defy the lullaby of conformity that had kept her obedient and well paid. [187] Her feet found the silver courtyard under snow behind the dome, where the light of the vault fell in clean white bars across untouched drifts. [212] The courtyard was silent with an alarming serenity that made her breath visible and then instantly erased. [229] No guards watched, because no one believed anyone would want to steal a sound. [243] At the center of the courtyard stood a door made of no wood at all, but of interwoven light and wire that sang faintly when the wind touched it. [272] She understood then that the dome did not need a key, but a gesture of unmaking. [288] Memory said the archivists could unlock it only by untangling threads of light with bare hands, a slow labor that burned if done with greed. [313] Lira had no greed left, only a hunger for a different quiet. [325] She stepped into the bars of light and knelt, letting the cold bite through her silk. [341] For a long time between sigh and smile she worked, her fingers finding the warm threads that held the door shut. [362] Each thread was a voice, her own from years ago accepting coins, thanking men, repeating the lullaby without believing or disbelieving it. [384] To pull them she had to listen and then forgive them. [395] The work left her hands red, and the snow around her knees melted into a perfect circle, exposing dark stone. [415] When the last thread fell, the door sighed and opened inward without sound. [428] Inside, the dome was empty except for light caught in its angles and for the echoes themselves, visible as dust motes under star. [451] They did not belong to anyone anymore, not to singers nor to censors nor to merchants who bought silence by the hour. [473] Lira breathed in and the echoes rushed to her, not into her ears but into the skin of her arms and throat. [495] She did not sing them back immediately. [502] Instead she sat in the center of the prism and let the light pass through her, and the dome made her body a lens. [526] For the first time she understood that being chaste had never meant being untouched, but being unborrowed. [543] Outside, snow kept falling on the courtyard, but inside no snow could ever land. [557] When she finally stood, the echoes stood with her, ready to leave as street songs, as lullabies with new endings. [577] She pushed the door open wider and walked back into the silvered world, carrying a chorus that no single house could contract. [599] Behind her the prism closed softly, not locked but waiting. [609] The city would hear its own voice tomorrow, and for once it would not know whether to sigh or to smile. [630]</story>
