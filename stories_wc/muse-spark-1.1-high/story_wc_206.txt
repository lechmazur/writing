<story>Silas was a music box repairman who lived inside the old water tower because its copper walls sang back everything he made. [22] The tower had been built as a resonance chamber for the valley's warning bells, and now it held his workbenches, his jars of tiny gears, and a single cot. [51] Above the cot hung a glass violet that his mother had grown from a cutting, a flower that wilts near dishonesty and therefore had never lied to him. [79] When Silas woke on the morning after the long midnight, the violet was pale and curled inward. [96] Three nights before, midnight had come early and had not left, stealing the sun and leaving the valley in a soft starless dusk. [119] The other townspeople had shuttered their windows, but Silas kept his tower door open, for his purpose was to reclaim the light stolen by midnight. [144] He believed light could be coaxed back, not commanded. [153] On his bench lay his most precious tool, a chip from philosopher's stone no larger than a tooth, warm as a coal and humming faintly. [178] He had found it twenty years earlier inside a broken ballerina box that no one else could fix. [196] The chip did not turn lead to gold, but it remembered how things were meant to sound before they tarnished. [216] Silas also kept an older bargain, a covenant of rust that he had made when he was young. [234] The covenant was simple: the tower would let his music live forever if he allowed all his tools to slowly rust and fail. [257] His screwdrivers were orange, his tweezers spotted, and that was why the chip mattered, because it never rusted. [275] This morning, with the violet trembling, Silas understood that the long midnight was not weather but a forgetting. [293] The valley had forgotten to listen for morning, and so morning had forgotten to come. [308] He lifted the largest music box in his shop, the town's old dawn box, whose song once told the sun where to rise. [331] Its cylinder was perfect, its comb was whole, there was nothing torn to see. [345] That was why only he could mend it, for his true skill was mending what isn't torn, the invisible frays in listening and memory. [369] Silas set the dawn box in the center of the resonance chamber and placed the chip at its heart, where the spring should catch. [393] He then began to tinker, and the tower filled with tinkersong wonder, little hammers ringing against rust like tiny bells. [413] The sound did not fix metal, it fixed attention. [422] Silas wound the dawn box, but its song came out thin, as if the notes were afraid of the dark. [442] The glass violet drooped further, and a single pedal fell. [452] He realized he was trying to be brave, which was a form of lying to a flower that hates pretense. [472] So he told the truth aloud. [478] I am scared that I have kept the covenant of rust too well, and that I have let everything else rust also. [500] The violet lifted its head. [505] Encouraged, Silas understood he could not push light back into the sky, he had to tether the sky to their listening. [526] He carried the dawn box to the open tower door and played its thin song into the unending dusk. [545] The resonance chamber caught the song and threw it wide, doubling and tripling it until each house heard its own small chime. [567] One by one, shutters opened. [572] People stepped out and tilted their ears, and in that listening, everything was in the balance. [588] For a long moment nothing happened, and then the chip from philosopher's stone burned brighter than ever. [605] Its light was not gold but clear morning, and it ran along the teeth of the music box comb and into the air. [628] The dawn box finally remembered its real song. [636] It was not a loud anthem but a small, honest tune, the kind you hum when you are trying to remember something kind. [659] The valley held its breath, the rust on his tools shivered, and far above, midnight thinned like old varnish. [678] Light returned not all at once but in quiet increments, as if newly mended. [692] Silas laughed, and the resonance chamber laughed with him, and the violet opened wide and did not wilt at all. [712]</story>
