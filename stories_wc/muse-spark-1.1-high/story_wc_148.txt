<story>I was born after the wolves stopped coming, which made me the boy who cried wolf's son in a village that had already learned to look away. [27] My father died with his name turned into a warning, and I left to find work where no one needed to believe me. [50] That work was keeping the lighthouse prison off a jagged reef, a tower that held six cells and a lamp no ships trusted anymore. [74] They sent me there before they find the bodies, back when the ledgers still listed prisoners as transferred. [92] Officially the prison was empty, but the sea keeps its own census. [104] On my third evening the wind fell and the old building settled into copperlight repose, the kind of light that makes even stone seem to breathe slower. [131] I had spent my life chasing hush, the space after a lie when no one answers, and I thought that tower might finally give it to me. [158] Instead it gave me a door. [164] On the third floor, behind the lamp room, there was a narrow door left ajar for decades, its paint blistered by salt and its hinges thick with rust. [192] The keeper before me had nailed a lattice from confessional over its upper half, as if someone inside still needed to be heard without being seen. [218] The priests on the mainland must have decommissioned a chapel and sold its pieces for scrap. [234] Through that lattice I could see nothing but dark, but dark has textures if you learn to watch. [252] I began to listen through the scuffs on a narrow door left ajar for decades, because the marks told me how many feet had hesitated there before mine. [280] Each scuff was a small panic, a boot dragged back, a man deciding not to enter. [296] On the seventh night I brought my father's name to that door and asked it aloud to see if the tower would answer. [319] The wind came in and made the lanterns hover for a second without swaying, just held in place by pressure. [339] In that stillness I heard breathing from the other side. [349] I am not brave, but I am practiced at being disbelieved, so I spoke anyway. [364] I said I did not want to be a footnote to his fable. [377] I said I came here to thread a labyrinth with your own story, to build something that would hold even if it was not believed. [402] The breathing steadied, as if listening sustains impossibilities that shouting never could. [414] That is when the voice finally came, not through the lattice but from just below it, low and worn thin. [434] It said it had been the last chaplain, left to record confessions when the prison closed, and then forgotten when the boat stopped coming. [458] He had lived in that small room for two winters, eating tinned beans and rainwater, writing names on the wall that no one would ever pardon. [484] He had not made it, obviously. [490] What I heard was not a ghost but a recording, a thin wire recorder still turning behind the door, powered by some trick of the draft and a loose spring. [520] The machine had been set to play when air moved through that gap, so it could keep confessing after its owner could not. [543] It was still speaking because the tower needed a voice to justify its own waiting. [558] I opened the door all the way and let the late light pour in. [572] The small room was empty except for bones neatly arranged under a blanket, and a notebook whose final page ended mid sentence. [594] I understood then that I would be the one to report it, and no one on the mainland would believe the boy who cried wolf's son had found a saint in a light tower. [628] So I sat with him until the copper light went blue, letting the recorder play its loop of apologies into the hush I had chased for so long. [656] When it finally stopped, I left the lattice in place, closed the door to a respectful crack, and climbed to tend the lamp that no longer guided anyone. [684] Some stories do not need belief to be true, they only need a keeper. [698]</story>
