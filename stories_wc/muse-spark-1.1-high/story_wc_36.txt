<story>After the guardianship ended, the order struck my name from its rolls and left me with empty hands. [18] I had been a Driven relic keeper for thirty years, and I did not know how to be anything else. [38] So I walked back into the Thunder-echo range where the last crossing had failed. [52] I told myself I returned not to reopen the wound, but To harvest insights from failure. [68] The Gilded reed flute still hung against my ribs, wrapped in oilcloth and old prayer. [83] The flute was not an instrument, but a key for weaving between worlds, a craft the keepers had kept for centuries. [104] My apprentice Joren had tried to use it alone, and the mountain had taken him between. [120] The trail up the high cirque was buried, so I began Arranging driftwood as directional markers to remember the safe line. [141] Each piece was flood-grayed and light enough to carry, yet heavy enough to stay. [155] In that altitude the air itself held a Canyon hush, as if the stone were listening. [171] When the wind dropped, the range became Avalanchingly quiet, a silence that seemed ready to slide and crush. [189] I reached the old threshold by mid-afternoon, a flat of frost-split slate marked by my earlier cairns. [206] The veils between were thinnest there, where the world wore itself out against itself. [220] My hands were numb, but I forced myself to Uncase the flute and feel its weight again. [237] The gilding had dulled where Joren had gripped it, and one reed edge was darkened. [252] I had told the council that his death was my error in teaching, not his in learning. [269] That was true, but incomplete, for I had never let him fail while I could prevent it. [286] Now I understood that a keeper who prevents all failure harvests nothing, and knowledge curdles into superstition. [303] Weaving between worlds requires a willingness to lose tension and recover it, not to hold it so tight it snaps. [323] I placed the driftwood in a wider arc, three lines pointing inward, to map where my feet should not go. [343] The flute was cold against my lips, and for a moment I could not breathe. [358] When I blew, no true note came, only a thin air that made the quiet fold itself in half. [377] Snow rose off the slate without wind, hanging like dust between worlds. [389] I saw Joren as he had been on that last day, stubborn and bright, refusing my hand. [406] He did not look at me, but at the invisible weave ahead, trying to read it too quickly. [424] In that shared instant I finally saw his mistake and mine braided together like two wrong threads. [441] He had tried to force the passage open, and I had taught him to value the opening more than the stepping. [462] The vision thinned, and the snow settled back onto my markers, half burying my little compass. [478] I lifted the flute again, not to cross, but to play the closing tone that Joren had skipped. [496] This time the note held, low and clean, and the Avalanchingly quiet air accepted it and deepened. [513] The slate warmed under my knees, and the pressure between worlds eased like a held breath released. [530] I wept then, not from grief alone but from relief that failure had finally taught me something I could keep. [550] I would leave the Thunder-echo range before night and never bring another keeper here. [564] Instead I would go down to the valleys and teach that weaving between worlds begins with learning how to stay. [584] I gathered the driftwood markers and stacked them as one cairn, so no other seeker would follow my line. [603] After the guardianship, I had thought I was done with duty, but duty had only changed its shape. [621] I wrapped the Gilded reed flute again and carried it home empty, which for once felt correct. [638]</story>
