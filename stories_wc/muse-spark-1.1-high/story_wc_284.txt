<story>At the tilt of the earth toward spring, Thomas came back to Zephyr Bluff. [14] He had not been there since they burned him, and the town still knew him as the lighthouse keeper's son, not as a burned spy who had traded maps for secrets. [45] The old light was dead, and his father was gone, leaving only the keeper's cottage and a job half-finished. [64] Fixing what's broken had been his father's phrase, spoken whenever the storm gear jammed or the lens clouded. [82] Thomas had left because he thought courage meant going far away, and now he had returned to find where unused courage waits. [104] It was early March, and a soft thaw had left the fields patched with white. [119] Over the zephyr bluff in mist, everything sounded distant, as if the world were holding its breath. [136] In that silver hush he could hear the loose shutter, the drip in the cistern, his own heart learning again how to be alone. [160] He climbed the iron stairs and found the Fresnel lens cracked from a winter gale. [175] His father had intended to repair it, and had left tools laid out like a promise. [191] On the workbench beneath a canvas tarp, Thomas found an engraved pillbox he did not remember. [207] The lid was etched with a gull in flight over a compass rose, and inside were three faded tablets and a folded slip of onionskin. [232] The paper held his father's small handwriting, a note that his hands had left medicine for the lens cleaner, Mrs. Albright. [253] Thomas pocketed the box, but that night the shortwave at the cottage crackled alive with a pattern he knew too well. [274] Someone on the other side was still trying to reach him, even though his handlers had told him he was done. [295] The next morning he walked to Mrs. Albright's and saw that her son had dragged a sled across the yard, leaving deep tracks. [318] The boy, Eli, waved and shouted that the hill was still good for running messages if you knew how to read them. [340] That afternoon, down in the cove, Eli showed him how his father had taught the children to leave codes in accumulated snow for the fishermen when the radio failed. [369] Lines and dots, long and short, pressed with a stick, meant wind, clear water, danger. [384] Thomas felt his old training rise, the instinct to become prudently daring again when no one was watching. [402] He knew he should not answer the signal on the shortwave, that any reply would pull him back into a life that had already ended. [427] Yet the lens still needed a sealant that only the classified optics lab could vouchsafe without questions. [444] If he called them, he would owe them one last small task, one last simple dead drop on the bluff. [464] He stood for a long time in the cold lantern room, turning the engraved pillbox over in his cold palm. [484] The gull on the lid looked like it was trying to stay aloft against weather that did not matter anymore. [504] He realized fixing what mattered was not the lens itself but the light it would throw for others. [522] He made the call, accepted the condition, and asked only for the sealant and for them to leave him afterward alone for good. [545] Two days later a crate came to the dock marked marine paint, and inside under foam was what he needed. [565] He worked through the night, prudently sealing each fracture, daring to polish the old glass until it caught even the faintest morning light. [588] At dawn he completed the small errand, leaving the sealed envelope not in the dead drop but tucked under Mrs. Albright's chipped bird feeder where Eli would find it first. [618] Inside it was only the engraved pillbox, empty now, and a note telling Eli how to keep the light when the town's power failed. [642] He did not send any intelligence, only kindness, and let the sea take the rest of the debt. [660] When he switched the lamp on, its beam cut clean through the lingering mist over the zephyr bluff in mist and found open water. [684] From the cove below he could see Eli's fresh marks in the snow, a new code that simply read LIGHT IS BACK. [706] Thomas sat in the silver hush and finally understood that unused courage had been waiting right there, in the quiet work of staying. [729]</story>
