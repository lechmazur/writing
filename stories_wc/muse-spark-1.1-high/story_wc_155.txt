<story>Mira had not picked up a brush in three years, not since the city quieted. [15]
She had once been a vivid mural artist whose walls bloomed across the transit tunnels and schoolyards. [32]
People remembered her because she paints with light, or at least that is how they described her use of thin glazes that caught the afternoon sun. [58]
Now she felt hollowed, and she wanted to rediscover lost joy but did not know how to begin. [76]
It happened within the fold of a daydream, while she was mending a torn map in the archive basement. [95]
Her fingers slipped, her head tipped back, and the dusty room dissolved into cool, damp air. [111]
She stood in the ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog, a place the municipal records called uninhabitable. [131]
The trees were black-boned and bare, their roots cracking the old sidewalk into islands. [145]
Fog moved like breath between trunks, and somewhere a wind chime made of spoons clinked softly. [161]
In the center of the orchard stood a low wall that had not collapsed, and on it clung a single fresco fragment. [183]
It showed the knee and laughing hand of a child, the pigment still absurdly bright against the gray. [201]
Mira knelt, and a strange ache rose in her throat because she remembered painting that exact color twenty years earlier. [221]
She reached out, and as her palm hovered, the fragment warmed as if recognizing her. [236]
The fog around her suddenly glowed, not with sunlight but with faint, floating lattices of names and sketches. [254]
This was the old knowledge network, abandoned after the city built its closed servers, still whispering orchard grafting techniques and children's games. [276]
Mira laughed, and the sound came out as pensive mirth, half grief and half amusement at her own forgetfulness. [295]
Of course she had learned the network's password as a girl, when her grandfather taught her how to prune these same trees. [317]
She whispered it, and the lattice brightened, projecting a map of the district as it had been: bright, noisy, full of ladders and low branches heavy with fruit. [345]
The light caught her hands and she understood what she had to do. [358]
She had no brush, no pigment, only the memory of how she paints with light. [373]
Mira lifted the fresco fragment, held it against her chest, and began to walk the ruined rows. [390]
Wherever she stepped, she tilted her hands and let the network's light spill through her fingers onto bark and stone. [410]
The fog behaved like exposed film, darkening and blooming wherever light touched it, holding the image a moment before fading. [430]
She painted quickly, laughing again, tracing the outline of children climbing, of baskets, of an old woman singing with flour on her hands. [453]
Each image stayed visible long enough for her to see it clearly, then dissolved, which made her careful and fearless at once. [475]
She worked her way back to the standing wall and knew she wanted to display one lasting piece before waking. [495]
She pressed the fresco fragment back into its mortar and surrounded it with new light, not restoring the old orchard but continuing it. [518]
She painted herself as a small girl, reaching for fruit too high, paint on her cheek, utterly failed and utterly delighted. [539]
Around the girl she added the other children who once ran here, their outlines overlapping like a crowd caught mid-game. [559]
The knowledge network pulsed, and for an instant every lattice line held her drawing steady against the fog. [577]
That steadiness felt like someone else agreeing, saying yes, this happened, this matters. [590]
Mira closed her eyes, and the daydream folded again, bringing the cool archive air back to her skin. [608]
She was back at the table, forehead still resting on the map, her palm warm as if still holding light. [628]
On the table lay a small, cold piece of plaster she had never seen before, its edge bright with a child's laughing hand. [651]
She stared at it and felt tears, but also that same pensive mirth, quieter now and rooted deeper. [669]
She picked up her real brush, mixed the absurdly bright color from memory, and began to paint on the archive's blank wall. [691]
