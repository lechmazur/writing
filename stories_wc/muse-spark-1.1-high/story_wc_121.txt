<story>Mara had been a gravity defier since the accident that unbound her weight from the earth. [16] She did not fly so much as she forgot to fall, which made her perfect for carrying messages across the chasm. [37] The city on either side was linked only by the viaduct, and tonight it was a glowing viaduct after rain. [57] Neon spilled into puddles and the wet concrete reflected a light that seemed to come from below. [74] Mara stood at its highest arch holding an ancient clay tablet with runes that pulsed faintly against her palms. [93] The tablet had been given to her by the archivist with a single instruction: do not read it while standing still. [114] She had disobeyed immediately, and seen her own lives laid out like cracked glass. [128] Every choice she had ever avoided created a separate thread, and the tablet revealed fractured timelines branching from a single moment of fear. [151] In one she had stayed on the ground and let her bones heal heavy; in another she had never stopped rising until the air thinned. [176] All of them led back to the same middle, a hallway that turned inward forever. [191] Mara understood then that her real assignment was not delivery but to conquer the labyrinth of self that the tablet had exposed. [213] The tablet was periodically eternal, its runes resetting every seven minutes to show a new pattern that was always the same truth. [235] Light fell from the viaduct struts in sheets, too bright to be comforting, a darkening brightness that hurt her eyes while promising clarity. [258] She stepped off the edge and let the wind hold her, suspended between the drop and the ripple where rain met river far below. [282] Time stretched there, and in that thin pause all the timelines vibrated at once. [296] Mara could see herself at twelve, at thirty, at eighty, each refusing to look at the others. [313] The only way forward was to retrace, not forward but backward through her own decisions. [328] The tablet taught her how, through procedural generation of every path she had not taken, rendering them in light until they became walkable. [351] Suddenly the air under the glowing viaduct after rain filled with ghost roads, each a different Mara failing differently. [370] One had clung to safety and become brittle; another had flown too high and forgotten faces. [386] Mara drifted among them, feeling their regret as weight returning to her limbs. [399] The oldest ghost, scarred and grounded, held out the tablet to her and whispered that the labyrinth has no center, only the choice to keep walking. [425] Mara realized the darkness in the brightness was not the night but the shadow she cast herself when she refused to land. [447] She pressed her palm into the wet clay and the runes burned cold, aligning all fractured timelines into one line that pointed down. [470] Between the drop and the ripple she finally let herself fall for exactly three seconds, enough to remember gravity. [489] The splash below never came because she caught herself again, but lighter now, no longer defying but negotiating. [507] The procedural generation collapsed, ghost roads folding back into the tablet, leaving only the real viaduct humming with rain. [526] Mara landed softly on slick concrete, her boots hissing, the tablet now blank clay cooling in her hand. [544] She understood that to conquer the labyrinth of self did not mean escaping it but accepting every corridor as hers. [564] The light above the viaduct steadied, no longer darkening but simply bright, warm against the receding storm. [581] Mara tucked the empty tablet into her coat and began to walk the long way across, finally feeling the weight worth carrying. [603]</story>
