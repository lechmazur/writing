<story>After the last forest burns, the world was ash and wind and empty track. [14] The freight still ran, because someone had to guard what remained. [25] He was the dreamless freight guard, hired for his inability to dream. [37] Dreaming men went mad in the vault, so they sent him instead. [49] The vault was a divergent archive, a seed library of every road not taken. [63] Inside it slept genomes, languages, blueprints, and childhood songs that no child would otherwise hear. [78] He did not understand its contents, but he knew the weight of it. [91] Once a month the track cut through a glowing dune in bloom, where glass flowers opened for a night. [110] They pulsed amber and hummed, and for a second the archive answered. [122] On his bench beside the vault door sat a chess clock frozen at midnight, relic of the keeper before him. [142] The old keeper had played both sides until the numbers stopped moving. [154] When the wind moved through the cracks, the man thought he heard a fractured lullaby. [169] It was his own mother, or perhaps no one, singing shhh, shhh, hold on. [183] He kept the train running to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners of whatever would come next. [201] The archive was occasionally everywhere, leaking at the edges when its lids sweated heat. [215] He felt it in his teeth, like static before snow. [225] He had learned not to fight the leak, but to charm it through wave functions, humming back in tune. [244] The dune taught him that frequency is kindness, and kindness is a kind of steering. [259] That night, as the train entered the bloom, alarms woke and the vault spiked hot. [274] A branch archive was trying to become real, pressing its world against his. [287] In that other world the forests had not burned and children rode this same freight for fun. [304] He saw it clearly because his guard protocol had failed to make him dreamless enough. [319] For the first time in eleven years, sleep pulled at him like a tide. [333] If he slept, the archive would root, and this ash track would be overwritten. [347] If he stayed awake, the other track would wither, unchosen again. [358] He stared at the chess clock frozen at midnight and understood its warning finally. [372] Midnight was not an hour but a choice, a line that never decides. [385] He laid his palm on the vault and began to sing, broken and low. [399] His fractured lullaby did not match the wind, nor the glass flowers, but both at once. [415] He sang to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners between worlds, where no light dared settle. [432] He did not charm one world over the other, he charmed the interval itself. [446] The divergent archive rippled through wave functions and settled, not branching, but braiding. [459] The heat dropped. [462] Outside, the glowing dune in bloom flared white, and glass petals fell on steel. [476] When he looked through the door window, the vault contained both tracks at once, laid one over the other like transparent film. [498] In one, a boy who was also him waved from a tree that still lived. [513] In the other, only ash. [518] He understood at last that guarding did not mean keeping one pure. [530] It meant tending both until either could wake. [538] He pressed the flag on the chess clock frozen at midnight and the flag finally fell. [554] Time started. [556] The freight rolled on, dreamless no longer, occasionally everywhere itself, through cinders and leaves. [570] He hummed shhh, shhh, hold on, to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners where tomorrow waited to be born. [590] The wind carried the tune forward along the empty track, ahead of the engine. [604] Somewhere behind him, a forest that had burned and a forest that had not both listened. [620]</story>
