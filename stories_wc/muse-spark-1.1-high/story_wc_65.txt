<story>For three winters Mara had kept the archive in the obsidian spire by a frozen lake, and the lake kept her. [21]
She was not a mapmaker of what was, but an ignorance cartographer, employed by the northern communes to chart what they did not yet know how to ask. [49]
The work was groundedly imaginative, requiring her to measure the shape of a questions absence and sketch its possible edges. [69]
On her desk lay the tools of that impossible trade: compasses of smoked glass, ledgers bound in seal skin, and a single kingfisher feather quill nib set in river bone. [99]
It had not yet touched ink this season. [107]
That was deliberate, because her true discipline was writing the future, and she held time in reverence before the pen meets the blank page. [131]
The communes had sent a problem sealed in wax. [140]
We have only the thaw for power, the letter read, and we must not waste it. [156]
The old turbines under the ice could produce a month of heat or a week of light for work, but not both. [178]
They wanted a plan, but what Mara wanted was to seed innovation within a constraint, to teach them to grow more than they spend. [202]
So she did not answer. [207]
Instead she walked to the edge of the frozen lake at noon, when the black face of the spire cast no shadow, and recorded the wind. [233]
At night she fed pine cones to the stove and built her models. [246]

There, in that hollow, she saw the chance for a sustaining transformation, one that would outlast any single winter's choice. [314]
She could tell them to bank the power, or spend it, but either choice was a straight line to scarcity. [334]
What if they used the week's light not to work longer, but to learn a new way to work? [353]
Her hands were cold before the pen meets the blank page, and that cold made her careful. [370]

Light becoming food becoming heat, with air left cleaner than before. [485]
She did not promise that it would work. [493]

Mara watched it and felt the future hold, not as fate, but as a workable draft. [580]
The communes would argue, fail, try again, and in trying they would learn to chart their own ignorance. [598]
That was enough. [601]
Spring would come late, but when it did, the light would find them already writing. [616]
