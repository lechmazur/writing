<story>Mara had been a lost soul collector for three centuries, though she counted her age in goodbyes rather than years. [20] Each morning she walked the rim of echo gorge, listening for the thin places where the dead lingered too close to the living. [43] It was in the hush of a hidden sunrise, when the sun had not yet cleared the eastern teeth, that she heard it first. [67] A misaligned harp was playing on its own near the old rescue station, its strings humming without a player. [86] Someone, or something, was trying to speak through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp, and the gorge answered every false tone with a wail. [111] Mara approached, modestly fearless as ever, because fear had stopped serving her after her first hundred crossings. [128] The harp belonged to Thomas, a boy who had fallen in the gorge during the flood of '82, and whose laugh she had never managed to collect. [155] His body had been found, but his soul had snagged on the rocks like a kite string, held by something unsaid. [176] Mara sat on the cold stone and opened her palm, where she kept a crocodile's tooth wrapped in river-blue thread. [196] It was not a weapon, but a promise from the river god that a bite could also be a holding. [216] She set the tooth against the lowest harp string to steady its vibration, and the air thickened with listening. [235] Then came the spectral confessions, not in words first, but in the shape of withheld breaths. [251] Thomas had not been afraid to die; he had been ashamed to be remembered only for crying. [268] He told her, through the shivering harp, that his mother told everyone he was brave, and he never corrected her. [288] He told her he had wanted to be the kind of boy who laughed even while scared, and that wanting had become a secret debt. [313] Other voices pressed in behind his, other lost souls caught in the gorge's old echo, each adding their own unfinished humor. [334] A miner who joked while his lamp went out, a climber who slipped while imitating a goat, a mother who sang off-key to hide her terror. [360] All of them had swallowed their laughter when they most needed to share it, believing grief demanded silence. [378] Mara understood that her work had grown too narrow, guiding souls onward but never letting them lighten. [395] She had spent centuries trying to perfect mourning, but had forgotten to integrate laughter into healing, though that was what every soul begged for. [419] So she did what no collector before her had dared, she answered the harp with her own undone laugh. [438] It cracked in the middle, rusted from disuse, yet it rang true against the gorge walls. [454] Thomas heard it and, startled, laughed back, a bright, hiccuping sound that finally matched the boy he wanted to be. [474] That single, forgiven laugh moved like a wind, and the other souls caught it and passed it on. [492] The harp shivered and, for a breathless measure, played in tune, the tooth glowing warm in Mara's hand. [510] One by one the spectral confessions thinned into sighs and then into chuckles, and the heaviness over the gorge lifted. [530] Mara felt them pursue the light she had not noticed before, the thin seam of sunrise bleeding through the rock. [550] She did not lead them; she laughed them forward, and they rose like startled birds into that pale gold. [569] When the gorge was empty at last, the harp string snapped, and the crocodile's tooth cooled, its work finished. [588] Mara remained seated, her own soul feeling strangely, tenderly sore, filled with what she could only call a luminous ache. [608] She understood now that collecting was never about holding, but about returning what had been held too tight. [626] She tucked the tooth away and stood, ready to walk the rim again when the next hidden sunrise called. [645]</story>
