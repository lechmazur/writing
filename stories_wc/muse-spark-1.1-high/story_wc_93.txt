<story>The man who tended the silver eels had not been seen in the lower market for three years. [18] They called him the elusive eel herder because his ponds vanished with the tide and his footprints never lasted. [37] I found him only because I followed the light. [46] It was after friction disappears that everything else began to forget how to stay. [60] Boats slid sideways off their cradles, tools slipped from hands before you could grip them, and even standing required leaning into the wind. [83] Only on the tidal bluff under aurora did things still hold, as if the old sky remembered weight. [101] There I saw what he had been building. [109] At the cliff edge the eels circled in shallow, glass-cut pools that he had carved by hand, their bodies bright as wire. [131] He was old, his face lined from salt, and precognitively cursed to see a few seconds ahead of every loss. [151] He had known I would come, known my boot would slip, known exactly when to catch my arm. [169] You should not have climbed, he said, but his voice was not unkind. [182] He told me he stayed because of a lingering promise made to his sister before the world went smooth. [201] She had made him swear that the eels would keep their way, even if men forgot theirs. [218] To understand sacred patterns was the only way he knew to keep it. [231] Each dawn the eels tried to run to sea, but without friction the water carried them sideways and they could not find the old track. [256] That was why he was placing painted rocks in mathematical sequences along the bluff. [270] White stones for primes, red for returns, blue for the numbers that only the eels remembered. [286] He said the patterns were prayers the water could read, a geometry that taught the tide how to grip again. [306] At the center of it all lay a jar of collapsed star dust, dull and heavy as a thumbprint of night. [327] The fishermen said it had fallen the year before friction left, and that it weighed more than any boat. [346] He intended to harness it. [351] Not for power, not for trade, but to give the rocks a little gravity, a little memory of hold. [370] That night the aurora lowered itself until the whole bluff glowed green and pink, and the light felt like borrowed dawn. [391] It was light we had not earned, light we were allowed to use for a little while, and it made every stone look tender. [415] The eels began to move. [420] He opened the jar, and the collapsed star dust rose not up but inward, as if folding distance. [438] He saw its touch on the water three seconds before it happened, grimaced, and still let it happen. [456] The dust settled on his painted stones, and the stones, impossibly, stayed. [468] For the first moment in a year friction returned in a small perfect circle around his work, a quiet that held. [489] The painted rocks held, the pools held, and the eels found the true line, silver bodies aligning to his prime-numbered path to the sea. [513] I watched them go, one by one, and felt the small field weaken as its weight was spent. [531] He did not try to keep the eels; keeping had never been his promise. [545] When the last eel slipped over the edge and vanished into the true sea, he smiled because his sister's old route would live somewhere else now. [571] The light of borrowed dawn lifted, the aurora rose, and the rocks began again to slide very slowly toward the cliff. [592] We left them there, mathematics becoming sand again, knowing that promises are not for holding but for pointing the way home. [613]</story>
