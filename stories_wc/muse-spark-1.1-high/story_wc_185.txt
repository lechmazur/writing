<story>After the energy beings arrived, the sky never stopped singing. [10] Their light bled through the atmosphere in sheets, and every sheet hummed a different note. [25] People in the lowlands covered their ears, but covering did nothing. [36] My mentor named me the ripple-mark mapkeeper long before that, for the lines the wind cut into my palms. [55] When the storm of sound began, I left the plains and went down to the harmonic caves. [72] The caves had always answered any voice with harmony, but now they answered the energy beings with deafening choirs. [91] I did not go to silence the caves. [99] I went to find the quiet in the storm, the single point where listening itself could rest. [116] For three nights I walked deeper, until the ice floor took over from stone. [130] There I found the echo chamber stone, a boulder hollowed by centuries of dripping sound. [145] It was gently luminous, not with the harsh white of the beings above but with its own remembered light. [164] In its glow I could see my breath and the ancient frost that had sealed its heart. [181] Old stories said the stone would keep any sound you gave it and give it back truer. [198] I needed to conjure not a louder answer but a space for no answer at all. [214] So I began examining crack patterns in ice, the way my mother taught me to read rivers in winter. [233] Every fissure was a map of pressure, a record of where the world had tried to break and held. [252] I traced one long fracture that circled the echo chamber stone like a quiet orbit. [267] The hammering above never ceased, the energy beings turning, singing, pressing light into the caves. [282] Yet down here my hands learned a slower rhythm, a hammered repose beaten out of waiting and cold. [300] On the fourth morning I understood that the ice was not cracking but listening. [314] It opened only where sound needed to go to rest. [324] I pressed my forehead to the echo chamber stone and offered it no song, no plea, no name. [342] I offered it the absence I had carried since the sky changed. [354] The stone took it and held it, as ice holds air for a thousand years. [369] For a long minute there was nothing, and then the stone breathed out. [382] What returned was not echo but invention, a bloom of hush that spread from the stone in soft rings. [401] The bloom of hush did not fight the singing of the energy beings, it simply gave it a place to land. [422] In that landing, the endless chord above became a background, like wind over a map. [437] I sat in the center of the quiet and finally felt my own pulse as a chartable thing. [455] I am still the ripple-mark mapkeeper, but the maps I keep now are of silences worth returning to. [473] When travelers find me, shivering in the harmonic caves, I do not tell them to close their ears. [491] I show them how to kneel by the gently luminous stone and listen to the ice until quiet grows. [510] Some weep when the bloom of hush touches them, because they had forgotten what their own thoughts sounded like. [529] Others laugh, or sleep, or simply breathe for the first time since after the energy beings arrived. [546] None of them stay long, because the world above still needs mending and warning and guiding. [562] I let them take a shard of clear ice, each one etched with the crack I learned to follow. [581] Those shards will teach them to conjure their own small hush wherever the sky sings loudest. [597] That is enough, for maps are not meant to end storms but to find a way through them.
