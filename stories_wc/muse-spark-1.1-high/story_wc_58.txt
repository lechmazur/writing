<story>I came to the port town of Mirelle after the last ship sails, when the harbor water turns to iron and the gulls finally go quiet. [26]

My clients are not dying but disappearing, and they pay me to make sense of the space they will leave behind. [81]

Her voice was vividly descriptive even in whisper, and she told me how the light fell in her kitchen in exact shades of late honey and rust. [138]
She wanted her obituary written now because she planned to become one of the town's explained absences, those orderly vacancies the town keeps like pressed flowers in a ledger. [167]
I asked what that meant, and her laugh settled in the cold air between us like smoke. [184]

Alwen was older than I had imagined, wrapped in a coat that had once been fine, and she led me to a low stone bench covered in damp leaves. [265]
She told me she had once worked in the palace as a mender, her entire life spent following a single palace carpet weft thread where it had snagged and pulled loose. [296]

I took out my notebook and began to write, not her death but her continuance elsewhere. [402]

She asked me to try, there on the bench, and for a long time I could not find the words. [540]
