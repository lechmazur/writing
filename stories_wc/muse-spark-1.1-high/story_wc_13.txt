<story>Mara had spent twenty years as a natural history illustrator before the city commissioned her to map the upper waters. [20] The city she knew was vertical, its towers linked by a rooftop canal system weaving between towers that carried rain, fish, and lost letters on slow brown currents. [48] She worked from a punt, sketchbook balanced on her knees, trying to find the place where echoes are born because the canals returned every sound wrong. [74] A child's laugh from the lower streets would come back up as a bell, and a bell would come back as wind through reeds that never grew up there. [103] The old keepers said it was because the titan beneath the district was sleeping, and all time here was measured across a giant's nap. [127] When he slept, they said, he sculpts time itself in his dreams, folding minutes and stretching hours like warm glass. [147] Mara did not believe in titans, but she believed in patterns, and the pattern was an interrupted cadence. [165] Every sound that entered the canal system expected a resolution, a return, but at the western curve near the Archivist Tower it swerved into something else. [191] She called that curve the broken bar, and in her illustrations she marked it with a small infinity symbol pendant she wore while she worked. [216] The pendant had been her mother's, a loop of silver that had no beginning, and she had always drawn it into the margins when she was stuck. [243] On the third day of mapping, she met the boy who tended the locks, and he was barely twelve and already gray at the temples. [268] You are looking in the wrong direction, he told her, because the echoes are not born ahead but behind where you already looked. [291] Mara lifted her brush and asked what he meant, and he pointed to the cistern door sealed with rust under the waterline. [313] That door is the throat, he said, and if you want to understand the song you have to learn how to uncage it. [336] Mara thought he was being poetic, but that night the titan shifted in his sleep and the canals ran backward for nine minutes. [359] Fish swam in reverse, her ink unbled from the paper, and she felt the pendant grow cold against her chest. [379] She understood then that to chart the born place of echoes she could not add more hours, she would have to work by borrowing time. [404] The lock boy showed her how, by skimming a thin slice from the titan's exhale, a moment that would otherwise be wasted in a snore. [429] He taught her to hold her breath, dip the pendant into the canal, and pull, and the water gave her an extra second that did not belong to any clock. [459] Inside that stolen second, the world was still and sharp, and she could see the sound as a silver thread looping under the cistern door. [484] She spent a week borrowing, each day taking only one second so the titan would not wake, collecting thirty thin glassy moments. [506] She strung them together on paper, drawing not the canals but the time that moved through them, each line overlapping the last like scales. [530] In the drawings the interrupted cadence became visible, a wave that climbed toward closure and then turned sideways at the cistern, birthing a new tone. [555] That was the place where echoes are born, not a place of reflection but of invention, where unfinished sounds learned to become something else. [579] She realized her work as an illustrator had always been this, not copying nature but catching the moment before it decided what it was. [603] On the eighth day she took her punt to the cistern, placed both hands on the rusted door, and used all her borrowed seconds at once. [629] Time thickened, the pendant burned white, and the door shivered as the trapped chord behind it swelled to find release. [649] The lock boy shouted for her to stop, but she understood that mapping was not enough and she had to uncage the sound. [672] She pulled, the water roared backward one last time, and the cistern exhaled a century of waiting notes that had been composting in the dark. [697] They did not crash, they rose through the towers like startled birds, each finding its own unfinished listener on a different roof. [719] For a breathless minute the whole rooftop canal system sang with echoes that had finally been allowed to be born. [739] Then the titan sighed, turned over, and regular time resumed, and Mara felt her borrowed seconds settle back into her bones as debt paid. [763] She dried her sketchbook, and on the last page she drew not a canal but the infinity pendant open, its loop broken into a single line that continued off the edge. [794]</story>
