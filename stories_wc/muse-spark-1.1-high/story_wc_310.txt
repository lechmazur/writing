<story>After the universe contracts, there was not enough room for regret, only for memory. [14] The ambered observatory deck was all that remained of the Last Institute, suspended in hardened light. [30] Mara had become its cast lots decision maker because no algorithm could be trusted to choose fairly anymore. [48] She kept a collection of foreign coins from dead worlds in a chipped ceramic bowl, and she handled them while quietly enthusiastic about each impossible morning. [99] The deck's amber had trapped not insects but starlight, so every step cracked faint constellations under her feet. [117] She looked through that trapped light and felt stellar doubt, the quiet certainty that all navigation had been a story we told ourselves. [164] Inside that noise she found a backwards prophecy, a message that only became true when read in reverse, ending before it began. [186] It said: you will have listened, you will have waited, you will have decided, you will have doubted, you will have been left alone. [210] The prophecy frightened her because she had already lived it forward and had not recognized it as prophecy at all. [230] Each evening she climbed to the center of the deck where the amber was thickest and emptied her bowl to cast lots. [252] The coins fell not by chance but by weight, and the heaviest world always pulled the others toward its ruined history. [273] She did not ask the coins where to go, only what to listen for next in the compression of everything. [293] Yesterday the lot had pointed to a frequency lower than any star had ever dared to sustain. [310] Today she carried her receiver, a lattice of copper and memory, and tried to distill that frequency from the hiss. [330] The hiss resisted meaning, as all ancient things do, until she stopped trying to decode and simply let it be company. [377] She understood then that the backwards prophecy was not prediction but receipt, a universe acknowledging that someone had stayed to listen. [483] She marked its coordinates on the amber with her thumbnail, not as a claim but as a promise to return. [503] Night did not fall here because there was no sky left to darken, only the deepening of static between listening cycles. [524] Still, she called it night when she lay back on the ambered observatory deck and felt the archived light warm her back. [546] In that half-sleep, she felt quietly enthusiastic again, not about survival but about the elegance of a puzzle that solves itself by being noticed. [570] The backwards prophecy finished where it had started, which meant its beginning was still ahead, waiting for her to choose it.
