<story>Sir Aldric had not worn his armor in seven years, though he still oiled it each dusk out of habit. [20] He lived now as a withdrawn knight in the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night, where glass panes held constellations that never moved. [44] The orchard had been his penance and his laboratory, for he sought to capture the shape of possibility itself. [63] The trees here grew factually mystical fruit, each one recording a different version of the season it might have lived. [83] For decades the greenhouse had been stable, but now its air trembled as reality shifts around its roots. [101] He felt it first in his quiet watch, when the hanging lemons began to hum in unison. [118] On the third evening of the tremor, he found something caught in the irrigation trough that did not belong to the orchard. [140] It was an abandoned marionette theater program, its paper swollen but still legible, advertising The Starless Orchard for one night only. [161] The ink showed tiny knights and puppet trees and a child in the audience circled in red. [178] Aldric understood then that the greenhouse had once been a stage, and that his solitude had been its intermission. [197] He remembered the stories that puppeteers used to navigate endless night through forgotten patterns stitched into their scripts. [215] That night the branches above him rearranged themselves when he was not looking, spelling a low doorway he had never pruned. [236] Beyond it, a small wooden theater stood intact among roots, its curtains breathing faintly despite no wind. [253] Aldric knelt, and with hands accustomed to gauntlets he lifted the damp program to read the final act. [271] The script was not printed in words but in grafting diagrams, each cut promising a different fruit, a different future. [291] His own armor, as if answering, began to ache at the clasps, and he had to unsnap the vambrace to steady his shaking wrist. [315] Inside the vambrace he had hidden, years ago, a copper seed no larger than a tooth. [331] It pulsed, warm, waiting for soil that had never existed before. [342] From the little theater, a voice like dry leaves asked him for a murmured allegiance, not to a king or kingdom, but to the orchard itself. [368] Aldric had broken one oath to retreat here, and the weight of that failure still pinned him more firmly than any armor. [390] He hesitated, because allegiance after withdrawal felt like a second death, or a first honest breath. [406] Yet the trembling in the glass, in the lemons, in his own newly bare forearm, was not fear but a shivering bliss he had not known since his knighting. [435] He stepped through the branch doorway into the marionette house and saw no puppets, only strings descending from dark into soil. [456] Each string was tied to a sleeping root, and each root waited for a promise. [471] Aldric pressed the copper seed into the richest shadow and whispered, I will tend what might be, not what was commanded. [492] The greenhouse exhaled, and every leaf turned its palm open to catch a light that had not yet arrived. [511] All at once, as reality shifts, the fruit ripened sideways into versions of themselves that had never been possible in his old world. [534] He saw golden apples that remembered being stars, plums that opened into compasses, and one small lemon that held his own unarmored face smiling. [558] The strings above the seed twitched and lifted, no longer puppeteering but pruning, guiding its growth into open air. [577] Aldric laughed, a rusted sound, and understood that knighthood had never been about holding a line, but about tending a threshold. [598] He would remain, not hidden, but rooted, keeper of the greenhouse that dreamed orchards. [612] And somewhere above the endless night cracked, just enough for a thin dawn to slip through like sap. [630]</story>
