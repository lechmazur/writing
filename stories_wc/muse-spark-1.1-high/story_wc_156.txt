<story>The curator asked if the chapel mural could ever truly be restored. [12] Mara had not answered yet, and in the pause between question and reply her mind unfolded elsewhere. [29] She was a vivid mural restorer, known for coaxing color back from smoke and neglect with fingers stained permanent blue. [49] She worked methodically playful, logging each solvent test like a game, each brushstroke like a secret rule she had invented. [69] For three months she had been restoring the frescoes inside the sound wave garden, a courtyard where copper chimes and curved walls amplified whispers into weather. [95] The garden was not planted with flowers but with resonance, each sculpture a tuned lens for sound. [112] Her grandfather's hobo nickel sat warm in her palm, the buffalo's face recarved into a huddled vagabond with a bindle. [132] He had told her never to trade it for certainty, only for a better question. [147] That was why she had come to exchange certainty for curiosity, leaving tenured museum work for crumbling walls that might answer back. [169] The chapel mural showed a crowd asleep under a painted sky, each figure dreaming a smaller crowd, which dreamed another. [189] She had been tracing that recursion for weeks, believing it was allegory, not mechanism. [203] Yesterday she had fallen asleep on the scaffolding, the chimes humming, and had awakened inside the mural itself. [221] In that dream she was still restoring, but the pigments were wet, and the sleepers watched her with half-open eyes. [241] She rubbed the hobo nickel for luck, and the metal caught a tone from the garden that should not have been there. [263] It was a voice, not heard directly but through reflected sound bouncing off the chapel's apse and the nickel's uneven face. [284] The reflection seemed to bewitch the air, slowing dust so it hung like levitating gold leaf. [300] A child from the painted crowd sat up and asked Mara if she knew she was dreaming. [317] Mara said of course, this is my second awakening, which made the child laugh. [331] The child told her there were more, nested awakenings layered like plaster, each revealing a larger studio. [348] To prove it, he pressed his forehead to hers, and she jolted awake back on the scaffold to real wind and real chimes. [371] She was alone, the nickel now cool, her cheek marked from the wooden plank. [385] At first she thought the earlier layer was just a nap, but the mural had changed; one small sleeper was now painted awake, holding something round. [411] She climbed closer, her heart hammering through reflected sound that returned her breathing as a chorus. [427] The object was her grandfather's hobo nickel, reproduced perfectly in pigment that was still drying. [442] That was the third awakening; she understood she had not left the mural at all. [457] The sound wave garden beyond the chapel doors was no longer an installation but a wilderness of vibrating glass reeds that sang with every step. [482] The sleepers were all standing now, watching her decide whether to restore or reveal. [496] Mara, still methodically playful even in terror, placed her real nickel beside its painted twin and listened. [513] Through reflected sound the two coins struck a single note that seemed to peel the painted sky away. [531] Underneath was not plaster but daylight, and she was back on the chapel floor, neck stiff, curator waiting. [549] Only a blink had passed, yet she held the nickel so tightly its edge had left a tiny wagon wheel in her palm. [572] The question hung again, and she finally had a true reply. [583] She said no, it cannot be restored to what it was, but it can be awakened into what it is becoming, and that is more interesting. [609] The curator frowned, then smiled, because in that answer he heard curiosity replace certainty like a fresh coat breathing. [628]</story>
