<story>Elias was a mirror maker who had spent forty years keeping his feet and his craft firmly grounded. [18] While other artisans in the lower town chased rumors of hidden light, he measured silver and glass with patience. [37] But last winter his daughter Mara began to sleep with a fever that gave her one endless dream. [55] She described it as a bright hare running through trees that never ended. [68] One night upon the shimmer of twilight frost, the dream finally tore loose from her and fled. [85] Elias woke to find her breathing shallow and empty, as if a part of her had escaped into the cold. [105] The old women said dreams that run away drift to the ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog. [125] No road led there anymore, only drowned roots and stones wearing lichen like blindfolds. [139] Elias packed his heaviest mirror, a small hammer, and the astronomer's broken sextant he kept for measuring angles. [157] The sextant had belonged to his brother, who had tried to map stars through the fog and failed. [175] Its arc was cracked and its sight was clouded, but it could still catch and hold a single reflection. [194] He walked into the fog until the town's bells could no longer be heard, and the ruined orchard rose around him like ribs. [217] The trees were black and twisted, and their bare branches rang faintly when touched, clear and brittle as glass mountains in winter. [239] Frost covered everything, turning each leaf and thorn into a sharp transparent edge that sang under his boots. [257] Between the trunks he saw it, Mara's dream, flickering as a hare made of pale warm light. [274] It darted and paused, always out of reach, and it did not cast a shadow in the frost. [292] Elias understood then that his purpose was not to chase it but to capture a runaway dream and return it gently. [313] He set his mirror upright against a dead apple trunk and tried to coax the hare closer with reflected moonlight. [333] The hare circled, curious, but it would not cross into any surface that demanded a boundary between true and untrue. [353] Elias remembered his brother once saying that navigation is not about fixing points but about erasing boundaries between sky and earth. [374] He lifted the astronomer's broken sextant and held its shattered mirror against his own perfect mirror. [390] The two reflections made a corridor with no end, a tunnel of frost and glass where inside and outside switched places. [411] He saw himself reflected infinitely, and between each self he saw the hare breathing softly, no longer alone. [429] Elias kneeled and began to recreate the rhythm of his daughter's sleep through synchronized breathing. [444] He inhaled for four counts and exhaled for four counts, and the light in the mirrors inhaled and exhaled with him. [465] Slowly the fog itself seemed to follow the pattern, thinning and thickening like a lung made of crystal. [483] The air became still and bright, and every frosted twig held its breath between him and the dream. [501] In that shared pause the hare stopped running and looked directly at him, trusting the stillness. [517] Elias did not grab, he only tilted the broken sextant so its imperfect light spilled over the hare like honey. [537] The hare stepped forward into the seam between the two mirrors, where reflection became refuge instead of trap. [555] With careful hands Elias folded the mirrors together, closing the dream inside a soft dark between glasses. [572] The journey back was harder, for the fog pressed close, but the weight against his chest kept his steps firmly grounded. [593] When he reached his house, twilight had turned to night and the frost had begun to melt from the eaves. [613] Mara lay still pale, and he opened the paired mirrors above her and exhaled once more in her rhythm. [632] The light hare leapt from glass to breath to skin and vanished, and her chest rose deep and warm. [651] She woke moments later speaking of apple trees that rang like glass mountains when the wind passed through them. [670] Elias said nothing, only polished his mirrors and mended the sextant into a single instrument that could both find and return. [691]</story>
