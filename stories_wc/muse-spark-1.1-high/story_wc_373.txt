<story>She had been the moonshine runner's daughter long before she had a name anyone in town used. [17] They called her Mae only at the general store, and even then it sounded borrowed. [32] Her father had taught her how to listen for the change in an engine when revenue agents were near, how to hold her breath through a checkpoint, how to be curiously steadfast when every instinct said to run. [70] He disappeared two winters ago, leaving behind a mason jar of river water and an antique watch fob with no watch. [91] The fob was brass, warm even in cold, engraved with a tiny ship that never stopped sailing. [108] Mae kept it in her pocket because it thrummed like a second pulse. [121] Tonight she slipped down to the river where the boards of the old pier sagged into black water, for the tide charts said the sellers would gather in the pirate market hidden beneath a rotting pier. [157] She had until sunrise to make the exchange her father had promised and never completed. [172] The market was not a market at all, but a truce of hush made of lowered lanterns and men who spoke with their hands. [196] The air smelled of brine, diesel, and sweet corn liquor sweating through pine crates. [210] It should have terrified her, but the low murmurs and the river lapping the pilings gave her a feeling of comforting unease, like being sung to sleep in a boat that leaks. [242] Every deal there was sealed with a sound, not a signature. [253] Mae came to find the man who bought her father's last run, to thwart the story that her father had simply run off with the money. [279] More than that, she had brought her father's old field recorder, wired to a single cracked headphone, to capture the sound of trust breaking. [303] She did not know what that sound was, only that she would know it when she heard it split. [322] The buyer was called Cobb, a broad man with tar under his nails who kept the fob's twin watch in his vest. [344] When he saw Mae's open palm and the fob glinting in it, his mouth tightened. [359] You ain't supposed to be here, girl, he said, not loud enough to break the market's agreement. [376] Mae tried to speak clearly through false starts, her voice catching on rehearsed accusations and then retreating. [393] I didn't come for money, she finally managed. [401] I came because nobody heard how it ended. [409] Cobb stared at the recorder's little red eye and laughed without sound. [421] He slipped his own watch out and paired its chain with Mae's fob, and for a second the engraved ship met its sea. [444] That was how it was supposed to have gone last time, he whispered, your daddy brought me three crates and I shorted him because I thought the agents were on him. [475] He was supposed to come back when it was safe and scream at me, so everyone would think we were square. [496] But he never came back, and the river kept his secret. [507] The red light blinked steadily, listening. [513] Mae felt the old shame loosen, then tighten into something sharper, because now she heard it, the faint, clean click as Cobb's chain snapped and his trust in his own lie broke. [545] She had wanted a loud betrayal, but the truth was small and metallic, like a fob hitting wet wood. [564] Cobb did not pick up the fob. [571] He pushed both pieces toward her and said, tell them he was square. [584] Mae tucked the joined set into her pocket, switched off the recorder, and let the market swallow her in its quiet again. [606] She climbed back to the road before the sky paled, the fob heavy against her thigh, the tape hissing softly with proof that some endings are quieter than debts. [635]</story>
