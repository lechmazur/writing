[48] Whenever his palms pressed against the humid bark of the living bridge garden, where woven ficus roots arched across deep abysses, he saw centuries of nameless pilgrims crossing in trembling devotion. [79] He did not labor merely to guide the wanderers through these suspended green aisles; his lifelong obsession was to discover the texture of time. [103] Did chronology rasp like desiccated moss, or did it yield like the wet pulp of young ivy? [120] In his heavy linen coat, he kept a bundle of fading photographs, their paper fibers brittle and their silver emulsions leaching into pale gray shadows. [145] Each portrait was an ancestor whose face was slowly evaporating into blank milkiness, losing its grip against the tide of oblivion. [166] Moros despised how flat these keepsakes were, how utterly incapable they were of preserving the tangible grain of a moment. [186] To grasp what lay beyond such phantom layers, he drew a halo fragment from his velvet pouch. [203] The relic was an arc of curved, unearthly aurum, salvaged from an icon shattered before the canyon gardens were ever seeded. [224] It vibrated faintly against his calloused knuckles, thrumming with a sacred, unspent geometry. [264] Here, he had shaped the hedges into a sprawling topological anomaly. [322] He wedged the halo fragment into the keystone junction of the central arch, precisely where the hyperbolic planes folded into an impossible vertex. [644] There was no pain in this dissolution, only the overwhelming awe of experiencing every grain of existence simultaneously. [662] His retrocognitive trap had become an open doorway, expanding outward into the infinite foliage of the living bridge garden. [681] The quaking reverie subsided into an eternal, quiet stillness as his consciousness wove itself directly into the roots and vines.
