Maren was an ardent ferry deckhand who believed every crossing mattered, even the ones that erased the harbor behind them.  Each night when clocks run sideways, her commuter ferry materialized inside the spectral grotto under starlight, where crystal spires chimed like wind chimes made of glass.  Passengers boarded with briefcases and bicycles, unaware they were stepping between parallel timelines until the deck plates flickered and the vessel vanished midroute.  Maren kept an empty hourglass bolted to her belt, its glass worn smooth by decades of sand that refused to fall in any single direction.  The foreman called the vanishing commuter ferry a curse, but Maren was resiliently positive; she treated every disappearance as a scheduling error the universe had forgotten to correct.  On her breaks she tuned a cracked radio to static, hoping to catch the voice of the schedulemaster who governed the grotto.  One sideways evening, a pattern emerged from the noise, a repeating sequence of clicks that matched the grooves carved into the grotto floor.  She realized the ferry was not lost but oscillating, its hull vibrating across neighboring worlds connected via interconnected patterns of light beneath the water.  If she could read the full design, she could calibrate the route to merge parallel timelines into a single shoreline.  Her passengers were not ordinary travelers; each one was an echo repeating the same commute on a different branch of existence, carrying identical newspapers with slightly different headlines.  The empty hourglass on her belt began to hum whenever the radio found a new station that did not exist in her world.  She filled her notebook with diagrams, linking the timing of vanishings to the phases of starlight and the slant of crooked clocks.  The grotto seemed to breathe with her, its rocks humming in sympathy, as if the cave itself wanted the discord resolved.  During the next crossing, she climbed the wheelhouse roof and held the radio toward the starfield, broadcasting the pattern she had decoded.  The answer came back as a chord, a harmonic ladder rising through the static that told her exactly where to turn the wheel when clocks ran sideways.  She adjusted the empty hourglass until it rang like a tuning fork, its void becoming a bridge between the versions of the grotto.  The ferry shuddered, and the spectral walls folded inward like pages of a book being shut by a gentle hand.  In that breath, the passengers on deck multiplied into translucent twins, then merged into single figures who blinked and looked around with calm recognition.  The headlines on their newspapers now matched their eyes, their steps landing on one solid timeline.  Maren laughed, resiliently positive to the end, and flipped the hourglass over, though it remained empty.  Sand was unnecessary when the pattern itself was the measure, and she was now the keeper of a route that no longer vanished.  Yet the grotto still shimmered between worlds, and the ferry still arrived and departed at angles that made navigation instruments weep.  She had merged the commuter lanes into one channel, but she remained a permanent transient, never meant to dock in any single harbor.  Each evening she radioed the now-settled passengers from the spectral grotto under starlight, singing schedules through static so they would remember the way home.  Her message was simple: every pattern, however tangled, could be rewritten into a bridge, and every empty vessel could carry hope across the dark.  And though the clocks still ran sideways, she no longer minded the tilt, for she had learned to dance inside the drift.  On calm nights she traced the grooves beneath the water, grateful that the ferry had finally stopped vanishing, even if she herself never fully arrived.  The empty hourglass stayed on her belt, a reminder that time is not measured by falling grains but by the courage to keep sailing when the map is gone.  Under starlight that never sat still, the ardent deckhand signaled once more into her radio, her voice bright as a lantern in fog, guiding the next lost commuter home.  Somewhere between the sideways hours, the grotto applauded with soft waves, and the ferry sailed on, permanent in its transience, whole in its wandering.
