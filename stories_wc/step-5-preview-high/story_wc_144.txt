The mist-coated rail depot breathed salt and steam as the morning tide pulled the harbor into gray silence. 
Inside the repair shop, a heartfelt artisan named Soren stood beside a workbench cluttered with brass feathers and quartz lenses, overseeing every tremor of the bench while blind. 
He had lost his sight to a workshop fire years ago, yet his hands remembered the grammar of engines better than his eyes ever had. 
Before him rested an artificial hummingbird drone, its iridescent wings cracked, its navigation core sputtering static that sounded like panic to his trained ear. 
Outside the shop windows, water features carved into the depot plaza sang softly, channeling rainwater through glass veins that ran parallel to the iron rails. 
Soren ran his fingers along a copper tube that fed the indoor calibration basin, feeling the cool pulse of water cycling through the shop like a second bloodstream. 
The depot was unusually quiet, save for the low groan of a winch far out on the pier, where the Meridian was preparing to depart. 
It was her maiden voyage, and the anchor would lift within the hour, carrying the city's hopes into open fog. 
The drone was meant to scout ahead, decoding feedback into direction so the ship could thread the harbor without human eyes. 
Soren's motivation was simple: he had built the drone's voice, and he would not let it fly broken. 
He called for his apprentice, Mira, whose footsteps arrived light and steady across the oiled boards. 
"Bring the basin to the window," Soren said, his voice carrying a magnetic calm that seemed to settle the dust around him. 
Mira positioned a shallow crystal tank where the depot's mist thinned against the morning light, and filled it until the surface trembled with tension. 
Soren pressed his palm to the tank's rim, examining light refraction through water features not by sight, but by the way Mira described the bent colors and by the heat signatures mapped across his skin. 
A hairline fracture in the drone's left optic flared violet in the water's prism, and Soren felt the temperature shift against his knuckles like a whispered word. 
"The patch must bridge the lens and the rotor joint," he murmured. 
Mira handed him a filigree of silver thread and translucent resin, and he began to patch the wound with motions as careful as a heartbeat. 
Each stitch sealed the crack, each layer restored the hummingbird's balance, and the static in the navigation core began to smooth into a chord. 
The drone's feedback no longer screamed; it hummed, waiting to be understood. 
Soren lifted the machine to the open vent where harbor air rushed through the shop, thick with salt and the groan of chains. 
He activated the receiver, and the drone's chirps poured into a braided speaker that translated light refraction data from the plaza's water channels into tactile pulses against his chest. 
Through the feedback, he could feel the harbor: the breakwater's angle, the deep channel's curve, the fog's soft magnetic pull. 
He decoded feedback into direction, turning scattered signals into a single luminous path that only the drone could see. 
Outside, the anchor lifted. 
The sound rang across the depot like a bell struck underwater, and every worker paused in the gray light. 
The Meridian was moving. 
Soren opened his hand. 
The artificial hummingbird drone shot from his palm into the mist, its wings stitching silver arcs through the salt air. 
It climbed above the rails, then banked toward the ship, guided by the heading Soren had drawn from static and water. 
In the repair shop, Soren listened as the drone's first clean transmission returned: the channel was clear. 
He smiled, a small magnetic calm settling over the depot as the Meridian slipped past the breakwater, her maiden voyage begun. 
Behind him, the water features continued their patient refraction, turning the morning into directions only the blind could read.
