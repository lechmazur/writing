The floating rock tea ceremony platform hung above the squall line in a saturated hush, its basalt rim wet from the storm that had just passed. 
Nima waited behind the low table, a budding rune scholar with ink-stained sleeves, for the second storm to gather beyond the horizon. 
The tea master had told her that the sky allowed only a breath between storms, a pause too short for lies and long enough for one honest inscription. 
During a held breath, custom forbade speech, so the only sounds were tea, wind, and the platform's runes humming. 
Her task was to carve a guest's wounds into living letters, because the order believed scribes must transform scars into stories. 
She set the crow quill trimmed tip beside the inkstone, its slanted nib darkened with storm-tea. 
Her sister had trimmed the quill from a crow that died warning their village of an early tempest. 
Across the table, a cloud-pilot waited with his burned wrist open, eyes fixed on the inkstone. 
Nima lifted the quill, but the old splinter of black glass in her forearm began to crystallize when stressed, spreading cold silver lines toward her elbow. 
She understood that panic would seal her hand and waste the breath between storms. 
The platform tilted; clouds churned below, and the hush grew so saturated she could hear her heartbeat. 
With no voice to steady her, she worked by revisiting youthful superstitions that had once kept her alive through thunder. 
She tapped her cup three times to keep the storm polite, then touched the rim again, letting the gesture settle her ribs. 
She laid a crumb of tea-cake at the platform's edge for the crows, as she had done when she was small and afraid. 
She pressed a smooth stone against her pulse, a childhood promise that the earth remembered her name. 
The superstitions were childish, but they gave her a shape to hold in the silence. 
She drew the first rune with the crow quill trimmed tip, a clear line meaning endurance without erasure. 
The pilot turned his scarred wrist, and she traced each ridge as if reading a sentence written in raised flesh. 
Every mark was meant to give the wounds grammar and witness, not to hide them. 
The crystal in her arm grew warmer, then stranger, as stress turned to frost and frost to ink. 
She did not dare to breathe, for the pause depended on every held breath aboard the platform. 
When the next rune demanded a curve she had never mastered, the splinter crystallizes when stressed in earnest. 
A bead of blood formed at her elbow, and she feared it would fall into the tea and break the ceremony. 
Instead of fleeing, she named the fear the way she had named storms as a girl, an old superstition that made terror small enough to carry. 
She set down the quill, touched the cup rim, and shaped without breath the childhood words that asked thunder to wait. 
The crystal cracked, not with pain but with release, and began to shed thin silver shells. 
The shed crystals fell into the storm-tea and dissolved into pale letters spelling the pilot's history. 
Nima realized the body had tried to write the story all along in the only hard language it knew. 
She lifted the crow quill trimmed tip and guided the floating runes into order: lightning, rescue, debt, and a scar turned vow. 
The guest's open hand relaxed, his burns no longer hidden or apologetic. 
The stones brightened as pressure dropped, and the hush held a fullness like a suspended note. 
Nima felt her own scars not as weights but as chapters waiting to be read. 
The final rune was a small circle, the sign her sister had drawn on bandages to mean not yet finished. 
As she closed the circle, the storm exhaled, and the held breath of the world ended in a rush of wind. 
Rain rattled the platform, but the letters in the tea did not wash away. 
The pilot bowed, pressed his wrist to his heart, and left into the rain carrying his story. 
Nima cleaned the crow quill trimmed tip, thinking of crows, superstitions, and children who talk to storms. 
The wound remained, but it had changed from silent splinter to sentence she could speak. 
The floating rock tea ceremony platform held, anchored by runes older than fear. 
She placed the smooth stone beside the quill as an offering to the girl she had been, and smiled into the saturated hush. 
Then the rain eased, and she prepared for the next guest, ready to hold her breath again.
