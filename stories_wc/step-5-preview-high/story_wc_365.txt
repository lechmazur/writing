In the murmur of half-remembered dreams, the abandoned spaceport repair bay breathed with the patience of rust. Kael worked there, a sensory map maker who charted worlds not by their stars but by the way their silence tasted. His hands moved across corroded consoles, recording the copper scent of ozone and the low hum of pressure seals long dead. On the workbench before him lay a green glass shard, no larger than his thumb, its edges catching light that had no source. It was cyclically spontaneous, flaring awake every seventh breath and singing a frequency that only he could feel in his teeth. Kael had learned to weave between worlds by riding those pulses, redirecting his mass through parallel universes where gravity pulled differently. He did not search for treasure or escape. His motivation was older and heavier: to discover the weight of a withheld truth. The spaceport had swallowed a cargo of silence eleven years ago, and every official record had blurred like wet ink. Somewhere in the drift of adjacent realities, someone had known what truly happened, and they had hidden it behind a door of not-quite-memory. The shard buzzed again, cyclically spontaneous, and the repair bay folded. Kael felt his senses peel sideways. The rust became violet. The silence turned sharp as a blade of citrus. He was weaving now, moving between worlds by the method his craft demanded: through parallel universes, each one a variation of scent and sound. In one, the bay was pristine and white. In another, it drowned beneath a black ocean. Kael mapped each crossing, but the shard led him onward, demanding he redirect his course toward a frequency that scraped the inside of his ribs. He obeyed, angling his sensory drift away from the easy paths and into a corridor where the air smelled of funeral lilies and cold iron. There, the shard showed him a version of the repair bay where he had never left. That Kael stood over a casket of frosted steel, and within it lay a child wearing a face that mirrored his own. The withheld truth struck him then with physical force. He had not been searching for another person's secret. He had been running from his own. The spaceport had not swallowed cargo. It had swallowed a child during an experiment in weaving, and the records had been blurred to protect him from a grief that would have unmade him. Now the weight of it settled into his chest, dense and cold as a star's heart. Kael understood why the shard was cyclically spontaneous. It woke every seventh breath because the boy in the casket still breathed on a rhythm not native to this world. The green glass shard had been the only fragment of his own memory that survived the crossing. To heal the fracture, Kael knew he had to redirect the weave back upon itself. He pressed the shard against his temple and turned his sensory map inward, charting the hidden interior where the withheld truth had grown heavy enough to bend reality. The repair bay trembled. Across parallel universes, a thousand versions of the spaceport exhaled at once. Kael felt the boy in the casket open eyes that matched his own, and for the first time, the silence between worlds carried no weight at all. He rose slowly, the green glass shard now dull and quiet, its cycle complete. The abandoned repair bay returned to its single, rusting shape. But the air had changed. It smelled of morning and wet soil, not ozone and regret. Kael walked to the bay doors and pushed them open onto a world he had charted only in dreams. He carried the weight with him still, but now it was bearable, a truth finally spoken in the murmur of half-remembered dreams.
