The arcane linguist descended the ninth stairwell at midnight, counting steps the way other people counted blessings. 
The bookstore occupied the entire mountain, a labyrinth of carved galleries where gravity obeyed cataloguing rules and echoes rehearsed symphonies no living ear had heard. 
For eleven years, she had led rescue missions into its throat, retrieving scholars who had wandered past the Forbidden Wing and become footnotes in their own narratives. 
She was carefully resourceful by necessity: the mountain did not permit torches, only borrowed lanterns, and the shelves rearranged themselves whenever a visitor felt certain of the way. 
After the time traveler arrived, however, the mountain began to hum. 
He had collapsed through the conservation dome in the Astrology Hall, his coat stitched with isotopes that no clock in the stacks could recognize. 
In his satchel lay a botanical diary, its pages swollen with pressed mosses that glowed when the mountain shifted, though he had claimed ignorance of how he came to possess it. 
She recognized the handwriting immediately. 
It belonged to Master Fenn, the Keeper who had vanished three winters ago while searching for the library of unlearned lessons. 
The linguist had never abandoned her own motivation to find that place, for she believed it held the single lesson that might have stayed Fenn's footsteps: that knowledge without rescue is merely a more elegant way to be lost. 
Now the diary offered coordinates written in chlorophyll and legal ink. 
She spread the pages across a reading desk that hovered over a chasm of unbound periodicals, carefully resourceful enough to weigh each leaf down with brass astrolabes looted from abandoned study carrels. 
The mosses were not decorative. 
They were acoustic instruments, recording vibrations from the mountain's hollow core, a silent symphony of footfalls and closing doors that Fenn had transcribed as directions. 
To reach the library, she would have to renegotiate the old charter with the Warden of Unfinished Volumes, an entity that dwelt in the sub-basement where the foundations were packed with rejected first drafts. 
The Warden spoke only in contractual gaps, exploiting every ambiguity in the lease between librarians and stone. 
The linguist had tried brute force once before, and the Warden had answered by sealing an entire quadrant with living ivy. 
This time, she would defeat it by researching gaps. 
She spent two days in the Index of Absences, a chamber where missing volumes were catalogued with greater precision than present ones. 
There she found that Fenn had quietly removed three clauses from the charter, replacing them with botanical notations that only bloomed under the light of a supernova. 
The time traveler, weak but lucid, explained that in his century those clauses had been restored by a linguist who had never learned to read flowers. 
The difference between the two versions was the renegotiation point. 
She descended to the Warden with the diary pressed to her chest and the time traveler's isotope coat draped over her shoulders like a banner of future rights. 
The Warden unfolded from the dark as a towering geometry of broken spines and cold starlight. 
"You seek the library," it whispered, and every unread book in the mountain shivered in sympathy. 
She answered not with threats, but with the gaps she had researched: the unlisted moss, the clause absent from both eras, the quiet measure where the symphony had no assigned instrument. 
She proposed a new arrangement. 
She would translate the mountain's unheard symphony into a language the Warden could hear, and in exchange the library of unlearned lessons would surrender its missing Fenn and the time traveler's lost route home. 
The Warden hesitated, for it had never been offered music, only silence and dust. 
Then it stepped aside. 
Behind it, a door of interlaced roots groaned open, revealing a spiraling gallery where every book was blank except for a single sentence of regret. 
At the center stood Fenn, alive, holding a pen that trembled with unfinished thoughts. 
The linguist guided him out, carefully resourceful to the last, using the diary's mosses as improvised rope when the stairwell contracted into a throat of paper. 
The time traveler followed, clutching the isotope coat that had become their passport backward through chronology. 
As they emerged into the conservation dome, the mountain released a chord so low and vast that the astronomers wept without knowing why. 
It was the first note of the unheard symphony finally given breath. 
The linguist remained behind for a moment, her hand upon the doorframe, knowing that some rescues end in departure and others in the quiet duty of cataloguing what has been saved. 
She closed the botanical diary and placed it upon the nearest shelf, where it belonged.
