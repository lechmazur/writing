The staircase had no beginning and no end, only the worn stone beneath her boots and the dim glow that never changed. 
She climbed because stopping had proven impossible, though she told herself each morning that ascent was a choice. 
They called her a grim optimist, though no one remained to speak; she had earned the title from her own reflection in polished brass dice. 
The pair were old, one corner chipped from a fall she did not remember, and she rolled them against each riser before stepping. 
Most days the chipped die landed true enough, its bias small, its flaw barely visible in the sourceless light. 
She was balancedly daring, neither reckless nor meek, trusting probability but verifying it with theorems scrawled in chalk upon the treads. 
The structure defied logic, an engine of perpetual motion that fed itself with her momentum, generating new flights above as she advanced. 
Every proof of thermodynamics she reconstructed ended in contradiction, yet the steps kept rising, cool and steady, without fuel or friction. 
Her motivation was not escape but recovery; long ago, perhaps at the first step or merely a dream of one, she had passed a doorway unopened. 
She wanted to capture the texture of that missed opportunity, the grain of wood under a hand she had never extended, the velvet of hesitation. 
Time offered no help, for the staircase existed in a timeframe none could name, a present tense stretched across eternity without decay. 
So she worked through mathematical proofs, filling margins of memory with lemmas about infinity, trying to force the geometry to confess its flaw. 
The chipped die was her instrument of controlled chaos, a reminder that perfection could not be perpetual, that all machines must contain an error. 
She learned to read its bias, turning the broken corner into a compass that pointed toward the seams in reality. 
On the ten-thousandth equation, or perhaps the first, she found the cancellation term, a hidden asymmetry in the staircase's self-sustaining loop. 
The rupture began not with thunder but with a whisper, a hairline crack racing along the balustrade as her chalk line completed itself. 
Stone peeled away from stone, revealing not void but a vast machinery of gears and light, grinding endlessly, powered by nothing but her refusal to stop. 
The boundless confinement had always been a prison without walls, its infinitude the very bars that held her. 
She stood balancedly daring at the edge of the tear, dice in her palm, the chipped one warm from her grip. 
To capture the texture of the missed opportunity, she would have to touch the rupture and remember everything she had chosen not to feel. 
She pressed her palm against the jagged wound in the architecture, and the perpetual motion stuttered. 
For the first time, silence had weight; for the first time, the stairs did not continue. 
She fell through the opening, not downward but inward, into the dense and rough fabric of the door she had never opened. 
The dice tumbled free, the chipped one striking first, and in its asymmetric spin she read the probability of second chances. 
She did not reach the bottom, for there was no bottom, only a landing of ordinary wood with a latch worn smooth by other hands. 
Her fingers found the grain exactly as she had imagined, rough and warm, the texture of a missed opportunity held at last in her grasp. 
The grim optimist turned the handle, knowing it might open to nothing, hoping it opened to work, content that the motion had finally ended. 
Behind her the infinite staircase sealed itself, patient and perpetual, waiting for the next climber to doubt the geometry of their cage.
