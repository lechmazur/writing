In the canyon of endless echoes, where towers of bone rose from centuries of layered dust, the harmonic stone skimmer kept vigil. 
Each morning the skimmer traced the grooves of fossilized ribs, reverent before the histories pressed into marrow and mineral. 
Being historically reverent, they believed every speck of dust held a map of footsteps that had long since stilled. 
The skimmer's own footprints had vanished beneath layers, yet their resolve weathered on, stubborn as iron in rain. 
For generations, the elders had spoken of a pulse left wandering, a heartbeat without a chest, searching the bone halls for its source. 
This pulse, they said, could not be caught by hand or snare, only understood by one who sought to learn to navigate without moving. 
The skimmer had inherited that same hunger, not to conquer distance, but to know the canyon as intimately as a held breath. 
One dusk, while brushing centuries of layered dust from a collapsed spire, they unearthed a stone arrowhead. 
Its edge was honed sharper than any echo, its flanks cool with a silence that predated the bone towers. 
When the skimmer pressed thumb to tip, the wandering pulse shivered in answer, a faint tremor climbing the arm like a memory. 
They could not yet read the language of that thrum, so they resolved to appraise its origin with patience older than the dust. 
The method came not from scrolls or chants, but through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight. 
Each dawn, jackdaws and ash-colored swallows wheeled between the towers, their wings sketching vectors across the vault. 
The skimmer watched until eyes stung, seeking in their swirling grammar a compass that required no step. 
Slowly, a truth emerged: the birds rode currents born of the pulse, and the pulse answered the stone arrowhead. 
To navigate without moving, one had to become as still as the arrowhead, as receptive as the dust, and as patient as bone. 
The skimmer sat cross-legged upon a plateau of calcified vertebrae, the arrowhead resting in an open palm. 
Above, the flock shifted in a sudden spiral, and the skimmer appraised the movement as a ledger of exits and entries. 
Here was a bend that signaled the deep eastern well; there, a dive that marked a shaft of whispering stone. 
Centuries of layered dust did not obscure these signs; rather, they provided the page upon which such knowledge was written. 
As the sun bled orange into the bone towers, the skimmer felt the wandering pulse align with a steady inner heart. 
It had sought a harbor, not a homeland, and in the stillness of a historically reverent witness, it found rest. 
The stone arrowhead grew warm, then cool, as if exhaling a tension older than the first echo in the canyon. 
In that moment, the skimmer navigated the labyrinth of valleys and ridges without lifting a single finger. 
They saw the flocks unite in a vast geometry, each turn a road, each descent a bridge, each rise a summit. 
The wandering pulse had become a map, and the skimmer, through weathered resolve, had learned to read it whole. 
No longer did they need to skim stones across dry lakebeds to know where hidden water lay. 
The canyon of endless echoes sang, and the harmonic stone skimmer answered in silence, holding the pattern fast. 
Travelers who came later would find the arrowhead embedded in a ledge of dust, humming a low chord. 
They would learn that stillness was not absence, but a vessel for every road that moved. 
Through centuries of layered dust, a pulse had wandered; through a single pair of watchful eyes, it had come home. 
And in the towers of bone, the dust settled gently, as if acknowledging a debt finally paid.
