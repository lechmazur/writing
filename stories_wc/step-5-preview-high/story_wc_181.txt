Mira arrived at the archive gate as a bewildered curator, expecting dust and instead finding an astral orchard blossoming on a lonely asteroid. 
The gate was only a hinged page of vellum, and the path beyond it was printed with silver type that shimmered like pollen. 
She had been hired to catalog a private collection, not to inherit one, and certainly not through a method her employer called nonlinear inheritance. 
The will was a single unbound leaf whose first line named a curator and whose last line kept rewriting itself whenever she tried to read ahead. 
Every steward, the note explained, served only between the first and last page, a brief interval in which the orchard decided who was worthy. 
Mira did not feel worthy; she felt simply complicated, as if her own thoughts had been pruned into espalier shapes by questions she had not yet asked. 
Still, she had accepted the post to pursue forbidden knowledge, and the orchard, glowing with petals like paper lanterns, seemed to promise exactly that. 
At the center of the asteroid stood a low dome of lacquered wood, and inside it she found a specimen tray holding one pistachio shell cameo blank. 
The shell was no larger than a thumbnail, its surface pale and faintly ridged, yet it radiated a warmth that made the orchard's borrowed sunlight feel cold. 
Beneath the tray lay a curator's log, and the handwriting was hers, though she had never written in that firm, unhurried hand. 
The first entry read, 'Inquire at the orchard when the blossoms turn silver; the blank will answer only when you stop pretending time is a straight line.' 
Mira looked up and saw the petals shift from white to silver, as though the trees were holding their breath. 
She walked the rows and chose the oldest tree, its trunk scarred with initials that ran backward from Z to A. 
Here she decided to inquire, not with a question spoken aloud, but by pressing the cameo blank against the bark until the shell grew damp with sap. 
Visions arrived in no order: a woman in a pressure suit planting a seed, a child hiding the seed inside a book, an old woman returning the seed to a walnut drawer. 
Each figure wore the same cameo pendant, and Mira understood that the orchard was not inherited forward but sideways, across the lives of those who read between the lines. 
The realization did not comfort her; it brightened her disquiet, turning fear into a strange, lucid urgency that made her palms sweat and her eyes shine. 
If inheritance was nonlinear, then the pistachio shell cameo blank was not an heirloom to be kept but a message to be carved, and she was both sender and recipient. 
She needed a quiet place to think, and the steward's cottage offered only one room with a lock: a narrow tiled bathroom that overlooked the orchard through a porthole. 
Mira had always had her best philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms, where the world was small enough to hold and the mirror showed her only what she was ready to see. 
She locked the door, ran the tap until steam fogged the porthole, and set the blank on the shelf beside the soap. 
In the mirror, her reflection lifted the shell a half-second before she did, and Mira finally asked the question the log had been waiting for. 
'What am I forbidden to know?' she said, and the word 'know' echoed as though the orchard were repeating it into a deeper room. 
The answer was simply complicated: the orchard was forbidden knowledge that had learned to grow, and every curator was a page through which it read itself. 
Mira's hand moved without her permission, carving a profile into the pistachio shell cameo blank as the silver blossoms outside brightened until the sky hurt. 
When the cameo was finished, the face looking back at her was older, kinder, and unmistakably her own, wearing a ring she had not yet bought. 
She understood then that she would leave this knowledge for a bewildered curator who would arrive between the first and last page, just as she had. 
The asteroid drifted on, a lonely lantern of blossoms, and Mira remained at the window, smiling nervously into the brightened disquiet of a story that had no end.
