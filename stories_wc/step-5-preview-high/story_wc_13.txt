Mara dipped her brush into the inkwell and sketched the ripple patterns circling the heron eel, but her gaze drifted toward the aqueduct bend where the morning light had begun to stutter. 
As a natural history illustrator for the Canal Conservatory, she was tasked with documenting the rooftop canal system that wove between the towers like silver thread through bone. 
The heron eel was common enough, but the stuttering light was not. 
It pulsed in a broken rhythm, as if some great metronome had skipped a beat. 
She touched the infinity symbol pendant beneath her collar, a family heirloom whose smooth surface grew warm whenever time itself was being sculpted by unseen hands. 
Her grandmother had taught her the forbidden method of borrowing time, pulling spare seconds from a sleeper's dreams to stretch one moment into an hour. 
Today, across the giant's nap, she intended to use it. 
The giant slept beneath the lower terraces, his snores rolling through the city like distant thunder, his breathing responsible for the tidal rise and fall of the rooftop waterways. 
During the deepest part of his nap, the canals ran low, exposing old mooring rings and phosphorescent moss that lined their beds. 
It was the only hour when the place where echoes are born might be reachable. 
Echoes here were not merely sound but pale, winged creatures hatched from reverberation, and Mara had spent three years trying to find their nursery. 
The Conservatory called her reckless; her ledger called her obsessed. 
She packed her sketchbook, her charcoal, and a brass tuning fork calibrated to the frequency of unfinished music. 
The interrupted cadence was the key. 
Every nursery in the natural world was guarded by a pattern, and the echo nursery was sealed inside a musical phrase that never resolved. 
To uncage the hatchlings, she had to break the cadence at its hinge, catching the reverberation in the breath between notes. 
She rowed her flat-bottomed skiff toward the bend where the light had stuttered, the oars dipping in time with the giant's slow, oceanic exhale. 
The water there was unnaturally still, and the towers leaned close enough that their windows became a canyon of whispering reflections. 
In the center of the bend hovered a sphere of compressed silence, visibly distorting the air like a lens of glass. 
That was the cage. 
Mara closed her eyes and reached for the pendant, feeling the infinity symbol grow cold as she began to sculpt time itself around her fingertips. 
She borrowed ten minutes from the giant's nap, feeling a subtle strain in her chest as his dream briefly stuttered. 
The canal water froze in place, droplets suspended like tiny planets, and the stuttering light solidified into a visible seam in the air. 
She struck the tuning fork against her thumb and let its clear tone roll across the surface of the frozen canal. 
The interrupted cadence answered, a chord that climbed toward resolution but broke at the last instant. 
The silence sphere cracked. 
From within spilled the newborn echoes, translucent and trembling, their wings beating out rhythms that had not yet been given names. 
Mara opened her sketchbook with trembling hands and began to draw, not to capture them, but to honor them. 
Each stroke of her pencil was an act of witness, recording the moment when uncaged sound first met the air and learned to fly. 
She worked in borrowed time, her fingers moving faster than biology allowed, the giant's nap lending her minutes she did not possess. 
When the last hatchling vanished into the canals, the sphere collapsed into a whisper of wind. 
Mara felt the borrowed time return to the sleeping giant like a tide going out, and her knees buckled against the skiff's edge. 
The normal rhythm of the city rushed back in: water lapping, gulls crying, the distant rumble of the giant rolling onto his side. 
She had found the place where echoes are born, and in her sketchbook she carried their first portrait, a page that would secure her reputation and damn her methodology in equal measure. 
As she rowed back through the weaving canals, the infinity symbol pendant cooled against her skin, and Mara smiled. 
Some things were worth a little stolen time.
