The insomniac poet had not slept since the reef forgot the color of peace. 
Their home was a gargantuan shell, a spiraling cathedral of nacre and brine. 
For centuries of longing, the mollusk-folk and the coral clans had turned tenderness into armor. 
Their spears were calcified ribs of grief, and no one remembered how to disarm. 
The poet wandered the corridors every night, too restless to dream and too gentle to kill. 
Their only hunger was to photosynthesize pain into beauty, to turn hurt into something that fed the light. 
Notebooks brimmed in their wake, filled with unwritten laments and maps of remembered stars. 
Each dawn the tide left new debris at the shell's lip: broken shields, torn flags, a child's wooden comb. 
The poet kept these relics not to hoard sorrow but to alchemize it into song. 
They believed beauty was not a luxury but a kind of oxygen for drowning souls. 
One dusk, a thread of current slipped through the shell's throat and left a damp rectangle on the tiles. 
It was a galaxy postcard, its corners soft with salt, its front bruised with violet nebulas. 
At the center bloomed an impossible flower, and beneath it, faded ink read: blossom armistice. 
The poet touched the petals with ink-stained fingers and felt a shyly unstoppable resolve take root. 
Sleep was a locked door, but this new purpose walked through walls. 
The poet chose to ensconce the postcard inside the shell's hollow nave, where sound pooled like tidewater. 
Strands of kelp-thread pinned the image to a pillar of mother-of-pearl so all could see its promise. 
Then, night after night, the poet climbed the spiral gallery above the warring quarters. 
They had no army or treaty hall, only a voice that cracked like dry coral. 
So they began to make peace via half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key wind. 
The gusts slipped through every fissure, tuneless yet intimate as a mother's breath. 
At first the sentries cursed the draft and stuffed their ears with wads of kelp. 
But the lullabies accumulated like silt, softening the joints of ancient grudges. 
The first creature to yield was a young hermit crab that had lost its borrowed home. 
It crept into the nave and curled beside the postcard as if the glow were a hearth. 
Then a sea hare followed, then a shrimp with a scarred tail, each drawn by the off-key lullaby. 
A coral general lowered her trident when she recognized her own childhood humming. 
A mollusk knight unlatched his helm so the off-key wind could comb his sea-worn hair. 
The poet sang until their throat tasted of brine and starlight, yet true sleep never came. 
Instead, the reef began to dream in green and gold and rose. 
Corals that had calcified into fortresses released new polyps, pale and curious as thoughts. 
The mollusk-folk opened their shells, revealing flesh that remembered how to be soft. 
In the nave, beneath the glowing galaxy postcard, the two factions met without weapons. 
They did not sign parchment; they planted cuttings in the cracks of old battlements. 
Roots drank from the condensation of tears, and petals pried open the dark. 
This was the blossom armistice, a treaty written in pollen and sealed with nectar. 
No trumpet announced it; only a reef-shadow joy moved beneath the coral like a tide. 
The poet watched from a shadowed corridor, still sleepless, still shyly unstoppable. 
Joy did not erase the centuries of longing, but it changed how the light fell. 
The poet returned each night to the gallery, ensconced by habit and by hope. 
They sang again, and the off-key wind learned to carry a tune but kept its charming flaw. 
Children of both factions played among the new blossoms, unaware of the old rules. 
The mollusk-folk brought offerings of pearls, not as tribute but as gratitude. 
The coral clans wove garlands of algae around the postcard's pearly frame. 
The poet began to sleep in snatches, dreaming of roots and open water. 
It was not the sleep of the innocent, but a restfulness that threaded through their bones. 
On the postcard's blank back, they wrote a final poem small as a breath. 
It read: Let our wounds flower, and let our flowers remember mercy. 
Across centuries of longing, that single line became the reef's first lullaby and its last war cry. 
Under the gargantuan shell, beauty kept blooming from the compost of old wars. 
And the insomniac poet, shyly unstoppable, photosynthesized pain into beauty until dawn.
