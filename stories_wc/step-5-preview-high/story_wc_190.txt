The drifting prism terrace turned slowly above the cloud sea, scattering pale fire across its glass walkways. 
In the quiet that follows a train whistle's echo, Corren, the gondola operator, tightened the brass clamps of the cable car and studied the horizon. 
The terrace was a cathedral of refracted light, and every footstep rang with the memory of sound. 
Corren's task was simple in design and fluidly logical in execution: keep the gondolas in transit between the floating spires without letting noise fracture the delicate glass. 
A small storm glass vial hung from the operator's belt, its contents shifting like frozen fog. 
Corren monitored noise levels by reading the vial, because on the prism terrace sound was not merely heard but grown into crystal. 
Loud grief or anger could spawn jagged spikes that made the whole terrace shudder. 
This evening a passenger waited at the far gantry, her shoulders tight, her boots making hard, uneven sounds against the plates. 
Corren knew the woman was not merely tired but carrying a grief too large for her throat. 
The operator pulled the gondola alongside with a hush of rope, gesturing her inside the red velvet cabin. 
As the car lurched into motion, the woman began to weep, and the storm glass vial clouded instantly. 
Corren's motivation had never been mere transport; it was to teach silence how to comfort, to show that quiet need not be absence but a hand upon the shoulder. 
The operator opened a panel beneath the dials, revealing a slender channel of phosphor and pulped reed. 
With steady hands, Corren took a dropper of liquid silver and let it fall into the storm glass vial to catalyze a calmer weather within the glass. 
The contents swirled, turned pale amber, then settled into a slow, breathing haze. 
Corren held the vial near the woman's ear, not as a trick but as a tuning fork for the soul. 
The gondola crossed the span where the prism terrace narrowed to a blade of violet light. 
Here the air was thinnest, and any cry might fracture the walkway into falling shards. 
Corren kept monitoring noise levels, fingers resting on a lever that adjusted the cabin's resonance to match the terrace's frequency. 
The woman's breathing slowed as the vial's soft light washed over her, and her weeping became a single, low note that harmonized with the cable's hum. 
In the quiet that follows a train whistle's echo, the cabin itself seemed to inhale, and the vaulting hush of the terrace wrapped around them like a lullaby made of glass. 
The woman looked up, her eyes clear, and whispered that the silence felt like being held. 
Corren nodded and guided the gondola toward the far platform, still in transit, still listening. 
On the drifting prism terrace, every crossing was a chance to prove that peace could be engineered without force. 
The operator's storm glass vial now showed a gentle blue, signaling that the passenger's inner weather had changed. 
Corren watched her step onto the crystal platform, her boots silent, her shoulders loosened. 
Another whistle rose in the distance, but this time its echo did not fade into loneliness; it dissolved into the vaulting hush that Corren had learned to shape. 
The next passenger approached with a rolling suitcase that clicked against the plates, and Corren prepared to monitor noise levels once more, adjusting the dials with a fluidly logical calm. 
Each journey across the span was a lesson in the physics of mercy, where the right frequency could turn sorrow into song. 
Corren lifted the storm glass vial to the dying light, watching the crystals settle into a perfect, restful clarity. 
In that vaulting hush, the gondola operator understood that silence, once taught to comfort, would cradle every traveler who dared to cross.
