The nocturnal observatory drifted above the cloud deck, its brass dome open to the star-stitched dark. 
On the floating isle below the structure, soil glowed faintly with residual enchantments that had never quite settled since the spoons bent reality. 
That calamity, three years past, had folded cutlery into the laws of physics, leaving shadows twitchy and afternoons hollow. 
Mira, a budding rune scholar with a quietly deliberate manner, climbed the spiral stair each dusk to study the vanishing. 
Her colleagues chased eclipses and comet tails, but she sought a single impossible fact: where shadows go at noon. 
The observatory's orrery, however, had fallen silent, its gears locked behind a seal that no modern incantation could break. 
In the vault beneath the transit chamber, Mira kept her sole inheritance: a clockwork automaton key no larger than her thumb, its teeth cut from meteoric iron. 
The device had belonged to her mentor, who had whispered that only by closing loops could the old machines wake again. 
After the spoons bent reality, loops had become literal things, circuits of cause and effect that no longer knew how to end. 
She spent the first month relearning the runic notation her mentor had scratched into the margins of star charts, each glyph a lesson in restraint. 
Quietly deliberate, she refused the shortcuts that warped metal into knots, knowing that any forced loop would snap. 
Instead she mapped the observatory's ley lines, tracing conductive paths from the horizon ring through the isle's core and back. 
Every path she completed turned the isle slightly, aligning its underbelly with constellations that had not risen for decades. 
On the forty-first night, the clockwork automaton key grew warm against her palm, reacting to a seam in the vault wall. 
She pressed the key into the seam and turned it; metal sighed, and a panel slid aside to reveal the sealed awakening chamber. 
Inside, an automaton the size of a chapel bell knelt among coils of silver thread, its face a blank mirror. 
The runes on its shoulders described not a spell but a method: to relearn what reality had misplaced, one must close every loop that grief or accident had left open. 
Mira understood then that the orrery's silence, the twitchy shadows, and the bent spoons were all symptoms of the same unclosed circuit. 
The spoons had bent reality by forcing open loops that should have ended, and shadows had been leaking into those gaps ever since. 
At noon, when light stood directly overhead, every shadow squeezed through the widest rift and disappeared into the fold. 
To find them, she had to wake the automaton, feed it the correct sequence, and close the loops that the spoon-benders had torn. 
She climbed back to the telescope deck with the silver thread, each strand a conductive memory of a star that had set before the disaster. 
The automaton followed in silence, its mirror face reflecting not her image but the loops she had yet to finish. 
For three nights she worked, quietly deliberate, connecting horizon to zenith, zenith to isle, isle to horizon, until the array hummed. 
The clockwork automaton key turned in the air beside her, spinning without a lock, seeking the final circuit. 
When the dawn finally threatened, she placed the key into the automaton's chest and closed the last loop, linking her own pulse to the machine. 
The sealed awakening began not with thunder but with a hush, as if the isle itself had finally drawn breath. 
The automaton's mirror face cleared, and it spoke in her mentor's voice, saying that shadows at noon retreat into the spaces between folded loops. 
They are not lost, it continued, only stored in the creases where bent spoons pushed the world aside, waiting for loops to close so they can flow home. 
Mira smiled, quietly deliberate even now, and asked how she might verify such a truth. 
The automaton raised its hand, and at that moment the sun cleared the horizon; a shadow pooled beneath a stool, dark and certain. 
She watched it until noon approached, and when the light stood overhead, the shadow did not vanish but stretched into the loop she had closed, returning as a cool ribbon across the floor. 
The budding rune scholar opened her notebook and began to relearn the proper notation for this new law, one patient loop at a time. 
Outside, the nocturnal observatory drifted into morning, its floating isle steady, its circuits finally complete.
