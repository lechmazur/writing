The beautician mortician knelt at the edge of the skyborne grotto under aurora, her tools arranged like surgical stars on a slab of frozen cloudstone. 
She had been summoned by the dying to catch the final shimmer of a soul, but tonight the task felt heavier, as if the wind itself held its breath. 
Her name was Sable, though few remembered it anymore, for she existed in the timeless hush where the horizon forgot to move. 
In her gloved hands she held an emerald chip, a sliver of polished memory that pulsed with a faint inner fire. 
It was her conduit, her anchor, the only thing capable of crossing a threshold of stillness without unraveling into silence. 
Sable was lucidly watchful, her gaze steady upon the shimmering curtains of light above, knowing that one blink might cost her the final window. 
Below the grotto lay an ocean of clouds, and above, the aurora danced in ribbons of green and violet, casting no shadow, only glow. 
The dead did not weep; they waited, their eyes open and fixed upon that radiant hush, begging for a beauty that would outlast decay. 
Sable had learned to infuse flesh with calm by finding patterns in static, by listening to the white noise between heartbeats until it sang a melody only the nearly gone could hear. 
She pressed the emerald chip against the brow of the woman before her, whose lips had already forgotten how to form words. 
The static rose, a soft hiss filling the cave, and Sable closed her eyes just enough to let the aurora bleed through her lashes. 
In that sound, chaotic and endless, she searched for the fractured rhythm of a life about to flicker out. 
It came to her as a stutter in the roar, a pattern shaped like a lullaby the woman had once hummed to her daughter. 
Sable traced it with her will, letting the emerald chip drink the note until the stone grew warm and luminous. 
She was no longer merely a beautician mortician; she had become a cartographer of last things, mapping the coordinates where existence surrendered to peace. 
Her hands moved without tremor, mixing pigment with dew, brushing color into cheeks that had surrendered their warmth. 
The threshold of stillness opened before her, a doorway made of absence, where the final spark hovered like a moth against a lantern. 
To capture one last spark before oblivion was her oldest vow, the reason she climbed each night to this skyborne grotto under aurora, far above the grinding clock of the world. 
The soul-fire flickered, blue and frail, caught between the static and the song. 
Sable infused the emerald chip with every ounce of tenderness she had hoarded across centuries of farewells. 
The chip flared, a green so deep it seemed to fold the aurora into itself, and the woman sighed, not in death, but in recognition. 
A smile bloomed on those still lips, a final masterpiece painted by the hush itself. 
Then the spark surrendered, slipping into the emerald like rain into deep water, leaving behind a body at perfect rest. 
Sable sat back on her heels, lucidly watchful even now, ensuring no fragment of the pattern would escape into the void. 
The aurora held its radiance, and the grotto echoed with a silence so complete it felt like applause. 
She tucked the emerald chip into her satchel beside hundreds of others, each one a captured sun going dark. 
Somewhere below, the living slept, unaware of the bargains made above the clouds. 
Sable prepared her instruments for the next arrival, her heart quiet, her purpose unbroken. 
To find patterns in static was to believe that chaos still loved form, and that love could be distilled from noise. 
There was no timeframe to her vigil, only when the sky remembered its color and the stones remembered warmth. 
Another figure appeared at the grotto's rim, carried by the wind, eyes wide and seeking. 
The mortician rose, brushing cloud-dust from her coat, and welcomed the silence that came before the work. 
There was no end to the dying, and no end to her watch. 
Yet in each emerald chip she felt the eternal contradiction of her craft: grief sharpened into beauty, oblivion robbed of its victory, a single spark preserved in green fire. 
She picked up her brush, and under the aurora's radiant hush, she began again.
