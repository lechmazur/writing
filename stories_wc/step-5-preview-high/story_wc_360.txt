The topaz workshop stood alone beneath the retreating sky, its amber walls catching the last light of vanishing stars, and inside, a cryptic letterpress operator named Sable Kestrel set type with fingers that remembered other people's gestures. 
Sable habitually borrowed personalities, slipping into the cadences of strangers as easily as slipping on borrowed coats, so no one in the valley could say whether she was the poet she printed or the widow she consoled. 
On her bench sat a halo fragment, a curved splinter of luminous metal that had fallen from a dying star, and it pulsed with a rhythm that seemed to solve and erase an equation at the same time. 
Her motivation was fierce and precise: to understand the mathematics of emotion, to map sorrow and delight onto axes where they would cancel or resolve into something beautiful and exact. 
The fragment held the key to transposed empathy, the capacity to feel another's grief in one's own geometry, and Sable believed the press could translate that capacity into language. 
Each night, between vanishing stars, she worked through deliberate ambiguity, setting lines that twisted meaning until the mind, disoriented, might catch a glimpse of the underlying formula. 
The topaz walls glowed with refracted starlight, and the air in the workshop seemed to breathe, a lucid turbulence that made thought fluid and perception as changeable as weather. 
The stars above had begun to vanish one by one, as if the sky itself were correcting an error, and the workshop felt like a punctuation mark suspended between two disappearing clauses. 
Tonight she printed a single sentence with no punctuation: If you are I then the distance between our heartbeats must equal the arc of this fragment. 
She pulled the lever, and the press struck the vellum, but the ink rose from the page and rearranged itself into a constellation of overlapping silhouettes. 
Sable leaned close and felt the exact weight of a stranger's betrayal, though she had borrowed that personality from a dead spy and never returned it. 
She recognized the sensation from a king she had mimicked and a child she had comforted, all their sorrows stacked like type in her mind. 
The fragment warmed, and she saw that transposed empathy was not a haunting but an operation: to love another is to transpose their variables into your own and solve. 
She realized that to understand the mathematics of emotion, she had to let the halo fragment rewrite her proof in another's hand. 
Through deliberate ambiguity she had learned to disorient her own defenses, stripping the labels from feeling so that only raw relation remained. 
Her hands trembled with a turbulence that was strangely lucid, each sensation arriving as a plotted point on a graph she was only beginning to understand. 
Each impression she pulled seemed to disorient the boundary between self and other, leaving her wondering whose proof she had really solved. 
She set another line, compressing letters until the words became illegible, forcing the reader, and herself, to approach the truth sideways. 
The halo fragment flared, and the topaz walls flashed with shadows of every personality Sable had ever borrowed, a gallery of ghosts arranged like proof. 
They whispered equations in languages she had borrowed, and the press answered with a rhythm like breathing. 
Between vanishing stars, the workshop became a proving ground where grief and gladness were exchanged like currency, each impression on the vellum a theorem tested in the dark. 
The vellum collected dew from the starless sky, and the ink ran in rivulets that spelled theorems no single person could have written alone. 
Sable pressed her palm against the metal and whispered the last unknown variable, allowing her own temperament to dissolve into the greater relation. 
She did not know if the next sentence would be hers, but she set it anyway, trusting the ambiguity to carry her. 
In that lucid turbulence she finally measured emotion not as number but as bridge, a transposed geometry that held because two people stood on opposite ends. 
The cryptic operator smiled with a mouth she had borrowed from a cartographer, then set the final proof among the stars, where every equation ended in a question mark that would outlast the dark.
