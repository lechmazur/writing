The immortal had counted every raindrop that ever struck the prismatic escondite, and the tally had long since ceased to matter.  Centuries of curated silence had rotted into a boredom so dense it settled in her bones like mineral sediment.  Vey had outlived empires, languages, and the heat of stars, and the universe had begun to feel like a locked room with no doors.  Tonight, a solar storm bruised the upper atmosphere with silent auroras, yet even the sky's fire could not quicken her pulse.  What did trouble her was the hushed thunder rolling beneath the stone floor, a subterranean murmur that had nothing to do with weather.  She stood at the heart of the escondite, a hidden vault of crystallized light where refracted shadows moved independently of their sources.  Vey was dimensionally aware, a rare perception that let her feel the thin membranes separating one geometry from the next.  Tonight those membranes were rippling, conspiring, folding in directions that defied the compact she had made with space long ago.  A spatial mutiny was underway, and the architecture itself had become traitor.  Angles sharpened in the periphery of her vision while distant corridors lengthened without moving.  The ceiling hovered as both floor and sky, and the prismatic walls conducted their rebellion through surging magnetic fields.  She recognized the method, for only magnetism could whisper such treason across the curved lattice of reality.  Vey smiled, the expression strange and unused.  From her robe she drew a cursed amulet fragment, a splinter of black glass that had once clasped the neck of the only soul she had ever failed to follow.  Their goodbye had been spoken in a garden of blue ferns, brief and absolute, and she had spent ten thousand years wondering what truly waited beyond that final syllable.  The fragment had cursed her with perfect memory, each detail of that parting preserved in cruel clarity.  Yet now, as the storm intensified, she sensed the curse might be inverted, becoming a compass rather than a wound.  The intrigue unfolded in frequencies beneath human hearing, a magnetic dialogue between the escondite's stones and the solar tempest overhead.  Forces were communicating, she realized, through fields of invisible attraction and repulsion, plotting a geometry of escape.  The escondite was not simply a refuge; it was a conspirator, a place that had grown tired of containing things.  Vey walked toward the central prism, her boots finding purchase on surfaces that had not yet committed to an orientation.  The cursed fragment pulsed against her palm, drinking the solar magnetism and translating the storm into coordinates she could almost decipher.  The spatial mutiny had an agenda, and she began to understand its terrible logic.  The rooms rebelled not against order, but against the finality of exit, refusing to let anything depart without the possibility of return.  It sought to fold goodbye into hello, to answer the question that had hollowed out her immortality.  The hushed thunder swelled into a resonance that vibrated in her marrow, low and inevitable as a second heartbeat.  She raised the amulet fragment, and the black glass bit into her skin, drawing a thin thread of blood that floated upward instead of falling.  Magnetic fields converged into a spiraling cataclysm, and the mutiny reached its crescendo.  The prismatic escondite groaned as its dimensions split open, revealing not darkness but a threshold woven from the static between heartbeats.  Through the rift, Vey finally beheld what happens after goodbye.  It was not an ending but a translation, a shifting of presence into a geometry that intersected every place at once.  The soul she had lost had not vanished, but had become dimensionally diffuse, still aware, still tethered by the silver line of their parting.  The cursed amulet fragment cracked in her grip, releasing its ancient burden into the storm, and with it the last residue of her boredom.  She stepped through the threshold, not into death, but into the intricate intrigue of reunion, her immortal heart finally beating in time with the hushed thunder.
