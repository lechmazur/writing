The medium arrived in the mangrove labyrinth just as the tide forgot which way to flow. 
She carried a satchel of unsent letters, their ink bleeding into salt gray pulp, each envelope bearing a name she had never dared to speak aloud. 
The mangroves leaned over black water, their roots forming arches that trapped mist and whispered. 
Somewhere above, the singing spirits threaded their voices through the branches, low and warning, a chorus that rose and fell with her breath. 
Their tune was not sweet, but insistent, each note a warning or a welcome, she could never tell which. 
She was a cryptic medium, known for answering questions with other questions, for reading futures in the curl of steam. 
Tonight she sought no vision. 
She sought the moment before deciding. 
Her wounds, raw as split coral, would heal in moonlight, but the moon had not yet climbed the tangled horizon. 
The shard in her pocket had begun to hum, resonating with the pain. 
She pressed her palm against a slick root and felt the space where an apology waits unspoken, a hollow in the air so sharp it sliced her thoughts. 
From her coat she drew a gargoyle's rainspout shard, jagged green stone that had once channeled storms from a church that no longer existed. 
She found a window in the labyrinth where none should be, a pane of warped glass wedged between two mangrove trunks, sweating with condensation. 
With the shard, she began to write messages in condensation on windows, carving words that steamed and vanished before her eyes. 
She needed the window, for in the open the spirits would only sing her to sleep. 
The word evoke slipped from her lips, and the spirits answered, their song twisting into language. 
The window fogged anew, and letters formed themselves, not by her hand but by the breath of the unsent. 
She read of a brother who had stood on this exact boardwalk, one shoe in the water, one on land. 
The space around her thickened, and she was inside the pause, the breath before the choice. 
A man stood there, young and terrified, holding his own unsent letter, his knuckles white. 
He had come to the labyrinth to decide whether to return home or vanish into the salt. 
The medium recognized the fierce tenderness in his hesitation, the love that made leaving cruel and staying feel like drowning. 
She wanted to reach through the glass and tell him that the moment before deciding was a place, not a cliff. 
Instead she traced another message in condensation, letting the gargoyle's rainspout shard guide her hand. 
The words appeared on the pane: Stay, and mean it, or go, and write the truth. 
The singing spirits swelled, and the moonlight finally broke through, silvering the water, silvering her skin. 
Her wounds closed beneath the light, flesh knitting in fierce tenderness, pain turning to quiet strength. 
The man in the vision looked up as if he had heard her, as if the condensation had spoken. 
He stepped back from the water, folded his letter, and placed it in his pocket. 
The vision held him there, suspended in the space where an apology waits unspoken, neither forgiven nor forsaken. 
The medium's own letters weighed heavy, but she left them in her satchel, unsent still, because some words must be carried, not delivered. 
One envelope bore her own name, sealed with wax and regret. 
She withdrew the shard, and the window cleared, the messages in condensation dissolving into the salt air. 
The singing spirits softened, their chorus thinning into the hush between waves. 
She walked back through the mangrove labyrinth, roots closing behind her like a door. 
The gargoyle's rainspout shard grew warm in her palm, a reminder that even stone remembers the rain it once turned away. 
She had not changed his past, only the breath before the choice, the flicker where love might still hold. 
And in that liminal dark, she felt her own apology begin to form, fierce and tender and finally ready.
