The icebound sanctum had waited three long years for Kaelen to return. 
He climbed the final ridge at dusk, his lungs burning with thin altitude and something warmer than grief. 
It was snowmelt ardor, the quiet heat of a river deciding to flow again after a long winter of self-blame. 
Kaelen was a lost wax caster, a man who shaped hollow forms in wax so that fire could free them for bronze. 
He was technically gentle by trade, his gloved hands able to coax wax around forms too fragile for rougher men to touch. 
Inside, the aurora rippled across the ice dome in silent curtains of green and violet. 
He had come only after forgiving himself for the kiln accident that had killed his apprentice and burned the original archive. 
On the altar rested a street organ punched card scrap, the only surviving transcription of a song that was also a memorial. 
Its perforations held not only notes but the spoken syllables of names the mountain had swallowed. 
Kaelen's motivation was simple and heavy: to preserve the sound of forgotten names before the last copy rotted into silence. 
The priests who built the sanctum had known that light could read what eyes could not, but only if a body unlocked the way. 
Hanging from the vaulted ribs of ice was a centuries-old ceremonial rope, its braids a language of knot and tension. 
To illuminate the card's hidden pattern, he would reconfigure the braids, opening channels that would draw the aurora inward. 
Kaelen approached the rope slowly, technically gentle even here, reading each twist as if it were a vein of unhardened wax. 
One wrong pull would shatter fibers older than the nation that had forgotten them. 
For hours he worked, sliding loops over frozen pegs, reversing the weave where the knots had tightened into ignorance. 
His breath came in pale clouds, and the ardor in his chest was not frantic but vast, like thaw water moving under a field of snow. 
When the final braid settled into its new position, the rope became a loom of shadow and sinew, perfectly aligned to catch the sky. 
Kaelen raised the punched card scrap into the nexus where the reconfigured strands met. 
The aurora flared, and light passed through the holes to illuminate the ice in scattered points of brightness. 
Each hole was a syllable; each syllable, a person once loved enough to name aloud. 
The transcribed memory was no longer trapped in fragile paper but written in the living geometry of the room. 
Then the light struck the bronze forks he had cast from wax that morning, and the sanctum filled with the sound of forgotten names. 
A mother called a daughter home; a captain hailed a ship lost to the floe; children laughed in a tongue older than the ice. 
Kaelen wept without shame, understanding at last that preserving the dead was not an attempt to undo his grief but to carry it forward with grace. 
He worked through the night, technically gentle with every tuning fork, sealing them into a new wax matrix for the bronze cast that would make the sound permanent. 
When dawn painted the ice rose and gold, he descended with the cast wrapped in furs, leaving the rope braided in its new and honest pattern. 
He would carry the recording south to the coastal towns, build street organs that could read the punched cards, and illuminate the plazas with voices the world had tried to erase. 
The icebound sanctum remained under the aurora, silent but no longer empty, holding its transcribed memory in every reconfigured braid.
