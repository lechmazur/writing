After the last echo of laughter fades, the subterranean crystal cave falls into a silence so complete that Elara can hear the slow thunder of her own pulse. 
She is a charcoal-fingered portrait sketch artist who has spent a decade tracing the faces of the dead for the city constabulary, rendering every hollow cheekbone with mechanical precision while her own imagination withered behind locked doors. 
The crystals lining the cavern walls catch the bioluminescent glow and scatter it into ribbons of rose and amber, filling the chamber with an auroral sorrow that seems to mourn her before she even speaks. 
She has come here because the calling is inescapable; it wakes her at night with the scent of graphite and turpentine, and it will not let her rest until she rectifies the wrong she did herself when she abandoned true art for mere documentation. 
Elara is mnemonically gifted, cursed with a memory that spares nothing, and it is precisely this curse that has allowed her to recall every angle of the path leading down to this hidden grotto beneath the old academy. 
Her motivation is simple and devastating: to uncork suppressed creativity, to release the flood of images she has dammed up since the night her sister's laughter turned to screaming somewhere in these tunnels. 
She moves deeper, her boots crunching on mineral dust, until the cavern opens into a round chamber where a single canvas stands propped against a slab of quartzite. 
The painting is old, its varnish crazed like spider silk, and it depicts two girls laughing in a shaft of dawn light, their joy preserved in oil and pigment. 
Elara's fingers leave fresh smears of charcoal as she reaches out, because she has been carrying a broken stick of vine charcoal like a talisman ever since she descended into the dark. 
She lifts the painting aside, and there, set directly into the stone behind it, she finds a small iron safe with a broken combination lock. 
The dial is bent, the shackle sprung, and the mechanism will not turn; it is a monument to violence, as though someone tried to force open the past by rage rather than memory. 
But Elara is mnemonically gifted, and she remembers the combination her sister set the summer they sealed away their dreams inside this vault: the date, the hour, the number of crystals in the central geode. 
Kneeling in the rose-gold light, she begins to rectify the broken combination lock, not by smashing it, but by aligning the wheels through touch alone, feeling for the notches that her memory insists are still there beneath the damage. 
It takes time, and the cave offers no sound but the drip of distant water, so she works in a silence that feels like the moment after the last echo of laughter fades, when only truth remains. 
At last the shackle clicks free, and the safe door swings inward to reveal a bundle of blank canvas, a set of oil paints still sealed in wax paper, and a journal in her sister's handwriting. 
The note inside speaks of the inescapable callings that haunt all true artists, warning that the only way to survive them is to feed them rather than starve them into silence. 
Elara opens the paints with trembling charcoal-stained fingers, and by the cold fire of the crystals she finally begins to uncork what she has suppressed for ten long years. 
She paints not from photographs but from memory and longing, letting the auroral sorrow of the cave bleed into color, and when the first true image rises beneath her hand she understands that some doors are not locked from the outside. 
The painting with something behind it has given her more than a hidden cache; it has returned her to the calling she cannot escape, and she rectifies her fractured soul with every stroke. 
Outside, the world may wait with its ordinary gray, but here in the subterranean crystal cave, after the last echo of laughter fades, Elara is finally, painfully awake.
