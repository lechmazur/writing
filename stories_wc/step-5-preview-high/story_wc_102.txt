The twilight viaduct in bloom smelled of rain and old iron, and Elara climbed its arch as the past life memory still burned behind her eyes.  She had been a camera obscura artist for nine years, but only yesterday, while breathing fog onto her ground glass, had she remembered being something else.  In that other life she had been a pilgrim of sparks, walking from shrine to shrine to gather the love that people had never spoken aloud.  The memory had surfaced without warning, and now, spiritually curious as ever, she had followed the pull to this overgrown crossing where night flowers opened like white lanterns.  She wanted to find where unused love is stored, because the memory had shown her vaults beneath the world, heavy with silence.  At the viaduct's crown she set down her wooden camera obscura and took from her coat the object she had carried since the memory first cleared: Kwan Yin's tear, a lens of pale amber glass said to weep for the lost.  She fitted the tear into the box where the brass lens had been, and the projected image on the frosted glass shifted from the bridge to a vast dim corridor.  Elara was not afraid; her curiosity had always been a kind of prayer.  She pressed her lips to the viewing aperture and began to breathe, slow and deep, knowing the method her teacher had taught her: revealing through breathing changes.  Each exhale fogged the tear, and in the moisture the corridor brightened, walls becoming shelves, shelves becoming seeds of light.  The viaduct seemed to inhale with her, its flowering vines rustling though no wind moved.  A spark drifted up from the image and passed through the glass, hovering before her like a firefly that had forgotten summer.  In her past life she had caught these sparks in clay jars and buried them where no grief could sour them.  But the memory had changed; now she understood that burying unused love was not preservation but suffocation.  She needed to heal the viaduct, this seam between memory and earth, by giving the sparks back their breath.  Elara adjusted the bellows of the camera obscura and changed her breathing to a sharp rhythmic pattern, and the foggy lucidity of the scene sharpened into terrible clarity.  Beneath the arch she saw the storehouse: a hollow beneath the tracks where every unspoken tenderness had pooled into a dark, breathing lake.  The flowers along the viaduct were its surface, blooming only at twilight because that was when the stored love pressed hardest against the world.  She lifted Kwan Yin's tear from the box and held it to the coming dark, whispering apologies to the dead and the living alike.  As she exhaled onto the tear, it began to weep real water, not of sorrow, but of release.  The drops fell onto the viaduct stones and steamed, and where they landed, sparks rose like slow, golden moths.  Elara guided them with her breath, longer and warmer, until the sparks swirled into a river of light that flowed east toward the sleeping city.  She was healing more than a bridge; she was healing the circuit between hearts that had forgotten how to give.  Her camera obscura sat open and empty, its purpose now to show nothing hidden, only the honest dusk.  The pilgrim of sparks was gone, yet she remained, spiritually curious, watching the last ember of unused love settle into a night-blooming bud.  It opened at her touch, and the fog around the viaduct cleared enough for her to see the stars with perfect, quiet clarity.  She finally knew the answer her past life had sought, and it was enough to breathe again.
