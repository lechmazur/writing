The late-shift pier lamplighter named Orin climbed the barnacled ladder at dusk. 
Above the harbor, the first sky lanterns rose, and their light fell like slow meteors across the black water. 
The crowd on the seawall spoke luminous vows into paper shells, and the promises caught fire without burning. 
Orin watched them only long enough to confirm that the lamps along Pier Nine were lit. 
His true work began when the tourists went quiet, for he had come to the harbor each lantern night to discover the color of sound. 
He believed that voices carried hues, and he wanted to see them with his own eyes rather than trust rumor. 
Whenever the harbor master asked why he lingered near the old wreck, Orin hid behind truth and said he was checking the lamp brackets. 
It was an honest answer, and honesty had become the curtain behind which he concealed his obsession. 
That night, halfway down the pier, his boot struck something small and metallic. 
He knelt and found a brass hotel skeleton key tag stamped with the faded number of Room 404. 
The tag was cold, yet it seemed to drink the lantern light and hold it. 
Orin slipped it into his pocket, though he did not yet know what it might signify. 
The tide had pulled back farther than usual, exposing the ribs of a ship that had sunk across the channel mouth decades ago. 
Locals called it the Meridian, and its forgotten library was said to remain dry inside the tilted hull. 
Orin approached by wandering purposefully, not drifting, letting his lamp lead him along sandbars and rusted anchor chain. 
He wanted the wreck to feel like a destination, not an accident. 
A gap in the hull opened like a mouth, and he stepped into the forgotten library inside the sunken ship. 
The air smelled of salt and pulped paper, and shelves leaned in drunk angles over warped reading tables. 
Water dripped in a slow metronome from the ceiling, and the sound made him think of questions he could not answer. 
He set his lamp on a table and opened a bloated guestbook whose pages were furred with mildew. 
The hotel skeleton key tag grew warm against his chest, and he understood that the Meridian had once sailed as a floating hotel. 
He laid the tag upon an entry written in a careful hand, and the ink began to glow. 
The words were a luminous vow, not merely printed but alive with a low amber pulse that matched the beat of his heart. 
Orin realized then that the vow had been made during a sky lantern release much like the one burning above the channel. 
Two travelers had promised to meet at Room 404, and the key tag had fallen from the captain's coat when the ship went down. 
The captain had hidden behind truth, recording the vow plainly so no one would suspect the cargo of hope he carried. 
Orin pressed his palm to the page, and the amber pulse traveled up his arm as a vibration. 
He closed his eyes and saw, at last, the color of sound, a deep and ringing amber that smelled of rain on lantern paper. 
The sky lanterns above were only echoes, but here, in the forgotten library, the hue had weight and texture. 
He understood that every vow was a note, and every note a shade, and the harbor was a score waiting to be read. 
When he left the ship, the tide was returning, and the last lanterns drifted into a meteor hush that blanketed the water. 
Back at Pier Nine, he trimmed the wicks and checked the brackets exactly as he always did. 
The harbor master passed and asked if anything unusual had happened. 
Orin shook his head and replied that the lamps were secure and the night was calm. 
It was true, and it signified nothing of the amber ringing still alive beneath his coat. 
He kept the tag against his chest, a small brass secret that proved sound had color after all.
