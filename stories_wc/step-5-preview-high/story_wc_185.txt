The energy beings had come in ribbons of copper light and never stopped singing. 
Three seasons after their arrival, the harmonic caves no longer held silence but a layered roar that pressed against the stone ribs like ocean tide. 
Vela was the last ripple-mark mapkeeper, keeper of the spiraling etchings that recorded how sound once moved through the deep stone. 
Her work was small and patient: tracing the petroglyph lines with her thumb, repairing frost-faded grooves, and waiting for the storm to falter. 
She kept an echo chamber stone in her lap as she worked. 
It was gently luminous, pulsing with a warm amber glow that softened whenever the caves' clamor rose to a shriek. 
The stone did not amplify; it swallowed. 
Inside it slept a memory of the quiet the caves had known before the energy beings arrived. 
Vela's motivation was not escape but retrieval. 
She intended to find the quiet in the storm and return it to the drowned stone. 
The energy beings had woven their resonance into the cave's natural chords, creating a maelstrom of harmonic interference. 
To unravel it, she relied on the oldest practice of her order: examining crack patterns in ice. 
Each morning she climbed to the Grotto of Hinges, where mineral-laden water froze in broad sheets across the cave mouth. 
The frost recorded every vibration as fracture, branching like black veins through clear blue depths. 
Vela learned to read those cracks the way others read weather. 
A hairline spiraling clockwise meant a coming lull. 
A shattered crosshatch meant the energy beings were gathering to sing a deeper note. 
But this dawn the ice told a different story. 
At the center of the sheet, where cracks usually fanned outward in chaos, there bloomed a rosette of perfect silence. 
The fracture lines curved inward, cradling a hollow no vibration could reach. 
Vela's breath caught. 
She had read of this in forbidden maps: the bloom of hush. 
It was a rare convergence, a pocket of absolute stillness that flowered for only moments inside the loudest storms. 
If she could hold it, expand it, she might conjure a sanctuary from the echo chamber stone. 
She hurried back down the limestone stair, the gently luminous stone bouncing light against walls slick with resonance. 
At her workbench, she set the stone into a cradle of woven reed and tuned the surrounding chimes to dissonance. 
Her hands shook. 
To conjure the bloom meant to pull the storm's own weight into the stone and let the hollow unfold. 
She placed her palms against the ice sheets she had carried down in her pack, reading the rosette's exact shape. 
The cracks were her notation; the bloom was her melody. 
Outside, the energy beings began a crescendo that made dust weep from the ceiling. 
Vela conjured. 
She drew the rosette pattern into the stone, let the gently luminous surface drink the resonance until it turned dark and heavy. 
For a long moment nothing happened. 
Then the stone exhaled. 
From its core spread a silence so complete it seemed to have texture, like felt laid gently over the cave's raw nerves. 
The storm did not vanish. 
Instead it flowed around the expanding bloom of hush, a sphere of repose carved from chaos. 
The energy beings circled the threshold, their songs dimming to lullabies. 
Inside, Vela sat on the cold floor and felt the quiet settle over her like a mantle. 
It was not the absence of work but its completion, a hammered repose earned by labor and precision. 
She had found the quiet in the storm. 
More than that, she had given it walls. 
In the hammered repose of the hollow bloom, the ripple-mark mapkeeper finally slept. 
The caves held their breath around her. 
Outside, the ice continued to crack and heal, writing new maps in its patient language. 
The echo chamber stone rested in her hands, gently luminous once more, its burden now a shared peace. 
When the energy beings sang at their lowest frequency, the bloom did not tremble. 
It had been hammered into shape, and shape endures. 
Vela dreamed of the old caves, and the old caves, in turn, learned the shape of her name.
