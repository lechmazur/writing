The feral library guardian crouched among the frostbitten roses, her breath hanging in the mist like pages waiting to be read.  She had no name that fit in this mouth, only the deep ache of being dimensionally homesick for shelves that turned like compass needles toward impossible stars.  Around her stretched the frozen garden in mist, every statue half-swallowed by fog, every path dissolving before it reached a horizon, every bloom locked in ice that rang like tiny bells when the wind remembered to blow.  She clutched the cultist's pendant, its green stone cold as a dead language, and remembered how she had torn it from the zealot who tried to burn the archives of the Boundary.  He had screamed about the light that separates worlds, about the blinding edges that kept true knowledge trapped in amber, but she had only wanted to protect the stacks where shadows learned to behave.  Now, through the veil of sleep, the garden breathed with her, and she understood that waking was merely another aisle in a larger library that no cartographer had ever mapped.  The boundaries of light were not walls but thresholds, she realized, bending like starlight through water, shifting whenever someone dared to look directly at them, shimmering most fiercely at the edges of comprehension.  Her feral nature had been born from running between those thresholds, from sleeping in the margins where reality grew thin and translucent, from eating bitterness because no one had taught her how to chew wonder.  She needed to wait, though every instinct hissed that waiting was for prey and not for predators.  But this was not the waiting of cages or traps; it was the patient wait of roots beneath frost, of ink drying into truth, of a seed deciding in silence to become a door.  So she began the slow work of interiorizing exteriors, drawing the frozen garden into herself branch by frozen branch, until the mist became her second spine and the marble statues rose as her ribs.  The cultist's pendant grew warm against her chest, and she saw that he too had been trying to make friends with uncertainty, though he had chosen fire instead of frost, conquest instead of quiet breath.  She pitied him the way one pities a reader who tears pages because he cannot bear the suspense of a story that refuses to end on command.  The boundaries of light pulsed above her, gentle now, no longer dividing her from the home she could not name but could now carry within like a lantern swallowed by dawn.  Her eyes, gone wolfish and gold, softened into the lucid calm of fog lifting from a field it had protected all night.  She waited in the garden, letting the veil of sleep settle like a quilt over her shoulders, and she smiled at the uncertainty that curled around her paws like a curious kitten.  In the silence, a fox stepped from behind the frozen fountain, and she did not startle, for she had finally made room for all that she did not know, all that she could not catalogue or conquer.  The pendant chimed, a single note of recognition, and the mist curled around her like a cat acknowledging its owner, acknowledging a keeper who no longer fought the boundaries of her own vision.  She was no longer dimensionally homesick, because she had learned to build her home from the uncertainty of the world, one interiorized exterior at a time, one breath of foggy lucidity at a time.  And so the guardian waited, feral no longer, a keeper of the in-between, where light learned to bend and every ending was merely a door ajar.
