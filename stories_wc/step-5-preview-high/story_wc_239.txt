The end of history witness came to the ancient temple library lost in time because no calendar remained to argue with her. 
The halls were a resounding hush, vast enough to hear the pause between two heartbeats. 
Shelves spiraled around a hollow center like a shell built by patient gods. 
She had traveled across a universe's hiccup to reach it, that flicker in which causality coughed and permitted one impossible appointment. 
In her coat she carried a softcover dictionary, cheap, bent, and heavier than any codex of law. 
Her motivation was not knowledge but release: to unravel cosmic illusions before they hardened into the only past anyone remembered. 
The librarians had vanished into their own indexes, leaving one final case for spiral arbitration. 
Their method was not judgment but descent; each answer produced a tighter question until the truth folded into itself. 
The witness opened her dictionary on a lectern of cooled starlight and read the first definition aloud. 
"Beginning," she said. 
The word climbed the spiral and returned changed, arriving as a word in a language not yet spoken. 
She marked it and asked the next question. 
Thus she journeyed inward while walking out, each step toward the center carrying her farther from the false histories that had taught her to grieve. 
Her mind was wanderingly exact, able to detour through every metaphor and still arrive at the same unarguable coordinate. 
The temple answered by rearranging its staircases into questions. 
A door became an inference; an inference became a corridor; the corridor became a test of whether she wished to be right or whole. 
The witness chose whole. 
The dictionary's margins began to glow with marginalia left by earlier witnesses, each of whom had scribbled corrections into the binding. 
They warned that the spiral would offer her a single moment of perfect vengeance, the past restored exactly, and that accepting it would end arbitration forever. 
She kept reading, and the softcover grew warm as if it recognized the risk of its own ordinary shape. 
Definitions peeled away from their pages and orbited her, not as spells but as candidate versions of reality. 
One claimed she had died at the close of history. 
Another insisted she had invented death to avoid the labor of remembering. 
The witness refused both. 
To unravel cosmic illusions, she had learned, one must refuse the flattering definition first. 
The spiral tightened around a single word she had carried for ages without speaking it: end. 
The library became the size of a throat, and the resounding hush sharpened into the pressure before a verdict. 
Here the spiral arbitration reached its center, where every contradiction was not erased but given a place to turn. 
She saw that history had not been broken; it had been duplicated by fear, each copy insisting it was original. 
Her task, then, was not to choose a copy but to unify them, binding the true and the falsely remembered into one accountable story. 
The softcover dictionary fell open to a blank page and waited. 
She took the stylus from her coat and wrote a definition that had no synonyms. 
"Witness," she wrote, "means one who survives long enough to refuse the easy past and then carries all versions forward." 
The ink became a thread and ran through the shelves, through the spirals, through the false certainties that had needed arbitration. 
The temple did not collapse; it reconciled. 
Staircases met, dust resumed falling, and the hollow center filled with a corridor leading both deeper and outward at once. 
She walked it without hesitation, journeying inward while walking out until the two directions became a single gait. 
The universe's hiccup ended, and in that ordinary breath the library was lost again, not to time but to use. 
The end of history witness emerged carrying the softcover dictionary against her chest. 
It now held the corrected version of everything, and the hush that followed her was no longer empty but resounding with what had been made one.
