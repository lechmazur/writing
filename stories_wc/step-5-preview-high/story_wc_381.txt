The solitary summoner arrived at the nocturnal haven beside a quiet brook as the last ember of daylight drained from the western hills and the first true stars emerged in the velvet dark. 
She had spent eleven years learning to hush a prophecy spoken in code, and tonight the heavens leaned close enough to judge her failure or grant her peace at last. 
The brook was her only witness, its water moving without sound over smooth stones, a black mirror for the heavens and the faint sparks of constellations that had no names in any mortal language. 
In her palm rested a crane's origami wing, creased from parchment that had once carried a confession of love between two scholars, now folded into an instrument of absolute silence by a master who had since turned to drifting ash. 
Around her, the forest murmured amid whispered secrets, exchanging fragments of the doomed verse in rustled leaves, cricket pulses, and the low chatter of waking owls that spoke in riddled clicks and broken rhythms. 
She could not burn the words or drown them; flame and current only carried them farther into the waiting world where ears were greedy and kings were paranoid. 
Instead, she worked by mapping silence, pressing her fingertips against damp earth to feel where the noise of prophecy had thinned to nothing, charting the hidden geography of quiet that no enemy could cross and no spell could corrupt. 
The brook's flow guided her hand, drawing invisible lines in the mud from the water's edge to the roots of an ancient oak that remembered older tongues, older griefs, and older ways of forgetting what must be forgotten. 
She knelt at the bank and drew the map in ash, each stroke capturing a pocket where sound dared not settle, building a lattice of stillness that would hold firm against the coded curse and all who tried to speak it aloud. 
She sought the smoothed hush, that rare quality of stillness which lay flat and seamless across the land, erasing sound rather than merely softening its edges into a comfortable and forgetful lull. 
Her power was terrestrially cosmic, drawn from the same stardust that spun in distant nebulae yet anchored in the soil beneath her knees and the grit beneath her broken nails, a marriage of mountain and meteor. 
She had learned long ago that only a force both earthly and celestial could flatten a prophecy, for words spoken in code hid in the narrow spaces between stones and stars alike, breeding in the dark. 
She traced the map until the patterns matched the rhythm of her heartbeat, and then she opened the crane's origami wing like a door swinging into a room where no voice had ever entered and no echo had ever dared to linger. 
The wing caught starlight and turned it into a pale flame that did not burn but instead began to flow outward, a tranquil blaze crawling through grass, stone, and root without heat or shadow, cooling rather than consuming. 
The whispered secrets stilled as the light reached them, the coded prophecy unraveling into meaningless threads that drifted upward on warm air and vanished among the needles of the tall pines, leaving nothing behind. 
For a long moment the entire haven existed inside the smoothed hush, complete and unbroken, a silence so perfect it felt as though the world were holding its breath before a first word or a final scream. 
She saw the prophecy's final syllable flicker above the water, a coal of red light trying to spark anew in the dark, and she pressed the wing gently downward until even that glow surrendered to the peace she had built. 
Then the brook resumed its quiet song, and the solitary summoner smiled, folding the crane's origami wing back into her pocket with hands that trembled only slightly in the cooling air. 
She remained beside the water until dawn threatened the horizon, guardian of the quiet, keeper of the hush, and the nocturnal haven lay smooth and perfect beneath the waking sky.
