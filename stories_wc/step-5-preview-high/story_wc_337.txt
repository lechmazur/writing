The prism sanctuary under starlight was never silent, but it was quiet in the way a held breath is quiet before it breaks. 
The crystal walls drank the starlight and returned it as pale rainbows that painted her bruised knuckles. 
Elara, a mask restorer whose own face had been cracked down the middle since the fire, sat among the crystal spires and waited for the tide of the dead to rise. 
She had come to ground restless spirits, though the task had hollowed her until she feared she might become one. 
In her lap lay a centuries-old fisherman's net, its cords stiff with salt and secrets, and a slender tapestry needle that she used not for thread but for memory. 
The sanctuary hummed with an interlaced hush, a silence so dense it seemed woven from the starlight itself, pooling in the crevices of her broken mask. 
Elara possessed the rare attribute that accumulates silence, gathering it into her ribs like sediment until she could shape it into an anchor. 
Tonight, across sharing a secret, she would exhume what the net had swallowed. 
She leaned forward, her breath stirring the dust, and began to unravel the knots in the centuries-old fisherman's net. 
Each knot loosened released a whisper, not of sound, but of absence, the negative space where a voice had once been. 
The tapestry needle slipped beneath a tight bundle, parting the hemp with surgical patience, and the interlaced hush deepened. 
You must understand, she whispered to the darkness, that silence accumulates not by being kept, but by being spent. 
The net gave up its first knot, and a memory exhaled into the air: a drowned boy, his eyes wide with moonlight. 
He was restless, untethered, drifting between the prisms, and Elara felt her own fractures ache in sympathy. 
She worked by unraveling knots in the centuries-old fisherman's net, each loosened cord drawing another fragment of the drowned boy's history into the lucid unrest of the sanctuary. 
The method was ancient, unspoken, and exact; one wrong pull and the spirit would scatter like ash. 
As the third knot surrendered, the boy's secret surfaced across sharing a secret, a confession he had never spoken above the waves. 
He had stolen bread from a neighbor and blamed the tide, a small sin that grew heavy as stone. 
Elara listened without judgment, her broken mask restorer's hands steady, and she felt the accumulating silence inside her shift, ready to be given. 
The prism sanctuary under starlight seemed to lean closer, its refracted light holding the boy in a tender, trembling cage. 
To ground restless spirits, she knew, she must first empty herself of all the quiet she had hoarded. 
She pressed the tapestry needle against the central knot, the one that bound the boy's chest to the seabed, and she pushed her accumulated silence through it like a thread. 
The interlaced hush wrapped around the knot, then around the boy, turning his unrest into stillness, his whispers into peace. 
He looked at her, no longer drowning, and nodded once before sinking into the sand at the sanctuary's heart, grounded at last. 
Elara sat back, her own mask slipping slightly, revealing the scar beneath that matched the cracks. 
She had exhumed a secret and laid a spirit to rest, but the act had cost her a measure of the silence she had gathered across sharing a secret. 
Outside, the stars turned, and the prism sanctuary held its breath again. 
Elara gathered the unraveled net and her tapestry needle, knowing that tomorrow another spirit would rise, and she would need to accumulate silence once more. 
For now, the lucid unrest remained, clear and awake, a quiet that knew exactly what it had buried.
