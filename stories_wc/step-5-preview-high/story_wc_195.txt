The withdrawn knight came to the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night because every waking map had failed him. 
He had survived wars and sieges, then retreated into silence, but silence had not prepared him for a sky that no longer turned. 
Inside the glass ribs, mercury lamps burned without flame above rows of alchemical trees. 
Their branches held fruit of cooled starlight, sap of copper, and blossoms that opened and closed in slow arithmetic. 
He moved down a gravel path that seemed to recalculate itself beneath his boots as reality shifted. 
At the path's end stood a small puppet stage, half-swallowed by vine and glass. 
On the seat lay an abandoned marionette theater program, its cover faded to the color of old bone. 
He lifted it with gauntleted fingers, and a brass clasp fell cold against his palm. 
He unsnapped the program and found pages not printed with names, but threaded with diagrams. 
They were instructions for strings, stages, and gestures, all conveyed through forgotten patterns. 
The knight did not yet know that these patterns were factually mystical, not metaphor. 
Each line was a live filament of causality, visible only because the orchard's night had thinned the veil. 
He had come to capture the shape of possibility, and now possibility hung in the dark like a puppet waiting for strings. 
To use the diagrams, he first had to offer a murmured allegiance. 
He pressed his forehead to the dusty program and whispered that he would remember the orchard's dead gardeners. 
He promised to carry their gestures into whatever dawn remained possible. 
The greenhouse answered with a shiver of sap and a faint clatter of unseen blocks. 
Then the patterns began to move. 
He traced the first pattern with one finger, and a strand of silver light rose from the page. 
It threaded itself between his knuckles as though recognizing an old performer. 
He pulled, and a marionette of vine and glass lifted from the stage, faceless but precise. 
Its joints matched the diagrams, and he moved it through forgotten patterns until his wrist burned. 
The orchard rearranged itself with every gesture. 
Trees bent into proscenium arches, and fruit swung like lanterns above the boards. 
Reality shifted again, not as catastrophe but as rehearsal. 
He saw branches becoming strings, and strings becoming the seams of the world. 
He had withdrawn from battle because he believed action only tore the world apart. 
Here, every pull asked him to hold tension without severing. 
The knight's fear loosened into something that resembled joy. 
A shivering bliss ran up his arm, cold and certain, as if the night had finally found a voice in him. 
He danced the marionette through the patterns, and the patterns danced him back into wanting. 
In the final diagram, a circle enclosed an open hand holding a seed-shaped absence. 
It meant not to own the future, but to trace the outline where it might enter. 
He understood then that possibility was not a thing but a posture held against collapse. 
To capture its shape, he would have to give the stage his allegiance fully, not in whispers. 
A second clasp waited at the program's back, smaller and older. 
He unsnapped it, and the pages became a lattice of threads that rose into the greenhouse air. 
The marionette stage filled with figures: not actors, but moments he had refused to live. 
They bowed to him, awaiting instruction. 
He set his palm against the silver web and murmured his allegiance a second time. 
This time he did not whisper of memory but of choice. 
He would hold the orchard's pattern in his hands and not look away. 
The greenhouse tilted. 
The endless night drew back like a curtain, revealing not dawn but a pale seam where dawn might be sewn. 
The knight guided the marionette toward that seam through forgotten patterns, each movement exact, each breath measured. 
The thread between his fingers grew warm, and the shape of possibility took form. 
It was a curved outline, fragile, alive, impossible to hold without shaking. 
He reached for it, and the orchard trembled with a shivering bliss that felt like forgiveness. 
When the light faded, the stage stood empty, and the program lay open in the straw. 
The withdrawn knight remained, but his shoulders had changed, as if he had finally unarmored himself. 
He closed the program, snapped its clasp, and carried it into the endless night as a seed of dawn. 
Behind him, the alchemical orchard greenhouse waited, still shrouded, still rehearsing the world.
