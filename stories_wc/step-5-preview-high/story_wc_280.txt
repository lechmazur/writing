The velvet curtains fell for the last time that evening, and the marquee outside went dark, signaling that the business had closed for another night.  Mara, the night groundskeeper, slipped through the service door with her tin lantern and walked the familiar gravel path until the hedge maze swallowed her whole.  Inside, the mystic orchard garden unfolded in terraces of silver-leaved apple trees, their blossoms glowing like paper lanterns against the indigo sky.  She had always found the garden after hours, when the hedge maze rearranged itself into passages only the lost could navigate.  Tonight, however, a glint at the base of the oldest tree stopped her breath.  It was a hand-painted tarot coin, its brass surface worn soft, one side bearing The Star and the other a cracked Tower.  When Mara lifted it, the air thickened with the smell of camellia powder and old stage dust.  A voice, warm as cellar sherry, drifted from the branches above.  "You found my encore."  Mara looked up to see a woman in a beaded flapper dress, her edges shimmering like a film reel running backward.  The stage ghost descended, her feet never quite touching the fallen petals.  Mara was synesthetically blessed, so where others might feel only a chill, she saw the ghost's presence as a braid of gold and violet light that sang in minor thirds.  She saw, too, that the braid was frayed, splitting into threads that tangled around the orchard without ever meeting.  "The night I vanished, the timeline cracked," the ghost said, touching the Tower on the coin.  "I keep reaching for the final note, but the curtain falls before I can finish."  Mara turned the coin over, feeling the raised paint of The Star buzz against her thumb.  "The orchard remembers," the ghost continued.  "If we intertwine the strands by producing echoes, perhaps we can lure possibility from the brink of despair."  "Despair?" Mara asked, clutching her lantern.  "Next week, the wrecking ball comes," the ghost replied.  "The garden dies in one thread; in another, it blooms forever."  Mara understood then that the business closing each night was not an ending but a rehearsal for the real finale.  She set the tarot coin in the hollow of the oldest tree and pressed her palm to the bark.  The ghost began to hum, a tune from a revue that had closed forty years ago.  Mara, whose ears painted each note in ribbons of copper and blue, answered with a whistle.  Their sounds rose into the hedge maze, and the garden gave them back.  The echoes were not mere reflections but looms, weaving the broken strands into a single cord.  Mara saw the gold and violet threads begin to intertwine, knotting at the center where the coin spun like a compass.  Nostalgia rose like sap, sweet and heavy, filling her with the memory of summers she had never lived.  She saw matinee crowds drifting through the hedges, lovers stealing apples between acts, children chasing fireflies along the maze walls.  The ghost's voice strengthened, no longer searching for an encore but leading a chorus.  The fractured timeline sealed around them with a sound like a violin bow drawn across eternity.  When Mara opened her eyes, the tarot coin had fused into the tree, The Star and The Tower now painted as one.  The ghost smiled, her form steady and warm.  "The demolition order dissolved in the new thread," she said.  "But the garden still needs a keeper who can hear the echoes."  Mara nodded, feeling the weight of the coin's twin sides settle into her pocket as a seed.  She whistled the old revue tune, and the orchard hummed it back, note for note, color for color.  Above them, the stage ghost took her final bow, not to an empty house, but to the stars themselves.  And though the business would open again tomorrow, Mara knew the real performance had only just begun.
