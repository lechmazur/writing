The tide whispered out, leaving the black throat of the cave open.  Mara pulled her mask down and stepped into the crashing hush, the roar of the surf muffled by stone into a waiting silence.  As a claustrophobic spelunker, she hated every inch the ceiling lowered, but she had learned to track invisible things: pressure seams, magnetic sighs, the tremors of things buried.  Today she followed the strongest invisible thread, the pulse of undersea cables that ran from the shoreline into the deep.  They led to the forgotten research facility, the one the maps pretended had never been built.  Barnacles crusted its railings, and rust wrote pale names across the steel.  It was accessible only between tidal changes, a gap of forty minutes when the sea held its breath.  She had to return; the unremembered deserved a witness.  The facility's outer door hung corroded but intact, yawning into a throat of rust and seawater.  Water dripped in a metronome rhythm, counting down the minutes until the sea returned.  Inside, the air tasted of salt and old ozone, and the crashing hush deepened into a pressure against her eardrums.  Mara's chest tightened at the narrow corridor, but she fixed on the magnetic thrum ahead and pushed forward.  The main hall had collapsed in a tangle of concrete and cable conduit, yet she sensed the invisible lattice still humming beneath the debris.  In her pack lay the object that had brought her back: a Codex margin illuminant gold fleck, pried from a drowned archive ledger.  It had cost her three years of debt to acquire, yet its worth was measured in memory.  Under the beam of her headlamp, the fleck flared, reacting to the cable current like a compass needle finding north.  It revealed what her eyes could not: the safe phase transition seams where matter had been tuned to allow passage.  Decades ago, the researchers had discovered how to slide between states, entering the flooded core through phase transitions of solid and liquid.  Mara pressed her palm to the wall and felt the telltale chill of a boundary ready to shift.  She exhaled, stepped into the phase seam, and the concrete became a gel she could wade through, a liquid that held her shape as she passed.  The transition was worse than any cave, a weightless crush that folded her ribs and sang in her teeth.  But her claustrophobia had a counterweight: the invisible map she tracked, the gold fleck blazing direction in her fist.  She emerged into the monitoring chamber, circular and glass-domed, directly beneath the seabed.  Silt floated like suspended stars, and pressure gauges clung to zero with stubborn pride.  There, preserved in pressurized stillness, sat the twelve researchers at their stations, faces turned toward the undersea cable readouts that had gone dead years ago.  They had not drowned; the phase field had frozen them in a moment of data, making them invisible to the tide and to history.  Mara understood then why the world above had forgotten them: the facility had slipped out of consensus reality, surviving only as a ghost frequency in the cables.  She felt their absence as a pressure behind her eyes, the specific weight of erasure.  She approached the central terminal and set the gold fleck into a hollow in the codex stand, completing a circuit older than the sea.  The illuminant fleck pulsed, and the readouts woke, broadcasting the names and final logs of the twelve into the undersea cables once more.  To stand by the unremembered had cost her everything, but now their record would ride the waves of the world again.  In the codex's margin, the gold fleck dimmed to a patient ember, its work complete.  A klaxon trembled through the dome as the tide began to turn, warning that the interval was ending.  Mara climbed back through the phase seam, the concrete hardening behind her like a held breath released.  Her shoulders screamed from the squeeze, but she did not stop until moonlight touched the water.  She scrambled up the corridor as the ocean's roar returned, the crashing hush collapsing into noise.  At the cave mouth she looked back once, watching the water reclaim the entrance between tidal changes.  She knew she would return again, as the tide would return, bound to the unremembered by invisible threads.
