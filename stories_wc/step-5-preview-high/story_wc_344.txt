The lunar-eclipse locksmith climbed the last stair into the frozen library above clouds. 
Frost clung to the banisters like old lace, and beyond the arched windows the dawn arrived under the soft hush of a distant morning. 
He had come here alone, carrying a wooden case filled with a clockmaker's gear assortment that had belonged to his grandmother. 
Each gear was brass or steel, worn smooth by decades of use, and they whispered against one another whenever he shifted his grip. 
He sought the rhythm of repair, not merely the motion of mending, but the pulse beneath broken things. 
In the library's eastern corner, behind a shelf of water-damaged atlases, he discovered scraps of prophecy tucked into a hollow spine. 
The paper was brittle and smelled of iron and rain, and the ink had faded to the color of rust. 
One scrap read: When the bright room opens, uncap the face that remembers. 
Another warned: The gears alone cannot heal what the mirrors have fractured. 
He had spent years collecting such fragments through scraps of prophecy hidden in a library's corner, believing they mapped the path to restoration. 
Now the words seemed to thrum against his ribs like a second heartbeat. 
He followed a corridor of frozen books where titles had been scratched away by ice and time. 
At its end stood a door of black oak, banded in silver, with no handle and no keyhole. 
The locksmith set down his case and opened the lid, revealing the clockmaker's gear assortment arranged by size and tooth. 
He selected a crescent wrench and a gear shaped like a waning moon, fitting them together into an improvised key. 
When he turned it, the door sighed inward, releasing a breath of air that had been still for centuries. 
He stepped into the hall of mirrors. 
The chamber was round and tall, lined with panels of ancient glass that had never cracked despite the cold. 
Morning light from some unseen aperture poured through the space, and everything was hauntingly bright, as though the room remembered a sun long dead. 
Reflections of himself stared back from every angle: young, old, mending, broken. 
At the center stood a pedestal holding a great circular mechanism, a clock face without numbers, its hands frozen at the threshold between night and dawn. 
He understood then that the library was not merely a place of storage but a body whose heart had stopped. 
The prophecy's words returned to him: uncap the face that remembers. 
He lifted the smallest gear from his assortment, a delicate spindle that once regulated a music box, and pressed it into a hidden seam on the clock's rim. 
The face of the clock lifted away like a lid, and beneath it lay a maze of springs and pendulums coated in rime. 
He worked by feel, aligning the clockmaker's gear assortment with the frozen joints, listening for the rhythm that the prophecy had promised. 
Tick. 
Tock. 
The sounds came slowly at first, then steadied, and the hall of mirrors began to change. 
The hauntingly brightness softened into something gentler, a glow that felt like driftwood warmed by a fire that had burned out long ago. 
In the glass, his grandmother's reflection smiled beside his own, her hands moving in the same pattern as his. 
He realized the rhythm of repair was not a technique to master but a conversation across time, a patience worn smooth by repetition. 
The distant morning deepened outside, but within the frozen library above clouds, the soft hush had become a lullaby. 
When the last gear clicked home, the clock struck once, not to mark an hour, but to release a memory. 
The locksmith closed his case, now lighter by one gear, and walked back through the corridor of frozen books. 
He left the scraps of prophecy where he had found them, through scraps of prophecy hidden in a library's corner, for the next seeker to read. 
Outside, the clouds below had begun to turn gold, and he felt the old nostalgia settle over him like a familiar coat. 
He had come to fix a mechanism, but he left understanding that repair is the art of holding on to what time has worn smooth. 
The hall of mirrors stayed with him, its hauntingly bright chambers echoing in his mind whenever he touched a lock or a gear. 
And under the soft hush of that distant morning, the lunar-eclipse locksmith descended toward the world below, carrying the rhythm home.
