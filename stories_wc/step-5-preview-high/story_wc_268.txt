The diminutive titan stood no taller than a candle upon the reading desk of the ancient temple library lost in time, yet her regally unpretentious bearing filled the rotunda with a monarch’s quiet grace.  For seven centuries she had guarded the margins, a witness folded between pages while constellations stalled outside in the pause of shifting constellations.  Her purpose was singular: to unmask a pretender to the throne who had rewritten the celestial chronicles and named himself heir to the sky.  The pretender’s falsehood had leaked into the world through cryptic messages left in library books, each instruction hidden in acrostics and marginal glyphs.  Only a true reader would find the iron gall ink pellet tucked inside a hollowed codex of salt and ash.  Its dark residue still smelled of storms, and when the titan ground it against her palm, the letters began to crawl.  She wrote the counter-sigil upon the blank leaf, a rune of drift that had no fixed place on the page.  The mark slithered across vellum and wood alike, tracing the seams of the library’s hidden machinery.  Above the shelves, the heavens held their breath during the pause of shifting constellations, freezing the temple between moments.  It was within this suspended interstice that falsehoods could be separated from bone and breath.  The titan followed the rune as it drifted through the stacks, her small shadow immense upon the vaulted ceiling.  At the archive’s heart she found the pretender’s seal, a bloated fiction pressed into the spine of the world’s first atlas.  He had not worn a mask of flesh but of language, convincing every historian that his line had always ruled.  The titan understood then that unmasking required no blade, only the correction of a single corrupted verse.  She uncapped the iron gall ink pellet and let one corrosive drop fall upon the seal.  The rune of drift caught the drop and carried it backward through every page the pretender had touched.  Ink became acid, and acid became revelation, until the false genealogy began to sunder along its own seams.  The library groaned as shelves realigned, releasing centuries of dust in a slow golden spiral.  This was not destruction but a graceful collapse, the elegant surrender of a lie that could no longer bear its own weight.  Constellations outside trembled, preparing to resume their ancient wheel now that the record had been set aright.  The pretender’s name dissolved into blankness, leaving a small hollow in the air where his tyranny had breathed.  Yet the titan knew the temple could not survive outside the pause, for it had been anchored to that celestial hesitation.  She did not flee, for her dignity was regally unpretentious, accepting endings with the same poise as coronations.  She opened the final ledger and wrote the truth in her own small hand, ensuring the next wanderer would know the rightful line.  As the first star lurched forward, the library began to fold into itself with the courtesy of a curtain falling.  Shelves became rivers of starlight, and the rune of drift lifted from the page to escort her through the dissolving dark.  The iron gall ink pellet cooled in her palm, its purpose spent, its corrosive grace forever etched into the archive of sky.  In the last instant before the constellations resumed, the diminutive titan smiled at the beauty of an honest ruin.  Beyond the mountains, a scribe opened a book to find a single marginal note written in faded iron gall.  It read: the throne belongs to the one who remembers, and the drift of stars is merely time correcting its course.  The scribe looked up, but the temple was gone, leaving only wind and the slow graceful collapse of an empire’s lies.
