The cobalt glade deepened into dusk, and its blue leaves seemed to hold their breath, as if the day had been told to arrive late and the night had arrived early instead.  Sena had been root-bound for three years, after the future arrived early and rewrote the calendar of the grove.  Bark had closed over her ankles, then her calves, then her knees, until she sat at the heart of the topiary maze like a queen who had swallowed her own throne.  They called her a root-bound visionary because she could read the clipped geometry of the hedges as a language of what had not yet happened.  She was purposefully curious, not idly so; every question she asked the maze was a seed she expected to harvest.  In her lap lay a maze topiary clipping, no larger than her thumb, its wound still pale and fragrant with the memory of shears.  It had been cut from the oldest hedge, the one that marked the eclipsed covenant between the glade and the dreams that slept beneath it.  That covenant had promised that the future would arrive on schedule, mature and kind, but the future had broken its promise and come early, raw and starving.  Since then, the glade's dusk had turned cobalt instead of gold, and every dreamer in the valley walked with their eyes half open, searching for something they could not name.  Sena's motive was simple and impossible: to find the intersection of dreams, the crossroads where all sleeping minds met, so she could renegotiate the broken schedule.  The clipping trembled, and a voice like sap moving in winter told her to heed the scars on the maze walls.  She did not flinch from the instruction, though anxious elation made her pulse hammer like a bird against glass.  For the method had always been linking through scars, binding one wounded place to another until distance became a rumor.  She pressed the clipping against a silver seam in the hedge where a branch had once been hacked away.  The scar accepted the wound of the clipping, and the maze inhaled.  A thread of cobalt light ran from her fingertips into the leaves, and suddenly she was standing in another person's dream.  It was a kitchen at the end of a street she knew, but the clocks were melting backward, and a child was crying because the future had come for her birthday early and taken her candles.  Sena understood then that the early arrival had not been an invasion but a theft, pulling tomorrow into today and leaving both hollow.  She moved through the child's kitchen by linking through the scars on its table, where rings and knife marks recorded every meal that had ever been hurried.  The child looked up and saw her, and the dream folded like paper into a corridor of hedges.  Sena felt the intersection of dreams opening around her, a cobalt clearing where thousands of sleepers stood with their palms upturned, waiting for the covenant to be spoken.  She lifted the maze topiary clipping like a key, but the eclipsed covenant was not written in ink; it was written in the shape of wounds that had been forbidden to heal.  A voice older than the glade asked what price she would pay to put tomorrow back where it belonged.  Sena looked down at her root-bound legs, at the bark that had become her bones, and she realized she had been afraid of exactly this: being needed.  The anxious elation in her chest sharpened into resolve, because a visionary who refused to see was only a garden ornament.  She chose to heed not the voice, but the child's silence, and she planted the clipping into the deepest scar on her own wrist.  Blood touched sap, and the linking through scars flared, tying the child's dream to the glade and the glade to every sleeper who had kept a scar as a promise.  The maze topiary clipping took root in her skin, and its first new leaf grew in the shape of a clock hand pointing neither forward nor back.  In the valley, clocks shuddered and stopped their backward melting, and the future, shamed but wiser, agreed to wait its turn.  The eclipsed covenant brightened like a moon leaving shadow, no longer broken, only restored to its proper orbit.  Sena remained root-bound, but now her roots ran along the intersection of dreams, and every night she tended the cobalt glade at dusk with purposefully curious care.  She had found what she sought, not an answer, but a place where wounds could be doors, and she held them open with anxious elation.
