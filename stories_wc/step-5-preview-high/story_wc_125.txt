The drifting roadside poet came to the terra-cotta grotto under starlight in the tense years after the energy beings arrived. 
They carried a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape, its fibers stiff with salt and old promises. 
The grotto walls were layered clay, red and ocher, holding warmth from a sun that no longer rose on schedule. 
The poet, ethically playful, had never stolen a life, only moments, and they intended to bargain for the texture of time. 
Starlight fell through the grotto mouth in bright stripes, and the poet saw a pattern repeating on the floor. 
Each ray landed on a coil of sediment as if marking a second, and the poet knelt to trace the design with careful fingers. 
By uncovering layers of clay with a shard of bone, they found older grooves beneath, each a frozen ripple of hours. 
The energy beings did not walk; they rippled at the cave entrance, translucent and hungry for geometry. 
They spoke in harmonics, asking what the poet offered for passage into the sedimented past. 
The poet smiled with passionate subtlety, hiding the rope behind their back while pretending to consider a silver button. 
"Tell me first," the poet said, "whether time is rough like hemp or smooth as a lie." 
The energy beings shimmered, and one replied that time could only be touched by one who understood impossible choices. 
The poet thought of the cell from which the rope had been smuggled, of a stranger who chose freedom and left another to the dark. 
That choice had followed the poet across provinces, a braid of guilt that no road could untangle. 
Now the grotto demanded a second impossible choice: surrender the rope to descend into the deep layers, or keep it and remain on the surface. 
The rope was evidence of mercy, but also of betrayal, and the poet was ethically playful enough to twist either truth. 
They looped the frayed coil around their palm and felt each strand catch on old scars, mapping a pattern of pressure. 
"You want an object," the poet said, "but objects are heavy with ethics, and I am a light traveler." 
The energy beings stilled, their glow dimming to a violet whisper that tasted of copper and unfinished symphonies. 
Then the poet knelt again and began to uncover layers faster, not to escape the choice but to widen it. 
Beneath the third stratum lay a lattice of fossilized roots shaped like a loom, and the poet saw that the grotto itself was a pattern. 
Starlight threaded the lattice, turning it into a clock face made of clay and shadow. 
The poet placed the rope into the groove between root and stone, letting it bridge the visible and the buried. 
"I do not choose," the poet murmured, "I pattern the choice until it becomes a texture." 
The energy beings vibrated, sensing the cleverness, the ethically playful defiance of a clean answer. 
One being offered a single bead of mercury that could sew the poet's name into the fabric of forever. 
Another offered to fray the rope into light so no prison could ever claim its origin again. 
Both gifts were traps, and the poet's chest tightened with the passionate subtlety of a heart learning restraint. 
They thought of the texture of time not as smooth glass but as woven fiber, rough where choices had been forced. 
So the poet pulled the rope taut and used it to saw a thin line across the wet clay, revealing a final layer beneath. 
There, in the dark red heart of the terra-cotta grotto under starlight, time pooled like water in a bowl. 
The poet dipped one finger, and the surface trembled with the memory of every impossible choice ever made. 
The energy beings leaned close, their heat drying the clay to powder, and the poet finally laughed, low and warm. 
"Time is coarse," the poet said, "because we rub against each other with decisions." 
The beings withdrew, for they could not consume a texture that admitted its own abrasion. 
The poet stayed until starlight faded, touching the rope, the pattern, the layers, and the water of hours. 
They rose with the coil still frayed but no longer a relic of escape; it was a tool for measuring. 
The poet left the grotto the way they had entered, drifting, ethically playful, carrying the roughness of time like a secret song. 
And somewhere beyond the crater, new energy beings arrived to find a world already patterned with quiet, impossible choices.
