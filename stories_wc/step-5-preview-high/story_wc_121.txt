The gravity defier walked upside down beneath the glowing viaduct, and the rain had just left the iron ribs warm and humming. 
She kept her balance not with ropes or faith, but with the ancient clay tablet pressed against her chest. 
Its runes had been written in a language of branching paths, each character a small fracture in time. 
They spoke of fractured timelines, of a city that existed in seven broken centuries and refused to agree on a single sky. 
Ilyse had spent years studying them because she intended to conquer the labyrinth of self. 
The labyrinth was not built of stone; it was a pattern she carried in her nerves, and it shifted every time she lied to herself. 
Tonight the city lay quiet, and the wet stone of the viaduct gave off a brightness so tender it seemed almost cruel. 
A single drop clung to the railing above her head, swollen with lamplight, neither falling nor still. 
In that hushed gap between the drop and the ripple, the tablet woke. 
It did not open a door so much as grow one through procedural generation, laying down stones according to rules only the runes remembered. 
Ilyse stepped through and felt the old weight return, then abandon her, as gravity remembered her name and let her pass. 
The corridor beyond was a mirror of the viaduct after rain, but the glow had gone sour, like a lamp that dims because it is burning its own oil. 
She walked it carefully, reading each rune before she placed her foot. 
The tablet's promise had been a hard one: in slow pulses she would be periodically eternal, undying only while the pattern held. 
If the runes faltered before she retraced the loop, her body would scatter across the timelines like grit in wind. 
The first chamber was made of her childhood: a house of bright windows, a garden that smelled of cut grass, and a father laughing behind a door she could not open. 
It was beautiful, and the brightness darkened a shade as she approached, because memory cannot survive being measured. 
The next room was a battlefield of her own making, where every enemy wore her face at a different age. 
She did not fight them; a gravity defier knows that victory is only another way to fall. 
Instead she retraced their steps, moving backward through the motions they had used to strike her. 
With every retraced movement the procedural hall rewrote itself, folding her mistakes into the floor as mortar. 
She began to understand that the labyrinth was not an enemy but a draft of herself, generated each time she tried to escape the parts she hated. 
Her breath grew short as the corridors narrowed toward a single final door. 
The glow around it was almost black, a brightness that had turned inward and begun to starve. 
Behind it stood the first version of Ilyse, the one who had carved the tablet and split the timelines by trying to win. 
That version was periodically eternal too, but it had forgotten the price, and so it repeated its reign in a loop of fading light. 
"You cannot conquer me," it said, and its voice was the sound of the viaduct stone cracking in cold. 
Ilyse lifted the tablet and set the runes against the door, not as a weapon, but as a record of every path she had walked. 
"I do not have to," she said. 
"I only have to retrace you until you are part of the pattern again." 
The first Ilyse trembled, because being remembered was the one defeat it had never generated. 
Its runes flared, then folded into the clay, and the timelines knit together with a sound like rain returning. 
The labyrinth fell away in layers, and Ilyse found herself once more beneath the viaduct, though the glowing stones were dimmer now, steadier. 
The drop had not yet touched the puddle, and the ripple had not yet begun. 
In that pause she placed the tablet on the rail, no longer a key, only a witness. 
The city would rebuild its broken centuries, and she would walk above it, lighter than light, darkening brightness at her back.
