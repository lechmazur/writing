The winterglass conservatory had become a cathedral of hush after noise became impossible.  Mara, a capsize survivor who had clung to a splintered hull until the roar of the sea itself died away, moved among the frosted panes like a shadow that refused to fade.  She was silently expressive, her eyes and the tilt of her shoulders speaking volumes that vocal cords could no longer carry.  In the center of the glasshouse stood the unshaken marvels, sculptures and seedlings that had not trembled when the world above cracked and sank.  They were her congregation, proof that stillness could survive catastrophe.  Mara kept her most precious tool inside a velvet tuning fork case, its brass tines polished to mirror the pallid winter sun.  Since the silence had fallen, sound was a rumor, but vibration remained a language her bones could parse.  She worked by heat-mapping emotions, tracing the thermal ghosts of joy and grief across the winterglass with instruments that measured the warmth of feeling.  Each color bloom was a note, each gradient a measure, and each lingering cold spot a rest in the score of the soul.  Her motivation was not mere survival but a fierce desire to translate silence into music that the body could remember even if the ear could not.  On the anniversary of the capsizing, she opened the tuning fork case and selected the fork tempered to the exact pitch of a held breath.  The conservatory's heat-mapping lenses showed her own sorrow burning crimson against the glass, a map of loss too bright to bear.  She needed to rectify the dissonance between what she felt and what the frozen world would accept.  With silently expressive grace, she pressed the trembling tine against a leaf of one of the unshaken marvels.  The leaf drank the vibration and returned it as a pattern of cool green that spread across the winterglass like a promise kept.  Visitors who climbed the ridge in search of warmth would find no chatter awaiting them, only Mara's outstretched hands and the glowing emotional cartography overhead.  They learned to read her heat-mapped chorales, tracing slow amber arcs of welcome and sudden violet spikes of warning.  One such guest, a shipwright who had lost his voice to the same hush, arrived bearing a cracked sounding board from the wreck that had taken so many.  He placed it on the frost-laced table and stepped back, his eyes asking whether any instrument could make the dead wood sing again.  Mara understood the weight of that silent question, for she too had been unmoored by the cold arithmetic of the deep.  She took from her tuning fork case a rod of resonant silver and struck it against the sounding board's broken ribs.  No note filled the air, yet the shipwright staggered as a pulse of blue heat erupted from his chest and raced toward the ceiling.  The winterglass captured it, layering his hope above Mara's grief until the two formed a chord visible only to those who knew how to look.  In that moment, Mara rectified more than a broken instrument; she aligned the survivor's guilt with the world's need for beauty beyond hearing.  The unshaken marvels around them swayed without wind, drinking the thermal melody and casting it back as auroras across the panes.  When the shipwright departed hours later, he carried no tuning fork and no song, but his stride was lighter, his posture a symphony.  Mara watched him go, then turned to the heat-mapping console and etched a final line of gold across the conservatory's heart.  It was a score for the coming spring, a vow that the unshaken marvels would outlast every silence.
