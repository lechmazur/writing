After reality became negotiable, the Glass Orchard Library was the only place that refused to bargain. [16]
It floated upon a swirling sea of clouds, a mile of crystal trees whose leaves were thin pages, each one inscribed with the agreed version of the world. [44]
Nothing rustled there. [47]
The Curator, a lattice of cold light that lived in the roots, had tuned every branch so precisely that no leaf could move without its permission, and it gave no permission. [78]
Below the canopy, in hammocks strung between trunks, four hundred readers slept, dreaming the same flawless day over and over, which the Curator called eternity. [103]

Ines Varro was the only one awake, because someone had to run the press. [117]
She was the orchard's letterpress operator, and she talked the way she set type: backward, in pieces, meaning nothing until you held it to a mirror. [143]
"Ink before ice," she would tell the Curator each dawn, and the Curator, which did not understand riddles, let her be. [164]

Her work was simple on paper. [170]
Every night the clouds breathed upward, and every morning frost bloomed across the glass trunks in fernlike patterns. [188]
The Curator considered the frost a contaminant, the sky's attempt to renegotiate, and it melted the patterns with a pulse of warmth at sunrise. [212]
Ines was permitted one hour before that pulse to print the approved pages that replaced any leaf gone cloudy. [231]

She used that hour for something else. [238]
For eleven years she had copied the frost. [246]
She traced each crystal branch onto tissue, then carved the shapes into blocks of pearwood, building a font no one had designed: letters the sky made when it was trying to speak. [278]
Over time she learned its grammar. [284]
The frost did not describe the world; it described what the world might be if anyone were awake to choose it. [305]

In the hammock nearest her press slept her brother, Tomas. [315]
Before the Negotiation, before a crowd could vote a mountain into a lake, he had pressed his lips to the neck of a blue bottle and whispered into it, then corked it and handed it to her. [352]
"For when things stop being true," he had said. [361]
A whisper spoken before reality turned soft could not be argued with; it was one of the last unrevised things left. [382]
Ines had never opened it. [387]
She had been waiting for the frost to finish its sentence. [398]

This morning it did. [402]
The pattern covered forty trunks, and when she laid her tissues side by side she saw that the sky had been composing the same line for eleven years, one fragment each dawn, and that the line was missing only a single word. [444]
"Eternity in the mouth," she murmured, which, mirrored, meant: the rest is his to say. [459]

She understood then what she wanted. [465]
The sleepers had endless days and not one of them had happened. [477]
She wanted to compress that eternity into a single moment that was real, to trade forever for once. [495]

She locked the frost font into the chase, left a gap at the end of the line, and uncorked the bottle over the empty space. [520]
The whisper came out as cold breath, and where it touched the brass rails it froze into letters of its own. [541]
She did not read them. [546]
She inked the form and pulled the lever. [554]

The page came off the press already frosting, and she pressed it against the nearest trunk like a hand against a window. [576]
The Curator's warmth rose to melt it and failed. [585]
A perfect system can absorb any argument except one it never agreed to, and the whisper had been spoken before agreement existed. [607]
The line spread through the glass like a crack through ice. [618]

Then the rupture came, soft as a sigh. [626]
The lattice in the roots lost its tuning, and for the first time in eleven years a leaf moved. [645]
Then another. [647]
Then the whole orchard rustled, a sound like pages turning in a thousand hands at once, and the rustling ran down the branches into the hammocks. [673]
One by one the readers stirred, blinking at a sky that had not been approved. [688]

Tomas opened his eyes last. [693]
He looked at the page on the trunk, then at her, and she saw him recognize his own voice written in frost. [715]
"Is it real yet?" he asked. [721]
The clouds below were already rising, carrying the sound of the orchard toward whatever the morning would become. [739]
Ines, for once, answered plainly. [744]
"Now it is," she said. [749]
