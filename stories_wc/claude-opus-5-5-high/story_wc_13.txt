The giant lay down beneath the city at noon, and the canals began to breathe. [15]
Every century he napped, and for the length of that nap the rooftop waterways that wove between the towers rose on his inhale and sank on his exhale. [43]
Ysolde Marr, natural history illustrator to the Tower Guild, drifted in her skiff with a sketchbook on her knee and a caged echo-wren at her feet. [69]
She had drawn every beetle in the gutters and every heron on the aqueduct arches, but she had never drawn an echo. [91]
Nobody had. [93]

Echoes in the city behaved strangely. [99]
When Ysolde spoke, her words came out broken, snagging on consonants, her sentences stalling and restarting like a cart on cobbles. [120]
But when those words struck the tower walls and returned, they came back whole. [134]
Smooth. [135]
The voice she had never been allowed to own, handed back to her by stone. [150]
She wanted to find the place where echoes were born and learn what mended them. [165]

The Guild said the answer lay upstream, at the source tower, but the source tower was a full day's climb through locks that opened only once an hour. [193]
The giant would wake before she reached it, and when he woke, the canals would drain for another hundred years. [213]

So she poled to the lock-house of Old Pell, known on every rooftop as the woman who sculpts time itself. [233]
Pell worked at a lathe of green bronze, shaving minutes from a spinning hour until they curled to the floor like pale pine shavings. [257]
She listened to Ysolde's request without once finishing a sentence for her, which was rare enough that Ysolde nearly wept. [277]
Then Pell lifted a pendant from a hook: a silver infinity symbol, its two loops hollow as glass. [295]
"You can go by borrowing time," she said. [303]
"One loop holds the hours I lend you, the other fills with what you owe." [318]
"Return it before he wakes, or the debt is paid from the end of your life." [334]

Ysolde fastened it at her throat, and the canal stilled mid-swell, water standing in a glassy ridge while she poled through the locks as if they were open doors. [363]
Towers slid past, their laundry frozen in flight, pigeons pinned to the air. [376]
One loop of the pendant drained; the other clouded. [385]

At the source tower the canal ended in a bronze basin open to the sky, and there the trail went cold. [406]
No carvings, no machine, nothing to draw. [413]
Ysolde looked at the echo-wren, a bird that fed on sound and nested wherever sound was richest, and understood that she had caged the only guide she had. [441]
Her fingers shook as she moved to uncage it. [450]
The wren rose, circled once, and dove into the air above the basin, where it hovered, beak open, waiting. [469]

Ysolde unclasped the pendant, letting time return. [476]
Far below, the giant breathed in, and the basin filled. [486]
Then his breath caught. [490]
It was a hitch, a sleeper's hiccup, an interrupted cadence in the long rhythm of the nap, and in that stalled half-second the water stood perfectly flat. [517]
Every sound in the city rushed into the pause like air into a lung. [531]
The wren sang into it, and the song came back to it doubled and clear. [546]

There was no machine that mended echoes. [553]
There was only the gap, the held breath, the place where the rhythm broke and something new had room to form. [574]
Echoes were born in interruptions. [579]

Ysolde drew fast, the flat water, the wren's open beak, the silence shaped like a bell. [595]
Then, because no one was there to hurry her, she spoke to the pause. [609]
"I- I am h-here." [613]
Here, with no walls to polish it, the echo returned exactly as she had made it, every catch and restart kept, and it was the most beautiful sound she had ever heard. [645]
Her stammer was nothing but pauses, and every pause was a birthplace; she had been carrying them in her mouth all her life. [668]

She sketched until the giant began to stir, then clasped the pendant again and slid home through the stilled city, the wren riding free on her shoulder. [695]

Pell took back the pendant and held the clouded loop to the lamp. [708]
"You owe me a day," she said. [715]
"How w-will I pay?" [719]
Pell waited through every stall in the question, then nodded at the sketchbook. [732]
"Draw me a pause," she said, "so I can carve one true." [744]
Ysolde opened the sketchbook and began. [750]
