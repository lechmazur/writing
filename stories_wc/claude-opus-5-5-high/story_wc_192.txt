The chapel had burned in autumn, and by midwinter it was only charcoal standing upright under the stars. [18] Maren kept it anyway. [22] She swept the ash from the aisle each dusk, though the wind returned it, and she lit no lamps, because the shadows she tended could not bear a flame. [51] Starlight was enough. [54] Starlight made everything gray and patient, and the shadows gathered in it like cattle at a fence. [71]

There had been nineteen of them at first, the ones who had sheltered here from the wildfire and found the roof a worse enemy than the sky. [98] They had died of smoke, not flame, and so they came back doing the only thing they remembered: fighting for air. [119] Their chests heaved without lungs. [124] Their mouths opened on nothing. [129] Maren had spent three months teaching them, one by one, that the smoke was over. [144] Eighteen had learned. [147] Eighteen had stepped through the chapel door and not come back. [158]

The door itself was long gone; only the frame remained, a blackened arch, and from its crossbeam hung the iron doorknocker ring that had once announced weddings and funerals. [187] It still worked, in its way. [193] Three knocks against the charred post, and the space inside the arch would thin, the way air thins above a road in summer, and the dead could pass. [221] But the frame was crumbling. [226] Every night a little more of it flaked into the ash, and every night the threshold it held grew fainter, a doorway being erased by weather. [252] Maren did not know what happened to a shadow left on the wrong side when it finally fell. [270] She did not intend to find out. [277]

The last shadow was her brother. [283] Tomas crouched where the altar had been, knees drawn up, heaving. [294] He had been the hardest because he was the one she had tried to drag out, and his panic knew her; whenever she came near, it doubled. [321]

That was why she had cut off all contact with the village. [333] Her mother wanted her home, wanted the ruin bulldozed, wanted a stone with Tomas's name on it and an end. [353] The priest wanted the land blessed. [359] Anyone who visited brought grief with them like a lantern, and the shadows shrank from it. [375] So Maren had done it the way she did everything since the fire: she burned her bridges carefully. [393] She paid the grocer. [397] She returned the priest's key with a note of thanks. [407] She wrote her mother a letter so kind and so final that there was nothing in it to argue with, no loose plank for anger to pry up. [435] No one came. [438] The silence afterward was enormous, and she lived inside it like a room. [451]

Tonight a whole corner of the arch had dropped away. [461] She could not wait for Tomas to come to her. [471]

Maren knelt in the ash and let herself encroach, a hand's width at a time, into the space he had made his own. [494] He thrashed. [496] Cold came off him in gusts. [502] She rolled back her sleeve. [507] The scar on her forearm was shaped like fingers, four ridges and a thumb, where his burning hand had gripped her in the doorway before the beam came down and tore them apart. [540] His shadow carried the same mark on its palm, a pale print where her skin had been. [557] Every shadow she had freed had worn its burns like a map, and she had learned to press her own scars against theirs, linking through the damage, because the damage was the only place the dead were still solid. [596]

She found his hand and fitted her scar into his. [606] It fit. [608] Tomas went still. [611]

"Listen," she whispered. [614] "In, like this." [617] She breathed slowly, filling her chest until it ached, then letting it go. [630] Through the scar she felt his false lungs stutter, catch, try. [641] "There is no smoke." [645] "The roof is gone." [649] "You can have all the sky you want." [657]

It took most of the night. [663] Somewhere near dawn his heaving softened into a sleeper's rhythm, and Maren realized that she, too, had not breathed properly since autumn. [685]

She led him to the arch. [691] She lifted the ring and knocked three times, and a flake of charcoal fell with each blow. [708] The threshold thinned, barely, a breath of a door. [717] Tomas's palm slid from hers, and he stepped through without looking back, which was how she knew he was well. [737]

Then the arch folded into the ash with a sound like a sigh, and the doorknocker ring dropped at her knees. [758] Maren picked it up. [762] There was no door left, no one to knock for, no road back she had not carefully closed. [780] She sat in the emptiness under the last stars and breathed. [791]
