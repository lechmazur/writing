After the last forest burned, the dunes learned to bloom. [10] Ilse Varga rode the roof of the freight crawler with a rifle she had never fired, watching the sand glow blue and violet beneath her like a slow tide of lanterns. [41] The petals opened only at night, and they hummed when the crawler passed, a thin three-note tune that broke off before its end. [64] Her mother used to sing it: hush now, the branches are, hush now, the branches. [79] Nobody remembered the rest. [83]

Ilse was the Company's favorite guard because she did not dream. [94] She had not dreamed since the night the Carpathian stand went up, the last green thing on the continent, and the doctors said something in her had simply closed its door. [125] That made her safe around the cargo. [132]

The cargo was the Divergent Archive: a steel drum the size of a chapel, holding every forest that might have been, suspended in superposition. [156] Oaks that had survived, pines that had never been planted, groves that grew in branches of history no one had lived, all overlapping like breath on glass. [183] An observer who dreamed could tilt it, the engineers warned; a strong enough longing, leaning on the wave functions, would collapse the whole archive into one wish. [210] The Company had already chosen the wish: a single timber forest, fast-growing and patented, planted in rows around Dome City. [230]

The trigger was bolted to the drum's hatch: an old tournament chess clock, both faces frozen at midnight. [248] Press the left button and the Company's side would start ticking, and the archive would fall into rows. [266] No one had assigned the right button to anything. [275]

On the ninth night the girl appeared again. [283] She was a flicker the archive leaked, a child made of moss light, and she was occasionally everywhere: crouched on the bumper, sitting on the antenna, walking beside the treads, all at once, then nowhere. [318] The drivers called her a ghost and turned up their radios. [329] Ilse, who had no dreams to be haunted by, simply watched where the girl kept pointing. [345]

Not at the bloom. [349] Between the glowing dunes lay hollows where nothing lit, troughs of cold sand where the flowers refused to root and where, Ilse knew, the ash camps huddled without fire or shade. [380] The girl pointed into each one, her many copies pointing together, like a compass that had learned to disagree. [399]

Hush now, Ilse sang under her breath, surprising herself. [408] The branches are, hush now, the branches. [415]

Every copy of the girl turned toward her. [423]

She understood then what the engineers had missed. [431] A dreamer would collapse the archive into one forest because a dreamer wanted one forest. [446] Ilse wanted nothing that clear. [451] Her longing had no picture in it, only a direction, and a direction could be divided. [467]

She climbed down to the hatch while the drivers slept. [477] The chess clock sat in its brace, its twin hands pointing straight up, two tiny towers at the end of the world. [499] She did not press either button. [505] Instead she laid her palm on the cold drum and sang the broken lullaby, slow, the way her mother had sung it during the evacuation, and she let the song mean only this: not here, not one, a little in every dark place. [548]

It was not a command. [553] You could not command a probability. [559] You could only charm it, the way you charm a feverish child toward sleep, easing the amplitudes outward note by note until the archive no longer leaned toward Dome City at all. [591]

The drum hummed back the three notes, then a fourth she had never heard. [605]

The girl stood at every hollow in sight. [613] Where she stood, a spark opened in the black sand: a single seedling, faintly green, no taller than a match flame, a different species in each trough, as if the archive were dealing out its forests one card at a time. [654] A beech in one, a cedar in the next, a birch trembling in a hollow where a child from the ash camp was already kneeling. [679]

The drum was lighter by morning, though no gauge could say by how much, and the Company's clock still read midnight on both faces. [703] Let them wait for their turn, Ilse thought. [711] The game was not over; it had only branched. [720] She would ride this route again, and the next, singing into the unlit corners, cultivating whatever sparks the archive chose to spend. [742]

That afternoon, curled in the crawler's shade, she slept and saw something for the first time in years. [760] It was only a branch, broken, green at the break. [770]

Hush now, it sang. [774]
