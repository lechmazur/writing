The sanctuary had been cut from white marble a thousand years ago, and the roof had fallen in two hundred years later, so now the aurora poured through the broken dome and lay green and violet across the floor. [39]
Ilse Varn knelt at the lectern where the charter lay chained, and she listened to the stalactites drip. [57]
By old law, a charter of succession could be completed only between the first drop and the last, while the aurora held, and only with a thorn from the heritage rose that grew from a crack beside the altar. [96]
The founders had planted it. [101]
Magistrate Corvane had merely ordered her to use it. [110]

"Your master began it," he said from the doorway, where his two guards stood with lanterns they did not need. [130]
"You will finish it in his hand. [137]
Nobody alive knows his hand better." [143]

That was true. [146]
For eleven years Ilse had forged for Corvane: tax remissions, pardons, the letters of dead men who needed to have said convenient things. [169]
She had endured it the way marble endures water, by yielding nothing that mattered and letting the rest wear smooth. [189]
Teodor had taught her that, before they took him. [198]

The charter was his last work, the Free Charter of the river city, and it stopped mid-line. [215]
"Authority shall rest with," it read, and then nothing, a pearl-grey silence of vellum where the council's name should have been. [236]
Corvane wanted his own name there. [242]

For years she had hated Teodor a little for that blank. [253]
He had been arrested in this room with the thorn still wet, and she had always believed that one more minute of courage would have saved the city. [281]

She had tried it in every lock she could reach. [316]
None fit. [318]

Now, kneeling, she saw what she had never been permitted close enough to see. [332]

The lock had been in plain sight the whole time, disguised as ornament. [369]

A drop fell into the basin behind her. [377]

She stopped hers a hair's width short, and pressed until the thorn split. [449]

He had not lost his courage. [488]

"It is done," she said. [560]
