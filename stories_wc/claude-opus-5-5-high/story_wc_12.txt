Father Ansel came to the Anchor carrying a loop pedal in a canvas bag and a battery pack strapped to his hip like a second heart. [26] The fog on the salt flats swallowed his footsteps, so that he arrived without a sound, which was how he preferred to arrive anywhere. [50] The Anchor itself was an iron spike as wide as a chapel, driven into the flats centuries ago to pin this world in place against the drift of the others. [80] A keeper lived at its base, and the keeper's only duty was to remain still. [95] The current keeper, Mira Voss, sat on a stool before an easel, painting the same grey horizon she had painted every morning for eleven years. [120] Her palette held one color, a flat pewter grey mixed from the salt itself, because the Order held that a keeper who reached for blue or red might start to want things. [152] Her brush hand no longer moved on its own. [161] Something else moved it, slow and patient, and the grey had crept past her wrist into the skin of her forearm. [182]

"They told me you would come," she said, without turning. [192] "They told me it was a demon from the drift." [202]

Ansel set the pedal on the salt and knelt beside it. [213] "They tell people a great many things," he said. [222]

He did not pray the way the old exorcists prayed, with shouting and water. [236] He recorded. [238] He asked her to say the vow of the keepers, and when she said it, he pressed the pedal with his heel and caught it: I keep the world still. [268] The phrase came back to them through a small speaker, dampened by fog, and he let it circle three times before he spoke over it. [293]

"Who keeps the world?" he asked, and recorded that too. [303] "Who keeps the keeper?" [307] "Who decided that still was the same as safe?" [316]

Between each layer he left a gap, a measured rest where nothing played at all. [331] This was his particular art; other priests filled rooms with incense and icons, but Ansel decorated with silence, hanging it in the loop like cloth over a window, so that whatever hid in a person would have somewhere to show its shape. [373] The questions stacked, answered by nothing, and in the gaps he plumbed her the way sailors plumb a harbor, dropping one word at a time and listening for how long it fell. [405]

The grey reached her elbow. [410]

"Say it again," he told her. [416] "Slower." [417]

She said it, and the pedal layered her new voice over the old one, and the two recordings slipped against each other by half a beat. [443] Through the muffle of the fog the overlap turned the words inside out, and what they both heard, clear as a bell under water, was: the world keeps me still. [473]

Mira's brush stopped. [476]

"There is no demon," Ansel said quietly. [483] "There is only the Anchor, and it does not hold this world by being iron. [498] It holds by taking keepers, one grey inch at a time, until they are part of the spike. [516] The Order calls it possession so no one has to call it payment." [529]

She looked at the palette, at the single color she had been told was humility, and understood that it was the Anchor's own color, and that she had been mixing herself into it every morning. [564]

"If I stop being still, the world drifts," she said. [574]

"Does it?" Ansel asked, and left a silence long enough to hang a coat on. [589] "Listen." [590]

The loop was still running, the same phrases circling, never identical, never ending. [603] It was a stillness that moved; a stillness unraveled into repetition, returning to the same place again and again without ever freezing. [625] And beneath their feet, the Anchor hummed with it, steady, holding. [636]

"It needs attention, not a statue," Ansel said. [644] "A returning thing can hold a place as well as a dead one." [657]

He unstrapped the battery pack and wired it to the old solar cells on the Anchor's flank, then set the pedal against the iron and let the loop run on its own. [689] The grey at Mira's elbow stopped, then, slowly, began to recede toward her wrist. [703] She picked up the palette, scraped the grey onto the salt, and pressed her thumb, still pink at the tip, into the empty wood. [727]

"Tomorrow," she said, "I'll need another color." [734]

Ansel smiled, and said nothing, and let the nothing speak. [744]
