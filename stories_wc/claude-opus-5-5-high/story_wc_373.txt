Under the Kessler Street pier, where the pilings wept green and the rotting planks let down thin threads of moonlight, the pirate market kept a truce of hush. [28] Anyone could sell anything there, rum or rifles or stolen pearls, provided they did it in whispers. [45] A raised voice meant the dunking stool, and the truce held until sunrise, when the tide came up through the stalls and everyone scattered like crabs. [71]

Wren Callis had grown up inside that quiet. [79] Her father ran moonshine down the coast in a lobster boat with a false bottom, and he used to bring her here as a child, wrapped in oilcloth, because a sleeping girl made a smuggler look honest. [116] She had loved it: the lanterns hooded in cheesecloth, the water lapping like a patient dog, the traders breathing prices into each other's ears. [140] It was the safest place she knew, and she had never once felt safe in it. [156]

Now her father sat in the county jail, taken by revenue men who had known exactly which crate to open. [176] He swore it was Harrow, the fence, who had sold him. [187] But Harrow never spoke above a breath, never broke the hush, and the runners' council would not condemn a man on a jailbird's word. [211] "Trust breaks quiet, and nobody ever hears it," her father had told her through the visiting grille. [228]

So tonight Wren carried a hatbox, and inside the hatbox sat a borrowed phonograph recorder with a fresh wax cylinder, its horn packed in felt. [253] She meant to capture the sound of trust breaking. [262]

She found Harrow by the chandler's stall, a soft, gray man who smelled of clove oil. [278] Across his vest hung an antique watch fob, a silver anchor on a chain, and Wren's throat tightened, because it was her grandfather's, the one her father had pawned to Harrow two winters ago. [312] Harrow was talking with old Mose Tillery, who ran the shallows past Gull Point, and a stranger in a clean pea coat watched them from the oyster barrels. [340] Too clean. [342]

Wren understood the method at once. [348] In the hush, Harrow didn't need words; he had only to lift the anchor fob and let it tap his watch case, one small bright click, and the revenue man would know which runner to follow home. [385] That was how her father had been sold. [393]

She set the hatbox on a coil of rope, eased the lid, and waited. [407]

Harrow's hand rose toward the fob. [413] Wren struck her thimble against a lantern hook first, click, and the stranger's head turned to her instead. [431] Harrow's hand fell. [434] He started again a minute later, and she dropped a brass button onto a tin plate. [450] Again, and she snapped her purse shut. [457] Each false start sent the stranger's eyes skittering across the market, from click to click, until he could not tell a signal from the ordinary tick of commerce. [485] Mose, oblivious, bargained on in his gentle rasp. [493]

Wren did not hurry. [497] People had always called her curiously steadfast, a girl who could hold a lantern steady in a squall, and she held herself that way now, one eye on the gray light beginning to thin the dark between the planks. [536] Sunrise was coming, and with it, Mose's walk home. [545]

On the fifth attempt, Harrow's patience cracked. [552] He yanked the fob against the watch case, hard, and when the stranger still hesitated, Harrow did the unthinkable. [571]

"Him, you fool," he said aloud, jabbing a finger at Mose. [582]

His voice rang off the pilings like a dropped bottle. [592] Inside the hatbox, the stylus trembled and cut it into the wax. [604]

Every face in the market turned. [610] The stranger bolted up the ladder, and Mose went still, understanding, and a dozen runners closed around Harrow without a single word, which was worse than shouting. [637] Wren lifted the cylinder from the machine, and Bess Tolan, who sat on the runners' council, looked at it a long moment and nodded once. [662] Her father's word would stand now. [668]

As they led Harrow away, the anchor fob tore loose and skittered to her feet. [683] She picked it up, warm from a traitor's vest, and closed her hand around it. [698] She had thwarted the sale and caught the sound she came for, and it was smaller than she had imagined, just one tired man's voice, though it would echo for years. [729]

Then the tide came in, cold and familiar, lapping at her boots, and the market dissolved into whispers again, lanterns winking out one by one. [754] Mose touched her shoulder as he passed, gentle as a promise, and Wren climbed toward the sunrise with the hush still ringing in her ears. [779]
