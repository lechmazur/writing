Ines had not spoken to anyone in four years when the postcard found her. [14] She kept a post office box two valleys down, checked once each spring, and nearly threw the card away until she saw the drawing on its face: a woman woven in red thread, her face torn out. [51] Underneath, Oma Vessel had written a single line in her slanting hand: follow what I made down into the caldera. [71] The postmark was eleven months old. [77]

Oma had been the librarian of the Calder Street Lending Library, and twenty-two years ago Ines had burned it to the ground. [99] She had been fourteen, and her mother had just died owing the library eleven dollars in fines, and the notice had come the same week as the funeral, and it had seemed, with the clean logic of grief, that something should burn. [141] What she remembered best was not the gasoline but the tapestry on the reading-room wall, a relic lent by some museum, a Veshti archivist's self-portrait worked in madder and ash-gray wool. [172] When the flames reached it she had torn the face away with both hands and run. [188] Oma found her on the curb, clutching the scrap, and did not call her a monster. [204] Ines, sobbing, had promised to bring the woven woman home. [214]

Then came detention, and drifting, and a string of rooms she left without explanation, and the promise sank beneath them like a coal under snow. [239] Only the fragment stayed, faded now to the color of weak tea, folded in a tobacco tin. [256]

The caldera took her three days to reach. [264] Steam threaded up through fissures in the black rock, and the air smelled of sulfur and hot iron. [282] At the base of the crater wall, a doorway cut in basalt opened onto the Veshti archive, the place named on the brass placard that had once hung beneath the tapestry: tier after tier of stone niches where a vanished people had filed their memory in cloth. [329]

Just inside sat the first of Oma's creations, a small clay figure of a girl on a curb, knees drawn up, something clutched to her chest. [355] Ines crouched and studied it the way she studied everything, measuring before feeling, then letting the feeling correct the measurement. [375] The girl's head was turned slightly left, toward a corridor. [385]

Down that corridor she found a charcoal sketch pinned in a niche, portraying the same girl older, asleep in a bus station; its shadows all fell toward a stair. [414] How had Oma known about the bus station? [422] Then Ines understood that Oma had followed her, at least for a while, the way someone watches embers, not to smother them but to keep them from going out. [451]

There were more: a watercolor of her washing dishes in a diner, a woodcut of her boarding a northbound train, a tiny oil of her cabin roof under snow, each angled like a compass needle, each deeper in the warm dark. [492] The stone grew hot enough to feel through her boots. [502]

The last niche held the tapestry. [508] She knew it instantly, the madder red, the ash-gray border, the scorch marks along one edge where the library fire had licked it. [531] The Veshti woman stood with her loom, her hands lifted, and where her face should have been was a ragged hole. [552] Someone had vandalized her, Ines thought, and then, with a lurch, remembered that the someone was herself. [569] Oma must have pulled the rest from the burning wall and carried it here, back to the people who made it, and then waited. [593]

Beside it, on a folded blanket, lay a needle already threaded with gray wool, and a card. [610] The card said only: turn her over. [617] Ines lifted the heavy cloth from its hooks, and on the reverse, stitched in new white thread around the empty place, were words in Oma's slanting hand. [644] You set the fire, they read, but you also carried her out. [656] I kept my half of your promise safe. [664]

Ines sat down on the hot floor with the tin in her lap. [677] Something moved up through her chest like magma finding a seam, twenty-two years of pressure breaking open, and she let it come. [699] Then she unfolded the faded face and laid it into the hole, and the torn edges met like hands that had been reaching for each other in the dark. [728] She sewed slowly, analytically, matching warp to warp, trusting her fingers where her eyes failed. [743] The stone beneath her breathed its slow heat. [751]

When she finished, the woven woman looked out at her with pale, patient eyes, and Ines, who had not spoken in four years, said the only thing that mattered. [780] "I brought you home." [784]
