The Meridian had been a surveillance airship once, before the government sold it for scrap and the Coastal Archive Trust filled its gondola with shelves. [25] Now it drifted a thousand feet above the Carolina dunes, a floating library humming with fans, and Odessa Varn worked in its hottest room because paper told her things there. [55] She was a solemn woman, forty years a civil-rights archivist, and she did not smile at donors. [72] Smiling promised them a kinder past than the one she usually found. [84]

It was three weeks after the letters were found. [93] Marguerite Lyle, eighty-one, had carried the trunk aboard herself, refusing the porter, and opened it on Odessa's desk like a coffin. [114] Inside were twelve letters from a federal agent thanking her father, Ezra, for his cooperation in the summer of 1965. [134] Ezra Lyle had organized the Wilmington voter marches. [142] Two of his marchers had died in an ambush that August. [153] Beneath the letters lay a diary written entirely in numbers. [163]

"Tell me what he was," Marguerite said. [170] "Then I'll decide whether to burn it." [177]

Odessa had spent the weeks since failing at the cipher. [187] Tonight, with the Meridian crossing the same coast Ezra had walked, she laid her bare palm on the diary's cover, knowing the cost. [210] Her gift had never been gentle. [216] When the past took her, it kept her, retrocognitively trapped until the memory finished its own sentence. [233] Once she had lost two days inside a lynching photograph. [243]

The gondola dissolved into white August light. [250] She stood on the beach below, sand scalding her bare feet, and watched a young man in rolled sleeves drag driftwood across the flats. [274] Ezra laid the wood in bars, then bottle glass, then kelp, then broken crab traps, arranging beach debris into shapes only something overhead could read. [299] Above him, silver and patient, hung the Meridian in its first life. [311] Odessa understood with a lurch that the agent in the letters had been watching from her own desk. [329] The shapes were numbers, and they were the diary's key. [339] She memorized them the way she memorized names on gravestones, holding each until it burned. [354]

A cutter came through the surf, and a man in a pressed gray suit waded ashore to parley. [372] Odessa could not move; the memory held her like a hand on the neck. [386]

"Route for Tuesday," the agent said. [392]

Ezra pointed at the sand. [397] "Already told your pilot. [401] Market Street." [403]

It was a lie. [407] Odessa knew the history: the marchers had taken Front Street that Tuesday, safely, while armed men waited on Market for nobody. [428] He had been feeding them wrong roads all summer. [437]

Then the agent leaned close. [442] "Your lies are costing me credibility, Lyle. [449] Give me one true thing by Friday, or I give them your daughter's school." [463]

The light went searing, unbearable, and the scene jumped forward like a skipped reel. [477] Night now, and Ezra knelt in a hollow beneath the dunes, beside the obsidian lagoon, a pool so black and still it looked like poured glass. [503] He was weeping and writing numbers. [509] Odessa could not read them over his shoulder, only watch his reflection's mouth shape two names. [525] She knew those names. [529] They were carved on the ambush memorial. [536]

The memory closed its sentence, and she fell back into the gondola, gasping, her palm blistered. [552]

It took her until dawn to decrypt the diary with the beach numbers. [565] The final entry read: I gave him the Castle Street meeting instead of my girl. [580] I told myself they would cancel it. [587] They did not. [590] One summer of lies saved hundreds, one truth killed two, and I cannot make the arithmetic come out clean. [609]

Marguerite came at nine. [613] Odessa did not soften it, because softening was its own kind of lie, and she had just watched a man drown in those. [636] She read the entry aloud, then the routes, then the names. [647]

"So he was both," Marguerite said at last. [655]

"He was a man cornered in a parley he could not win," Odessa said. [669] "The record should hold all of it." [676]

Marguerite looked out the window at the dunes sliding past, at the black eye of the lagoon. [693] "I was the girl he chose," she said. [701]

She did not burn the diary. [707]

That afternoon, when the Meridian moored at the beach, the two women walked down to the flats and arranged driftwood and bottle glass into the names of the dead, large enough for anything overhead to read. [743] Then Odessa photographed it for the archive and stood in the searing light until the tide came to read it too. [764]
