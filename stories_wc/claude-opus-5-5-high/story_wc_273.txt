The eclipse ended the way a held breath ends, all at once and with a small ache. [17] Sefa stood at the mouth of the chronicler's hollow and let the returning light sting her pink eyes. [35] Every other tracker in the encampment had stayed inside the wind-carved rooms, where centuries of gusts had scooped the sandstone into bowls and ribs and whistling throats. [62] She had spent the whole dark minutes outside, because dark was the only time the world treated her fairly. [81]

Oren was gone. [84]

His inkpot lay on its side on the stone table, and a black lake had crawled across the floor toward the doorway. [106] He used dusk ink, the old camp recipe of soot and beetle resin that stayed visible only in shadow and faded when the sun touched it. [132] Chroniclers wrote the dead in it, so that grief could be read at night and set down by morning. [151] Now the sun was sliding back through the doorway, and the spill had perhaps a quarter hour before it vanished. [171]

Sefa knelt. [173] She had tracked goats across bare rock and children through sandstorms, and she had learned that liquids lie less than feet do. [195] The ink had not simply pooled. [201] It had been disturbed while wet, and the disturbance was fading from the edges inward, pale ring by pale ring, like a tide going out. [226] In the fading rim she read a heel print, then the fan of toes turning, then a long smear where a sleeve had dragged. [250] Oren had not been taken. [255] He had stood in his own spilled ink, deliberately, and walked out. [267]

The hummingbird hummed at her shoulder. [273] It was Oren's machine, copper feathers over a heart of wound springs and glass, built to hover in front of a face and remember it. [298] He had told her its wings beat eighty times a second, and that to such a creature a falling stone would seem to drift like a leaf. [325] "It lives the way we live in the worst moment of our lives," he had said. [341]

That was the question that had bound them. [349] When the cliff shelf broke under Sefa's mother, the fall had lasted, in Sefa's memory, longer than a season. [368] She had seen every grain of dust lift from the rope. [379] She had wanted, ever since, to know why time slows when it should hurry, why the world grants the most seconds to the moments no one wants. [406] Oren had promised to find out with her. [414]

The drone dipped toward the spill and hovered, and she understood that it was not idling. [430] Its wingbeat was shivering the surface of the ink, and the ink was answering in patterns: rings, spokes, lattices that held for a breath and dissolved. [456] Oren had tuned it. [460] He had left her a message written in vibration, in a medium that would erase itself before anyone else thought to look. [482]

She read it the way she read wind on dunes, letting the shapes become words. [497]

Time does not slow. [501] The frightened eye becomes a scribe and writes down every instant, so the memory is crowded, and when you read it back the moment seems long. [527] We do not get more time; we thicken it. [536] I have written enough of other people's endings, Sefa. [545] I want to live thickly before mine, out where nothing is recorded. [557]

The sun reached the center of the spill, and the last lattice went gray, then clear, then nothing. [575]

Sefa found that she was angry, and that the anger was very quiet, like a lamp in a closed room. [595] He had answered their question and then used the answer as a door, and he had not asked whether she wished to walk through it with him. [622] Or perhaps he had asked, and this was the asking. [632]

Consciously wild, the elders called her, as if it were a diagnosis. [644] She had refused the shaded rooms and the sun veils they wove for her, choosing instead to learn the country by night and by feel, because a life measured in safety seemed to her a life written in ink meant to fade. [686]

She wiped the ink from her fingertips onto the stone, where it vanished at once. [701] Then she held out her hand, and the hummingbird settled on it, warm with its own frantic, patient heart. [720] "Show me his face," she said, and it lifted and led her out between the singing ribs of rock. [739] By the time the elders looked into the hollow, there was no ink on the floor, no machine in the air, and no pale tracker anywhere in the encampment, only her footprints, already filling with wind.
