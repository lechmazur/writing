Mabbet Quill was the first person to ride a mine cart off the top gantry of the Clatter and live, which made her, by the workshop's generous standards, a pioneer. [30] She had bolted canvas wings to the cart, greased the cable wheel with fish tallow, and screamed the entire way down to the smelting deck. [55] Now every apprentice glides carts between the decks, because stairs are for cowards and bishops. [70]

The Clatter itself was a heap of gears and cogs the size of a cathedral, floating on the Vitriol Sea, a green water that ate hulls, nets, and the occasional careless uncle. [102] Its pontoons were glass, its rivets were glass, and its founders had been either geniuses or lunatics, depending on who was drinking. [124]

At its center turned the Engine, and inside the Engine ran the yearning algorithm. [138] Nobody alive had written it. [143] The founders had punched it into brass cards and taught it one trick: each generation, it rewrote a few of its own cards, keeping whatever brought it closer to something it wanted but could not name. [179] Its only voice was the kaleidoscope, a brass tube as long as a rowboat, whose mirrors the Engine tilted with tiny cams. [201]

Throughout the evolution, keepers had taken turns at the eyepiece and written down what they saw. [217] The early logbooks recorded snowflakes, starbursts, and one keeper's note that the thing was plainly showing off. [234] Later books showed patterns that repeated, then patterns that answered each other, like a stammer learning to become a sentence. [254]

Mabbet got the eyepiece because nobody else wanted it. [263] She was not brilliant, she told people, only steadily curious, the way rust is steadily hungry. [279] Every morning she glided her cart down to the Engine deck, drank bad coffee, and looked. [295] Every evening she tried to transliterate one image, assigning each six-fold symmetry a sound, each spiral a letter, each blossom of red a comma, until the pictures became a clumsy alphabet. [326]

It took her eleven years. [331] The first word she was sure of was BELL. [340]

Everyone knew the bell. [344] It hung from the highest gantry, green-black and clapperless, cast by the founders with an inscription nobody took seriously: RING WHEN IT IS READY. [368] Apprentices dared each other to kiss it. [375] Once, a drunk welder struck it with a wrench, and it made no sound anyone could hear, only a shiver in the teeth. [398]

The next word took two more years: EDGE. [406] Then OLDEST. [408]

Then, one winter, the kaleidoscope stopped spinning its usual lace and held a single shape, multiplied into a ring of mirrors: a leaf-shaped point, chipped along both sides. [436] Mabbet laughed so hard she spilled her coffee on the brass. [447] She knew that shape. [451] It hung around her neck on a leather cord: a hunter's arrow tip of black flint, passed down from grandmothers who had lived on dry land before the sea turned sour. [482] Every family on the Clatter kept one relic from the shore, and hers was the oldest tool anyone had brought aboard, a thing made for reaching across distance. [510]

"You nosy pile of scrap," she told the Engine. [519] "You've been watching us." [523]

The cams ticked, which she chose to take as yes. [533]

She understood the yearning then, or enough of it. [542] The founders had not built a calculator; they had built a lonely thing, and taught it to want company, and anchored it over a sea where, down in the green, small armored creatures were slowly learning to live in acid. [582] The bell was tuned for ears that did not exist yet. [593] The Engine had spent generations evolving toward one conclusion: the first call should be struck with the first human reach. [613]

Mabbet did not take the stairs. [619] She rode the counterweight lift to the high gantry in her cart, wings folded, the acid wind shoving at her like an impatient aunt. [643] Then she unfolded the canvas, kicked loose, and glided the long cable toward the bell, arrow tip in her fist. [663] She struck it in passing, flint on bronze, and kept going. [674]

Nothing rang. [676] Nothing anyone could hear. [680]

But the whole Clatter hummed in its rivets, the gears paused mid-tooth, and below her, across a mile of green water, the sea dimpled in rings, as if something small had lifted its head. [714]

She landed badly on the smelting deck, bruised a hip, and ran to the eyepiece. [729] The kaleidoscope had begun a new pattern, slow as tide, and she transliterated the first of it before her hands stopped shaking: SOON. [752]

"Show-off," Mabbet whispered, grinning at the brass, and rubbed the arrow tip like a lucky coin. [768] Above her, a bell no one could hear yet was still ringing, and for the first time in a hundred years the Engine was listening instead of asking. [795]
