Mercy had stopped finding the dead the spring the guns went quiet, and for three years she had slept beneath the tin awning of the cylinder shop, letting the wasteland go on without her nose. [35] The settlement of Tallow Reach sprawled across the cracked plain like something dropped and never picked up: bus hulls, gutted tanks, radiator walls, miles of rust stitched together with rope bridges over fissures that breathed cold air at night. [74] It smelled, to her, of old pennies and patience. [83]

Wren smelled of solder and worry. [89] The girl came in at dusk with a phonograph cylinder wrapped in a scarf, and Mercy lifted her heavy head, because beneath the solder there was something else, something that did not belong to any season left in the world. [129] Tucked into the scarf was a maple leaf sealed in a disk of beeswax, red gone to rust at the edges, and it smelled of wet bark and smoke from chimneys that had not burned since before the war. [168]

"A trader brought it from the Glass Ward," Wren whispered to the dog, because everyone in Tallow Reach whispered after dark, as if the wasteland were a sleeping animal. [197] "That's where they keep the lung-sick, past the long crack. [207] Mom went there four winters ago." [213]

The shop's player had been broken since the war, so Wren did what her mother had taught her: she clamped the cylinder under the lamp and read the grooves themselves. [243] During the last resource war, couriers had hidden messages there, letters scratched so fine they rode between the sound, visible only through a jeweler's loupe fitted with colored glass. [272] Each tint showed a different layer. [278]

Under blue glass, Wren found a list of medicines, crossed out one by one. [292] Under green, a column of names from the ward, most followed by a small cross. [307] Mercy knew that shape of silence in the girl's breathing; she had heard it over a hundred collapsed cellars. [326]

Under amber, the last layer surfaced, and it was a riddle in her mother's slanted hand. [342]

I am the season that no longer falls. [350] I kept my color by refusing air. [357] Let the nose that knows endings find a beginning instead. [367]

Wren looked at Mercy, and Mercy looked at the leaf, and neither of them wanted to be the first to understand. [388] The answer was the leaf, and the leaf was a scent, and scents were what Mercy had once followed into ruins until she lay down beside whatever waited at the end. [419] Lying down was the only signal she had ever been taught. [430]

Still, when Wren held out the wax, the old dog rose, joints clicking, and put her nose to it. [449] Beneath the maple and the smoke she found a third thread, faint and warm, the same wax burning somewhere far off, night after night, like a lantern held up for a nose instead of an eye. [485]

They left at moonrise. [489] The night was hushed and not at all tame: wind combing wire fences, a loose sheet of tin ticking like a clock, the fissures exhaling dust that tasted of iron and dead engines. [522] Mercy led them over rope bridges and through the ribs of a downed airship, past families asleep in cargo containers, past the scrap-shrine where people left bolts for their lost. [552]

Wren talked the whole way, low, to keep from thinking about the crosses. [565] She said her mother used to say that a letter was just distance folded small enough to carry. [583] She said maybe the cylinder was her mother trying to fold the whole wasteland into something a daughter could hold. [603]

At dawn the Glass Ward rose from the plain, a long barracks roofed in salvaged windshields, every pane a different tint. [624] Only one pane was amber, and behind it a candle guttered in a saucer of red-flecked wax. [641]

Mercy slowed. [643] Every muscle in her remembered the old ending, the lowering of the body, the soft groan that told handlers to bring the stretcher. [666] She stood at the door with her legs trembling, sorting the air. [678] Wax, maple, medicine, sweat, and under all of it breath, thin and rattling but moving, in and out, the way a tide must once have moved. [704]

Mercy did not lie down. [709]

She threw back her head and bayed, a sound she had never been trained to make, and it rolled across the cracked earth and set every tinted pane humming. [738] Inside, a thin woman lifted her face to the amber glass, and Wren, laughing and crying at once, pressed both palms flat against it until there was nothing between them but color. [770]
