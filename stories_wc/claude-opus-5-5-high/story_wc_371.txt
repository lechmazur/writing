Ines kept a notebook of things that had stopped being strange. [11] The first entry was the checkpoint on Linden Street, which had seemed monstrous for a week and then became a place to fix your hair before work. [38] The fortieth entry was the Varga bakery. [45] One morning its shutters were down and sealed with a municipal ribbon, and by the following Tuesday the women in her queue had stopped saying the name. [72] Ines had written it down in her small square hand, at her desk in the city registry, and that was all she had done. [96] Noticing, she had learned, was not the same as refusing. [106]

After the birds flew south, the city went quiet in a way she could not enter into the notebook, because it had no single cause. [131] It was during that silence that she smelled caraway near the old palace. [144] The palace had been closed since before the current government, its gates chained, its courtyard given over to bindweed and fig. [165] But the smell was unmistakable, warm and dark, and it led her along the east wall to a gap where the stones had been loosened by roots. [192] Inside, under a collapsed arcade roofed with tarpaulin and ivy, someone had set out low tables, mismatched cups, and a brazier with a kettle ticking on it. [219] Six people looked up at her. [225] An old man in a gardener's apron rose, poured a cup, and set it in front of the empty cushion nearest the gap. [248] "We don't ask names," he said. [254] "We don't ask why." [258]

His name, she learned later, was Tomas, and his gentleness ran on rules of thumb rather than sentiment. [276] Pour for the newcomer first. [281] Seat the frightened nearest the exit. [287] Give the heel of the loaf to whoever looks hungriest, because the heel keeps longest in a pocket. [305] He cut the loaf, and Ines saw the braid pressed into its crust, the three-strand mark of the Varga ovens. [325] She could not swallow the first bite. [332] "The daughter left us the starter," Tomas said, reading her face. [343] "In a jam jar, the night before the ribbon." [352] "Is she alive?" [355] "The starter is." [358]

Ines came back every evening. [363] She learned that the tea house was only the courtyard's quiet face. [375] The palace beds, tangled as they looked, were a nursery: rye and caraway, marigold and lavender, all grown from seed the guests carried out in coat linings. [402] At night they went into the city and planted where things had disappeared. [415] A pharmacist's doorstep. [418] The bench where a teacher used to read his newspaper. [428] The gutter outside a print shop now painted over grey. [438] "Autumn is the right time," Tomas said, pressing seed into a crack with his thumb. [453] "Nobody watches the ground once the birds are gone. [462] Things sleep, and then in spring they are simply there, and everyone has to decide whether that is normal." [481] Ines understood then what the work was. [488] It was her notebook turned inside out: not recording the slow erasure, but seeding its opposite, patiently, in the same small places. [510]

On the last night of October she asked to plant the Varga doorway. [523] Before she went, she told Tomas about the notebook, about entry forty, about the Tuesday she had stood in the queue and said nothing. [547] She wanted him to forgive her. [553] He considered this the way he considered everything, by rule. [563] "I can't forgive you for what was done to them," he said. [575] "That isn't mine to give." [580] He wrapped the heel of that evening's loaf in a napkin and put it in her hand. [597] "But you can carry this, and it will keep a while." [608] It was not absolution, only its outline, thin as a crust. [619] She took it anyway. [623]

At the bakery she stopped beneath the municipal ribbon, now faded to the color of weak tea. [640] She had worn her grey registry coat, the one that made the patrols nod and look away, and she felt it on her shoulders like a lie she had been telling for years. [673] She uncloaked, let the coat drop onto the step, and knelt on it bare-armed in the cold. [690] Then she pressed rye and caraway into the seam between the threshold and the street, crumbling a little of the heel over them like an offering at a shrine. [719] The rest she ate slowly, kneeling there, and it tasted exactly as it always had, which was the holiest thing she could imagine. [742]

At home she opened the notebook and wrote entry one hundred and six. [755] Varga doorway, rye and caraway, planted. [761] Check in spring. [764] See if anyone thinks it normal. [770]
