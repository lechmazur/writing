The lighthouse prison stood off Graymouth on a reef like a broken jaw, and its lamp had been dark for three hours when Pell Harrow decided to leave his cell. [30] He did not hurry. [34] Nobody hurried for the son of the boy who cried wolf, and he had long ago stopped hurrying for anyone. [54] His father had been that boy, once, on the hills above town, and the sheep had died of it, and the town had never forgiven the name. [81] So when Pell rowed out that evening to tell Warden Hesk that a brig was running without lanterns toward the reef, Hesk had laughed, taken his oars, and locked him in the confession cell. [115] Then Hesk had climbed the stair and put out the light. [126] Pell had heard the brig strike an hour later, a sound like a barn door torn off in wind, and then the long silence of the sea keeping what it took. [157] By dawn the cutters from Graymouth would come to salvage, and before they found the bodies, Hesk would have his story ready: the liar's boy had broken the lamp. [186]

Pell sat now before the lattice. [192] It had come from the drowned chapel at Saint Brine's, a panel of oak worked into small diamonds, and the prison used it the way the priests had. [220] Words spoken through it did not fall. [227] They hovered on the far side in threads of pale copper, holding their shape until someone read them, and the old keepers said the lattice sustained impossibilities: a lie would hang there as sweetly as a truth, a promise to fly would float as long as a promise to repent. [277] It judged nothing. [280] It only kept. [283] That was why Hesk had fixed the lattice into the cell wall, so prisoners would confess into the corridor and he could harvest their sins at leisure. [310] But the lattice was only pinned by rusted pegs, and Pell's father had taught him one useful thing: that people believe what stays in front of them longest. [338] He worked the pegs loose and lifted the panel free. [348]

At the back of the cell, where the corridor lantern never reached, stood a narrow door that had been left ajar for decades, its hinges rusted half-open, too slim for a grown man's shoulders. [382] Pell was not a grown man's shape; he was a liar's son, thin from being fed last. [399] He ran his fingers over the wood and felt the scuffs, generations of them, where other prisoners had squeezed through into the walls: boot scrapes low, knuckle marks high, and between them a pale worn arrow some patient hand had rubbed toward the left. [443] Through the scuffs he went. [448]

The tower's inner walls were a labyrinth, built double by a mad engineer to stiffen the stone against storms, and the passages wound and forked and doubled back. [476] Wind shrieked in the dead ends. [482] Pell learned quickly to chase the hush instead, to follow whichever turning went quiet, because quiet meant the passage was sealed at the top by the lamp room and not open to the sea. [516] At every fork he held the lattice to his mouth and spoke. [528]

"I saw the brig at sundown." [534] "I rowed out to warn the warden." [541] "He took my oars." [545] "He climbed up and put the light out." [553] "I am going up to light it again." [561]

The words hung behind him in copper filaments, turning slowly, lighting the stone just enough to read by, and he left them there like thread in a maze. [589] His father had cried wolf into open air, where words scattered and only the shame of them stayed. [607] Pell was threading his own story through the dark, one plain sentence at a time, so that anyone who came after would have to walk inside it. [634]

The last passage opened beneath the lamp room, where Hesk slept in his chair beside a crate of salvage hooks. [654] Pell stepped past him softly, trimmed the wick, and struck a match. [666] The great lens caught and turned, too late for the brig, but not too late for the cutters. [684] Its beam swept down through the seams of the tower, and every word he had left in the labyrinth flared like wire in a forge. [709]

Then he sat on the top step with the lattice across his knees, in the warm copper hush, and did not shout, and did not run. [735] Let them wake Hesk. [739] Let them find the bodies. [744] They would find his story first, hanging where it could not blow away. [757]
