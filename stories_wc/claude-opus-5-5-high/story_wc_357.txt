On Pellum, the orchards grew on stone that refused to touch the ground. [13] Ten thousand arches floated over the mist, each one furred with apple and quince, and Ines Varga had flown between them for nineteen years in a skiff patched with fruit crates. [44] Now the blight was taking them. [50] Leaves curled black on the eastern arches first, then the southern, and the growers who stayed were saying the planet had simply grown tired. [74] Ines did not believe planets grew tired. [81] She believed in the Old Pear, the tree on Heron Arch that had survived four hundred Turns without losing a single branch. [103] Something kept it strong, and if she could find the source of that resilience, she could carry it to every dying grove. [125] Her only clue hung from a cord around her neck: a broken arrow tip of pale green stone, left to her by her grandmother Aurel. [150] Pressed into soil, the shard made seedlings leap a hand's width overnight. [162] Aurel had dug it from beneath the Old Pear's roots and spent her last years hunting the arch whose stone matched it. [184] Before she died she had said only, "Follow the water when it forgets which way is down." [201]

The water forgot once a year, when gravity inverted for an hour. [213] Growers called it the Turn and spent it strapped inside their huts, because nothing flew in the Turn. [231] Compasses spun, altimeters lied, and the arches themselves drifted like leaves on a pond. [245] Ines launched ten minutes before it began. [252]

When the Turn came, it came without a sound. [261] Her stomach lifted, the skiff rolled, and the mist became a ceiling above her. [275] Fallen pears rose off the arches in slow spirals, and every stream on Pellum peeled away from its bed and poured upward in glittering ropes. [300] The silence was enormous and it whirled, a quiet so total she could feel it turning around her like a slow wind. [322] Her compass needle spun uselessly, so she watched the water instead. [333] The ropes were not scattering. [338] They bent, all of them, toward a single point beyond the eastern arches, converging like threads pulled into a needle's eye. [359] Ines banked and followed the current, skimming beside the largest stream, reading its curve the way her grandmother had once read the moods of bees. [384] Where it braided with others she turned; where it thickened she climbed. [396]

Twenty minutes in, she saw it: an arch no chart recorded, hidden in the shadow of three larger ones, green as the shard at her throat. [422] The water poured into a hollow at its heart. [431] She tethered the skiff and pulled herself inside, hand over hand along the stone. [445]

Inside, the silence broke. [449] Voices filled the chamber, faint and layered: a child laughing on some far arch, a pruning saw, rain on leaves, a woman humming a harvest song Ines had not heard since she was six. [483] It was a gathering of echoes, every sound the orchards had made, drawn here by the curve of the stone and the pull of the water. [509] The walls trembled with them, and where they trembled, moss sprouted and unfurled while she watched. [525] The stone was not magic by itself, she realized. [534] It was fed. [537] Every voice of Pellum rang into it, and it rang back into the water, and the water carried that ringing home. [558]

In a crack near the floor she discovered the rest of an arrow, its shaft fletched in the first settlers' style, its point snapped away. [583] She held the green tip to the break, and it fit. [594] Someone had carved that point from this very wall and fired it here as a marker; when it snapped, the returning water must have carried the tip down to Heron Arch, where a young pear grew around a splinter of the chorus. [636] Ines listened again, harder. [640] The echoes from the east were thin, almost gone. [649] Those were the arches whose families had sold their hives, bought harvest drones, and stopped singing in the rows. [668] The blight had not come to Pellum because the planet was tired. [680] It had come where the orchards went quiet. [688] The chamber could only return what it was given. [697]

So Ines opened her mouth and sang Aurel's harvest song into the hollow, badly, loudly, until the stone shivered and green threads raced along the walls. [723] Then the Turn ended. [727] Gravity took her back, and outside the water fell from the sky in bright spirals, carrying her voice down toward the eastern arches. [750] She tucked the arrow tip against her heart and untied the skiff. [762] She had found the source of resilience, and it was not a stone she could carry. [778] It was a song she would have to teach. [787]
