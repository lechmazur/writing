The bees of Marrow Spring were the last thing in the Scald that still hummed, and Tamsin Hale had kept them alone for eleven years. [25] The oasis had once fed three towns with honey and wax, but the aquifer sank, the convoys left, and the white hive boxes stood in rows like abandoned headstones along the dry irrigation cuts. [59] Only the spring itself remained, a thread of water under a stand of tamarisk, enough for one woman and nine stubborn colonies. [81]

Three years ago the dead had become visible. [89] Travelers crossing the salt flats reported them everywhere, standing in doorways and on rooftops, silent as furniture, unable to speak or touch anything. [112] Tamsin had seen none. [116] She had assumed the dead, like the living, found nothing worth crossing the Scald for. [131]

So when she came out at dawn with her smoker and found a woman standing between the fourth and fifth hives, she dropped the smoker in the dust and did not pick it up. [165]

It was Maren. [168] Maren, who had left with the last convoy, who had never written, whose absence Tamsin had learned to carry the way the hives carried heat, without complaint and without relief. [198] Tamsin had not known she was dead. [205] Now she knew. [208]

The ghost did not wave or weep. [215] She only turned her head, slowly, and let her gaze settle on the honey house. [230]

Tamsin had lived too long in quiet to demand explanations from anything, bees or weather or the dead. [248] She had learned instead to read what things did: the pitch of a hive before swarming, the color of the horizon before a dust wall. [273] She followed the gaze. [277]

In the honey house, on the shelf above the extractor, sat the stained glass box. [292] Maren had made it the winter before she left, soldering amber and cobalt panes into a small locked casket, and had set it there without a word. [319] Tamsin had dusted it for eleven years and never opened it, because the key had gone with Maren. [337]

She lifted it into the window light and looked, really looked, as she would at comb. [353] Each pane held a scene in miniature. [360] On the first, two women knelt before a hive, one hand each upon its lid: the old custom of telling the bees, which Tamsin and Maren had done the summer they promised to stay at the spring together. [398] On the second, an old man lay in a bed, and three small girls stood at its foot. [416] Maren's father had died that winter, Tamsin remembered, and Maren's sisters had been children. [430] On the third pane, a truck crossed white ground, and the same three girls sat in its bed, older now, alive. [451] The fourth pane was clear glass, and behind it, pressed flat, lay a sheaf of folded paper. [468]

Tamsin sat down on the extractor step. [475] She understood now what the panes said without saying it: two vows, one to her and one to a dying man, knotted so tightly that keeping either meant breaking the other. [506] Maren had chosen the sisters, and she had left the choosing here, sealed in glass, rather than speak it aloud and make Tamsin forgive her to her face. [534] The papers would be the rest of it, every word Maren had not been able to say. [551]

A chisel lay within reach. [556]

Tamsin looked at it, then out the doorway. [564] The ghost had not moved, but her gaze had shifted from the honey house to the hive with the cracked lid, the one they had knelt before. [591]

Not the box, then. [595] Maren had not come back to be read. [603]

Words spoken are spent, Tamsin thought; words kept keep their weight, and Maren had carried hers across the whole Scald and past the end of her life to set them down whole. [635] Breaking the glass would turn a confession into mere information. [645] She left the chisel where it lay. [652]

She carried the box out into the heat and set it on the cracked lid, and she knocked three times, the way her grandmother had taught her. [679] Bees must be told of a death, or they leave. [689]

"Maren is gone," she told them, and nothing else. [698]

The hive hummed lower, a sound like a held breath. [708] The sun climbed, and light fell through the panes, printing the kneeling women, the dying man, and the truck in amber and cobalt across the white wood, while the clear square cast only the shadow of its folded secret. [747]

Maren watched the colors for a long time. [755] Then she looked at Tamsin, and Tamsin looked back, and between them passed everything neither would ever say, heavy and intact, humming. [777]
