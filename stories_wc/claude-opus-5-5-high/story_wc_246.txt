The Kettle pulled at everything a little sideways, so that bread rose crooked and rain fell toward the east. [19] Ossa Venn had lived at its rim for eleven years, interpreting the signals that fell into the anomaly from the listening buoys beyond it. [43] Light arrived there bent, and messages arrived out of order, sometimes days before they were sent. [59] Her job was to unbend them. [65]

Last spring she had unbent one badly. [72] The survey buoy had sent a fragment about soil chemistry, and Ossa, reading its pulses against the ones that came before, told the settlement to sow the red seed instead of the gold. [105] The farmers trusted her; they always had. [112] Three weeks later a delayed pulse arrived, the one that should have come first, and it reversed everything. [130] Gold, it said. [133] By then the red was in the ground, and what came up was not the squat grain anyone expected but tall, glassy stalks that leaned toward the center of the Kettle and hummed when the wind moved through them. [172]

Now it was autumn, and the council wanted the record changed. [183] Mayor Tull came to her tower with a lamp and a careful face. [196] "The log is yours," he said. [202] "Put the second pulse first, where it belongs, and the error becomes the buoy's, not ours." [218] "People will stop looking at you in the market." [227]

Ossa had noticed the looking. [232] She had also noticed that the children slept better since the stalks began to hum. [247]

After Tull left, she took out her mother's fountain pen. [257] It had been dry for years; ink would not stay in it this close to the anomaly, where it crept up the barrel and pooled against gravity's wrong direction. [286] Its nib was a sliver of meteor glass, split twice instead of once, so that a line drawn with it forked into three. [309] Her mother had used it to annotate star charts, scratching grooves into the wax log so that later readers could see every path a signal might have taken. [337] "Meaning is contingent," she used to say. [344] "A pulse means nothing alone." [349] "It means something next to its neighbors, and something else beside different ones." [362]

Ossa opened the wax log to spring. [369] Her own annotations were there, single lines, confident. [377] She could smooth them with a warm blade and cut new ones, and no one would know the order had ever been different. [400]

Instead she walked down to the red field. [408] The stalks chimed around her, bowed toward the Kettle's heart. [418] She cut an armful and carried it to the old threshing floor, and there, alone, she beat them with a flail until the husks split. [443] She did not know what she was looking for. [452] She only knew she could not edit a season she had not yet understood. [466]

The kernels that fell were small and dark and heavy, heavier than grain should be. [481] When she dropped one, it did not roll sideways toward the east like everything else in the settlement. [499] It fell straight down. [503]

Ossa sat on the floor with the kernel in her palm and thought about contingency. [518] The red seed had been wrong for the soil the buoy described. [530] But the soil was only one neighbor. [537] Beside the anomaly, beside the bent wind and the crooked bread, the wrong seed had grown into something that held its own center, a plant that stood where it was put. [568] Whether that made it a mistake or a gift depended on what came next, and nothing had come next yet. [588]

That night she returned to the tower and found that the pen's nib had bent in her pocket, one tine curled against the others. [612] She unbent it slowly with her thumbnail, feeling the strange glass resist and then give. [627] Then she opened the log beside her spring entry and drew with the dry nib, pressing hard. [644] The groove forked into three. [649] Along the first path she wrote the order the pulses had arrived. [661] Along the second, the order they had been sent. [670] Along the third she wrote: red sown in error, harvest falls true, meaning pending. [684]

It was not the history Tull wanted. [691] It was an edit that kept every version standing, and she was certain of it in the way she was certain of nothing else: certain that she did not know, and that the not knowing belonged in the record. [730]

In the morning she carried the kernels to the market, set them on a table in front of everyone who had been watching her, and told them plainly what she did not understand. [763] A child picked one up, dropped it, and laughed when it landed exactly where she aimed. [779] Above them the stalks hummed on, leaning toward the center, patient as a question. [793]
