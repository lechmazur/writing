Tamsin Vell sorted seeds the way other people prayed, with her whole attention and no hurry. [16] On the Ashen Arbor, which drifted above the cloud-sea like a grey lantern on a string of wind, someone had to. [37] The trees were old ash, pale as hearth-leavings, their roots knotted through a floating island no bigger than a village green, and every seed they dropped went into her cabinet of four hundred labeled drawers. [72] Plump, thin, speckled, sound, hollow: each had its place. [81] But this year the leaves had fallen early and brittle, and the seeds inside the drawers had begun to rattle like dry teeth. [104]

Everyone on the Arbor knew the old saying. [112] Once a year the wind stopped for a single hour, the island hung between the mountain it had broken from and the horizon it was fated to reach, and in that Hush past met prophecy. [147] The leaves that fell during the Hush carried a message from the trees, and whatever went into the Hush emerged transformed, always, without exception. [171]

Tamsin's grandmother Mab had been the last person who could read them. [183] Mab had also spent her evenings building tiny monuments to forgotten things: a pebble cairn for a lost button, a twig arch for a song nobody sang anymore, a thimble shrine for the name of a cow. [220] Tamsin had loved her and found the habit dreadfully untidy. [230] Mab had been buried under the eldest ash in spring, and now the Hush was coming with no one to listen. [251]

So Tamsin prepared the way she prepared for everything. [260] She swept the grove bare, laid out a grid of chalk squares, and sharpened three pencils. [276] When the wind died and the whole island seemed to hold its breath, the leaves began to fall, and she recorded each one: position, angle, color of vein. [304]

They meant nothing. [307] They landed at random, drifted off her squares, and crumbled when she prodded them with her pencil to check their orientation. [328] Half the hour was gone. [333]

Then she noticed that not all of them were crumbling. [343] At the edge of the grove, where she had not swept, a small heap of leaves lay green and soft as moss atop one of Mab's old monuments, the thimble shrine for the cow. [377] Another cluster glowed on the button cairn. [384] The leaves were not falling at random at all; they were falling on remembrance. [398]

Tamsin's heart gave an unruly thump. [404] What had the Arbor forgotten? [409]

She thought of the drawer she never opened, number four hundred, labeled IRREGULAR, where she swept the seeds that fit nowhere. [430] She ran for the cabinet and hauled the drawer out into the silver stillness. [444] Inside, among lopsided seeds and lichen-furred husks, something winked: a speck of moonstone cabochon dust, a bluish mote no bigger than a pollen grain. [468] She knew it at once: Mab's ring, its moonstone cabochon worn soft by sixty years of sorting, had shed flecks into every drawer she ever touched. [494] This one had settled among the seeds Tamsin had judged too strange to keep. [508] Mab had never thrown those out, Tamsin realized; she had planted them, a few each Hush, and the old ash had kept its leaves. [532]

With minutes left, Tamsin did the most disorderly thing of her life. [544] She built a monument. [548] It was crooked, three pebbles and a curl of bark over a hollow scraped in the moss, and into the hollow she pressed the irregular seeds and, last, the moonstone speck, for Mab, whom she had been trying very hard not to remember. [591]

The leaves came down all at once, a soft green avalanche, and settled over her cairn in a shape she could finally read. [614] It was not a word. [619] It was a spiral, the one Mab used to trace on Tamsin's palm whenever she whispered, Keep what does not fit. [640]

The wind returned, and the island moved on toward its horizon. [651] By the next morning the monument had sprouted a tuft of moss-velvet seedlings no one on the Arbor had a name for, and the ash boughs overhead were budding silver. [681] Tamsin emerged transformed too, of course; everything does. [689] She still sorted seeds with her whole attention, but that evening she relabeled drawer four hundred in her neat hand: FOR PLANTING. [711] And beside the cabinet, where anyone might trip on it, she began a second small cairn, for the next forgotten thing, whatever it turned out to be. [738]
