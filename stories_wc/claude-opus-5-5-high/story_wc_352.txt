Ilse Varo had kept the Coil for eleven years, and in all that time she had never read a page only once. [22] Her mind worked like a prism; a line of ink entered whole and left as seven readings, each bent at its own angle, and she had learned to trust the colors that disagreed. [55]

The Coil was a serpentine grotto beneath Harrow Ford, a limestone throat that wound downward through nine bends, each shelved with the founders' ledgers. [79] Mist lived there the way dust lives in attics, pale and patient, beading on vellum until the archive smelled of wet stone and old promises. [104] At the bottom of the ninth bend stood the Vigil Doors, three brass wheels set into rock and sealed since the founders died. [127] Nobody remembered the combinations. [131] Nobody, the council insisted, needed to; whatever lay behind them was a century cold. [145]

Ilse disagreed because of a doodle. [151] In the margin of the first ledger, beside a dull column of grain tallies, someone had inked seven seeds curling in a spiral. [174] By lamplight it was nothing. [179] But one night she had shuttered her lantern to rest her eyes, and the seeds had glowed, faint and clandestinely luminous, as though the ink remembered a fire it had once been mixed with. [213] Beneath them, too small to notice in light, ran a line of script: We plant the seeds of vigilance; look up when the world does. [238]

In Harrow Ford the world looked up once a year. [248] For a single hour after the autumn equinox, gravity inverted, and townsfolk lashed themselves to their floors while rain fell toward the clouds. [271]

Last year Ilse had gone into the Coil alone and hung by a rope in the third bend, watching the mist pour upward and pool against the ceiling like milk in an upturned bowl. [305] She had seen nothing, and she had come out bruised and certain she had missed something simple. [322]

The answer arrived in winter, while she watched frost on her windowpane. [334] For hours the glass stayed bare, and then the cold crossed some invisible line and ferns of ice flowered everywhere at once. [356] Threshold effects. [358] The seeds would not wake by degrees; they needed the mist to thin past a certain point, and mist thinned only where the grotto pinched narrow, at the arches between chambers. [389] Passing through once would stir it; passing out and back would tear it. [402]

So when the hour came again, Ilse did not simply enter; she meant to reenter every threshold, crossing each arch and returning, so her body would drag the mist thin behind her. [434] She roped herself to the iron rings, shuttered her lantern, and waited in the dark. [449]

The ground let go. [453] Her stomach turned over, the ledgers strained against their straps, and the mist rose like a slow tide toward the ceiling. [474] Ilse climbed hand over hand along the rope, crossed the first arch, turned, and crossed it back. [491]

Above her, a point of amber opened in the stone. [501] Real seeds, pressed into the ceiling a century ago and glazed with the same ink as the doodle, glowed in the shape of a numeral. [526] Bend by bend she crossed and recrossed, and at every arch the mist tore and the ceiling kindled, until she held nine numerals in her head and the Vigil Doors hung above her like a hatch into stone sky. [565]

Her prism-mind would not take them plainly. [572] She tried them in order, then reversed, then in the order the doodle's spiral curled, from the outside in, and on the third reading the brass wheels gave with a sound like a held breath let out. [609]

There was no treasure inside, only a ledger and a clay jar of seeds. [623] She read the first page by the ceiling's ember glow. [633] Harrow Ford was built above a peat seam that caught fire in the founders' youth and never fully went out. [653] It smoldered still, the ledger said, breathing through vents the founders had kept open and watched, and whoever forgot to watch would one day wake to the whole valley burning. [683] The seeds were for planting at each vent; the vines that grew from them wilted at the first rising heat. [703]

Ilse thought of the council's new road, and of the vents it had paved over in spring. [720]

Then gravity returned, and she fell with the jar clutched to her chest, and in the settling mist she caught, beneath the smell of stone, the faint sweet breath of smoke. [751] She began to climb, already counting vents. [758]
