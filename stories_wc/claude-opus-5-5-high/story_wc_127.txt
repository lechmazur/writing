Ines Varro arrived at the amber grotto at dusk, when the low sun came through the cliff seams and lit the resin walls like the inside of a honey jar. [30] She was late by four minutes, which she noted in her ledger, because she was professionally thorough and did not forgive herself small things. [54] Olun Marr was already waist-deep in the wall. [62] The old fragrance chemist had chosen the grotto the way some men choose a chair to die in, and the resin had accepted him slowly, rising over his hips like warm syrup and hardening to glass behind it. [100] People of the coast did this when they were finished; the grotto kept them, and pilgrims came to stand before the preserved faces and feel whatever the faces had felt at the end. [133]

"You came," he said. [137]

"You asked," Ines said, and set down her case. [146] Inside, in velvet slots, were the vials she sold in the night markets of three cities: Grief, Lightly Aged; First Love, Coastal; Pride of a Mother, Restrained. [173] None of them were real. [178] They were accords of scent and suggestion, built from Olun's formulas, and customers inhaled them and wept on cue and paid well. [200] She had never pretended otherwise, at least not to herself. [210]

"I want you to take my last one," Olun said. [220] "A true one, for once. [225] Something you don't have to fake." [231]

She took out a blotter petal, one of his own: a thin tongue of absorbent paper cut in the shape of a lily petal, the kind he had dipped ten thousand times into trial essences to smell how they would open on skin. [274] He had taught her that scent was only half the work. [285] The other half was charge. [290] She drew the petal briskly along the amber beside his shoulder. [301] The resin gave up its static with a faint crackle, and the fine hairs on her wrist lifted. [319] Then she held the charged paper an inch from his throat, where the feeling would rise, and read him the way he had shown her: not by smell but by the tiny pull and prickle of the field against her fingertips. [360]

"Tell me what you feel," she said. [367]

"Peace," said Olun. [370] "I'm not afraid. [373] I've had a full life, and I'm ready." [381]

The petal tugged toward him, steady and warm, and then jittered, a quick sour snap like a spark off a wool sleeve. [403] Half of it was true. [408] The full life was true; the readiness was true. [417] Not afraid was a lie, and not a small one. [427] Ines had spent twenty years selling half truths, and she recognized the shape of one the way a jeweler recognizes paste. [448]

Her instinct, the thorough one, was to correct the record. [458] She could isolate the fear, name it, bottle the peace alone as the genuine article, or bottle the fear and call it honesty. [481] Either would be accurate. [485] Either would be a counterfeit. [490]

The amber had reached his collarbones. [496] His breath was shortening against it, and the light through the walls had gone from gold to the color of strong tea. [518]

"Is it any good?" he asked. [524]

She understood then what her vials had always lacked. [533] She had insight enough to see through anyone; what she had never included was the reason a person lies to comfort the one listening. [557] He was afraid, and he was lying so that she would not have to be. [572] That was the whole feeling, and it needed both halves to exist. [584]

"It's very good," she said, which was entirely true. [593]

She let the petal subsume the whole of it, the calm and the tremor braided together, and held it there while the resin closed over his chin, his smile, his eyes still open and fond. [628] As the transformation completed, the static bled out of the paper with a last soft tick, and the grotto was quiet. [649]

Ines sealed the petal in glass. [655] She wrote the label carefully, checked the spelling twice, and logged the time. [668]

Courage, Offered. [670] Not for sale. [673]

Then she stood a while before the new face in the wall, as pilgrims did, and felt precisely what he had felt, and did not pretend otherwise. [700]
