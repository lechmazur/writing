Wren's father built bunkers, and Wren could not stay inside one for longer than eleven breaths. [16] She had counted. [19] In the concrete throat beneath their house, the air pressed on her like a thumb, and the silence there tasted of rust and old pennies, a flat metal hush that sat on her tongue until she clawed up the ladder gasping. [60] Her father, Tomas, never scolded. [65] He only said, "Shelter works by limiting exposure, love, and so does fear." [78] "Twelve breaths tomorrow, then thirteen." [83] But the storm season was coming, the kind that stripped roofs from the valley and flung them into the next county, and twelve breaths would not be enough. [111]

So Wren collected silences instead. [116] She had begun as a small girl, tasting the quiet of the hayloft (dust and clover), the hush of the church after the last hymn (candle smoke, a little salt), the silence between her parents the winter her mother left (bitter, like chewed aspirin). [160] She believed that somewhere there was a silence that did not taste like a lid, and that if she found it, she could carry it underground. [186]

She kept them in a tiny porcelain doll no longer than her thumb, the last thing her mother had painted, hollow inside, its head a stopper. [212] Silence caught that way was intangibly tangible: it weighed nothing, yet the doll grew cool and heavy in her palm with each one, and when she twisted the head she could taste the old quiet leak out. [249] Her father's rule applied here too. [255] Open it for a breath and the silence stayed pure; leave it open and the world rushed in and spoiled it. [276]

The rarest silence lived on the trellis. [283] It rose from the ridge behind the village, a lattice of pale living vine older than any map, climbing past the pines, past the hawks, until it pierced the cloud floor and spread out above it like an open hand. [323] The trellis was xenial, the old women said, a host to anyone who climbed it: it curled footholds toward strangers, offered dew in its leaf-cups, and knotted itself into benches where a guest might rest. [358] Once a year, on the solstice, gravity inverted for an hour, and the vine became the only thing keeping climbers from falling up into the blue. [384] In that hour, the old women said, the sun struck the clouds from beneath and the whole sky went gilded and hushed, and nothing made a sound because nothing weighed enough to. [416]

Wren climbed the day before the storms. [423] The trellis greeted her, tendrils looping her wrists like a cousin taking her hands, and she rose through the white wet of the cloud into a brightness so wide she forgot to count anything at all. [459]

Then the world turned over. [464] Her stomach lifted. [467] Her hair rose. [470] The clouds were suddenly a ceiling and the endless blue a floor she was falling toward, and the vine cinched gently around her ankles and held. [496] She hung upside down over infinity, and for once the enormity did not rescue her; it squeezed. [513] Too much sky was its own small room. [521]

Then the light came. [525] Sun poured up through the clouds, gilding every leaf, every bead of dew, the fine hairs on her arms, and the hush arrived: not empty, but full, the way a held breath is full. [559] It tasted of honey and warm bread and something like her mother's laugh. [572] It tasted like being inside and outside at once. [581] Wren twisted the doll's head, counted one breath, and sealed it. [592] When gravity righted itself she climbed down with the gilded hush pressed cold against her heart. [608]

The storm came that night. [613] Tomas held the hatch open, rain lashing his face, and did not ask her to hurry. [629] Wren went down. [632] Concrete. [633] Iron air. [635] The thumb pressing. [638] Eight breaths, nine, and she unstopped the doll. [646]

The hush poured out, and like anything carried down from that hospitable vine, it began to sprout. [663] Pale tendrils of quiet unfurled along the walls, soft as light, and wherever they climbed the concrete seemed to thin, until the ceiling felt less like a lid than like the underside of a cloud. [698] Her father stared, then touched a tendril; his fingers passed through it, and still he flinched, as if something had squeezed back. [720]

Above them the storm tore at the valley. [728] Below, Wren breathed, and stopped counting. [734] Her father sat beside her in the golden quiet, and for the first time since her mother left, their silence tasted the same. [757]
