Oriel Vask had kept the ripple-mark maps of the harmonic caves for thirty-one years, and in all that time the sand had never lied to her. [26] The old river wrote its patterns across the cavern floors, ridge after patient ridge, and she copied them onto vellum so the miners above would know where water ran, where rock was thin, where the stone would sing if a careless wind got in. [70]

Then the energy beings arrived, and the caves began to sing without stopping. [83]

They had come down through the chimney shafts in early winter, drifting coils of pale light that nobody could touch, and settled in the deep galleries as if they had been searching for them. [117] Wherever they moved, the limestone hummed. [123] The hum became a drone, the drone a roar, and by midwinter the whole mountain rang like a struck bell that refused to fade. [147]

Sand danced in the tunnels. [152] Her ridges smeared and reformed every hour, and her maps, her mother's maps, and her grandmother's maps became records of a floor that no longer existed. [178] Up in the village, children woke screaming from dreams of bells, and two ceilings had already come down in the eastern drifts. [200]

Oriel did not want to drive the lights away. [209] She wanted, simply and stubbornly, to find the quiet in the storm, because every flood her family had ever charted had left a still pool somewhere, and a ripple-mark mapkeeper who could not find it was only a woman holding paper. [250]

The sand could not help her now, but the lake could. [261] The beings fed on warmth, and the underground lake in the Cistern Hall had frozen solid for the first time in living memory. [284] She walked out onto it with a lantern and a chalk line and knelt, examining crack patterns in the ice the way her mother had taught her to read the grain of a riverbed. [318]

The song had written itself there. [324] Every tremor of the mountain had split the surface along the lines where sound pressed hardest, and the fractures ran in spirals and petals, a vast white flower etched from shore to shore. [357] She mapped it for two days, blowing on her fingers, following each crack to its end. [373] And at the center, a stride from where the old spring used to rise, she found a disk of ice the size of a cart wheel that no line had touched. [404]

A node. [406] The one place in the mountain where the ringing canceled itself out. [418]

A still point is only a seed, though. [426] To make it grow she needed the echo chamber stone. [436] The guild had kept it for generations, a hollow geode the size of a fist, its inner crystals tuned so finely that any sound spoken into it came back reversed, crest for trough. [469] Mapkeepers had once used it to silence rockfalls before they happened. [480] No one had used it in a century, because using it meant standing inside the noise long enough to answer it. [501]

Oriel set the stone on the untouched ice, took up her map-hammer, and struck it once. [517]

The roar of the Cistern Hall poured into the geode. [527] She struck again, and again, each blow timed to the mountain's pulse as she read it in the ice, and with each strike she felt the reversed wave climb back out and meet the song head-on. [563] Her arms burned. [566] Her ears bled a little. [571] She kept the rhythm the way she kept a survey line, without hurry and without mercy. [587]

Then she conjured it: the bloom of hush. [595]

Silence opened from the stone like a flower unfolding, petal over petal, following the very crack lines she had charted, spreading outward across the ice and up the walls and into the galleries beyond. [629] The mountain exhaled. [632]

Oriel set down the hammer and waited for the beings to flee or rage. [646]

Instead they came to her. [651] One by one the coils of light drifted down from the vaults and settled into the hush, and as they settled their glare softened until they were only gently luminous, like embers banked for the night. [687] She understood then that they had not come to sing. [697] They had fallen through some greater storm of their own, and had been ringing in the dark all winter because they could not find its center either. [724]

Oriel sat on the cold ice among them, hands aching, the stone warm beside her knee. [740] In the morning she would begin a new map, one that marked where the quiet lived. [756] For now she rested, the way iron rests after the last blow, shaped and still. [771]
