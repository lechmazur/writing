When the aurora came down over Port Salvane, it came down green and wrong, and every screen in the harbor went white. [22] The cranes froze mid-reach. [26] Radios hissed like frying fat. [31] Down in the Stack, the forty-high labyrinth of shipping containers that the port had grown instead of warehouses, Ines Varga set her coffee on a twistlock and listened. [59] She had maintained its alleys for eleven years, oiling hinges, repainting lane codes, chasing gulls out of open reefers. [78] The GPS tags on every box were useless now, and the geomagnetic storm, the forecasters said, would last until dawn. [98] Somewhere inside, a new hire named Teo Brask had gone to check a seal on row ninety and not come out. [119] When power returned, the automated stackers would resume their night shuffle, and a lost man in a moving maze was a man under forty tons. [144]

Foreman Dario Kett arrived with a bullhorn. [151] He climbed the ladder of a blue reefer and roared Teo's name, and the Stack roared it back from forty directions at once, every steel wall a mouth. [179] Nobody could tell where the voice came from, so nobody could tell where to answer. [194]

"He'll hear that and walk toward the echo," Ines said. [204] "Straight into the stacker lanes." [209]

Dario lowered the horn. [213] "You have something quieter?" [217]

"I have something that carries." [222]

He laughed, dug in his pocket, and flipped her the coin he always flipped, a brass zodiac piece stamped with a bull. [244] "Taurus, loud and stubborn, like me." [250] "Find him before I do and it's yours, along with the argument you keep losing at lunch." [267]

The argument was old: Ines said whispers traveled farther than shouts, and Dario said that was the kind of thing people who whispered told themselves. [292] She accepted the challenge the way she did everything, as if it had just occurred to her, though her jacket pockets were already stiff with preparation. [318] Every storm season she hand-drew the Stack on butcher paper and folded each map so that only one junction showed at a time, a route revealed crease by crease, useless to anyone who did not know where to start unfolding. [358]

The dockhands had gathered at the gate in the green light, and she sorted them without looking up, by the sound of their boots. [382] She knew every walk in the port. [389] Marguerite rolled heel to toe and never hurried; she got row thirty. [401] Big Sione shuffled but never lost count of turns; row sixty. [412] The twins, who walked in step even when they were fighting, took the long east alley together. [429]

"Go to your last fold," she told them softly. [438] "Put your ear to the steel, and tell the next one what you hear." [452]

Then she took out her key. [458] It was an old iron thing, found in a scrapped container a decade ago, and it fit no lock in the port; she had tried them all. [485] What it did fit was her hand, and its heavy bit made a clean, bright note against corrugated steel. [504] Air scattered a shout; a stack of locked boxes, corner kissing corner, carried a tap for a quarter mile without losing its direction. [527]

She pressed her cheek to a wall and tapped three times, slow. [539] Minutes later Marguerite's whisper came back along the chain of dockhands: a scraping answer, deep in row ninety-two. [557] Then Sione, lower: footsteps on a container roof, uneven, left boot dragging. [569] Ines smiled, because Teo had twisted that ankle on his second day and never reported it, and she had noticed on his third. [592] She tapped the pattern she taught every trainee and assumed none remembered: stay still, stay still, stay still. [610]

The dragging stopped. [613]

The twins reached him at twelve past two, sitting cross-legged on a reefer roof with his palm flat against the steel, feeling her taps like a pulse. [640] They walked him out by the creases of their maps, and at two-forty the aurora thinned, the power surged, and the stackers woke and began rearranging row ninety in the dark. [671]

Dario stood at the gate with his silent bullhorn and watched them come. [684]

"He heard me, though," Dario said. [690]

"He heard you everywhere," Ines said, "which is the same as nowhere." [702]

She pressed the zodiac coin into Teo's shaking palm, for luck he had not needed, and handed Dario a folded map instead. [724] "Start at the corner," she said, already walking away as if the thought had only just arrived, "and unfold it quietly." [745]
