Since the fog turned solid, nobody had climbed to the observatory, so Ilse Varro kept the stairs swept for no one. [21] Below the dome, where the valley had once breathed white each dawn, the cloud had set like poured marble, a plain of pale stone that swallowed the towns and the road and the river's sound. [56] Only the storms still reached her, rolling across the hardened cloud and breaking against the brass. [72]

She was a summer-quill archivist, the last of the order that wrote only with feathers shed by swifts in July, because summer ink was said to remember warmth after the page forgot it. [105] She had three quills left. [110] No swifts had come since the fog closed. [118]

Ilse missed things before they ended; it was her flaw and her discipline. [131] She had grieved the swifts while they were still nesting in the eaves, grieved each quill as she cut its nib, and now, each time rain fell, she grieved the forecourt before it had even begun to speak. [169]

The forecourt was crystal, laid by the founders in hexagonal slabs around five granite monuments. [184] After rain, water settled into its facets and the crystal whispered back every voice that had ever crossed it: bakers bargaining, children counting steps, a man proposing and a woman laughing yes. [216] The valley folk had climbed here for festivals and eclipses for four hundred years, and the stone had kept them all. [237] It was the town's memory now, the only one left, and each storm it spoke a little more softly. [256]

Ilse's task was to fix those voices to paper, but voices without hours were only noise. [272] So she dated them by the shadows. [279] When the crystal whispered, the old light came with the sound, and each monument cast not its present shadow but the one it had thrown at the moment of speaking. [309] She lay on the wet slabs with a knotted cord, measuring from the base of the Cartographer's statue to the tip of its ghostly shade, and from length and bearing she worked out the sun's height, the season, the hour. [349] A long shadow pointing east meant a winter evening, lanterns, someone's cold hands. [362] A stub of shade at the statue's feet meant midsummer noon. [373]

Tonight lightning split the dark every few breaths, and in one white flash she heard a girl's voice near the Lamplighter monument say, I will remember this, I promise, even when I'm old. [406]

Ilse went still. [409] She knew that voice the way one knows a stair in the dark. [422]

She stretched the cord. [426] The Lamplighter's remembered shadow was short and leaned north: late June, near noon, in a year before the flood-mark was carved into the plinth. [450] That was the week the order took its apprentices, choosing one valley child each midsummer and washing the rest of her life out of her with vows and silence. [479] Ilse had always believed she came to the tower as an infant, belonging to nowhere but the archive. [497]

Now she conjectured otherwise, carefully, as she would any undated page. [508] If the girl was nine or ten, and the voice was hers, then she had once had a family down there beneath the stone, a street, a name before this one. [539]

Around her the forecourt murmured on, fainter than last storm, and she understood that she had been recording the town as though she stood outside it. [565] She had not. [568] Her own promise was in the collective memory, one thread in the weave, as ordinary as the bakers. [586]

For thirty years she had tried to be the archive's instrument, a self made of other people's hours. [604] But a self, she thought, was not given by vows; it was carved, slowly, out of what one had actually lived, the way the founders had cut these monuments from raw granite. [636] She had lived that June. [641] She had lived every storm since. [647]

She knelt and uncapped her ink. [653] Already she felt the ache of missing this night, the cord cold in her fist, the girl's voice thinning, and for once she let the ache instruct her rather than stop her. [685] With the second-to-last summer quill she began a page that belonged to no one else: late June, near noon, the Lamplighter, a girl who promised. [710] The last quill she set aside for whatever name she might find if the stone below ever softened. [728]

The storm moved off. [732] The crystal drank the last of the rain, and the voices sank one by one into the facets, until only the hush remained, deep and listening, and Ilse sat inside it, keeping her promise. [766]
