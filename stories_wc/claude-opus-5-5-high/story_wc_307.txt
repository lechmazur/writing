The sycamores on the floating rock never rustled, even when the wind came up from the valley, and Oren had long ago stopped asking why. [25] It was the grotto beneath them, humming its iron song, holding the platform in the air and every leaf in a kind of held breath. [50] Tonight the stars were out, and under starlight the magnetic grotto grew strong enough to lift the fine hairs on his arms. [72]

Dr. Ilse Varga sat cross-legged at the edge of the meditation pond, her bare feet not quite reaching the water. [92] They used to reach, Oren thought. [98] When he had first climbed the rope ladder at eleven years old, she had been a stooped woman of ninety with a cane of sycamore wood and a laugh like gravel. [129] Now he was sixty-one, and she looked about nine. [138]

"Throw it," she said, and handed him a smooth black stone. [149] "Then go under." [152]

This was her method, the one she had spent four decades refining while her hair darkened and her spine unbent. [172] The pond water was thick with dissolved iron from the grotto, and when a stone broke its surface, the rings did not spread evenly. [196] They bent around the field lines, crowded here, stretched there, carrying a record of every pull beneath them. [214] A waking eye saw only ripples. [220] A mind slowed by trance could read them like a page. [231]

"What am I measuring?" he asked, though he had asked a hundred times. [244]

"The grotto," she said, as she always said. [252] "How the field changes when something inside it changes." [261]

She wove truth carefully, he had always thought, the way a spider did: every strand real, the center always hidden. [281]

He threw the stone. [285] It struck with a sound like a closing book, and he let his breath fall to the bottom of his lungs and stay there. [309]

The trance came the way dusk comes under sycamores, not falling but gathering. [322] The rings slowed. [325] He watched them widen, pinch, and bloom, and the pattern resolved into a shape he knew because he had drawn it in his notebooks for fifty years: the grotto's field, its familiar knots and loops. [360] But there was a second pattern inside the first, small and faint, moving against it. [375]

Through the trance he followed it, ring by ring, back through the years of records he had taken without understanding. [395] It had been large once, a heavy iron presence woven into the field. [408] Every season it had grown smaller. [414] It was not the grotto changing. [420] It was the thing inside it. [426]

It was her. [429]

The iron in her blood had been writing her into the water every night he threw a stone, and every night there had been less of her to write. [458] He could see where the line was going. [466] He could see, in the last narrow ring, how little of it was left. [480]

He surfaced slowly, because she had taught him never to break a trance in haste. [495] The pond was flat again. [500] A sycamore seed spun down through the hush and landed without a ripple. [513]

"How long have you known?" he asked. [520]

"Since before you could read the rings," she said. [529] Her voice was high and thin now, a child's voice, but the pauses in it were still ninety years old. [549]

"Why did you withhold it?" [554]

"Because a thing told is a thing you only hear." [564] She drew her knees up to her chin. [572] "When I was old, I watched my friends go, one by one, and each time it was like a door closing in another house." [596] "No sound, no shape, just absence." [602] "I wanted to teach vanishing to appear." [609] "I wanted my leaving to have a shape you could hold, one you found yourself, so you would believe it." [629]

Oren looked at his notebooks, stacked on the rock beside him, fifty years of careful ink. [645] He had thought he was mapping a grotto. [653] He had been drawing her portrait, line by line, as it faded. [665]

"And when the rings show nothing?" he asked. [673]

She picked up another stone and put it in his hand, closing his old fingers over it with her small ones. [694] "Then you will see exactly where I was," she said. [704] "An absence with edges is not nothing." [711]

Above them the stars turned, and below them the grotto held its iron breath, and the sycamores kept their silence as he threw the stone and went under again, to learn her while she could still be read. [749]
