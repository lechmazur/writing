The library had been dying for forty years, and the studio had paid to keep it that way. [18] Its iron shelves had rusted into orange ribs, and the dome had fallen in long ago, so the stars looked straight down on Mara Venn as she stood at the base of the reading-room ladder with her hands shaking. [57] She was afraid of heights, of the dark, of rust in cuts, of narrow places, and of the moment before a fall when the body understands what the mind has agreed to. [89] For eleven years she had been the stunt double for Lena Cross anyway. [102]

Three days ago she had learned there was no Lena Cross. [113] There was only a licensed face, a digital mask the studio laid over Mara's body in every frame: her climbs, her tears, her trembling mouth. [138] Audiences adored Lena's famous fragility, the way her fingers quivered on a ledge before she found the courage to reach. [158] Those were Mara's fingers. [162] She had never been the double; she had been the original, hidden so well she had believed the lie herself. [182]

Dex had arranged it. [186] Dex, who had pulled her out of a failed audition at nineteen and taught her to climb by talking her up every rung. [209] Dex, whose voice lived in the whispering seashell half she wore clipped behind her ear, a cream-colored earpiece he had carved himself so the cameras would read it as jewelry. [239] Tonight it hissed with his breathing. [245] "Face down on the climb, kid," the shell murmured. [254] "Keep it in shadow like always." [260]

She did not know why tonight mattered until Pip found her by the card catalog. [275] Pip was Dex's daughter, eight years old, with a plaster cast on her left arm from a fall off the craft truck. [297] Half the crew had signed it. [303] Near the wrist, in purple marker, were rows of backward letters, the mirror code Mara had taught her during long waits between setups. [326] Pip said nothing; she only held out her arm and looked at the trailer where her father sat with his headset. [347]

Sweat and a sleeve had smeared half the words. [356] Mara crouched and worked it the way she worked a bad handhold, testing each stroke, refusing to let go of what she could not yet see. [382] A smudged loop could be an E or an O; a broken bar could be a T. [399] She shifted, tried again, let the context carry her like a rope. [411] The message read: DAD SAYS LAST SCAN TONITE, FACE SHOWING RUINS IT, HE GETS PAID IF YOU HIDE. [429]

Mara understood the rest without help. [435] Tonight's footage would finish the model, and after that the studio would never need her body again. [452] Dex had not just lied to her; he had sold her fear, and now he was selling the last of it. [473]

The betrayal should have broken her. [479] Instead it rearranged her, the way a fall rearranges the air. [490] Every fear she had apologized for was the thing they had been harvesting. [503] Then it was worth something, and it was hers. [512]

She kissed Pip's cast, told her to stay by the catalog, and walked to the ladder. [528] On the way she passed the sound cart and, without breaking stride, unclipped the seashell and taped it to the boom operator's spare lavalier, the one feeding the director's headphones. [558] Then she climbed without a voice in her ear for the first time in eleven years. [574]

The rungs were flaking, and her palms came away orange. [584] At the eighth rung her knees locked, so she hooked an elbow through the rail and breathed until they unlocked; at the twelfth a bolt shifted, and she moved her weight to the shelf beside it without deciding to. [623] Below, she heard the director's chair scrape as Dex's whisper poured into his headphones: "Face down, Mara, face down or the whole deal is dead." [648]

At the top, under the open dome, she turned. [657] She let the lens see all of it: the wet eyes, the chattering jaw, the terror she had never been allowed to own. [680] Her fear was not a flaw to be masked; it was the performance, and a performance with a face attached had a name. [703]

"Mara Venn," she said to the camera, her voice shaking and absolutely sure. [716]

Far below, the director pulled off his headphones and stared at the trailer. [729] Beside the catalog, Pip raised her cast toward the stars like a small white flag that meant the opposite of surrender. [750]
