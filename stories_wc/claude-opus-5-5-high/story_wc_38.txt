The spider began its web above the mess hall lamp just as Mara opened her journal. [16] She took that as permission. [21]

The laboratory had been drowned for thirty years, and the Directorate liked it that way. [36] Its corridors ran beneath the reservoir that had swallowed the valley, and the rivets wept a brine that tasted, Mara thought, like the well water of her childhood. [64] Somewhere overhead lay the roofs of Vell, where she had learned to dance before she learned to read. [82]

Warden Brenn required a journal entry from each cryptographer every night. [93] "So I know your minds," he said, and he read them over supper while rolling the hangman's dice. [111] They were two yellowed bone cubes, each face carved with a piece of a stick figure: head, torso, arm, arm, leg, leg. [133] Whatever showed, he chalked onto the slate beside the name of whoever had disappointed him that day. [150] When a figure was finished, its owner was escorted up the ladder to the surface, and the surface, everyone knew, was only water. [173]

Tobiah's figure lacked a single leg. [179] He was nineteen and brilliant and could not stop his hands from shaking, which Brenn called disappointing. [196]

Mara wrote slowly, the way she broke codes, letting each line find its meter. [210] She had been the Directorate's finest breaker for eleven years because she heard ciphers as songs, their repetitions as refrains, their errors as a singer drawing breath. [237] Tonight she wrote what Brenn loved best: confession. [245]

She wrote that she feared the dark beyond the portholes. [255] She wrote that she missed her mother's voice, that her knees ached in the cold, that some nights she wept until the rivets blurred. [279] Every word was true, which was why Brenn would never think to doubt it. [293] But each admission opened on a chosen letter, and those letters, counted against the three-beat cadence of the old Vell waltz, named the drainage valves of the flooded east wing in the order that would open the forgotten culvert beneath the village church. [336] Her weakness was the lock; her weakness was also the key. [347]

The spider had finished its spokes. [353]

Tobiah could not hear the waltz in a page, so Mara did not ask him to. [369] While the others waited for supper, under the listening grilles, she stood and began to dance, humming as if grief had loosened her. [392] Heel, toe, pause; heel, toe, turn. [398] Each turn meant skip a letter, each pause meant keep one. [409] She ended facing the sealed east-wing door. [416] The guards laughed at the gray-haired breaker waltzing alone. [425] Tobiah did not laugh. [429] He watched her feet with the stillness of recognition, because his grandmother had danced exactly so on the kitchen tiles, murmuring that Vell remembered everything it could not write down. [459] Mara met his eyes once. [464] It felt, to both of them, like meeting a cousin at a funeral. [477]

At supper Brenn read her entry aloud, as he always did with the weak ones, lingering over every tremor. [496] "Eleven years, and still afraid of the dark," he said, almost fondly. [508] He was from Vell too; she had known him as a boy who threw stones at swallows. [525] Across the table, Tobiah's shaking fingers tapped out three-beat measures against his cup. [538] Then he excused himself to be sick, and Brenn, disgusted, let him go. [551]

Brenn rolled the dice. [555] A leg. [557] He chalked the last stroke beside Tobiah's name and sent two guards to fetch him. [572]

Somewhere in the east wing iron groaned, and the whole laboratory shivered as the culvert beneath the drowned church drew breath. [593] The guards came back with nothing but wet boot prints that ended at an open grate, and beyond the grate, a current running upward toward daylight. [619]

Brenn turned to Mara, the dice still warm in his fist. [630] She did not rise. [634] She did not plead. [638] She reached across, took the hangman's dice from beside his plate, and turned them over in her fingers the way she had once turned river stones for him in Vell. [668] "You read every word," she said softly. [675] "You never once asked why a frightened woman would write so carefully." [687] She dropped the dice into her cup of brine-tinged tea, where they sank without a sound. [703] Let him chalk whatever he liked beside her name; she had written her fear so plainly that it had become a door. [725]

Above the lamp, the spider drew its final thread tight and settled, patient, at the center of everything it had made. [746]
