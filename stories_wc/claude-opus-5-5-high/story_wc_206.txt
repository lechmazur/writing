Odo Fennick repaired music boxes, and for forty years he had done it in the resonance chamber beneath the clock tower of Brassmere, where every tune in town eventually came to be heard. [33] The chamber was a copper bell turned inside out, ribbed like a lung, and when a box played on his bench the walls sang it back thrice over, so that no false note could hide. [68]

On the fourth night of the endless midnight, the walls were silent. [80] Above him the great pendulum hung at the top of its swing, gleaming, caught in the balance like a breath no one could release. [104] Nothing below it had moved since the stroke of twelve. [114] The dawn had not come, the lamps burned blue and thin, and the children of Brassmere had begun to forget what color the river was. [139]

On his bench stood the tin lily his wife had built before she died, a clever thing of wire petals that wilted near dishonesty. [163] She had made it to keep him from being cheated by merchants. [175] Tonight it was bent nearly double. [181]

Midnight sat in his customer's chair. [187] She was tall and velvet-dark, with stars pinned sloppily in her hair, and she turned something small and golden between her fingers. [209]

"You know what this is," she said. [216]

He did. [218] It was a chip from the philosopher's stone, no bigger than a fingernail, and he had seen what it had done: the escapement of the great clock, once honest iron, now shone pure gold. [252] Gold does not rust. [256] Gold does not wear. [260] Gold does not agree to anything. [266]

"I only wanted to last," Midnight said. [273] "Every night I am struck and gone, a single chime, while the day gets whole hours of ceremony." [290]

The lily trembled but bent no further, because that part was true. [302]

"Fix the clock so it keeps me, and the chip is yours; your tools will never dull again." [320]

"And the light?" Odo asked. [325]

"The light is resting," she said, and the lily's petals folded shut with a tiny clatter. [341]

Odo nodded slowly. [344] He had learned long ago that the trouble with a music box is rarely the broken part. [361] A snapped tooth on a comb is honest; it tells you where it hurts. [375] The trouble is the part that looks perfect and is quietly lying, the pin polished so smooth it no longer catches, the spring wound so tight it has forgotten how to give. [407] His whole craft was mending what isn't torn. [415]

"There's an old covenant of rust," he said, taking the chip from her palm as though accepting. [432] "When the first smith poured the first gear, iron made a bargain with time: you may eat me slowly, and in exchange you will let me move." [459] He lifted a rusted key from the pegboard, the key to his own front door, orange with decades of weather. [479] "Every turning thing in Brassmere pays that toll, a little red dust each day." [493] "Your gold wouldn't pay, so the clock stopped." [501]

"Then gild the rest," Midnight said. [507] "Make it all perfect, and it will all stay." [516]

Instead he climbed the ladder to the pendulum, hummed a low note that the chamber faintly took up, and began to work. [538] First he drew the key hard across the escapement's flawless tooth, scoring a thin orange line into perfect gold, a wound where none had been. [563] From his coat he drew a coil of old iron wire, flaking and brown. [577] He wound it through the key's bow and around the chip, and he tethered both to the tip of the golden pendulum. [599]

"Forever is heavy," he murmured. [604]

The chip dragged at the pendulum like a stone in a pocket, the whole weight of an unending hour pulling down, and the rusted wire, which had kept its covenant all its life, did what gold never would: it gave. [644]

The pendulum fell. [647]

It swung through the bottom of its arc with a sound like a spoon ringing a glass, and the resonance chamber woke, catching the note and throwing it back threefold, fourfold, until every music box on every shelf in Brassmere began to play at once. [692] Tick, said the escapement, and a fleck of red bloomed along its golden tooth where the key had scored it. [712]

Midnight rose, stars sliding from her hair. [719] "I will be gone in a moment," she whispered. [728]

"You'll come back tomorrow," Odo said, climbing down. [736] "That's the bargain too." [740]

On the bench, the tin lily lifted its petals one by one. [752] Through the high grates came a thin grey seam, then a pink one, then gold, real gold, the kind that moves. [773] Odo swept the fallen chip, dull now and spent of forever, into his jar of useless springs. [790]
