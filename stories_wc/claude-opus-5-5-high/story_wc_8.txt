In Wrenfield they called Marta Quill the fictional historian, fondly, because her history of the town read like a novel. [20] Only she knew how exact the joke was. [28]

For twenty-two years she had repeated the town's noble lie: that the 1921 derailment at Wrenfield Junction was the work of a drifter who loosened a rail, and not the fault of the Coldwater Rail Company, which had ignored a failing signal to save money and which still paid every wage in the valley. [79] Eleven people died. [82] The lie spared the company, and so it spared the town, and Marta had told herself that a comfortable fiction fed more children than an expensive truth. [110]

Agnes Hale had disagreed. [114] Her father had been the signalman who reported the fault three times and was blamed for nothing only because the drifter was invented to take the blame instead. [143] Agnes planted the rosebush beside the platform that winter, and she told Marta, years ago, that when the last rose fell she would stop keeping quiet. [170] She was ninety-six. [173] The bush outlived her by four days. [180]

After the final petal dropped from the old rosebush, Marta went to Agnes's cottage to collect what had been promised her: a vellum page Agnes had illuminated as a girl, a great capital I twined with ivy and gold. [220] Hidden among the painted leaves, in script no larger than seed, Agnes had copied her father's three signal reports. [239] The page was gone. [243] The neighbor said Tobias Hale, Agnes's grandson and chair of the town council, had come by with a lantern and a canvas satchel. [266]

Marta knew where he would go. [272]

The old junction had been disused since the line closed, and ivy had swallowed the station whole, pulling down gutters, blinding the windows, climbing the iron stove in the waiting room like green smoke. [305] Tobias was kneeling at that stove when she came in, feeding it kindling, the vellum on the bench beside him. [325]

"Don't," he said, without turning. [330] "You wrote the book, Marta. [335] You of all people know why." [341]

"I do." [343] She sat on the far end of the bench, the page between them. [356] "You're frightened, and that's reasonable. [362] If this comes out, Coldwater may pull investment, and the council will be blamed for losing it. [379] Your fear is accurate. [383] I just think it's pointing at the wrong conclusion." [392]

He struck a match and let it burn down to his fingers. [404]

"Listen to the shape of it," she said. [412] "In 1921 a signalman filed three reports about a fault. [422] The company judged repair too costly. [428] Afterward, the town agreed on a story because the truth would hurt too many people at once." [445] She waited. [447] "Last spring an engineer filed three reports about the cracks in the Coldwater viaduct. [461] The council judged repair too costly. [467] You told me yourself." [471]

Tobias went very still. [475]

"The same bridge the school bus crosses," Marta said. [484] "If this lie survives tonight, it becomes the model for the next one. [497] That is what noble lies do. [503] They don't stay buried; they teach." [509]

"That's not fair." [512]

"It isn't about fair." [516] She kept her voice level, because shouting would only feed his panic, and panic would feed the stove. [534] "Your grandmother held this for seventy years because she loved the people it would hurt. [549] She let go of it only when she was sure there'd be someone to carry it. [565] She wasn't reckless. [568] She was patient." [571]

He looked at the page for a long moment, at the gold catching the lantern, the ivy on the vellum echoing the ivy at the windows. [597] His hand drifted toward it and then, slowly, withdrew. [606]

Marta took the page. [610] It was a small motion, almost gentle, but it was a recapture all the same, and they both felt it. [631]

"I'll print the reports alongside the viaduct findings," she said, sliding the vellum into her coat. [647] "Side by side. [650] Let people see the rhyme. [655] If the town is angry, better it be angry about ink than about another eleven graves." [671]

"They'll call you a liar," Tobias said. [678] "You wrote the first version." [683]

"Then I'll tell them that too." [689] She stood, brushing ivy leaves from her sleeve. [697] "A historian who corrects herself is still a historian. [706] One who doesn't is only a storyteller." [713]

Outside, the rosebush was bare, its thorns dark against the platform. [724] Marta walked past it toward the lights of the town, carrying eleven names, three reports, and a gold letter that had waited seventy years to be read aloud. [752]
