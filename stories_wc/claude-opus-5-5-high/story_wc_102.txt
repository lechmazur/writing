The memory surfaced on a Tuesday, while Ines was sanding a lens, and afterward nothing looked quite right side up. [20] She had been someone else once, a bead-maker in a harbor town, bent over a small flame. [37] That woman had blown a glass teardrop for a sailor, pressed a figure of Kwan Yin into its inner curve, and never given it to him. [63] The ship left, the fever came, and she died with the tear in her fist. [78] Ines woke from the remembering with one question lodged under her ribs like a splinter: where does love go when it is never used? [102]

She was spiritually curious in the untidy way of people who build dark rooms for a living. [119] Her camera obscura was a black canvas tent with a brass aperture, and she believed, without being able to prove it, that light entering a darkness carried more than pictures. [149] A week later she found the tear in a flea-market bowl of buttons, cloudy, chipped at the tip, unmistakable. [168] When she breathed on it, the fog of her breath outlined the little goddess pouring water from a vase, then faded as the glass cleared. [193]

So she carried it, and her tent, to the old viaduct at twilight, where no trains had crossed in forty years and the arches had gone wild with jasmine and trumpet vine. [225] It seemed a place that had carried a great deal and might know where things were kept. [242] The blossoms held the last light the way paper holds a stain, and mist rose from the river far below. [262]

That was where she met the pilgrim of sparks. [271] He was an old man in a railwayman's coat, walking the rails from end to end, and every few steps he struck a steel against a flint so that a small orange spray jumped and died in the dusk. [310]

"My wife said sparks are prayers too small for heaven to refuse," he told her, not stopping. [327] "I used to fire the engines here, and she would stand on the hill to watch them fly off the wheels." [348]

His name was Teodor, and his wife had been dead two winters. [360] Ines noticed his breathing before she noticed his grief, because they were the same thing: short, high, stopped at the collarbone, as if something below were not permitted to move. [390] When he said the word wife, the breath caught entirely. [400]

"Did you tell her?" she asked, and did not need to say what. [413]

He struck the flint twice, hard. [419] "We were not people who said things." [426]

She raised the tent between two iron posts and brought him inside, into the close dark that smelled of canvas and river. [448] Through the aperture the whole valley poured in upside down across the white screen: the hill, the flowering arches, the pale thread of mist, all of it hanging from the ground like fruit. [481]

"That hill," he whispered. [485]

She put the tear in his palm. [492] "Breathe on it," she said, "but slowly, from lower down than you want to." [506]

His first breaths were thin and left no mark. [515] She breathed beside him, audibly, the way she once counted exposures, four in and six out, and gradually his rhythm slid down to meet hers, the stiff ribs loosening, the belly finally moving. [548] On the next long exhalation the glass clouded, and the goddess appeared, tipping her vase. [563] Teodor made a sound like a door giving way in wind, a low astonished release. [578] Then he was weeping, and each sob fogged the tear again, so the goddess kept returning, pouring and pouring. [597]

"I loved her," he said to the upside-down hill. [606] "Every day, I loved her, and I kept it in here like coal in a tender." [622] He pressed his chest. [626]

And there, in the foggy dark, Ines understood with sudden clarity what the bead-maker had been trying to learn across two lives. [648] Unused love is not lost; it is stored in the breath we refuse to let all the way out, banked like a fire, heavy as fuel. [674] It waits for an exhalation long enough to make it visible. [685] The sailor was gone, and the wife was gone, but the breath remained, and breath could still be spent. [704]

When they stepped out, full dark had come and the jasmine was loud with scent. [719] Ines folded Teodor's fingers over the tear. [726] Then she breathed out, all the way, emptying herself of something that had been waiting three hundred years, and felt lighter than mist. [749] Teodor struck the flint once, and the spark rose instead of falling, and neither of them could say it had not. [770]
