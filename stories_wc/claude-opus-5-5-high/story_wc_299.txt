The quarry office trailer sat on cinder blocks at the lip of the pit, and when Dale Pruitt shut his truck door the sound went down into the stone and came back twice. [33] He had not been anywhere that answered him in three weeks. [44] Three weeks since the county fair, since the local station's cameras, since he had held up Mrs. Harlan's walnut mantel clock and announced to a folding-chair crowd that it was an 1830s Seth Thomas, worth eleven thousand dollars. [82] They had applauded. [85] Two days later her nephew posted the stamped movement online: made in Taiwan, 1978. [99] The clip had eight hundred thousand views, and the estate houses had stopped calling. [113]

Ellen Cask had not stopped. [118] She met him at the trailer steps in a hard hat, keys in her fist. [133] "Bank takes the quarry in June," she said. [141] "Whatever's in here, I need to know what it is before somebody else decides." [155] "You saw the video," he said. [161] "Maureen would have laughed at the video." [168] Maureen had fronted the Slate Choir for eleven years, and Dale had hauled its amplifiers through every bar between here and Scranton. [190] She had died in March, and he had not gone to the service, because he had not known where a roadie was supposed to stand. [215]

Inside, the trailer smelled of diesel and damp paper. [224] He went to work the way he always did, reciting. [234] Steel tanker desk, Cole Steel, early sixties, sixty dollars on a good day. [247] Blasting permits, 1971 through 1989, historical society might want them. [257] Facts were load-bearing; you stacked them between yourself and whatever was pressing in, the way he used to stack road cases against a trailer wall so nothing shifted on the turnpike. [288]

Then he saw what Ellen had been using as a coffee table. [300] It was a speaker cabinet, four-by-twelve, tolex scuffed to the wood, a sheet of plywood screwed over the top. [319] Most of the grille cloth was gone, but a scrap still clung to one corner, tan threads woven with a thin oxblood stripe. [342] "Fender wheat," he said, too fast. [348] "Late fifties pattern, reproduced from the nineties on. [356] Could be either." [359] But he knew the stripe. [364] He had painted it himself with Maureen's nail polish in a motel in Allentown, so he could spot their cabinet in the dark behind a stage. [390] The fact did not insulate anything. [396]

He knelt, got his fingers under the staples, and had to wrest the scrap free, because forty years of glue and quarry dust did not want to let it go. [426] Behind it, on the baffle board, in black marker faded to gray, someone had written a list. [443] It was not a set list. [449] It was the load-out order, his order, the one he had shouted at every stagehand for a decade: drums, bass rig, this cab, keys, lights last. [475] Underneath, in Maureen's looping hand: DALE'S SHOW STARTS AFTER THE APPLAUSE. [486]

He remembered her saying it, half joke, half toast, in the parking lot of some roadhouse at two in the morning while he coiled cable and the crowd's noise drained out of his ears. [520] The band got the cheering. [525] He got the quiet, and the care, and the long drive with everything accounted for. [540] At the fair he had wanted, just once, to be the one they clapped for, and he had rushed the clock to earn it. [564]

Ellen was in the doorway. [569] "Well?" [570] He stood, the scrap in his palm, and looked out past her at the pit, the terraced gray walls where the band used to rehearse on summer nights because Maureen swore the stone sang harmony. [605] "Honestly?" he said. [608] "I don't know what most of this is worth yet." [618] He said it plainly, confidently uncertain, the way a man says the road is long when he means to drive it anyway. [640] "I'll need a month. [644] Every box, every drawer, done slow." [650] "Nobody's going to film it," Ellen said. [657] "Good." [658]

She took the scrap from him, turned it to the light, and her mouth did something he had not seen since March. [680] Then she folded it back into his hand. [688] "That one's not on the inventory." [694]

After she left, Dale walked to the lip of the quarry and said Maureen's name, not loud. [711] The stone took it down and handed it back, once, twice, fainter, the way applause fades, the way a crowd goes home. [733] He waited until it was entirely gone. [740] Then he went back inside, opened the first drawer, and began. [751]
