After the nostalgia ban, Iselle Marrow stopped dancing the tides for the city and went to live where the sea had eaten the old botanical terraces. [26] No one exiled her. [30] The Ministry of Forward would have preferred she stay, stepping the flats at ebb and reading the gaps between waves so the herring boats could slip home, but she had watched them burn her mother's letters in the square and had decided that a city which forbade remembering did not deserve her feet. [83] Elective exile, the clerks wrote in her file, as if choosing it made it smaller. [98]

The garden lived in permanent dusk. [104] Salt had killed the roses, but night-blooming cereus climbed the broken glasshouses, and at the hour when the sky turned the color of a bruise healing, the first stars came out over black water that lapped at the lowest beds. [144]

Iselle kept one contraband thing. [149] Folded inside a square of waxed paper was a single speck of dust, all that remained of the moonstone cabochon her mother had worn at her throat, which Iselle had ground down grain by grain for ink over three hard winters. [190] Every verse she had written with it on paper, the patrols had found and burned. [205] Paper stayed where you left it; that was its flaw. [215] Skin walked. [217] So she had resolved to pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin, the old names of the constellations that the ban had struck from every almanac, written in moonstone ink that would glimmer only under starlight. [253] She lacked only a body willing to carry it. [262]

The boy arrived at the wet hour, soaked to the thighs, carrying a scribe's satchel with the Ministry seal cut away. [283] His name was Teo, he said, and he had copied the ban into four hundred ledgers before he realized he could no longer remember his grandmother's face. [310]

"They say you remember things," he said. [317] "I want to be written on." [323]

Iselle studied him the way she once studied surf, looking not at the crests but at the spaces between them. [343] A tide dancer lived by observing gaps: the lull where a foot could land, the breath where the water hesitated. [363] In Teo she saw a gap where fear should have been. [374]

"It will not wash off," she warned. [381]

"Then it will be the first thing I own," he said. [392]

She mixed the last speck into a thimble of lamp-black and seawater, and the ink took on a faint milky shiver, like the moon seen through fog. [419] Teo lay face down on the cold stone of the old fountain, and she began. [434]

She did not compose the verse; she read it. [443] Above them the stars hung in their banned arrangements, and Iselle traced not the lights but the darkness between them, the black shapes her mother had taught her to name: the Heron's Hollow, the Empty Cradle, the Door Left Open. [483] Each gap became a word, and each word went into his shoulders with the needle timed to the space between his breaths, because a dancer knew that anything entered cleanly only at the pause. [517]

The tide turned while she worked. [523] She felt it the way she always had, as a pull at the backs of her knees, the old summons to rise and step and keep ahead of it. [552] For years in the garden she had danced each flood, retreating terrace by terrace, never letting the water touch her, never staying anywhere long enough to be caught. [580]

Tonight the sea slid over the fountain's lip, cold around her ankles, and she did not move. [597] She was spontaneously anchored, held fast by nothing but the last line she was writing, her mother's final word for the sky: Remain. [620] The water rose to her calves, hesitated, and withdrew, as if it had found in her a gap of its own and chosen to respect it. [646]

When Teo sat up, the verse on his back caught the starlight and shone faintly, a constellation of absences only the night could read. [670] By day he would pass any patrol. [677] By night he would be a map of everything the city had chosen to forget. [692]

"Where will you go?" she asked. [698]

"Back," he said, pulling on his wet shirt. [706] "Someone should be seen remembering." [711]

He climbed the ruined terraces toward the lights of the city, unsteady at first and then sure, the way a young bird fledges, falling a little before the air decides to hold it. [744]

Iselle unfolded the waxed paper and found it empty. [753] Then she stood in the shallows under the old stars and understood what her exile had been for: not fleeing the forgetting, but staying still long enough for something to be carried out of it. [788]
