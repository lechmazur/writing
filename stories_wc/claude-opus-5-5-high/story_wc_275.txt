The glass dome held the heat the way a palm holds water, carefully and never for long. [17] Outside, the city of Hesk was still burning three days after the tower fell, and the smoke laid a copper film over every pane. [41] Inside, Wren Vasko walked the rows of desert roses with a watering can and a stylus, speaking to each plant in turn. [63] "Patience," she told a swollen trunk, and carved the word into the rim of its clay pot. [80] "Mercy," she told the next. [85]

Her mother had taught her that a plant raised beside a spoken value grew toward it, the way it grew toward light. [107] Whether that was true hardly mattered. [113] The roses bloomed, the words stayed carved, and every morning the farm remembered what it was for. [130] After the tower fell, remembering felt like the only work left. [141] The tower had rung the hours for Hesk for two centuries. [152] Now nobody knew when to open markets or close gates, and people were looting in the dark. [169] Wren kept the farm's schedule by hand, pot by pot, word by word, as if she could stitch the broken days back together with a stylus. [195]

The airship's ballast sandbag had come through a roof vent at dawn. [207] It burst on the gravel between the benches and sprayed pale sand across the leaves. [222] Wren picked up the torn canvas. [228] A brass tag was riveted to one corner, stamped with the tower's seal and a number from its seed vault. [248] She knew, without any reason she could name, that the pilot who dropped it had crashed on the ridge and was now hiding in her potting shed. [275] That was how her perception always worked, arriving senselessly and never wrong. [287] As a child she had known which of her father's hunts would end in blood before he had packed his rifle. [308] She had known her mother was dying a full season before the cough. [321] Knowing had never once let her change anything. [329]

The pilot was a girl no older than Wren, soot-streaked, clutching a satchel of paper seed envelopes against her chest. [349] "The vault was in the tower's foundation," she whispered. [358] "I got out what I could. [364] The Magistrate says I stole it." [370]

"Did you?" [372]

"I saved it." [375]

Wren believed her, though belief was the wrong word; she simply knew. [387]

Her father arrived at sunset, when the dome turned to amber and the fires beyond it looked almost gentle. [406] Dace Vasko came through the airlock with dust in his beard and a hunting knife loose in his hand. [425] He had not visited in a year. [432] She knew the Magistrate's bounty notice was folded in his coat before he reached for it. [448]

"Pilot came down near here," he said. [455] "Ballast marks all over the ridge." [461]

"I found a sandbag," Wren said, and held up the scrap of canvas. [474]

He looked at the tower's seal for a long moment. [484] "Where is she, Wren?" [488]

She did not answer. [492] Instead she led him to the oldest pot on the farm, the one beneath the center of the dome where the light fell straightest. [516] The rose in it was taller than she was, its trunk fat as a barrel, its flowers the color of banked coals. [538] Carved into the clay in her mother's careful hand was a single word: QUIET. [552]

"She made this one for you," Wren said. [560] "The day I was born. [565] She said you would need it most." [572]

Her father's jaw worked. [576] "Quiet doesn't pay." [579]

"It keeps," Wren said. [583] "Everything else in Hesk is falling down, and this is still growing. [595] That girl has the seeds from the tower's vault. [604] If you take her to the Magistrate, he sells them for bread money and next spring there's nothing to plant." [624]

The fires outside flared as a roof collapsed somewhere to the east, and the light moved over the glass like slow water. [646] Wren stood very still. [650] She had never struck anyone, never carried a blade, never lifted a hand in anger. [665] She did not know how to fight her father; she knew only how to wait beside a thing until it grew. [686] For the first time in her life, her strange knowing gave her nothing, and she understood that this silence was the gift. [708]

Slowly, Dace Vasko sheathed his knife. [714] He crouched and ran a thumb along the engraved word, feeling each letter the way he might read tracks. [733]

"Tell your pilot she can stay, and the seeds with her," he said. [746] He picked up the stylus from the bench and turned it over in his scarred fingers. [762] "Show me how you do it." [768]

So Wren handed him a young rose in a new pot, and in the amber quiet beneath the burning sky, she taught her father the first word. [795]
