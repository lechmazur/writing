The mist came up Zephyr Bluff each morning like something breathing, and Mara Keel climbed into it with a walking stick and no weapon. [24] Four years ago her own service had burned her, circulating her face to every border post after the Halden crossing failed. [45] Since then she had lived in the old keeper's cottage below the bluff, mending nets for fishermen who never asked where she had learned such knots. [71]

She climbed because of the snow. [77] In the old days her network had used the long drifts on the summit as a slate: holes punched with a stick, short and long, read through the lighthouse glass when the mist parted. [111] The west wind erased everything by noon. [118] No paper, no radio, nothing to find. [125]

Nobody had written there since Halden. [131]

This morning, at the tilt of the earth toward spring, when the drifts had gone granular and gray at their edges, someone had. [154]

Mara stood very still in the silver hush, then read it with her feet, pacing the rows. [171] Short, long, long: the old cipher. [177] It said: TOMAS LIVES NORTH HUT THREE DAYS BRING DOOR. [187]

Tomas. [188]

She touched the pillbox in her coat pocket, a silver thing worn smooth where her thumb had rubbed it for four years. [210] Tomas had given it to her the night before Halden, its lid engraved with a lark and the words FOR WHEN YOU MUST. [233] Inside was the pill every courier carried. [240] She had never needed it, because she had never let herself get close enough to need it. [257] When the patrol came to the crossing she had waited in the reeds, calculating, and by the time the numbers favored her they had taken him. [283] She had been told he was dead. [290] She had believed it because it was easier than the alternative: that somewhere he was still waiting for the courage she had never spent. [314]

"Door" meant papers. [317] Mara still had one blank passport rolled inside a hollow net float, because a burned spy always keeps one thing her service does not know about. [343]

It could be a trap. [348] Her old service might want her quietly retrieved; the other side might want her loudly. [363] So she spent the day thinking the way she had been trained, prudently, listing every path off the bluff, the tide tables, the hour the fog thickened, the one trawler captain who owed her his life. [399] Then she did what she had not done at Halden: she decided before the numbers were perfect. [416]

That night she climbed again and punched her reply into a fresh drift, deep enough to outlast the dawn breeze: COME TO THE BLUFF AT SIX ALONE. [443]

The mist rose at six, and a boy walked out of it. [455] He was perhaps fifteen, thin as a stick, with Tomas's heavy eyebrows, and he was shaking. [471]

"My father taught me the snow," he said. [479] "He's in the hut with a broken leg and no papers. [490] He said you would read it, because careful people always come back to fix what they broke." [507]

Mara felt the words land beneath her ribs. [515]

She opened the pillbox and tipped the pill over the edge of the bluff, into the white nothing. [533] Then she wrote on a scrap of net tag the captain's name and the phrase that would make him sail without questions, folded it into the empty box, and closed the engraved lid. [566] She had never vouchsafed that phrase to anyone, not even the service that burned her. [581]

"Take this to the harbor tonight, after the second bell," she said, pressing the box into his palm. [599]

"And you?" [601]

"I'm going north with a door, and I'm carrying him down myself." [613]

It was a bold plan, but not a reckless one; she had counted the paths, and the mist would hold until evening. [635] Four years she had wondered where unused courage goes, whether it spoils like bread or simply leaves. [652] Now she knew: it waits, patient as snow, exactly where you left it. [665]

Behind them the zephyr began smoothing the drifts, and by noon the bluff was blank, the old codes softening into water, running downhill toward spring. [690]
