Oriel had read futures in the Singing Hollow for thirty-one years, and in all that time nothing had surprised her. [20] The cave was a throat of quartz beneath the hill, and when the wind moved through its upper vents the crystals rang like struck glasses, low notes in the deep galleries and bright ones near the mouth. [57] She kept seven lanterns on iron hooks, and where their shadows fell across one another on the crystal walls, the future showed itself in the overlap, dark on dark, like ink spreading in water. [91]

Villagers climbed up with their questions, and Oriel gave them small answers on purpose. [105] "You will find a blue egg under the hedge on Thursday," she told the farmer who wanted to know about his wealth. [127] "Your daughter will laugh at supper so hard that milk comes out her nose," she told the widow who asked whether grief would end. [151] Big fates made people forget to look; little ones sent them home watching hedges and suppers, and that was the whole of her craft, to magnify everyday magic until nobody could miss it. [184] Still, it wearied her, seeing everything a week before it happened. [195] The eggs never astonished her. [200]

Then the pilgrim came up the path in autumn rain, and Oriel had not known she would. [217]

She was an old woman in a potter's apron, clay still grey beneath her nails, and she carried a wooden box against her chest the way one carries a sleeping bird. [248] Oriel lit the lanterns out of habit and looked for her in the shadows. [262] Where the woman's future should have pooled, there was only crystal, clean and ringing. [276]

"What do you want to know?" Oriel asked, her voice thinner than she liked. [290]

"Nothing," said the pilgrim, whose name was Wen. [298] "I came to give something back." [304]

She opened the box. [308] Inside, on a bed of wool, lay a cave pearl the size of a thrush's egg, milk-white and faintly glowing, as if it had swallowed a little of the lantern light and meant to keep it. [344]

"My husband took it from your lowest gallery when he was nine," Wen said. [358] "He kept it on our windowsill for fifty years, and he died last spring asking me to bring it home." [378]

Oriel knew the place, a shallow stone cup beneath a single drip, where for as long as she had lived here the water had struck bare rock with a flat, dead tick. [410]

They went down together, the lanterns swinging, their two shadows sliding over each other on the walls and showing Oriel nothing at all. [433] The blankness should have frightened her; instead it felt like the dark before a curtain rises. [449]

Wen knelt at the cup and explained, as if to a child, how a pearl is made. [466] A grain of grit, a drip of lime-heavy water, and forces nobody can see: the fall of each drop, the breath of the cave, the slow pull of the hill itself, turning the grain a fraction every time, never the same way twice. [509] "It is steadily unpredictable," she said, "like a heartbeat, like a marriage." [521] "That is why it comes out round." [528]

She set the pearl in the cup. [535]

The next drop struck it, and the dead tick became a note, small and silver, and the note ran up into the crystal. [558] The gallery answered it, and the gallery above that, until the whole Hollow was humming a chord Oriel had never heard in thirty-one years, a tune that shifted each time the pearl turned beneath the drip. [594] She looked to her lanterns, and for the first time the overlapping shadows moved on their own, trembling with the music, refusing to hold any shape long enough to read. [624]

Oriel laughed, and the cave laughed back in a dozen glassy voices. [636] She had spent her life making small wonders loud for other people, and she had forgotten what it was to be handed one without warning. [661]

Wen was watching the pearl, not the walls. [669] "I have no house to go back to that he isn't missing from," she said. [684] "Could a person inhabit a place like this, just to listen?" [695]

Oriel tried, from long habit, to see the answer. [704] The shadows only danced, bright darkness folding over bright darkness, keeping their secret. [717]

"Yes," she said, before she could know whether it was wise, and found she did not mind the not knowing at all. [739] "Stay, and we will find out together what the pearl sings tomorrow." [751]
