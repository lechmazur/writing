The lime works shut down on a Tuesday, and by Friday the rain over Harrow Cliffs smelled like itself again. [20] Ines Garrow stood on her porch with a funnel, a cork, and a notebook, catching it. [36] She had collected petrichor for thirty-one years, and every vial on her shelves bore the same careful label: date, barometric pressure, wind direction, and what she had eaten for breakfast, because appetite alters the nose and she was not a fool. [77] The town called her odd. [82] She preferred sensibly eccentric, which meant she wore two watches, one set to the factory whistle and one to the tides, and trusted neither completely. [107] The whistle watch was useless now. [113]

She knew what petrichor was. [118] Geosmin from soil bacteria, oils shed by thirsty plants, ozone dragged down by lightning; she could recite the chemistry the way her father had recited kiln temperatures. [145] That was the trouble. [149] Everything she loved had an explanation, and lately the explanations felt like lids screwed down over something still breathing. [168] She wanted, just once before she died, to find the place where explanations failed. [182]

On Saturday, Tom Reyes brought her the compass. [190] He had been clearing the foreman's office and found it mortared into the wall behind the payroll safe, brass gone green, lid engraved VELL EXPEDITION 1911. [216] Everyone knew the story: six surveyors entered the cliffs looking for the underground lake the quarry company wanted drained, and none came out. [239] The company drained it anyway. [244] Its pumps had run for a century. [251]

"You do the thing with objects," Tom said, embarrassed. [260]

She did. [262] She called it psychometry only in private; in public she called it a trained nose and patient fingers, which was also true. [284] She held the compass until the metal warmed, then thumbed open the hinged lid. [298]

Something woke. [300] Wet stone under a man's palm, a lantern hissing, the taste of limestone in the back of a throat. [319] She snapped the lid shut and it slept. [327] She opened it again and the memory woke further, like a door swinging wider on the same hinge: water, black and enormous, and a voice saying this is not on any map. [359]

The needle did not point north. [365] It pointed at the cliffs. [370]

She packed sensibly: headlamp, spare batteries, rope, thermos of tea, six empty vials, and a sandwich she noted in pencil so the breakfast column would not lie. [397] With the pumps silent, the old drainage adit behind the works was already ankle-deep and rising. [413] She delved anyway, following the needle through galleries her father had never been allowed to see, opening the lid at every fork. [435] Each time, the memory woke a little more. [443] Six men laughing at an echo. [449] One man, the one whose hands she wore, deciding to stay. [460]

The passage opened beneath the cliffs into a lagoon returning to itself. [472] Water crept up pale limestone ledges that had been dry for a hundred years, and the dripping from the vault fell into it with a sound so soft it seemed to arrive before it happened. [507]

Then the smell reached her. [512]

Petrichor. [513] Rain on dry earth, here, where no rain had ever fallen. [524] She began, out of habit, to explain it: dry ledges, returning water, dormant bacteria stirring. [539] The explanation held for three breaths. [545] Then she recognized the rain. [550] It was not today's rain. [555] It was the rain from the vial she had filled the morning of her father's funeral, the one she had never opened, its breakfast column left blank. [582] That vial sat corked on her shelf two miles away. [592]

She opened the compass one last time. [599] This time nothing woke in her hands. [606] Instead something woke in the lagoon, and she felt herself being read: a woman with two watches, cold feet, a sandwich, a grief she had labeled instead of carried. [635] The hush of the cave did not break; it deepened, the way a held note deepens when the singer stops pushing. [656]

She understood, without being able to say how, why the Vell man had stayed. [670] Why someone at the company had walled his compass up rather than let it point anyone here. [687]

She took out a vial, then put it back. [696] In her notebook, under the breakfast column, she wrote one word: enough. [708] Then she closed the lid gently, the way one closes a door on someone sleeping, and climbed back toward the rain while the water rose behind her, reclaiming its place. [738]
