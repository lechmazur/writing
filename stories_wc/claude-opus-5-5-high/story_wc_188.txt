Oro Vasquez came to the fortress of Castel Verd at dusk, when the moonflowers on its walls were opening like slow applause. [22] She was a wandering fireworks designer, which meant she owned a cart, a mule named Tuesday, and more gunpowder than any sensible inn would allow. [47] She was also, by habit, a woman who chanted while walking. [58] "Nitrate, sulfur, charcoal, plea," she sang under her breath as she climbed the switchback road, "one for the dark and two for me." [81] The chant was nonsense, but it kept her feet honest. [91] In her coat pocket rode the last thing Maren had given her: a fire spinner's kerosene rag knot, the fist of cotton Maren had once soaked and lit and whirled on chains until crowds forgot their debts. [128] It still smelled of her work, sharp and oily, even two winters after the fever took her. [145] Tied around it was a scrap of paper in Maren's crooked hand: I left you a note in the mosaic at Castel Verd. [168] Play it when the sky forgets itself. [175]

The sky had forgotten itself for months. [182] Ash from a mountain that had split open far to the south hung over the whole province, and no one in Castel Verd had seen a star since spring. [211] The garrison had stopped patrolling the battlements. [218] Mothers stopped telling children the names of constellations, because names without lights attached felt like lies. [234] People walked with their chins down, as if the heavy sky were pressing on the backs of their necks. [253] Oro understood that kind of gravity. [259] She had carried it since Maren's funeral, and she had come here precisely to throw something back at it. [278]

The courtyard mosaic was enormous, a whirl of blue and ochre tiles showing a dragon eating its own tail. [297] Thousands of pieces, and no writing anywhere. [304] A boy with a torn collar watched her circle it twice. [315] "Are you lost?" he asked. [320] "Professionally," said Oro. [323] "Everyone here is lost," the boy said, very seriously. [332] "Then I'm in good company." [337]

The hush came down with full dark, the thick starless hush that swallowed even the fountain's gossip. [354] Oro stepped onto the mosaic and began to chant again, walking the dragon's spine. [368] "Nitrate, sulfur, charcoal, plea." [372] She did not look for the note; Maren had never written anything that could be read with eyes. [390] Instead she listened, letting the whole fortress pour into her: the moths bumping the moonflowers, a guard's dice rattling in a cup, Tuesday chewing, the boy breathing behind her. [419] Then, one by one, she filtered those sounds away, the way she filtered lumps from powder through silk, until only her own chant remained, bouncing off the tiles. [447] Most tiles gave back a dull tock. [454] A few, scattered through the dragon's scales, rang. [462] They rang at different pitches, clean and bright, like cups of glass. [474] "Those are hollow," the boy whispered, joining her without being asked. [485] "No," Oro said, kneeling, "those are tuned." [492]

Maren had been a fire spinner, but before that she had been a tile setter's daughter, and she had always said a flame was only music you could see. [521] Oro tapped the ringing tiles in the order her feet had found them. [534] Seven notes climbed out of the courtyard, a small crooked melody, the lullaby Maren used to hum while she soaked her knots in kerosene. [558] Oro laughed, and it came out wet. [565] Beneath the last tile, when she pried it up, lay a folded square of oilcloth. [580] Inside was a formula in Maren's hand: copper, chlorine, a pinch of something Oro had never thought to try. [599] It was the recipe for true star-white, the color fireworks people chase their whole lives and never catch. [617]

She worked for hours in the dragon's mouth, the boy grinding powder and chanting her nonsense back at her, getting the words wrong on purpose to make her scold him. [647] Near midnight the people of Castel Verd heard a mule bray and a woman shout, "Chins up, you miserable darlings!" [667] Oro held Maren's rag knot to the fountain lamp until it caught, then touched it to the fuse. [685] The shells climbed above the moonflowers and broke into hundreds of small white points that hung, and hung, refusing to fall. [706] All along the walls, faces tilted upward for the first time in months. [719] Under the borrowed stars, the boy began pointing and naming them, inventing constellations with ridiculous names. [735] "That one is the Mule," he said. [742] "That one is Maren," said Oro, and nobody on the battlements corrected her. [755]
