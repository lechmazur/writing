[181] She had never forgiven the word. [187] A miracle, she believed, was a thing aimed. [195] This one had missed its target by the width of a gearshift and landed on the wrong sibling. [213]

[236] She found the paramedic and asked him to describe Theo's face. [247] She asked her mother, at birthdays and funerals and once at a gas station, whether she wished it had gone the other way. [270] Mara called this seeking closure. [275] Everyone else called it opening wounds, and eventually stopped answering. [285]

Her grandmother, a seamstress, used to say scars were stitched stories, and that anyone patient could read them. [303] Mara had never been patient, but she wanted that literacy badly, wanted to press her fingers to the pale seam on her shoulder and finally understand what it was trying to say. [335]

[446] Six days, the plant had said, and she understood she had been under far longer than one night. [464]

[483] There were dozens, overlapping, some dark and fresh, some faded to the brown of old tea stains. [500] Every one had a small notch in its rim, a gap where the mug's lip was chipped.
