The elevator at Skarn Spire was a brass birdcage hung on a chain, and it climbed the way old men climb stairs, with complaint and dignity. [26] Wren Tallow stood inside it with a lamp at her feet and a parcel in her arms, because that morning she had been promoted from the scullery to the tenth floor, and nobody at Skarn ascended empty-handed. [63] The parcel held two things the old captain had sent down to be burned: a leather diary and a pair of airship goggles with cracked, many-faceted lenses. [90] Wren had not burned them. [95] She was the sort of girl who asked the coal man about his mother and the cook about her knees, and she had never once been able to leave a closed thing closed. [128]

First floor. [130] Through the bars, ice furred the spire's stone, and below, the clouds lay flat and white as a tablecloth nobody would ever clear. [153] Wren opened the diary. [157] Every page was the same: a column of names, each struck through, and beside each a date and a single word such as lost, drowned, fell. [183] It was the ledger of the Merrow's crew, forty souls, and Captain Ines Varga had outlived every one. [201]

Third floor. [203] Between the lines, Wren noticed, ran rows of tiny dots and hooks, too faint and too regular to be stains. [223] A cipher, then. [226] She tipped the pages toward the lamp, and nothing came of it but her own squinting. [242]

Fifth floor. [244] The goggles lay in the bottom of the parcel, their strap stiff with old salt. [259] Airship captains, the cook had told her once, ground their own lenses to cut the glare off the cloud tops, and some of them ground in secrets as well. [288] Wren had assumed this was a kitchen story. [296] She lifted the goggles anyway and held the cracked left lens between the lamp and the page. [313] The flame broke apart in the facets and fell across the paper in a dozen small rainbows, and where the scattered light touched the dots and hooks, they gathered, leaned together, and became letters. [347] Wren laughed out loud, alone in the cage, and the chain groaned as if in reproof. [363]

Sixth floor. [365] Beneath "Tomas Reyes, drowned," the light unfolded a second line: Tomas sang flat on purpose to make the rigger boys throw boots at him. [389] Beneath "Aggie Moll, fell": Aggie kept a cat in the gasbag housing and swore to me it was a very large rat. [411] Beneath every name, some small bright thing: a joke, a burnt pudding, a sunrise over the Kettle Straits that the whole watch had stopped work to see. [438]

Seventh floor. [440] Wren understood, turning pages, that the captain had written her grief in plain ink for anyone to read and hidden her happiness where only she could find it. [468] It struck her as backward, the way people so often are. [479] Then she understood why the captain wanted it burned: the old woman's eyes had clouded last winter, as everyone below stairs knew, and the hidden lines had gone dark to her, leaving only the names she knew by heart. [518]

Eighth floor. [520] Wren was not sentimental, or she told herself she was not. [531] She had scrubbed too many pots for too many people who never learned her name to believe that one kindness mended anything. [553] But she was curious, warmly and incurably, about what a face did when it was handed back something it had given up. [575]

Ninth floor. [577] She marked Tomas and Aggie with scraps of her apron string. [588]

Tenth. [589] The cage shuddered to a stop before a round room of frosted windows, where Captain Varga sat wrapped in a flight coat older than Wren, facing the white nothing. [618]

"Is it done?" the captain asked. [624]

"No, ma'am," said Wren, and set the lamp on the sill, and put the goggles to her own eyes. [643] "But I have a question about Tomas Reyes, if you'll allow it. [655] Did he really sing flat on purpose?" [662]

The captain was silent for a long moment. [670] Then something moved in her ruined face, slow as ice deciding to become water, and she said, "Badly. [688] He sang it so badly." [693]

Outside, the clouds went on being clouds, and the spire went on being cold, and neither of them was asked to change. [715] Wren turned the page toward the scattered light and asked about the cat. [728]
