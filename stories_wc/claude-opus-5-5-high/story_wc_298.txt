The lamps in the lower ossuary were only ever half lit, and Wren had learned to graft by their glow. [20] Down here the city buried its dead among apple trees, and the roots had long since found the bones, threading eye sockets and curling through ribcages like patient fingers. [49] Every tree drank one life, and every fruit carried that life's unspent future. [62] Eat an apple, surrender your own fate to the broker, and walk out wearing a dead stranger's luck. [80] That was the barter, and the barter had eaten her brother. [91]

Tobin had traded his destiny in the hungry winter for a sack of coin and a sailor's long life. [110] The coin had fed them until spring. [117] The sailor's life did not fit him. [124] He woke at night reaching for ropes that were not there, forgot Wren's name at supper, and stared at doorways as if something important had just left through them. [153] There were holes in him now, and the wind of other people's years blew through. [168]

All he had left of himself was the receipt the broker gave every customer: a tarot card, burned at the center so the old fate could never be read again. [198] His was the Star. [202] Only the edges survived, seven scorched points around a black wound. [213] Wren carried it into the orchard wrapped in linen, as if it could still feel cold. [229]

She was, everyone said, a reckless idealist. [236] She believed a thing broken could be mended if you loved it precisely enough, and she had spent three years as the orchard's apprentice grafter proving it on split branches and blighted roots. [269] Her method was the old one her mother had taught her: inlay meaning everywhere. [283] A sliver of knucklebone set into bark to remind the tree it was rooted in someone. [299] A name carved under a graft so the new limb knew whose sap it drank. [314] Trees, her mother said, heal toward what they remember. [323]

She found Tobin's tree by following the ash. [331] The broker, Sallow, burned each card beneath the trunk that received the fate, and a gray smudge still marked the roots of a young apple leaning over an unnamed child's grave. [362] One fruit hung there, gold as a lamp. [370] Someone would buy it soon. [375] Then Tobin's future would be eaten, and the holes in him would never close. [389]

Sallow's lantern swung around the corner of the corridor, throwing rib-shadows across the ceiling. [403] "A buyer comes at the bell," he said, not unkindly. [413] "If you want your brother's fate back, you know the price." [424]

Her own destiny, whole, for his. [430] An even trade, and Tobin would be himself while Wren wandered hollow in some stranger's years. [446] The reckless part of her nearly said yes. [454] The idealist part refused to believe that love had to be an exchange of ruins. [469]

"No," she said, and knelt at the roots. [477]

She did not cut. [481] Cutting was the broker's way. [486] Instead she drew grafting twine from her apron and began to trammel the root that carried Tobin's fate upward, binding it to the bones it had grown through, loop by loop, fiercely gentle, the way she would hold a frightened animal still for splinting. [530] The root shuddered and slowed. [535] The apple overhead dimmed, its sap no longer rising. [544]

"You'll kill it," Sallow warned. [549]

"I'm reminding it," Wren said. [554]

She laid the burned Star against the bark and pressed the seven surviving points into the wood with her thumbnail, inlaying what remained. [577] Then she filled the black center with herself. [585] Not her whole fate, only pieces: a strand of her hair, a chip of her mother's rosary bead, the word Tobin carved in the scar where the ash had lain. [615] A graft, not a trade. [620]

She felt it take. [624] Something went out of her, the way warmth leaves a hand. [635] Some small future she would never know she lost, a road not walked, a year she would not have. [654] The apple brightened again, but its gold had a darker grain now, half his and half hers, and it dropped into her palm as if it had been waiting. [683]

Sallow looked at the twine, the carving, the card fused into living wood. [696] "That fate is no longer for sale," he said slowly. [706]

"No," Wren agreed. [709] "It's for keeping." [712]

She climbed the stairs through the half-lit corridors with the apple held against her chest, knowing the holes in Tobin would not close in a night. [738] Patching a soul was slow work, stitch by stitch, meaning by meaning. [750] But she had twine, and a knife for carving names, and the rest of her life to spend on it. [770]
