Maren had never wanted the orchard, and the orchard, as far as she could tell, had never wanted anyone. [19] It grew in the cliff's throat, a labyrinth of carved stone corridors where twisted nut trees shouldered out of the walls like old men leaving church. [45] Anyone who touched their bark took root by morning. [54] Her grandmother had touched one on purpose, thirty years ago, and now stood near the eastern stair with sparrows nesting in her elbows. [77]

The mechanical birds were hers too. [83] She had built them from brass and clock springs to patrol the cliffside paths, and they still did, forty of them, wheeling on shrieking hinges above the corridors and diving at anything that strayed. [117] Nobody remembered what they were meant to protect. [125] The blueprint of the maze had gone into the old woman's head, and her head had gone into the wood. [145]

Maren stayed because someone had to keep the gate chained, and because leaving felt like a verdict she was not ready to deliver. [168]

On the evening the travelers came, she was sweeping hulls from the gatehouse step and saying, to no one, that the sun had fourteen minutes left. [194] She was usually right about such things without trying to be. [205] Then she heard a child crying somewhere deep in the corridors, and a man's voice, too loud, promising it was only a little farther. [229]

They must have climbed around the chained gate from the sea road. [241] Maren swore, set down the broom, and did not move for a long moment. [255] Frost came hard here, and it came fast; the trees drank the cold and breathed it back, and anyone still inside when the rime formed would sleep and not wake. [285] She had found three such sleepers in her years here, and buried none of them, because by spring they had leaves. [306]

She could not simply run in after them. [314] The corridors she knew were the near ones, and the birds did not care whose granddaughter she was. [332]

In her apron pocket was the small nut her grandmother had pressed into her hand the morning she walked to the tree, saying only, "Keep this dry." [359] Maren had carried it for thirty years out of spite and sentiment, which in her family were the same muscle. [379] Now, for no reason she could name, she cracked it on the step. [392]

The shell split into eleven pieces. [398] There was no meat inside, only lines: fine scratches on the inner walls, cut with a jeweler's needle. [416] Maren knelt and began to assemble them on her palm, turning each fragment until its scratches met the next, muttering angles the way other people hum. [442] Twenty degrees, no, twenty-two. [446] When the last piece clicked home, she was holding the forgotten blueprint of the labyrinth, and beside each true path was a tiny carved wing. [471]

The birds did not guard the dead ends. [479] They guarded the way out, driving strays toward it, and everyone had spent thirty years running from them in the wrong direction. [501]

She went in with the blueprint cupped in both hands. [511] Where the trees leaned close she turned sideways and held her breath, and where the corridors forked she stopped at the cold stone and breathed on it, long and deliberate, until a pale smear bloomed on the granite. [549] The roots ran through every wall here; a fingertip would have been enough. [562] She shaped each one into a crooked arrow with the angle of her mouth, never letting her lips meet the rock. [583] The arrows were ugly, lopsided, already fading at the edges, and they were the most beautiful thing she had ever made. [604] They would hold until the frost came and erased them. [614]

She found the man and the girl huddled beneath a bird-scarred overhang, the child's hands tucked in his coat. [633] Above them a brass bird screamed and dove. [641]

"Don't touch anything," Maren said. [646] "Follow the fog, and let the birds chase you." [655]

The man stared at her as though she were one of the trees. [668] Then the bird struck the stone beside his head, and he ran. [680]

They went arrow to arrow, the flock clattering behind them like a kitchen falling downstairs, herding them exactly where the fog pointed. [702] They reached the gatehouse step as the first white needles grew across the stone, swallowing every arrow behind them. [721] The birds settled on the wall in a row, ticking as they cooled. [734]

Maren looked toward the eastern stair, where her grandmother's branches caught the last light, and for the first time in thirty years spoke to her without accusation. [761]

"Six minutes to spare, Gran." [766]

Then she went to hang a lantern at the gate. [776]
