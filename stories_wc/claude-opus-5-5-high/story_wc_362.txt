Orla Venn did not float. [5] She wore steel-toed boots to seances, carried a tape measure in her coat, and believed anything worth calling mystical should survive being kicked. [28] That was why she had driven three hours before dawn to the quartz arbor above Callis Quarry, where the white crystal columns leaned together like the ribs of something buried, and where Tom Harrow's green sedan had been parked for eleven months. [70] The mist came off the quarry pond in slow sheets. [80] It threaded through the quartz and turned every facet into a dim, sweating lantern. [94]

Dell Harrow stood by the driver's door with his hands jammed in his pockets. [108] "The police said he just sat here," he said. [117] "Heart gave out, they said, and nobody found him for two days. [129] I want to know if he was scared." [137]

Orla had heard that question in forty kitchens and nine hospital corridors. [149] She had built a modest career answering it, and a less modest private obsession around the theory underneath: that objects absorb emotions the way stone absorbs heat, slowly, and give them back when the air goes cold. [186] Nobody at the Society had ever let her test it properly. [197] A car left untouched in a crystal grove, frosting over every night, was the closest thing to a laboratory the dead had ever offered her. [222]

She took the glass prism from its felt pouch. [231] It was an ordinary schoolroom prism, chipped at one corner, and she liked it for exactly that reason; it lied about nothing. [253] White light went in and honest colors came out. [262] She crouched on the gravel, boots planted, firmly grounded as a fence post, and held it against the frosted driver's window as the first grey sun cleared the ridge. [291]

A thin rainbow fell across the ice. [298] The frost had grown in ferns and feathers, the usual branching lace, but under the prism's light the lace resolved into lanes and eddies, like handwriting seen through water. [327] This was how she communed: not with trances, but with attention, reading a surface until it admitted what had pressed against it. [349]

Then the hush shifted. [353] The arbor had been quiet already, but the quiet changed weight, as if the mist had stopped moving to listen, and the drip from the quartz ribs fell out of time. [384] Orla felt it in her knees first, the way she felt thunder. [396]

The patterns were not old. [401] She followed them with a gloved fingertip held a breath above the glass. [414] Frost forms where warm breath meets cold glass, and these ferns had seeded from a small, clear oval at face height, the size of a forehead resting against the window. [444] But it was on the outside. [450] Someone had been standing here, leaning in, breathing on the glass, night after night. [464]

That was the moment her assumptions shattered, quietly, the way ice does. [476] She had come expecting the car to hold Tom's last hour. [487] It held nothing of Tom at all. [494] It held eleven months of a living man pressing his face to a window, grief warming the glass, the cold writing it down. [517] Objects did absorb emotions. [521] Just not the ones she had been hired to find. [531]

She looked up at Dell, at the red crease across his brow that she had taken for a hat line. [551] "You come here," she said. [556] He did not answer, which was an answer. [564]

Orla had a rule about truth, and it was a grounded, practical rule: say only what the evidence will carry, and let people carry the rest. [590]

"He wasn't scared," she said. [595] "He was waiting for someone, and he was glad, at the end, that someone would come." [611]

It was a lie, and she knew it; the glass had told her nothing about Tom. [627] It was also the truest thing written on the window, because someone had come, every single night, and the frost had kept the record. [651]

Dell's mouth worked. [654] "You can see that?" [658]

"I can see that he isn't alone here," Orla said. [668] She tilted the prism so the colors slid across his hands. [679] "Nothing in this grove has been alone for eleven months." [689]

He laughed, or sobbed, and the hush shifted back, the mist moving again, the drip finding its rhythm. [707] Orla stood, knees cracking, and wrote in her notebook the only line the Society would ever get from her about Callis Quarry: Objects keep what we give them. [735] The dead are only the reason we keep giving. [744]
