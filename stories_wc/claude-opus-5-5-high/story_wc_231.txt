Ottilie Varn kept the Gannet Head light and a ledger of other people's memories, and in the village below they called her mad for both. [25] She wore three coats at once, talked to the lens as if it were a moody cousin, and fed the gulls only on Tuesdays, but she was honest about the trade. [56] People brought her what they could not bear to carry, and she held it for them, a grief or a shame poured into her like oil into the lamp. [85] Borrowed memories, she called them, because some people came back. [95] The stranger did not come back; he climbed into an oarless skiff and let the ebb carry him into the mist. [116] It happened at nautical twilight, that hour when the horizon is still a faint seam and the first stars are good enough to steer by. [141] Ottilie watched from the gallery rail, as she watched everything, too high and too far to do anything but see. [161] The opal dock glowed below her, its old shell-stone planks catching the last blue light so that the whole pier seemed to float on milk. [186] The stranger had been coming there for six evenings, a tall man in a mountain coat, softly persistent as the fog, knocking at her door each night and then saying nothing at all. [219] On the seventh, before he stepped into the skiff, he unpinned something from his lapel, fixed it to the last mooring post, looked straight up at the lantern room, and touched two fingers to his temple and then to his heart. [260] Then the ebb took him, and the mist closed like a book. [272]

Ottilie went down with a storm lantern and a notebook, because a keeper who cannot decode a signal is only a woman standing in a tall room. [299] Pinned to the post was a dried Edelweiss boutonniere, its woolly star gone the color of old paper, tied with a thread of faded wedding blue. [325] She had learned long ago that people are easier to investigate by what they leave out than by what they say, so she began by observing gaps. [352] There was a gap in the dew along the planks where he had stood each night, always at the same spot, facing not the sea but her tower. [380] There was a gap in the boutonniere itself, a missing petal, pulled cleanly, the way a nervous groom might worry one loose at the altar. [405] And there was a gap in her ledger, which she climbed back up to check, sixty-one steps with her knees complaining. [426] Every borrowing was recorded there in her cramped hand: name, date, weight of sorrow, terms of return. [443] Every one but the first page, which had been torn out so long ago that she had stopped seeing its stub. [464]

She sat beneath the turning lens and did the thing she never did, which was to look for the gaps in herself. [486] She knew the smell of mountain snow, though she had never left the coast. [500] She knew a chapel with a cracked bell, and a woman in a gray dress laughing as she pinned a small white star to someone's coat, and the terrible Tuesday that woman went sailing and did not come home. [539] She had always believed them hers, the way one believes in a childhood. [552] Now she saw their seams: in every one of those memories she looked down at the woman from a tall man's height, and the hands were broad, rope-scarred, and not her own. [584] In the chapel, those hands had worried a petal from the boutonniere while the bell cracked out its single note. [604] Her first borrowing, then, the one she had taken so completely that she had torn out even the record, as he must have asked, so neither of them could ever come looking. [636] Yet he had come looking, for six nights, and each night had knocked and then lost his nerve. [654]

She turned the gesture over like a coin: two fingers to the temple, then to the heart. [671] Not take them back, which a dying man might want, but keep them, remember her for me, here where someone can still see the water she sailed on. [699] The boutonniere was the last piece he had kept, and he had left it where it belonged, with the rest of him. [721] Ottilie pinned the dried star to the innermost of her three coats. [733] Then she wrote a new first page, his description in place of a name, and under terms of return she wrote: none; held in trust; witnessed. [759] Below, the opal dock went dark, and she stayed at the rail while the lamp turned, keeping watch over an empty stretch of sea, which was all either of them had ever asked. [792]
