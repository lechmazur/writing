Orsolya Vane had guided visitors through the Museum of Quiet Hands for twenty-two years, and she still began every tour the same way, by asking them to listen. [28] Outside the museum, beyond the carved balconies of the grotto city of Cascadel, the Veil fell six hundred feet in a curtain so thick that no sun had touched the streets in four centuries. [62] This winter, for the first time in living memory, the Veil was silent. [75] It had frozen into a cathedral of blue ice, and through a crack in it no wider than a coffin, the strangers had come. [99] There were forty-one of them, burned out of the valley towns, and they slept now in the museum's lower halls between glass cases of lacework and bellows-organs and hand-lettered hymnals. [129]

Orsolya had let them in because the beekeeper had told her to. [141] Old Tamas had climbed the cliff stair in autumn with his usual crate of wax and honey, but he had not unpacked it. [164] He had pressed it into her arms and said the hives were ash. [177] "Keep whoever reaches you," he told her, and walked back down into the smoke. [191] That was the beekeeper's last visit, and the crate was the last honey in Cascadel. [206]

Now the Council of Wardens smelled of rosewater. [214] They always did; the city was damp, and the wealthy perfumed their collars against mildew. [229] But this week the scent arrived before them at every door, sweet and patient, like a letter no one wanted to open. [251] Warden Castellan had stood in the rotunda that morning, dabbing his lips with a pink handkerchief. [267] "The thaw comes at dawn tomorrow," he said gently. [276] "When the Veil runs again, the crack closes, and anyone without a place in the city registry goes back out through it tonight." [299] He smiled as if offering her a chocolate. [307] "We are not unkind, Madame Vane." [313] "We are simply full." [317]

Orsolya was a historically minded woman; she knew that every art in her cases had died exactly this way, politely, one closed door at a time. [343] Among the strangers were a lacemaker whose pattern had never been written down, a boy who could tune a bellows-organ by ear, and a widow who sang the harvest psalms in the old mode no living choir remembered. [381] Send them into the smoke and the museum would become what it had always threatened to be: a mausoleum with good lighting. [403]

So that afternoon she took her grandmother's opera glass from its velvet case and unscrewed the emerald-tinted lens. [421] The Vanes had founded the museum, and the founding charter hung in the archive, its ink so faded that visitors believed half the page was blank. [447] Orsolya knew better. [450] The first curator had written the final clause in rose-madder ink, faded to the pink of the parchment itself, but beneath green glass the letters rose black as burnt sugar. [480] Her grandmother had shown her once, when she was nine. [490] "Whosoever has broken the lettered bread of this house is of this house, and the house may keep them as it keeps its treasures." [514] Every Vane child had been given a loaf shaped like the first letter of their name on each birthday; Orsolya had thought it merely sentimental until that moment. [542]

She fired the museum's old bread oven, the one in the Hall of Domestic Arts that no one had lit since her great-grandfather's war. [566] She sweetened the dough with the last of Tamas's honey and asked every stranger for their name. [583] The lacemaker braided her L like a bobbin thread; the boy's A leaned like an organ pipe; the widow's M came out of the oven cracked, and she laughed for the first time since the fires. [619] All night the ice groaned overhead, and the halls filled with the smell of scorched sugar and wet stone. [638]

At dawn the Wardens came, rosewater drifting ahead of them, and found forty-one strangers breaking bread among the display cases. [658] Orsolya handed Castellan the lens and pointed to the charter. [668] He read it twice, his handkerchief going still against his mouth. [679] "They are not in your registry," she said. [687] "They are in my collection." [692]

Outside, the Veil began to thaw, first as a trickle and then as a roar that shook dust from the vaults, and the crack sealed itself in white water. [721] Castellan bowed, too politely, and the Wardens withdrew. [729] Orsolya knew charters could be amended, and that sweet smells always come back. [742] But by noon the widow was teaching the harvest psalm to a gallery of schoolchildren, and Orsolya stood at the back, listening, as she always told visitors to do. [771]
