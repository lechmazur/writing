The lights of the city came back the way a fever breaks, in shivers. [14] From the grate beneath the reference stacks, Nell watched the fluorescent tubes stutter on, die, and stutter again, throwing the shadowed library into brief false noon before dropping it back into the dawn. [47] Outside the tall windows, the sky had gone the color of sapphire, and everything in the reading room held still inside it, as if the air itself had been told to whisper. [79]

Nell had lived in the crawl space for three winters, ever since the county cut the archive and her job with it. [101] Nobody upstairs knew. [104] She kept her nest dry, her footprints swept, and her habits exact: she moved only when the boiler covered her sounds, and she never took anything that could not be given back. [136]

Her work, as she still thought of it, lived in the pipe chases, the narrow vertical shafts where the building hid its plumbing and, it turned out, a century of dropped things. [168] Rings slipped down drains; letters fell through gaps in old floorboards; once, a child's tooth in a folded napkin. [187] Nell uncovered each one with a headlamp and a dental mirror, wiped it clean, wrote a card describing where it had been found, and cached it in a labeled cocoa tin until she could slip it, unseen, into the lost-and-found box by the circulation desk. [232] Kindness, she believed, was mostly a matter of filing things correctly. [243]

Overnight the outage had turned the library into a warming center, and cots now lined the periodicals room. [261] Through the grate she could see the old man, Mr. Abara, who came every morning to ask whether the oral history reels had survived the flood of 2009. [289] They had not, the librarians always told him gently. [298] He sat awake on his cot now, humming three notes over and over, stopping, starting, like a man trying a key in the wrong lock. [323]

A volunteer asked if he was all right. [331]

"My wife used to sing it," he said. [339] "I remember the smell of her hands when she did, the almond cream she used, but the tune is gone." [359]

Nell's breath caught. [362]

Two nights before, working the east chase behind the old audiovisual room, she had found half a tape reel wedged on a pipe bracket, its flange snapped, a few loops of ferromagnetic tape still wound around the cracked hub. [401] On the surviving flange, in grease pencil, someone had written ABARA, R., SONGS FOR NNEKA. [416] When she had lifted it to the headlamp, the smell had come up at her: rust and vinegar and warm dust, the exact smell of her father's repair bench, where she had sat as a girl while he coaxed voices out of dead machines. [460] Her fingers had remembered before she did: how to hold tape by its edges, how to dry it slowly against a warm steam pipe, how never to rush. [488] She had cached it in the tin marked EAST CHASE, UNKNOWN, meaning to return it the usual way. [506]

But a box by the desk could not play a song. [517] The only working deck left in the building sat under a dust sheet in the basement, and only she knew how to thread it. [541] To help him, she would have to be seen. [550]

The lights flickered again, and in the dark between flickers Nell pushed up the grate. [565] She climbed out with dust on her knees and the tin under her arm, and walked, slowly, so as not to frighten anyone, toward the cots. [591]

The volunteer stood up. [595] Mr. Abara only looked at her, and then at the name on the flange. [609]

"Rose," he said. [612]

"I think I can make it play," Nell said. [621] "It will take an hour, and I will need someone to hold the flashlight if the power goes again." [640]

He held it for her, in the basement, without asking where she had come from. [655] She spliced leader onto the ragged end, wound the fragment by hand, cleaned the heads with a cotton swab, and threaded the path the way her father had taught her, counting each guide under her breath. [691] Upstairs the grid steadied, and the old motor turned. [700]

Hiss first, like rain on a window, and then a woman's voice, low and unhurried, singing three notes and the rest of the song after them. [726] Mr. Abara closed his eyes and raised one hand to his face, as though he could smell almonds. [744]

When the fragment ran out, he asked her name, and Nell, who had not said it aloud to anyone in three years, told him. [768] Then she went upstairs in the sapphire light to tell the librarians what else was waiting in the tins. [787]
