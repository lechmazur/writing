The tide went out at noon, and Ines went down with it. [12] She worked the sublittoral fringe, that strip of kelp and broken pilings the low water uncovered for an hour, and she moved through it the way she had once moved across a boarding deck: weight low, feet reading the slime, every step a small decision. [57] The other dock hands called her graceful. [64] They did not know where the grace had come from. [74]

Three years had passed since the Stilling, since the morning every raised fist in the world slowed in the air and stopped a finger's width from its target, and no one had yet explained it. [109] Knives turned in the hand. [114] Harpoons fell short. [117] The whalers of Harrow Isle had gone broke inside a season, and the whales, as if they had heard, came back to the shallows to sing. [143]

Now Harrow itself was leaving. [148] Each spring tide took another street. [154] The chapel steps were under kelp; the fish market was a reef. [166] The last evacuation boat would leave at dusk, and Tobiah Crane, harbormaster, widower, and the most stubborn man Ines had ever failed to hit, was sitting on the drowned end of Pier Nine with both arms wrapped around a mooring post. [207]

"Get up," she said. [211]

"No." [212]

She swung at him anyway, an open-handed cuff she had been saving for a week. [227] It slowed, softened, and settled against his ear like a cupped palm. [239] He leaned into it, the old goat, and grinned. [248]

"You always did hit like a mother," he said. [257]

"I used to hit like a cannon." [264]

"I know." [266] "I've heard." [268]

Everyone had heard, though no one said it to her face: that the graceful woman on the docks had once been Red Ines of the Marrow, first mate to Captain Dace, and that she had put more men into this water than the tide ever had. [314] Dace had died the year before the Stilling, and all he left her was the scrimshaw that hung from a cord at her throat: a sperm whale's tooth, yellow as old butter, carved with a woman floating in a whale's open eye. [356] "It opens a shut heart," he had told her, dying, "but only when the deep is singing." [373] She had thought it was one of his lies. [382] Then the whales came back, and she learned otherwise, and she had kept it hidden ever since, because she did not want anyone opening hers. [407]

The water was at her knees now. [414] The fringe was closing. [418]

"Tobiah, the boat." [421]

"Mara's in that churchyard." [425]

"Mara's under forty feet of sea and a crab is wearing her ring." [438]

He laughed, then stopped laughing, and his arms tightened on the post. [450] She could not pry him loose; force had left the world, and she had never learned what came after it. [470]

So she knelt in the kelp, lifted the cord over her head, and pressed the whalebone into his fist. [489] She kept her own hand closed over his. [497]

"Don't you dare," he said. [502]

"Too late." [504]

Far out past the drowned breakwater, something vast exhaled. [513] Then the call came up through the water, through the pilings, through the bones of her shins: a long falling note from the deep, so low it was less a sound than a leaning of the whole sea. [551] The scrimshaw hummed between their palms. [557]

Tobiah's face came apart. [561] "I'm not staying for Mara," he said. [568] "I'm staying because out there nobody knows what I am, and here I'm the harbormaster." [583] "Without the harbor I'm just an old man who's afraid of boats." [595]

The note went through her too, and the words came before she could stop them. [610] "I was never Red Ines," she said. [617] "I was a girl who could dance, and Dace needed someone quick with a blade, and I let him name me." [638] "When the Stilling came, I cried for a week, and it wasn't grief." [651] "It was relief." [654] "I've been hiding the dancer on these docks ever since, afraid she's what I really am." [670]

The whale called again, nearer. [675]

Tobiah looked at her for a long moment, then, one finger at a time, he detached his hands from the post. [696] "Then go be her," he said, "and haul me to that bloody boat." [709]

She did not haul him. [714] She took his arm, and they went up the drowning pier together, and she moved the way she had always wanted to move, not like a blade but like water finding its way around stone. [749] At the ladder he gave the scrimshaw back, and she let it fall into the kelp of the fringe, where the sea was already closing over Harrow. [776]

"Still hit like a mother," Tobiah muttered as she boosted him up the rungs. [790]

Behind them, the whale sang the island down. [798]
