At the Cirrus Library, dusk arrived from below, rising through the clouds like warm water, and Ilse Varga was paid to notice what it touched. [25] Since dreams had become shareable, the stacks had learned to bloom. [36] Every night, thousands of sleepers uploaded what they saw, and by morning the dreams opened on the shelves as pale flowers, petals printed with other people's childhood kitchens and drowned cities and first kisses. [70] Visitors came up the long stair to smell them. [79] Ilse came for the hour when the light went sideways and things that had hidden all day showed their edges. [99] She had the sharpest eyes on the staff, everyone said, though she had not seen her father dying until he was already gone. [122]

Three weeks after the funeral, she noticed the shadow. [131] It stood in the west alcove beside Reading Desk Nine, where a gray river stone had sat as a paperweight for as long as she could remember. [158] The shadow belonged to no one. [164] No patron cast it, no lamp explained it; it simply leaned toward the window, one arm lifted, the way a man reaches for something just beyond a high shelf. [193]

Ilse did not report it. [198] She was softly deliberate in all things, a woman who closed doors with two hands, and she knew what the curators did with anomalies: they filed them, which here meant they let the vines take them. [234] Instead she went down to the old security vault, where the library kept its silent camera reels from before the blooming. [255] She found the west alcove, forty-one years ago, and threaded it beside last night's feed. [270] Two screens, no sound, the same angle. [277]

In the old footage her father was young, thin, his hair still dark, and he carried an infant in the crook of one arm. [301] Her. [302] He set the river stone on Desk Nine, the stone from the bank where she had been born during the flood, and then he turned to the window and lifted his free arm toward the last light, as if asking it for something. [345] His shadow reached with him, fastened at the heel. [354]

On the new screen, the desk, the stone, the lifted arm; the same gesture exactly, frame against frame, but no man. [375]

Ilse sat a long time in the humming dark. [384] She understood, the way you understand a word in a language you have been overhearing for years. [401] In his last months her father had shared every dream he had, nightly, generously, until the stacks were fragrant with his river and his wife and his daughter's small fists. [431] He had kept nothing back. [436] But hope was not a dream; it had no pictures to give away, only a shape, and with nothing private left to hold it, the shape had come unbound and gone home to the place he first made it. [475]

She scrolled back through the recent weeks and saw what she had been too grieving to notice. [492] Each dusk the shadow was thinner at its edges, and each dusk a tendril from the nearest shelf crept closer. [512] Last night a green bud had opened in its lifted hand. [523] It was beginning to metamorphose, to become one more flower anyone could pick, a public thing with no one's outline. [543]

She climbed back up before the sun reached the cloud line. [554] The alcove was gold. [558] The shadow stood where it always stood, reaching, and the bud in its palm had become a blossom, its petals just beginning to print themselves with light. [585]

Ilse did not rush. [589] She lifted the river stone from Desk Nine, cool and heavy, and set it on the floor at the shadow's heel, exactly where his foot had been forty-one years before. [619] Then she stood behind it, and raised her own arm into the same angle, and let her shadow fall across his. [640] For a moment there were two reaching figures on the stone floor, one inside the other. [656]

Then the blossom closed like a fist, folded back into bud, into nothing, and the older shadow drew down into the stone the way water finds the lowest place. [685]

When she picked it up, the gray had darkened, and the grain ran through it in the shape of a lifted arm. [707] She wished, standing in that luminous hour, that she had asked him what he was reaching for. [724] She would never share it, never dream it onto a shelf. [735] Some things kept their shape only by being held. [744] She put the stone in her pocket, where it was warm, and watched the light go down below the clouds. [764]
