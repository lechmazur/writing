Ines Arrow had been lore keeper of the Sunken Stacks for nineteen years, long enough to remember when the city's billboards still showed rain instead of advertisements for it. [29] Now the Consortium had given her until sunrise. [37] At dawn on the Charter's hundredth year, the old law said, the throne in Accord Hall would pass to whoever held the city, unless someone proved the stairway of ascension still existed. [69] For a century the seat had stood unclaimed, a chair of ruby lacquer under a dusty skylight, and children had been told that no one sat there because no one had yet climbed high enough. [104] Ines carried the only piece of it anyone had ever been allowed to touch: a ruby flake that had fallen from the armrest the year she was apprenticed, sealed now in a locket of cloudy glass. [140]

At dusk she made her sally. [146] She did not run, because running drew the patrol drones, and she did not wait, because waiting was how the archive had lost half its floors to damp and decree. [176] She slipped out the flood door with a coil of rope, a flask of solvent, a hand lamp older than she was, and a folded transcript of a lullaby nobody sang anymore. [208] The lullaby spoke of apples behind colored water. [216] Everyone assumed it was nonsense, but Ines had spent two decades learning that nonsense was usually a map someone had grown too tired to explain. [241]

She followed the dry canal past the rusted monorail pylons to where the old prism dam still worked, throwing the last light of the solar towers into a waterfall that fell in bands of violet, amber, and green. [279] Tourists had once paid to photograph it. [286] Now it was just a kaleidoscope nobody looked at. [295] She tested the ledge with her boot before trusting it, tied the rope to a pylon bolt, and stepped through the colors. [317]

Behind the waterfall lay an orchard. [323] Apple trees climbed a hillside in terraces, tended by gardening units so old their chrome had gone the soft gray of pewter, clipping branches with the patience of machines no one had remembered to switch off. [359] The air smelled like a childhood Ines was not sure had been hers. [372]

At the foot of the terraces stood a pavilion, its walls painted the flat Consortium gray that covered every public surface in the city. [396] Ines uncapped the solvent. [400] She worked through the peel of paint layers the way she read damaged books, a little at a time, stopping whenever the color beneath looked fragile. [426] Under the gray was a glossy gold from the Regency years, all crowns and eagles. [441] Under the gold was a layer of blue slogans she half recognized from newsreels. [455] Under the blue, at last, was ruby. [462] She held the locket to the wall, and the flake matched the pigment exactly. [476]

The mural beneath showed the orchard itself. [483] Hundreds of figures, farmers and clerks and children, each planted a tree on a terrace, and the terraces rose together into a staircase that ended at the top of the falls, where a single empty chair faced the city. [522] Beneath it, in the founders' blocky lettering, ran a line: The stair is everyone who plants; the seat is for looking, not keeping. [545]

Ines sat down in the wet grass and laughed until her ribs hurt. [558] The throne had never been unclaimed by accident. [566] It had been left empty on purpose, a reminder carved into law, and the stairway of ascension had been hidden in plain sight as a hillside of apples someone kept forgetting to visit. [599]

She could have run back with the news, but a story told secondhand at dawn would be called a forgery by breakfast. [621] So she photographed the mural with the hand lamp's ancient camera, then climbed, terrace by terrace, recording the brass planting tag on every tree. [645] One of them bore her grandmother's name. [652]

She reached the top as the sky over the solar towers turned the color of weak tea. [669] There was the chair, carved from the same stone as the dam, mossy and plain. [684] Ines did not sit in it. [690] She stood beside it, patched her camera into the old public broadcast relay still bolted to the cliff, and sent the mural, the tags, and the lullaby to every screen in the city just as the sun cleared the horizon. [730]

Below her, the waterfall caught the first light and broke it into every color at once. [746] Then she opened the locket, let the ruby flake fall into the soil at the chair's foot, and started down to find a place to plant. [772]
