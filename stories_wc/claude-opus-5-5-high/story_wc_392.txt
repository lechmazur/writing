The drought ended in October, and three days after the first real rain, Teodoro Reyes drowned in a subway maintenance tunnel that had been dry for two years. [28] Mara Quell went down with her kit at dawn, because that was her job: the city paid her to stand where people died and decide whether they had to. [57] Preventable Death Investigator, the badge said, as if prevention were something you could investigate after the fact. [74]

Her phone buzzed before she reached the bottom of the ladder. [85] Her father. [87] Victor Quell had retired from the Transit Authority six years ago, but he had never retired from her. [105] He had chosen her college, her first apartment, the husband she divorced, and the quiet, careful voice she used when she spoke to him. [129] She let it ring. [133]

The water had receded to her ankles, leaving a brown line on the tiled wall at chest height. [151] She photographed it, measured it, and wrote the number down before she let herself look at the tarp where Reyes had been. [173] Numbers first; grief was not evidence. [179] It was the only rule she had ever made for herself. [190]

The body was gone, but his crew had left what he'd been holding: a tin lantern, rust-bloomed, its sides punched with a pattern of holes that threw a scatter of stars when she clicked her flashlight inside it. [228] The stars were not random. [233] Seven bright holes formed a dipper, and the handle pointed toward a patch of older brick where the tunnel's tile gave out. [255]

Something opened in her like a door she had painted over. [266] She remembered this lantern, or one like it, swinging in a man's hand in a tunnel very like this one, and a voice telling her that people had followed these little stars north, out of the cellars under this part of the city, toward the river and freedom. [314] But whose voice? [317] Her father had brought her to work sometimes when she was small, and she had always believed the story was his. [338] Yet the hand in the memory was dark and knuckled, and it wore her grandmother Ada's copper ring. [356] Ada, who had died when Mara was nine, whose stories Victor had retold so often at dinner parties that they had come to wear his fingerprints. [382] The two memories lay tangled together, and she could not pull one free without the other unraveling. [399]

She set the lantern down and did what she knew how to do. [412] She looked. [414]

The old brick was stained darker than the tile, which meant water had once moved through it steadily, for decades, not in a single flood. [439] At its base, a steel plate had been bolted over an arched opening, the bolts still bright. [456] The stamp read DROUGHT RETENTION PROJECT, and beneath it was the name of the consultant who had designed the program: V. Quell. [478]

She knelt and struck the plate with the lantern's iron base. [489] The sound did not stop at the steel; it reverberated back and back, a long hollow throat running north under the city toward the river, the way the stars had said. [520] For a hundred and seventy years that passage had carried people out and then carried water out, and two summers ago someone had decided no one would ever need it again. [551] Reyes had understood it too: fresh scratches ringed every bolt, where a wrench had slipped in rising water. [569]

Her phone buzzed again. [573] This time she answered, watchfully calm, the way she approached a body. [585]

"Worker error, sweetheart," Victor said, before she had spoken. [594] "Rains like that, nobody could have predicted." [601]

"Grandma Ada predicted it," Mara said. [607] "She told me the water always remembers the way out." [617]

Silence on the line, then the old warning tone: "You're confusing things, Mara. That was my story." [634]

Her hands shook, and she let them; she was done pretending that shaking meant she was wrong. [651] "No," she said. [654] "It was hers, and you only told it louder." [663]

She ended the call and opened her report. [671] Under cause, she typed only what empirical observation had given her, the stained brick, the bright bolts, the waterline, each fact laid down like a stone she could stand behind. [701] Under determination, she typed one word: Preventable. [708]

Then she set her flashlight inside the lantern and climbed toward the sound of rain, a dipper of small stars turning on the wall beside her, pointing the way out. [738]
