Dell Okafor carried his guitar up Sage Ridge the way other men carried sleeping children, slowly and with his whole body listening. [22] Behind him, Posy Hale pushed her grandmother's wheelchair along the gravel path, eclipse glasses bouncing on a string around her neck. [43] It was early March, the season the ranchers called the hush of dreaming roots, when the old sage lay gray and silent above ground while, underneath, something slow was deciding whether to wake. [76]

Marguerite Hale had not recognized her granddaughter in eight months. [86] She called Posy "the nice girl" and asked her, politely, whose child she was. [100] Posy, who was nine, had started finding reasons to stay in the car. [113]

Dell had worked with Marguerite every Tuesday at Juniper House, and he had learned that her memory was not a locked room but a weather system. [139] Some days it cleared. [143] Most days it did not. [148] He had also learned not to promise anything, which was why, when Posy's mother asked if bringing them up the ridge for the eclipse was foolish, he had only said, "It might just be ordinary, and ordinary is allowed." [187]

At the overlook, he set the brake on the wheelchair and tucked a blanket over Marguerite's knees. [204] In her lap, as always, sat the tin locket she would not let the nurses take. [220] Inside it, under a scratched oval of glass, lay a single pale curl of baby hair tied with blue thread. [240] Marguerite touched it constantly, the way people touch a word on the tip of the tongue. [256]

"That's mine," Posy had told Dell once, in the parking lot, not crying. [269] "From when I was a baby. [275] She kept it, and now she doesn't know." [283]

Dell tuned by ear, listening less to the strings than to the people. [296] That was the whole of his method, if it was one: he let his playing go softly adaptive, bending tempo to match Marguerite's shallow breathing, then easing it, measure by measure, toward something steadier. [330] He had seen it slow a panicking veteran and coax a mute toddler into humming. [345] It was not magic, exactly. [350] It was the ordinary kind, the kind that hides in breath and rhythm and waiting, and he believed in it the way he believed in spring. [376]

What frightened him was not Marguerite's forgetting but Posy's learning, too young, that the people you love can go dim and stay that way. [400] He wanted her to leave this ridge still expecting the world to surprise her. [414] Wonder, he thought, was a muscle you kept for a lifetime or lost by nine. [429]

The moon took its first bite of the sun. [438] Posy slid on her glasses and gasped, then glanced at her grandmother as if gasping might be rude. [456]

"You can be excited," Dell said. [462] "Just go slow with it." [467]

The light thinned to pewter. [472] The wind dropped, and the meadowlarks stopped mid-phrase, and the whole ridge seemed to lean toward the dark. [490] Dell shifted into the lullaby Marguerite's daughter had written down for him, a French tune about a moon rocking in a boat. [512] He played it barely above the silence, three notes, then a rest for the quiet to fill. [529]

Totality came like a held breath. [535] A ring of white fire hung over the valley, and everyone pulled their glasses off. [550]

In that strange twilight, Marguerite began to hum. [558] Not along with Dell, but ahead of him, carrying the melody where he had left space. [574] Her thumb opened the locket. [579]

Dell caught Posy's eye and nodded toward the chair, and after a moment the girl knelt, and without quite deciding to, she sang the second verse, the one her grandmother used to sing at bath time. [615] Their voices found each other in the dark, old and young, the song a link neither of them had to remember how to make. [639]

Marguerite looked from the curl to the child. [647] She lifted one trembling hand and touched Posy's hair, which was darker now, straighter, but grew from the same whorl at the crown. [670]

"Ma petite," she said. [674] "You got so big." [678]

Posy did not shriek or cry. [684] She held very still, the way you hold still when a deer steps into the yard, and let herself be touched. [705] Dell kept playing, gently, the way you shelter a match. [715]

Then the sun broke free at the edge of the moon, and the meadowlarks resumed, and Marguerite's gaze drifted back toward the valley, pleasant and far away. [742]

Posy turned to Dell, her face careful and blazing at once. [753] "Will it happen again?" [757]

"I don't know," he said honestly. [763] "But now you know it can." [769]

Beneath their feet, under the gray sage, the roots went on dreaming toward spring. [783]
