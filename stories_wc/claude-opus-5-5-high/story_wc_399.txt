The prophet arrived at dusk, and by morning the whole camp had gone quiet enough to hear the pavilion. [19]
Iset heard it first, as she always did, because she was listening sideways. [32]
The murmuring pavilion stood at the heart of the oldest clearing, its canvas the color of weak tea, and the voices of dead line walkers moved through it like breath through a reed. [65]
No one could make out the words by facing them. [75]
You had to turn a little away, let the sound arrive at the edge of you, and then the sentences assembled themselves: keep the lines slack, never walk angry, the sand forgives slowly. [108]

She wore the half-mask because of the lines. [116]
Twelve years ago she had looked straight at one, full-faced and proud, and the buried light had taken her left eye in a single white stroke. [142]
Now the lacquered mask covered that ruin, and her right eye had learned the only trick that mattered: to see the world through peripheral awareness, trusting what glimmered at its margins. [173]

The lines were how the clearings were conjured. [181]
Beneath the dunes ran the kinetic parapet, a wall of bronze plates older than any map, hinged and humming, forever shifting under the sand. [205]
When a walker traced its faint seams across the surface, barefoot and slow, the plates rose to meet her steps and shouldered the dunes aside, and a clearing opened where a caravan could sleep. [239]
Every clearing held for a season, then the sand returned. [249]
Only the pavilion's clearing had held for three hundred years, because the walkers who made it had spoken into its canvas as they died. [273]

The prophet came to her tent at noon, a thin man with sand in his eyebrows and nothing in his hands. [294]
"The great dune is walking," he said. [301]
"In nine days it covers this clearing." [308]
"I dreamed it, and I have walked beside it, and it does not stop." [322]
The elders had wanted her to call him a liar. [332]
Iset was strangely honest, the way some people are strangely tall; it simply happened to her, and she had long ago stopped apologizing. [355]
"You're right," she told him. [360]
"I've felt the parapet straining for a month." [368]
"It can't hold that much sand." [374]

He seemed surprised to be believed. [380]
"Then we leave, and the voices are lost." [388]
"I came to say it gently, before your elders made it sound like a curse." [403]

That night Iset sat alone in the murmuring pavilion and turned her face away from the voices. [420]
The calm there was the calm of a whisperfield, grass that isn't grass, sound laid down so softly it becomes a kind of ground. [444]
At the edge of her sight, she watched the old sentences drift loose from the canvas like pollen. [462]
They were already fading. [466]
She had not told anyone that, either, because no one had asked. [478]
The canvas was not what held them. [485]
She saw it only because she wasn't looking: each drifting phrase bent downward before it vanished, toward the sand, toward the bronze. [507]
The pavilion had never been the vessel. [514]
The parapet had been listening all along, and the pavilion was only where it chose to surface. [531]

In the morning she found the prophet and asked him to show her the walking dune. [547]
"Why?" he said. [550]
"Because I need to consecrate a path, and you know where the sand is going." [565]
"I don't want to save the clearing." [572]
"I want to preserve the echo of wisdom, and echoes travel." [583]

They walked for six days ahead of the dune, the prophet reading its hunger, Iset reading the seams. [601]
She never looked at the lines directly. [608]
She let them shimmer at her margins, and wherever she stepped she spoke one remembered sentence into the sand, consecrating each footfall the way the old walkers had consecrated canvas: keep the lines slack; never walk angry; the sand forgives slowly. [649]
Beneath her, plate by bronze plate, the parapet hummed the words back and carried them west. [665]

On the ninth day the dune swallowed the pavilion without a sound. [677]
Far from it, at the end of Iset's path, the sand slid open into a new clearing, round as a held breath. [699]
The caravan came down into it at dusk. [707]
No one spoke, and in the quiet, if they turned a little away, they could hear the ground murmuring. [726]
The prophet wept, and asked what it was saying. [735]
"Mostly that I was wrong about the canvas," Iset said, because it was true. [749]
