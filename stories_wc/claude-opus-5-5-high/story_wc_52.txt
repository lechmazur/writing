Marguerite filled the kettle at seven, as she had every morning since the funeral, and set it on the ring with the flame turned low. [25] Low flame meant eleven minutes. [30] She had timed it the first week, standing in her dressing gown with Tomas's stopwatch, because grief without edges had nearly drowned her, and eleven minutes was a thing a person could survive. [63]

The loft above the old winter quarters had one good feature, a skylight the size of a circus wagon door, and this morning it was beaded with rain. [91] Each drop held a small copy of the grey sky, trembled, and slid away to join the others at the frame. [112] Here, then gone. [115]

Tomas used to say that about Bertrand, the old lion, whenever someone asked how he stayed alive in the cage. [135] "You never hold a lion," he told them. [143] "He is here and gone a hundred times a minute, and you only hold the moment before he moves." [162] In the end it was not Bertrand who took him but a slick step on the wagon ladder in March, which Marguerite found almost insulting. [187]

She sat at the workbench under the skylight and unwrapped the ash blank from its flannel. [203] He had bought it the autumn before, a pale block of ash the length of her forearm, meaning to carve Bertrand for their retirement mantel. [228] Ash, he said, was what they made whip handles and tent stakes from, wood that bends before it breaks. [247] He never made the first cut. [253]

So she made the cuts herself, one kettle at a time. [264] That was the rule, and it was hauntingly practical, the kind of rule Tomas would have made for a nervous new cat: eleven minutes of remembering, knife in hand, then tea, then the day. [298] She could contain almost anything inside eleven minutes. [306] She had tried containing it inside days and it had leaked into everything, the groceries, the bills, the way she could not look at sawdust. [331] Now the grief had a kettle's shape. [338]

And every session she asked herself one question before the whistle: what did this morning teach me? [355] She wrote the answers in pencil on the flannel. [364] Buy the cheaper coffee; he was the only one who liked the dear kind. [378] Call his sister before she calls you. [385] Ladders need rubber treads. [389] Love is not a thing you are owed back. [398]

Forty-one kettles had passed through the ash by now. [407] Turning the block under the rain light, she could see them, each session crystallized in the grain like layers of sediment: the angry morning when she gouged the mane too deep, the tender one when she smoothed the shoulder for the whole eleven minutes and cut nothing at all. [456] The time was not gone; it had simply become wood. [466] Bertrand's head had emerged from the top of the blank, great and patient, but his haunches were still raw square ash, still waiting. [489] This morning she meant to free his hind legs. [498]

The kettle began its low tick and mutter. [506] She set the knife against the corner of the block and stopped. [518] From where she sat, with the rust-colored light of the lamp mixing into the grey from above, the lion looked exactly as he had in the ring: half-risen, gathering himself, the instant before he moved. [553] Here, and about to be gone. [559]

She understood then that a finished lion would be a lie. [570] Tomas had never been finished; he had been interrupted, mid-gesture, mid-joke, one boot on a wet rung. [587] The block honored that. [591] It held the part of him that had arrived and the part that never would, and it held them in the same piece of wood, which was more than she had managed until now. [625]

She folded the knife and laid it in the tin with his old whip ferrule, the iron gone to a soft orange crust. [648] Then she took up the pencil and wrote the forty-second lesson on the flannel, in letters smaller than the rest. [668] Some things are finished by being left. [675]

Above her, the rain eased, and the last drops on the skylight caught the first thin sun of the day, each one a bright and trembling world, here and gone. [705] She watched them without needing to keep them. [713] The kettle whistled, and Marguerite rose, and poured, and the steam climbed toward the glass like something released. [731] It was, she thought, the first morning the tea tasted like tea. [743]
