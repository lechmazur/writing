Ines Varo woke on the four hundredth tide of the long sleep, because someone had to learn the dark before the others opened their eyes. [25] She was a sensory map maker, which meant her charts of the coastal cave network were not drawn in lines but in textures: brine thickness, drip rhythm, the way a passage breathed when the sea pushed in. [62] Nine thousand people slept in the alcoves behind her, sealed in resin cradles, waiting out the heat that had made the surface unlivable. [85] Each held one small thing from before. [92] The engineers had called them transitional objects, borrowing the old nursery term, and the sleep doctors had insisted on them: a hand that grips something familiar does not panic in the long fall of dreaming. [127]

Ines checked the sleepers the way she checked the caves, by feel. [139] Her visor laid a thermal skin over every cradle, and because dreaming bodies warm and cool with what they fear or love, the colors were a map of emotion. [168] Contentment glowed a steady amber. [173] Grief sank to violet at the edges. [180] Terror went blue and spread like spilled ink, and blue, left alone, could stop a heart. [196]

In alcove C-19 the blue had reached a boy's shoulders. [206] His tag read Tobiah Ren, age eight, and his right hand was open and empty. [221] The manifest listed his object as an elm leaf fossil, and Ines remembered it, because she had packed the cradles herself: a gray stone the size of a biscuit, pressed with a leaf from a tree that had not grown anywhere for a century. [265] His grandmother had found it on this same coast and told him it was magic. [280] Ines had corrected her gently at the time: it was pumice, volcanic glass so full of air that it would float. [301] The grandmother had laughed and said, "A stone that swims and a leaf that outlived its tree." [318] "Tell me which part isn't magic." [324]

The resin seal on C-19 had a hairline crack at its base, and the floor below sloped toward the sea. [344] A surge must have seeped in, lifted the fossil from his loosening fingers, and carried it out when the water drained. [365]

Ines shut off her visor and let her own map take over. [377] Water from C-19 ran to the Whispering Gut, where the walls hummed at low tide, then down to the Cold Ear, a chamber that tasted of iron and swallowed sound. [407] She had named these places alone over four hundred tides, and naming them had been the only conversation she had. [427] The tide was turning. [431] She felt it in the pressure on her eardrums as she crawled through the Gut, palms reading the wet rock like braille. [453]

In the Cold Ear the pool was already knee-deep and rising, black and silent. [467] Anything sunk there was gone. [472] But the fossil had not sunk. [478] It rode the surface in the far corner, turning slowly, the elm leaf's veins catching her headlamp like the lines of a palm. [501] A stone, floating in the dark, exactly as physics said it would. [513] Ines waded in to her waist and closed her fist around it, and for the first time she understood the grandmother completely. [535] Explaining a wonder did not make it smaller. [543] It only made it true. [548]

Back in C-19 she patched the seal and pressed the fossil into Tobiah's palm. [562] His fingers curled. [565] On her visor the blue held for a long, terrible minute, then thinned at the edges, then gave way to amber, blooming outward from his hand like a lamp being lit. [596] She sat with him until the whole cradle glowed. [605]

Then she went to her desk and looked at the heat map of nine thousand dreamers, and saw what she had missed: a faint blue rim around every single cradle, the same shade, the same shape. [641] It was not terror of the dark behind them. [650] It was fear of the world ahead, the unknown they would wake into. [663] Their objects could carry them through sleep, but nothing had been packed for waking. [677]

Ines opened a new file and began to record, not coordinates, but the Gut's hum and the Cold Ear's iron taste and a stone that floats because it is full of breath. [709] When they woke, the unknown would already have a voice, and it would be hers, telling them that every part was magic and every part was true. [736]
