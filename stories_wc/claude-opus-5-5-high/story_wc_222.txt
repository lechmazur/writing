The sky had been wrong for nine days when Corwin Vell reached the top of the Needle. [17] The sun rose in the north now, pale as a coin under milk, and the stars refused to leave at dawn. [38] Below the icebound spire, the clouds lay flat as a frozen sea, and beneath them the city of Harrow had stopped trusting its clocks. [62] Merchants paid fortunes for anything that could still tell true time. [73] Corwin carried half of such a thing in his coat: the brass lid of a pocket sundial, lifted from a collector who had beaten his servants. [99] He only stole from people like that. [106] It was the one rule that let him sleep. [115] Inside the lid, someone had engraved a line in tiny letters: Ask the Warden for the rest. [132] So he had climbed, roped and spiked, testing every hold twice before trusting it once. [147]

The door at the summit was unlocked. [154] The Warden sat by a stove of blue coals, an old woman wrapped in fox fur, with a chessboard set before her. [176] "You're late," she said. [180] "The sky has been waiting for a thief with manners." [190]

Corwin bowed, because he always bowed, and because her hands were shaking. [202] "I came for the dial, madam." [208] "I won't take it from you by force." [216]

"Good, because you couldn't." [220] She tapped the board. [224] "Play me for it." [228]

The pieces were hand-carved from whalebone and pine, each worn smooth by decades of fingers. [243] When Corwin captured her first pawn, she laughed and twisted its head off. [256] Inside lay a curl of paper. [262]

"Every piece holds a secret," she said. [269] "Take one of mine, I take one of yours, and we swap what we learn." [284]

"My pieces are empty," Corwin said. [290]

"Then fill them." [293] She said it with such solemn mischief that he understood the game was a kind of prayer to her, a thing too holy to be taken seriously. [320]

So they played. [323] Her pawn's paper read: The sun is not broken; the world has tilted. [336] When she took his knight, he wrote on a scrap and slid it inside: I have never kept anything I stole. [357] Her bishop confessed: Every forty years the sky goes wrong, and someone must amend the hours so the people below can plant and sail and bury their dead on time. [387] His rook admitted: I am afraid of heights, and of staying anywhere. [399] Her queen, surrendered almost gently, said: I am too old to do it again. [413]

Corwin looked up. [416] The Warden was smiling, and her eyes were wet. [425] She drew the sundial from her furs, a brass dish with no cover, its gnomon bent at a strange new angle. [446] "The lid was stolen from my last apprentice, long ago," she said. [458] "He carved that message so it would find someone curious enough to climb." [471] "I don't take apprentices of skill, Master Vell." [479] "I take apprentices of wonder." [484]

He fitted the lid onto the dial. [491] It clicked shut like a held breath released. [499] Only when lid met dial did the scratches along both rims line up into words: The unknown is only the next hour. [521]

He could still climb down. [526] The dial alone would buy him a house, a name, a life with walls. [540] Instead he reached for the one piece left untouched, her king, and unscrewed it. [554] Its paper was blank. [558] The Warden offered him her pencil with the grave, sly courtesy of a child passing a secret in church. [577] Corwin wrote: Show me. [581]

They worked through the wrong night by lamplight, charting the stubborn stars, and she taught him to amend the old hour lines one careful scratch at a time. [609] At the north-born dawn, she sent him onto the balcony with the dial. [622] Ice groaned beneath his boots, and the drop to the clouds made his knees go loose. [638] He clipped his rope to the rail, checked the knot twice, and then, because a man cannot hold a strange sky with his fists clenched, he let go of the rail and opened his arms. [673] The wrong sun struck the gnomon, and a shadow fell across his newly amended lines, true to the minute. [692] He flashed the hour down through a rift in the clouds with the polished lid, and far below, the bells of Harrow began, for the first time in nine days, to agree. [724]

Behind him the Warden set a fresh, empty pawn on the board, waiting for his next secret. [741] Corwin, who had never kept anything, decided to keep this. [751]
