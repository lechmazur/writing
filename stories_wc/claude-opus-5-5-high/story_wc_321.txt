Wren Talley had worked the deck of the commuter ferry Marigold for eleven years, and she still whistled when she threw the bowline. [23] "Nobody whistles at a rope," Captain Aldous used to say, and she would answer that the rope never complained. [42] That was before the evening the clocks ran sideways. [51]

It happened halfway across the strait, at the 6:40 crossing, with forty-one passengers reading newspapers and eating pretzels. [69] The wheelhouse clock did not stop; its hands swung flat and pointed east and west together, as if time had decided to spread out instead of moving on. [97] Fog came up like breath on glass. [104]

When it cleared, the Marigold was drifting inside a grotto that had no business existing, a sea cave whose ceiling had fallen open to the stars. [130] The walls glowed faintly, pale as the inside of a shell, and through them Wren could see other ferries. [149] Dozens of Marigolds, translucent as ghosts, each drifting in its own copy of the cave. [164] In one, the passengers were asleep. [170] In another, the deck was empty. [176] In a third, Captain Aldous stood at the rail holding the brass hourglass he used to time the tide turns, and in that version there was still sand in it. [206]

Wren turned to her own captain, who held the same hourglass. [217] It was empty. [220] Not broken, not tipped, simply empty, both bulbs clear as rinsed glass. [232]

"The sand's gone somewhere else," Aldous said quietly. [240] "So have we." [243]

Wren understood, with a deckhand's certainty, that none of those ferries would reach the dock alone; each carried only part of the crossing. [266] If she could pull them back into one boat, everyone would get home whole. [280]

The passengers crowded forward, frightened, phones showing no signal and times that slid across the screen like beads on a wire. [301] Wren clapped her hands once, the way she did when a gangway stuck. [314] "Right," she said. [317] "Every crossing ends at a dock. [323] This one's just taking the scenic route." [330]

She did not feel as cheerful as she sounded, but she had learned long ago that cheer was a tool, like a boathook, and you picked it up whether or not you felt strong. [364]

She climbed to the wheelhouse and took the VHF handset off its hook. [377] Channel sixteen hissed like surf. [382] "Marigold to Marigold," she said, feeling foolish. [389] "Any Marigold, come back." [393]

A voice answered, and it was hers. [400] Then another, and another, a chorus of Wrens on a chorus of decks, each one reporting what she saw. [419] One had stars in the northern gap of the ceiling; one had a crack in the cave wall shaped like a heron; one had a current tugging her hull toward the west. [451] Separately, the reports meant nothing. [456]

Wren spread a chart on the console and began marking them anyway, because a deckhand learns that the sea is never one thing but many things touching. [483] Tide lines, star lines, the grain of the glowing rock: when she laid them over one another, they met. [502] The patterns interlocked like the knots she tied every morning, and at the center of the weave sat a single point where every ghostly ferry would pass at once. [531]

"There," she radioed. [534] "Everybody steer for the heron. [539] Same speed, same heading. [543] We cross together." [546]

"And if it doesn't work?" asked one of her voices, the tired one. [559]

"Then we'll have had a lovely view," Wren said, and meant it. [571]

Aldous took the wheel. [575] Across the grotto, the translucent ferries turned in unison, their wakes braiding into one wake, and the walls brightened until Wren could not tell starlight from stone. [602] The hulls slid into one another like hands into gloves. [612] She felt herself double, then settle, carrying every version's memory at once: the sleeping passengers, the empty deck, the captain with sand still falling. [636] In Aldous's hands the hourglass filled, grain by grain, as if the sand were coming home from a long commute. [656]

The fog took them. [660]

When it lifted, the Marigold was nosing into the terminal at 7:02, clocks upright, forty-one passengers blinking and grumbling that the crossing had felt long. [685] Wren threw the bowline and whistled. [691]

Later, Aldous turned the hourglass over and watched the sand run. [702] "Doesn't stay put, does it," he said. [709]

"Nothing on a ferry does," Wren said. [716] "That's the part that lasts." [721]

The next evening she was back at the rail for the 6:40, the same crossing as always, never the same water twice. [743]
