Nobody on Hask Bluff had spoken a word in forty years, and nobody missed it. [15] Above the cloud floor the wind did the talking, combing the long grass into murmurs, and the bells did the rest. [36] There were three hundred of them strung along the cliff edge on iron gallows, and each one, struck true, told you something you could not argue with. [63] A lie rang flat. [67] Grief rang low and wide. [72] Joy came out as a bright splinter that made the goats lift their heads. [86] The villagers called these tones the symphonies of truth, though they no longer called anything out loud; they simply gathered at dusk, rang what they meant, and listened. [114]

Mira Vell kept the bells honest. [120] She was the bluff's tuner, sixty-one, with ears like cupped palms and a file worn thin as a fingernail. [139] She tuned the old way, by humming a pitch into the bronze and shaving metal until the bell hummed back in perfect agreement. [162] It was the only sound her throat still made, and only for half the year. [177] When the frost came, Mira went seasonally mute. [185] Her voice simply folded itself away each winter, like a moth in a seam, and returned with the thaw. [204] For decades it had not mattered, because bells hold their pitch through cold. [217]

This winter it mattered. [221]

Something had been going gray. [226] When the bells rang true, a faint blue used to shimmer in the air above them, the pale, impossible blue of deep ice, and the children would reach for it. [256] The elders held that it was the color of wonder, and that it came from the glacier melt the founders had used to quench the bronze when the bells were cast. [287] Now the blue came thinner every season, and the youngest children had never seen it at all. [304] Mira had spent the autumn listening and understood why: the bronze was forgetting its cold, drifting a hair off true, and only a new quench of ancient ice could recall it. [335]

There was no ancient ice left anywhere on earth, except the fragment. [347] It had arrived in November with a salt trader who climbed out of the clouds wrapped in felt, carrying a lead box packed with snow. [372] Inside lay a piece of iceberg no larger than a heart, calved from the last floe off the southern coast, cloudy at its rim and clear as a held breath at its core. [405] The trader had rung a bell at the gallows, one long note that meant this is the last, and Mira had traded him her own house for it. [433]

But she could not hum, and without a hum she could not match three hundred bells to their quench before the fragment melted. [456] So she built something that could listen for her. [465]

She worked in the drafty bell shed through the short gray days, and the village came to watch without being asked. [486] Out of spokes, counterweights, and the tuning forks of her dead teacher she assembled a kinetic sculpture, a tall mobile of arms that turned in the wind of the bluff. [516] Each arm carried a fork set to a bell's lost pitch. [527] At the hub, in a copper cradle, she mounted the iceberg fragment. [539] As the machine turned, the forks brushed the bells and sang, and where a bell answered out of true the arm tilted, and a single drop of meltwater ran down a channel onto its lip. [574] Ice older than language, touching bronze, and the bronze remembering. [584]

She wheeled it to the cliff edge on the first clear morning of the year, when the clouds lay below them like a frozen sea. [609] The wind took the arms. [614] The forks began their slow circuit, and the grass whispered, and the fragment shrank drop by drop as the sun climbed. [635] Mira stood with her hands folded, voiceless, feeling each correction in her teeth. [648] By noon the fragment was the size of a plum, and it began to glisten, catching the light of every bell at once until it looked less like ice than like a small, cold star. [683] Then it was gone, and the copper cradle was dry. [693]

A girl of six stepped forward and struck the nearest bell with her fist. [707] The note was clean, a truth with no edges, and above it the air turned blue. [723] Not faint this time, but deep, the blue of a crevasse at dawn, and it spread down the line as the others rang, bell after bell, until the whole bluff stood inside it. [756] The girl reached up with both hands. [763]

Mira, still voiceless, rang the last bell herself, and it came out as a bright splinter. [779] The goats lifted their heads. [784]
