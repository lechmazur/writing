In autumn the cobalt observatory rang with nothing. [8] Leaves ticked against the blue-glazed dome, and every footstep inside came back doubled, as if the building were repeating a secret it had not quite understood. [34] Vesna Oar had embroidered galaxy charts there for thirty years, silver thread on indigo silk, and she had learned that the dome's echo carried whispers farther than shouts. [62] So she made herself the loudest fool in the city. [72] She counted stars aloud and got them wrong. [80] She dropped needles and hummed nursery songs to the telescope. [90] She asked Astronomer Corwin whether the moon was jealous of the candle. [102] He laughed, as men laugh at furniture that talks, and after that he spoke freely in front of her. [121]

The candle stood in a niche beneath the meridian slit, a column of wax taller than a child, notched for the hours. [143] Its flame had not gone out in four hundred years, and every bell in the city was synchronized to it, because the flame did not argue. [169] When the flame reached the midnight notch, a bell-ringer named Pim hauled the great rope in the tower, the other towers answered, and the palace guard changed its watch. [198] Pim was deaf. [201] Nobody asked him anything; they only pointed at the candle. [211] Vesna had stayed thirty years for one reason: to listen for stories like his, the ones no one bothered to hear, and to stitch them invisibly into her charts, a knot for every silence. [245]

On the eve of the Regent's jubilee, she heard a different story. [257] Corwin and Captain Maddox stood at the far rail, their voices folding up the curve of the dome and falling into her lap like dropped thread. [283] They said nothing openly treasonous. [288] They spoke of wax, of a knife, of how an hour was only a matter of where one drew the line. [309]

Vesna embroidered the way she always worked, through adjacent possibilities. [319] When a star was hidden by cloud, she did not guess at it; she stitched its neighbors and let the empty place describe what belonged there. [345] Now she stitched the neighbors of their words. [353] Wax and a knife meant the notches. [360] An hour moved meant the bells would ring early. [369] Early bells meant the old guard would march off before the new guard arrived, leaving the palace gates open to whoever waited in the dark with Maddox. [396] And the one blamed afterward would be the deaf boy who rang too soon. [410]

When the men left, she shuffled to the niche, giggling, and ran her fingers down the candle as if admiring it. [431] The notches had been freshly recut, every one shifted a thumb's width higher. [444] The flame would touch midnight an hour before the sky did. [455] She could not snuff the candle; the whole city would take it as an omen and riot, which might suit Maddox even better. [478] But the sky itself could not be recut. [486] She knew exactly when the Pleiades would cross the meridian slit, because she had stitched it there a thousand times. [506]

She climbed the tower stairs with a basket of mending and found Pim sitting beside his rope, watching the candle through the far window. [530] She did not speak; speaking had never helped him. [539] Instead she unrolled a small chart she had sewn that afternoon: the candle drawn in gold, a false notch crossed out in red, and above it the Pleiades, stitched touching a slit of black silk. [574] Then she lifted a taper she had lit from the eternal candle, carrying its flame like a borrowed word. [593] She pointed to the taper, then to the dome, and pressed her hand over her heart: wait for my light. [613] Pim studied the red thread a long time. [621] Then he nodded, slowly, like someone who had long suspected he was being used and had never been asked. [640]

At the false midnight, the flame kissed the notch. [649] Corwin looked up at the tower and waited. [657] The bell did not ring. [662] The silence spread across the city, and the dome gave it back, again and again, an echo made of nothing, while the old guard stood at the gates and wondered. [692] Maddox's men, crouched in the lanes, grew restless, then careless, then visible. [704]

An hour later the Pleiades slid into the slit, and Vesna raised the flame high on the gallery. [722] Pim pulled. [724] Every tower answered in true time, and the new guard marched out to find the conspirators already standing in the torchlight, waiting for gates that had never opened. [752]

Corwin found Vesna in the dome afterward, humming, unpicking a knot. [763] "Foolish woman," he whispered. [767] "Yes," she said, and the dome said it back to him until he ran. [781] Then she threaded a new needle and began to stitch Pim's story in plain sight. [796]
