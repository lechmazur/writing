The fog came down off the ridge after midnight and settled inside Ines Varga's workshop as if it had been invited. [21] Through the broken panes of the roof, stars hung close and unblinking, and every jar and wire cage below them wore a skin of silver droplets. [47] Nothing moved except her hands. [52]

On the bench lay the crate Tomas had brought her three weeks ago, the one stenciled CONFISCATED, URGENT CARE, the one she had cried over when she first lifted its lid. [83] Inside it had been a panther chameleon, starved to the color of ash, missing the last inch of its tail. [103] Tomas had wept too, telling her about the smugglers he'd intercepted at the coast, and the village had pooled its money so the sanctuary could care for the animals he kept rescuing. [135]

This afternoon the ranger from the district office had shown her the photographs. [148] Tomas at the coast, yes, but loading crates, not seizing them. [159] Her sanctuary's signature on permits she had never signed. [168] The rescues had been a laundry, and she had been the soap. [180]

The ranger needed proof that the animals had passed through Tomas's hands before they ever reached hers, and he needed it by dawn. [203] So Ines was trying to reconstruct the chameleon's journey the only way the animal had recorded it: by shedding layers. [223]

Chameleons do not slough off whole like snakes. [231] They come away in flakes and ribbons, and a stressed juvenile sheds every few weeks. [246] Pressed into the crate's damp lining, under a wad of newspaper, she had found them: four translucent strata, each paler than the last, each stuck to a different dated page. [276] Four sheds meant months, not the two days Tomas had sworn to. [288]

She peeled them apart with a wet brush, slow as breathing, and laid them in order under the starlight. [307] With each one she felt something peel from herself as well. [318] The belief that anyone who cradled a frightened animal must be kind. [330] The belief that she could see a liar in the eyes. [341] The belief that gentleness was a kind of armor. [350] Her convictions had been thin as these sheds, and just as easy to mistake for the living creature. [368]

The last piece was the tail tip. [375] She had found it wedged in the crate's hinge, dried into a tiny green spiral no bigger than a fingernail, and she had kept it in a vial because throwing it away felt like a second injury. [412] Tonight, under the lens, she saw what she had refused to see before. [425] The skin still clinging to it matched the oldest layer, not the newest, which meant the tail had been crushed in this crate months ago, long before any rescue. [454]

The door creaked. [457]

Tomas stood in the fog with his cap in his hands, and she understood that he had come for the crate. [478]

"My brother owes them," he said. [484] "If I stop, they take his boat, maybe his hands." [494]

Ines did not doubt it. [499] That was the terrible part; she believed him, and she had believed him before. [513]

In its cage by the window the chameleon shifted on its branch, and in the mist its skin went quietly vivid, turquoise rising under rust like a banked coal catching air. [544] It had not been ash-colored for a week. [552] It was healing without its tail tip, balancing a little crookedly, but balancing. [565]

She had spent the night wondering which of two women she would have to become: the fool who forgave, or the stone who didn't. [589] Watching the animal grip its branch, she saw there was a third way. [602]

"The sheds go to the ranger at dawn," she said. [612] "I won't hide them for you." [618]

His face folded. [621]

"But I'll drive you there myself, before the sun is up, so you walk in and tell him first, about your brother and the men who hold his debt." [650] "A man who confesses can bargain for protection; a man who is caught cannot." [664]

"And you?" he asked. [668]

"I'll sit beside you while you say it." [676]

She slid the tail tip back into its vial and set it with the sheds, the whole small record of harm in a row. [700] For a long time neither of them moved. [708] The mist hung glittering between them, and the stars held still above the broken roof, and the chameleon, patient in its new colors, waited to see what they would become. [738]

Then Tomas set his cap on the bench beside the vial, and Ines reached for her keys. [755]
