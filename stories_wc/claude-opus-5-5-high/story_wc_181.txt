Odile Marsh had been curator of the Lanthorn Orchard for eleven years, and she was still bewildered by it. [19] The asteroid was a lonely lump of nickel drifting far past the shipping lanes, yet its crust split every spring for trees whose blossoms burned like small blue stars. [48] Nobody had planted them. [52] Nobody could say what spring meant on a rock that never faced the same sun twice. [68]

The orchard's one building held a greenhouse, a vault, a cot, and a tiled washroom where Odile did her best thinking. [89] She had understood the irrigation cycle while brushing her teeth, and the grafting schedule while sitting on the lid with her head in her hands. [114] Bathrooms were the only rooms that closed completely, she decided, and a closed room let a thought finish. [132]

In the vault lay the Ledger, a book of curators, and in a velvet tray beside it lay the cameos. [152] Each was carved from the shell of the orchard's fruit, a pale green nut split like a pistachio, and each bore the profile of a curator. [178] The last slot held a blank, smooth as a fingernail, waiting for whoever came after her. [194]

The trouble was that nobody ever came after anyone in the usual way. [207] Odile had inherited the orchard from a woman named Wren Marsh, whose signature filled the Ledger's first page, and who, according to the family records Odile later dug up, would be born sixty years after Odile died. [244] Her great-granddaughter, apparently. [247] The names between the first and last page ran every direction: grandsons bequeathing to grandmothers, a cousin handing the keys to an ancestor who had never seen a starship. [276] The orchard's inheritance moved like its roots, sideways and backward, knotting the family into one tangled trunk. [293]

Beneath her signature, Wren had inked a single warning: "The rule is simply complicated; do not read the last page." [313] The last page was sealed with resin from the trees, and it hummed faintly when Odile touched it. [331]

For eleven years she obeyed. [336] She pruned, she catalogued, and she inquired of the trees in the only way they answered, by pressing her palm to the bark and asking aloud whose face belonged on the blank. [368] The blossoms only brightened, which felt like laughter from something too large to be unkind. [383]

But her lungs had begun to rattle, and the station medic, three weeks away by courier, had used the word months. [404] If no face was carved, the margins warned, the orchard would seal its crust and sleep. [420] Odile wanted, more than she had wanted anything, to know what the last page knew. [435]

So she took the Ledger into the washroom, ran the tub until steam blinded the mirror, and cut the resin with her grafting knife. [459]

The last page was blank. [464]

She laughed until she coughed, then sat on the tiled floor, furious and oddly relieved. [479] Then she looked up. [483] On the fogged mirror, letters were surfacing where no finger had drawn them, backward, in handwriting she knew because it was her own. [506] The words read: "Carve Wren; she gave it to you because you give it to her." [522]

Odile sat very still while the tub overflowed and soaked her skirt. [534] That was the breakthrough, and like all her bathroom breakthroughs it was obvious once the door was shut. [552] The last page was not a secret someone had hidden from her; it was a page she had not written yet. [573] The inheritance did not flow down a family like water; it circled, like sap, and every heir was both giver and gift. [595] Simple: give the orchard to the one who gave it to you. [607] Complicated: she would never meet the girl. [614]

She carved through the night by blossom light, working from the only likeness she had, the curve of her own jaw and her mother's nose, guessing at the rest. [643] The pistachio shell took the knife gently, as though it had been waiting to be someone. [659] At dawn the face looked back at her, young, stubborn, a little bewildered. [672]

Odile set it in the empty slot, then opened the Ledger to its last page and wrote, in the hand she had seen on the mirror, the words she already knew. [703] Outside, the orchard blazed brighter than she had ever seen it, beautiful and unsettling, like a promise kept by someone who has not been born. [728] On the first page, Wren's signature glistened as if it had just been written. [742]
