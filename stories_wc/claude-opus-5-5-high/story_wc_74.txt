<story>
Nell Varga had not slept properly in eleven years, so she gardened at night. [14] Her greenhouse sat on the roof of a laundry in the Ninth Ward, where the smog came down so thick that the streetlamps looked like saffron smeared on wet paper. [44] Inside the glass, the air was filtered and still, and crocuses bloomed in trays, their red threads glowing under grow-lamps she kept dimmed for her own tired eyes. [72] Across the alley, one floor lower, was the window of Room 4 at the Saint Ambrose care home. [90] Mr. Heng lived there now. [95]

Two winters ago he had been a stranger shivering under the laundry awning during an acid rain, his paper portfolio clutched to his chest, and Nell had brought him up the fire stairs to wait it out among the flowers. [135] She had made him tea. [140] He had written her a single character in an ink that smelled of pine soot, and told her it came from a script his village had used for nine hundred years and nobody alive could read anymore except him. [179] "It means the rain was shared," he said, and left her his brush. [192] It was an old calligrapher's brush, bamboo-handled, its tip bound from the pale winter hair of some animal and dipped, he claimed, in powdered mother-of-pearl, so that under moonlight the point shone like a small crescent. [228]

Last month he had suffered a stroke. [235] The nurses said he no longer spoke English, or anything, and slept eighteen hours a day with his face toward the glass. [257] One nurse, leaning out for a cigarette, had told Nell the family was far away and that strangers did not visit, because strangers did not remember. [283]

Nell intended to prove her wrong. [289]

She was not a woman given to miracles; she was a woman given to lists. [304] In a ledger by the potting bench she recorded each night the phase of the moon, the hour the smog thinned enough to let it through, and the glyph she had managed to recover. [338]

Recovering was the strange part. [343] When the waxing moon found a gap in the haze and fell across the brush, Nell's sleepless mind loosened into daydream, and in the daydream Mr. Heng sat beside her among the crocuses, guiding her wrist through strokes she had never been taught. [386] She did not decide whether it was memory, exhaustion, or something the brush carried. [400] She only noted that the brighter the moon, the longer the daydream lasted, and the more characters she surfaced with, wet on the paper. [424]

Night one of the crescent gave her one glyph. [433] Night four gave her a line. [439] By the first quarter she was painting whole sentences, large as doors, in black on the inside of the greenhouse panes that faced Room 4, so that, lit from behind by the grow-lamps, the words hung dark against the glow for anyone across the alley. [484] She did not know what most of them said. [493] She trusted that he would. [498]

The nurse thought she was mad, and said so, and then stopped saying so, because on the ninth night Mr. Heng's eyes opened for eleven minutes and stayed on the window. [529] On the tenth, his lips moved. [535] Nell wrote it all in the ledger, under the date, beside the moon's slow fattening, and did not allow herself to call it anything yet. [560] Hope, she believed, was a thing you measured before you trusted it. [572]

On the thirteenth night the moon rose nearly round, and a wind off the river scraped the smog thin as muslin. [593] Light poured across the bench. [598] The brush's tip burned white, and Nell fell into the longest daydream yet, the old man's hand warm over hers, the two of them painting not a word but a whole afternoon: rain drumming the roof, a teacup steaming, a stranger laughing at the red threads of the crocuses. [647]

When she surfaced, the pane in front of her held a single enormous character. [661] She knew this one. [665]

Across the alley, a light snapped on in Room 4. [675] The nurse was at the window, waving both arms, and behind her Mr. Heng was sitting up, roused and trembling, his breath fogging the glass. [700] With one slow finger he drew the same character in the mist, backward from where Nell stood and perfect, the one that meant the rain was shared. [727]

Nell opened the ledger. [731] Under the date she wrote the moon's phase, the hour, the glyph, and then, in her own plain hand, a single line of evidence: He remembered. [757]

Then, for the first time in eleven years, sitting among the saffron threads in the quiet, she slept. [775]
