When the guardianship ended, the Order of the Pass took back its bells, its ledgers, and its keys, and left Maren Oddvik only the thing no one else would touch. [30] She carried it up the Thunder-Echo Range in a case of oiled goatskin, strapped against her spine like a second backbone. [51] The range was named for what storms did there. [60] A single crack of thunder over the high walls would fold back on itself a dozen times, each echo thinner, until the last one slipped between the canyon's layered stone and into somewhere else. [94] Keepers had always known that sound could go where bodies could not, and that a skilled player could follow it. [114] Maren had been skilled. [118] That was what she could not forgive. [125]

A year ago her apprentice, Teodor, had walked the seam beside her while she played, and she had pushed the note too hard, wanting the passage wide enough to show him everything. [157] The thunder answered her. [161] The echoes multiplied, the stone shivered, and when the ringing cleared she stood alone on the dry riverbed with the flute hot in her hands. [186] The Order called it a tragedy and closed the pass. [196] Maren called it a failure, which was worse, because failures had causes, and causes could be learned. [213]

Now she knelt on the same riverbed, where flash floods had left pale driftwood scattered across the gravel like the bones of some long animal. [238] The canyon held its breath around her. [245] No wind, no birds, only the enormous hush of walls that had been listening for ten thousand years. [263] She had spent the autumn charting echoes. [270] Each morning she clapped once and walked to where the returning sound seemed loudest, and there she laid a branch of driftwood, its narrow end pointing toward the next echo's landing. [301] Teodor had laughed when she first taught him the method, calling it a game for shepherds, but he had learned it anyway, because she insisted. [326] Now the markers spiraled across the riverbed, a map of how sound travelled here, and at the spiral's heart lay the spot where he had vanished. [352]

Something was wrong with the map. [358] She had known it for weeks without understanding it. [367] Every marker pointed inward, toward the center, as echoes should. [377] But the final echo, the one that slipped between worlds, did not travel inward at all. [393] It reversed. [395] The riverbed ran through both worlds, the old keepers wrote, the way one thread shows on both faces of a woven cloth. [417] On the far side, everything she had built would read backward. [428] If Teodor had tried to come home by the markers she had taught him, he would have walked away from her, again and again, following her own careful arrows deeper into the other place. [462]

Her hands were shaking as she went from branch to branch, turning each one end for end. [479] It took until dusk. [483] When the spiral pointed outward, toward the canyon mouth and the road home, she sat at its center and began to uncase the flute. [507] The goatskin peeled back slowly. [512] The gilded reed caught the last light, its gold leaf worn thin where generations of keepers had pressed their fingers. [532]

The other lesson of her failure had taken longer to harvest. [543] She had believed the seam opened to force, because thunder was force. [555] But thunder was not what crossed. [561] Only the last, faintest echo crossed, the one almost too small to hear. [574] So she breathed into the flute without pushing, a note so soft it seemed to sink rather than rise. [593] She held it, and the canyon took it, and something began to gather. [606] The silence grew heavier, layer on layer, the way snow builds on a high slope before it lets go, avalanchingly quiet, until the air itself seemed to lean. [634] The stone between the strata darkened, as though a weaver had parted the warp. [648]

She did not walk into it. [654] That had been the rest of the lesson: a keeper holds the door, she does not rush through it. [673] She played on, barely breathing, while the stars came out over the Thunder-Echo Range and her arms burned. [691]

Near midnight, gravel shifted at the far edge of the spiral. [702] Then a footstep, and another, moving inward from marker to marker, slow and careful, the way she had taught him. [722] Maren did not stop playing. [727] She did not look up until a thin, sunburned hand came to rest on the driftwood beside her, and a voice, hoarse from a year of shouting into echoes, said her name very softly. [761] Only then did she lower the flute, and let the canyon have its hush again. [776]
