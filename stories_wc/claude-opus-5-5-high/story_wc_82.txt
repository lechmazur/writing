The tea on Hollow Isle is picked at dusk, when the leaves glow faintly with whatever the pickers dreamed the night before. [22] Oriel Vane had made lanterns for the terraces for thirty years, and she had never once cried in front of anyone. [43] Her son's shadow detached nine days after his funeral, which was two days late, the way Pell had always been late. [64] It peeled from the floor of his empty room like wet paper and slipped out into the tea rows, where it stood among the bushes and would not leave. [93] On Hollow Isle, a shadow that lingers means the grief holding it is unfolded. [107] The custom is simple: the mourner folds a thousand paper cranes, and the shadow carries them to the glass basilica at the island's crown, and both are gone by morning. [137] Oriel folded two hundred cranes in a week. [145] Each one came out perfect, crisp-winged and empty, and each one the shadow let fall through its fingers into the mud. [166] She understood the problem the way she understood a lantern that would not draw: there was nothing inside to burn. [186] Her grief was real, but she had packed it so tightly that none of it reached her hands. [204]

So she climbed toward the basilica, because the basilica remembers. [214] Every dream dreamed on the isle drifts upward in sleep and settles into its panes as colored frost, layer on layer, a century of nights stacked like leaves in a jar. [245] The keepers let no one read it. [252] They said the stair inside descends while ascending, and whoever climbs it toward the top of the record walks down into the bottom of themselves. [277] Oriel was crafty, and she was tired of being careful. [287] She brought the keepers a lantern as a gift, a beautiful thing of pierced brass, and when they lit it in their lodge its smoke was sweet with valerian from her own garden. [320] They slept, and she took the stair. [327]

It rose in a spiral of green glass, and with every step up, the light around her thickened, as though she were sinking through a pond. [353] The panes burned with other people's dreams: a picker flying over the clouds, a widow dancing, a child counting stairs that never ended. [376] The higher she climbed, the heavier her chest became, until she was on her knees beneath the topmost window, and there was Pell. [399] His last dream was clouded, crusted over by the frost of every sleeper since. [413] The topmost window was not glass but one great faceted stone, and Oriel took out the chamois and jeweler's rouge she kept for lantern lenses and polished it the way a gem must be polished, in small patient circles, breathing on each facet to warm it. [459] Facet by facet, the dream cleared. [465] He was small again, maybe six, sitting in her workshop while she cut paper for lantern shades, and he was trying to fold a crane and failing, and she was laughing at him, and he was laughing too. [503] That was all he had dreamed, the night before the fever took him. [516]

The grief came up through her then, bright and unbearable, and she did not pack it away. [533] She lit the small lantern at her belt, set it behind the polished stone, and held a sheet of tea paper against the far side, and the dream poured through the gem like sun through a burning glass, scorching its colors into the fibers. [577] She did it again, and again, sheet after sheet, until her fingertips blistered and her face was wet and the image of her son laughing had been printed a thousand times. [608] Then she sat on the stair, which went on carrying her down even as it rose, and began to fold. [628] Every crease transformed the flat, scorched picture into something that wanted to fly, the laughing boy bending into a wing, the laughing mother into a beak. [654] She wept the whole time, and her folds were not perfect. [665] They did not need to be. [671]

At dawn the keepers woke and found her at the basilica doors with a thousand glowing cranes heaped in her apron, and Pell's shadow waiting on the path below. [700] It held out its hands. [705] This time it did not let a single one fall. [715] It carried them up the stair, into the green glass, and as it climbed it grew fainter, going down into the root of the island even as it rose toward the light. [747] Oriel watched until the topmost window flared once, gold, like a lantern catching. [760] Then she went down to the terraces, where the pickers were waiting, and for the first time in thirty years she let them see her cry. [786]
