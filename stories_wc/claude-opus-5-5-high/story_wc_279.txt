The mist in the grotto was the color of old lilac, and Pim did not trust it. [17] She had chalked sidewalks in Vell for twenty years, drawing grinning moons and dogs with wings, and she knew a mood when she saw one. [42] This one was a held breath. [48]

It was the ninth day of the Unlearning, when the valley forgot what it had decided to forget. [66] Every generation chose one thing to set down, and this time the council had chosen grief: the flood, the drowned quarter, the names of the ninety who had not climbed the hill in time. [100] The monks of the grotto had spent the season building a sand mandala of those names, and tonight they would sweep it into the underground stream, and by morning no one would remember why the lower streets smelled of silt. [140]

Pim had come because her pockets would not stop whispering. [150] In the left one, wrapped in a handkerchief, was a flake of blue tile she had pried from the ruin of the Whispering Gallery after the water went down. [179] The Gallery had been a dome where lovers stood at opposite walls and heard each other breathe; the tile still carried fragments, a laugh, half a promise, someone saying Mira, Mira, come up. [212] Since the Unlearning began, the voices had grown louder, as if the flake knew what was coming. [229] She wanted the clutter of voices to mean something before it was swept away. [243]

The grotto was crowded. [247] Families stood shoulder to shoulder on the wet stone, and the cave did what it had always done, gathering every murmur and carrying it along the curved ceiling until the whole chamber hissed like surf. [282] Pim watched faces instead of listening. [288] The council woman, Aud, kept touching her collar where a locket used to hang. [302] A boy tugged his father's sleeve and was hushed too quickly. [313] The eldest monk, Tobin, smoothed his robe three times before lifting the broom, and his eyes kept sliding to the eastern quadrant of the mandala as though something there had moved. [344]

Pim edged closer. [347]

The mandala was a wheel of colored sand, each name a tiny glyph, the ninety arranged in rings around a pale center. [369] But the rings were wrong. [374] Grains had drifted in fine threads, crossing from glyph to glyph, as if the grotto's endless whispering had been nudging them for weeks. [397] The mess looked like vandalism, or weather. [404] Pim, who had spent her life turning cracks in pavement into rivers, saw lines. [418] A thread of red ran from the baker's name to a child's; ochre joined two sisters; blue looped outward from the glyph for Mira to a name Pim did not know, and then kept going, off the edge of the cloth, toward the crowd. [462] It ended at Aud's feet. [467]

Tobin raised the broom. [471]

Pim had never interrupted a ceremony in her life; she preferred to leave her opinions on the ground in pastel and let rain decide. [495] But she interjected anyway, her voice thin against the hiss. [505] "Wait. [506] Everyone, please, just stop talking." [511]

It should not have worked. [516] But one by one, the families quieted, not from obedience but from the peculiar contagion of a room noticing itself, and the silence spread outward like a ring in water until the whole grotto held it. [552] No one had planned that hush; it simply emerged, and in it the ceiling had nothing to carry except what was already there. [575]

Pim unwrapped the tile flake and held it up. [584] In the stillness its whisper traveled the curve of the cave and fell, clear as a bell, over the mandala: Mira, Mira, come up. [608]

The blue thread trembled. [612]

Aud made a sound that was not a word. [621] "That was my voice," she said. [627] "On the stairs. [630] She was still inside." [634]

Pim looked at the threads again, at the way they bound the dead to one another and then, stubbornly, to the living. [656] "The sand isn't ruined," she said softly. [663] "It's a map of who was holding whose hand." [672] "If you sweep it, you won't just forget them. [681] You'll forget each other." [685]

Tobin lowered the broom halfway, looking to the council woman. [695] Aud's hand found her collar, found nothing, and stayed there. [705] The lilac mist pressed closer, cold and patient, and Pim felt the unease of a choice not yet made, of a valley deciding what it could bear. [732]

Then Aud knelt, took the stick of lavender chalk Pim offered without a word, and began to trace the blue thread onto the stone, where no broom was meant to reach. [763] Around them, the hush held, and in it, softly, other families began asking for chalk. [778]
