The liquidators had left the Halvorsen orchard office the way locusts leave a field: stripped, tidy, and humming with absence. [20] Ines Varga found the shredder bins behind the filing cabinets, where no one auditing assets would think to look, because paper confetti has no resale value. [46] She had spent eleven years dismantling other people's certainties: the moon-base hoaxes, the poisoned-well panics, the whispering networks that swore every dust storm was engineered. [71] Most of those stories were false, and she said so in print, and people hated her for it. [89] The Halvorsen curse was different, because people loved it. [98]

Everyone in the settlements knew the tale. [105] Forty years ago, a picker had turned up a diamond in the orchard soil, a stone the size of a thumbnail with a black inclusion curled through its heart like a sleeping worm. [138] The picker died within the year, coughing blood. [146] The next owner, a foreman, died the same way, then the one after. [159] The Widow's Eye, they called it, and the company kept it in a glass case in the lobby as a warning and a marvel, proof that the orchard was blessed and punished by forces nobody could sue. [196] The case was empty now, its lock pried. [204]

Ines spread the shreds across the conference table and began. [214] Outside, the storm season had arrived, and the windows rattled with ochre grit that clicked her dosimeter into a nervous stutter. [235] Beyond the glass, the apple trees stood green and heavy, absurdly alive in a valley where nothing else grew. [254]

She worked by texture as much as text. [262] The company used two papers: cheap gray stock for routine memos, and a cream bond, watermarked, for anything the founder signed himself. [284] She sorted the cream strips first, matching the drift of the typewriter's slightly raised letter e, the coffee ring that bled across three fragments, the fold lines that told her which pages had been mailed and which had been kept. [324] By the second night she had four letters taped together, and they did not mention curses at all. [342]

They mentioned the ring. [346]

"Rotate crews through the ring no longer than six weeks," one read. [358] "Men who ask questions should be reminded of the stone." [368] Another, to an engineer, was worse: "Your survey confirms the stillpoint holds, but keep it private, because this orchard's reputation depends on mystery, not meteorology." [393]

Ines sat back and looked out at the trees. [402] She had walked the rows on arrival and noticed things she had not yet let herself connect: the trunks at the valley's center stood perfectly upright, while those along the outer rows leaned inward, and the dust drifts piled not evenly but in a crescent, a pale bank circling the orchard like a moat. [456]

Now she intuited the shape of it before she could prove it. [468] The ridges around the valley bent the storms into a slow spiral, and at its center lay a hidden stillpoint, a column of calm where the air barely moved and the fallout never landed. [502] The trees thrived because they lived in the eye. [511] But everything the spiral carried had to settle somewhere, and it settled in the ring. [526] The pickers who cleared that crescent of drifted dust breathed it for weeks. [539] The first man to find the diamond had been digging there, and so had every owner after him, because the stone passed from hand to hand among ring crews, the only men desperate enough to keep it. [576]

The curse had never killed anyone. [582] It had only explained the killing, gracefully, in a way no widow could take to court. [598]

On the third morning the storm broke, and Ines walked out with her dosimeter held before her like a lantern. [618] Its clicking quickened as she crossed the pale crescent, then faded almost to silence among the upright trunks. [636] At the center, beneath the oldest tree, the soil had been freshly turned. [649] She knelt and brushed it aside and found the Widow's Eye wrapped in a liquidator's handkerchief, hidden by someone who had believed enough to steal it but not enough to carry it home. [682]

Ines held it up to the still air. [690] The black flaw was only carbon that had failed to become brilliant, a small, ordinary imperfection. [706] People would be angry when she published, she knew; a curse asks nothing of anyone, while negligence asks for names. [726]

She pocketed the stone, walked back through the ring with her mask sealed tight, and at the conference table began typing the first name from the rotation lists. [754]
