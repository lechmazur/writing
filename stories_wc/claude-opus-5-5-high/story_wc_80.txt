The drawbridge rose at seven, as it always did, and with it went the last sound of the mainland. [19] Ilse Marrow waited until the chains stopped groaning before she untied the skiff. [32] She was the firm's only shore surveyor, which meant the Harbor Board paid her to measure what the sea had taken and to recommend what should be surrendered next. [61] Tomorrow they wanted her verdict on the Tessaly Pavilion. [70] Tonight she wanted to hear it first. [77]

The pavilion stood a hundred yards off the breakwater, drowned to the waist, its copper dome gone green and its dance floor lost under four feet of brackish water. [106] At dusk the sun lay across the lagoon like a coal someone had forgotten to bank, and the whole ruin burned without a sound. [130] Ilse rowed into that quiet fire and tied off to a column carved with lilies. [145] She did not hurry. [149]

People in Vell said she had no feeling in her, that she could stand on a collapsing pier and read the level without blinking. [173] It was true she did not blink. [180] It was not true about the feeling. [187] She simply kept it folded small and sealed tight, the way her father had kept his. [203] He had walked every mile of this coast with an antique traveling inkwell clipped to his belt, logging tides in a hand as neat as stitching. [229] Then one night he took the mainland road and did not come back. [242] She was eleven. [245] The drawbridge had risen behind him, and she had watched the gap open like a held breath. [262]

Three years ago, repairing a cracked footing on the pavilion's east stair, she had found the first note. [280] It was rolled inside a glass vial, set into the old mortar where a mason would only find it by breaking what he meant to mend. [306] The hand was her father's. [311] "Here Anna Coyle meant to be married in the spring of the flood; she never was, and someone should remember that she meant it," it read. [337]

Since then she had found nineteen more. [344] A fisherman who had wanted to sail to Lisbon. [353] A girl who had rehearsed a violin solo on this floor for a recital the tide cancelled. [370] A widower who had planned to build a glasshouse on the point. [382] Each dream was sealed inside one of his repairs, and those repairs had quietly held the pavilion up for thirty years. [403] He had not abandoned the place; he had been cataloguing everyone who had. [416]

That was when Ilse understood what she wanted to be. [426] Not the woman who measured loss for the Board, but the archeologist of abandoned dreams, the one who dug them up gently and put them back where they could bear weight. [457]

Tonight there was one footing left she had not opened: the keystone under the bandstand, his final repair, already weeping salt. [478] She waded in to her hips, cold biting through the canvas, and set her chisel to the seam. [496] The mortar had cured hard as bone, and she had to wrest the block free with her whole body, bracing a boot against the column, until it gave with a sound like a knuckle cracking. [531]

Inside lay no vial but a brass cylinder no longer than her thumb, its screw cap green with verdigris. [550] His inkwell. [552] Wrapped around it, in oilcloth, was the last note. [561]

"Here Tomas Marrow meant to stay," it said. [569] "He could not, and he is sorry, and he leaves the well so that whoever mends this next will have something to write with." [593]

Ilse stood a long while in the burning water. [602] The sun slid lower, and the copper dome kindled one last time and went dark. [617] Her face did not change, but her hands did; they shook as she unscrewed the cap and found, dried at the bottom, the black crust of his last evening's work. [647] She wet it with lagoon water until it ran again. [657]

On a page torn from her survey book, with the drafting nib she always carried, she wrote her own entry. [677] "Here Ilse Marrow was meant to condemn this place, and will not," she wrote. [691]

She tucked it beside his note, the two absences folded into each other like hands, and packed the keystone back with fresh mortar from her bucket, the inkwell set inside for whoever came next. [725]

The drawbridge would not come down until six. [733] By then the stone would be set, and she would walk into the Board's office with numbers proving the pavilion could stand, because she had made sure it could. [762] Behind her the water held its low red glow, and under it the dreams held the floor. [779]
