Wren woke the way she had every morning since the memory came back: carefully, as if her ribs were a pane of old glass. [24] She lay still in the narrow cot between Ornithology and Weather, listening. [36] Below the reading-room windows the cloud tide was coming in, white and soundless, swallowing the lower stacks shelf by shelf. [56] Beyond it, on the summit terrace, the nine standing stones had begun to hum. [70] The hum meant high water within the hour; it was the only clock that worked up here, where the sun was a rumor half the day. [96]

She had been alone in the Library of Ebb for three years, and for most of them she had not minded. [117] Then, one evening in spring, cataloguing a crate of taxidermy that had come up on the last supply kite, she had lifted a passenger pigeon from its straw and remembered, all at once, a cage in Cincinnati. [154] She remembered the smell of millet and the September heat of 1914. [166] She remembered being a keeper named Anna, twenty years old, who fed the last of a species that had once darkened the sky for three days at a time. [195] The bird was called Martha, and in her final weeks she would wake each morning, shake her feathers, and look around for others who were never coming. [222] Anna had been her contemporary, the last human face in a vanished world, and Anna had watched her die. [241]

Since that evening, Wren's awakenings had turned brittle. [249] Each dawn she opened her eyes as Martha had, already searching the empty room, and each dawn something in her cracked a little further. [273] The solitude she had chosen now felt like a cage someone else had built. [287] A letter lay on her desk, addressed to the Directorate below, requesting a replacement. [301] She meant to send it down with the ebb. [310]

She climbed to the terrace while the stones were still humming. [321] At the center of the circle stood the brass sundial, older than the library, its face polished by some forgotten process until it was atomically reflective: a mirror without a single scratch, so perfect that the old founders claimed it showed the world exactly as it was, with nothing added. [371] Wren had avoided it for months. [377] She did not want to see her own face doing what Martha's had done. [391]

But today the cloud tide crested just below the terrace and stopped, as the hum promised it would, and for a few minutes the sun broke clean across the stones. [421] The gnomon threw its shadow; the hum dropped to a low, steady note, the pitch the founders had called slack water. [442] She made herself look down. [447]

The mirror held her face, tired and older than she remembered, and around it the sky, and in the sky, wheeling low over the white sea, a dozen swifts. [476] She turned. [478] They were real, cutting arcs above the stones, diving under the library eaves where, she now saw, they had built nests of cloud-damp moss and shredded index cards. [506] They must have been there all spring. [513] She had been too busy mourning one bird to notice the living ones. [526]

The mirror added nothing, and it showed her something plain: Martha had been alone because she was kept. [544] Wren was alone because she had climbed. [551] No one had closed the door behind her; the kite line ran down through the clouds every week, and she could follow it whenever she chose. [577] Anna's grief was real, but it belonged to a cage in Cincinnati, and she did not have to carry it into a house with open windows. [603]

The stones' note began to rise again; the tide was turning. [614] Wren went down to the reading room, tore the letter in half, and set the passenger pigeon on the shelf above her desk, where the swifts could see it through the glass. [646] Then she opened the catalog to a fresh page and wrote the entry she had been unable to write for months. [667]

Ectopistes migratorius. [669] Last known individual, Martha, died 1914, in the company of a keeper who loved her. [684] Specimen now housed at the Library of Ebb, where it coexists with swifts. [697]

Below that, in smaller letters, she added a note for whoever came after her: The keeper here is not kept. [717]

The next morning she woke, opened her eyes, and heard wings under the eaves, and nothing in her broke at all. [738]
