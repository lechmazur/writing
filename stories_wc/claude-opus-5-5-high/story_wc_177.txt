The forest people called her Foresight-Hindsight-Only, because Mael dreamed every true thing before it happened and understood none of it until after. [22] She kept the dreams in a ledger inside the bunker, a concrete throat swallowed by kudzu and wild grape, where the rewilded oaks had grown so close that daylight arrived green and late. [55] Her method was precisely playful. [60] Each dream got a column, a date, and a score out of seven for how badly she had misread it. [80] The drowned fox had meant the flood of the lower creek: six out of seven, embarrassing. [96] The laughing kettle had meant old Ansa's death, and she had given herself no score at all, only a blank square she had never filled. [121]

On the steel desk beneath the ledger lay the directive. [131] It had been left by whoever built the bunker, folded envelope-tight and closed with a wafer seal, a thin disc of red flour paste pressed with a spiral. [159] The instructions on the outside were decaying the way everything here decayed, from damp and patience. [175] When Ansa kept the bunker, it read KEEP THIS DOOR SHUT UNTIL THE STORM FINDS YOU. [191] Now mildew had eaten the middle, and only KEEP, SHUT, and STORM remained, like three stones left of a wall. [211] Mael obeyed the fragments because obeying was easier than wondering what had rotted away. [225]

But the paths troubled her. [230] In the forest, trails did not stay where people cut them. [241] They wandered overnight, braided through bracken, and closed behind anyone who tried to turn back. [256] Nine years ago one had taken her, a grieving girl of fourteen, straight from the village well to this door, and she had wanted ever since to discover why some paths choose their walkers and leave others standing at the edge. [297]

The barometer began to fall on a windless afternoon, and the storm approached the way this forest's storms always did, first as silence, then as birds flying the wrong direction. [327] That night Mael dreamed of a boy carrying a lantern made of rain. [340] He stood outside the bunker, and the door was open, and the spiral on the seal was turning like water down a drain. [363] She woke with grit in her teeth and the old reflex rising: write it down, wait, let the world explain. [383] She opened the ledger and looked at the blank square beside the kettle. [396] Ansa had coughed for a month before she died, and Mael had dreamed that kettle three times, laughing, boiling dry, and had called it a joke about breakfast. [424] Hindsight had been her whole gift, and it had never saved anyone. [436]

So she tried something she had never allowed herself. [445] She read the dream forward. [450] A lantern of rain meant someone walking in the storm with nothing but the storm to see by. [468] An open door meant a door she would open. [477] The turning spiral she could not read, so she scored her guess four out of seven in advance, which was cheating, and laughed at herself, which helped. [504]

The wind came at dusk, tearing vines from the concrete in long wet ropes. [518] KEEP, said the directive on her desk. [525] SHUT. [526] Through the slit window she saw a small light bobbing among the thrashing oaks, and she saw the path itself, pale as a vein, unspooling toward her door. [554]

Mael broke the wafer seal with her thumbnail. [562] Inside, protected for decades from damp, the full directive was written small and unhurried. [576] Keep this door shut until the storm finds you, and then understand that the storm is only weather. [594] What finds you is whoever the path is carrying. [603] Paths choose the ones already lost enough to follow them, and they bring them to whoever was lost the same way before. [625] Open it. [627] The seal was never meant to hold, only to wait for a keeper who would stop waiting. [644]

Mael understood, and for the first time the understanding came before the ending instead of after it. [661] She felt her gift transcend its old narrow shape, the way a root cracks the concrete it was planted under. [681] She hauled the door open against the wind. [689] The boy stood there drenched and shaking, holding a jar lantern whose candle had drowned, rain pooling in the glass. [709] Behind him the path was already closing. [716]

"I didn't mean to come here," he said. [724]

"Nobody does," Mael said, and pulled him in out of the storm. [736]

Later, while he slept wrapped in Ansa's old blanket, she opened the ledger, found the blank square beside the kettle, and at last gave herself a seven. [763] Then, next to the lantern of rain, she crossed out her four and wrote a zero. [779]
