The saws had gone quiet at the foot of the great elm, and the men who held them stood in the frost like fenceposts, waiting for first light. [28] Inside, Ines Marrow listened to the tree not breathe. [37] For three hundred years the Guild of the Ring had hollowed it, a chamber for each century, shelving cut into the heartwood so that the books followed the grain upward in a spiral no one could walk without feeling small. [77] Now the trunk was rotten at the root, the city had condemned it, and at dawn it would come down. [97]

Ines painted portraits, which was a strange trade for a woman who could not recognize a face. [114] Features slid off her like rain from oilcloth; her own mother had been a voice and a smell of cloves. [134] So she painted everything else with fierce care, the knuckles, the collar, the particular way a window lay across a cheek, and patrons swore she caught them better than their mirrors did. [166]

Warden Oda, last of the guild, had hired her for one final commission: the founder, Scholar Aurel Vane, painted for the new archive in the city, before his tree was gone. [197] No likeness survived. [200] There was only the coin. [205]

Oda had brought it down from the fourth chamber inside a secret book safe, a copy of the Guild Charter with its pages glued and carved into a cradle. [234] The coin was bronze, as old as the guild itself, minted by Vane for his first students and worn by centuries of thumbs until the head on it was a blur. [265]

"You won't see much," Oda said. [271]

"I never do," Ines answered, and closed her eyes. [280]

She read it with her fingertips first, then laid paper over it and rubbed graphite across in strokes so light they barely whispered. [303] Faint indentations rose from the grey. [309] Not a nose, not a brow. [315] Points. [316] Hundreds of them, clustered, and within each cluster smaller clusters, and within those, pricks so fine she found them only when she tilted the paper toward the lamp. [344] The pattern was fractally precise: every knot of marks repeated the shape of the whole, the way a twig repeats a branch and a branch the tree. [371]

Ines had spent her life being told that the face was the center of a person. [387] She looked at the rubbing and felt, for the first time, the edge of that idea, like a hand finding the end of a wall in the dark. [415]

"This isn't a man," she said. [421] "It's a sky." [424]

Oda opened the charter to its only uncut page, the frontispiece, and read aloud the founding sentence she had recited every morning for forty years without once thinking about it: We build to mirror the sky inside walls. [462]

"We thought it meant knowledge," Oda whispered. [469] "The stars of learning." [473] "A metaphor." [475]

"He was showing you his face," Ines said. [483] "The one he chose." [487]

Outside, the grey had begun to thin. [494] There was no time to paint, and paint would not have been right anyway. [508] Ines took her awl, the one she used to stretch canvas, and Oda held the lamp, and together they climbed to the thinnest place in the trunk, where the heartwood had been carved almost to bark. [544]

Ines scaled the rubbing by eye, a coin's width becoming an arm's span, and began to craft. [561] She pierced the wood cluster by cluster, the large holes first, then the smaller ones inside them, then the finest, twisting the awl until her wrist burned. [588] Once she hesitated, the point trembling above a knot she was not sure of, and understood that there were marks too small for any hand to repeat, that the pattern went on past what she could make. [625] She let it go. [629] That was the other limit, and it did not frighten her as much as she expected. [645]

When she stepped back, dawn arrived. [651]

It came through the holes as needles of pale light, crossing the dark chamber and landing on the far wall, the shelves, the spines of abandoned books, a small cold constellation spread across the inside of the tree. [689] Nobody outside moved. [692] Nobody inside breathed. [695]

The light fell also across Oda's face, a scatter of bright points on cheek and brow and the corner of her mouth. [717] Ines looked at her and, for one held breath, saw a face she could keep, not by its features but by its stars. [740] When the sun climbed it would slide away like every other, and she found she could bear that too. [759]

Below them the foreman lifted his horn. [766]

Ines closed the book safe over the coin, and they went down through the spiral together, slowly, carrying the only portrait the founder ever wanted. [791]
