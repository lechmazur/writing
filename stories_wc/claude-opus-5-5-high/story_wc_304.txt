The skyborne grotto hung a mile above the ice fields, a hollow of blue stone held aloft by the old lift-roots, and every night the aurora poured through its mouth like slow green water. [34] Ilse worked beneath that light because the dead looked kinder in it. [46] She was the grotto's beautician mortician, the one who washed the departed, set their jaws, and brushed color back into cheeks the wind had already begun to claim. [74] Tonight the body on her slate was Maren, who had taught her every stroke. [88] For three winters Maren had been forgetting. [95] First the names of pigments went, then the names of friends, and finally Ilse's own name, so that in the last months the old woman had looked at her with polite, empty warmth, the way one looks at weather. [134] Ilse had not been there when she died. [142] That was the wound she carried up the stair to the grotto. [154]

There was an old teaching, one Maren herself had whispered to her apprentice years ago: that a dying mind does not go out at once. [179] For the length of one aurora, it lingers at a threshold of stillness, a narrow place between the last breath and the true quiet, and the sky carries what remains as static. [211] After dawn, nothing. [214] Oblivion, clean and total. [218]

Ilse uncovered the listening horn, a brass throat set into the grotto wall, and let the hiss fill the chamber. [238] Most morticians never used it. [243] The static was the sound of the whole sky dying and being born, and a person could go mad searching it. [264] But Ilse had always been lucidly watchful, the apprentice who noticed a tremor in a dead man's eyelid or the faint yellowing that meant a bruise was hiding under the skin. [295] She sat cross-legged beside the slate, closed her eyes, and began finding patterns in static. [310]

It was like sorting sand by color in the dark. [320] There were the long sighs of the aurora itself, the ticking of cooling stone, the dim crackle of some herder who had died on the ice fields that week. [349] She let them pass. [353] Hours slid by. [356] The green overhead thinned toward violet, the color that meant morning was coming. [369]

Then, beneath the hiss, she heard it: three rising notes and a falling fourth, broken, stubborn, repeating. [386] It was the tune Maren had hummed over every body she ever painted, the tune she hummed while teaching Ilse to blend rose into ash. [411] Even forgetting everything, she had kept that. [418]

Ilse's hands shook as she took the emerald chip from her apron. [430] It had broken from Maren's ring on the day the old woman first failed to know her, and Ilse had kept it wrapped in lint ever since, unable to throw it away. [462] Emerald was the one stone that drank auroral light, the herders said, and held it the way a cupped hand holds water. [484] She lifted the chip into the stream of light pouring through the grotto mouth and hummed the tune back, matching each broken note until the static and her voice were one thread. [516] The chip warmed. [519] A small green pulse woke inside it, beating in time. [529]

She did not hesitate. [533] On her mixing stone she ground the emerald to powder and stirred it into Maren's own rouge, the pot the old woman had labeled with trembling letters before the letters left her too. [566] The pulse did not die in the grinding. [574] It spread, and the pigment glowed faintly, as if something breathing had been folded into it. [590] To infuse the dead with color was her whole craft; tonight she would infuse her teacher with what was still alive. [611]

She worked the rouge into Maren's cheeks with the old circular strokes, humming. [624] Under her fingers the gray skin took on the flush of someone listening to a song she loved. [642] The mouth, which Ilse had set so carefully, softened at one corner. [654] It was not a miracle, she knew. [661] The spark would not wake her or speak. [669] But for as long as the pigment held its glow, the face on the slate was not a stranger's face. [689] It was Maren at her work, Maren who knew exactly who was humming beside her. [704]

Dawn came through the grotto mouth in a single pale sheet, and the static in the horn went quiet all at once. [726] Ilse did not cover the body yet. [733] She sat in the radiant hush, the last of the aurora fading from the ceiling, and watched the small green warmth in her teacher's cheeks until it, too, crossed over into stillness, recognized and unafraid.
