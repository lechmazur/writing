The six-fifteen from Harrow Gap always whistled twice, and Ines Varga always waited for the second echo to die against the gorge before she released the brake. [27] It was her one rule as gondola operator, and she kept it the way other people kept prayers. [45] The echo rattled the prisms of the terrace below, and a rattled terrace drifted badly. [60]

Tonight she had one passenger, a girl of about eleven with a canvas duffel and a train ticket still pinned to her coat. [83] Her name, according to the ticket, was Tamsin Orr, destination Aunt Philippa, Terrace House, via cable. [99] She had not stopped talking since the platform. [107]

"Why are we waiting, is it broken, how high is it, does it swing?" [121]

Ines glanced at the brass meter bolted beside the lever. [131] Its needle measured noise, and she had logged it every crossing for four years, a column of numbers in a notebook that her late husband would have called morbid and she called research. [164] The needle trembled at sixty-two: whistle, wind, girl. [172] Then the echo thinned, folded into the rock, and was gone. [183] Forty-one. [184]

Ines released the brake, and the gondola slid out over nothing. [195]

Below them the drifting prism terrace turned slowly on its thermals, a raft of cut glass the size of a village square, catching the last light and throwing it back up the cliffs in long violet ladders. [232] People went there to scatter ashes, to propose, to be alone. [243] Mostly they went there because the terrace was the quietest place anyone in the valley had ever stood, and most of them came back up the cable frightened by it. [273]

Ines understood that. [276] After Tomas died, the silence of their flat had pressed on her like water at depth. [292] She had reasoned her way out of it the only way she knew, one step flowing into the next: if silence could hurt, it could be studied; if it could be studied, it could be taught; and if it could be taught, it could learn to comfort. [339] Four years of numbers had told her one thing. [348] Silence was never the problem; empty silence was. [356]

"My mum talked all the time," Tamsin said, faster now, watching the car sway. [370] "Aunt Philippa doesn't talk at all, I've met her, she just nods, and if it goes quiet I'll hear it, that nothing, and I can't." [395]

Ines reached up and unhooked the storm glass vial that hung from the gondola ceiling. [410] Tomas had given it to her for forecasting the thermals, a finger of sealed glass filled with camphor and spirit. [430] Tonight the liquid was clear. [435]

"Hold it," she said, "and tell me what happens." [444]

Tamsin cupped it in both hands. [450] "Nothing." [451]

"Your hands are warm. [455] Give it to the air." [460]

Ines slid the side window open, and the cold of the gorge came in, and with it the hush of the terrace rising to meet them, a quiet so large it seemed to vault over the car like the ceiling of a cathedral. [503] Tamsin held the vial to the window and, for the first time since the platform, said nothing. [520]

The meter needle sank: thirty-four, thirty, twenty-six. [527]

Inside the glass, a single white thread appeared, then feathered, then branched into fronds like frost on a pane. [546] Stillness and cold catalyzed them; shaking dissolved them. [554] Ines had tested it a hundred times. [561]

"It's growing," Tamsin whispered. [565]

"Only while it's quiet," Ines said, just as softly. [574] "That's the thing nobody tells you about the nothing. [583] Things grow in it." [587]

The gondola docked against the terrace with a sound like a held breath let go. [602] A tall woman in a grey shawl waited at the rail, and she nodded, exactly as promised. [619] Tamsin stepped out, still carrying the vial, then turned back. [629]

Ines closed the girl's fingers around it. [636] "When it goes quiet up here, watch it," she said. [646] "You won't be hearing nothing. [651] You'll be hearing something grow." [656]

Aunt Philippa looked at the vial, then at Ines, and something in her long face loosened. [672] She put a hand on Tamsin's shoulder and said nothing, and for once the nothing held. [688]

Ines rode back up alone, the ceiling hook empty above her. [699] In the notebook she wrote the number, nineteen, the lowest she had ever logged. [713] Beside it, where for four years she had written empty, she wrote a different word: full. [729]

Far above, the six-fifteen was already gone, and the quiet it left behind stretched over the gorge like a hand held open. [751]
