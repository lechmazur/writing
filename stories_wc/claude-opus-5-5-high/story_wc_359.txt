Ilse Varda heard the aurora before she saw it, a low violet hum that pressed against the ice cavern like a hand against a window. [25] She was the last prism-song bell ringer of Hallow Reach, and she had come down the glacier's throat with a lantern, a mallet, and a page of amber no thicker than a fingernail. [58] Above her, in the town square, Tomas Reyl stood before a block of white marble with his chisel raised, waiting for the council's signal. [82] The block would become the Winter Charter, and whatever words he carved into it would be the only things the town fed through the dark months. [108] Grain. [109] Fuel. [110] Medicine. [111] Rope. [112]

Magistrate Orrin had read the list aloud that morning, and when Ilse asked where the bells were, he had looked at her with honest, tired kindness. [138] "Wonder does not keep anyone warm," he said. [146] "Prove otherwise before the first strike, and I will have it carved." [158]

So she had come here, to the Hush. [166] The founders of Hallow Reach had carved this cavern three centuries ago, not for shelter but for silence. [184] Every wall curved at a precise angle; every ridge of ice had been shaved and polished until quiet itself seemed to have a shape, a sculpted hush that gathered sound and funneled it upward through the glacier to the square. [224]

Ilse let the stillness settle over her like snowfall. [233] Then she breathed, and the breath came back to her as a faint blue ribbon, because she had always been synesthetically blessed: sound arrived in her as color, and color as sound. [265]

In the center of the cavern hung the Founders' Bell, a dome of clear crystal locked inside a pillar of ice. [286] No one had rung it in living memory. [294] The amber sheet had been found in the town archive, wedged behind a ledger of grain accounts, and no scholar had been able to read it; its markings were not notes but tiny inclusions, specks and threads suspended in resin. [334] Ilse had guessed what they needed. [340]

She set down the lantern, pressed her palms to the pillar, and began to unfetter the bell. [357] She worked slowly, tapping the ice with her mallet at the seams where it rang hollow and green, pausing whenever the cavern's hush thickened, as if the room were listening. [387] Tap, then rest. [390] Tap, then rest. [393] Her pulse rose and fell with the rhythm, alarm and calm trading places like tide. [408] The last shell of ice slid free and shattered into a sound like scattered pearls. [423]

Overhead, the aurora deepened. [427] Its light poured through the thin ceiling of the cavern in bands of green, rose, and violet, and when Ilse lifted the amber sheet into that light, the inclusions woke. [457] Each speck caught a different color. [463] To anyone else it would have looked like dust glowing. [473] To Ilse, it was a melody, written through the colors of the aurora by founders who must have seen the way she did, green for the low tones, rose for the rising line, violet for the long held note at the end. [515]

Beneath the melody, in finer threads, were words. [523] We carved grain into our first charter, and we starved in spirit. [535] We carved wonder into our second, and we endured. [544]

Ilse raised the mallet. [548] She struck the bell, then waited, then struck again, following the colors one by one while the sculpted hush gathered each note and lifted it through a thousand feet of ice. [579]

Far above, the magistrate lowered his hand, and Tomas brought the chisel down. [592] At the moment the steel first struck the marble, the violet note broke out of the ground beneath the square. [612] The stone answered it, humming, and the crowd went quiet as the aurora seemed to lean down, spilling color across faces that had been gray with arithmetic all week. [641] Children stopped shivering. [644] Old Maren, who had not sung since her husband died, began to hum. [657] Tomas stood with his chisel still touching the stone, afraid to move and break it. [672]

When Ilse climbed out of the glacier an hour later, frost in her hair, Orrin was waiting at the cavern mouth. [693] She held the amber up to the sky so he could see it glow, and read him the founders' words. [713] He was silent for a long time, and she let him be. [725]

"The first word is already cut," he said at last. [735] "Tomas struck it while your bell was ringing, and he carved the only thing he could think of." [753]

She did not ask what it was. [760] Below them the freed bell still hummed, rising and settling, rising and settling, and she heard the answer in its color long before she saw it in the stone. [789]
