The oasis at the center of Glasshollow dried in the ninth month, and after that everyone in the greenhouse city spoke less. [22] Talk costs breath, and breath costs water, and the sky above the panes stayed the color of old mustard, too poisonous to open a single vent. [48] Ines Varo still worked, because contracts still needed signing. [57] She was a gilded-leaf calligrapher, the last in the eastern domes, and she pressed gold onto living fig leaves with a brush made from her own hair. [84] Merchants trusted her leaves because a gilded leaf wilts near dishonesty; set one beside a man who means to cheat you, and it curls like a burned moth. [112] She had three leaves left. [117] The fig trees had died with the oasis. [125]

She had also, four months ago, told her sister Tamsin a lie so large that no leaf in the city could have survived it. [149] Tamsin had wanted to leave through the outer airlock and walk to the rumored clean valleys; Ines had said, Go, then, I won't miss you, and Tamsin had gone. [178] Their grandfather, a postman before the domes were sealed, used to say that unfinished conversations did not vanish. [196] They sank, he said, like water finding the low place. [206] Ines wanted to know where that place was. [214]

She found the map because she was desperate for moisture. [224] An old irrigation pipe ran beneath her workshop, and she pried up the floorboards hoping for a trapped pocket of condensation. [245] Instead she found a tin box of shredded paper, the strips inked in her grandfather's cramped hand. [262] It took her two nights to piece it together on her worktable, strip by strip, the way she laid gold. [282] The shape that emerged was a treasure map of Glasshollow's forgotten postal tunnels, marked with an X and the words WHERE THE LETTERS WAIT. [306] But the center was missing, a ragged hole exactly where the tunnels converged. [319] She stared at it until her eyes burned, and then she looked at her paperweight. [334]

It was a zinc-etched postal plate fragment her grandfather had given her as a child, a jagged gray square she had always assumed was junk. [359] She rubbed gold dust into its grooves, and the etched lines bloomed bright: tunnels, a spiral stair, the dry basin of the oasis itself. [383] It fit the hole like a tooth in a jaw. [393] She reread the map three times, then a fourth, because the X sat beneath the place where the whole city had once gathered to talk. [418]

The basin was cracked mud now, ringed by empty benches. [428] Under the lowest bench she found an iron hatch stamped with the old postal horn. [443] The stair below smelled of damp stone, the first real damp she had breathed in months. [459] At the bottom was a dead-letter room, shelves to the ceiling, and every envelope bore the same red stamp: UNDELIVERABLE, SENDER NEVER POSTED. [482] Her grandfather's note was pinned to the first shelf. [491] People wrote these and could not send them, it said, so I kept them near the water, where things stay alive. [512]

She opened one at random. [517] It said, The soup you made on Tuesday was good, and I should have told you. [533] Another said, I was frightened at the wedding, not angry. [543] Another said only, Sorry I was late. [550] None of them were grand confessions. [556] They were the small true things people had swallowed, and the swallowing, she understood, was how whole conversations got lost. [576] They did not vanish; they waited down here in the dark for someone brave enough to carry them up. [595]

In a corner, a brass pneumatic tube still hissed faintly, its label reading OUTER AIRLOCK STATION. [611] Ines sat on the cold floor and took out one of her three leaves. [625] She began to write, Tamsin, I hope the valleys are everything you wanted, and the leaf sagged in her hand before she finished the line. [650] She did not hope that. [655] She hoped Tamsin would come home. [661] On the second leaf she wrote nothing noble, only what was true, that she had lied at the door and missed Tamsin every morning at breakfast. [687] The leaf stayed green, stiff and bright, the gold lying flat as still water. [701] She rolled it into a capsule, fed it to the tube, and listened to it rush away toward the edge of the city. [724]

Then she picked up the soup letter, tucked it into her coat, and climbed the stair to find whoever had been waiting to hear it. [749]
