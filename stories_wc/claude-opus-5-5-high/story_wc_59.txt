Prince Tavish had not slept in three days, and it showed in the lichen on his cuffs and the rust in his hair. [23] The palace of Hollowmere hung beneath him and above nothing, bolted by iron cables to six stalactites the size of cathedrals, and every hour it shivered as the gears in its belly turned. [56] He clung to the fourth stalactite, where the anchor bolt had begun to weep white dust, and he hypothesized. [75] Perhaps the stone was tired. [80] Perhaps the bolt had been set wrong on the first day, two hundred years ago, when his great-grandmother declared that nothing living would be permitted in her kingdom because living things decayed. [112] Perhaps everything was set wrong on the first day. [121]

He had always thought this way, testing every idea until it broke, which was why the court called him the Questioning Prince and why he was never invited to dinner. [151] Hollowmere was a marvel of precision: brass finches that chirped on the quarter hour, copper roses that opened by spring-tension, a chandelier-sun that rose and set on rails. [179] Nothing in it had ever surprised anyone. [186]

Then the dust fell away beneath his fingers, and he saw the egg. [199]

It was sealed inside the stalactite like a seed in amber, pale blue, freckled with gold, larger than his fist. [219] The bolt, he realized, had been driven straight through the nest. [230] He knew the markings from a forbidden book in the archive: a vesper lark, the bird whose evening song was said to make listeners weep and laugh at once, hunted to nothing when the palace was raised. [267] Hypothesis: the builders had found the last nest here and anchored their kingdom through it, deliberately or carelessly. [285] Hypothesis: the dripping mineral had preserved it. [292] Hypothesis, the one that made his heart knock: it might still be alive. [305]

The palace quaked. [308] Below him a cable sang with strain, and the brass finches all chirped at the wrong time. [325] Tavish worked the egg loose with his penknife while the stone groaned around him, and he climbed down with it pressed inside his shirt, against the warmth of his own ribs. [356]

In his workshop he began to assemble an incubator from whatever he could steal. [370] He took the heating coil from the chandelier-sun, so that the eastern halls fell into a dusk no clock had scheduled. [391] He took the humidity bellows from the copper rose garden and the regulator from his own father's pocket watch, and he fit them together with trembling hands, guessing at every measurement. [422] Would a lark want the heat of a hen or of a stone? [435] Would two centuries of sleep leave anything to wake? [444] Was he saving a bird or merely proving that his great-grandmother had been wrong? [458]

The court came knocking on the second night, furious about the dark halls and the frozen roses. [475] His father came too, and stood in the doorway, and said nothing at all, which was worse. [492] Tavish did not unbolt the door. [498] The quakes grew closer together, and in the reverie of exhaustion he began to believe the whole palace was a held breath. [520]

On the third dawn, which he could only guess at, the egg cracked. [533]

The chick that tumbled out was wrong. [540] One wing was stunted, curled like a question mark, and a pale streak of calcite had grown into the feathers of its breast, as if the stone had tried to finish what the mother could not. [576] Tavish sat on the floor and wept, because he had hypothesized every outcome except the one where he succeeded badly. [596]

Then the lark opened its beak and sang. [604]

It was not a clean song. [610] It cracked on the high notes and wavered on the low, and it was bitterly sweet, like fruit left too long on the branch, like his mother's voice in the years before the court replaced her singing with a music box. [651] Beyond the door, the voices stopped. [657]

Tavish unbolted it. [660]

The courtiers stood in the unscheduled dark with their faces slack, and his father's hand was pressed flat against his mouth. [681] Somewhere above, the brass finches had fallen quiet, as if listening. [692] The palace quaked once more, gently now, and Tavish offered his final hypothesis aloud. [706]

"We were never meant to be perfect," he said, cradling the crooked bird. [719] "We were only meant to begin." [725]

Above them, in the fourth stalactite, water began to drip into the empty hollow where the nest had been, patient as any mother, starting over. [750]
