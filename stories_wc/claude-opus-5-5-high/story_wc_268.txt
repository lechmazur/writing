The temple library had been lost in time for eleven hundred years, and Mab had swept its floors for all of them. [22] She was a titan, last of the line that once carried the kingdom's mountains on their shoulders, but as belief in her people thinned, so had she, until she stood no taller than the lowest shelf. [58] Above the lintel, carved deep, glowed the rune of drift, the old sign that let the temple slip loose from the hours while the heavens rearranged themselves. [85] Outside, the constellations were shifting, and across that long pause the kingdom waited for a new sky and, by ancient law, a new sovereign. [109] Whatever claim the temple sealed before the stars locked would hold forever. [121]

Lord Aurel Vane arrived with six scribes, a velvet cloak, and a charter proving his descent from the titan kings. [141] Mab, who knew every titan who had ever lived, had never heard of his grandmother. [156]

She did not announce herself. [161] A creature the size of a watering can declaring herself the rightful queen would be laughed out of the nave, and besides, she had never cared for announcements. [189] Her crown, if she had one, was the ring of keys at her belt, and she wore it the way she wore her patched apron: plainly, without apology. [217]

Instead, she took down her last iron gall ink pellet, a black bead of oak galls and rusted nails pressed centuries ago, and shaved a sliver into rainwater from the cracked dome. [249] Iron gall does not sit on a page; it bites into it, and nothing written with it can be coaxed away. [270] That night she climbed the stacks and wrote in the margins of the books Vane's scribes would open next. [289]

In a genealogy of the river dukes: What drifts was never anchored. [301] In a treatise on dyes: Ask the ink what it remembers. [312] In a book of hymns, beneath the verse for the fallen giants: The smallest of us is still standing. [331]

The scribes whispered. [334] The youngest, a girl named Tessaly, began checking her master's charter each morning, and each morning she grew paler. [353] Vane's charter was written in lampblack, soft soot bound with gum, and under the rune of drift, unanchored things wandered. [373] Letters loosened like tired moths. [378] A great-grandfather slid off the vellum and pooled in the fold. [389]

Mab left one more message, in the ledger of seals itself: Read it aloud when the stars stop. [407]

On the final night the dome filled with slow, turning light as the new constellations eased into their places. [426] Vane stood at the altar in his velvet, the wax hot, the seal raised. [440] Tessaly unrolled the charter to read it into the record, and her voice failed, because the vellum was blank except for a gray smear drifting toward the edges like smoke leaving a room. [473] In the silence, the scribes turned to the ledger, where Mab's black words sat exactly where she had set them, sharp as the day they were written. [500]

Mab stepped out from behind the lectern and set down her broom. [512] "A true line is written in something that bites," she said. [523] "Yours was only resting on the page." [530]

Vane looked down at her, and for a moment he laughed, and then he saw how the six scribes had knelt, not to him. [554] Vane sank onto the altar steps, a man holding nothing at all. [566]

A truth sealed in a lost place helps no one, so Mab climbed to the lintel, laid her small hand on the rune of drift, and with the last of her mountain strength, sundered it. [601]

Eleven hundred years came in at once, gently. [609] The shelves sighed and softened, and the books unbound themselves into drifting pages that fell like leaves in no hurry. [629] The dome opened petal by petal onto the new stars. [639] Columns knelt into dust as courteously as old servants taking their leave. [651] Only the pages written in iron gall held together, her margins and the old titan rolls, the ink knitting them the way it had always bound the truth, and they settled into Tessaly's outstretched arms. [686]

When the dust cleared, the scribes were kneeling in a roofless ruin under an unfamiliar sky, and the stars had locked. [707] Mab, smaller now than ever, brushed grit from her apron. [717]

"Get up," she told them kindly. [723] "Queens have floors to sweep like anyone else, and there is a great deal of mess to carry home." [742]

In her pocket, the last grain of ink waited for whatever needed writing next. [756]
