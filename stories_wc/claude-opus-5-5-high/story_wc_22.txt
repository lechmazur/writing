The coach from Gloom Hill came down to the sea for the last time on the evening the terns began to leave. [22] Ansel Vey had driven that road for thirty years, one lane of gravel pinched between the moor and the drop, and he had never carried a passenger who wanted to arrive. [53] Mourners went down, mostly, to look at the Salt Field where their grandfathers had killed each other, and came back up with nothing to say. [78] Tonight the coach was empty except for a cedar box on the seat beside him. [93] Inside, in paper twists labeled in his wife's hand, rattled the dried seed pods Mara had gathered from every hedge and grave on Gloom Hill before the cough took her: poppy, thrift, vetch, sea-kale, wild pea. [129] She had meant them for the Salt Field. [137] Nothing grew there. [140]

At the war's end, each army had salted the ground so the other could never farm it, and the concord that finally stopped them had never been written down. [169] The Carrish and the Venn trusted no ink. [177] Their singers had rowed out over the reef and sung the terms into the water, both tongues braided together, and the reef had kept them, the old people said, the way a reef keeps anything: by growing over it. [216] A submerged concord. [219] Peace that lived underwater, where no one could argue with it. [230]

Now the reef was dead. [235] It had bleached over two summers and then crumbled, and the fish had gone, and the birds that had fed above it for a thousand years were gathering to follow them. [266]

Ansel left the horses at the rail and walked out onto the jetty with the box under his arm. [285] It was the narrowest thing in the world, two planks wide and half a mile long, and it ran straight into a sky the color of a bruise healing. [314] On either side the bay opened, enormous and gray, and far out the dead reef showed as a white seam, like a scar where something had been stitched shut. [343]

And the jetty was blooming. [348] Between every plank, along every piling, pink thrift and golden samphire had pushed up thick as fur, bright even in the failing light, so that he walked a ribbon of flowers laid across nothing. [382] The terns crowded the rail in their hundreds, and they were calling. [394]

Ansel was the last man on Gloom Hill who spoke Old Carrish, and one of three alive who spoke Venn, both learned at his grandmother's knee from songs no one else remembered. [426] He had always half heard words in the birds, the way a man hears his name in rain. [444] Tonight he stopped pretending it was fancy. [451] The calls came in pairs, a harsh Carrish rasp answered by a falling Venn whistle, over and over, the way the singers had braided them. [476] Generations of chicks had learned the concord from their parents without knowing it was anything but the sound of home. [496]

He crouched among the flowers and listened, and translated under his breath. [508] Most of it he knew: no blade past the river, no fire in the hay month, the dead of both sides to lie in one ground. [534] But there was a clause at the end his grandmother had never sung, perhaps because it had seemed impossible, and the oldest birds repeated it with a kind of urgency. [564]

When the sea's bones are bare, carry them to the salt, and the salt will be answered. [581]

Ansel looked down at the jetty and understood why it bloomed. [592] For two starving summers the birds had roosted here, and their droppings had fallen with the white grit of broken coral the tide threw up, lime and dung together, and on that the flowers had thrived in the salt wind. [632] The singers had known. [636] The reef was never only a hiding place; it was a store, kept against the day it would die. [655]

He worked through the dark with a shovel from the coach, filling sacks with the crushed white shore and the stinking crust off the pilings, and at first light he drove them up to the Salt Field. [692] It took eleven trips to fertilize a single furrow as long as the jetty. [706] Into it he pressed Mara's seeds, one twist at a time, speaking each name in Carrish and then in Venn, so both sides of the ground would hear. [734]

The field around him ran gray to every horizon. [743] His furrow was a thread in it, narrow as the road home. [755] Overhead, the terns wheeled, and did not go out to sea, and one by one came down calling onto the turned earth. [777] Ansel answered them in both dead tongues, and for once the words were the same. [792]
