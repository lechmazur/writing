The mist came off the harbor before dawn and settled into the old rail depot like a cat into a warm lap. [22]
Odile Marchetti had kept her repair shop in the depot's east freight office for thirty-one years, and for the last nine of them she had not seen it. [50]
She knew it anyway. [54]
She knew the tick of condensation on the tin roof, the cold breath of the dead tracks, the three steps from her bench to the stove. [80]

The runner from the shipyard arrived out of breath, holding something in cupped hands the way a child holds a moth. [101]
"The Albatross sails at the turn of the tide," he said. [112]
"The captain says her scout won't fly straight, and nobody can pilot the channel blind in this soup." [130]
Then he heard himself and flushed, but Odile only smiled. [140]
"Put it in my hands," she said. [147]

It was a hummingbird drone, one of her own, built years ago when her eyes still worked. [164]
She recognized the brass ribs of its wings, the tiny weight of its heart-battery, the seam she had soldered while her daughter slept in a crate of packing straw beside the stove. [196]
Her daughter was a harbor pilot now, and would be on the Albatross's bridge this morning. [212]

Odile ran a thumbnail along the left wing and found the tear at once, a split in the membrane no wider than an eyelash. [236]
"Teo, the silk and the fish glue," she said, and her apprentice was already moving. [251]
She did not need to watch him to know he laid the tools in the right order; she heard it, the small correct clicks of a shop running as it should. [282]
She cut the patch by feel, pressed it with a warm spoon, and counted forty heartbeats while it set. [301]

But the wing had never been the real trouble. [310]
When she woke the bird, it chirped in a stuttering scatter of tones, its telemetry song, and none of it made sense. [332]
The drone found its heading by reading how light bent through droplets in the air, and the mist was so thick that every droplet was telling it something different. [361]
It was not broken. [365]
It was overwhelmed. [368]
"It needs a reference," Odile said. [374]
"Something steady to measure the chaos against." [381]

The depot still had its old water feature, a cast-iron fountain in the waiting hall where travelers once tossed coins before boarding. [403]
Its basin was still, and above it hung the great glass lantern of the station clock, whose lamp Teo lit every morning out of habit. [428]
Odile carried the bird there and held it over the water. [439]
"Describe the light on the surface," she told Teo. [448]
"There's a band of color where the lamp shines through the basin edge," he said. [463]
"Red on the north side, violet toward the platform." [472]
She let the drone taste that fixed spectrum, one known angle of refraction, and listened. [487]
The stutter thinned. [490]
A single tone rose out of it, then a second, lower, and she turned the bird slowly until the two notes met. [512]
"There," she whispered. [515]
"Now it knows which way the light is lying to it." [526]

She had taught herself this in the first dark year, when feedback was all she had: the pitch of a kettle, the echo off a wall, the rasp of a gear about to fail. [560]
Noise was only direction that had not been sorted yet. [570]

From the harbor came the long groan of chain on iron. [581]
The Albatross was lifting her anchor. [587]
Odile walked to the loading door, the runner and Teo at her shoulders, and opened her hands. [604]
The bird rose without hesitation, its patched wing humming true, and vanished toward the water in a clean straight line. [624]
"Is it going the right way?" the runner asked. [633]
Odile tilted her head toward the harbor, where the chain had stopped and a ship's horn now sounded, low and unhurried, answering the bird's faint high call. [660]
Somewhere on that bridge, her daughter was reading the same song Odile had written into its wings. [677]
"Listen," she said, as the mist loosened around them like a held breath let go. [692]
"They're following it out." [696]
Then she went back inside and put the kettle on for whatever broken thing the mist brought next. [714]
