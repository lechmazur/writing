Tamsin Vey was the carnival's culture shock absorber, which meant she met every newcomer at the gangway and took the first blow of the place for them. [27] She explained why the fire-eaters worked breakfast shifts, why the Ferris wheel doubled as a desalination pump, why the whole city tilted gently whenever the calliope played in the lower key. [58] Nobody on the Revel had ever stopped celebrating since the rafts were lashed together forty years ago, and nobody arriving from the dry cities was ever ready for it. [87]

Lately the arrivals came with deeds instead of duffel bags. [97] The Harbor Consortium had been buying stalls one at a time, paying her salary to make its tenants comfortable, and in nine days the Consortium would put its offer to the Tide Council: purchase the Revel outright, privatize the moorings, charge rent on the sea. [142]

Tamsin kept two ledgers, because she believed feelings were only chaos until you filed them. [157] In the blue one she logged each newcomer's fears in tidy columns: noise, motion, strangers touching their shoulders. [175] In the red one she logged what the carnival folk feared, and every column there said the same word: gone. [195] She loved both books, which was the problem. [203] The newcomers were not villains; the banker's daughter who had wept at her first acrobat now brought the acrobats soup. [223]

But the Consortium's lawyers had noticed something in the founding charter, and Tamsin had noticed them noticing. [240] Where the foundation stone rests, there the city's heart is moored, and the rafts nearest the heart hold the council's votes. [261] The stone itself sat on the Hub raft, which the Consortium had bought in spring. [276] Its closest neighbors were now all Consortium tenants. [284] On the vote, the Revel would sell itself. [292]

Tamsin, however, wore a chip of that stone on a cord around her neck. [306] Old Marisol, the first shock absorber, had struck it loose with a hammer on founding night and passed it down with a lawyer's grin: the charter says the stone, she liked to say, not the whole stone. [343] Tamsin had always thought it was a joke. [351] Now it was a lever. [356]

She could not simply carry the chip to the old-timers' rafts at the edge; a heart needs a body around it, and the city's lashings would have to be redone overnight, before the Consortium's surveyors measured anything. [393] She would have to reorganize the entire Revel without a single meeting the Consortium could overhear. [409]

So she went to the painters. [415] Graffiti had always been the carnival's newspaper, sprayed across hull boards and tent canvas, rewritten nightly by kids with dripping cans. [436] The Consortium's tenants called it charming. [442] Tamsin sat up two nights drawing a grid, assigning every raft a color and every lashing point a glyph: a gold spiral meant cast off at the second bell, a green fish meant tie to whoever paints the matching fish, a red eye meant keep this one quiet, she frightens easily. [493] The grid was orderly; the red eyes came from intuition, from years of knowing which faces went pale when the deck moved. [515]

And here she made the choice that let her keep both ledgers. [527] She did not push the newcomers to the edge. [536] She painted green fish on the banker's daughter's stall and on the acrobats' raft, gold spirals on tenants who had learned to laugh at the calliope, weaving the new blood in beside the old around the place her chip would rest. [577] The new heart would be ringed by people who had learned to love the Revel, whatever they had paid for it. [598]

At the second bell, the city came apart. [606] Ropes slithered, hulls groaned, and the calliope played in the lower key the whole time to hide the creaking while two hundred rafts drifted across black water, hunting their painted fish. [637] Tamsin stood on the empty center with the chip in her fist, terrified that one knot would fail and the whole Revel would scatter into separate, purchasable pieces. [665] Then the banker's daughter threw her a line, laughing and crying at once, and the acrobats caught the other end. [685]

By dawn the Hub raft floated at the rim, stone and all, and the surveyors found the charter's heart beating in a small pewter dish on a stall that sold soup. [716] The lawyers argued for six days about whether a chip was a stone. [729] On the seventh, the council voted, and the tenants nearest the heart voted no. [743] It would not hold forever; the Consortium would come back with better lawyers. [756] Tamsin opened both ledgers that night and, for the first time, wrote the same word in each: stay. [774]
