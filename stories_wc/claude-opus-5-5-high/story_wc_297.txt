The glade turned cobalt at dusk, the way it always had, but tonight Tamsin could hear the new trains humming beyond it. [22] The future had arrived eleven years early, three weeks ago, on a Tuesday. [35] Orla had foretold it all from her seat among the blue ferns: the rail, the glass depot, the surveyors with their humming tablets. [58] She had simply named the wrong decade. [65] Now the village was scrambling, and Orla, whose feet had long ago grown into the loam, could not scramble anywhere. [85]

Tamsin knelt in front of her and unwrapped the cloth. [95] Inside lay a single clipping of boxwood, green-black and still wet at the cut. [109] It was the last living piece of the old hedge maze, taken from the center turn an hour before the bulldozers cleared the ground for the platform. [136] Orla's bark-grey fingers closed over it, and her breath went out of her like wind through reeds. [153]

"You cut yourself taking it," she said. [160]

Tamsin turned her palm up. [165] A thorn from the climbing rose that had choked the maze's heart had opened a line from thumb to wrist, and it had already sealed into a pale seam, as maze wounds always did. [199] She had not flinched when it happened; she had watched the blood with the attention she gave to everything she did not yet understand, which was most things lately. [228]

"Good," Orla said. [231] "Then you can carry it." [236]

Everyone in the village knew the story, though no one had spoken it aloud in years. [252] When the founders came to the glade, they made a covenant with the one who dreamed there: the village would keep the maze, and the dreamer would keep the village's dreams from scattering. [285] Each founder had pressed a thumb to a blade and then to her bark, and the scars were still there, forty-one of them, spiraling up her trunk-thick legs like the rings of the maze itself. [320] But the depot's floodlights were brighter than any lantern, and the surveyors' promises were louder than any old vow, and the covenant had slipped into shadow like a moon behind a planet. [352] Nobody trimmed the maze; then nobody remembered why they should; then the maze was gone. [367]

And the dreams had begun to scatter. [374] Tamsin's mother woke weeping about staircases that went nowhere; the new engineers, lodging above the bakery, woke shouting numbers. [393]

"Old dreams and new ones are passing each other in the dark," Orla said. [407] "Somewhere they cross. [410] If we find the intersection and plant the maze there, the covenant can hold both." [425]

She took Tamsin's scarred hand and laid it against the oldest scar on her own bark. [441] "Heed me now," she said. [446] "Whatever you see, do not let go until it stops moving." [457]

The seam in Tamsin's palm went hot, and the glade fell away. [469] She was inside forty-one scars at once, each one a door, each door opening onto a sleeper. [486] The founders were dust, but their blood ran in half the village, and the scars remembered it. [503] She saw her mother's staircases, climbing and turning, and she understood with a jolt that they were the maze stood on end. [525] Then her own scar pulled at her, and she followed it to the rose thorn and the other hand it had torn that morning: the chief engineer's, marking out the platform. [556] He was dreaming of track switches branching and rejoining in a pattern he could not stop sketching, a pattern he did not know he had walked as a boy, here. [586]

The two dreams slid toward each other like hands feeling for a banister. [599] Tamsin's heart hammered; she wanted to shout, wanted to pull away, and did neither. [613] She held on until they locked, and the place where they crossed was not a place in the glade at all. [634] It was the center of the new platform, the exact point where the maze's heart had been. [651]

She opened her eyes to cobalt dark and Orla smiling, tears running into the grooves of her cheeks. [669]

"He dreams it too," Tamsin whispered. [675] "He just doesn't remember why." [680]

"Then go and remind him," Orla said, pressing the clipping into her scarred palm. [694] "Ask him to leave one square of earth unpaved, and show him your hand, and his." [710]

Tamsin ran out of the glade toward the floodlights, the boxwood cold against her seam, terrified that he would laugh, certain that he would not, and happier than she had been since the future came. [745] Behind her, the scars on Orla's legs glowed faintly, and above them a smooth patch of bark waited for a stranger's thumb. [767]
