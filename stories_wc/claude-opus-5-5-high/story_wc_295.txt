The observatory on Mount Serel kept its dome open until the frost came in, and Ines Varga kept her notebook open longer. [22] She was the station's lucid dream chronicler, a title the director had invented so the budget would allow her a cot and a lamp. [46] The mappers slept in shifts, and when they woke she wrote down what they had walked through with their eyes shut: drowned stairwells, a sky with no stars left to count. [77] Then she read the pages back to them in her low, careful voice, and something in their faces unclenched. [96]

People said she healed with words, which embarrassed her. [105] She only thought a fear named aloud was a fear that had to share the room. [121]

Tonight she had not come for the mappers. [129] In her coat pocket lay a telegram gone soft as cloth, a daylily pressed flat inside its fold, the orange petals bleached to the color of old brass. [157] Her brother Miklos had sent it from the dig at Tessaret three weeks before the fever took him. [175] The message read: FOUND THE WATCHERS LINE STOP IT COUNTS A WINK STOP FLOWER FROM TEMPLE STEPS STOP BRING YOUR DREAMS INES. [197]

She had brought her dreams. [202] For a month she had gone to sleep holding the telegram and woken, lucid, inside the temple he had died excavating, walking a corridor carved with glyphs no scholar had read: rows of paired figures lifting a single lamp, and beside them tallies, notched and gapped. [248] Each morning she copied the marks before they faded. [257] Each morning they meant nothing. [262] She had come up the mountain because the corridor in her dreams ended in an open roof full of stars, and she meant to decode his inscriptions if it took every night. [294]

Old Teodor Hask found her at the dome's rail after midnight. [305] He had mapped variable stars on this mountain for forty years, and since his wife died in spring he had stopped going down to sleep. [330] He glanced at her notebook and stopped. [337] "Those are not tallies," he said. [343] "That is a light curve." [348]

He turned the page toward the lamp and traced the gaps with a thick finger. [363] A long steadiness, a sudden fall, a quick return, the pattern repeating every two days and twenty-one hours. [381] "Algol," he said. [384] "The Demon Star." [387] "It winks." [389]

"Why would a temple count a winking star?" [397]

"Come and witness it, and maybe you will tell me." [407] By his tables the eclipse would begin within the hour. [417]

They sat together at the eyepiece of the old refractor while the dome ticked in the cold. [434] Teodor talked the way lonely men talk at night, about the tea his wife burned and the socks she darned wrong on purpose so he would notice. [461] When he finished, she gave his stories back to him in her own quiet sentences, the way she returned the mappers their dreams, and his shoulders came down from around his ears. [493] Then, because it was his turn to listen, she told him about Miklos: the way he laughed with his whole back, the fever, the flower he had picked for her on steps she would never climb. [529] Neither of them said it helped. [535] Both of them breathed easier. [540]

Toward three in the morning, Algol dimmed. [547] Through the lens it faded to a third of itself, a coal cooling in a grate, and Ines felt the old hollow open under her ribs. [573]

"Is it dying?" [576]

"No." [577] Teodor's voice was gentle. [581] "Algol is two stars." [585] "The dim companion passes in front of the bright one, and for a few hours it carries the whole weight of the dark." [608] "Then it moves on, and the light comes back, because the light was never gone." [623]

Ines looked down at her notebook, at the paired figures and the single lamp, and understanding came up in her like dawn. [645] The glyphs were not a prayer to the star. [654] They were a roster. [658] Priests had kept the temple lamp through the dark hours in pairs, their names cut beside each eclipse, so that when the sky dimmed no one watched alone. [686] The watchers line. [689] Miklos had found a vigil kept in turns, and he had sent her a one-day flower and asked for her dreams. [710] He had known he might not come back. [718] He had been telling her how to carry it. [727]

She read the glyphs aloud against the curve. [735] "When the lamp is eaten, two keep it." [743] "No dark is borne by one." [749]

Teodor wiped his eyes with the heel of his hand. [759] "I will take the next hours," he said. [767] "Sleep, and tell me what you dream." [774]

She lay beneath the open dome with the telegram on her chest, and above her the Demon Star began, slowly, to brighten. [796]
