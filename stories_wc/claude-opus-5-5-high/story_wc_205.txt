On Santa Clemenza, the old asteroid of gilt and marble, time now fell like pollen. [15] It had been visible for eleven years, ever since the Unwinding, and it gathered in the fluted niches and on the plump cheeks of the cherubs in amber drifts that hummed faintly when touched. [49] Ilse Varro swept it from the balustrade with the side of her hand and watched a Tuesday from her girlhood crumble through her fingers. [73]

Once she had been the poet of the Gilt Rising. [83] Her couplets had been painted on the Duke's own ceilings, her rhymes chanted as the palace doors came down. [102] Now the Rising was a closed file, its committees dissolved into the Office of Plain Stone, and she was a relic the young clerks greeted politely and did not consult. [132]

The Office meant to grind the palace into ballast. [141] Its chief archaeologist, Tomas Reyl, had written the finding himself: the ornament of Santa Clemenza was carved by indentured hands under the lash, and every garland was a monument to cruelty. [172] Ilse had believed that once. [177] She had written it, in fact, in meter. [185]

But in her coat pocket lay a crystal vase shard she had pried from the rubble of the Mirror Hall, and it disputed him. [209] Etched along its inner curve, too fine for any Duke to notice, were tiny signatures: a hammer, a sparrow, the initials of a guild. [233] She had found the same sparrow hidden in the curls of forty cherubs. [246] Reyl called it wishful graffiti. [251] At the hearing he had smiled at her with the patience reserved for the old, and said, "Citizen Varro, you taught us that beauty is the tyrant's alibi." [279] He was quoting her third pamphlet. [285]

She had no rebuttal except the storm. [292]

When time storms crossed the asteroid, loose hours blew through the corridors like sleet, and any mirror left bare would catch them and play them back. [318] So everyone covered their mirrors during storms, the way their grandmothers had covered them for the dead: grey cloth, knotted tight, the glass turned to the wall. [345] The Office enforced it as a matter of public calm. [355] No one wished to see what the rooms remembered. [364]

Ilse spent a month devising her heresy. [371] She mapped the Mirror Hall's thirty-one surviving panes, calculated their angles from the ceiling frescoes, and ground the shard's edge until it would sit in a brass ring and fold light the way a sonnet folds grief. [408] Set at the focal point, it would gather every mirror's catch into one image. [422] Then she sent Reyl a note: Come to the Hall when the sky turns. [436] Bring no cloth. [439]

He came, of course; archaeologists cannot resist a forbidden stratum. [449]

The storm broke over the gilt domes an hour after dusk. [460] Hours hissed across the floor, some bright as new coin, some tarnished to the color of old tea. [478] Ilse tore the grey cloths from the mirrors one by one, and Reyl, protesting, did not stop her. [496]

In the shard, the Hall assembled itself backward. [504] They saw carvers on scaffolds, paid in silver at a long table, arguing about a cherub's nose. [521] They saw a woman with a sparrow tattooed on her wrist press her thumb into wet plaster and laugh. [540] They saw the Duke, too, small and bored, never once looking up at what they had made for him. [559] And then they saw the night of the Rising: torches, a girl with ink on her hands and a poem in her mouth, lifting the sparrow woman's vase and hurling it at the glass. [593]

Ilse had known it would be there. [600] She had come anyway, because a civilization cannot be reimagined by someone unwilling to be seen in it. [618]

Reyl was silent for a long time. [625] "We were not wrong about the Duke," he said at last. [636]

"No," Ilse said. [639] "Only about who the palace belonged to." [646]

She had wanted, all these years, to write a different history for Santa Clemenza, one in which the people who carved the angels were not erased twice, first by the Duke's indifference and then by her own rage. [684] The ornament was not the tyrant's alibi; it was the carvers' testimony, and a new republic could inherit it without inheriting him. [706]

When the storm passed, Reyl did not reach for the cloths. [717] He took out his field book, crossed through his finding, and asked her how the sparrow woman's guild had spelled its name. [739]

Ilse set the shard on the sill, where the settling hours gilded its broken edge, and began, slowly, to compose the first line of an apology in verse. [767]
