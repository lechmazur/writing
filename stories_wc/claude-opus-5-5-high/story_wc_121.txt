When the rain stopped, the Calloway Viaduct began to glow. [10] Its old stones had soaked up the lamplight all evening, and now every arch shone amber and green, like a bridge remembering a sunset it had never seen. [38] Ilse Varr walked along its underside, boots planted on the keystones, the river road hanging above her head like a second sky. [60] She had fallen upward at nine years old, off this same parapet, and gravity had never fully taken her back. [80]

Tonight she carried the tablet against her ribs. [88] It was clay, older than the city, dug from the viaduct's flooded footing by workmen who had dropped it and run when the runes began to crawl. [115] Ilse had not run. [119] She had spent eleven years learning that the marks were not a message but a grammar: a small set of rules that, fed a single seed, would unfold into corridors without end. [151] The seed was always a raindrop. [157]

She reached the central arch and waited. [164] Water gathered at the lip of the stone, swelled, and let go. [176] It fell past her face toward the puddle on the road, struck the surface, and stopped. [192] The ripple did not come. [197]

This was the gap she lived for, between the drop and the ripple, where the world held its breath and Ilse became, for a while, eternal. [223] Not always; only periodically, only after rain, only here. [232] But while the drop hung in its crown of splash, no clock could touch her. [247]

She pressed her thumb to the tablet, and the runes read the drop. [260] Its weight, its angle, the eleven beads of splash frozen around it became numbers, and the numbers became a labyrinth. [280] The viaduct's arches multiplied, branching into arches that had never been built, each one opening onto a version of her life. [301] Through one she saw the girl who had fallen down instead of up, and broken, and grown old in a chair by a window. [325] Through another, the girl who had never climbed the parapet at all, who had married and laughed easily and never once looked at the sky with suspicion. [352]

Every rain generated a new maze from the same rules, and every maze was made of her. [369] She had been retracing these fractured timelines for years, walking each corridor back to the parapet, searching for the one moment where she had split. [394] If she could find it, she believed, she could stand at the center of herself and choose, and the labyrinth would finally be conquered. [418]

Tonight she walked faster. [422] The glow was dimmer than it used to be. [431] She had noticed it for a year and refused to name it: each pause she stretched drew its light from the stones, and the viaduct was running out of evenings to give. [463] Amber faded to bruise-violet as she ran. [470] The corridors rearranged themselves behind her, obedient to the runes, until she turned a corner and found the parapet again, and a nine-year-old girl standing on it with her arms spread. [501]

Ilse stopped. [503] The girl was not frightened. [508] She was grinning, face tipped toward the clouds, already leaning past the point of return. [523]

There was no split here, Ilse realized, no single moment when a better life had been stolen. [540] The tablet had never been showing her where she broke. [550] It had only been doing what it was built to do, taking whatever she fed it and generating more of her, forever, a maze whose walls grew exactly as fast as she could walk. [584] You could not conquer a labyrinth by retracing it. [593] You conquered it by leaving. [598]

The girl looked back at her, curious. [605] "Does it hurt?" she asked. [610]

"Sometimes," Ilse said. [613] "Mostly it's just lonely up there." [619]

The girl considered this, then laughed and let go of the parapet anyway, rising into the violet dark like a released breath. [641] Ilse did not follow. [645] She lifted the tablet, found a corner the rain had softened, and with her thumbnail scratched a single line: an ending, the one rule the grammar had lacked. [673]

The corridors folded shut like a closing hand. [681] On the road, the drop finally finished its fall, and the ripple spread across the puddle in widening rings of dying gold. [703] The viaduct went dark, stone by stone, and for the first time in twenty years Ilse felt time touch her, cold and ordinary, like the rain. [729] Then she loosened her hold on the sky and drifted down through the dark, slowly, the right way at last. [749]
