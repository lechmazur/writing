The leaves were turning on the hill above Oskar Brandt's cavern, and Ines Varga could see them rustle without hearing a single one. [23] Two winters ago a fever had taken her hearing, and the school had kept her on out of kindness, a deaf music teacher tapping rhythms onto children's shoulders. [51] She did the work well. [56] What frightened her was that she no longer cared. [65] Grief had come first, loud and wet, and then it had gone quiet, and the quiet had spread until her own laughter felt like something happening to a stranger. [94]

She had come to the cavern because Oskar's note said only: Bring the choir discs. [109] He made drums in the hollow of the hill, where the stone held the damp steady while the hides cured, and the walls were hung with frames of goat and calf skin stretched like pale moons. [145] This time of year he wrapped every finished drum in felt, because the change of season tightened and loosened the heads unpredictably, and an unwrapped drum would crack itself singing. [175] The whole cavern was audibly muted, he wrote on his slate, as if that were a joke she would enjoy. [195] She did not smile. [199]

On a bench sat her old phonograph, the one she had lent him years ago to play for customers. [218] Its needle had snapped at the tip, leaving a jagged sliver. [229] Played forward, Oskar wrote, it only chewed the grooves. [238] But the broken edge faced backward now, like a hook, and when he spun the platter in reverse by hand it rode the groove cleanly. [263] He had discovered this by accident, testing drumheads against it, and had thought of her at once. [280]

He unwrapped the largest drum, a bass as wide as a cart wheel, set the horn of the phonograph against its skin, and guided her palms onto the head. [309] Then he began to turn the platter backward with one steady finger. [321]

Ines had felt music through floors since the fever, and it had always arrived as a blow, a thud that faded before she could hold it. [347] This was different. [350] Backward, every note began as nothing and swelled, gathering like a breath drawn in, rising toward its own beginning. [369] She could feel it coming. [374] Between swells the broken needle skipped once per turn, and in that skip was a filament of hush, a hair-thin line where the drum went still beneath her hands. [403] The stillness made the next swell unbearable to wait for. [413]

She knew this disc. [417] It was the spring concert from four years ago, thirty children singing the river song she had taught them, and backward she could feel the place where little Tomas always came in early, a small wrong bump the size of a thumbprint. [459]

She laughed. [461] It startled her, the laugh, because it was hers. [470] Then she was crying, which she had not done since the first winter of her silence, and Oskar kept turning the platter and did not look at her, which was the kindest thing he could have done. [507] At the cave's mouth, leaves drifted in and settled on the felted drums without a sound, and for once the silence did not feel like an absence. [534] It felt like the pause before an entrance. [542]

She came back every afternoon while the season turned. [551] By the first frost she had asked Oskar for twelve small drums, and he had built them, grumbling on his slate about the cost of good calfskin. [578] She brought her students down into the hill in a line, each child's hand on the shoulder ahead. [596] She taught them to sing a phrase, then to sing it again backward, starting soft and swelling toward the first word, while their palms rested on the drums. [624] They were enthralled, not by the strange sounds, which the muffled cavern swallowed, but by the tremor under their hands, the way a song could be felt arriving. [652] Tomas, taller now, came in early again. [659] Ines felt the bump beneath her fingers and grinned at him, and he grinned back, delighted to be caught. [678]

Afterward, when the children had climbed out into the cold, she stayed by the big drum with Oskar. [696] He held up a new needle, still in its tin, and raised his eyebrows. [710] Ines shook her head. [714] She laid her hand flat on the drum, and he laid his beside it, and they waited together in that mirrored hush, two people listening with their hands for the song to come back toward them. [750] Then he turned the platter, and it did. [758]
