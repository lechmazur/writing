The mist came up out of Harrow Quarry like breath from a sleeper, and Mira Achterberg set her easel on the ledge where Lena Voss had last been seen alive. [30] She could not have told you what Lena looked like. [40] Mira could not have told you what her own mother looked like, or the man now climbing the gravel path behind her, wheezing, his boots crunching in a rhythm she had memorized on their first meeting. [76] Faces slid off her like water off slate. [84] So she painted everything else. [89]

"You're early," said Aldous Kett, retired detective sergeant, left knee bad, voice like a drawer of loose screws. [107]

"I'm on time," she said. [112] "You're late by four minutes, which is consistent with the last three times." [125]

She was very methodical; it was how she survived a world of strangers who all claimed to know her. [144] She kept notebooks of gaits, coughs, rings, the way people held cups. [156] Kett held his in both hands, as if it might be taken. [168]

Lena's sister had commissioned the portrait. [174] Twenty-six years after the masquerade, eighteen months after the county evidence store flooded and the Voss box came out as pulp, there was no photograph left of Lena anywhere. [203] The family albums had burned with the Voss farmhouse years before. [214] The case was cold in the way a stone is cold: nobody expected it to change. [230] Kett had worked it, and Kett had let it go. [240]

"I told the sister," he said, lowering himself onto a block of cut limestone, "I only saw the girl dead." [260]

"That's why I need you." [265]

Her method was simple and slow. [271] She painted every subject twice, on the same canvas, from opposite witnesses: once from someone who loved them, once from someone who did not. [295] Love exaggerated; indifference erased. [299] Where the two layers agreed, she found the person. [308] The sister had given her the bottom layer over six afternoons: a chipped front tooth, a laugh that started in the nose, a habit of tucking hair behind only the left ear. [340] Now Mira needed the top. [345]

Kett described what he remembered: the gray skin, the pooled mist, the black velvet mask lying a yard from her hand with one ribbon gone, the conclusion everyone reached by lunch that a drunk girl from the ball had wandered too near the edge. [389] She painted as he spoke, a mask-shaped darkness over the face the sister had built, and the mist thickened until the quarry floor below became rumor. [415]

"There's something else," she said, not looking up. [423] "The sister says you kept something." [429]

The rhythm of his breathing changed. [435] After a long while he took out his wallet and, from behind a library card thirty years expired, drew a strip of red satin no longer than a finger. [464] "Found it on a thorn up here a week after," he muttered. [476] "Case was already closed, so I never logged it." [485]

"Then it's the only piece of her that didn't drown," Mira said. [497]

She took the fragment and did what she always did with details, because details were all she had: she looked at both ends. [520] One end was frayed where it had torn from the thorn. [531] The other was sheared straight, the satin threads crisp as a ruled line. [544]

"Somebody cut her mask off," Mira said quietly. [552] "A girl who falls doesn't need scissors." [559]

Kett said nothing. [562] The hush between them furrowed, drew tight. [569]

Then Mira took her palette knife, the thin one she used for scraping highlights, and lanced through the still-wet black at the eyes in one clean stroke, the way someone had once cut a ribbon. [604] Beneath it, the sister's layer showed through: two eyes, slightly uneven, the left one creased as if about to laugh. [624] Love and indifference, bright and dark, met along the edge of the cut, and where they met was a girl. [644]

Mira could not hold the face in her mind; she never could. [656] She could only see Kett's hands, and they had begun to shake. [668]

"She was a kid," he said, and the words came out rough, as if pulled through a wall he had built one closed file at a time. [695] "She was a kid, and I wrote her down as drunk." [706]

"Then write something else," Mira said. [712] "Who at that ball carried scissors?" [718]

He looked at the painted girl for a long time, then down into the mist, where the quarry kept its patient dark. [740] "The hotel kept a guest list for the costume prize," he said slowly. [753] "Hotels don't flood." [756]

Mira wiped the knife on her sleeve and wrote the time in her notebook, beside a new entry for Kett: hands steady again. [779]
