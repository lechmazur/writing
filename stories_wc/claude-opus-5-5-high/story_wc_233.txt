The last bell from the observatory rang at dusk, and after the final warning there was nothing left for the court to do but pray or pack. [27] Queen Ysolde did neither. [31] She had been disenchanted with crowns since the winter her mother died, and she no longer trusted prayers, which in her experience were only requests politely ignored. [58] Instead she took off her rings, laced her boots, and began to climb. [71]

Pell, the goatherd's boy who knew the ridge path, went ahead with a lantern. [85] She had not ordered him to come. [92] She had only said, softly, that she would be grateful, and he had discovered himself already walking, which was how most people obeyed her. [116]

Above them the sky was coming undone. [123] For nine nights the stars had been bleeding, thin silver threads unspooling from each point of light and drifting down through the dark like loosened embroidery. [149] Where the threads touched the ground, frost bloomed in lace patterns, and the lace was sharp enough to cut. [168] A shepherd in the lowlands had lost three fingers to a doily of light on his gate. [185] The astronomers said that when the last star emptied, the lace would cover everything. [199]

Ysolde had dreamed of this before it began. [207] Every night for a year she had seen the same thing: a blue cave above the clouds, a small hollow of coals in its wall, and a stranger's face beside hers, both of them drawing breath as if about to speak. [248] She always woke before the breath came out. [256] She had told no one, because queens who confess dreams are either anointed or deposed, and she wanted neither. [275] But the dream would not leave her, and now the sky would not wait. [289]

They broke through the cloud line past midnight. [297] The mist fell away beneath them like a dropped veil, and there, cut into the mountain's shoulder, was the cobalt grotto. [318] Its walls were a blue so deep they seemed wet, and bleeding starlight had gathered along the ceiling in filigree, dripping in slow lacy strands that hissed where they struck stone. [349] Pell stopped at the entrance. [354] "It's pretty," he whispered, "the way a snare is pretty." [364]

Ysolde went in anyway, stepping between the falling threads. [373] One brushed her sleeve and the silk parted without a sound. [384] At the back of the cave, just as in the dream, a niche opened in the wall toward the outer ridge: the ember grotto, a hollow no larger than a cradle, lined with old soot and a bed of coals gone gray. [426] Carved above it, worn nearly smooth, were two figures facing each other, mouths open. [440] One wore a crown. [444] The other wore nothing to mark it at all. [453]

Ysolde knelt, and understanding arrived the way cold does, all at once and everywhere. [467] Her mother had climbed here every summer with some servant chosen at random, calling it a pilgrimage and refusing to explain. [488] Ysolde, grieving and furious, had let the custom die with her, and had not climbed last summer at all. [507] The stars had started bleeding within the year. [515] The dream had not been a prophecy; it had been a reminder, patient as lace, knotting itself tighter each night. [535]

"Pell," she said, very gently, "come and kneel beside me." [545] He came, ducking a strand that sliced a lock of his hair loose. [558] The lace on the ceiling was thickening now, lowering like a canopy, and the air tasted of cold metal. [577] "What do we do?" he asked. [583] "I don't know," she admitted, and found that saying it felt better than any decree she had ever issued. [602] "We wait for something, I think." [608]

They waited, shoulders touching, while the canopy of light sank until its lowest threads hung a hand's width above their heads. [629] Then, through the mouth of the ember grotto, far down where the clouds thinned over the summer valleys, a single green spark rose. [652] Then another, and a dozen more. [658] The first fireflies of summer, drifting up out of the dark. [669] Queen and goatherd saw them at the same instant, and the breath each had been holding left them together in one soft, astonished exhale. [693]

It touched the gray coals, and the coals woke. [702] Red light swelled out of the ember grotto, rolled across the cobalt walls, and rose into the lace, which curled back from it like thread from a candle. [730] Outside, the fireflies climbed on the warm draft, higher than any insect should, and where they reached the stars the bleeding stopped, each wound sealed with a tiny green stitch. [760]

Ysolde watched until dawn. [764] She was still tired of crowns, but now she knew what hers was for: not to command the sky, but to kneel once a year beside anyone at all and breathe. [795]
