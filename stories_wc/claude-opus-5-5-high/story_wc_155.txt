Oda Venn had painted the loudest walls in Carrow, tangerine giraffes and violet trams and a forty-foot grandmother eating soup, but lately her brushes dried stiff in their jar. [29] She kept two things in her coat pocket that spring. [39] One was a roll of her father's film, the last he ever shot, which she had pried open at nine years old because she could not wait to see his pictures. [70] The daylight had rushed in and eaten every one. [79] The other was a fresco fragment the size of a playing card: half a painted mouth, open in a laugh, beside the curve of a pear. [105] Her father had pried it loose from a wall in the orchard district, where the fog had sat for three hundred years and where nobody else bothered to go. [134]

On a Tuesday, waiting for a kettle, she leaned her head against the cold window and let her thoughts go soft, and the daydream folded open like a letter. [163]

Inside the fold, she was standing among ruined pear trees, their branches black and bearded with moss, the fog so thick it seemed to have been poured rather than breathed. [193] Broken walls leaned between the rows, and every wall was blank. [204]

"Overexposed," said a voice, and a small man in a picker's apron stepped out of the white, holding a ladder he did not seem to need. [230] "That's what the fog is, miss. [236] The district remembered itself too hard, all at once, and the light got in." [250]

Oda thought of the roll of film in her pocket and felt, absurdly, understood by a stranger with a useless ladder. [271] She showed him the fragment. [276] He squinted at the half mouth and grinned, missing two teeth. [287]

"Harvest Hall," he said. [291] "Nobody alive has seen it whole, but between us we remember the lot." [304]

He climbed his ladder, which leaned on nothing, and knocked three times on the nearest trunk. [320] The knock traveled down the row, tree to tree, and answers came knocking back out of the fog. [338] This, he explained, was how the pickers kept their knowledge network: every orchard ghost held one small true thing, and the grafted roots carried questions from keeper to keeper until an answer ripened. [371]

A widow two rows over knew the ceiling had been blue. [382] A boy who had fallen asleep in a cider barrel two centuries ago, and saw no reason to wake, knew that the laughing mouth belonged to Brother Anselm, who had slipped off a ladder into the pressing vat during the blessing of the fruit. [426] The painter had set the whole village around the vat, and Anselm, dripping, had laughed loudest of all. [444] Piece by piece the network gave her the picture: the pears, the red kerchiefs, a dog stealing bread, a mayor pretending not to see. [468]

"But you can't fix exposed film," Oda said. [476] "I tried, once." [479]

"No," said the picker. [483] "But a wall that's gone white will take whatever you shine on it." [496]

She had no paint, but it was her daydream, so when she lifted her empty hand the fingertips came alight, amber and rose and orchard green. [522] She painted with light the way she used to paint with house enamel, recklessly, in big wet arcs, laying Anselm into the fog above the ruined hall and the village around him. [554] The fog held every stroke, the way a darkroom holds a secret. [566] When she set the fragment into the place where his mouth should be, the whole fresco steadied, and the pickers came out of the trees to see it on display, a harvest three centuries late. [601]

The widow wept a little. [606] The boy in the barrel pointed at the dog and snorted. [617]

Oda looked at Anselm, soaked to his tonsure, laughing at his own ridiculous fall in front of everyone he loved, and understood that joy had never been the picture itself. [647] It was the willingness to be seen falling, and to make light again after light has ruined everything. [665] She laughed, and the laugh startled her like an old friend knocking at the wrong door. [681]

The kettle screamed. [684]

Oda lifted her head from the window, and her pocket held only the ruined film, the fragment gone wherever daydreams keep their furniture. [707] She did not mind. [711] She took the stiff brushes from their jar, set them to soak, and went looking for the largest, whitest wall in Carrow. [733]
