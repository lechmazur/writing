After the resuscitation, every photograph of Ilse came back wrong. [10] There was always a smear of pale light at her left shoulder, like breath on cold glass, and inside the light stood a tall figure in a long opera cloak. [40] Her mother burned the prints. [45] Her friends stopped taking pictures. [50] Ilse kept buying Polaroid film anyway, because a curse you can photograph is a curse you can study. [68]

"It followed you out of the water," Halloran whispered. [77] He had pulled her from the flooded Tunnel of Love beneath the old funhouse and shouted for help until his throat tore, and now he could only whisper. [105] He had owned Mirthland once, before the river took the midway. [116] Now he haunted its edges the way the thing haunted her pictures, and he spoke so softly she had to lean close enough to smell the peppermint and rust on him. [147]

"Fear does that," he breathed. [152] "It comes home with whoever survives." [158]

She still had the opera cloak clasp. [165] The paramedics had needed ten minutes to pry her fingers open around it: a silver moth with garnet eyes, its wings tarnished green. [188] Halloran recognized it at once. [193] It had fastened his wife's cloak the night she walked into the tunnel forty years ago and did not walk out. [214]

He gave Ilse his wife's dream dictionary. [221] It was swollen with damp, its entries crowded by Marguerite's handwriting, and every note contradicted the printed meaning beside it. [241] Beside WATER, the book promised cleansing; Marguerite had written, To dream of drowning is to be held. [258] Beside MIRROR: To be seen, turn your back. [266] Beside FEAR, underlined twice: It cannot eat what you give it freely. [278] Unbosom, and it must show where it is thin. [287]

"She was trying to trick it," Halloran whispered. [295] "To make fear reveal its weakness." [301] "She never finished." [304]

So on the first dry night of October, Ilse climbed through the clown's mouth into the funhouse. [321] The painted lips were split and sagging, the teeth furred with moss, and still the entrance was lovely in the way a bruise is lovely, violet and gold under her flashlight. [352] The air tasted of spun sugar gone to rot. [361] Somewhere below, water ticked against the tunnel boats. [369]

The mirror maze had kept its glass. [376] Her reflection multiplied into a corridor of Ilses, and at the edge of each one the pale luminance lingered, brighter here than in any photograph. [401] The cloaked figure did not need film anymore. [409] It stood in every mirror, close behind her, velvet the color of drowned lilies, and it was beautiful. [427] She hated how much she wanted to lean back into it. [438]

She opened the dictionary to the page Marguerite had dog-eared. [448] To dream of hiding is to be found. [456] To dream of confessing is to be the one who finds. [467]

Ilse turned her back to the mirrors, as the note said, and raised the camera over her shoulder. [485]

"I didn't fall," she said aloud. [491] Her voice cracked against the tin ceiling. [498] "I climbed the fence and walked into the tunnel because I wanted to stop. [512] I told everyone it was an accident. [519] I let Halloran ruin his voice saving someone who went in on purpose." [532]

The secret left her like a stone rising out of her chest. [544] Behind her, the luminance guttered. [549] She pressed the shutter. [553]

The flash tore white through the maze, and in the afterglow burned into her eyes she saw the figure clearly for the first time. [577] The cloak hung open. [581] Where the clasp should have held it closed there was nothing, and beneath the velvet there was nothing either, only a hollow shaped exactly like the place she had kept her secret. [613] Fear had worn Marguerite's cloak, and then her silence, because it had no body of its own. [630] It needed something unsaid to fill it. [637]

That was the weakness. [641] It could only stand where she stood silent. [649]

The figure reached for the silver moth in her fist. [659] Its fingers were cold and sweet as lily stems. [668] Ilse did not give it back. [674] She pinned the clasp to her own collar, closed her coat with it, and said Marguerite's name, and then her own, into the dark. [698]

The luminance thinned to a thread and went out. [707]

Outside, Halloran waited by the ticket booth. [714] She showed him the Polaroid as it developed: a girl alone in a maze of glass, eyes red from the flash, a small silver moth at her throat. [742]

He studied it a long time, and his eyes were wet. [753]

"Now," he whispered, "tell me the rest." [760]

And she did. [763]
