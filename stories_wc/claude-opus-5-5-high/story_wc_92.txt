At twilight Fort Candela always glowed, its walls veined with pale fire drawn up from the Cradle of Sparks, and the town below, which paid for that glow in oil taxes, sat in the dark and watched. [37] Tonight the fort went dark too. [43] No one knew why. [47]

The lamps simply sighed out, bastion by bastion, like a choir losing its breath, and in the sudden hush Idris Vane, lock sentinel of the inner keep, set down his tea and counted the shouts. [82] He had kept the fort's two hundred and twelve locks for nineteen years, and he knew that panic, like rust, always started at the hinges. [107]

The captain came first, sword half drawn against nothing. [116] "Sabotage," he hissed. [119] "Is the Cradle sealed?" [123]

"Seven bolts, three wards, and my own bad temper," Idris said mildly. [135] "Nobody has touched it, Captain, which I suspect is the problem." [146]

The captain did not understand that, which was the point, and he hurried off to shout at the gunners instead. [166]

Idris lifted the bronze quill holder from his desk. [175] It had been a surveyor's gift, a squat bronze cup whose fluted rim was etched with degrees, and for weeks it had been his secret instrument. [201] He tipped the quills out, tucked it under his cloak, and climbed to the east bastion, where the half-painted sundial waited on the wall. [225]

The Governor had commissioned it from Wren Ashby, a town painter permitted inside the walls because she was cheap and because he thought painters harmless. [250] She worked slowly, one hour line each evening, and none of her lines fell where a sundial's should. [268] The Governor never noticed. [272] Idris had. [274]

He had set his quill holder against the painted gnomon on the first night, read the angle off its rim, and found a letter. [298] Twenty-five nights had given him a sentence: THE CRADLE BELONGS TO THE TOWN. [311] He had answered the only way a lock sentinel could: by leaving the east postern's bolt oiled, and the painter's ladder unsearched. [333]

Tonight a new line glistened, still wet, though Wren was nowhere in sight. [346] Idris held up a shuttered candle, fitted the bronze rim to the gnomon, and read. [361] Not a letter. [364] A number. [366] Twelve degrees past noon, which in the shorthand they had built line by line meant one word: carry. [384]

He smiled into his collar. [389] He had not caused the blackout, and he doubted Wren had, but a calmly inventive man did not waste a gift simply because he could not read the card. [418]

He went down to the Cradle. [424] Behind its seven bolts lay a basin of cinder-stones, each one a slow ember that could light a street lamp for a month, and the Governor had spent years hoarding them to keep his fort luminous and his town obedient. [464] Tonight the stones were dim, sulking, as though something had simply told them to rest. [479] Idris opened the bolts in the order only he knew, scooped the smallest stones into the bronze quill holder, and felt them warm against his palm like hatchlings. [507] Bronze would not burn; that, too, had been part of the plan. [519] Then he relocked every bolt, because a sentinel's report ought to be true. [532]

At the east postern, the oiled bolt slid back without a whisper. [544] Wren stood outside with paint on her wrists and a dozen townsfolk behind her, each holding an empty lantern. [563]

"Did you do this?" he murmured, nodding at the dark fort. [574]

"I was going to ask you," she whispered, and her eyes glittered with the sort of mischief that intrigued him more than any cipher. [598]

He tipped the stones into her lanterns one by one. [608] There were no speeches, no torches raised, no bells. [617] The townsfolk simply walked home and hung their lanterns in their windows, and street by street the town began to glow. [638] The cradle of sparks, Idris thought, had never really been a basin in a vault; it was any place a light was first handed to someone who had been told to live without it. [672]

By midnight the fort's lamps flickered back, as mysteriously as they had failed, and from the battlements the Governor stared down at a town that no longer needed him to see. [703]

"Report, sentinel," the captain demanded. [708]

"All locks secure," said Idris, and it was perfectly true. [718]

The next evening, Wren painted one more line on the sundial, and Idris did not need the bronze cup to read it; it sat at the exact angle of a wink. [749]
