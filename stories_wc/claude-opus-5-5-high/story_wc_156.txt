Marisol Okafor wore forty years of pigment on her coveralls, and children in the sound wave garden liked to point at the colors and guess which wall each one came from. [31] She always told them the answer, and she was always right. [42] The garden had been built in 1936 by a relief crew: nine concrete dishes and a long curved wall arranged so that a whisper at one end arrived, reflected and whole, at the other. [76] The wall carried a 1974 mural of dancers, and Marisol had been hired to restore it. [92] Three weeks in, her solvent swab had lifted a dancer's knee and found an older face beneath: a woman in a gray cloche, painted in the flat WPA style, looking straight out. [124] Lodged in the plaster beside her cheek was a buffalo nickel. [135] Someone had carved away the buffalo with a knife point and left, in its place, the same woman, cloche and all, no bigger than a thumbnail. [161] A hobo nickel, the kind drifting men once traded for a meal. [173]

Now Dale Pruett from the arts commission stood beside her scaffold with his clipboard, and he asked the question she had been dreading. [196] "So which one is the real mural?" [203]

For thirty years she would have answered before he finished: the oldest, always the oldest, strip everything back to first intent. [224] Certainty had made her reputation. [229] Lately it had also made her tired, as if every wall were a test she had already graded. [247]

So instead of answering, in the pause between his question and her reply, she did what she did for the children. [268] She held up one finger, the way a magician announces the rules, and flicked the nickel against the rim of the nearest dish. [291] It rang like a small bell. [297] She had timed this garden with a stopwatch, the way other people learned hymns. [311]

The first echo returned from the second dish in under half a second, and with it came the first waking. [331] The carver had not preserved anything. [337] He had taken a government coin and cut a buffalo into a woman, and nobody who held it afterward would call the buffalo more real. [362]

The second echo came off the curved wall itself, softer, and she woke inside the first waking. [379] The 1974 painter had covered the woman everywhere except her left eye, which sat in the crook of a dancer's elbow like a wink. [403] Marisol had catalogued it as an error on day one. [413] It was not an error. [418] It was a message one painter had whispered down the wall toward the next. [432]

The third echo was the faintest, returning from the far dish near the gate where the relief crew had pressed their initials into wet concrete, and inside it she woke a third time. [465] She was a layer too. [470] Whatever she chose, someone with a swab would one day find her decisions under their own and wonder what she had been thinking. [493] The only thing she could leave them worth finding was a good question. [506]

The ringing faded. [509] Dale was staring at the dish, then at the wall, then at her, his clipboard lowered, his mouth slightly open like a child who has just heard his own whisper come back from across a park. [545] She had meant to bewitch him a little; she had not expected to bewitch herself. [560]

"Did you hear that?" she asked. [566]

"Three times," he said. [570] "Each one farther away." [574]

"That's the answer," Marisol said. [579] "None of them is the real mural, and all of them are." [591]

She climbed down and sketched on his clipboard: restore the dancers, but cut a window at the elbow, framed and lit, so the woman in the cloche could look out through the 1974 paint the way she had looked out of the nickel. [634] Beneath it she added a plaque line: Who was she? [644]

"That's not an answer," Dale said. [650]

"No," Marisol agreed, pocketing the nickel, and for the first time in years she felt the pleasant vertigo of not knowing. [671] "It's better." [673]

Across the garden, a child at the far dish shouted hello, and the wall carried it back to them, whole. [693]
