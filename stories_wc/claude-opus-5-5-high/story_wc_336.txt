The orchard grew on the old parapet, where the fortress wall had long ago surrendered to moss and roots. [19] Beside it the waterfall fell in a white, unending sigh, and its mist kept every leaf a green so deep it seemed lit from within. [44] Orla had pruned those trees for fifty-one springs. [52] She knew which branch was hungry and which was proud, and she cut only what the tree was already trying to let go. [75]

It was the week of the hush, when every bud was swollen and none had opened, and the whole terrace seemed to hold its breath. [100] Orla loved it best of all the weeks, because it was made entirely of promise. [115]

In her apron pocket, as always, she carried the seed. [125] It had come to her from her grandmother, who had it from hers, a brown teardrop no bigger than a thumbnail. [146] The family said it came from the first tree of the orchard, whose blossoms had been a color no one could name. [168] Not pink, not gold, but the color a child sees when she first understands that the world is larger than her house. [190] The color of wonder, her grandmother called it, and it had not been seen since the old tree died. [209]

Orla had planted it a dozen times, in a dozen soils, and dug it up each autumn unwoken. [227] So she had tried, instead, to catch the color some other way. [239] She could never leave a thing alone. [246] She had boiled bark for dyes, ground petals into paint, grafted plum to quince to see what flowered, and braided willow into lattices so fine the mist beaded on them like strung pearls. [279] None of it was the color, but every failure taught her something about the shape of what was missing. [298]

That morning, a girl stepped out of the waterfall. [307] She was not wet. [311] She was grey, entirely, from her hair to her bare feet, as if drawn in ash on the air. [330] She shivered on the moss and stared at the green as if it hurt. [344]

Orla had heard the stories: that the falls were a seam, and that on the other side lay a place very like this one, only faded. [370] She set down her shears, wrapped her shawl around the child, and gave her bread and warm cider, because succor comes before questions. [393]

When the girl could speak, she said her name was Wren. [404] "Our trees stopped blooming," she said. [410] "First the color went out of the blossoms, then out of the fruit, then out of us." [427] "My grandmother said a tree on your side was our tree's sister, and that they used to bloom together." [446]

Orla closed her hand around the seed in her pocket. [456] At last she understood why it had never woken in her soil alone. [469] The first tree had not grown in one world, but between two, its roots in this bank and its crown in the mist, drinking from both. [495]

She fetched her finest willow lattice, the one she had never known what to do with. [511] Together they carried it to the lip of the parapet, where the falls broke into spray, and Orla wove its loose ends into the mist while Wren, reaching back through the seam, wove from the other side. [548] Their hands met in the white, one warm and one cold. [559] In the crook of the weaving, where the two worlds knotted, Orla pressed the seed into a pocket of moss. [579]

Nothing happened for a long time. [585] The falls hushed to a murmur, and the whole orchard seemed to lean toward them. [600] Then the seed split, and a single leaf unfurled, and it was that color. [614]

Orla did not weep, though she wanted to. [622] She had spent her life trying to hold that color still, and now she knew the trick was not holding but catching. [644] She uncorked the little glass vial she used for grafting sap and tipped it beneath the leaf, and the first bead of dew rolled in, carrying the color with it like a coal carries fire. [679] The essence of it, bright and small. [686] She corked it and folded it into Wren's grey hand. [696]

"For your trees," she said. [701] "The sapling will grow on both sides now, but your orchard will need reminding what it was." [718]

Where the vial touched her, Wren's fingertips blushed with the faintest wonder. [730] She smiled, and stepped back into the falls, and the lattice trembled as she went. [745]

Orla picked up her shears and stood a while in the radiant hush, watching the new leaf breathe in two worlds at once, already thinking of what else might be woven there. [777]
