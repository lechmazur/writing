The heat wave broke at four in the morning, and by dawn the fog had come in off the strait like something apologizing. [23] Wren Halloway, assistant lighthouse keeper of Saltmarrow Point, climbed the cobalt tower with her palms on the cool iron for the first time in eleven days. [49] The whole lighthouse was painted blue, glazed blue, even the lantern glass tinted cobalt by some keeper a century dead, so that at sunrise the room at the top filled like the inside of a wave. [85] Tobias was already there, shouting. [90] He had the brass speaking trumpet jammed against the open gallery door, and he was bellowing his son's name into the white. [112] "Ansel, ANSEL!" [114] The boy had taken the dory out at midnight to set pots in the first cool air anyone had breathed in over a week, and the fog had eaten him. [144] The horn's diaphragm had split in the heat on the eighth day; Wren had written the request for a new one herself, and the Board had not answered. [172]

"Stop," she said. [175] "You're only pushing him away." [180]

He turned on her, red-eyed, seventy years of weather in his face. [192] "Don't start with your whispering, girl." [198]

It was an old argument between them. [205] Wren had grown up in a house where everything was shouted, and she had learned early that the shouted things never reached anyone; they struck the walls and fell. [234] It was her mother's whispers, at the bedroom door, that she still carried. [247] She had come to Saltmarrow partly because of the story every keeper here told: that the cobalt glass did not carry sound but vibrations of intent, that it hummed with what a person meant rather than what they said. [286] Tobias called it sailor's nonsense, and he had spent a year trying to cure her of it. [303]

Now she put her hand flat on the glass while he roared, and felt it. [318] The pane shuddered under her palm, harsh and jagged, the tremor of a man who was furious at the sea and at himself and at the boy for going. [347] That was what went out into the fog. [355] No wonder nothing came back. [360]

From her coat pocket she took the vial cork. [369] The vial itself had broken years ago, but the cork had drunk so much of her grandmother's tincture that it still smelled of it: the night garden behind the cottage at Fennick, moonflower and white stock and jasmine opening in the dark, damp soil under them. [415] Her grandmother had said the scent purifies intentions, that no one could stand in a garden at night and mean anything cruel. [437]

Wren held it under Tobias's nose. [443] He swatted at her, then stopped. [449] His hand stayed in the air. [455]

"Maren kept stock under the window," he said, and his voice broke on his dead wife's name. [472] "Ansel used to sleep under it." [478]

"Then tell him that, not the fog," Wren said. [487]

The old man lowered the trumpet. [493] He leaned his forehead against the cobalt glass, and with the cork held between his cracked fingers he whispered, so softly she barely heard it, that he was not angry, only frightened, that the kettle was on, that he loved him. [534]

The glass began to sing. [539] It was not a sound so much as a pressure, a low sweet ringing that went through Wren's teeth and out through the lantern room and into the fog, and the fog seemed to lean toward it. [576] She opened the gallery door wide and let the land wind, cool at last, carry the scent of the cork out over the water, and she began to beckon with both arms, though there was no one to see. [615] The smell swelled as the glass rang, until the whole gallery was a garden at night, until the strait itself seemed to bloom. [638] Tobias kept whispering, tears running into his beard, the tenderest things said with the whole force of his body, as if he were screaming them. [663]

Twenty minutes later they heard oars. [669] The dory came out of the white a hundred yards south of where Tobias had been aiming his shouts, Ansel pulling hard and steady, his face lifted like a dog's into the wind. [702] When he reached the landing he called up, breathless, that he had lost the shore light an hour ago and heard nothing at all, but he had smelled his mother's window, and followed it. [736]

Tobias looked at Wren for a long moment, the cork still pinched in his fist. [751] "You win, girl," he whispered, and it was the loudest thing she had ever heard him say. [768] The blue tower went on humming faintly until the sun burned the fog away. [782]
