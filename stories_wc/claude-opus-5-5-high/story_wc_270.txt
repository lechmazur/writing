The kite had been caught in the steam above the third spring for eleven days before Maren climbed the scree to fetch it. [23] No one in Hollow Vent owned it. [30] Children flew kites again now that the ash had settled and the reconstruction crews had cleared the terraces, but this one was old silk, the red of Keeper banners, and its tail was stitched with the looping script she had spent nine years of the insurgency learning to read. [79]

She sat on a warm basalt ledge and untangled it while sulfur moss breathed against her ankles. [96] The moss was saying something, as it always did since the eruption. [108] She had never asked to hear plants. [115] It had started the night the Keepers' archive burned, when she lay half-dead in a steam channel with her cheek pressed to the roots of a fern, and woke understanding that the fern was thirsty and did not like her. [155] Since then the green world leaked into her the way groundwater leaks into a cellar, without permission. [172] Today the moss kept repeating one idea: warm, warm, open. [182]

The script on the tail was a Keeper cipher, the kind they used for instructions they did not want farmers to read. [204] Maren worked it the old way, counting loops against knots, reversing every third glyph, and by the time the sun dropped behind the caldera rim she had it. [232]

THE RESIN REMEMBERS BEING SAP. [237] MOTHER SPRING, THREE BREATHS, THEN BARE HANDS. [244]

She read it twice and understood who had flown the kite. [255] Somewhere a surviving Keeper, perhaps one of the young ones who had surrendered at the terraces, had sent the secret out on the wind rather than let it die. [284] It was the one thing the insurgents had never learned: how to unseal an amber without killing what was inside. [304]

In Hollow Vent, every child had one. [311] At birth the Keepers pressed a seed embryo into warm resin along with a slip of bark naming the child's work, potter or miner or wife or soldier, and set the hardened drop in the family niche. [348] People grew into their ambers. [353] Refusing one's destiny was refusing one's household, and few could bear it. [365] Maren's own amber said WEAVER, and she had woven, for thirty years, before she wove fuses instead. [382]

The Keepers were gone now, but the niches were not. [392] In the rebuilt meeting hall people argued nightly about whether to smash every amber or keep making them in secret, and Maren thought both sides were wrong. [419] Smashing them would tell the children that where they came from was shameful. [432] Keeping them would mean she had fought for nothing. [441] Her granddaughter Tessaly was eight months pregnant and had already asked, quietly, who would make the amber. [458]

Maren went home, took her own amber from the niche, and carried it back down to the mother spring, the oldest vent, where the water was too hot to touch for longer than a breath. [493] Tessaly followed with a lamp, because Tessaly always followed. [502]

Maren lowered the amber in a copper ladle and counted three breaths. [514] Then she lifted it out and pressed it between her bare palms. [526] It burned, and she held on, and in the heat she heard it: a voice smaller than the moss, older, patient, a seed that had been waiting sixty-one years to be asked what it wanted. [561]

The resin softened like honey. [566] She peeled it back with her thumbnails. [573] The bark slip fell into the spring and curled into nothing. [584] The embryo lay on her palm, pale and whole, and it wanted soil. [597]

"It's alive," Tessaly said. [601]

"They all are," Maren said. [606] "That was the part they never told us." [614]

She planted it that night in a terrace bed the crews had just rebuilt, and the next week she stood in the meeting hall with the kite tail draped over her arm and proposed something neither side had considered. [653] Keep the ambers. [656] Keep the naming day, the resin, the niche. [664] But in a child's twelfth year, the family would carry the drop to the mother spring, unseal it together, let the bark dissolve, and plant the seed wherever the child chose. [695] The destiny would be released, not erased. [702]

Some argued; the vote passed by nine. [709]

Tessaly's daughter was born in the thaw, and Maren pressed the seed into the resin herself, and on the bark slip she wrote nothing at all. [735]

On the terrace, her own seedling had put out two leaves, and when she passed it in the mornings it told her, without being asked, that it was not a weaver. [766]
