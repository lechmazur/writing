Odile Varn lit the dock lanterns of Brinmouth for eleven years before the whale beached, and in all that time the flames had stood straight as candles in a church. [30] Now they leaned. [33] Three weeks after the whale came ashore, gray and enormous and silent on the shingle, every lantern on the east quay bent its fire inland, as if listening for something. [63]

The fishermen blamed the carcass. [68] They said it had drawn the tide wrong, that its rot had soured the air. [83] But Odile had felt the other thing: stepping off the pier at dusk and finding her body briefly twice as heavy, her ladder sinking into planks that should have held it; walking ten paces on and weighing nothing, her coat lifting like a sail. [127] Pockets of gravity, the harbor master called them, when he bothered to call them anything. [142] They fluctuated mysteriously, drifting from street to street, and the town learned to walk carefully. [157]

Odile learned to read them. [162] Every morning she drank her coffee black, as her grandmother had, and turned the cup upside down on its saucer. [182] Her grandmother had read futures in the grounds. [190] Odile read direction. [193] Since the whale, the grounds no longer spread in lazy fans; they crawled overnight toward one rim of the cup, all of them, the way iron filings crawl toward a magnet. [224] Each morning she marked the rim against the compass. [233] Each morning it pointed at the Salt House on the hill. [244]

She knew what the Salt House did, the way everyone in Brinmouth knew and did not say. [261] For a price, the clerks there would draw a memory out of you and dissolve it in brine. [279] Widows went. [281] Sailors went, after bad winters. [286] Nobody asked where the memories went afterward, because the point was not to have to think about them. [304]

Odile had gone once. [308] She knew because of the book. [314] On her shelf was a field guide she did not remember buying, and inside it, pressed flat between the pages on thistle and campion, were wildflowers: a blue scabious, a stem of sea pink, a sprig of yarrow gone the color of weak tea. [358] Beneath them, in her own cramped hand: To M, Kettle Hill, the day we didn't go home. [375] She could not picture M. [380] She could not picture the hill. [386] There was only a smooth place in her where something had been, like a tooth socket her tongue kept finding. [406]

She wanted to know where it had gone. [414] Not to take it back, necessarily. [420] Just to know that it was somewhere. [427]

On the twenty-second morning after the whale, the grounds pointed so hard they had climbed the side of the cup. [447] That night Odile finished her rounds, kept one lantern lit, and walked up the hill. [462]

The Salt House door was not locked; nobody stole from it. [473] Below the clerks' offices a stair descended into the salt crystal growing chamber, a long vault where brine lay in shallow stone beds and salt built itself up in cubes and spires. [505] Her lantern turned the room into a field of white fire. [516] No drip, no draft, no footstep but her own. [525] The silence gleamed. [528]

The pull found her halfway down the chamber. [536] Her knees buckled; the lantern flame lay nearly flat, pointing at the farthest bed. [550] Here the crystals had grown wrong, dense and clouded, heaped far above the brine line like a drift of dirty snow. [571] The clerks had kept dissolving memories long after the beds were full, she understood. [585] They had pumped the overflow into the bay. [593] And something in the bay had swallowed it, mouthful after mouthful of grief, until it was too heavy to swim. [613] Now the whale lay dead on the shingle and its cargo was seeping loose, wandering the town in pockets, straining back toward the rest of itself. [639]

Odile knelt and began to exhume the bed with her bare hands. [651] Beneath the crust, each crystal held a faint shape, like a moth caught in ice: a kitchen, a coffin, a boat going over. [674] She dug until she felt a small crystal warm against her palm and lifted it to the lantern. [692]

Inside it a woman laughed on a hillside of blue scabious. [703] Mara. [704] Her sister, who had drowned the next spring, two hundred yards from the lamp Odile had failed to light in time. [725]

The weight came into her all at once. [733] She sat down hard on the salt and let it. [743]

When she walked back down the hill at dawn, she walked slowly, heavily, the crystal in her pocket beside the book of flowers. [766] Below her, the last lanterns on the quay burned a little straighter. [778] Some things were meant to be carried. [785]
