The barges on the Tallow Cut lit their lamps at twilight, one by one, like a sentence being spoken slowly. [20] Wren Hollis tied her borrowed skiff to the rusted ring above the spot where the Constance had sunk forty years before. [41]

She had been called errant by three employers, one fiance, and a harbor magistrate, and she had earned the word honestly. [62] For eleven years she had chased legends up and down the waterways: the singing lock at Marrow, the ghost tug of Fenwater, the bride who walked the towpath with a lantern made of ice. [96] Every one had been false. [101] Every one had been invented by her father. [109]

Aldous Hollis had sold stories to tourists from the stern of a painted barge, and when he died he left her a ledger instead of money. [135] Inside, drawn like blueprints, were the legends he had built: a foundation of local grief, walls of repetition, and always, at the center, a keystone of one small true thing that held the whole structure up. [171] Wren had not come looking for belief. [178] She wanted to understand the architecture of lies, because she had lived her whole childhood inside one and never once seen the seams. [201]

Only one legend was missing from the ledger. [209] The Drowned Gnomon: an antique pocket sundial lost with the Constance, said to cast a shadow toward anyone who lied in its presence. [232] He had told it to her every night of her ninth summer, and he had never once smiled while telling it. [253]

She had until the silence of morning radio. [261] Each dawn the canal station at Upper Tallow read the water levels in a flat, patient voice, and when the broadcast ended and the static went quiet, the sluice gates opened and the whole cut ran fast and brown for an hour. [303] Anything left on the bottom after that would be carried to the weir and ground to nothing. [320]

She dove at full dark. [325] The water was black and cold, and her torch showed only a brown fog of silt, so she switched it off and let her hands do the seeing. [353] This, too, her father had given her. [360] Summer after summer he had tossed pennies off the stern and made her find them blind, eyes shut, counting breaths, until she could tell brass from bottle cap by the shape of the cold. [394] He had called it a game. [400] It had been a lesson in salvage by training instincts instead of trusting eyes, and now she worked the hull of the Constance rib by rib, surfacing, breathing, descending again. [430]

Near four in the morning, on her ninth descent, her fingers closed on something round and hinged, wedged beneath a fallen lamp bracket in the captain's cabin. [457] It was wrapped in oilcloth, sealed with wax, too carefully kept to be forty years drowned. [473]

She hauled herself into the skiff, shivering, and pried the lid open by lamplight. [487] A brass gnomon, a compass needle for aligning it north, and inside the lid, scratched in her father's thoughtfully clear hand: For Wren, when she finally comes looking. [515] Below that, smaller: A sundial never lies, but it only tells you where the sun has already been. [533]

The upheaval came gently, the way the water rose around the barges each dawn, lifting everything without a sound. [552] He had not lost the sundial forty years ago. [561] He had placed it here himself, the summer she turned nine, and built a legend around it the way he built all the others: grief, repetition, and one true keystone. [591] The pennies had been the foundation. [597] The unsmiling voice had been the walls. [604] And the keystone was this: he had known he was a liar, and he had wanted his daughter to be the one person who could find the truth underneath him, blind, in the dark, long after it could matter to him. [645] Lies were built forward, she understood now, into a future that could not check them; truth arrived only in hindsight, clear and late as a shadow. [671]

On the barge beside her, a radio crackled awake, and the station at Upper Tallow began reading the levels in its flat, patient voice. [695] Wren rowed to the bank, climbed the towpath, and set the sundial on a bollard, turning it until the needle settled north. [717]

The broadcast ended. [720] In the silence, the sluices groaned open upstream, and the canal began to run, and the first sun touched the gnomon. [741] A thin shadow fell across the brass, marking an hour already passed, and Wren, errant no longer, read it and found it perfectly true. [765]
