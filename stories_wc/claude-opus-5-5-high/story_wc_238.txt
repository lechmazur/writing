The orchard had gone quiet the way a room goes quiet before a verdict. [14] Yellow ribbons circled every trunk, and the saws waited on a flatbed by the road, their teeth sheathed in canvas until dawn. [36] Mara Quill let herself into the pressing barn with a key she should have returned thirty years ago. [54] The Varn family had turned it into a sheet music archive after the cider business failed, and the long shelves still smelled faintly of fermented apples and foxed paper. [83] Hymnals, dance-hall reels, a century of parlor songs, all boxed and labeled for a university that would take them away on Tuesday. [105] Everything here was leaving. [109]

Mara knew about leaving. [113] For thirty years she had written The Restless Column for the county paper, and people read it the way they picked at scabs. [136] Every ghost carries one secret like a stone in its shoe, she liked to write, and it cannot walk on until someone takes the stone out and shows it to the neighbors. [168] So she found the secrets, printed them between the church bake sales and the obituaries, and the haunted houses of the county went still, one by one. [195] A gossip-column exorcist, the minister called her, not kindly. [204]

But her first secret had not belonged to a ghost. [214] At nineteen, hungry for a byline, she had printed that Elsie Varn, the orchard's pianist and her dearest friend, had been seen kissing the bandleader behind the cider press. [243] The engagement broke, the town turned its back, and Elsie died of a winter fever the following year without ever answering Mara's letters. [266] The letters had been excuses dressed as apologies, and both of them had known it. [281] Now the barn would come down with the trees, and whatever was left of Elsie would have nowhere to stand. [301]

Mara set her satchel on the reading table and unpacked the tools of her trade. [316] A spool of copper wire. [321] A hand-cranked magneto salvaged from an old party-line telephone. [330] And the antique perfume bottle, cut crystal gone cloudy, with a silver collar and a stopper Elsie had once pressed against Mara's wrist, laughing, saying, Now you smell like someone worth gossiping about. [363] Mara had stolen it from the funeral parlor, which was the kind of thing nobody printed. [379]

Ghosts were weather, not wishes; they moved like charge in a cloud, and the living could only coax them with a moving field. [402] She wound the copper around the bottle's collar in forty tight turns, clipped the leads to the magneto, and began to crank. [424] Inside the housing, magnets spun past their coils, and the induced current ran down the wire and into the glass, looking for something to wake. [449] The residue in the bottom of the bottle began to glow the color of weak tea. [465] Then the barn filled with pear blossom and lamp oil, and every loose page on the shelves lifted an inch, as though the paper had taken a breath. [493]

Elsie did not appear. [497] Mara had not expected her to; her miracles always came out crooked, a voice without a face, a face without a name. [519] Instead, the upright piano in the corner, shrouded in a moving blanket, played a single cracked chord. [536]

Mara kept cranking, her shoulder burning. [542] "I told the town a lie that happened to be true," she said, "and I told it because I wanted them to look at me instead of you." [570] "Jealousy is a knife that thinks it is a lamp." [580] "I am sorry, Elsie." [584] "Not for the words." [588] "For the reason." [591]

The piano answered with a melody Mara had not heard in thirty years, a waltz Elsie had been writing the summer of the scandal, one she had refused to finish. [621] It stopped where it had always stopped, on an unresolved note that hung in the rafters like frost. [639] A folder slid from the nearest shelf and fanned open at Mara's feet. [652] Inside was the waltz in Elsie's slanting hand, and the last bar was not blank after all. [669] It held one chord, penciled lightly, and beneath it the title she had never shown anyone: For M., Who Cannot Keep Quiet. [691] The pencil had smudged; half the chord was gone, and Mara would never know whether it resolved to forgiveness or only to rest. [714]

She stopped cranking. [717] The glow drained from the bottle, and the scent thinned to cold barn and apples. [732] Some bridges get built from one bank only, she thought, and you walk them anyway. [747]

At dawn, when the saws began, she sat at the piano and played the waltz through, guessing at the missing notes, and the next morning The Restless Column printed only one secret, and it was hers. [783]
