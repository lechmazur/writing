After the carousel halted, the lagoon kept moving without it. [10] Its water circled the island in tight, restless bands, spinning leaves and rope ends and the occasional drowned gull, while beneath it the clouds lay packed like wet wool, and nothing, anywhere, made a sound. [45]

Maro waded the outer band at dawn, or what she had decided to call dawn. [60] The light no longer agreed with itself. [67] Since the painted horses froze mid-leap, noon sometimes came before midnight, and yesterday occasionally arrived twice. [83] She had stopped trusting her own hands, which were spotted and old on some mornings and smooth as a girl's on others. [105]

The islanders said the carousel had been winding the hours for three hundred years, keeping them in their proper order, and that when its engine seized, time had simply spilled. [135] Most of them had fled down the rope ladders into the clouds. [147] Maro stayed, because scavengers stay where things are left behind. [157]

What the lagoon left behind now were voices. [165] They surfaced in bubbles along the current, each one bursting with a fragment of some conversation spoken in a year that had come loose: a woman laughing about a burned cake, a boy swearing he would reach the ground someday, an old man counting painted horses aloud. [212] Maro caught them in a jar with a cork lid and wrote each one on a strip of sailcloth, pinning the strips to the wall of her shack in the order she guessed they belonged. [247]

She was looking for one voice in particular. [255]

Her ghost had appeared the week the carousel stopped, sitting sidesaddle on the white horse at the center of the platform, and it had not moved since. [282] It wore her face at nineteen. [288] It held, in its translucent lap, a cardboard disposable camera, the same one Maro had bought from a trader the summer she meant to climb down through the clouds and photograph the whole unseen world below. [324] She had taken twenty-three pictures of the lagoon to practice. [334] Then her mother fell ill, then the ladders needed mending, then there was always another reason, and the last frame waited, unwound, for forty years or four days, depending on the light. [366]

The ghost's thumb rested on the little ridged wheel, but it would not turn it. [381] And while it sat there, the carousel would not turn either. [392]

Maro had tried everything else: oil, crowbars, prayers. [400] Ghosts, the old islanders used to say, could only be bargained with in their own words. [416] So she gathered fragments, and slowly, among the cake and the quarrels, she found the girl's voice. [433]

Hold still, I want to remember this. [440]

I'll send you the pictures from the bottom. [448]

When I come back, I'll be someone. [455]

And the last one, which she found on an evening that was also, somehow, a morning: Did she ever go? [475]

Maro rowed to the platform at the hour she chose to believe was midnight. [489] The horses hung on the stilled brass poles, hooves raised, manes carved into wind that had stopped blowing. [507] Around them the lagoon kept circling, frantic and soundless, like something trying to remember a song. [523]

She sat on the black horse beside her ghost and read the strips aloud, one by one, handing the girl back her own sentences. [547] The ghost listened without blinking. [552] When Maro reached the last fragment, she lowered the cloth. [562]

"No," she said, "I never went." [568] "I told myself the ground would wait, and then I told myself it was foolish, and then I stopped telling myself anything." [590]

She had expected the words to feel like surrender. [599] Instead they felt like setting down a bucket she had carried so long she had mistaken it for her arm. [619]

"I regret it," she whispered. [624]

That was the bargain, then: not a trade, but a confession. [635]

The ghost looked at her, and for the first time its face aged, a line appearing at the mouth, another at the eye, until for one fractured moment they wore the same years. [668] Then it held out the camera. [674]

Maro took it. [677] The wheel was stiff with salt, and it rasped under her thumb, click by click, advancing the final frame into place. [698]

Somewhere beneath the platform, a gear answered. [705] The white horse shivered on its pole, and the ghost was gone, and the carousel, groaning, began to turn. [724]

Maro did not photograph it. [729] She put the camera in her coat, climbed into her boat, and rowed toward the top of the longest ladder, where the clouds opened, grey and endless, onto a world she was finally old enough, or young enough, to see. [769] The last frame was for the bottom. [776]
