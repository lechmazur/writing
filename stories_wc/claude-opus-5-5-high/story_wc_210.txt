The last wish in Lantern Cove was granted on a Tuesday, and by Friday the alchemist was the only person left. [21] For three hundred years the tide-spirit had answered one wish per lantern, and the cove had hung its paper lanterns from every eave and mast until the harbor glowed like a lung breathing light. [55] Bread, rain, a cured fever, a son returned from sea: the town had wished itself into being and never learned to do anything else. [79] Then the council, tired of rationing, wished for everything they would ever need. [92] The spirit gave them an amber bottle the size of a fist, sealed seamless as an egg, and did not come back. [114]

Oriel Vask had examined it for the council. [122] It had no cork, no seam, no weak point under the hammer or the acid. [137] When the mayor asked whether it might open in time, whether everything would be fine, she said she did not know, and that she would not pretend to. [165] The mayor preferred the fortune-teller's answer. [171] The fortune-teller said the bottle would open somewhere richer, and the town packed its carts and followed her inland, leaving the lanterns dark and Oriel with the bottle nobody wanted to carry. [203]

She spent the first month trying to remember the town into the glass. [216] It was the obvious theory: the bottle held everything they would ever need, so perhaps it wanted to be told what that was. [239] She sat on the cold quay with it in her lap and recalled the bakery, the rope-walk, the chapel bell, the smell of tar and anise. [265] The amber stayed dull as old honey. [272] Memory, she wrote in her notebook, is not enough; it is a record, and the spirit never granted records. [291]

The discovery came by accident, while she was calculating the load of a new roof beam in her head, holding the span, the weight of slate, the angle of rafters, and the give of damp pine all at once. [330] The bottle warmed against her hip and began, faintly, to glow. [341] When she dropped one number, the light guttered. [349] It did not want remembered things. [355] It wanted what was held in the mind that moment, turning, the way a sum is juggled before it is written down. [377]

The trouble was that working memory is small. [385] No one can hold a civilization in their head at once; she could manage four things, perhaps five, before the first began to slip. [409] So she stopped trying to rebuild Lantern Cove and began to conceive a different one, a town small enough in each of its parts to be thought whole. [437]

A well that needed no wish: depth, stone, bucket, rope. [447] She held the four until the bottle burned gold, then wrote them on a paper lantern and hung it over the dry well-head. [470] By morning the lantern was lit from inside, though she had put no candle there, and the well smelled of cold water. [492] An oven: clay, draft, heat, the hands that knead. [501] A school: a question, a slate, a patient voice, a door that opens outward. [515] Each lantern was a thought she had carried whole, and each one lit. [528]

In the second spring her old apprentice, Pell, walked back down the coast road, thin and sunburned, and stood blinking at the glowing eaves. [552] "The others will come back now," he said. [560] "It will be just like it was." [567]

"No," Oriel said. [570] "It won't, and I don't want it to be." [579] She handed him the bottle, which dimmed at once in his grip. [591] "The old town was built on asking." [598] "This one is built on holding." [604] "You can't remember your way into it; you have to think it, piece by piece, every day." [621]

Pell frowned, closed his eyes, and began to murmur. [630] "A boat," he said slowly. [635] "Keel, sail, tiller, a reason to come home." [643] The amber flared warm between them. [649]

Oriel understood then what the spirit had actually granted. [658] Everything they would ever need was not sealed inside the bottle; the bottle only showed them they could hold it themselves, a few pieces at a time. [685]

By autumn there were forty lit lanterns in the cove and eleven people, some of them strangers who had followed the light from the sea. [710] Each newcomer was taught the same first lesson on the quay: hold four things, no more, and do not let anyone tell you it is easier than that. [738] Oriel kept the bottle on her workbench, still sealed, and every night before sleep she held one more piece of the civilization she meant to build, until it glowed. [767]
