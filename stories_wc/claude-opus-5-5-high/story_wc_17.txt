After the climate shifted, the northern estates stopped being houses and became weather. [13] Rain arrived warm now, eleven months a year, and ivy took every wall that no one could afford to scrape. [33] Maren Oake walked into Halvard House carrying a tuning fork, a bundle of graphite, and her grandmother's unfinished atlas. [52] She was a corridor echo cartographer, the last one licensed by the regional archive, and her job was to record how sound moved through buildings before the damp pulled them down. [83] Her grandmother Ilse had invented the trade. [90] Ilse had also claimed, in lectures that ended her career, that corridors kept the voices of the dead the way riverbeds keep stones, and that walls, left alone long enough, dreamed. [121] Maren had inherited the atlas, the debts, and the ridicule. [131] She had not decided whether she had inherited the belief. [141] She had come to Halvard to find out, once and for all, what walls dream about. [157] It was the atlas's final blank page. [164] Ilse had died here, in the study, eight years ago, and Maren had never come. [179]

The study smelled of wet ash. [185] Someone had stuffed the fireplace with notebooks and lit them, but the chimney had flooded before the fire could finish. [205] Maren knelt and lifted them out one by one, their edges black lace, their centers legible. [221] They were Ilse's diaries. [225] She knew the handwriting the way she knew the pitch of her own footsteps. [239] Maren had always been sensitively perceptive, the kind of child who could tell which floorboard her mother stood on from two rooms away, and Ilse had trained that gift until it hurt. [271] Now the same attention caught what the fire had missed. [281]

The surviving entries were all about the east passage, the covered walk that ran from the house to the old chapel, now so thick with ivy that it glowed green at noon. [313] The passage hums back in my voice, one entry read. [323] Another said, It is not repeating me; it is rehearsing me. [334] The last legible page said only, Walls dream of whoever listens to them longest. [348] Beneath it, half consumed: Do not let Maren finish this one, or it will begin dreaming her. [365]

Tucked into the spine was a film negative strip, four frames, curled from heat. [379] Maren held it to the window. [385] In reversed light she caught only a glimpse before the rain darkened the glass: the passage without ivy, a woman with her ear against the stone, and in the last frame a small girl beside her, listening the same way. [425] Maren did not remember ever being here. [432] She remembered only that her childhood summers had gaps, afternoons Ilse called naps. [445]

She went to the passage anyway, because not going would have been another kind of inheritance. [461] The ivy-veiled passage was cool and close, leaves sealing the arches so the rain sounded far away. [478] She struck the fork against her knee and let the note travel. [490] It came back wrong. [494] It came back lower, slower, and underneath it, like breath under a sheet, a woman humming the lullaby Ilse used to hum while she drew. [519] Then, fainter, a child's thin voice joining in. [527]

Maren understood that her grandmother had not burned the diaries out of shame. [540] Ilse had burned them to hide a door. [548] She had spent her last years listening here, letting the stone learn her so thoroughly that it could hum without her, and she had realized too late that she had brought a child who listened just as well. [586] The legacy was not the atlas. [592] The legacy was the listening, and the walls wanted the rest of it. [605]

Maren stood a long while with her palm an inch from the stone. [618] She wanted, badly, to lean in and hear what else it dreamed, whether it had kept her grandmother's last words, whether it would say her name. [644] That wanting was the whole of her motive for coming, and she saw now that it was also the trap. [664]

She drew the passage instead, from the outside, in plain graphite: its length, its arches, its pitch. [681] Where the echo should have gone, she left the page white and wrote one line: Occupied. [697] Then she slid the film negative strip between the stones, where the ivy roots would close over it, so the passage could keep the only picture of them together. [726]

Outside, the warm rain was loud and ordinary. [734] Maren walked through it without once turning to listen for her own footsteps. [747]
