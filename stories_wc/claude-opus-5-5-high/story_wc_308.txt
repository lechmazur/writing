Maren Tell had skimmed stones in the Canyon of Returning for fifty-one years, and now her knees would no longer carry her down to the rim. [26] The towers of bone rose around her perch like the ribs of something that had lain down to die before language, each one hollowed by wind and ringing faintly whenever the air shifted. [59] A harmonic skimmer's work was simple to describe and impossible to teach: you threw a flat stone so it kissed tower after tower, and from the chord it left behind you learned which passages were open and which had collapsed. [99] Travelers paid in salt and lamp oil for those chords. [109] Without them, the echoes swallowed people whole, bouncing a shout for help so many times that rescuers ran toward walls. [129] But a skimmer had to walk the rim to read the returns, and Maren could not walk. [146] So she sat in the dust archive beneath the tallest tower and tried to learn to navigate without moving. [165]

The archive was only a trench, cut by her teacher's teacher, where the canyon's dust lay layered in bands through the centuries: gray ash from the burning year, red grit from the flood, a pale seam from when the swifts first came. [207] Maren honored each band the way other people honored graves; she brushed them with a feather and never let a child step on the edge. [232] The skimmers before her were in that dust, and she believed that what they had heard was still sounding somewhere. [252]

In the lowest band, older than the swifts, she found the stone arrowhead. [265] It was knapped from black glass-stone, no longer than her thumb, and when she lifted it the tower above her answered with a single low beat. [291] Then another, farther off. [295] Then another, fainter, fading west. [300]

Every skimmer knew the old story of the Wandering Pulse: that the first skimmer had not thrown a stone at all but shot an arrow into the bone, and the note it struck had never stopped traveling. [337] A pulse left wandering, the song went, keeps the canyon's shape the way a heartbeat keeps a body's. [355] Maren had always thought it a pretty lie. [363] Now she held the arrowhead to her ear and felt it hum in time with something moving through the towers. [383]

She could not follow the pulse, but the swifts could. [393] At dusk they poured from their roosts in a single black ribbon, and for the first time she watched them as a skimmer watches a stone. [419] The flock flinched where the pulse passed, a ripple running through a thousand wings, and they banked only through passages that were open, because the beat struck back hard off every fallen wall. [452] The birds had been reading the canyon for as long as the pale seam had existed. [468]

Night after night she appraised their patterns, turning the arrowhead in her fingers, matching each ripple to a chord she remembered from her walking years. [493] She charted which turns were true and which were the birds chasing gnats, and slowly the whole canyon assembled behind her eyes. [515] She learned the pulse was failing. [521] Each circuit it came back softer, the swifts' flinch smaller, as if the first skimmer's arrow had finally begun to tire. [542]

In the dry month a salt trader's boy went missing in the western forks. [556] His mother came up the switchback and knelt at the trench's edge, careful not to touch it, and asked if the old woman could still hear. [582] Maren watched the evening flock split around the third fork, reform, and dip twice where a new rockfall had closed the lower way. [605] "He is past the Weeping Rib," she said. [613] "Tell your runners to take the high ledge, not the floor." [624]

But the flock was already thinning its turns; the pulse was nearly gone, and without it Maren would be blind by morning. [646] She fitted the stone arrowhead into her skimming sling, where no arrowhead belonged, and threw it with everything her shoulders had left. [668] It struck the tallest tower at the exact beat of the passing pulse. [681] The note doubled, deepened, and went rolling west, strong as the day it was first loosed, and the swifts rose in a great shuddering arc that pointed the runners down the high ledge like a finger. [717]

They brought the boy back before moonrise. [724]

The arrowhead was lost somewhere in the forks, and Maren did not send anyone to look. [740] Let it settle into a new band of dust, she thought; some future skimmer would find it there and wonder. [760] Each dusk after that she sat at her perch, knees wrapped in wool, reading the birds, her own throw folded inside the wandering pulse. [784] She did not need to move. [790]
