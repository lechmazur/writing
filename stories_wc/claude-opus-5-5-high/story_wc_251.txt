The Merrow Public Library was painted cobalt in 1911, and on autumn evenings the harbor mist wrapped it so thoroughly that it looked like a piece of sky that had sunk and decided to stay. [35] Wanda Pell had coached the after-school chess club in its basement for nineteen years, through three budget cuts, two floods, and one boy who ate a rook. [62] Now the council meant to sell the building to a hotel developer, and the vote was set for Thursday dawn. [82]

Wanda had never shouted at anyone in her life. [91] She had a different gift, one she did not advertise: she heard the words people swallowed. [107] Not their thoughts, exactly, but the sentences that rose to the throat and were turned back, like a player's hand hovering over a piece before retreating. [133] At the council's public hearing she had heard a dozen of them. [145] I learned to read in that building. [152] My father proposed to my mother in the map room. [162] And, loudest of all, from Mayor Harlan Voss, who had voted yes on every sale for a decade: I have not let myself think twice. [187]

That was the problem, Wanda decided. [193] Nobody in Merrow thought twice anymore. [199] She could not stop a vote, but perhaps she could plant a forest of second thoughts, one seed in every window, and let the mist do the watering. [227]

Her seed came from the rotunda. [233] Under the library's glass dome sat the Grotto, a hollowed boulder of lodestone the founder had hauled up from the sea caves. [255] Its inner wall was ruled into sixty-four squares, and on it clung iron chessmen, held by magnetism in the position of a game Augusta Merrow had adjourned the night before she died. [287] A brass plate beneath it read: THE TOWN WILL FINISH IT. [298] For a century, no one had dared, and the prophecy had become a joke, then a decoration, then nothing at all. [319] On clear nights, starlight fell through the dome and silvered the pieces until the whole grotto seemed to be thinking. [339]

Wanda's students copied the position onto fourteen boards. [347] Then she walked the town with a clipboard and a thermos, asking shopkeepers for a single favor. [364] The baker said yes before she finished. [371] The hardware man said no aloud, and yes underneath, and she waited politely until the underneath won. [388] By Wednesday evening, fourteen windows along Harbor Street held a board, a small lamp on a timer, and a card lettered by ten-year-olds: WHITE TO MOVE. THINK TWICE. [416]

At nine o'clock, every lamp clicked on at once. [425] There were no chants, no signs, no speeches. [433] It was a humble rebellion, fourteen small squares of light in the fog, and the silence around them grew so large that people began to lower their voices as they passed, the way one does in a library. [471]

Wanda stood at the corner and listened. [478] Is that the old game? [483] Grandpa said it could not be won. [490] What would I play? [494] The unspoken words drifted up the street like smoke from many small fires. [507]

Near midnight, in the hush where the town's past leaned against its prophecy, she let herself into the library with her old key. [530] Someone was already in the rotunda. [536] Mayor Voss stood before the grotto in his overcoat, starlight on his bald head, hands clasped behind him as if afraid of what they might do. [563]

"I came to see what the fuss was," he said. [573]

Wanda heard the rest: I played here as a boy. [583] I was good. [586] I stopped. [588]

She said nothing, which was what he needed. [596] Instead she reached into the grotto and lifted the white knight free, feeling the lodestone tug at it like a reluctant tide, and she cradled it in her palm the way she cradled every frightened beginner's first piece, warm and without judgment. [638] Then she held it out to him. [645]

The mayor looked at the knight for a long time. [655] The mist pressed its face against the dome. [663] Somewhere down Harbor Street, the timers clicked, and fourteen lamps went dark together, and the quiet became enormous. [681]

He took the knight. [685] He did not place it yet. [691] He only turned it over and over, the way a man turns a question he has decided, at last, to ask. [712]

At dawn, the council met. [717] The mayor moved to table the sale for one year, pending study. [729] Wanda, in the back row, heard nothing swallowed at all. [739] That afternoon, the chess club gained its oldest member, and around the grotto fifteen people argued, very quietly, about what White should play. [762]
