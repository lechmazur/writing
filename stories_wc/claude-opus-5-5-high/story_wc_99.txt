Oren Vale spoke the way his junipers grew, one careful ring at a time. [14] On the islet of Carrow, where the rain had just finished and the wind came combing through the gorse, his students learned to wait for his sentences the way fishermen wait for slack water. [48] He had only one student now. [54]

Tove had come on the first ferry after the severance, the autumn storm that tore the causeway loose and left Carrow an island in earnest. [79] She carried a bundle of tissue paper and a letter in handwriting Oren had not seen in eleven years. [98] Maren is gone, the letter said, and she wanted you to have this. [111] Inside the tissue lay a square of antique lace, yellowed at the picots, worked in the Bruges pattern Maren had learned from her grandmother. [135] Tove was that grandmother's great-granddaughter, and Maren's last apprentice. [144] She had asked to learn bonsai because, she said, Maren told her the two crafts were cousins: both were patience made visible. [166]

Oren did not tell her that he and Maren had once been married, or that she had left on the last boat before an earlier storm, the night he said nothing when he should have said everything. [203] He only said, after a long while, "Wire first. [212] Then waiting." [214]

For three weeks they worked on the bench beneath the tamarisk, his voice rhythmically placid as the swell against the rocks, until one evening Tove held the lace to the lamp and frowned. [247] "These aren't flowers," she said. [252] "They're pricking marks. [255] Maren's notation. [257] She taught me to hide lists in the ground." [266]

She read it slowly, finger tracing the twisted pairs, and Oren listened the way he listened to rain in the eaves. [287] "Lowest tide after rain. [291] Walk to the Hesper. [295] Take what she kept. [299] Follow it home. [302] Ask him the color of the apology neither of us said." [313]

The Hesper was a ghost ship only in the way islanders spoke of it. [327] She had broken on the northern bar a century ago, and on certain fogbound nights her lantern was said to move along the reef as if she were still trying to finish the voyage. [361] Maren had loved that story. [366] Oren had called it nonsense, gently, which was the only way he said anything. [380]

They went at dawn, when the ebb had pulled the sea back farther than he had seen in years, and the wet sand shone like pewter under a scoured blue sky. [411] The islanders called such mornings the tides of remembrance, because the drawn-back water exposed whatever the sea had been keeping: bottles, bones, a child's boot, the black ribs of the Hesper. [442] Wedged in the hollow of her binnacle, wrapped in oilcloth that could not have been there a century, sat a brass compass. [464] Oren knew the knot on the oilcloth. [471] He had tied it himself, around Maren's wrist, the day he taught her to wire a branch without scarring it. [491] She had come back, then, at least once, alone at low water, and had not climbed the path to his door. [512]

The needle did not point north. [518] It swung past the reef, past the lighthouse, and settled, trembling, on the islet itself. [533] They followed it up the rain-dark path to his own garden, to the one tree he never let students touch: the shore juniper he and Maren had collected from the cliffs their first spring, which he had left unpruned since she went. [575] It had grown dense and furious, a green fist. [584]

"She wanted to know the color," Tove said quietly. [593]

Oren took up his shears. [598] He worked until the sun was high, opening the crown the way she had always begged him to, thinning each pad so the light could enter rather than strike. [627] "A tree too full," he said, "cannot diffuse the sky." [637] It was the first sentence Tove had heard him finish without a pause. [650]

When he stepped back, the breeze moved through the opened branches, and the light that fell on the wet stones beneath was no longer white or gold but a soft, sifted blue, the blue of rain leaving, of water drawing away and not yet returning. [695] Cerulean, Maren would have called it; she had a name for every thread. [708] Oren knelt in it. [712] The compass needle had gone still, pointing at nothing now, or at everything. [725]

"I was sorry," he said, to the tree, to the tide, to the girl who had carried her lace across the water. [747] Tove folded the lace into his palm. [754] "She knew," she said. [758] "That's why she never let it point anywhere else." [767]

Out past the reef, the tide turned and began, patiently, to come back. [780]
