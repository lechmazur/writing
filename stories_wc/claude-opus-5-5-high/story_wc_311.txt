The observatory had been dying longer than anyone in the valley had been alive, and the reaper liked it for that. [21] At dusk the copper dome went the color of old blood, and the last light slid through the cracked slit in the roof like a blade that had forgotten what it was for. [52] He had come every evening since finding the old recordings. [62] There were eleven wax cylinders in a hatbox beneath the star charts, each labeled in a hand he knew too well: M. Aldous, Catalogue of Unnamed Things. [89] He had collected Marta Aldous forty-one years ago, on a night of clear sky, and he had not been able to stop thinking about her since. [115]

Reapers are not built for grief. [121] They are built to carry, and what they carry are last words, the phrases people say at the edge and then forget they said. [145] The words settle in a reaper like sediment, and sometimes they rise unbidden. [158]

"Tell the baker I paid," he said now, to no one, in the voice of a man drowned in 1893. [178] "Is it morning yet," he said, in the voice of a girl. [190]

He wound the phonograph and let Marta speak instead. [199]

"Cylinder seven," her voice said, thin and crackling, as if coming from the far side of a frozen lake. [218] "The fear of the stair with one more step than you counted," she said. [232] "I call it the Extra Step, and if you meet it in the dark, you are allowed to laugh." [251]

She had made the recordings for the dying of the valley, he understood now. [265] She had sat at their bedsides and asked what frightened them, and when they could not say, she had given the shapeless thing a name, because a named thing has edges, and a thing with edges can be walked around. [305]

Pinned above the eyepiece of the great telescope was her reason, a page torn from an atlas three centuries old. [325] Its coastlines were confident, but its seas were crowded with monsters: a whale with a pig's snout, a serpent coiled around a galleon. [348] Beneath each creature a tiny name was inked in Latin. [358] He remembered her explaining it to him the night he came, while she refused to look away from the sky. [378]

"They drew monsters where they did not know the water," she had said. [391] "Not to frighten sailors, but so the sailors would know what they were frightened of." [406]

She had not been scheduled to die. [413] Her nephew had, a boy of six burning with diphtheria in the house below the hill, and she had climbed the observatory stairs and waited for the reaper and offered him a trade. [446] The ledgers allow it, if the offer is made freely. [456] He had taken her hand, the only hand he had ever held longer than the task required, and her last words had gone into him like a coin into a well. [487] He had never let them rise. [493]

Now he had heard all eleven cylinders, and the valley was still full of people dying without her. [511] He carried their fears too, though he had no names for them: the weight on the chest at three in the morning, the sound a house makes when it is emptier than yesterday. [544] He wanted to give them names, the way she had, and he did not know how a thing like him could make anything. [567]

The sun touched the horizon, and the light through the roof slit struck the cracked objective lens and burst apart. [587] Scattered light fell across the atlas page in splinters of red and violet, and where it touched the blank water between the monsters, it seemed to wait. [614]

"Is it morning yet," he said, without meaning to. [623]

A shard of light trembled, and the phrase crystallized on the page in small bright letters beside a sleeping sea-calf: The Long Night Before Morning. [648] He felt the girl's words leave him, lighter than he expected. [659] Each forgotten phrase he gave up became a name, and each name was a voice he would never hear in his own mouth again. [683] He gave them anyway, one by one, as the dusk thinned: the drowned man's debt became The Unpaid Bell, an old woman's muttered prayer became The Door That Stays Shut. [713]

At last only Marta's words remained, the coin at the bottom of the well. [727] He understood that she had spent herself on a boy who grew old without knowing, and that the spending still rang, forty years on, in this room. [754]

"Look up," he said, in her voice, the last time he would ever hear it. [769]

At the page's empty edge, the light named his own fear: The Sky Still There. [784] The sun went down, and he found he could walk around it. [796]
