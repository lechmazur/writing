The prep room of Halloran and Daughters smelled of formaldehyde and lilies, and at two in the morning its only living occupants were Nell Varga, her father, and the man on the porcelain table. [34] He was not dead, though Nell had powdered him grey enough to fool a patrol flashlight. [50] "Breathe slower," she told him. [55] Ilie Brana, whose poems had been read aloud in the square the week before the square was cleared, obeyed. [74]

Anton Varga leaned against the cold drawers, smiling the way he smiled at bishops and bankers. [90] He had sold forged passports, forged pedigrees, and once a forged saint's knuckle. [103] Tonight he had sold Ilie a transit visa for the poet's entire savings, and Nell had known it was fake before her father finished saying "guaranteed." [129] The proof sat on the steel counter between the trocar and the eye caps: his vintage ink pot, cut glass gone cloudy, its brass lid engraved for a notary ninety years buried. [161] Its iron-gall ink had dried to a black crust, and the visa stamp had been mixed from its dregs. [180]

"Checkpoint guards scratch stamps with a thumbnail," Nell said. [189] "Ministry ink holds; yours flakes, and they will shoot him in the snow." [202]

Anton spread his hands. [206] "Then tell me, honest girl, what a man's escape is worth." [217]

It was the question she had been working since she was nine, tallying the coins he took from widows against the coins he returned when she cried. [244] She wanted the mathematics of a fair trade, a sum in which nobody walked away lighter than they came. [263] She had taken this job because the dead were the only customers who never haggled. [278]

The prep room's back wall was old brick, and beyond it lay the walled garden of the Nordvik consulate, where no policeman in the city could lawfully follow. [306] Since the square was cleared, Nell had been waiting for someone to need that garden, and she had not waited idly. [327] Every night of vigil she had knelt behind the third cold drawer with an embalmer's spatula, scraping mortar only when the compressor shuddered on, pausing when the patrol car's tires hissed past every forty minutes. [362] Four bricks were loose now. [367] One more hour would breach it. [373]

But the Nordvik consul, a careful man, accepted asylum only on a petition signed in ink that could not be washed away, and the only such ink in the building was the forger's. [406]

She set the pot in the warm basin of the arterial pump and watched the crust soften, darken, and loosen into something that moved. [430] Something in her moved with it. [436] For years she had believed honesty meant refusing every tool a liar had touched, as if truth were a wall and she its only brick. [461] Yet here she was, painting a living man dead and digging through a sovereign wall, and none of it cheated anyone owed the truth. [485] Ink was only ink; it confessed whatever hand held it. [495]

"Sit up," she told Ilie, and handed him a pen. [505] He wrote his own petition in the notary's ink, every word true: the poems, the square, the names of the men who had come to his door. [532]

Then Nell turned to her father. [538] "Here is the sum," she said. [544] "He gives up his country. [549] I give up my job and whatever the police decide I owe. [561] You give back every coin and carry the bricks." [570]

"That is not equal," Anton said. [576]

"It is fair," she said. [581] "Fair is when each of us pays with the thing that costs us most." [595]

Her father drew the envelope from his coat and studied it the way other men study the dead. [613] Then he pressed it into Ilie's grey hand and knelt beside her at the wall. [628]

They worked between compressor cycles, father and daughter, passing bricks hand to hand while the drawers hummed with their patient tenants. [649] At a quarter to four the last brick fell outward into frost, and the smell of pine and cold earth rushed into the room like breath into a newborn. [678] Ilie crawled through with the petition against his chest, the ink still wet enough to shine. [694] A light came on in the consulate kitchen. [702]

When the police hammered on the front door at dawn, Nell answered with plaster in her hair and the empty ink pot in her pocket. [727]

"Where is Brana?" [730]

She did not lie; she never would. [737]

"In Nordvik," she said, and behind her, among the dead, her father laughed out loud. [752]
