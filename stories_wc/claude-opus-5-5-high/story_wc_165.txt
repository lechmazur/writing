The tide came into the Saltmarket Arcade the way Ines Varga liked her audiences to arrive: late, uninvited, and exactly on schedule. [22] Between acts she sat on the iron stair above the flooded promenade, violin across her knees, and watched the sea slide through the colonnade under a sky so thick with stars it looked spilled. [56] The arcade had lost its glass roof in a storm forty years ago, and nobody had bothered to replace it. [76] Now the shops sold tide tables and fried cockles to people who came to see the only market in the country that drowned twice a night. [102] Ines played the dry hour, when the tiles were bare, and the high hour, from the balcony, while the water shone. [123] The forty minutes in between belonged to her other work. [133]

She took the bishop from her coat pocket. [141] It was driftwood, pale and salt-cured, carved by someone with more patience than skill, and she had found it wedged in a drain grate her first winter here. [169] Since then she had spent three years mapping the arcade's water with the diligence other musicians gave to scales. [188] She knew which pillars split the current, which cracked tiles made eddies, how a south wind bent the flow toward the chandler's door. [211] The sea was wild, but it was not random, and anything that was not random could be arranged. [229]

Every night she chose a person. [235] Not by staring; staring made people guard themselves. [243] She stayed lightly attentive, letting her gaze drift along the balcony rails and noticing without seeming to notice: the cockle seller who counted her coins twice out of boredom, the clerk who read the same tide table every evening, the boy sent to sweep who swept the same six tiles. [293] People worn smooth by repetition, like stones. [300] Then she calculated where to set the bishop afloat so the tide would carry it to their feet. [318] They always picked it up. [323] Who could resist a chess piece arriving from the sea? [333] And when they looked up, puzzled, the high-hour music would begin, a tune she had built from whatever she had learned of them, so that for four minutes their ordinary night became a small, deliberate miracle. [369] Afterward she would trade a coin for the bishop's return, and they would give it gladly, as if handing back a secret. [391] She called it serenading monotony, and she believed that luck was simply what happened when someone did the arithmetic in advance. [412]

Tonight she chose the boy with the broom. [420] She waited for the wind, knelt on the lowest dry step, and released the bishop into the third pillar's slipstream. [440] It went diagonally, as bishops do, bobbing past the chandler's, past the boy, past every calculation she had ever made, and fetched up against the boots of old Teodor, the night watchman, who sat on an upturned crate beneath the clock. [481]

Ines went down the stairs with her trousers rolled, water to her shins, furious in a way she did not understand. [502] Teodor was already turning the piece in his fingers. [511] Beside him on the crate lay a chessboard, and on the board stood thirty-one driftwood pieces, carved by the same patient, clumsy hand. [534]

"I lost him down that grate the winter you arrived," he said. [546] "Took me a while to see where he'd gone." [555]

He nodded at the pillar nearest him, and she saw it now: a line of oyster shells laid along the tiles, a little breakwater no one would notice unless they already knew the water. [589]

"You've been watching me map it," she said. [597]

"Three years of a woman pacing a flooded arcade with a notebook and a stopwatch," Teodor said. [614] "A watchman watches." [617] He set the bishop on its square, completing the board. [627] "You send him to the lonely ones, and it's a kindness," he said. [640] "But a bishop only ever travels one color." [648] "You never once sent him to yourself." [655]

Ines stood in the rising water and felt the strange vertigo of being the one arranged for. [672] She had spent three years ordering randomness for strangers, never guessing that someone was learning her arithmetic in order to answer it. [694] Above them the clock ticked toward the high hour, and her audience was gathering on the balconies. [711]

"I have four minutes," she said. [717]

Teodor moved a pawn. [721] "Then play me something with a tide in it, and come back after." [734]

She climbed the stair, lifted the violin, and for once did not know what the music would become; she only knew, with the calm of someone who had done the arithmetic, that something was about to happen. [771]
