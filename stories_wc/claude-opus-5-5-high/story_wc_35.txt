The rain came to Qasr Ammar once in a generation, and when it stopped, the fortress smelled like a drowned thing remembering how to breathe. [25] Mahra woke at the close of the dream nobody in the garrison ever spoke about, the one where the dunes rose into green water and every soldier stood on the ramparts, unafraid, while fish passed through the arrow slits. [64] She pulled on her gloves before she touched anything. [73] Her gift had two edges. [78] She could taste memory in food, the way other cooks tasted too much salt, and whatever she tasted leaked out through her skin into anyone who touched her. [106] A careless hand on her shoulder could pass a stranger's grief like a fever. [120] So she cooked, and she did not let herself be held. [131]

For eleven years she had fed the garrison bread and lentils and the thin sour stews of the border, and in every pot she had worked the same quiet labor: finding the rough memories in the men who ate and sanding them down. [174] A burned village became a campfire. [180] A brother left in the sand became a brother asleep. [190] It was not forgetting, only smoothing, the way a stair wears soft under years of feet. [206] She had begun with her own, and she had never finished. [217]

This morning the courtyard was full of pools. [225] The flagstones of Qasr Ammar had been cut from an old seabed, and after rain the water did not sit still in them. [248] It rose and fell, slow as breath, three hundred miles from any sea. [261] Mahra knelt and studied them the way her grandmother had taught her to read a tide pool on a coast she had never seen: where the silt ridged, which way the grit leaned, how the little rings of salt stacked like the growth lines of a tree. [308] Every ripple pointed west, toward the tallest dune. [316] She dipped a gloved finger, then touched it to her tongue. [327] Remorse. [328] Old and cold and packed tight, like snow in a cellar. [339]

Commander Oren found her there. [344] His eyes were red, and half the sentries were weeping at their posts without knowing why. [360] "It is in the water," she said. [367] "Something under the dune is melting." [373]

The founders' ledgers, when Oren brought them, confirmed it in a cramped and guilty hand. [388] Beneath the dunes stood a pavilion of white coral, built when the sea still reached here and sealed in sorcerer's ice when it left. [412] Into its chambers the first garrison had poured everything they regretted about taking this land, so that their sons could hold the walls with clean hearts. [438] Stored remorse did not spoil, the ledger warned. [446] It only waited. [449]

Mahra went down alone at noon, following the line the pools had drawn, digging where the sand was darkest until her shovel rang on coral. [474] The pavilion was beautiful and cracked, its pale arches sweating meltwater, its floor a lake of thawing grief. [492] She had brought a pot and a fire and her knife. [503] She meant to do what she had always done: take the rough thing in, subsume it, render it soft, and carry it where it could hurt no one but her. [533] She boiled the meltwater with salt and bitter herbs and drank it cup by cup. [548] It tasted of wells poisoned, of herders driven into the deep sand, of a girl's name the founders had scraped off every stone. [571] Her sandpaper would not bite. [576] These memories were not rough; they were edged, and they were meant to be. [590] By the fourth cup she could not stand. [598]

Oren came down after her, against orders she had no right to give. [611] He found her folded against a coral pillar and, before she could warn him, took her wrist where the glove had slipped. [633] The remorse went into him like water into a crack. [643] He staggered, and wept, and did not let go. [652] And the weight on her halved. [658]

She understood then, too late for eleven years and just in time for this one. [673] The founders had not failed by feeling their guilt; they had failed by burying it where no one else could share the load. [696] She had done the same, one bowl at a time. [706]

That night she cooked the meltwater into a broth and did not smooth it. [720] She served it in the courtyard, where the pools had gone still, and asked every soldier to take her hand before he drank. [743] Some refused. [745] Most did not. [748] The grief moved through the garrison thin and bearable, a hairline crack running through a wall that still stood. [767] Oren carved the girl's name back into the gate at dawn. [778] Later, for the first time in memory, someone spoke aloud of the dream. [791]
