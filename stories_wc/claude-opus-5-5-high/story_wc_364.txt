On Pellwyn, the apples grew beneath stone bridges that belonged to no river. [13] The arches floated, great rust-veined spans hanging above the orchards, and for three hundred years their shadows had kept the fruit from scorching. [36] Then the Harrow Company came for the skyiron that held them aloft, drilled its shafts into the hills where the arches were rooted, and left when the veins ran thin. [66] Now the arches were falling. [71]

Oren Tull was the blacksmith of Low Cider, and he apologized to horses when he shod them. [88] He did not argue at town meetings. [95] He did not argue when the East Arch came down in the night and flattened the Mereweather rows, or when the council voted to abandon the valley by harvest. [124] He simply walked out to the rubble each evening with a hammer and a lamp, and tapped the broken stone, listening. [145] It rang faintly, like a bell remembering its note. [154]

That was how the magician found him. [161] She wore a coat of patched velvet and carried a length of silk so blue it seemed cut from the sky between the arches. [185]

"Stone sleeps when it breaks," she said, settling onto a split boulder. [197] "This silk wakes things. [201] Pull it through a sleeping thing, and it remembers what it was." [213]

Oren looked at the cloth, then at her. [221] "What does it cost?" [225]

"An awakening for an awakening." [230] She smiled without cruelty. [234] "Every morning you wake, you know who you are and why you rose. [247] I take that. [250] For each thing the silk wakes, you will spend one dawn between understanding and confusion, knowing your hands but not your purpose." [272]

Others in the valley had refused her; Oren could see it in the tiredness of her shoulders. [289] Purpose was all a farmer had when the sky was falling. [300] But Oren had spent his whole life being unsure of what he was for, and he had always known his hands. [321]

"How many arches are left standing?" he asked. [329]

"Eleven." [330]

"Then I'll need eleven dawns," he said, and held out his palm. [342]

The silk could not wake an arch from the outside; the magician said the sleep lived where the skyiron had been, deep in the hollowed roots. [368] So Oren used the wound to reach the heart. [377] He went down the abandoned Harrow shafts, the ones the company had sealed with warning boards and left to drip. [397] He forged iron rings and set them into the tunnel walls, and he threaded the silk through them like a needle through a torn hem, from the orchard floor up into the dark root of each arch. [434] He worked slowly. [437] He measured every ring twice. [442] A hurried stitch, the magician warned, would wake the stone angry, and angry stone falls. [457]

The first morning after, Oren woke and did not know why he was dressed in miner's leathers. [474] He sat on his cot, hands folded, feeling the absence like a missing tooth. [488] Then he saw the note pinned to the door in his own blocky letters: YOU CHOSE THIS, GO TO THE NEXT SHAFT. [510] He went. [512]

Each dawn was the same fog, and each dawn he trusted the man who had written the note. [530] By the eighth, the townsfolk had noticed the arches humming. [540] Cracks sealed themselves with seams of pale blue light. [549] Loose stones that had tumbled into the orchards rose, trembling, and fitted back into place. [564] The council postponed the evacuation, then canceled it. [572]

On the eleventh night, the magician found him lacing the silk into a twelfth shaft, the one beneath the East Arch's rubble. [594]

"That arch is broken, not sleeping," she said. [602] "Waking rubble will cost you more than a dawn." [611]

"How much more?" [614]

"Perhaps every dawn." [617]

Oren tapped a shattered block with his hammer. [625] It still rang, faint and stubborn, the same note he had heard the first evening. [640] He did not remember that evening, but he recognized the sound the way a man recognizes his own name. [659]

"Iron doesn't know why it's struck," he said. [667] "It just learns to hold." [672]

He pulled the silk through. [677]

The rubble lifted in a slow blue storm, stone finding stone, until the East Arch hung again over the flattened Mereweather rows, its seams glowing like a scar that refused to be ashamed. [710] The magician folded her silk, studied him for a long moment, and walked away without naming the price. [728]

Now Oren wakes each morning not knowing why, and each morning he reads the note on his door. [746] Then he walks out beneath the blue-seamed arches, hears the stone ringing its stubborn note, and understands enough. [764]
