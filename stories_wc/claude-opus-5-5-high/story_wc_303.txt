The observatory was lined in plum velvet so that no starlight would bruise the instrument, and at midsummer it was the only cold room left in the empire. [28] Ines Carrow, provenance researcher to the Crown Archive, had been given until dawn. [41] That was when the violin's glow would fade, the curator said, and with it the maker's promise, whatever that had been. [62] The Empress wanted it certified as imperial property before then, so it could be played at her coronation and bound, by law, to the throne forever. [88]

It lay in its case like a held breath. [97] The body was carved from a single crystal of ice, clear enough that she could see the velvet's weave through its belly, and faint light moved inside it like a pulse under a wrist. [131] Beside it lay the ownership roll, a long strip of vellum punched with holes, the old imperial way of recording lineage: one perforation for each owner, each transfer, each death. [161] Archivists called such rolls destinies, because once a thing was punched, it could not be unpunched. [177]

Ines had spent twenty years reading them under a lamp, and she knew at once this one was wrong. [196] The first holes were fine and even, made with a jeweler's awl. [208] The imperial holes that followed were larger, punched through older ones, as if someone had tried to overwrite a sentence by stabbing it. [231]

Then the mulberry spoke. [235] It did not speak, exactly; it never did. [243] Ines had been accidentally telepathic with plants since a fever at nine, and she had learned to hide it the way other people hide a stammer. [269] But the old tree whose branches had split the observatory's dome pressed into her mind like a stained thumb, sweet and aching, and showed her a girl. [296] A gardener's daughter, fingers purple with fruit, freezing water drawn from the tree's roots in a mold of her own design through one long winter. [321] Her promise, made with her palm flat against the bark: I will play it for you once, at midsummer, and then let it go back to you. [348]

She never had. [351] Soldiers came in the spring, and the first large hole was punched through her small one. [367] For a hundred years the violin had been kept frozen in vaults, and for a hundred years the tree had waited, growing toward it through stone. [393]

Ines sat down hard on the velvet bench. [401] Her coffee had gone cold beside the roll, and when she lifted the cup, it left a ring on the curator's blotter, a brown moon with a broken rim. [430] Her grandmother had read futures in such stains, and Ines, who trusted documents and nothing else, had always been ashamed that she could too. [454]

She looked. [456] If she certified the roll, the stain said, the violin would be played once at the coronation by a man who did not love it, then sealed in cold forever, and the mulberry would die reaching. [492] Her name would be printed in gold on the archive door. [503] If she did not, the stain's broken rim showed a road, a cart, a life with no title at all. [523]

She weighed them the way she weighed forgeries, by what each would cost the truth. [538] An empire was a very large thing, and a dream was a girl's purple fingerprint, and the scale did not tip where it should have. [563]

Then she held the roll to the lamp. [571] Light poured through every hole and threw onto the velvet not a lineage but a spray of leaves, as though each stab meant to close the girl's claim had only let more of her through. [606] A destiny full of holes, Ines thought, was not finished; it was a stencil. [620]

She lifted the violin before the glow could go out. [630] She did not know how to play, but the tree did, and it moved her hands. [646] One note, thin as frost, and then another, and the sound was the color of mulberry juice on a sleeve that will never wash clean. [671] Midsummer heat found the crack in the dome, and the violin softened in her arms, dripping onto the roots that had come so far to meet it. [698] By dawn there was only water, and the promise was kept. [709]

Ines cut six green shoots from the tree, wrapped them in wet pages of her own notes, and carried them out past the guards. [733] She would propagate them in every province the empire had punched through until every wrong hole had a root beneath it. [754] Her report, left on the blotter beside the coffee ring, said only: Provenance, the orchard. [769]
