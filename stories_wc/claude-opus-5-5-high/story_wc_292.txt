The island gave under Odile Marsh's boots like a loaf left out in rain. [14] Everything on Skerra had gone soft since the jet stream shifted north, dragging a permanent belt of storms across a rock the Admiralty had once pretended did not exist. [43] The experimental weather station squatted on the highest ground, a cluster of white domes and anemometer masts that dripped steadily, as if the whole place were sweating. [70]

"You're the map librarian," said Pell, the station's only year-round technician, taking her case. [84]

"I'm the map librarian," she agreed, and heard her voice come out steadier than she felt. [100] She had rehearsed confidence on the ferry the way other people rehearsed apologies. [113] She believed, perhaps foolishly, that a true map could correct anything. [124]

Pell led her to a shed that smelled of kelp and wet cardboard. [137] On the bench lay eleven corroded tins, and inside each, swollen like drowned things, a ship's journal. [154]

"The new storms scour the sand bar," he said. [163] "Every week the sea coughs up another wreck from eighteen-ninety-something. [173] Nobody at the Met Office cares. [179] You wrote back within an hour." [185]

She had, because of the stanzas. [191] Pell's photographs had shown them clearly: verses pencilled into the margins of dead men's logbooks, all in one slanting hand, all signed with a small drawn anchor that Odile knew from the inside of her grandmother's Bible. [228] It was the mark of Anneke Marsh, the cartographer who had been exiled to Skerra in 1887 for publishing an honest chart of its reefs that shamed the Admiralty. [257]

The family story said Anneke had been a disgrace. [266] The exile order, which Odile had found in the national archive and read until the paper felt warm, said something stranger. [287] The sentence would stand "until such time as Skerra appears upon the Admiralty chart." [301] Then the Admiralty had removed Skerra from every chart it printed, and a reversible exile became a permanent one by the simple act of erasure. [326]

Odile opened the nearest journal. [331] The pages sagged under her thumb. [337]

"Where the grey hand holds the drowning keel, count seven breaths and bear to the seal." [353]

She laid a transparent grid over the page, the way she had practiced for two months on the scans, and counted syllables against the bearings in the log. [381] The verses were soundings. [385] Each stanza encoded a depth and a heading, a safe channel through the reef, and each tin had been sealed and set adrift for the mainland, because every letter she wrote was read and burned. [420]

"She was guiding the rescue boats," Odile said. [428] "She rowed out herself, look, here, and here." [436]

Survivors' names appeared under the anchor mark, one journal after another, forty-one men who had lived because a woman nobody would name had mapped the water that was trying to kill them. [468]

Pell rubbed his neck. [472] "Director's pouring footings for the new radar array tomorrow. [481] Old graveyard on the east slope." [487]

The dread arrived the way the damp did, not in a blow but through the seams. [503] Odile felt it soak into her. [509]

"Did anyone question whose graves they are?" [516]

"Nobody's asked anything," Pell said. [521] "Ground's sinking anyway. [524] Half the stones are gone into the peat." [532]

She took the copper nail from her coat pocket. [541] It was green along its length and bright at the head where generations of Marsh thumbs had rubbed it, the only thing sent home when Anneke died, pulled from the lid of her coffin by a sexton with a conscience. [581] The last stanza, in the last journal, was different from the others. [593]

"Where copper sleeps the needle wakes true; from my bones, daughter, the reef starts anew." [608]

"Copper doesn't pull a compass," Odile whispered. [615] "She had herself buried with copper so her grave wouldn't deflect a bearing. [628] She made her coffin a survey mark." [635]

They went out into the fog with a lantern and a probe. [647] The east slope quivered underfoot, and every step squeezed brown water up around their ankles, the peat trying to take them in. [669] The domes behind them hummed, measuring a sky no one could see. [681] At the ninth probe, metal rang faintly against metal, and Odile knelt in the wet and dug with her hands until she found a row of green nail-heads in black wood, one missing. [714]

She did not cry. [718] She pressed the copper nail back into its empty hole, set the station's theodolite above it, and drew the reef from the dead cartographer's verses, channel by channel. [746]

In the morning she questioned the director for an hour, with Pell behind her, and her voice shook only at the start. [768] The footings moved forty metres west. [774] A week later the chart went to the Hydrographic Office with Skerra on it, and a petition to reverse one exile. [795]
