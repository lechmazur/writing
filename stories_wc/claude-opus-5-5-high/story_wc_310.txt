The observatory deck had gone amber in the last century, when the glass began filtering a sky that was falling inward. [21] Every star now burned blue at the edges, crowding closer, and Tamsin Orr had stopped trusting any of them. [40] They looked certain, the way a liar looks certain. [49]

She sat cross-legged beneath the great receiver dish with a velvet tray across her knees, sorting coins. [66] There were two hundred and six of them, every one from a world that no longer answered hails: square bronze from Hesket, a pierced silver ring from the Nine Moons, a glass disc from somewhere nobody remembered naming. [104] Refugees had pressed them into her hands as payment when the station still took refugees. [119] Now the coins were her office. [125] When the council of Kettle Station could not agree, they came to the deck, and Tamsin cast lots, and whatever faces landed upward became law. [150] It was fairer than voting, people said, because the dead worlds had no stake. [164] Tamsin privately suspected it was only easier. [171] What she wanted, and had never admitted, was what the station creed promised every keeper: to find the wavelength she was built for and align with it. [198]

Tonight the receiver crackled. [202] Static bursts had been arriving for eleven days, each one louder, each one sharper, as though the collapsing universe were clearing its throat. [225] The engineers called it noise from the compression front. [234] Tamsin, who had spent twenty years listening to nothing, knew better, and the knowing lit something in her chest that she kept carefully off her face. [260]

She fed the latest burst into the old resin still, a coil of amber tubing meant for purifying coolant, which she had rewired to slow signals until they condensed into sound. [291] Drop by drop, she distilled the hiss. [298] A voice emerged, thick and reversed. [304] She played it backward, and it spoke plainly. [312]

It described the fire in Hydroponics Two, eight years past. [322] Yesterday's burst had recounted her mother's death last spring. [331] The one before that had described the council's quarrel over rations, three days before the council quarreled. [348] The prophecy was running backward, walking from the end toward the beginning, and she had been receiving it upside down. [368] Which meant the first burst, the one she had shelved eleven days ago as garbage, was the furthest ahead. [387]

Her hands were steady as she threaded it through the still. [398]

Footsteps rang on the gantry. [403] Councilor Brask arrived breathless, the others behind him, their faces gold in the amber light. [418] "The lifeboat," he said. [422] "Half of us want to ride the collapse inward and gamble on whatever begins after. [437] Half want to stay and die slow." [444] "Cast for us." [447]

Tamsin looked up through the glass. [453] The stars pressed nearer, blazing, and she could not tell whether they promised rebirth or only brightness before the end. [473] No one could. [476] That was why they wanted coins. [482]

The still finished condensing. [486] She reversed the recording and let it play for everyone. [496]

It was her own voice. [501]

"This is Kettle Station, falling inward," it said, calm and warm. [512] "We chose to go. [516] The lot-caster did not cast. [521] She chose, and we followed her, and we are sending back everything we were, so that it reaches you before it happens." [543]

The deck went silent. [547] Brask stared at the tray of coins. [554] "So it is already decided." [559]

"No," Tamsin said softly. [563] "It is only already said." [568]

For twenty years she had hidden behind the coins, letting dead worlds carry the weight of choosing, waiting to learn what she was tuned for. [593] The static had been answering all along. [600] Her purpose was not to guess the future but to become the signal that carried it back. [617]

She still doubted the stars. [622] She doubted the lifeboat, the collapse, the promise of any beginning after. [634] But the voice in the recording had not sounded afraid, and it was hers. [648]

She tipped the velvet tray, and two hundred and six coins from two hundred and six silent worlds slid into her satchel uncast. [671] They would come too. [675] Someone ought to remember those worlds on the other side. [685]

"We go," she said, and felt the words settle into her like a needle finding its frequency. [702]

Then, quietly delighted, she switched the receiver to record, cleared her throat, and began to set down everything the station had been, so that when they fell, the static would have something true to carry back. [738] Above her, the stars burned on, uncertain, and for once she did not need them to be sure. [756]
