The rain had stopped an hour ago, and the dock at the Seventh Lock glowed cobalt, every plank holding a skin of water that trembled whenever the canal breathed. [29] Oskar had kept the lock for thirty years without saying a word, because a fever in his boyhood had taken his voice and left him the habit of listening. [58] Tonight he was listening to Mara. [64] She stood at the edge of the dock with her lamplighter's pole held crosswise, like a barrier, and she had just said, "The night the Vell boy went under, I-" and then nothing. [97] The word hung between them like a mooring line pulled taut, neither slack nor snapped. [112] Oskar did not reach for it. [118] He knew what lived on the far side of that pause. [129] For six weeks the lamp at Heron Bend had stayed dark, and for six weeks the city had blamed the fog for the skiff that split against the pilings there. [159] Mara had not lit a single lamp since. [167] She walked her rounds in the dark now, and when people asked, she said she preferred it, that darkness was honest, that it did not pretend anyone was safe. [196] Oskar understood that she was trying to become the dark so that she would not have to be seen inside it. [217] He felt his own calm settle downward in slow turns, the way a leaf spirals toward the canal bed, each turn quieter than the last. [242] It was the only gift he had ever been good at giving. [254]

He took the dental mirror from his coat pocket. [263] It had belonged to his father, a dentist who had despaired of a mute son, and Oskar had found a better use for it: slipping it into the narrow gaps of the lock gears, reading their worn teeth by lantern, finding rot before it found the gates. [310] Now he held it low over a puddle on the planks and tilted it until the small round glass caught what the clouds had just surrendered. [336] A star. [338] One hard white point, trembling in a circle no bigger than a coin. [351] He offered it to her, handle first. [358] Mara looked at it and did not take it. [367] Her mouth was still half open around the rest of her sentence. [379]

So Oskar went to the lockhouse. [385] Its windows faced the bend, and its heavy shutters had once been a signal board, opened and closed in sequence to warn the barges of low water or a jammed gate. [416] No one used the code anymore. [422] He lit the lantern inside, then began to work the shutters, left and right, upper and lower, and the light went out across the wet cobalt water in bars and gaps. [453] He was weaving. [456] Dark strand, bright strand, dark again, the pattern laid over the canal like cloth on a loom, and in every dark strand the reflected stars came through clearer, and in every bright one the pilings at Heron Bend stood out, black and certain, where a boat could see them. [505] It was not an argument. [510] It was simply what was true: the dark was where the stars could be seen, and the stars were what made the dark crossable. [534] Neither was worth much alone. [539] He wove the old code last, the one the old keeper had taught him as a boy, the one that meant lamp needed here. [563] Then he closed every shutter and let the night have the water. [575]

On the dock, Mara had not moved. [582] The canal lay flat as held breath. [589] A rat crossed the far lock beam and paused, as if it too were waiting for her to finish. [608] Oskar came back and stood beside her, not facing her, the way you stand beside someone at a grave. [627] He did not need the rest of the sentence. [636] He had watched her face every evening for six weeks, and silence had taught him to read what people carried more accurately than they could say it. [663] Guilt did not need confession to be known; it needed only to be stood beside. [678]

At last Mara turned the pole from crosswise to upright. [688] She took the dental mirror from his hand and looked into it for a long moment, at the single star, and at the edge of her own eye beside it. [718] "I-" she said again. [722] It was still the edge of the secret, still unfinished. [732] But she tucked the mirror into her coat, lit her pole's wick at the lockhouse lantern, and walked toward Heron Bend with the flame held high, like something she had been told was precious and was only now beginning to believe. [773] Oskar watched the dark receive her, and keep her, and let her shine. [786]
