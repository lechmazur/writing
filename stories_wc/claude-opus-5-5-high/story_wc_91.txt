Oren Vale had given away three fortunes, and still he did not feel different. [14] He had funded clinics in mountain towns, bought back a fishing fleet from its creditors, and paid off the debts of strangers whose names he never learned. [41] Now his coat was patched at both elbows, his boots were borrowed, and the only thing he still owned was a wooden case of glass jars, each holding a single lightning bug from a place he had helped. [79] The bugs should have died within a week of capture. [89] They had not, and he had stopped asking why. [98]

He was not a saint, whatever the newspapers once claimed. [108] His true gift was arranging chance. [114] He would leave a wallet on a bench so that two estranged brothers reached for it at once, or miss a train on purpose so a widow would be forced to share his cab with the doctor who would later marry her. [156] Every coincidence he orchestrated changed someone else. [163] He wanted, just once, to feel the texture of transformation from the inside, to know whether it was rough like pumice or smooth like water, and money had never let him touch it. [196]

He came to Kaleo Island on the last ferry before the harbor closed. [209] The volcano above the village had been grumbling for a month, and that week the sky was performing its own slow drama: Venus and the moon drifting toward each other across three nights, closing on a conjunction the fishermen called the Embrace. [251] An old woman selling salted fish told him the prophecy without being asked. [264] "When the lovers meet overhead, the mountain will draw a breath, and the world will go quiet," she said. [283] "Then a man with empty pockets will carry the stars down to the black sand, and the fire will turn aside." [304] Oren smiled politely. [307] He had built too many coincidences by hand to believe in the ones that built themselves. [323]

On the third night the hush came. [330] It began as a whispering along the ridge, the palms rustling with no wind, and then even that faded until Oren could hear the jars ticking softly against each other in their case. [363] Dogs stopped barking. [366] The surf seemed to hold itself back. [373] Overhead, the moon slid its pale shoulder against Venus, and below it the crater glowed the color of a struck match. [394]

He had meant to walk the coast road to the evacuation boats at the northern cove. [410] Halfway there, a fresh crack had split the asphalt, steaming, and he was forced into a detour down a goat path to the beach below. [435] The sand there was black as spilled ink and warm through his borrowed boots. [449] The ash had swallowed the moonlight. [455] He could not see his own hands. [462]

Then he heard the children. [467] There were six of them, a school group separated from their teacher, huddled against a boulder and too frightened to cry out loud. [490] The hush had gotten into them too; they were whispering, as if the mountain might hear. [506] Oren knelt, fumbling with the case, and understood he had no lantern, no match, nothing but forty small lights in glass. [527] He opened the first jar. [532] The bug rose, green-gold, and hovered. [538] He opened another, and another, each one a clinic, a fleet, a forgiven debt, until forty lights were drifting above the black sand like a small, slow constellation. [566] They did not scatter. [570] They drifted north along the shoreline, and the children, holding hands, followed, and Oren followed the children. [587]

Behind them the mountain exhaled. [592] Lava poured down the slope toward the village, reached the crack that had forced his detour, and slid into it as into a waiting throat, turning seaward and hissing into the surf far from any roof. [628] Steam rose white over the black beach, and the hush broke into shouting, into bells, into the teacher's voice calling names from the cove ahead. [653]

At dawn the fish seller found him sitting on an overturned boat with an empty case across his knees. [672] "Empty pockets," she said, nodding at the case. [680] "Stars on the sand." [684] "I didn't plan any of it," Oren said. [692] "That is how prophecies prefer it," she said. [700]

He looked at his hands, dusted gray with ash, and felt it at last, the thing he had crossed oceans to touch. [722] Transformation was not rough or smooth. [728] It was the texture of an opened hand, of releasing the last thing you owned and finding that the one coincidence you never arranged had been arranging you all along. [758] Above the steaming shore, one lightning bug still hovered, blinking slowly, as the moon and Venus drifted apart. [776]
