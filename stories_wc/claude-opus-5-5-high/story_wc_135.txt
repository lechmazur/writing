The rain stopped an hour before the first ship was due, and the ivory harbor steamed like a bath drawn too hot. [22] Wren Asher walked the seawall with her satchel of blotting sheets, pressing each one flat against the pale stone where the spray had dried in lines of salt. [50] The city called her the ocean-spray archivist, half as a joke, because for twenty years she had collected nothing else. [70] Every tide left a different pattern, and every pattern had gaps where no salt settled, as if something invisible stood between the sea and the wall. [96] She had eleven thousand sheets in the archive tower and no idea what they said. [111] She kept them anyway, certain the fragments would add up to something. [123] She had never been able to think her way to meaning; it came to her only when something struck her first, a smell or a sound, and then whole shapes would open behind her eyes. [158] Her mother had called it being reactively visionary and had not meant it kindly. [172]

Today the exile ended. [176] Twenty years ago the council had banished the heralds for sounding a flood warning the council had not approved, and since then the harbor had no horn, no bell, no voice at all. [209] Ships came in by daylight or not at all. [218]

The first returning ship docked without trouble, and down its plank came a man so thin his coat seemed to hang from a nail. [242] Wren knew him from the old broadsheets: Oda Venn, once Chief Herald. [254] He carried nothing but a small brass cylinder, which he turned over and over in his fingers. [271] "The trumpet valve," he said when he saw her watching. [281] "They melted the rest of the horn at the border. [291] I kept this in my cheek." [297] He pressed it absently as he spoke, click, pause, click-click, a long pause, click, a rhythm with uneven spaces in it. [318] "Herald cadence," he said. [322] "We counted the rests, not the notes. [329] A call was mostly silence, placed in staggered intervals so it could carry through storm noise." [345]

The clicks went into Wren like a key into a lock. [356] She saw her eleven thousand sheets laid end to end, and the salt-free gaps were no longer blanks; they were rests, spaced exactly the way his fingers spaced them. [385] "The wall," she said. [389] "Something in the wall breathes in your rhythm." [397]

Oda stopped clicking. [400] "The harbor pipe," he said slowly. [406] "We built it into the seawall so the waves would blow the call for us, even when no herald was standing watch. [428] They tore out the valve housing the week we left. [438] Without it the air just escapes." [444] He looked at the brass in his hand as if it had failed him personally. [459] "I thought I was bringing back a keepsake." [467]

Out past the breakwater, fog was rising off the warm rainwater, and a second ship's lantern wandered in it, too far north, heading for the reef. [493] Wren had spent two decades learning where the spray did not fall. [505] She knew every silent patch on the wall, and the largest one, the one that never wavered, sat under the old herald tower. [528] She took his wrist and ran. [534]

The vent was a round mouth in the ivory, rimmed with green, exhaling in pulses as the swell pushed beneath it. [555] Oda knelt, shaking, and could not make his fingers fit the valve to the socket. [570] "Twenty years," he said. [574] "I don't remember the harbor call." [580] "I do," Wren said, and pulled a sheet from her satchel, still damp, the gaps in the salt plain as notation. [601] "The wall kept it for you. [607] You only have to read it, and I only have to hold the page." [621] It was the only way she knew to hearten anyone: to show them the piece they were missing had been kept. [642]

His hands steadied. [645] The valve seated with a click, and he began, following her finger along the gaps, pressing and releasing through the staggered intervals while the sea supplied the breath. [673] The note that came out was low and cracked and enormous. [684] It rolled across the white stones and into the fog, mostly silence, the way a herald's call was meant to be. [705]

The lantern hesitated, then swung south. [711] The exiles aboard had navigated twenty years of absences, and now they steered by one more, the spaces between the notes, until the channel took them in. [738]

Later, in the archive tower, Wren laid the eleven thousand sheets in order and began, finally, to transcribe them. [757] Oda sat beside her, tapping the cadence on the table so she could see it. [772] Twenty years of tides, it turned out, had been rehearsing the call in silence, over and over, waiting for someone to put the fragments together. [797]
