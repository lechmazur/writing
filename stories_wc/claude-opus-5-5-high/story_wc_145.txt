The falcon came back on the ninth morning, alone, with nothing tied to its leg but frost. [17] Marta Kell let it in through the outpost's broken shutter and gave it the last of the salted hare, because someone ought to be rewarded for keeping an appointment. [46] Anselm had not kept his. [51]

The Survey had sent her north to fix a single embarrassment in the new continental atlas: three great rivers, drawn in confident blue across four hundred miles, each ending at the top of the page in the same dotted line and the same word, unknown. [96] "An atlas that says unknown three times is not an atlas," her publisher had complained. [111] "It is an honest atlas," Marta had replied, "which is why nobody buys it." [125]

Anselm, the outpost's keeper, was supposed to meet her with his notes. [137] He spoke eleven languages and had written to the Survey in all of them, each letter insisting he was close. [157] She had found only rust. [162] The outpost stood on iron legs at the edge of a frozen lake, its tin roof bleeding orange down the walls, its radio mast bent like a question nobody wanted answered. [193] Inside were a cold stove, a cot, and a phrasebook with a cracked green spine. [208]

The lake was very beautiful and very quiet. [216] That was the part she liked least. [223] Ice should talk; it groans and pings and cracks as it settles. [235] This ice lay silent as a held breath, gray at the center, as if something beneath it were keeping warm. [255]

Now, with the falcon hunched on the stove pipe, she opened the phrasebook again. [269] Only one page was dog-eared, folded so many times the corner had gone soft as cloth. [285] It held a single phrase in forty tongues: Where does the water come from? [299] Beneath the columns Anselm had written, in a cramped hand, three short lines. [312] Every language asks it the same way. [319] Every river answers it the same way. [326] Watch the glass at dawn. [331]

Marta had edited enough explorers to distrust poetry in margins. [341] Poetry was usually where the measurements should have been. [350] Still, she had nowhere else to look, and she wanted, more than she would admit to the Survey, to find where the rivers begin before someone less careful drew them in. [381]

So she sat up through the night, feeding the stove with broken crate slats, and at first light she looked at the lake-facing window. [405] The warmth of the room met the cold pane, and the condensation did not bead evenly. [421] It ran in thin clear channels, branching and rejoining, fed by drafts that slipped through the rusted frame from the lake itself. [443] She knew the shape before she understood it. [451] There was the Vell with its oxbow, the Harrow with its long western elbow, the Sable splitting into its delta at the bottom corner of the pane. [478] She had corrected their curves for eleven years. [486] And at the top of the glass, where the atlas said unknown, all three narrowed to threads and met at one clear point, exactly level with the gray heart of the lake. [518]

Not three sources. [521] One. [522] A singular convergence, a spring under the ice so warm and so old that the whole continent drank from it without knowing. [544]

She understood, too, where Anselm had gone. [551] A polyglot cannot hear a question in forty languages without wanting to answer it in person. [567] Out on the lake, the low sun picked out what noon had hidden: a faint line of bootprints running toward the gray and not coming back. [593] The falcon had come home because it had nowhere else to return. [605]

Marta did not follow the prints. [611] She was not brave, she reflected, merely accurate, and accuracy has the decency to stay on shore. [628] She traced the pane onto onionskin before the sun erased it. [639] Then she tore the dog-eared page from the phrasebook and, beneath forty versions of the question, added one answer in her plain editor's hand: Here. [664] She rolled page and tracing together and tied them to the falcon's leg. [677] It would herald the news south faster than any sledge: the rivers had a mother, and she was not asleep. [697]

The bird went up into a sky without a single cloud. [708] Below it the lake lay perfectly still, gray at the center and a little grayer than yesterday, and Marta, who did not guess at things, poured herself tea and began to revise the word unknown. [743] It was very peaceful. [747] She was quite certain it would not stay that way. [757]
