Ione Vashti came down to Meridian Deep with a bag of charts that no longer applied. [16] She was a landscape astrologer, trained to read the sky as it pressed itself into ground: the Plough in a furrowed field, Orion in the fall of three hills. [45] Every village she had served had paid her, thanked her, and let her go. [59] She had never learned how to stay. [66]

The drowned city lay two hundred meters below the swells, its avenues silted, its spires furred with anemones, and the research station sat in its old square like a glass lung that no one remembered to empty. [103] Funding had ended a decade ago. [109] Only Tobiah Crane remained, a caretaker with salt-white stubble and a habit of patting bulkheads as he passed, the way a farmer pats a horse. [134] He had written to the guild asking for someone who could map the ruins before the sea took the last of them. [156] Nobody else had answered. [160]

"There's no sky down here," Ione said on her first night, pressing her palm to the viewport. [177]

"No," Tobiah agreed. [180] "But the city was laid out by people who worshipped one." [191]

She did not believe him until the third night, when she spread her sonar prints across the drafting table and saw it. [213] The plazas were set like the Pleiades, the canals bent in the long arc of Draco, and the old observatory dome stood exactly where Vega should burn. [240] The streets were a summer sky, drowned and faithful. [249]

But the chart was incomplete. [254] One constellation was missing its heart, and the gap fell on the station itself, on the sealed hydroponic garden Tobiah never opened. [276]

"My wife's," he said when she asked. [283] "She kept it alive until she couldn't." [290] Then, as if it followed: "The air in there has gone wrong." [302] "The scrubbers cycle, but I can't find where it breathes out." [313]

That night Ione took the only tool she had never replaced: a heron feather, its tip burnt black from years of dipping it in lamp flame to draw stars on stone. [344] Tobiah unsealed the garden door, and humid dark rolled out smelling of loam and rust. [359] Inside, ferns had overgrown their troughs and a meadow of pale grasses leaned under dead grow-lamps, every stem bowed the same direction, like pilgrims. [383]

Ione held the feather at arm's length and let it hang from two fingers. [397] It stirred, stilled, stirred again. [402] She walked where it pointed, pausing when it paused, and slowly she understood that the garden was not leaking but breathing, drawing in through the floor grates and sighing out through a vent choked with dead leaves, in a slow cadence of eleven counts in and eleven out. [450] The scrubbers had been set to a rhythm, not a schedule. [461]

"She tuned it to her own breathing," Tobiah said, very quietly. [472] "I stopped coming in because I couldn't match it anymore." [482]

Ione cleared the vent with her hands. [489] Then, because the feather still trembled, she began to breathe with the room, and after a moment Tobiah did too, two strangers in the dark learning a dead woman's tempo. [519] Eleven in. [521] Eleven out. [523] It was the first time in her life she had kept pace with anything that was not a star. [542]

On the fortieth breath, clean air reached the grasses, and something in the meadow woke. [557] A single green light rose from the stems, then another, then a dozen, fireflies descended from whatever stock his wife had carried down, waking into their first summer in years. [587] Ione and Tobiah let go of the same held breath at once, a shared exhale that was almost a laugh. [607]

The fireflies drifted into the shadows between the ferns and hung there, blinking, and she saw the missing heart of the constellation assemble itself in living light, exactly where her chart had failed. [640] His wife had not forgotten the star the city was missing; she had planted it. [655]

They did not speak. [659] The lights captivated them the way a hearth captivates travelers, not by blazing but by staying, a luminous respite in the seam where the dark pressed closest. [686] They sat among the grasses until sunrise, when the surface buoy sent its signal down and the station lamps warmed from blue to gold. [710] The fireflies dimmed and settled. [715]

Ione held the burnt feather's tip to a work lamp until it blackened, then drew the finished constellation on the garden's steel wall and added two small marks beside it where no star had ever been. [751]

"Who are those?" Tobiah asked. [756]

"The ones who keep the rhythm," she said, and for the first time, she did not reach for her bag. [776]
