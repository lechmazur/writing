Odile Varn had catalogued broken mirrors in the forgotten library for thirty-one years, and she had never once let an apprentice touch the good shards. [25] Tamsin Hale was not an apprentice; she was an exile, sent up the hill by the town council with a satchel and a sentence. [49] Calder's exiles were reversible, which everyone said was merciful and no one explained. [62] Odile explained it the first morning, over tea she did not offer to share. [76] "The council caught the afternoon they banished you," she said, and set a sliver of glass on the reading table. [96] Inside it lay a shadow, long and slanted, the shadow of the schoolyard swing at four o'clock on the day Tamsin had been named a liar. [122] "Annotate it truthfully, in the margin of the frame, at the moment the real shadow matches it, and you walk home." [143] "Lie in the margin, and the glass keeps you." [152]

Tamsin took three steps backward, heels first, until she bumped a shelf of cracked hand mirrors. [168] She always walked backwards when she was nervous; her mother called it retreating politely. [182] "Stop that," Odile snapped. [186] "You'll scuff the floor and learn nothing." [193]

"You don't know what I learned." [199]

"I know you learned to step away from anything that looks at you." [212]

The library had been forgotten when the school below it closed, its windows gone milky, its stacks leaning like tired men. [233] Every shelf held shards: a wedding smile in one, a flood's gray light in another, each labeled in Odile's cramped hand. [254] The trouble was timing. [258] The swing still hung in the empty playground, and its shadow crossed the yard at a slightly different angle every afternoon, drifting with the seasons and the wind. [286] To predict the match, Odile analyzed the geometry of tea leaves. [297] Each dusk she drained her cup and measured the leaves with a brass protractor, reading their clumps as angles, their stray stems as the lean of light. [324]

"Superstition," Tamsin said. [327]

"Geometry," Odile said, and made her do it herself. [336]

For weeks Tamsin's readings were wrong. [342] Odile told her so without softening it, then poured her a fresh cup without comment. [357] When Tamsin threw the protractor, Odile handed it back. [366] When Tamsin cried, Odile found urgent work in the far stacks and returned with a shard nobody had asked for, a child laughing on a sled, and left it by Tamsin's elbow. [398]

"You're cruel," Tamsin told her once. [404]

"I'm consistent," Odile said. [408] "Cruel is letting you believe the glass will be kind." [418]

In late October the leaves finally agreed with each other: a wedge at thirty-eight degrees, a stem pointing west. [437] Tomorrow, at the hush. [441]

They waited at the window the next afternoon, the shard between them, a pen in Tamsin's fist. [458] Below, the playground was empty, and the wind died, and in that hush the swing drifted on its chains, alone, until its shadow slid across the gravel and lay down exactly inside the one caught in the glass. [496]

Tamsin's feet began to move backward. [502]

Odile did not grab her. [507] She only stepped behind her, close, so that there was nowhere to retreat but through the old woman. [525] "Go on," Odile said roughly. [529] "Tell it." [531]

Tamsin wrote in the margin: I broke the school mirror and let my brother take the blame. [548] I was afraid. [551] I am still afraid, and I am writing it anyway. [561]

The shadow in the glass shivered and drained out like tea from a tipped cup. [576] The shard went clear. [580]

Tamsin turned. [582] Odile was already walking to the shelves, her back straight, labeling nothing. [594] On the highest shelf sat a shard Tamsin had never been allowed to touch, and in it lay a shadow thirty-one years old. [616]

"You never annotated yours," Tamsin said. [622]

"Somebody had to keep the library." [628]

"That's a lie." [631]

Odile's hands stilled on the frame. [637] For a long moment she said nothing, and then, very slightly, she took one step backward. [653]

Tamsin did not grab her either. [659] She stood behind her, close, and put the pen in the old woman's hand. [673] "Exile's reversible," she said, fierce as a slammed book. [682] "The leaves say spring." [686]

"You'll get the angle wrong," Odile said. [693]

"Then you'll correct me, and I'll stay until you do." [703]

Outside, the swing hung still, waiting for its shadow to come around again. [716]
