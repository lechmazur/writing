The sun had perhaps nine minutes left, and Maren Voss spent the first of them counting. [16] Drift ice had packed the marina overnight, a white field groaning against the trawlers, heaping in slabs against the stone arch of the crypt beneath the breakwater. [43] Behind that arch hung the Absolution Bell, which the village rang each sunset so the drowned could hear their names forgiven. [64] The wall was bowing. [68] She could see mortar weeping from the joints like sweat. [78]

Maren buttoned her azure coat to the throat. [86] It had been Tobiah's color before it was hers, the blue the old keepers wore so boats could find them against snow, and it still smelled faintly of his pipe. [116] Tobiah had been crypt keeper for forty years. [124] Eleven days ago he had walked onto the ice in fog to free a snagged mooring, and the foghorn she was supposed to have repaired had stayed silent, and he had not come back. [158]

She did not permit herself to cry about that. [167] She permitted herself to measure it. [173] Grief, she had decided, was a load like any other: it had a weight, a direction, a point of failure. [193] Her failure point was the bell. [199] If the wall gave and the bell went into the sea, there would be no one left to forgive Tobiah, or her. [221]

From her pocket she drew the crypt keeper's key. [230] Tobiah's will had left it to her with the coat, a hand-long bar of black bronze whose teeth fit no lock she had ever seen. [255] Last night, unable to sleep, she had tapped it against the lamp housing and understood. [270] It was not a key for a door. [278] It rang, clean and low, the exact note of the bell, cast from the same pour. [294] Keepers carried it to tune the bell after every winter. [304] And the oldest entry in the crypt ledger claimed that note had once split a floe that was crushing the harbor. [325]

The bell itself could not be rung loudly enough; it was built for the dead, who listen closely. [343] But the foghorn could shout. [348] She had spent the week fixing what she had failed to fix, and now she had to adapt it again, retuning a machine meant to warn into one meant to sing. [379]

She climbed to the horn room as the light turned the color of honey and then of struck brass. [398] Everything glowed: the ice, the rigging, her own raw knuckles, as if the world were lit from inside its skin. [418] She opened the compressor valve and let the horn moan. [428] Then she struck the key on the railing and clamped its shank between her teeth. [443] The two tones met in her skull and fought, a sour throbbing that rattled her fillings and made her eyes water. [464] Tobiah had taught her this for the lamp motor: listen with your bones, chase the wobble until it dies. [483] Matched vibrations stop quarreling. [487] She shortened the resonator a quarter turn. [494] The throbbing slowed. [497] Another eighth. [499] Slower, a heartbeat, a held breath. [505] Then the wobble vanished, and the horn and the key and the bone of her jaw were singing one note. [525]

Below, faint and deep, the bell answered on its own. [535] The ice heard it too. [540] A crack ran from the seawall like a drawn line of light, then another, then a hundred, and the pack against the crypt shivered apart into floating plates that the tide began, gently, to carry off. [576] The wall stopped weeping. [580]

Maren did not close the valve. [586] The note was the one the village used for absolution, and she had never heard it this loud, rolling out across the marina and the whole gold-burning sea. [614] Somewhere under that light was Tobiah. [620] She said his name around the key, a mangled syllable, and the sound carried it out to him. [638] Then, because the logic was symmetrical and she could not argue with it, she said her own. [655] The tears came then, hot on cold cheeks, and she let them, because a load that has been named can finally be set down. [679] The sun slipped under. [683] The note hung in the dark a long moment after, as if the whole harbor were holding it for her, and then it too let go. [709]
