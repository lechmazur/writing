The fever broke a little after three in the morning, and Ines woke to the sound of the anemometer cups spinning on the roof like a coin that would not fall. [31]
For nine days the Kessel Peak weather station had held only her, the diesel heater, and the Oracle. [49]
The engineers who installed it in 1987 had called it a fault-tolerant forecasting system: three processors that each built a forecast and then voted, so that no single failure could lie to the observers. [83]
The observers had called it the Oracle because it never stopped speaking, even when half its sensors were buried in rime. [104]
Ines sat up, soaked and light-headed, and through the window she saw the aurora hanging over the ridge in green folds, swaying like curtains in a house where someone has just died. [136]
Someone had. [138]
Tomas had gone up that ridge twenty-three days ago to find a late-blooming snow gentian, the last plant missing from the book of illustrations he had been drawing for six summers. [169]
He had asked her for the forecast, and she had read him the Oracle's majority vote: clear until evening. [188]
She had not read him the rest. [195]
The screen had flashed VOTE 2-1, DISSENT PRINTED, and she had not wanted to cross the room while he stood grinning in the doorway with his pack already on. [224]
In the fever she had dreamed of that omission as a stone lodged under her breastbone, something she had swallowed whole on the morning he left and had never let herself weigh. [256]
The search team had found his body in the couloir after the storm, and she had told them, truthfully, that the station predicted clear skies. [281]
Truthfully, and not at all. [286]
Now, with her head finally clear, she needed to know exactly how heavy it was. [301]
She wrapped herself in a blanket and crossed to the console. [312]
The Oracle's third processor had always been the odd one. [322]
It was peripherally aware, the engineers had said, wired to weight the outlying sensors, the ridge anemometer and the valley barometer that the other two processors discounted as noise. [351]
When it lost a vote, the system was designed not to erase its dissent but to print it by occupying margins, tiny dot-matrix figures squeezed along the edge of whatever paper sat in the tray, so that the minority opinion survived without cluttering the official line. [397]
The memory logs rolled over every fourteen days. [405]
The paper was all that remained. [411]
But the tray was empty, and the printout from that morning was not in the binder. [427]
She searched the logbooks, the stove box, the drawer of dead batteries, her certainty swaying with each empty place until she remembered the paper shortage that week, and how she had fed the printer loose sheets from the only stack at hand. [467]
Tomas's rejected drafts. [470]
His portfolio leaned against the bunk where he had left it. [481]
She untied it with shaking fingers and turned through saxifrage, rock jasmine, alpine poppy, each botanical illustration rendered in his patient ink, each with a failed version behind it. [510]
Near the bottom was a gentian he had sketched from a photograph and abandoned, its petals too pale, and running down its margin in faded purple dots were numbers she had never read. [543]
The figures read STORM BY 1600, CONFIDENCE 71, RIDGE GUSTS 94 KM/H. [555]
Seventy-one percent. [557]
The dissenting processor had not whispered; it had been nearly sure, sure enough to be heard over any majority, and the only thing that had kept its voice from Tomas was her unwillingness to walk six steps. [594]
Her thumb, still damp with fever, brushed the numbers and they began to smudge, violet bleeding into the pale petals as though the flower had finally taken the color he wanted. [625]
She pulled her hand back. [630]
It would be easy to let the ink blur into nothing, to keep the stone where it was. [648]
Behind her the Oracle clicked, three processors arguing quietly about tomorrow, one of them always listening to the far edges where the others would not look. [674]
A system built to survive its own faults, she thought, did so by refusing to throw away the voice that might be right. [697]
She laid the gentian flat under the lamp, photographed the margin before it could fade further, and opened a blank incident report. [719]
The first line she wrote was the one she had swallowed: There was a dissent, and I did not read it. [740]
It weighed seventy-one percent, and it weighed a man, and setting it down on the page did not make it lighter, only visible. [763]
Outside, the aurora swayed over the ridge, green and certain of nothing, and she let herself watch it until the light went out of the sky. [789]
