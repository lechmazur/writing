Maren was the last cosmic janitor of Hollow Ridge, a shooting star tracking station built into the spine of a mountain nobody climbed anymore. [24] The title had once been a joke among the astronomers, who charted meteors while she cleared the grit those meteors left in the collection troughs. [49] Then the funding ended, the astronomers left, and the joke became simply true. [62] No one sent a replacement, and no one sent word to stop. [74] So she stayed, through the hush of an overlooked era, when the cities below had turned their faces from the sky toward screens and wars and weather. [101]

The station did not die in her keeping. [109] It went quiet, and in the quiet it grew. [118] Lichen spread gold across the dish mounts. [125] Swallows nested in the cable ducts. [131] Frost ferns bloomed each dawn on the observation windows and melted by noon, as if the mountain were breathing slowly against the glass. [154] Maren came to think of it as a flourishing stillness, the kind of peace that is not empty but full, like a held breath before a song. [181]

She had always been spiritually curious, the sort of child who asked the priest where prayers went after they left the mouth. [203] Now, alone, she asked the same of stars. [211]

The heart of the station was the old sighting lamp, a brass cage holding a dome of yellow lantern glass. [231] The astronomers had used it before the computers came, lighting it so the trackers on the ridge could fix their bearings in the dark while they followed each streak across the sky. [263] The glass was cracked now, a single seam running from crown to rim. [276] Maren had meant to replace it for years. [284] She had also meant, for years, to tell someone about the tremor in her hands, and about the night she had fallen on the east stairs and lain there until morning, listening to the swallows. [319] There was no one to tell. [325] There had been no one, if she was honest, even before the astronomers left; she had made herself small and useful so that no one would see how frightened she was of being seen. [359]

On the night of the Perseids, she lit the lamp anyway. [370] The flame swelled inside the yellow dome, and where the crack ran, the light split and doubled, throwing two golden paths across the floor. [394] She carried the lamp to the ridge, set it on the old tracker's plinth, and lay back on the cold stone. [415]

The first star fell at midnight. [421] She raised a shaking finger and traced its path, the way the trackers once had, from the point it caught fire to the point it vanished. [447] Then another, and another. [451] By tracing the path of falling stars, one after the next, she began to feel something she had not expected: not wonder, but kinship. [475] Each one was a stone that had drifted cold and unnoticed for ages. [488] Each one became visible only as it fell, only as the air tore it open. [503] Its light was its breaking. [508]

She began to empathize with them, and it frightened her how easily it came, how she could feel the heat of entry in her own chest, the long dark before, the sudden unbearable brightness of being known. [545] None of them hid. [549] None of them apologized for burning. [555]

Near dawn she looked at the lamp beside her. [564] The crack in the yellow glass had not weakened the light; it had given the flame a second path out. [584] On the frosted stone, two beams lay side by side, reaching farther than one unbroken flame ever had. [602] Maren held up her trembling hand in that light and, for the first time, did not tuck it away. [621]

In the morning she walked down to the radio shack, cleared the swallow feathers from the transmitter, and did the thing she had avoided for eleven years. [648] She called the valley. [652]

"This is Hollow Ridge," she said, when a startled voice answered. [663] "I am old, and I am sometimes afraid, and I would like help keeping the station." [679] She paused, listening to the long silence of the wire. [689] "The stars are still falling," she said. [696] "Someone should be here to see them shine." [704]

Outside, the lichen glowed on the dish mounts, the swallows rose from the ducts, and the whole still mountain seemed to lean, very slightly, toward the sound of her voice. [734]
