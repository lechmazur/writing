After the Butterfly Effect, the county no longer touched the ground. [11] Someone at the university had changed one decimal in a cloud-seeding model, the radio said before it went silent, and the change had rolled outward until the fields tore loose and rose, each acre sealed in its own slab of glassy light. [53] Now they drifted, prism terraces turning slow over the drowned valley, throwing rainbows that broke apart whenever a cloud passed. [73] Nell Venn lived on the one that had been her father's last maze. [86] She had been nine, running the unfinished paths, when the Lift tore the field loose and her father's shout fell away beneath her. [109] Three years later the corn still stood in its green corridors, rustling in a hush that seemed cracked down the middle, as though every sound had to squeeze through a seam. [140] The things that had risen with her stood at the maze's heart, a white oak table and two ladder-back chairs, and along its paths stood seven doorframes with no walls. [170] Her father had set the frames there as a joke, gates to nowhere. [183] Nell could walk through five of them. [190] The other two stopped her every time, not with force but with a pressure behind her ribs, the feeling of stepping into a church in muddy boots. [217] She wanted to know why. [222] Some thresholds were only wood, and some were sacred, and nobody was left to explain the difference. [239]

Her father had designed mazes by reading furniture. [247] She remembered him bent over this same table, tracing the grain with a pencil stub. [262] "Wood grows toward light and around its wounds," he had told her. [274] "Follow the lines and you walk. [280] Hit a knot and you wait." [286] So Nell, featherly determined, light enough to be pushed anywhere and still always rising, knelt with her cheek against the tabletop and studied. [309] The grain ran in long pale corridors, and they were the maze, the exact maze around her, turn for turn. [329] Two dark knots sat where the two forbidden doorframes stood. [339] She checked the chairs. [343] Their grain, cut from the same tree, curved around the same knots, as if the wound had been carried into everything the oak became. [367]

Last autumn another terrace had passed close in the fog, and something had thudded into the corn: her grandmother's pilot logbook, its margins crowded with weather code. [394] She had never learned to read it. [401] Now she laid it beside the knots and saw that the last entry was in her father's square hand. [420] Beneath a sketch of the table he had written a single line: WND 270/12 VCSH, HOLD AT KNOTS. [438] Wind from the west at twelve knots, showers in the vicinity. [449] Knots meant speed to a pilot. [455] To her father they meant both. [461]

She began to intuit it slowly, the way you sense a stair in the dark. [476] Everyone knew the terraces could not touch; she had watched two brush once, their light shattering, both slabs dropping soundless into the water. [499] She carried the logbook to the western rim, where the Lift had torn the maze in half and the two forbidden doorframes stood with nothing beyond them but falling light. [529] The knots in the table were wounds, places where a tree had broken and grown around the break instead of through it. [551] These doorframes marked the same kind of place, the seam where the field had split along the table's old injuries. [571] Some thresholds felt sacred, she understood, because crossing them would cost everything, and the only faithful thing was to stand inside them and wait. [595] So she stood in the left frame and held. [604]

The wind turned west before dawn. [610] Rain hazed the valley, and out of it slid a terrace whose corn ran in corridors matching her own. [629] In a doorframe on its eastern rim stood a man with a pencil stub behind his ear. [646] Twelve knots carried him closer: thirty yards, then twenty, then ten. [657] The hush fractured into small sounds, rain on glass, corn shifting, someone's breath catching, hers or his. [674] Neither stepped forward. [677] He lifted his hand, and she lifted hers. [685] The distance between them was not cruelty. [692] It was the thing holding them both in the air. [702] Light poured through the rain into the gap and broke there into a rainbow, a bridge no one could walk, and they stood at either end of it until the wind carried him on. [736]

Then Nell opened the logbook to a clean page and wrote, in careful code, her answer for the next west wind: HOLD. [758]
