The tower had been built to look old, and the spring rain was doing its best to finish the lie. [20] Mara Oyelaran stood at the foot of the overgrown sculpture path with her collar up, her temples giving off the faint green light the review board had called a disgrace. [50] She had injected the tracer herself, a luciferase tag bound to her own olfactory receptors, because no committee would approve a chemist who wanted to watch memory happen in real time. [81] Now she glowed whenever something smelled like the past. [90] This morning she was barely lit. [96]

Four months after first contact, the visitors had asked a single question through the translation lattice: Where do you go when you go back? [120] The ministries had panicked, argued, and then built an answer. [130] In six weeks they raised a stone tower on a hill outside Lisbon, stained it with tea and acid, transplanted moss onto its shoulders, and lined the approach with bronze figures cast from a dozen cultures: a kneeling fisherman, a woman with a suitcase, a child pointing at nothing. [179] They called it the Pilgrimage of Return. [186] Mara called it a stage set, loudly, in a plenary session, and lost her seat on the contact team before lunch. [207]

Her argument had been simple. [212] Homecoming was not a monument; it was chemistry. [220] Geosmin lifting off wet soil, terpenes from a particular pine, the sulfur edge of a grandmother's kitchen, molecules docking in the nose and dragging whole decades up behind them. [249] You could not cast that in bronze. [256] You could not fake it for beings who, by every measurement, tasted the air with a sensitivity that made dogs look blind. [278]

So the night before the visitors walked the path, she had come here uninvited and done the most unscientific thing of her career. [301] She had left her grandfather's fly amber at the base of the tower. [314] It was a honey-colored lump from the Baltic shore, a fly suspended inside for forty million years, rubbed smooth by a man who had carried it through three borders and warmed it in his palm every night until he died in a Porto apartment that never smelled like pine. [363] Warm amber breathes resin. [367] It was the only true thing on the hill, and she wanted to see whether the visitors could tell. [386]

Now the rain had stopped, and the path lay under its canopy of wisteria and unclipped laurel, the flagstones furred with yellow pollen the storm had not reached. [414] The dust held footprints. [418] There were the officials' polished soles, marching dutifully from statue to statue in the prescribed order. [434] And there, overlaying them, were the visitors' prints: long, three-lobed, pressed so lightly they looked like breath on glass. [453]

Mara followed them, crouching, her light rising a little with the green smell of crushed leaves. [469] The visitors had not followed the route. [476] Their tracks wandered off the flagstones, circled the bronze fisherman once without stopping, passed the suitcase woman entirely, and then, from every scattered point along the path, turned and converged. [506] Dozens of lines of them, bending toward the tower's base like iron filings toward a magnet. [522] Toward the amber. [525]

The ground around it was trampled into a soft ring. [535] Someone, something, had turned the stone so its clearest face looked up at the tower, and beside it lay a small disc of their own making, gray and faintly warm, that smelled, when she lifted it, of nothing she had ever encountered. [577] Yet her temples flared so bright she saw her own glow on the wet stones. [592]

She sat back on her heels in the bronze quiet of the garden, the statues dripping, and understood what the ministries never would. [615] The visitors had not been fooled by the tower, and they had not cared. [629] A place could be counterfeit; a carried smell could not. [639] They had found the one object an exile had warmed in his hand for sixty years, and they had answered it the only honest way, with a piece of their own distance. [671]

Her light was not memory, she realized. [678] It was the receptors learning a new shape, the first chemistry of a home that did not exist yet. [697]

She would be censured again for this, she knew. [706] She would write it up anyway, unauthorized, in plain language. [716] Then she slipped the disc into the pocket where the amber had always lived, left the fly to keep watching the tower, and walked back down the path, stepping carefully inside the visitors' prints.
