The ban lifted on the first night of the thaw that never came, and by morning the lantern moths had found the atrium. [23] They clung to the cobalt glass in pale thousands, wings folded, as if the whole building were holding its breath. [43] Ilse Varro stood at the doors with frost in her eyelashes and did not go in. [59] Since girlhood she had been a cloud-shadow poet, which was the only kind the Council permitted. [75] She composed on the frozen lake, reading the gray shapes that slid across the ice and speaking lines aloud to no one, because a shadow leaves no record and a spoken verse no evidence. [109] Anything more lasting belonged to the Hymn, and the Hymn belonged to the atrium, carved into the humming quartz colonnade that ringed its floor. [133] Only the Keepers could enter, and only to recite it word for word; to paraphrase even a line had been a crime punishable by exile onto the ice. [161] Now the ban was gone, voted away by a Council grown too old to care, and Ilse found that freedom felt a great deal like cold. [187]

"You may as well come in," said a voice from within. [198] "The moths already have." [202]

Brother Aurel was the last Keeper, stooped inside his gray robe, a lantern trembling in his hand. [219] She stepped over the threshold, and the columns greeted her with a low, sustained note, like a finger circling the rim of a glass. [243] The sound entered her teeth. [248] She had imagined this room her whole life, and she had imagined it wrong: it was lovelier, and it was ruined. [269] Frost had crept through a cracked pane and feathered across the quartz, and where it had thawed and refrozen, season after season, the carved letters had softened into ripples. [298] Whole stanzas were gone. [302]

"I cannot read the ninth column anymore," Aurel said. [311] "My teacher could. [314] I only remember the rhythm, not the words, and the Hymn permits no rhythm without its words." [331]

"So it dies with you." [336]

"So it dies exactly," he said, "which is how it lived." [347]

Ilse walked the colonnade slowly, touching nothing. [354] She had never cared much for doctrine; she had cared for the shape of things, for the way a sentence could lean like a heron or break like lake ice, and it offended her more than any law that something this beautiful should be allowed to go silent because no one dared say it differently. [409]

At the ninth column she stopped. [415] A cloud crossed the sun outside, and its shadow poured through the cobalt glass and over the quartz, and for a moment the line between the room and the lake seemed to dissolve, dim blue into dim blue, the ice and the floor one surface. [460] It was the light she worked in. [467] She read the ruined letters the way she read shadows, not for what they had been but for what they were becoming. [489] A fragment: the cold that keeps. [495] A curve that might once have been "mercy" or "merely." [505]

"Say the rhythm," she told Aurel. [511]

He hesitated, then hummed it, cracked and faint, and the column answered in the same pitch. [527] Ilse closed her eyes and spoke into the hum. [536]

"What winter holds, it holds in trust; what we cannot keep, the ice keeps for us, and gives back when we have learned to ask." [561]

It was not the Hymn. [566] It was a paraphrase of a ghost, made in a cloud's shadow, and the moment it left her mouth the colonnade changed its note. [590] All twelve columns rose together into a chord so full that the lantern moths lifted from the glass at once, a soft storm of wings circling Aurel's light. [618]

The old man's lips were moving. [624] "They never did that for the words," he whispered. [633] "Not once in sixty years." [638]

Something moved through Ilse then that was not quite courage. [648] It was a tremor of certainty, small as a moth landing, that the quartz had been tuned all along to meaning and not to syllables, and that the Keepers had guarded the husk while the seed waited for someone willing to replant it. [691]

Beyond the last column, a silver line was inlaid in the floor, marking where the sacred ended and the lake began; no Keeper had ever crossed it with the Hymn in his mouth. [724] Ilse strode toward it, still speaking, making the next stanza from frost and rhythm and the drift of cloud. [743] She did not pause at the silver. [750]

Behind her the colonnade kept humming, and after a long moment she heard Aurel's cracked voice join hers, carrying the Hymn out onto the ice where, at last, it could be heard. [782]
