The phoenix rose at midnight, as it does once a century, and took the last of the ash with it into the sky. [23] Maren watched it go from the edge of the orchard, where the pear trees bloomed white under a moon that had not set in three hundred years. [50] Nothing in the orchard ever fruited; the blossoms simply opened and opened, the way a held breath refuses to end. [70] Everyone in the valley knew what the phoenix's rising meant. [80] For one night afterward, the dead could be reached, if you knew their language. [94] Maren knew every language that did not use words. [103] She had calmed a bull elk with a hum, coaxed an owl down from a burning chapel, and talked a river otter out of grief. [128] Villagers called her the beast whisperer and paid her in eggs and wool. [141] None of them had ever seen her whisper to Hollow, because Hollow had never needed it. [157] The gray wolf had simply walked beside her for eleven years, a shoulder at her hip, until the winter he lay down under the oldest pear tree and did not get up. [189]

Now she knelt at that same tree and unwrapped the spices she had spent a year's wages on. [207] Black cardamom smoked over mountain fires, long pepper from islands that appeared on no map, and a single thread of blue saffron the merchant swore had grown in a dead man's footprint. [239] Wolves remembered by scent long after they forgot faces, and she meant to write Hollow a letter in the only alphabet he had ever read. [264] The merchant had also pressed a cookie into her palm, folded like a small ear, and laughed when she frowned at it. [286] She cracked it open now for luck. [293] The slip of paper inside read: The one you call cannot cross. [305] You must arrive in a shape he can follow. [314]

Maren tucked the fortune into her sleeve and struck the flint anyway. [326] The spices caught, and the smoke rose thick and golden, curling through the blossoms. [340] She did not chant; she simply breathed out his name the way she used to at dusk, low in her chest. [361] At the end of the smoke, she began to conjure him: a gray shape, pale as frost on glass, standing where the trees gave way to stars. [388] Hollow lifted his head and scented her. [395] Then he whined, took one step, and stopped, as if the air between them were a river he could not ford. [416] She called again, and again he tried, and again the distance held. [428]

Overhead the phoenix was a dwindling ember, and the stars leaned so close she could hear them tick, patient as old clocks. [450] The night would not wait for her to be brave. [460] She understood the fortune, and she hated it. [468] Her whole life had been a careful standing-between, a woman at the fence line translating one world to another. [487] To cross over would mean giving up the fence, the words, the self she had built to be useful. [506] Her heart was hammering, yet her hands, paradoxically steady, did what they had always done in a crisis: they reached for the smoke and gathered it close. [533]

She breathed the spices in instead of out. [541] Cardamom went down into her bones and made them long. [551] Pepper burned along her spine until it bristled. [559] The blue saffron found her tongue and took her words one by one, gently, like a mother taking scissors from a child. [581] She fought it for a moment, clutching at his name as a word, and then she let the word go and kept the name as a smell: rain on fur, crushed pear, woodsmoke. [614] She dropped to four feet. [619]

The distance was nothing now. [624] Hollow came through the blossoms like a wave breaking, and they ran, two gray shapes beneath a moon that seemed to lower itself just to watch. [650] They ran the whole orchard, and the rows of trees became rows of stars, and the stars were only more trees. [671] At the edge of the sky he stopped and pressed his cold nose to her throat, and she knew without language that he was asking her to go back. [700] Then he stepped into her the way a scent steps into a room, and stayed. [715]

Maren woke under the oldest pear tree at what should have been dawn, human again, mostly. [731] Gray fur ran along the insides of her wrists, and her eyes caught the moonlight and threw it back. [750] The fortune was still in her sleeve. [757] She walked home with a shoulder she could not see pressing warm against her hip, and when the villagers' dogs came to greet her, they did not wait for her to whisper. [789] They already knew her. [793]
