At the top of its arc, the pendulum bob hung still, and Oren Vask held his breath with it. [19] It was the pause he had measured ten thousand times, the half-heartbeat when brass forgets which way it is falling, and it was the only moment he ever let himself remember. [50] Below the louvered window, the tide was drawing off the obsidian ridge, and the black glass emerged in long wet blades that caught the moon and burned white along their edges. [81] Soon the gatherers would go down with their rakes to harvest the shards the sea had loosened. [98] Soon the tower would ring them back before the water returned. [109]

That was the bargain of Saltmarrow: the sea gave glass, the clock kept time, and the town trusted the clock. [129] Oren was its winder, the most precise man on the ridge, who oiled each pivot by the drop and logged the drift of the escapement to the quarter second. [158] He had not always been so careful. [165]

Eleven years ago, the ridge master had pressed three silver coins into his hand and asked for a little more tide. [186] Just four minutes, he said, for the crews who were paid by the basket. [200] Oren had climbed up here and turned the rating nut beneath the bob one full revolution, lowering it the width of a fingernail. [223] The clock slowed. [226] The bell rang late. [230] On the fourth evening, Teodor Hale was still raking the far shelf when the water came back over the ridge like a closing mouth. [254]

They found only his rake, its handle snapped against the glass. [265]

Oren had asked Teodor's widow, Liss, if he could keep it. [276] He had not told the council about the coins, because confession all at once would have been a kind of theft too, a single loud payment that bought him silence afterward. [307] Instead he set the nut right, returned the silver to the tide, and began to carve. [323] He was not trying to feel better; he was trying to restore a balance that had never been his alone to tip. [345]

Every week he cut one tiny inscription into the broken handle with a jeweler's burin, one stroke during each pause of the pendulum's swing, when the tick fell silent and his hand could not hide behind the noise. [383] The first was the date. [388] The second was the number of turns. [395] Then came the names of every gatherer who had worked those four stolen minutes, the weight of the coins, the ridge master's words, his own. [420] He wrote each thing only when he could state it without softening it. [433] Some weeks he sat with the handle and wrote nothing, because the truth he had in mind was still wearing an excuse. [455] The letters crept down the ash wood in a spiral no wider than a grain of rice, and each one felt like drawing a splinter out of his own palm. [485]

Now the spiral had reached the break. [492]

There was room for one line more, and he had known for a month what it must be. [510] It was the thing he had avoided longest: I did not do it for the crews. [526] I did it because I wanted to be the man who could give the town more than the sea allowed. [546]

He heard feet on the stair. [552]

Liss emerged from the dark of the stairwell, gray now, carrying her own rake over her shoulder the way she always did on low-tide nights. [577] Watching her, he felt the old reverie rise again, searing and bright: Teodor laughing at the ridge edge, the coins, the nut turning under his thumb, all of it at once. [608]

"Read it," he said, and gave her the handle. [617]

She turned it slowly under the lantern, following the spiral with a cracked fingernail. [631] The tide went on retreating; the ridge glittered like a field of knives. [644]

When she reached the end, her mouth tightened. [652] "You left the last line empty." [658]

"I wanted you to see it before I cut it." [668]

She held out her palm for the burin. [676] She carved, badly and with great care, a single small mark beside the break: a notch, the kind gatherers cut on a rake to count a full basket. [704]

"One," she said. [707] "Not all." [709] "One." [710]

It was not forgiveness. [714] It was the first increment, and he understood the rest would be counted the same way, basket by basket, if he earned them. [737]

"Tell the council tomorrow," she said. [743] "The whole spiral." [746] "Then the clock belongs to all of us again." [755]

He nodded. [757] Then he set the burin to the last clean inch of ash and watched the bob climb, slow, and hang. [777] In that pause, he cut the first letter of the truth. [788]
