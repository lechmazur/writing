Oda Marsh kept her workshop roofless on purpose, because a person who refuses to hold a rifle should at least be willing to hold the sky. [26] Tonight the sky held back, starry and patient, over benches crowded with gutted radios, a cello missing its neck, three pinned moths, and a ledger titled VARIETIES OF SILENCE in her looping hand. [59] Below the hill, the town waited for the armistice fireworks. [69] Every chimney, every dog, every drunk uncle had gone quiet at once, and Oda, lamp shuttered so the shadows could crouch where they liked, reached for her pen. [97]

Entry two hundred and six, she wrote. [104] The silence of a crowd holding a match: bright at the edges, hollow in the middle, like a bell that has decided not to ring. [129]

She had always named things by how they felt rather than what they were, which made her a poor engineer and a remarkable listener. [153]

Beside the ledger lay her brother's diary. [160] Tomas had come home from the front in spring, thinner, polite as a stranger, and had not said ten words to her since. [183] She had catalogued that silence too, entry one hundred and twelve, under the heading Contempt, because she was certain he despised her for staying behind with her pamphlets and her refusal. [214] This morning he had left the diary on her doorstep with a note: "If you can open it, you may." [234] Then he had gone down to the square to help the fireworks crew, because the mayor wanted a veteran's hand on the fuse. [257]

The clasp was no ordinary lock. [263] It was a resonance latch, the kind trench clockmakers sold to soldiers who feared their letters being read: a brass reed sealed inside, tuned to a single pitch, and a pin that dropped only when the reed sang. [301] No key, no numbers, only the right note. [309]

Oda set the diary on the cello's belly, which would carry any tremor, and wheeled over her oscillator. [327] She had built it from a bomber's salvaged radio set, a small private joke: a machine made for finding targets, repurposed for finding tones. [351]

She turned the dial slowly. [356] Low frequencies first, a growl like a cart on cobbles. [366] Nothing. [367] Higher, a hum like bees behind plaster. [374] The clasp shivered and went still. [380] She pressed her cheek to the cello and closed her eyes, the way she did when cataloguing, and let the tone climb through her teeth. [405] There, a thin, nervous note, the pitch of a kettle deciding whether to scream. [419] The reed answered it, sweet and sudden, and the pin fell with a click as small as a swallowed word. [439]

The diary fell open, and by starlight she began to recover the brother she had filed away. [456]

The pages were not letters. [461] They were lists. [464]

The silence after the barrage, which is not peace but a held breath with teeth in it. [481] The silence of a man counting his friends and stopping early. [492] The silence at my sister's door, when I want to tell her she was right and cannot find a sound small enough to carry it. [517]

Oda put a hand over her mouth. [524] Her ledger and his diary lay side by side, two catalogues of the same weather, and she understood at last that she had read his quiet in her own language and gotten the translation wrong. [559] It had never been contempt. [564] It was a man standing at the edge of noise, afraid of what came next. [579] And in a minute, the town would ask him to light the loudest thing it owned. [595]

She switched the old bomber set from tone to voice, spun its dial to the crew's field band, and keyed the microphone. [617] Static bloomed like breath on glass. [623]

"Tomas," she said, and her voice came out grand and shaking, an actress's voice, because sincerity that large needs a stage. [644] "I opened it." [647] "I was wrong about your silence." [653] "You do not have to be brave for them tonight." [663] "Let someone else hold the match, and come up the hill, and we will listen to it together from far away." [684]

For a long moment there was only the hiss of the band. [696] Then a click, soft as the diary's pin, and his voice, rough with disuse: "Coming." [711]

Footsteps sounded on the hill path, and as the first rocket climbed, Oda opened her ledger to entry two hundred and seven. [733] The silence between two people who have finally heard each other: not empty at all, but full, like a room just before the music begins. [758]

Then the sky broke open in gold, and she let it. [769]
