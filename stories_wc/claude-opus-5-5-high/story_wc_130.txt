After the orchard grows silent beneath the moon, Mara Venn goes down to the stepping stone. [16] The silence is exact. [20] For hours after dusk the stalactites keep dripping onto the roots of the pale cavern apples, a thousand small ticks like clocks disagreeing, and then the cold settles, the water thickens, and the dripping stops all at once. [58] Moonlight falls through the shaft in the ceiling, and the orchard holds its breath. [72]

The villagers above know Mara as the woman who pulls ribbons from children's ears at harvest fairs. [89] They do not know about her real conjuring, the kind she practices only here, alone, with a slate and a stub of chalk. [112] She is trying to solve her brother. [119]

Tobin laid the mosaic stepping stone himself, the spring before he walked into the deep passage behind the last row of trees and did not walk out. [146] No body was found. [150] No note, no torn coat, no answer. [157] The searchers came back with muddy boots and apologetic faces, and after a season they stopped using his name in the present tense. [180] Mara could not decide which tense to use, so she stopped using it at all. [195]

What she has instead is arithmetic. [201] Grief, she reasoned, ought to be a subtraction: a person, minus a person, equals a clean zero you can mourn. [221] But Tobin is not subtracted. [226] He is a variable left open, and every sum she writes with him in it refuses to close. [244] If she could only understand the mathematics of emotion, she thinks, she would know how much hope is correct, and how much is foolish, and she could finally stop carrying the wrong amount. [277]

So she reads the stone. [282] It sits beneath the oldest stalactite, a spiral of blue and ochre tiles, and the falling water has been chipping it for two years. [306] Each night she counts the new chips and charts them on her slate, as though the damage were a language, as though the cave were doing sums on her behalf. [336]

Tonight she brings the glass eyes. [342] They were the last thing Tobin ever ordered for his taxidermy bench, a matched pair of amber irises meant for a barn owl he never finished. [368] The parcel arrived a week after he vanished. [376] Mara has kept them in her pocket ever since, two cold beads that click together when she walks, like a question asked twice. [399]

Her idea is small and a little absurd, which is how most of her good ideas begin. [416] She sets one eye in the deepest chip at the spiral's center and one in the chip nearest the edge, then tilts her lantern until the moonlight and the flame pass through both lenses together. [451] Two amber discs swell across the tiles and overlap. [460] In the lit space where they meet, the chips are no longer random. [473] She sees that the water has not been breaking the stone so much as carving it along the grout, following the seams Tobin laid, deepening the spiral he designed. [502] Loss has not erased his pattern. [508] It has worn him in. [513]

Mara sits back on her heels. [519] For two years she has been solving for an answer, alive or dead, one or zero, and the stone has been quietly showing her another kind of equation, one that never finishes and still holds its shape. [556] Grief and hope are not opposite terms that cancel. [565] They are two edges cut to fit. [572]

She has always been better with her hands than with her conclusions, so she answers with her hands. [590] From her satchel she takes the spare tiles she once meant to use for patching, and with a file and patience she shapes their edges into little flared tongues, the way Tobin taught her to join the drawers of his bench. [631] She does not fill the chips. [637] She dovetails the new pieces into their broken edges, so the damage becomes the joint, so the old spiral and the new one lock and neither can be pulled away without the other. [670]

Hours pass. [672] Near dawn the first drip returns, then another, and the orchard begins its ticking again. [687] Mara leaves the two amber eyes set in the stone, facing the deep passage. [701] Someone should keep watch, she decides, and it need not always be her. [714]

On her slate she erases the old unfinished sums and writes a single line in their place. [731] Tobin is. [733]

She leaves the rest of the sentence open, the way the passage is open, and climbs toward morning. [751]
