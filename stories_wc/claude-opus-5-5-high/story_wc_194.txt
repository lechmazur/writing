In the first yawn of evening, when the sun slid low enough to turn the dust into drifting coins, Ada Varne unlocked the reading room for the last time. [29] Everything inside had gone the color of old tea. [38] Four generations of Varnes had printed the books that lined these shelves, and every spine had faded to the same sepia, as if the room itself had been photographed long ago and forgotten in a drawer. [74] Tomorrow she would sign the papers that dissolved Varne and Sons, Publishers, and the newspapers would call her what her cousins already did: the dynasty ender. [100] She did not mind the name. [106] She minded not knowing why her grandmother, on her deathbed, had whispered that the firm was built on a borrowed voice. [127]

Tobiah was already there, dusting nothing. [133] The old caretaker had not spoken in the forty years Ada had known him, yet he was the most eloquently silent man she had ever met; his eyebrows alone could deliver a eulogy. [166] Now he stood beside the gramophone in the corner, its brass horn blooming like a lily, and he held out his palm. [188] On it lay a gramophone crank handle, black with age, its wooden grip worn pale by some long-dead thumb. [207]

Ada stared. [209] The gramophone had been crankless since before she was born; her father claimed the handle had been lost in the war. [230] "Where did you find it?" [235]

Tobiah tapped his own chest, then pointed at the single record resting on the turntable, its paper label blank. [254] Kept it, he meant. [258] Kept it until someone was ready to end things properly. [268] Between them there had always been a bridge of intuition, built plank by plank over decades of shared silences, and Ada crossed it now without hesitation. [294]

She fitted the handle into its socket and wound. [303]

The record did not play music. [309] It played a single held note, a woman's voice, low and unwavering, so pure it seemed less sung than poured. [329] The note filled the long narrow room, struck the far wall, and folded back upon itself. [345] Ada felt it thicken in some places and vanish in others, as though the air had been pleated. [363] She had studied physics before the family called her home, and she knew what this was: standing waves, the sound meeting its own reflection and holding still. [390] The room was tuned to that voice. [397] Someone had built it that way. [403]

Dust lifted from the reading tables and drifted, grain by golden grain, into the quiet places where the wave rested, drawing pale lines across the floorboards. [429] The lamps swayed on their chains, throwing golden shadows over the lines, and Ada saw that every one of them pointed to a single oak panel beneath the portrait of her great-grandfather, Elias Varne. [463] Where the voice was loudest, the panel hummed. [471]

Ada pressed it, and it swung inward on a hinge no one had oiled in a century. [488] Inside lay a manuscript tied with ribbon. [495] She knew the title at once: The Lantern Year, the novel that had made Elias rich and founded everything. [514] But the handwriting was not his, and the name on the first page was Miriam Hale. [530]

Tobiah Hale made a small sound, the first she had ever heard from him, and touched the ink as if it were a face. [554]

The record wound down, and the note sagged into silence. [564] Ada sat on the floor among the lines of dust and let herself muse on what she was holding. [583] Miriam had written the book; Elias had printed it under his own name and paid her, perhaps, in silence. [602] Yet he had kept a recording of her singing and built a room to remember her voice, a confession too cowardly to speak and too guilty to destroy. [630] The borrowed voice. [633] Her grandmother had known, and so, somehow, had Tobiah's family, keeping the crank handle like a key to a door they could not open themselves. [658]

She understood then that ending a dynasty was not the same as closing its doors. [673] Tomorrow she would still sign the papers, but first she would call the printers one last time and have them set a new title page. [698]

She handed the manuscript to Tobiah, who held it against his chest. [710] He did not need to say anything. [717]

Outside, evening finished its yawn and settled into night, and the reading room, for the first time in a hundred years, held nothing hidden at all. [743]
