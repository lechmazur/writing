The rain had been beading on the skylight since the coin collapsed, as if the sky itself were counting losses in small glass drops. [24] Ines Varga worked beneath it in the loft her grandmother had left her, painting cards in onyx ink that dried the color of wet slate. [49] Since the crash, her clients had changed. [56] They were no longer bored heiresses asking about lovers but former traders in hooded sweatshirts, their phones face-down on her table like dead birds. [80] Ines wrote down every question they asked. [87] She was not selling comfort so much as collecting it, trying to map how a whole generation grieved when its future turned out to be arithmetic. [113] The pattern would not hold still, and she was steadily curious about why. [126]

Her grandmother, Magda, had kept ledgers too. [133] They sat in a cedar box with the old deck and a cloth measuring tape, yellowed, sixty inches long, its numbers overwritten with tiny notches in three different inks. [162] Magda had never explained it. [167] When Ines was small, she had watched her grandmother unroll the tape across the table before every reading and lay cards along it, never looking at the numbers, only at the marks. [199] The ledgers named the years: 1987, 2000, 2008. [207] Every entry was dated after a crash. [214]

So that winter Ines began to reverse engineer her inheritance. [224] She took a reading from Magda's 1987 ledger and laid it out exactly as recorded, card by card along the tape. [245] At each notch the entry split: one card went left, one right, and each of those split again at the next mark. [267] Magda had not read futures in a line. [275] She had read them by multiplying paths, two, four, eight, until the table was a fan of possible lives. [294]

Ines rebuilt the 2000 reading next, then 2008, then a dozen more. [306] Rain ticked overhead while the fans spread across the floorboards. [316] And she saw it: however the paths branched, however desperate the first question, they all narrowed back toward the forty-first inch, where a small star had been inked in black. [346] The card meant to sit there was missing from the deck. [357] In every ledger the space was left blank. [365]

She was still kneeling among the cards when Dario arrived for his appointment, shaking water from his coat. [383] He had come twice before, both times asking whether the market would recover. [396] Tonight he did not take out his phone. [404] He looked at the fans of cards, at the tape running through them like a spine, and sat down on the floor beside her. [428]

"I need to confide something," he said. [435] "I don't care about the money anymore." [442] He rubbed his thumb along the tape's frayed edge. [451] "My daughter is six. [455] She asked me what I'm going to leave her, and I had nothing to say, because I used to think it was a wallet." [479]

Ines looked at the star at forty-one inches. [487] She opened her own ledger, the one she had filled since the crash, and ran her finger down the months of questions. [509] Will it come back. [513] Who did this to us. [518] What do I build now. [523] And lately, again and again, what do I hand down. [533] Magda's ledgers followed the same slope, crash after crash, forty-one inches from panic to inheritance. [548] The blank was not an absence. [554] It was the question every era arrived at and had to answer in its own hand. [570]

So Ines confided back. [574] She told him about the cedar box, the three inks, the grandmother who measured grief in inches and never said why. [595] "I think she wanted me to find it myself," she said. [606] "Maybe the finding is what gets handed down." [614]

She took a blank card and dipped her brush in the onyx ink. [627] She did not know what to paint, so she asked him to describe his daughter, and as he talked she drew a small figure holding one end of a measuring tape, the other end trailing off the card, unfinished, toward whoever came next. [670] She laid it at the forty-first inch. [677] Every path on the floor seemed to lean toward it. [687]

When Dario left, carrying the question he had come with as if it weighed less, Ines added a fourth notch to the tape in her own black ink, and then, still curious, she wondered who would someday unroll it and ask what the marks meant. [732] Above her, the rain kept beading on the glass, patient as a family secret. [746]
