The gallery had been built to lie. [7] Its walls leaned inward as they ran, its floorboards narrowed, and its frames shrank from life-size to thumbnail until the whole corridor seemed to pour into a single dot of dark. [38] Visitors called it the Vanishing Point, and most believed it ended there. [50]

Ines knew better, because Aurel Vask had told her where the painted dot concealed a door, and because he had told her almost nothing else. [75] She knelt at the far end with her mortar, grinding willow charcoal together with a spoonful of his wife's ashes. [95] This was her trade: she drew the dead in a medium made partly of them, then scattered what remained wherever the family asked. [118]

Aurel had asked for something harder. [124] Before leaving, he had set Mira's old rocking chair swaying beside the hidden door. [138] "In our village we say the dead stay until the chair stops," he said. [152] "Draw her before she goes." [157] "Draw the face she wore the night the soldiers walked past." [168]

That was all: a night, soldiers, a face. [176] He had not said what the face held, only that he had never seen it again in forty years, and that he wanted it fixed where it could not fade. [206]

Ines did not invent faces. [211] She wove truth carefully, thread by verified thread, and where she had no thread she left the paper bare. [230] So she went looking for thread. [236]

Behind the painted dot lay a room no larger than a wardrobe, with a cot, a water jug, and on a nail, a mask. [260] It had been pieced from shattered porcelain, blue-rimmed plates and a cup's handle curving over one brow, the seams filled with plaster gone yellow. [284] Ines recognized the pattern from the dish Aurel still kept on his sill, the last whole one. [301] The soldiers must have smashed the rest searching the house. [311] They had stood in the gallery, looked down its shrinking length, seen only a wall, and gone. [328] The illusion had held. [332]

In the gallery the chair creaked, its arcs already shorter. [342]

She turned the mask over. [347] On the inner faces of the shards, in a small slanted hand, Mira had written a sentence that broke across the fractures, so that no single piece said it whole. [377] Ines read it the way she read every family's grief, through fragments, fitting edge to edge. [393]

"The captain saw the door." [398] "I gave him my mother's rings." [404] "I smiled so Aurel would believe the wall had saved us." [415]

Ines sat back on her heels. [421] So the safety had been a mirage, painted as surely as the vanishing point, and Mira had spent forty years inside it beside him, keeping it lit. [448] The face Aurel remembered was not relief. [455] It was a gift made in terror, and it had lasted only as long as the soldiers' boots on the stairs. [476] She understood then what she was meant to crystallize, and why he had never seen it again: a person can only spend that kind of courage once. [503]

She returned to the paper and worked fast. [511] The chair's rocking had shrunk to a tremor. [519] She drew the smile first, soft at the corners, then the eyes, which she made steady and far too wide, the eyes of a woman listening for footsteps. [547] Both were true. [550] She would not choose between them. [556] With her fingertip she powdered the ash-charcoal across the cheeks in a fine gray veil, blurring the boundary between the two expressions so that a viewer standing close saw fear and a viewer at the gallery's mouth saw only tenderness. [596] Distance, here, had always decided what was believed. [604]

The chair gave one last small nod and was still. [614] Ines laid down her hand. [619] The portrait looked back at her with Mira's double face, and for a moment the gallery seemed to lengthen, as if the vanishing point had drawn a breath. [647]

When Aurel came back she hung it at the far end, where the frames were smallest, and let him walk toward it. [669] She did not show him the mask. [676] She had left it on the cot behind the painted door, face down, for whenever he was ready to read it. [697] He stopped halfway, where the lie of the walls was most persuasive, and wept at the smile. [714] Then he took one step closer, and another, and Ines watched his face change as the eyes came into focus. [734]

Outside, she scattered the last of Mira's ashes into the wind, where nothing could be hidden and nothing needed to be. [755]
