Nell Arbry repaired broken clocks, and she liked them best when she could say exactly why they had stopped. [19] Her workshop on Tanner Lane had burned in March. [28] When the insurance man left, she went back with a sieve and a bucket and worked through the ashes the way she worked through a stuck movement, one layer at a time. [60] Beneath the ashes, under a floorboard her grandfather had nailed down fifty years earlier, she found a brass disc wrapped in oilcloth. [82] It was a compass rose, thirty-two points engraved by hand, and scratched along the rim were the words ADM. HESKETH, HMS CORVUS. [104]

Every child in Garrow knew the story of the admiral's lost compass rose. [117] Hesketh had come home from forty years at sea, built a glasshouse on the hill, and walked into it one autumn evening without walking out. [142] The rose from his ship's binnacle had vanished with him. [152]

Nell did not believe in stories. [158] She believed in schedules, in parts lists, in the calm of knowing what came next, and the fire had taken every one of those things along with her tools. [187] Her sister said she should learn to live with not knowing. [198] Nell said she would make a plan for that. [207] She meant it more than she let on; she wanted a way of planning that could make room for not knowing. [228]

Working from her kitchen table in the months after, she took in whatever clocks people brought her. [245] The first had stopped at 4:17. [251] So had the second, a mantel clock from the baker. [261] By the ninth she had stopped calling it chance, because nine clocks from nine houses do not agree on anything unless something makes them. [285]

She marked each house on a town map. [293] They fell along a line so straight it looked ruled, running east from Tanner Lane. [308] When she centered the compass rose on the ashes of her shop, the line passed beneath the one point Hesketh had marked with an engraved star, and ended at his glasshouse. [339] She was uncertainly certain, the way she sometimes was about a fault she could hear but not yet see. [358]

She went up the hill the next afternoon with a lamp, a coil of rope, a written list, and her grandfather's pocket watch, which ran eleven minutes slow and which she had never been able to fix. [395] The glasshouse was cracked and green with moss, and dead leaves lay knee-deep between rusted benches. [411] Her list ended there, because she did not know what she was looking for. [425] So she waited. [428]

At seventeen minutes past four the floor trembled, and the leaves rose. [440] They swirled in a slow column near the far wall, lifted by air pushing up from somewhere below, and Nell waded through the swirling leaves until her boot struck an iron grate. [472] Beneath it, iron rungs led down into a roar. [481]

The old tin mines ran under Garrow, everyone knew that, but no one had said there was a river in them. [502] At the bottom of the shaft a waterfall dropped out of the dark into a black pool, and she understood at once. [524] Each afternoon something upstream sent a surge through, and the shock ran along the old seam, stopping any clock with a worn escapement. [547]

Behind the falling water, on a ledge, sat a sea chest. [558] Then the spray reached her lamp and it went out. [568]

She had no plan for this. [574] For a long moment she stood in the roaring dark with her heart going like a runaway spring, and then, because nothing else was on offer, she began to improvise. [604] She tied the rope to the ladder and paid it out behind her. [617] She followed the cold edge of the spray with one hand, and when the ledge narrowed she felt ahead with her grandfather's watch on its chain, listening to it tap stone. [648]

The chest was not locked. [653] By touch she found a bundle of papers and a flint lighter, which sparked on the fourth try. [671] The papers were Hesketh's charts of the river, every route crossed out and redrawn, and on the last sheet he had written: Every course I set was wrong by morning. [701] The trick was never the plan; it was knowing where you started. [713]

Nell followed the rope back to the ladder and climbed toward the grey square of daylight, the papers buttoned inside her coat. [735]

That winter she rebuilt the shop on Tanner Lane and wrote her plans in pencil, so they could change. [754] She set the compass rose into the new floor with its star facing the hill. [769] Her grandfather's watch still ran eleven minutes slow, and she let it. [781]
