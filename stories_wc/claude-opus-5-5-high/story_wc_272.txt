<story>
Wren Hollis had not finished a face in eleven months. [10] Every portrait she began stopped at the same place, an eye she could not hold, a mouth that slid off the canvas like water off glass, and her sitters went home looking faintly unfinished themselves. [43] The town had started doing it too. [50] Half a bakery stood on Quay Street, its missing half not rubble but simply absent, and children walked through the gap as if it had always been there. [78]

So when the letter came saying her brother's lighthouse must be cleared, she took the ferry out to Gull Rock with her brushes and no hope at all. [106]

Tomas had kept the light for nine years, though the light itself had been dark for longer. [123] What he kept, he said, was the library. [131] It spiraled down beneath the lamp room, shelf after curved shelf following the tower's throat into the rock, a forgotten collection of logbooks, charts, and keepers' diaries no one had catalogued since the war. [165]

She found the broken tarot card box on his desk. [175] It was walnut, split along one seam as though something inside had pushed outward. [189] Seventy-eight cards had spilled across the blotter, each one hand-painted by Tomas, and each one bound to the others by fine silk threads that ran from their corners like rigging. [219] The threads had knotted into a gray snarl the size of a fist. [232]

His last diary lay open beside it. [239] The deck holds the coast together, he had written. [248] Every night I lay it out and the pieces stay where they belong. [261] What is a lighthouse, after all, but a promise that things will be where you left them? [278]

"A tower with a lamp," Wren said aloud. [286] "That's all it is." [290] She had always answered rhetorical questions; it was the only argument she ever won against him. [306]

But the snarl explained the bakery, and the faces. [315] The box had broken the night Tomas went down the stairs and did not come back up, and since then the world had been coming apart at its seams. [344]

At dusk the walls began to close in. [352] It happened slowly, the plaster breathing inward, shelves leaning until their spines touched her shoulders. [367] A diary slid free and fell open to a line in Tomas's hand: When the walls close in, the reader has one night. [390]

Wren sat down and began untangling threads. [397]

It was portrait work, she realized, patient and exact. [406] Each thread had a direction, a tension, a face it wanted to lead back to. [421] She teased loose The Star, The Hermit, the Nine of Cups. [432] Tomas had painted himself into the deck as The Magician, and the Magician was gone; every thread tied to him pulled toward an empty place. [457]

She would have to redesign it. [463]

She scraped the Magician clean with her palette knife and held the blank card under the lantern. [480] Who could paint the dead honestly? [486] "I can," she answered herself, though her hand shook. [495] But she could not paint him from memory. [503] Memory was what had failed her all year, giving her eyes that slid and mouths that ran. [520]

One thread still led away from the desk and down the stairwell, into the lowest shelves where the tower met the sea. [542] The walls pressed nearer. [546] What waited below was obvious; why pretend otherwise? [554] "The Death card," she said, "and him." [561] She had spent a year refusing that darkness, and it had cost her every face she tried to paint. [580]

She left the lantern on the desk and descended. [589]

The dark down there was thick and wet, lit only by the sea's own phosphorescence seeping through cracks in the stone, a cold green glow that made the spines of drowned books shine like scales. [624] The thread ran taut to the bottom step, where the Death card lay face up in a shallow pool. [643] Beside it, his reading glasses. [648] The tide had taken the rest. [654]

She sat on the step and let herself look at the absence properly, the way she used to look at a sitter before the first mark. [678] It hurt precisely, and it was the first true thing she had seen in months. [693]

Then she climbed back up with the card and painted. [703]

She did not paint Tomas's face. [709] She painted the lamp room from the outside, black against a green sea, one window lit by someone who had just left it. [732] She called it The Keeper and tied every Magician thread to its corners, loosening each until the tension ran even. [752]

When she laid the new spread, the shelves eased back with a long wooden sigh. [767]

By morning, the ferryman reported Quay Street's bakery whole again. [777] Wren glued the walnut box along its seam and began a portrait of herself, which she finished. [794]
