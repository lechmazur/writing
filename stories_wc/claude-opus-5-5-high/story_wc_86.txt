The parapet of Harrow Keep belonged to no one, and on nights like this it seemed to know it. [19] Wind came off the ridge in long wet shoves, and mist poured over the broken merlons like milk over a lip. [40] Sabine Oreq stood at the eastern stair with her collar up, listening to rope creak behind her. [57]

By the old treaty, the wall's width was neutral territory, forty paces of stone that neither the valley nor the highland could claim. [80] It was the only place the two peoples could hand back their captured without admitting the other existed. [98] Sabine had negotiated nine such exchanges. [104] Tonight she had four highland prisoners roped to four valley guards, and somewhere in the white across the wall, Teodor Vask had three of hers. [129]

Last spring there had been four of hers. [137]

The last exchange had been at the new moon, chosen for secrecy. [149] In that blackness a highland sentry had seen movement where none should be and fired, and Corin Hale, nineteen, had died two steps from home. [174] Teodor's apology had come a week later, three pages in a cramped, honest hand. [188] Sabine had read it eleven times and answered none of it. [199] Forgiveness, she had found, was not a door but a distance, and she was still walking it. [216]

She had spent the winter doing what she always did with a failure, which was to take it apart like one of her father's clocks until every gear lay visible on the table. [249] Darkness had been the first wrong gear, because in the dark every shape was an enemy. [265] Time was the second, because the valley kept its hours by bells and the highland by drums, and the two had drifted a quarter hour apart before anyone noticed. [295] So she had proposed the moon. [301] The two sides kept different calendars and trusted neither clocks nor couriers, but the moon was the same moon to everyone. [322] Third night past full, when it cleared the shoulder of Crane Peak, both sides would walk. [338] Teodor had agreed by return messenger in a single line. [348]

The third wrong gear was the middle. [355] Last spring no one had been able to see where it was. [367]

Sabine took from her coat an antique drawer pull, a brass lion's head clenching a ring, pried from the ruined treaty desk in the gatehouse below, where two grandfathers had once signed away their right to this wall. [405] She had polished it every evening for a week. [414] Alone, she walked out and counted twenty paces with her boots, then knelt and set the lion on the wet stone, its ring facing the highland. [441]

Then there was nothing to do but wait. [449]

The mist thickened. [452] Nobody spoke; even the guards breathed carefully, as though sound itself might be mistaken for a weapon. [469] The youngest prisoner, a highland girl with a bandaged wrist, turned her head toward Sabine. [484]

"Is it time?" she whispered. [489]

"When the moon says," Sabine answered. [495]

It took nearly an hour. [500] Then the wind tore a seam in the cloud, and a hard white light fell across the parapet, and the lion's head blazed at the center of the wall like a small struck coin. [534] Across the stone, a hand rose out of the fog. [544]

They walked forward together, prisoners tethered to guards, guards trailing negotiators. [556] Sabine had insisted on one more change: no soldier would untie a single knot. [570] At the lion, she stopped her line and began to untether the prisoners herself, fingers numb, working each rope loose while every guard kept both hands visible. [597] Three feet away, Teodor was doing the same, head bowed, his gray beard beaded with mist. [613]

"You rebuilt the whole thing," he murmured. [620]

"I rebuilt what we broke." [625]

The last knot came free. [630] Her three crossed toward the valley, and his four crossed toward the highland, and nobody hurried, because under the moon nobody needed to. [654] An old ferryman from Sabine's side touched her sleeve as he passed, a pressure so light she might have imagined it. [675]

Teodor did not leave. [679] "The letter," he said. [683]

"I read it." [686] She looked at the brass lion, bright between their boots. [696] "Eleven times." [698]

He waited, uncommonly patient for a highland man, and she honored that by not pretending. [713] "I am not there yet," she said. [720] "But I am on the way." [726]

He nodded as if that were a treaty in itself. [736] "Third night past the next full?" [742]

"Third night past the next full." [748]

He reached for the drawer pull to return it, and she shook her head. [762] "Leave it," she said. [766] "Someone should always know where the middle is." [774]

The cloud closed again, and the lion went dark, but they both knew exactly where it lay as they turned and walked back into their separate fog. [802]
