The icicles along the old cooling ducts began to thaw a little after noon, and Mara was waiting for them. [20] She knelt on the concrete between the moss beds with a notebook open on her knee, watching each drop fall and strike. [42] Over the winter the drips had worn a lattice into the moss, pale dimples in straight lines, one row after another, and she had learned that the lines were not random. [73] They traced the ghost of the building. [80] Every duct had once hung above a row of server racks, and every row of racks had once held the memories of strangers: photographs, voice notes, videos of birthdays nobody had bothered to copy elsewhere. [115] Nine years ago the river had come through the lower floor, the backups had failed, and the company had shut the building and walked away. [140] Mara's collective had leased it for a dollar a year and filled it with moss. [155] Now the light came through the high clouded windows the color of honey, and the green carpet swallowed every sound, so that the whole hall sat inside a hush thick enough to lean against. [189]

She counted drips down the fourteenth line and marked the spot where rack C-14 had stood. [205] That was where her sister's videos had lived, the last footage of Lena laughing, taken the summer before she got sick. [226]

The door at the far end scraped. [233]

She did not startle; she had been expecting it since the video went viral three days ago. [250] In it a tired man named Theo Varga, once the night administrator here, had looked into his phone and said that the backups had not failed. [276] He had skipped them for months to cover a second job, and when the river rose there was nothing left to restore. [298] Four million people had watched him cry. [305] Forty thousand had found his address. [311] At the end he had said he only wanted to stand in the building once more and apologize to the empty room. [333] So Mara had quietly turned off the gate camera, cleared a cot in the old battery room, and left the side door unlatched. [356] She had told no one. [361]

Theo stood in the doorway with his hood up, staring at the green. [374]

"It's not empty," he said. [379]

"Nothing here stays empty long," Mara said, standing and brushing off her knees. [392] "You can shut the door." [397] "These walls were built to keep out storms, and they keep out everything else as well." [413]

He closed it and came forward slowly, as if the moss might accuse him. [427] She watched his hands, his shoulders, the way he flinched at a drip landing near his shoe; she had always watched first and dreamed second, and for nine years she had dreamed of meeting whoever had lost Lena, never once in forgiving colors. [470]

"The drips," she said, pointing at the dimpled lines. [479] "Every winter they draw the old floor plan." [487] "Row C, rack fourteen, is right there." [494]

Theo looked, and his face changed; he knew the number the way people know the street they grew up on. [514]

"My sister was on that rack," Mara said. [522] "Lena." [523] "Four years of her." [527]

He sat down hard on the edge of a bed, and for a long time the only sound was the icicles letting go. [550]

Mara reached under her collar and drew out the amber pendant she had made the first spring, when she had found a cracked sliver of drive platter glinting in the mud beneath C-14. [583] She had sealed it in resin with a sprig of moss, so that the ruined silver lay wrapped in green, preserved like an insect in old sap. [610] For nine years it had meant only what was gone. [620] She lifted it over her head and proffered it to him on her open palm. [635]

"I can't take that," he whispered. [641]

"I'm not giving you her," Mara said. [648] "I'm giving you the part that grew back." [656] "The moss came up through the racks because something broke open." [667] "I need what happened to her to mean more than your mistake, and I can't do that alone." [685]

She told him the rest plainly: the cot, the closed gate, the work. [698] Every thaw, the drips would show them where the racks had stood, and they would plant each footprint for someone who had lost something here, row by row, until the building held what it had failed to keep. [736]

Theo closed his fingers around the amber. [743] It was warm from her skin. [749] Above them the honeyed light held still, and the hush folded over them both like a wall that had decided, at last, to protect what was inside. [776] Mara knelt again by rack C-14 and began, very gently, to dig. [788]
