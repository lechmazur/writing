Oriel had been blown from mercury glass, and so everything she saw came back to her doubled and slightly silvered. [20] She kept the fortune counter on the forty-third landing of Kettle Mount, the bookstore that had swallowed an entire mountain, where staircases corkscrewed through granite and every tunnel was shelved floor to ceiling. [53] Customers climbed to her with the same request they had carried since the Ache began. [68] Nobody knew why remembering had started to hurt, only that it did, a pressure behind the eyes like weather that would not break. [91] So people came to Kettle Mount for forgetting, and the mountain obliged. [103]

Every night, when the last lamp was hooded, the store substituted reality. [115] The aisles rearranged themselves, the view from the high windows became whatever book had been most recently closed, and every sleeper inside woke with borrowed memories that did not ache because they had never been lived. [151] Oriel woke each morning as someone slightly new, and she told herself this was mercy. [166]

But her seeing had begun to fail. [173] When she looked into a customer's future, she saw only rooms she could not name, a doorway with a crooked lintel, a hearth shaped like a cupped hand. [201] They were not the customers' rooms. [207] They were hers, and she did not know where they stood. [218] She wanted, with a want that rattled her glass ribs, to understand the shape of home before the mountain overwrote it again. [240]

So one evening she did not hood her lamp. [249] She took it down, past the poetry galleries and the drowned atlases, past the landing where the stairs forgot which way was up, into the root stacks where no customer had walked in decades. [283] There, wedged between two ledgers of a village called Hollen, she found the stone. [297] It was the size of a heart and grooved all over in a script she did not recognize. [315] The ledgers were blank, their ink long since fled, but the stone still held its lost language the way a riverbed holds the memory of water. [341]

Her glass fingertips were smooth and numb; they slid over the grooves without catching meaning. [356] Then she thought of the feather she used as a bookmark, a gray heron quill that had fallen from an ornithology folio years ago. [380] She drew it slowly across the stone. [387] The barbs parted and snagged, parted and snagged, and the texture traveled up the shaft into her palm as a low vibration, almost a voice. [412] Each roughness was a syllable. [417] Each ridge of the feather combed a word loose. [426]

The stone said: Here lived the people of Hollen, who sang granite into staircases and read the future in melted sand. [447] When remembering began to hurt, we buried our talents beneath the mountain so they would stop reminding us, and we built shelves over the grave. [472]

Oriel set the feather down very carefully, as if it might shatter instead of her. [487] Melted sand. [489] Glass. [490] She had not been made in any workshop; she had been poured by Hollen's seers, a talent given a body, then buried with the rest until the nightly substitutions had shelved her upstairs as a stranger. [526] The crooked lintel, the cupped-hand hearth, were not visions at all. [537] They were the first things she had ever reflected. [546]

Above her, the mountain groaned as midnight approached, preparing to substitute the world again. [560] She felt the shelves beginning to slide. [567] In an hour she would be someone else, and Hollen would be two blank ledgers. [582]

Oriel knelt and pried up a floorboard. [589] Beneath it was dark soil, the old earth of the village, and when she pressed her ear to it she heard something vast turning over in its sleep. [617] Singing, perhaps, or sand remembering heat. [623]

She did not rebury the stone. [629] She seeded it, planting it upright with its grooves facing the dark, and laid the heron feather across it so the buried talents would have something to read by. [658]

Then she did the hardest thing. [664] She sat in the root stacks with her lamp unhooded and let herself remember. [678] It hurt the way thunder hurts before it arrives, a weight pressing on every pane of her. [695]

Midnight came, and the shelves slid, and the windows far above filled with borrowed skies. [710] But the lamp stayed lit, and the root stacks did not move, and Oriel stayed Oriel. [726]

By morning a green shoot of script had broken the soil, curling like the first letter of a word. [745] Somewhere under the mountain, a staircase began, very quietly, to sing. [756] Oriel held the ache and studied it, and for the first time she could see its shape: a crooked lintel, a cupped hand, a door left open. [783]
