The campfire's last spark died at the foot of Gannet Rock, and Wren Halloway stood, brushing sand from the grey cloak she wore for work. [25] Magistrate Tobias Venn had paid her three silver crowns to sit beside him all night and agree with everything he said. [46] That was the job of a conscience for hire, as far as most clients understood it. [62] She agreed beautifully. [65] She was sincerely insincere, which is to say she never lied about lying; every "you are right, sir" came wrapped in a tone that let a man hear, if he wished, the question folded inside. [100] At noon Tobias would sentence Ilse Marrow, keeper of this lighthouse, for letting the lamp go dim the night the Cormorant broke on the reef. [125] Eleven sailors drowned. [128] He was certain of her guilt, and he had spent the night saying so to the fire. [145]

"Then why hire me?" Wren had asked near midnight. [154] He had not answered, only stared at the coals as though his late wife might speak from them. [172] Wren knew the story anyway; everyone in Harrowgate did. [181] Margit Venn used to tell her husband he wore certainty like armor and would one day drown in it. [200]

Now dawn came up pale and breezy over the water, and the lighthouse above them began to sing. [218] Ilse had hung wind chimes along the gallery rail, dozens of them: shell, brass, bone, bottle glass. [235] The fishermen called it clutter. [240] Wren, who had grown up among keepers, called it a language. [251]

"Come up with me," she said, "and I will agree with you at the top." [266]

They climbed the iron stair while wind pushed through the broken panes and the chimes answered one another overhead. [285] On the gallery Wren read them the way her grandmother had taught her, deciphering wind chime selections as a clerk reads ledgers. [307] Keepers did not choose chimes for prettiness. [314] Shell meant fog sighted; brass meant the lamp was lit; bone meant a request refused. [329]

"She hung a new cluster every night," Wren said, running her fingers along the rail. [344] "A diary nobody thought to read." [350]

Tobias folded his arms. [354] "And the night of the wreck?" [360]

She found it near the lamp room door: shell, brass, and three bones, strung beneath something that did not ring so much as rattle. [384] Fog, lamp lit, and a request refused three times. [393] Wren reached up to unhook the rattling piece and laid it in the magistrate's palm. [408]

It was a hollow mirror shard, curved, taken from the great reflector behind the lamp. [423] Its silvered face looked whole, but the backing had rotted away inside, so that it weighed almost nothing and reflected almost nothing either. [446] Wren turned it toward the rising sun; the light went into it and did not come back out. [464]

"She asked the Harbor Trust to replace the reflector," Wren said. [475] "Three times, by these bones." [480] "Her lamp burned; it simply had nothing left to throw its light." [492]

"Or she hung these afterward to save herself," Tobias said, but his voice had lost its polish. [509]

"You are right, sir," said Wren, sincerely. [516] "She might have." [519]

The wind picked up and he shivered in his thin court coat. [531] Wren unclasped her grey cloak and held it out. [540] Every conscience in the guild wore one; they called it the cloak of doubt, and it was stitched from retired judges' robes, heavy wool lined with a hundred small pockets, each one empty. [573]

"Doubt is not the opposite of judgment," she said. [582] "It is the lining." [586] "It keeps the cold out while you decide." [594]

He hesitated, then let her settle it across his shoulders. [604] Wrapped in it, he looked smaller and, for the first time all night, afraid. [618] Wren understood then why he had hired her. [626] Not to agree, but to stand close enough that he could be uncertain without being alone in it. [644]

"Margit would have read these," he said quietly, looking along the rail. [656] "She read everything twice." [660]

"Then read them twice," said Wren. [666]

He did, slowly, lips moving, while she translated each cluster: fog, lamp, refused; fog, lamp, refused; a week of the same patient complaint spelled in shell and bone. [694] When he finished, he held the hollow shard up to the dawn and watched it swallow the light. [712]

"She may still have erred," he said. [719] "But not alone." [722]

"You are right, sir," Wren said, and this time there was nothing folded inside it at all. [739]

He did not give the cloak back. [746] She did not ask for it; she had others, and the wind was warming, and at noon he would need something between himself and his certainty. [772]
