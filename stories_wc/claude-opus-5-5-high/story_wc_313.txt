The wind chime above the gauge house fell silent at a quarter past eight, and Idris Pell stopped walking. [19] For twenty-six years it had rattled every dusk, seven blue bottle-necks strung on fishing line, and its quiet meant the valley wind had died entirely. [44] Below the Midnight Ridgeway, the reservoir lay so still it held the whole bruised sky, and the drowned town of Hollin stood up out of it like a thought someone had tried not to finish. [79] The drought had taken the water down forty feet since June. [90] Rooftops first, then the chapel spire, then whole streets of silted cobble, each one doubled in the mirrored shallows so that there seemed to be two Hollins, one rising and one sinking. [122]

Idris walked the levee crest each twilight with a lantern and a logbook, because a levee was a promise and he was the one who kept it. [149] He noted seepage to the half-inch. [155] He noted every trespasser, too, though when the Harker boys had come swimming in August he had written only "two juveniles, advised," and then driven them home with towels. [184] The Water Board called him thorough. [190] Its latest memo called on him to expunge all unauthorized material from the exposed bed: flowers, crosses, the small shrines former residents kept leaving. [214] Liability, the memo said. [218]

Tonight there were photographs. [222] The first was pinned to the fence post at marker nine, a black-and-white square of a bakery window, loaves stacked like sleeping cats. [245] The second hung from the steps that led down to the old lane, showing that same lane full of bicycles. [265] The third was wedged in the cracked mud at the lane's foot. [277] Idris gathered each one as he went, because that was the order, and because he wanted to see where they led. [298] The trail went past the chapel, past a pump trough furred with dried weed, to the brick shell of the old distillery. [320] An old woman sat on its threshold with a shoebox on her knees. [333]

"You're trespassing, Mrs. Garrow," he said, gently, because he had logged her twice already that month. [349]

"I'm finishing," Nell Garrow said. [354] "There were four hundred of us, and when they flooded the valley they scattered us across three counties. [372] Everybody kept a piece. [376] Nobody kept the whole thing." [381] She tapped the box. [385] "I've been writing to them all, collecting their pictures, their stories, putting them where they happened so the stories can talk to each other before the water comes back." [414]

She held out one last photograph. [420] "This one's yours." [423]

It showed the distillery yard in summer, a woman in an apron laughing, a small boy on her shoulders clutching a bottle. [445] Someone had hand-tinted only one thing: the label, a deep sapphire blue, with a heron printed in silver. [463] Hollin Heron Liqueur. [466]

Idris knew that blue. [470] It was the blue of the bottle-necks on his wind chime, the one his father had hung without explanation the year they moved to the ridge. [496] And then, as if the stillness had opened a door, he remembered: the smell of bitter sloes and sugar, his mother lifting him to press labels onto wet glass, her voice saying, straight, love, the way the heron stands. [535] He had been four when the water came, and she had died the winter after, and his father had never once said the town's name. [560]

"She designed that label," Nell said. [566] "Everyone in Hollin had one tacked up somewhere." [574]

Idris stood a long time in the mirrored hush, the drowned street below his boots and the drowned sky below that. [595] Then he opened his logbook. [600]

The order said expunge from the exposed bed, and he was a precise man. [614] So he helped Nell carry every photograph back up the lane, and together they pinned them along the Midnight Ridgeway, post after post above the old waterline, where the Board's memo did not reach and anyone walking the levee could read Hollin from beginning to end. [660]

At the gauge house he wrote: "Unauthorized material removed from reservoir bed. Area clear." [674] Every word of it was true. [680]

He tucked his mother's photograph beneath the chime's crossbar, the sapphire label turned outward to the dark. [697] Out past the spire, a heron lifted from the shallows, and its reflection lifted with it. [713] Near midnight a breath of wind came down the valley, and the blue glass began, very softly, to speak. [732]
