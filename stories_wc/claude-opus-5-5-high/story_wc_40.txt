The last dictionary was printed forty-one years ago, and Ottilie Marsh still kept her copy wrapped in a flour sack, as if it might catch cold. [26] The Lexicon Board had declared the language complete that year, every word weighed and pinned like a moth, and the presses were melted down for tram rails. [53] Nobody had asked the words whether they agreed. [61]

At dawn she let herself into the Hollin Reading Room, where the shadows lay in the stacks like sleeping dogs and the high white dome waited above her. [89] The dome had been built by a man who hated librarians, or loved them, depending on which story you believed: its plaster was so sonically reflective that a dropped pencil came back three times, and a whisper spoken at the east desk could be heard, perfectly, at the west. [138] Ottilie had learned to be quiet in it the way other children learned to swim. [153]

At noon she was to testify before the Board. [162] The question was simple and had been sent to her in a stiff envelope: Is the language still growing, yes or no? [184] There were rumors of new words in the river districts, in the schoolyards, words the Board called weeds. [202] If she said yes, they would send wardens with red pencils. [213] If she said no, she would be lying, and she had never been good at it; her face gave her away before her mouth could. [238] But she did not yet know how they grew, and she could not bear to answer a question she did not understand. [260]

From her coat pocket she took the crystal. [268] It had fallen from the reading room chandelier the winter the heating failed, a teardrop of cut glass the size of a thumb, and she had kept it because it hummed. [299] For sixty years it had hung at the dome's focal point, where every sound in the room gathered and went round, and it seemed to have kept some of them. [329] When she held it into the first thin light from the clerestory, the glass warmed, and the dome began to speak. [350]

It never spoke in order. [355] The sounds came back through fractured sequences, broken the way the light broke on the facets: a cough from some forgotten Tuesday, then half a proposal of marriage, then a boy sounding out a word he could not find, then the proposal's answer, then the cough again. [402] For months she had been writing them down on index cards and laying the cards across the long oak table, trying to decode the pattern of growth, the way a gardener studies where the moss comes up between the stones. [442]

This morning she laid the last card and stepped back. [452]

The new words were never in the talking. [460] They came after the gaps. [465] Each one followed a suspended hush, a breath where someone stood at the edge of what they meant and found no bridge: the boy, stuck, and then out of him a sound no dictionary held, "glimmock," and his sister laughing because she knew exactly what it meant. [512] The old woman at the proposal, silent so long her suitor had begun to apologize, and then a word like a key turning. [535] Words grew where silence was allowed to last. [543]

Ottilie sat down on the cold floor with her knees up, as she had at nine. [559] All her life people had filled her pauses for her, kindly, impatiently, finishing her sentences like tidy aunts folding someone else's laundry. [581] She had thought her quiet was a lack. [589] Perhaps it had been a field left fallow. [597]

At noon the Board sat in a row beneath the dome, five gray men and one gray woman, their red pencils laid out like cutlery. [622] "Miss Marsh," said the chairman, "is the language growing?" [631] His question went up into the plaster and came back, softer, three times. [644]

Ottilie held the crystal and did not answer. [652] The chairman began to repeat himself, and she raised one hand, very gently, the way you stop a child from stepping on a snail. [676] The hush spread. [679] It filled the stacks, the dome, the six gray faces, until the only sound was the glass warming in her palm. [700] She let it last longer than was polite, longer than was bearable, long enough that the woman at the end of the row leaned forward, lips parted, reaching for something. [730]

What came out of her was not in any dictionary. [740] The dome caught it and gave it back to all of them, and the woman put her hand over her mouth, startled and pleased as a girl. [767]

"That," said Ottilie, "is my testimony." [773]

In her hand, the crystal kept it. [780]
