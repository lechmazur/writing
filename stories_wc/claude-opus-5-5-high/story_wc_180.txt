The contract had been broken for eleven days when Marit Holl climbed into the orchard without permission. [17] The diocese had stopped paying the menders at midsummer, then sent a letter explaining that St. Aldric's would be pulled down at the thaw, rafters, roots, and all. [45] Her tools had been seized as forfeit, which she thought was a very lawyerly way to rob an old woman. [65]

Above the nave, the orchard spread across the beams like a second, greener roof. [79] Two hundred years ago her order had grafted apple and quince into soil troughs laid along the rafters, and the trees had answered by growing through the broken slates toward the light. [111] Now the branches were thick with ribbons. [118] Every autumn the couples of the parish climbed up and tied their deciduous vows: a promise meant to last one season, until the leaves came down. [144] In spring they chose again, retying or releasing, and Marit's work had always been to keep the trees healthy enough to hold whatever was asked of them. [171]

Her quartz eye caught the fault before her good one did. [182] The stone had been set in her socket after a fall in her twentieth year, and it saw nothing of color, only stress: the pale seams where wood was tearing itself apart from the inside. [217] The oldest quince, the one above the altar, was splitting at the fork. [230]

"It's dying," said a voice behind her. [237]

"It's cracking," Marit said without turning. [243] "Cracking is not dying, Pim, and you are not supposed to be up here." [257]

Her granddaughter swung onto the beam with the balance of someone who had never once been told to be careful and listened. [279] Pim was nine, freckled, and endearingly feisty, a child who argued with weather. [292] "Neither are you," she said, "so we're even." [300]

Marit had wanted, for years, to teach her the trade the way her own mother had taught her: slowly, by hand, with the patience of seasons. [326] But Pim's mother had left the craft, and the town, and finally the vow she had tied to this same quince, and Pim had inherited her impatience like a nose. [356] Now there was no time for slowly. [363]

"I need a binding," Marit said, patting her empty belt. [373] "Wax, twine, a splint." [377]

Pim scowled, then crawled along the beam to the old storage niche where the menders once kept spare gear. [396] She came back dragging a canvas bag furred with dust. [406]

Marit knew it at once. [411] Her daughter's apprentice bag, abandoned the winter she walked away. [421] Inside were forgotten things: a stub of grafting wax gone amber with age, a hank of hemp, a pruning knife rusted shut, a pencil drawing of a lopsided tree, and a blue hair ribbon, faded almost white along the folds. [461]

"That's Mum's," Pim said, and her voice lost its edge. [471] "She wore it in the picture on your shelf." [480]

They worked as the light went orange through the broken rose window. [492] Marit warmed the wax in her palms and showed Pim how to press it into the wound, how to wedge the knife, still sheathed in rust, as a brace across the fork, how to wrap the hemp so it bit but did not strangle. [536] The hemp ran short a hand's width from the end. [546]

Pim held up the ribbon. [551]

Marit hesitated, and her quartz eye, which could not see blue, saw only that the silk was sound. [569]

"Why do you keep doing this," Pim asked while she tied it, "if they're knocking it down anyway?" [587]

Marit opened her mouth to say something about duty and found she had no answer that did not sound like the diocese. [609]

Pim answered for her, knotting the ribbon with her tongue between her teeth. [622] "I suppose it's like the vows," she said. [630] "You don't promise forever." [634] "You just promise until the leaves fall, and then you see if you still want to." [650]

She meant the tree, Marit was almost sure. [658] But it struck her like sunlight through slate: her daughter had not broken a vow, only declined to renew it, and the orchard had been built for exactly that. [687] The diocese could breach its contract; trees kept a different kind of promise. [700] They persisted one season at a time. [707]

"Will you teach me the rest?" Pim asked. [715] "Until spring," she added quickly, fiercely, as if bargaining. [724]

Marit laughed, the first time in eleven days. [732] "Until spring," she agreed, "and then we'll see if you still want to." [745]

Below them the empty nave waited for the wreckers, but above, the quince held, bound in blue, and Marit had already begun choosing which cuttings would travel. [772]
