The tremors came to Mount Asca every evening now, just after the clouds settled below the observatory like a second, softer floor. [22] Maren Oduya had repaired cameras there for forty-one years, and she had learned to hold a screwdriver still while the walls hummed. [44] The engineers from the valley said the foundations would not survive the winter. [57] They had already stopped sending film. [63] What worried her was not the cracks but the clock. [73]

When the first quake struck in September, the great regulator in the dome had jolted, and its minute hand had slipped the escapement and begun to spin. [100] It had not stopped since. [105] It did not slow, did not wobble, did not answer to friction; it turned once every four seconds, changelessly constant, as though it had found some private escape velocity and broken free of time altogether. [140] Tomas, her apprentice, called it haunted. [146] Maren called it the only reliable thing left on the mountain. [157]

In three nights the moon would pass in front of Aldebaran, the red eye of the Bull, grazing it so closely that the star would flicker among the lunar peaks before vanishing. [189] Nobody had ever photographed a grazing occultation from Asca. [198] The old lunar camera could have done it once, but its shutter had shattered in the October tremor, and there were no parts left in the world for a machine that age. [230]

"Then we restructure it," Maren said, unscrewing the regulator's glass. [240] Tomas watched her lift the spinning hand free on its arbor, still whirling in her palm like a trapped insect. [260]

"You want to build a shutter out of a broken clock?" [271]

"Out of the one thing here that does not change." [281]

She worked for two days, coupling the hand to a cam, the cam to a blade of blackened brass, so that each revolution would sweep the blade open and closed. [311] The problem was the gearing. [316] Moonlight on the old silver plates wanted an exact exposure, and she had no meter that still worked. [334] Her own teacher, forty years dead, had given her the measure: the length of one leaf falling in still air. [354]

So on the second evening she carried a stool into the courtyard, where a ginkgo her husband had planted grew stubbornly out of the rock, and she closed her eyes. [384] Tomas held the spinning hand beside her ear. [392] She waited for the tiny snap of a stem letting go, then listened to the whisper of gold turning over and over through the dark, and counted the arbor's soft clicks until the leaf touched stone. [420] Eleven clicks. [422] Then a second leaf, and a third. [429] Always eleven. [431]

"Why does it matter so much?" Tomas asked. [439] He was leaving on the last cable car, the morning after the occultation, and they both knew it. [457]

Maren opened her eyes. [461] "Because everything worth keeping is already leaving," she said. [470] "Light off the moon, a star going under, a leaf, a boy." [482] "You cannot slow any of it down, so you learn to meet it at its own speed." [499]

On the third night the clouds lay white and endless beneath them, and the moon rose so bright the dome seemed filled with water. [523] Aldebaran burned at the lunar edge. [529] Maren's hand rested on the release. [535]

Then the mountain groaned. [539] The floor lurched, plaster rained from the ribs of the dome, and the telescope swayed on its pier while Tomas grabbed the rail and shouted her name. [566] Everything shook except the spinning hand, which went on turning, click after patient click, as it had since September. [585] Maren listened past the roar for its rhythm, found it, and waited for the star to touch a mountain on the moon. [607] The shaking eased to a shiver. [613] She pressed the release, and the brass blade swept open and shut in the space of a falling leaf. [632]

They developed the plate by candlelight, because the power had failed. [643] Slowly the lunar limb surfaced in silver, jagged and pale, and between two peaks hung a single broken bead of light, the star caught in the instant of its going. [673] Tomas stared at it for a long time. [681]

"Come down with me," he said. [687]

Maren wrapped the plate in felt and placed it in his hands. [699] "Take this down instead," she said. [705] "Show them what the mountain could do." [712]

In the morning she stood in the cracked courtyard and watched the cable car sink into the clouds, carrying him away at his own speed. [737] A ginkgo leaf let go above her. [744] She did not count this time. [750] Behind her, inside the old camera, the clock hand kept turning, bright and faithful, measuring nothing but the ache of everything that leaves. [773]
