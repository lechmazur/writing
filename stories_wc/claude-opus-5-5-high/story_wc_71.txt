Nell Varga cleaned the houses of Pellow Bay the way the rental agency required, which meant she erased people. [19] Every spring the lockboxes multiplied on the porches like barnacles, and every spring she wrote the new number in the back of her ledger, because no one else seemed to notice. [50] Nine houses, then fourteen, then twenty-two. [56] The bakery closed an hour earlier, then two. [64] The church bell was replaced by a recording that never cracked on the high note. [79] People called it quiet, and meant it kindly. [87] Nell called it creep, and wrote it down. [95]

Her job was to reset each rental to its photographs: cushions squared, remote controls parallel to the table edge, a single decorative oar above the mantel. [121] She was good at it. [126] That was the trouble. [130] When she finished, the houses felt like held breath, and she could not say why the same bay held others that seemed to breathe on their own. [157]

Tobiah Crane's cottage at the end of the seawall was one. [168] It sagged, it smelled of diesel and marmalade, and when she passed it at dusk it seemed to lean toward her like a dog hearing its name. [195] She wanted to understand that lean more than she wanted anything, because her grandmother's house had once leaned the same way, before the agency bought it and Nell began cleaning it every Thursday. [228]

Her grandmother had been a perfumer, the last one on the coast who still cured her own orris. [246] Nell kept a single shard of the root in her coat pocket, grey and hard as driftwood, its violet scent so faint it seemed less a smell than a lowering of volume. [278] "Orris needs three years of stillness before it finds its voice," the old woman used to say. [295] "It's a shard of hush, love." [301] "You wait on it." [305]

On the first warm evening of April, Nell sat on the seawall while the streetlights daydreamed, that pink undecided minute when the lamps are lit but not yet sure of it. [336] The bay lay flat and burning, the sunset laid across it without a sound. [350] Tobiah came out with two mugs, one of which had a spoon still standing in it. [366]

"Your house," Nell said. [370] "Why does it feel awake?" [375]

He squinted back at it as if he had never considered the question. [388] "Couldn't say." [390] He blew on his tea. [395] "Nothing's where it goes, I suppose." [401] "My boots are on the stairs because Ada left them there in nineteen ninety-one, and I never had the heart." [421] "A house is only alive where somebody forgot to put it back." [433] He said it the way he might mention rain, and went back inside to find the biscuits. [450]

Nell sat with the sentence until the lamps committed to their light. [462] Then she walked to her grandmother's house and let herself in with the lockbox code. [477] It was perfect. [480] Linen folded into envelopes, a bowl of glass lemons, the decorative oar. [492] Guests would arrive Friday and feel nothing, and leave five stars. [503]

She began with the kitchen chair, turning it a quarter away from the table, as though someone had just stood up to answer the kettle. [528] She opened the atlas on the sill to the Azores, where her grandmother had always meant to go. [546] She hung one guest towel crooked, deliberately, the way a person makes the sign of the cross. [563] Each small wrongness sanctified the room a little, like a candle lit where she had been told to keep dark. [583]

Last, she climbed to the upstairs landing where her grandmother's workbench had stood, now a reading nook with a staged throw. [604] She took the orris shard from her pocket and set it on the third stair, exactly where it did not belong. [625] It gave off almost nothing. [630] It gave off enough. [634] The house did not creak or sigh; it simply seemed, for the first time in three years, to be waiting for someone. [656]

Nell knew the agency would find the chair, the atlas, the towel, and she knew she would be told to put them back. [679] She would put them back slowly, and move something else each Thursday. [691] Creep, after all, worked in both directions. [698]

In her ledger that night, under the number twenty-two, she drew a new column and titled it Forgotten on Purpose, and beneath it, in careful hand, the first entry: one shard of hush, third stair, waiting. [734]
