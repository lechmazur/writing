The Arbor Station hung where the great trees broke through the clouds, its platforms lashed to branches that rose and fell with the vapor tides. [25] Every evening at the turning of the tide, the compasses flickered. [36] Needles shivered in their glass, swung east, swung north, then spun like bored children, and no skiff captain dared cast off into the white until the horizon invited night and the stars took over the work of direction. [74] So the station kept a cinema, and the cinema kept Ines Varga. [86] She played the upright beneath the screen for stranded pilots and sap-merchants, scoring the same black-and-white film she had scored for eleven years: a lighthouse keeper waiting for a ship that never comes. [119] On the piano lid sat a brass anniversary clock, stopped at twenty to seven, the hour Tomas had flown out under a trembling needle and not returned. [146] She had never repaired it. [151] A broken thing, she believed, at least told the truth. [161]

Tonight the truth was that the Tidewardens were aboard. [170] They wore gray oilcloth and carried steady compasses, lodestone-weighted instruments only Wardens were licensed to own, and they were searching the platforms for the Perrins, a family who had refused the grain levy and now ran with three children. [209] Ines knew they were already in the third row, the mother gripping a ticket stub, the eldest girl staring at the screen as though it might open. [236] It could, if they knew how to look. [244]

Over eleven years Ines had spliced hidden sections into the reel, single frames tucked inside the lighthouse beam, each one a thin white line of the canopy path to the Hollow, a grove where the needles held still and the Wardens had never thought to search. [290] At normal speed they flashed too quickly for any eye. [300] But old Anselm cranked the projector by hand, and Anselm cranked to her tempo. [314] When Ines slowed into a lament, the film slowed with her, and the beam lingered long enough to be read. [334] The Wardens watched the audience, never the screen. [342] Men who trusted steady needles did not think to look for maps inside a story. [357]

She began the overture, and the lighthouse flickered onto the screen. [368] She played the keeper's first vigil slow and grieving, and the first line of the path glowed in the beam: left from the ferry root, three branches down. [396] In the third row, the girl's lips moved. [404] Ines played the storm scene hard enough to rattle the clock on the lid, then drew the second vigil out like thread, and the second line held. [431] One Warden turned his head toward the music, frowning, and she let her shoulders sag like a woman tired of her own song. [454] He looked away. [457]

But the final turn of the route was not in the film proper. [470] It lived in the tail of the reel, after the word FIN, where Anselm always stopped cranking because Ines always stopped playing. [492] She had spliced it there that morning, a door that opened only if the pianist broke her own ritual, because a ritual broken once would not look like a signal. [522] It would look like grief, and nobody arrests grief. [531]

The keeper on screen turned from the empty sea, and FIN bloomed white. [544] Ines did not lift her hands. [550] She glanced at the brass clock, its hands frozen at twenty to seven, and knew without turning that the station clock above the door agreed with it tonight. [578] Five years ago fate had taken that minute from her. [588] She meant to take it back. [594]

She began the encore, a melody that had never belonged to the film, the waltz Tomas used to hum while he wound the clock, and behind her Anselm, bless his crooked spine, kept cranking. [628] The tail of the reel ran dark gray, then showed a single white line curving past the third great trunk and down into mist. [652] Someone in the audience laughed at the odd scratched frame. [662] The Wardens yawned. [665] The girl in the third row did not laugh. [674]

When the lights rose, the Perrins filed out with the crowd, and as the girl passed the piano, Ines pressed the broken clock into her hands. [700] "When the needle fails, trust the hands," she whispered. [709] "Twenty to seven is the bearing out of the Hollow." [719] "He found it first." [723]

Then the family was gone down the ferry root, and Ines sat alone at the keys while the compasses along the rail spun, stuttered, and at last went still as the horizon invited night. [757] The lid looked bare without the clock. [764] She played the waltz once more, to no one, to him, to whatever had thought it knew how her evenings ended. [785]
