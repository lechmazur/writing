The lost-and-found office of Harrow Street Station had been a library for two hundred years, and no commuter had ever noticed. [21] Its shelves held forgotten umbrellas, single gloves, and eleven thousand books that the Society of the Folded Margin had shelved there on purpose, because nothing hides a secret better than a place where things are expected to be lost. [60] Oda Venn had kept it for thirty-one years. [68] She slept in the back room, ate soup from a thermos, and spoke to almost no one, yet travelers who stumbled in weeping over a lost ring or a dead father's hat left feeling strangely held. [104] She could read grief in the angle of a shoulder, embarrassment in the speed of a signature. [121] It was why she lived alone; other people arrived in her like weather. [134]

At eleven fifty-one, with the great clock over the concourse leaning toward midnight, a young man slipped through the door and said, "Please, I need to hide." [161] She knew him: Tomas Ferrier, the Society's newest candidate, a boy who shelved books as if apologizing to them. [180] His breath was ragged, but it was the shame that she felt first, hot and sour behind her own ribs. [200] She led him between the shelves to the alcove behind the umbrella rack, where the lamplight pooled like still water. [220]

"Who are you hiding from?" [225]

"Everyone," he said. [228] "Myself, mostly." [230]

He pulled a ball of cream paper from his pocket and smoothed it on the reading table. [247] It was an invitation to the Inner Reading, the ceremony where candidates became keepers, and it was ruined, creased into a hundred small mountains. [271] "I thought it was a prank from the other candidates," Tomas said. [283] "I crumpled it and tossed it in a bin, and by the time I dug it out, Master Ilse had watched the whole thing from the platform." [310] He swallowed. [312] "It says to present it flat and unmarked at midnight." [322]

Oda did not reach for it at once. [330] Mistakes were the only thing she had ever truly collected; every object in the office was one, a moment of forgetting frozen into leather or wool, and she had learned that each held a lesson folded inside like a pressed flower. [371] She wanted this one's lesson for him, and, she admitted, for herself. [383] Thirty-one years ago she had received the same cream envelope and had never answered it. [398]

She tilted the lamp. [402] Light slid sideways across the creases, and in the shallow valleys letters appeared, printed in an ink so pale it could only be seen where the paper bent. [430] Tomas leaned in. [433] The hidden words read: ONLY THE FOLDED PAGE CAN HOLD WHAT THE FLAT PAGE HIDES. [448]

"It was never meant to arrive flat," Oda said softly. [458] "The instruction is the test, and the mistake is the answer." [469]

She felt his relief bloom and then falter into doubt, and she understood why, because it was her own old doubt returning. [491]

"But if I go, I become one of them, and I don't know if I want that." [508]

Oda looked around the office, at the shelves nested inside shelves, the library inside the lost-and-found, the Society inside the ordinary night. [530] Everything here was a paradox tucked within another, and she was one of them. [544]

"I never went to my Inner Reading," she said. [553] "I was afraid that belonging would swallow me, so I stayed here and called it leaving." [569] "I have kept their books for three decades and refused their name, and do you know what I learned?" [588]

The clock outside ground toward its last minute. [596]

"That staying while leaving only works if you mean both halves." [607] "I hid inside my own doorway, and I gave strangers a welcome I never let anyone give me." [625] "You can go to them and still be yourself; you can leave this hiding place and still carry it." [644]

Tomas folded the invitation along its old creases, carefully, as if pressing a flower. [658] Then he held out his other hand. [665]

"Come with me," he said. [670] "The Readers meet in the waiting room, don't they, right in plain sight?" [683] "You wouldn't be leaving the station at all." [691]

Oda went to the back room and found her own envelope, yellowed, never opened, and she crushed it in her fist until it crackled like kindling. [717] The first stroke of midnight rolled across the concourse, vast and unhurried, and the whole station seemed to breathe out. [737] She left the door unlocked behind her, so that the library could stay open while its keeper walked, at last, into the room where she already belonged. [764]
