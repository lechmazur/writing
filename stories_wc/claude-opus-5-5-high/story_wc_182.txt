The bookstore occupied the whole of Mount Carrow, and after the time traveler arrived, its shelves began to play. [19] No one heard the music, exactly. [25] Ines Vell felt it as pressure in her molars, a slow chord moving through forty thousand staircases, every stack of unsold atlases thrumming like a string waiting to be bowed. [55] She was the store's arcane linguist, which meant she read the dead grammars printed on spines and translated them into directions, and which also meant she led the rescue missions. [85] Customers wandered off in Carrow every week, lured down some aisle of maritime law until the mountain folded around them. [105]

The time traveler had come out of the local history section on Tuesday, soaked in rain from 1887, clutching a botanical diary against his coat. [130] His name was Teodor Hask, and his assistant had stepped through behind him and never emerged. [146] "Mila is careful," he told Ines, turning the diary's pages with shaking fingers. [159] "She would not wander." [163]

Ines had heard that before. [168] Her own sister, Pell, had been careful, and eleven years ago she had gone looking for the library of unlearned lessons, the chamber rumored to lie at the mountain's root, where every lesson anyone refused was shelved in a book no one would open. [212] Pell had not come back, and Ines had volunteered for every rescue since, searching for that library between the names of strangers. [234]

The diary was the key, though not in the way Teodor believed. [246] He wanted her to read his entries; Ines read what was missing. [258] There were blank days, torn stubs, and brown ghosts where pressed flowers had fallen out. [273] She laid them against the store catalogue, researching gaps the way other scholars researched texts, and found that each absence matched a shelf number the catalogue skipped. [300] The mountain had silences in its music, and the silences were doors. [312]

They descended with one lamp, because oil was rationed below the seventh landing. [325] Ines was carefully resourceful in the way the stacks demanded: she marked turns with sprigs of dried thyme from Teodor's pockets, measured distance in chapters, and asked the spines for directions in Old Carrovian, which they answered only when addressed politely. [366] Where the catalogue skipped shelf 4411, a narrow stair spiraled down. [377] Where the diary lacked the entry for the ninth of May, a door stood half open, smelling of crushed mint. [397]

The library of unlearned lessons was smaller than legend claimed, a round room of pale books with uncut pages. [416] Mila sat among them, unharmed, holding a volume stamped with her own name. [429] She had not been lost; she had been kept. [438]

The Clerk arrived as it always did, assembled from receipts, its voice the rustle of an adding machine. [456] "Every rescue is a purchase," it said. [463] "The traveler's arrival tore pages from my stock, and the girl stays until the ledger balances." [479]

Teodor offered himself, and the Clerk considered it, and Ines understood that the standard terms would simply trade one captive for another. [501] "Let us renegotiate," she said. [506] "You are a bookstore, and a captive earns you nothing." [516]

She had found her own book by then, on the shelf nearest the door, where it had waited eleven years. [536] Its title, in her sister's handwriting, was the sentence she had refused since the day Pell left: She was not taken; she chose. [559]

"This room has never made a sale," Ines said. [568] "I will buy this one, and the price is that I read it." [581]

The Clerk's receipts fluttered, tallying. [586] A lesson learned was worth more than any hostage, because it could never be returned for credit. [603] It printed a single receipt and nodded. [610]

Ines slit the pages with Teodor's pruning knife and read by the last of the lamp. [626] Pell had written it plainly: a letter about a mountain she wanted to walk out the far side of, a life she wanted without rescue, a sister she hoped would one day stop searching. [660] Ines wept, and learned it, and the book went light in her hands. [673] Mila stood, free. [676]

On the climb back, Teodor pressed a sprig of mint into the empty page for the ninth of May, closing one gap of his own. [701] Around them the mountain went on playing, and for the first time Ines let the rests in its music stay silent, and did not go looking for what was missing. [731]
