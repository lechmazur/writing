Maro was the only summoner in the valley who called up nothing that could be seen. [16] She summoned quiet, and quiet does not keep company, so she lived alone in the nocturnal haven beside the brook, where the willows leaned over water so still it held the stars without trembling. [50] Villagers came there after dark to unburden themselves. [58] It was a custom older than the mill: you knelt on the flat stones, whispered your secret to the current, and walked home lighter. [82] Maro heard every confession and repeated none. [89] That was the other half of her calling. [97]

In the week of the fireflies, amid those whispered secrets, the brook began to answer. [112] At first it was only pebbles knocking in the shallows, click and pause, click click and pause. [129] Then Maro noticed the rhythm repeating, and she felt the old cold settle behind her ribs. [145] The stones of this brook had fallen from the sky long before the valley had a name, and once a generation they remembered it. [169] They were terrestrially cosmic things, star-iron worn smooth by mud and patience, and when they spoke, they spoke prophecy. [188] Always in code, and always with the same terrible rule: whoever decoded it would make it true. [205]

Her teacher, Oda, had told her this the night before he walked downstream and did not come back. [223] He had left her a single paper wing, folded from a crane that was never finished, its creases dense with tiny ink marks. [246] For twenty years she had kept the crane's origami wing in a tin by her bed without understanding it. [265]

On the third night of clicking, the miller's boy came to the stones with a slate and chalk. [283] He was not there to confess. [289] He was counting. [292] Maro watched him tally the knocks, lips moving, and she knew he was clever enough to finish before the moon waned. [313] She could not frighten him away without telling him why, and telling him why would only sharpen his hunger. [332] She had to hush the prophecy itself. [339]

So she took out Oda's wing and, for the first time, unfolded it. [352] The ink marks were not words. [358] They were gaps, carefully measured, each pause between knocks drawn as a small empty circle, the circles joined by faint lines like a chart of constellations. [384] Oda had not decoded the sounds. [390] He had mapped the silence between them, and a prophecy's silence, folded along its own shape, would carry off the words it framed. [414]

Maro lit no lantern. [418] She sat through the night with rice paper on her knee and listened not to the clicks but to the spaces, marking each one with a dot of ink while fireflies drifted over the water in a slow, tranquil blaze. [458] Across the brook the boy's chalk scratched on, racing her. [468] Behind them both, a widow whispered to the current about a letter she had burned, and a farmer confessed a stolen goat, and Maro held their secrets as gently as her own breath. [501]

Near dawn the dots resolved into a shape she recognized. [511] It was the missing wing. [516] She laughed once, softly, and understood why Oda's crane had never been finished: each prophecy gave only half its silence, and the valley had waited a generation for the other half. [547] She folded her map along the circles, crease by crease, and joined it to his. [562]

The boy looked up as she knelt at the water. [572] His chalk hovered over the final line. [579] Maro set the crane on the surface and let it flow. [590] It turned once in the starlight, then slid downstream, and wherever it passed the knocking stones fell still, one after another, as if a hand were smoothing a sheet. [619] The hush it left was not empty. [626] It was smoothed, like river glass, and it held the reflected stars more clearly than before. [642]

The boy stared at his slate. [648] The half-written code meant nothing now, just chalk marks without a rhythm to hang on. [663] "What did it say?" he asked. [669]

"Nothing anyone has to carry," Maro said. [676]

He wiped the slate with his sleeve, knelt beside her, and after a long moment whispered a secret of his own into the smoothed hush of the brook. [704] Maro listened, and kept it, and for the first time in twenty years the haven did not feel solitary at all. [725] Far downstream, the paper crane sailed on toward the sea, both wings open at last, carrying its folded silence to wherever Oda had gone. [749]
