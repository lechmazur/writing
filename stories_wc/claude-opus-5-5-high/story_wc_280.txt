The last visitors left Hollin's Maze at six, and Ada Hollin locked the gate behind them for what she believed was the final time. [24] The buyer's contract waited on the ticket counter, folded around a pen. [36] She would sign it in the morning, and by summer the hedges would be bulldozed for condominiums. [53] Instead of going home, she walked the maze one last time, trailing her fingers along the yew. [70] She had always known the turns by their colors rather than their shapes. [83] Her grandmother had called it a blessing: Ada heard the rustle of leaves as green-gold, tasted birdsong like pear skin, and saw every voice as a ribbon of light. [112] Most people called it strange, so she stopped telling them. [122]

At the heart of the maze, where no map had ever led the paying guests, the hedges opened onto the orchard. [143] Her great-grandfather had planted it around a small wooden stage in 1911, when Hollin's was a pleasure garden with lanterns and a traveling bill of singers. [169] The apple trees still bloomed out of season there, and the quince fruited in winter, and Ada had never questioned it. [190]

Tonight a man stood on the rotting stage in a swallowtail coat, bowing to empty grass. [206] He was not quite solid; the moonlight passed through his cuffs. [217]

"You're late," he said, and his voice arrived in her eyes as a faded violet, the color of old programs. [237]

"I'm closed," Ada said, absurdly. [242]

"So was I, in 1914. [247] Heart gave out on the second verse of my closing number, right here, and the crowd went home without their encore. [268] Julian Marsh, tenor, at your service." [274]

He held up a coin between two fingers. [282] It was brass, hand-painted in chipped enamel: the Wheel of Fortune on one face, the Tower on the other. [301] "Your great-grandmother painted this for me as a stage token. [311] She read cards by the gate. [317] She told me the Wheel meant every ending turns into a beginning, if someone is listening." [333]

He flipped it, and it spun in the air far longer than it should have. [348]

"This orchard broke when I died," Julian said. [356] "Time cracked around the unfinished song. [362] That's why the trees forget their seasons. [369] Every night the pieces drift a little farther apart, and tonight, with your signature waiting, they are almost gone." [388]

Ada looked, and saw it: the orchard was not one orchard. [399] Among the rows stood fainter rows, saplings overlaying old trunks, a lantern-strung 1912 flickering behind a weedy present, and further still, something she could not name: children she had never met, running under branches heavy with fruit. [436]

"A future," Julian said softly. [441] "Or the possibility of one, standing at the edge, deciding whether to leave. [454] Possibility is shy, Miss Hollin. [459] It needs coaxing." [462]

"How?" [463]

"The way I always did," he said, and began to sing. [474]

It was an old parlor song, sentimental and slightly flat, and the orchard sang it back. [490] Each note returned as an echo, but not from the hedges; the echoes came from the fractured years themselves. [509] Ada saw them as threads of color: the violet of 1914, the rust-orange of her grandmother humming while she pruned, the pale blue of her own childhood voice calling Marco Polo through the maze. [543] She tasted them too, quince and woodsmoke and the burnt sugar of the fair she had forgotten until now. [562]

"Help me," Julian said between verses. [568] "You can see where they go. [574] I can only make them." [579]

So Ada reached into the air and gathered the threads, and with her hands she intertwined them, violet over orange over blue, braiding the broken years into a single rope of sound. [611] The saplings and the old trunks drew together. [619] The lanterns of 1912 lit the present grass. [627] And the children at the far edge stopped running, turned, and came closer, curious, as if they had heard their names. [648]

Julian reached the second verse, where his heart had failed, and sang straight through it. [663]

When the last note faded, there was a silence the color of dawn. [676]

Then Ada clapped. [679]

The echoes clapped with her, a century of audiences, and the unborn children clapped loudest of all. [696] Julian bowed, grinning, and sang his encore at last, a short bright chorus, and then he was only moonlight. [715]

The coin lay on the stage, Wheel side up. [724]

In the morning Ada tore the contract in half and wrote a new sign for the gate: Open Late, Music in the Orchard. [747] She kept the coin in her pocket, where it hummed faintly, the color of something beginning. [763]
