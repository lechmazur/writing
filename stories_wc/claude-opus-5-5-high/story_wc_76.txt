The clockmaker Maren Hollis built every clock to run counterclockwise, because she had watched forward-running time take her mother, her brothers, and half the harbor town of Skel, and she wanted at least her own hands to refuse the direction. [40] Skel sent her to the lighthouse on Gannet Rock because no one else would go. [55] The lamp had been dark for three winters, the keeper drowned, and the fishing boats now crept home by guesswork through the teeth of the reef. [81] Oil was gone, coal was gone, and the grain barge from the mainland had not come since spring. [99]

On her first night she found the yoyo. [107] It sat in a velvet cradle bolted beneath the great lens, two bronze discs the width of her palm, joined by an axle and wound with a cord of braided gut gone black with age. [142] Each face was engraved with concentric rings of symbols, and the rings turned. [155] A puzzle, then, and old: the keeper's logbook said it predated the tower, that the first builders had found it in a cairn on this very rock and raised the light around it. [188] No keeper had ever solved it. [194]

Maren was neatly inventive in the way of people who have lost too much to waste anything. [211] She did not force the rings. [217] She laid out her jeweler's cloth, sketched each symbol, and worked backward from the only thing she knew for certain: a yoyo is a machine for storing motion and giving it back. [249] Whoever built this had meant the throw to do something. [259]

By the fourth night she understood the rings were a tide table. [271] The outer ring marked moons, the inner marked the drop of water on the rock, and the smallest symbol, repeated only once, was a crude carved face with its mouth open. [302] She knew that face. [306] Every child in Skel knew it. [312] It was cut into the hunger stone, the black boulder at the island's foot that surfaced only at the lowest ebbs of famine years, and beneath it the old words: WHEN YOU SEE ME, WEEP. [347]

The town treated the stone as scripture. [354] When it showed, they stopped mending nets, stopped planting, and waited to starve, because the stone had said so and it had always been right. [379] That was the design Maren could not abide: not hunger itself, but the town kneeling to it. [396]

The stone surfaced at dusk on the ninth day. [405] She watched from the gallery as the sea drew back under the slow exhale of low tide, the rocks glistening like the ribs of something enormous, and there it was, the open mouth rising out of the kelp. [443] On the mainland shore, lamps went out one by one as Skel went to bed hungry and resigned. [461] But out past the reef, she could see the grain barge at last, a smudge of sail, groping blindly for a channel it would never find in the dark. [490]

Her hands were already moving. [495] She set the rings to that night's moon and to the carved face, and the axle clicked free. [513] Inside, where she had expected gears, lay a core of pale crystal, dull as old soap, veined with something that seemed to hold its breath. [538] The builders had not hidden a riddle; they had hidden a reservoir of sunlight, centuries of it, soaked in and sealed, a latent radiance waiting for the one tide the stone warned of. [571]

The stone was never a sentence, she realized. [579] It was an instruction. [583] Weeping was just what people did when they forgot the second half. [595]

The cord had to be wound counterclockwise, against every clock in the world but hers. [610] She laughed aloud at that, a raw sound in the salt wind, and wound it tight. [626] Then she climbed into the lens housing, braced her boots against the brass, and threw. [641]

The yoyo spun down its cord, whining, and at the bottom of the throw the crystal caught its own speed and woke. [663] Light came out of it the way water comes out of a broken dam. [677] The great lens took it and flung it across the reef in a white blade, and she felt the heat blister her knuckles and did not care. [704] She had one throw's worth, maybe two, before the crystal went dark forever. [717]

Far out, the barge turned toward the channel. [725] She threw again, and again the rock blazed, and across the water the lamps of Skel began, uncertainly, to relight. [745]

The hunger stone still grinned below her, and the winter would still be hard, and she would never make another light like this. [768] But she had initiated something the stone had not foreseen, and for once the backward hands in her workshop seemed to keep perfect time. [792]
