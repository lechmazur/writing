After the first snowmelt, Maren Holt carried her loom down into the bronze grotto, where the walls sweated copper and the roof opened on a round of stars. [28] She was the last fractal weaver on the island, and her nets were made the old way, every cord splitting into two finer cords, and those into two finer still, until the edges went soft as breath. [65] Such a net could hold things that had no shape. [75] All winter the villagers had walked to the headland and spoken their burdens into the snow: the debt, the drowned son, the wife who no longer turned toward them in bed. [106] Now the thaw was carrying every word downhill, through cracks in the rock, into the grotto. [122] If no one caught them, they would drip from the ceiling and calcify, and the island would grow another stone tooth for every sorrow left unweighed. [148] The grotto already bristled with them, pale and ribbed as the jaw of a whale. [163]

Maren came to collect the weights of different burdens, because her mother had done it, and her grandmother, and because a village that knew what it carried could decide how to carry it. [196] That was what she told people. [202] The truer reason hung from her belt on a length of tarred line: the brass burner from a kerosene lamp, its wick long burned away, its little crown of prongs bent where the sea had worried it. [239] It was all that had washed back from the whaler Constance, which had taken her husband Tobiah north eleven years ago and never returned him. [264] She had never spoken him into the snow. [272] Without a body, without a grave, without even a date, her grief had no walls, and she lived inside it boundary-lessly trapped, the way a gull is trapped by an ocean with no shore in sight. [308]

She strung the net between two bronze outcrops and waited for the shingled hush. [322] It came, as it always did, at the hour when the meltwater slowed. [335] One silence laid itself over another, and another over that, each overlapping the last like slates on a roof, until the quiet was thick enough to keep weather out. [364] Under that roof the burdens could finally be heard. [373]

But a net only catches by orienting properly, and a fractal net is fussier than most. [389] Its finest threads must lie along the lines the stars draw, or the burdens slip between them like fog through a comb. [411] Maren tilted her face to the opening, found the Hook and the Harpooner, and turned the frame a finger's width east. [432] The copper walls caught the starlight and passed it along the threads until the whole net glowed like baleen in moonwater. [453]

The burdens arrived. [456] The debt of Ansel Pike sagged the northern corner, heavy and square. [468] The drowned son was lighter than she expected, a long thin weight that trembled, as if his father had already begun to let him go. [493] She lifted each one onto her whalebone scale and wrote the figures in her book, and each was different, and each was true. [516]

Near dawn, when the net had gone still, she unhooked the lamp burner and held it over the scale's empty pan. [537] "Tobiah," she said, into the hush, and the word did not echo. [549] She set the burner down. [554] The pan dropped so hard the bone arm groaned. [563] She stared at the needle, and for the first time in eleven years her grief had a number, and a number is a kind of edge. [589] It was heavy, heavier than the debt, heavier than the son, but it ended somewhere. [604]

She could have taken it home again. [611] Instead she carried the burner to the wettest place in the grotto, where the oldest drip fell, and wedged it upright among the stone teeth, its bent crown pointed at the Harpooner. [643] Let it calcify, she thought. [648] Let the water wrap him in white the way the sea had not, slowly, patiently, year over year, until he was a thing with a shape she could visit and then leave. [680]

The first drop struck brass with a small, clear note. [690] Maren rolled the net, tucked the book under her arm, and climbed toward the morning, and behind her the shingled hush settled one more slate over the grave she had finally given him. [723]
