The storm had emptied Gilder Lane, and the bell above the door of Halloran's Music Box Repair gave a single cautious note when Ruth Abernathy came in out of the rain. [31] Every shelf glittered with silent things: lacquered boxes, tin carousels, a brass bird frozen mid-song, all of them waiting under the dim green lamps like a choir holding its breath. [61] She had been chaplain at Harlow State Penitentiary for nineteen years, and she knew that kind of quiet. [79] It was the quiet of men deciding whether to speak. [89]

The repairman, Mr. Halloran, was older than most of his inventory, and he looked up from his bench with the aptly deliberate slowness of someone who had spent a lifetime among springs that snapped if rushed. [125] Ruth set a cloth bundle before him and unwrapped it. [135] Inside lay a heart the size of a fist, hammered from salvaged copper, its chambers riveted shut, a tiny crank jutting from the aorta like a broken stem. [163]

"A man named Emil Varga built it in the prison workshop," she said. [176] "It was meant to play something." [182] "The shop on Carver Street told me it was scrap." [192]

"And you wanted a second opinion." [198]

"I have been giving opinions for a long time," Ruth said. [209] "I have learned to distrust the first one." [217]

She did not tell him the rest. [224] Six years ago, the parole board had asked her whether Emil Varga had changed, and she had said no. [243] She had meant it kindly, even carefully: he rarely spoke, never came to chapel, sat through her visits staring at the wall. [265] She had mistaken silence for absence. [271] Last month she had finally gone to his cell and apologized, and Emil had listened with that same flat patience before answering. [293]

"Sorry is a word, Chaplain," he had said. [301] "Words are cheap in here." [306]

Then he had pressed the dead copper heart into her hands, and she had understood that she was being asked for something more expensive. [330] His second hearing was Thursday. [335]

Halloran turned the heart over, pried the seam with a blade no thicker than a fingernail, and lifted out a pinned brass cylinder and a comb of steel teeth, three of them bent flat. [369] "Carver Street saw the teeth and quit," he murmured. [378] "Teeth can be coaxed." [382] "The question is whether the pins are true." [390]

"How do you tell?" [394]

"Wait," he said, and nodded at the window. [402]

Outside, the rain was thinning. [407] The clouds over the rooftops had gone the color of pewter, and somewhere behind them the sun was working at the seams. [429] Halloran held the cylinder up to the glass and waited, perfectly still, until a single blade of light broke through and struck it. [452] The brass blazed. [455] Each pin threw a small, sharp shadow, and the shadows lay across the drum in a spiral, clean as handwriting. [475]

"Lamplight lies," Halloran said softly. [480] "Storm light breaking through is honest. [486] It hits from one angle, all at once, and nothing crooked can hide." [499] He tilted the drum a quarter turn. [506] "Every pin is true." [510] "He built this slowly, and he built it right." [519]

The light held for perhaps a minute. [526] In that bright, suspended calm, while Halloran straightened the bent teeth with a burnisher and the shelves around them flared gold and rose and green, Ruth felt something approaching that she could not yet name, the way a congregation feels a hymn coming before the organ sounds. [573]

He reassembled the heart with the same unhurried care, then handed it back to her. [588] "Yours to wind," he said. [593]

She turned the little crank. [598]

The melody that rose was thin and sweet and wandering, nine notes that climbed, faltered, and resolved. [615] It was no hymn. [619] It was nothing anyone had ever written down. [627] It was the tune Ruth hummed without thinking as she walked the tiers at night, a habit so old she had forgotten she had it. [652]

Emil had never come to chapel. [658] But he had been listening, every night, through the bars, and he had carried her small song in his head until he could pin it into brass. [685]

Ruth sat down on Halloran's stool because her knees asked her to. [697] Six years ago she had told the board the man had nothing inside him that answered to anything. [715]

"Does he deserve it?" Halloran asked quietly, meaning the repair, meaning perhaps more. [728]

Ruth closed her hand around the singing copper. [736] "Deserving was the question I asked the first time," she said. [747] "On Thursday I'll bring the board my second, and I'll let them hear it." [761]

Outside, the clouds had closed again, but the heart kept playing. [772]
