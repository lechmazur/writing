The fog came in at six, which was the first thing that went wrong, and Mara Voss laughed at it the way she laughed at most disasters now. [28] She stood on the concrete ramp of the Hollis Point kayak launch with her father's ashes in a coffee can and her son's text still glowing on her phone. [56] Can't make it, it said. [61] Maybe next year. [64] So much for the sunrise, the scattering, the long-promised talk in two boats side by side. [80] Everything she had built the morning around had folded like wet cardboard. [92] "Well, Dad," she told the can, "at least you were always early." [104] It was the kind of joke people at the launch loved her for, merrily melancholic, a grin with a bruise under it. [126] Paddlers tended to gravitate toward her rental shack the way boats drift toward a lit harbor, never guessing that the woman handing out life vests had once gotten a man killed. [157]

Twelve years ago she had been second navigator on the research ship Calloway. [170] A squall, a failed GPS, a skiff sent out to retrieve a drifting buoy, and her own frozen hesitation over charts she suddenly didn't trust. [195] By the time she gave a heading, Tomas Reyes was gone. [206] She had quit the sea, quit her marriage, and very nearly quit her son, all in the same slow retreat. [226] A broken navigator, she called herself, and she said it cheerfully, which made it worse. [241]

In her jacket pocket rode the one instrument she had kept: her father's brass astronomical compass, a sun-and-star device from his Navy days that found true north by the sky instead of by magnetism. [275] He had given it to her the day she earned her license. [287] "Magnets lie near iron," he'd said. [293] "The heavens don't." [296] Today there were no heavens, only gray. [303]

She was locking the shack when she noticed the green rental kayak missing from the rack. [319] The sign-out sheet said Ben Aldana, age fifteen, departed 5:40, return by 6:30. [332] It was 6:55. [335] The boy came most mornings and asked her questions about stars she pretended not to remember. [351]

The old regret rose in her like a wave doubling back off a seawall, heavier on the return than it had been going out. [375] Call the Coast Guard, it told her. [382] Wait on shore. [385] Don't freeze out there again. [390] She did call, and the dispatcher promised a boat in forty minutes. [402] The tide was ebbing hard toward the jetty rocks, and forty minutes was a long time to be fifteen and cold. [423] For twelve years she had felt one thing and done another; she was tired of the gap. [440] She set the coffee can on the ramp, dragged a kayak into the water, and pushed off into nothing. [459]

The compass was useless without a sun to sight, and she almost laughed at that too. [475] Then, through a thinning seam of fog, she saw the amber glow of a streetlamp mounted on a utility pole along Shore Road. [498] Another bloomed fifty yards beyond it, then another. [506] The road hugged the coastline all the way to the jetty; she had driven it a thousand times. [524] Keep the lights off her left shoulder, count the poles, and the shore would draw her a line. [542] One, two, three, she counted, paddling between the humming glows as they faded and returned, faded and returned, like a faith she kept losing and finding again. [569]

At the eleventh pole the swell grew steeper, and she heard it: a thin voice calling, not for help, but for her name. [592] Ben was clinging to his overturned kayak thirty feet from the rocks, lips blue, grinning with terror. [609] "I knew you'd come," he chattered. [615] She hadn't known it herself, not until she had him draped across her bow, paddling hard away from the jetty, pole by pole, back toward the launch. [642]

The Coast Guard boat met them at the ramp just as the fog began to tear. [658] Wrapped in a foil blanket, Ben watched her pull out the brass compass and tilt it toward a pale coin of sun. [680] For a moment the shadow pin fell true, and north was exactly where her father had always said it would be. [701] Then the gray closed again. [706]

"Will you teach me how it works?" Ben asked. [715] Mara looked at the coffee can, at her phone, at the boy. [727] Then she typed a message to her son, the one she should have sent years ago. [743] Next year works, it read, and I'm coming to you. [753] "Tomorrow," she told Ben, and for once her hands and her heart agreed, even with the sky gone gray again. [773]
