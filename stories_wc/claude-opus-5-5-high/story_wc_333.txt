The locksmith kept no shop, only a stool and a canvas roll of picks at the place where seven alleys met beneath a gap in the roofs. [27] People called it the Knot. [32] By day it was only shadow, but after dusk the starlight fell straight down the shaft of brick, and anything carried into that pale column looked briefly uncertain of itself. [62] Nobody knew his name, where he slept, or why he refused payment in coin. [76] He asked instead for a sentence: one true thing you had never said aloud. [90]

Ilse Varga came to him on the last evening of October, carrying an envelope. [104] She was small and plainly dressed, the kind of young woman clerks talked over, yet she walked straight into the column of light and set the envelope on his knee as if she had an appointment. [140] It was old vellum, sealed not with wax but with a tiny brass hasp stitched through the paper, and a keyhole no wider than an eyelash. [166] Through the vellum she could feel something soft and coiled. [176]

"My grandmother left it to me," she said. [184] "She wrote on the front: Open when you are braver than I was." [197] "I am not braver, but I have a letter from the observatory at Kerrow offering me a post across the sea, and I have not answered it in three weeks." [227] "Something in me is locked, and I think the key is in here." [240]

The locksmith turned the envelope over, and for the first time anyone at the Knot could remember, his hands were not steady. [262] He knew the hasp. [266] He had made it forty-one years before, for a woman named Marit who had come to him in this same light with a lock of her own dark hair tied in blue ribbon. [299] She had asked him to seal an idea away so thoroughly that she would stop hearing it. [316] He had done it, because she asked, and because he loved her in a way that had no reasonable future, and because he had also been afraid. [343] Her sentence that night, the payment, had been: I want to go where the sky is larger. [360]

"This lock cannot be picked," he said at last. [369] "I built it to answer only to the hint of the first star, the moment when the day has not quite ended and nothing has been decided." [396] "That hour has passed tonight." [401]

"Then I will wait," Ilse said, and sat down on the cobbles as though a whole night and day were a small thing to spend. [426]

She did not leave. [430] She brought him bread at noon and asked nothing, and when the light began to thin the next evening she was still there, watching the brick shaft like a telescope. [460]

Twilight settled across the Knot, a hush so complete it seemed the whole sky was holding its breath over every road not yet taken. [484] Then a single star pricked through, low in the gap, and its reflection slid across the brass hasp and lit one hair-thin groove. [507]

"Stand up," the locksmith said. [512] "Fear keeps its bearings by home." [518] "We must disorient it." [522]

He took her by the shoulders and turned her slowly, once for each alley, until the seven mouths of the Knot blurred and she could not have said which one led back to her rented room, her ledgers, her unanswered letter. [563] Dizzy, she held the envelope up to the star. [572] The locksmith slid his finest pick along the lit groove, and the hasp sighed open. [587]

Inside lay the lock of hair, gone gray at the tips as though it had kept aging in the dark, its blue ribbon faded to the color of early evening. [617] Beneath it was a folded star chart of the southern sky, and one line in Marit's hand: I was afraid, so I stayed; you go. [642]

Ilse read it twice. [646] Then she looked at the locksmith, and she saw in his face what he had carried for forty-one years, a longing that had never been sensible and had never once dimmed. [677]

"You knew her," she said. [682]

"I locked her in," he said. [688] "Now you owe me a sentence." [694]

Ilse tucked the chart inside her coat, where the observatory's letter already waited. [707] She pressed the ribbon-tied lock of hair into his palm and closed his fingers over it. [723] "I am going," she said, quietly, the way true things are said. [735]

Then she chose an alley without knowing where it led, and walked into it, and above the Knot the first star was joined by another, and another, until the sky was too full of possibility for anyone to lock away. [775] The locksmith sat on in the starlight, holding the hair, and fastened nothing at all. [790]
