The terra-cotta arbor on the ridge had been built by someone's great-grandfather to shade grapes, but during the sanctions period it shaded flour. [23] Every few nights a sack appeared beneath its cracked clay pillars, sometimes with a tin of oil or a bundle of candles, never with a name. [49] Teodora Vask, who had led the forty exiled families on the hillside since the border closed behind them, did not ask who left them. [73] She asked only who would carry them down. [81]

At midnight she stood beneath the vines and rang her grandmother's antique silver bell, its clapper wrapped in a strip of wool so that the sound came out no louder than a held breath. [115] Patrols drove the valley road enforcing the embargo, and a food parcel moving between houses after curfew could be called smuggling. [136] So the village learned to listen for the smallest things: the muffled bell, the scrape of a sandal on shale, a cricket going silent when someone passed. [163] Quiet became a language, and everyone on the ridge grew fluent. [174]

Teodora's rule for the midnight food runs was simple and, to many, irritating. [187] Every load went down the goat paths in pairs: one person over sixty, one under twenty. [203] The old knew which paths the patrols could not see from the road, and the young had knees that did not complain. [225] "The old will not always be here," she told anyone who argued, "and the young will not always be strong." [245] "Someone has to hand the paths down." [252] She said it softly, the same way every time, until even the complainers could recite it. [268]

Luka, sixteen and restless, complained most. [274] Teodora kept assigning him to old Irina Dal, who had refused every run for three months and who spoke to no one except her chickens. [299] Each night Luka knocked on Irina's door, the door stayed shut, and Teodora sent him back the following evening anyway. [319]

"She is stubborn," Luka said. [324]

"So am I," Teodora said, "only quieter." [331]

Out of spite, Luka decided to unmask the donor. [340] Some in the village whispered that the flour came from informers buying trust; if Luka caught the donor, he could prove them right or wrong, and either way he would be the one who knew. [375]

One moonless night he lay flat in the dry grass beyond the arbor, so still that a beetle walked across his wrist. [397] An hour before midnight, he heard the careful drag of something heavy. [409] It was Irina, bent nearly double under a sack of flour, her breath whistling. [423] She set it beneath the pillars, pressed her palm to the clay, and turned to find him standing. [441]

"My husband's watch," she said, before he could ask. [450] "The baker in town trades flour for gold and asks no questions." [462] "Last month it was my rings." [468]

"Why hide it?" Luka asked. [473]

"Because the moment people know, it becomes a debt," Irina said. [484] "Nameless bread is just bread." [489] "And because my legs cannot carry it any farther than this." [500]

Luka looked at the sack, then at her shaking hands, and understood why her door had stayed shut. [518] She had been spending all her strength on the climb up, with nothing left for the paths down. [536]

Behind them, muffled and faint, the silver bell sounded once. [546] Teodora stood at the arbor's far end, where she had evidently been standing for some time. [562]

"You knew," Luka whispered. [566]

"I knew someone on this ridge was giving more than they had," Teodora said. [580] "I did not need to know who." [587] She nodded at the flour. [592] "Carry it down, Luka, and let Irina show you the path." [603]

That night they went down together, the old woman's hand on the boy's shoulder, and no one in the valley heard a thing. [626]

The next evening, with every family gathered under the vines, Teodora declared a change to her rule. [643] "The bell will no longer stay with me," she said, and placed it in Luka's palm. [659] "Whoever carries tonight rings it tomorrow, and teaches the next one the paths." [672] "And the flour belongs to no one, so it belongs to all of us." [686]

Luka did not look at Irina, and Irina did not look at him. [699] That was how they kept the secret. [706] At midnight he rang it, wool still wrapped around the clapper, and across the ridge old and young stepped out together into the listening dark. [731]
