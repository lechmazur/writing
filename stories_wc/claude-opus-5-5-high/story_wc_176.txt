The sanctum had been cut into the glacier so long ago that its doorway now leaned like a tired shoulder, and Ilse had to duck beneath a lintel of blue ice to enter. [33] Overhead, through a skylight of clear frozen water, the aurora moved in green folds, and its light fell on the rope. [54] It hung from the old bell in the center of the chamber, thick as her wrist, braided from sinew, wool, and something like hair, older than any keeper's record. [83] Every braid in it meant a name. [90] Raised strands caught the clapper's lever in a set order when the rope was pulled, and the tolling sounded out the dead of the ice villages, one by one, so that no drowned fisher or frozen child would go unspoken. [130] But the rope had been neglected for eleven winters, and its braids had slackened and slipped, and now when the wind moved it the bell only muttered. [157] Ilse knew exactly how many winters, because she was the reason. [168]

She had been the keeper's apprentice then, a lost wax caster by trade, sent north to recast the bell's cracked clapper. [189] She had rushed the burnout, left moisture in the mold, and the bronze had spat and shattered, scalding the keeper's hands so badly that he never braided again. [217] He died the next spring without teaching anyone the pattern, and she had fled south, certain that the names had died with him because of her. [243] It had taken her ten years in a city foundry, casting doorknobs and saints, to understand that the keeper had forgiven her before the burns had healed. [270] It had taken one more year to do the same for herself. [282]

She pulled off her mittens and let her breath warm her fingers. [294] From inside her coat she took the scrap: a stiff strip of street organ card, brown at the edges, its holes punched in neat uneven rows. [320] Her father had cranked an organ on the harbor streets of Vell, and when she was small he had played one tune more than any other, a slow, strange, stepping melody he said a northern sailor had taught him. [359] The sailor had hummed the bell's tolling from memory, name after name, and her father had punched it into card so he would not lose it. [385] Most of that card had gone to mice and damp. [395] This hand-span was all she had found in his trunk after his funeral, and it had been months before she recognized what it was: a transcribed memory of the keeper's bell, carried south by one homesick man and kept alive in a beggar's instrument. [439]

Now she held the scrap up to the skylight. [448] The aurora poured through the punched holes and threw a small constellation of green onto the ice wall, and in that light the pattern was plain. [474] Hole meant raised strand; blank meant sunken. [481] She had to illuminate the rope before she dared touch it, so she tilted the card until the green points fell across the frayed braids, marking where each ought to rise. [512]

Then she began. [515] Her foreman in the foundry had once called her technically gentle, which was not quite a compliment; he meant that her tenderness was a skill she had practiced, not a mood she fell into. [549] She used it now. [553] Old fibers could not be forced. [559] She warmed each braid in her palms until the frost let go, loosened the slipped strand with a bone pick, and coaxed it back over its neighbor, the way she would ease a wax model from a rubber mold without tearing a single eyelash. [603] Meltwater from her hands dripped onto the floor, and the ice beneath her knees began to shine. [620]

It took the whole night. [625] The card covered only a stretch of the rope, perhaps a dozen names, but once those braids stood right she could feel the logic of the weave running on beyond them, the way a melody tells you where it wants to go. [667] She followed it down the length of the rope, humming her father's tune and letting it lead her fingers. [686]

When the sky had faded to pale rose, she stood, took the rope in both hands, and pulled. [704] The bell spoke. [707] It was not music, exactly, but it had syllables in it, hard and soft strokes rising like a voice calling across water: Aska, Tovvi, Maret. [732] Name after name rolled out over the glacier, and last came one she had woven in herself, a single new braid for the keeper, Orrin. [757] Ilse wiped her eyes and opened her pack; she would cast a bronze copy of every braid, and this time she would wait for the mold to dry. [785]
