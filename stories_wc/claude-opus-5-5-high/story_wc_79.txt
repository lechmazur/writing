By noon the light on the shell-streets was white enough to bleach the shadows out of doorways, and Nerit Osk walked the ridges of the Eldest Turtle with her chalk and her measuring cord. [34] She was a spiral-shell cartographer, which meant she mapped the growth whorls of the three gargantuan turtles on whose backs the city of Ammon Drift was built. [61] Every year the scutes widened by a finger's breadth, and every year a street shifted, a well tilted, a family woke to find their kitchen had crept into a neighbor's courtyard. [92] Nerit's maps kept the arguments small. [98] She did not argue with anyone herself. [105] She measured, and wrote, and went home, and in the evenings she looked east. [119]

The watchers stood there on the horizon, twelve of them, pale as salt and taller than the masts of any ship that had ever tied up to the turtles' flanks. [149] They had appeared the summer the turtles began to swim more slowly. [161] They never moved, and in the glare they seemed to face the city no matter which way the city turned. [181]

Eleven years ago her brother Tamsin had led eleven others out through the Brass Gate in a reed boat to ask them what they wanted. [206] Each of them carried a reed whistle cut from the marsh that grew in the Eldest's neck-hollow, because Tamsin believed the watchers might not understand speech but would understand pattern. [236] The turtles spoke in pattern, after all; everyone knew the scute-whorls repeated like verses. [250]

The expedition never came back. [255] The council waited a year, then two, and after the third they stopped oiling the Gate. [271] Salt did the rest. [275] Now it was a red lump fused to its hinges, and no one mentioned the east at all. [293]

Nerit had spent those eleven years learning the pattern languages Tamsin had only guessed at. [308] The whorls on the Eldest's shell were not decoration. [317] They were grammar: ridge meant motion, groove meant stillness, a doubled spiral meant a question held open. [334] She had filled four ledgers with them and told no one, because no one wanted to know. [351]

That noon, measuring the newest ring near the eastern rim, she found a crack. [365] It ran straight through the growth line like a sentence struck out, and wedged deep inside it, grey and brittle, was a reed whistle. [389] Tamsin's initials were burned into the mouthpiece. [396] The shell had grown around it. [402]

Nerit sat down on the hot bone of the ridge and did not cry. [416] She traced the new whorls outward from the crack instead, reading them the way she had taught herself, and her chalk slowed. [438] The pattern was a reply. [443] The turtle had heard the whistle, years ago, and answered it the only way a turtle could, by growing the answer into itself. [466] Ridge, ridge, doubled spiral, groove. [471] We are swimming toward them, it said. [478] They were riders once, as you are. [485] They stand where we set them down. [492]

Nerit looked east. [495] The watchers had not moved, but they were larger than they had been in the spring, and the noon glare made haloes of their shoulders. [520] Or closer. [522] For eleven years the city had believed the turtles were tiring. [533] They were only steering. [537]

She could take this to the council, and they would call it a cartographer's fever and send her home to rest in the shade. [561] She could pry at the Gate until her hands bled, and it would not open. [576] But the turtle had answered Tamsin's question, not the watchers, and Nerit wanted to hear it from them. [594]

She worked the whistle loose, gently, so the crack would not spread. [606] She blew the dust from it. [612] Then she stood on the eastern rim, where the shell sloped down into glittering water, and played the doubled spiral: a rising note, a falling note, the rise again, held open. [643] Do you want us? [647]

The sound was thin and cracked, and the sea swallowed it. [658] For a long while nothing happened. [664] Gulls wheeled. [666] Somewhere behind her a child laughed in a courtyard that would be a street by winter. [682]

Then, far off, the nearest watcher lifted one pale hand, slow as a tide, and she saw that it held something small and grey against its lips. [709] The note reached her a full minute later, bleached by distance: ridge, ridge, groove. [723] Come. [724] Come. [725] Stay. [726]

Under her feet, the Eldest Turtle shifted its great head toward the horizon, and the whole bright city leaned with it. [747] Nerit uncapped her chalk and began, quietly, to map the way. [758]
