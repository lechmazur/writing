The grotto sat above the clouds like a held breath, a cave mouth so deep in the mountain's shadow that the sun had never once touched its floor. [28] Still the vines grew there, pale and patient, feeding on what the villagers carried up the switchback path. [46] They carried their dead. [50]

Ilse had tended the Umbral Vineyard for thirty-one years, and in that time she had laid four hundred bodies beneath the roots, pressed the grey grapes that rose from them, and sent the wine down to be touched to the lips of every newborn in the valley. [97] What went into the dark came back as breath. [106] That was the whole of her faith, and it had always been enough. [119]

Tonight the body on the laying stone was Aurel Venn's. [129]

Twenty years ago, drunk and furious over a debt, he had set fire to the press-house at the foot of the path, not knowing her mother slept inside. [157] He had dug through the ashes himself the next morning and come up with only one thing, which he placed in Ilse's hand without a word: her mother's antique hairpin, silver gone black, its head shaped like a curled vine leaf. [198]

It had taken her eleven years to forgive him. [207] She had done it on this path, meeting him halfway, and he had wept into the fog while she held his shaking hands. [230] She had expected the forgiving to feel like the end of a long climb. [244] Instead it had felt like arriving at a door and finding no room behind it. [259] Nine years since, and she was still waiting for the contentment the priests had promised, still wondering where it lived. [279]

Aurel's widow had stood at the grotto's lip an hour ago, twisting her shawl. [293] "The council says he should go in the far rows," she had whispered, "away from your mother. [310] They say you shouldn't have to bear it." [318]

Ilse had not answered. [322] She had gone, as she always did when a thing sat crosswise in her, to the old shade-oak that grew from a crack at the cave mouth, its roots drinking cloud. [353] On the ground her thoughts ran in ruts; twenty feet up, with the white sea of cloud below and the dark of the vineyard behind, they loosened and found new ways. [384] She had climbed until the branches thinned and sat listening to the vineyard hush, that particular silence of roots working and fruit slowly filling, of patience that did not need to be watched. [417] The vines did not choose what they drank. [425] They took the gentle and the cruel alike and made the same grey grapes, and the same wine wet every infant's mouth. [447]

She had come down knowing. [452]

Now she unrolled the linen and began to scallop its hem, as custom demanded, one curve for every year of the dead one's life. [476] She scored each arc first with the point of her mother's hairpin, then followed it with the shears. [494] Fifty-eight curves. [496] The scallops let the cloth unravel faster in the soil, so that nothing stood long between a body and the roots that wanted it. [520] Her hand was steady; she noticed that it was steady. [530]

When the widow returned with two men and a lantern, Ilse had already dug the grave, and it was in the first row, an arm's length from her mother's stone. [560]

"You don't have to," the widow said. [567]

"I know," Ilse said, and smiled at her so the woman would not have to carry the refusal home as a wound. [589] "This is where the oldest roots are. [596] He'll come back sooner here." [601]

They lowered him together. [605] Before the first spade of earth, Ilse knelt and slid the antique hairpin through the linen above his heart, where the cloth would give way first. [631] She had carried it for twenty years, first as proof of what he had taken, then as proof of what she had forgiven. [654] Either way, she had been holding it. [661] That was the trouble. [665] Forgiveness kept in a fist was only a smaller kind of grievance. [677] The silver would blacken and sink into the roots, and in some autumn she would not live to see, a child in the valley would taste her mother and Aurel together in one drop of grey wine. [714]

The men filled the grave and left. [721] Ilse did not climb the oak that night. [729] She sat on the cool earth between the two graves and listened to the vines drinking, and found that the contentment she had searched for was not waiting behind any door. [760] It was here, in the open hand, in the slow dark work of letting go. [775]

Above the clouds, the grotto held its breath, and let it out. [787]
