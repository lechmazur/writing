The sun had been gone for fifty-one days, and today at noon it would touch the rim of the crater for exactly four minutes. [24] The people of Hollin called those minutes the held breath, because even the wind stopped to watch. [41] Mirela Ost walked down into the crater through the withered orchard carrying her kit, a tin of wax, and a copper plate wrapped in flannel. [66] The apple trees stood in their frozen rows like people who had forgotten why they had come to a room. [86] Old Tomas Fenn was waiting in his hut at the center, where the stone had fallen ten thousand years ago. [106] He had planted the orchard as a young man, when the crater still held a little warmth, and he had watched it wither branch by branch as the warmth leaked away. [137] Now his heart was leaking away too. [144]

"Lina will come back someday," he said when Mirela knelt beside his cot. [157] "She will want to find the trees I loved, and I cannot draw anymore." [171]

Mirela was a flash-paper alchemist, the last one in the north. [182] She made her paper from cotton and acid and a pinch of ground meteor glass dug from the crater floor, and when it burned it vanished in a single white breath of light. [215] Her mother had taught her that the glass was safekept starlight, a gleam that had crossed the dark for an age and then slept inside stone, waiting to be spent. [245] She had spent eleven years learning to translate heartbeats into maps. [256] A heart, she believed, remembered the paths it had quickened on, the places it had slowed in, and if you listened long enough, the rhythm drew the ground. [284]

She unwrapped the copper plate. [289] It had come from the Hollin printer after he shut his shop, a storybook illustration plate for a tale never finished, and it showed a girl standing in an orchard with stars caught in the branches like fruit. [327] Tomas smiled when he saw it. [333] "That was supposed to be this place," he said. [342]

"It still is," Mirela answered, and laid two fingers on his wrist. [354]

Then she did not move. [359] She had trained her body for this the way other people trained for running. [373] Her knees pressed the frozen floor, her shoulders loosened, her own pulse sank until it barely stirred, so that only his would speak through her fingertips. [399] An hour passed, then two. [404] Frost climbed the inside of the window and her legs went numb, and still her hand rested on him as lightly as a leaf on water. [430]

With her free hand she laid out, across the copper plate, what the crater had thrown away. [447] A rusted nail for a strong beat, a button for a faint one, a split bottle cap where the rhythm stumbled, seeds from shriveled apples for the long pauses between. [477] Across the engraving the discards spread into rows and curves, and slowly the pattern stopped looking like a heartbeat and started looking like a path. [502] Here the pulse leapt, and the line bent toward the east slope, where, Tomas whispered, he had once kissed his wife beneath the first blossoms. [527] Here it slowed to almost nothing beside the third row, where his son was buried. [542] Here it ran quick and bright along the rim trail, where he had carried Lina on his shoulders to see the sun come back. [566]

At noon the light arrived. [571] Through the window a thin gold edge touched the crater rim, and the whole world seemed to stop, no wind, no creak of timber, only the old man's heart under her fingers. [603]

Mirela laid a sheet of flash paper over the pattern and touched it with a match. [619] The paper vanished in one silent white flare. [627] For an instant the hut held more light than the sun could give, the old starlight finally spent, and every nail and button and seed threw a sharp shadow onto the copper. [659] When she brushed the discards away, the shadows had burned into the plate as fine dark lines, winding between the engraved trees, stitching the girl in the picture to every place he had loved. [693]

Tomas's pulse beat once more against her fingers, slow and content, and then it rested. [708] Mirela kept her hand there until the gold left the rim. [719]

Then she warmed the wax over the stove and poured it across the copper, sealing the map beneath a clear amber skin so no frost or hand could wear it away. [750] She wrapped it in flannel and wrote Lina's name on the cloth. [762] Outside, the withered trees stood still in the returning dark, waiting, she thought, for someone to come home. [780]
