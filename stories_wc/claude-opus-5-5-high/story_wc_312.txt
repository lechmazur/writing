The sapphire viaduct wept at dawn, the way it had every morning since the last fish was caught. [18] Tobin Aske sat on its rim with his boots dangling over the dry town and counted drops. [35] He was a mage the way some men are husbands: faithfully, and without much heat. [50] His fire had never risen past the temperature of bathwater, and his belief in the Tide Ledger had cooled to match. [71]

Pell Harrow climbed the service ladder with the bell in a flour sack. [84] She was eleven and believed everything she had ever been told, which Tobin found exhausting and, lately, contagious. [102]

"Da says you can fix it," she said, tipping the pieces onto the blue stone. [117]

"Da says a lot of things." [123]

The bell had hung on the Constance for sixty years, rung once for every fish hauled aboard, so the sea would know what it was owed. [149] That was the Ledger: take a fish, sound a note, and the deep would send another to balance the sum. [169] Pell's father had pulled up the last fish in the world, a grey cod no longer than his forearm, and swung the clapper so hard in his grief that the bell split into four curved shards. [205] The note never finished. [209] The sea never answered. [213] Since then the ocean had been climbing the viaduct and leaking through its sapphire seams, as if it meant to leave. [234]

Tobin picked up a shard and felt the cold of it, the old deep-water cold that bronze never lost. [253] A real mage would melt the pieces and recast them. [263] He could only coax metal soft enough to remember its shape, and only if he knew how long it had been apart. [285]

"Eleven weeks," Pell said. [289]

"Not to the bell." [293] He pointed at the drops beading beneath the arch, falling past the chimneys toward the empty market. [310] "Bronze keeps sea time." [314] "Those drops are the only clock the sea has left." [324]

He had been counting them for a week out of a professional curiosity he refused to call hope. [342] Nine drops a minute at the first arch, seven at the second, five at the third, and the intervals between them were not random. [366] Laid end to end, they slowed the way a ringing bell slows, each pause a little longer than the last, a note decaying across a span of stone. [394] The sea was still hearing the unfinished toll, stretched thin, waiting for it to end so it could reply. [413] By his arithmetic, the last drop would fall this morning. [423] After that there would be no note to finish and no sea to finish it for. [439]

"So we fix it before then," Pell said, as though it were a chore. [453]

Tobin nearly told her the truth: that the Ledger was a story fishermen told to feel less like thieves, that his own devotion had cracked years ago and he had only kept it on the shelf because throwing it out felt rude. [495] But she was already fitting the shards together, and her faith was so whole and so thin he could see light through it. [518] He did not want to be the thing that broke it. [529]

So he warmed his hands. [534]

His lukewarm magic, useless for forges, was exactly right for this: bronze softened at blood heat into something like cold honey, and each shard began to lean toward its neighbors. [564] He pressed the seams in time with the drops, one push per fall, matching nine, then seven, then five, rebuilding the bell at the pace of its own fading note. [594] Too fast and the metal would forget; too slow and the sea would. [607] Pell held the clapper and counted aloud, getting it wrong twice and correcting herself without embarrassment. [623]

The sun came up behind the arches and turned the whole viaduct the color of deep water seen from underneath. [643] The last seam closed as the last drop gathered, trembling, under the third arch. [657] The bell was whole, though four bright scars ran down it like a map of roads. [673]

"You ring it," Tobin said. [678] "It was your father's fish." [683]

Pell struck once, gently, and the note that came out was not new but the old one, finally allowed to end. [704]

The drop fell. [707]

Then another, faster, and another, and the channel at their backs began to murmur as water ran the right way, downhill, home. [729] Something small and silver flicked in the current, going with it. [740]

Tobin watched it go and felt his belief, cracked and mended and still cracked, hold its shape one more morning. [760] That, he decided, was probably what devotion had always been. [770]
