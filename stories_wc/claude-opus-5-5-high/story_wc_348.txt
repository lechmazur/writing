The river came down the ravine in seven arguments, and Mara Quist had spent the morning listening to them disagree. [20] That was how her mind worked, how it had always worked, even through twenty years as a polymer chemist: she heard systems as orchestras. [44] The widest channel carried the melody, fast and cold with meltwater. [55] The smaller braids ran counterpoint around gravel bars, splitting, rejoining, splitting again, and somewhere under all of it the stones kept a low continuo. [79]

Her sister used to laugh at her for it. [88] "You can't just enjoy a creek, can you," Tess had said, the last summer they came here together. [106] "You have to score it." [111]

That was eleven years ago, before the divergent path, before Mara took the job at Veratech and Tess stayed in her underfunded lab breeding silk moths. [137] They had planned to build something together: a fiber with the strength of Mara's polymers and the gentleness of Tess's silk, a thread that would hold for a decade and then quietly return to the earth. [173] Then Veratech offered money, and Mara said yes, and the sisters stopped speaking in any way that mattered. [191] Mara's coatings went into fishing line, grocery film, the linings of a billion cups. [205] Tess's moths went nowhere. [209]

Tess died in March, of an aneurysm, at her bench. [219]

The grief had been a fist for months. [227] Now, in September, it had opened into something more like a hand, and Mara could carry it without it carrying her. [248] In her jacket pocket was the thing the lab manager had given her when she cleared out Tess's desk: a glass vial holding an empty chrysalis, split neatly down its back, with a strip of tape that read, in Tess's cramped hand, FOR M. WHEN SHE'S READY. [295] Mara did not know what she was supposed to be ready for. [307] She had brought it here because this was the last place they had been sisters without conditions. [324]

She crouched on a gravel bar and watched the foam. [334] Wherever two braids met, a line of white froth marked the seam, and she began, idly, to trace those lines with her finger in the air. [360] The foam did not simply merge. [366] It laced. [368] Each seam stretched downstream in a long braid of its own, strands from the left channel and the right channel twisting over one another, holding together for twenty meters before loosening and releasing. [401] Strong where they crossed, weak where they ran alone. [410]

She stopped breathing for a moment. [416]

For years she and Tess had argued about blending, about whether silk protein and synthetic chain could be mixed into one material. [438] They couldn't; the phases always separated, oil and water, sister and sister. [450] But the river was not blending. [456] It was letting each strand stay itself and cross-linking them only at the seams, at intervals, so the whole could hold and still come apart when the current changed. [485] As the pieces fell into place, she heard it the way she heard everything: two instruments, never unison, meeting on chosen notes. [507] A block copolymer, braided rather than stirred, with silk segments and synthetic segments joined by bonds that water and time could eventually cut. [530]

She took out the vial and looked at the chrysalis again, really looked. [543] The split along its back was not a wound. [552] It was a door the moth had made by dissolving what it no longer needed and keeping what it did. [572] Tess had known. [575] Of course Tess had known; she had spent her life watching things take themselves apart in order to become. [594] She had left the answer where Mara would have to go find it, and she had left the door open. [614]

Mara laughed, and then she cried, and the two did not fight each other the way they would have in spring. [635]

She knew what atoning would look like, and it was not guilt. [647] It was work. [650] She would resign from Veratech. [655] She would take Tess's moths and Tess's notebooks and her own stubborn chemistry, and she would build the braided fiber, and she would publish it openly under both their names, Tess's first. [687] Not to erase the eleven years they had spent apart. [697] To make those years the separate strands that let the thing hold. [709]

She set the empty chrysalis on a flat stone at the edge of the confluence, where the foam laced tightest. [729] Then, on second thought, she put it back in her pocket. [740] Tess would want to be in the lab. [748]

Downstream, the channels argued and agreed and argued again, and for the first time since March, Mara heard the whole score. [769]
