Oren Vasse brokered light for a living, but he had not trusted air in two years. [16] His shop beneath the viaduct was a thicket of hanging lanterns, brass carbide lamps, paper globes, the squat violet ones that miners and forgers bought, and he moved among them with a linen scarf folded over his nose. [54] Customers assumed he was ill. [59] He was only careful. [63] He crossed the street to avoid bakeries, held his breath past the florist, and had once abandoned a full cart of stock at the market because a woman in a green coat walked by trailing a scent he knew, and he had wept against a pillar like a boy. [112] Photographs of Mara did not do that to him. [121] Her letters did not. [125] Only smells ambushed him, and he wanted, more than he wanted comfort, to know why. [140] In his coat pocket he carried her crystal perfume vial, stoppered, unopened since the funeral, heavy as a held breath. [160]

The order from the Ashgrove Conservatory came on a Thursday: one ultraviolet lantern, strongest available, delivered by nightfall. [178] Ashgrove was the glass house on the hill where Dr. Ilse Kade grew plants that no longer existed, coaxing them back from pressed herbarium leaves and seeds found in the stomachs of museum birds. [212] Oren climbed the path at dusk with the lantern wrapped in felt and his scarf tied tight. [229] Inside, the panes held the last light like ice holds a fish, and every frond threw a sharp, faceted shadow across the gravel. [252] Labels stood in the beds like small headstones: Saint Helena olive, cry violet, a fern last seen in 1840. [271]

Dr. Kade was a narrow woman with soil on her cuffs, and she led him to a bench where a single pale bud trembled on a stalk. [298] "Night orris," she said. [302] "Extinct since the forties. [306] Tonight it finishes turning from bud to flower, and I need to see its nectar guides the moment it opens. [326] Bees saw them; we can't, not without help." [334]

He unwrapped the lantern and thumbed the switch. [342] The greenhouse went violet and strange, white labels blazing, dust motes turning to sparks. [356] Something in his pocket glowed through the wool. [364] He drew out the vial. [369] Under the ultraviolet, a dried film on its inner walls burned a speckled gold. [383] On the bench, the seams of the splitting bud flared the identical shade. [396]

Dr. Kade took the vial from his hand and held it beside the flower, and her breath caught. [414] "Where did you get this?" [419]

"It was my wife's," he said. [425] "An old perfumer on Calder Street made it for her wedding, from the last oil he had." [442]

"Then your wife wore night orris. [448] I rebuilt this plant from pressed leaves, but pressed leaves don't keep a smell. [462] I never knew if I'd gotten it right." [470]

The bud creaked, a sound like a knuckle, and the first petal peeled back. [484] Oren understood what was coming and stepped away, one hand pressing the scarf to his face. [500] "I can't," he said. [504] "Every time I smell her, I'm in the room again. [514] A picture is a window, but a smell is a door, and it shuts behind me." [530]

Dr. Kade did not argue. [535] "Scent is the oldest sense," she said quietly. [543] "It goes straight to the parts of us that never learned the past tense. [557] That's why it hurts. [561] That's also why it's the only way back." [569]

The second petal fell open, then the third, and in the violet light the gold guides spread across the flower like a map drawn for someone small and certain. [598] As the transformation completed, the last petal settled with a sigh, and the air changed. [613] Even through linen, it reached him: cold pear, wet stone, something like the inside of her wrist. [630]

Oren untied the scarf. [634]

He breathed, and Mara was laughing in their narrow kitchen, and Mara was asleep with a book on her chest, and Mara was in the hospital bed asking him to open the window, and all of it was now, and all of it was gone. [679] He grieved then, finally, fully, kneeling on the gravel among lost things called back to life, and Dr. Kade stood beside him with the lantern and let him. [707]

When he could speak, he asked, "Did you get it right?" [718]

She uncorked the vial, and for a moment two scents met in the glass house, the remembered and the returned. [738] They were the same. [742]

Oren wiped his face and did not put the scarf back on. [754] Around them the panes held the violet glow, and every leaf cast its crystal shadow, sharp and clear, the way a memory does when you finally let it in. [783]
