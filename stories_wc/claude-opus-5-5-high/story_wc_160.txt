The amphitheater inhaled, and Orsa Vell felt the cold draft tug her collar as it slid down the tiers toward the vents beneath the stage. [25] Every eleven minutes the dormant volcano breathed through those vents, drawing air in, holding it, then exhaling a warm sulfur sigh that loosened the mortar and made the whole crumbling bowl creak like a moored ship. [61] Orsa had spent twenty years keeping that breath in rhythm. [71] She was the floodgate machinist, the ingenious one who tuned the bronze sluices under the orchestra pit so the crater lake on the upper shelf drained slowly instead of all at once. [103]

Tomorrow the town would vote. [108] The farmers wanted the gates thrown wide, the lake emptied, the lakebed turned to rich black soil. [125] The Keepers wanted the gates welded shut, because the lake's weight pressed the amphitheater's cracked walls together like hands, and without it the tiers where their grandparents had sung would slump into rubble. [157] Both sides were right, which was the worst kind of argument. [168] Orsa wanted, more than she wanted sleep, to find the place where both rights could stand. [184]

Her teacher, Hollis, might have settled it, but Hollis had died in the spring, leaving her his tools, his antique surveyor's chain, and an envelope addressed to the Council. [213] Councilor Dace had opened it at the funeral, found a single blank sheet, and laughed that the old man had lost his wits at the end. [239] Orsa had kept the empty envelope anyway. [246]

Now she laid it on the stage's cracked marble and waited for the exhale. [260] When it came, warm and sour, it softened the strip of gum along the torn flap, and she leaned close with her loupe. [283] Hollis never bought glue; he sealed everything with pine resin cut with lamp oil, and he wrote with a steel nib that bled. [306] Pressed into the amber residue, mirrored and faint, were numerals. [316] He had not written on the paper at all. [325] He had written on the wet resin itself before sealing the flap, and when Dace tore it open, the ink had stayed trapped in the gum, where only someone who knew his habits would think to look. [362]

Her hands began to tremble. [367] It was encryption, but not the kind meant to hide a secret from enemies. [381] Hollis had hidden it from the argument itself, knowing that any plan handed openly to the Council would be seized by one faction and burned by the other. [409] He had tucked it where only patience and affection would find it. [421]

The figures were link counts. [426] She uncoiled the surveyor's chain, one hundred iron links with brass tallies every ten, sixty-six feet of her teacher's careful life. [447] Anchoring the first handle at the center of the stage, she paced up the aisle, counting: forty-three links to the seventh tier, then ninety turning east, then twelve down. [476] The chain trembled in the next inhalation, singing faintly against the stone, and stopped at a seat she knew. [495]

It was the seat where she had sat at nine years old, swinging her boots, watching Hollis rebuild the main sluice while he pretended not to notice her. [523] Beneath it, behind a loose facing stone, she found a rusted wheel. [535]

She understood before she turned it. [541] A second channel, older than the floodgates, ran from this tier to the eastern slope. [556] Opened halfway, it would lower the lake enough to expose the high terraces for planting while leaving sufficient weight on the walls to hold the amphitheater together. [583] Not everything for anyone; enough for everyone. [590]

Scratched on the wheel's hub, in Hollis's cramped hand, were five words: Measure twice, forgive them once. [607] Orsa laughed, and the laugh broke into something wetter. [616] Slyly heartfelt, that was him, burying his tenderness under a joke so no one could accuse him of it. [635]

At dawn she brought the Council and the Keepers into the amphitheater together, both groups scowling from opposite tiers. [654] She did not give a speech. [660] She laid the chain along its path, let each of them walk it, and showed them the resin under a lamp. [681] Dace read the mirrored numbers and went quiet. [689]

When the volcano exhaled, Orsa set her shoulder to the wheel and turned it halfway. [704] Far below, water began to move through a throat of stone no one living had heard. [720] Light came through the fissures in the rim, falling in coins across the worn seats, gold and shadow and gold again. [741] The walls creaked, settled, and held. [747] She coiled the chain slowly, link by link, and waited for the next breath. [761]
