Above the clouds, where the air was thin enough to ring, the Library of Hesk kept its windows sealed with frost. [21] Ines Varo, orderly records chief and the last of its staff, preferred it that way. [36] Cold kept glass honest. [40]

On forty thousand shelves, in felt-lined drawers, lay the magic lantern slides of the valley below: every oath, marriage promise, apology, and deathbed pledge that the old lanterners had caught in their brass cameras and fixed onto painted glass. [79] Held before a flame, each slide threw its vow onto the reading wall, not as words but as light shaped by intent: a ring of amber for fidelity, a blue arch for forgiveness, a trembling white for a promise made in fear. [121] The valley called them luminous vows, back when the valley still sent anyone up the mountain. [137]

Now the lanterners were gone, and the projections were fading. [147] Ines had noticed it first in the drawers from the Year of Floods, whose amber rings had gone the color of weak tea. [170] Then the fading spread, drawer by drawer, until whole decades threw nothing onto the wall but a faint gray breath, like frost on the inside of an eyelid. [198] The images were becoming what they had always secretly been: memories that no one was holding. [214]

She could not stop it, so she decided to measure it. [225]

Ines was gently meticulous in all things, from the way she thawed ink over a candle to the way she lifted a slide by its wooden frame, never by the glass. [256] She built her instrument from what the library had: a quartz tuning rod, a lens, and a copper plate that hummed when light struck it. [281] Every vow, she discovered, had a pitch. [288] A fresh promise sang high and clear, almost too fine to hear; a failing one sank toward a low, patient drone. [309] Testing every slide would take longer than the light had left, so she worked by sampling frequencies: one slide in fifty, touched to the rod, its note written in her ledger beside date and drawer. [344] Plotted across the pages, the notes made a slow descending staircase toward silence. [357]

She told herself the ledger was for whoever came next. [367] The truth was quieter: if she could chart how meaning left the world, perhaps she could learn to call some of it back, to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion before the edge swallowed the shelves. [404]

In the ninth week, the staircase broke. [411]

Drawer 2291, from the year she arrived as a girl, gave the usual drone at every sampled interval. [429] Except one. [431] When the thirty-first slide touched quartz, the copper plate rang so high and bright that frost cracked from the nearest window. [452]

Ines sat very still. [456] Then she lit the reading lamp and slid the glass into the gate. [469]

On the wall bloomed a small, stubborn star, the pale gold of candle wax, with a thread of blue running through it like a vein. [494] She knew the shape before she read the card. [503] At twelve, orphaned by fever and carried up the mountain in a mule basket, she had stood before the old chief's camera and promised, in a voice she barely owned, that she would keep what others let go. [541] She had forgotten the words long ago. [548] She had never stopped keeping them. [554]

The understanding arrived the way cold does, all at once and from everywhere. [567] The slides were not failing; the vows were. [575] A promise shone only while someone still carried it, and the valley had long since set its promises down. [594] Glass could hold the shape of a vow, but not its weight. [606]

Ines closed the ledger. [610]

She opened the drawer from the Year of Floods and lifted the first slide by its frame. [627] Its card read: Teodor Ansel, to his brother, I will rebuild the mill. [640] The brother was surely dead, the mill surely moss. [649] But the vow was here, and she was here, and her own promise had never specified whose things she would keep. [670]

She read the words aloud into the quartz hush of the stacks, slowly, as if taking them into her own mouth for safekeeping. [693] Then she copied them into a fresh ledger, and beneath them she wrote, in careful ink: Kept. [710]

When she touched the slide to the rod, the drone lifted, barely, by a single hair of pitch. [728]

It was not much. [732] It was a note higher than oblivion. [739]

Ines reached for the second slide, and outside, beneath the frozen windows, the clouds went on moving like a slow white sea that had forgotten its shore. [766]
