The guardian of the Library of Sleepers had forgotten its name, but it had not forgotten the rules. [18] Nobody entered except through the veil of sleep, and nobody left carrying more than they brought. [34] It kept those rules from the frozen garden behind the reading room, where the lamplight stopped and the mist began. [54] Once it had worn a cardigan and stamped due dates, or it believed so, but centuries of guarding had worn it down to something simpler. [79] Now it went on knuckles and bare feet, its hair a matted hood of frost, its teeth good for tearing the spines off stolen books. [104] It did not speak. [108] It listened to the hedges tick as the ice thickened on them. [120]

The garden was where the light gave up. [128] You could see the boundary if you knew how to look: a seam in the fog where gold turned to pewter, where a rose was a rose on one side and only a rumor on the other. [165] The guardian had come from the rumor side, long ago, before the library caught it. [180] It remembered nothing of that place except the ache, a homesickness not for a town or a person but for a direction nobody could point to, a dimension folded somewhere behind the mist. [213]

So it did what it had always done with things it could not reach: it took them inside. [231] Each night it breathed the fog until its lungs were gray, memorized the angle of every frozen branch, and shelved them in its chest the way it once shelved atlases. [261] The outside became a room it carried. [268] It was the only map it trusted. [275]

The girl arrived in her nightgown, sleepwalking through the reading room, clutching a pendant on a black cord. [293] The guardian smelled it before it saw it: hot brass, lamp oil, zeal. [306] It knew the sigil, an eye with no lid. [315] The Order of the Unshuttered Lamp had tried to burn through the garden a hundred years ago, chanting that darkness was only ignorance and ignorance was a sin. [343] The guardian had bitten three of them awake. [351]

"My grandmother's," the girl said, not quite asleep, not quite anything. [362] "She said it shows you what's past the edge." [371] "She said nobody should have to wonder." [378]

She held it up to the seam, and a needle of light pierced the mist. [393] For one breath the guardian saw it: a slope of blue shadows, a sky with too many angles, the shape of a door it had once slept beside. [421] Home. [422] Then the light touched it, and the blue went flat and gray, and the angles straightened into nothing, and the door was only a place where frost had cracked. [451]

The guardian understood, with the sharp clarity that sometimes surfaces in dreams, why the cultists had failed. [468] What lived past the boundary of light could not survive being looked at directly. [482] Certainty was a kind of weather that killed it. [491]

It closed its hand over the girl's, over the pendant, and the needle went out. [506] Slowly the blue returned to the far fog, not visible, only possible. [518]

"Don't you want to see it?" the girl asked. [527]

The guardian had not spoken in a century. [535] Its voice came out rusted. [540] "I want it to stay." [545]

It had spent so long hungering to know the way back that it had never tried simply keeping company with the not-knowing, the way one keeps company with a sleeping animal, careful and warm. [579] Now it wanted to try. [584]

It could not let the lamp burn here again, so it did what it always did with the world it could not hold. [607] It swallowed the pendant, cord and all, and felt the small hot light settle among the gray branches in its chest, a lamp in a room with no windows, harmless there, even kind. [640] Inside, light had edges, and the edges were its own. [650]

The girl blinked, thinning, already half returned to her bed. [660] "Will you still be here?" [665]

"I don't know," said the guardian, and for the first time the words did not hurt. [681]

When she was gone it sat down at the seam where gold met pewter, its back to the lamps, its face to the mist. [705] The hedges ticked. [708] Somewhere beyond sight, a door might have been opening, or might not. [720] The guardian waited, the way you wait for a friend whose face you have not yet learned, and found that the waiting was warm. [744]
