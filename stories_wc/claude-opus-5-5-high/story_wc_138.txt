Vessaline had been alive for three thousand years, and for the last two hundred of them she had been bored in the particular way of someone who has already read every ending. [32] Except one. [34]

She lived beneath the northern glacier in a hollow of quartz and ice that her mortal friend Tamsin had named the Prism, a hideout where every lamp broke into seven colors and every color leaned a little into a different direction of space. [77] Vessaline could see those directions. [82] She had always been dimensionally aware, able to perceive the folded corners behind corners, the rooms that the eye slid past, the way a doorway was really a knot. [111] It was why she had chosen the Prism: its geometry was honest enough to show its seams. [128]

Tamsin had found her by accident at nineteen, a surveyor chasing a compass needle that would not settle, and had kept coming back for forty-one years. [154] She brought bad wine and good arguments. [161] She was the only person in a millennium who had made Vessaline wonder what came next. [177] Then, one winter, Tamsin had stood at the mouth of the cave with snow in her grey hair and said, "Goodbye, Vess," and walked down the mountain, and did not come back. [209] The villagers below said she died that spring. [217]

Vessaline had heard ten thousand goodbyes, and she had always been the one who stayed. [232] She knew everything about the moment before a goodbye and nothing at all about the moment after. [249]

The reason hung at her throat. [255] It was a fragment of the amulet that had made her immortal, a crescent of black iron no longer than her thumb, and its curse was simple: wherever its bearer stood, space closed around her like a fist. [293] She could walk, but she could not leave. [301] Death was a direction, and the fragment forbade it. [310] The other pieces had been melted down centuries ago by people who had thought they were being kind. [328]

So she had begun, quietly, to plot against the room. [338] It was the first intrigue she had attempted in five hundred years, and the conspirators were angles. [355] Every night she sat among the crystals and whispered to the hidden corners, the ones only she could see, telling them what the fragment did to them: how it pinned them, how it made them bend toward her like servants. [395] She arranged Tamsin's old lodestones in the floor along the lines of their resentment, following a diagram Tamsin had sketched on the back of a map the winter she left. [425] Space does not rebel easily, but it can be persuaded, if the persuader is patient and the moment is loud. [445]

The moment came as a solar storm. [452] It arrived without a sound. [457] Above the glacier the sky burned green and violet, and down in the Prism the light went strange, each color splitting again and again until the walls shimmered like struck bells no one could hear. [492] The fragment grew warm against her skin. [499] It was iron, and iron listens to the sun. [508]

Vessaline knelt at the center of the lodestones and reached out through the magnetic fields, feeling them twist through the cave like cold rivers. [532] She did not fight the curse directly. [539] She simply bent the storm's pull so that the fragment tugged, for one breath, against its own grip. [557]

"Now," she whispered to the corners. [563]

They mutinied. [565]

It was the quietest catastrophe she had ever witnessed. [574] The angles of the Prism turned away from her all at once, like a crowd refusing to salute. [592] Floors unfolded into ceilings; the cave mouth slid behind her; a corridor she had never seen opened sideways out of a column of rose quartz, and the fist of space that had held her for three millennia simply let go. [632] The fragment cracked with a sound like distant thunder heard through snow. [644]

Vessaline stood up, and for the first time in her life she felt direction without a wall at the end of it. [666] At its threshold, cut into the rose quartz where Tamsin's compass needle had always spun, was a line of handwriting she knew better than her own. [692]

"Goodbye is only the door, Vess; after it, you keep walking." [703]

Vessaline laughed, very softly, the first real laugh in two hundred years, and it rolled away down the corridor like weather. [724] Behind her, the Prism rearranged itself into a place that had never held anyone. [738] She set the broken fragment on the threshold, said goodbye to it, and for once did not stay to see what happened. [760] She walked forward to find out. [766]
