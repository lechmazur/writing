The junkyard prophet woke knowing what he would forget by nightfall. [11] It arrived the way it always did, a cold pull behind the eyes, like water sliding back from a beach and leaving the stones exposed. [36] Today the tide was taking the path to the listening bench. [47]

Ossie sat up in the trailer and let the feeling settle. [58] Around him the videotapes stood in their towers, three hundred and twelve of them, labeled in June's round hand and never once played. [81] Their hush filled the room more thickly than dust. [90] Each spine held a date and the same two words: Fog Garden. [102]

People drove out from town to have their futures read in rusted hubcaps, and he was right often enough that they kept coming. [125] What none of them understood was that his gift ran backward. [136] He did not see what would happen. [143] He saw what he would lose. [149] For eleven years he had known, each morning, which small things the coming day would erase: a neighbor's name, the taste of plums, the reason he kept a key on a nail. [181] He let most of them go. [187] But not this. [190]

He found a piece of plain chalk in the coffee can by the door, the white kind June had bought by the box for marking salvage. [216] He put it in his shirt pocket and walked out past the stacked washing machines toward the sea. [234]

Beyond the fence lay the fog sculpture garden, which the city had built on the old pier pilings and then forgotten to fund. [257] Nozzles hidden among steel frames still breathed out mist at dawn, and the mist took the shapes the artists had designed: a heron, a doorway, a woman turning her head. [287] June had loved it. [291] She had spent every morning of their last years on a bench deep in the garden with a camcorder on a tripod, recording nothing but fog and sound. [319]

"What are you trying to catch?" he had asked her once. [330]

"Growing older," she said. [334] "It has a sound. [338] The fog holds onto it longer than air does. [347] Listen, and you can hear the year leaving." [355]

He had laughed at her. [360] Then she was gone, and the tapes came home in boxes, and he could not bear to hear what the fog had kept of her. [385]

Now the tide in his head was pulling at the route to that bench, the left turn at the heron, the long walk under the doorway, the gap between the pilings. [416] By tonight it would be smooth sand. [423] Tomorrow he would not know the bench existed, and nothing in him would ache to go. [439]

So he gathered driftwood from the tideline, gray limbs worn soft as bone. [452] At the garden's mouth he laid the first piece on the ground and turned it until it pointed toward the heron. [473] With the chalk he wrote a single word along its length: JUNE. [485] At the heron he laid another, angled left. [493] At the doorway, a third. [498] The fog swallowed each marker behind him, but he trusted they were there, the way a swimmer trusts the bottom. [518]

He had to locate the bench almost by feel. [527] The shapes had drifted since her time; the woman no longer turned her head but simply stood, looking out. [546] Twice he lost the thread and stood still, clinging to echoes, his own footsteps coming back to him from the steel in soft delayed doubles. [571] Then his knee struck wood, and there it was, salt-furred and empty, the tripod's three scars still pressed into the planks. [592]

He sat. [594] He listened, and at first there was only the hiss of the nozzles and the sea underneath. [611] Then he heard it: a faint crackle, like paper folding, under his own breath. [625] It was in his chest, in the slow grind of his knees, in the way his heart paused before each beat as though reconsidering. [649] The fog gave it back to him a half second late, and inside that delay, very faintly, he thought he heard another breath keeping time with his. [676]

He had brought no recorder, and he understood now that he did not need one. [691] She had already caught it, three hundred and twelve times. [701]

Walking home, he laid the last driftwood across his trailer step, pointing inward at the towers of tape, and wore the chalk down to a nub writing PRESS PLAY. [730]

In the morning the tide had taken the path, as promised. [741] Ossie stood frowning at a gray stick he did not remember placing. [753] Then he knelt, took the first tape from the tower, and did what it told him. [769] Fog hissed from the speaker, and beneath it, like water coming back up the sand, a woman began to breathe. [789] He sat and listened to them both grow older. [798]
