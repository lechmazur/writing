The anesthetic took Mele at a quarter past six, and her small fist loosened around the brain coral fragment she had carried all the way down from the hills. [29] Sister Ruth pressed it into my palm before they wheeled the girl toward surgery. [43] "She said you would know what to do with it," Ruth said, and then the doors swung shut. [61]

I did not know. [65] For eleven years I have known what to do with words, not with gifts. [79] I translated for the ceasefire committees on this island, sitting between men from the coast and men from the ridges, turning their threats into language the observers from Canberra and Suva could write down. [113] I translated the words that killed my brother, too, though I did not know it at the time. [131]

The mission hospital is built around an open atrium, and at twilight the mist comes down off the mountain and fills it like milk poured into a bowl. [159] The frangipani trees drip. [163] The lamps along the colonnade wear halos. [170]

I sat on the stone bench where Mele had sat an hour earlier, her plaster cast propped on my knee while she explained her plan. [195] She was seven and had broken her forearm falling down a ravine when the shooting started near her village. [214] For three days in the ward she had asked every patient, every visitor, every nurse, to write on her cast. [234] "Not your name," she told them. [240] "Write what you are scared of. [246] Then the doctor will cut it out with the bad bone." [257]

It was a child's logic, and it was not. [266] She was shrewdly innocent, that girl. [272] She had noticed that the ward held wounded men from both sides, lying three beds apart and pretending the other did not exist, and she had passed her arm between them like a talking stick. [307] But the men wrote in their own languages, and the ward's humidity had smeared the ink, and nobody could read anyone else's fear. [330]

So she had brought the arm to me. [338] "You talk all the tongues," she said. [345] "Read them before I sleep, so I know what to carry in." [357]

Decrypting them took the whole hour. [363] The ballpoint had bled into the plaster's weave, so I worked the way I once worked through static on a field radio, guessing at the shape of each dialect, testing a word against the ones around it. [400] A coastal fisherman with a shattered knee had written, in Tok Pisin gone blurry at the edges, that he was afraid his daughter would forget his face. [427] A ridge fighter missing two fingers had written, in a hill language I had not spoken aloud since the talks collapsed, the same thing about his son. [454] A nurse feared the next boat of supplies would not come. [465] Someone feared the reef was dying. [471] Someone feared sleeping, because of what came in dreams. [480]

Mele listened, nodding solemnly, and when I finished she said, "They are all scared of the same things." [498] Then she held out her other hand, and I saw the brain coral fragment, bound in a wad of knotted fishing line. [520] "My father tied it before he went up the ridge," she said. [532] "He didn't come back to untie it." [539] Then she yawned, and Ruth came with the needle, and the fragment went with her into sleep. [556]

Now it sat damp in my hand, pale and grooved like a small grey mind. [571] I began to unknot the line. [577] My fingers remembered my brother's net-mending, the patience of it, loop by loop. [590] The mist thickened until the lamps were only rumors. [599]

When the last knot gave, I saw what the line had been hiding: a crack through the coral, and a tuft of staghorn lashed into the break. [626] Her father had not been wrapping a keepsake. [634] He had been grafting, the way coast people mend a reef, the way my brother taught me before the war taught us both other things. [659] A ridge man, doing coastal work. [665]

I had spent years carrying words across that divide as if they were live rounds. [680] Tonight I had carried a cast full of fears across it, and every one had landed softly on the other side. [701] I retied the staghorn more carefully, with my brother's knot. [711]

When Mele wakes, her bone will be set and the fears will still be written on the old plaster they cut away. [733] I will ask Ruth to save it. [740] Then I will take the girl down to the lagoon, and we will tie her father's coral to the reef, and I will tell her that what the men wrote is not a list of wounds but a map of where we are the same. [785]

The mist held the atrium like cupped hands. [793]
