Every morning for forty-one years, Maren Coll set her easel at the end of the north quay and painted the harbor as though it might leave without her. [28]
She knew every boat by the way it sat in the water. [40]
She knew the Petrie brothers argued on Tuesdays, that old Fen hummed hymns while mending net, that the harbormaster's daughter had cut her hair after the wedding was called off. [70]
She had painted all of them, lovingly, a hundred times. [80]
She had never said more than good morning to any of them. [92]

The fishermen called her the Ghost, not unkindly. [100]
She preferred it; a ghost could watch forever without being asked to stay. [113]

She worked with a small mirror clipped to the easel, an old painter's trick, glancing into it to see the quay reversed and catch the lies her eye had told. [143]
On the first gray morning of October, the mirror cracked. [153]
It was only the cold, probably, but the fracture ran straight across the reflected harbor, and for a long moment Maren could not tell the crack from the rigging of the Petries' trawler. [186]
She lowered her brush. [190]
The blur had been creeping in at the edges of things all summer. [203]
The doctor in town had used the word cataract and then the word soon. [217]

That night she took the seashell down from the shelf above her bed. [230]
Her father had painted it before she was born, on the inside of a whelk the size of her palm: a round pool of green water under a circle of sky, ferns hanging over it, a fig tree growing where no tree should grow, all of it walled in black rock. [281]
He had been a quay painter too, and he had told her once, drunk and happy, that he had found a garden inside the sea cliffs. [307]
He drowned the next spring. [312]
She was nineteen, and she made herself a promise she never spoke aloud: I will see it for myself, before it is too late. [336]
Then she had spent forty years painting the harbor instead, because the harbor stayed put and asked nothing of her. [356]

At the lowest tide of the month she walked out beneath the headland, where the caves opened like mouths along the shingle. [378]
The coastal network ran for miles, and the town told children it was full of drowned men. [395]
Maren carried a lamp, a flask, her smallest paint box, and the shell. [408]
Her father's directions had been nothing but a sentence: left past the chapel, where the water sings. [425]
She found a chamber with a pointed roof like a nave and turned left, but the passage narrowed and dropped, and she knew within twenty steps that she had chosen wrong. [456]
She should have turned back, but she squeezed through a crack barely wider than her shoulders, because her eyes were going and there would be no second month, and came out into light. [489]

The roof had fallen in a thousand years ago. [498]
Above her hung a circle of sky. [505]
Below lay a pool of fresh water, green as bottle glass, fed by a spring trickling down the rock, and ferns, and a crooked fig tree heavy with small dark fruit. [536]
It was exactly the shell. [541]
It was nothing like the shell, because it breathed. [550]

Along a dry ledge above the high-water stain sat dozens of whelks, each painted on the inside. [567]
She lifted one with shaking hands. [573]
The north quay, in her father's brushwork, with a small girl in a yellow coat sitting on a bollard. [592]
Another: the same girl, older, sketching. [598]
Another, another, all of them her. [604]
He had not come here to paint the garden. [613]
He had come here to keep the harbor, and her, somewhere the town could not reach. [629]

The lower shells, the ones nearest the stain, had faded almost to bare pearl. [643]
Spring tides crept up this far, she understood, and washed the paint away a little each year. [660]
Nothing here was forever. [664]
It was only forever for a while, which, she thought, was the only kind of forever anyone ever got. [683]

She opened her paint box and chose an empty shell from the pool's edge. [697]
With her failing eyes she painted the quay as it stood that morning: the Petries' trawler, Fen with his net, the harbormaster's daughter with her short hair. [724]
Then, for the first time in forty-one years, she painted herself into the picture, not at the easel but standing among them, mid-sentence. [747]
She set it beside her father's work and climbed out ahead of the tide. [761]

The next morning she left her easel at home, and when Fen looked up from his net, she told him about the garden. [784]
