The map came to Ines Varga folded in a bread bag, and it had torn exactly where the ink ran out. [21] East of the rip lay the parish of Hollin, surveyed to the last hedge; west of it lay nothing but a few brown water stains and a drawn circle of eleven stones. [53] The tear cut the henge in half. [60]

Tomas Reil, who had carried the bag up her stairs, did not explain. [73] He had not spoken in fifty years, the town said, not since the chapel under the stones was bricked shut. [93] He set a note beside the map: Frame it whole. [103]

Ines had framed maps for twenty years, and she had learned that what people asked for was rarely what they meant. [124] A widow wanting a coastline behind glass wanted the house on it remembered; a son wanting a battlefield mounted wanted permission to stop hating it. [149] She did not ask Tomas what whole meant. [157] She asked, instead, whether she could see the place. [166] He hesitated, then wrote a second note: There are films at the library. [179]

Two reels had survived, both without sound. [186] The first was Super 8, shot in 1971 by a schoolteacher for a harvest pageant. [201] The second was a county survey tape from 2016, made the year a storm stripped the turf from the vault beneath the stones. [224] Ines ran them side by side on two monitors, stepping frame by frame, marking the glass with a grease pencil the way she squared the corners of a mount. [253]

In 1971 the frescoes were bright: saints with ears instead of halos, a painted river running into a painted mouth. [273] Villagers filed past them into a low doorway, and every mouth moved, close to a neighbor's cheek, close to the stone. [294] Nobody shouted. [296] Nobody even opened their lips wide. [302] It was a whispered chapel, she realized, the kind her grandmother had described from the old country, where you went to say the thing you could not say at home, and the walls kept it. [337]

In 2016 the saints were ghosts. [343] Forty-five years of rain had scoured them to plaster, and the doorway was a wall of mismatched brick. [361]

But the difference that held her was smaller. [369] In the old film, a boy of perhaps fifteen stood at the threshold while a girl leaned to his ear. [389] Ines slowed it to single frames and watched his face change from waiting to something like fright, then to a kind of flooded joy he tried at once to hide. [419] His mouth opened and did not move. [426] The girl stepped back, nodded as if something had been settled, and walked out of frame. [442] The boy was Tomas; the ears had not changed. [451] In the survey tape, shot from the rim of the henge, a single figure stood outside the bricked door in the rain, a grey-haired man with his hand flat on the mortar, his lips still. [486]

Ines did not decide that his silence was grief, or guilt, or stubbornness. [499] She had framed too many maps to believe a blank meant emptiness; usually it meant someone had stopped, afraid to draw wrong. [521]

She drove out to Hollin with the map in a portfolio and a borrowed pry bar in the trunk. [540] The henge stood exactly where the paper failed, eleven grey teeth on a hill, and she understood why the surveyor had quit there: past the stones the ground fell into bracken and fog, and the vault beneath yawned open to the weather. [582] Tomas was waiting under the lintel; she had written to tell him when she would come, and that he could say no. [604] He had not said no. [609]

Rain ran down the ruined saints in grey threads. [618] She showed him the frames on her phone, the girl leaning in, his young face opening. [634] He looked a long time. [639] Then he took the pry bar from her hands and set it to the first brick himself. [656]

They worked without talking until the doorway gaped, and the old cold air of the chapel breathed out over them. [676] Inside, the frescoes had survived, sheltered from the rain: the river, the mouth, the listening saints. [692] Tomas stepped in, and Ines stepped back into the rain, far enough that she could not hear, near enough that he would know she had stayed. [718] She watched only his shoulders, which loosened slowly, the way paper relaxes when it is finally allowed to lie flat. [738]

A week later she framed the map with the tear left open, the two halves floating a finger's width apart behind the glass. [761] In the gap, at Tomas's request, she lettered nothing at all. [772] Some things, his note said, were whole only when no one filled them in. [786]
