The carnival left town on a Tuesday, and by Wednesday the only thing still spinning in Harrow Flats was the weather vane on the old spaceport. [26] Nell Achebe, nineteen and three weeks into her internship with the county water authority, was sent to decide whether the passenger terminal should be condemned. [51] Her supervisor had phrased it more kindly, but she understood the assignment. [63] Nobody launched anything from Harrow anymore. [69] The last shuttle had left eleven years ago, and the town had been subtracting itself ever since. [86]

She walked the concourse with a clipboard and a flashlight, stepping over the carnival's leavings: popcorn cartons, a snapped ticket spindle, and dozens of green garden hoses snaking across the terrazzo. [117] The carnies had tapped the terminal's old cistern for their dunk tank and their log flume, clamping into whatever valve they could find. [140] Nell traced the lines with the patience of someone who liked knowing where things went. [155]

The deeper she followed them, the stranger the plumbing became. [165] Here was a copper main laid by the original architects, sized for a thousand travelers a day. [182] There, a cheaper PVC bypass where some later manager had cut costs. [194] Beyond that, a capped line labeled in marker, DO NOT OPEN FOUNTAIN, and then the carnival hoses, bright and careless, laid over everything. [217] It was a maze of intentions, each layer built by someone certain they were right, none of them talking to the others. [239] She thought it resembled the town. [245]

The fountain stood at the center of the departures hall beneath a domed ceiling of blue glass. [262] Atop it rose a bronze statue of Ines Varga, the explorer who had charted the southern ocean before anyone thought of charting the sky, holding a sextant raised toward the dome. [293] Except the sextant was incomplete. [298] Its brass index arm was missing, leaving only the frame. [308]

Nell found the arm twenty minutes later, wedged in a drain grate where a carnival hose had knocked it loose, its mirror still intact. [332] When she turned it over, she saw that its pivot was hollow and threaded, less an ornament than a valve key. [353] The architects had hidden the fountain's control in the explorer's hand. [364]

She should have written her report then. [371] Structural cracks, dead systems, recommend demolition. [377] Instead she opened her notebook to the page she had been filling since spring, the one headed in her small square print: EQUATIONS FOR HOPE. [402] It was mostly crossed-out attempts. [407] Her father's closed hardware store did not balance against anything. [417] Neither did the empty school, or the carnival that came each summer promising the town was still worth visiting, then left. [438]

But water she understood. [442] Water did not care what anyone intended; it only went where the pressure allowed. [456]

She had no permit and no budget, so she worked with what the carnival had abandoned. [472] She cut two hoses with her pocketknife and spliced them with the ticket spindle's hollow shaft, bridging the PVC bypass back into the copper main. [497] She clamped the dunk tank line shut with a bent tent stake. [509] Each redirected flow undid a small selfishness, sending the cistern's weight back toward the center of the hall. [527] Then she uncapped the fountain line, climbed the basin, and threaded the index arm into Ines Varga's sextant. [545] She swung it along the arc as if taking a star's altitude. [557]

The pipes groaned. [560] Nothing happened for long enough that she began composing the demolition report in her head. [575]

Then the basin trembled, and water rose in a single column beside the statue, thin as a pencil, then thick as an arm. [598] It climbed twenty feet and broke against a small turbine hidden in the dome's apex. [613] The turbine turned, and somewhere a dynamo woke. [621] One by one, the pinholes in the blue glass lit, and the old departure constellations appeared overhead, the routes to orbit and moon traced in faint gold lines. [649]

Nell did not cheer. [653] She stood in the spray and looked up for a long time, the way the explorer had been looking up for sixty years. [676] She had not resurrected the building by adding anything to it. [687] Everything it needed had always been here, only pointed in the wrong directions. [700]

She took out her notebook and, beneath the crossed-out lines, wrote her first equation that held: hope equals flow, redirected. [720] Then she turned to a fresh page and began her report, which did not recommend demolition. [736] It recommended a tour, and a list of everyone in Harrow who still knew how to fix a pipe. [755]
