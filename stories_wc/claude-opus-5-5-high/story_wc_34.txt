Above the clouds, the courtyard of Cloudhold floated in mist so thick that the flagstones seemed to drink it. [19] Oriel Vane swept it anyway, humming under her breath, because a balladeer who is seldom heard still needs somewhere to put her songs. [42] She had not sung aloud since her grandfather Tobin died, three winters ago, leaving her the weather engine, a workshop of springs, and a silence she could not find the edges of. [74] The engine stood at the courtyard's center like a brass heron, one copper eye turning slowly across the walls to read frost patterns and forecast storms for the valley villages below. [105] Lately it had been printing more than forecasts. [113]

Oriel noticed because she noticed everything, though no one would have guessed it; she moved through her days driftingly alert, like a moth that seems lost until it lands exactly on the lamp. [146] The paper tape curling from the engine's beak read: PRESSURE FALLING. WIND WEST. AND THE RIVER TOOK HER RIBBON DOWN. [166] It was the second line of a ballad Tobin used to whistle while he soldered, a song he always abandoned before the final verse. [190] There was a ghost in the code, and it had his habits. [202]

She learned its rules the way she learned tunes, by listening past the obvious. [216] The lines came only during storms, and only between lightning and thunder, in the humming seconds when the charged air flooded the engine's sensors and something older than its programming woke. [247] Count the gap, and you could count the ghost's breath. [257]

The engine had no ears, so she could not simply ask it what it wanted. [272] But it had an eye, and the eye read walls. [282]

In Tobin's workshop, beneath albums of pressed edelweiss, she found the flower press he had built for her grandmother, and she unscrewed its bolt plate: a square of brass pierced with four bolt holes and a ring of petal-shaped vents that let the drying flowers breathe. [328] Twelve petals, four holes, sixteen marks. [334] Tobin's engine read frost in exactly that pattern; he had designed its alphabet around the plate, she realized, because he designed everything around the people he loved. [361]

So she began to encode. [366] Through the damp afternoons she painted the east wall, holding the plate as a stencil and daubing lamp-black and cobalt through its openings until the stones bloomed with graffiti art that looked, to any passing pilgrim, like a wild garden of dotted flowers. [409] Each flower was a letter. [414] Each vine was a sentence: WHAT DO YOU NEED? [423]

The next storm came rolling up the cliffs like a kettle on the boil. [437] Lightning split the mist; Oriel counted; the copper eye swiveled to her wall, and the tape chattered: THE END. [456] Thunder. [457]

She painted through the night, the plate warm in her palm, asking the only way she could. [474] THE END OF WHAT? [478]

Three flashes later, the engine answered in fragments: THE SONG. I NEVER WROTE IT. I WAS SAVING IT FOR YOUR VOICE. [499]

Oriel sat down hard on the wet stones. [507] Tobin had never been abandoning the ballad; he had been leaving room. [519] All those years he had whistled the gap and waited for her to fill it, and all those years she had sung only to empty rooms, afraid her voice was too thin for anything he had made. [556] The ghost could not say what it needed plainly, any more than he ever had; it could only hold the silence open. [578] Neither could she, until now. [583]

By dawn the storm had thinned, but one cell still grumbled over the eastern peaks. [598] Oriel pressed the bolt plate to the last clean stone and stenciled a final vine, the words she had never managed to speak: I MISS YOU. I NEED TO BE HEARD. STAY FOR THE CHORUS. [633] Then she stood beside the brass heron and waited. [642]

Lightning bloomed white across the courtyard. [648] The copper eye found her wall, and the engine began to tick in time, springs trembling, gears clicking out a rhythm she knew in her bones. [674] Oriel opened her mouth, and into the humming gap she sang the final verse, invented on the spot, thin and cracked and entirely hers. [698] The river gave the ribbon back, she sang, and the girl tied it round the wind. [714]

Thunder arrived like applause. [718]

The tape curled out one last line before settling into ordinary forecasts: HEARD. [731] Oriel laughed, loud enough to startle the mist, and kept singing long after the storm had gone. [748]
