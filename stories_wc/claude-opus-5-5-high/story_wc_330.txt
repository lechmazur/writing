The wind bells began at dusk, as they always did, and Bram Holt set down his chisel to listen. [19] Lina had strung them along the roof of the Marchetti Brothers circus train eleven summers ago, brass tubes and tin cups and one cracked sleigh bell, and they still rang whenever the evening wind came off the salt marsh. [58] The train had not moved in six years. [66] When the circus went bankrupt, the owners uncoupled the engine and left the rest on a rusting siding: nine cars of faded red lacquer, a menagerie wagon, and a flatcar holding the dismantled carousel Bram had built with his own hands. [107] Everyone else left too. [111] Bram stayed, and after Lina's funeral he stopped speaking, not from grief exactly, but because every word he tried felt like a door slammed on the quiet she had left behind. [142] He restored the carousel instead, one horse at a time. [152]

Tonight he was finishing the angel that crowned the center pole, the figure Lina used to wave at from the calliope bench. [174] Her gilded halo had cracked when the figure toppled during the last winter tour, and only one curved fragment remained, a crescent of hammered brass he kept in his shirt pocket like a coin he could not spend. [212] For six years he had searched every car he could open for the missing half. [227] He had pried up floorboards, emptied costume trunks, sifted gravel beneath the couplings. [240] He did not stop, because stopping felt like agreeing that some things stay broken. [254]

Now the bells chimed, and with them came the mist. [264] It rose from the marsh every evening while the wind held, a pale tide that swallowed the tracks and softened every edge, and it carried a strange hush with it, as though the fog drank sound. [300] Inside the chime, the bells seemed to ring from very far away, and the world went silver and close. [319] Bram usually waited it out in the workshop car. [328] But tonight he had left his gouge on the flatcar, and he climbed down to fetch it. [345]

Six steps into the mist he lost the train entirely. [355] He put out a hand, found cold iron, and followed it, but the rail curved where no rail should curve, and when the fog thinned he stood before the menagerie wagon, which he had never entered because its doors had rusted shut. [397] Tonight the side panel hung open, eaten through at the hinge. [408] He ducked inside and stopped breathing. [414]

The roof had caved years ago, and rain had pooled in the old elephant trough until it overflowed into a clear, shallow spring. [437] Ferns spilled from the straw bins. [443] Wild roses had climbed the bars of the lion cage and bloomed through them. [457] Moss glowed green over the painted tigers on the walls, and in the fading light every droplet held a small, whispering gleam. [479] A hidden oasis, six years growing beside him. [487]

Then he saw the nest. [492] It sat in the crook of the cage door, woven from straw and ribbon and tinsel scraps, and threaded through its rim, bright as a struck match, was a curve of hammered brass. [525] The other half of the halo. [531] Three speckled eggs lay inside it. [537]

Bram's hand rose, then stopped. [542] A small brown finch watched him from the roses, perfectly still, making no sound in the misting hush. [560] It was not an empty silence, he realized. [568] It was the silence of something waiting to happen: eggs not yet hatched, roses not yet open, a song held in a small throat until the fog lifted. [596] Lina used to say that the pause before the calliope's first note was the best part of any show, because in it anything could still be played. [623] He had spent six years hearing only the ending in his quiet. [635]

He drew the fragment from his pocket and held it beside the nest, and the two curves nearly touched, a ring broken by a gap the width of a heartbeat. [665] Then he did what he had not done since the funeral. [676] He opened his mouth and sang. [682] It was Lina's calliope waltz, rusty and low, barely louder than the bells. [695] The finch cocked its head, and from somewhere in the ferns another bird answered, and then another, until the ruined wagon was trilling inside the fading chime. [722]

He left the brass where it was. [729] In the morning he would mount his own fragment on the angel's head, open at the top, a crescent with room enough for a bird to perch in the gap. [759] The wind died, the mist thinned, and Bram walked back toward the carousel, humming, the silence ahead of him no longer a door shut but a door ajar. [787]
