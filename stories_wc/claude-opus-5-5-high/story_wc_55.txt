<story>
Ilse Marrow had kept the hours of the Sanctuary of Oreth for nineteen years, and in all that time she had never once rung them. [25] The sanctuary was soundless by vow and by design, cut into the north face of a mountain where even the wind seemed to lower its voice. [51] No bells hung there. [55] Instead a clock scribe sat in a stone alcove beside the great water clock and wrote each hour by hand into the long ledger, so the brothers and sisters could glance at the ink and know where the day stood. [95]

She suited the work. [99] She had always been quiet, the kind of child who answered questions with nods, and silence had never frightened her. [119] What frightened her, lately, was the other hush, the one the old sisters called glacial. [134] It came on slowly, over years, grinding everything under it so gradually that nobody noticed until a whole valley of feeling had been scraped smooth. [159] Ilse had felt it creeping through her for a long time. [170] She no longer remembered what her own voice sounded like singing. [181]

The hardest entry in the ledger was sunrise. [189] The mountain blocked the east, so the sun rose hidden, and the scribe had to mark it without seeing it. [209] The water clock drifted with the seasons. [216] The only true witness was the forecourt. [223]

It had been laid centuries ago from slabs of pale crystal quarried somewhere far below. [238] When rain fell in the night, the water seeped into hairline seams, and when the first unseen warmth of dawn touched the stone, the crystal began to whisper. [266] It was not a human sound, and so the vow allowed it: a faint ticking, like frost letting go of glass, spreading across the courtyard as the light arrived beyond the ridge. [298] Ilse wrote "sunrise" when the whispering began. [305]

Last night it had rained. [310] So before dawn she climbed down from her alcove carrying the thing she had hidden in her trunk since she arrived. [331]

It was the clapper of a bell. [338] Iron, as long as her forearm, heavier than it looked, pitted where the casting sand had bitten it. [356] Before Oreth, she had been apprentice to Tamsin Hale, the bellfounder of Lowmere, and her dream had been to cast a bell of her own, a bell for the valley that would sound at every sunrise so no one would sleep through the light. [400] She had built the mold herself. [406] She had poured the bronze on a wet October afternoon, the foundry yard steaming with that smell of rain on hot stone. [428] At its first strike the bell had cracked from lip to shoulder, a sound like a door slamming on a whole life. [450] Tamsin had said it could be recast. [457] Ilse had walked up the mountain instead, carrying only the clapper, and taken the vow of quiet. [474]

She had borne it for nineteen years the way one bears a grudge, or a debt. [490] Now she meant to set it down. [497]

The forecourt glistened in the grey. [503] The air smelled of wet slate and moss and, faintly, of iron, and the smell undid something in her chest; for a moment she was twenty again, standing in the steaming yard with Tamsin's scarred hand on her shoulder, before the crack, when the bell was still only a promise. [553] She knelt at the center of the courtyard. [561] Beyond the ridge, somewhere she would never see, the sun was coming. [573]

She lowered the clapper with excruciatingly gentle care, her wrists trembling with the effort of making no sound. [591] Iron met crystal. [594] Something rang, very softly, a single low note so faint it was almost a thought. [609]

And then the forecourt answered. [614] The whispering began all at once, rippling out from where the iron lay, thousands of tiny voices ticking and chiming as dawn warmed the rain-dark stone. [640] It was not a bell. [645] It was better than her bell had ever been, because it had not been made by her, only woken. [664] Ilse stayed on her knees and listened to the hidden sunrise sing across the stone, and she felt the glacial hush inside her crack, not like the bronze had cracked, but the way river ice cracks in spring, making room for water. [706]

She did not pick the clapper up again. [714] She left it where the crystal could keep it, and climbed back to her alcove, and dipped her pen. [733] Beneath the date, in her small careful hand, she wrote: Sunrise. [744] Rain in the night. [748] A bell, briefly. [751] Then, after a long moment, she added one more line, which no one else would understand: It was enough to have wanted it. [774]
