Hollis Marrow had bought the drought the way he bought everything, quietly and through intermediaries. [15] Three dams upstream, a fleet of seeding planes that coaxed the clouds east, a lobbying firm that called it watershed stewardship. [36] The river that fed Keverin Pan had died in eleven months, and the lake it once filled had shrunk to a white crust of salt that hurt the eyes at noon. [67] The farmers downstream had lost their orchards, and Hollis had endowed a clinic, a school, and a relief fund with his name carved above every door. [93] He had also bought their land, at a fair price, which was to say his price. [109] Generosity, in his experience, was simply the most efficient form of leverage. [121]

None of that was why he had come. [129] He walked the old riverbed at dusk with a leather case under one arm, following the cracks. [146] The mud had split as it dried, the way mud always does, into polygons and fissures, but here the fractures ran in long deliberate lines, branching and rejoining, and from the survey drone they had resolved into something no geologist could explain. [188] A map. [190] Not of the land, but beneath it: the silted channels of an older river, and at their convergence, a hollow in the salt where the ground rang faintly when struck. [220]

He had first read of the place in a naturalist's letter from 1891, sold to him at auction with a clutch of other curiosities. [244] The lantern plovers nested at the heart of the Pan, the letter said, and when they called, the ground called back, doubled and slow, as though the earth were learning the song. [276] The last plover was shot in 1904. [283] The lake rose over the nesting ground six years later. [293] Hollis had needed the water gone, so he had taken it. [304]

Now thunder muttered somewhere past the western ridge, the storm his planes had been turning away for a year. [323] Then it stopped, and in the lull the flat went utterly still. [335] He reached the hollow and knelt. [341] The wind came across the salt, dry as paper, and slid into the cracks, and the ground began to sing. [361] It was not a song exactly. [367] It was a thin, rising whistle that broke in the middle and mended itself, over and over, the shape of a voice worn down by a century of silence. [396] The hollow had kept it, the way a shell keeps the sea. [408]

Hollis opened the case. [412] Inside, on gray felt, lay a lantern plover's egg, speckled like dusk, cool and weightless and hollow as the ground beneath him. [434] His board believed it was insured for four million dollars and destined for a museum wing bearing his name. [453] No one knew it had belonged to his mother, who had kept it on a windowsill in a rented room and whistled to it on the nights his father did not come home. [486] She had told him the story of the singing ground, and years later, rich enough to buy anything, he had tracked down the letter to prove she had not invented it. [517] She had whistled it wrong, he heard now; she had never known where the note broke. [533]

He had planned to keep the drought forever. [541] Water would fill the hollow and drown the echo again; he had the planes, the dams, the money, and a lifetime to spend on a sound. [567] The orchards could die for it, and he would build another clinic. [579] But the echo was fading even as he listened, thinning with each pass of wind, and he understood that the dry air was scouring away what the lake had kept sealed. [610] The drought had not uncovered the song; it was spending it. [621] A week more and there would be nothing left but salt. [632]

He set the egg in the hollow, in the crook of the loudest crack, where the whistle broke and mended. [652] Then he took out his phone and called the airfield. [662] "Ground the planes," he said. [667] "Open the dams." [670] "Release all of it." [674] His director asked about the farmland, the valuation, the press. [684] Hollis told him to announce it as a gift. [693] It would photograph well, the billionaire who ended the drought, and he would let them believe it. [710]

The thunder began again, closer now, unopposed. [717] Hollis stayed on his knees in the salt and whistled once, badly, the way his mother had, and for a moment the ground answered in both voices. [744] Then the first drops struck, dark as ink on the white, and he walked back along the map before the water could erase it. [768]
