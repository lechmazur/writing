On the night the dead call, Wren Adair was sent to the Halloway Atrium with a clipboard and a cold. [20] She was a contact tracer for hearts, which the city pamphlets described as a public health role and which everyone else called a professional mourner with a map. [48] When a heart broke, she found out who it had been close to, because grief spread the way fevers did, and it spread fastest on the one evening each autumn when the dead rang through anything with a coil of wire in it. [91] Telephones, radios, doorbells, pacemakers. [95] The living who were already faint heard them loudest. [104]

The atrium belonged to a care home that had been emptying since spring, and its heating had failed, so the glass roof sweated a mist that hung between the palms like breath nobody had let go of. [141] Twilight came through it bruised and violet. [148] Her file concerned Odile Fenn, ninety-one, the last resident, who had died three days earlier in a wicker chair beside the fishpond. [170] No next of kin listed. [175] Her effects fit in a shoebox: a cardigan, a tin of peppermints, a beige hearing aid the size of a walnut, and a photograph. [199] The night nurse who signed the release swore it was the last photograph Odile had ever let anyone take. [218] It showed her at perhaps thirty, standing in this same atrium when the glass was new, holding the hand of a small boy with that same walnut of beige plastic tucked behind his ear. [252] On the back, in pencil: 1968, before they took him. [262]

Wren's job was finished the moment she wrote no exposure found. [273] She did not write it. [278] She had always been persistently curious, the kind of child who unscrewed telephones to find the voices, and the kind of adult who still could not leave a question lying face down. [310] She turned the hearing aid over, found the tiny switch marked T, and flicked it. [325] Old aids had telecoils, she knew, little windings meant to catch the magnetic hum of telephone lines. [342] Tonight of all nights, a coil might catch anything. [351] She fitted it to her ear. [357]

At first there was only the drip of condensation and, underneath it, a crowd of whispering she refused to listen to closely. [379] Then, threaded through the whispering, a click. [386] A pause. [388] A click, softer. [391] It was too regular to be the dead, who never kept time. [403] It was a machine keeping time for someone. [411]

She walked, and the pulses thinned; she turned toward the overgrown ferns at the north wall, and they thickened. [430] The mist closed behind her like a curtain drawn in a sickroom. [442] The whispering grew fonder, almost tender, and she understood with a lurch that it was not meant for her. [461]

He was sitting against the cold iron of a radiator, a gray-haired man in a good coat, one hand pressed flat to his chest where the pacemaker made a small square under the wool. [495] His lips were blue. [499] His eyes were on nothing she could see. [507] "She's calling me," he said, in the careful, too-loud voice of a man who had not heard himself in years. [527] "I read the notice and came to see where she lived, and then my heart started saying my name." [546]

Wren knelt and dialed the ambulance with one hand, the photograph still in the other. [561] Through the hearing aid, his pacemaker stuttered, and the whispering leaned in, patient as water. [576] This was what her job had always been, she realized: the dead spoke loudest to the faintest, and someone had to stand between them. [600]

She held the photograph where he could see it. [609] "She kept this," Wren said, shaping every word so he could read her lips. [623] "She wanted you found, not followed." [629] She took his cold hand and pressed it to the picture, over the boy's small fingers. [645] "You don't have to answer her tonight," she said, and kept saying it, to assure him and perhaps herself, until his mouth began to move with hers. [672]

The clicks steadied. [675] The whispering, as if chastened, withdrew into the palms. [684]

When the paramedics came crashing through the mist, one of them called her a hero, and Wren almost laughed, because she had only wanted to know whose face was in the picture. [716]

Later, under the violet dark, she filled in her form. [726] Exposure found: one son, living. [731] She left the hearing aid switched on the whole drive home, listening past the fading whispers for anyone else too faint to be heard. [755]
