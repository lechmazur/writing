Maren woke on the fourth morning in Vardshavn and could not remember the word she had rehearsed for the doctor. [20]
She lay under the rented quilt and listened to the harbor grinding like teeth, and understood that something had shifted in her overnight, a slippage the brochures had promised would come. [51]
The clinic above the drift-ice marina called people like her euthanasia tourists, though never to their faces. [68]
They flew in from Lyon and Leeds and Osaka with their paperwork in order and their goodbyes already said, and then they waited out the mandatory fourteen days. [96]
Maren had spent three of them impatient, and now, pulling on her boots, she found her impatience had folded over on itself like a letter she could no longer open. [126]

The storm had come in the night. [133]
From the window she saw the pontoons buckled, two trawlers heeled against the breakwater, and floes the size of kitchen tables jostling among splintered hulls. [158]
On the quay, people in orange vests were handing out gloves. [169]

She checked her coat pocket for the parasol tip before she went down, as she always did. [186]
It was all that survived of her grandmother's summer parasol: a finger of tarnished brass, wrapped at its collar with a band of yellowed Flanders lace that had outlived the silk, the ribs, and the woman. [222]
Her grandmother had died slowly, forgetting everyone, and Maren had come north so that she would not. [239]

The relief coordinator, a broad man named Joakim with frost in his beard, did not ask who she was. [258]
He put a gaff in her hands and set her beside a boy hauling a weighted net between the slips. [278]
"We trawl for fuel cans and loose timber," Joakim said. [288]
"Anything the ice can pick up and drive through a hull." [299]

They worked through the gray forenoon, and the wind came off the fjord carrying gull cries and diesel and the voices of strangers calling to one another. [326]
Twice Maren saw her grandmother at the end of the pier, parasol open against a sun that was not there, and twice she simply nodded to her and kept pulling rope. [357]
Since waking she held the world the way one holds a wet line, loosely, letting it run where it wanted. [377]
It was less frightening than gripping. [383]

At noon the ice shifted. [388]
Joakim raised a hand and everyone stopped. [395]
Beneath the slap of water came a long, low groan, then a series of ticks like a clock running down, and a pressure ridge folded open twenty meters out and swallowed a skiff without hurry. [430]

"You hear that?" Joakim said. [435]
"The ice always tells you. [440]
Nothing out here ends without announcing itself first." [448]

Maren realized this was what she had truly crossed the sea for, more than the clinic's quiet room: to learn the sound an ending makes on its way, so that she would recognize her own. [483]
She had feared that forgetting would arrive silently, erasing her before she could choose. [497]

"Show me," she said. [501]

Joakim led her onto the fast ice near the fuel dock and showed her how to tap. [518]
She used the parasol tip, brass against white, and the lace fluttered at its collar like a small flag. [537]
Sound ice rang high and clean. [543]
Rotten ice answered with a dull hollow thud, a note that seemed to hang in the wind before the wind carried it off. [566]
She tapped until her wrist ached, until she could tell a week of safety from an hour of it by ear alone. [588]

In the evening she climbed to the clinic and asked to speak with the doctor. [603]
She still could not find the word she had rehearsed, but she found others. [617]
"Not yet," she told him. [622]
"I thought I had to go before I stopped knowing myself. [633]
But endings knock first, and I know the sound now." [643]

He studied her a long moment, then wrote a new date in her file, six months out, the old fourteen days tucked inside it like a hem folded under. [672]

Walking back along the marina, Maren heard the ice ticking in the dark, patient and unhurried, and the wind carried the sound to her like a message from someone who loved her. [704]
At the end of the pier her grandmother lowered the parasol and waved. [717]
Maren waved back, holding the brass tip, and did not correct either of them. [731]
