The moon was still snagged behind the grain towers when Ada Finch set her coffee tin on the circulation desk, which had no roof above it anymore. [27] The Carnegie branch on Pell Street had burned in the second week of the riots, and what remained was a skeleton of iron shelving gone orange with rust, open to a sky so clear the stars looked sharpened. [65] Ada had perhaps an hour left of herself. [73] That was how it worked: every month, as the moon cleared the skyline on the first full night, she shed the person she had been like a snake leaves a stocking of skin in the grass. [109] This month she had been patient, wry, fond of semicolons. [119] Last month she had been furious and funny and had written limericks about the mayor. [134] Next month she would be someone she could not imagine, which was the whole problem. [149]

She pried the lid off the tin. [156] On top lay the day's pennies, forty-one of them, each paid by a stranger for a poem Ada wrote on the spot and handed over on a torn index card. [186] She tipped them into her palm, pressed a thumbnail into the seam, and lifted the false bottom. [203]

Beneath it were the letters. [208] Thirty-two of them, one from every Ada who had ever lived in this body, each addressed to whoever came next. [228] The oldest was in pencil, written in a cell at Harrow Women's Correctional, and it said only: Find Marisol in the library. [250] She knows which one you are. [256]

Marisol had been serving eleven years for a fire she swore she did not set, and she ran the prison library like a small, fierce nation. [282] Ada, serving three years for vagrancy and a smashed shop window, had been assigned to sort donations beside her. [301] The first month, Ada shelved by color. [308] The second, she shelved by the first line of each book, alphabetically, and Marisol laughed so hard a guard came running. [329] By the third, Marisol had worked it out. [337]

"You're not crazy," she'd said, sliding a water-stained Neruda across the cart. [349] "You're a library. [352] Every month somebody reorganizes you. [357] So write yourself a catalog." [362]

Now Marisol was out too, one of hundreds turned loose when the riots emptied Harrow and its records burned. [381] The city had no government worth the name, only checkpoints, looters, and bulldozers the provisional council sent to flatten anything it called a hazard. [405] The library was scheduled for Thursday. [411]

So they had decided to rebuild it first. [419] Not the roof; they could not afford a roof. [428] They rebuilt the shelves, wiring the rusted uprights straight with coat hangers, filling them with books scavenged from gutters and burned-out apartments. [450] The furious Ada had hauled cinderblocks. [456] The patient Ada had planted morning glory seeds at the base of every stack, and now vines climbed waist-high up the iron, blue trumpets folded shut for the night. [485] People came at dusk to read by lantern, and each night more of them stayed until dawn. [502] Marisol said that if forty people were sleeping between the shelves on Thursday, the council would have to decide what kind of government it wanted to be. [529]

Ada uncapped her pen. [533] She had been putting off the letter all week, because this self liked being herself, and it felt like writing her own obituary. [556]

Dear next one, she wrote. [561] You will wake up in a ruin that is not a ruin. [573] The city broke itself open, and we are filling the cracks with something that grows. [588] You may not like poetry; you may not like me. [598] Stay anyway. [600] On Thursday, sit down between the shelves and do not move, and when the machines come, sell the drivers a poem for a penny. [624] Beauty is only anarchy that someone decided to keep. [633] Marisol will tell you which one you are. [641]

She folded it, laid it on top of the thirty-two, and sealed the false bottom over them all. [659] The pennies went back on top, forty-one small copper suns. [669]

Marisol came down the aisle with two cups of chicory and sat beside her on the desk. [686] Neither of them spoke. [690]

Above the grain towers the moon lifted free, white and entire, and its light came down through the rusted ribs of the building in long bright fractures, laying silver across the spines of the books and the closed blue mouths of the flowers. [733] Ada felt herself loosen, like a knot someone patient was working out. [745]

When she opened her eyes, a woman with a scar through one eyebrow was watching her closely. [762] "Hello, stranger," the woman said. [767] "Let's find out who you are." [773]

Ada looked at the stars, the vines, the tin warm in her hands, and found she was already reaching for a pen. [795]
