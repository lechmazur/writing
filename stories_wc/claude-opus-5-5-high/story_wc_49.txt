Odo Pell arrived at the arbor before the birds, pushing a barrow that clinked like a pocketful of spoons. [19] The vines of Heddle Hill arched overhead in a green tunnel, their leaves still beaded from the night, and at the far end the harvest moon hung low and enormous, the color of a peach left too long in the sun. [60] Behind him, the east was beginning to pale. [68] Moon ahead, sun behind, and the old arbor running straight between them like a needle's eye. [84] His grandmother had called it the Threading, the one dawn a year when everything aligns, and she had sung the vines awake every harvest until the year she could no longer climb the hill. [118] Nobody had sung since. [122]

Now the Mother Vine at the heart of the arbor was grey to the elbow, and the grapes on every row around her had come in small and sour. [151] The village council had tried sulfur, then copper, then a man from the city with a clipboard. [168] Odo had tried telling them it was the silence that was killing her. [181] They had laughed kindly, because everyone laughed kindly at Odo. [191] He was the man who fixed clocks by talking to them, who built a weathervane shaped like a heron that actually flapped, who sat on the fountain steps every market day and spun golden stories for the children about bees that kept ledgers and rivers that ran backward on birthdays. [241] The Threading, they assumed, was one more of those. [250]

But a few of them had followed him up the hill anyway, because it was a story, and stories were free. [271] The baker came, and Marra from the mill, and a knot of children in nightshirts, and old Fen Ashby, who had not spoken much since his wife died. [299]

Odo set up his machine beneath the Mother Vine. [308] It was a brass drum on a crank, a horn made from a watering can, and a delicate arm tipped with a sliver of goose quill. [334] From his coat he drew a vine tendril, dry and pale, coiled tight as a watch spring. [351] He had found it pressed in his grandmother's hymnal, marking a page with no words on it at all. [370]

"Vines listen," he told the children, winding the tendril around the drum. [382] "When she sang, this one was growing, and it grew to the shape of what it heard, the way a footpath grows to the shape of feet." [409]

The baker snorted. [412] Odo only smiled and lowered the quill. [419]

Nothing happened. [421] The moon slid lower; the sun's first edge cut the hill behind them, and a single line of gold ran the length of the arbor toward the setting moon and struck the brass. [454] Odo turned the crank. [458]

Out of the watering-can horn came a thin, scratching sound, and then, underneath it, a melody. [474] It was slow and rising, the kind of tune that seems to remember you before you remember it. [492] A hush gathered in the arbor, the way hush gathers in a church before anyone has told it to. [511] The children stopped fidgeting. [515] The baker took off his cap. [521]

Then the tendril reached its end, and the music frayed into scratching again, and Odo's hand went still on the crank. [542] It was only half a verse. [548] He had known it would be; the tendril was short, and he did not know the words. [565] He had simply hoped, the way he always hoped, that a story told well enough would find its own ending. [585]

In the silence, Fen Ashby began to sing. [593]

His voice was cracked and low, but the words came out whole: something about the moon laying down its lantern, the sun picking it up, the vine keeping the light between them. [625] His wife had sung beside Odo's grandmother, he said later, and he had stood at the back every year pretending not to know it. [649] Marra from the mill caught the second verse, and then the baker, humming where he lacked the words, and then the children, who needed no words at all. [677] Odo sang too, badly and gladly. [683]

The moon went down into the far rows as the sun cleared the hill, and for one breath the arbor held them both. [706]

No one could say, afterward, that the grey had left the Mother Vine. [719] Remedies are rarely so quick. [724] But low on her oldest cane, where the light had struck, a new tendril had unfurled, green as a held breath, and it was already curling to the shape of the song. [756]

"Next year," Fen said, "we'll need more voices." [764]

Odo was already, in his head, spinning the story that would bring them. [777]
