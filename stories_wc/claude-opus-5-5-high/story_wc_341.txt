The gondola stopped where Ansel always stopped it, halfway up Kestrel Ridge, swaying over a thousand feet of nothing. [19] Mira Sato pulled the brake lever herself this time, because there was no one else to pull it. [37] Below her, the airfield runway lights blinked in their patient rows, and above her the stars had come out thin and grainy, like light that had traveled too far and picked up dust along the way. [73]

She had forecast weather for that airfield for eleven years. [83] She knew dew points, lapse rates, the exact hour fog would lift off the river. [98] What she did not know was how to stand in the space where a person used to be. [116]

On the bench lay what the gondola company had returned to her: Ansel's brass telescope, his ledger, and the wooden reel of kite string, still fat with line. [144] Nine days ago he had been flying his red kite from this car, as he did every evening after the last passengers rode down, when his heart quit. [172] The string had snapped against the window frame as he fell, and the kite had gone east over the ridge and never come back. [196]

She opened the ledger. [200] Ansel had kept it for forty years, and it was the reason they had argued. [215] He categorized clouds by emotion. [220] Low stratus was Sullen. [224] Mackerel sky was Restless. [228] A towering cumulus that had not yet begun to rain, swelling white and silent in the afternoon, he labeled Quietly Explosive, and beside it, in pencil, he had written: Mira. [258]

She laughed, and the laugh hurt. [264] It was fair. [267] She had never shouted at him, not once, but she had slammed her forecasts on his counter and told him, very softly, that feelings were not data. [294]

"Air remembers," he would say, tapping the telescope. [302] "Empty spaces remember. [305] You just have to know what they're feeling." [313]

She had called that sentimental. [318] Now she needed it to be true. [325]

The airport authority wanted to memorialize him with a plaque on the terminal wall, bronze and polite. [342] Mira wanted something that would prove he was still written into the sky he had spent his life reading. [361]

She extended the brass telescope, its seams worn smooth by his thumbs, and aimed it east. [377] The west wind was steady tonight, fifteen knots, the same as the evening he died. [392] She knew what that meant, meteorologically. [398] Air pouring over Kestrel Ridge would fold downward and rebound, a standing wave in the lee of the mountain, invisible unless there was enough moisture to mark its crest. [427] The wave did not move. [432] The wind passed through it, but the shape stayed, held in place by the memory of the rock beneath. [451] It was the lift his red kite had always found, rising just there, a little north of the second pylon. [471]

She waited. [473] The stars shifted their dusty faces. [479] Her breath fogged the eyepiece, and she wiped it on her sleeve the way he used to, which made her furious, which made her keep looking. [505]

Then it came: a lens of cloud, smooth as a lid, forming out of nothing above the exact place the kite had flown. [528] A lenticular, silver in the starlight, perfectly still while the wind rushed through it. [542] The empty space had remembered its shape. [549]

Mira picked up the reel. [554] Ansel used to say a spool was wholeness wound up and waiting, that every foot you let out was still part of the same line. [579] The part that had broken away was out there somewhere with the kite. [592] The rest was here, in her hands. [599]

She lashed the reel to the window rail, slid the glass open, and fed the frayed end out into the wind. [620] It sagged at first, a pale thread against the dark. [630] She let out more, and more, feeling the reel lighten, until the line reached the rising air and lifted, taut and humming, pointing straight at the cloud like a finger tracing a name. [663] Nothing held it up but the memory of a mountain and an old man's evenings. [678]

She opened the ledger to a clean page and wrote the date, the wind, the altitude of the wave. [697] Then, under category, after a long moment, she wrote: Faithful. [707] Beneath it, in pencil, she added his name. [715]

The line hummed on in the dusty starlight, and for once the quiet in her did not need to explode. [735]
