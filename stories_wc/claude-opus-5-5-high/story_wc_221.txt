After the shutdown, the observation deck kept only one sound, and it was mine. [14] Every word I spoke down there rang off the curved glass and returned a moment late, so that I lived inside a silence that answered. [39] The deck hung at the bottom of Meridian Station's long spoke, closest to the black hole, where one of my hours was nearly a day for the people above. [68] I was born there, which is why the chaplains called me blessed. [80] It is also why nobody would sit beside me in the mess. [92] A child raised in stretched time comes out wrong in small ways: I blinked too slowly, laughed a beat behind the joke, and at thirty-one I still had the face of a girl of nineteen. [127] The crew said I had outlived my own parents without trying, and they said it the way people speak about a curse. [149] So when the reactor faulted and the evacuation shuttles filled, no one argued very hard when I volunteered to stay behind and log the Well until the station went dark. [179]

It went dark quickly. [183] The navigation core shut down first, then the beacons, then the charts, and I was left with a single lifeboat, a failing deck, and no idea where anyone had gone. [213]

What I had was my mother's sketchbook. [220] Our family kept a custom older than the station: on every anniversary of the day my great-grandparents left Earth, whoever was living drew the night sky exactly as they saw it. [251] The cover bore a gilded compass rose applique my grandmother had stitched from ship's wire, its north point worn bright by generations of thumbs. [275] Before each drawing, we laid the rose against the glass and aimed that point at whatever we had agreed was true north that year. [299] Out here there was no pole, no magnet, no Polaris, only the argument. [312] My father had chosen the Well's spin axis. [320] My mother, stubborn and sentimental, always chose the faint yellow dot she swore was the Sun. [336]

The anniversary came three station days after the shutdown, which down on the deck was barely an afternoon. [354] I almost skipped it. [358] Then I heard myself say, too loudly, that nobody would ever look at the drawing, and the glass returned the sentence to me a heartbeat later, small and unconvinced. [387] So I sat and drew. [392]

Because the sky outside ran fast against my slow hours, I could watch the lensed stars crawl along the Well's bright ring while I drew, and because I was alone, I let myself take the whole night. [429] Near the end I glimpsed something I had never drawn: a pale, steady point sliding against the background, too regular for a star. [452] I flipped back through forty anniversaries of pencil. [460] It was there in my mother's last page, faint, labeled with a question mark, and in my father's page before that, and in my grandmother's, always a little farther along the same line. [493] No one else had noticed, because no one else had kept every page. [506]

The fear that had sat in my chest since the lights died began to change shape. [522] Confusion is only a map with the names rubbed off, my mother used to say, and I had decades of names in my lap. [546] I plotted the point's path across the pages the way a sailor plots a current. [561] It moved the way only built things move, in a patient ellipse around a second, darker mass beyond the Well's glare, and something on it was still lit. [589] Perhaps the shuttles had gone there; perhaps no one had been there for a century. [604] Either way, it was a place, and it had been waiting in our family's handwriting long before I knew how to read it. [627]

I had spent my life being told that my slowness set me apart, and it had, but it had also let me see the whole slow dance at once. [656] The blessing and the exile were the same gift, pointed in different directions. [669]

I laid the gilded rose against the glass and turned it until its bright tip rested on the lit point. [689] Not the spin axis, not the Sun, but somewhere no one in my family had gone. [705] True north, this year, was a question mark. [713] I wrote the date beneath the drawing, packed the book against my ribs, and walked to the lifeboat. [731] Behind me the deck held my last word, "north," and gave it back once, softly, to no one. [749]
