Ines Varga heard the pilgrim before she saw him, because the goats on the observatory terrace scattered down the scree. [20] She was crouched beside her drone, picking grit from a rotor, and she lifted the squint that had won her every mapping contract in the valley. [46] Nothing moved on the mountain that Ines did not eventually see. [57]

He came up the last switchback at dusk, a thin man in a road-grey coat, and his face wore an expression of total astonishment. [81] It did not change when he saw her, or the drone, or the cracked copper dome behind them. [99] "Oh," he said, as if the ancient observatory had just been set down for his benefit. [115] "It is still here." [119]

His name was Oszkar, and within an hour Ines understood that he was perpetually surprised. [134] The kettle boiling surprised him. [139] When she told him the guild had sent her to survey the ruin before the dome was sold for scrap, his eyebrows climbed even higher. [164]

"Then I have arrived just in time," he said, and unwrapped a scrap of oilcloth. [179]

Inside lay a small brass block no longer than her thumbnail, its face sunk with the reversed shape of a letter she did not recognize. [204] "A matrix chip," Oszkar said. [209] "From the type foundry in my grandmother's town." [217] "They cast every letter of our alphabet from blocks like this, and this is the last one left." [235] "The others were melted for bullets." [241]

Ines turned it toward the failing light. [248] "And you brought it here." [253]

"My grandmother said the astronomers built a receiver under the dome." [264] "Its dial was set with our letters, because our people paid for it." [277] "Every night it listened for a signal the founders called the frequency of belonging." [291] "If you were lost, you tuned to it, and you would know where home was." [306] He smiled, amazed at himself. [311] "Soldiers pried one letter from the dial during the war, and the signal went silent." [326] "This matrix is the mold that made it." [334]

Ines had been raised in guild barracks, handed from crew to crew like a spare battery. [350] She had never once known where home was. [358] She told herself she was curious only as a surveyor, and did not believe it. [373]

They searched the dome until the brilliance drained from the sky. [384] The floor was rubble, the telescope long gone, every wall bare. [395] "She said the dial sits where the sky does not turn," Oszkar offered, delighted by the riddle as if hearing it fresh. [417]

So Ines launched the drone. [422] She set its camera to a long exposure and flew it above the broken dome while the stars came out, cold and bright against the deepening blue. [449] On her screen, the minutes smeared each star into an arc, hundreds of pale rings turning around one fixed point. [469] She followed the star trails inward, tilting the drone until their center sat dead in her frame, and then flew backward down that same line, the sky's one unmoving axis, until it met a crack in the terrace she had walked over a dozen times. [514]

Beneath the loose stones was a slanting shaft, and at the bottom of the shaft, a brass dial the size of a cartwheel. [537] Its rim was studded with cast letters, dull and green, each one wired from behind like a contact. [555] One socket was empty. [559]

The matrix was a mold, not a letter, so Ines pried the lead ballast weight from the drone's belly and melted it over the camp stove. [585] Oszkar held the chip steady with pliers while she poured. [595] The letter came out bright and rough, and it seated in the empty socket with a click that made him gasp. [616]

A tuning lever jutted from the dial's hub, and Ines clipped her spare drone battery to the terminals beside it. [636] Then she gripped the lever and began to pull. [645] It resisted, then gave, and the old speaker horn above the shaft filled with static, then with something beneath the static: a slow, patient pulse. [670]

She listened with her whole body, the way she watched. [680] It was a question, repeating, leaving a gap after each beat, and the gap was the length of a breath. [700]

"It is waiting for an answer," she said. [708]

Beside the horn hung a speaking tube, its mouthpiece furred with dust. [720] Oszkar looked at it, then at her, astonished as ever, and stepped back. [733]

Ines understood then that the frequency had never promised to tell anyone where they belonged. [748] It only asked who was there, and waited for someone to stay long enough to reply. [764]

In the dimness, under the turning stars, she leaned into the tube and said her name. [780]

Somewhere across the dark valley, faint and startled, a light came on. [792]
