The Ember Nexus lay where the seven forge-chimneys of Hallowmere met beneath one cracked dome, and at twilight its coals glowed the color of old wine. [26] Snow drifted through the broken ribs of the roof and hissed into nothing before it touched the heat. [44] Maren Oake knelt there with the bishop's crozier across her lap, its holly-oak shaft split clean through the crook. [63] Everyone in the city called her the hopeless alchemist, and she had never argued. [77] Her gold came out as lead, her elixirs as vinegar, her cures as mild headaches. [92] When the guild masters refused to touch a cracked relic, she had volunteered, and the chapter, with no better choice, had let her. [115] They did not know why. [120]

Nine days ago, at the Naming, the crozier had flowered in her sister's hand. [134] That was how the old staff chose the next anchoress: a single white blossom opening on dead wood, and then a lifetime behind a bricked window in the cathedral wall, receiving bread through a slot. [169] Ilse was fifteen. [172] She had gasped when the flower appeared, and the old bishop, startled, had stumbled on the altar steps, and the crozier had cracked beneath his weight. [198] The rite could not be sealed until the staff was whole and flowering again. [212]

Maren had wept exactly once, alone, in the coal cellar, and then washed her face and gone to work. [231] She felt everything; she had simply learned to carry it the way a kiln carries fire, behind thick walls. [250] Her plan was not to mend the crozier but to vivify it, to wake the holly-oak so completely that it became a tree again and could never be carried to an altar. [282] A living tree names no one. [288]

She had worked through successive approximation, because that was the only method a failure could trust. [304] On the first night she painted the split with sap and ash, and nothing happened. [319] On the third she added snowmelt and the wood sweated. [329] On the fifth, bone meal and a drop of quicksilver, and a green thread crept along the grain. [347] On the eighth, buds rose along the shaft like blisters, pale and tight, and froze before they opened, rimed in frost that did not melt even here, beside the coals. [377] Each attempt came closer, and each stopped at the same wall. [388]

Tonight was the ninth, and Candlemas was tomorrow. [396] She measured her last reagents into the crucible and found herself short of copper for the binding. [413] From her pocket she took the only coin she had kept from her mother, a thin penny worn smooth, stamped with a rose. [436] Her thumb hesitated over the face. [442] Then she let it fall. [447]

The penny struck the crucible's rim, rang once, and vanished into the melt, the rose gone in an instant, and the copper bloomed through the mixture as a bright, living orange. [478] It had to stop being a coin to become anything else. [489]

Maren sat very still. [493] For nine nights she had been careful with the crozier, painting and coaxing, preserving the crook, the silver ferrule, the saints carved on the knop, as though the thing she was freeing could keep its old shape. [530] The frost on the buds was not failure. [538] It was the staff refusing to be both a relic and a tree. [551] Nothing grows inside a shape that is still being carried. [561]

She stood, set the crozier across her knee, and broke it the rest of the way. [577] The crook snapped with a sound like river ice. [586] She pried off the silver ferrule and dropped it into the crucible after the penny; she scraped the saints from the knop with her knife; she split the shaft again and again until she held only raw holly-oak, fibrous and wet. [627] Her hands shook, and she let them. [634] She poured the copper-bright melt over the splinters and pressed them into the deepest coals of the Nexus, where the seven fires met. [657]

For a long moment there was only smoke. [665] Then roots came down through the embers, pale as candle wicks, and found the cracks between the flagstones, and gripped. [685] A trunk thickened out of the fire, black-barked and steaming, and by full dark it stood taller than she did, branching up toward the hole in the dome. [713] The falling snow settled on its twigs and did not melt, and where it rested, white flowers opened, hundreds of them, each one cased in ice and each one alive. [743]

No bishop would ever pull it from those stones. [752] No window would be bricked tomorrow. [758] Maren brushed the ash from her skirt, folded her hands, and allowed herself, at last, a small and steady smile, while above her the frozen blossoms kept on opening in the heat. [790]
