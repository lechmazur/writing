Ines Varga narrated the sweeping of the floor as if it were the siege of a city. [16] "Behold," she told the three children crouched by the dead consoles, "the broom advances upon the dust of four winters, and the dust, outnumbered, flees toward the stairwell." [44] Tavi laughed. [46] Pell, who had not spoken since her father drowned, did not laugh, but she watched the dust go. [64] Outside the great slanted windows of the control room, the reservoir was breaking up. [78] Every few minutes a sound came off the ice like a cello string snapping, and a new black seam opened across the white. [101] The thaw came every March, but nobody in Lower Stell called it spring anymore. [115] They called it the season, and they did not ask questions in it. [128]

Ines had hauled her small furnace up the dam's service road on a sledge in February, when the ice still held. [149] The council had sealed the control room after the flood, and the town had sealed its mouth along with it. [169] Asking why the alarm had not sounded was called picking at the dead. [182] Asking whether the operator had abandoned his post was worse. [192] The operator had been her brother, Tomas. [199]

So Ines, who had spent twenty years turning slowly at the end of a blowpipe, thinking before every breath, decided to build a room where children could ask anything. [228] She could not make the town brave. [235] She could make the questions small and ridiculous first, so the big ones would have somewhere to stand. [253]

"And now," she said, lifting the gather from the glory hole, "our heroine removes a molten star from the belly of the beast, and she is, frankly, underpaid." [281] "Why is it orange?" Tavi asked. [287] "Heat makes it confess," Ines said. [293] "Why does glass break?" [297] "Because it remembers being sand and gets homesick." [305] "Is that true?" [308] "No, and that is an excellent question, and you are allowed to ask it again tomorrow." [324]

Every rule in the room was like that. [332] No question was stupid; every answer could be wrong; anyone could leave, and anyone could come back. [349] She blew floats for the children to carry home, each one holding a trapped bubble, and she called them pocket rooms. [370] A question fits inside, she told them. [377] You carry it until you find someone safe. [385]

On the operator's desk lay the logbook, and in the logbook lay a cracked leather bookmark she had stitched for Tomas when she was twelve. [410] It marked the last page he had written. [418] For three months she had dusted around it, narrating that too, "the brave rag skirts the forbidden book," and never opened it. [440] Her heart had frozen in the flood year the way the reservoir froze, from the edges in, until only a narrow dark channel moved beneath. [465] She had made a place for other people's questions because she could not bear her own. [481]

On the day the ice gave way entirely, Pell walked to the desk and put one finger on the bookmark. [501] "What does it keep?" she said. [507] Her voice was rusty and very small. [514] Ines set down the pipe. [519] The glass on its end sagged, and she let it. [529] "I don't know," she said. [534] "That's an excellent question." [538] She opened the book. [542]

Tomas's handwriting leaned the way he always had, into the wind. [553] Alarm dead again, third time, reported to council twice, no reply. [564] Gate three jammed with ice. [569] Going down to clear it by hand. [576] If I don't come back, don't let them stop anyone asking why. [588]

He had not abandoned his post. [594] He had left the room to save it, and the council had buried his reason with him. [611] Ines felt the channel in her chest widen, a black seam cracking across the white, and she found she was crying and laughing at once, which was undignified, and she said so aloud. [644] "Our heroine weeps upon the evidence," she announced. [652] Pell almost smiled. [655]

Below them the dam groaned, and meltwater began seeping under the door from the stairwell where the dust had fled. [675] The room had never been meant to last. [683] Ines banked the furnace, tucked the logbook under her coat with the bookmark still inside, and gave each child a float. [704] "And now," she said, "the expedition abandons the fortress, carrying its questions to the council hall, where they are overdue." [724] They went down the road in single file, splashing, the glass bubbles bright in their fists, and behind them the reservoir ran open and loud, the way it always had, the way it was supposed to.
