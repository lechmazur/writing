After the radio static cleared, the silence that replaced it was worse. [12] Maren sat still on the watch platform, one hand on the dial and the other pressed to the living rail of the arbor. [35] Below her the clouds lay flat and white as a frozen lake, and above them the old grafted cedars of Hollin Crown knotted together into a roof of branches that held the night in place. [70] Lamp moths drifted through the lattice, and she watched them the way she had watched them for eleven years, counting. [90]

The couriers called her the midnight pattern seeker, half as a joke. [102] They dropped from the mail dirigible at the darkest hour, when the updrafts slept, and they trusted her to tell them where the air would hold. [128] The moths told her first; they bunched before a gust and scattered after, and their small lights wrote the wind in loops no instrument could match. [154] Tonight they were bunching. [158]

Then a voice came through, thin and careful. [166] "Maren, it's Sef." [169] "I'm hung up in the east canopy, and my harness won't release." [181]

She knew the rig he wore, because it had been hers. [192] Before her knee gave out, before she traded the sky for the watch, she had jumped with that harness forty times, and its buckle had always stuck when it was cold. [223]

Twenty minutes ago she had told him the night was calm. [234] She had written it in the log in her own neat hand. [246] Now the moths gathered in a tight, trembling knot above the east ridge, and she understood that a shear would tear through those branches within minutes. [272] If he stayed tangled, the canopy would fill and drag him off the Crown into the clouds. [289]

This was the part nobody envied, the weight of seeing what others could not and deciding what to say. [308] A soothing lie would keep his hands steady. [316] But every courier who had ever stepped off that gondola had done it because the watcher's word was exact, and a single kind lie would make every word after it suspect. [347]

She opened the log and drew one clean line through calm. [358] Beside it she wrote: revised, shear inbound, east. [366] Then she keyed the radio. [371]

"Sef, I was wrong earlier." [376] "Wind is coming in about four minutes, so we are getting you out now." [390]

The pause on the line felt like a held breath. [400] "Okay," he said, and she heard him believe her. [409]

The manual was no help; it described a buckle that behaved. [420] So Maren pulled her spare harness from the hook by the stove, the one she still oiled out of habit, and closed its buckle in her lap. [447] She shut her eyes. [451] Her fingers did not need her mind; they went to the cold steel the way they had on forty frozen mornings, thumb under the tongue, a twist she had never once put into words. [485] Now she had to. [489]

"Left thumb beneath the bar, not on it," she said, feeling each motion as she named it. [506] "Press toward your chest, then turn it a quarter, like you're opening a jar upside down." [522]

"Nothing," he said. [525]

She did it again, slower, and caught what her hands knew that her words had skipped: a small upward lift before the turn, to free the ice-bitten spring. [553] "Lift first," she said, revising herself without shame. [561] "Lift, press, turn." [564]

The moths above the east ridge broke apart all at once, a silent burst of light. [580] Across the dark she saw the cedars bow, and a pale canopy tore loose and sailed off into the clouds. [600] The radio gave her nothing. [605] She waited, the buckle open in her lap. [613]

Then, faint and breathless, Sef's voice: "I'm on the branch." [623] "It let go exactly how you said." [630]

Maren let out a breath that seemed to leave no sound at all. [643] She took up the pen again and added a line beneath the correction, for whoever kept the watch after her: When cold, lift before you turn; the hands remember what the manual forgets. [676]

Out over the clouds the moths were drifting back together, slow and loose, rewriting the air. [692] She watched them, because someone had to, and the silence in the arbor rang like a bell no one had struck. [713]
