The towers of Hollin Gorge were built from the bones of things no one remembered, and every one of them was written on. [23] Ilse Varr ran her fingertips along the nearest spire at dawn, reading ridges and pits the way her grandmother had taught her, while the canyon returned her breathing to her a hundred times. [56] Nothing spoken in Hollin ever truly ended; the walls simply passed it along until it thinned into wind. [74]

The script on the bones was called Ossen, and it had never been meant for eyes. [90] It was a language of texture, grain against thumb, and when Ilse was a girl there had been forty people who could read it aloud. [115] Now there was Old Baret, whose fingers had gone numb, and there was Ilse, who could feel the words but not say them the way they were meant to be said. [146]

Her wrists showed the reason she had been chosen. [155] Beneath the skin her veins ran green-gold, the copper blood of the storyteller line, and it was said that copper carried a voice farther than iron ever could. [183] She did not talk much. [188] When she did, people stopped to listen, because she chose each word as carefully as a climber chooses a handhold. [208]

Baret found her at the base of the tallest tower that evening, leaning on his stick. [224] "When I go, Ossen goes," he said. [231] "You can feel it, but feeling is not speaking." [240]

"Then I will find a way to turn the feeling into sound," Ilse said quietly. [255] "Texture is only a shape the hand remembers, and sound is only a shape the air remembers." [272]

He laughed, then coughed, then pressed something cold into her palm. [283] It was a diamond the size of a sparrow's egg, clouded through the middle by a fracture like a frozen lightning stroke. [305] Every child in the gorge knew the story: the Hollow Stone swallowed the voice of whoever sang into it, and gave it back only to the canyon. [332] Three singers had lost their speech to it, and so it was called cursed. [346]

"The flaw is not a curse," Baret said. [354] "It is a door." [358] "Set it in the Long Throat and it will hold whatever you give it, forever." [373]

The Long Throat was the tunnel that bored through the eastern wall, where an echo could circle for a generation before fading. [395] But a stone could not read bone, and Ilse still had no way to make Ossen audible. [412]

The answer came with the swifts. [418] Each dusk they poured from their nests in the tower crevices and wheeled between the spires, and Ilse, watching from a ledge, finally saw what her grandmother never had. [447] The flock was not flying at random. [454] The carved ridges bent the wind, and the birds rode those bends, so that every rise and dip of the flock traced the grain of the bones beneath it. [483] The swifts were reading Ossen aloud with their bodies, a sentence at a time, every night. [499]

For eleven evenings she learned to hear what she saw: a steep climb became a rising tone, a scatter became a click of the tongue, a long glide became a held vowel. [531] Baret, too weak now to walk, lay by his window and corrected her when she was wrong, tapping the sill. [551]

On the twelfth evening he stopped tapping. [558]

She carried the diamond into the Long Throat that same night, while the swifts rose behind her in their great dark script. [580] She set it in a niche where the fracture faced the tunnel's mouth, and she began to harness the flock, singing each turn of it as it happened, the texture of the towers pouring through her copper-veined throat into the flawed stone. [623] The fracture caught every note and split it, and the tunnel took the pieces and began to circle them. [642] She sang until the last swift folded into its crevice. [652]

When she tried to speak afterward, to say Baret's name, nothing came. [664] The rumor had been true, in its way; the stone had kept her voice. [678] But from the Long Throat, faint and endless, came the sound of Ossen spoken as it had not been spoken in fifty years, rising and gliding, clicking and holding. [707] Children would learn it from the walls now. [715]

Ilse walked home through the towers in the dark, fingertips trailing the bones, while the canyon said in her own voice everything she had once only been able to feel. [745]
