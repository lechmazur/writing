The fog came in off the salt marsh on the third night after the first letter, and with it came the library. [22] Ilse saw it from the causeway: a tower of windows glowing amber inside the mist, as if someone had lit a lantern in a cloud. [47] She lifted the hem of her veil an inch, then let it fall again. [61] In Harrow Fen, dreamers who dreamed true wore gauze over their faces so their visions would not spill onto waking people, and Ilse had worn hers since she was nine, the year she dreamed her father's arrest a week before the constables came. [104]

Tomas Varga had been a confidence man, charming and quick, and the prison had not changed his handwriting. [122] His letter had arrived with a pocket watch wrapped in newspaper and a single instruction: When the fog brings the library, bring this. [145] The second letter was the one Ilse still had to write: her testimony to the parole board, due Friday, deciding whether he came home. [169] She had not decided what it would say. [177]

Everyone in the Fen knew the story of the Library of Unspoken Words, where every apology swallowed and every confession choked back was shelved in the handwriting of the person who had failed to say it. [213] No one knew how to get inside. [220]

Ilse walked until the causeway ended in cold grass and the tower rose above her, doorless on every side. [239] She opened the watch. [243] It was a cheat's instrument, she knew; her father had used it at card tables to lose track of hours on purpose. [265] The second hand ran forward for twelve minutes, then reversed and ran backward for twelve, cyclically, endlessly, so that a mark who glanced at it never quite knew how long he had been playing. [299] Now it ticked against her palm like a small, guilty heart. [310]

Then she noticed the windows. [315] They did not glow steadily; they pulsed, all at once, a slow bright breath. [329] But the mist here was old and heavy, and the light crawled through its thicker banks, so each window reached her a beat after its neighbor. [355] The delays were not random. [360]

Ilse sat in the wet grass, took out the notebook where she recorded her dreams, and began to document them. [380] She timed each lag against the watch, and a strange thing happened: while the hand ran forward, the delays read as nonsense, but when it swung backward the same numbers resolved into letters, A for a lag of one second, B for two, and onward. [425] The cipher unfolded one word per cycle, like a letter being opened fold by fold. [440] She had to wait through each forward stretch, pencil trembling, hardly daring to hope. [454] The first word was SPEAK. [459] Then WHAT, then YOU, then KEPT. [465]

Ilse laughed aloud, a startled, delighted sound, and then pressed her hand over her veiled mouth, afraid the fog would take the joy as an answer. [491] Of course the library spoke backward; her father had always said the truth came easiest in reverse, the way a con unwinds once the mark has already walked away. [520]

What had she kept? [524] She knew. [526] At nine, watching the constables lead him down the lane, she had not said the one word crowding her throat, because a dreamer who had seen it coming had no right to say it. [560] She lifted her veil fully, let the mist touch her face, and said, "Stay." [574]

A door opened in the stone where no door had been. [585]

Inside, the light was warm and patient, and the shelves went up farther than the tower could possibly hold. [604] She walked carefully, the way one walks on thin ice that might also be a floor, reading spines until she found two thin volumes side by side. [631] One bore her name. [635] The other bore his. [639]

She opened her father's first, bracing for a trick. [648] Every page held the same sentence in his quick, charming hand, written over and over across twenty years: I knew you dreamed it, and I never once blamed you. [677]

Ilse held the book against her chest and did not cry, not yet, because the watch was still ticking and the fog would not last forever. [703] Then she opened her own volume, found twenty years of things she had never said to him, and began to copy them out, as carefully as she had ever recorded a dream, for the second letter. [739] At the top of the first page, in her own hand, it said: Come home. [754] She smiled, her veil still lifted, and wrote quickly, before the mist could change its mind. [770]
