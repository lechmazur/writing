The gardener's boy found the body on a Tuesday, under the quince tree in the garden nobody was supposed to know about. [22] By Wednesday all of Ashby knew it was not a body at all, but an infant carved from alder wood, swaddled in rotted lace, boxed beneath a stone that read AGNES HARLOW, TAKEN. [55]

"A stock," said the baker's wife, touching her throat. [64] "What the fair folk leave behind." [70]

Marta Vell, who had set every broken bone in the parish for thirty years, said nothing. [86] She did not believe in fair folk. [93] She believed in levers, in the patience of ligaments, in the small wet click a joint makes when it remembers where it belongs. [116]

She also believed in Wren, who was twelve, and had been left on Marta's doorstep in a basket with a dislocated hip and a note that said only: Not ours. [146]

"Agnes," Wren said that evening, turning the name over like a pebble. [158] She was sanding a splint at the kitchen table, one of Marta's new hinged ones, copied from a drawing of a Roman brace but fitted with a watch spring. [187] "Is that my name, do you think?" [194]

Marta looked up at the ceiling, where she had painted a small star for every bone she had ever set wrong. [215] There were forty-one. [218] The first was Wren's hip. [223] She had pushed it into place too early, and it had slipped, and she had wept, and done it again properly. [244] Over the years the stars had grown into shapes. [253] Her apprentices learned them the way sailors learned the sky: here the Crooked Thumb, here the Farrier's Knee, here the Foundling. [274] A mistake remembered, Marta told them, was a light to steer by. [286]

"I think," Marta said, "that someone was ashamed of a baby with a bad hip, and told the world the fairies had swapped her, and buried a doll so they could be sad about the right child." [323]

"That's a very sure thing to think," said Wren, blowing dust from the hinge. [337]

Marta laughed, because the girl was right, and because Wren always asked the question underneath the question with the face of someone asking for the salt. [363]

They went to the Harlow house the next morning. [372] The hedge maze had been waist-high when Marta was young; now it rose over their heads, clipped sharp as a new coat. [394] Wren led the way without hesitating. [400] Left, left, right, through a gap no wider than a shoulder. [411] Marta did not ask how she knew. [418]

The secret garden was small and very tidy. [426] Someone had weeded around the grave for twelve years. [435] Fresh snowdrops lay on the overturned stone. [442]

Lady Harlow was kneeling beside the empty hole, her right hand held against her chest. [457] The wrist was bent like a branch that had healed wrong. [468]

"I broke it digging," she said, not looking up. [477] "The night I buried her. [482] I couldn't send for you. [487] You'd have asked what I was burying." [494]

Marta had come ready with anger, old and well-kept. [503] She found it had nowhere to go. [510] She looked at the carved baby in its lace, at its hips, which were perfectly straight, and understood that this was not a lie told to the village. [538] It was a wish, whittled by hand. [545] Lady Harlow had buried the daughter she had prayed for, and mourned her every day, and clipped the hedge high so no one would see her kneel. [572]

That was the cleft in Marta's certainty: she had been sure of a monster, and found only a woman who had made a monstrous mistake and then lived inside it. [602]

"I can fix that," Marta said, nodding at the wrist. [612] "It will hurt. [615] I'd have to break it again." [621]

Lady Harlow finally looked at Wren. [627] Something in her face yearned so openly that Marta had to look away. [640]

Wren dug in her pocket and held out a tiny yellowed tooth. [652] Marta had kept it in a jar since the girl was six; Wren must have taken it that morning. [671]

"The baker's wife says a changeling's tooth proves what she is," Wren said. [684] "If it crumbles, fairy. [688] If not, human. [691] You could test it." [695]

Lady Harlow closed her crooked hand around it. [703] She did not test it. [708] She wept. [710]

Marta knelt, took the ruined wrist, found the old seam of the break with her thumbs, and began. [728]

That night she painted a new star on the ceiling, not for her own mistake but for someone else's, set right at last. [751] Wren named it the Mend. [756] Beside it, very faintly, Marta painted a second, for the question she no longer needed answered. [772]
