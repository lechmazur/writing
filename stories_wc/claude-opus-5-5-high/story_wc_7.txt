The greenhouse behind the old Institute had rusted to the color of paprika, and half its panes lay in the weeds like dropped plates. [24] Beatriz Ocampo had cooked for the Directorate canteen for thirty years, and every meal told her something she was not supposed to know. [47] A carrot remembered the hands that pulled it; a stew remembered the cook's divorce; bread remembered, faintly, the field before the war. [69] She tasted memories the way other people tasted salt, and she had learned to keep her mouth shut about it. [89]

Tonight the sky was doing something no one alive had seen. [100] Five planets hung in a single bright seam above the city, the Lantern Alignment, and the Directorate had ordered curtains drawn and radios tuned to approved music. [127] Beatriz had ordered herself to the greenhouse instead. [135]

Teo followed with the shovel, still wearing his dishwasher's apron over the gray jacket he had worn as a border guard. [156] "If they catch us, they'll call it sedition," he whispered. [166]

"They'll call it gardening, because that's what it is," she said. [177] "Nobody ever got shot for a tomato." [184]

She was seventy-one, and she wanted, just once, to make something beautiful out of what the Directorate had left in ruins. [205]

From her apron pocket she took the seed, black and wrinkled as a raisin, which her grandmother had pressed into her palm sixty years ago. [230] It was a knowledge fruit seed, the last from the orchards the Directorate had burned, because a people who could learn quickly could also remember what had been taken from them. [261] Such seeds would sprout only under the doubled light of the Lantern, the old books said. [277]

She planted it in the one bed still holding soil, beneath the broken roof where the planets poured through. [296] By midnight a shoot had broken the surface, and by the third hour it was taller than Teo, branching like a startled nerve. [319]

Beatriz pinched a leaf and set it on her tongue. [329] She tasted smoke. [332] She tasted the Institute's last day, the students herded into trucks, the glass cracking in the heat, her own young self hiding in a cistern. [357] The tree was drinking the ruins, and it would fruit with whatever it drank. [371]

"Give me the shears," she said. [377]

Teo stared as she began to prune, snipping every branch that tasted of fire. [391] "Isn't that lying?" [394]

"It's cooking," she said. [398] "Your head does the same thing every night, mijo." [407] "It trims the paths you don't walk and thickens the ones you do; the doctors call it synaptic plasticity, and my mother called it getting used to things." [435] "This tree thinks with its branches." [441] "I'm not erasing the fire. [446] I'm just not letting it be the only road." [455]

So they worked through the alignment, and the tree rerouted itself around each cut, its new growth reaching toward other memories stored in the soil. [480] Beatriz tasted each tip and chose: a botany lecture, a lullaby hummed over seedlings, the arithmetic of grafting, a first kiss behind the potting shed. [505] She kept one small branch of smoke, because a tree with no scars could not be trusted to tell the truth. [526] Teo, who had been taught to follow orders and nothing else, found himself holding the ladder, then the shears, then asking which branch next. [550]

When the planets slid apart at dawn, the tree hung with forty golden fruits and then went still, finished, a monument grown out of wreckage. [575]

That noon, Beatriz baked them into the canteen's tarts, the way she had once folded bay leaves into ration soup without asking permission. [598] No speeches, no posters, only dessert. [604]

The clerks ate, and something loosened in their faces. [613] A typist began naming the flowers outside the window, Latin and all. [625] Two guards hummed a lullaby they had never been taught. [635] The deputy director asked, very quietly, whether the old Institute greenhouse might be repaired. [649] And beneath the flowers and the lullaby, each of them now carried one small, clear knowledge of fire, so that no one could learn the orchards without learning who had burned them. [681]

In the kitchen, Teo scraped plates and grinned at her. [691] "That was a revolution," he said. [697]

Beatriz tasted the crumb he offered her and found in it his first choice made without an order. [715] "No, mijo," she said, pressing four black seeds into his palm for a Lantern she would not live to see. [735] "That was lunch." [738] "Revolutions burn orchards; we just grew one back." [746]
