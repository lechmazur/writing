The first time I knocked on Mr. Aurel Stane's door, I told him I had run out of sugar. [19] I had a full bag in my cupboard, and he knew it, because the walls of the Halloran Building were thin in the way of old things that have stopped pretending to keep secrets. [53] He filled a chipped cup anyway and asked if I would like tea while I was here. [70] That was how it started: a lie we both agreed to hold, like a teacup passed carefully between two sets of hands. [92]

Every Thursday afterward I came up the stairs with an empty cup, and every Thursday he filled it and put the kettle on. [115] The brass plate by his door said he tutored dead languages: Latin, Coptic, Old Norse, Akkadian by appointment. [133] I never saw a student arrive. [139] What I saw instead were his hands, patiently nimble, turning the pages of grammars so worn they sighed when opened, and pressing an antique desk seal into pools of red wax on letters he never mailed. [175]

The seal was brass gone brown, its handle carved into a coiled fern, its face cut with a mark like a doorway inside a doorway. [200] I had seen that mark before. [206] It was chiseled above the lobby elevator, on the boiler-room lintel, and faintly on the underside of my own windowsill, where no mason would have bothered to sign. [234]

I had moved into the Halloran because I restored old buildings for a living, and because lately I suspected they were trying to tell me something. [260] Cornices cracked in patterns. [264] Radiators knocked in rhythms that repeated like sentences. [272] Stairs groaned on certain steps and not others, and the groaning changed with the season, as if conjugating. [290] I wanted to learn that language the way other people want to learn Italian before a trip, except I had already arrived and could not find the phrasebook. [318]

So over tea I let Mr. Stane teach me Latin, and he let me pretend that was what I wanted. [338] Oddly, his lessons wandered. [342] He would explain the ablative case, then mention that a keystone is simply a word that holds the others up. [362] He would drill me on declensions and ask, casually, which stair on the fourth floor I thought sounded most like a question. [384] I answered correctly every time, though I could not say how I knew. [397]

In August he invited me to the rooftop garden for the Perseids. [409] The garden was his, as far as anyone could tell: tomato cages, a fig tree in a half barrel, climbing roses strung along the parapet where gargoyles hunched with their mouths full of rainwater. [443] We sat on overturned crates and watched the sky come apart in thin silver threads. [458] Beneath us, every meteor seemed to draw an answer from the building: a tick in the drainpipes, a sigh through the vents, a slow settling of stone. [485]

"You hear it," he said, not as a question. [494]

That was when I decided to interrogate him properly, the way he had taught me to parse a hard sentence, one piece at a time. [519]

"How long have you lived here?" [525]

"Longer than the elevator." [529]

"The elevator was installed in 1911." [535]

"Then longer than 1911." [539]

"Why is your seal on my windowsill?" [546]

He turned the seal over in his fingers. [554] "Because the building asked who should sleep in that room, and I answered." [567]

"And the sugar?" [570]

"You never needed sugar, and I never needed a student." [580] He smiled, and the smile was as familiar as a worn banister, something my hand seemed to have known before our first Thursday. [603] "We agreed on those lies so the truth would have somewhere to wait." [616]

He explained, while the sky kept falling, that Latin was never the dead language he taught. [632] The builders of the Halloran had spoken to stone in a tongue older than their contracts, and when they died, only the building remembered it, and only someone who listened could keep it from falling silent. [668] "I am tired," he said. [673] "My hands are still quick, but my ears are going." [683]

He pressed the seal into my palm, warm as a stair tread in afternoon sun. [698]

A meteor crossed the whole sky at once, and below us the Halloran shifted its weight, every beam and lintel speaking a single word I suddenly understood. [725] It meant welcome, and it also meant stay. [733]

Next Thursday I will climb the stairs with an empty cup anyway, and he will fill it, and we will both pretend nothing has changed. [758] It is the kindest lie we know, and the building, listening, agrees. [770]
