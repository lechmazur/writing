Maren Holloway collected lost souls in Echo Gorge, where every word spoken came back three times, a little more honest on each return. [23] The dead drifted down into it the way leaves drift into a drain, and they lingered there, murmuring, until someone came to hear them out. [48] She carried a harp for that purpose, an old pine-framed thing whose neck had warped in a flood long before she inherited it. [71] No string sat true. [75] Every chord came out bent, sour at the edges, like a song remembered by someone half asleep. [92] Her teacher, Old Tobiah, had explained why that mattered. [101] A clean note echoed back clean, and the dead could hide inside it. [114] A crooked note echoed back stranger each time, and somewhere in the distortion a ghost would lose its footing and start to talk. [137] Spectral confessions, Tobiah called them, and he had taught Maren to gather them with a bowed head and a long face, because grief, he said, was the only door out. [167]

Maren had followed his method for eleven years. [175] She had watched hundreds of souls confess and rise toward the rim, and she had watched most of them leave gray, lighter but not whole, like laundry wrung and never dried. [206] Only once had she seen a soul go out shining: her own mother, who had died in a hospice bed laughing at a joke about a goat, and whose ghost had never needed collecting at all. [242] Maren suspected Tobiah was wrong about the door. [250] She suspected it had two hinges. [256]

Tonight she was chasing a runner. [262] Souls usually waited to be found, but this one had fled up the gorge the moment her harp began, leaving behind only the thing he had dropped: a crocodile's tooth, yellowed, bored through for a leather cord. [299] She tucked it into her collar and began to pursue him. [310]

The gorge was blue-black, the stars pinched to a ribbon overhead. [321] She climbed the goat ledges one-handed, the harp strapped to her back, and when the path crumbled she simply stepped across the gap, because it was only a gap and the drop was only a drop. [357] Tobiah used to call her reckless. [363] She thought of it as not making a fuss. [372]

Halfway up, she stopped on a shelf of wet shale and played. [384] The warped strings moaned, and the gorge threw the moan back twisted, then more twisted, until out of the third echo a man's voice broke loose. [410]

"Leave it," he said. [414] "Leave me." [416]

He was crouched above her, thin as smoke, wearing a showman's striped waistcoat. [429]

"I'm not taking anything," Maren said. [435] "I'm only listening." [438]

She plucked another crooked chord, and the confession came tumbling out through its echoes, as confessions always did. [456] His name was Augustin Pell. [461] For thirty years he had toured the river towns as The Man Who Wrestled the Nile, and every night he had shown the crowd that tooth, pried, he swore, from the jaw of the beast that nearly killed him. [500] His grandchildren had worshipped him for it. [507]

"I bought it," he whispered, and the gorge repeated it three times, each one smaller and more miserable. [525] "From a pawnshop in Memphis, for two dollars." [533] "I've never touched a crocodile." [538] "I was afraid of the ducks in the park." [547]

Tobiah would have bowed his head. [553]

Maren felt the laugh arrive before she could stop it, a bright, undignified snort that the gorge seized and flung back doubled. [575] Pell stared at her, wounded. [580] Then the echo reached him, and something in his smoky face cracked, and he laughed too, helplessly, the sound of a man setting down a trunk he had carried up too many stairs. [613]

"Ducks," he wheezed. [616]

"Vicious creatures," Maren agreed, and played on. [623]

Now the distorted notes carried both sounds, shame and laughter braided together, and the gorge returned them woven so tightly she could not tell which was which. [650] That, she thought, was the point. [656]

The sun never showed itself in Echo Gorge. [664] It rose somewhere beyond the rim, and the only proof was a hush, a softening, the black walls going pewter, then rose, then gold along the highest ledges. [692] In the hush of that hidden sunrise, Augustin Pell stood up, still chuckling, and began to climb toward the light. [712] He did not go gray. [717] He went the color of her mother's last morning. [726]

Maren held out the tooth, but he shook his head. [736]

"Keep it," he called down. [741] "Tell the next one it's fake before they ask." [750]

Then he was gone, and the gorge was quiet except for the last of his laughter, still echoing, a little more honest on each return. [775] Maren sat on the cold shale, letting it ache and shine, and tied the tooth to her harp's warped neck. [795]
