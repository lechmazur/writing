The thunder had spoken once over the hills, and the village of Harrowmere was still waiting for it to speak again. [21] Aurel Casimir walked into that waiting with his boots soaked from roads that no longer had names for him. [40] Rain hung above the square in glass beads, unfallen. [49] A baker stood with a loaf halfway to a basket, her mouth open on a word she had not finished. [69] A dog was frozen in the arc of a leap, ears flung back. [82] Nothing breathed but Aurel, and the tiny steel ball rolling inside the platinum labyrinth in his palm. [99]

He had carried the labyrinth for nine years, since the Margrave's court stripped him of his title and sent him east with a single coin. [124] His pupil Jory had given it to him on the boy's twelfth birthday, laughing that his tutor needed something to do with his hands besides tapping the board. [152] Aurel fidgeted the way other men prayed, tilting the silver-white maze until the ball found its corridors, and somewhere in that motion his mind would come unknotted. [179] He had solved a hundred endgames that way. [187] He had never solved himself. [192]

The silence here was not empty. [198] It had texture, a close grain like cloth pulled tight, and it led him through the square to the chapel, where the hush grew thickest. [223] Inside, a loom stood beneath the rose window, and a young man sat at it with the shuttle lifted, caught between one throw and the next. [249] Aurel knew the jaw, the narrow shoulders, the crease of concentration he had once corrected with a gentle rap on the table. [271]

Jory. [272]

Nine years ago, at the Margrave's tournament, Aurel had stood behind the crowd and signaled moves to his pupil with a cough, a shifted cane, a hand to the collar. [302] He had wanted the boy to win so badly that he forgot the boy could. [317] The judges caught him, and Jory's name was struck from every roll in the province alongside his. [334] Aurel had fled before he could look the boy in the face. [346]

Now he looked at the tapestry instead. [353] Its field showed Harrowmere itself, the square, the baker, even the leaping dog, as if Jory had woven the village into stillness thread by thread. [378] But the border was stranger: a long row of frayed ends, some knotted, some split, some merely worn. [396]

Aurel crouched, and his thumb began to tilt the labyrinth without his asking. [409] The ball ran left, doubled back, found a gate. [418] He began to winnow. [422] Moth damage he discarded; its edges were ragged and random. [432] Wear from fingers he set aside. [438] What remained were deliberate frays, each tied in the old weavers' notation he had taught Jory as a game: a double knot for a file, a twist for a rank, a red strand for a capture. [474]

The border was a chess game. [480] It was the tournament game. [485]

He read it move by move, the ball clicking through corridors in his palm, and at the twenty-third move his breath stopped as surely as the rain. [512] He had signaled bishop to g5. [518] Jory had not played it. [523] The knot showed knight to e4, a quieter move, the boy's own, and a worse one; it was the move that lost the game. [547] Jory had refused the cheat and been punished for it anyway, and had never once said so to anyone. [566]

After the final move, the border went on: one loose thread, unknotted, trailing toward the floor. [582] Beside it, in plain letters stitched small, Jory had written: WAITING FOR MY TEACHER TO RESIGN. [598]

Aurel understood then what the hush was. [605] Jory had not frozen the village out of anger; he had woven a pause, a held breath between thunders, and left it for the one player who had walked away from the board. [638] For nine years Aurel had punished himself by never finishing anything, turning the labyrinth in endless loops so he would never have to reach the center. [664] Resignation was not the same as ruin, he had told a hundred pupils. [677] It was how a player honored the truth of the board and was free to play again. [694]

He took the loose thread and tied the resigning knot, the tipped king, a single loop closed on itself. [713] In his other hand, the steel ball slid into the heart of the labyrinth and rested there. [730]

Far off, the thunder spoke again. [736] Rain struck the square, the dog landed barking, and the shuttle flew. [748] Jory turned from the loom, and his face held no surprise, only a fierce and patient quiet. [765]

"Set up the board," he said. [771]

Aurel's hands, for once, were perfectly still. [778]
