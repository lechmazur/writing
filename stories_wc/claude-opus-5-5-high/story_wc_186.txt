Orla Venn had kept the beacon tower for all eleven years since civilization collapsed, though it had not shone once in that time. [23] She swept the stairs anyway, oiled the dead gears, and each dusk walked down to the glass humming pavilion at the tower's foot to listen. [48] The pavilion had been built by the harbor guild, a ring of tuned panes that sang whenever wind came off the sea, and the fishermen of Salt Hollow used its pitch to judge the weather. [83] They did not use the tower. [89] Without its prism, the tower was only a tall stone with a woman in it. [104]

The prism lay on a workbench inside the pavilion, broken into thirty-one pieces the night the grid failed and the looters came. [126] Tamsin had carried it down for cleaning that evening, and he had died holding it together with his hands. [145]

People called Orla retrocognitively blessed, when they were being kind, and cursed when they were not. [161] When she touched a thing, she saw its past: the hands that shaped it, the rooms it had crossed, the weather of its long-ago days. [186] It made her strange company, because she kept noticing patterns. [196] Tamsin had been born the same hour the prism was installed. [207] He had died on the anniversary. [213] Thirty-one pieces, and he had lived thirty-one years. [221]

"Coincidence," said old Bram at the net sheds, not unkindly. [231] "The world broke, Orla." [235] "Things break in numbers." [239]

She wanted to prove him wrong, not for pride, but because if coincidences meant nothing then Tamsin's death meant nothing, and she could not carry that up the stairs every night. [270]

In late autumn, when thistledown drifted through the pavilion like slow snow, she found the thirty-second piece. [287] It was wedged beneath a floor pane, no bigger than a fingernail, and when she lifted it the pavilion stopped singing. [308] Every pane fell silent at once. [314] The wind still moved; the thistle seeds still turned in the air; but the glass held its breath around her palm. [335]

A shard of hush, she thought, and then the past opened. [346]

She saw the lensmaker who had cut the prism, a gray-bearded woman tuning its core crystal to a note so pure it cancelled the pavilion's chord. [372] The hush was the heart; the other pieces only carried light around it. [385] She saw the lensmaker at night on the tower gallery, tracking star paths with a brass sighting rod, marking where each constellation rose so each facet could be set to catch it. [417] The beacon had never burned oil alone. [424] It had gathered starlight and thrown it back doubled, and the pieces had to be arranged by the sky that would light them. [447]

Then, last, she saw Tamsin. [452] Not his death, which she had watched a hundred times through the broken glass, but a moment before it: her brother on his knees in the dark, prying the shard of hush loose and pushing it under the floor pane while the looters' boots rang across the pavilion floor. [501] He had hidden the heart. [506] The looters had broken only the body. [513]

Orla sat a long time in the silence, the shard warm against her lifeline. [527]

That winter she climbed to the gallery on every clear night with Tamsin's surveying compass, tracking the star paths the lensmaker had tracked, matching each fragment's memory to the patch of sky it had been cut to hold. [565] Touching a piece, she would see which stars had first struck it; watching the heavens, she would wait for them to rise. [587] She set them in pine resin and copper wire, one facet at a time, around the hush. [604] The fishermen watched her lantern bob on the gallery and shook their heads. [617]

The last fragment went in on the anniversary night, which she had not planned, and which she noted without comment. [637] She lit the small oil wick at the prism's center and waited for the constellation called the Heron to clear the eastern headland. [660]

When it rose, the beacon rekindled. [666]

Starlight poured into the facets and came out as a white road across the water, and below it the pavilion began to sing again, one clear new note woven through the old chord. [699] Down at the net sheds, doors opened. [706] Far out, beyond the reef, a second light answered from a boat Salt Hollow had given up for lost three days before. [728]

Bram climbed all two hundred stairs at dawn, his grandson, salt-crusted and alive, behind him. [743] "They were steering by nothing," he said. [750] "Then you." [752]

"Tonight of all nights," Orla said. [758]

He looked at the prism, at the last thistledown of winter still drifting past the gallery rail toward whatever ground would take it, and did not say coincidence. [786] Orla did not need him to. [792]
