Ines Varro came down to the grotto before the gulls woke, her saxophone cold against her ribs and her grandfather's waistcoat buttoned over her coat. [25] The waistcoat was the only badge of her office, felt pads from dead instruments stitched into its lining, and the harbor still called it what Tobiah had called it: the vested hush. [57] Whoever wore it became the morning fog saxophonist, entrusted with the one silence the town could not afford to lose. [77]

The fog lay thick on the shingle, and the cave mouth breathed it out in slow white tongues. [95] Inside, the walls glowed, veined with quartz that drank the barest light and gave it back doubled, so the whole grotto seemed lit from within, radiant and drowned at once. [125] At its center, on a plinth of black basalt, stood the prism atrium. [138] It was no bigger than a birdcage, a little glass court of faceted columns and open arches, built by someone no one remembered, and the mist moved through its rooms as if it were visiting. [173]

Every morning, as dewdrops formed on its columns, the atrium began to speak. [186] Not in words. [189] Each bead of water bent the cave light into a sliver of color, and the colors fell across the quartz in sequences, and the quartz hummed a note for every hue. [220] It was a cipher, Tobiah had told her, a prophecy spoken in code, and a code only waits. [238] The moment someone understood it, it would stop waiting. [247] Nobody knew what it foretold, only that the last time a keeper had fallen ill and the hum had gone unanswered for three dawns, the herring had vanished and the chapel bell had cracked in a windless night. [285]

So every morning Ines played it quiet. [292]

She set the reed to her lip and waited for the first color. [305] Tobiah had taught her the method in this same cave when she was nine, his hands over hers on the keys. [326] You do not fight the prophecy's notes, he said; you play the spaces between them. [341] A message is shape as much as sound, and if you fill its silences, it has no shape left to hold. [362] She worked through negative space, laying soft tones into every gap until the code blurred into one long even murmur that meant nothing at all. [387]

The first dewdrop swelled on the eastern arch and threw a thread of amber against the wall. [404] The quartz answered with a low tone, and Ines slid a breath of sound beneath it. [420] Then violet, then green, and she felt the pattern before she heard it, the way you recognize a staircase in the dark. [442]

It was her grandmother's lullaby. [447] The one Mara Varro had sung in the kitchen over the smell of tar and bread, the one with a skipped beat in the third line where she always paused to breathe. [479] The prophecy had never borrowed a human tune before. [488]

Ines's fingers faltered. [491] The colors on the wall were quietly vivid, the soft saturated shades of a photograph left too long in a drawer, and in them she could almost see the kitchen, the blue cups, her grandmother's hands. [527] If she stopped playing, she would hear the whole song again. [538] If she heard it whole, she would understand it. [547]

That was the trick of it, she realized, cold spreading under the waistcoat. [560] The code had learned what she missed. [567] It was offering her the past as a key, and the lock was the future. [582]

She played on, harder now, filling every rest, smothering each familiar phrase in a gray wash of tone. [600] But the lullaby had one silence she could not fill with the horn: the skipped beat, the place where Mara breathed. [621] Her own lungs gave out at exactly that place, as they always had, because she had learned the song on her grandmother's breath. [644] The skipped beat was coming, and the prophecy would ride through it. [656]

So Ines lowered the saxophone, leaned close to the prism atrium, and did the one thing Tobiah had never taught her. [677] She whispered into the silence herself. [683] Not a word of the song, only what Mara used to murmur there between breaths, the single syllable hush, placed precisely in the hollow of the tune. [710]

The quartz caught it and gave it back doubled. [719] The colors shivered, ran together, and slid down the columns as plain water. [732] The grotto dimmed to its ordinary glow. [739]

Ines stood a long time in the fog, trying to remember the rest of the lullaby, and found that she could not; the song had gone into the hush with everything else it had carried. [774] Only the breath remained, and she buttoned the waistcoat over it and walked home to wait for tomorrow's dew. [793]
