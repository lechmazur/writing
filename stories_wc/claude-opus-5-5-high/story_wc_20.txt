The search party disbanded at the foundry gate a little after midnight, lanterns bobbing away down the hill like embers someone had given up on. [25] Oriel Vance stayed. [28] She had been called the balanced alchemist for thirty years, because her scales never lied and neither, people said, did her face. [50] Tonight her face was steady and her hands were not. [60]

Rain had washed the foundry yard black and silver, sluicing soot from the chimneys, pooling in the old casting molds until each held a small, trembling sky. [87] Inside, the furnace breathed low under banked coals. [95] This was where every child in Hollin was given a coin. [106] The foundrymen struck it at birth, a zodiac sign on one face and the child's name on the other, and the coin weighed exactly what the name weighed. [134] It was not superstition; it was measurement, entered in the foundry ledger in grains and tenths, as factual as tide tables and just as mystical. [159] A name inherited from the dead ran heavy. [167] A name chosen in joy ran light. [174]

Mara's had been heavy. [178] She had come to Oriel at twelve, orphaned, carrying her drowned mother's name on a Pisces coin so dense it dragged her pocket crooked. [202] She slept badly. [205] She flinched when anyone called her. [211] And Oriel, who believed in balance above all things, had decided to correct it. [225]

Three nights ago she had taken the coin from Mara's coat and filed its rim, grain by grain, until the scale read like a happy child's. [251] She had meant it as mercy. [257] In the morning Mara had touched her pocket, gone pale as ash, and said, "Something is missing from me." [276] By noon she was gone, and the coin with her. [286]

Now Oriel found it where she had feared she would: in the iron redemption box beside the ledger, where the coins of the lost were surrendered. [312] The foundry law was plain. [317] A surrendered coin could be redeemed only by paying back its recorded weight, and until then its owner belonged to no one, not even herself. [342] Mara had not run away from Oriel; she had run away from a name that no longer fit. [360]

Oriel set the coin on her own brass scale and opened the ledger. [373] Mara Solway, Pisces, forty-one grains. [378] The coin now weighed thirty-three. [383]

She could not restore the filings; they were swept, melted, gone. [394] But she remembered the lacquer bowls her own teacher had kept, cracked and mended with seams of gold so that the breaking became part of the bowl's history rather than its shame. [426] Kintsugi, he had called it, and said the art lived less in the gold than in the intention: you did not hide what you had done. [452]

She stoked the furnace. [456] She did not simply pour gold onto the rim. [465] A name was not a debt to be settled by any metal at hand; she had to learn what it would accept. [487] So she explored its limits, one careful thread at a time. [498] She laid a filament of gold into the scarred edge and weighed it. [511] Thirty-five grains, and the letters of MARA brightened. [519] Another thread, thirty-eight, and the Pisces fish seemed to turn in the lamplight. [532] At forty she tried more, wanting to overpay, to make it heavier than before, and the scale would not settle; the coin shivered and the gold beaded off like rain from glass. [564]

That was the limit. [568] A name could not be lightened by her will, and it could not be made heavier by her guilt. [587] It weighed what it carried: a mother, a river, a girl who had chosen to keep both. [604] Oriel had only been allowed to mend, never to decide. [614]

She drew the last thread with the finest wire she owned. [625] Forty-one grains. [627] The pans went level, and a clear note rang through the wet iron rafters, the sound the ledger called a name coming home. [650] Oriel wrote beneath the entry, in her own hand, redeemed by the one who wronged it, and did not blot the ink. [672]

Behind her, from the dark of the slag loft where the searchers had not thought to climb, someone stirred. [691] Mara came down the ladder slowly, soaked, hollow-eyed, one hand pressed to her chest as if something had just been returned there. [713] She looked at the coin, at the gold running through it like lightning caught in a river. [730]

"You can see where you broke it," she said. [739]

"Yes," Oriel said, and the word cost her more than any grain of gold. [753] "Always." [754]

Mara closed her fingers around the coin, and outside the rain kept falling, washing everything clean except the seams, which shone. [775]
