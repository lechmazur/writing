The wind came through Morrow Vale the way water comes through a cracked bowl, quietly and everywhere at once. [19] Tamsin Reed had stopped hearing it years ago, the way she had stopped hearing the bell. [35] The bell had not rung since the mine closed and the last wagons rolled out beneath the ancient archway at the top of Cinder Street. [60] Now only she remained, along with two goats, the widower Hask, and a crack in the archway's keystone that widened each frost. [82]

The stranger arrived on the first morning of thaw. [91] He was tall and gray and carried nothing but a leather case, and when Tamsin asked his business, he said, "I tune what has forgotten its note." [118]

"The bell's cracked," she told him. [124]

"The bell is fine," he said. [130] "The arch is not." [134]

He opened the case. [138] Inside, on faded velvet, lay a tuning fork carved from ebony, black as a well bottom, its tines so fine they looked like two held breaths. [164] He struck it against his knee and raised it to the arch. [176]

Tamsin heard nothing. [179] Then she heard everything: the wind snagging in the stones, a loose pebble ticking down the mortar, the faint groan of the keystone shifting a hair's width lower. [207] The whole street seemed to lean toward the sound in a wild hush, like grass bowing before a storm that has not yet arrived. [231]

"It sings flat," the tuner said. [237] "It was built to sing with the town." [245]

"There isn't a town." [249]

"There were footsteps once," he said, and knelt to touch the cobblestones worn into shallow cups beneath the arch. [268] "Miners going up at dawn, down at dusk. [276] Children running. [278] Carts. [279] Every step was a small hammer on the arch's foundation, and every hammer kept it true. [295] Stone and people, leaning on each other. [302] That is the balance. [306] When the people left, only the stone kept leaning." [315]

"How long before it falls?" [320]

He studied the crack as if reading a letter. [329] "Before the harvest moon." [333]

Tamsin laughed, because it was that or weep. [341] "And you'll save it by what, walking?" [348]

"We will," he said. [352]

When she asked why a stranger should care, he only touched the arch's flank and murmured, "I hung that bell when this arch was young, and a debt of balance is shared or it is nothing." [388] The bell was two hundred years old. [395] She decided not to ask again. [401]

Each dawn he walked from the chapel to the arch and back, his boots striking the cobbles in a pattern she could not follow: three steps, a pause, two, a long drag of heel. [435] Each dusk he struck the ebony fork, listened, shook his head, and began again. [449] He was infinitely patient, the way a river is patient with a canyon. [462]

On the ninth day, tired of watching, Tamsin fell in beside him. [474] She stumbled the rhythm, cursed, and tried again. [482] When the fork rang that evening, the flat note had lifted, only a little, like a sleeper turning over. [501]

On the twentieth day Hask joined them, cane and all, his uneven gait somehow fitting the pause. [518]

"Three is not a town," Tamsin said. [525]

"No," the tuner agreed. [529] "A town is whoever answers." [534]

So she wrote letters. [538] She wrote to her brother in the river cities, to the Pell sisters, to the schoolmaster's son, to every name she could find in the old mine ledgers. [566] She did not beg. [570] She wrote only: The arch is falling; come walk under it once before it does. [585]

Some came from nostalgia, some from curiosity, some to say goodbye. [596] The tuner met each at the arch and taught them the steps without a word, tapping the cadence on his palm. [617] By midsummer there were forty pairs of feet on Cinder Street. [628] By late summer there were a hundred, and those who had come for a day had begun to rebuild the bakery roof, clear the well, and argue about reopening the school. [659] Children who had never seen Morrow Vale ran the rhythm wrong and laughed, and the tuner said the wrong steps were needed too. [682]

Still the crack did not close. [688]

Then, on the eve of the harvest moon, the whole valley walked beneath the arch at once, and Tamsin felt the stones shiver under her soles. [714] A low hum rose from the foundations, climbed, and settled, and the keystone slid upward with a sound like a door clicking shut. [737] High in the chapel tower, untouched, the bell rang once. [747] The hush afterward was wild and enormous, a hundred people holding one breath. [760]

By morning the tuner was gone, and the ebony fork lay on Tamsin's doorstep with a note: It will need tuning every spring. [783] So will you. [786]

She struck it, heard the true note, and went out walking. [797]
