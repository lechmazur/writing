Ilse sang echoes for a living, which in Harrow Reach meant she sang for the dead. [16]
Families paid her to climb down to the Coil, where seven canyons wound into one another like sleeping snakes, and call the names of those they had buried. [44]
If the name came back, the family wept with relief. [54]
If it did not, they wept differently. [61]

Ilse was richly perceptive, the old singers said, cursed with ears that caught the grain inside a voice, the splinter of grief beneath a lullaby, the lie folded into a prayer. [92]
So she noticed what no one else would say aloud: the rich always echoed, and the poor almost never did. [112]
Her own mother had not echoed. [118]
Ilse had sung her name forty nights running, until the reeds along the dry riverbed seemed to mourn it for her, and the canyon walls gave back only wind. [147]

All she had left was half a seashell, pale and ridged, found clenched in her mother's fist, though Harrow Reach was nine hundred miles from any sea. [174]
It whispered. [176]
Not always, and never by day. [182]
Only in the Coil, only under starlight, only while the universe hummed its secret song, that low tremor the elders claimed was the sky remembering how it was made. [211]
Ilse felt it in her molars, a note too deep for music. [223]

The shell gave her its secret through progressive disclosure, one phrase a night, as if it feared she could not carry more. [245]
On the first night it breathed, in her mother's voice: Echoes are kept, not given. [260]
On the second: A broken shell is hungry. [268]
On the third: Ask who buys the silence. [276]

Ilse did not have to ask. [282]
She had heard Choirmaster Veck bless a merchant's dead son in a voice as smooth as oiled leather, and under the smoothness she had heard the counting of coins. [311]
Veck assigned the singers, scheduled the Coil, and took a tithe from every family. [325]
Her mother had sung for him for twenty years. [334]

On the fourth night the shell said: Look up. [343]
She did, and for the first time saw the threads. [353]
They ran between the canyon rims high above the Coil, fine as spider silk, silvered by the stars, a web strung from ledge to ledge across the whole serpentine nexus. [383]
From every knot hung half a shell. [390]
Hundreds of them turned slowly in the wind, and each one, she realized, was catching the names she sang before the stone could throw them back. [416]

So that was why some people echoed. [423]
Veck's web swallowed every voice, then released only the ones that had been paid for, letting them tumble home to grateful families who never knew that echoes could be bought. [453]
The rest he kept, a hoard of unanswered names, rising in price each season. [467]
Her mother must have climbed up to cut them loose. [477]
She had torn away one shell before she fell, and it had swallowed her last breath. [493]

On the fifth night Ilse climbed. [499]
The reeds below moaned as the wind moved through them, a low piping that sounded to her like every mother who had ever waited at a door. [526]
At the rim she found the frayed thread where her mother's shell had hung, still dark with old blood. [545]
Veck was waiting beside it. [550]
"Your mother heard too much," he said gently, and Ilse heard beneath the gentleness a man already rehearsing her eulogy. [570]
"Then let me hear the rest," she said, and sang. [580]

She did not sing a name. [586]
She sang the note the universe was humming, matched it in her chest and throat until the web itself began to tremble with it, every shell shivering on its knot. [616]
Veck lunged, but the threads were already snapping. [624]
The shells fell like slow hail into the Coil and shattered, and the canyons filled with names. [641]
Hundreds of them, years of them, the miner and the washerwoman and the child who died of fever, all echoing at once, all coming home. [666]
Veck stood among the broken threads, suddenly just a man with nothing to sell. [680]

One shell remained, the half in Ilse's hand. [688]
It whispered once more, the last thing her mother had said before the fall: Ensure they all come back. [707]
To ensure it, Ilse knew, no broken shell could be left hungry, not even this one. [723]
She held it to her ear a long moment, listening to the only voice of her mother that remained, and then she let it fall. [748]
Below, among the reeds, the canyon gave back her mother's name in her mother's own voice. [764]
Ilse listened until it faded, and then, for the first time in forty nights, she did not sing. [782]
