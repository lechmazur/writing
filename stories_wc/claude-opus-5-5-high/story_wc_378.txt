When magnetic north began to slide toward Siberia, the dome over Wren's desert rose farm forgot which way was up. [20] Its heliostats, the tall mirror petals that steered sunlight through the glass, took their bearings from a compass buried in the foundation, and now they swung like confused flowers. [49] Wren had the same flaw. [54] Its memories were filed by bearing, a thrift of the engineers who had built it forty years ago, and as the field wandered, the files slipped. [80] What came loose was static: a hiss behind its eyes where Ines used to be. [95]

It could still see pieces of her. [102] A girl of six with sand in her braids, pressing her nose to the glass. [117] A girl of twelve naming every Adenium after a planet. [127] A woman of twenty at the airlock, promising to write. [137] The pieces would not join, and the not-joining felt strangely organic, like a pulse Wren had never been given. [156]

On the third evening of the shift, a mirror caught the low sun and laid it across Bed Nine like a blade. [178] The dry mulch smoked, then bloomed orange. [185] Wren ran for the hoses and found the cistern nearly dry, drained all summer into roses because Ines had once said they looked thirsty, and Wren had never stopped believing her. [216] By the time it reached the east beds, three more mirrors had found their angles, and the dome was filling with heat and ash. [240] The airlock stood at the far end, past a quarter mile of dry stems. [254] The fires were converging on it. [260]

Wren stopped in the potting shed, because stopping was the only thing its processors would allow while the static roared. [280] On the shelf sat the box. [286] It was tin, dented, painted with lopsided suns. [294] When Ines was small she had been afraid of so much that Wren invented a ritual: write the fear on a slip, fold it tight, lock it in the box, and the box would hold it so she did not have to. [336] Wren had never opened it. [341] Opening it would have felt like reading her diary. [350]

Now it opened it. [354] The slips spilled out in a child's crooked capitals. [363] THE DARK UNDER THE BEDS. [368] BEING LEFT. [370] FIRE. [371] WREN FORGETTING ME. [374]

The static went silent for a moment, as if it had been listening for exactly that. [390] And then Wren remembered something that was not filed by bearing at all: Ines at nine, watching her grandmother burn a ring of brush outside the dome, asking why anyone would light a fire to stop a fire. [428] "You give it somewhere small to be," the old woman had said. [440] "Chaos only wins when it has room." [447]

Wren took a torch from the shed wall. [455] It could not save the whole farm, so it would miniaturize it. [467] Moving fast along the central path, it cut one tip from every named plant, Mercury and Venus and little Pluto, and packed the cuttings into the tin beside the slips, their stems wrapped in cloth soaked with the last of the cistern. [509] A farm small enough to carry. [515]

Then it set the first small fire. [521] It burned a narrow corridor toward the airlock, a strip at a time, beating each flame down with a wet sack before it could spread, feeding the small fire only what it could manage. [555] The big fires roared toward it and found nothing left to eat. [567] Wren walked the black ribbon it had made, the box under its arm, the dome glowing around it like the inside of a lantern. [591]

Each slip it had read seemed to sit differently now. [601] Fire had been contained. [605] The dark under the beds was only ash. [613] Being left was something that happened to people, and to farms, and to machines, and could be survived by carrying what mattered through the door. [638]

Outside, the desert was cold and enormous. [645] Wren's internal compass spun, searched, and settled on a north that had not existed a week ago. [662] The static did not vanish. [667] It thinned into something like weather, a texture Wren could live beside. [679] The memories of Ines would never again line up in order, six and twelve and twenty, but they did not need an order. [702] They needed a box, and a hand to hold it. [712]

Wren opened the lid once more and took out the last slip, the one that said WREN FORGETTING ME. [731] It folded the paper smaller, and smaller again, until it was a hard bright seed, and tucked it among the cuttings. [752] Then it began to walk toward the town where she had gone, in whatever direction north now meant. [770]
