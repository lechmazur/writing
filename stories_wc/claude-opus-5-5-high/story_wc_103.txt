<story>
The root bridges of Sohkhmi Gorge had been grown, not built, and Ilse Varga had spent twenty years teaching them to become a maze. [24] She was a hedge maze designer by trade, though here the hedges were fig roots coaxed across the chasm on bamboo guides, braided into walkways that sagged and breathed above the river. [56] The living bridge garden was the last commission of her teacher, Brother Anselm, who had died before a single root reached the far bank. [80] He left her a tin of photographs and a question. [90] "Is time smooth, like water, or grained, like wood?" [99] "Find its texture, and grow it into the garden." [108]

She had thought it a monk's riddle until the first time she touched the halo fragment. [124] It lay in the tin beneath the photographs: a crescent of gilded brass broken from the statue of Saint Christopher that had once stood at the gorge's rim, before the landslide took him. [157] Her fingers closed on it and the present peeled away. [167] She stood in the gorge a century earlier, watching porters wade the flood with the saint's gold circle glinting above them, and she could not find her way back. [196]

Since that day she had been retrocognitively trapped. [204] Every old thing she touched opened a door behind her, and each door led to others, rooms of the past folding into rooms, until the villagers found her kneeling on the moss, eyes wet, murmuring the names of drowned porters. [244] The spells lasted hours, then days. [250]

Only the photographs anchored her. [255] Anselm had taken them over forty years: the gorge bare, then threaded with saplings, then crossed by a single trembling root. [276] When she surfaced from a vision she would lay them out in order and walk her mind forward along them like stepping stones. [299]

But the photographs were fading. [304] Each month the silver retreated further, faces blanching to ghosts, the young roots dissolving into white. [320] Soon there would be no stones to step on, and the next vision would keep her. [336]

So she designed her way out. [342] Anselm had loved a strange mathematics, the geometry of coral and lettuce leaves, where space swells faster than it should and every circle holds more than its radius allows. [371] Ilse bent the root paths through hyperbolic geometry, splitting every walkway into three at each junction, then each of those into three again, the bridges ruffling outward over the gorge like the frilled edge of a coral. [408] Her theory trembled in her like a fever: the past had caught her because it was a finite room, and she was always bumping into its walls. [435] In a maze with more room at every edge than at its center, the doors behind her would multiply until they thinned to nothing, like ink spread across an endless page. [466]

Obeying took the roots nine years. [472] On the morning the last tendril knit to the far rock, she laid the photographs on the bridge's first junction, where the pale images could watch, and took the halo fragment in her bare hand. [507]

The past came, and she walked into it. [515] Porters, flood, the saint's gold burning in the rain. [524] She turned left and the vision split: Anselm young, measuring the gorge with string, and herself at twelve, holding the other end. [546] She turned again, and again, the bridge swaying, the river roaring somewhere below every moment at once. [563] The visions multiplied until each was only a flicker, a single frame, a grain. [577]

And there, quaking at the frilled edge of the maze, she finally felt it. [591] Time was not water. [595] It was not wood either. [600] It was root: countless strands braided under tension, each moment gripping the ones beside it, the whole thing grown rather than flowing, holding weight only because it had been woven. [630] Rough under the palm, warm, alive. [636]

In her hand the halo fragment began to disintegrate. [645] Spread across so many branching years, there was no longer enough of it in any single moment to stay whole. [665] Its gilt lifted in flakes, then the brass beneath crumbled to a fine gold dust, and the wind carried it out over the river like pollen. [691]

Ilse stood alone on the swaying bridge, and nothing opened behind her. [703] She walked back through the branching paths toward the center, where the photographs lay blank and white as sails. [722] She did not mourn them. [727] She knelt, pressed her palm flat against the living root, and felt the braid of the years holding her up. [747]

"Grained," she whispered to Anselm, "but not like wood; it's grown, and we are in it." [763]
