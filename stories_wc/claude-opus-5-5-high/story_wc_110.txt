Nobody in Carrow had spoken aloud for eleven years, not since the Attunement taught every mind to hum its meaning into the next. [23] Words had become unnecessary, and so they had quietly become rude. [34] Ilan Brisk, the quiet night registrar, still kept a pen. [44]

He sat in the glass booth at the edge of the Hall of Records from dusk until the milk carts, logging the births and deaths that arrived in the dark, and the townsfolk who brought him their news always left frowning. [85] His mind, they said, was ambiguously clear. [92] They could feel him the way you feel a window: plainly there, plainly transparent, yet something hung behind the pane that none of them could name. [118] In a town where every feeling was public, a man with a hidden room inside him was a man you counted your change around. [142]

The first ticket appeared on a Tuesday, slid beneath his door. [153] It was a stub from the old Meridian concert series, the gilt edges worn soft, and on its back someone had drawn a violin with its strings cut. [181] Ilan knew the date printed on the front. [189] He had registered a death that night. [196]

He turned the ticket over for a long time, then drew a small closed box on its back and left it on the step. [220] By morning it was gone, and the next evening another waited: a violin, a box, and a question mark shaped like a hook. [243]

He knew who sent them. [248] Mara Venn had been nine when her father, the Meridian's first violin, was entered in the ledger as lost in the river. [270] She was twenty now, and her mistrust of Ilan pressed against the booth glass every time she crossed the square, a cold flat hand. [294]

The doodles went on for weeks, a conversation conducted in the only language left for someone whose mind could not be read. [316] She drew rivers; he drew a bridge. [323] She drew an eye; he drew an eye closed. [332] She drew the registry ledger with a crack down its spine, and Ilan sat with that one until his tea went cold. [354]

The truth was held in the forecourt. [361] The plaza before the Hall had been paved, generations back, with singing quartz, and after rain the stone gave up what it had absorbed: murmurs, footfalls, the ghosts of old voices whispering up from the wet crystal. [398] Most people crossed it quickly. [403]

Ilan went out at two in the morning, when the puddles still ringed the lamps, and knelt with the lens. [423] He had taken it from the Meridian's projection booth when the hall was gutted, a thick disc of ground glass that once threw films across a ceiling. [450] Held to the quartz, it gathered the whispers and cast them as faint, trembling pictures on the wet paving. [469] He had used it before to eavesdrop on the stone, to hear his mother laughing in a year before the Attunement. [490]

Tonight he searched for one particular night. [497] There: a man in a rain-dark coat, violin case under his arm, bending to the booth window. [514] Ilan heard his own younger voice, and Tobiah Venn's, speaking aloud, because it was the week before the Attunement and speech still meant something. [538]

"I cannot let the whole town inside my head," Tobiah whispered from the crystal. [552] "Write me drowned. [555] Let Mara hate the river, not me." [562]

Ilan had written it. [566] He had carried the lie for eleven years, and the lie was the shape behind his glass. [583]

The next ticket he left was different. [590] On its back he drew the forecourt, a raincloud, and a clock with both hands at two. [607]

She came. [609] She stood at the edge of the wet quartz with her arms folded, her distrust so loud in the hum between them that the lamps seemed to lean away. [638] Ilan did not try to explain; explaining was for the old world. [650] He placed the projector lens in her palm and guided her hand down toward the stone. [666]

The pictures rose. [669] Her father's face, younger than her memory of it. [678] The violin case. [681] The words, whispering up from the rain. [688]

Ilan felt the moment honesty freed him, a latch lifting, the hidden room swinging open until there was nothing behind the pane at all. [712] Mara looked up, and for the first time her mind met his and found only glass. [728] She did not forgive him, not yet; he could feel that too, honest and bruised. [743] But the cold hand was gone from the air. [752]

In the resonant hush of the forecourt, with the last drops ticking off the eaves, she took a ticket from her pocket and drew a road leading out of Carrow, then two figures walking it. [787] She pressed it into his hand, a question and an invitation. [798]
