The lightning hit the tether at 2:14, and every light in the Kestrel Observatory went dark except the red one over the breaker panel. [24] Samira Haddad pressed her palm to the panel's metal skin and felt the heat coming off it like fever. [43] Forty minutes, the manual said, before the breakers cooled enough to reset. [55] Forty minutes of the balloon drifting at the end of its cable, eleven kilometers above the desert, with no one in the control room below able to see what she saw. [86] She had been waiting eleven months for this. [94]

The telescope ran on its own battery, sealed and separate, but the software that sat between the sky and her eyes did not. [117] The contractor that leased Kestrel called it the Clarity Suite. [127] It scrubbed the incoming data of anything it judged to be noise: cosmic ray hits, satellite glints, the small unexplained wobbles of distant rocks. [151] Samira had read the patent. [156] The same company had written the classifier that once looked down at a courtyard in Wadi Sirr and decided that two hundred people dancing under strung lights were a pattern of life consistent with hostile assembly. [192]

Her wedding had been filtered, too. [198] She had been inside the house fixing her veil when the first missile came. [212] Karim had been outside with her brothers. [219] What survived was her, and the geode he had given her that morning, a gray lump the size of a fist, split cleanly to show the violet teeth inside. [248] "Ugly on the outside," he had said, grinning, "like me." [258] "All the good stuff is where nobody looks." [266]

She set the geode on her notebook now, to keep the pages flat in the draft from the vents, and pulled up the raw feed. [291] It was hideous and beautiful. [296] Unfiltered, the sky crawled. [300] Streaks, sparks, ghosts of reflected sunlight, and underneath all of it the faint slow tracks of the outer rocks she had been following in secret, their positions logged by hand every night the Suite went down for maintenance. [338] Seven of them. [341] Seven small bodies that did not go where Newton said they should. [353]

The method was two centuries old. [359] Le Verrier had used it to find Neptune without ever looking for Neptune: you calculate the orbit a thing ought to follow, you measure the orbit it actually follows, and you treat the difference as a small disturbance, a perturbation, laid on top of the clean solution. [406] Then you work backward, term by term, until the disturbance tells you the shape of what is pulling. [424] You hunt the invisible by the bruises it leaves on the visible. [436]

Samira worked fast, the pencil tearing the paper. [444] First-order corrections, then second. [448] The seven tracks bent toward a region past the Kuiper cliff where the catalogues showed nothing, a place the Suite had smoothed over a thousand times as statistical error. [477] Nothing there, and yet everything leaned toward it. [485] A potential well, deep and unspent, where energy pooled and waited, never converted into motion. [500]

She knew that shape. [504] It was the shape of every year Karim did not get to live, every child, every argument, every ordinary Tuesday, all of it still exerting its pull on her from the place where it should have been. [541] Intangibly tangible, she thought: nothing you could hold, and yet it moved the whole sky. [556] She had come to Kestrel to find where unused potential pools, because she needed proof that it did not simply vanish when a classifier called it noise. [583]

Now the numbers converged. [587] A mass roughly five Earths, cold and dark, on an orbit of twenty thousand years. [602] She slewed the telescope by hand, the gears grinding in the silence, and took a single long exposure into the coordinates. [623] A faint smudge, reddish, barely brighter than the dark. [632] Something enormous that no one had been allowed to see. [642] Her laugh came out as a sob, and the sob came out as a laugh, and she did not try to separate them. [665]

The red light over the panel flickered to amber. [674] In four minutes the breakers would reset, the Suite would wake, and the smudge would be classified, filed, and erased. [694] The dead would stay dead. [699] The company would keep its contracts. [705] Nothing she found tonight would change the courtyard. [713]

She photographed the frame on her own phone, then her notebook page, the geode resting on the equations like a heart split open to show what it had been holding. [743] She sent both over the emergency satlink to every observatory address she knew, one after another, until the lights came back. [764] Then she named it, quietly, for a man who had liked ugly things with good insides. [780]
