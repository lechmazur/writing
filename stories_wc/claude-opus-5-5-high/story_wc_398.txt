The final bus pulled away from Sorra station at nine, its taillights sliding down the switchbacks until the valley swallowed them. [21] Benedek watched from the ridge, lantern low, as he had watched every harvest night for thirty-one years. [38] A lone sentinel was the vineyard's oldest custom: someone awake among the rows when the grapes were heaviest and the boars and thieves most hungry. [63] The station below was vacant now, its bench unlit, except for a young woman who had not boarded. [81] He saw a small flame bloom in her hands, then die. [92] By the time he reached her, she was grinding ash into the gravel with her heel, and only a curled edge of paper remained between her fingers. [119]

"There is no other bus until Monday," he said. [128] "The pavilion is warm, and the frost will come before midnight." [139]

She looked at him the way people look at uniforms, measuring threat, and then at the dark hill, measuring cold. [159] She followed. [161]

The tasting pavilion stood at the head of the oldest rows, eight panes of amber glass set in oak, so that even by lantern light the room glowed like a cask held to the sun. [196] Fallen leaves had drifted against the door, copper and rust. [206] Benedek poured her a cup of warmed wine, spiced with clove, and did not ask. [221] She told him anyway, because the glass made the silence feel like a confessional. [235]

Her name was Anika; the letter was from Tomas, who pressed grapes at this very estate and who had written that he would not follow her to the city. [264] "He says we come from different roots," she said. [273] "Four years, and he calls us mismatched in ink." [282]

She held out the surviving edge. [288] In brown, singed script it read only: "...not the same root, and..." [300]

Benedek knew Tomas, knew his careful hands and his inability to say aloud anything that mattered. [316] He had watched the boy draft and redraft that letter at the press-house table for a week. [333] It was not his to explain, but it might be his to translate. [346] He had guarded this hill against theft for three decades, and he knew understanding could be stolen, too, by a single misread line. [369]

He lifted the lantern and led her to the nearest vine, its trunk thick and black and knuckled. [387] "Look here, at the knee," he said gently, touching a seam in the bark. [401] "A hundred years ago a louse came through this valley and ate every root to nothing." [417] "The only cure was to graft our grapes onto wild American stock, a root nobody here wanted, rough and foreign and sour." [439] "Not the same root." [443]

Anika frowned at the seam. [448] "So they were mismatched." [452]

"Destined to be," Benedek said. [457] "The root cannot make a grape worth drinking, and the vine cannot survive the soil alone." [473] "Every bottle from this hill is an argument between two things that will never agree, held together at the knee." [493]

Anika stood very still, the amber light pooling in her upturned palm, and he watched memory arrive on her face like weather. [515] "He said that to me once," she whispered. [523] "Our first autumn, in this pavilion, when I asked why he loved a girl who hated the country." [541] She closed her eyes. [545] "Not the same root, and still one wine." [553]

The words sat between them, and Benedek let them sit. [563]

"I read it as goodbye," she said. [570] "I burned it before I let myself reach the word and." [581]

"Most people stop at the hard part," Benedek said, not unkindly. [592] "I keep this watch alone because I once did the same." [603]

He did not say more, and she did not ask; the frost was coming, and some grafts only take in the dark. [625]

Near dawn she walked down to the press house, where a light had burned all night, the charred edge folded in her fist like a seed. [651] Benedek returned to the ridge and watched the empty station, where no bus would come until Monday, and found he did not mind the waiting. [676]
