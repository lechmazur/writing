The Ember Nexus opened at twilight, when the city's nine forge-vents exhaled at once and their heat braided into a single slow column above the square. [26] Mara Voss came there with a failed sun in her coat pocket and a decision she had not yet said aloud. [47] Tonight was the last attempt. [52]

She was a pocket-sun alchemist, which meant she spent her nights coaxing light into marble-sized spheres, and she was scientifically romantic, which meant she believed that anything felt could, with patience, be measured. [85] For three years she had tried to make a sun that held the density of presence, the particular weight of an evening spent beside someone who mattered. [112] Every sun came out bright and light as foam. [121] She dropped each one into the vents afterward, because a failure kept was a failure studied, and she could not bear to study them anymore. [146]

Pell was already waiting on the warden's step, soot to the elbows, his lantern-rake leaned against the rail. [164] He tended the Nexus the way other men tended gardens, and he had watched every one of her attempts without once calling them failures. [188]

She set out her tools on the stone between them. [198] First came the columella curl, the spiral spine of her grandmother's nautilus shell, worn by the sea until only the inner twist remained, pale as a knuckle. [225] She used it as a mold because the logarithmic spiral packs the most into the least, and presence, she reasoned, ought to be packed. [249] Then came the tin she called her palette of resolve: seven pigment salts in seven wells, each labeled in her student handwriting. [271] Begin was copper green; Endure, strontium red; Admit, a sulfur yellow; Ask, Forgive, and Stay in blues and violets she had mixed herself. [294] The seventh well, Return, was still full, a white salt that had never touched a flame. [310] She had never seen the point of returning to anything; she always started clean. [324]

She was reaching for Begin when Pell leaned close, his breath smelling of cinder and cardamom, and whispered into her ear. [345]

"I never let them burn out." [351]

It took him less than a breath to say, and it was the longest moment of her life. [369] Inside it, she understood. [373] Every sun she had thrown away, he had caught with the rake before it reached the deep coals, and carried down to the cooling chamber beneath the square, where the vents' leftover heat could keep them lit. [410] Three years of evenings, each one sealed in glass. [419] Each one spent beside him. [424] She had always carried her suns home to weigh them alone on the attic bench, and found them weightless. [443]

But a nautilus does not discard its old chambers, she thought, her thumb on the columella; it seals them and threads a living cord through all of them, and that is how it floats. [477] Density was never in a single chamber. [484] It was in the sum. [489]

She did not plan what she did next, but she aimed it, the way she aimed a flame. [507] Her hand left Begin and pressed into the white well instead, and the salt of Return clung to her fingers like frost. [529]

"Bring them up," she said, before Pell had even straightened. [539]

He did not ask why; he opened the grate, and the chamber below breathed out a hundred dim lights, amber and green and red, climbing the heat column like bubbles rising through dark water. [573] Mara held the columella curl into the braid of heat and dusted it with the white salt. [590] The old suns found the spiral as if they had been waiting for it, one after another, spinning inward along its turns, each one entering not as it had left but carrying the colors she had given it, endurance and admission and the small hard violet of staying. [638] They had gone into the dark as failures; they came back as chambers. [651] She tossed the failed sun from her pocket in after them, and the decision she had carried with it went out instead. [673]

When the last one settled, a single sun hung at the heart of the curl, no bigger than a plum, and when she cupped it, it was heavy. [701] Not with mass. [704] With evenings. [706]

For three years she had tried to escape her work by making something new; the work had only been waiting to be revalued, looked at again with someone beside her. [736]

Pell was watching her, soot on his cheek, the whisper still warm between them. [750]

"I kept them because you were in them," he said. [760]

"So were you," she said, and put the heavy sun in his hand so he could feel it too. [779]
