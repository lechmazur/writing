The archive of extinct languages kept its dead tongues the way other houses kept bees, and on the last night before Tomas left for the southern post, the oldest hive began to scream. [33] Ines Varo heard it from the reading room, a grinding of vowels behind the bronze door of the Aurel Covenant. [53] She had spent thirty years as an arcane linguist, coaxing grammar out of ash, and she knew the sound. [72] A promise-language was knotting itself. [77] The Aurelians had spoken only in oaths; every sentence bound the speaker, and every clause hooked into the one before, so that their whole literature hung together like chain mail. [107] When one link slipped, the rest pulled tight, and a snarled Covenant could crush its own meaning to silence in a single night. [130]

She found Tomas already there, coat on, satchel strapped, staring at the page as it curled like a burning leaf. [150] They had not spoken in three months. [157] Not since the tribunal, when the Provost accused him of losing the Merrow codex and Ines, who knew he had not, said nothing at all. [182]

"It takes two voices to unsnarl it," he said, without turning. [193] "You taught me that." [197]

"I did." [199]

She set the glass ink well on the lectern. [208] It was the archive's oldest tool, blown from sand the Aurelians had melted on their wedding hearths, and the ink inside it would only hold a true promise. [236] Write a lie with it and the letters boiled off the page, taking a little of the writer's voice with them. [257] Ines had once lost a whole week of speech to a promise she only half meant. [273] The method was simple and terrifying: each speaker wrote one promise, and each new promise had to rest on the one before it, until the chain was long enough to take the Covenant's weight. [307]

Tomas eyed the ink. [311] "We don't have to," he said. [317] "I'm gone at dawn; let it die." [324]

"I'm not letting anything else die in silence." [332]

She had been careful about this for three months, which was its own kind of recklessness; now she would be reckless carefully. [354] She weighed each word the way a climber tests a hold, then dipped the pen and wrote the first link in Aurelian: I promise that my silence was fear, not belief. [385]

The ink held. [388] Somewhere in the vault a single low note sounded, clear as a struck bell. [402]

Tomas stared at it, then at her. [409] He took the pen. [413] Because your fear was true, he wrote, I promise to believe it, though I am still angry. [430]

A second note answered the first, a fifth above. [439]

Ines laughed before she could stop herself, a short startled sound, and wrote faster. [453] Because you are angry, I promise to hear all of it, and not to flinch. [468]

Because you will hear it, I promise to say it plainly, and then to let it end. [485]

The notes multiplied. [488] Each promise leaned on the last, and each drew a new voice out of the Covenant, until the archive was ringing with harmonies no living throat had made in a thousand years. [520] The Aurelians had written of symphonies of truth, and Ines had always assumed they meant a metaphor. [537] On the lectern, the curled page began to relax, its knotted clauses sliding loose one by one as the new chain took their weight. [561]

Then Tomas hesitated, pen above the ink. [568] The last link was always the hardest; it had to reach forward, into a future neither of them could see. [588]

"I'm still leaving," he said quietly. [594]

"I know." [596]

"If I promise something I can't keep, it'll take my voice." [607]

"Then promise something small," Ines said. [613] "Small and true is enough to hold a Covenant." [622]

He considered it for a long time, the way she had taught him to consider a verb. [639] Then he wrote: Because the anger ends here, I promise to write to you from the south, and to tell you the truth even when it is hard. [667]

The ink trembled, weighing him, and Ines held her breath. [677]

It held. [679]

The final chord rose through the archive, every dead tongue in every hive humming along, and the Covenant lay flat and whole and quietly singing. [704] Tomas capped the glass ink well with shaking fingers. [713]

"Dawn's in four hours," he said. [719]

"Then we have four hours," said Ines, and for the first time in three months she was not afraid of what she might say. [743] She kept her joy small, the way you keep a new flame cupped in both hands, but she let it burn. [764]
