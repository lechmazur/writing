The sea outside the workshop was not water but vitriol, green as a bruise, and it had been eating the hull for three hundred years. [25] Tonight it broke through. [29] Wenna Coil heard the seam split beneath the great escapement wheel, a sound like a knuckle cracking, and then the hiss of acid finding brass. [54] Every cog in the floating workshop shuddered, the clock-lungs that kept the place buoyant wheezing out of time. [72]

She was an alchemist the way a child is a swimmer after one lesson: by title, by hope, by nobody else being left. [95] Her grandmother Ysolde had built the gears and taught her the formulas, then died before teaching her how to believe them. [116] Half of Wenna's transmutations curdled. [121] The other half, she suspected, were luck. [128]

The breach was below, in the ballast well, where the old statue stood drowned in the only fresh water for a hundred miles. [151] Ysolde had hauled it up from the sunken city of Halm when Wenna was small, a stone woman with her hands cupped as if around a flame, her whole body pitted where the sea had chewed her. [188] Every pockmark is a forgetting, Ysolde used to say. [197] The sea eats what nobody holds. [203]

Wenna climbed down the ladder with the lantern in her teeth and the key ring charm clenched in her fist. [223] It was the last thing Ysolde had pressed into her hand: a brass disc no larger than a coin, dangling from a ring that opened nothing, punched through with a scatter of tiny holes. [257] For years Wenna had thought it was decoration. [265] Now, with green smoke curling from the crack in the well wall, she saw the pattern for what it was. [285] A map of the statue's wounds. [291]

She set the lantern on the ledge, filled her lungs, and went under. [304]

The cold closed over her like a held door. [313] In the green murk she ran her fingers over the stone woman's collarbone, her cheek, the hollow of her throat, turning the charm against each cluster of pits until, at the breastbone, seven holes aligned with seven marks exactly. [352] The brass warmed. [355] Somewhere far above, the workshop groaned and tilted. [363]

She understood then what her grandmother had never managed to say outright: the statue did not need repairing, it needed remembering, and remembering had to be spoken. [390] So, in the long hollow between one breath and the next, Wenna began to narrate. [405]

She could not speak aloud, so she spoke inward, the way Ysolde had taught her to recite formulas, and the charm listened. [427] She told the stone about the night her grandmother burned her eyebrows off distilling moonlight. [442] She told it about the first gear she had cut herself, lopsided and still turning in the kitchen clock. [461] She told it about her mother's voice, which she could no longer hear, and the shape of the silence where it used to be. [485] Each memory came out ragged, and she did not smooth it; she laid it beside the next and let the charm quilt them together, edge against frayed edge, the way Ysolde had quilted scraps of every dress Wenna had outgrown into the blanket on her bed. [531]

Where the memories met, a thread of light ran out from the charm and into the nearest pockmark, then the next, filling each wound with something that gleamed like gold. [561] Her chest burned. [564] Her vision spotted. [567] She doubted, as she always doubted, that any of it was real, that she was not simply drowning and dreaming a rescue. [589] But the gold did not care whether she believed. [598]

It moved along the veins of the stone, out through the statue's cupped hands, across the floor of the well, and into the smoking crack, where it seamed the breach shut with a bright, crooked line. [634] Not hidden. [636] Not smoothed. [638] Honored, the way the old menders of Halm had honored a broken bowl by making its scar the most precious part. [659]

Wenna broke the surface gasping. [664] Above her, the clock-lungs caught their rhythm again, and the workshop righted itself on the vitriol sea with a long, relieved creak of cogs. [688] She clung to the ledge and looked down. [696] Beneath the water, the stone woman glowed, her wounds veined with gold, and in her cupped hands, where there had been only emptiness, Wenna thought she saw the faint shape of a flame. [729]

Thousands of pockmarks remained. [733] Thousands of forgettings, and the sea still hungry for more. [743] She did not know if she was a real alchemist. [753] She knew she had a great deal left to tell. [763]
