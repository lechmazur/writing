The orchard hung from the ravine walls on iron cables, three hundred pear trees rooted in baskets of stone, and every storm set them swinging like bells that had forgotten how to ring. [33] Ines Varga foraged beneath them, where the truffles grew in the root-mats that dangled over nothing. [49] She worked clipped to a line, her terrier Pilar sniffing along the underside of each basket, and she trusted only what she could weigh. [73] Since the empathy virus, that list had grown short. [82] The fever had passed through the ravine six winters ago and left everyone porous. [96] You felt your neighbor's grief like weather on your skin, and worse, you answered it: the survivors were involuntarily truthful, their mouths opening to say exactly what the ache in them was. [128] Ines had learned to keep her sentences short, so that the truth leaking out of her would at least be tidy. [149]

Her nephew Tomas had not learned. [155] He was eleven, and when the cider-press crew laughed at his stammer he told them, in front of everyone, that he dreamed of his dead mother's hands every night and woke up hating whoever he saw first. [192] After that he stopped speaking, and then stopped leaving the storm shed, and Ines watched him fold smaller each day like a leaf in frost. [217]

The idea came from a book she was ashamed to own. [228] It was a water-stained copy of Kessler's Remedies of the Inner Room, one of the discredited textbooks the Concord had pulped after the pandemic, full of rumored cures for the unguarded mind. [260] Most were nonsense: willow ash under the tongue, sleeping with the head north. [273] But one chapter described the glass eye of Blessed Aurel, a lens-grinder saint, which was said to occlude the bearer's heart from other hearts, the way a shutter closes on a lamp. [305]

Ines did not believe it. [310] She believed, however, in what Tomas needed, which was a door he was allowed to shut. [326]

The eye sat in a lowland reliquary the Concord had seized, because concealment objects were illegal now, symbols of the old cruelty of hidden things. [351] A clerk who bought her truffles owed her a favor. [361] In autumn it came back up the ravine inside a black winter truffle the size of a fist, hollowed and resealed with its own soil, riding in her basket past the checkpoint where the inspectors felt nothing from her but fatigue. [402] They asked what she carried, and she said truffles, which was true enough to pass. [417]

The relic was smaller than she expected, blue iris gone milky, a hairline crack across the pupil. [434] She tested it that night the only way she knew, carefully. [445] She held it and sat beside her neighbor Oren, whose sorrow over his failing trees always pressed on her like wet wool. [467] It pressed exactly as hard as before. [474]

Oren asked what she was holding, and her mouth said, "A dead man's glass eye that does nothing." [492] Then, because she could not stop it, "I wanted to give Tomas a place to hide, and I have brought him a marble." [515]

Oren was quiet for a long time while the wind swung them both. [528] "Then we make it do something," he said. [536]

It was, Ines realized, the most rational proposal she had heard since the fever. [550] A cure need not work on the body if it worked on the village. [564]

She went cable to cable before the storm broke, telling every household the plain truth, because she had no other kind. [585] The eye blocked nothing. [589] But whoever held it would be treated as closed: no questions, no answers demanded, their spilled feelings received like rain, without comment. [611] People felt the truth of her request move through them, and one by one, reluctantly, then gladly, they agreed. [630] Some of them, she felt, wanted a turn. [638]

She gave the eye to Tomas in the storm shed while hail rattled the tin. [653] He turned it over, found the crack, and looked up at her with fear she could taste. [670]

"It's broken," he said. [674]

"So is everyone," she said, and it was true, and for once the truth did not hurt going out. [693]

The next morning he walked the lines with her, the eye in his fist, and when the cider crew called to him he did not answer, and no one pressed. [723] Pilar found a truffle under the third basket. [731] Tomas knelt, dug it free, and held it up, and Ines felt his gladness arrive in her the way it should, not pried loose but offered. [757] Above them the orchard swung, cracked and holding. [765]
