Oren Vell turned the hourglasses every hour, as his mother had, and her father before her. [16] There were three hundred of them in the amber warehouse beneath the cliffs, ranked on shelves of pale resin that held the lamplight the way honey holds a spoon. [45] Each glass counted down toward the same morning, the morning the canyon had promised, the morning the cliffs would come down. [66]

Nobody in Haldry remembered the canyon speaking. [73] It had gone silent ninety years ago, and its last words had been caught by a quick-handed shepherd in a stoppered jar that now sat on the warehouse's highest shelf. [103] When you tipped it, the echo rolled against the glass like pebbles, a tumble of syllables that almost made sense. [123] Stone, fall, morning, yours, and a dozen words too worn to hear. [135] The elders had long ago decided what it meant. [144] The stone will fall on the morning; the town is yours to lose. [157] So Haldry waited, and Oren turned the glasses, and the silence in the warehouse grew thick as sediment. [175]

His other duty was the buttons. [181] Every button lost in Haldry, in the fields, under floorboards, in the mouth of the canyon, eventually came to the warehouse, because the old custom said a lost button carried a piece of whoever had worn it. [218] Oren sorted them into drawers by year, by color, by the family who brought them in. [234] It was dull work, and he loved it. [242]

One winter evening, cataloging a tin of bone buttons from the canyon floor, he noticed scratches on the underside of one. [263] Under his lens they became letters: BUILD. [270] He turned over the next, and the next. [278] Most were blank, but some bore a single word: AFTER, WHAT, THE, and once, in a child's crooked hand, YOU. [298]

Oren sat very still among the drawers. [305] He thought of the shepherd with the jar, and of everyone else who had stood in the canyon that day ninety years ago, each hearing the echo from a different ledge, each catching a different word. [341] Perhaps some had scratched what they heard into a button and let it fall back into the canyon, an answer returned. [362] The jar held only what one pair of ears had caught. [373] The rest of the prophecy had been lost the way buttons are lost, a little at a time, by ordinary people who never thought it mattered. [399]

He did not tell the elders. [405] He was not a bold man, and the idea that stirred in him was not a bold idea, only a stubborn one: that a doom pieced together from one shepherd's hearing might be undone by listening to everyone. [443] Quietly inspired, he worked through the nights between turnings, emptying every drawer his family had ever filled. [460] He found forty-one marked buttons. [465] He laid them on the amber floor in rows and began to unscramble them, sliding words beside the jar's worn syllables, tipping the jar again and again to hear where the gaps fell. [498] The dates helped, and so did the families, since kin had usually stood together on the same ledge. [516]

As the truth unraveled, the old reading came apart thread by thread. [528] On the eleventh night the sentence lay whole across the floor. [539] THE STONE WILL FALL ONE MORNING, AND WHAT YOU BUILD AFTER IS YOURS. [552] Not the morning, but one morning, any morning, the way all stone falls eventually. [566] There was no date at all. [572] The hourglasses had been counting toward nothing. [579]

Oren understood then that he could not simply announce this, as the elders had announced their doom. [596] A prophecy handed down from one mouth had made Haldry afraid; he wanted this one to feel like theirs, to spin it from a sentence into raw possibility, something the town would pick up with its own hands. [634]

So in the morning he opened the warehouse doors and left the buttons where they lay. [650] People came because the doors had never been open before. [660] They walked the rows in silence, finding their grandparents' buttons, their great-aunts', reading each word aloud until the whole sentence had passed through every mouth in the room. [688] No one argued. [691] The silence that followed was not the old silence of waiting but something older and dug up, the hush of people hearing a thing they had always half known. [720]

At the next hour, Oren walked the shelves as always. [730] He did not turn the glasses. [736] One by one the sand ran out and stayed still, and a child, the same age perhaps as the one who had scratched YOU into bone, asked what they were for now. [768]

Oren took the canyon jar down from its shelf, pulled the stopper, and let the echo go home to the cliffs. [789] "Whatever we build," he said. [794]
