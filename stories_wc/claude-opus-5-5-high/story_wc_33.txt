The day's lone mail drop came at twilight, when the cobalt arcade of Vessk turned the color of deep water and the checkpoint lamps had not yet been lit. [29] Oren Talic arrived with his satchel, his permit stamped by both governments, and a smuggling drone wrapped in a flour sack under his arm. [53] Nobody spoke loudly in the arcade anymore. [60] The glass roof had cracked during the second shelling, and people had learned to walk as if their footsteps might finish the job. [83] Lieutenant Hask of the eastern authority waited at the sorting table, and Captain Mirel of the western one waited beside her, because the valley belonged to both flags on paper and to neither in practice. [118] Oren saluted each of them in turn, precisely, the way he had for eleven years. [133]

"What is in the sack, postman?" Hask asked. [141]

"Returned mail, Lieutenant." [144]

He set the drone on the table. [151] It was a western courier, four rotors, one of the black-market machines that ferried cigarettes and insulin over the line at night; he had found it three weeks ago in the orchard with a snapped arm and a battery gone cold. [192] He had fostered it since, the way his mother once fostered injured swifts, splinting the arm with a pencil and feeding it current from the post office generator a little each evening. [224]

"It came from the west without a return address," Oren said. [235] "Regulation fourteen says undeliverable items go back to their point of origin." [247]

Mirel almost smiled. [250] Hask did not. [253] "And the bottle?" [256]

The bottle sat inside the drone's cargo cradle, corked and wax-sealed, no bigger than a lamp chimney. [273] Inside it a thunderstorm turned slowly, a bruise of cloud no larger than a fist, stitched through with lightning that made no sound through the glass. [299] The old glassblowers of Vessk had caught storms off the ridge for two centuries, and every bride in the valley was given one to uncork on her wedding night, so that the first rain of her marriage would be her own. [340] Both authorities had since classified the bottles as weather ordnance. [350]

"A personal effect," Oren said. [355] "From Widow Anka to her daughter in Pellin." [363]

Anka stood at the arcade's far pillar with her shawl pulled tight. [375] She had promised her daughter she would be at the wedding, and then the line had moved four hundred meters in a single night, and Pellin had become a foreign country with a closed gate. [410]

Oren carried a ledger no one had asked him to keep. [421] In it he wrote down not the letters that came but the ones that did not: the son who stopped writing, the husband whose envelopes arrived opened and empty, the birthday card that never crossed. [456] Beside them, in a second column, he wrote the other absences: the hours when the eastern patrol drones went dark to recharge, the minutes when western radar swept south, the gap in the fence where the orchard wall had fallen. [496] By linking absences, one to the next, he had drawn a path through the border that existed only in what was missing. [518] He had loaded it into the drone that morning. [527]

"You understand I have to log this," Hask said quietly. [537]

"I would expect nothing less, Lieutenant." [543]

"Log it as returned mail," Mirel said, and signed the form before Hask could argue. [558] Oren had not told her that her own name was in the ledger's first column, twice; he suspected she had guessed. [579]

He carried the drone to the arcade's broken pane, where the cobalt glass framed a strip of violet sky, and woke it with his thumb. [604] The mended arm trembled, held. [609] The rotors lifted it through the gap, and it went west low over the orchard, into the eastern patrol's ninety dark seconds, and was gone. [634]

Anka did not move. [638] No one in the arcade moved. [644]

Oren had spent eleven years wondering where broken promises went to heal, whether there was a post office for them somewhere beyond both flags. [668] His own was in the ledger too, the first entry: a brother in Pellin, a visit promised for a spring that never came. [691]

Twenty minutes later, far past the gate, thunder rolled once over Pellin, soft as a door opening in another room. [712] Then the rain came, crossing the line without papers, and the cracked cobalt roof above them ticked with the first drops. [733] Anka closed her eyes and wept without a sound. [742]

Oren opened his ledger, found her name in the first column, and moved it, carefully, to a third column he had never needed before. [766] He headed it: Arrived. [770] Then he saluted Hask, and Mirel, and went to sort the rest of the mail. [785]
