Ossel had kept the chronicle so long that she no longer trusted the difference between remembering and dreaming. [18] Beneath the forgotten city, in catacombs where scholars had once buried their heretical books alongside their bones, the library had gone green. [40] Roots braided the shelves, moss softened the reading tables, and pale ferns uncurled from the spines of atlases no one would ever open again. [64]

Ossel did not mind. [68] Her task, inherited from a keeper whose name had rotted off the ledger, was simple: to witness change without judgment, and to write it down. [93] So she wrote that the roots had reached the fourth stair. [104] She wrote that the river beneath the stacks had begun to sing in a lower key. [120] She wrote that dust fell from the vaulted ceiling each morning, if it was morning, like a slow and patient snow. [141] She did not write whether any of it was good. [151]

The trouble was that Ossel gripped reality loosely, the way a sleeper holds a cup. [166] Across the drift of untold seasons, the edges of her mind had softened like the pages around her. [184] Some days she argued with a portrait that was only a water stain. [197] Some days she wrote the same entry four times and believed each one was news. [212] When the chronicle began to repeat itself, she knew it was time to go down. [227]

For that she needed the swan. [233] It sat on her desk, grey with handling, its paper worn to the texture of cloth. [249] The first keeper had folded it, and every keeper since had learned to read it. [264] Its creases were not decoration but a map, cryptic folds that shifted meaning depending on how the wing was turned. [284] A valley fold meant descend. [289] A mountain fold meant the wall is false. [297] The reverse fold at the neck meant there was no passage at all, only earth, and she would have to make one. [319]

Ossel tucked the swan inside her collar and went to the collapsed end of the Hall of Heresies. [337] There she knelt and began to burrow. [344] She dug with her hands and a brass page-weight, pulling loose soil and fragments of bone and the brittle ribs of books older than language. [369] Every few lengths she stopped, unfolded a wing by the light of her lamp, and let the creases tell her where to turn. [392] Above her, the library groaned, and she felt a shelf give way somewhere in the dark, a soft avalanche of words. [413] She did not stop to mourn it. [420] Mourning was a kind of judgment. [426]

The soil turned cold and wet, and then her fingers broke through into air that tasted of stone and moving water. [447] The river ran black beneath the catacombs, and on its bank stood the obsidian refuge. [462] It was a single chamber carved from volcanic glass, smooth as a held breath, older than the library and indifferent to it. [484] Roots could not crack it. [489] Water could not wear it. [494] Sound could not enter it. [499]

Ossel stepped inside and sat on the cold floor, and the silence closed over her like a healing hand. [518] This was why the keepers came here. [525] The silence was not empty; it was regenerative, the way winter is regenerative, stripping away what had grown tangled so that something true could return. [550] In that stillness her memories settled like silt in a glass, and she could see them clearly again: which entries were real, which were echoes, which were dreams she had written as fact. [583] She stayed until her hands stopped shaking. [590]

Then, because the silence had made her brave, she unfolded the swan entirely for the first time. [607] Flattened, the creases formed a lattice, and at its center, hidden inside the innermost fold, the first keeper had written a single line. [630] It read: The library is also a thing that changes. [640]

Ossel laughed, and the obsidian swallowed the sound whole. [649] She had spent untold seasons recording the city, the river, the roots, and never once thought to record the library's own slow unmaking as simply more of the world moving. [679] She had been guarding it without knowing, and guarding is a judgment too. [692]

She refolded the swan with care, crease by crease, and climbed back up her tunnel. [707] In the green hall, beneath the patient snow of dust, she opened the chronicle to a clean page. [725] The roots have reached the ceiling, she wrote. [733] The Hall of Heresies has fallen. [739] The keeper is well, and the peace is breaking, and both of these are true. [754]

Then she set the swan on the desk, facing the river, and waited to see what would grow. [772]
