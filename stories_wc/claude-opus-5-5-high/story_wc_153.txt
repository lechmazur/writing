The medium lived where the mangroves forgot which way the sea was, in a stilt house whose windows were always fogged. [21] Dessa Brune found it on the third night, after the roots had turned her boat in circles and the spirits in the channels had sung her own name back to her in her brother's voice. [56] She tied up at the ladder and climbed with her satchel pressed to her ribs. [71] Inside were forty-one letters, all addressed to Tobin, none of them stamped. [83] Wrapped among them was a curl of carved limestone as long as her forearm, the broken lip of a gargoyle's rainspout. [104]

The medium was a small woman with barnacle-grey braids who did not look up from her tea. [121] "You came the long way," she said. [128] "Everyone does." [130] "The short way is saying it." [136]

"I don't want to say anything," Dessa said. [144] "I want to find the moment before I decided." [153]

She set the shard on the table. [160] Two years ago she and Tobin had been restoring the north tower of Saint Ambrose, and the gargoyle had come loose while he held the ropes. [186] It had missed her by a hand's width and shattered on the steps. [199] She had screamed at him that he never checked anything, that he would kill someone one day, that she was done. [220] Later the inspectors found the anchor bolt had rusted through from inside, where no one could have seen it. [239] By then Tobin had moved to the coast and changed his number, and Dessa had begun writing letters she could not bring herself to send. [264]

The medium touched the stone with one finger. [272] "This remembers falling," she said. [277] "Things that remember falling can remember the instant before." [286] "But you are asking me to open a door in a house with no walls." [301] "Out here, we live in the space where an apology waits unspoken." [313] "Every singer in these channels is a sorry somebody kept too long." [325]

She opened the shutters, and the moon came in white and heavy across the table. [340] Where it touched the shard, the jagged edges softened, and hairline cracks drew closed like mouths sealing a secret. [359] "Stone that was loved heals in moonlight," the medium said. [369] "Your brother carved this spout's tongue, didn't he?" [377]

Dessa had not told her that. [383]

Then the medium began to hum, low and toothed, and outside the singing in the mangroves shifted to match her. [403] She was not calling the dead; she was evoking a second, pulling it up from the silt of Dessa's memory the way a heron pulls a fish. [430] The fog on the windows thickened. [436] Letters appeared in it, traced from the outside by fingers no one could see. [450]

The first pane read: ROPE BURNS ON HIS PALMS. [459]

The second: HE IS REACHING FOR YOU. [466]

The third, dripping as it formed: YOU ARE NOT ANGRY YET. [477]

And Dessa was there, on the cathedral steps, dust in her teeth, the roar of stone still in her ears. [497] Tobin was running down the scaffold, his hands bleeding, his face gone white with a terror that was entirely for her. [518] In that breath, before the words, before the choosing, what she felt was not blame. [533] It was a love so fierce it frightened her, a love that had nearly lost everything and needed somewhere to put its fear. [556]

She had put it on him. [562]

The panes ran clear, and she was back at the table, weeping into her sleeves. [577] The medium let her cry without comfort, which was its own kind of kindness. [591]

"Now you know," she said at last. [598] "But here is the part nobody wants." [605] "The moment before deciding was not only then." [613] "It is every morning you look at those letters and choose again." [625] "It is now." [628]

Dessa looked at the shard, whole again, the gargoyle's tongue curled in its old stone grin. [644] Then she breathed on the nearest window until it clouded and wrote, with a shaking finger, the sentence none of the forty-one letters had managed. [669]

I WAS ONLY EVER AFRAID FOR YOU. [676] FORGIVE ME. [678]

Outside, one voice in the channels stopped singing, as if a held breath had finally been let go. [696]

At dawn she rowed out with the shard in her lap and the letters bundled for the post, all forty-two of them, the last written in the boat. [724] The mangroves, for once, let her go straight. [732]
