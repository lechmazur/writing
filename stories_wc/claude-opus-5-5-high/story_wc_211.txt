After the foghorn fell silent, the oath scribe climbed to the orchard alone. [13] For three hundred years the horn had sounded from the harbor at dusk, and for three hundred years the city had sworn its promises beneath it, sixty stories up, among apple trees older than the tower that carried them. [52] Now the harbor keeper was buried, the great brass throat had cracked in the frost, and no one had come to swear anything for eleven days. [78]

Ossery Vell was eighty-nine. [82] She had recorded forty thousand oaths on wax cylinders, not for their words, which the clerks downstairs copied into ledgers, but for what trembled underneath them. [108] A bridegroom's swallowed breath. [112] A debtor's too-steady vowels. [116] The long pause of a widow promising to raise a stranger's child. [128] Her charter said she kept the archive so the city would remember how its promises had felt, and whether that feeling changed over centuries, the way a river changes course so slowly no one living notices. [164] She wrote her notes in Old Tessic and Low Varric, languages no one else still spoke, because a dead tongue could not be bribed into flattering anyone. [191]

Without the horn, the councils argued, an oath was only talk. [202] Without oaths, her archive was a museum of a city that had stopped meaning things. [217]

The orchard was quiet in a way she disliked, soft as a hand over a mouth. [233] Wind moved through the gnarled branches and made the cold fruit knock together like knuckles on a door. [251] She carried a small glass jar she had not opened in eighty years. [264] Inside lay a curl of brown wax no longer than her finger: a phonograph shaving. [279]

She had been in the third grade when her class toured the rooftop archive. [293] The scribe then, a stooped woman named Maren, had let each child speak into the brass horn of the recorder, then played their voices back while they shrieked with delight. [323] Afterward Maren shaved the cylinder smooth on her lathe so it could be used again, and the wax had peeled away in a ribbon. [347] Little Ossery, curious past all manners, had asked what happened to the voices. [360] "They sleep in the curl," Maren said, and let her keep it. [372] "If you ever want to wake them, immerse it in water warm as blood, flatten it gently round a blank drum, and listen with a light needle." [399]

She remembered, too, what she had said into the horn. [409] Showing off, she had tried to read aloud the Old Tessic words carved over the orchard gate, words she could not yet understand. [432] Maren had laughed and said she had gotten every ending wrong. [443]

Now, beside the rain cistern, Ossery warmed a bowl of water over her spirit lamp and lowered the shaving in. [463] It loosened like a sleeper turning over. [470] She wound it round a blank, fitted the needle, and turned the crank with fingers that shook more than she liked. [491]

A child's voice rose out of the hiss, thin and eager and eighty years away. [506]

The gate read, "The orchard keeps what the horn calls forth." [517] But the girl's wrong endings had bent every word, and Ossery, who had spent a lifetime in that language, heard what she had actually said. [542]

When the horn is closed, the oldest keeper calls the orchard forth. [554]

She sat very still. [558] It was nonsense, a nine-year-old's stumble, a prophecy made by accident. [569] Yet her shelves held forty thousand voices proving that most true things were spoken by people who did not mean them yet. [591]

She rose, set a fresh cylinder on the recorder, and turned its brass horn not toward herself but toward the trees. [612] Then she spoke, in Old Tessic, the first oath sworn without the foghorn: that she would keep this orchard as the city's witness, and teach the next scribe, and the next, until someone thought of a better sound to promise by. [653]

The wind came up while the needle cut. [661] Every branch in the orchard creaked at once, a long wooden groan, low as the old horn had been, and the cold apples knocked and knocked. [687]

Later, playing the cylinder back, she studied her own voice the way she had studied everyone's. [703] Under the steadiness she heard it plainly: fear, and beneath the fear something warmer, like a hand finally lifting from a mouth. [725] She wrote the pattern down in a dead language, dated it, and shelved it beside the girl. [742]

Below, in the fog, someone was climbing the stairs. [751]
