After the spoons bent reality, the isle of Keth kept floating, but nothing on it would stay quite straight. [19] Nobody agreed on who had stirred first, only that one evening every spoon on the island curled in its cup, and the world curled with it. [45] Straight lines leaked afterward. [48] Doors drifted open, ropes unknotted themselves, and the old runes carved above the granaries bled their meaning into the air like steam. [70] Worse, at noon, when the sun stood directly overhead, shadows fell straight down through the rock of the isle and did not come back. [94] Most people learned to stay indoors at midday. [102] Tomas, who never learned anything he was told, went fishing off the east ledge. [116] His shadow fell, and he lay down on the path and slept, and no one could wake him. [134] The healers called it a sealed awakening: the body ready to rise, the door to rising locked from the far side. [155]

His sister Ines was a budding rune scholar, which meant she had read more than she understood and knew it. [175] She was quietly deliberate in everything, the kind of person who counted stairs, and she set herself a single question: where do shadows go at noon? [201]

The only place on Keth built to answer questions about light was the observatory on the western spur, which opened only after dark. [224] Its dome held two telescopes, one aimed at the stars above and one aimed down through a shaft in the rock at the stars below the isle. [251] It also held the Warden, a brass automaton the last astronomer had shut down and sealed before she died. [270]

Ines found its clockwork key in the astronomer's desk, wrapped in a note: Do not wind it with open letters. [290] The key's bow was engraved with runes, and every one of them had been scratched out and redrawn. [308] She studied them for three nights before she saw it. [318] The old forms ended in tails and hooks, strokes that trailed off into nothing. [332] The new forms did not end at all. [340] Each line curved back and met its own beginning. [349] After the Bending, a straight stroke was a leak; meaning held only by closing loops. [364]

So Ines had to relearn her alphabet. [371] She sat under the dome with chalk and slate and drew the rune for wake a hundred times, bending its arm around until it touched its root, erasing, starting again. [401] Her hand kept wanting to lift away early, the way it had been taught. [415] She did not hurry it. [420] When the slate glowed faintly instead of smoking, she cut the closed rune into the key's bow beside the others and turned it in the Warden's back. [447]

The automaton's eyes lit like lamps coming up through fog. [457] Its first words were a question about the date, and its second were an apology. [472] "She sealed me because I kept trying to go after them," it said. [485] "The shadows." [487]

It led her to the downward telescope. [494] Through the lens, beneath the isle, the lower sky should have been full of stars, but a great dark bowl hung there instead, curved like the inside of a spoon. [524] It moved faintly, the way a crowd moves. [532]

"At noon they fall straight down," the Warden said. [541] "They always did. [544] Then they climbed back up the far side of the day, along the evening, and returned to their owners by morning. [565] The Bending cupped the path. [570] Now they fall in and cannot climb out. [578] Their road is a line that no longer meets itself." [588]

Ines understood then why the astronomer had failed. [596] She had tried to pull the shadows out, one at a time, with straight ropes of light. [613] Ines did not want to pull anything. [620] She wanted to close the road. [626]

She spent the rest of the night with the Warden, drawing the rune for return, one continuous line, around the rim of the shaft, so that the shaft became a ring and the ring became a way back. [664] Her chalk ran out twice. [669] The Warden ground more from the dome's limestone without being asked. [680] At the last stroke she stopped, breathed, and brought the chalk down exactly where she had begun. [697]

The bowl beneath the isle tilted, and dark shapes began to rise through the shaft like smoke finding a chimney. [717] By dawn the observatory was full of shadows that belonged to no one in the room, and they slipped out the door toward the village one by one. [745]

Ines closed the dome, pocketed the key, and walked home in the gray light. [759] Tomas was sitting up on his cot, rubbing his eyes, his shadow lying across the blanket where it belonged. [778] She sat beside him and began teaching him the new letters, and for once he listened. [794]
