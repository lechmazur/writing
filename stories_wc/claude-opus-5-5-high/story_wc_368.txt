The first stranger told Maelis Tarn a story three weeks after she received the donor heart. [16] He was a mule driver waiting by her indigo vats, and without being asked he described the night his brother drowned in the river below the terraces. [43] Then came a weaver, a tax clerk, two children, and a widow who spoke for an hour about a coat she had never finished. [67]

Maelis had been dye master of the valley for twenty years, and in all that time no one had confided in her. [89] She was watchful the way her craft demanded, reading the copper skin on a vat, the green that bloomed to blue as cloth met air, and people felt that gaze and kept their secrets folded. [124] Her apprentices called her quietly explosive; she never shouted, but when a batch streaked she would stand so still that the whole dye yard held its breath. [151]

Now the heart in her chest beat slower than her old one, and the stories kept arriving. [168] She wanted to know why some people magnetize stories, and whether she had borrowed that pull along with the muscle. [188] The surgeon would say only that the donor had kept a tea house. [201]

There was one tea house in the valley worth the name, and it floated. [215] The rock hung above the river mist like a loosened tooth, anchored by four iron chains, its cedar pavilion reached by a rope bridge that swayed with every step. [244] Maelis crossed at dawn, when the mist still filled the valley so completely that the rock seemed to rest on milk. [265]

A young woman named Lio was sweeping the veranda. [274] When Maelis explained, the broom stopped. [280] "My grandfather Tobin," Lio said, and pressed her palm flat against Maelis's sternum as if listening through a door. [299] Then she brought out a narrow frame of cedar and glass, and inside it lay a single blue morpho wing. [319] "He left this for whoever came asking," Lio said, "and he said to follow the sun trails." [336]

Maelis knew blue better than anyone alive, yet this blue unsettled her. [348] It held no pigment at all; the color lived in the ridged architecture of the scales, and when she tilted the frame it slid from sapphire to violet to a brown as dull as bark. [383] No vat of hers could fix such a thing. [392]

The sun trails were thin brass lines inlaid across the pavilion floor, each marking where light from the east window fell at a given hour. [417] Beside each line Tobin had set a cushion. [425] Maelis spent the day moving from one to the next, tracking the light as it crawled westward, and Lio served her tea at every station. [450] At each cushion the view changed: the terraces, the chains, the drop, the far snow, the dye smoke rising from her own yard. [473] Tobin, she understood slowly, had sat his guests where the light would soften them, and moved himself so they always saw him from a new angle. [499]

Near sunset the last trail ended at a small hook beneath the west window. [513] Maelis hung the frame there, and the low sun struck the wing and threw a trembling blue across the plaster opposite, illuminating words brushed in a wash so pale she had walked past them all day. [549]

The wall read, "Stories come to whoever shifts with the light." [560] "Be careful, as the wing is careful; turn too far to please them and the blue goes to bark, and no one is left to listen." [586]

Maelis read it twice, and the new heart thudded against her ribs like something wanting out. [602] She understood then the wing's iridescent caution: Tobin had been all angles and still himself, while she had made herself one fixed, unfading blue that nothing could enter. [630] The strangers had not come for Tobin's heart; they had come because, since the surgery, she had been frightened and new and looking at the world from angles she had never allowed. [662]

What had built in her for twenty years broke the way indigo floods wet cloth, silent and total, and she cried, then laughed, and then began to tell Lio about her mother's hands. [695] Below them the mist rose to fill the valley, and the blue on the wall slid to violet, then to dusk, and Lio listened until the stars came out. [724]

In the morning Maelis carried the wing down the swaying bridge, and at the vats she set a cushion where the evening sun would find whoever came. [751]
