The orchard grew under black glass, and so it was always night inside. [13] Master Orrin had painted every pane with soot and lacquer, because his mercy apples ripened only in darkness, swelling pale as moons on branches fed by copper pipes and slow green fire. [45] Outside, the winter fields lay cracked and hushed, furrows split like old pottery, and Tomas had not seen them in daylight since autumn. [68]

He found the bead while turning soil beneath the oldest tree. [79] It was carved stone, gray as a heron, etched with two linked rings, and when he tapped it against the watering can it sang a note that did not stop when it should have. [113] In the steam rising from the nearest retort, a woman stood. [124] She wore a bridal veil the color of cold ash, and beneath it her mouth was set in something between fondness and contempt. [147]

"Still eating what you're given, darling," she said. [155] "You always were a trusting idiot." [161]

Then the steam thinned, and she was gone. [169]

She came back the way a splinter comes back, never long enough to pull out. [184] She appeared in the frost bloom on a pane, in the brass bowl of the scale, once in the glaze of an apple he raised to his lips. [212] "Spit it out," she hissed, and he did, before he knew why. [224] Each visit lasted a breath, and each left him with a memory he had no right to own. [242] A wedding table under lanterns. [247] A girl with stained fingers grinding foxglove in a mortar while laughing at him. [261] A silver cup. [264] The bead rang louder with every memory, as if it were remembering too. [277]

He began to notice what she had been warning him about. [288] His gums were gray. [292] His hands shook at the grafting knife. [299] Orrin watched him eat his supper apple each night with the tender patience of a man watching dough rise, and wrote numbers in a ledger labeled Tincture of Blood. [328]

On the longest night, Tomas struck the bead against the black glass until it rang like a temple bell, and she came and did not leave. [354]

"Who are you?" he asked. [359]

"Your wife, for one evening, two hundred years ago," she said. [370] "I was a poisoner's daughter, and they married me to you to settle my father's debts, and I meant to kill the lord who held them before the dancing started." [400] "You saw me pour it." [405] Her voice cracked, venom and honey at once. [413] "And you, you gorgeous fool, drank it yourself so I wouldn't hang." [425]

Tomas remembered it then, fully, the bitter almond on his tongue, her scream, the cup rolling under the table. [444] The memory woke in him like a second heartbeat. [453]

"You died for me," she said. [459] "I burned my veil on your pyre and wore its ash until I followed you." [474] "I have been waiting a long time to pay that back, and Orrin is making you into medicine for himself with the very recipe my father sold his great-grandmother." [503]

"Is there a cure?" [507]

"Mourning ash, steeped in snowmelt." [512] She lifted the veil from her face, and he saw she was smiling, and that it hurt her. [530] "There is only one veil made of it." [538]

"Then you'll be gone," he said. [544]

"I've been gone for two centuries, love; I've only been visiting." [555]

She pressed the veil into his hands, and it weighed nothing, and it crumbled into gray powder that smelled of lanterns and cut grass. [579] "Drink it all, and don't you dare waste my death on sentiment," she said, and kissed him, and was steam, then frost, then nothing. [603]

He broke the black glass with the grafting knife's handle to reach the snow. [617] Pane after pane fell away, and starlight poured over the mercy apples, which shriveled in it like guilty things. [636] Orrin shouted from the stairwell, but Tomas was already outside, kneeling in the cracked field, stirring ash into melted snow with his finger and drinking it down. [663]

It tasted of her. [667]

His hands steadied. [670] The gray ebbed from his skin by the time the east turned pink over the hushed and broken furrows, and he wept and laughed at once, because the dawn was the first he had seen in months, and she was not in it, and she was in all of it. [720] The bead in his pocket rang once, softly, and was still. [731] He understood it was not a goodbye but a receipt: paid in full. [744]
