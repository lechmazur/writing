The prism sanctuary stood on the cliff above Varne, a chapel of cut glass that broke starlight into bands of violet and green across the floor. [26] Ilse worked there at night because the masks would not keep still by day. [40] There were forty-one of them hung along the prism walls, plaster faces cast from the drowned men of the village, and every one was cracked. [65] She had spent eleven years mending them, and eleven years watching the seams split open again, as if something beneath the plaster were still trying to speak. [92] The villagers called her the broken mask restorer, and she never asked whether broken described the masks or her. [111]

She had not spoken since the storm that took her brother Tomas. [123] Silence accumulated in her the way salt accumulates in a rope left on the rocks, stiffening every fiber until nothing bent. [144]

Tonight the starlight was very clear, and so was the problem. [155] Glue did not hold the masks; the spirits behind them were restless, and restless things do not stay mended. [174] Old Brennick, the last net-maker, had told her so before he died, pressing a bundle into her arms: the village net, knotted by every fisher of Varne for three hundred years. [205] Each man tied one knot before his first voyage, he said, and tied into it the thing he could not say aloud. [227] The drowned could not rest while their secrets stayed buried in the cord. [240] Someone had to exhume them. [245]

Ilse spread the net across the floor where the colors fell, and the old hemp gleamed like a map of veins. [266] She chose a tapestry needle from her kit, its point blunt and its eye wide, a tool made to slip between threads rather than pierce them. [292] A sharp awl would have shredded the fibers; this would only coax. [304] She worked it into the first knot and eased the loops apart. [316]

A voice rose out of the loosened cord, thin as breath on glass. [329] "I was afraid of the water," it said, "and never told my father." [342] On the wall, a mask's crack sealed with a small, dry click. [354]

She kept going. [357]

Knot by knot the secrets came up like stones from a grave: a stolen coin, a daughter never claimed, a love for another man's wife, a prayer said to the wrong god. [389] None of them were terrible, and all of them had been carried to the bottom of the sea. [407] Between the knots lay the interlaced hush, the silence each man had woven around his words so tightly that the net itself seemed made of it. [433] As she freed each confession, the hush thinned, and the masks went quiet one by one, their faces settling into something like sleep. [456]

By the time the stars had wheeled to the western panes, forty masks were whole. [471]

The forty-first was Tomas. [475]

His crack ran from brow to chin, and it was widening. [486] Ilse searched the net and found his knot near the edge, small and hurried, the way he did everything. [505] She slid the needle in, and the loops fell open, and there was nothing inside. [520] No voice. [522] Only the cord, clean and empty. [528]

She understood then, with the sharp clarity that comes at the far end of a sleepless night. [545] The night before the storm, his hands too cold to work the cord, Tomas had asked her to tie his knot for him. [568] She had tied it quickly and put nothing in, because the only secret in the room that night was hers. [588] The mask could not rest on a silence that did not belong to it. [602]

Ilse knelt in the violet light and opened her mouth, and eleven years of stiffened quiet cracked in her throat like old plaster. [625]

"I saw the sky that morning," she said. [633] "I knew the storm was coming, and I let you go, because you wanted it so badly and I did not want to be the one who kept you ashore." [663]

Her voice was ugly and small, and the prism walls caught it and split it into colors she had never seen. [684] She did not tie the cord again. [691]

On the wall, the crack in Tomas's face drew closed, slowly, the way a wound decides to heal. [709] It did not look peaceful, exactly. [715] It looked like someone who had finally been told the truth and was deciding what to do with it. [734]

Ilse stayed kneeling until the starlight faded from the glass, her own crack still open, and for the first time the silence inside her had somewhere to go. [762]
