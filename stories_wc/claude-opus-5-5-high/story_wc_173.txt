The snow fell on the silver courtyard of Vell without a sound, because the Magistrate had long ago harvested even that. [21] Ilse crossed the flagstones barefoot, her breath rising white, and felt nothing but peace, which was how she knew something was wrong. [43] Every citizen felt peace now. [48] Each dusk the Chorus sang the Lullaby from the towers, and each night the city's unsanctioned words drifted up from bedrooms and taverns like steam, to be caught in the threads strung across this courtyard. [83] The harvest of echoes, the clerks called it. [91] Complaints, confessions, a husband's secret tenderness, a child's bad joke: all of it frozen on silver filament, gathered at dawn, and locked inside the prism sanctuary at the courtyard's heart, where starlight turned it into more Lullaby. [128] What people said in secret became the song that taught them to stop saying it. [143]

Ilse was a courtesan, the most expensive in the river district, and she had never taken a man to bed. [163] Her clients paid for something rarer. [169] She had been mute since the Chorus took her voice at twelve, and so she had learned to speak with her body instead: a tilt of the wrist for doubt, a slow unfolding of the spine for forgiveness, whole stanzas in the angle of a shoulder. [215] Men came to her rooms to confess, and she answered in verses no ear could harvest. [231] But their confessions could be harvested, and lately they had begun arriving smiling, with nothing left to say. [249] Last week old Brannoch, who had wept for thirty years over the brother he betrayed, sat on her couch and could not remember he had ever had a brother. [278] He had looked so serene that she had gone to the window and been sick. [293]

Now she stood before the sanctuary. [299] It was a tall wedge of flawless glass on a plinth of frost, and in its depths the stolen voices of Vell hung like insects in amber, faintly colored, faintly moving. [330] Starlight entered its upper face and left the lower face as a hum so gentle it made her eyelids heavy. [350] Its lock was not iron. [355] Every harvest thread in the courtyard ran to a single knot at its base, a snarl the size of a heart, and the clerks said no hand could loosen it, because each thread resisted in its own voice. [393] Ilse had spent eleven years learning to listen without ears. [403] She knelt in the snow and laid her fingers on the knot. [415]

The Lullaby had one mercy, or one flaw. [423] When a citizen of Vell sighed, some true grief escaping, the song needed a moment to smooth that grief into a smile. [445] Between sigh and smile, a person was briefly their own. [455] Ilse had learned to live in that gap, to stretch it with discipline the way a dancer lengthens a held pose. [476] Now she let herself sigh, a long ache for Brannoch and his forgotten brother, and she began. [493]

She did not pull. [497] She read each thread with her whole body, answering it. [507] A thread that hummed with a baker's fury she met with a clenched jaw and a lowered brow, and it slackened, recognized. [529] A girl's first shameful love she answered by pressing her palm to her own collarbone, and it slipped free. [548] Brannoch's thread was grey and bitter, and for it she bowed until her forehead touched the snow, holding the posture of a man begging a brother who would never answer. [578] Each echo only wanted to be heard by someone before it was sung away. [592]

The Lullaby pressed on her the whole while, soft as a mother's hand, whispering that she was tired, that the snow was a bed, that nothing needed to be different. [622] Her mouth began to curve. [627] She bit the inside of her cheek until she tasted copper and kept untangling. [641]

The last thread was hers: a twelve-year-old's scream, still bright after all these winters. [655] She did not know how to answer it, so she simply opened her arms. [669]

The knot fell apart like wet bread, and the prism sanctuary unlocked with a sound like ice deciding to become water. [690] Its face swung open, and the starlight inside it had nowhere to go. [703] The voices rose out of the glass without any music at all, thousands of them, quarrelsome and tender and ugly, drifting back across the rooftops toward the mouths that had made them. [735] Somewhere in the river district, an old man woke and said his brother's name. [749]

The snow went on falling, and now it hissed faintly where it met the silver. [764] Ilse's throat ached with something that was not yet a word. [775] Then, deliberately, because she chose it, she smiled. [783]
