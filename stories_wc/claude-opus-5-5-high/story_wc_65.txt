The page lay on the obsidian table like a second lake, white and unbroken, and Ilse Marrow had not yet let the pen touch it. [25] Below the spire the real lake was frozen to the horizon, its surface scarred with the cut circles of fishing holes. [46] Every winter the town of Halder granted one sheet of vellum to its ignorance cartographer, and every winter she filled it with the shape of what nobody knew. [74] Other mapmakers drew coastlines. [78] Ilse drew edges: where the soundings stopped, where the records thinned, where a question sat unasked like a stone under snow. [99]

The charter that bound her was older than the spire's first cracked window. [112] Nothing may be written that cannot be measured. [120] The founders had fled a city ruined by prophets, and they wanted no more of them. [136] So the town gave her one sheet and one rule, and whatever Ilse wrote would be read aloud at the thaw feast and followed for a year. [163]

She turned the pen in her fingers. [170] Its nib was cut from the quill of a kingfisher feather her mother had found forty winters ago, when kingfishers still hunted the open water at the lake's southern mouth. [200] The shaft was no longer than a fingernail, bound into a holder of bone, and it held ink better than any goose quill, her mother claimed, because the bird had been made to pierce a surface and come back up. [240] Now the southern mouth froze every year, and no one in Halder under forty had seen that flash of blue. [260]

On the table beside the vellum lay her model, a lattice of copper wire strung with river stones, each stone a sounding she had taken through the ice that month. [290] Depth, cold, and the air left in the water, measured by how long a netted minnow stayed quick in a jar of it. [313] She had slid the stones along forty years of her mother's records, tilting the wires to follow each winter's trend, and every time the lattice sagged toward the same place. [343] The ninth week after midwinter, the fish would begin to suffocate beneath the ice. [357]

The door below groaned, and Elder Tace climbed the spiral stair with frost in his beard. [373]

"You have not written," he said. [379] "The council expected the usual. [384] The blank beyond the north reeds, the unknown depth at the Kettle." [396]

"The usual will starve us," Ilse said, and showed him the lattice. [408]

He watched the stones slide and settle, and his face went gray. [420] Then he shook his head. [425] "A prediction is prophecy wearing a scale. [432] The charter forbids it." [436]

"The charter forbids writing what cannot be measured," she said. [446] "Every stone here is measured. [451] What I cannot measure is the cure, and that is exactly what I am permitted to map." [468]

Her mother used to call her imaginative and then, frowning, grounded, as if the two words had never before been made to share one girl. [493] Ilse did not dream past the evidence; she walked to its very edge and described the drop. [510] By old custom, Halder sent searchers to every blank she drew. [521] If she drew the blank precisely, the town would walk into the future already facing a door. [538]

"Say it plainly," Tace said. [543]

"The ice fails the fish in nine weeks. [551] That is measured. [554] Unknown: whether a channel kept open at the southern mouth would let the lake breathe. [569] Unknown: whether the idle mill wheel could be rigged to keep it churning. [582] Unknown: who in Halder is clever enough to try." [591]

Tace was quiet for a long time, and the wind moaned through the spire's black ribs. [607]

"That last line is an insult," he said at last. [617]

"It is a dare," she said. [623] "Dares are measured by who answers." [629]

He laughed despite himself, a short cracked sound, and she knew then that she would prevail, not tonight but at the council, because he would carry her words there himself. [659] The change would not come as a miracle. [667] It would come as a channel cut and kept open, week after week, by people taking turns in the cold, the way anything living is kept. [693] Ilse had never believed a pen could write the future, only the precise shape of the space it would have to fill. [715]

After he left, Ilse dipped the kingfisher nib and held it above the vellum. [729] A single bead of ink trembled at its point, blue-black, ready to break the white surface. [745] Somewhere past that surface, if she had drawn the edges true, there was open water, and a flash of blue diving home. [767]
