The clinic promised a clean slate, and for eleven thousand dollars it delivered one. [14] After the procedure, Wren Calder could list every knee he had broken for Marty Voss and feel nothing heavier than the recitation of a grocery receipt. [40] That had been the point. [45] Guilt was a tumor, the brochure said, and they cut it out clean. [58] What the brochure did not mention was that guilt had roots, and the roots ran into everything else. [76] When his sister Dana wept at his kitchen table about her divorce, he watched the tears the way he might watch rain on a bus window. [102] She noticed. [104] She stopped coming. [107]

Three weeks later, a baseball arrived in a padded envelope with no return address. [121] The leather was yellowed, the red stitches frayed but unbroken, and across the sweet spot someone had signed in blue ink: W. Calder, October 2051. [146] It was his handwriting, only shakier, the way his father's had gone at the end. [161] Beneath the name, smaller, was an instruction he had apparently written to himself: read the Ledger between the lines. [180]

He had stopped believing in miracles around age nine, and he did not start again now, but he bought a copy of the Harbor Ledger anyway. [206] It took him two nights to see it. [214] In the police blotter and the obituaries, the gaps between certain lines were a hair too wide, and the first letters of the words bordering those gaps spelled directions: NORTH GRATE, CANAL STREET, LEFT AT THE SAINT. [251] Worse, the words themselves belonged to names he knew. [260] Ruth Okafor, whose bakery had burned the week she missed a payment. [272] Tomas Reyes, who had pitched two seasons in Double-A before Wren took a hammer to his right hand. [290]

The north grate on Canal Street opened onto the old aqueducts, and the aqueducts opened onto a labyrinth. [308] Every fork carried a chalk mark, and every mark matched a line from the paper folded in his pocket. [327] To choose a tunnel, he had to read a name, and to read a name he had to remember what he had done to it. [352] He remembered all of it, perfectly and without pain, which was its own kind of darkness. [368] Left at the plaster saint with no face. [376] Right at the drain marked with an O. [384] Down a stair slick with forty years of seep. [393] Somewhere past midnight he realized the labyrinth had no wrong turns, only longer ones, and that every path he had ever taken had worked the same way. [420]

Then the tunnel ended, and the dark turned blue. [429] The lake lay under a vault of limestone, black and still until his boot touched the shallows, and then light bloomed outward in a ring, pale as gas flame. [458] He had seen fireworks and casino ceilings and the inside of a man's mouth full of blood, and still some tired animal part of him stopped breathing. [485]

On a flat stone at the water's edge sat a tin box and a note in the same shaky hand. [505] "The light is made of cells so fragile a fingertip ruptures them," the note read. [520] "They have survived ten thousand years down here anyway, because they answer every touch." [534] "Delicately robust, the old biologists called them." [541] "The procedure did not cut out your guilt; it cut out the nerve that carries it, and nerves grow back only when you use the limb." [567] "You will not feel anything for a long time." [576] "Walk toward them anyway, and let the light come after." [586]

Inside the tin were Dana's new address, Ruth Okafor's, and Tomas Reyes's, and beneath them a photograph dated 2050. [605] In it, a gray-haired Tomas guided a boy's fingers across the seams of a baseball with his crooked right hand, and behind them, older and unmistakable, Wren stood holding the glove. [636] Both men were smiling as if it had cost them something. [647]

Wren looked at the photograph for a long time and felt nothing, exactly as promised. [662] Then he stepped into the lake, and the cold bit his shins, and the water lit around each step, breaking and answering, breaking and answering. [687] He turned the old ball over in his palm, frayed stitches holding after thirty years of somebody's handling, and thought that a clean slate was only a blank page, and a blank page was only paper until someone wrote on it. [728] It was not feeling, not yet. [734] But it was a direction, and he had always been good at following those. [748]

He waded back to shore, pocketed the addresses, and started the long climb toward Tomas Reyes's door, the ripples he had made still glowing faintly behind him. [775]
