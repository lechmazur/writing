The fog in Carrow Bay did not lift so much as rearrange itself, and the Marigold drifted inside it like a painted thought. [23] Ines Varga tied her skiff to the carnival ship's rusted ladder and climbed, the emerald swatch card buttoned in her breast pocket. [45] She found lost things for a living: wrecks, wills, heirs who did not want finding. [60] Six months ago she had found herself. [67]

A routine blood test before surgery had come back wrong, and then a cheek swab had come back wrong differently. [87] Her blood belonged to one woman and her skin to another. [98] Chimera, the geneticist said, smiling as if handing her a prize: in the womb she had absorbed a twin sister, and the sister had never entirely left. [125] Since then, her memories had begun to alter. [133] She remembered a calliope she had never heard, the smell of spun sugar on salt air, a man in a striped vest lifting her onto a horse with a gilt mane. [164]

Her mother, dying slowly in a hospice on the mainland, had painted signs aboard the Marigold the summer she was pregnant. [185] When Ines showed her the results, her mother pressed the swatch card into her hand and spoke of a garden of forgotten names, where a leaf had been left unfinished. [215] Then she slept, and did not say anything clear again. [225]

The deck was a ruin of bright things gone soft. [235] Ticket booths leaned like tired drunks; the carousel stood frozen mid-gallop, its horses bearded with gull droppings. [252] The ship had slipped its anchor chain years ago, yet the bay's mouth was narrow and the currents circular, so it wandered forever and went nowhere. [278] Ines understood that kind of freedom. [284]

Red yarn ran from the carousel into the dark companionway, strung at shoulder height. [298] She remembered, suddenly and impossibly, why: the performers had rigged it so no one got lost below decks when the fog came in. [321] Every few feet a paper tag hung from the line, and on each tag someone had written a sentence. [340] One tag read: the strongman cries at weddings. [348] Another: the juggler's daughter drowned in 1971, so now he juggles three, never four. [362] A third, in her mother's slanted hand: the sign painter is expecting two. [375]

Ines stopped breathing for a moment. [381] She followed the narrative threads deeper, reading each tag, and with each one her memory shifted like sand under a wave. [402] She was trying to recapture something she had never possessed, and the ship seemed willing to give it back. [421] Two heartbeats under her mother's ribs. [427] A doctor's grave face in a mainland clinic. [435] A tag where two had been struck through and one written above it. [448]

The yarn ended at a door marked CONSERVATORY in flaking gold. [459] Inside, beneath a cracked glass roof milky with fog, every wall was painted as a garden. [475] Thousands of leaves climbed toward the light, each one lettered with a name: stillborn calves, lost brothers, a trapeze artist's first wife, a dog called Admiral. [501] The carnival had kept its grief here, where the world could not tidy it away. [516]

Near the floor she found a leaf outlined but never filled, its interior bare primer. [531] She took out the swatch card. [537] Twelve greens ran down it, each labeled in her mother's hand: Moss, Bottle, Verdigris, Parrot. [552] The deepest emerald was labeled Wren. [558] She held it against the outline, and the neighboring leaves matched its shade exactly. [572] Her mother had mixed the color, named it, and then could not bring herself to use it. [589] Thirty-one years of comfort, prepared like kindling and never lit. [599]

Ines had brought no paint, but beneath the leaf sat a tin labeled Wren in her mother's hand, skinned over and still soft underneath. [623] She broke the skin with her thumb and painted with her left hand, the hand the geneticist said was mostly her sister's. [645] Then she lettered the name. [650] When she finished, the calliope memory returned, but now she saw both of them on the gilt horse, one small girl holding another. [673] It had never happened, and it was hers anyway. [682] She felt blissfully aberrant, a woman with two maps in one body, belonging to no single story. [699]

Back on deck, the fog had closed completely, and her skiff knocked against the hull like a patient question. [718] The Marigold would circle the bay tonight as it always did, bound and unbound at once. [734] Ines sat on the carousel's edge, the swatch card warm in her fist, and waited for the fog to let her go, in no hurry at all. [761] She had someone to talk to now. [768]
