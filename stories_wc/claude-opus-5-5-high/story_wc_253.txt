The turbines had been built the same year as the Happiness Mandate, and Ilse Varga still thought of them as one thing. [22] Three white towers stood in the east field, each blade longer than her barn, and their shadows swept the farmhouse every four seconds like a hand passing over a sleeper's face. [53] The mandate was repealed now, but the towers stayed, and so did their sound, a low wide breathing that had replaced every silence she used to own. [80]

The performer arrived on a Tuesday with a canvas bag and no expression at all. [95] She recognized the type. [99] During the Mandate years, the Ministry had licensed thousands of them to tour the provinces, smiling for people who could not, and when the law fell their faces had simply stopped, as if a wire had been cut. "I'm here about your son," he said. [144] "Grief visitation. [146] It's voluntary." [148]

[199] Maintenance work, they said, was good for the unsmiling; height gave perspective. [212] On the kitchen table sat the bouquet from his funeral, lilac and yarrow gone brown and papery, which she had not thrown out and could not look at. [240]
