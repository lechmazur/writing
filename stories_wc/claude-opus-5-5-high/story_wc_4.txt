Odile Varga kept the town's puzzles the way other people kept bees, patiently and with a little smoke of worry always hanging nearby. [23]
Her archive occupied the back room of the Hollin library, where donated jigsaws waited in labeled boxes: COMPLETE, NEARLY, and HOPELESS BUT LOVED. [46]
She also kept riddles people slipped under her door, and the occasional mystery nobody had asked her to solve. [65]
That spring, the mystery arrived in a dove gray map case left on her desk without a note. [83]
Inside were forty-one black-and-white photographs of the Hollin Button Works, taken in the years before the boiler exploded and the factory became a rusted skeleton on the edge of town. [113]
Nine people had died that morning. [119]
Odile's mother had been one of them. [126]

Hollin did not talk about the explosion; it only walked faster past the ruins, the way you hurry past a dog that once bit you. [151]
Odile, who was perceptively calm in the manner of someone who has sorted ten thousand sky pieces, did not hurry. [171]
She laid the photographs across her worktable and looked until the looking turned into seeing. [186]
In every picture there was an arc. [193]
A foreman's lunch pail caught mid-swing. [199]
The bent back of a woman lifting a crate of buttons. [210]
A thrown cap, a rainbow of spilled water, the curve of the smokestack's shadow at noon. [226]
When she slid two prints edge to edge, the pail's swing flowed perfectly into the woman's spine, as if the photographer had been composing a single line across years. [255]
Tucked in the case's lid was a stamp: M. Varga, Photographs. [266]
Her mother had carried a camera to every lunch break, and Odile had never known what became of the pictures. [286]
Now she understood that her mother had built a puzzle and died before anyone could be told there was one. [306]

But forty-one pieces did not close the line. [314]
There were gaps, and Odile knew exactly where the missing pieces lived: in shoeboxes and drawers all over Hollin, in the homes of every family who had lost someone and quietly kept a copy. [348]
She wanted more than to solve it. [355]
She wanted to take the worst morning of her life and turn it into something so beautiful the town would have to stop walking fast. [380]
So she posted a notice on the library door in her smallest, most polite handwriting, inviting anyone with a photograph from the Button Works to the lavender field behind the ruins on Sunday. [413]
The field had grown there by accident, from a single pot a worker once kept on a windowsill, and now it spilled purple right up to the broken brick. [442]

The first Sunday, only old Tobiah Fenn came, the foreman with the lunch pail, and he confessed he had left the map case on her desk. [468]
"Your mother asked me to keep it safe the week before," he said. [481]
"I was a coward for thirty years about giving it back." [492]
Odile only handed him a photograph and asked where his swing belonged. [504]
The next Sunday, the Okafor sisters came with a print of their father tossing his cap. [520]
Then the baker, then the retired mail carrier, then a teenager holding a picture of a great-grandmother she had never met. [541]
They knelt among the lavender, sliding prints along plywood boards, arguing gently over whether a curve was a shoulder or a hill. [563]
Odile never told anyone where a piece went; she only asked what they noticed, and waited. [579]
People began to tell stories while they searched. [587]
Some cried, and the bees did not mind. [595]
Someone brought lemonade, and someone else brought a folding table, and by June nobody hurried home when the light went gold. [616]
They lingered, which in Hollin was a kind of miracle. [626]

All summer, as vines climbed the factory's rusted ribs, the line grew too. [639]
By August it had become a spiral of two hundred and six photographs, every arc handing itself to the next, the smokestack's shadow curling at the center like a sleeping cat. [670]
One gap remained, near the spiral's outermost edge. [678]
Odile knew what belonged there before anyone said it. [687]
Her mother had never photographed herself. [693]
So Tobiah loaded an old camera with black-and-white film, and the whole field posed together in the lavender, arms linked, bending in one long, laughing curve toward the ruins. [722]
When the print came back, Odile set it into the last space, and the line closed. [738]
They mounted the spiral on the factory's last standing wall, where the vines could frame it. [754]
In her archive, Odile made a new label for the empty dove gray map case and tucked it inside: COMPLETE. [774]
Then, after a moment, she added a second word beneath it: SHARED. [786]
