Maren Oduya taught navigation in a city where no one had ever seen a star. [15] Halcyon Deep lay three thousand meters down, a cluster of pressure domes stitched to the basalt beside the vent fields, and its streets were lit only by the pale sodium of maintenance lamps. [48] It had been built forty years earlier, in the frantic season after the survey drones found the Creased living in the trench. [70] Nobody had expected the first aliens to be found at the bottom of the sea rather than in the sky. [90] They were folded things, sheets of living mineral that creased and uncreased around the vents, and they moved through the black water by reading edges. [115] Every ridge, fissure, and fallen pillar had an angle, and every angle, to them, was a word. [132] Maren taught that language to cadets as edge geometry: count the bevels of a lava tube, measure the dihedral where two slabs meet, and you would know where you were within a meter. [165] It was precise, and it was small. [172]

Her grandmother had sailed the surface before the descent, and in her last months she had talked of nothing but stars. [193] Polaris for north, she would whisper, the Southern Cross for the bottom of the world, Sirius burning low over the winter swell. [215] When she died she left Maren a single link of boat anchor chain, rust-black and heavy as a fist, cut from the cable that had lowered the first dome. [244] Maren wanted to learn to navigate by starlight, the way her grandmother had, though the surface storms had not broken in a generation and the stars were only a rumor overhead. [275]

She did not want it recklessly. [281] Her colleagues called her balancedly curious, which was meant as a compliment and a warning: she asked every question, but she asked them one at a time, and she waited for answers. [313] So when she finally suited up and walked out to the vent field with the chain link in her glove, she did not demand anything. [338] She set the link on a flat stone before the oldest of the Creased and stepped back. [355] It was a human edge, she reasoned, worn by a human voyage, and it was the only question she knew how to ask in their language. [381]

The alien considered it for a long time. [389] Then it touched the oval of iron with one creased margin, tracing the smooth inner curve and the flat worn place where the link had ground against its neighbors for years. [420] It began to unfold. [424]

Maren had seen the Creased open before, a fold or two, but never like this. [439] The sheet of it expanded across the vent field, pleat after pleat, until it hung above her like a luminous tent, and on its inner surface the edges arranged themselves into points. [471] Hundreds of points. [474] Thousands, each one the meeting of angles, each one burning faintly. [485]

It was a sky. [489] She knew it at once from her grandmother's words, though she had never seen one: a horizon folded down into the dark and now unfolded, the whole dome of heaven carried in the creases of a body. [526] The Creased had not come from the trench. [534] They had come down into it, long ago, and they had navigated here by starlight. [549]

The alien shifted its edges, and the map rotated, and Maren saw what it was showing her. [566] At the center of the sky was a gap, a cluster of points dimmed to gray, and around it the angles bent inward like a mouth closing on grief. [595] Their home star was gone. [600] They had fled its death and sunk themselves in the deepest water of a strange world, and for longer than humans had kept records they had been rehearsing a sky they could never return to. [635]

Maren stood beneath it with her breath fogging the faceplate. [645] She understood that she was being taught, and that the lesson cost the teacher something. [660] Then the alien lowered one margin to the chain link and made a new point near the edge of the map, small and steady, and joined it with a line of light to the star her grandmother had called north. [700] Here, it was saying. [704] You are here, and that is north. [711]

The next morning Maren hung the link above her classroom door, and when the cadets asked why, she began a new unit on the sky. [736] She drew each star as the meeting of two edges, the way the Creased had shown her, and told them the first truth of starlight: it comes from places that may already be gone. [770] Then she taught them to steer by it anyway. [779]
