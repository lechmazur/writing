<story>After the tower falls, the sky over the desert turned to salt and static. [14] Mira lived in a glass-domed enclosure where desert roses grew in rows of stubborn crimson. [29] She was the bounty hunter's pacifist daughter. [36] Her father had hunted men across the wastes before the tower fell. [48] Now his old airship ballast sandbag scrap hung from the dome ribs, holding soil for climbing roses. [65] The scrap still smelled of high wind and burned hydrogen. [106] The marks did not mean silence exactly. [113] They meant a pause wide enough for mercy. [121] People came to the dome for water, seed, and rumor. [131] Mira was senselessly perceptive. [135] She knew a stranger's hunger before he spoke, knew which rose would open by the angle of its thorn. [154] She could feel the tower's absence like a missing tooth in the world's jaw. [168] When raiders crossed the dunes, her father's blood woke in her hands. [180] She hated that waking. [184] At dusk, three scavengers arrived with knives and empty canteens. [194] Their leader demanded the water tanks. [200] Mira's hand went to the pruning knife at her belt. [210] She looked at the engraved quiet on the nearest sandbag scrap. [221] Then she sheathed the blade. [226] "You can drink," she said, "but you cannot take." [235] The scavengers stared at the rows of desert roses. [244] In the glass dome, the light was a tranquil blaze, gold and rose and slow. [259] Mira poured water into clay bowls. [265] She did not ask them to leave. [519] She watered the roots and whispered the next value: endurance. [529] The roses leaned toward her like listeners.
