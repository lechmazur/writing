<story>After the whale beaches, the harbor drowned in a gleaming silence. [11] Corin, the dock-side lantern lighter, walked the pier with his tin lighter and a pocket of pressed wildflowers. [29] He lit each lantern, but the flames only sharpened the gleaming silence. [41] His sister Lila had lost three years of memory to the wardens, and he needed to find where deleted memories go. [62] The pressed wildflowers in his pocket were the last proof she had once loved the salt flats. [79] After the whale beaches, strange pockets of gravity opened along the tideline. [91] They fluctuated mysteriously, bending the lantern light into soft, wrong angles. [102] Corin followed the bent light to the old cannery, where a salt crystal growing chamber hummed beneath the floor. [121] There, Marn the coffee reader sat among brine tanks and read the future through patterns in coffee grounds. [139] "Deleted memories do not vanish," Marn said. [146] "They sink into pockets of gravity and wait to be exhumed." [157] Corin asked how to find them, and Marn tilted the cup. [168] The grounds formed a spiral like a whale's eye, then a map of the salt chamber. [184] "Exhume the small iron box beneath the third crystal," she said. [195] Corin took up a chisel and knelt in the glowing brine. [206] The chamber's gravity fluctuated mysteriously, making the chisel heavy, then light, then heavy again. [220] He worked until the salt cracked and he could exhume the box from its crystalline bed. [236] Inside lay a bundle of pressed wildflowers, identical to the ones in his pocket. [250] Beneath them was a note in Lila's hand: "I chose to forget the fire, not you." [266] The gleaming silence around him tightened, as if the harbor held its breath. [279] Corin understood then that deleted memories went into the pockets of gravity that opened when a whale beached. [297] The whale's death released a tide of forgotten things, and the salt chamber preserved them. [312] But the pockets were unstable, and their pull fluctuated mysteriously with the moon. [325] If he wanted Lila's years back, he had to carry the box to the beached whale before high tide. [344] He ran along the dock, lighting no lanterns, letting the gleaming silence guide him. [358] At the whale's side, gulls stood frozen like white chess pieces. [369] Corin pressed the wildflowers against the whale's eye and opened the iron box. [382] A pocket of gravity swelled, pulling the note, the flowers, and a rush of cold air. [398] He saw Lila at the salt flats, laughing, before the fire took her memories. [412] Then the pocket snapped shut, and the whale exhaled its last long breath. [425] The tide came in, covering the whale and the box with foam. [437] Corin returned to the dock, his pocket empty, his lantern lighter cold. [449] He lit the first lantern, and the flame burned steady in the gleaming silence. [463] The next morning, Lila remembered the salt flats, but not the fire. [475] Marn read new coffee grounds and found a smaller pocket under the pier. [488] Corin knew deleted memories would keep surfacing, one beached whale at a time. [501] He filled his pocket with fresh wildflowers and waited for the next gleaming silence. [515] The harbor did not forget the whale. [522] Its ribs became a salt cathedral beneath the waves. [531] Corin visited the chamber each dawn, where crystals grew around the shadows of lost hours. [546] He learned that pockets of gravity were not holes but folds. [557] The pull of each pocket fluctuates mysteriously because regret has no fixed weight. [570] Marn taught him to read coffee grounds for the living, not the dead. [583] The patterns showed where a memory might rise, but not what it would cost. [597] Corin kept the pressed wildflowers in a jar of brine, a promise and a warning. [612] When another whale beached, he would exhume what the sea could not hold.
