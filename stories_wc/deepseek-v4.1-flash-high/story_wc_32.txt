At twilight the ember nexus breathed like a dying forge, and the hopeless alchemist knelt among cinders that refused to cool. [21] He was stoically sensitive, which meant he felt every failure without letting it crack his face. [37] Before him lay the broken bishop's crozier, a curled staff of blackened silver split along its spiral. [54] It had chosen him at birth, or so the bishop claimed, marking him for a destiny he never wanted. [73] That destiny was a stifling corridor of ritual, inheritance, and unbroken ceremony. [85] He wanted to interrupt it, to vivify something else from its ruins. [97] The crozier was not merely broken; it was a fossil of frozen bloom, beautiful and dead. [113] Its silver petals had never opened, only hardened into the shape expected of them. [127] The alchemist set a copper basin at the nexus center, where embers climbed the air like reversed rain. [145] He worked through successive approximation, because perfection was a lie that only destroyed progress. [159] First he dissolved a shard of the crozier in acid and got a gray smoke that smelled of old sermons. [179] Second he added salt from a meteor and the smoke twitched, almost alive. [192] Third he fed it a drop of his own blood, and the mixture sang a note too low for hearing. [212] Each trial failed differently, which was how he knew he was getting closer. [225] He did not weep when the fourth attempt turned his left hand to glass. [239] Stoically sensitive, he simply wrapped the glass fingers and continued. [249] Then, when the penny dropped, twilight deepened and the ember nexus went silent. [262] The penny was not a coin but a thought: creative destruction. [273] He had been trying to preserve the crozier while changing it, and that contradiction had kept it dead. [291] To vivify, he had to destroy the old shape completely, not mend it. [304] He shattered the bishop's crozier against the basin rim. [313] Silver fragments flew like cold sparks, and each one cut a memory from him. [327] He let them fall without reaching to save the familiar pieces. [338] Then he poured the unfinished elixir over the wreckage. [347] The nexus flared white, then blue, then a color that had no name in any ledger. [363] From the basin rose a thin green stem, jointed like a bishop's staff. [376] At its tip opened a flower of ice and fire, a frozen bloom that did not melt. [393] The alchemist felt its pulse, small and stubborn, a life that had never been foretold. [408] He had interrupted the stifling destiny by refusing to let the old form survive. [422] The flower turned its face toward him, and he saw in its translucent petals the bishop's seal dissolving. [440] It did not offer forgiveness or rebellion; it offered only a different beginning. [453] He named it vivification, though the word felt too clean for what had happened. [467] The ember nexus dimmed, and twilight folded around them like a hand. [479] The hopeless alchemist was still hopeless, but now his hopelessness had a root. [492] He was stoically sensitive to the end, so he did not smile. [504] He simply carried the frozen bloom out of the cinders, and behind him the broken crozier was gone. [522] Ahead, a destiny that had never existed waited to be approximated, trial by trial. [536] The penny had dropped, and its echo sounded like a living thing. [548] That was enough. [551] He walked until the ember nexus became a memory of heat. [562] The frozen bloom chimed against his glass fingers. [570] Each step was a small destruction of the path he had been promised. [583] Each breath was a small approximation of the man he might become. [595] When dawn finally came, it found no bishop and no heir, only an alchemist carrying a strange flower. [613] The flower's roots, he knew, were already threading through his ruined hand. [625] It would grow, and it would demand more than destiny ever had. [637] He welcomed that demand, because it was alive. [645]
