<story>Mara Voss was a broken-clock tinkerer who kept the city's stopped hours in neat drawers. [15] She repaired escapements, pendulums, and mainsprings, but she never promised they would run on time. [30] Her shop smelled of brass filings and cold tea. [39] When coincidence becomes pattern, even a tinkerer's bench can look like an altar. [52] The admiral's lost compass rose arrived in a cigar box beneath a heap of ash. [67] A widow said fire had taken the admiral, his charts, and his famous instrument. [81] Mara turned the brass rose over and found no north. [91] Its points aimed at a crack in the floor, then at the cellar wall, then at her own left hand. [111] She was uncertainly certain that this was not random. [120] Her motivation was simple: to befriend uncertainty in planning. [129] Plans had failed her before, so she kept them loose, like rope. [141] She decided to improvise. [145] The compass rose led her through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse. [158] Glass panes were fogged with green dust, and vines had knotted the door handles. [172] Leaves circled her ankles as if reading her steps. [181] She followed the rose's silent point past pots of dead orchids and a dry fountain. [196] At the back, a stairwell breathed cold air. [204] She descended until the greenhouse sounds faded and water spoke from stone. [216] Beneath the ashes of the old observatory, a subterranean waterfall fell into a black pool. [231] The compass rose glowed faintly, its cardinal points cutting the dark. [242] Mara saw a broken marine chronometer on a ledge, its face shattered at 3:17. [256] All the clocks in her shop had stopped at 3:17 too. [267] When coincidence becomes pattern, the waterfall seemed less like an accident and more like a sentence. [283] She had come to befriend uncertainty, not to worship it. [293] So she studied the scene and improvised again. [301] The admiral's compass rose fit into a groove on the chronometer's back. [313] When she pressed it, the subterranean waterfall changed its fall. [323] Water divided around a hidden wheel, and the wheel began to turn. [335] The chronometer ticked once, then paused, then ticked again. [344] Mara felt no triumph, only a quiet adjustment in her chest. [355] She spoke aloud with no tone at all. [363] "North is a habit," she said. [369] "The water is a better direction." [375] The compass rose spun, uncertainly certain, and pointed toward a tunnel. [386] Beneath the ashes, she found the admiral's logbook wrapped in oilcloth. [397] Its last entry described a plan to let the river choose the observatory's schedule. [411] He had written, "I am uncertainly certain that order is a story we tell after the fact." [428] Mara laughed, but there was no tone in it either. [438] She realized the lost compass rose had never been lost. [448] It had been waiting beneath the ashes for someone willing to plan less and listen more. [464] To befriend uncertainty in planning was not to abandon purpose. [474] It was to leave room for the waterfall. [482] Mara carried the chronometer and compass rose back through the neglected greenhouse. [494] The swirling leaves parted before her, then closed behind like a curtain. [506] In her shop, she set every stopped clock to 3:17 and did not repair them. [521] Instead, she built a new mechanism that would run only when the river ran. [535] Customers asked for certainty, and she offered estimates, weather, and tea. [546] Some left; some stayed. [550] When coincidence becomes pattern, a broken clock can become a door. [561] Beneath the ashes, the admiral's lost compass rose still pointed to water. [573] Mara visited the subterranean waterfall each week and improvised her route. [584] She was uncertainly certain that this was enough. [592] No tone, no trumpet, no final answer, only the next turn of the wheel. [606] And when coincidence becomes pattern, she welcomed it without a plan. [617]</story>
