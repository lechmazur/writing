<story>The cryptic medium, Ilyra, kept the gargoyle's rainspout shard wrapped in oilcloth against her sternum. [15] She could heal only in moonlight, so she waited for the moon to rise over the mangrove labyrinth before she dared enter it. [38] The labyrinth's singing spirits threaded through roots and brackish water, their voices soft as wet leaves. [54] They sang the unsent letters of everyone who had ever loved and hesitated. [67] Ilyra collected those letters, not on paper, but as messages in condensation on windows. [81] Every dawn, the glass of her shack spelled out apologies strangers never spoke. [94] But one apology waited unspoken in her own throat. [103] It was meant for her sister, Nema, who had vanished into the mangrove labyrinth three monsoons ago. [120] The shard grew warm against Ilyra's chest whenever she thought of Nema. [132] It was a piece of a gargoyle's rainspout, torn from a drowned cathedral, and it remembered how to guide water and truth. [154] Tonight, the moon would be full enough to heal the crack in the shard and the crack in Ilyra's courage. [174] She wrapped the shard in oilcloth, stepped into the labyrinth, and let the singing spirits close around her. [192] Their song was fierce tenderness, a lullaby with teeth. [201] It asked her to evoke the moment before deciding. [210] Ilyra followed the spirits' voices through black roots that rose like ribs from the water. [225] She passed windows suspended in the mangroves, each pane fogged with a message someone had almost sent. [242] One read, "I was afraid you would say yes." [251] Another read, "Come home, and I will listen." [259] Ilyra touched each window, and the condensation shivered into new words. [270] She whispered, "Nema, I am sorry I told you to go." [281] The mangrove labyrinth answered with a chorus that sounded like rain on old bells. [295] A path of moonlight opened across the water, and Ilyra walked it with the shard held high. [312] The shard drank the light, and its crack closed like a seam of silver. [326] Healing in moonlight was not a cure; it was a way of remembering without bleeding. [341] At the labyrinth's heart, a single window stood on a stilt above the tide. [355] Its condensation was blank, waiting in the space where an apology waits unspoken. [368] Ilyra understood then that the spirits had not brought her to send a letter. [382] They had brought her to find the moment before deciding. [392] In that moment, Nema stood on the other side of the glass, younger, rain-soaked, and furious. [408] She held a letter that Ilyra had written but never sent. [419] The words were still wet: "Stay, and I will learn how to be brave." [433] Ilyra pressed her palm to the window and evoked the moment exactly. [445] She felt the apology rise, but she did not force it into words. [458] Instead, she let the condensation carry a new message: "I am here. Decide when you are ready." [475] The singing spirits fell silent, and the mangrove labyrinth breathed like a sleeping animal. [489] Nema's reflection smiled, not forgiving, but recognizing. [496] Then the window cleared, and Ilyra stood alone with the gargoyle's rainspout shard. [509] The shard was whole now, though its healing had nothing to do with stone. [523] Ilyra walked back through the roots, leaving unsent letters glowing on every window. [536] At the edge of the labyrinth, dawn broke, and the spirits sang one last phrase. [551] It sounded like fierce tenderness learning to wait. [559] She returned to her shack and found condensation on her own window. [571] The words read, "I am sorry. I am listening. You can decide." [583] Ilyra touched the glass, and for the first time, she did not need to send it. [599] The moment before deciding had been enough to heal her. [609] She wrapped the shard against her sternum and waited for moonlight, patient as a letter that might still be answered. [629]</story>
