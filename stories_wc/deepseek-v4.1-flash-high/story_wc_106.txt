<story>At Emerald Station, moonlight slid through the green glass roof while a fleeting golden sunset burned beyond the platforms. [19] Iris Vane was a golden-hour portrait thief, and she moved like a rumor through the waiting crowd. [36] She was quietly savvy, reading faces, exits, and lies without ever letting her own face answer. [52] Her method was codes in flower arrangements, left on benches by clients who never spoke her name. [69] Tonight a bouquet waited under a clock that had stopped at 7:13. [139] The real prize was a jar of captured moonbeams hidden in his office. [152] She slipped past the ticket gate as the sunset deepened from gold to ember. [166] The emerald station by moonlight felt suspended between two worlds. [176] Moonbeams pooled on the floor, cold and silver, while sunset light painted the walls honey. [191] In the stationmaster's office, she found the portrait: a man with tired eyes holding a glass jar. [208] Inside the jar, captured moonbeams swirled like slow white fire. [218] Iris knew the core concept of her trade: portraits held illuminated moments. [230] A golden-hour portrait could catch the instant when a person's destiny became visible. [243] That was why collectors paid. [248] That was why she stole. [253] Her motivation was not greed but to press her ear against destiny's door. [266] She wanted to hear the future breathe before it opened. [276] She lifted the portrait from its hook and turned it toward the fading sunset. [290] The painted jar seemed to glow. [611] The emerald station vanished behind her, but the moonbeams remembered. [621] Destiny's door had opened, and she had finally heard it click. [632] It was not a theft.
