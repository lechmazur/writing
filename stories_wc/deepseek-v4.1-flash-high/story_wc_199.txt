<story>In the ashen skyborne arbor, Tamsin was the orderly seed sorter, and she kept every seed in its proper drawer. [20] The arbor floated through a gray sky on roots wrapped around a singing stone, and moss drank the ash like sweet rain. [42] Every evening, during the hush where past meets prophecy, leaves fell from the high branches and spelled out messages no one could read. [65] Tamsin's motivation was simple and stubborn: to decode the message in falling leaves before the hush closed. [82] The regional folklore of the arbor said that a moonstone cabochon dust speck could catch a leaf's true word. [101] Such a speck was rare, a grain of pale fire lost from a jewel long forgotten. [117] One morning, while sorting seeds by shape and memory, Tamsin found one in a drawer of withered plum pits. [136] It lay in the crease of a pit, cool and bright, no bigger than a gnat's sigh. [153] She fetched her hawthorn needle and gave the speck a gentle prod. [165] It emerged transformed always: first a tiny silver fish, then a key, then a moth with mossy wings. [183] Tamsin laughed with mossy delight, for transformation was the arbor's oldest habit. [195] She knew the speck would not hold one shape long enough to read the leaves directly. [211] So she turned to her grandmother's method: building tiny monuments to forgotten things. [224] Each monument was made from ash paste, twig bones, seed husks, and a whispered name. [239] She built one for the blue barley that no longer grew in the arbor's lower terraces. [255] She built another for a dialect of wind that only the oldest leaves remembered. [269] She built a third for her own childhood fear of falling, which had fallen away already. [285] When the hush arrived, she placed the moonstone cabochon dust speck on the first monument. [300] The speck flickered, prodded by wind, and became a tiny compass needle. [312] The falling leaves paused, then rearranged themselves around the monuments like curious birds. [325] Tamsin saw a sentence form: "What is forgotten is not gone; it is only waiting for a root." [343] Her heart thumped with mossy delight, and she prodded the speck again. [355] It emerged transformed always, this time as a small green door standing in the ash. [370] The leaves swirled into another line: "The past is a seed; prophecy is its shoot." [385] Tamsin understood that the message was not a map or a warning but an invitation to tend memory. [403] She built more tiny monuments, one for every forgotten thing the leaves named. [416] She made a monument for a lost song, a cracked seed, a child's first word. [431] Each time she placed the speck, it transformed always, and each shape taught her a different grammar. [448] A fish meant water, a key meant opening, a moth meant night, a door meant choice. [464] The ashen skyborne arbor creaked softly, as if the old roots were listening. [477] Regional folklore had warned that the hush could steal a sorter's name if they read too greedily. [494] But Tamsin was orderly, not greedy, and she gave each leaf its proper time. [508] By dusk, the monuments formed a spiral around the arbor's central trunk. [520] The dust speck rested on the last monument and became a single luminous seed. [534] Tamsin planted it without hesitation, prodding the soil with her hawthorn needle. [546] The leaves fell one final time and spelled: "You are the message, and you emerge transformed always." [563] Mossy delight filled her chest, green and warm and impossibly old. [574] She understood that decoding the falling leaves meant becoming part of their story. [587] The hush closed, the past and prophecy folded together, and the arbor drifted on. [601] Tamsin remained an orderly seed sorter, but now she sorted memories as carefully as seeds. [616] And in every drawer of the ashen skyborne arbor, tiny monuments to forgotten things waited to be built. [634] She would prod the moonstone cabochon dust speck whenever the hush returned, ready to learn what the leaves had to say. [655] The message was never finished, because forgetting and remembering were roots of the same tree. [670] With mossy delight, Tamsin tended that tree, one tiny monument at a time. [683]</story>
