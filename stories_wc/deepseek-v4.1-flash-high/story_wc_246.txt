<story>Tamsin was a celestial signal interpreter stationed at the edge of a gravitational anomaly, where light arrived late and memory arrived early. [22] After planting the wrong seeds in the station's garden, she had expected nothing but sterile dust. [55] Tamsin listened, because listening was her work, and the vines repeated a signal no telescope had caught. [72] She carried a dried-up fountain pen with exotic nib, a relic from a dead archivist who had claimed it could edit history. [94] The pen had never held ink for her, yet it warmed when the anomaly surged. [109] Its power worked only through contingent relationships, so no edit stood alone. [121] A changed word required a changed witness, a shifted promise, a door left open somewhere else. [137] Tamsin was confidently uncertain, which was the only honest posture before a collapsing star. [151] Her motivation was to thresh meaning from experience, not to win arguments with the past. [199] According to that record, the seeds were a failure, and the station had never recovered. [214] But the botanist's grief over a lost sister had made him tend them in secret. [342] The gravitational anomaly loosened its grip, enough for light to move like water. [355] Tamsin felt no triumph, only a quiet, confidently uncertain awe. [365] She had edited history, but history had also edited her. [375] The dead archivist's voice returned through the pen, soft and amused.
