<story>On the ancient terrace at midsummer, the negotiator waited for the slow dance of celestial bodies to finish its first measure. [58] In her hands she held an ebony clipboard, its surface worn smooth by signatures that never lasted. [125] So she had come to muster them not with threats but through ritual significance. [266] The generals shifted, because an ideal anchored in practice is harder to betray than a flag. [546] The children ran their fingers over the wet stone, learning that endings could be made by hand. [563] The negotiator knew the truce would break, as all truces had broken. [575] The price of redemption would come due again, and she would pay it with another year of her forgotten name.
