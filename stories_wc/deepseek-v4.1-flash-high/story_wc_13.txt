<story>Mara was a natural history illustrator who mapped creatures that lived only on the rooftop canal system weaving between towers. [20] She worked during the giant's nap, when the city held its breath and the water ran uphill. [37] The giant slept beneath the foundations, and its slow dreams leaked into the canals as borrowed minutes. [54] Mara wore an infinity symbol pendant that let her sculpt time itself by borrowing time. [69] Each twist of the pendant slowed her pulse until a step took an hour and an hour took a breath. [89] She needed that borrowed time because her motivation was precise: to find the place where echoes are born. [107] Her sketchbook held drawings of every echo she had caught, each one a pale ring around a vanished sound. [126] The oldest sketch showed her mother ringing a canal bell, but the cadence stopped before resolution. [142] That interrupted cadence repeated along the rooftops, always breaking where a listener expected peace. [156] Mara followed it across locks, aqueducts, and narrow bridges that crossed the sky. [169] The canals wove between towers like silver nerves, and she painted the strange moths that drank from them. [187] None of the moths sang, but their wingbeats made a rhythm that matched the broken cadence. [203] She reached a hexagonal reservoir where seven channels met and fell silent. [215] There, a glass cage hung from a crane, and inside it an echo circled like a trapped bird. [233] She knew this echo: it was her mother's unfinished note, caged by the city's acoustic wardens. [249] To uncage it, she would have to borrow more time than the giant's nap could safely lend. [266] She turned the infinity symbol pendant three times and felt the giant's dream hesitate. [280] The water in the canals slowed; a falling drop hung in the air like a bead of glass. [298] Mara stepped onto the reservoir rim and sculpted time itself into a shallow stair. [312] Each stair was a borrowed second, and each second carried her closer to the cage. [327] She drew a key in her sketchbook, not a physical key but a natural history of release. [344] The drawing showed a lock opening, a wing unfolding, a mouth unlearning its silence. [358] When she pressed the page against the cage, the echo recognized the shape of its own escape. [375] She uncaged it. [378] The echo shot upward, not away but inward, toward the place where echoes are born. [393] Mara followed, climbing the interrupted cadence as if it were a staircase of sound. [407] The rooftops tilted, the canals braided, and the towers leaned close enough to whisper. [421] At the center of the canal system, a dry fountain waited beneath a cracked observatory dome. [437] There, echoes were not reflections but seeds, small knots of sound waiting to be spoken. [452] Mara understood that every voice she had ever loved had begun in that basin. [466] She also understood the cost: borrowed time must be repaid before the giant woke. [480] The pendant grew cold, and the infinity symbol began to flicker like a dying star. [495] She had sculpted time itself too deeply, and now time demanded its shape back. [509] The echo settled into the fountain, and the interrupted cadence finally resolved. [521] It was a quiet resolution, without triumph and without grief. [531] Mara felt none of the awe she had expected, only the clean weight of a completed drawing. [548] She sketched the fountain, the seed-echoes, and the giant's slow breath moving beneath the city. [563] Then she returned the borrowed minutes one by one, turning the pendant backward. [576] The canals resumed their downhill rush, and the rooftops stopped leaning. [587] The giant stirred once, not waking, and its nap closed over the city like a lid. [603] Mara climbed down from the observatory with the echo's birthplace folded in her sketchbook. [617] She no longer needed to cage sounds; she knew where they began. [629] Across the giant's nap, she had borrowed time, sculpted it, uncaged an echo, and found the place where echoes are born.
