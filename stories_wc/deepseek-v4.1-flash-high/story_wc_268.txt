<story>In the ancient temple library lost in time, the diminutive titan Orin ruled no kingdom, yet he carried himself with a regally unpretentious calm. [24] He had come to this dust-lit sanctuary during the pause of shifting constellations, when the sky held its breath and every hour belonged to no century. [50] His motivation was simple and sharp: to unmask a pretender to the throne before the imposter could wear the crown beyond the reach of memory. [75] The library offered no armies, only shelves that breathed like sleeping giants and the iron gall ink pellet he kept in a copper vial. [99] That pellet was the key to his method: when dissolved, it darkened into ink that could cling to parchment for centuries without fading. [122] With it, he left cryptic messages in library books, small marginal riddles meant for the one reader clever enough to notice them. [144] Each message pointed toward a page, a date, a stain, or a name erased by the pretender's scribes. [162] Orin was not the only one reading them. [170] The pretender's agents had learned to hunt for the iron gall scent, so Orin wrote in code and hid the pellet inside different spines each night. [196] The rune of drift was carved into the library's central dais, a spiral of old words that let the temple slide across eras like a boat on black water. [225] As the constellations paused, the rune glowed faintly, and the shelves shifted their arrangement with a sound like distant chimes. [245] The pretender had used that same rune to rewrite his lineage, inserting false ancestors into royal chronicles while true heirs vanished from the index. [269] Orin found proof in a battered genealogy bound in green leather, its margins crowded with his own earlier notes. [288] One note read, "The third seal is borrowed; the blood beneath it is not." [302] Another said, "Ask the iron pellet what the crown forgets." [312] He crushed the pellet into a shallow bowl of water and watched the ink bloom like a dark flower. [331] In that ink, hidden letters appeared on the genealogy's final page, naming the pretender as a court clerk's son. [350] The truth was not enough while the rune of drift protected him, for the pretender could flee into any century and rule from a throne no one remembered. [378] Orin decided to sunder the rune, though he knew the act would break the library's long drift and bring its graceful collapse. [400] He approached the central dais as the constellations paused at their widest turning. [413] The pretender appeared then, wearing a borrowed crown and a smile too polished for a temple of dust. [431] "You are small for a titan," the pretender said. [440] "And you are false for a king," Orin replied, regally unpretentious even as the floor began to tremble. [458] He struck the rune with the copper vial, and the spiral cracked along its oldest line. [474] Light spilled from the fracture, not violent but slow, like honey poured from a broken jar. [490] The pretender lunged, but the drifting shelves moved between them, each book falling open to a page Orin had marked. [510] Cryptic messages fluttered into the air, spelling out the confession the pretender had buried in ink and time. [528] The crown slipped from his head as the library's roof opened to a sky resuming its ancient motion. [546] Orin did not need to kill him; the truth unmasked the pretender more completely than any blade could. [564] The temple library collapsed gracefully, shelf by shelf, century by century, until only the dais remained under the shifting stars. [584] Orin stood among the ruins, diminutive and unbroken, with the empty vial still warm in his hand. [601] The constellations resumed their long procession, and the false king was left in the open air of a forgotten age. [621] No throne welcomed him, and no chronicle spared him a kindly line. [633] Orin walked away with no kingdom, but he had done what titans are meant to do: he had set a broken truth back into the world. [659] Behind him, the ancient temple library faded into a pause between stars, and its final collapse sounded almost like a bow. [680]</story>
