[243] He knew he could never reproduce those pure, inhuman tones directly, for a throat of flesh could only tear against such jagged purity. [266] Instead, his purpose was to paraphrase that monumental drone, translating its austere geometry into syllables of mist, memory, and breath. [286] He stepped forward onto the lapis-inlaid tiles, his sandals making no sound against the glazed stone. [460] The transformation did not happen through force or alchemy, but through dissolving boundaries between the singer and the sung. [527] He walked farther along the colonnade, pacing his breath to the chime of the air.
