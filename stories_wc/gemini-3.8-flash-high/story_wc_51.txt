<story>The water entered the bronze grotto under starlight through hair-thin fissures, carrying the salted silt of the uplands after the first snowmelt. [22] Within the metallic hollow, silence did not arrive as an emptiness, but settled in slanting, overlapping layers, a shingled hush that deadened the drip of the thaw until each drop landed without an echo. [56] Naela sat where the pool met the curved floor, working her spools of filament as a fractal weaver of the deep earth. [78] Her vocation was patient and heavy: to collect the weights of different burdens that the upper world shed into the spring runoff. [100] The cold water held the residue of spent winters, bearing a farmer's ruined ledger, an unresolved estrangement, and the dull ache of a mother's worn hands. [126] She plucked these dissolved griefs from the current with bone needles, knotting them into repeating patterns where small griefs nested inside larger regrets, identically shaped across every scale. [154] Yet she was not a free archivist of these sorrows. [164] Because her loom opened into every fold of the bronze chamber without perimeter or fence, she was boundary-lessly trapped within the very web she produced. [189] No wall detained her; rather, every direction she turned presented only another replica of the same intricate snare, receding infinitely toward both the cosmos and the grain of dust. [218] Before her, perched on a slab of verdigris stone, rested a brass kerosene lamp burner, stripped of its glass chimney and font. [240] Its toothed thumbwheel and perforated gallery held no wick of cotton, but served instead as the spindle around which she wound her raw strands of memory. [266] Tonight, the pressure in the grotto was mounting. [274] The runoff came too swift, thick with the unsaid confessions of a dying century, threatening to saturate the cavern until the sheer weight pulled the vault down upon her lungs. [304] A mind submerged in such endless, fluid grief could not endure without form. [317] She needed the burdens to solidify, to cease their restless churning and anchor themselves into permanent architecture. [334] She needed them to calcify. [339] If she could transmute the flowing sorrows into chalk and ivory ribs, they would bear their own gravity instead of crushing her under their wet, drifting mass. [366] She took the kerosene lamp burner between her trembling fingers, feeling the cold green patination on its brass teeth. [385] The transformation could not occur by force or invocation, but only by orienting properly the brass slits toward the aperture in the roof where the stars crossed the meridian. [414] High above, through a fissure in the domed ceiling, the constellations gleamed like powdered salt on dark water. [432] Naela lifted the metal mechanism, tilting its circular collar until the starlight pierced the aeration holes at an exact thirty-degree slant. [453] The light did not illuminate the dark; rather, it drew a needle of stillness straight through the chamber's damp murmur. [473] Through the wick-slot, she drew a single, heavy thread woven from the regret of an old carpenter who had abandoned his craft. [495] As the starlight caught the fiber within the bronze teeth, the damp hum of the grotto altered into something arid and vast. [517] The fluid weave stiffened in her hands. [524] A brittle whiteness crept along the interlocking loops, turning the wet grief pale, dense, and rigid as bleached marrow. [543] The transformation blossomed outward through every self-similar fold, spreading along the geometry of the web like frost upon winter glass. [563] What had been an overwhelming tide became an array of towering, ossified arches, curving upward to buttress the bronze dome. [583] They resembled the clean, hollow ribcage of a leviathan resting on an ancient ocean floor, stripped of flesh and preserved in salt. [605] Naela leaned back against the smooth bronze wall, her breath misting faintly in the cold chamber. [621] The cage around her was no smaller than before; she was still bound without borders to this endless subterranean gallery. [641] Yet the burdens no longer threatened to drown her beneath their formless, chaotic mass. [655] They had become statues of bone, bearing their own measured sorrows upon the floor, silent monuments that held up the dark sky. [677] In the pale, oceanic stillness, she listened to the shingled hush settle once more over the stone, a gentle funeral of time where even grief could find rest. [705]</story>
