[27] High beneath the limestone dome, Maren kept vigil within the circular acoustics of the whispering gallery. [69] Then the incendiaries fell on the studio bunker, incinerating the directors and extras alike; she crawled from real ash and broken marrow, the sole crisis survivor of a catastrophe she had once been hired to mimic. [105] That baptism of flame stripped her of theatricality, leaving behind a luminous absence where vanity and fear once lived. [124] Now, her solitary ambition was to become the historian of small gestures. [245] The rooks, knights, and crowned monarchs remained frozen in carved walnut and bone, staring across vacant squares where their disposable defenders had once stood. [269] Whoever had swept the small pieces into the fire had treated them precisely as the ministry had treated its citizens: expendable numbers omitted from the final tally. [296] Maren's discipline was benevolently ruthless; she felt no hatred for the fallen regime, yet she refused to let its curated fictions outlive its ruins. [336] To preserve what had happened, she could not use ink, for ink could be burned by the incoming commanders. [672] The pawns had not vanished after all; their vanishing was simply an unwritten testament, now engraved in sound by the friction of her boots against the stone.
