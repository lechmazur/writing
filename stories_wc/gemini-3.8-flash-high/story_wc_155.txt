<story>Between the wet dip of his coarse brush and the waiting wall, Corin found an hour slipping sideways into eternity within the fold of a daydream. [26] He had always been a vivid mural artist whose pigments could wake a sleeping street, yet recently his palette had felt heavy, drab, and strangely hollow. [52] His longing was neither grand nor solemn, but merely to rediscover lost joy, the sort of bubbling amusement that once made his knuckles dance across stone. [78] So his mind drifted inward, depositing him beneath the dripping boughs of a ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog. [100] The trees were ancient, knotted cripples whose branches clutched damp vapor instead of pears, their calcified bark gray as forgotten bone. [121] "Well, old fellows," Corin murmured with a wry twitch of his lips, "you certainly know how to keep a secret." [141] It was a place suspended between memory and sleep, where every breath smelled of petrified moss and cold dew. [160] In his pocket rattled a jagged fresco fragment, salvaged years ago from an abandoned town square where children had once thrown plums at painted clowns. [185] The fragment possessed a stub of cobalt sky and a smear of yellow smile, chipped yet stubbornly radiant against the gray ruin. [207] Corin was no ordinary traveler here; he possessed an uncanny gift where he paints with light, weaving beams from the misty atmosphere as easily as others spread tempera. [235] With a fluid flick of two fingers, he drew an arc of pale amber through the gloom, watching the vapor hiss in mild, glowing astonishment. [260] The vapor itself was peculiar, clinging to the orchard like vast sheets of exposed film. [275] Every century of sunless damp had sensitized the air, turning each suspended droplet into a sensitive grain that remembered every blossom that had ever withered before it could blush. [304] If one struck a match too carelessly, the whole district risked bleaching into blankness, washing away the ghosts of summer forever. [325] To navigate this delicate emulsion without ruining its preserved traces, Corin consulted the orchard's buried knowledge network. [342] This subterranean web of fungal nodes and copper root wires had once logged the seasonal health of every tree, archiving songs, weather reports, and the raucous toasts of long departed cider makers. [374] Kneeling upon the mulch, Corin pressed the fresco fragment against a damp copper root protruding from the loam. [392] The network hummed in polite recognition, whispering back through the soil with the dry rustle of parchment. [409] It offered him coordinates of merriment: a forgotten terrace where orchard laborers used to dance when the press vats brimmed over with sweet foam. [433] A soft, reflective chuckle broke from his chest. [441] How delightfully absurd it was, he thought, that centuries of solemn mist had been guarding nothing more sinister than cider jokes and muddy boots. [465] Rising to his feet, Corin allowed the knowledge network to guide his strides along the mossy stone steps. [483] He reached the terrace circle, where broken wine jars stood like sentinels draped in wet cobwebs. [499] Here was the grand canvas he required. [506] He knew he could not force the orchard to yield its spirit; he had to display the buried merriment against the vapor for the trees to remember themselves. [534] Stepping into the clearing, Corin began to display his craft with theatrical exuberance, whipping ribbons of violet, gold, and vermilion from his radiant fingertips. [558] He did not merely splash color; he painted with light so deftly that the damp air reacted, treating the orchard like an enormous darkroom plate. [583] The exposed film of the mist seized the luminance, developing ancient silhouettes of laughing harvesters, swirling skirts, and spilling mugs in shimmering hues. [606] In the center of the glowing swirl, Corin slotted the fresco fragment into the empty eye of a dry stone fountain. [627] The fragment caught the refracted gleam, casting its old yellow smile across the weeping branches until the entire orchard sighed with luminous, pensive mirth. [651] The irony was exquisite: in searching for lost warmth, he had turned a cemetery of apples into an ephemeral carnival of phosphorescence. [673] Corin laughed aloud, a clean and unburdened sound that tasted of tart fruit and sunshine. [688] He had found it at last, tucked into the stubborn refusal of damp stone to forget a good song. [707] Then, as swiftly as a shadow dodges noon, the reverie folded shut. [719] Corin blinked, standing once more before the damp plaster in his waking studio, his brush still dripping vermilion.
