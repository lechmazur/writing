[118] Beside her elbows, potted ferns and climbing jasmine stirred against their trellises, their cellular murmurs vibrating directly inside her skull. [138] Ever since a childhood fall into an alchemical root cellar, she had been accidentally telepathic with plants, burdened with hearing their silent, frantic desires for lime, phosphorus, and moisture. [209] The violin was the foundational relic of the drowned Republic of Vael, which the Emperor claimed had surrendered its sovereignty voluntarily; if Clara registered it as legitimate imperial lineage, the crown's reign remained untouchable, but if she revealed the frozen ledger carved into its body, the fabrication would fracture the realm. [293] By reading the future in coffee stains, an outlawed divination passed down from her Vaelian kin, she deciphered the dark grit clinging to the porcelain curve. [638] Through their shared botanical link, she felt the cuttings greedily absorb the melted history, drawing the stolen song of Vael into their cambium and bark. [663] When the song finished, the violin was gone, dissolved into memory and mist, leaving only a pool of water that the saplings had consumed down to the last drop. [692] The phosphorus vial flickered once and went dark, its violet promise spent just as boots clattered on the iron staircase outside. [713] Clara tucked the wet cuttings inside her woolen cloak, feeling the cool pulse of the lost music humming against her ribs.
