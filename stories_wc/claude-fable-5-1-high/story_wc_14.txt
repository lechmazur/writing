Ines had gilded the edges of the town's ledgers for thirty years, laying gold leaf so thin that daylight came through it green. [23] When the valley was sold to the reservoir and the last trucks went down the switchbacks, she did not go with them. [45] She went up. [48]

Old Teodor's garden crowned the mountain: a labyrinth of bonsai forests on stone terraces, hundreds of trees no taller than her knee, their hedged paths turning back on themselves like an argument. [80] Teodor had gone down with the others, too old to argue with water. [93] He had left the potting shed unlocked and its single fluorescent tube burning off a solar battery, as if he expected someone to need the light. [119]

The trees were blooming when she arrived, which seemed indecent. [129] Quince the color of struck matches, crabapple foaming white, azaleas so dense the miniature forests looked like they were on fire and refusing to say so. [155] Every pot had a tag, and every tag was blank, the ink gone to weather. [170]

So she took out the battered notepad she had carried in her apron for a decade, its cardboard cover soft as cloth, its pages ruled for orders nobody would place again, and she began to give the trees their histories back. [211]

She invented them. [214] She knew she invented them. [219] That was not the same as lying. [226]

A stationer learns paper by touch: the tooth of laid stock, the slick of coated, the way a 1920s ledger sheet drinks ink differently than one from 1965. [254] Time has textures, and she had spent her life collecting them without ever admitting that was what she was doing. [274] Now, with the town silent below her, she admitted it. [284] She was earnest about it in the way of a student who knows she is behind. [300]

She ran her thumb along the fissured bark of a black pine and wrote: Planted 1897, the year the mill burned, by a woman who kept the seed in her mouth walking up from the ashes. [336] The bark felt like that. [341] A juniper with a deadwood spine felt like 1944, like a letter that arrived after the person it was for. [361] She wrote that down too. [366]

The fluorescent tube was the problem, and then the solution. [376] Its hum had no texture she could name; it was the sound of a time that had never quite happened, a 1970s that the valley had only heard about. [405] It kept her awake. [409]

So at dawn, when the battery was lowest and the hum thinnest, she sat in the shed doorway and charted it: a line across a notepad page that rose as the tube warmed, dipped when a cloud crossed the panel, steadied with the sun. [453] Seven mornings of it, and the lines began to look like tree rings. [466] Seven mornings, and she understood the hum was not a time but a way of hearing one. [483]

The shed's old glass was full of flaws, and when the first sun came through it beside the tube's pale steady burn, the two lights refracted across the terraces in bands, warm and cold, so that a single crabapple stood half in 1897 and half in the hum. [531] She laid a sheet of gold leaf on the page where the two bands met and the leaf took the light and let a little through, green, the way it always had. [563]

That was the texture she had been missing. [571] Not any year. [574] The seam between them. [578]

On the eighth morning she found the one tag Teodor had written in pencil, which weather cannot take. [596] A trident maple, small enough to cup. [603] The tag said: Cutting from the tree outside the stationer's shop, 1991. [615]

She sat with that for a long time. [623] Her father's shop. [626] Her tree, which was under the water now, or would be by autumn. [639] She had passed it every morning for thirty years and had never once thought to write down how its bark felt. [660]

She did not cry, though the restraint cost her something she could feel the price of. [676] Instead she turned to a clean page and wrote the maple's history honestly, which meant writing that she did not know it, only that it had been true without her. [706] Then she gilded the edge of that page alone, so it would catch the dawn before the others. [724]

The tube went on humming. [729] She went on charting it, one line each morning, because the labyrinth had more trees than she had years, and the notepad, battered as it was, still had pages. [758]
