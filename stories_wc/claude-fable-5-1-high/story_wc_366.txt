Tove Lind spoke to the sea more than to people, and the sea never asked her to repeat herself. [19] Twice a month she ran diesel and lamp parts out to Skarholm Rock, where the light had been automated since old Halvard died and nobody lived anymore. [46] Nobody lived there, yet every trip she found stones stacked on the landing step. [60] Three flat ones meant all well. [66] Two flat with a tall one on top meant something wanted. [77] Halvard had taught her the code when she was eight and her father ran the supply boat, because a keeper on a rock with no radio needed some way to speak to the mainland. [111] She never told anyone about the stones. [118] She noticed things and kept them, the way she noticed which fishermen had stopped sleeping and which wives had begun waiting on the quay too early. [144]

In her coat pocket rode a letter that had come to her without a name on it. [161] It was thick with other people's pages, each unsigned, each answering the same question: when did you last feel safe? [181] The chain had been moving along the coast for a year, from harbor to harbor, and the rule was simple: add your page, pass it on, tell no one. [210] Tove had carried it for three weeks and written nothing. [220]

Around her neck, in a stoppered vial the size of a thumb, she kept a piece of green sea glass. [240] Halvard had pressed it into her hand during a gale, up in the lantern room, while the beam swung over black water and her father's boat could not come. [269] Broken thing, he had said, that the sea made gentle. [279] She had been trying to bottle that hour ever since, and the vial was the closest she had managed. [298]

The night the lamp flickered out, she was moored in the lee of the rock, waiting for weather. [316] One sweep, a stutter, then nothing, and the dark came down like a lid. [330] Somewhere west the Brodersen brothers were running for home with a hold full of herring and a compass their father had never trusted. [353] She had seen their faces on the quay that morning and known they would go out anyway. [370]

The tower stairs were wet and the wind spoke in the iron. [382] Everyone on the coast said the lantern room was haunted, and Tove did not argue, because she had always felt someone standing behind her there, patient, the way Halvard used to stand. [414] When she pushed the hatch open, her torch found stones on the floor. [427] Not on the landing step this time, but here, beside the dead lamp, arranged in a pattern she had not seen in thirty years. [451] One stone laid on its side, pointing seaward. [459] Boat out there. [462]

Her hands did not shake; they had work. [470] The relay had failed, and she bypassed it the way Halvard had once shown her father, wire to wire, and the lamp caught and climbed to white. [497] The beam went out over the water and she watched it go, searching. [510] Far off, a small light answered, swung, steadied, and turned toward home. [522]

She sat down on the cold floor with her back against the pedestal, where a girl of eight had once sat, and felt the presence behind her settle, satisfied. [551] She understood then what she had been trying to keep in the vial. [564] Not the glass. [567] The feeling of a light kept on for you by someone who would not say your name, who did not need to. [589] It could not be corked. [594] It could only be passed. [599]

She took the letter from her pocket, and by the turning light she wrote her page, and it was the most words she had put together in years. [627] She wrote about a gale and a keeper and a piece of green glass, and about a stone on its side that had been set down for her by no one at all. [660] She did not sign it. [665] Then she unstoppered the vial, righted the fallen stone, laid a second beside it, and set the sea glass down as the third, flat and green and all well. [694]

In the morning she would put the letter in the Brodersens' mailbox with no return address, and they would read that someone had felt safe, and they would sit with that and add their page. [729] The lamp turned. [732] The ache of it, all that light spent on the dark and asking nothing back, was the closest thing to a keeper's voice she would ever hear again, and she found she could bear it. [767]
