Ilse Varan wore the saffron veil because the Order required it, and because she had not yet earned the right to be seen. [23] Historians of her rank were meant to look, not to know. [34] She knelt anyway at the threshold of the Caldrene Archive, the last standing structure of a civilization that had vanished without a single recorded grievance. [59] That absence was the forbidden question. [65] The Order taught that the Caldrene had simply ended, the way a song ends, and that asking why was a form of vandalism. [88] Ilse had stopped believing that the day her teacher walked into the Archive and returned unable to remember her own students, humming a Caldrene lullaby no living person had taught her. [119] Nostalgia, the Order said, was the Archive's only defense. [128] Ilse suspected it was the Archive's only illness. [136]

She entered at twilight, because the door would open at no other hour. [149] Inside, the air was thick with particles that caught the last light and turned it gold, drifting like pollen with intention. [170] The Caldrene texts called them atoms of nostalgia: the smallest indivisible units of longing, shed by every record in the vaults as it aged. [194] One landed on her wrist and she was suddenly seven, standing in her mother's kitchen, certain that nothing would ever be taken from her. [218] She brushed it away and her eyes stung. [226]

At the center of the first hall stood the bronze prism pagoda, seven tiers of faceted metal that drank the twilight through a skylight and split it into slow, wandering bands across the floor. [260] Where a band of light touched the golden atoms, they burned clean and fell as ordinary dust. [277] This was how the Archive purified itself, and it was failing. [288]

Ilse understood the mechanism within minutes, and understood in the same breath why her teacher had lost herself. [306] The pagoda's bands moved at a perfectly steady pace, one degree per heartbeat, but their direction was never the same twice. [327] Steadily unpredictable, the Caldrene engineers had written, as if it were a virtue. [340] It was. [342] Nostalgia lived on repetition; it fed on every path walked twice, every ritual, every habit of the hand. [360] Her teacher had come in as historians did, methodical, following the shelves in order, and the atoms had gathered on her like frost on a fence until there was more past in her than present. [395]

To reach the sealed vault behind the pagoda, Ilse would have to cross the hall without ever making a pattern. [415] She took off her sandals so her stride would have no rhythm. [427] She stepped left, then waited four breaths, then took three steps forward and one diagonally back, letting the wandering light find her rather than chasing it. [453] Each time a band swept over her, the gold on her skin flared and died, and each time a memory went with it. [476] Not her own memories, she realized, but borrowed ones: the kitchen that had never been her mother's, a harbor she had never seen, the taste of a fruit extinct for centuries. [507] The Caldrene had been leaking into her since the door. [517] She laughed, once, and the sound came out cracked. [526]

The vault door had no lock, only a lintel carved with a single sentence in the old script. [544] We preferred our memory of ourselves to ourselves, and so we stayed there. [557] That was all. [560] No plague, no war, no flood. [566] A whole people had walked into their own archive to remember what they had been, walked the same routes among the same shelves, and let the gold settle on them until they were content and gone. [602] The Order had forbidden this knowledge not because it was dangerous but because it was ordinary, and because the Order walked the same routes too. [627] Somewhere between the door and the lintel her innocence had ended, and she could not have marked the step. [646]

Ilse stood in the vault a long time, weeping in a way that felt indistinguishable from relief. [663] She had wanted an answer that would make the Caldrene strange, and instead she had found them familiar, which was so much worse and so much better. [690] Twilight was ending; the pagoda's bands were thinning to threads. [700] She crossed back the way she had not come, inventing every step, and at the threshold she unpinned the saffron veil and let it fall into the drifting gold. [729] The atoms took it hungrily; it was already the most nostalgic thing she owned. [743] Outside, the first stars were coming through, and she found she could not predict where she would go next, and that this was the cleanest she had ever felt. [772]
