The workshop had been sinking for three days, and Ilse still could not say whether that was a slow death or a long one. [24] Around her the great cogs turned in their brass housings, keeping the floating hull level on the acid sea, and every gear tooth screamed a little as the fumes ate it. [55] Below the main floor, the lower hold had flooded with lye brine from the ruptured neutralizing tanks, and in that murky water stood the founder, carved from stone four centuries ago, up to his chin. [90]

Ilse crouched at the hatch with Odo's key ring in her fist. [102] Seven keys, all to doors that no longer mattered, and one charm: a brass disc no larger than a coin, its face pitted like something left out in a hundred years of rain. [135]

"The pits are a map," Odo had told her, the week before his lungs gave out. [151] "Every splash the sea ever threw on the old man's face, the founder marked on the charm as well, so someone would know where the heart is." [178]

She had not believed him. [183] She did not, as a rule, believe herself either; twelve years an alchemist and she still tested every gold seam twice, certain the metal would prove to be brass and the mend a lie. [217] But the hull had three cracks she could not reach, and the sea was coming in, and doubt was a luxury for people with dry feet. [243]

She filled her lungs and went down. [250]

The brine stung her eyes but did not burn. [259] The statue's face swam up out of the green: a broad forehead, a broken nose, cheeks cratered where centuries of acid spray had found the stone. [285] She pressed the charm flat against his left cheek and turned it, feeling for the moment when pit met pit. [305] Nothing. [306] Her chest tightened and she rose. [312]

In the gasping dark between dives, because silence would have finished her, she talked to him. [328]

"Odo dropped a crucible my first winter," she said, water running from her hair. [342] "Shattered it on the deck and I thought he would throw the pieces to the sea, but he spent nine days fitting them back with gold in the cracks." [371]

She breathed, and dove, and tried the right cheek. [380] Nothing. [381] Up again. [383]

"He said the gold was not to hide the break." [393] "It was to say: this was broken, and somebody decided it was still worth holding." [408]

Dive. [409] The forehead. [411] Nothing. [412]

Each breath she surfaced with, she stitched another memory onto the last, the way the women on the salt barges quilted rags into blankets, no single scrap worth anything alone. [442] The first time acid took the skin off her knuckles. [452] The night the western gear train seized and Odo sang to it, filthy songs, until it turned. [469] The morning she found him cold and could not decide, for a full hour, whether she was allowed to grieve someone who had never once called her anything but "apprentice." [499]

She did not know why she was telling a statue these things. [511] Perhaps because if the hull went under tonight, she wanted them said aloud once, so that they had happened somewhere outside her skull. [534] Perhaps that was all meaning ever was: a seam of gold laid across the edge of nothing, by someone too stubborn to let the pieces go. [560]

The seventh dive, she tried the throat. [567]

The charm caught. [570] Every pit on the brass slid into a pit in the stone, a perfect negative, and at the center one hollow went deeper than the rest. [596] Her lungs burned. [599] She fumbled the smallest key from the ring, pushed it through the charm into the founder's throat, and turned. [618]

The statue's chest swung open on a hinge that had waited four hundred years. [632] Inside sat a gear, the master gear, the one the whole floating workshop had been built to carry, cracked clean through and mended, long ago, with a thick seam of gold. [663] Beside it, a second crack had opened, fresh and dark. [673]

She surfaced screaming for air and laughing, both at once. [683] The founder had not left a way to stop the sinking. [694] He had left a broken thing, and the invitation to mend it again. [707]

Ilse mixed lacquer and gold dust on the tilting floor. [717] The gold might be gold. [722] The gear might hold long enough to drive the pumps, or it might not. [736] She was, as ever, uncertain. [741] But she took one more breath, and held it, and went down to lay her seam beside the old one, so that whoever found the founder next would read two lines of gold in his heart and know that twice, at least, someone had decided.
