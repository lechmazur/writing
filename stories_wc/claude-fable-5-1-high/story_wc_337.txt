Odile Marrow repaired broken masks for a village that no longer believed in them. [14] The masks were woven, not carved: each one a face of knotted twine laid over a driftwood frame, worn by widows on the night a boat failed to return. [43] A knot, the old makers said, was a hush tied tight. [54] Every time a name went unspoken at a graveside, a maker tied a knot for it, and the masks cut from that twine accumulated silence the way a hull accumulates barnacles. [85]

She told none of this to Tamsin until the night they climbed to the prism sanctuary. [101]

The sanctuary stood on the headland, a chapel whose walls were slabs of cut glass fitted edge to edge. [120] By day it threw rainbows over the pews. [128] By night, under starlight, it did something stranger: it split the faint light into threads so thin they seemed to lie across the floor like the warp of an unfinished loom. [159] Odile set her lantern down unlit. [165] She did not want to see clearly. [172] She only wanted to see truly. [178]

"The masks have stopped holding," she said. [185] "You have seen it." [189] Tamsin nodded. [191] He had seen it: the widows' faces of twine unraveling in the middle of the vigil, the cords loosening as if something inside were trying to breathe. [218] He had also seen what came after, the doors rattling in houses where no wind blew, nets on the drying racks knotting themselves overnight into shapes like mouths. [246]

"They are restless because they were never named," Odile said. [256] "Four hundred years of drownings, and the makers tied every one of them shut." [270] She knelt and pulled back a flagstone that had never quite fit. [282] Beneath it, in a hollow lined with salt-crusted oilcloth, lay a fisherman's net so old its cords had gone the color of bone. [305] "This is the first net," she said. [312] "Every mask since has been cut from its pattern." [321] "We are going to exhume what it swallowed." [329]

She drew a tapestry needle from her sleeve, blunt-tipped and long, meant for working thread through thread without piercing it. [349] A restorer's tool, not a fisherman's. [355] Tamsin understood then that this was the secret she had carried up the hill: the masks could not be repaired, only undone, and the undoing had to be done by someone who knew how they were made. [392]

"Hold the edge taut," she said. [398] He did. [400] She slid the needle into the first knot and worked it loose. [412] Nothing sounded. [414] Then something did, faint as breath on glass: a name, spoken in a voice neither of them owned. [432] The prism walls caught it and drew it out into light, one pale thread lying down among the others on the floor. [454]

"Interlaced hush," Odile said. [458] "That is what the old makers called it." [466] "Not one silence but hundreds, woven over each other until no single thread could be pulled without the others tightening." [486] "So you loosen them all at once," Tamsin said. [495] "Slowly." [496] "In order." [498]

They worked through the night. [503] Each knot she freed gave up a name and a thread of starlight, and the floor of the sanctuary filled with a faint, shifting weave that was almost a face and then almost another. [537] Tamsin's hands began to ache. [542] He wanted to stop, and could not have said why the wanting frightened him more than the voices did. [561] Odile's expression did not change. [566] She had the look of a woman reading a ledger of debts she had known about for years. [584]

Near dawn, with a dozen knots left, she paused. [593] "My grandmother tied the last one," she said. [601] "For my grandfather." [604] "She could not bear to say his name, so she made a mask instead, and I learned the craft from her, and I have spent my life mending what she should have spoken." [637] That was the rest of the secret, and Tamsin saw that she had needed the whole night to reach it, that the sharing had to be paced to the unraveling or it would not hold. [672]

She worked the needle into the final knot. [680] The name came, and it was one Tamsin had heard her murmur over her work a hundred times without knowing whose it was. [703] The thread of light lay down. [709] The floor went still. [713] Outside, the racks of nets on the shore hung slack and ordinary in the first gray light, and nothing in the village rattled. [736]

Odile folded the empty cords and set them back beneath the flagstone. [748] "Now the masks can break," she said. [755] "Now they are only masks." [760]
