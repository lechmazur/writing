The pine had been complaining about the kite for three days before Ilse understood what it meant. [17] Not-bird, it kept insisting, paper and salt and a girl's hands, snagged where the steam curls. [33] She had never asked to hear trees. [40] The gift, if it was one, had come from the eleven months she spent hiding in the sulfur bogs above Tephra Basin, breathing the spores of the hot-spring mosses while the Cradle Ministry's hounds quartered the slopes for the woman who had burned their vaults. [85] She came out grey-haired and able to hear moss grumble about the heat. [98]

Now the Ministry was ash under the new lava field, the eruption having finished what her insurgency started, and the basin was rebuilding. [121] Scaffolds ringed the terraced pools. [126] The survivors wanted a founding child for the reconstruction, the way every generation of the basin had one, and they knew Ilse held the last amber. [152]

It sat on her windowsill: a fist of golden resin with an embryo curled inside, no larger than a bean, its destiny already written into it in the Ministry's thread-script. [182] Governor, the thread said, or Tither, or Guard. [190] She had never been able to read which. [198]

The tradition was old and not entirely the Ministry's; the basin had unsealed children in the springs for centuries, letting the geothermal heat soften the amber, naming them in the steam. [229] The Ministry had only added the implant, the thread of fate that told a child what it was before it drew breath. [251] Ilse did not want to end the unsealing. [259] She had spent her life breaking things and had learned that a broken thing leaves a hole the same shape as itself. [281] She wanted to keep the rite and change what it did. [292]

But the amber would not open without its key, and the key had died with her daughter. [309] Mara had been a cipher clerk in the vaults, the insider who passed her mother the floor plans. [327] She had also stolen this one embryo, the last the Ministry sealed, and vanished the week the mountain woke. [346]

A girl's hands, the pine said again, and Ilse put down her tea. [359]

The tree stood at the lip of the highest pool, where the vents breathed hardest and the reconstruction crews had not yet reached. [382] Twenty feet up, half-cooked by steam, hung a kite: red silk gone brown, its long tail knotted with strips of oilcloth. [403] Ilse was seventy-one and climbed anyway, the pine guiding her feet to the branches that would hold. [420]

The strips were written on, each one crowded with Ministry thread-script, the looping notation that Ilse had spent years learning to sabotage but never to read. [446] Mara had flown it here, she understood, the night before the eruption, when the Ministry watched every road and no one watched the sky. [470] She had let the string go into the pine, trusting that her mother always came back to the springs. [489]

Decoding took eleven nights. [493] The script was cryptic even by Ministry standards, because Mara had keyed it to the only cipher she and her mother shared: the order of the vents. [520] Each strip corresponded to a pool, and the pools erupted in a sequence every child of the basin learned before they learned letters. [543] Read in that order, the loops resolved. [550] The first half was the unsealing key, the resonance the amber wanted. [562] The second half was something the Ministry had never written down: how to draw the destiny thread out of an embryo without killing it. [586] At the bottom of the last strip, in plain script, Mara had written: She does not have to be anything. [606]

On the morning of the founding, the basin gathered on the new scaffolds around the highest pool. [623] Ilse waded in to her waist, the water hot enough to sting, and lowered the amber. [639] She spoke the key in the order of the vents, and the resin went soft as honey and slid away. [659] Then, while the crowd waited for her to read the child's fate aloud as every elder before her had, she pinched the destiny thread between two fingernails and drew it out, a filament of gold that dissolved in the mineral water. [700]

"She was going to be a Governor," Ilse said, because she had finally read it. [715] "Now she is going to be asked." [722]

Someone protested that this was not how it was done. [732]

"No," Ilse agreed. [735] "It is how it is done now." [742]

Above the pool the pine said nothing about kites anymore. [752] It said, in the way of trees, that the girl would grow toward whatever light she chose, and that this was, after all, the only tradition it had ever understood. [782]
