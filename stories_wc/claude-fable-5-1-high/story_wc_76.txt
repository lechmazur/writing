Every clock Maren Holt had ever built ran counterclockwise, and every one of them kept perfect time. [17] The trick was not in the gears but in the mind that read them: you had to unlearn the direction you were told was inevitable. [42] She had been unlearning it since she was nine, when her mother rowed out to the lighthouse on Skerrick Rock and did not row back. [67] Her grandmother had gone the same way, at the same age, forty-one, in the same month. [83] Maren turned forty-one in eleven days. [89]

She went out under the slow exhale of low tide, when the sea drew back from the island like breath held too long and the black rocks stood bare and streaming. [120] Only at low tide could you see the hunger stone. [130] It sat at the lighthouse's foot, a slab of granite older than the tower, and the words cut into it had been recut by generations of hands: WHEN I AM SEEN, THE LIGHT IS OWED. [165] Beneath the words, a column of dates. [172] Her mother's was the last. [177] There was room for one more. [183]

The lamp room was smaller than she had imagined and colder than she had feared. [198] No wick, no oil, no mirror, only a great lens of clouded crystal on an iron spindle, and on the sill beneath it, a yoyo. [223] She almost laughed. [226] It was carved from something pale and dense, whalebone perhaps, two discs joined by a brass axle, wound with a cord gone grey with salt. [248] On the rim, in her mother's hand, a scratched note: IT ALWAYS COMES BACK. [262]

Maren was neatly inventive by trade, the kind of woman who kept her screws sorted by thread pitch and could hear a bent tooth in a wheel train from across a room. [294] She did not throw the yoyo. [300] She took it apart. [304]

The cord, unwound fully, was not cord but a braided fuse of copper hair and something that glittered. [322] The axle was hollow, threaded to receive the lens's iron spindle. [333] And the discs, when she held them to the grey window, were not opaque at all; light lay inside them the way heat lies inside a banked coal, waiting. [362] Latent radiance. [364] The lamp had never burned oil. [370] It had burned keepers. [374]

She understood the mechanism the way she understood any mechanism, by running it backward in her head. [391] A keeper climbed the stair, seated the yoyo on the spindle, wound the fuse tight, and threw. [408] The discs spun down, the fuse paid out, the fuse drew what it needed from the hand that held it, and the discs came back up blazing. [435] It always comes back. [439] The keeper did not. [443] Forty years of light for one life, the ships kept off the rocks, the date cut in the stone, and then a daughter grew into the age and felt the pull like a tide in the blood. [480] That was the design. [484] It was tidy. [487] She could admire it and still refuse it. [495]

Every clock she had ever made ran the other way. [505] So she rewound the fuse against its lay, counterclockwise, each turn fighting her, the braid creaking like a rope that did not want to learn. [530] She seated the yoyo on the spindle. [537] Outside, the tide had turned; she could hear the first slap of water returning to the hunger stone, covering the dates, covering the empty space. [562] If she was going to initiate anything, it had to be now, while the stone could still see her. [581]

She threw. [583]

The discs dropped, spinning the wrong way, and the fuse ran out through her fingers and took nothing, because a fuse wound backward does not draw, it gives. [611] Forty years of hoarded light, her mother's, her grandmother's, every keeper's back to the first, came up the cord and into the bone and out through the clouded lens in a single soundless detonation of white. [647] The whole store of it. [652] Not rationed across decades but spent in one breath, a beacon so fierce that the mainland would speak of it for a hundred years. [676] Then the lens went dark, and cracked, and would never light again. [688]

The yoyo did not come back. [694] It lay at the end of its slack cord, two dull discs of bone, finished. [709] Maren stood in the dark with her hands whole and her heart going like a mainspring let loose. [727] There would be no light on Skerrick Rock now, not ever; ships would have to find their own way, as she had. [749] Her mother's date would be the last one cut. [758] Below the tower the sea closed over the hunger stone, and Maren, forty-one in eleven days and owed nothing, began the long climb down toward a boat she fully intended to row home. [791]
