After the last forest burned, the dunes learned to glow. [10] Nobody knew what fed on the ash, only that it bloomed in pale blue ridges after dark and died back by noon, so the freight crawlers ran at night. [39]

Ines Varga rode the last crawler because she did not dream. [50] A fever at six had burned that part of her clean, the way the forests went, and left nothing behind but sleep like a shut drawer. [76] The Archive Board had hired her for exactly that. [85] "It talks to sleepers," the recruiter had said, not meeting her eye. [97] "You will be very boring to it." [104]

The cargo was the Divergent Archive, twelve steel cabinets bolted to the deck, and it did not hold seeds so much as arguments about seeds. [129] When the botanists had realized they could not save every forest, they had refused to choose, and instead sealed each sample unresolved, a smear of every oak it might have been, every pine, every blight and mast year, held in a cold that kept the wave functions from settling. [178] Open a drawer and you would collapse a whole possible woodland into one seed, one future, and the others would be gone. [200] So the drawers stayed shut, and the seeds stayed occasionally everywhere, which the manifest listed as a storage condition. [219]

Bolted above the cabinets was a chess clock, both faces frozen at midnight. [232] The story went that the Board had used it to ration debate, one side's minutes against the other's, and had stopped it the night the last forest went up, when nobody could bear to be the one whose time ran out. [273] Ines thought it was a fair emblem for the whole enterprise: two players, no move, midnight forever. [290]

The Archive did not talk to her. [297] It hummed. [299] On the fourth night, crossing a dune in full bloom, blue light pouring up the crawler's flank like water running the wrong way, she felt the cabinets vibrate at a pitch just under the engine's, and she caught herself humming back. [340] It was her mother's lullaby, or half of it. [349] The fever had taken the rest. [355] Hush now, the hush the hush, and then a gap where the words had been, and then something about a window. [376]

The hum leaned toward her. [381] She stopped, and it fell back into noise. [389]

She understood then what the sleepers had been hearing, and why the Board had feared it. [405] The seeds were not luring anyone. [411] They were unresolved, and unresolved things drift toward any steady rhythm, the way a held note pulls a room into tune. [432] A dreamer's mind was a rhythm. [438] So was a broken song. [443] She could charm them. [447] Not command; charm, the way you coax a shy animal, by being somewhere it wants to go. [464]

She tested it the next night, in the dark hollow between two blooming ridges where the light did not reach. [484] She opened one drawer a finger's width, hummed the half a lullaby, and felt the smear of possible trees gather toward the tune, not into one seed but into a leaning, a preference, a forest that liked the sound of a window and a hush. [529] Then she shut it and shook for an hour. [538]

The mountain vault lay six nights ahead. [545] There the Archive would be lowered into deeper cold and the clock would stay at midnight and nothing, ever, would be allowed to become anything. [570] Ines looked at the dune. [575] The bloom was a kind of promise, she thought, ash proving it could still be eaten by something living, but the promise stopped at the edges, and the unlit corners of the desert stayed unlit. [610] Nobody was tending them. [614] Nobody had a rhythm to spare. [620]

She did not dream, and so she had no future to be afraid of losing; she only had the shut drawer of her own sleep, and the half a song, and a job she was about to do badly on purpose. [661]

Each night after, at the darkest point of the route, she stopped the crawler, opened one drawer a finger's width, and hummed. [683] Hush now, the hush the hush. [689] A gap. [691] Something about a window. [695] When the leaning grew strong enough that one seed fell out of everywhere and into her palm, small and specific and no longer arguable, she knelt in the cold sand beyond the glow and pressed it in. [732] A spark, she called each one, though nothing lit. [741] Sparks were what you kept in the corners against a long dark. [753]

On the last night before the mountain, she climbed onto the cabinets and pressed the button on the chess clock, and one face began to tick. [779] Somebody's move, she thought. [783] Hush now. [785]
