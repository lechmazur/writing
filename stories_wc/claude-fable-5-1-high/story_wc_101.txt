The sanctions had been in place for eleven months when the flour ran out at the Kessler bakery, and Zora Anselm understood that the ridge would have to feed itself. [30] She had led the exiles of Varne for twenty years, ever since the first families climbed the hill road with suitcases and nothing else, and she knew their pride the way she knew the cracks in her own kitchen table. [70] No one would take charity from a neighbor's hand. [79] So the hand had to disappear. [85]

The terra-cotta arbor on the ridge had been built by the old men the first summer, clay tiles fired in a borrowed kiln, grapevines that never quite fruited in this colder soil. [117] From its center beam hung an antique silver bell, small as a fist, carried out of Varne in the lining of Zora's coat. [140] It had once called her grandmother's village to prayer. [149] Now it would call for something else. [156]

She began with the young ones, because the young ones had cars and restlessness and no memory of Varne to make them ashamed. [179] Dario, nineteen, born in the hospital down in the valley, listened to her with his arms crossed. [196] "The old ones don't even talk to us," he said. [206]

"Then you will learn where they live," Zora said, "and what they eat, and you will never tell them it was you." [228]

The donations came anonymously, as she had asked. [236] A sack of lentils left under the arbor before dawn. [246] Jars of preserved plums with no label. [253] An envelope of valley currency, folded into the bell's mouth so it would not ring. [268] Zora never asked who. [272] She suspected the widow Ferenc, who had a pension and no children; she suspected the Kesslers themselves, who were not exiles at all but had grown up beside them. [301] Suspicion was not knowledge, and she let it stay that way. [312]

The runs happened at midnight. [317] The ridge road was quiet then in a way that seemed louder than daytime, every gravel crunch enormous, every closing car door a small announcement to the dark. [345] Dario and two cousins loaded boxes under the arbor by phone light, Zora dividing portions with the arithmetic of someone who had rationed before. [369] Then they went down the hill on foot, boxes on hips, and set them on doorsteps without knocking. [387] When the last box was placed, Zora would climb back to the arbor and ring the bell once. [405] One note, thin and silver, traveling down through the sleeping streets. [416] It meant: something is at your door, and you are not alone. [428]

The elders never spoke of it. [434] But old Mr. Anselm, her own father-in-law, began leaving his porch light on at midnight. [449] Mrs. Ferenc left a thermos of coffee beside her empty box, and the boys drank it standing in the road. [469]

Zora was gently persistent about the silence. [476] When Dario wanted to knock, to see a face, she shook her head. [489] "Not yet," she said, every time, the same two words, until he stopped asking and started listening for the porch lights instead. [511]

In the fourth month, the valley council sent a letter asking her to declare the source of the goods moving through the exile quarter. [535] Sanctions had rules; undeclared supplies invited questions. [542] She read the letter twice under the arbor, the bell above her holding its one note in reserve. [560] She could not declare what she did not know. [569] What she could declare was different. [575]

On Sunday she gathered everyone at the ridge, elders on the low clay wall, young ones standing behind them, the first time in years the two had shared the same shade. [606] She held up the council's letter. [612] "They want a name," she said. [618] "I am giving them this one: the arbor." [626] "I declare it a common kitchen, registered to no household, belonging to all of them." [641] "Whoever leaves food here leaves it to the ridge." [650] "Whoever carries it down carries it for the ridge." [659] "No one gives; no one receives; the hill feeds itself." [669]

Nobody clapped. [671] The quiet after her words had weight, the kind that fills a space rather than empties it. [688] Then old Mr. Anselm rose from the wall, walked to Dario, and held out a folded paper: a list, in his shaking hand, of which families needed salt and which needed oil. [720] He did not say he had known. [727] He did not have to. [732]

That night the runs went out with an old man riding in the passenger seat, pointing at doors. [750] And when Zora rang the bell, the note went down the hill and did not come back alone; from three porches, then five, small bells answered, a spoon against a glass, a bicycle bell, a scattered chime rising until the whole ridge was quietly, unmistakably awake. [796]
