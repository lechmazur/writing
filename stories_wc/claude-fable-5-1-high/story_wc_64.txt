Tamsin Reed whistled while she deadheaded the roses on the Halloway plot, because a cemetery, she had long ago decided, was simply a garden with more patient tenants. [28] Only Ansel Marrow's absence dulled her cheer. [35] For six years the old widower had arrived at dusk to sit beside his wife's stone, and then to follow Tamsin across the fence to the silo. [62] The silo was his, cobalt paint peeling off its ribs, roof long gone, so that the two of them played chess at a card table with the whole night sky poured in above them. [96] He had died on a Tuesday with a game unfinished and four minutes left on his clock. [113]

The lawyer's parcel held two things: an antique sovereign case, brass worn to the color of honey, and a tapestry no bigger than a dish towel, woven by Ansel's wife Ilse forty years before. [147] The case held a single gold coin and no note. [157] Tamsin puzzled over them for a week, blithe by day among her roses, baffled by night. [173] A man did not leave his wife's weaving to a chess partner without meaning something, and she wanted to know the shape of that meaning, the outline of what he had intended and never said. [208]

So on the seventh night she crossed the fence to the silo with the case in one pocket, the tapestry in the other, and a lantern hooked on her belt. [238] Inside, the board still stood as they had left it, his king hemmed in, her knight waiting, the chess clock stopped on his side at four minutes. [265] The silo swallowed sound strangely; Ansel had called it the well of uncharted echoes, because a word spoken there came back altered, from a direction no one could predict. [294]

She sat in her chair, set the lantern down, and pressed the button to start his clock. [311] It seemed the only decent way to finish. [319] The ticking climbed the cobalt walls and came back down to her as something like rain. [335] Above, the stars sat in the round mouth of the silo like coins in a dish. [351] The dark was not frightening; it was plush, close, the velvet of a jeweler's tray, a gloom you could rest your hands on. [374]

When the flag dropped and the clock ran down, Tamsin let out a breath and unfolded the tapestry across the board. [395] The center showed a garden, nothing remarkable, but the border was a mess of frayed threads, and Ansel had been a tidy man who would have trimmed them unless he wanted them left. [428] She bent close with the lantern. [434] The loose ends were not random. [440] They came in clusters, knotted at the base, one to eight threads each, in only two colors, cobalt and gold. [460]

Ilse had taught Ansel chess, he once said, and notation too, so that even her shopping lists came in files and ranks. [482] Cobalt for the file, gold for the rank. [490] She began to untangle them, cluster by cluster, teasing each knot apart with a rose thorn from her pocket, and laid the freed threads on the squares they named, tenderly organized, a pawn's width apart. [525]

When she stood back with the lantern raised, the threads on the board made a shape: two long rectangles side by side, ringed by a border one square wide, with a gap at the foot like a gate. [563] She knew those proportions the way she knew her own hands. [574] It was the Marrow plot: Ilse's grave, and the raw earth beside it where Ansel had gone down last week, and around them both the strip of ground he had asked her, every spring for six years, to leave bare. [614] She had thought it grief, that bareness. [621] It had been a plan. [626] Ilse had woven the garden in the center of the tapestry, and its plot in the border, forty years ago, for a bed she never lived to dig, and Ansel had waited to lie down in it before he let anyone plant it. [669]

Tamsin took out the sovereign case and turned the coin in the lantern light. [683] The rest of Ilse's dowry had gone to doctors, he had told her once, then changed the subject. [701] One coin, kept back. [705] Enough for seed, and bulbs, and a season of her hands. [716]

"I see it," she said aloud, and the silo took the words up its cobalt throat and gave them back from somewhere behind her, slower and lower, so that for a moment it was not her voice at all. [755] She reached over and moved her knight, finishing the game, then gathered the threads and went out under the stars to wait for the seed shop to open. [783]
