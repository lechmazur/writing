Mara had not left the glade in nineteen years, and the glade had noticed. [13] Her heels had gone first, splitting like bark and sending pale threads into the soil; now the roots reached her knees, and she could turn but not step. [41] From the center of the hedge maze she directed the shears of hired boys by shouting, and she dreamed each new corridor a season before it was cut. [69] The maze had one purpose, and it was not a garden's purpose. [81] It was a promise she and her sister Wren had cut into their palms under a total eclipse, when they were eleven and the sky went cobalt at noon. [110] Mara dreamed of hedges; Wren dreamed of the sea. [119] At the next eclipse, thirty-one years on, they would meet where those dreams crossed, and Mara would have the maze ready to hold that crossing. [144] Wren left for the coast the following spring and did not come back. [157] Mara stayed, and staying became rooting, and rooting became the maze. [168]

The eclipse was still twelve years off when the boy walked into the glade at dusk. [184] He could not have been more than ten. [192] The bluebells that carpeted the center were closing, and the last light coming over the yew walls turned everything the deep cobalt she remembered from the day of the promise, so that for a moment Mara thought the sky had broken its schedule. [235]

"Nobody solves it," she said. [240]

"I didn't solve it," the boy said. [247] "I followed the one that matched." [253]

He held up a topiary clipping, a yew sprig no longer than his forearm, its base wrapped in wet cloth. [273] It had been trained in a tight spiral, the way Mara trained her corners, the way nobody else on earth trained yew. [295]

"My mother grew a maze out of this," he said. [305] "On the cliffs. [308] She said she took a cutting from yours before she left, and she kept it alive in a jar on every boat." [330]

Mara's roots tightened in the soil. [336] "Said." [337]

"She died in March." [341] He said it the way children say hard things, fast, looking at the object in his hands rather than the person. [362] "She said if anything happened I should bring this here before the eclipse, because you'd be waiting for it and by then you'd be all tree." [389]

So the future had come early, and it had come wrong, and it had come as a boy holding a jar-raised branch. [411] Mara's heart sank for Wren and leapt because Wren had kept the covenant after all, only somewhere Mara could not see. [432]

The boy was looking at her legs now, at the bark climbing past her knees, with a curiosity that was not rude because it was so plainly working toward something. [462] "Does it hurt where it split?" [468]

"It scarred. [470] Scars don't hurt." [473]

"Mum said that." [476] He turned the clipping so she could see its base, where a knuckle of pale callus marked the cut his mother had made. [499] Then he opened his own left hand, and there was the line, the same line Mara carried, thin and white across the heel of the palm. [525] "She did it when I was eight," he said. [534] "She said two of us only made a line, and lines can't cross by themselves." [549]

Mara heeded that the way she heeded dreams, which is to say she did not argue with it; she asked for his hand. [572] She pressed her scar to the callus of the clipping, and he pressed his scar to the other side, so that the three healed wounds met in a stack. [601]

The vision came the way her dreams came, without asking. [611] Salt wind. [613] A maze of yew on a headland, its corridors opening not onto walls but onto air and gray water, so that every wrong turn was a view. [640] And at its center, where Wren had stood every dusk for eighteen years, a ring of bluebells going cobalt in the last light, exactly here, exactly this. [667] Two mazes, one on a cliff and one on a lawn, had been growing toward the same room. [685]

Mara laughed, and it came out shaking. [692] "Plant it," she said. [696] "Here, between my feet, where I can't." [703]

The boy knelt in the bluebells and dug with his hands, purposeful, unhurried, glancing up now and then to ask how deep, how firm, whether yew liked its roots crowded. [733]

"It likes company," Mara said. [738]

When the clipping stood in the earth she felt it at once, a small new pressure against her own roots, a stranger's dream pressing into hers. [764] The eclipse would come in twelve years and find her fully tree. [776] The covenant had outgrown its eclipse; it had a boy asking, already, which corridor they should cut next. [794]
