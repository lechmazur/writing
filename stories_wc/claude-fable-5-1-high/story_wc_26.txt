The library bloomed at dusk, which was why Ines worked then. [11] Vines of paper-white jasmine had grown through the stacks since the seeding, and the flowers opened only when the sun dropped below the cloud floor and the whole archive turned the color of a held breath. [47] She was a noticer by title, sharp-eyed and paid to see what the daytime cataloguers missed. [63] Since dreams had become shareable, there was less to miss. [73] People uploaded their sleep each morning, and the strange private wishes that had once made a face worth watching were now public, smoothed by a million viewings into the same few shapes. [105] Only the old silent footage still surprised her. [113]

Tonight's donation had arrived in a cedar box: two reels, a river stone, and no note. [129] The stone was gray, flat, worn to the softness of a thumb. [141] She set it beside the viewer and threaded the first reel. [152]

A boy, perhaps nine, stood on a riverbank in light that had been afternoon eighty years ago. [169] He crouched, chose a stone, and skipped it. [177] Four hops. [179] He turned to the camera with his whole face open, and his shadow turned with him, exactly, the way shadows did. [200]

The second reel was the same bank, the same bend of water, decades later. [214] The trees had thickened. [218] An old man stood where the boy had stood, holding a stone he did not throw. [234]

Ines ran the reels side by side, frame against frame, the way she had been trained to compare things that could not speak for themselves. [259] It took her eleven minutes to see it. [267]

The old man's shadow was not standing still. [275] While he held the stone at his chest, the dark shape at his feet crouched. [290] It drew back an arm. [295] It threw, softly deliberate, and its wrist snapped forward with the same small flourish the boy's had, and then it straightened and waited, as if counting hops on a river only it could see. [329]

She stopped the frame and pressed her fingers to the glass. [340]

A shadow unbound. [343] She had read about them in the early dream-sharing papers, before the phenomenon was dismissed as artifact. [360] When a person gave away every dream they had, the theory went, whatever wish they had kept back from childhood had nowhere left to live inside them. [387] So it moved into the shadow, and the shadow began, very slowly, to metamorphose into the person they had meant to become. [409]

The man had never thrown his last stone. [417] He had carried it home, and something in him had kept throwing it anyway. [431]

Ines picked up the river stone from beside the viewer. [441] It was warm, which it should not have been. [450]

The jasmine had opened fully now, and the stacks glowed with a soft interior light, petals drifting down onto the reading tables like slow weather. [475] Below the windows the clouds lay flat and pink, hiding the cities where everyone was already asleep and already sharing. [495] She thought of her own dreams, all of them uploaded, all of them flattened into the common shapes. [513] She looked down. [516] Her shadow lay on the floor in the shape of a woman looking down, obedient, ordinary. [532] She had kept nothing back. [537] There was nothing in her that wanted anything badly enough to leave her. [550]

The regret came gently, the way the light did, and it did not make her want to weep so much as to be careful. [574]

The archive's policy was to digitize, annotate, and shelve. [583] Digitizing would run the frames through the same compression that had smoothed a billion dreams. [598] The shadow's small flourish would be corrected as noise. [607]

Ines wrote a false catalogue number on the cedar box, the kind that sent items to the uncatalogued room where the jasmine grew thickest and nobody went. [634] She was not preserving the man, or even his dream. [644] She was preserving its shape: a crouch, a snapped wrist, a figure waiting to count. [659] Somebody, someday, might need to know what hope had looked like before it was shared into nothing. [676]

Before she closed the box, she held the river stone a moment longer. [689] Then, softly, deliberately, she did not put it back. [698] She slid it into her coat pocket, against her hip, where it could stay warm and unthrown. [715] It was a small thing to keep back. [723] But when she stood and the petal-light fell across the floor, she watched her shadow for a long time, sharp-eyed, hoping to catch it doing something she had not told it to do. [756]
