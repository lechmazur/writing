The demolition crew would come at first light, and the great hollow ash had already been emptied of everything but the two of them. [24] Wren stood inside the trunk with her palms flat against the wall, feeling the grain the scholars' guild had smoothed four centuries ago, when they hollowed the living tree into a library. [56] Above her, the ceiling still held its painted sky, a vault of gilt stars gone brown with smoke. [74] The tree was dead now, rotten through the heartwood, and the city had condemned it. [89]

"You understand what I am asking," said Halloran, the last keeper. [100]

She did not look at his face. [107] There was no point; his face would slide off her memory the moment she turned away, as every face had since she was born. [131] She knew him by his cough, his ink-stained left cuff, the way he stood with his weight on one heel. [151] It was a strange trade for a woman like her, painting portraits, and yet her sitters swore no one had ever caught them so exactly. [176] She did not see a face; she measured one. [185] The distance from tear duct to nostril, the exact curve where the lip met the philtrum, each subdivided again and again until the likeness assembled itself out of pure geometry. [215]

"There is no portrait of the founder," Halloran said. [224] "There is only this." [228]

He drew a thick volume from the one shelf still standing, a treatise on optics whose pages had been cut away into a hidden compartment. [253] Inside the book safe, on a bed of velvet worn to thread, lay a single coin. [269]

"Struck in his lifetime," Halloran said. [275] "The guild wants him on the wall of the new hall, and there is no one else who works the way you do." [298]

Wren took the coin. [302] It was warm from the velvet and nearly smooth, its profile rubbed to a ghost by four hundred years of pockets. [323] She sat on the floor beneath the painted stars, set her calipers and her lamp beside her, and began. [342]

The hush of the place settled over her. [350] Outside, the crew's machines waited in the dark like patient animals, and the whole city seemed to be holding its breath around this one dying tree. [376] She read the coin with her fingertip first, then with the caliper's needle, tracing the faint indentations that were all that remained of a brow, a jaw, an eye. [405] Each depression she plotted on paper, then divided, then divided again, until her sheet was a fractal lattice of marks finer than the coin's own grain. [431] This was how she had always crafted a face: from the outside in, from the measurable toward the unknowable. [450]

For three hours she worked in the stillness, and for three hours the lattice grew more precise and the face did not come. [473]

She understood, slowly, what she was looking at. [481] The indentations were too shallow. [486] Below a certain depth the metal simply held no information, and no amount of subdivision could conjure what time had erased. [507] She had always believed that if she measured finely enough, any face would surrender to her. [523] Here was the edge of that belief, hard as the coin's rim. [535]

She set down the calipers and looked up. [543] The painted sky above her had been the founder's one recorded wish, Halloran had told her once: that a scholar reading inside a tree should never feel shut in, that the walls should mirror the sky. [579] She looked back at her lattice of marks, the hundreds of points where the coin had given up its faint, insufficient depths. [601] They were not a face. [606] Scattered across the paper, they looked like nothing so much as stars. [618]

When Halloran returned, grey light was leaking through the door and the machines outside had begun to tick and settle. [638] She held up the sheet. [643]

"I cannot give you his face," she said. [651] "It is gone, and I have found where my measuring ends." [662]

"Then we have nothing." [666]

"You have this." [669] She tilted the paper toward the ceiling so that he could see how the marks fell, how they clustered and thinned in the same slow drift as the smoke-browned constellations overhead. [700] "Every mark is true." [704] "Every one is exactly where the coin put it." [713] "Paint this on the ceiling of your new hall, and he will be there the only way he can be now, not as a face but as the thing he wanted the walls to hold." [748]

Halloran was quiet for a long moment. [755] She could not read his expression, and for once she did not need to. [769] He took the sheet with his ink-stained hand, and together they walked out of the tree into the last stillness before the engines started. [793]
