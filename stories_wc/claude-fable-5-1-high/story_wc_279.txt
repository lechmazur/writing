The grotto took every sound and handed it back to a stranger. [12] That was why the town had chosen it for the unlearning. [23] Pell stood at the mouth where the sea mist thickened to lilac, chalk dust on her knuckles from habit, though nobody had asked for a drawing. [49] In her pocket she carried a flake of tile from the old whispering gallery, the domed hall that had cracked open in spring after the last shouting match. [77] It was no bigger than a thumbnail, glazed blue on one side, and it still seemed to hold the argument. [97] She had spent the whole summer drawing on the pavement outside the ruin: fish with umbrellas, a mayor made of teacups, a staircase that went nowhere. [123] People laughed and stepped around them, and for a moment forgot which side of the gallery they had stood on when the roof came down. [148] But the rain always took the chalk, and the town always remembered. [160]

So the council, what was left of it, had declared an unlearning. [172] Forty days in the grotto, no speeches, no petitions, no lists of who had said what. [188] Instead the sand. [191] Old Dorran had brought the idea back from somewhere far off: a mandala, built grain by grain on the flat stone at the grotto's heart, then swept away. [219] Everyone would add to it. [224] Nobody would own it. [228]

Pell went in. [231] The mist followed her like a curious dog. [239] Inside, forty or so of her neighbours knelt in a loose ring around the stone, and the design had already grown to the width of a cart wheel in ochre and rust. [271] Nobody spoke. [273] It was not the silence of a rule. [281] It had grown out of the place itself, because here a whisper became a voice at the far wall, and anything you murmured arrived in someone else's ear as an accusation. [312] So they had stopped. [316] The hush had simply emerged, the way frost does, and now it sat on all of them. [333]

Pell had always read rooms the way other people read weather. [344] She saw it at once: the mandala was not one pattern but two. [357] The eastern half was tight and geometric, the work of families who wanted the gallery rebuilt exactly as it was. [377] The western half sprawled in loose spirals, from those who wanted it left as rubble, a monument to what talk had cost. [399] Between them ran a seam of bare stone, a hair's width, where no one had dared to lay a grain. [419] Dorran saw her looking and shook his head very slightly. [429] Do not. [431]

She understood him perfectly. [435] That was the trouble with understanding people; you could not pretend otherwise. [447] The Marsh sisters on the east side were breathing in the short way that meant they were counting grievances. [466] The fisher boys on the west had stopped adding sand and were only watching the seam, waiting to see who would cross it first so they could hate that person cleanly. [497] The unlearning was failing. [501] It was just learning to hold the old shape in silence. [512]

Pell took the tile flake out of her pocket. [521] She had meant to keep it, a souvenir of the mess she wanted so badly to make sense of, but meaning did not live in things you kept. [549] It lived in what you did with them. [557]

She knelt at the seam. [562] She did not pour sand. [567] Instead she crushed the flake between her thumb and a stone, and the blue glaze came apart into powder, finer than chalk, glittering faintly in the lilac light. [595] Then she blew. [598] Not a word. [601] A breath. [603] The grotto caught it anyway and carried it around the walls, a long sighing shhh from everywhere at once, and the blue dust drifted down across the seam into both halves. [634] It respected neither the geometry nor the spirals. [642] It lay where it fell, a crooked, whimsical scatter of old gallery across the whole design, like a laugh in the middle of a funeral. [667]

Nobody breathed. [669] Then one of the Marsh sisters reached across the seam and, without looking at anyone, brushed a little of the blue into her own tight ring. [695] A fisher boy did the same on his side. [704] The seam was still there. [709] But it had colour in it now. [716]

Pell sat back on her heels and felt the hush change temperature. [728] A held breath was becoming a listening. [760] Outside the mist thinned, and along the walls her breath was still going round, softer each time, until she could not tell it from the sea. [786]
