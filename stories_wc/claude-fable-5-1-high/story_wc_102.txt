The viaduct had carried no trains for forty years, and in that time it had learned to carry flowers instead. [20] Marisol Teng hauled her folding tent up the service stairs at twilight, when the valerian and the wild sweet pea were only shapes, and the fog was beginning to rise off the river beneath the arches. [56] She built camera obscuras for a living, or for something adjacent to a living: black canvas rooms with a single hole, inside which the outside world fell upside down onto paper. [87] Tonight the hole would hold Kwan Yin's Tear. [95]

Her grandmother had bought the droplet of glass at a temple in Penang, where the sellers swore it had fallen from the eye of the goddess of mercy herself, and Marisol, who believed nothing and wondered about everything, had spent a month grinding it into a lens. [142] Three nights ago, testing it in her studio, she had sat in the dark and remembered a life that was not hers. [164] Not a dream; a memory, with the weight and grain of one. [176]

She had been a pilgrim of sparks, one of the women who walked the river road between shrines carrying a live coal in a clay bowl, feeding it with her breath so that no temple's lamp would ever have to be lit from cold. [220] Too little breath and the spark died; too much and it became a fire she could not carry. [238] She had loved the ferrywoman who rowed her across each spring, and she had never said so, because every exhale she owned belonged to the coal. [264] Then she had died, somewhere on the road, and the love had gone nowhere at all. [280]

Light, she knew, could be stored; that was the whole trick of her trade. [294] Where, then, was unused love kept? [300] That was the question that had driven her up the stairs. [311]

She raised the tent between two clumps of buddleia, fitted the Tear into its brass ring, and crawled inside. [330] The viaduct arrived on the paper inverted and faint, purple flowers hanging like fruit from a sky of dark gravel. [350] The fog thickened, and the image softened with it, but softened lucidly, the way a thought does just before you understand it. [372]

Someone coughed outside the canvas. [377] "Is this an art thing?" a voice asked, and a woman ducked through the flap without waiting for an answer, bringing the smell of cold river with her. [405] She was perhaps sixty, in a gardener's jacket, and she breathed in short, guarded sips, as if air were rationed. [425] "I planted most of what you're looking at," she said, nodding at the upside-down blooms. [440] "My husband and I." [444] "He's been dead a year." [449] "I keep coming up to check on it." [457]

Marisol said nothing, because she had noticed something. [465] The image on the paper was trembling, and it trembled in time with the woman's breath. [481] Each shallow inhale drew the fog thin across the Tear and sharpened the flowers; each held breath let the fog settle and the flowers dissolve. [506] The lens was reading her. [511]

"Breathe out," Marisol said quietly. [516] "All the way." [519]

The woman looked at her, then at the paper, and let out a long breath that shook at the end. [539] On the paper the viaduct went briefly white with fog, a blank cloth, and then the fog thinned and the flowers returned, but larger, closer, as if the whole garden had leaned in. [572]

"Again," Marisol said. [575]

The woman breathed, and her breathing changed; the sips became draughts, and with each one the image steadied, and with each one something in her face came unlocked, until she was crying without any sound and the paper was full of blossom. [617] "I've been holding it," the woman said. [624] "Since the funeral." [627] "I didn't know I was holding it." [634]

Marisol watched the flowers hang steady in their upside-down sky and felt the pilgrim's question turn over in her and lie down. [656] Unused love was not stored anywhere. [662] It was held, the way a breath is held, in the lungs of whoever would not spend it, and it did nothing there but ache. [687] Spent, it went where this woman's had gone: into a dead railway, into forty years of soil, into buddleia and sweet pea that would keep blooming for strangers long after anyone remembered who planted them. [722] The pilgrim had been wrong about the coal. [730] Breath given to a spark was not stolen from anything; it was the only way a spark became light. [749]

The woman wiped her face with a sleeve and asked, in a voice that was fuller than before, what the little lens was called. [773] "Kwan Yin's Tear," Marisol said, and for the first time did not add that she didn't believe it. [791]
