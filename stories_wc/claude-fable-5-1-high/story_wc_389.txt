The observatory on Mount Sennet sat above the clouds like a rusted teapot forgotten on a stove. [17] Orla Vance had kept it alone for six years, ever since the Institute stopped sending money and started sending apologies. [37] Lightning walked the cloud floor below her most nights, and she had learned to read it the way other people read weather from their knees. [62]

The entrance was a cobalt glass portico, six panes of blue the old builders had set facing east, so that every dawn poured indigo across the stone floor. [90] Scratched into that floor were lines, hundreds of them, each marking where the portico's shadow had fallen on some dawn two centuries gone. [113] Orla called them the ancient shadows, mostly to annoy the goat. [124] The goat, Bishop, had wandered up the service road one autumn and never wandered down, and he was the closest thing she had to a colleague. [150]

The radio was her other companion, a war-surplus receiver held together with solder and spite. [165] On lonely nights she used to read the star logs aloud into a dead frequency, just to hear a voice that was not the wind. [190] She stopped the night she heard herself answered. [198]

It came in pieces, through fragmented radio frequencies the storms chewed and spat back: a man's voice, low and lyrically prophetic, saying that the sky kept a ledger and the ledger was nearly full. [232] Those were her words. [236] She had said them about a comet, three winters back, half asleep. [248]

Over the following months she pieced him together like a broken plate. [260] His name was Aurel, and he was rising down in the valley towns, preaching from a truck bed with a transmitter bolted to the cab. [285] He had found her dead frequency and mistaken her tired poetry for scripture. [298] Worse, the mountain broke his signal into shards, and each town caught a different shard. [313] Harlow heard that the ledger demanded fire; Pell heard water; Dunmore heard only that a ledger existed; the mill camps heard nothing but the word "dawn" repeated until it sounded like a threat. [346] One prophet, four gospels, and every one of them armed. [356] Orla took to calling it variant allegiance, because it sounded like a disease, and because it behaved like one. [375] By spring the valley roads carried more rifles than grain. [385]

She could have stayed quiet. [390] Nobody knew the voice under Aurel's voice was hers; nobody knew the observatory still had a keeper. [407] But six years had taught her that a shadow, properly read, tells you exactly where the light will be. [426]

The old builders had scratched their lines for a reason. [436] Cross-checked against the logs, they gave her the one dawn each year when the sun cleared the eastern ridge dead center through the portico and every shadow on the floor converged on a single point. [471] It was eleven days off. [476] On that morning, and only that morning, the valley would see the whole mountain lit blue from below, like a lantern somebody had finally remembered to fill. [503]

She spent the eleven days rewiring the receiver into a transmitter, which involved a great deal of swearing and one small fire Bishop put out by standing on it. [532] She could not make the mountain stop shattering signals. [541] So she would not send one signal; she would send four, one on each town's frequency, each carrying a different fragment. [562] Fire, water, dawn, ledger, each sentence useless alone, each ending with the same instruction: look east at sunrise, and listen to your neighbor. [585] The prophecy would only unfold if the towns put their shards together. [597]

The night before, a storm boiled up from the valley floor and lit the observatory white and black, white and black, so that Orla and the goat looked like a film running too slow. [631] She sat with her hand on the key and waited for the clouds to thin. [646]

At the first grey she began to send. [654] Her voice went down the mountain in four ragged streams. [664] She did not preach; she read, the way she always had, tired and plain, and let the portico do the rest. [685]

The sun cleared the ridge. [690] Blue light struck the glass and fell across the floor, and two hundred years of scratched shadows slid together into one bright point at her feet. [716] Below, she would learn later, four towns stood on four roads and saw the mountain burn cobalt, and turned to each other with their fragments in their mouths. [744] Aurel, so the story went, sat down on his truck bed and stayed there some time. [760]

Orla switched off the transmitter and scratched a new line into the stone, the first in two centuries. [778] "Colleague," she said to Bishop, "I think we may have started something." [790] The goat ate the star log. [796]
