Ingrid woke in the boathouse on the third morning and knew something had shifted in her while she slept. [19] The cots around her were empty, the volunteers already out on the ice, and the wind was pushing through the plank walls with a sound like someone exhaling slowly. [48] She had come to this coast to die on a Tuesday. [59] The clinic in the harbor town took foreigners, quietly and legally, and she had paid the fee and signed the forms and flown north with one suitcase and her grandmother's lace parasol. [91] The diagnosis had a long name, but the neurologist had put it more simply: her brain was learning to grip reality loosely, and the grip would only loosen further. [120] Then the storm had come in off the strait, and the drift ice with it, and the marina had filled overnight with grinding white slabs that crushed hulls and tore pontoons from their moorings. [154] The clinic's one doctor was also the town's one doctor, and Tuesday had been postponed. [169] Ingrid had not planned for a delay. [176] She had spent her patience carefully for two years, the way you spend the last of anything, and she had budgeted exactly enough to reach the appointment and no further. [206] The relief coordinator, a broad woman named Solveig, had found her standing outside the shuttered clinic and handed her a pair of waders. [229] "If you are waiting anyway," Solveig had said, "wait usefully." [239] So Ingrid had spent two days on the marina, trawling the black water between the floes with a long-handled net, hauling up what the ice had chewed loose: fenders, a child's boot, tangles of line, a wooden sign that said BERTH 14. [281] The parasol came with her. [286] The lace had rotted to a few gray threads decades ago, and the ribs had gone, but the shaft was sound and the tip was solid brass, worn to a dull gold by four generations of hands. [323] She used it to test the ice before each step, tapping ahead of her and listening. [339] Solid ice answered with a flat knock. [346] Ice about to give made a different sound, higher and hollower, a sound with a question in it. [364] This was, she understood now, why she had really come north, beneath the forms and the fee. [381] She had wanted to learn the sound of approaching endings, so that when her own arrived she would recognize it and not be caught unprepared in some blurred hallway of her mind. [413] She had thought the clinic would teach her that. [422] The marina was teaching her instead. [428] Now she pulled on the waders and went out into the wind. [440] What had shifted in her was hard to name. [449] The fog in her head was still there, the slippage at the edges of things, but overnight it had stopped feeling like a leak and started feeling like a loosened knot. [480] She found Solveig at the end of the broken breakwater, hauling a net. [493] "The doctor called," Solveig said without looking up. [501] "She can see you Thursday." [506] Ingrid nodded and took the other end of the net. [516] Together they dragged it along the seam where two floes ground against each other, and the ice made its sound, the low complaint of something enormous coming apart slowly, and the wind lifted the sound and carried it out over the strait and away. [560] It did not sound like terror. [566] It sounded like weather. [570] Ingrid understood that this was what she had been afraid of missing: not the ending itself, but the approach, the long honest groan of it. [595] The clinic would have given her the ending clean and silent, and she would never have heard it coming. [614] The net came up heavy with a length of rusted chain. [625] Ingrid planted the parasol tip in the ice beside her to steady herself, and the brass rang against the floe, one flat certain note. [649] Solid. [650] "Tell her I will come," Ingrid said. [657] "But not Thursday." [660] Solveig looked at her then, and there was no argument in her face, only weather. [675] "Then when?" [677] Ingrid listened to the ice. [682] Her patience, which she had believed spent to the last coin, had not run out after all; it had only folded, the way a map folds, so that a place she thought was far away lay suddenly against her hand. [722] Inside the fold there was room. [728] Not years, perhaps. [731] But enough to trawl the marina until the floes let go of the harbor, enough to learn the whole vocabulary of the ice, enough to hear her own knock go hollow and know it for what it was. [769] "When it sounds right," she said, and the wind took the words, as it took everything here, and did not seem to mind carrying them. [794]
