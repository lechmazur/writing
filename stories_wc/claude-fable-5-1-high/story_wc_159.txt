The opal library had been built above the clouds so that its readers would never be distracted by weather, and for two hundred years that had been considered a marvel. [30] Now it was only deserted. [35] Ilse was the last caretaker, and what she cared for were the glass orchids. [49] They grew in the reading stalls where readers had once whispered, one flower for every name that had been spoken aloud in this place and then, somewhere below, stopped being spoken at all. [82] A glass orchid stays clear as long as its name is still sounded now and then. [98] When the world forgets, the petals cloud, then frost, then crack. [109] Ilse's work was to keep them clear. [116]

The trouble was that she had never heard most of the names. [128] So she was gently systematic about it. [135] Every evening she began at the north stair and worked across the museum of extinct feelings, which occupied the library's lower gallery, lighting each lamp in turn. [162] The exhibits were modest: a chair labeled the anticipation of a letter, a cracked mirror labeled shame that could be outgrown, an empty case labeled homesickness for a house still standing. [193] Nobody came to see them anymore, which she supposed was the point. [205]

As she lit each lamp she sang. [212] It was a song so old that no one remembered its words, only its shape, a lullaby that climbed six notes and came down five. [237] The opal vaults took the song and gave it back changed, and in the echo, if she listened, she could hear the pitches that the names had once been spoken in. [268] The stone had kept them the way a shell keeps the sea. [280] She would hum the returned note into the nearest orchid, and if it was right, the glass warmed and cleared. [300] She could not give the names back to the world, but she could keep the sound of them from going out. [321] It was a small kind of preservation, and it was the only kind left to her. [337]

One orchid resisted. [340] It grew at the west window where the clouds came closest, and every night it was a shade whiter than the night before. [363] The echo gave her five clear notes for it and then a sixth that the stone would not hold, a flattened, uneasy tone that fell between the pitches she knew how to sing. [396] She tried for eleven nights. [401]

On the twelfth, the wind came up through the west window and moved the old chime that hung there. [420] It was bamboo, seven tubes, and one of them had been cracked for as long as she could remember. [439] She had always meant to replace it. [446] The cracked tube struck the others and gave out its dull, wrong note, and Ilse stood very still, because it was the sixth note exactly. [471]

She sang the five and let the chime sound the last. [482] The orchid warmed under her hand and cleared to the root, and the name came together in her mouth before she understood she was saying it. [508] Teodor. [509]

He had hung the chime. [514] He had cracked the tube the day he left, dropping it while he took it down to bring with him, and then had left it after all, saying the library should keep something of his. [549] He had asked her to come down with him, to the towns under the cloud, and she had said the orchids needed her. [572] She had not said his name since. [579] Nor, it seemed, had anyone. [584]

The regret came up in her slow and sweet and poisonous, like foxglove, the kind that steadies the heart in small doses and stops it in large ones. [612] She let herself have a small dose. [619] She had spent the years keeping the names of strangers from going out, and she had let the one she knew go grey in a window she passed every night. [649] It was not a marvel, what she did here. [658] It was housekeeping in a building the world had put down and walked away from. [673]

She would not replace the cracked tube. [680] Some notes only a broken thing can make, and some names need them. [693]

In the morning she wrote a new label and carried it down to the empty case in the museum of extinct feelings, the one that had held homesickness for a house still standing. [726] She took the old label out. [732] That feeling, at least, was not extinct after all. [741] That evening she climbed to the west window, wound the chime's cord a little tighter so the wind would find it, and began the lighting at the north stair, singing the six notes up and the five notes down, listening for whatever the stone still had to give back. [790]
