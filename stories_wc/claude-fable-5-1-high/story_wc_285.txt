The Gloom Hill coach came down to the Sallow crossing every morning the way water comes down, without deciding to. [20] Aldous Penhale, its coachman, held the reins loosely, because the horses knew the road better than any man's hands did. [40] The fog was already waiting at the river, thick as fleece, and the ferry bell rang once from somewhere inside it. [61] "Closed till it lifts," the ferryman called, not unkindly. [70] Aldous nodded and drew the coach under the eave of the ferry house, where rain from the night still ticked in the gutters. [93] He had one passenger, a girl going across to a post in a draper's shop, and she seemed content to wait. [114] So did he. [117] Waiting was most of the work, and he had learned to do it well. [131] He did not think, exactly. [136] He let things percolate. [140] He was closely attentive, but to nothing in particular, and to everything at once: the horses' breath blooming and fading, the ferry rope groaning in its iron ring, the tick of the gutters, the girl humming under her breath. [179] Merren had called it his weather sense. [186] She said he could stand in a room and know what it wanted without asking. [201] He reached into his coat and found the scarf. [210] It was her last piece of knitting, grey and blue, finished except for the loose ends she had never woven in, and her yarn needle was still threaded through the edge where she had left it. [246] He had carried it all winter without touching it. [255] The promise came up with the scarf, as it always did. [266] He had made it to her the year they married: one more season on the hill, then the coach could go to someone younger and they would take a house by the water. [299] The next spring he had made it again, written over the first. [311] Then again, when the roof needed mending. [318] Then again, when she took ill and needed the doctor from across the river, and the fare was the coach. [338] Each new promise had been laid down over the old one without quite erasing it, so that now, when he looked, he could see every layer at once, like a parchment scraped and rewritten until the earliest words showed through the newest. [380] It was too much to read. [386] That was why he had never read it. [394] The girl stopped humming. [398] "Is that for someone?" she asked, nodding at the scarf. [408] "It was for me," he said. [414] "Then you ought to finish it." [420] She said it the way the ferryman had said closed, plainly, as a matter of weather. [436] He drew the yarn needle free. [442] It was blunt and broad-eyed, made for weaving rather than piercing, and it fit his fingers better than he expected. [462] He took the first loose end and worked it back through the stitches, under and over, the way he had watched her do a thousand evenings. [488] He did not think about the promise. [495] He let it sit in the fog with everything else, the rope and the bell and the ticking, and trusted the whole of it to settle. [521] One end, then another. [525] The layers began to thin as he worked, not because he sorted them but because he stopped holding them apart. [545] Every version of the promise, he saw, had been about the same thing. [558] Not the house, not the water, not the coach. [567] Under all the rewriting, in her hand, in the oldest ink: come down off the hill and rest. [585] That was the whole of it. [591] The rest had only been circumstances. [597] He wove in the last end and drew the needle out clean. [609] He had told himself he would wait until the gutters stopped ticking, and when he listened, they had. [627] The fog was coming apart over the river in long pale shreds, and the far bank showed through it, green and ordinary. [649] The ferryman rang his bell twice. [655] Aldous wound the scarf around his neck, and the girl smiled as if she had expected nothing else. [673] He handed the reins to the ferryman's boy, who would drive the coach back up the hill, and the boy took them without a word, as though he had been waiting for this longer than Aldous had. [710] Then he stepped onto the ferry with the girl and her small trunk, and the rope groaned, and the flat boat moved out into water so still it hardly seemed to know they were crossing. [745] The yarn needle rode in his breast pocket, empty now, its work done. [758] Behind him the hill kept its gloom, as hills do. [768] Ahead the last of the fog blew off the river like spindrift, and he let it. [784]
