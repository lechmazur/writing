The hollowfinch sang the first six notes of the Academy anthem and stopped, as it always did, one note short of home. [22] Ilse Marrow set down her brush for the ninth time that morning. [34] Twenty years of illustrating natural history had taught her that a specimen in a cage is a lie with feathers, but she had never met a lie so stubborn. [63] Outside her studio window, the rooftop canals lay glassy between the towers, troughs of slow water braided from spire to spire, thirty stories above the streets. [89] The city of Orrun was built along the spine of a sleeping giant, and when he breathed the lower streets tilted, so the founders had put their waterways where the ground stayed truest: on the roofs. [125] A nap lasted nine years, give or take a season. [135] This one was in its ninth. [141] The Academy wanted twelve plates of the hollowfinch before the giant woke and the canals sloshed and the whole city spent a month bailing. [165] She had produced eleven, and every one of them was wrong. [176] The bird's throat swelled in her drawings but never sang; its posture was that of a thing waiting for a note that would not come. [201] The trapper who sold it had said it was netted at the giant's ear, far along the canal at the head tower, where every sound the city made went in and came back changed. [235] The place where echoes are born, he called it, and laughed, because trappers do not believe in such places. [254] Ilse did. [256] She had drawn enough animals to know that none of them makes sense apart from the place that shaped it. [276] If she wanted the twelfth plate, she would have to carry the cage there and open it. [293] The trouble was distance. [297] Two days by punt to the head tower, and the giant had begun to murmur in his sleep that week, the water shivering in its troughs at dusk. [325] She touched the pendant at her collar, a small pewter infinity, two loops crossing. [339] Her mother, a clockmaker, had made it and warned her about it in the same breath. [355] It sculpts time itself, she had said, the way a potter pulls a wall up from a lump: it does not make more clay, it only moves what is there. [385] Turn it once, and you borrow an hour from the far end of your life and spend it now. [404] She turned it now, and the shiver in the canals went still as poured glass. [419] The journey took two days that did not happen. [428] She poled the punt through channels that bent around bell towers and under the arches of laundry bridges, past windows where people stood frozen mid-yawn, and each time the water began to tremble she gave the pendant another turn and felt something at the back of her life come loose and drift forward. [481] The bird rode in its cage on the bow and sang its six notes and stopped, six notes and stopped, an interrupted cadence that had begun to sound to her less like a failure than a question. [518] The head tower rose where the giant's skull rose, and the canal poured off its edge into the dark curl of his ear. [541] Ilse moored at the lip and listened. [548] Every noise of Orrun came here: cart wheels, arguments, the bells, a child's scale on a flute somewhere far behind her, and all of it went down into the ear and came back a half-beat late and a little softer, unfinished, handed back to the city before the city had finished saying it. [601] She understood the bird then. [606] It had not been failing to finish. [613] It had been born of a place where nothing finishes, where every phrase is returned before its last note, and it was singing the only music it had ever heard. [643] She uncaged it. [646] The hollowfinch stepped to the wire, considered her, and dropped into the ear's darkness, and from below came six notes, and then, from the dark, the seventh. [673] Not the bird. [676] The echo, finishing what the bird had begun, the way it must have done for every hollowfinch since the first, the two of them making one song between them. [705] Ilse drew fast, in the stopped light, the bird in flight with its beak open and the rest of the cadence rising to meet it. [730] When the plate was done she let the pendant go. [740] The canals lurched; the giant sighed and rolled, and somewhere behind her the city began its month of bailing. [759] Her hands, when she looked at them, had aged two days and some years besides. [774] She thought it a fair price for the only true drawing she had ever made. [789]
