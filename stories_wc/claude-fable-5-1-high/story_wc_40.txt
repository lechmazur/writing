The last dictionary was printed in the autumn Mira turned sixty-one, and after that the words were on their own. [20] Nobody had decided this; the presses simply stopped, the way rivers stop when no one is watching the source. [39] Mira kept watching. [42]

She came to the library before dawn each morning, when the reading room was still a well of shadow and the tall windows had not yet begun to bleed grey. [72] She did not speak to the night guard, who had long ago stopped expecting it. [87] Speech had always felt to her like spending something she could not earn back. [101]

In her coat pocket she carried a single crystal from the chandelier that had fallen the winter the heating failed. [121] The others had been swept away; this one she had found under a radiator, unbroken, a teardrop of cut glass the length of her thumb. [146] It was sonically reflective in a way that puzzled her at first and then became the whole of her method. [166] Held to the ear, it did not amplify a sound so much as return it, folded, a half-second late and slightly changed, as though the sound had gone somewhere and come back with news. [200]

Mira used it to listen to the city. [208]

Without the dictionary, words had begun to grow. [216] Not multiply, exactly; grow, the way ivy grows, finding the cracks in older structures and widening them. [233] Children in the market said things their parents could not parse. [244] Bus drivers announced stops that had no names last year. [254] Mira wanted to decode the pattern of that growth, to find its grammar before it outran anyone's ability to write it down. [276] She filled notebooks with what the crystal gave her: syllables caught off the plaza at first light, the overheard shape of a greeting she did not know. [303]

This morning was different, because at nine the council would sit in the reading room and she would have to testify. [324] The library was to be closed. [330] Nothing new was being printed, the argument went, so nothing new needed shelving; the building could become housing, which the city needed more than it needed shadows. [357] Mira had been asked to speak because she was the only person who still came. [372]

She sat now in the dark between the stacks and rehearsed nothing, because she had learned that rehearsed sentences left her mouth as ash. [396] Instead she held the crystal to her ear and waited for the hush. [409]

It came every dawn, a suspended minute when the night noises had ended and the day's had not begun, when even the building's timbers held their breath. [436] In that hush the crystal returned not sound but the memory of sound, and she had come to believe that this was where the new words were born, in the gap, before anyone owned them. [471]

Light reached the high shelves. [476] The council arrived in coats, six of them, kind and tired, and took the long table where students had once slept on open atlases. [500] Someone asked her to state her case. [507]

Mira stood. [509] She could not do it in paragraphs; she had never been able to. [522] So she testified the only way she had ever learned anything, through fractured sequences, reading from her notebooks the pieces the crystal had returned to her. [548] A word for the smell of rain on a bus stop, spoken by a girl in March. [565] A verb meaning to wait for someone who is not coming, which she had heard twice in a week from two people who had never met. [591] The dates. [593] The intervals between them, shortening. [598]

She did not explain what the fragments proved. [606] She had spent a lifetime believing that meaning was something you assembled quietly and alone, and she was too old to trade that for eloquence now. [632] But she set the crystal on the table where the light could find it, and she let the room be silent long enough for the council to hear what the silence held. [664]

It held the hush, still suspended, still waiting. [672] One of the councillors, the youngest, leaned toward the crystal as if it had said her name. [689]

Mira did not know whether the library would be saved. [699] She knew only that the words would keep growing whether or not anyone kept a room dark enough to hear them arrive, and that she would come tomorrow, before dawn, with her notebook, in case. [734] It was a small faith, worn thin, but it had never once failed to be true. [750]
