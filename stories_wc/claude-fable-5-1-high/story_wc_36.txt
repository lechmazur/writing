The guardianship ended the way the Thunder-Echo Range ended a storm: not all at once, but in diminishing repetitions, each fainter than the last. [24] First the council took Idren's title, then her key, then the small stone room where the flute had rested for eleven years. [46] They did not take the flute itself, because no one else would touch it. [60] So she carried it back into the canyons where she had failed. [72]

The range was quiet now, the kind of quiet that gathered weight the longer it held. [88] Idren had once heard a snowfield go silent before it slid, and this was the same silence, avalanchingly quiet, stacked against the red walls until it seemed a single dropped pebble might bring the whole hush down. [125]

She walked the dry riverbed gathering driftwood. [132] Flash floods left it wedged in the rocks every spring, bleached and light as bone, and she chose pieces with a clear grain so they would point without argument. [161] At the mouth of the side canyon she laid the first one, its narrow end aimed inward. [178] Every forty paces she laid another. [184] She was not marking a way back for herself. [193] She was rebuilding the path Tam had taken three years ago, step by step, so she could stand where he had stood and see what she had not seen. [222] A keeper's duty had been to protect the flute. [231] A failed keeper's duty, she had decided, was to understand exactly how. [243]

The flute was made of a single river reed, gilded so thinly that the fibers still showed through the gold like veins under skin. [267] Played correctly, it did not open a door. [275] It wove one, note over note, pulling threads of this world across threads of the next until a person could walk the seam between them. [300] Tam had been her apprentice, eager and nineteen, and she had let him walk it. [315] The weave had come apart behind him. [322] The council called it recklessness. [327] Idren called it a result, and results could be studied. [337]

The side canyon narrowed to a slot where the walls leaned together overhead. [350] She laid the last driftwood marker at the exact spot, a forked piece, one tine pointing where Tam had gone and one pointing back the way she had come. [379] Then she knelt and uncased the flute. [386]

The case was oiled leather, and the hinges made a small sound, and the small sound went on far longer than it should have. [410] It came back to her from the walls four times, five, six, each return thinner, until the canyon had finished repeating it and settled again. [435]

Idren sat very still. [439]

Three years ago there had been a storm. [447] She had thought that fitting; the old texts said the flute was first played in thunder, and she had wanted the ceremony to feel true. [472] So she had played, and the thunder had rolled, and the range had done what the range did. [490] It had echoed. [493] Every note she wove, the walls had woven again a heartbeat late, and again, and again. [509] She had not made one seam for Tam to walk. [519] She had made six, overlapping and out of step, and he had walked into a tangle. [535]

The insight arrived without any feeling of triumph. [543] It arrived like the canyon's quiet did, heavy and complete. [553] The texts had not said the flute was played in thunder because thunder was sacred. [568] They had said it because the first keepers waited for the thunder to stop. [582] The weaving needed the hush after, when the range had spent every echo it owned and would return nothing. [601] A silence like this one. [606]

Idren looked at the forked driftwood at her knee. [615] She had come to harvest a lesson and carry it home to a council that no longer wanted her. [634] But a keeper who was no longer a keeper had no rule against walking. [648]

She lifted the flute. [652] The gold was warm from her hands. [659] She waited until even her breath had stopped coming back to her from the walls, until the quiet had piled so high she could feel it against her teeth. [688] Then she played one note, and the canyon did not repeat it. [700]

The note went out alone and stayed alone, and where it stayed, the air between the leaning walls took on a grain, like driftwood, like reed, a single clean line running inward. [732] She played the second note over it, and the third across both. [744] One seam, unhurried, true. [748]

Idren left the forked marker where it lay, one tine for whoever came after her, one for the way home, and stepped along the line she had made. [776] Behind her the hush held, and nothing echoed. [784]
