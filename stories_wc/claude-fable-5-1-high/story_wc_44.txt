The spoons bent at noon, seventeen years before Iven was born, and no shadow has behaved properly at midday since. [20] That was the Bending, the morning every spoon in the world curled like a fern and the isle of Cathe tore free of the sea and rose. [47] The old rune masters said reality had been bent along with the cutlery, and every rune they knew stopped closing. [67] A rune is a loop; an open loop is only a scribble. [79] So they sealed what they could not explain, and the observatory on Cathe became a place that studied only the night. [100]

Iven was a second-year scholar, which meant she was permitted to copy runes but not to draw them. [118] She had a question the masters refused to hear: where do the shadows go at noon? [134] On the mainland, noon shadows merely shrank. [141] On Cathe they vanished, every one, for the length of a held breath, and returned as if they had never left. [162] The masters called it a daytime matter and slept through it. [173] Iven did not sleep through it. [179]

She worked the way she did everything, quietly deliberate, one line at a time. [193] Each night, after the masters climbed to the great lens, she stayed below in the archive and traced the old runes with a stylus of soft lead. [220] They did not close. [224] But when she bent her wrist the way a spoon bends, letting the line curve past where her training said to stop, the loops met. [249] The runes had not died in the Bending. [257] They had only bent, and the masters had never relearned them. [268]

The key hung in the sealed wing, on a hook beside a door no one opened. [284] It was brass, thick as a thumb, its bow worked into a single rune that had been left, deliberately, unfinished. [304] Behind the door stood the observatory's automaton, the Noon Warden, built to track the sun by day while the scholars slept. [325] After the Bending it had spoken once, and what it said frightened the masters enough that they broke the rune on its key and locked it in the dark. [354] A sealed awakening, the ledger called it. [361] Iven had read that entry so many times the ink had thinned under her finger. [376]

She took the key down an hour before dawn, when the masters were most tired and the lens most crowded. [396] With her lead stylus she drew the missing arc of the rune onto the brass, bending the line as a spoon bends, and felt the loop close under her hand like a latch. [429] The door opened without a key at all. [437] It had only been waiting for the rune to be whole. [448]

The Warden was taller than she expected, all gears and green-black bronze, its face a blank dial. [465] She fitted the key into its chest and turned it, and the ticking began slow and then steady, like something remembering how to breathe. [489] "You closed the loop," it said. [495] "The others opened them and called the opening a wound." [505] "Where do the shadows go?" Iven asked. [512] "Come and see." [515]

They waited on the sealed wing's balcony while the sun climbed. [526] Iven had never watched noon arrive from this side of the isle, where the rail overhung open air and the sea lay a mile below, small and wrinkled. [554] The Warden's shadow shortened across the flagstones, and hers beside it, until both were thin as pen strokes. [572] Then, in the held-breath instant, they did not vanish. [581] They poured. [583] Every shadow on Cathe ran like ink toward the edges of the isle and over them, down, gathering beneath the stone in a great dark cushion that Iven could see, leaning far out, spread wide under the island's belly. [622] The isle rose, very slightly, the way a boat lifts when a wave passes under it. [638] Then the breath ended and the shadows came home. [647]

"That is what I told them," the Warden said. [656] "The Bending happened at noon. [661] The shadows went under and never fully came back, and Cathe floats on what they left behind. [678] Each noon the isle borrows its shadows again to stay aloft. [689] The masters did not want a home that depended on a thing they could not draw." [705]

Iven looked at the lead smudge on her fingers, at the closed rune on the key in the Warden's chest. [725] Sealing a question had not made it go away; it had only left the loop open, and open loops hold nothing up. [747] "I will need to draw it," she said. [755] "The rune for the underside. [760] The one that closes." [764] The Warden's dial face turned toward her, catching the light that shadows had just abandoned. [779] "Then relearn it," it said, "and this time, do not stop where they stopped." [793]
