Oru could no longer say the word for wind, so he gave Vael the diamond instead. [16] It sat in her palm like a chip of dirty ice, veined inside with fractures that caught the lamplight and broke it. [38] "Cursed," he said, in the trade tongue, because the old tongue was leaving him one word at a time. [57] "Three readers before you, and all of them went silent." [67]

Vael turned it over. [71] She was a storyteller of the canyon people, and her forearms showed the green-blue veins that came from forty years of drinking copper water, which the storytellers believed kept the throat honest. [103] She did not answer him right away. [110] She rarely did; she had learned early that a sentence held longer if you let it settle before you spoke it. [131] "Then I will read it differently," she said. [139]

The Hollow language had never been written in ink. [148] Its people had carved it as ridges into bone, and the bone was the canyon itself, where the spines of some vanished animal stood as towers a hundred feet high, every surface worked with script. [183] You read it with your fingertips. [189] But the sounds had lived only in mouths, and Oru's was the last, and his was failing. [206] The texture would survive him. [211] The voice would not. [215]

Vael walked into the canyon at dawn. [222] It was a tunnel of echoes; a dropped stone spoke back for the better part of an hour, and a shout could still be heard the next morning, worn thin but recognizable. [254] That was the curse, she suspected. [260] Say a word wrong here and the canyon repeated the error a thousand times until the speaker could not remember the right shape of it at all. [287] The three silent readers had not been struck dumb by magic. [298] They had been shouted down by themselves. [305]

She climbed the nearest tower and laid her hand on the script. [317] Ridges, deep and shallow, spaced close and far. [325] Texture. [326] She held the diamond up to the sun. [334] Its flaws threw a pattern onto the bone, thin lines and forks, and she saw at once that the fractures inside the stone were cut to the same rhythm as the ridges under her palm. [369] Someone had carved the first word into a stone that could not be worn away. [384] But a pattern of light was not a sound. [393] She needed a way to see the sound before she made it. [405]

The swifts gave it to her. [411] Thousands of them nested in the towers, and at first light they poured out and wheeled through the canyon in a single flock. [434] Vael had watched them all her life without understanding what she watched. [446] Now she hummed one low note, and the flock bent. [456] The echo rolled down the canyon and the birds folded away from it, then closed behind it, and for a moment their bodies drew the shape of her voice against the sky. [488] She hummed higher, and the shape changed. [495]

So she harnessed them. [499] Fingertips on the ridges, she made a sound and watched the flock, then looked at the pattern the diamond cast, and adjusted. [521] Wrong sounds she made softly, so the canyon would forget them fast. [533] Right ones she gave her full voice, and let the tunnel carry them, and the swifts drew them again and again, and the shape in the birds began to match the shape in the stone. [568] It took the whole day. [573] By dusk the flock was spelling the first word of the Hollow language in the air, and the canyon was speaking it back to her in her own voice from every direction, and the stone in her hand had gone warm. [614]

She brought it to Oru that night. [621] He was very small in the bed. [628] She did not tell him what she had done; she only bent and said the word into his ear, quietly, the way she said everything. [653] Something in his face let go. [659] He answered in the old tongue, a full sentence, and she did not understand it, and then he was gone. [679]

The language died that night. [684] Vael knew this. [687] A word is not a language, and one speaker who can read texture is not a people. [704] But she walked back into the canyon anyway and stood among the towers and spoke his sentence as best she could remember it, and the swifts rose, and the tunnel of echoes took it up and carried it into the dark, where it would sound for a long time yet. [754]
