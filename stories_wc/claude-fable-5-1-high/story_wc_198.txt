The fog came up from the river before the sun did, and Ivy came up behind it with the saxophone case knocking against her knee. [25] The Halloway Conservatory stood where it had always stood, a prism atrium of leaded glass grown over a limestone grotto, its panes gone milky with age and mist. [53] Nobody had tended the ferns since Teodor died, but the spring still ran, and the drips still fell. [71] That was the trouble. [75]

She let herself in through the broken side door and went down the wet steps into the grotto, where the first grey light was already finding the prisms overhead and breaking into soft color against the stone. [112] Radiant was the word Teodor had used, though the light was never bright, only quietly vivid, the way a memory is vivid without being loud. [137] She assembled the horn by feel. [143]

Teodor had been a cryptographer before he was a gardener, and a gardener before he was the old man who taught a river-town girl to play in exchange for weeding. [173] He was the one who had noticed that the grotto spoke. [184] Every dawn, as dewdrops formed on the prisms and slid to the edges and fell, they struck the pool and the stone in a rhythm that was not random. [213] Long and short. [216] Pause and pause. [219] He had spent a winter with a notebook on his knee before he would admit what it was: a message, repeating, in a code he knew from a war he did not like to name. [254]

"A prophecy," he told her once, and would not say more. [265] "Some things come true only when they are finished being said." [276]

So he had made a practice of unfinishing it. [285] Not by drowning it out; noise only made the drips more insistent, the way shouting over a whisper makes the whisper sharper. [307] He worked instead through negative space. [313] A code lives in its gaps, he said, in the silences that separate one mark from the next, and if you fill the silences with something the pattern cannot use, the message stays a handful of loose sounds and never becomes a sentence. [356] The saxophone, breathed rather than blown, was the thing the pattern could not use. [370]

On his last morning he had put the horn in her hands and said the hush was hers now, that it was vested in her the way a title is vested, and that she should not ask what she was keeping quiet, because knowing would make her want to listen. [420] That was eleven years ago. [425] In the town they called her the fog saxophonist, the woman who played to nobody at dawn, and she let them. [446]

Now she wet the reed and waited. [453] The first drop gathered on a prism above her, swelled, caught a thread of rose light, and let go. [472] Tock. [473] She let the silence after it begin and then, before it could become the right length of silence, she whispered into the horn: a low F so soft it was mostly air, a sound the fog could carry without noticing. [513] Another drop. [515] Another breath laid across the gap. [521]

It was not music, exactly. [526] It was the shape of music with the middle taken out, and she had been playing it so long that she sometimes heard it in her sleep, Teodor's cough in the corner, the smell of wet iron and old ferns. [566]

The trouble was that eleven years is long enough to learn a thing without meaning to. [582] She had never studied the code. [588] But she had stood in the gaps of it every dawn since she was nineteen, and the gaps have a grammar of their own, and lately, in the moment before each breath, she found she could feel the next drop coming and knew, without words, whether it would be long or short. [640]

This morning the knowing had edges. [646] Three short. [648] She breathed across them. [652] Three long. [654] She breathed across those too, but not before some small treacherous part of her had already read them, and read the letters that followed, and found in them the shape of her own name. [688]

The grotto glowed on, indifferent, rose and green and violet on the wet walls. [702] She understood then why Teodor had never told her what he was silencing, and why his hands had shaken that last morning, and what it costs to stand every day in the negative space of a sentence about yourself. [741]

The drops kept forming. [745] The sun rose another inch and the mist began, as it always did, to thin. [760] Ivy closed her eyes so she would not see the pattern in the light, and whispered into the horn, and kept the hush she had been given, one unfinished morning at a time. [793]
