Iris did not believe in quarantined memory, and she said so to the seashell. [14] It lay on the garden bench where her sister had left it, one half of a nautilus split clean along its spiral, its chambers open like a row of small mouths. [45] Wren had been the believer. [50] Wren had spent her last two years turning this hillside into what she called a listening garden: hedges of lamb's ear and dusty miller trimmed into shallow curves, moss velvet under every step, a bowl of green that swallowed the wind and gave back silence. [95] Iris archived sound for a living, cataloging field recordings in a basement in the city, and she knew what a parabolic shape did to a whisper. [121] It gathered it. [124] It aimed it. [127] That was physics, not magic. [132]

Still, at twilight, when the last light went blue and the leaves turned the color of old theater curtains, she sat where the curves converged and lifted the half-shell to her ear. [164]

"You came," said Wren. [168]

Iris nearly dropped it. [172] Then the skeptic in her began working: a recording, some tiny device fitted in the spiral, motion-triggered, timed. [190] Wren had been clever. [194] Wren had been dying. [198] Dying people planned things. [202]

"I know what you're thinking," the shell whispered, calm as a nurse, "and you're partly right. [218] The garden is a resonance structure. [224] So is the shell. [228] So was I." [231]

She explained it the way she had explained it a hundred times over the phone, in the voice Iris had always found too gentle to argue with. [258] A memory that would not stop playing could be quarantined, moved out of the body and into a form that held its frequency. [281] Not erased. [283] Housed. [284]

"You said that was grief with a dimmer switch," the shell said, and laughed, and the laugh was so exactly hers that Iris felt the argument leave her. [312] Charmed, she thought bitterly. [316] Charmed by a dead woman and a mollusk. [324]

"It plays once more," Wren said. [330] "When the last star fades into morning. [337] I need you to record it. [343] Not to keep. [346] To file. [348] Then the garden can be just a garden." [356]

"I meant to do it myself," the shell said, quieter. [366] "I wanted to walk out of that day on my own legs, to translate all that alone into something I chose. [387] I ran out of mornings." [392]

The night passed. [395] Iris set her recorder at the focus of the hedges, checked its levels by habit, and lay back on moss that gave under her like upholstery. [421] She thought about Marta, Wren's wife, dead six years, who had planted every soft-leaved thing on this slope because she liked gardens you could touch in the dark. [449] After the funeral Wren had stopped answering the door. [458] Iris had called it hiding. [463] Wren had called it listening. [468] Now, at three in the morning, with dew soaking her collar, Iris understood that both were the same word for being alone with one unbearable, radiant thing. [495]

The stars thinned. [498] The east went from black to bruise to pearl. [507] One star held out above the hedge line, stubborn, and Iris found she was holding the shell against her chest, waiting on it like a pulse. [533]

When it dimmed, the garden began to hum. [541]

It came up through the moss, through the bench, through the ribs of the shell, a chord with no instrument, and inside the chord was a morning: Marta at the kitchen window in a yellow robe, laughing at something the kettle had done, sunlight so full the whole room looked lit from within her. [587] That was all. [590] A woman laughing. [593] Tragically radiant, because it had been enough to keep another woman in a house for six years, and then in the ground. [615]

The recorder's needle swung and settled. [621] The hum thinned into birdsong. [626]

Iris sat up. [629] The shell was only a shell now, cool and silent, and the garden was only a garden, wet, ordinary, smelling of soil. [651] She labeled the file the way she labeled everything: date, location, duration, source. [664] Then she added a line she had never written in a catalog before. [677] Quarantine lifted. [679] Subject released. [681]

She meant Wren. [684] Walking down the hill toward the road, phone already in her hand to call a friend she had not called in a year, she admitted she also meant herself. [713]
