The year the casting-engines learned to compose spells no human mind could hold, Teodor Vance failed his final at the Academy for the third time. [25] He had cast a lamp-light by hand, slowly, the way his grandmother taught him, while the other candidates fed intentions into brass looms and watched cathedrals of fire assemble themselves. [55] The examiners called his work quaint. [61] So he took the only thing he owned that the engines could not have made and went up the mountain. [81]

It was a keystone, wedge-shaped and grey, strapped to his back in a leather harness. [96] It had come from the folly in his grandmother's garden: a little ruined arch she had built on purpose, holding up nothing, leading nowhere, useful only for framing the pear tree at dusk. [129] Every evening for forty years she had lit it with the same small spell, and the stone had drunk that light the way stone does. [154] Lingering luminance, the old texts called it: the residue a hand-cast spell leaves behind, too faint to see anywhere the engines burn. [176] Engine-light left nothing. [179] It was too perfect to linger. [185]

Above the treeline the path gave out into cloud, and Teodor did the one thing he had always been good at. [206] He mist-walked. [208] It was a beginner's trick, the first spell any child learned before the looms made learning unnecessary: you asked the mist, politely, to be solid for the length of one step, and then you asked again. [244] The engines could bridge a canyon in a heartbeat. [253] Nobody bothered to ask the mist anymore. [260] Teodor asked ten thousand times, one grey footfall after another, until the cloud thinned to nothing beneath him and the spire stood alone in the thin blue air. [288]

The Old Observatory had been abandoned when the engines learned to see. [300] Ice sheathed it now, a needle of black rock wearing a coat of glass, and inside the ice, faintly, something glowed. [321] Not brightly. [323] Nothing here was bright. [327] It was the dimmest place Teodor had ever stood, and the dimness was the point: this was the last height in the world where no engine-light reached, and so it was the only place the old light could still be seen. [368] Every stone of the spire held it. [375] A thousand years of astronomers casting small warming spells on cold nights, small lamp-lights over their tables, small kindnesses to keep the ink from freezing. [400] None of it had been ambitious. [406] All of it had stayed. [411] If anything was holy, he thought, it was this. [420]

He climbed the frozen stair to the summit platform, where a broken arch stood against the sky, its crown missing, the two halves leaning toward each other across a gap no ordinary stone could close. [455] The astronomers had framed the stars through it, once, the way his grandmother framed her pear tree. [472] Teodor knelt and unstrapped the harness. [478] His fingers were clumsy with cold, and the buckles fought him, and for a moment he was back in the examination hall, fumbling a lamp-light while the looms roared. [507] Then the last strap came free and the keystone was in his hands, heavier than it had any right to be. [528]

It was the wrong shape for the gap. [536] He had known it would be. [542] But his grandmother had cut this stone to hold up a ruin that was never whole, an arch built already broken, and a keystone like that learns something engines never need to: how to accommodate an impossibility. [579] He lifted it into the gap and it settled there as if it had been waiting, wrong angles meeting wrong angles and somehow, gently, agreeing. [604] The arch held. [607]

And the light in the stone, forty years of a small woman's small evening spell, woke and found the light in the spire, a thousand years of cold astronomers keeping their ink warm, and the two dim glows recognized each other. [648] Nothing flared. [650] No cathedral of fire assembled itself. [656] The whole spire simply grew a shade less dark, the way a room does when someone you love walks into it. [677]

Teodor sat down beneath the arch and looked through it at the first stars. [691] Far below, under the cloud, the engines were composing spells of unbearable brilliance for people who no longer had to imagine anything, and every one of those spells would leave the world exactly as it found it. [728] He cupped his hands and cast a lamp-light, slowly, the quaint way. [740] It was not much. [744] It was enough to read by, and enough, he thought, to stay. [756]
