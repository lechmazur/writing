The rebellion had not ended; it had simply run out of breath. [12] Six weeks after the Directorate flooded the sky with jamming satellites, the last four ships of the free fleet sat grounded in the repair bay of the old Halden spaceport, their optics fried, their navigation blind, their crews learning how to wait. [54] Wren waited differently. [57] Every evening she climbed the collapsed gantry to the roof, barefoot on the cold ribbed steel, and looked up. [76] The others thought the bare feet were an affectation. [85] They were not. [88] Beyond the bay's fallen west wall, on the ridge the spaceport builders had been too superstitious to level, stood a circle of eleven standing stones older than any nation, and at a certain hour they hummed. [124] Shoes muffled it. [127] Skin did not. [130] Tonight the hum came early, rising through the steel into her heels like a held note from something enormous and patient. [151] She lifted the opera glasses. [156] They had belonged to her grandmother, who had used them to watch sopranos die beautifully in a theater that no longer existed; the brass was worn to pink where thumbs had held them for a century. [192] No circuits, no lenses that could be blinded by a beam from orbit. [205] Just glass and the slow honesty of light. [213] The sun had gone below the plain, but the sky had not yet agreed to it. [229] This was the margin she lived for, the twenty minutes when the ground was dark and the high orbits still burned with daylight, so that anything up there caught fire against the deepening blue. [263] There: a bead of light sliding east, then another, then the hum swelling in her feet as each passed overhead. [283] She had worked it out on the ninth night. [292] The jamming beams swept the ground in bands, and the quartz-veined stones rang under them like struck bells, too low for ears, not too low for bone. [319] Whoever raised that circle had aligned it to stars they would never reach. [332] Now it was counting satellites. [337] She thought that was the most meaningful accident she had ever witnessed. [349] Below her the bay was a graveyard of intentions: hull plates stacked like fallen dominoes, a drive cone half rebuilt and abandoned, coffee rings on a strategy table nobody had touched in days. [382] The mess had frightened her at first, the way it frightened everyone, because it looked like the shape of losing. [402] But she had spent her whole life staring at scattered points and refusing to believe they were random. [420] Stars became constellations if you were stubborn enough. [428] So did satellites. [431] The third bead crossed, and then nothing, and the hum in her heels sank away into ordinary cold stone. [450] She counted. [452] Forty-one seconds of silence before the next band began. [461] Same as every night. [465] A gap, stitched into the enemy's sky as regular as a heartbeat. [477] Wren climbed down and walked into the strategy room with the opera glasses still around her neck, which she realized too late looked ridiculous. [501] Commander Osei looked up from a cot. [508] "There is a hole," Wren said, too loudly, then too quietly. [519] "In the coverage. [522] Forty-one seconds every ninety minutes, dusk and dawn, when the beams overlap wrong." [535] She did not know where to put her hands. [544] "I am certain," she added, and she was, which was the strange part; she could not hold a conversation about the weather without losing her way, but about this she had no doubt at all. [579] "How do you know?" [583] "The stones told me." [587] Osei stared at her for a long moment. [595] Then he looked past her, at the drive cone half rebuilt, at the people who had stopped fixing things because fixing implied a future. [619] "Show me," he said. [623] At dawn they stood on the roof together, Osei in boots, Wren without, and when the hum came she raised his hand and set it flat against the steel. [652] He felt it. [655] She watched his face change, watched the stones' ancient patience pass through the bones of his wrist and into whatever part of a man decides to keep going. [683] By that evening the drive cone was finished. [691] By the next, the first ship sat on the pad with its nose to the margin of daylight, and Wren stood barefoot on the roof with her grandmother's glasses raised, counting beads of fire. [725] The third one crossed. [729] The hum fell silent in her heels. [736] She raised her arm. [740] The engines lit, and the sound that rolled across the plain was enormous and somehow calm, a thunder that did not argue with the sky but simply rejoined it. [769] Somewhere on the ridge, eleven stones kept time, indifferent and exact, and the mess below her had a shape at last. [790]
