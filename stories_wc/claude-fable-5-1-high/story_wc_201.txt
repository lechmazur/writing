The anesthesia left Ilse the way water leaves a shore, in stages, each retreat exposing more of what had been buried. [21] First her fingers returned, then the exact angle of her left knee, then the weight of her tongue against her teeth, every fact of her body arriving with a precision she had never owned before. [56] The surgeon had promised this. [61] "You will know where you are," he had said, "down to the millimeter." [74] What he had not promised was where she would be. [84]

She was in a forest, and the forest was violet. [94] Lamps hung from cables strung between the trunks, and under their light the bark of the birches glowed like bone, and the lichens burned a chemical green that did not exist in daylight. [127] The recovery cot had been placed at the grove's edge, as she had requested, because she wanted to wake here and nowhere else. [150]

This was the penance grove. [155] The men who had run the facility, the ones who had not fled or died, worked here under the ultraviolet lamps eight hours a day, tending trees that would never see the sun, their skin reddening and peeling and reddening again. [196] The tribunal had called it restorative. [202] Ilse, who had been a mathematician before she was a detainee, called it a poorly defined function. [219] Harm went in. [222] Sunburn came out. [225] Nobody had shown her the proof. [231]

In her jacket pocket her fingers found the strap. [240] Pewter buckle, leather gone stiff, returned to her in a manila envelope along with her watch and her mother's ring, as if it had been her property all along. [269] It had held the mask to her face for eleven days. [280] She could not remember the eleven days. [287]

That was the problem, and the reason for the surgery. [297] Her mind had filed the interrogation somewhere she could not reach, but her body had kept every page, and her body spoke only in flinches. [322] Now, with the new nerves threaded through her spine, she could read the flinches. [336]

She stood, and knew precisely how she stood, and walked into the violet. [349]

The men did not look up. [355] They were forbidden to. [359] They raked needles and pruned dead branches and wore no protection, and the light made them look already half erased. [379] She did not know his face. [385] She had never seen it. [390]

But at the fourth tree her shoulders climbed toward her ears by nine millimeters, and her jaw locked, and her weight shifted onto the balls of her feet, and she understood that this was a brace. [426] Her body was preparing for a blow it remembered. [435]

The man at the fourth tree was small and grey and blistered at the neck. [450] He had not moved. [454] He had not even seen her. [460] And still her right hand had curled, and her breath had stopped at the top of the inhale, and the muscles of her neck had tightened as if against a strap being cinched. [493]

So. [494] It was him. [497]

Her body had done the identification her memory could not, and it had done it in numbers. [514] Nine millimeters. [516] Four seconds of held breath. [521] A heart rate she could feel as a figure. [530]

She had come here for exactly this, because she had believed, in the way that only mathematicians and the badly hurt believe things, that mercy must have a structure. [559] If she could measure the brace, she could measure its decay. [570] If it decayed, there was a rate. [577] If there was a rate, there was a limit, and the limit was where forgiveness lived, or did not. [596]

She stood under the violet lamps and let the brace hold her, and counted. [610]

At forty seconds her shoulders dropped one millimeter. [618] At ninety, another. [621] The man raked. [624] His skin, in this light, was the color of the inside of a shell. [638]

She took the strap out of her pocket and held it, and the brace surged back, seven millimeters, and she made herself stay, and it fell again, slower. [666]

Not a line, then. [670] A curve. [672] Something that approached and never touched. [678] She thought she had known that already, in the abstract, but knowing it in the spine was different. [696] It was, she decided, a kind of answer. [704] Not the one she had wanted. [710] A truer one. [713]

She hung the strap on a low branch of his tree, pewter buckle turned outward, where the light would find it every day he worked. [738] He would not know what it was. [745] That was allowed. [748]

She walked back, and her body told her every step of the way where she was, which was the surgeon's strangest gift, because for the first time in years she had no idea where she was going, and her feet knew the ground anyway. [792]
