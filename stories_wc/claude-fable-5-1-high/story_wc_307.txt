The sycamores around the grotto shed their bark in pale curls, and the sound they made was almost no sound at all. [22] Tam waited beneath them for the researcher who had once been old. [34] She came down the path at dusk, six years old to look at, carrying her notebooks in a satchel that had fit her properly a decade ago. [59] "You're taller," Tam said. [63] "You're kind about it," said Doctor Ilse Varga, and did not correct him, because she was smaller, and they both knew what smaller meant. [87]

She had been seventy when he first came to study under her. [99] She had shrunk a year for every year he grew, and in all that time she had never once told him why. [120] She did not lie. [124] She wove truth carefully, a strand at a time, and left the loom unfinished when she judged he could not yet bear the cloth. [147] Tonight he had asked her plainly, and she had said only, "Come to the platform." [161]

The grotto opened in the hillside like a held breath. [171] Its walls were veined with lodestone, black and faintly humming, and its roof had fallen in centuries ago so that the stars looked straight down into the pond on its floor. [203] Above the water, a slab of the same dark ore hung in the air, repelled by the rock around it, turning by a hair's width every hour. [230] Ilse had measured that turning for forty years before the grotto did anything to her. [245] She climbed the rope to the floating platform with a child's ease and sat cross-legged at its center. [263] Tam followed, heavier, and the slab dipped and steadied. [273]

"Trance," she said. [277] "Until dawn. [279] Watch the water." [282]

He knew the discipline. [286] Breath slowed, eyes half-lidded, the mind allowed to settle like silt. [298] Through the trance the hum of the lodestone became a pressure behind his teeth, and the stars in the pond stopped being reflections and became simply stars, a second sky he could have fallen into. [334]

Somewhere past midnight she threw a stone. [341]

Rings opened from the strike, bright with starlight, ring after ring widening toward the grotto walls. [357] "What appears," she said. [361] He watched them. [364] She threw a second stone, a hand's width from the first. [375] The two sets of rings met and crossed, and where they crossed a strange thing happened: along certain lines the water went perfectly flat, as though nothing had disturbed it at all, while on either side the ripples doubled. [416]

"There," she said. [419] "The still lines." [422] "Two motions meeting and canceling. [427] The water hasn't stopped. [431] It is moving twice as hard and showing nothing." [439]

Tam stayed in the trance, but understanding rose in him slowly, the way the rings rose. [455] He had come to her at nineteen after his brother drowned, wanting the past pried open. [470] She had never refused him outright. [476] She had withheld, and taught him to sit still, and let him think stillness was the lesson. [492]

"You came here grieving too," he said. [499]

"I sat where you're sitting," she said. [506] "I wanted a year back. [511] I held the trance for three days and the ore gave me what I asked for, exactly and forever. [529] Every year, one more year returned. [535] I have been unliving my life in order, and the order is nearly done." [548]

"You could have told me the first night." [556]

"You would have sat here for three days." [564] Her voice was a child's, but the patience in it was older than the sycamores. [578] "I needed you to see the still lines first. [587] Where I'm going is not nowhere, Tam. [594] It is the place two waves meet. [601] I vanish, and you appear a little more, standing where the water looks calm, moving twice as hard." [619]

The sky over the broken roof was greying. [627] Tam turned to answer her and found the satchel, the notebooks, and a hollow in the air the size of a small girl, already closing. [652] The slab turned its hair's width toward morning. [660]

He did not throw a stone. [666] He sat until the rings from the last one had reached the walls and come back and canceled themselves to glass, and then he climbed down and walked out under the sycamores, whose bark was still falling with almost no sound at all. [710] The hollow in the air was gone. [717] He was not. [720] That, he understood at last, had been the entire lesson, and she had withheld it only until it was true. [740]
