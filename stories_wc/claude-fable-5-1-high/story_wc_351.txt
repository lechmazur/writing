Dalia Vance had never been able to think in a straight line. [12] Her thoughts came in helices, each idea coiling back over the one before it, tightening, climbing, so that even her grief had a pitch and a radius. [39] It was why she had become an engineer, and why she had built the coupler, and why she was now climbing down into Sable Canyon at midnight with a broken periscope mirror wrapped in her jacket. [75] The mirror had been their father's, salvaged from the submarine he served on before the sea gave him back. [94] She and Rafe had used it as children to spy over the canyon rim, passing the world back and forth between them at an angle. [119] Rafe had cracked it the summer he was nine, and the crack had become the most honest thing they owned. [139]

The canyon was moonlit and changed. [145] Three years of drought had drained the reservoir that drowned it in their grandparents' time, and now the old riverbed lay exposed like a spine, silver silt and the black stumps of a forest nobody living had seen standing. [184] Everything was in the middle of becoming something else. [193] So was she. [196]

The coupler had been her dream and Rafe's, a neural bridge that would let two minds share working memory the way two hands share a knot. [222] They had tested it on themselves, twins, the closest thing to a control the universe offered. [238] It worked too well. [242] Their memories did not merely share; they entangled. [250] She woke remembering his fever at six, the taste of the pennies he had hidden under his tongue. [268] He woke knowing the exact moment she had first understood a differential equation, and he wept, because it had not been his to know. [292]

Rafe left within a month. [297] He took a job writing code for an insurance firm in a city with no canyons, and he stopped answering, and the company folded, and the dream went where dreams go when their dreamers put them down. [334] Except it had not gone anywhere. [340] It was still in her, tangled in his memories, and his memories were tangled in hers, and every time she recalled the canyon she recalled it half through his eyes. [370]

Memory is not a recording; it is a rebuild. [379] Each time a memory is retrieved, the synapses that hold it loosen for a few hours, plastic, willing to be modified by whatever the mind is doing while the memory is open. [411] Dalia had spent eleven months designing a protocol around that window. [422] Her notebooks were chaos, spirals of ink annotated in three colors, arrows doubling back, but the numbers inside the chaos were exact to the second decimal. [448] Rafe used to call her chaotically precise. [455]

She reached the riverbed and knelt in the silt. [464] She unwrapped the mirror and propped it against a stump at thirty-seven degrees, the angle from her calculations, so that the moon fell into the crack and came apart. [493] Two moons, misaligned, each showing her the other around a corner. [504] Then she began to remember on purpose. [511]

She remembered the night Rafe told her he was leaving, and she let it come the way it always came, through his eyes as much as hers: the lab lights, her own face gone hard, his terror at knowing her too well. [553] Her hands shook. [556] The whole canyon seemed to shake with them, the stumps trembling, the silver silt rising like breath. [573]

She was not here to untangle him. [580] She had tried that and found it was like pulling one strand from a helix; the whole structure came with it. [601] She was here to modify what the memory meant. [610] While the synapses were open, while his leaving was live in her, she looked into the broken mirror and held the two moons in her mind and told herself, precisely, the thing she had decided: that the dream had not died when he put it down. [656] It had been buried. [660] Buried things could be dug up. [666]

She felt the memory take the new shape, the way a bent wire remembers the bend. [682] Rafe's terror was still there, but beside it now lay the thing he had been too frightened to keep: the coupler, whole, the two of them in the lab at four in the morning laughing at a signal that finally matched. [723]

Nine other pairs had been tested before the funding ended, nine people still carrying dreams abandoned by the ones who dreamed them. [745] Dalia looked at the drowned forest rising out of the reservoir's retreat and understood what she would be next. [764] Not an engineer. [767] An archeologist, kneeling in the silt with a broken mirror, excavating what other people had left behind in each other. [787]
