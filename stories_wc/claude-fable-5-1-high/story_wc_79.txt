The city of Halm rode on the backs of nine turtles, each one wide as a harbor, and it had ridden that way for longer than anyone kept records. [29] Except Wren, who kept records on shells. [36] She was the spiral-shell cartographer, the last of a guild of one, and every morning she walked the seams where scute met scute and pressed nautilus shells against the horn to see whose whorls matched. [71] The turtles grew their shells the way the small sea-snails did, in curling ridges laid down one season at a time, and a ridge that curled left meant a westward year, a ridge that tightened meant a year the herd had slowed. [113] It was a pattern language, and Wren was the only person in Halm who still bothered to read it. [132] This was because the reading had become frightening. [140] The light in Halm was always enormous, a white glare off the shallow sea that bleached the rope walks and made the awnings snap, and under that light the newest ridges on the lead turtle's shell had begun to tighten, and tighten, and tighten. [184] The herd was slowing. [188]

On the horizon, where the sea went from green to brass, stood the watchers. [202] Eleven shapes, domed and motionless, too far for detail and too near to ignore. [216] Children were told they were islands. [222] Wren had matched their silhouettes against the curl of her shells and knew what they were: turtles that had stopped, with dead cities baking on their backs. [249]

Twelve years ago an expedition had gone to ask them why. [260] Forty people, led by Wren's mother, had climbed down through the iron gate in the lead turtle's flank, where the shell met the great folded neck, and rowed toward the brass line of the horizon. [295] None came back. [298] What came back, a year later, snagged in the weed of a tide pool on the shell's rim, was a reed whistle. [320] Her mother's. [322]

The council read the whistle as a warning and let the gate rust. [335] Nobody decided it, exactly; nobody oiled the hinges, nobody climbed down to scrape the salt, and after the gate rusted shut the city breathed easier, as though sealing itself in were the same as being safe. [371]

Wren did not argue. [375] She was not the arguing kind; she was the kind who kept walking the seams with her shells while the sun hammered her neck, and who noticed, one glaring noon, that the whistle's reed had been cut in a spiral. [415] Not decoration. [417] Notches, spaced like ridges, curling left and tightening. [425] Her mother had been a cartographer too, and had written home in the only language that could survive the sea. [445]

It took Wren four days under the white sky to read it against the lead turtle's shell, notch by ridge. [465] The watchers had not been killed. [471] They had stopped because their cities had sealed every gate, and a turtle that is never spoken to, never called, never climbed down from, simply forgets it is carrying anyone, and settles, and sleeps, and the sun does the rest. [511] The last notch was a single deep cut across the spiral, which in shell-language meant only one thing: crack. [530]

Wren went to the gate at dawn, before the heat, though the heat never truly left. [546] The iron was one orange scab from hinge to hinge. [556] No bar would move it; the council had made sure of that by making sure of nothing. [573] She put the reed whistle to her lips and blew the spiral: long, short, short, tightening. [589] The note was thin and ugly in the huge bright air. [600] She blew it again. [604]

Far below, something the size of a hillside drew a breath. [615] The shell shifted under her feet, a slow roll like a sleeper turning toward a voice, and the rusted gate, anchored in living horn, was asked to bend with it. [645] It cracked instead. [648] Orange flakes rained past her into green water, and the hinge side sagged open onto a ladder no one had touched in twelve years. [672]

Behind her, Halm was waking; she could hear shutters, and a bell, and someone calling a question she did not turn around to answer. [696] Ahead, the eleven watchers stood in their brass haze, silent, baking, patient as only the dead are patient. [714] Wren did not know if a stopped turtle could be woken, or whether her mother had found out and the answer had been no. [738] She knew that she had questions, and that the watchers were the only ones left to ask, and that a city which never asked was already halfway to standing on the horizon itself. [771] She tucked the whistle into her collar, where it lay warm against the sunburn, and began to climb down. [790]
