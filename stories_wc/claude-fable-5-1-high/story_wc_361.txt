Pell counted her steps because counting kept her hands from shaking. [11] Forty-one thousand from the coast station to the mountain vault, give or take the detours she took when the wind on the ridgeway leaned too hard. [37] Overhead the aurora spilled green and violet across the snow, and she kept her eyes on her boots. [55] Relic couriers were not paid to look up. [63] They were paid to arrive. [68]

The satchel held a sealed clay cylinder from a drowned monastery, and every hundred steps she touched the strap to be sure it was still there. [94] At her throat hung the other thing she touched: a dragonfly wing sealed in amber resin, its veins fine as frost. [115] Ostrava had pressed it into her palm three winters ago, the night before she walked north and did not come back. [136]

"It hums when the sky does," the old courier had said. [147] "Same pitch, same tremble. [151] When you feel it, stop." [156]

Pell had asked why. [160] Ostrava had laughed the way she laughed at every question, as if the asking were the answer. [177] "Because you walk this road two hundred times a year, and if you never stop, it will only ever be a road." [199]

Pell had not stopped. [203] Stopping meant standing still with a relic worth more than her life, meant strangers, meant the shaking getting worse. [222] So when the pendant began to tremble against her collarbone, thin and steady, she nearly kept walking. [239]

But the aurora above had begun to crackle, a sound like a distant sheet shaken out, and the wing answered it note for note. [263] Sympathetic vibration, Ostrava would have called it. [270] Two things tuned alike, one setting the other singing. [279]

Pell stopped. [281]

A figure came up the ridgeway from the south, lantern swinging, and the hum did not fade. [298] It deepened. [300] The traveler was a woman with a child asleep on her back, and she nodded the brief nod of the road, the one that means I see you, keep going. [330] Pell should have nodded back. [335] Instead the pendant pulled a question out of her, the way Ostrava's laugh used to. [350]

"Where are you taking her?" [355]

The woman looked surprised, then set down her lantern. [364] What should have been a passing glance became a quarter hour under the lights. [378] The child was sick; the mountain vault had healers; the woman had never walked this far and was afraid of the stories. [400]

"Which stories?" [402]

"The courier who never arrives," the woman said. [410] "She walks the ridge under the aurora with a bag that is never delivered, and if she stops for you, you get a wish, and if she does not, you get lost." [442]

Pell's hands went still for the first time in three years. [453]

Three winters was not long. [458] Yet somewhere between the coast and the vault, Ostrava had already stopped being a woman who talked too much and become weather, become warning, become the shape a stranger's fear takes on a dark ridge. [493] This was how it went, Pell understood: memory thinned, and myth grew in the gaps, and nobody chose which. [512] Except that Ostrava had chosen, in her way. [520] She had stopped for people. [525]

"She was my teacher," Pell said. [531] "She had a bad knee and cheated at cards. [540] If she stopped for you, it was because the sky was humming and she wanted to know your name." [559]

The woman laughed, and the child stirred, and the aurora flared and the pendant sang with it. [576] Pell walked them the last eleven thousand steps to the vault. [587]

The clay cylinder arrived intact, and the vault keeper broke the seal to log the contents: seeds, a hundred of them, from a garden nobody had seen in a century. [617]

"Will they germinate?" Pell asked. [622]

The keeper shrugged. [625] "Some relics only want to be kept. [632] Some want to be planted. [637] You never know until you try." [643]

Pell asked for three, and the keeper, who had known Ostrava too, gave them to her without a word. [662] She planted them on the ridgeway in the thin soil where the woman had set down her lantern. [680]

Then she went back to counting steps, because the shaking had not left her and probably never would. [698] But now, when the wing trembled against her collarbone, she stopped. [709] She asked names. [712] She let brief encounters lengthen into quarter hours, into shared bread, into the kind of talk that walks a stranger eleven thousand steps farther than they meant to go. [741]

Years on, travelers began telling her about the two couriers of the ridge, the one who vanished and the one who stays, and about the flowers that grow only where the lights hum loudest. [775] Pell never corrected them. [779] Some stories, like some seeds, are better let go into the ground. [791]
