Ines Vardo mapped routes for the Halden Transit Authority the way other people prayed: nightly, precisely, and without expecting an answer. [21] Every curve of every tram line passed through her drafting table, measured to the meter and then inked in colors she chose by feel, indigo for the routes that ran past the harbor, ochre for the hills. [58] Her supervisor called this habit decorative. [64] Ines called it accuracy of a different kind. [72]

The holes began in October. [77] Each morning she found one new crescent punched clean through the previous night's draft, small and curved like a fingernail paring. [98] She knew the shape. [102] Her grandfather Teodor had carried a ticket punch for forty years on the northern line, and every conductor's punch cut a different mark so that fares could be traced back to the hand that took them. [138] His had been a crescent. [143] It had vanished from his coat pocket the week he died, and Ines had searched the apartment twice before deciding that grief did not deserve a third pass. [171]

She did not believe in ghosts. [177] She believed in patterns, and a pattern was only a mystery that had not yet been laid on a light table. [198] So on the last night of December she stacked eleven weeks of punched drafts, aligned their corners, and switched on the lamp beneath. [221] The crescents lined up into a dotted path. [229] It left her own street, crossed the canal, and ended at the old Varga Street roundhouse, closed since before she was born. [251]

Her hands were shaking, but her mind was not. [260] That was the strange thing about certainty; it could arrive trembling and still be certainty. [275] The clock read twenty minutes to midnight, the hour when the city would tip from old bad habits into new starts, and Ines knew that if she waited until morning she would file this under coincidence and never open the folder again. [317] She dared herself the way she had once dared her grandfather to let her punch a ticket: quietly, expecting refusal. [337] Then she put on her coat. [343]

Outside, the mist had come up off the canal thick enough to erase the streetlamps into smears. [360] She could not see ten meters ahead, so she looked down, the way she always had, at the pavement she had surveyed a hundred times for the authority's accessibility reports. [390] She knew these cracks. [394] Tonight they had rearranged themselves. [399] Three fissures met at the corner of Brand Street in a clean chevron pointing east, and at the next corner another, and another, each arrow exactly where the crescents said the route should turn. [433] The analyst in her noted that frost could not do this. [444] The artist in her noted that it was beautiful, and kept walking. [456]

The roundhouse doors stood open. [461] Inside, half the roof had fallen decades ago, and the mist pooled beneath the remaining iron ribs like water in a bowl, so that the space felt less like a ruin than a vault, dark and rounded and hushed. [500] Above the fog, through the gap, the stars were sharper than she had ever seen them over Halden. [518] Teodor had brought her here once, when she was seven, and told her this was where the old conductors came to say the things they could not say on the trains. [549] He had called it the fortress. [555] She had thought he was joking. [561]

On the rusted turntable at the center lay the punch. [571] Brass, worn to a dull shine where his thumb had rested, the crescent jaw open. [586] Beside it lay a single ticket, the old cardboard kind, printed with her name and no destination. [603]

Ines stood very still and let the mystery finish unraveling. [613] For eleven weeks something had been punching her maps, not to frighten her, but to make a map of its own, one she would only read if she treated it as she treated everything, as data worth the trouble. [652] And the truth it had been carrying was not about Teodor at all. [665] It was about her: that she had spent twelve years drawing routes for a city and never once boarded one to leave it, that every color she chose was a wish she filed instead of followed. [701] That was the unspoken thing, and the fortress had kept it for her until she was willing to walk in and hear it. [724]

Somewhere across the canal the bells began to count toward midnight. [735] Ines picked up the punch. [740] It was cold, and it fit her hand, and her fingers shook so badly that she nearly dropped it, but she did not doubt for one second what she was doing. [771] On the twelfth bell she punched the ticket, one clean crescent, and wrote a destination in the blank. [789]
