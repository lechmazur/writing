Ilse Varga wore the azure coat of the Northern Light Service the way other women wore their skin, and by October the salt had stiffened it into a second hide. [30] She stood at the end of the Kaldvik pier where the drift-ice marina began, a field of white plates grinding against the pilings with a sound like teeth. [58] In her pocket the crypt keeper's key was warm from her thigh, an iron thing older than the lighthouse it opened. [79] Old Brandt had pressed it into her palm in March, dying, and said only, "Sunset on the equinox; the bell does the rest." [102]

The bell was the Drowned Bell, hauled from a sunken chapel two centuries ago and hung in the crypt beneath the tower, and Kaldvik believed its tone could absolve the dead of whatever they had carried under. [139] Three fishermen had gone under this season. [146] Their widows waited on the quay behind her, breath rising in columns, and the sun had begun its brief slide toward the water. [169]

Ilse did not believe in absolution. [175] She believed in frequencies, and that was the problem. [184]

For a week she had lain on the crypt floor with a stethoscope against the stone and a tuning fork in her teeth, listening to the ice press the foundation. [214] The drift pack had wedged itself against the tower's seaward wall and was holding it in a tension she could feel in her own molars. [239] The bell's natural pitch, struck as Brandt had always struck it, would ring the wall like a wineglass. [257] She had run the numbers three times and cried once, and then run them again, because the crying had not changed the numbers. [280] That was how her mind worked: the grief came first and the arithmetic followed to explain it, and she trusted neither without the other. [304]

If she rang the bell, the crypt would crack and the bell would go down into the marina with the ice, and Kaldvik would lose the one thing it had kept from the sea. [338] If she did not ring it, three men stayed unforgiven in the minds of the women behind her, which was its own kind of drowning. [363]

So she had adapted. [367] Under her coat she carried a collar of lead sheet she had beaten flat on the workshop anvil, a coil of wire, and a tuning fork she had filed until it sang the same low groan as the pack. [406] The ice had a voice, a fundamental note, and everything that pressed against the tower pressed at that pitch. [425] If she could weight the bell's lip until it rang not its own note but the ice's, the two vibrations would meet and match, and the energy would pour into the pack instead of the wall. [461] Ice that is sung to at its own frequency does not hold; it shivers and lets go. [478]

She turned the key. [482]

The crypt breathed out at her, cold and mineral and faintly sweet, the smell of old bronze and older bone. [502] Gold light came down the stair behind her and lay across the bell's green shoulder like a hand. [520] She worked fast because the sun would not wait, wiring the lead collar around the lip with numb fingers, striking the fork against her knee and holding it to the bronze until the two hums crawled toward each other and locked. [561] Her heart was going hard; she noted it, timed it, decided it was reasonable. [575] Through the seaward wall she felt the pack shift like something turning in sleep. [589]

She took the clapper rope in both hands, thought of the three men and of Brandt, who had never told her the bell was also a weapon, and pulled. [618]

The note that came out was not the Drowned Bell's note. [629] It was lower, wider, a sound with the ice's own throat in it, and it did not stop at the wall. [650] She felt it leave the stone and enter the pack and the pack answer, a vast slow chord rising through the soles of her boots, through her shins and pelvis and the wet hinge of her jaw, until she could not tell where the bell ended and her body began. [700]

Outside, the marina cracked open. [705] She heard it from the stair as she climbed: a hundred plates loosening at once, the grinding gone to a long exhalation, and under it the widows' voices, not weeping, just saying the men's names into the last of the gold. [746]

The sun went under. [750] The ice drifted out on the tide, singing faintly at the bell's borrowed pitch, and the tower stood. [768] Ilse locked the crypt, put the key back against her thigh, and allowed herself, on the numbers, to believe a little. [789]
