The universe hiccupped at the end of history, and Oren Vale, its appointed witness, was the only one awake to hear it. [22] He had been chosen for the dullest of virtues: he noticed things, and he did not look away. [40] For ten thousand years the Registry had trained witnesses to stand at the last moment and record it, so that whatever came after would know that something had come before. [70] The hiccup was not in the plan. [77]

Between one heartbeat of the cosmos and the next, the stars held their breath, and in that held breath Oren found himself standing on stone. [102] It was a library, or had been, built inside a temple that no chronicle mentioned. [117] Shelves curved away from him in a great spiral, coiling toward a center he could not see, and the air held a hush so complete it rang. [144] Every scroll and codex here had been lost in time, misfiled by the universe itself, and the silence had the resonance of a struck bell whose note refused to fade. [174]

On a lectern at the outer edge lay a softcover dictionary, its spine cracked, its cover the cheap blue of a school supply. [197] Among the vellum and gold leaf, it looked like a joke. [208] Oren opened it, because opening things was his profession. [217] The first entry read: ARBITRATION, SPIRAL, n. The settling of a dispute by walking it. [232] See also: HISTORY. See also: YOU. [238]

He understood then what the library was for. [246] The shelves held every account of every age, and the accounts did not agree. [260] One codex said the first city was founded in mercy; the one beside it said in conquest; a third insisted there had never been a first city at all. [289] History was not a line but a quarrel, and the spiral was its courtroom. [303]

The dictionary would be his guide, and it was a strange one. [315] Its definitions wandered. [318] STAR led him through three digressions about grief and one about salt before arriving, with a snap of precision, at exactly what a star was. [343] It was wanderingly exact, and Oren realized that this was the only honest way to define anything. [360]

So he began to walk. [365] He walked outward, away from the hidden center, along the widening curve of shelves, and with every step outward he found himself going in. [389] In toward his own training, the Registry's cold rooms, the oath he had sworn to observe without judgment. [407] In toward the child he had been, who believed that endings were real and that someone, somewhere, kept the true record. [428]

The spiral did not let him choose between the quarreling codices. [439] It made him carry each one a little way, feel its weight, set it down, and pick up the next. [459] Mercy and conquest were both true, the way a word is true in every one of its wandering senses. [478] This was the arbitration: not a verdict but a circuit, walked until the dispute dissolved into a single shape. [497]

He had been sent to witness an ending. [505] But the dictionary's entry for END was the longest in the book, and it wandered for pages before landing, exactly, on the word BEGIN. [529] The end of history was an illusion, and so was its beginning, and so was the line the Registry had drawn between them. [552] The universe did not end; it hiccupped, caught its breath, and went on quarreling with itself. [568] Oren had come to unravel that illusion, though he had not known it when he was chosen. [585]

He reached the outermost shelf, where the spiral opened onto nothing, and turned to look back at the whole coiled library at once. [608] From out here, every contradictory codex was one text, the way a spiral seen from above is one line. [627] He had unified nothing by force. [633] He had only walked far enough to see that it had always been whole. [647]

The hush around him deepened until it resounded, a silence with the full weight of every voice inside it. [666] He wrote one line in the dictionary's margin, beneath YOU: The witness is part of the record. [683]

Then the stars exhaled. [687] The heartbeat resumed, and Oren stood again at the last moment, which was also a first one, holding a cheap blue book that no chronicle would ever mention. [715] He noticed everything. [718] He did not look away. [723]
</story>
