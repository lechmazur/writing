The hedge maze had been my grandmother's idea, and the garden at its heart was mine. [16] She planted the yew to keep the curious out; I planted comfrey, knitbone, and rue in the clearing she left, because a bone-setter needs a place to fail where no one is watching. [49] On the ceiling of the shed there I keep my failures. [60] Every bone I set wrong, every splint that slipped, I mark with a drop of white paint, and once a year I draw lines between them until they make shapes I can steer by. [94] The village calls it witchery. [99] I call it bookkeeping. [103]

Three days after they pulled the small body from the mill-race, I came through the last turn of the maze and found Fen Aldery sitting in my comfrey with his arm held against his chest like something borrowed. [141] Fen, whom the whole parish had buried the day before under the name of substitute. [156]

A fever took the boy at seven and gave him back too quiet, too watchful, asking questions a child should not know to ask. [180] His mother Alys decided the fairies had swapped him and left a stock in his place. [196] She did what the old rite says: set him at the mouth of the maze at dusk and walked away, so the fair folk could collect their own and return hers. [227] In the morning he was gone. [233] When the mill-race gave up a child's body, bloated past knowing, the parish said the fairies had done their part and Alys had only to wait for her true son to walk home. [266]

I do not believe in fairies. [272] I believe in cold nights and fast water and a woman so frightened of her own child that she needed him to be someone else's. [297] I also believe that fear is not a sin, and I have never met a person I could not forgive once I understood what they ran from. [324]

"It's broken," Fen said, before I could ask. [332] "Radius, I think, near the wrist. [338] I fell off your wall the first night." [346]

"You know the word radius." [351]

"You said it to old Marten when you set his. [361] I was behind the hedge." [366]

Nothing in him was uncanny, only a mind that noticed everything and a face that had not learned to hide it. [387] Innocent, and shrewd about it. [392]

I knelt and took the arm. [398] Bone setting is a nostalgic trade; my hands do what my grandmother's did, and hers did what her grandmother's did, and none of us ever saw inside a wrist. [427] But I have changed the craft where I could: traction with a padded strap instead of a knee, splints of steamed ash bent to the arm rather than the arm forced to the splint. [461] Every improvement came from a white dot on my ceiling. [471]

I found the break, felt the ends grate, pulled, and settled them. [483] Fen made no sound, but something small and white dropped from his mouth into the comfrey. [499] A milk tooth. [502] He had been holding it on his tongue. [510]

"I saved it," he said. [515] "Changelings don't lose their teeth. [520] Everyone says so. [523] Give it to her, and she'll have to think." [532]

Which is when the crack opened in me. [540] Not belief; I am too old for that. [548] But he had lain here four nights with a broken arm, planning how to prove himself a boy to a mother who wanted a fairy, and somewhere down the hill a drowned child no one could name had been given his grave. [590] Where had that child come from? [596] Now it had a cleft in it, narrow as the gap between two bones before they knit, and I did not know what would grow there. [622] I yearned, suddenly and without dignity, for my grandmother, who would have had an answer, or a firmer silence. [641]

I splinted him, and fed him, and walked him through the maze at dusk, the hour Alys had chosen to lose him. [663] She opened the door and saw him and did not scream. [674] Fen held out the tooth on his good palm. [683] She looked at it a long time, and I watched a woman's certainty crack the way mine had, and I did not enjoy it. [707]

"I'm not asking you to be sorry," I told her. [717] "I'm asking you to keep him warm for six weeks while the bone sets." [731]

She took him in. [735] He kept the tooth; he had earned it. [743]

That night I climbed to my shed ceiling with the white paint and made a new dot, not for a bone this time but for a certainty, and drew a line from it to the others. [779] The shape it made was new, and I think I can steer by it. [793]
