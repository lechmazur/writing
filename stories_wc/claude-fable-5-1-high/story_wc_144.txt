The fog at Halloran Depot never quite lifted; it only thinned enough to let the rails glint. [17] Marguerite had not seen that glint in twenty-two years, but she knew its hour by the way the platform's cistern began to hum with dripping. [42] Her repair shop occupied the old ticket office, its counter scarred by a thousand small machines laid down in hope. [62]

The boy came in running, which she heard before the door did. [74] "From the Corvina," he said, breathless. [80] "They lift anchor at the noon bell." [87]

Something small and cold was set into her palms, and she turned it the way she turned everything, slowly, letting her thumbs read it. [111] A hummingbird drone, brass and glass, no heavier than an egg. [122] One wing hung loose on its hinge, and the throat, where the lens sat, was cracked. [138]

"The pilot bird," she said. [143]

"Yes, ma'am." [145]

She knew the design because she had argued with its maker in this very room. [160] A ship the size of the Corvina could not read the harbor's shoals from her own deck; the water was too close and too tall. [185] So the bird would fly ahead, low over the surface, and watch how light bent going down through the water. [205] Shallow water refracted one way, deep another, sand and rock each with a different signature. [220] The bird sang what it saw, a pitch for every angle, and the helm turned by the song. [238]

"Who dropped it?" [241]

"Nobody knows." [243]

She almost smiled. [246] Nobody ever knew. [249]

"Tomas," she called, and her apprentice set down whatever he had been pretending to fix. [264] "Bring the fine solder and the cistern lamp." [272]

People assumed a blind woman could not run a repair shop. [283] Marguerite had stopped correcting them years ago; the work corrected them. [294] She did not need to see the wing to hear that the hinge pin was bent, not broken. [312] She guided Tomas's hands with two fingers on his wrist, felt the tiny give as he eased the pin straight, and said, "Good." [335]

The lens was harder. [339] A crack in glass changed how light passed through it, and a bird that misread light would sing a wrong note, and a wrong note would put the Corvina on the Teeth before she had cleared her own harbor. [378] There was no replacement lens in the shop. [386] There was resin, and there was time until noon, and there was the cistern. [400]

"We patch it," she said. [405] "You fill the crack, thin as breath, and we teach the bird what its own patch does to the light." [425]

Tomas worked while she listened to the mist settle on the iron roof. [438] When the resin had set, she had him carry the bird out to the platform, where the cistern stood open and the lamp hung over it. [464] She could not see the light bend through the water. [474] She did not have to. [479] The bird could, and the bird would tell her. [488]

"Hold it over the shallow end." [494]

The hummingbird whirred awake in Tomas's hands and gave a note, clean and high. [508] She knew that note; she had heard it a hundred times over this same water when the maker had tuned the first bird. [531]

"Now the deep end." [535]

The note dropped, but not far enough. [542] The patch was bending the light a hair too much, telling the bird the bottom was closer than it was. [562] A ship steered by that error would flinch from water that was safe and drift toward water that was not. [582]

This was the whole of her craft, really: taking what a thing told you back and turning it into where to go next. [605]

"Open the throat," she said. [610] "There is a screw behind the lens, left side. [619] A quarter turn toward you." [624]

He turned it. [627]

"Deep end again." [630]

The note fell and landed exactly where it belonged. [639]

"Shallow." [640]

High and clean. [643]

She let out a breath she had not noticed holding. [653] "It will sing true," she said. [659] "Run." [660]

The boy ran, and the platform was quiet again except for the drip of the cistern. [676] Tomas stood beside her, and she could feel him wanting to ask how she had known about the quarter turn. [696]

"Everything you fix talks to you," she said. [704] "Most people are only waiting for it to stop." [713]

Far off, past the yards and the fog, the noon bell struck, and beneath it came the long iron groan of an anchor chain climbing its capstan. [740] Then, faint and threading through the mist, a single high note, moving away. [753]

Marguerite turned her face toward the harbor she could not see and listened to the Corvina find her way. [772]
