The drawbridge rose at six, as it had every evening since the reservoir took the low town, and Marit Voss waited for it. [23] Once it stood upright against the sky, the lock gates opened downstream, and for three hours the water fell far enough to show the pavilion's roofline. [49] Nobody crossed after the bridge rose. [55] That was the point of the schedule, and the point of her. [67] The council had hired her to survey the firm shore, to drive stakes where solid ground ended, so that everything below the stakes could be declared lost and filled with gravel by spring. [100] She was good at firmness. [105] People mistook it for not feeling. [111]

She waded out with her lantern, the cold climbing her thighs, and set her transit on the pavilion's drowned steps. [131] It had been a dance hall once, open on all sides, a roof on twelve columns where the town courted itself on summer nights. [155] Now the columns wore weed to the knee. [163]

She had noticed the repairs on her second night. [172] Someone had patched the foundation many times, and not all at once. [184] The mortar came in bands of different gray, one on top of another, like rings in a tree. [202] On her third night a corner of oilcloth had shown through a crack, and she had worked it out with her knife. [224] Inside was a note in brown ink, dated eleven years back. [235] Lene, the floor is level again. [241] If you come back, it will hold you. [249]

Since then she had found nine more. [256] Each was sealed in the repair of that year, each addressed to Lene, each signed only with a mason's mark like a small anchor. [280] He had never written what happened, and she had never written back. [292] The notes were two absences laced together, his waiting and her silence, and the water had risen over both. [311]

Marit did not tell the council. [317] She wrote the stake positions in her ledger with a steady hand and kept the notes in her coat, and if her jaw was tight when the clerk asked about progress, the clerk did not notice. [353]

Tonight the water was the lowest she had seen. [362] The topmost band of mortar, the newest and palest, stood clear for the first time. [377] It was not shaped like the others. [384] It bulged, as though the mason had set something larger than paper and troweled around it in a hurry. [403]

She set the lantern on the step. [410] Its light lay on the water without a sound, a low blaze that did not crackle, and she worked in it with her chisel and the heel of her hand. [440] The mortar was poor, mixed too wet, and it came away in wedges. [453] Behind it sat a brass box the size of her fist, and she had to wrest it free, rocking it while the stone fought her and the lake tugged at her coat. [485]

It was a traveling inkwell, the old kind with a screw lid and a glass bottle inside, the brass worn to the color of a coin held for a lifetime. [515] The bottle was dry. [519] A last note was folded around it. [526] Lene, they are raising the water in the autumn. [535] I have written to you every year with this ink and it has run out, as I have. [553] I leave the well here in case you were ever going to answer. [566]

Marit stood a long while with the cold in her legs and the inkwell in her hand. [583] She knew how to mark where firm ground ended. [592] She had never once been asked what lay past the stake, in the soft country of things people stopped hoping for. [613] The gravel would come in spring regardless. [620] She could not stop that, and she was not the kind to pretend she could. [635] But a surveyor makes a record, and a record is a kind of holding. [649]

She unscrewed the lid, tipped a little lake water into the dry bottle, and worked the crusted brown residue loose with the tip of her pencil until it ran thin and dark. [681] Then she tore a page from the back of her ledger, the part meant for remarks, and wrote. [699] To the mason with the anchor mark. [706] She did not come. [710] I did. [712] I have your letters and I will keep them where the gravel cannot reach. [726]

She folded the page small, set it in the cavity, and packed it with mortar from her own kit, smoothing it level with the heel of her hand. [754] The inkwell went into her coat with the letters. [763] There were eleven other drowned sites on the council's list, and she meant to survey every one before the gravel came, and to dig. [787] Behind her, the drawbridge began to lower. [794]
