Wren Halloway had chronicled the River Tarrow for eleven years, and she had never once written a lie in it. [20] Floods, drownings, the year the mill wheel seized: each entry carried a date and a six-figure map reference, because a river without coordinates was only a rumor. [47] Only one entry was missing. [52] Three years ago her brother Cal went into the water below Ashcombe weir, and she had never written it down. [72] She could not, because the true entry began with her own voice telling him to get out of her kitchen. [92]

The tablets had helped with that for a while, the way fog helps a drowning man by hiding the shore. [112] Three weeks after stopping the medication, the shore came back, sharp and terrible, and with it came a need she recognized from her work: she wanted the facts. [140]

The facts started in a cupboard. [146] Cal's accordion lay where the police had left it, the bellows patched with strips of silver tape where the cardboard had split, because he had never owned anything he had not first broken. [179] When she lifted it, the bellows exhaled, and a folded bus ticket slid from a fold. [195] On the back, in his crooked hand: SU 418 226. [205]

She had taught him grid references when he was nine, kneeling over the Ordnance Survey sheet on the landing. [224] He had used them ever since the way other boys used a diary, scratching where he had been onto any surface that would hold ink. [249] She plotted the reference and it landed on Ashcombe Halt, the railway station closed since 1966, half a mile upstream of the weir. [272]

She went at dusk, because that was when he used to go. [284]

Ivy had taken the halt the way water takes a low field, patiently and completely. [299] It hung over the platform canopy in green curtains and pushed its fingers through the ticket window. [316] Wren carried the accordion by its strap and a torch in her teeth, and she was afraid in the clean, useful way she had forgotten how to be. [344]

The gents' toilet still had its door. [351] Inside, the walls were a chronicle of their own: forty years of names, phone numbers, threats, and the kind of aphorisms that only get written where nobody has to sign them. [382] She did what she did with any flood record: she started at the top left and worked across, line by line, refusing to skip. [406]

Cal's hand was easy to find once she knew to look. [417] He had written grid references in black marker among the graffiti, each one dated, each one paired with a scrap of borrowed wisdom from the wall around it. [445] Beside NOBODY GETS OUT OF HERE SOBER he had put the reference for the Swan and the words: played three hours, tips paid for strings. [470] Beside someone's shaky IT GETS BETTER he had written the reference for the footbridge and: not yet, but the acoustics are good. [492]

She read every one, torch shaking, and did not let herself jump to the end. [507]

The last entry was low on the wall by the cistern, where a man would have to kneel to write it. [528] Above it, in ancient blue paint, some stranger had left: THE RIVER DOESN'T KEEP WHAT IT TAKES. [545] Beneath it Cal had written the reference for the weir. [555] He had written it eleven times. [561] The dates ran from March into April, one every second night, the last one nine days before their argument. [580] The note beside them was a single line: loudest place on the river, only place I can't hear myself. [599]

Wren sat down on the wet tiles. [606]

He never said where he was going, that night in her kitchen, and she had spent three years certain her anger had chosen the place for him. [633] The wall did not say she was blameless. [641] It only alluded, in a dead man's marker and a stranger's paint, to something she had refused to plot: he had been walking toward that water for weeks, and she had not been the current. [676] She had been a sister who missed it. [684] That was a smaller crime, and a truer one, and she found she could carry it. [700]

She pulled the accordion onto her knees and opened the taped bellows, and the halt filled with a sound like a big animal breathing. [724] She could not play. [728] It did not matter. [732] The ivy took the noise and the river beyond took the rest. [744]

Then she took out her notebook and wrote the missing entry, date and reference and all of it, her kitchen included, in the same steady hand she used for floods. [774]
