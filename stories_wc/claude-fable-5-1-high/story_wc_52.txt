The kettle went on at a quarter past five, and Ilse gave herself until it sang. [16] That was the rule she had made for grief in the loft: it could have the time water takes to boil, and no more. [40] Anton would have called it practical. [46] He had spent thirty years putting his head into the mouths of lions, and he knew that the whole trick was the clock. [69]

Rain beaded on the skylight above her bench, each drop holding a bit of the low sun, so that the light came down the color of old iron. [97] She took the ash blank from the shelf. [105] It was a rough block the size of two fists, pale and close-grained, cut from the tree that had stood behind the winter quarters in Rouen where the cats were fed. [136] A storm had taken the tree the same autumn a stroke took Anton, and the yard man, not knowing what else to send a widow, had shipped her a piece of it. [168]

Not a lion, then. [172] People always assumed a lion. [177] He had gone on the caravan step with a cup of coffee, between one sip and the next, while Caspar and the old lioness slept in the straw twenty feet away. [208] Here, and gone. [211]

Ilse turned the block under the iron light and counted the rings on its end. [226] Forty-four. [227] The tree had been planted the year they married. [236] She did not believe in signs, but she believed in arithmetic, and the arithmetic was there in the wood, every one of their years laid down in a pale band and a dark one, wet season and dry, crystallized time she could put her thumbnail into. [282]

The kettle began its low mutter. [288] She set the gouge to the end grain and began to hollow. [300]

The box would be small. [305] It had to be; she had kept very little of him. [316] The cats had gone to a sanctuary in the hills, the wagon to a man in Lyon, the whips and the sequined coat to a museum that wrote her a kind letter. [348] Her sister called it cold. [353] Ilse called it the rule of the cage: you do not leave things lying about that a grief could get its teeth into. [376] What remained fit in a tin the size of a tobacco box, and the tin offended her. [393] Anton in a tin, like biscuits. [399]

The shavings curled off pale and fell into her lap. [409] She worked with the rings, not across them, so that when the box was done the years would run unbroken around whatever it held. [433] That was the practical part, and she knew it was also the haunted part, and she had stopped pretending the two were different. [456]

What she wanted from the wood was a lesson, and she went at it the way Anton had gone at a new cat: patiently, without flattery, expecting to be bitten. [486] He used to say the lion is never tamed, only persuaded, one evening at a time, and that the persuasion never carried over to the morning. [512] You began again every day from nothing. [519] She had thought that was a thing about lions. [528]

The kettle rose from mutter to hiss. [535] Rain thickened on the glass, and the beads ran together and slid, and the light in the loft went from iron to rust, the exact red of the manes when the cats stood in the sawdust with the spotlights on them. [576] The hollow was deep enough now to hold the tin. [586] She tested it, and it sat, and she took it out again, because the lid was not yet made and she was not going to leave him half-contained overnight. [615]

Here was the lesson, then, and it was smaller than she had hoped and larger than she could carry in one evening. [637] Nothing stays persuaded. [640] Not lions, not grief, not the light on a skylight. [650] Anton had known it every night for thirty years, had put his head in the mouth regardless and taken it out in time, every single night, and then had gone anyway on a caravan step, with no mouth anywhere near him. [691] You did not get to keep the persuasion. [699] You got the evening, and the evening was the whole of it. [711] So she would make the box, and it would hold him inside forty-four years of one tree, and the holding would not fix anything, and tomorrow she would need to begin again from nothing, and that was not a failure. [751]

The kettle sang. [754] Ilse brushed the shavings from her lap into the stove, set the blank back on its shelf beside the tin, and went to make the tea, leaving the rust light to finish going out on its own. [791]
