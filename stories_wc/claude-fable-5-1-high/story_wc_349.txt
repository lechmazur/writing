The first rush ended a little after ten, when the last customer carried her blank slate down the glass stairs and the store went quiet. [25] Ora counted the empty hooks on the wall. [33] Eleven slates sold. [36] Eleven people had knelt at her counter, taken the stylus, and incised their worst year into the black stone until the stone drank it and their faces went smooth. [65] That was the trade. [69] A clean slate vendor sold forgetting, and forgetting sold well anywhere, even here. [82]

Here was the Orchard. [86] Rows of glass trees grew through the floors of the library, their trunks full of shelved light, their branches heavy with fruit that were books, each one a pear or plum of pressed pages. [120] Below the lowest root, clouds turned in a slow gray sea, and the whole structure rode them without complaint. [139] Ora had rented the stall by the east stair because the librarians had told her, kindly, that nobody came to the Orchard to forget. [163] She had wanted to be told that. [170] She had wanted, for once, to fail at the thing she was good at. [184]

The bottle sat on the counter where the customers could see it. [196] Moonlight, corked. [198] It was the term of her parole, and it glimmered. [208] Twenty years earlier she had incised a slate for a man who should not have been allowed to forget, and the court that handles such matters had let her out of its cellars on one condition: carry the light, keep it lit, and never again take a story without being asked. [259] When it dimmed, they would know. [265] It had never dimmed. [269]

She did not think of the man's face. [277] She had put that thought in a drawer in a town she no longer visited, the way she put most things she could not carry: a little here, a little there, mindfully scattered, so that no single loss could empty her. [318]

In the lull she pocketed the bottle and left the stall. [329] The glass trees chimed when the clouds shifted. [337] She walked the rows and did what she had come to do, which was not to sell slates. [355] She was looking for the place where stories begin. [364] Every story she had ever touched had already ended; that was when people came to her. [380] She knew endings the way a surgeon knows skin. [389] The Orchard held every book that had ever been finished. [399] Somewhere in it, she reasoned, there had to be a seed. [410]

She did not look for it directly. [417] A vendor of absences learns to measure by what is not there. [429] She counted the gaps on each branch where a fruit had been picked and not returned. [445] Three on the first tree. [450] Seven on the second. [454] The numbers rose as she went inward, toward the trunk of the oldest tree, whose branches were nearly bare. [473] Where the absence was greatest, she thought, the hunger was greatest, and hunger stands closest to a beginning. [491]

At the trunk there was no seed. [498] There was a shelf, and on the shelf, eleven black slates, warm, still faintly humming. [513] Her slates. [515] A librarian was fitting each one into a socket in the glass, and where the stone touched the tree, a bud was swelling. [538]

"They bring them to us," the librarian said, not looking up. [549] "Your customers." [551] "Every one, since you set up this morning." [559] "A person who has emptied a year of themselves wants it kept somewhere." [572]

Ora put her hand against the trunk. [579] It was warm with eleven worst years. [586] She understood, then, that she had been wrong about her trade for twenty years and probably longer. [603] A clean slate was not where a story ended. [612] It was where one was cut loose from its owner and set down in soil. [627] She had spent her life at the place where stories begin, mistaking the incision for a wound. [644]

The bottle in her coat pocket grew warm. [652] She took it out. [656] The moonlight had not dimmed. [661] It had brightened, which the terms of her parole had not mentioned, because no one had thought it possible. [680] Somewhere in a cellar a clerk would be looking up, puzzled, at a gauge. [694]

Ora went back to her stall. [700] A boy was waiting there, thin, holding a coin he had clearly stolen. [713] He wanted to forget something and did not want to say what. [725] She gave him the stylus. [730] She did not tell him where the slate would go afterward. [741] She did not tell him that in a season it would hang from a glass branch, ripe, and someone with a different hunger would pick it and read him from the first page. [774] She only watched him cut, and kept the moonlight where he could see it, and counted what was gone. [793]
