After the shutdown, the ship stopped humming, and Ilse learned what silence really sounded like. [15] It was not empty. [19] It echoed. [21] Her boots on the spine corridor came back to her twice, three times, as if the Meridian were repeating her steps to make sure they had happened. [48] Nobody followed. [50] Nobody had followed her anywhere for six years, not since she had stood in the nav bay and said that the course was wrong. [74] The computer had disagreed. [78] The council had chosen the computer. [84] They did not exile her, exactly. [90] They simply stopped meeting her eyes, and started leaving small gifts outside her cabin: bread, a mended glove, once a child's drawing of a star. [115] Cursed, blessed; on a ship, the words meant the same thing. [126] You were the one who saw what others could not bear to. [138]

Now the computer was dead, and the crew huddled in the galley asking each other where they were, and no one could answer. [161] Ilse could not answer either. [166] But she carried her grandmother's sketchbook under one arm, and on its cover, worn soft as skin, sat a gilded compass rose applique, pressed on three generations ago by a woman who had never seen an ocean. [203] The gilt had flaked from the eastern point. [211] North was still bright. [215]

The observation deck sat at the bottom of the ship, hard against the mass shielding of the drive core, where gravity pooled and time thickened. [240] An hour on the deck was nearly nine days for the rest of the Meridian. [255] The engineers had built it as a curiosity and then avoided it, because coming back up meant finding that everyone had moved on without you. [280] Ilse had never minded that. [285] Everyone had moved on without her already. [292]

The hatch sighed open. [296] Beyond the glass, the stars hung thick and cold, and her own breath came back to her from the curved walls, delayed, doubled, as if someone in the dark were breathing with her. [329]

She opened the sketchbook. [333] Every page was a sky. [338] Her family had a custom, older than the voyage: on each anniversary of the launch, you sat where you could see out, and you drew the stars by hand, no instruments, no plotting, only your eye and a pencil. [377] Her grandmother had called it keeping faith with the dark. [387] The council had called it sentiment. [393] Eighty-one skies, eighty-one years, in four different hands. [401] Ilse had drawn the last twenty-three alone, on this deck, where the stretched hours gave her time to be careful. [421]

She laid the pages side by side along the sill and began to look. [435] Not calculate. [437] Look. [438] The computer had calculated for a century and had steered them, patiently, confidently, toward nothing. [453] Her eyes moved from her great-grandmother's shaky clusters to her own, and the stars crawled. [468] The whole sky had drifted, decade over decade, sliding down and to the left like a picture on a tilting wall. [489] All of it but one. [494]

A single point, faint, unnamed on any chart she had ever been shown, sat in the same place on every page. [515] Eighty-one years, and it had not moved. [522] Everything else swung around it. [527]

She pressed her hand flat to the glass. [535] Outside, in the fast world, days were passing; she could glimpse it, if she held still, in the faint slow shiver of the nearer stars against the far ones, a parallax no one upstairs could ever see because upstairs the hours were too short. [579] The faint star did not shiver. [585] It was not near. [589] It was not behind them, where home had been. [598] It was ahead, and it was the only thing that had ever stayed still. [612]

Ilse laughed, once, and the deck handed the sound back to her a moment later, softer. [628] She turned the sketchbook in her hands until the bright north point of the applique lined up with the faint star. [649] A compass rose without a needle points wherever you turn it. [660] Her grandmother had known that. [665] True north was never a place you were given. [674] It was the one thing you chose to hold still. [684]

The crew upstairs were asking where they were, as if the answer were a room they had wandered out of and might find again. [708] They were lost. [711] But lost only meant you had not yet decided which way was forward. [724]

She took her pencil and began, on a fresh page, the eighty-second sky. [737] It was not an anniversary. [742] She decided it was one anyway: the first day of the voyage they had actually meant to take. [760] When she climbed back up, nine days would have passed, and the others would be nine days hungrier for an answer. [781] She would not give them one. [787] She would give them a direction, and a pencil each. [797]
