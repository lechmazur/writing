<story>
Ottilie kept the labyrinth. [4] Not the tower at its heart, which belonged to no one now, and not the visitors, who belonged to their own troubles, but the yew, eleven acres of it, which had opinions about where it wanted to grow. [42] She pruned it under the moon because that was when it healed. [54] Cut a yew at noon and the wound browned and wept for a season; cut it at midnight and by morning the bark had closed over like a mouth deciding not to speak. [87] Nobody had told her this was remarkable, so she filed it with the other facts of the job, next to the shears and the ladder. [112]

What she could not file was the growth. [120] The hedges did not thicken evenly. [126] They surged in the dead ends where people stopped walking and started weeping, and Ottilie, who measured everything, had begun to keep a ledger. [150] A widow from the village: three inches of new needle in a week. [163] A boy who had lost a dog: half an inch, tender and pale. [176] Sorrow, it seemed, could be quantified, if you were patient and owned a tape measure. [191] What she wanted to know was what the trees were witnessing that made them answer like this. [208]

The tower held the only instrument on the estate that had ever been pointed at anything with intent. [226] An astronomer had lived up there a century ago, and had left behind a brass sextant with its index arm bent and its horizon mirror cracked clean across. [254] Ottilie carried it up the spiral stair on the night of the equinox storm, along with her ledger and a lantern she did not need. [279] Lightning did the work of the lantern, and between the flashes the moon did the rest, pouring through the torn clouds onto eleven acres of black hedge below. [307]

The ledger held more than inches. [313] Visitors who lost their way overnight often slept in the dead ends, and in the morning, dazed, they told her what they had dreamed before she could stop them. [342] A staircase of water. [346] A sister's voice from inside a wall. [353] A door that opened onto the same room. [361] She had written each one beside the corner where it was dreamed, and the dreams, like the growth, were scattered without pattern, or without any pattern visible from the ground. [391]

From the tower, she thought, with the astronomer's arc to sight along, she might map them. [407] The sextant would not cooperate. [412] The bent arm jammed at forty degrees, and the cracked mirror showed her two moons, neither quite where the moon was. [433] So she did what she did with any tool that had stopped being useful in its intended shape: she took it apart. [455] Arm from frame, mirror from arm, the small brass screws lined up on the sill by size. [472]

The storm chose that moment to hold its breath. [481] The wind dropped, the rain stopped as if switched off, and the long stone corridor behind her went so silent that when the sextant's pivot pin rolled off the sill and struck the floor, she heard it ring, once, all the way down the length of the dark. [529] In that silence she looked down instead of through. [538]

The pieces of the sextant lay on the sill in moonlight, and the curved arc, freed from its frame, lay in exactly the shape of the labyrinth's outer wall. [567] She set the ledger open beside it and began, with a pencil, to place each dream at its angle on the brass. [589] Staircase of water, eleven degrees. [594] Sister in the wall, thirty-four. [599] The same room, sixty. [603] When she had marked them all, she held the arc up to the window and sighted along it, and the dreams and the thickest hedges fell into a single line, and the line pointed not at any star but at the moon. [645]

Of course it did. [649] The trees were not witnessing grief; they were witnessing the light grief was carried under. [664] They stood all night in the dead ends with the sleepers, and they healed in it, and they grew toward it, and what they grew was the only record anyone had kept of who had wept there. [701] The astronomer had spent a life measuring the distance to that light. [713] The yew had simply stood in it and kept count. [723]

Ottilie found the pin at the far end of the corridor, where the silence had put it. [740] She did not reassemble the sextant. [746] She left the marked arc on the sill for whoever came next, and went down to check the hedge nearest her own cottage, which she had never measured, and which she suspected, looking at it now under the returning rain, of being the thickest on the estate. [793]
