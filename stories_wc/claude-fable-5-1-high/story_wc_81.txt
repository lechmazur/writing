Wren Halloway indexed pages for a living, which meant she believed in order the way other people believed in weather. [20] The library of Old Sallow had none left to give her. [31] The city had been sunk on purpose sixty years ago, when the reservoir engineers decided a valley was worth more as water, and the library had been sealed inside a pressure dome as a gesture nobody remembered making. [69] Wren had come down the service shaft with a lamp, a ledger, and a question the county archive refused to answer. [90] The relocation deed that had emptied the valley bore the town clerk's seal, and the clerk's granddaughter swore he had never signed it. [113] Everyone above the waterline called that grief talking. [121] Wren called it an unindexed page, and unindexed pages bothered her more than ghosts. [135]

She was three hours into the clerk's cabinet when the blackout came. [147] The pumps above stopped first, a fading drone she had not noticed until it was gone. [163] Then the emergency strips along the shelving died, and the dome went as dark as the inside of a closed book. [184] Her lamp held. [187] She did not panic, because panic was a category error; the air would last hours, and the shaft ladder did not require electricity. [210] What she noticed instead was the silence. [217] Without the pumps, the water pressing on the glass overhead went still, and the reservoir light that filtered down through it stopped shivering. [240] It lay across the reading tables in slow patches, green and gold, the way sun lies under trees. [258] Wren realized the library had been waiting sixty years for someone to stop humming at it. [274] She set the lamp down and let her own thinking settle to match. [287]

The accepted hypothesis was simple: the clerk had signed, the town had been paid, and the granddaughter was mistaken. [306] Wren had come down expecting to confirm it, because most unindexed pages turn out to be filing mistakes. [324] But in the stillness she let herself hold the alternative hypothesis properly, the way she would hold a fragile folio: the seal was real, and the hand that pressed it was not the clerk's. [358] If that were true, the stamp itself would have had to leave this room. [372] Or it would have had to stay, and something else would have gone up the shaft in its place. [391]

She readjusted the lamp, angling it low across the clerk's desk so the light raked the wood instead of flooding it. [412] Raking light finds what flat light forgives. [419] A drawer that had looked flush showed a hairline of shadow along one side. [433] Inside, wrapped in oilcloth gone stiff as bark, was the antique ink stamp: brass handle, boxwood grip worn pale where a thumb had rested for thirty years, and a die cut with the Sallow oak. [468] Beside it lay the pad, its ink long since turned to a black crust. [482]

Wren did not touch the die. [488] She opened her ledger to the photograph of the relocation deed she had copied from the county archive and laid it beside the stamp in the dappled light. [516] The oak on the deed had eleven leaves. [524] The oak on the die had twelve. [531] She counted again, because a tranquilly perceptive person checks herself most carefully when she is about to be right. [550] Twelve. [551] Eleven. [552] Someone had carved a copy from memory or from a smudged impression, missed a leaf, and stamped a valley out of existence with it. [576] The clerk had kept the true stamp locked in a drawer no one thought to open, and then the water had closed over the drawer, and that had been that. [606]

Wren sat with it. [610] She did not feel triumph; triumph was for people who had wanted a particular answer. [625] She felt the quieter thing of a page finally finding its place in the index, the origin of a document traced back to a hand and a die and a drawer. [656] Honest origins were rarely dramatic. [661] They were mostly a matter of counting leaves. [669]

Above her, faintly, the pumps coughed back to life, and the light on the tables began to tremble again. [688] The stillness the blackout had returned to the library began to leave it. [701] Wren wrapped the stamp in its oilcloth and put it in her satchel, then wrote the drawer's position in her ledger, because provenance without location is only a story. [730] She climbed toward the surface, where the argument would be loud and long and would not want to hear about leaves. [751] That was all right. [755] She had learned, sixty feet under a reservoir, that the quiet did not need to last to have done its work; it only needed to be noticed once, and then carried. [786]
