The moon over the orchard had not moved in three hundred years, and neither, for the last nine days, had Ilse. [21] She sat where the ash had cooled, among pear trees that bloomed silver and never fruited, and she waited for the phoenix to remember her. [46] It did not. [49]

It had risen on the tenth night, exactly as the old books promised, unfolding from its pyre of cinnamon bark and myrrh like a thought the sky was having about fire. [80] Its flame was paradoxically steady, burning without a single flicker, a candle that did not know wind existed. [98] And it looked at her with new eyes, eyes with nothing behind them but the deep dark between stars, and it did not know her name. [124]

Ilse had been a beast whisperer for forty-one years. [133] She could talk a wolf out of its hunger and a horse out of its fear. [149] She had talked to this bird, or the bird this one had been, every morning since she found its egg in a burned barn at nineteen. [175] That same night she had eaten a fortune cookie from a traveling peddler's cart and read the slip by candlelight: You will have to become what you keep. [203] She had laughed and kept the fortune anyway, pressed in the same tin as the eggshell. [219] Now the tin sat open in her lap, and the paper had gone soft as skin. [235]

Whispering was not words. [239] It was scent and stillness, the offering of one animal's breath to another until the two breathings agreed. [257] So she did what she had refused to do for nine nights. [269] She opened the second tin, the one she had carried from the spice roads of her youth, and let the orchard smell what the phoenix had smelled when it died. [299]

Saffron, threads red as the inside of a wrist. [308] Grains of paradise. [311] Long pepper, cassia, a single black cardamom pod she had been saving for a funeral and had not known whose. [331] She crushed them in her palm and breathed, and with the breath she conjured. [345]

Not the bird. [348] You could not conjure the dead; every whisperer learned that first. [359] She conjured the shape of forty-one years of mornings, the warmth of a head heavier than it looked resting on her shoulder, the particular way the old bird had tucked one foot up when it was content. [396] She built it out of spice and moonlight and held it in the air between them like a lantern. [415]

The phoenix turned its head. [420]

And Ilse felt the pull she had been dreading, the reason she had sat nine nights with the tin closed. [440] A bond does not cross death for free. [448] The bird had gone into fire and come out new, and if she wanted to meet it on the far side she would have to go partway herself. [476] Already the skin along her forearms was warming. [484] Already her thoughts were loosening from their words. [492]

She did not want it. [497] She had liked being a woman with a garden and a kettle and a tongue that made sentences. [515] She had wanted the bird to come back to her, not to have to go out to meet it. [534]

Become what you keep, said the soft paper in her lap. [545]

The phoenix stepped closer through the silver blossoms, and its steady flame did not waver even now, and Ilse understood at last why. [568] It was not that the fire did not change. [577] It was that it was made entirely of changing, so thoroughly that nothing was left over to flicker. [595] She could be steady like that, if she stopped holding on. [606]

She let the fortune go. [611] It curled and lit in the spiced air, and the heat went into her instead of out, and something along her spine that had never been feathers began, reluctantly, to consider it. [643]

The phoenix lowered its head onto her shoulder. [651] It was heavier than it looked. [657] It tucked one foot up. [662]

Above them the moon, which had not moved in three hundred years, seemed to lean a fraction closer, the way one leans toward two voices in a far room saying something very quiet. [695] She did not know yet what she was becoming. [704] But the two breathings had agreed, and the smell of saffron hung in the orchard where no season ever came, and whatever she was, she was known. [731]
