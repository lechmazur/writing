The arbor at Kestrel Reach grew from the crown of a tree older than the maps, and at night its platform floated above a sea of cloud that swallowed every sound. [31] Mara Voss had kept the midnight watch there for eleven years, and she had learned that the sky spoke in patterns if you were patient enough to listen with your eyes. [62] Tonight the pattern was wrong. [67]

The relay airship Corvid was due at the top of the hour, carrying fever serum for the valley below and three jumpers who would ride it down through the cloud on silk. [99] Mara's job was simple to describe and impossible to forgive if done badly: read the wind, read the stars, and flash the lamp pattern that told the jumpers when to leave the gondola. [132] The valley never saw her. [137] It saw only serum arriving or not arriving. [145]

The radio had been nothing but static since dusk, a hiss that filled the arbor like wind through the leaves, though the leaves themselves hung motionless. [171] Then, at eight minutes before midnight, the static cleared. [180] A voice came through, thin and clipped: "Reach, Corvid. [189] Wind shear at the drop line, we are holding two miles east. [201] Lead jumper is Teodor, first live drop. [208] Confirm standard pattern." [211] Then the hiss swallowed the channel again, and she could not answer. [223]

Mara stood very still and let the information settle into shapes. [234] Two miles east put the Corvid over the Split, a rift in the cloud where the updraft died and anything falling kept falling until the rocks stopped it. [262] The standard pattern would kill them. [268] She could flash a hold signal, but a hold was only a delay, and the serum did not have a night to spare. [291] What she needed was a new pattern, one that said drop now, but drift west, and hold your pull until the cloud thins. [314] No such pattern existed. [318] And Teodor, on his first live drop, would trust nothing he had not drilled a hundred times. [335]

Her hand went to her pocket without asking her permission. [345] The parachute buckle was there, as it always was, steel worn to a dull shine by eleven years of her thumb. [366] It had come off her own harness the night she made her last jump and her first watch, and she had kept it the way other people kept a name. [396] She had also used it to teach. [403] Every jumper who trained at the Reach had sat on this platform with a blindfold on while Mara pressed the buckle into their palms and made them work it until their fingers stopped thinking. [437] Lock, lock, release, pause, lock. [442] Three quick, one slow, one quick. [448] Teodor had learned it here last spring. [455] His hands knew that rhythm better than his mind knew his own name. [468]

Mara pressed the buckle now, lock, lock, release, and felt the sequence rise out of her own muscle like a tide coming back in. [492] A signal did not have to be in the book to be trusted. [505] It only had to be in the body. [513]

She revised the drop pattern in the four minutes she had left, working by starlight on the back of the wind log. [535] Three quick flashes for go. [540] One long flash held toward the west, the way she would hold a buckle open before the final lock, to say drift that way. [564] Then one quick flash, the lock itself, the moment to pull. [575] She would send it not once but over and over, at exactly the tempo she had drilled into his hands, until the rhythm itself became the message. [602]

At midnight the Corvid's running lights appeared to the east, small and steady, a constellation that had wandered off course. [622] Mara lifted the lamp and began. [628] Three quick. [630] One long, swung west. [634] One quick. [636] Again. [637] Again. [638]

The cloud sea took every sound the airship made, so she heard nothing, not the gondola door, not the wind, not the three bodies leaving the light. [665] She only saw them, three dark seeds falling, and then, impossibly late, impossibly far west of where the book would have put them, three pale canopies opening above the cloud like her own signal thrown back at her without a voice. [706] Then the cloud closed over them, and they were gone. [716]

This was the watcher's burden, and she had never learned to set it down. [730] She would not hear them land. [736] She would not know until the radio cleared again, tomorrow or the day after, whether the valley had its serum or three more graves. [760] She had been trusted, and she had answered with something invented in four minutes on the back of a wind log. [781] Mara put the buckle away and let her thumb find it in the dark. [795]
