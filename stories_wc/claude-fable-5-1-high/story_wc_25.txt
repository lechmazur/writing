The port called it the Labyrinth, and Ines Calder was the one who kept it walkable. [16] Containers stacked nine high along the Vasquez Quay, shuffled nightly by cranes that never asked her opinion, and every dawn she walked the new corridors with a paint can and a bundle of maps. [50] The maps were paper because paper did not need a satellite. [61] She never redrew them; she refolded them. [68] A crease here meant a row had been pulled, a tuck there meant a stack had been doubled, and after six years the sheets were soft as cloth and truer than anything on the dispatch screens. [104]

On the night the sky went green, the dispatch screens went with it. [117] A geomagnetic storm, the radio said before the radio stopped, and the aurora leaned down over a city that had no business seeing one. [141] The cranes froze mid-lift. [145] The GPS tags on the containers began reporting positions in the harbor. [157] And somewhere inside the stacks, the night electrician had not come out. [169]

Rennick, the new yard superintendent, stood at the mouth of Row C with a bullhorn and the confidence of a man who had never been lost. [195] "TEO," he roared, and the corridor threw the name back at him in pieces. [209] "He'll hear that," Rennick said. [214] "He'll hear something," Ines said. [219] "He won't know where it came from." [226] Steel corridors did that to a shout; they split it, bounced it, and handed it back from six directions at once. [247]

She had been trying to explain this to superintendents for six years. [259] Tonight she was done explaining. [264] "I'll find him before you do," she said. [272] "And I won't raise my voice once." [279] Rennick laughed, which she had expected. [285] She took the zodiac coin from her pocket and flipped it, which he had not. [300] It was old brass, twelve signs worn to smudges, and it had belonged to a grandmother who worked the same quay when the boxes were wood. [326] It landed with the fish facing up. [333] "Row F," she announced, as if the coin had decided. [343] The coin had not decided. [348] The maps had decided an hour ago, when she felt the storm in her fillings and refolded them twice for luck. [369] But people followed a coin more easily than they followed a woman with paper, so she let the coin take the credit. [391]

Row F was a dead run of forty-foot boxes, end to end, doors aligned. [405] Ines pressed her palm flat against the first one and felt the cold hum of it. [421] Then she took out the key. [427] It had come with the job, on a ring with the others, and it opened nothing anyone had ever found. [447] Rennick's predecessor had told her to throw it out. [456] Instead she had learned what it was for. [464] She tapped it against the corrugated wall, three times, slow, and the note ran down the row like water down a pipe. [486] Steel carried what it was given. [492] Give it a shout and it panicked. [499] Give it a whisper and it listened. [506]

She put her mouth to the vent grille and spoke barely above breath. [519] "Teo." [520] "Stop walking." [522] "Tap back." [524]

Behind her, the bullhorn bellowed again, three rows over, useless. [534] Ines waited, key against steel, and after a while the steel answered. [546] Not a tap. [549] Footsteps. [550] A scuff and a drag, a scuff and a drag, the left boot always a half-beat late. [567] She knew every walk on the quay the way other people knew faces; it was what six years of listening to corridors did to a person. [593] That was Teo, and he was moving, which meant he had not heard her. [607] Or he had heard Rennick and was walking toward the noise, deeper in. [620]

She unfolded the Row F map by feel, found the crease where the row had kinked last Tuesday, and moved. [640] Twelve boxes down, the aligned doors broke, and there was a gap where a vent faced the cross-corridor Teo's boots were dragging through. [663] She tapped the key once. [668] She whispered his name into the steel. [675] The dragging stopped. [678] "Ines?" [679] It came back through the vent thin and close, closer than the bullhorn had ever been. [695] "Left at the blue box," she breathed. [702] "Then follow the tapping." [706]

Above them the sky rippled like something breathing, and every instrument in the port lied. [721] Ines walked backward down Row F, tapping the wall with a key that fit no lock, and listened to the drag of a left boot coming closer with every fold of the map. [754] When Teo stepped out at the mouth of the row, grease-faced and grinning, Rennick was still shouting into Row C. [774] Ines flipped the coin, caught it without looking, and put it away. [786] She already knew how it had landed. [793]
