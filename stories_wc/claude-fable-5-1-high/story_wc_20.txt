The search party disbanded at dusk on the sixth day, when the constable said the river had made its decision and no amount of lanterns would argue with it. [29] Idrenne thanked each of them by name, because names were the only currency she had not yet spent, and walked back alone to the foundry. [54] Rain had been at it all week, washing the slag floors until they gleamed like wet slate, running in bright threads through the channels where molten bronze should have gone. [84] The furnace was cold. [88] She had let it die on the first night, which was the first thing she regretted, though not the largest. [108]

She was called a balanced alchemist in the trade, and she had worn the title without irony for twenty years. [128] It meant she did not chase gold from lead or immortality from mercury. [141] It meant she owned a true balance, a brass instrument sensitive enough to weigh the difference between a page before and after a word was written on it, and she recorded what it told her in a ledger. [179] Nothing she did was faith. [184] The weights moved or they did not, and she had learned long ago that the mystical was simply the factual that had not yet been measured. [210]

On the bench beside the balance lay the zodiac coin. [220] Wren had cast it two winters ago from the tailings of a bell pour, twelve signs crowding the face, the ram nudging the fish, the scales sitting at the top where a boy who worked for an alchemist would naturally put them. [262] It had cracked in the mold, a clean fracture from the crab to the goat. [277] He had wanted to throw it back into the crucible. [287] She had taken it from him and filled the break with gold, the way certain menders across the sea repaired their bowls, so the wound became the brightest part. [316] "You don't hide where a thing broke," she had told him. [327] "You make the break the thing worth keeping." [335] He had looked at her as if she had handed him a religion instead of a coin. [352]

She set the coin on the left pan now and let the beam settle. [366] Then she did what the search party could not: she began to explore the limits. [381]

She spoke her own name into the right pan, softly, and watched the beam. [395] Nothing. [396] She spoke the constable's name, and the baker's, and the names of the nine other people who had walked the riverbank with her, and the beam held level, indifferent, as it should. [428] She wrote each result down. [433] Then she spoke the name of her old master, dead eleven years, and the right pan dipped a hair's breadth and rose again, the way a held breath goes out. [463] So the dead had weight, but it was small and it did not stay. [477] She wrote that down too, and her hand was steady, because the hand of a balanced alchemist is always steady. [497]

Then she said, "Wren." [501]

The right pan fell. [505] Not a hair's breadth. [509] It fell as if she had set a fist of iron on it, and the coin on the left rose until it caught the grey light from the broken roof, and the gold seam through its middle flared like a struck match. [551] She said his name again and the pan did not return. [562] She said it a third time, a tenth, a fortieth, waiting for the limit, the place where a name wore thin from use the way every other substance in her ledger eventually did. [595] It never came. [598]

Rain came through the roof and ran down the beam and gathered in the low pan around the sound of him. [619] She understood then what she had been measuring for twenty years, and what she had failed to weigh until it was on the wrong side of the balance. [647] A name weighs what you have not yet given it. [657] Every unspoken praise, every lesson she had postponed because there would be another winter, every time she had let him carry the heavy crucible so she could keep her hands steady for the scale. [691] That was the weight. [695] That was why the dead grew light: there was nothing left owing. [707]

Wren was not light. [711] Wren was the heaviest thing she had ever put on the balance. [723]

She took the coin from the pan and closed her hand around it, felt the gold ridge press into her palm, and did the last thing a balanced alchemist should do. [754] She lit the furnace. [758] Not to melt the coin, but to redeem the light she had let go out. [773] If a name that heavy was still out there in the rain, it would need something to walk toward. [792]
