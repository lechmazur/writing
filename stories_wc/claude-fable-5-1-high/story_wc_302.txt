The first letter came in March, on solicitor's paper, and it said my father was dead. [16] Folded inside it was his watch. [22] No note, only a line in his looping hand on the back of the envelope: count what arrives late. [41]

I wore the black veil for him, though half the county said a confidence man deserved no mourning, and I was known already as the daughter who dreamed too much, who believed his stories long after the creditors stopped. [80] So I became the veiled dreamer at the window, turning the watch over and over. [95]

It was a strange instrument. [100] Every twelve hours the hands halted, shuddered, and ran backward, then at the next twelve righted themselves again, cyclically reversed like a tide. [123] My father had used it on his marks. [131] A man watching a watch run backward, he said, forgets to watch your hands. [145]

I began to document it because I did not know what else to do with grief. [161] A ledger, a pencil, a chair by the cold hearth. [171] On the third night I noticed that the second hand jumped and the tick followed after, not at once but a breath behind, and that the breath was never the same length. [203] Sometimes the sound came instantly. [208] Sometimes it lagged so long I thought the mechanism had died. [219] Propagation delays, my father would have called them, borrowing the telegraphists' phrase, the interval between the signal and its arrival. [239] Count what arrives late. [243]

So I counted. [246] I marked each delay with a stroke, short or long, and when the hands reversed at midnight I found the strokes reversed too, the pattern of the forward hours running mirror-wise through the backward ones, so that only a full cycle made sense of either half. [292] The cipher unfolded a letter at a time, one word a day, and I did not trust it. [310] He had died owing money to eleven families. [318] Whatever he had left me could be another game, and I might be the last mark. [334] Yet I woke each morning hungry for the ledger, and hated the hunger a little, and fed it anyway. [353]

Fen, then north, then glass, then unspoken. [360] By the tenth day the message had opened like a paper flower: the library of unspoken words lies where the fen glows. [382]

He had told me of that library when I was small. [393] Every sentence swallowed, every confession that died in the throat, he said, was shelved somewhere, and a good confidence man could hear the shelves. [417] I had wanted to find it my whole life, mostly so I could hear the one thing he never said to me. [439]

The solicitor had written that a second letter, the codicil, would follow in fourteen days. [454] That left me four. [458] I went north with the watch in my glove and the ledger against my chest. [473]

The fen at night was a white country. [481] Mist lay so thick the cart track vanished a yard ahead, and I walked by the watch's ticking, timing the echo of each tick as it came back off the wet air. [513] The delays lengthened toward the east, shortened toward the north; the mist itself was propagating his signal, and I followed the shortest lag like a scent. [539]

Then the glow. [542] Windows, dozens of them, hanging in the fog with no walls I could see, gold and steady. [559] I laughed aloud, one short bark, and clapped my gloved hand over the veil as if the sound could be taken back. [581] A dreamer had come this far. [587] It was the daughter of a swindler who reminded herself that light in a marsh is usually a lure. [606]

The door was oak and real. [612] Inside, shelves rose into the mist that had followed me in, and every spine was blank. [628] I opened one and the pages were blank too, until I breathed on them, and then a line surfaced like something drowned rising: I should have stayed, that night. [657] Not his hand. [660] Some stranger's silence, kept here. [665]

At the desk lay a letter with my name on it, sealed with a coin pressed into wax, his old trick. [686] The second letter, and early. [691] I stood a long while before I broke it. [700]

You counted, it said. [704] Good. [705] I have told lies all my life for money, and I told them to you for practice, and I am sorry, and this is the only sentence I ever meant, so I left it where the unsaid things go, in case my mouth failed me at the end. [753] It did. [755]

I sat down among the blank books with the watch reversing in my palm, and I did not know yet whether to believe him. [779] But I opened the ledger and began, carefully, to document the light. [791]
