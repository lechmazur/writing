Ren flew the drone the way other people held their breath. [11] She had the kind of face that looked perpetually surprised, eyebrows lifted, eyes wide and sharp, though nothing had surprised her in years. [34] Clients hired her for that sharpness: she could spot a hairline crack in a bridge from two hundred meters, a stray heat signature in a field of sheep. [62] Tonight the client was a heritage trust, and the job was the old observatory on the ridge, photographed at dusk before the roof came down for good. [89]

The pilgrim arrived as she was calibrating. [96] She heard him before she saw him: boots on gravel, the slow rhythm of someone who had walked a long way and intended to arrive. [121] He was old, wrapped in a coat the color of the hillside, and he carried nothing but a small tin box. [142]

"You are photographing it," he said, not a question. [151] "Before it goes." [154]

"Before it goes," she agreed. [159]

He opened the tin. [163] Inside, on a bed of felt, lay a rectangle of brass no bigger than her thumbnail, one face struck deep with the reversed shape of a letter. [190]

"A matrix," he said. [194] "From a type foundry." [198] "My grandmother cast the almanac here, every year, from a font the astronomers designed." [212] "When they closed the observatory, the matrices were scattered." [221] "Hidden, she told me, where the stars would point." [230] "I have this one." [234] "I have been coming for thirty years to find the rest." [245]

Ren felt her eyebrows climb, which meant nothing, and then felt something under them shift, which did. [262] She had spent her life in rented rooms and airport lounges, scanning other people's places for damage. [279] She thought of belonging as a signal she had never managed to tune, a frequency everyone else received. [297] Here was a man who had walked to the same ruin thirty times for a letter. [313]

"Where the stars would point," she repeated. [320] "Do you know what a long exposure does to stars?" [330]

The dome had a slit in it, rusted open for decades. [341] Ren set the drone to hover inside the dome, lens aimed up, shutter held wide, and let the sky turn. [361] Dusk thickened. [363] The room went the particular dim that is not dark, brass fittings on the old telescope mount catching the last light and holding it like coals. [389] On her screen the first stars stretched into trails, thin bright arcs bending around the pole. [405]

She overlaid the image on the floor plan she had mapped that afternoon. [418] The trails, extended downward through the slit, landed on the meridian line cut into the stone, and along that line the sharp-eyed part of her saw what thirty years of daylight visits had not: a row of slots, each plugged with a stone the exact width of a matrix. [467]

"They only show at dusk," she said. [474] "The shadows fall the right way for ten minutes." [483]

She knelt where the first trail landed, worked a fingernail into the seam, and pulled. [498] The plug came free with a gritty sigh. [506] Beneath it, a chip of brass, an E struck backward, waiting. [517] The pilgrim made a sound she would remember. [525]

They worked down the line together, following the star trails one by one, pulling plug after plug. [542] Not every slot held a letter. [548] Some were empty, taken by weather or by someone long ago who had not known what they were taking. [567] When the light failed entirely they had nineteen matrices in the tin, arranged in the order of the sky. [586]

"Not the whole alphabet," Ren said. [592]

"No," he said, and he did not seem grieved. [601] "A font with missing pieces still prints." [608] "You set what you have and leave a space for the rest." [620] "My grandmother used to say the spaces were where the readers stood." [632]

Ren looked at the tin, at the letters glowing faintly in the dark, and understood something about the signal she had been failing to tune. [657] Belonging was not a frequency you received. [664] It was one you returned to, at intervals, like a man walking to a ruin every year, like stars coming around the pole. [687] The frequency was the returning. [692]

"The roof comes down in spring," she said. [700] "I could come back before then." [706] "Better lenses." [708] "Longer exposures." [710] "There might be slots we missed." [716]

He closed the tin and held it out to her, and for once her surprised face was telling the truth. [736]

"You keep it until then," the pilgrim said. [744] "A thing has to be somewhere to be missed from." [754]

She brought the drone down through the brilliant dimness of the dome, its small light the last star in the room, and took the tin in both hands. [782]
