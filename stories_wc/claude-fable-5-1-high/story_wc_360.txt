The workshop was cut into a seam of topaz, and when the stars still crowded the sky its walls held their light like honey holds a bee. [27] Now the stars were going out one at a time, and Orla measured her work by the gaps between them. [47] One star, one sheet. [51] She was a letterpress operator, or had been, before the fragment. [62] It sat in a dish of iron filings beside the platen: a curved sliver of something that was not gold, warmer than gold, the width of a fingernail. [90] A pilgrim had traded it for a printed prayer and called it a piece of a halo. [107] Orla did not believe in halos, but she believed in what the sliver did when she locked it into the chase with the type. [131] Whoever read the page afterward felt what she felt, and she felt what they felt, the two of them swapped like transposed figures in a ledger. [157] It was the closest thing to an instrument she had ever found. [169] Because she wanted, more than sleep or the return of the stars, to know the mathematics of emotion. [187] Type was arithmetic; every line had a measure, every space a fixed width, and a good compositor could feel a single point of error under her thumb. [214] Grief, she reasoned, must also have a measure. [222] Shame must justify to some margin. [228] The trouble was that people lied about their feelings, even to themselves, so she had learned to print pages that were deliberately ambiguous. [251] Disorient a reader and the truth spills out before the mind can tidy it. [265]

The man who came that night wanted a letter to his brother. [277] "Say I am sorry," he told her, "but do not make me sound weak." [291] Orla answered in a voice that was not hers. [300] It belonged to a fisherwoman who had come three stars ago, flat and salted, and Orla had not yet managed to put it down. [324] This was the cost of the fragment. [331] Each transposition left a residue, and she had worn so many strangers that she now reached for their manners the way other people reached for a coat. [358] "Sorry is a word with two doors," she said. [367] "I will print you a page that opens both." [376] He did not understand, which was the point. [384]

She set the line by hand: I HAVE CARRIED WHAT YOU LEFT ME. [397] It could be gratitude or accusation; it could mean an inheritance or a wound. [411] She wedged the halo fragment into the furniture beside the type, inked the forme, and pulled the lever. [429] Above the open roof, a star in the Plough went out with no sound at all. [445] She handed him the sheet and watched. [452]

What she expected was his guilt, arriving in her chest as a definite quantity she could hold against the fisherwoman's grief and the pilgrim's dread, a third figure in a column that might finally sum. [487] What arrived instead was pity. [492] His, and aimed at her. [497] He had read the ambiguous line, and being disoriented, had felt across the transposition not his own doubled sorrow but the thing she had been hiding underneath her borrowed voices: a room in a topaz cliff with nobody left in it. [538] "You are the one who carried it," he said, and his eyes were wet, and the sentence had only one door now. [560]

Orla gripped the edge of the press. [567] Her mind was perfectly clear and everything in it was moving. [578] She saw the error the way she would have seen a bent em-quad: she had been trying to add feelings as though they were lengths, when the fragment had been showing her all along that they were not sums but exchanges. [619] Rows swapped for columns. [623] Nothing could be measured from inside the matrix, because the measurer was one of its terms. [639] The fisherwoman's flatness slid off her like a wet coat. [649] Underneath was a voice she barely recognized, thin from disuse. [659] "Take the page," she said in it. [666] "Both readings are true, and your brother will choose the one he can bear, and that choice will tell you more than I can." [690]

When he had gone she lifted the fragment from the forme. [701] It was warm, as always, and told her nothing, as always. [712] Another star had gone while they talked; the topaz walls were dimmer by one more shade of honey. [730] In the interval before the next, she set a single line without ambiguity, for no reader but herself, and left the fragment in its dish of iron. [757] I AM STILL HERE. [761] She pulled the lever, and the press answered with the only quantity she had ever truly measured: the weight of her own hand. [784]
