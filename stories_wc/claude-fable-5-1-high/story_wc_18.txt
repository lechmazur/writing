Ilse made mirrors the way other people made bread: by weight, by heat, by hands that knew when a thing was done. [22] She kept her feet on the shop floor and her eyes on the silvering pan, and she had never once wanted to look up. [46] Anselm had looked up for both of them. [54] He was the astronomer, and their house stood at the last lit street before the orchard district, where the fog had sat for three hundred years, thick as wool, swallowing the apple rows and the boundary stones and finally the difference between ground and sky. [99] People who walked in did not come back, because nothing in there told them which way was down. [117]

Anselm dreamed of the orchard as it had been, stars hung over blossom, and one night the dream pulled him out of bed and through the door. [144] He took his sextant. [148] In the morning Ilse found it at the fog's edge, index arm snapped, horizon glass cracked clean across, as if the instrument had tried to measure the boundary and the boundary had refused. [181]

That was the winter before. [186] Now the dream had come to her. [193] Every night she stood among heavy boughs with Anselm beside her, breathing slow, pointing out Deneb and the Plough. [212] Every dawn she woke to find the dream gone from the bed like a cat through a window, and she knew where it went: back into the fog, where he was. [243] A dream that ran was a dream that could be caught, if you built the right trap. [260]

She reground the sextant's horizon glass herself, half silvered and half clear, the way it wanted to be, and she fixed the index arm with brass from a candlestick. [289] Then she made forty small mirrors, each one weighted at the base with lead so it would stand upright on frozen earth and not tip, because she did not trust anything in that district to stay where it was put. [329] A sextant needed two things, Anselm had told her once: a star and a horizon. [344] The fog had eaten the horizon. [350] She would recreate one. [354]

She went in at twilight, when frost came up shimmering on the dead grass and the last light turned the fog to something like the flank of a glass mountain, cold and lit from inside and going on forever. [393] She tied a rope to the last gatepost and paid it out behind her. [407] Every ten paces she planted a mirror, angled so it caught the one behind it, so that a line of silver ran back through the murk toward the lamp she had left burning at the gate. [443] A level line. [446] A made horizon, low and true, dividing the fog into a below and an above. [461]

The trees rose around her, black and knotted, hung with frost instead of fruit. [475] The fortieth mirror went down beside a stump. [483] She raised the sextant to her eye and there in the horizon glass was her own line of light, steady, and above it, nothing. [507] No star. [509]

Then she heard breathing. [513] Not her own, though it was close to it, slow and long, the breath of someone standing in an orchard under stars with nowhere to hurry to. [540] She knew that rhythm. [544] She had slept beside it for eleven years and dreamed beside it every night since. [559]

Ilse planted her boots, let her lungs empty, and waited for the fog to draw breath. [575] When it did, she breathed with it. [582] In. [583] Out. [584] Her breath frosted the horizon glass and cleared; the fog's breath frosted the mirrors along her line and cleared, in the same measure, so that the whole silver road pulsed like something alive. [617] On the tenth breath the fog above her horizon thinned. [627] On the twelfth there was a point of light in it, small and white, and she did not know or care whether it was Deneb or a lamp or the dream itself looking back at her. [663] She swung the index arm until the star sat down on her made horizon, and she locked the screw. [682] Caught. [683]

The breathing stopped being somewhere else. [689] It was beside her, at her shoulder, and a hand that had forgotten the feel of ground found her arm and held on. [712] "Which way is down," Anselm said, not quite a question. [722] "Where I am," said Ilse. [727]

She did not look up to see if the star was still there. [740] She put the sextant in her coat, took the rope in one hand and him in the other, and walked them both back along the line of mirrors, breathing in step, toward the one boundary she had never let the fog erase: the lamp at the gate, the edge of home. [791]
