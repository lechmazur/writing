The cobalt observatory kept only one light that never went out. [11] It was a candle in a brass cup at the center of the star hall, and the astronomers swore it had burned since the tower was raised, its flame so steady they used it to calibrate their clocks. [49] Wren embroidered beside it every autumn, when the sky cleared and the commissions came in: galaxy charts stitched in silver thread on indigo cloth, each pinprick of a star placed by measurement. [81] The astronomers thought her simple. [86] She hummed while she worked, mislabeled Cassiopeia as "the chair," and laughed at things nobody else had said. [104] She let them think it. [109] A fool at the table hears every story a wise woman is asked to leave the room for. [127] That was why she stitched here at all. [135] Not for the coin, which was thin, but for the stories that fell between the astronomers' sentences like dropped pins, the ones nobody thought were worth picking up. [163]

The first wrong thing was the flame. [170] For eleven years it had stood straight as a needle. [180] That October it began to lean, a little after midnight, always toward the east stair. [195] Wren said nothing, but she began to count. [203] Each night she stitched one star for every heartbeat between the lean and the recovery, and by the fourth night she had synchronized her thread to the footsteps of someone who came through a door that was supposed to be sealed. [244] Three people, by the weight of the draft. [252] Never the same three. [256]

The second wrong thing was in the charts. [264] Wren did not embroider a galaxy once. [271] She embroidered it several times in her head first, each version a stitch or two different, adjacent skies that almost were, so that when she committed the needle she knew the shape of every error she was not making. [310] It made her slow. [314] It also made her notice when the master's new ephemeris, handed down to be copied, placed the winter eclipse a full day late. [337] Not a copyist's slip. [341] A deliberate neighbor of the truth, close enough to pass, far enough to matter. [355] She stitched it as given and hummed. [362]

The observatory was so quiet that autumn that she could hear the leaves letting go outside. [378] The silence had a way of echoing, as if every unsaid thing bounced once off the cobalt dome before it settled into her lap. [402] She listened to it the way she listened to everything, with her mouth slightly open and her eyes on the thread, and the stories came. [427] A junior astronomer, thinking her deaf to meaning, told a colleague that the queen would watch the eclipse from the palace roof and that the guard rotated at the shadow's edge. [458] The master, thinking her foolish, asked whether she could stitch a chart that showed the sky as it should be rather than as it was, and laughed when she asked if the sky had been misbehaving. [494] The leaning flame, thinking nothing, kept its appointments. [502]

She began to unravel. [506] Not the conspiracy, not yet, but the chart itself, the false one, pulling the silver thread out star by star in the dark hours while the candle leaned and recovered. [536] Each thread she pulled she laid beside the one from her true copy, and where they diverged she knotted them together. [557] By the end of the week she held a small ugly cord of knots, and each knot was a lie about the sky, and the lies had a shape. [586] Their shape was a schedule. [591] On the day the false eclipse was promised, the guard would stand in the wrong place looking at the wrong hour, and three people who knew the true hour would not. [622]

Wren finished the false chart beautifully. [628] Then she sewed the knotted cord along its hem, where a fool might add a fringe. [644] She carried it to the palace steward with the master's own seal on the wrapping, curtsied badly, and told him it was a gift for the queen, for luck, and that the fringe was the best part, because it was the sky that almost happened. [689] He looked at her the way everyone did. [697] Then he looked at the fringe, and she saw him find the eclipse on the chart, and count the knots beneath it, and she saw his face go still. [726]

She walked back through the falling leaves and sat down beside the candle. [739] The flame stood straight. [743] Somewhere above her, the dome held its silence and gave it back to her, one echo at a time, and she threaded her needle and waited to hear what would happen next. [775]
