Ilse Varro had been a chess tutor once, before the duke's wager and the blunder on the forty-first move, before the word exile was read aloud in a hall she was no longer permitted to enter. [36] Now she walked into Sallow Hollow, where nothing walked at all. [47] A woman stood frozen at the well with the bucket half raised, water arrested in a bright rope from the lip. [68] A boy hung mid-leap over a puddle. [75] Smoke from a chimney stood like a gray column that had forgotten how to lean. [90] Over the hills to the west, thunder rolled, real thunder, moving, the only thing in the valley that still kept time. [111]

Ilse's thumb found the platinum labyrinth in her coat pocket, a palm-sized disc etched with a single winding path, and began to trace it. [135] She had always fidgeted, but productively; the duke's boy used to laugh at how her hands worked the disc under the table while her face stayed still as a pond. [165] The hands thought so the face did not have to. [175]

The hush led her to the meeting hall. [183] It was not an absence of sound but a presence of it, a silence with weight and weave, and inside the hall she found its source: a tapestry the width of the wall, showing Sallow Hollow itself on an ordinary afternoon, the well, the boy, the leaning smoke. [231] Someone had woven the hush into the cloth, knot by knot, and the village had obeyed it. [248]

Only the border moved. [252] There the threads had frayed, hundreds of loose ends stirring in air that stirred nowhere else, and among them Ilse saw a pattern she knew in her thumb before she knew it in her mind. [287] The frayed threads turned the way the labyrinth turned. [296] Left, left, a long sweep right, a doubling back. [305] Not all of them, though. [310] Some ends had simply rotted, and to pull the wrong one might tear the hush apart all at once, the way a tent collapses when you cut the ridge line. [340] She would have to winnow them, true thread from dead thread, and she would have to do it in the lulls, because each time the thunder spoke the loose ends shivered and lost their shape. [375]

Ilse sat on the cold floor and went still. [384] Not the stillness of the village, which was a stopped clock, but the stillness she had learned across a thousand boards: repose with teeth in it, the calm of a thing that has decided to fight by refusing to hurry. [424]

Thunder, then the lull. [428] Her thumb walked the labyrinth's first turn; her other hand found the matching thread, tested its tension, and set it aside as true. [451] A rotted end beside it she left to hang. [460] Thunder, lull, another turn. [464] As the true threads gathered under her fingers, they began to spell something, the way a good opening spells its intention to anyone who has eyes. [490] The weaver had seen a fire coming, or a fever, or soldiers; the border did not say which. [508] It said only: I stopped them so nothing could hurt them, and then I could not bear that nothing could happen to them either, and I began to unpick it, and I did not finish. [543] Forgive me. [545]

Ilse's hands stopped. [548] On the forty-first move she had pushed a pawn to keep a position safe, and safety had cost her the game, the duke his charter, and a town its name. [578] For three years she had replayed it, freezing herself at that square as surely as this weaver had frozen a village, telling herself that if she only stayed there long enough she would find the move that made it never have happened. [620] There was no such move. [625] There was only the next one. [631]

The thunder came closer now, the lulls shortening, the storm no longer content to be distant. [647] She gathered the true threads into one fist, every winnowed strand, and felt the whole hush pull taut against her knuckles like a held breath. [672] "I forgive you," she said to the weaver, and found that her mouth had said it to someone else as well. [693] She pulled. [695]

The hush came away in her hand like a sheet drawn off furniture. [708] Outside, water fell into a bucket. [714] A boy landed in a puddle and shouted at the cold of it. [727] Smoke leaned, and the first real rain hit the roof, and whatever the weaver had feared was somewhere out there again, possible, moving, alive. [751]

Ilse put the platinum labyrinth back in her pocket, and for the first time in three years her thumb did not reach for it. [775] She walked out into the weather to see what the next move was. [788]
