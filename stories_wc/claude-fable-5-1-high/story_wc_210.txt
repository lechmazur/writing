The last wish was granted at dusk, and by morning Ione was the only person left in Marrow Cove. [19] The paper lanterns still hung along the harbor wall, four hundred of them, one for every wish the amber bottle had ever swallowed. [42] None of them were lit. [47]

She walked the empty street with the bottle in her coat pocket, feeling its warmth through the wool. [65] It had never had an opening. [71] That was the first thing she had noticed about it, thirty years ago, when she was an apprentice and the town elders had brought it to her master's workshop to be understood. [103] Wishes went in through the glass and never came back out. [114] What came out instead was the thing wished for: a healed knee, a full net, a daughter home from the war. [135]

Her master, the town alchemist, had spent his life trying to open it. [148] Ione had spent hers watching what it did to people. [158] The fishermen stopped mending nets. [163] The midwife stopped learning. [167] The shipwrights wished for hulls and forgot the grain of oak. [178] By the time the bottle grew dim and the council announced there was room for one final wish, Marrow Cove could no longer feed itself, and everyone knew it. [207] So they wished, together, to be somewhere else. [215] Somewhere easy. [217]

They had asked her to add her voice to it. [227] She had refused, and they had left without her, and the bottle, now a plain warm stone of amber, had been left on her workbench like a tip. [255]

She could have gone. [259] She knew exactly how the somewhere-else would feel: soft, endless, a bed that never needed making. [275] It was the same feeling the bottle had been handing out for three centuries, and she had watched it hollow a whole town from the inside. [301] Comfort that cost nothing was not comfort. [308] It was anesthesia. [311]

Ione climbed to the workshop and found what she expected: nothing. [322] Her master's notebooks, the town charter, the harbor charts, the recipes for mortar and dye and salt-cure, all of it had been wished away over the years by people who found it easier to ask than to read. [360] The shelves held dust and one cracked crucible. [368] What remained of Marrow Cove existed in a single place now, and that was behind her eyes. [385]

She sat on the floor with the bottle in her lap and began. [398] Not remembering, exactly. [401] Remembering was a drawer you opened and shut. [409] This was holding: the tide table and the kiln temperature and the ratio of lime to sand, all lifted into the front of her mind at once and kept there, turning, while she asked how they might fit together differently. [449] It hurt the way lifting hurts. [455] She could keep perhaps seven things aloft before one slipped, so she chose the seven that mattered and let the rest wait their turn. [479]

A town that could not wish would need to teach. [489] So the workshop became a school in her mind, and the school needed a kiln, and the kiln needed the clay bank north of the cove, which meant a road, which meant the quarry her grandfather had described but never shown her. [531] She was not rebuilding Marrow Cove. [537] She was conceiving a place that had never existed: one where every object had a maker who could be named. [557]

When it was dark she went down to the harbor wall with a taper. [571] The lanterns were paper, and paper burns, and she had made paper once, badly, from rag and river water. [590] She lit them one by one. [596] Not for wishes. [599] For anyone out on the water who might be looking for a shore that still expected something of them. [618]

The amber bottle sat on the wall beside her, sealed, useless, warm. [630] She would set it in the lintel of the school so that every child who walked under it would learn the first lesson of the new town: this is a thing that gives you what you want, and it has no opening, and that is why we are still here. [680]

Three nights later a boat came in. [687] Two brothers who had wished themselves to the easy place and found they could not sleep there. [704] They asked what she needed. [709]

Ione, who had been holding the road and the kiln and the quarry in her head for seventy hours without a single page to set them on, said the first true thing that came to her. [745] Paper. [746]

Then she taught them how to make it. [754]
