After the great fire, the plaza was the only floor left in the city that still remembered being a floor. [20] Above it hung the dome, cracked into a thousand panes, a shattered sky that let the real one drip through in cold grey slices. [44] Mara walked its mosaic stepping stones the way the blind walk a familiar room, because to her eyes the present was fog and only the past held edges. [72] The fire had done that to her. [79] She had always seen backward a little, the way some people hear a note before it is played, but the night the minaret came down she had been standing beneath it, and the heat had fused her to that moment. [119] Now every stone she touched showed her what it had been, and nothing she touched showed her what it was. [139]

Her arms had burned to the bone. [146] The smith had built her new ones from salvaged rail steel, hinged at the elbow, cold as well water even in sun, and she had thanked him and meant it. [176]

The council had gathered at the plaza's center, where the Naming Stone stood, and they had brought the boy. [195] He was perhaps twelve, ash-streaked, wrists bound in rope that no one had thought to loosen in three days. [214] "Firestarter," said Ostrel, the miller, and the word went around the ring of survivors like a cup being passed. [233] "Carve it on the Stone and let him carry it." [243]

This was the old custom: a name given at the Naming Stone could not be taken back, and a child named for his crime would answer to it until he died. [274] Mara had spoken against the custom for thirty years, quietly, at every council, in a voice that never rose. [293] They called her the soft knife. [299] They had never once listened. [304]

She knelt on the seventh stepping stone, the one nearest the minaret's stump, and laid her steel palm flat against it. [325] Steel did not see. [329] She needed skin, so she bent and pressed her cheek to the stone instead. [343]

The past came up through it like cold water through cloth. [354] A boy running across the plaza at night with a lamp held high, not to burn but to see, because his mother had stopped breathing and the physician lived past the minaret. [386] A loose stone. [389] A fall. [391] Oil spreading toward the prayer-cloths hung to dry on the minaret's lower rail. [404] Then wind, which no one names. [410]

She lifted her head. [414] Something glittered in the crack where the stone had chipped, and she worked it loose with a steel fingertip: a single tessera from the minaret's mosaic, lapis blue, one corner sheared away. [446] It had fallen from the tower and lodged in the stepping stone the same night the boy fell. [464] The stone's chipped pattern, she saw now, was not damage at all but an interrupted word in the old tile script, and the tessera completed it. [490] Lamp-bearer. [491]

She stood, and the council quieted, because a woman with steel arms rising from her knees has a gravity to her. [512] "I have read the stone," she said. [519] "The plaza has already named him." [525]

Ostrel's mouth tightened. [528] "The plaza does not name." [533]

"It did tonight." [536]

She crossed to the boy and, though her hands could not feel him, closed her steel arms around his shoulders with a care she had to calculate rather than sense, as one carries an egg in a bucket. [574] He was shaking. [577] The metal must have been cold against him, and she was sorry for that, and could not help it. [596]

"His name is Lamp-bearer," she said to the ring of faces she could barely see. [611] "Carve that, if you must carve." [617]

"It is a lie," someone said. [623]

"It is a mercy," Mara answered, "and I know it is the wrong shape." [637] "Mercy is supposed to come after the debt is paid, and I am giving it before, and to the wrong person, and for a fire that took my own hands." [667] "I only know what I saw." [673]

There is a particular silence that falls when people are deciding whether to be ashamed, and it fell then, under the broken dome, while the grey sky leaked down through the cracks and settled on everyone's shoulders like more ash. [713] Ostrel cut the rope. [717] He did not apologize, and she had not expected it; the soft knife does not need thanks, only to keep cutting. [738]

Later, when the plaza had emptied, the boy asked her why she had not simply told them the truth of what she saw. [761] "Because they would have argued with the truth," she said. [771] "Nobody argues with a stone." [776] She pressed the tessera into his palm and did not need to see what it had been. [793]
