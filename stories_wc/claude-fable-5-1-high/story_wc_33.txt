The Seam had no post office, so it had Teodor. [10] Both republics claimed the four-kilometer strip and neither would deliver into it, which left one man on a bicycle with a canvas sack as the only thing that crossed without a permit. [42] The arrangement was tolerated on one condition: a single drop, once a day, at the cobalt arcade, at twilight, when the border drones swapped batteries and the sky went briefly deaf. [73]

Teodor kept the condition the way he kept his uniform: pressed, exact, and worn slightly wrong. [89] He saluted the northern sentry and the southern one with the same two fingers, signed both ledgers in the same neat hand, and carried in his inside pocket the letters that neither ledger knew about. [124]

The arcade had been a market once. [131] Now it was a tunnel of blue tile and dead shopfronts, the glaze so deep that at dusk the walls seemed to hold water. [155] Ines was waiting on the steps, knees drawn up, eleven years old and already good at the Seam's particular quiet, the kind you could crack with a cough. [183]

"Anything?" she said. [186]

"Not today." [188]

She nodded like an adult and he hated it. [197]

Her mother had gone north eight months ago on a work visa and a promise: I will send for you before the rains, and I will send you a storm of your own so you can practice. [234] In the north they sold weather in glass, small sealed thunderheads that flickered on a shelf, a novelty there and contraband here, where anything that made light in the dark was classed as a signal. [269] The letters had come weekly for three months. [277] Then nothing. [279]

Teodor had fostered Ines because nobody else was legally allowed to, and because he had learned, over twenty years of other people's envelopes, that a child left waiting at a mail drop is a kind of unfinished sentence. [317] He did not believe the mother had forgotten. [325] He believed in absences, and he had begun to link them. [336]

Rojas on Fourth Street stopped hearing from his brother the same week Ines did. [350] The widow Anselm's pension slips from the north stopped nine days later. [362] Teodor kept the dates on the back of an old route card, a column of silences, and when he laid them against each other they made a shape: not a scatter but a line, everything from one northern postal district, all cut off inside the same fortnight. [409] Silence that organized itself was not forgetting. [416] It was a wall. [420]

So people had done what people do around walls. [429] Small drones, no bigger than a loaf, came over at dusk with parcels taped to their bellies, and the big border drones hunted them, and the arcade roof was pocked with the wreckage of the ones that lost. [467] Teodor had never touched one. [472] A mailman who picked up smuggled parcels was not a mailman anymore, only a smuggler with a better hat, and the Seam needed exactly one man both sides could pretend not to see. [505]

The sky went deaf. [509]

In the hush he heard it: a stutter of rotors, then glass on tile, high in the arcade's far end. [529] Ines was already running. [533] He followed, because the blue walls were flickering, and the flicker was not electric. [547]

The drone lay broken under a shuttered pharmacy, and beside it, unbroken, rolled a bottle the size of a fist. [567] Inside it a thunderhead the color of the tiles turned over on itself, and every few seconds a thread of lightning crossed it, and the cork glowed. [594] Taped to the drone's belly was a label in a hand Teodor had sorted weekly for three months, then not at all. [616] Ines. [617] Care of the mailman. [621]

He stood with the bottle in one hand and twenty years in the other. [635] He had wondered, on the long flat ride between checkpoints, where broken promises went to heal, whether there was a district for them, a sorting room. [661] Now he thought the answer was smaller and more embarrassing: they healed wherever somebody finally stopped pretending not to see. [681]

Above the roof the border drones woke, one by one, humming back into the dark. [696]

Teodor took out his pen. [701] On the label, under the mother's writing, he wrote the date, the arcade, and his initials, the way he signed every ledger, and put the bottle in Ines's hands. [730]

"Delivered," he said. [733]

Her face in the small lightning was not an adult's at all. [745]

"She didn't forget," Ines said. [750]

"No," he said. [753] "They just closed the road. [758] And there's more than one road." [764]

Tomorrow he would salute both sentries, sign both ledgers, and carry in his inside pocket a route card with a column of dates and, beside each one, a question about drones. [795]
