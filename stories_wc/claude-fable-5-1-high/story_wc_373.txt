The market beneath Culler's Pier opened when the tide went out and closed when the sun came up, and in between nobody spoke above a whisper. [26] The harbor watch walked the boards overhead, and the pier was so rotten that lantern light came down through the gaps in long yellow needles, so a raised voice would go up just as easily. [61] They called it the truce of hush. [68] Mara Vell had grown up inside it. [75] Her father had run corn liquor down the coast in a skiff with muffled oars, and he had brought her here from the time she could keep quiet, which was early. [106] She found the whispering a comfort even now, the way some people find the sea a comfort, knowing full well what it does to boats. [131]

Tonight she had until sunrise, and she had come to hear a sound. [144] Her father had told her once that every trade under the pier had its own sound: the tap of a coin on a barrel, the wet cork, the click of a knife folding shut. [178] He said trust breaking had a sound too, and that he hoped she never learned it. [194] Then Amos Reed, his partner of eleven years, had sold the location of the still to the excise men and taken the watch fob off his coat while he lay bleeding in the marsh. [228] Mara had been in the skiff, holding the oars still, and had heard nothing at all. [244] She wanted the sound. [248] She wanted to capture it and keep it, so she would know it next time. [263]

Reed's stall stood near the third piling, where the sand stayed driest. [275] The fob hung from a nail above his ledger: a brass anchor gone green at the flukes, on a chain her grandfather had worn. [299] He was showing it to a buyer, a woman in a sealskin coat, and Mara understood at once what he was selling. [321] The fob was known on this coast; whoever carried it carried Tom Vell's routes, his buyers, his name. [339] Reed was selling her father a second time. [347]

She meant to thwart it, and she did not manage it cleanly. [359] The first time she approached, she got as far as the ledger and found she could not unclench her jaw, and she turned aside and bought a paper of salt she did not need. [393] The second time she had a knife in her sleeve and a speech in her head, and halfway there she heard boots on the planks above and the whole market went still, a hundred breaths held together, and by the time the boots passed her speech had gone soft and stupid. [444] The third time she simply stood at the edge of his lantern and waited, and that was the one that worked, because Reed looked up and saw Tom Vell's face on a girl and put his hand flat over the ledger like a man covering a wound. [491]

"Mara," he whispered. [494] People had called her curiously steadfast since she was small, and it had always sounded like an accusation, as though a runner's daughter ought to be quicker to boil. [523] She did not shout. [527] She leaned close to the woman in sealskin and whispered, plainly, where the still had been, and the date the excise men came, and who had drawn them the map. [557] The woman looked at the fob, and then at Reed, and then she put her purse back inside her coat. [577]

Reed said her name again, louder. [583] Then he said it again, and it was not a whisper. [594] Every head under the pier turned. [600] Overhead the boots stopped. [604] Lanterns went dark one after another, and in the dark Mara heard it at last, the sound she had come for: not a shout, which was only noise, but the small dry scrape of the sealskin woman's boots turning away, and the nail giving as Reed snatched the fob down, and beneath all of it the market's held breath deciding, together, that Amos Reed was no longer one of them. [674] It was quieter than she had expected. [681] It sounded almost like the hush itself. [688]

Something small and cold pressed into her hand. [696] The chain. [698] Reed's whisper came with it, wrecked: "Take it, take it, only stop them." [711] She could not have stopped them if she wanted to; the market did not take instruction from anyone. [729] She closed her fist on the anchor and walked toward the seaward end of the pier, where the sky between the pilings had begun to go the color of watered milk. [760] Behind her the hush was closing over the place where Reed had been, gentle as it always was, as it would be for her too if she ever raised her voice. [791]
