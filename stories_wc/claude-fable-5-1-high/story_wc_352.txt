Teodora Wenz could not look at anything whole. [8] A word split for her into its ancestors, a lock into its tumblers, a candle flame into the seven colors it was pretending not to be. [34] The other archivists of the Sael Undercroft called her prism-minded, half in affection and half in warning, because a mind like that could not stop refracting once it started. [63]

The Undercroft wound through a serpentine grotto beneath the mountain, passage coiling into passage, every chamber breathing the warm mist of the springs below. [87] Seven vaults were sealed in the deepest coil, and their combinations had been lost when the first archive burned two centuries ago. [109] Teodora refused to accept the loss, because she did not believe things were ever truly lost, only refracted somewhere no one had thought to look. [134]

She found the doodle in the margin of a water ledger, the dullest manuscript in the collection. [151] A coiled serpent, drawn in a bored hand, with nine small dots along its spine. [166] Any other reader would have seen idleness. [173] Teodora saw the serpent split into the shape of the grotto itself, coil for coil, and the dots split into positions. [194] Then she saw the ink split, too. [201]

Beneath the ordinary brown lay a second pigment, ground so fine it hid inside the first. [217] She had read of it in the founders' charter, in a line the others took for poetry: We plant seeds of vigilance in the margins, to wake when the watchers have forgotten how to watch. [252] The pigment was clandestinely luminous. [257] It would not glow in light and would not glow in darkness. [269] It glowed only at the threshold between them, in the few breaths when one state was becoming the other. [288]

Every forty days, gravity in the deepest coil inverted for exactly one hour. [301] The archivists called it the Turn and evacuated well before it, strapping the shelves and sealing the door. [319] During the Turn the mist, which always hung against the ceiling, fell away toward the floor and left the roof of the grotto bare for the only hour anyone could have seen it. [352] No one ever had, because no one was ever inside. [362]

Nine dots along a serpent's spine. [368] Nine places on a ceiling that was only ever visible when the rules broke. [382] The founders had not lost the combinations. [389] They had hidden them where forgetting would keep them safe, and left a doodle to remind anyone who still knew how to look. [412]

On the fortieth day she left with the others, then waited in the antechamber with the ledger open across her knees. [433] When the lamps began to gutter and the mist began to slide the wrong way, the serpent woke on the page, a thin green fire tracing its own coils. [462] She unsealed the door and stepped through at the exact instant of the Turn, that weightless breath when down had not yet decided to become up. [488] To reenter the grotto any other way would have meant a fall. [500] At the threshold there was no fall, only a slow tumble, and then her boots touched stone that had been a ceiling for two hundred years. [526]

The mist hung above her now, pressed against the old floor like a low grey sky. [542] She followed the serpent's coils, and at each of the nine places she found a number cut into the rock, worn but legible, waiting. [566] Not seven vaults, then, but one that mattered, and the rest were decoys. [579] The seventh vault hung upside down beside her, and she turned the dial with the mist already sinking back toward her as the hour began to thin. [606]

The door opened on a small dark room, and it was not empty. [619] Shelves of jars, each one holding a fist of black ash, and in every jar the ash was faintly glowing. [639] Not glowing, she corrected herself, splitting the light the way she split everything: smoldering. [653]

The first archive had not burned by accident. [661] Something in these jars had burned it, and had never finished burning, and the founders had sealed it here with the mist to cool it and the Turn to keep every careless hand away. [695] They had not hidden the combination so it would be found. [706] They had hidden it so that whoever found it would be the kind of person who could see a fire inside an ember and understand what it meant to keep watch. [737]

Teodora closed the vault and spun the dial back to nothing. [748] She came back through the door in the last weightless breath of the hour, and in the antechamber, with the lamps steadying, she took a stub of pencil from her sleeve and in the margin beside the serpent began, very carefully, to doodle.
