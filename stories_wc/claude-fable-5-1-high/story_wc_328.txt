The herbalist's garden grew forty feet beneath the hardware store, under grow lights the color of a bruise healing. [19] Wren had been Mags's apprentice for six weeks, long enough to learn the names of the mosses and short enough that nobody had checked her references. [45] Her real employer was a university that would not put its name on the grant. [60] Her real subject was the Returned. [66]

Eleven years ago, nineteen people from Coldwater had walked into the birch woods and not come out, and the town called them the Missing. [90] Three winters ago, they came back, all nineteen, wearing the same clothes and a different weight. [106] That was the polite way to say it. [114] The impolite way was that Wren's sister Dell now drifted an inch off the kitchen floor when she laughed, and she laughed at everything. [138]

The Returned were gravitationally playful, was how Wren wrote it in her field notes, because gravitationally wrong sounded like grief. [158] They tossed stones that fell slow. [164] They set coffee cups down a hair above the table and let them settle. [178] And every one of them, sooner or later, came down the ladder into Mags's garden to use the phone. [197]

It was an old cordless handset, gray plastic gone yellow, with no base and no line, hanging from a nail between the valerian and the black hellebore. [224] "They talk into it," Mags said, the first time Wren asked. [235] "Something talks back." [238] "I don't listen." [241]

Wren listened. [243] She had hidden a recorder in the seed drawer, and at night, in her rented room, she played the confessions back through the static. [267] The mailman admitting he had stolen a decade of birthday cards before the woods took him. [283] The Kessler twins, speaking in unison, confessing they had never once been sorry for anything and were trying to learn. [303] Under every voice, a hum, like a choir warming up in a cathedral built inside a beehive. [320]

Wren had catalogued cryptids for nine years: lake shapes, moth men, a thing in Nebraska that turned out to be a very sad deer. [344] She knew the difference between a phenomenon and a hoax. [354] She had come to Coldwater to prove the Returned were one or the other, and to make her sister stop laughing. [375] Because Dell had missed eleven years of Wren's life and come back giggling, and Wren had spent those years with their mother's ashes and a bitterness that had calcified into method. [406]

In February, digging a bed for the mandrake, she found the arrowhead. [418] Flint, black, knapped so fine the edge caught the grow lights like a wet eye. [433] It weighed nothing. [436] Not almost nothing. [439] It sat on her palm and pressed upward, faintly, like a held breath. [452]

"There are eighteen more down there," Mags said, not looking up from her cuttings. [466] "They bring them back from the woods and bury them where I can keep them fed." [482] "That one's your sister's." [486] "She said you'd come." [490] "She said you'd dig." [494]

The phone rang. [497] It had no line and no battery, and it rang, and Wren picked it up with the arrowhead still floating against her palm. [520]

Static, thick as frost. [524] Then Dell's voice, not from the woods eleven years ago and not from the kitchen across town, but from somewhere the static had folded between. [549]

"I'm sorry I was gone," Dell said. [556] "I'm sorry I came back light." [562] "We didn't get taken, Wren." [567] "We heard something out there, and it was so much bigger than being angry that we stopped being able to hold on to the ground." [592] "It's a joke." [595] "The whole thing." [598] "Mom dying, us leaving, you waiting." [604] "Not a cruel one." [608] "The kind you tell at a funeral so everyone can breathe." [619]

Under her voice, the hum rose, and Wren understood it was not a choir at all but nineteen throats and one more, a harmony nobody had written, untamed, unrehearsed, and utterly unwilling to resolve. [653] The one more was her. [658]

Her boots left the soil. [663] Half an inch, then a whole one, the arrowhead tugging her wrist toward the grow lights like a dog toward a door. [685] She thought of the grant, the recorder in the seed drawer, the nine years of proving things. [702] She thought of how it would look in the field notes: subject began to metamorphose at 4:12 p.m., laughing. [721] Because she was. [724] It hurt, the way fingers hurt coming in from the cold, blood returning to places that had gone quiet to survive. [745]

Mags reached up without ceremony and caught her by the ankle. [756] "Not all at once," the herbalist said. [763] "The ground will still be here." [769] "Your sister will still be here." [775] "Go tell her the joke landed." [781]
