The storm stayed below her. [5] From the observatory catwalk Ilse watched lightning bloom inside the cloud deck like lamps switched on under wet paper, and the thunder arrived late and softened, a rumor of violence rather than the thing itself. [40] Up here the air rarefied until every breath felt borrowed. [50] That suited her. [53] Three years ago the Ministry had printed her second collection with every dangerous line struck through in black, so the poems read like ruined fences. [78] She had stopped writing and taken the caretaker's post on the mountain, where the only readers were stars. [96]

Inside, on the desk beside the telescope logs, sat Marek's darkroom timer. [108] Its hand stood at four minutes and seventeen seconds, exactly where he had stopped it the night the vans came to his studio. [131] She had never let it run. [137] A timer that finishes rings, and a ring means the exposure is over, the image fixed, the waiting done. [156] So her vigil stayed in pause, four-seventeen forever, a held breath she refused to release. [171]

The headlamps came up the switchbacks a little after midnight. [181] No one drove the mountain in weather. [188] She opened the door to a soaked man in a Ministry coat, and it took a full second of lightning to recognize the face. [212] Havel. [213] The censor. [215] The hand that had held the black pen. [223]

"I am not here officially," he said, water running from his hat brim. [236] "I am not here at all, if anyone asks." [245]

She let him in because the storm was louder than her anger. [257] He stood dripping under the dome and looked at the timer, then at her, then at the old bus timetable pinned by the door, Route 9, the valley line that had not run in a decade. [293]

"Marek said you would keep it," he said. [301] "He said it under questioning, and I wrote in the record that he said nothing useful." [317]

Something in his voice slipped then, the way a mask slips when the string finally frays, and she saw not the Ministry but a tired man who had been carrying a sentence too long. [351] Her own mask went with it. [357]

"I am not calm," she said. [363] "Everyone thinks I came up here for peace." [371] "I came up here because down there I would have burned something." [383]

"I know," Havel said. [387] "I read every line you wrote before I blacked it, and I kept them all in my head." [405] "It is a heavy place to keep a book." [414] "Marek died in February." [418]

The thunder took a long time to arrive. [426]

She unpinned the timetable with steady hands, furiously tranquil, the storm beneath her feet and none of it in her fingers. [447] Wedged between the folded pages of departures that no longer departed was a sealed envelope, soft with age. [465] Inside lay one photograph and a tracing. [472] The photograph was Marek's own back, shot in a mirror, every scar the interrogators had left him. [489] On the tracing he had copied each mark in ink and labeled it: a street, a date, a name, the place where a photograph had been taken and the beating that had answered it. [523] He had made his body a map of the city. [533]

This was what she had climbed the mountain to understand and had been afraid to ask: what a scar is, exactly, once you stop calling it damage and start reading it as a route. [567] At the bottom, in his cramped hand: Start the timer. [577]

Lightning filled the dome, and for one white instant she could see every rivet, every mote of dust, Havel's wet face open and unguarded, and her own hands, which were not shaking. [609] She turned the dial. [613] The timer ticked, and the sound was enormous in the room, a small mechanical heart resuming after three years of stillness. [634] Four minutes and seventeen seconds. [639] Long enough to write something true if you have already spent three years deciding what to leave out. [657]

She turned the timetable over and began on its blank back, and she wrote the way the mountain had taught her to breathe: rarefying each line until only what was necessary remained, thin and cold and impossible to blacken because there was nothing left to remove. [703] Havel watched without speaking, and when she finished he held out his hand, not for the page but for the map. [724]

"I still know which desks the copies pass through," he said. [735] "Let me be the one who does not stop them." [745]

The bell rang. [748] Below them the clouds went on flashing, a city of lightning with no one in it, and she stood above the storm and finally felt the whole of it, the wonder and the fury together, indistinguishable, like weather. [786]
