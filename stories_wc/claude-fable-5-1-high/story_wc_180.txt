Wren had not opened the bag since the winter her daughter died, and she did not intend to open it now. [21] She only carried it because her hands felt wrong without weight in them, the way a quartz-eye mender's hands always do. [42] The cathedral of Saint Averil had lost its roof forty years ago, and the sky had taken the opportunity to plant an orchard in it. [67] Birds had dropped seeds into the rot of fallen beams; rain had done the rest. [82] Now pear and quince and crab apple grew from the rafters themselves, roots knotted around the oak like fingers gripping a rail, branches spanning the nave so that the light came down green and moving. [117] People had once come here to swear deciduous vows, the kind sworn in spring and released when the leaves fell, unless both parties climbed up and swore again. [145] Wren's own apprenticeship had been sworn here and renewed nine autumns running, until her master's leaves fell for good. [164]

Pim was already up in the rafters, sitting on a limb thick as a horse, kicking her boots at nothing. [184] Her left eye caught the light and threw it back hard and bright, because her left eye was quartz, and Wren had ground it herself the year Pim turned six. [214] "You climbed," Pim said. [218] "You're seventy." [220] "Sixty-eight, and I have climbed worse than a church." [229] Wren hauled herself onto the beam and sat, breathing, pretending she was not breathing. [243] "You breached your contract," she said, once she could say it evenly. [255] "I let it fall," Pim said. [261] "That's what the vow is for." [267] "The leaves came down and I didn't come up." [276] "That's not breaching, that's the terms." [282] "The terms were a year of learning eyes." [290] "I learned eyes." [293] "I don't want to do it for fifty years in a shop that smells like wax and you." [311] Pim said it the way she said everything, chin first, and Wren felt the old absurd fondness rise in her even as she wanted to shake the girl off the beam. [342]

Pim turned to say something else and the light hit her wrong, and Wren saw the crack. [359] Hairline, running from the iris to the rim, the kind that spreads with every cold morning until the eye splits in its socket. [382] "When did that happen?" [386] "Doesn't matter." [388] "It matters to your skull." [393] Wren was already opening the bag before she remembered she had decided not to. [407] Inside was the past, smelling of tallow and cedar shavings. [417] Her daughter Ilse's fine files, still wrapped. [424] A tin of rouge polish gone hard. [431] A half-ground pupil blank Ilse had abandoned the week before the fever. [443] And coiled beneath it all, faded from red to the color of old brick, Ilse's hair ribbon, the one she had worn every day in the shop so her braid would not catch at the lamp. [479]

Wren's hands did what hands do. [485] She wet the rouge with spit, warmed it between her palms, and wrapped the ribbon around two fingers for a polishing pad, because it was the softest cloth in reach and a crack must be sealed with warm wax and burnished before the cold finds it. [531] "Hold still." [533] "Look at the pear tree." [538] Pim held still, which she never did, and Wren worked the wax into the fault line in slow circles, the way her master had taught her and she had taught Ilse and Ilse had taught, briefly and badly, a six-year-old with one eye. [581] "She tied that every morning," Wren said, to fill the silence. [592] "Every morning, a new knot." [597] "Never once held past supper." [602] "Her hair was too fine." [607] "I told her to buy a clasp." [614] "Why didn't she?" [617] "She said a knot you have to keep tying is a knot you keep meaning." [632] Wren stopped. [634] The wax was seated; the crack had gone silver and then gone. [646] She had only meant to explain the ribbon. [654] She had not meant to hear her daughter answer the argument for her, twelve years late, in a voice that sounded suspiciously like Pim's. [678]

Pim was looking at her with both eyes, the quartz one whole again. [691] "So the vow isn't the point," she said slowly. [700] "The climbing up is." [704] "I didn't say that." [708] "You did, though." [711] "You just said it with wax." [717] Wren unwound the ribbon from her fingers and found she did not want to put it back in the bag. [737] She held it out instead. [742] Pim took it, turned it over, and tied it around her wrist in a knot that would certainly not hold past supper. [764] "I'm not swearing another year," she said. [771] "A season." [773] "Then I'll climb up and see." [779] "That's the terms," Wren said, and reached for the next branch down. [791]
