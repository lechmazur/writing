The tide gave Teodor forty minutes between acts, and he had learned to spend them like a gambler who knew the odds. [22] The arcade sat on a shelf of rock below the promenade, a row of shuttered game booths and a coin-operated fortune teller, and twice a day the sea walked in through the arches and took it back. [59] Performers worked the low-tide windows, and Teodor worked them harder than most, because he did not believe in luck. [78] He believed in arrangement. [82]

His first set had gone the way it always went: three-card shuffles, a mind-reading bit with a borrowed watch, applause that sounded like coins. [106] The crowd had been lightly attentive, the way seaside crowds are, half watching him and half watching the dark water climb the pilings. [129] That was fine. [132] The set was not the point. [138] The set paid for the point. [144]

Now, with the stars out and the second act still twenty minutes off, he opened the small tin he kept in his coat and considered its contents like a surgeon considering instruments. [176] A brass button. [179] A folded bus ticket. [183] A bishop from a chess set, carved dark wood, its mitre worn smooth by someone else's thumb. [200] He had found it years ago in a gutter in another city and had never played a game with it. [220] It was not for playing. [225] It was for placing. [229]

Teodor's real work was this: he made things happen to people. [240] Not tricks, which they expected, but coincidences, which they did not. [251] He had watched enough faces to know that most lives ran on rails, the same bench, the same hour, the same sigh, and that a person could go a decade without anything genuinely unexpected touching them. [287] Monotony was the real audience. [292] He wanted to serenade it. [297]

Serenading it required method. [301] Randomness, left alone, was just noise; the sea threw up a thousand objects a night and nobody was moved by any of them. [324] But order the randomness, choose the object, the angle of the lamp, the minute the tide turned a path into a dead end, and chance began to sound like a message. [355] He thought of it as calculated wilderness. [362] The woods were real; he simply decided where the clearing would be. [374]

Tonight's subject was the woman in the grey coat. [383] She came every Thursday and sat on the third bench under the dead neon sign that said WIN, ate half a sandwich, and left before his second act began. [412] He had timed her for six weeks. [419] She always exited by the north arch. [426] Tonight the tide would close the north arch at nine forty-one, four minutes before she stood up. [443] She would have to go south, past the chess table nobody used, and the one working lamp would be shining on it. [465]

He walked over as if stretching his legs and set the bishop on a black square, alone, facing the sea. [485] One piece on an empty board asked a question no full board could. [498] Then he went back to his pitch and did not look. [509] Looking ruined it. [512] Serendipity could not feel watched. [517]

At nine forty-one the water slid across the north arch like a curtain drawn. [531] At nine forty-five he heard her heels change direction. [540] He counted eleven seconds of silence where the footsteps should have continued. [552] Then they resumed, slower, and faded south. [559]

He allowed himself to turn. [564]

The bishop was gone. [568] In its place, on the same black square, stood a white knight. [580]

Teodor stood very still while the tide breathed against the pilings. [591] He had never placed a knight. [597] He did not own a knight. [603] He walked to the table and picked it up; it was warm, and under it lay a bus ticket folded exactly the way he folded his. [629]

Six weeks, he thought. [633] He had been timing her for six weeks, and someone had been timing him. [647]

He looked south along the arcade, at the dark booths and the sky full of stars over the water, and for the first time in years he had no idea what was going to happen next. [683] It felt like music. [687]

The second act was due. [692] He pocketed the knight, went back to his mark, and began to shuffle, lightly attentive now to every face in the crowd, wondering which one of them was arranging the clearing he was standing in. [727]
