Ilsa Venn had skimmed stones for the canyon since she was nine, and her knees had finally told her they were done. [22] The towers of bone rose around her in the morning haze, ribs and femurs of things nobody living had names for, stacked by hands nobody living had met. [50] A harmonic stone skimmer's work was simple to describe and impossible to teach: throw a stone so it kisses the floor and rings the towers, then listen to what comes back. [81] The echoes here never died. [86] They thinned, wandered, folded into one another, and a good skimmer could pick a single pulse out of the murmur and follow it to a wall, a passage, a drop. [116] That was how the caravans crossed. [122] That was how Ilsa had earned her bread for forty years, walking ahead, throwing, listening, walking again. [139] Now the walking was over. [144]

She sat on a shelf of packed dust and turned the arrowhead in her fingers. [159] It was flint, gray and thumb-long, flaked by someone who had stood in this canyon before the first tower was raised. [180] Her teacher had given it to her with instructions never to throw it. [193] Skimmers did not waste the old things; they kept them, and they kept the names of the dead who had kept them before. [216] Ilsa could recite eleven owners back. [222] The dust she sat on held all of them, layer over layer, centuries pressed flat. [237] She was not going to be the one who broke the chain by dying useless. [252]

"You can't map what you can't reach," young Teodor said, crouching beside her with the caravan's water. [269] He had good arms and a bad ear, and he knew it. [281] "The north passage is three hours in." [288]

"Then I'll learn to reach it sitting down," she said. [298]

She had been watching the birds. [304] Cliff swifts nested in the hollows of the towers, thousands of them, and every dawn they poured out in a single body and wheeled through the canyon. [331] For forty years she had treated them as noise, a rustle to filter out of the echoes. [348] Yesterday, unable to stand, she had simply looked, and had seen the flock flinch. [362] Not from a hawk. [366] From a sound. [369] Teodor had thrown a skim stone a hundred paces off, and the pulse had gone wandering through the towers as pulses did, and the birds had bent around it like water around a post. [403] The swifts heard what she heard. [409] They just heard it everywhere at once. [416]

"Throw," she told him. [420] "Toward the north, skipped low." [425]

He threw. [427] The stone sang across the floor, struck a tower, and the ring went out. [441] Ilsa did not listen. [445] She watched. [447] High above, the flock rippled, a crease running through it left to right, then a second crease, fainter, angling back. [467] A wall there, where the first crease broke. [475] Open air where the birds flowed smooth. [482] She appraised the shape of it the way she had once appraised a stone before throwing it, weighing it for truth. [503]

"Again," she said. [506] "Twenty paces right." [509]

By the time the sun cleared the rim she had the first hour of the north passage in her head, turn by turn, and had never left the shelf. [538] It was slow. [541] It was crude compared to what her own feet and ears had given her. [555] But it was a way, and a way could be sharpened. [566]

Teodor came back flushed, sweating, delighted. [572] "That's not skimming," he said. [577]

"No," Ilsa agreed. [580] She looked at the arrowhead in her palm. [588] Whoever had flaked it had not built the towers or thrown the first ringing stone, but they had stood here, in this same murmur, and found their own way through it. [619] Every layer of dust beneath her was someone deciding the old way was not enough and refusing to let that be the end. [642] Reverence, her teacher had said, was not keeping things still. [652] It was carrying them forward. [657]

She closed her hand. [661] "Tomorrow you throw and I read," she said. [669] "And you watch the birds while you do it, because someday your arm will go too, and I want you to have something left." [693]

Above them the swifts settled back into the bone, and the last pulse of the morning's throwing went on wandering through the canyon, thinner and thinner, never quite gone. [722]
