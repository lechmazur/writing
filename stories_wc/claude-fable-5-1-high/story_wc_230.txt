The orchard hung from the ravine wall by its roots. [10] Old pear and hazel trees leaned out over the drop, their trunks bent by decades of storms that came up the gorge like something looking for a door. [38] Ilse worked the terraces beneath them with a rake and a spotted dog, hunting the black truffles that had learned to grow in the wet roots. [64] She was a rational woman, which after the empathy virus was a strange kind of loneliness. [80]

The virus had burned through the valley three winters ago and left the survivors unable to lie. [97] Not forbidden, unable. [100] A falsehood caught in the throat like a fishbone, and the listener felt it catch. [115] Everyone was involuntarily truthful now, and Ilse had found that truth without shelter was just another weather. [132]

Her apprentice, Teodor, was seventeen and could not stop confessing. [142] He told her when he was afraid of the drop. [152] He told her when he resented the dog for finding more than he did. [166] He told her, one evening with the rain coming sideways through the hazels, that he had not slept a full night since his mother died, because every thought he had arrived already spoken, and he had nowhere left inside himself to put anything down. [210] Ilse did not know how to answer that, and she said so, because she had to. [226]

The textbooks came up the ravine with the peddlers. [235] They were old medical manuals, discredited long before the virus, their spines held with tape. [250] Someone had gone through them with a pencil and underlined every mention of occlusion. [264] The rumor was that certain objects from before, things made to see without feeling, could occlude the shared field the way a shutter occludes a lamp. [290] A glass eye, the margins said. [296]

Ilse read the pages twice and closed the book. [305] It was nonsense. [308] It was the kind of nonsense that grows on grief the way truffles grow on dying roots, and she said that too, aloud, to the dog. [334] Then she went to find the smuggler anyway. [342]

He kept a boat below the falls and dealt in relics from the flooded museums downriver, anatomical models, saints' fingers, whatever the water had not taken. [368] He showed her the eye in a tin lined with velvet gone green. [381] It was antique, hand-blown, the iris painted a brown so patient it seemed to be waiting for a face. [400]

"Do you believe it works?" he asked, because the virus made even smugglers ask honest questions. [416]

"No," she said. [419]

"Then why?" [421]

"Because he does." [424]

She paid him the whole autumn's truffles, two sacks that smelled of earth and thunder, and carried the tin back up the ravine while the wind tried to take it from her. [456]

That night the storm came properly. [462] It tore a pear tree half out of the wall and left it hanging by three roots over the dark, still holding its fruit. [486] In the shed, Ilse set the glass eye on the shelf above Teodor's cot, facing the wall. [503]

"I have to tell you something true," she said. [512] "I do not think this works." [518]

He watched her from the blanket, the lamp shaking with the walls. [530]

"I think the book is wrong and the man who sold it to me is a thief." [547] "I think nothing made of glass can stand between you and what you feel." [561] She stopped, and felt her own throat, and found the next thing was also true. [576] "But I gave a year of work for it, because you needed one place where something looked at you and did not feel back." [600] "That is what I could give." [606]

Teodor was quiet. [609] It was the first quiet she had ever heard from him, and she understood, rationally, what it was. [627] Not the eye. [630] The eye was a dead thing in a tin. [639] It was that she had stood in front of him with nothing hidden, not even her doubt, and had stayed. [659] She had wanted to cultivate a safe place for his mind the way she cultivated the ground, not by force, but by tending. [682] That was the shelter. [686] Not a shutter over the lamp, but a second person willing to sit in the light. [702]

He slept. [704]

Toward morning the wind dropped, and Ilse went out to the torn pear tree. [718] She could not save it. [723] She wedged stones under its roots so it would fall more slowly, and picked the pears while it hung there, one by one, so that nothing it had made would be wasted. [755] The dog watched her with its two living eyes, and in the shed the glass one went on facing the wall, seeing nothing, which was exactly what it was for. [785]
