The archive smelled of old solvent and older dust, and Ilse Varrow, lore keeper of Meridian Hab, had never minded either. [21] What she minded was the ruby flake. [28] It had drifted down from the atrium wall that morning, no bigger than a fingernail, and landed on her reading slate like a message. [52] Under the lens it was not one color but eleven, paint stacked on paint, and at the very bottom lay a curl of gold brushwork that belonged to no era she had catalogued. [85]

The Founders had painted something on those walls before the Erasure. [96] Every generation since had painted over it. [103] Tomorrow was Renewal Day, and at sunrise the crews would spray the atrium fresh and seal the whole history under sensible grey. [125] She had until sunrise. [129]

Ilse was fifty-three and had spent thirty years being careful. [139] She packed the heat-blade, the mild solvent, a lamp, and a flask of tea, because recklessness was for people who did not intend to survive their discoveries. [166] Then she sallied out into the sleeping Hab. [174]

The atrium at night hummed with the low voice of the recyclers, the old Founder-built machines that everyone loved and nobody could repair. [197] She found the bare spot where the flake had fallen, warmed the edge of the paint, and began to peel. [217]

Grey came away and showed the blue of the Council years, cheerful and flat. [231] Blue came away and showed a mural of hands passing a loaf, the Famine Generation reminding itself to share. [250] Beneath that, children flying kites under a painted sky no one alive had seen. [264] Each layer was a letter from the dead, and Ilse read every one before she lifted it, because a lore keeper who destroyed a record to reach another was no keeper at all. [297] She photographed, she annotated, she sipped her tea, and the hours went. [309]

Near the eleventh layer her hands began to shake. [318] Gold bloomed under the blade. [323] The Founders' mural was a map. [329] It showed the atrium as it had first been, and where the east wall now stood solid there was a waterfall, painted in every color at once, and behind it trees heavy with fruit, and rising from the trees a stair. [370] Beneath it, in the Founders' square script: THE STAIRWAY OF ASCENSION. [381]

Every child in Meridian knew the story. [388] The Founders had left a throne at the top of the Hab and a law beneath it: whoever climbed the stair could sit and rule. [413] Then the stair had been lost, and for two hundred years the throne had stayed unclaimed while councils argued in its shadow. [435]

Ilse pressed her palm to the east wall and felt it thrum. [447] Not solid. [449] Hollow, and wet behind. [453] It took the heat-blade twenty minutes to find the seam and her shoulder one hard shove to open it. [472]

Water roared. [474] The recyclers' overflow fell in a sheet from far above, and the old prism-lamps behind it threw the fall into a thousand shifting colors, a kaleidoscope no one had watched in two centuries. [507] Ilse stepped through it and came out soaked into an orchard. [518]

Apple trees, real ones, fed by the spray and lit by the prisms, their trunks thick with age and their branches bent with fruit that no living mouth had tasted. [548] The Founders had hidden the stair behind the one thing they knew a hungry, careful people would never think to look past: abundance. [571] The stairway rose from the center of the orchard, iron and narrow, spiraling up through the dark toward the crown of the Hab. [594]

Ilse looked at it for a long time. [602] Then she looked at the trees. [608]

She climbed, because she was adventurous, but she climbed slowly and counted the steps, because she was wise, and at the top she found a plain chair facing a window, and in the window the pale curve of the world the Founders had left. [652] Carved into the chair's arm: SIT, AND REMEMBER WHY. [661]

She did not sit. [665] A throne claimed becomes a throne defended, and she had read enough history to know how that story ended. [684] Instead she photographed the window, the chair, the words, and went back down through the orchard, filling her satchel with apples as she went. [708]

The sky-panels were greying toward sunrise when she stepped back into the atrium. [721] The Renewal crews were already unrolling their hoses. [729] Ilse set an apple beneath each of the eleven layers she had peeled, one for every generation that had painted over the way up, and then she stood before the gold map with her arms folded and waited for them to ask. [771]

The throne could stay unclaimed a while longer. [779] The orchard, she decided, was the part worth sharing. [788]
