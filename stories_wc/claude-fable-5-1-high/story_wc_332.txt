The library on Skerra kept odd hours because Ines kept them. [11] It opened at midnight, when the last ferry unloaded its insomniacs, and closed at dawn, when the light came in over the water and made the shelves impossible to look at. [42] Nobody on the archipelago complained. [47] The islands themselves were nocturnal in temper, forty-one shards of granite that talked to each other all night through the channels between them, a sound the old people called the whispering and the young people called the wind. [85]

Ines knew better than to call it either. [93] She had grown up listening, and what she heard was longing: water wanting to be somewhere else. [110]

Her brother Teo had heard it too, and he had gone. [121] Eleven years now. [124] He left one thing behind on purpose, and she kept it in the desk drawer beneath the returns ledger: a tin watercolor palette, twelve wells, the lid dented where he had once used it to prop a window. [162]

She understood the palette the way she understood the channels. [172] It was a cipher, and it was written in absence. [182] Six wells were scraped down to bare metal: cobalt, cerulean, ultramarine, indigo, a violet that the label called dusk, and a pale gold that had no name at all. [211] Six wells were untouched, still smooth as the day they were pressed: the greens, the browns, the red of Skerra's roofs. [232] Teo had painted only the things that were leaving. [241] He had never once painted home. [247]

For years she had read this as an accusation. [256] Then, in the third year, she had begun to read it as a map. [270]

She could not paint. [274] Her hands were made for stamping dates and reshelving, and every brush she had ever held produced mud. [292] But at the end of a shift, in the hush of improbable beginnings, when the sun had not yet decided whether to bother, she carried a camera down to the eastern rocks and painted with the only medium that would obey her. [334] Light. [335] Long exposures, the shutter held open while the horizon bled through cobalt into cerulean into that nameless gold, the water whispering under the tripod legs. [360] She was trying to fill his six empty wells. [369] One photograph for each color he had used up chasing whatever had run ahead of him. [385]

Because that was what it had been, she decided, a runaway dream. [397] Not a woman, not a job on the mainland, not the ordinary escapes. [410] Something had bolted from him at the edge of the water, some picture of a life so bright he could only see it in blues, and he had gone after it without so much as a coat. [447] If she could capture it, hold it still in silver and paper, then it would be here. [464] And things that were here could be returned to. [473] She was a librarian. [477] She believed in returns. [481]

The morning she took the sixth photograph, the gold one, nothing happened. [493] No ferry unloaded a tall man with paint under his nails. [504] The islands whispered exactly as before. [510]

She went back to the library, spread the six prints across the circulation desk in the order of the wells, and set the palette beside them. [536] The empty pans caught the daylight and threw it back, small mirrors, six little suns. [551] The full pans stayed dark. [556] And she saw, for the first time in eleven years, that the cipher had two halves and she had only ever read one. [579]

He had not refused the greens and browns and roof-red. [589] He had left them. [593] Whole, pressed, waiting, the way you leave a bed made for someone who will come in late. [610]

She went to the sink and filled a jar. [619] She had never once wet the palette; it had seemed like disturbing a grave. [633] Now she dipped the cheap brush she kept for mending spines, touched it to the untouched green, and watched the dry cake vitalize under the water, going from dust to color to something almost alive, the way a pressed flower will not but a seed will. [679]

She painted Skerra. [682] Badly, gloriously, straight onto the sixth photograph, over the gold. [692] Roof-red, granite-brown, the green that no one had ever thought was leaving. [704]

Outside, the channels kept up their whispering, wanting to be elsewhere. [715] Inside, the six empty wells held the light and the six full ones held the island, and Ines understood that she had never been trying to bring him back. [744] She had been trying to find out what he had made room for. [757] The library would open again at midnight. [764] She left the palette open on the desk to dry. [774]
