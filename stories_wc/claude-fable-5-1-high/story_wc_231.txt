Nautical twilight is the only hour Mireille Achterberg trusts, because it is the only hour that admits it is between things. [21] The horizon still holds a line, the first stars have not yet committed, and the lamp above her has permission to remain dark for eleven more minutes. [48] She spends them on the opal dock, where the old planks have gone pearly with forty winters of salt, and where the mist comes in low enough to hide her boots. [79]

Yesterday, at this exact hour, a stranger stood where she is standing now. [92] He had come off the supply boat with no luggage, only a grey coat and a dried Edelweiss boutonniere pinned over his heart, the petals gone the color of old paper. [123] He did not speak. [127] He unpinned the flower, pressed it into her palm, touched two fingers to his own chest, then pointed past her at the lighthouse, and walked into the mist toward the water's edge. [159] By the time she lit the lamp and swung its beam down, the dock was empty and the tide was going out. [181]

The mainland constable called it a probable drowning and asked her to write it up. [196] Mireille has written up many things. [202] The islanders think her strange because she keeps ledgers of ships that never arrive, and because she has spent thirty years collecting other people's memories the way other keepers collect shells. [233] Fishermen tell her about their mothers' kitchens; ferry passengers tell her about the streets of cities she will never see; she writes them down, rereads them, and after enough winters they feel borrowed rather than heard, worn soft at the edges like a coat that has learned her shoulders. [282] She has never been to the mountains, but she remembers them. [293] A woman from Graubunden once described, over tea and a broken radio, how her husband climbed above the tree line to pick Edelweiss for their wedding, and how the flower is a promise made at altitude, carried down to be kept at sea level. [337]

So tonight she has come back to investigate, not the drowning, which the sea has already filed, but the gesture, which is hers to decode. [362] Her method is the one the lamp taught her: you do not learn a coastline by looking at the light, you learn it by watching where the light fails. [391] She observes the gaps. [395]

The first gap is the luggage he did not carry; a man who intends to arrive brings something, and a man who intends to leave brings only what he means to hand over. [428] The second gap is the pin. [434] She turns the boutonniere over in the last of the light and finds the brass pin bent from decades of the same lapel, the same angle, the same hand fastening it each morning. [467] Not a souvenir, then. [471] A vow, worn daily, long after the person who received it had stopped being there to see it. [489]

The third gap is the one that undoes her: he did not point at the water. [505] A man explaining his death points at the sea. [514] He touched his chest, where the flower had lived, and then he pointed at the lighthouse, where she lives. [533]

The mist is softly persistent tonight, thinning and closing, thinning and closing, and in one of its gaps she sees the whole shape of it. [558] He had carried a memory for someone until there was no one left to carry it for, and he had looked for a place where memories are kept by strangers. [588] Perhaps the woman from Graubunden had told him, years ago, about a keeper on a fogbound rock who writes everything down. [609] She does not know. [613] She knows what the gesture said, because she has spent her life on the receiving end of it: this was mine, and now it is yours, and you are the one who keeps the light. [648]

The horizon line goes. [652] Her eleven minutes are spent. [657] She climbs the hundred and twelve steps with the Edelweiss in her breast pocket, and when the lamp catches and the beam swings out over a sea that has no one on it, she understands that yesterday, for once, she was not the only person on this rock who was alone. [708] For a few minutes yesterday, someone stood on the opal dock and saw her, one lonely person handing his loneliness to another, and that too is now in her keeping. [738] She opens the ledger and writes it down, the flower, the two fingers, the hand raised toward the light, and beneath it, in the space reserved for the vessel's name, she writes nothing, and leaves the gap for him. [777]
