The thaw came early to Hall C, and Ilse was there to watch it. [14] All winter, ice had grown along the broken louvers of the old cooling vents, and now the icicles let go one drop at a time, striking the raised floor in a rhythm she had learned to read. [51] Where the drips landed, the concrete darkened, and by afternoon the dark spots joined into a pattern like a constellation nobody had named. [74] That was where she would plant next. [81]

Eleven years ago this room had held four thousand servers, and the fire that gutted them had also gutted her name. [102] She had been the junior technician who signed the coolant log that night, and the inquiry had needed one signature to point at. [125] When the city bought the shell for a pittance and called for a caretaker to turn it into a moss garden, she applied under her own name and was hired by people too young to recognize it. [162] She did not tell them what the building had cost her. [173] She simply came back and started planting. [180]

Now cushion moss furred the server racks and haircap climbed the cable trays, and the hall held a green quiet so thick it felt sweet on the tongue, a honeyed hush that made weekend visitors lower their voices without knowing why. [221] It was a weekday, though, and the man standing in the doorway was not a visitor. [237]

Marek had aged the way the building had, all at once and then not at all. [253] Six days ago he had posted a video from his kitchen table, forty minutes of him explaining that he had overridden the temperature alarm that night to finish a migration on schedule, that Ilse had signed a log he had already falsified, that he had watched her take the blame and said nothing for eleven years. [309] The confession had gone viral by morning. [316] Her phone had filled with strangers who wanted to be angry on her behalf, and she had turned it off and gone to check the icicles. [342]

"I didn't know if you'd let me in," he said. [352]

"The door's been off its hinges since the fire," she said. [363] "Everyone gets in." [366]

He came down the center aisle slowly, careful of the moss, which she noticed and did not comment on. [385] A drop fell between them and she watched it land, adding one more point to the map on the floor. [405]

"I thought you'd want to say something," he said. [414] "To me, to a camera, to anyone." [421]

"You left me a lot of things to say," she said. [432] "I've been saying them to the moss." [439]

"It's a better listener than I was." [446]

"Yes." [447]

She reached into her collar and drew out the amber pendant she had worn since before the fire, a lozenge of gold resin the size of a thumbnail with a single seed caught inside it. [482] He recognized it; he had bought it for her at a market the week she was hired, back when she was the new dreamer on the night shift who watched the temperature graphs like weather and talked about what the building might become someday. [526]

"Do you know what amber is?" she asked. [534]

"Resin." [535]

"It's a tree's wound," she said. [541] "Something cuts the bark and the tree bleeds this to seal it, and then it hardens, and a few million years later someone hangs it on a chain and calls it beautiful." [573] "The tree didn't mean it to be beautiful." [581] "It just refused to stay open." [587]

She unclasped the chain and proffered it across the space between them, flat on her palm, not quite close enough that he could take it without stepping forward. [615]

"I don't want to keep this the way I've been keeping it," she said. [629] "As proof of something." [633] "I'd rather you hold it while you help me plant." [643]

He looked at the pendant and then at the constellation of wet marks spreading across the floor, and understood, she saw, that she had already decided what today would be before he arrived. [676] That the forgiveness, if it was forgiveness, would not be spoken. [687] It would be a morning of kneeling on cold concrete, pressing moss into the places the thaw had chosen, letting the building's leaks decide where the green should go. [716]

He stepped forward and took it. [722]

She handed him a tray of cushion moss and pointed to the first dark spot. [737] Above them the icicles went on letting go, and the hush closed over the two of them, sweet and gold and almost enough. [760]
