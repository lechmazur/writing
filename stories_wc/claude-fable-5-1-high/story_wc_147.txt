The tether went taut and then wrong, and Mara Tesch knew before she turned that it had snarled. [18] Behind her the line looped twice around a fallen pillar and vanished into a nest of its own coils, forty meters below a sea that had forgotten this temple was here. [49] She thumbed the beacon on her wrist. [56] Somewhere above, on a boat that had grown small and doubtful, a light would begin to blink, and she would become what the manuals called the sender: a coordinate, a countdown, a voice reduced to a pulse. [93] The boat was an hour out. [99] Her gauge gave her forty minutes. [105]

She had come down for the vials. [112] The house of Orrande paid absurdly for them, small crystal perfume vials the drowned priests had sealed with wax and left in the niches of the walls, each holding a scent that was said to touch memories the way a hand touches a bruise, gently and exactly. [159] Mara did not care about Orrande's money. [166] She cared that the vials worked. [172] Her brother had smelled of cedar and engine oil and the particular salt of a person who never quite dried off, and for three years she had been trying to bottle him, to harness a thing that by its nature was already leaving the room. [217] The priests had known how. [222] One vial was in her thigh pocket now, cold through the neoprene, its wax seal intact. [238]

Instinct said pull. [241] Every cell she owned wanted to haul on the line, to kick toward the pillar and tear at the knot with both hands, and she knew, because she had watched a man die this way off Kefalos, that pulling was how a snarl became a noose. [287] So she did the unnatural thing. [293] She stopped. [295]

She let her arms drift and her breath slow until the regulator's hiss thinned to almost nothing, and the temple, which had been waiting a thousand years for someone to be quiet in it, gathered its hush around her. [334] Her lamp swung slowly across the nave. [341] Columns rose in a blue so deep it was nearly a sound. [353] Fish moved through a mural of mourners like thoughts through a sleeping head. [366] Nobody had seen this in a thousand years, and nobody was seeing it now but her, and the loneliness of that was so complete it turned over into something like splendor. [397]

Thirty-one minutes. [399]

She finned backward, toward the pillar, feeding slack into the line instead of taking it out. [415] The knot was ugly. [419] The tether had passed under itself twice and hooked on a bronze ring set into the stone, the kind of ring the priests had hung their lamps from. [447] She found the loop that wanted to tighten and made herself lift it the other way. [463] Her fingers, stupid with cold, argued. [469] She overruled them. [472]

It was the same argument she had lost every night for three years, the one where she stood in her brother's empty flat and tried to hold the smell of him by breathing harder, and only breathed it away faster. [512] Grasping was how the ephemeral escaped. [518] The priests had not grasped. [523] They had let the scent settle, then closed the crystal around it without hurry, and walked away. [540]

The first coil unsnarled and came free. [547]

Twenty-two minutes. [549]

The second coil had swollen against the ring, and she worked it with the patience of someone who no longer had time for anything but patience, easing, not pulling, letting the line find the way it had come. [587] When it slid loose she felt it in her teeth. [597]

She rose slowly, because rising fast would kill her too, and the temple sank away beneath her fins, blue into deeper blue into nothing, closing over itself like a held breath finally let go. [631] At the safety stop she hung in green light with six minutes of air and the beacon still blinking against her wrist, calling to a boat that would arrive to find her alive and irritated at the fuss. [669]

She took the vial out of her pocket and held it up. [681] The crystal caught the light and made a small, private sun of it. [694] Whoever it held had been loved enough to be sealed and left in the dark for a stranger to find. [714] She would not sell it. [719] She would not open it either, not yet, not gasping, not the way she had tried to open everything. [738] She would wait for a room with the door closed and the evening settled in it, and then she would breathe once, slowly, and let whoever it was touch her and pass through, and she would let them go. [777] That, she understood now, was the only way anyone had ever kept anything. [790]
