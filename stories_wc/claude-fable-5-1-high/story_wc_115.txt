Dell had taken the valve wheel because it was the only thing in Harrow Mill that anyone had bothered to decorate. [21] Somebody had wound a rosary through its spokes, glass beads gone milky, the crucifix wired to the hub so the wheel could not turn without dragging Christ around with it. [51] She had cut the wire with her multitool and felt nothing, which was the point of her. [68]

Now she sat in the rail tunnel that ran under the mill's east wing, waiting for the county deputy's headlights to finish their slow lap of the lot above. [97] The tunnel smelled of wet coal and the tracks were gone, leaving only the ties like the ribs of something that had lain down here to die. [124] Her pocket radio hissed against her knee. [131] She kept it tuned to Night Frequencies, the overnight psychic call-in show, not because she believed a word of it but because the callers' voices kept her from falling asleep in places where falling asleep could kill her. [169]

"The whistle went at midnight," a Harrow woman was saying, her breath ragged. [182] "There's no boiler in that place anymore, Madame Ondine, there's nothing to blow it, and it went." [199]

Dell's eyelids drifted and she let them, half-listening, one hand resting on the cold iron rim of the wheel in her lap. [221] Every mill town had a ghost shift story. [229] Nine men on the night shift in 1971, a steam line let go, and ever since, the locals claimed, the dead clocked in at midnight and worked until the whistle released them at dawn. [263] A second caller, then a third, all talking over the host's velvet reassurances: lights in the carding room, a smell of scorched cotton drifting down Front Street. [290]

Dell listened the way she listened to everything, appraising. [299] A salvage dealer in Pittsburgh would give her forty dollars for a cast-iron valve wheel; the rosary was worth nothing, dime-store glass. [321] But provenance changed a price, and a whole town calling a psychic hotline at three in the morning was provenance of a kind. [344] She turned the wheel slowly, beads clicking, and tried to hear in the callers' panic how much the thing in her hands was worth. [368]

Then an old man's voice came on, and he did not want a reading. [382]

"I was the one on the gauges," he said. [391] "I opened the bleed valve on number four because I read it wrong, and I closed it again before anybody came, and I put my mother's rosary on it so I would never touch it again, and I let them call it a bad weld for fifty-two years." [439]

The radio went silent. [443] Not static; silence, the dead-air kind that broadcasters are trained to fear, Madame Ondine with her mouth open somewhere in a studio and nothing coming out. [469]

Dell was fully awake now, though she had not moved. [479] In the hush she lifted the wheel off her knees and held it out in front of her, arms straight, the way you would hold something to a scale. [508] Eleven pounds, maybe twelve. [512] But she understood, with the strange clarity of the very tired, that she was not weighing iron. [529] She was weighing what it cost a man to hold nine deaths in his throat for half a century, and the answer was that it weighed exactly this, a thing you could lift with both hands and never once put down. [570]

She did not believe in God and she did not believe in the ghost shift. [585] She did believe in the old man, because she knew what a swallowed thing sounded like when it finally came up; she had one of her own, a girl named Casey and a floor in a grain elevator that Dell had said was fine. [629] That was why she went into these places alone, and why she went in fast, and why she stole what she stole: to see if anything down here weighed as much as that. [662]

On the radio, Madame Ondine finally said, "Sir, are you still with us," and the old man said, "No," and the line clicked. [685]

Above, the deputy's headlights swung away toward the highway. [694] Dell stood, her knees complaining, and walked back up the tunnel toward the mill instead of down it toward her truck. [715] Forty dollars was the wrong price. [721] The right price was the twenty minutes it would take her to find the stem of number four in the dark, seat the wheel on it, and rewire the crucifix to the hub so it could not turn. [759] Not for the dead, who were not clocking in anywhere, but for the man who had finally let it out of his mouth and would need, come morning, to know it was still holding. [793]
