Denny Kowalczyk waits for the hourglass. [6] It sits on the mantel where his wife Marta turned it every night at eight, back when eight meant teeth and pajamas and Tomas singing his little sister down into sleep. [37] She still turns it. [41] Nobody has said why. [45] Nobody in the house says much of anything now, and Denny has learned that some things are not spoken but tended, the way you tend a grave, on your knees, in the dark, with your hands. [81]

When the final grain slips through, he stands. [89] Marta does not look up from the dish towel she has been folding for twenty minutes. [105] Lucia's door is shut. [109] Seven years old and she has not laughed since March, not once, not even at the dog. [126]

The truck is loaded already. [131] Six leaf blowers, borrowed from the crew one at a time so no one would ask. [147] In his coat pocket, the pull cord handle from the old Stihl, the one Tomas used to yank with both fists and his whole weight, shouting "Contact!" like a pilot in a movie. [180] The cord frayed through in April. [186] Denny kept the handle. [190] He does not know what else you keep. [198]

The storm tunnel runs under Cedar Street and comes up through a grate at the corner of their yard, ten feet from Lucia's window. [222] Tomas discovered the echo the summer before, hollering nonsense down the grate and waiting for the tunnel to holler back. [242] Lucia had laughed until she hiccupped. [248]

Denny parks at the outfall and carries the blowers in two at a time. [262] The tunnel is a long concrete throat, ribbed and dripping. [272] Last autumn's leaves lie in the standing water along the walls, macerating into a black sweet-rotten paste that smells like the bottom of every October. [297] He sets the machines in a half circle on the dry hump of the floor, the way an orchestra seats itself, and it does not feel foolish anymore. [325] It felt foolish the first night. [331] Now it feels like the only sensible thing a man can do. [343]

He has learned them. [347] The Echo throttles low to a drone near a G. [357] The two Husqvarnas, choked halfway, hold something like a fifth above. [368] The little electric one whines high and thin and never quite in tune, and that is the important one, because Tomas never sang in tune either. [394]

Denny starts them one by one. [400] The tunnel takes each note and stretches it, folds it back, makes six machines into a hundred. [417] The air begins to move. [422] It pushes down the concrete toward the grate at Cedar Street, a warm off-key wind with the rot of leaves in it, and Denny takes his place in front of the half circle with the pull cord handle raised in his fist. [464]

He conducts his symphony. [468] He is mournfully eager about it, leaning into the downbeat like a man leaning into weather, and he begins to sing, or half-sing, because he does not really know the words. [499] Tomas made them up. [503] Something about a moon that lost its shoes. [511] Something about a goose in a rowboat. [518] Every night a different verse, sung wrong on purpose to make Lucia snort into her pillow, and every night the same crooked tune under it, the tune Denny hums now against the drone of the machines and lets the tunnel carry, along the pipe, up through the grate, across ten feet of frozen grass, to a window. [575]

He never tells Marta where he goes. [582] She never asks. [585] This is the shape of their tending. [592]

On the ninth night, the echo comes back wrong. [601] He is on the verse about the goose, the handle keeping time, when the tunnel returns a sound he did not put into it. [625] High, short, hiccupping. [628] A child, laughing. [631]

Denny stops singing. [634] The blowers drone on without him. [640] The laugh comes again, down the pipe from the direction of Cedar Street, thin and off-key as the electric whine, and he cannot tell, he honestly cannot tell, which child it belongs to. [673] Lucia at the grate in her socks. [680] Or the tunnel giving back a summer afternoon it has been holding, the way it holds the leaves, the way he holds the handle. [704]

He does not go and look. [710] He raises the pull cord and finds the downbeat and sings the next verse, the one about the moon, and he sings it as wrong as he can. [738]

When he comes home, the hourglass is on the mantel, and Marta has turned it over again. [755] The sand is running. [759] Neither of them says a word about it. [767]
