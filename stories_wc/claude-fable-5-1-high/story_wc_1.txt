Ilse made glass that remembered light. [6] Sand from the pale river, a pinch of the grey mineral the miners called moonsalt, and charcoal enough to hold her furnace at white heat for three days: that was the recipe, and the charcoal was the crime. [44] The province had closed its forests to burners a decade ago, so she bought from Dov, who came down the canyon on moonless nights with sacks that smelled of oak and kept his accounts on a strip of paper rolled in his boot. [87]

Three nights ago someone had wedged her workshop door and fed the furnace until the roof caught. [104] She survived because glass had taught her that heat announces itself before it arrives: the hum of the kiln changed pitch, and she was through the vent shutter and rolling in the frostbitten grass before the first beam fell. [143]

Now she sat in the quiet canyon under stars, her back against cold sandstone, and in her hand was the only thing she had pulled from the ash that was not hers. [175] Dov's ledger strip, or a finger's length of it, scorched brown at both ends. [189] His marks were a smuggler's shorthand, but she had watched him write them for six years: a circle for a sack, a stroke for a coin, a small hooked sign for each buyer. [222] Her own hook was there, with four circles beside it, dated the night of the fire. [238] She had never received those sacks. [244] Dov had not come that night, and someone else had carried his ledger to her door and lost a piece of it in the burning. [269]

A scorched testament, she thought, turning it so the starlight caught the char. [282] Glass that cooled too quickly kept a record of its own panic in the crazing; this paper kept a record of hers. [304]

The canyon was very still. [309] Her whole craft was made of pauses: the wait while a gather reheated, the wait while an annealing oven crept down through its hours, the wait between one breath into the pipe and the next. [344] She had always treated them as gaps to endure. [353] Now her life had become one long pause, no furnace, no house, and she wanted to know what it was for. [374]

She did not sleep. [378] Toward dawn the stars thinned and the cold thickened, and frost began to feather across the canyon floor, white ferns growing on stone the way devitrification crept across a badly tended melt. [410] She had read those patterns for years; a glassmaker learns to see heat's history in the shape of what it leaves behind. [432] So she saw it when the first grey light came: the frost lay evenly over the old stones and thinly, unevenly, over a line of boulders near the seep, stones that had been turned and set back, their earth-warmed undersides up. [473] And beyond them a trail of boot prints ran up the canyon, each one frosted brighter than the grass around it, the crushed blades holding cold the way thick glass holds heat. [505]

She rose, stiff, and began to retrace them. [513] This was how she worked at the bench, too: not forcing, only following, adjusting her grip as the material told her what it was becoming. [538]

Under the turned boulders she found Dov's cache: four sacks, oak-smelling, untouched. [550] So the fire had not been fed with these. [559] The ash in her ruin had smelled of pine, and only the province's sealed woodyards burned pine. [576] Someone with a warden's key had taken Dov's ledger, and with it the mark of every buyer in the valley, and had started at the top. [602] The boot prints climbed on toward the rim, toward the mining camp where the moonsalt came from, toward the next hooked sign. [624]

She stood a long moment while the frost began to go grey and soft around her. [640] Her glass worked because it held what the sun gave it and gave it back in the dark, slowly, when nothing else was shining. [664] That was not the absence of light. [671] That was what the pause was for. [678]

She folded the scorched strip into her sleeve, shouldered one sack of oak against the cold nights ahead, and went up the canyon after the prints, steady, ready to change her grip whenever the way told her to. [716] Behind her, in the last shadow of the wall, the frost still held the shape of where she had sat all night, a small dark human hollow ringed in white, remembering. [747]
