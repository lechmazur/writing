The bookstore occupied the whole of Mount Teller, from the paperback foothills to the frost-rimed folios at the summit, and Ines Marrow had spent eleven years learning to read its silences. [31] She was an arcane linguist by training, which in Teller meant she translated absence: the gap on a shelf where a volume should stand, the dust that fell unevenly, the sentence a catalog card declined to finish. [68] Readers went missing in the stacks every week, and the store paid her to bring them back. [85] That was the contract: rescue missions only, outward always, never deeper than the third switchback. [100]

The time traveler arrived on a Tuesday, smelling of ozone and wet soil, and everything after that belonged to a different clause. [122] He gave his name as Teodor and set a botanical diary on her desk, its cover warped, its spine held together with surgical tape. [146] "My sister kept this," he said. [152] "Forty years from now, in a garden that stops existing because of something I never learned in time." [170] He had read, in a future edition of the store's own guidebook, that somewhere on the mountain was the library of unlearned lessons, and that a linguist named Marrow had once found it. [203] Ines had not. [206] Not yet. [208]

She turned the diary's pages while he waited. [216] Pressed leaves, watercolor sketches of seed pods, careful notes in a hand that grew shakier toward the back. [234] And gaps: nine pages razored out cleanly, the stubs still showing faint pencil ghosts where ink had bled through. [253] "Who cut these?" [256] "She did, after." [259] "After you failed her." [263] He did not argue, which she respected. [270]

Ines understood then why the guidebook had named her. [279] The store did not shelve lessons the way it shelved books; it shelved them as holes, and nobody had ever thought to research the holes as a collection. [307] She pulled thirty years of loss reports from her filing drawer and spread them across the floor. [324] Every volume the store had swallowed and never returned, every reader who came out unable to say what they had gone in looking for. [348] Plotted on her map of the mountain, the absences made a spiral, and the spiral tightened toward the fifth switchback, two turns past where her contract ended. [375]

The store would have to be spoken to. [383] It did not have a voice, exactly. [390] It had a symphony nobody could hear, the sum of every page turning and every page not turning, and she had learned over eleven years to feel where the music thinned. [421] She went to the third switchback with a spool of red thread, a stub of chalk, a jar of the diary's loose seeds, and Teodor a step behind. [449] At the boundary she stopped and said, aloud, to the shelves: "I want to renegotiate." [464]

The silence changed key. [468] She laid out her terms the way she read them: one reader in, one reader out, and one linguist who would not take a single book from the fifth switchback, only borrow the shape of a missing one. [506] In exchange she offered what the store had never been given: the nine razored pages, which no one alive possessed, and which she therefore promised to reconstruct from their ghosts, one letter at a time, until the diary and the mountain shared the same wound. [551] The unheard music resolved into something like consent. [559]

They climbed. [561] She tied thread at every turn and chalked the pencil ghosts onto the stone as she deciphered them: a sketch of a nightshade, a note about frost dates, a single underlined word, listen. [594] The fifth switchback was a room with no books at all, only shelves, and every shelf held a rectangle of clean dust the size of a lesson. [621] Teodor walked the aisles until he stopped before one gap and went very still. [635] "That's it," he said. [639] "She told me the garden needed a second gardener, and I said next year." [653]

Ines did not hand him anything, because there was nothing to hand. [665] She only pressed his palm into the dust so that the shape would travel with him, and then she did what she was paid for. [690] She followed her thread back down, one reader out, and left the gap in the diary's pages exactly where it was. [711] Some lessons had to stay missing for a while longer, so that the ones who needed them would know where to look. [733]
