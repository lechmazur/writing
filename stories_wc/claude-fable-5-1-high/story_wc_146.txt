The bell above Harrow Cross had not rung in nine years, and the archway that held it leaned like an old man listening for his name. [26] I was the last one left, and I stayed only because leaving felt like admitting the town had died with my father. [48] The surveyor from the county had chalked a date on the archway's keystone: the day it would come down. [67] Three weeks before that date, the bell tuner walked up the hill. [79] She carried nothing but a canvas roll and a tuning fork carved from ebony, black as a burned matchstick, which made no sense to me because wood does not sing. [109] "It does," she said, before I had asked anything. [118] "It just waits longer than metal." [124] Her name, she said, was whatever the bell called her, and she would let me know once it spoke. [143]

Harrow Cross had been a mining town, and the mine had not collapsed; it had simply run thin, and the people ran thin with it. [168] Grass split the cobblestones. [172] Doors stood open on rooms where the wind was the only tenant. [184] The silence was not empty; it was the loud kind, full of birds and creaking shutters, a hush that felt wild, as if the town had gone feral without us. [214]

The tuner sat down beneath the archway on the first morning and did not get up. [230] "Walk," she told me. [234] "Where?" [235] "Through." [236] So I walked under the arch, and she closed her eyes and listened to my boots on the stones. [255] She had me do it again the next day, and the next, and never once struck the fork, with a patience that seemed infinite. [279] When I lost my temper and asked what she was waiting for, she said, "For you to stop walking like a man alone." [302]

I did not understand her, and I told her so. [312] She explained it the way you explain weather to a child, slowly and without pity. [327] A bell is not tuned to a note, she said. [337] It is tuned to a town. [343] The founders had hung this one to match the rhythm of feet crossing the square at dusk, the miners and the bakers and the children, hundreds of steps folding into one pulse. [375] The bell answered the feet, and the feet answered the bell, and that answering was the balance that held a place together. [397] When the feet left, the bell went sour, and a sour bell pushes people away without their knowing why. [416]

"So you rebuild the tone," I said. [423] "No," she said. [426] "You rebuild the town, and the tone follows." [434] "There is no town." [438] "There is you." [441]

On the eleventh evening I walked through the archway thinking not of my father but of the baker's daughter who had sent me a letter from the coast, asking if the hill still smelled of coal smoke in autumn. [480] My steps must have changed, because behind me the ebony fork struck stone. [493] It did not ring so much as remember, a low black hum that took the shape of my walking and held it. [515] She climbed the archway that night with the fork between her teeth and worked on the bell until dawn, filing and hammering by touch. [539] At first light it rang once, and the sound was my own footfall made enormous. [554]

Two days later a truck came up the hill. [563] The baker's daughter stepped down and said she had heard a bell in a dream that sounded like home. [582] The tuner made her walk through the arch, too, and struck the fork, and climbed again. [598] Then a family. [601] Then the old schoolteacher, then a stranger who simply liked the sound. [613] Each time, the tuner waited beneath the stones for as long as it took, a day or a week, until the walker walked like someone who belonged, and each time she climbed and folded that rhythm into the bronze. [652]

The chalked date on the keystone came and went. [661] The archway did not fall. [666] When I pointed this out, she shrugged and said that stones, like people, only collapse when nothing is asking them to stay. [688] "We are still before the collapse," she said. [696] "The work is making before last." [702]

By the first snow there were thirty of us, and at dusk the square filled with the sound of feet on cobblestones, uneven and human, and above us the bell rang in a voice that was all of ours at once. [743] The tuner rolled up her canvas the next morning. [752] I asked her name one last time. [759] She tilted her head toward the arch as the bell spoke, and smiled, and said, "There, you heard it," and walked down the hill in perfect time with nothing at all. [790]
