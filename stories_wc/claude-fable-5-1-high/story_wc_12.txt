The silo swallowed sound the way the painter's palette swallowed color. [11] Father Teodor Vasq noticed both before his eyes adjusted to the dim. [23] Felt hung in gray sheets against the curved walls, and every footstep arrived a half-second late, wrapped in wool. [42] At the center of the round room, a lead plumb bob hung motionless on a wire that vanished into the dark above. [64] The town called it the anchor, and the town was not wrong. [76] Whatever held this place in this world held it by that thread. [88]

Ilse Marren stood at her easel with her back to him. [99] Eleven canvases leaned against the felt, each one the same flat gray from edge to edge. [115] The twelfth was nearly finished. [120] A strip of white primer, two fingers wide, remained along the bottom. [132] Her palette held one color, mixed fresh that morning, the exact shade of every morning before it. [149] Teodor knelt and set his loop pedal on the boards. [159] He had never learned to cast anything out. [167] He had learned, instead, that possession lives in the sentences a person stopped questioning, and that a thing which feeds on stillness cannot survive a question asked twice. [195]

He pressed record. [198] "Who told you gray was peace?" [204] He released, and his own voice returned through the small speaker, muffled by the felt, clearer for having lost its edges. [225] He let it repeat once, then pressed again and left four beats of nothing. [239] The silence sat in the loop like a set stone. [249] Other priests filled rooms with words; Teodor decorated them with the gaps, because the gaps were where the possessed could still speak. [271]

Ilse's brush did not stop, but her shoulders changed. [280] "It is not peace," she said, wool-soft, from far away. [290] "It is agreement." [293] "Agreement." [294] He recorded it, layered it beneath the question, and now the room asked and answered itself in his voice and hers. [315] "If gray is every color agreeing, what did the colors surrender to agree?" [328] The brush slowed. [331] Teodor had watched this before: the antithesis catching on something buried, the way a plumb line catches the true vertical only after it stops trusting the walls. [358] He was here to plumb her, not to save her. [368] Depth first; rescue was her own work. [375]

"You think stillness is safety," he said, and did not record it. [387] "You think if nothing moves, nothing can be lost." [396] "Nothing moves here," she agreed. [401] "Then how did it get in?" [407] The loop turned under them, question over answer over silence, and the silence was what she flinched from. [451] It wanted the strip filled with the same color, the last disagreement in the room erased, so that the anchor would hold a place with nothing left in it to argue. "Ilse, look at your palette." [510] She looked. [512] "You mixed that from blue, from ochre, from a red you have not named in eleven days." [529] "Where are they?" [532] "In the gray," she said. [665] Stillness, Teodor thought, unravels from a single pulled thread. [674] The room was never anchored by the bob hanging still. [684] It was anchored by someone watching whether it moved. [693]

[750] "What do I put first?" she asked. [757] "Whichever one you have to argue for," Teodor said, and unplugged the pedal, and let the felt take the last of his voice. [780]
