The bluff was called Zephyr on the old survey maps, though the wind that combed it in March was no gentle thing. [22] Mara Voss had come here to be forgotten, and for eleven months the mist had obliged her. [39] A burned spy learns to love weather that hides things. [49] Each morning she opened the engraved pillbox on the windowsill and counted what it held: one white tablet, never taken. [69] The lid read, in her handler's looping script, For the hour you choose. [82] She had been given the hour, in a basement in Tallinn, and she had chosen to talk instead. [100] Teodor, the watchmaker who had fed her three years of shipping manifests, had vanished within the week. [117] The service had cut her loose with a pension and a silence, and she had accepted both. [134]

The first message appeared in late February, when the snow on the bluff path had accumulated deep enough to hold a shape. [156] Footprints, but wrong: short stamps and long dragging steps, arranged in groups. [168] Morse, written by someone walking. [173] She read it from her doorway without stepping out: STILL WINDING. [184] Her stomach turned over. [188] It was what Teodor used to say when she asked if he was afraid, tapping his chest like a clock. [208]

She did not run down the path. [215] Prudence had kept her alive when daring had not, and she knew how a lure was built. [232] Instead she photographed the snow, watched the mist for a full day, and let the wind erase the words. [251]

More came after each fresh fall. [257] NOT WHAT YOU THINK. [261] ASK THE BOX. [264] Whoever walked the bluff came in the mist and left in it, and she never saw a coat or a shoulder, only the silver hush of the fog closing behind the letters. [296] The snow was a careful choice, she understood. [304] No paper to be found, no signal to be intercepted, and a message that destroyed itself by noon. [322] It was the work of someone who had also learned to be afraid on purpose. [337]

The days lengthened. [340] By the third week of March the earth had tilted far enough toward spring that the snow arrived thin and left by afternoon. [363] The code would soon have no page to be written on. [374] So on the morning of the equinox, before light, she went out and wrote her own answer with her boots, a line of stamps and drags along the ridge: WHERE. [404] Then she went back inside, put the pillbox in her pocket, and waited with the door unlatched, which was the most reckless thing she had done in a year. [433]

He came at dusk, a thin shape resolving out of the grey, walking with a limp she did not remember. [453] Teodor was older by more than a year. [461] She did not ask how he had found her; she asked why he was alive. [476]

He sat at her table and held out his hand, and she gave him the pillbox because it seemed to be what he wanted. [500] He turned it over, found the hinge she had bent that night in Tallinn trying to open it with numb fingers, and took a tiny screwdriver from his coat. [529] While he worked, he vouchsafed her the thing the service never had. [541]

The address she had given under the lights was the decoy flat, the one they had agreed on years before and never used. [564] She had not remembered choosing it. [570] Some part of her, below the pain, had chosen it anyway. [581] He had heard the raid on the wrong street and walked out the back of his shop with a bag of tools and nothing else. [606] The service had known within a month. [613] It had been simpler to let her believe she was broken than to admit they had burned an officer who had held. [635]

Mara sat very still. [639] For eleven months she had come to this bluff to find where her unused courage waited, certain it was in the tablet she had failed to swallow. [666] It had never been in the box. [673] It had been in the lie. [679]

The hinge clicked. [682] Teodor closed the lid, opened it, closed it again, satisfied, and slid it back across the table. [699] Fixing a thing, he said, is mostly finding the part that still moves. [712]

She took the tablet out and carried it to the door. [723] The last thin snow was going grey at the edges, and the mist had pulled back far enough to show the sea. [745] She pressed the pill into the melting crust, one short white mark, and let the wind decide what it spelled. [765]
