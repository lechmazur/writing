After the fog became solid, the observatory stopped being a tower and became a shore. [15] The cloud-sea beneath it hardened to a grey plain that no rope could pierce, and the lift cables sagged into it and stayed. [38] Wen counted the days by storms, because storms were the only weather left. [51]

She was the archivist, which meant she owned the summer-quill and the duty that came with it. [68] The quill had been cut from a sun-heron in the last warm season before the fog set, and it would take ink only while the air held heat. [96] In autumn it went dry as a bone, and whatever had not been written stayed unwritten until spring. [114]

Tonight the storm broke over the dome in sheets of violet, and Wen went down to the forecourt to listen. [134]

The forecourt was paved in crystal, quarried by the founders from the mountain the observatory once stood on. [152] Dry, it was only stone. [157] Wet, it whispered. [160] Every voice that had ever crossed it had sunk in, and rain drew them back up like breath on a window. [181] The keepers had held every council here for two hundred years for exactly that reason: so the crystal would hold what they could not. [205]

Wen stood under the eave with the quill warm in her fist and let the murmur rise around her ankles. [225] Her grandmother's laugh. [228] A man reading harvest tallies from a valley that no longer had a sky. [242] Children reciting the names of rivers. [248]

She had come to love these voices the way one loves a house already sold, and she knew it was a fault in her. [272] She missed things before they left. [278] She had mourned the quill while it was still on the bird. [290]

When the rain thinned she crossed to the monuments, three founders in weathered stone at the forecourt's edge, and knelt with her measuring cord. [314] The storm clouds tore, and a low sun came through the gap and laid the founders' shadows long across the wet crystal. [336] There were no calendars anymore; the calendars had lived below. [346] So she measured. [349]

Nine knots and a hand from the first founder's heel. [359] Last storm it had been eight. [365] The shadows were lengthening; the sun was sliding south. [374] She could not know the date, but she could conjecture, and her conjecture was that summer had perhaps twenty days left in it. [397] Twenty days of ink. [401]

Behind her, the crystal was still whispering, and she understood what the keepers would ask when they woke. [419] They would ask her to spend the twenty days copying the voices down, every river name and harvest tally, before the rain stopped bringing them up. [445] The old world, saved in her hand. [452]

It was what she wanted too, and that was why she distrusted it. [465] A people made only of what they had lost would be a monument, not a people. [481] They would stand at the forecourt's edge casting shadows and waiting for someone to measure them. [497]

Wen looked at the crystal for a long time. [506] Then she sat down on it, wet through, in the middle of the murmuring, and opened the ledger on her knee. [527]

She did not write the rivers. [533]

She wrote: On the ninth storm after the fog became solid, Teodor mended the eastern lens with wire from his own bedframe. [555] She wrote: Marit has begun singing to the goats because there is no one else to sing to, and the goats do not mind. [579] She wrote: The archivist misses a summer that is not yet over, and is trying to stop. [596]

The quill moved easily; the heat of the day was still in it. [609] Around her the voices of the drowned world went on, softer as the crystal dried, and she spoke over them, quietly, so that what she said would sink into the stone beside them. [642] Not instead of. [645] Beside. [646]

The next rain would bring up her grandmother's laugh and Teodor's wire together, and whoever knelt here with a cord would find both, and would have to decide, as she had, which shadow to measure by. [682]

The sun slipped behind the cloud-plain. [688] The founders' shadows ran out to nothing and were gone. [698] The whispering thinned to a single voice, then to the memory of a voice, then to the sound the crystal makes when it is only stone. [724]

Wen kept writing until she could no longer see the page, and then a little longer, by feel. [742]
