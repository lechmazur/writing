The greenhouse had not seen a dawn in eleven years, and Sera Vaun had not spoken to another person in nine. [21] The alchemists who built it had wanted a night that never lifted, because the orchard's fruit would only set in darkness, so they had poured night into the glass itself and then, one by one, they had died or left. [61] Sera stayed. [63] A knight without an order has no reason to be anywhere, and so anywhere becomes the same as here. [82] She kept her armor on because taking it off felt like a decision. [95]

The trees did not care. [100] They were quince and pear and something the alchemists had called mirrorfruit, and their branches moved when nothing moved them, slow gestures against the black glass. [126] This was not a feeling Sera had about the trees. [136] It was a fact; she had measured it. [144] The greenhouse was mystical the way a river is wet, and she had stopped arguing with it. [161]

What changed was the world outside. [167] In the third week of what should have been spring, the road beyond the glass began to rearrange itself. [186] Hills folded. [188] A village she remembered burning appeared unburnt on the ridge, then vanished, then reappeared as a lake. [205] Reality was shifting, and the only place it did not shift was inside the alchemists' night. [221] The mirrorfruit swelled. [224] Each one, when she held it to her eye, showed a version of the road that might yet settle. [243] None of them were ripe. [248] None of them would hold a shape. [255]

She found the program on the fourth night, wedged in the roots of the oldest pear. [271] It was a folded playbill, foxed and soft, from a marionette theater that had toured the provinces when she was a girl. [293] The Company of Hanging Stars, it said. [300] Twelve Figures in the Dance of the Orchard. [308] Below the title, someone had drawn the puppets' movements in a shorthand of strokes and hooks: lift, cross, sink, turn. [328] Sera had seen this show. [333] She had been seven, standing on a bench, and the puppets had been trees. [347]

She looked up at the branches gesturing against the glass and understood, with a lurch, that the alchemists had not invented their orchard. [370] They had copied it from a puppet show, or the puppet show had copied something older, and the pattern had passed from strings to roots and been forgotten in both. [400] The trees were moving through it still, but alone, with no one answering. [413] A dance needs a second body. [419]

Her hands shook. [422] She told herself it was the cold. [429] She unsnapped the gorget at her throat first, because a marionette cannot turn its head in steel, and the clasp gave with a sound like a small bone. [457] Then the pauldrons. [460] Then the rest, piece by piece, nine years of not deciding laid on the flagstones. [475] She stood in her shirt in the endless night and read the first stroke. [489]

Lift. [490] The pear lifted with her. [495] Cross, and the quince crossed. [500] It was not graceful; she was forty-one and had never danced sober. [512] But the orchard did not want grace. [519] It wanted the pattern completed, and as she sank and turned, the mirrorfruit on the nearest branch began, at last, to darken toward ripeness. [543]

Outside, the road folded again. [548] She felt the shift as a pressure behind her eyes, the world trying on shapes, discarding them. [565] If she could finish before it chose, she could pick one fruit and hold in her palm the shape of what was still possible, before possibility hardened into whatever came next. [596] That was all she wanted. [601] Not to fix the world. [606] Only to have seen it while it could still be anything. [617]

Twelve figures. [619] By the ninth her legs burned and her breath came white, and she was laughing, a thin cracked sound she did not recognize as hers. [644] The shivering had stopped being cold and started being something else. [655] On the twelfth figure she knelt, as the program showed, and the oldest pear bent its lowest branch down to meet her, and she found that she was murmuring. [684] Not the oath of her dead order. [691] She had not said those words in nine years and would not say them now. [706] Something smaller. [708] I am here, she told the tree. [715] I will keep coming. [719] An allegiance, if that was the word for it, given to the only thing that had kept its shape. [738]

The mirrorfruit dropped into her hand, ripe and heavy and cold. [749] She raised it to her eye. [755] Inside, the road was still moving, but slowly now, and every shape it tried was one she could bear. [774] She sat down among her armor in the dark and watched it, trembling, and did not want to be anywhere else. [795]
