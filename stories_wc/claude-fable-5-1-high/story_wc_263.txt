The archway had never learned to keep dates. [8] It stood at the edge of the fen where Marit's grandmother had once counted winters by the color of the aurora rather than by any almanac, and Marit had come to it now because every other place had grown too loud. [49] Not loud with voices. [53] Loud with everything people did not say. [60]

Since childhood she had heard unspoken words the way others heard rain on a roof. [75] A baker's swallowed grief, a lover's unconfessed relief, the thousand small cowardices of a market square: all of it poured into her until there was no room left for anything of her own. [108] She had been a composer once, or had meant to be. [119] Now the staves in her notebook stayed empty for so long that the paper yellowed around the silence. [137] The village called her moonstruck, a dreamer who stared at the sky while her bread burned. [153]

She carried her grandmother's astrolabe, a brass disc worn smooth by generations of thumbs. [167] The old woman had called it a listening instrument, though it was made for measuring the heights of stars. [186] "Every body is built from stardust," she used to say, "and stardust remembers where it fell from." [203] "When you cannot hear yourself, child, go and ask the sky what you are made of." [219]

Marit had laughed at that, once. [225] She was not laughing now. [230]

The aurora came up green over the stones, then violet, then a red so deep it seemed to bleed out of the dark rather than shine into it. [258] Under the archway the light bent strangely, pooled in the carved grooves, and made the frost glitter like ground glass. [278] Marit stood in the center and felt, for the first time in years, that the unsaid words of the world had thinned to a whisper. [303] There was no one here to not-speak. [310] Only the fen, the stones, and the sky. [318]

Then the first star fell. [323]

She raised the astrolabe without thinking and sighted along its rule, tracing the bright scratch from Perseus down toward the treeline. [344] Something answered. [346] Not a voice, not exactly; a note, low and burnished, as if the brass had remembered a sound it was never built to make. [370] Her hand shook. [373] A second star crossed, and she traced it too, and the note found a companion a fifth above it. [392]

She understood then what her grandmother had meant by remembering. [402] The stardust in her blood was older than any grief she had ever absorbed from a stranger, older than the fear that she had nothing of her own to say. [432] Each falling star was a kinsman, and each one she traced sang back a fragment of a melody she had carried since before she was born, buried beneath the noise of everyone else's silence. [466]

She traced until her arms ached. [472] The aurora shifted overhead, marking hours that belonged to no calendar anyone had written; the archway measured time in colors, and she lost count of the colors. [499] Green might have been a night. [505] Violet might have been a week. [511] The melody grew, note by note, and with it a hunger so fierce it frightened her, because she could feel the music straining against something in her chest that would not open all the way. [546]

She traced faster. [549] The notes came thinner. [553] She traced with both hands and the astrolabe went cold and dumb. [565]

Marit sank against the stone, defeated, and let the instrument fall into her lap. [579] Her eyes closed. [582] For the length of one red sky she simply rested, doing nothing, wanting nothing, hearing nothing but her own breath under the arch. [605]

And in that rest, the thing in her chest unlatched. [615]

The melody did not need to be chased. [623] It had only needed her to stop listening to the unsaid grief of every passing stranger long enough to hear what she herself had never said aloud. [650] The unspoken words she had always heard in others had a twin in her: her own unspoken music, waiting under the same silence. [673] Now it rose, whole, dark and shimmering, joyous in a way that hurt. [686]

She wept, and she did not know if it was rapture or mourning, and she suspected the difference had never mattered. [707]

When the aurora faded to grey she walked home across the fen, the astrolabe warm again in her coat. [726] The village would still be loud with everything it would not say. [738] But she carried a louder thing now, and when she reached her notebook the yellowed staves would finally have something to hold. [760]
