Veyle had been bored for three hundred years, and she had counted every one of them. [16] The escondite helped, at first. [21] She had folded it herself out of quartz and refracted light, a hideout tucked along the fourth axis where no one else could see, its rooms hung like beads on a thread of magnetism she spun from the earth's own field. [62] Being dimensionally aware was a lonely gift; she saw the seams of the world where others saw walls. [80] Everything she built along those seams stayed. [87] That was the curse. [91] The amulet fragment hung at her throat, a chip of something older than iron, and its one gift was that nothing which loved her could ever leave. [118] Lovers came back. [121] Dogs came back. [124] Even the dead drifted at the edges of her sight, unable to finish the sentence of their going. [142] She had said goodbye ten thousand times and never once learned what came after it. [157]

The rooms knew this about her. [163] Over the centuries the prisms had grown aware, the way anything does when it is looked at long enough by someone who sees too much. [188] The library hummed when she entered. [194] The east chamber turned its facets toward her like a face. [205] They loved her, and so they stayed, and their staying was a low, constant pressure at the back of her skull, like weather that never broke. [231] So she began the intrigue. [236]

It was a small treason, conducted in whispers. [244] She could not speak to the rooms directly; the fragment would hear, and tighten. [258] But the magnetic thread that held them was hers, and she learned to tremble it. [273] A flutter in the field meant: you are not prisoners. [283] A long slow pulse meant: I would not stop you. [293] She fed the rooms doubt the way one feeds a fire, one breath at a time, and she watched the mutiny take root in their angles. [319] The corridor stopped meeting the stair. [325] The library turned its back. [330] It was the first interesting thing that had happened in a century, and she nearly wept from the pleasure of it. [351] Still, doubt alone could not break the fragment. [359] For that she needed the sun. [365]

The storm arrived on a Tuesday, which pleased her. [374] She had watched it leave the sun's surface three days earlier, a coronal fist flung outward, and she had timed her last whisper to meet it. [400] When the charged wind struck the earth's field, the field bucked. [411] Her thread bucked with it. [416] The whole escondite rang like struck glass, and every prism in it flared with colors that had no names in three dimensions. [438] The fragment at her throat went hot, then cold, then quiet. [449] Its grip, for one long moment, was a hand that had forgotten what it held. [464] Now, she thought, and did not send it down the thread. [475] She only let go of the thread entirely. [483]

The rooms understood. [486] The east chamber went first, folding itself along an axis she had never shown it, a bright hinge closing on nothing. [507] Then the corridor, unspooling like a ribbon dropped from a height. [518] Then the library, which paused, and hummed once against her spine, and went. [531] Each departure landed in her like a distant detonation heard through water. [543] She stood in the last room and watched the walls consider her. [555] "Go on," she said aloud, the first words she had spoken in a decade. [569] "I want to see it." [574] The room's facets dimmed in sequence, a slow blink, and then it too found its hidden hinge and closed, and she was standing on bare rock under a sky torn open with aurora. [607]

The fragment at her throat was only a stone now. [617] She waited for the pressure at the back of her skull to return. [630] It did not. [633] What came instead was harder to name: a widening, an ache the exact shape of every room that had ever loved her. [655] So this was after. [659] Not silence; not nothing. [663] A hollow that kept the outline of what had filled it. [674] Grief, she supposed, though the word felt too small for something so architectural. [687] The sky above her shivered green and violet as the storm poured itself out against the world. [704] Veyle sat down on the rock and let the ache do its slow work, and for the first time in three hundred years she was not bored. [731] Somewhere along the fourth axis, her rooms were learning what it meant to be elsewhere. [746] She hoped it was interesting. [751] She hoped, quietly and with a thunder she had not felt in centuries, that one day they would not come back. [772]
