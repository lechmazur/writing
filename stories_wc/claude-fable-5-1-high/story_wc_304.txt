The grotto hung a mile above the salt flats, a hollow shard of basalt that the old lifting-engines had never brought back down. [23] Ilse had worked there eleven winters, painting the faces of the dead so they would look at peace when the aurora took them. [46] Tonight the body on her table was Oren, who had taught her the work. [60] He had been beautician and mortician both, as she was now, because in the grotto the two were one craft: the dead did not go down into earth here but up into light, and light was unkind to a face left plain. [102]

Green fire moved across the cavern mouth. [109] It spilled over the basalt and lay on Oren's cheekbones like water. [121] Ilse mixed her pigments by that light, as he had shown her: ochre for warmth, a breath of violet under the eyes, wax to seal the lips. [148] Her hands were steady. [152] She was lucidly watchful in the way he had demanded, seeing the face and not the man, until she reached his brow and saw the man anyway. [179]

On the shelf behind her sat his receiver, a box of copper coil and cracked bakelite, and in its cradle the emerald chip. [202] Oren had used the stone as a detector, the way older sets used galena. [216] He said emerald heard differently. [221] On long nights he would touch its wire to the temple of whoever lay on the table and listen to the hiss the aurora made in a body that had just stopped. [253]

"There is a threshold of stillness," he told her once. [263] "A few hours where the nerves are quiet but not yet nothing. [275] The static is mostly sky. [280] Mostly." [281]

She had never heard anything in it but noise. [290] He had claimed, more than once, that if you were patient the noise arranged itself, and that somewhere inside it was the last spark of a person, going out. [319] She had thought it was grief talking, or the altitude. [329]

Now the threshold was his, and it was closing. [338]

Ilse set down her brush. [343] She lifted the receiver, wound the coil, and laid the emerald's wire against the skin she had just painted. [362] The earpiece filled with hiss. [367] It sounded like snow on a roof, like the sea heard through a wall. [381] Above her the aurora widened, and the hiss widened with it, and she understood that most of what she heard truly was sky. [404]

So she did what he had done. [411] She stopped trying to hear him and let the static be static, the way she let a face be a face. [432] Minutes passed. [434] The green light dimmed to a rose color and came back. [445] And under the roar, faint as a pulse under a wrist, there was a shape. [460]

Three short surges, a rest, two long. [467] Again. [468]

It was the rhythm he used to tap on the table with his ring while he waited for wax to set. [489] She had heard it a thousand times and never once thought of it as his. [504]

The pattern was already thinning. [509] Each pass came back softer, the rests a little longer, as if the hand were tiring. [525] Ilse knew what the receiver could do, because he had told her that too, on a night she had not believed him. [547] The emerald did not only hear. [553] Held long enough against a signal, it took some of it in, the way a cold stone takes the warmth of a palm. [576]

She pressed the wire closer and held it there while the aurora poured over the two of them, and she let the rhythm infuse the stone, three short, a rest, two long, until the pattern under the static was too faint to count and then was not there at all. [626] Only sky remained in the earpiece. [632] The threshold had closed. [636]

She took the wire away and finished his brow. [645]

At dawn she wheeled him to the cavern mouth, where the wind came up from the salt flats and the last of the aurora hung thin as breath. [673] His face was warm-colored and quiet, and it would hold in the light. [686] She let him go the way she had let others go, and did not look away. [702]

Afterward she sat on the cold basalt with the emerald chip in her fist. [716] She did not put it in the earpiece. [724] She did not need to. [729] Against her palm, so slight she might have invented it, the stone ticked: three short, a rest, two long. [748]

She understood then why he had spent his nights listening to noise. [760] Not to keep the dead. [765] To learn the one small thing in each of them that was not sky, and to carry it a little way past the edge. [789]
