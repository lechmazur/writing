Odun had stopped counting the mornings he woke somewhere else. [10] He would close his eyes beneath a brass palm and open them under the ribs of an iron kapok three valleys east, the dew of condensed oil cooling on his cheek. [41] It had started the night his sixth sense woke. [50] Not sight, not sound, but a hum behind the sternum whenever a thought came close to being true. [68] The academies had called him a stray philosopher, meaning he belonged to no school and kept no roof, and he had taken the insult as a map. [95] He walked into the mechanical rainforest because it was the last place where nothing had been explained. [112]

Nobody remembered who built it. [117] Copper vines threaded the canopy, ferns of stamped tin unfurled at dawn with a sound like drawers opening, and rain fell from cloud-engines that no one had ever seen refuel. [147] Miracles here were not rare; they were merely probable, and Odun had learned to greet them with a nod. [166]

In his pocket he carried a small gear, no wider than a coin, which he had found clenched in his fist the first morning he woke displaced. [193] Its teeth were worn on one side, as though it had turned for a very long time against something that resisted. [214] He wanted to unravel the pattern of his wanderings, and he wanted something larger, which he could only phrase as a question: where had the wild frontier of his mind gone, the untamed country he had felt as a boy before every thought arrived pre-labeled? [259] The forest, he suspected, knew. [264]

Since he could not stay awake forever, he learned to listen instead. [276] Wind through the brass palms whistled high and thin, like a kettle deciding. [289] The iron kapoks groaned in slow chords. [296] The copper ferns ticked, and the tall pewter figs, hollow to the root, produced a note so low he felt it in his teeth. [320] Each morning, before opening his eyes, he named the tree above him by its voice and scratched a mark on the gear with a flint. [345] Palm, kapok, fern, fig, kapok, palm. [351] After forty nights the marks made a sequence, and the sequence, when he hummed it, made the hum in his chest answer. [373]

It was not random. [377] It was a phrase, and it was repeating with variation, the way a mind circles a problem it has almost solved. [398] He understood then that he was not wandering. [406] He was being passed along. [411] Each tree handed him to the next in the dark, the way one neuron hands a spark to its neighbor, and the forest was thinking with him as its idea. [441] The hum behind his sternum was not his sixth sense alone. [452] It was the forest's insight, moving through him, seeking somewhere to land. [464]

The phrase, he realized, always faltered at the same place: after the pewter fig, where a note should follow and did not. [486] A silence, like a word on the tip of the tongue. [497] He walked for two days by daylight, following the fig's low note upwind until it led him to a clearing where a single tree stood unlike the others. [525] It made no sound at all. [531] Its leaves hung frozen, its trunk a column of interlocked wheels, and at the height of his heart one socket sat empty, its neighbors worn smooth on one side. [560]

Odun took out the small gear. [566] Of course it fit. [570] In a forest of probable miracles, the astonishing thing would have been if it had not. [586] He hesitated anyway, because he saw what the forest had done: it had stolen a piece of its own stuck thought, pressed it into the hand of a stranger, and sent him walking in circles for forty nights until he learned to hear where it belonged. [632] It could not solve itself from inside. [639] Neither could he, he thought, and that was the answer to his other question. [653] The wild frontier had never been inside his skull, where the academies kept looking. [667] It was the border where one mind lends itself to another and comes back changed. [682]

He pressed the gear home. [687] The silent tree shuddered, its wheels caught, and its leaves began to turn in a wind that had been waiting for them. [709] The missing note rose, warm and exact, and across three valleys he heard the palms and kapoks and ferns and figs take it up, the whole phrase finally closing like a hand. [741] The hum in his chest did not fade. [749] It deepened, and he understood it would go on as long as there were thoughts too large for one head. [769]

That night, Odun lay down beneath the tree that now sang, and he did not wonder where he would wake. [789] He only hoped it held a question. [796]
