On the isle of Amaranth, the shadows left at dusk. [10] When the tea garden rose above the last cloud bank, every shadow peeled loose from its owner and sank toward the earth like a dropped shawl, and the island, unburdened, climbed a little higher. [44] Ines Varo had watched this happen every evening for forty years and had never once felt lighter. [61]

She was the lantern maker, and the basilica needed her. [71] It stood at the garden's heart, a cathedral of green glass grown from the same sand as the tea bowls, and inside it the priests kept the dreams. [99] When someone on Amaranth died, a priest pressed a rough garnet to the cooling forehead and recorded the final dream, then set the stone in a lantern to burn. [128] But a raw stone gives only a smear of light. [138] The dream must be polished out of it, facet by facet, and only Ines had the hands for that. [157] She had built the whole lit interior of the basilica, four hundred lanterns of other people's endings. [174]

Her son's garnet came to her in a wooden box, the same as any. [188] Tomas had fallen from the garden's rim while pruning the outermost hedges, and the priests, embarrassed by her, spoke of honor and duty and left quickly. [214]

Ines sat with the box until the shadows went. [223] Then she did what the grieving of Amaranth were supposed to do: she took a sheet of tea paper and tried to fold a crane. [248] The custom said a thousand cranes would carry sorrow off the island's edge. [261] Her fingers, which could coax a dream from stone, could not make the paper hold a shape. [278] Every crane sagged open. [282]

So she was crafty instead. [287]

She told the priests the garnet needed a fortnight on the wheel. [299] It needed three days. [303] On the first night, after the shadow detached from her and slid down through the mist, she set the stone against the polishing leather and gave it one face. [332] The dream came up through the garnet like a fish rising. [343] Tomas at six, standing in the tea rows, arms out, pretending the island was a ship. [359]

She had to stop. [363] The light of it was so exact that it burned. [373]

This was the thing she had never told the priests: a polished dream did not simply shine. [390] It could be cut. [394] Each facet she ground was a fold. [401] She had learned this by accident decades ago, shaping a stranger's dream too far and finding the light came out of it in creases, in wings. [427] She had kept the trick to herself, as craftsmen do. [437] Now she meant to use it. [443] If paper would not hold her grief, she would fold the dream itself, one facet at a time, until the light that came off her son's last thought took the shape of a crane and flew. [479]

She worked the second night and the third. [488] Each pass of the leather brought Tomas nearer and made him harder to look at. [503] The dream transformed as she cut it. [510] Not a memory after all, she understood, but a dreaming: he was on the rim, and he was falling, and in the dream he did not fear it, because the island above him kept rising and he thought he was the one going up. [554] He descended believing he ascended. [559] He was happy. [562]

Ines put down the stone and wept in a way that made no sound. [576] Then she picked it up and finished. [583]

The last facet went on at the hour the shadows left. [594] She carried the garnet through the tea rows to the basilica, where the priests were lighting the evening lamps, and set it in the empty lantern they had hung for him. [625] The light that came out was not a smear, and not a beam. [638] It came out in folds. [643] A crane of pale red light unfolded from the lantern, then another, then more, each one leaving as her own shadow left, though the cranes went upward through the green glass and out over the garden while the shadows went down. [684]

The priests said nothing. [688] Later they would call it a miracle and argue over whether to forbid it. [702] Ines did not stay for that. [708] She walked back through the tea rows, and the island climbed, and for the first time in forty years she could not tell whether she was rising with it or had begun, very gently, to fall. [744] She thought Tomas would have said it did not matter. [754] She thought he might have been right. [761]
