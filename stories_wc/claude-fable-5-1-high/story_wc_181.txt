The pistachio shell arrived on a Tuesday, though Tuesdays meant little on an asteroid with no sun to rise. [19] Ines Vell, curator of the Orchard of Provenance, turned it over under the lamp and found it had been sawn flat and polished into a cameo blank, waiting for a face. [50] The provenance tag read: carved by Ines Vell, bequeathed by her granddaughter Oda. [63] Ines had no granddaughter. [67] She had no children, no partner, and no pistachio trees that had ever fruited, only the forty saplings blossoming outside the dome in the thin starlight, their petals drifting upward because gravity here was a suggestion. [103]

The Orchard's rule was simply complicated. [109] Simple: every object that arrived had already been given away. [119] Complicated: given away by whom, and when, was not the curator's business. [131] The Ledger, a bound book thick as a door, recorded each bequest between its first and last page, and the last page was sealed with wax the color of a bruise. [162] Curators were permitted to write in the Ledger. [170] Curators were forbidden to read its ending. [177] Ines had accepted this for eleven years the way one accepts weather. [189]

Now she held a shell from a tree that had not fruited, carved by hands that had not carved it, given by a child of a child she did not have, and she felt bewildered in the old sense, wandered off into wilderness. [232]

She did what curators do. [237] She inquired. [239] She asked the intake system where the parcel had originated; it answered with the Orchard's own coordinates. [256] She asked the Ledger's index for Oda and found the name entered forty times, always as donor, never as recipient, in handwriting that slanted exactly like hers. [283] She asked the saplings, which was not procedure, and they shed a few more petals into the dark and told her nothing. [305]

Then she went to the bathroom, because that was where she thought. [317] It was the one room on the station with a mirror and no window, the one room where the orchard could not watch her back. [342] Every real idea of her life had come to her sitting on the closed lid, elbows on knees, staring at the grout. [364]

She stared at the grout. [369]

Inheritance, she had always assumed, ran downhill: the dead to the living, the old to the young, a river. [388] But the Orchard had never said that. [395] The Orchard had said only that everything arriving had already been given. [407] Given was a tense, not a direction. [414] If a granddaughter could bequeath backward, then the saplings outside were not the Orchard's beginning but its bequest, seeds sent from an ending to a start, and Ines herself was not the keeper of the collection but one of its items, catalogued between the first page and the last. [463] The blank was blank because she had not yet carved it. [474] Which meant the face it wanted was one she would have to meet. [487]

She laughed once, sharply, at the mirror, and the laugh had light in it and something else underneath, the way a blossom has a bruise. [512]

The rule against reading the last page had always seemed like modesty. [524] Now it seemed like a lock on the only door that led anywhere. [537] If Oda existed, she existed on that page. [545] If the pistachios would ever fruit, the date was written there. [556] And if the knowledge was forbidden, that was only because whoever wrote the rule had understood that a curator who knew her own ending might refuse to walk toward it. [586]

Ines washed her hands, dried them, and went to the Ledger. [597]

The wax came away under her thumbnail with less resistance than she had expected, as though it had been softened many times before. [620] The last page held a single entry in her own slanted hand. [632]

Item: one pistachio shell, cameo blank. [638] Donor: Oda Vell, aged nine, who will find it in a drawer and ask what it was for. [656] Recipient: the curator, who will inquire, and who will therefore begin. [667]

Beneath it, in the same hand, smaller: Carve her as you imagine her, and the trees will know what shape to fruit. [689]

Ines stood a long while in the reading light. [698] She was not afraid, exactly. [703] She was something adjacent to afraid, a brightness with a shadow cast forward instead of back. [719] She had wanted to know, and now she knew, and the knowing did not close anything. [735] It opened a drawer in a house she had not built for a girl she had not imagined yet. [754]

She took the blank to her workbench and picked up the smallest graver. [767] Outside, in the thin starlight, forty pistachio trees leaned very slightly toward the window, as if to see. [785]
