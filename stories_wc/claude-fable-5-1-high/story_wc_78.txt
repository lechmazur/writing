At the blue hour, when the sea and the sky agreed on a single color and neither would admit which had chosen it, Halde knelt on the leeward rim of the Wreath and put her ear to the water. [39] The Wreath was an orchard afloat on a circle of driftwood, forty trees rooted in soil packed between lashed timbers, turning slowly on the open sea. [65] Windward, the waves argued. [69] Leeward, in the ring's shelter, the water lay flat enough to read, and reading it was her office. [87] The orchard folk called her the tide prophet, though she had never once been asked to prophesy anything more mystical than where the current meant to carry them by noon. [117]

In her left hand she held a xylophone mallet, the last piece of an instrument she had lost overboard as a girl. [139] The bars had sunk; the mallet had floated. [147] She had decided then that the world itself would have to serve as the missing keys. [163]

She tapped the nearest timber. [168] It answered low and damp, a note with a long belly. [179] In her book she sketched the sound, not as a letter but as a shape: a branching line that split, and split again, the way a plum tree splits toward light. [210] Years of these sketches had taught her something the others found unnerving: every resonance she drew looked like every other, only larger or smaller. [234] A struck twig made the same figure as a struck trunk, which made the same figure as the whole ring humming in a swell. [258]

She looked up. [261] Clouds were her second text, and this morning they were combed into long hooks curling from the west, hurrying faster than the surface wind explained. [286] Beneath them the leeward water showed a slow lift, a swell too wide to belong to weather. [303] Something large was displacing the sea. [309]

She stood and called across the trees. [316] By the time the others gathered, the shape had risen out of the blue: a drift of wreckage, half a hull and a snarl of spars and iron, riding the current straight at them. [350]

"It will break the ring," said Tobin, who tended the pears. [361] "Cut the two old apples for poles and we fend it off." [373]

Halde shook her head. [377] Every timber under their feet had once come at them exactly like this, a threat riding a swell, and someone before her had chosen to catch it rather than push it away. [409] That was how a circle grew. [415]

"Give me the skiff and ropes," she said. [423] "We take it in." [427]

She rowed out while the light stayed blue and the wreck loomed, groaning. [440] Up close it was not one thing but a hundred, spar and plank and rusted band, each with its own voice waiting. [462] She struck the first spar with the mallet and listened. [472] Bright, short, a note that would split cleanly. [480] "I dub you Ash," she said, and tied a red cord to it, and sketched its shape. [497] The hull's rib answered deep and sullen; that one she dubbed Keel and marked in black. [513] Piece by piece she named the wreck into an inventory of tones, and as she drew, the pattern held. [532] Small pieces branched like small notes; large ones branched the same way, larger. [545] So she worked as the pattern told her to, fitting the bright pieces at the outer edge where the twigs of the ring already lived, and the deep ones inward against the trunks, generating the circle's next rank from the rule of the last. [589]

The orchard folk hauled where she pointed. [596] Tobin, grumbling, lashed Keel beneath the pears and struck it once himself with the flat of his hand, and looked surprised at how it sang. [621]

By the time the blue gave way to gold, the wreck was gone. [634] In its place the Wreath had widened by a full stride, and the trees, which had leaned toward the old rim, now had new ground to lean toward. [662] Halde walked the new edge with the mallet, tapping. [671] Ash, Keel, Band, Rib: each note rose and fell against the next, none of them finishing, every one of them handing the phrase to its neighbor. [697] It was not a song that resolved. [704] It was a song that kept asking, and the ring kept turning under it, restless, tuned, afloat. [721]

She opened her book to a fresh page and drew the whole circle, and it looked, as she had known it would, exactly like a twig. [747] Tomorrow's clouds would bring something else. [753] She would kneel leeward and listen, mallet ready, for the next obstacle worth a name. [768]
