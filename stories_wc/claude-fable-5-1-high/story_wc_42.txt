The tremors began the week the observatory was condemned, and by the time Ilse sent for the camera repairer, the great dome had learned to hum. [26] Aurel Vance climbed the last stair above the clouds with his tool roll on his back, seventy-one years old and unbothered by the shaking. [50] He had repaired cameras for fifty years in a shop that smelled of brass and cedar, and he had learned that most things shake before they break, and most do not break at all. [84]

"The plate camera has stopped exposing," Ilse said, meeting him at the door. [97] She was young, sleepless, with the look of someone already half gone. [109] "We have nine nights before they pull the funding and the mountain pulls the rest." [124]

The lunar observatory had photographed the moon every clear night for ninety years, one plate per night, a record its founders called changelessly constant. [148] Ilse showed him the archive: thousands of silver moons in drawers, each one the same, each one different by a hair of libration and dust. [173] "Constant," Aurel said, "is a word people use when they have not looked closely." [187]

The camera itself was a cathedral of brass bolted to the refractor, and its shutter was governed by the observatory clock. [208] That was the trouble. [212] The first tremor had sheared the clock's escapement, and now its second hand spun without stopping, a thin black blur that made a full circle every heartbeat. [239] The shutter, slaved to it, opened and closed too fast to gather any light. [253]

"I keep trying to stop it," Ilse said. [261] "It won't be stopped." [265]

Aurel watched the hand fly. [270] Then he asked her what she would do when the nine nights were over. [284] She told him about the launch program in the lowlands, the job offered and not yet accepted, the moon she had watched all her life through glass and might one day help someone reach. [318] "Escape velocity," she said, and laughed without joy. [326] "Eleven kilometers a second, and I cannot get myself down one staircase." [338]

He understood then that the camera was not the only thing spinning too fast to hold light. [355]

He did not try to stop the clock. [363] Instead he restructured the whole mechanism, unbolting the shutter from the ruined escapement and building a new train of gears that took the spinning hand as its engine rather than its master. [395] Each frantic revolution now advanced a slow wheel by one tooth, and the slow wheel would hold the shutter open for exactly as long as the moon required. [423] The trouble was timing it, because he had no working clock to time it against. [438]

Ilse kept a potted birch in the dome, the only tree above the clouds, dropping yellow leaves onto the iron floor as the season turned. [463] Aurel sat beneath it for an hour with his eyes shut. [474] "My teacher timed shutters by the sound of a leaf falling," he said when she asked. [490] "A birch leaf from that height takes a little over a second to reach the floor, and it makes one sound when it lets go and another when it lands." [520]

"You cannot hear a leaf let go." [527]

"You can if you have stopped wanting anything else." [536]

So they listened together while the mountain shifted under them, and when a leaf released, he tapped the shutter open, and when it touched iron, he tapped it shut, and he filed the slow wheel until the mechanism matched that interval and its multiples. [580] It took four nights. [584]

On the fifth, the sky cleared, and the moon came up over the cloud floor like something remembered. [602] The rebuilt shutter breathed open, held, and closed, while beside it the useless second hand spun and spun, feeding its panic into patience one tooth at a time. [630]

Ilse developed the plate herself. [635] The moon on it was sharp and silver and not quite the moon of any drawer in the archive, because no moon ever was. [659]

"That is all a camera does," Aurel said, holding it to the lamp. [672] "It cannot keep anything. [676] It only harnesses what is leaving and lets it leave more slowly." [688]

The floor trembled, a long low note through the iron. [698] Ilse looked at the plate, and at the spinning hand, and at the staircase that went down into cloud. [717] "Escape velocity isn't about going fast," she said slowly, as if reading it off the mechanism. [733] "It's about spending the pull instead of fighting it." [742]

Aurel packed his tools. [746] On the last of the nine nights she exposed the final plate, and then she carried the birch down the mountain in her arms, its remaining leaves letting go one by one behind her, each fall a second she could hear. [787]
