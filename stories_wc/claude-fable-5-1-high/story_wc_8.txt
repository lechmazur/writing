The town of Hollin Cross had agreed, without ever voting on it, that the last petal from the rosebush on the old platform marked the day the dead could be spoken of. [32] Ilse Varga, who had written the town's history and therefore invented half of it, waited beneath the ivy until the petal fell. [54] It landed on the rusted rails. [60] Then she heard footsteps on the gravel, and Nell Brenner came through the gap where the ticket gate had been. [80] The girl was twenty-three and carried a shoebox of letters against her chest like something that might bite. [98]

"You said you would tell me here," Nell said. [107]

Ilse nodded and sat on the bench the ivy had not yet swallowed. [120] She had spent her working life inside the feelings of other people, sorting their grief and their pride into paragraphs, and she had learned to feel a thing fully and still reason through it. [154]

"Your grandmother planted that rose the spring after the crash," she said. [166] "She asked me to write the account for the memorial plaque." [177]

"I know what the plaque says," Nell said. [185] "Signalman Tomas Brenner remained at his post and threw the points, saving the passenger coach at the cost of his own life. [207] I have read it every year since I could read." [217] She lifted the lid of the shoebox. [224] "These say he was drinking that winter. [231] They say he wrote to a friend that he had fallen asleep in the box twice." [247]

Ilse reached into her own bag and took out a single page in a plastic sleeve. [263] It was a facsimile of a twelfth-century chronicle, and at its top a great letter B blazed in gold leaf and lapis, vines curling through it, a small painted monk kneeling in the bowl of the letter. [300]

"Before I came here I studied this man," Ilse said. [310] "Brother Anselm of Wexford. [314] He wrote that his abbey's founder had healed a leper at the gate, and pilgrims came for three hundred years because of it. [337] In the margin, in ink so faint it was missed until 1961, he confessed he had made the miracle up because the village was starving and the abbey's stores were the only thing that would feed it." [374]

Nell looked at the gold letter and then at the ivy strangling the signal box. [389] "You are telling me you did what he did." [398]

"I am telling you the shape of it is the same," Ilse said. [411] "Your grandmother had two children and no pension, and this town had four coffins and no one to blame but a man who was already dead. [437] I found the same letters you found. [444] I read them, and I put them back in the drawer, and I wrote a hero." [460]

The wind moved the ivy, and somewhere behind the station a loose sheet of roofing iron knocked against a beam. [480]

"Why show me the monk?" Nell asked. [487] "To make it noble?" [491]

"No." [492] Ilse turned the page so the small kneeling figure caught the last light. [505] "Anselm painted himself on his knees inside the lie. [514] He fed the village, and he still wrote the confession, because he knew that a kindness which buries the truth is a debt, not a gift. [540] It took eight centuries for someone to collect. [548] I did not want it to take that long for you to recapture what was yours." [564]

Nell was crying now, but she was not looking away, and Ilse thought that was the whole of it, the entire reason she had come. [589] The lie had kept a family fed and a town from tearing at a widow. [604] It had also left a girl to grow up measuring herself against a man who never existed. [621] Both of those were true at once, and Ilse could hold both without either one cancelling the other. [639]

"So what was he?" Nell said. [645]

"A tired man who drank because the night shifts were long, who loved his wife badly and his children well, and who did not wake up," Ilse said. [673] "That is a smaller story than the plaque. [681] It is also yours, and I have kept it from you long enough." [694] She held out the illuminated page. [700] "Take this with the letters. [705] When the town asks why you want the plaque changed, show them Anselm, and tell them a good lie can still be corrected by the people it was told to protect." [736]

Nell took the page and the box and stood a long while looking at the bare rosebush. [753] Then she walked back through the gap in the gate, and Ilse stayed on the bench until the ivy and the dark were the same colour, feeling lighter than she had in twenty years and knowing precisely why. [791]
