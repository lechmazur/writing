Hallam Vey built bunkers for people who feared the sky, and he built the best one for his daughter. [19] Every day at noon, gravity inverted for one hour, and anything unstrapped fell upward into the clouds. [36] The wealthy paid him for rooms lined with lead and gold leaf, because gold was heavy and looked like safety. [56] Ilse, who was eleven, could not breathe in any of them. [67] The walls came close. [71]

The hush in the family bunker was gilded, a silence so expensive it had a taste, like sucking a coin. [91] She had learned to survive it by limiting exposure: three seconds of the hush on her tongue, then she hummed, then three seconds more. [115] In sips, a silence could be studied instead of drowned in. [126]

She kept what she learned in a porcelain doll no longer than her thumb, hollow, with a cork where its hat should be. [149] Her mother had left it on the table the noon she did not come down to the bunker. [167] Ilse whispered each silence into the doll: the copper hush of the bunker, the chalk silence of the schoolroom, the wet silence of the cellar drain. [193] They were intangibly tangible, weightless things that still made the doll feel fuller in her fist. [209] She wanted every flavor there was. [215] She suspected the sky had one. [221]

The noon she was ready, she let her father buckle her in, kissed his knuckles, and waited for the hour to turn. [243] When her hair lifted, she unclipped the harness. [251] Hallam shouted her name, but he was strapped and could only watch her rise to the hatch, spin the wheel, and fall out into the noon. [277]

The garden dropped away beneath her, then the roofs, then the town, and her chest, which had been a clenched fist for eleven years, opened like a hand. [305] The clouds were cold and brief. [311] Above them hung a trellis. [316]

It stretched for miles, a lattice of rope and living vine strung between drifting anchors of sod, and people were tied to it by the ankles, gardening upside down. [345] A man caught her wrist before she could sail past. [355] "Guest," he called over his shoulder, and the word traveled along the trellis like a bell. [371] Someone looped a vine around her waist. [378] Someone else pressed a cup of warm water into her hands, because here a guest was given a drink before a question. [400]

"These only sprout during the hour," the man said, showing her a row of pale seeds. [416] "Too much ordinary weight and they rot." [423] "We keep them wrapped below and only bring them up at noon." [435] "Sixty minutes of sky, then dark again." [442] Ilse understood him perfectly. [446] As she watched, a green thread pushed up from one seed, thin and shaking, into the wind. [463]

She uncorked the doll and held it toward him. [472] "Taste," she said. [475] He bent his mouth to the tiny opening, and his face changed the way faces change at a funeral. [494] "That is a locked room," he said. [501] "That is my father's," said Ilse. [507]

Then she held the doll open to the trellis, and let it drink for three seconds, no more. [525] The silence up there was not empty. [532] It tasted of rain before it falls, of many hands working without needing to speak, of a door that is never shut because no one has thought to build one. [562] She corked it. [565]

"You could stay," the man said. [571] "Most guests do." [574] She thought of her mother, and looked down the long lattice for a face, and did not find it, and understood that not finding it was its own kind of answer. [605] The vines began to sag as the hour thinned. [614]

"My father is strapped to a wall," she said, "tasting copper, and he thinks that is all there is." [633] The man untied the vine from her waist himself. [642] "Come back at noon," he said, which is what a host says. [654]

Gravity remembered itself, and she fell. [660] The clouds again, the town again, the roofs, the garden, and the sky closed over her like a lid, and her chest closed with it. [685] But she landed in the long grass beside the hatch, which was standing open, and her father was halfway out of it with his harness dragging behind him. [713]

He did not shout. [717] He sat down in the grass, which no bunker builder does at noon, and she put the doll into his hand and pulled the cork. [742] "Three seconds," she told him. [747] "It is a lot at first." [753]

He tasted, and his shoulders, which had been braced against the sky for as long as she could remember, came down a fraction, then a fraction more. [780] Tomorrow at noon, she would show him where it grew. [790]
