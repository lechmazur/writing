The tower took its first lightning of the season the same evening Pell came up the toll road. [17] Wren watched him from her booth, counting his steps the way she counted everything, because counting kept a toll keeper honest, and she was honest whether she liked it or not. [48] "Two coins," she said when he reached the window. [57] "You will think that is absurd, since you are the engineer sent to fix the hive, and you will be right." [78] Pell paid. [80] "They warned me about you." [85] "They warn everyone." [88] "Lightning does not make a person wise, only unable to lie." [99] "I was nineteen." [102] "I have not managed a polite untruth since." [110]

Above them the lightning collection tower rose black against a sky the color of warm honey, its copper crown already ticking with stored charge. [134] For three days the city below had been dark, its streetlamps dead, its rooftop orchards blooming for nobody. [152] The drone hive on the tower's crown, forty thousand mechanical bees that had pollinated those orchards since the real ones vanished, had gone silent the first night of the blackout and had not stirred since. [187]

"Power came back this morning," Pell said, squinting up. [196] "The coils are full." [200] "The hive should have woken." [205]

"It will not wake on its own," Wren said. [214] "I know that because Aldous told me so, and because I am about to tell you something I would rather keep." [235] She reached beneath the counter and brought out the lacquer box. [246] It was black, small enough to sit in one palm, painted with a single gold bee whose wings had worn thin from eleven years of her thumb. [273] "Aldous built the hive," she said. [279] "He kept this booth before me." [285] "When he died he left me the box, and inside it a matchbox, and inside that a riddle." [303]

She lifted the lid. [307] The matchbox was ordinary, blue cardboard gone soft at the corners. [318] Inside lay one match and a strip of paper in Aldous's cramped hand. [331] Pell read it aloud. [335] "I drink the storm but never swallow." [342] "I feed the flowers but never eat." [349] "Wake me with the fire the sky forgot." [357]

"That is a riddle about the hive," Pell said. [366] "And the answer is the match." [372] "The coils charge from above, but the hive needs a flame at the pilot arc to start its cycle." [391] "Everyone has been looking at the sky." [398]

"Yes," Wren said, and hated that she had to. [407] "I solved it in my second year." [414] "I never struck the match, because once I did the riddle would be a set of instructions, and I would be a woman who takes coins and pulls a lever." [444] "I would have told anyone who asked." [451] "Nobody asks the toll keeper anything but the price." [460]

Pell turned the match in his fingers. [467] Behind him the last light slid down the tower like something poured. [479] "You held up the orchards for a riddle." [487]

"For one day," Wren said. [492] "I am not proud of it, and I am not able to pretend otherwise, so we had better climb." [511]

The pilot arc was a brass cup at the base of the hive, cold as a coin. [528] Through the hive's glass wall the drones hung in their cells, forty thousand small dead suns. [544] Wren struck the match. [548] It was eleven years old and caught on the first pull, which felt like Aldous laughing. [564] She set it in the cup. [570] The arc took the flame and ran it upward in a blue thread, and the hive answered with a sound she had not heard since the blackout began, a hum that started in one cell and spread until the whole crown of the tower was singing. [616] The first drones lifted, gold in the last of the dusk, and dropped toward the city and its waiting blossoms. [636]

"Well," Pell said. [639] "Now you know how it works." [645]

"I always knew how it worked," Wren said. [653] "That was never the point." [658] She looked at the empty matchbox in her hand, at the paper with its three lines, and felt the plainness of tomorrow arrive early: coins, lever, coins. [685]

Pell watched the drones go. [690] "Write another one," he said. [695] "You cannot lie, but a riddle is not a lie." [704] "It is a truth that has not finished arriving." [713]

Wren opened her mouth to say that was sentimental nonsense, and found, to her irritation, that she could not. [732] She put the empty matchbox back in the lacquer box, closed the lid on the worn gold bee, and went down to find a pencil. [757]
