Mara Voss tracked invisible things for a living. [8] Cable faults under the sea sang to her instruments in frequencies no one could hear, and she followed them through the dark until she found where the copper had begun to weep. [40] She was also a caver who could not stand caves. [50] Every squeeze, every low ceiling, closed a hand around her lungs, and she went in anyway, because the fear was hers and the work was not. [76]

The landing station on Kell Skerry had been a research facility once, before the funding drowned. [92] Now it was a concrete shell on a tidal rock where the transatlantic cable came ashore through a sea cave, and the cave was open only between tides, a window of four hours when the sea drew back and held its breath. [134] Nobody at the company remembered the station. [141] Nobody remembered the woman who had worked there either, which was why Mara had taken the job. [158]

In her breast pocket, sealed in a glass slide, she carried a fleck of gold leaf no bigger than a grain of salt. [181] It had come from the margin of a codex, an illuminant flake lifted from a saint's halo by a conservator named Ilse Voss, who had spent three years on Kell Skerry studying how salt water unmade manuscripts recovered from a wreck. [222] The fleck was all that had come home. [230] Ilse had not. [233]

The tide turned at ten past two. [240] Mara went down the cable trench with the sea roaring at her back and the silence of the cave swallowing it whole, so that the noise became a kind of pressure, a crashing hush that filled her ears without ever quite arriving. [282] The walls narrowed. [285] Her chest answered. [288] She counted her breaths and kept her eyes on the cable, black and thick as a thigh, running ahead of her into the rock. [312]

The fault would not show itself to instruments down here; the rock was too wet, too loud with iron. [331] So she watched for phase transitions instead. [338] Everything in the cave was sweating, seawater condensing on every cold surface, but a fault leaks current, and current makes heat, and heat turns water into air. [365] Somewhere ahead, the cable would be dry. [372]

She found it forty meters in, where the passage bent and the ceiling dropped to her shoulders: a stripe of matte sheath in a world of glistening, the damage announcing itself by what it refused to hold. [409] She marked it, photographed it, and should have gone back. [419] Instead she looked up. [423]

Above the bend, a steel hatch was set into the rock, rust bleeding down from its hinges in long orange tears. [444] The old facility had used the cave as a service shaft; the plans had said so, in a footnote nobody had read in twenty years. [469] Mara had read it. [473] Ninety minutes until the sea came back. [480] Her hands shook on the hatch wheel, and it was not only the cold. [494]

The room above was small, windowless, lined with steel tanks that had once held desalination baths. [510] Salt had crusted white over everything, the sea's own slow phase change, liquid to crystal, twenty years of it. [529] On the workbench, under a plastic sheet gone brittle and opaque, lay a codex. [543] Its vellum had fused into a single brown brick, but the margins still held ghosts of gold, and in one halo there was a gap the exact size of the fleck in her pocket. [577]

Beside it, a logbook in her mother's hand, the last entry dated the day the sea had come early. [596] "Hatch jammed from below. [600] Water rising in the shaft. [605] The gold is loose along the whole margin. [613] I have saved one flake in case the rest is lost. [624] Someone should know this book existed." [630]

Mara sat down on the salt-stiff floor, and the walls did not close in. [644] For the first time in a cave, her lungs were her own. [656]

The company had a form for this, a procedure for the recovery of remains, a box to tick that would turn her mother into an incident and the codex into salvage. [687] Mara did not fill it in. [693] She photographed every page of the logbook, pressed the glass slide into the gap in the halo where the fleck had been lifted from, and left it there. [721]

Then she went down through the hatch and back along the cable, past the dry stripe of the fault, toward the sound of the sea returning. [747] The roar grew, and the hush grew with it, and she walked out into the light with twenty minutes to spare. [768]

The fault report she filed was accurate. [775] The second report, the one she wrote for no one, began with a name. [789]
