The offshore turbines hummed through the storm, a low note that Maren Voss had long ago stopped hearing and started feeling in her teeth. [24] She was hauling a bleached spar up the shingle when the headlamp came stumbling out of the rain, attached to a man who looked like he had been arguing with the sea and losing. [58] "GPS died an hour ago," he shouted. [65] "Every channel marker I had was a number, and now the numbers are gone." [79]

Maren pointed her chin at the great rock archway behind her, where the swell was slamming through the gap and coming out the other side as white smoke. [107] "That one isn't a number," she said. [114] "Come inside before it teaches you something the hard way." [124]

His name was Teodor, a survey engineer for the tidal array, bound for the maintenance dock when the storm ate his satellites. [146] He dripped in her doorway and stared at the cottage, which was less a house than a tide pool that had learned to stand upright: driftwood figures on every surface, gulls and keels and women with kelp hair, all of them carved from wood the sea had already half-decided about. [196]

"You made all these?" [200]

"I finished them," Maren said. [205] "The water starts everything around here." [211]

She lit the stove and, because the power was out, went to the shelf where she kept the old lantern. [231] It was a brass magic lantern, older than the turbines by a century and a half, and beside it lay a single glass slide in a wooden frame. [259] She slid it in, struck a match, and the wall bloomed. [270]

The painted image showed the archway itself, a three-masted ship framed dead center in the gap, a crescent moon riding above, and a line of tiny ochre dots trailing from the arch toward the shore. [305]

"Pretty," Teodor said, in the tone of a man who measured things for a living. [320]

"It's a picture," Maren agreed. [325] "It's also a chart, a tide table, and a treaty." [335] She traced the ship with a chisel-scarred finger. [343] "My great-great-grandmother painted it when she kept the light out on the point. [356] If you see the arch with a ship's width of sky on both sides, you are on the safe line. [376] The moon tells you which tide she meant. [384] The dots are the cairns her husband built, one for every boat the arch took before they figured this out." [404]

"And the treaty?" [407]

Maren shrugged. [409] "She made the picture. [413] He made the cairns. [417] My grandmother made the story into a rhyme, and I learned the rhyme before I could swim. [434] Everyone who has ever used it owes everyone who came before, and everyone after is owed. [450] That is a treaty, whether or not anyone signed it." [460]

Teodor sat down slowly among the driftwood women. [468] "I have a hundred and forty pages of current models for this bay," he said. [483] "She had a moon and a gap." [490]

"She had a hundred and forty drowned boats, first," Maren said. [501] "The simplicity is what you build after the complexity, not instead of it. [514] It's the scaffold an ordinary frightened person can climb in the dark." [526]

She had been thinking about this since the array went in and the hum became the bay's new heartbeat. [545] The turbines had changed the currents. [551] Her great-great-grandmother's safe line was now a lie by about eleven degrees. [563] Maren had been learning the new water the only way she knew, by watching what it brought and where it left it, but she could not yet say what she knew. [594] Now a man with a dead GPS and a hundred and forty pages sat in her kitchen. [611]

"Show me the models," she said. [617] "I'll show you the wood." [622]

They worked until the storm blew itself hoarse. [630] Teodor's laptop, running on its last battery, gave her the eddies as colored arrows; Maren gave him the spar, the keel-shaped root, the way a certain plank always fetched up under the arch's northern foot now and never the southern. [670]

By dawn she had reimagined the slide. [677] Not repainted; she would not touch the glass. [685] Instead she built the image again in driftwood, a tall frame to stand on the shingle where the old cairns had crumbled: two uprights, a lintel, and between them a ship-shaped gap set eleven degrees off the old line, with a small turbine blade turning in the lintel like a moon that hums. [738] Line the wooden arch up with the stone one and you were safe, satellites or no. [754]

Teodor photographed it for his report, then put the phone away and simply looked. [768] "Who's the treaty with now?" he asked. [775]

"Whoever gets lost next," Maren said, and went to see what the storm had started. [790]
