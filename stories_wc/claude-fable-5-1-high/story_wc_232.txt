Ines Varga sold antique maps to people who wanted to know where they had been, and she had not been anywhere herself in eleven years. [25] The shop was quiet the way a drained pond is quiet. [36] On the counter, where a cash drawer should have been, sat a small brass block no larger than a sugar cube: a type foundry matrix chip, the mold for a single letter, with an ampersand cut into its face in reverse. [77] Teodor had cast type for forty years and left her only this. [89]

"Midsummer," he had said from the hospital bed, "when the creek rises, follow the ants home." [105] "I'll show you the doorway of decisions, and you can decide again." [117] He had died in March. [122]

A promise made by the dead does not expire; it lingers, like the smell of ink in a room where no press has run for years. [148] Ines was not a mystic. [153] She was empirically intuitive: she authenticated maps by hunch first and chemistry second, and the hunch was right often enough that she had learned to test it rather than dismiss it. [184] So on the twenty-first of June she closed the shop, put the matrix in her pocket, and drove to the valley where they had been young. [210]

The creek was already climbing its banks, fed by a week of storms in the high meadows, brown and loud and impatient. [232] She found the ants where she expected to, on the flat stone by the ford where they had eaten lunch the day they decided to marry instead of leaving. [261] Harvester ants, large and rust-colored, each carrying a seed or a petal fragment, moving in a line uphill away from the water. [283]

Following ants home is slow work. [289] She lost the line twice in the grass and found it again by kneeling and waiting, the way she waited for a watermark to show under raking light. [317] Behind her the creek rose another hand's width, and she understood the timing now: Teodor had chosen the one day when the ford would soon be impassable, so that she could not turn back halfway. [352]

The ants vanished into a crack in the limestone bluff no wider than a door. [367] She squeezed through sideways and stopped. [373]

The cavern beyond was blooming. [378] A shaft of midsummer sun fell through a hole in the ceiling onto a floor of soil the ants had built grain by grain over decades, and in that soil grew columbine and wild geranium and a hundred thin white flowers she could not name, all leaning toward the light. [428] The seeds the ants carried in had grown here, sheltered, for as long as the ants had been carrying. [447] This was the doorway of decisions: not a metaphor after all, but a place he had found and never described, saving it. [469]

On a ledge at the back, where the sun reached only at this hour on this day, something glimmered. [488] Type. [489] A full case of it, cast in his hand, every letter of the alphabet lined up in brass-bright rows, and two words set in a composing stick: STAY, and beneath it, GO. [521] Between them, an empty slot exactly the size of an ampersand. [532]

She turned the matrix over in her fingers. [540] He had never cast the character; he had left the mold with her, so that the joining would be hers to make or refuse. [564] Forty years ago they had chosen STAY and been happy, mostly, and then he had died and left her staying in a shop full of other people's journeys. [592]

Ines tested the hunch the way she tested everything. [601] She set the matrix in the slot and read the line he had built for her: not stay or go, but stay and go. [625] Keep the shop and leave it; hold the promise and put it down. [638] It was not a riddle. [643] It was a man who knew her telling her that the doorway had two sides and she was allowed to use both. [665]

Outside, the creek had covered the ford. [672] She would have to wait until dark for it to fall, sitting among the flowers with the ants walking over her boots, which was, she realized, exactly what he had intended. [703] A person could not decide anything while running. [711]

She left the matrix in its slot, where the sun would find it for one hour each year, and sat down among the flowers to wait for the water to fall. [742] The promise had stopped lingering. [747] It had simply been kept. [752]
