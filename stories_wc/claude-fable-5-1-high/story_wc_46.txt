Mara had once steered by the sky, and now the sky was something she visited only from the ground. [19] Every evening she climbed down instead of up, into the grotto the villagers called the Serpent, where green-veined serpentine rock coiled through the cliff and mist pooled like held breath. [49] From its mouth she could see the stars without being asked to follow them. [63]

She had been a navigator on the Kestrel for eleven years. [74] On the last night, a lamp had tipped in the chart room while she was on deck naming constellations to the cabin boy, and the fire had taken the ship faster than any storm. [108] The boy had lived. [112] Four others had not. [116] Since then she could not bring herself to light so much as a candle, and she could not bring herself to leave the shore. [140]

But the village fishing boats still came home through the reef in the dark, and the old lighthouse had been shuttered for want of oil. [165] So Mara had begun to gather sea glass. [173]

The tide left it in the grotto's inner pools, frosted and dull, every color the ocean had been patient enough to soften. [195] She polished each piece the way her grandmother had polished agates: coarse grit first, then finer, then finer still, until the surface remembered how to be clear. [222] The last stage was a paste of cerium on a damp leather pad, worked in small circles for an hour or more. [244]

Her idea was simple and perhaps foolish. [251] Set enough polished glass into the grotto's seaward lip, angled toward the reef, and moonlight and starlight would catch in it and throw back a low glimmer, a marker that burned nothing. [283] Light shared without fire. [287] She wanted that more than she had wanted anything since the Kestrel. [299]

Tonight the mist was thick and mild, moving through the grotto in slow exhalations that stirred her hair and cooled the back of her neck. [324] She took up a piece of white glass, round as a coin, and began the coarse grit. [341]

The work was rhythmic, and rhythm had a way of loosening what she kept tied down. [357] With the coarse grit came the fire itself, the sound of it, the cabin boy's hand in hers. [375] With the medium grit came the weeks after, the inquiry, the captain's kindness that she could not accept. [393] She had learned to let these memories surface and pass like weather. [405]

But with the fine grit, something older rose. [413] Her grandmother's kitchen, the tumbling drum turning all night, and the old woman's voice: "You don't weigh a stone by how heavy it is, girl." [438] "You weigh it by how much light it can hold." [448]

Mara stopped. [450] She had been weighing herself for two years by the heaviest thing she had ever carried. [466]

The white glass sat in her palm, its frost half gone, and in the dim she saw the first star of the evening caught small and steady inside it. [495] It looked like a pearl. [500] Not a pearl of the ordinary kind, grown around an irritation, but a pearl of stillness, grown around nothing at all, around the simple fact of having stopped tumbling. [529]

She took up the cerium pad and finished it slowly, and the glass came alive under her hands, not bright in itself but reflectively vibrant, returning every scrap of light the mist let through and adding a faint milk-blue of its own. [571] She set it at the center of the arc she had been building along the grotto's lip, the keystone among forty-one pieces of green and brown and amber. [599]

Then she stepped back to the grotto's mouth and waited. [609]

The mist thinned the way it did on mild nights, in slow petals peeling back from the water. [627] The moon cleared the headland. [632] And the arc of glass took the light and gave it back, a soft curved gleam low on the cliff, exactly where a helmsman coming in past the reef would look for it. [665]

It was not a lighthouse. [670] It would never be mistaken for one. [677] But out on the black water a lantern winked twice, the fisherman's signal for a mark sighted, and Mara felt something in her chest unfold like a leaf finding warmth. [707]

She looked up. [710] The stars were where they had always been, and for the first time in two years they did not seem to be asking her for anything. [736] They were only shining, and down here, in the coiled green dark, so was she. [751]
