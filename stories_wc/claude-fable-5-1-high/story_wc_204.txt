After reality became negotiable, the Concord bargained for permanence, and permanence took the form of a library. [17] It grew as an orchard of glass on an island of nothing, floating upon a swirling sea of clouds, and every tree bore books instead of fruit. [44] Nothing in it could be lost, revised, or forgotten, which was the whole point, and which was the whole problem. [64]

Ines ran the letterpress in the root cellar, the only machine the Concord had permitted, because a press could only repeat what it was given. [89] The readers who drifted up on the morning ferries called her cryptic, since she answered questions with proofs pulled backward, mirror-writing that had to be held against the glass trunks to be read. [122] She was being careful, because the Concord listened to plain speech and filed it forever, and she had one thing left that she did not want filed. [149]

It sat on the shelf above the type cases: a small green bottle, corked with wax, holding the last thing her brother had said before the bargaining began. [177] He had whispered it, and she had caught it in the bottle the way you catch a moth, without knowing yet why it mattered. [201] Then he had signed the Concord with everyone else, and become a book on the ninth tree, and stopped. [220]

She had never opened it. [225] A whisper is not a record. [231] It is unfinished by nature, a sentence that expects an answer, and the library had no category for things that expected anything. [253]

What the Concord had not accounted for was frost. [262] Each dawn the clouds breathed cold against the glass, and the orchard woke covered in patterns: ferns, spirals, long branching cracks of white. [285] By noon they were gone, unarchived, the only text on the island that had never been catalogued because it never held still long enough. [309]

Ines had spent eleven years learning to read them. [318] She walked the rows before the ferries came, tracing the frost with a gloved finger, and found that it was not random. [340] It was the books dreaming. [345] Every frozen volume on every glass branch pressed its cold outward in the shape of the sentence it would have said next, if saying had still been allowed. [373] She copied the shapes down each morning and set them in type at night, backward, letter by letter, until the forme was nearly full. [397]

It was the ninth tree she was waiting for. [406] Eternity, she had come to understand, was not long. [415] It was wide and flat and identical in every direction, and the Concord had spread her brother across all of it, so thinly that no single moment of him remained. [445] She did not want him back forever. [452] She wanted all of that forever gathered up and pressed, the way the press pressed, into one impression she could hold in her hand. [476]

On the last morning the frost on the ninth tree formed a single line, and she recognized it before she finished tracing it. [499] It was the second half of a sentence. [507] The first half was in the bottle. [514]

She went down to the cellar and set the frost words, and left the space before them empty, and locked the forme, and inked it. [539] Then she broke the wax. [544] The whisper came out small and warm, in his voice, and she did not hear it so much as let it pass through her onto the bed of the press, into the empty space, where it settled among the type like a missing sort. [588] She pulled the lever. [592]

A perfect system can survive a great deal, but it cannot survive a complete sentence it did not authorize. [611] The impression came off the press and the cellar ceiling cracked along the exact line of the frost pattern, and the crack ran upward through the trunk of the ninth tree, and then through the eighth, and the tenth, in a sound like a whole forest of paper turning over at once. [663]

The books rustled. [666] Every one of them, on every branch, stirred in its glass and began to say the next thing. [684] The island tilted. [687] The clouds below it began to swirl faster, uncertain now what they had agreed to hold up. [704]

Ines climbed the cellar stairs with the sheet still wet in her hands. [717] On it, printed the right way round for once, was the whole of what her brother had meant to say, the whisper and the frost together, no longer than a breath. [748] It was enough. [751]

Behind her the orchard was shattering slowly, book by book, each one talking as it fell, none saying the same thing twice. [773] She folded the sheet into her coat and went to meet the ferries, to explain, in plain words this time, what she had done. [797]
