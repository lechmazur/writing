The Halden went over in four minutes, and Ilse Varga went with it, into water so cold it did not feel like water at all. [25] She remembers the pressure in her ears, a thumb pressed on each side, and then a pop she felt rather than heard. [47] When the lifeboat crew pulled her out, their mouths were moving and nothing came from them. [63] Noise had become impossible. [67] Still clipped to her coat was the tuning fork case, six forks in a leather roll, the tools of a piano tuner who would never again hear a piano. [96]

A year later she keeps the boiler at the winterglass conservatory, a long hall of frosted panes where the city grows oranges against all sense. [121] Steam has no argument with her, and the glass sweats in a language of drips she reads by eye. [140] The gardener, Tomas, has not spoken since some old grief of his own, and he never learned sign. [158] He does not need it. [163] His face is a weather system; his hands close a question or open a joke, and Ilse understands him more completely than she understood anyone who talked. [190]

It was Tomas who handed her the thermal camera. [199] The conservatory uses it to hunt cold spots before frost finds the citrus. [212] She was sweeping it along the north wall when a school group passed through the screen, and she saw them as they were beneath their coats: a boy burning orange with excitement, a teacher cooling to blue at the temples, one girl with a bright hot core and hands gone violet with dread. [265] Feeling has a temperature. [269] She began mapping it. [273] Reds became the upper register, blues the bass, the flicker between them a rhythm. [287] She filled a notebook with faces turned into staves. [296]

It was a private madness until Tomas found the notebook and pointed, with his whole eloquent body, toward the palm house. [317] There is a piano there, a concert grand left behind by some vanished recital series, swollen with humidity and a semitone flat. [339] The families of the Halden dead were coming on the anniversary. [350] The city had chosen the conservatory because it was warm, and because a ship should be remembered somewhere that does not rock. [372] Tomas tapped the notebook, tapped the piano, tapped her chest. [382] She shook her head; he shook his back, harder. [391]

So she unrolled the case. [396] The forks had been under the strait with her, and they were untouched by it. [411] Steel does not forget its pitch because a ferry rolled. [421] She struck the A and set the stem against her teeth, and 440 cycles went through her jaw into the old bones of her skull, a note she could not hear and could not mistake. [456] Then she pressed the fork to the soundboard, played the string, and felt the two pitches fight. [473] A tuner calls it beating: the slow pulse of a wrong interval, faster as the gap widens, gone when it closes. [494] She had always heard it. [499] Now she felt it in her fingertips, and it was the same thing. [512] It took nine days to rectify the whole instrument, string by string, each one argued back to true. [530]

On the anniversary the palm house filled with wool coats and the peculiar hush of people who have agreed not to weep yet. [553] Ilse stood at the piano with the thermal camera propped on the music stand where a score would go. [572] The room would be the score. [578] On the small screen the mourners bloomed: a man in the front row, deep blue everywhere except two hot coins where his eyes were; a woman holding a child, the child a furnace of boredom, the woman cooling from the shoulders down; an old couple sharing one warmth between them like a coat. [631]

She played what she saw. [636] Blue for the man, low and held; the child's high restless orange over it; the couple as two voices a third apart, never quite resolving because they had not. [665] She heard none of it. [670] She felt the soundboard shiver under her wrists and watched the screen, and as she played the colors moved. [689] The man's blue began to warm at the throat. [698] The woman's shoulders came back up to red. [706] The room was answering the music with more music, and she was translating it back, a conversation held entirely in heat and steel. [729]

When she stopped, she looked not at the crowd but at Tomas, by the boiler door. [745] His face told her the rest. [751] Everything she had been given had gone under the water, and the marvels had come up unshaken: six forks that held their pitch, a body that could still find true, a room that had learned to be heard.
