Ossa had once carried a quarter of the sky on her shoulders, and now she could stand inside a reading lectern. [21] Each century in the temple library took a hand's width from her, as if time, denied entry, collected its toll in flesh instead. [44] She did not resent it. [49] She had cut the rune herself, copying it from the first Ashen king's seal. [63]

The rune of drift lay in the threshold stone, a single spiral that unmoored the library from the river of years. [84] Outside its doors the constellations wheeled, and only when they paused, once in a long generation, did the temple touch the world again. [107] In that pause a book could slip out on the tide of time and wash up on some ordinary shelf below. [128] Nothing else could pass. [132] Not Ossa. [134] Not the true genealogy of the Ashen Kings, which she had sworn to keep out of the usurper's reach until an heir came asking. [158]

Nine pauses she had waited. [163] Now a man named Corvane sat the throne, waving a lineage he had bought from a forger, and the last of the Ashen blood mucked out stables in a border town and did not know her own name. [201] Ossa knew it. [204] She had watched the girl through the drift the way one watches fish beneath ice. [219] Wren. [220] Regally unpretentious, Ossa thought, which was the only kind of regal worth the word: she shared her bread, kept her back straight, and asked for nothing. [246]

So Ossa wrote to her. [251] She could not write plainly; Corvane's readers combed every library in the realm for exactly such a thing. [269] Instead she left riddles in margins, a line of verse turned sideways, a genealogy disguised as a recipe for dye. [289] She chose books a stable girl might touch: a farrier's manual, a psalter with a cracked spine, a cheap romance. [309] Each pause, one book went out. [315] Each pause, Wren found it, frowned, and understood a little more. [326]

The ink was the trouble. [331] Ossa had one iron gall ink pellet left, a dark bead of oak tannin and rust no bigger than a lentil, and it was the last in the world that could survive the drift. [365] Ordinary ink faded between one moment and the next out there; iron gall bit into the page and stayed, growing darker with age, the way a true thing does. [394] One pellet meant one message. [399] One message had to unmask a king. [406]

She dissolved it in a thimble of water and her own tear, because that was what was left of her. [426] Then she climbed the lectern and wrote, in a hand smaller than a gnat's track, inside the cover of the farrier's manual. [448] "Ask the king to read his own seal aloud." [457] "The Ashen seal is a rune of drift, and only Ashen blood can hold it still enough to read." [476]

But the pause had already begun to close. [484] Through the high windows she saw the constellations shiver and lean, ready to resume their turning. [500] The ink was still wet. [505] A wet page would not survive the tide. [513] And Wren would not live another generation to receive it. [523]

There was one way to send a book without waiting for the tide. [536] Send the library. [539]

Ossa climbed down. [542] It took her most of an hour to cross the floor she had once crossed in a step. [560] At the threshold she set her palms on the spiral. [570] She was small, but she was still a titan, and a titan's hands remember what they held. [587] She sundered the rune. [591]

The stone did not crack so much as sigh. [600] Time came in like a guest too long kept waiting, polite and irreversible. [613] The pillars bowed. [616] Vellum yellowed, then browned, then lifted off the shelves as a soft weather of pages, drifting down out of the pause into the one year that still mattered, onto the stables and courts and cellars of a kingdom that had never known a temple hung above it. [663] The farrier's manual went among them, ink drying in the fall. [674] Ossa felt the centuries she had held off arrive all together, and she was not afraid; she had simply grown small enough, at last, to fit inside them. [702]

In the border town a stable girl opened a book that had fallen from a clear sky and found, in the darkest ink she had ever seen, a sentence she did not understand and could not put down. [740] By spring she stood in the throne room, straight-backed, asking nothing but a question. [754] Corvane lifted his seal and read it aloud, or tried to, while the letters slid from under his eyes like fish beneath ice. [777] Wren read it in one breath. [783] It said her name. [787]
