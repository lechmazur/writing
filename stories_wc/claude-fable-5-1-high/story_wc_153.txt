The mangroves took the boat the way a throat takes a swallow, and Ines stopped rowing. [16] Roots rose on either side like ribs, and somewhere in the green dark the spirits were singing, low and wordless, a hymn made of tide. [41] She had been told the medium lived at the center, if the labyrinth had a center, if the medium could be said to live. [65] In her lap sat a bundle of letters tied with fishing line, eleven of them, all addressed to her brother Teo, none of them sent. [90] In her pocket sat the shard. [96] It had broken from the gargoyle's rainspout on Saint Brendan's roof the stormy night she and Teo climbed up there to scream at each other where the town could not hear. [127] The stone had cut her palm as she grabbed it, and she had kept it, because two days later Teo was gone under the same water that now carried her. [157] The cut never quite closed. [162] By day it wept; by moonlight it knitted shut, pink and clean, and every dawn it opened again like a mouth remembering what it had not said. [189]

The houseboat appeared without warning, lashed between two trunks, its windows fogged from the inside though the night was warm. [209] The medium sat on the deck with her feet in the water, an old woman or a young one, Ines could not decide. [232]

"You brought paper," the medium said. [238] "Paper is heavy here." [242]

"I want to go back," Ines said. [249] "Not to change it." [253] "Just to the moment before I decided not to call him back." [265] "I want to see what I was." [272]

The medium tilted her head toward the singing. [280] "There is no before," she said. [286] "There is only the room where the apology waits, and the door you keep not opening." [302] "They will show you, if you ask correctly." [310]

"And if I ask wrong?" [315]

"They will show you anyway." [320]

Inside, the cabin smelled of salt and candle fat. [329] The medium did not light anything. [335] She touched the fogged glass with one knuckle and began to hum, and the spirits outside took up the note and lifted it, and Ines felt the shard grow warm against her hip. [368]

"Evoke him," the medium said, "not with his name." [377] "With what you owe him." [382]

Ines closed her eyes and thought of the roof, the rain, Teo's face when she told him he had never once in his life been brave. [408] She thought of him turning to climb down, and of her hand, bleeding on the gargoyle, not reaching. [426]

When she opened her eyes, the condensation on the window had begun to move. [440] Letters formed, dragged through the wet by no finger she could see. [452] She expected his handwriting. [456] It was hers. [459]

TEO, the glass said, I WAS WRONG ON THE ROOF. [469]

The first line of the first letter, the one she had written the morning after and folded and never carried to the post. [492] The window fogged over, cleared, and wrote again: the first line of the second. [506] Then the third. [509] Outside, the spirits were no longer wordless. [516] They were singing her letters back to her, every unsent sentence, in voices that did not sound like Teo and did not need to. [540]

"This isn't him," Ines said, and her voice cracked down the middle. [552]

"No," the medium agreed. [556] "You asked for the moment before deciding." [563] "Here it is." [566] "It was never on the roof." [572] "It has been every morning since, with those letters in your hand." [584]

Ines looked down at the bundle. [590] Every morning since, she had woken already deciding, and called it grief, and called it too late. [607]

She pulled the shard from her pocket. [614] Moonlight came through a gap in the roof, and where it touched her palm the wound drew closed, and where it touched the shard the stone seamed silver along its broken edge, as if it too were trying to be whole. [655]

"Things that break in anger heal in a gentler light," the medium said. [668] "But they will not stay healed unless you finish the sentence." [679]

Ines untied the fishing line. [684] She did not read the letters to the window. [693] She carried them out onto the deck, into the roots and the singing, and she read them to the water, each one, aloud, her voice fierce and shaking and refusing to stop. [725]

I was wrong on the roof. [731] You were the bravest person I knew. [738] I should have reached. [742]

The spirits went quiet to listen. [748] When she finished, the last letter dissolving in the tide, the window behind her wrote one more line, in a hand that was not hers, and then let it run. [778] She did not need to turn around. [785]
