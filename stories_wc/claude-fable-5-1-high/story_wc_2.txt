Ilse Varga only mended clocks that had stopped. [8] A running clock bored her, but a stopped one was a problem with edges, and she planned her life around problems with edges. [31] Every job began with a list, and every list ended with the clock ticking again. [46] Then the clocks began stopping at the same minute. [55] The first was a mantel clock from the burned Halloran house, its hands frozen at four seventeen. [72] The second, a week later, came from a farmhouse two valleys away and had stopped at four seventeen too. [91] She wrote it down as coincidence. [97] By the fifth clock she could not write the word anymore, because coincidence, repeated often enough, becomes pattern, and a pattern is an instruction. [121] Ilse did not like instructions she had not written herself. [131] Still, she went back to the Halloran ruin with a notebook, a lantern, and a plan in six numbered steps. [151] The house had belonged to Admiral Halloran, dead forty years, whose ship had gone down with its charts and whose compass rose, the engraved brass card that gave a compass its directions, had never been recovered. [187] Local children said he had brought it home before the wreck and hidden it. [201] Ilse did not believe children. [206] Step one was to search the hearth, since the mantel clock had sat above it. [221] Beneath the ashes, under a slab that shifted when she leaned on it, her fingers found brass. [238] It was the compass rose, blackened but whole, its thirty-two points still sharp. [251] Something was scratched across the back: NORTH IS WHERE THE WATER FALLS. [263] This was not in her six steps. [270] She stood in the ruin holding a direction without a needle and felt, for the first time in years, uncertainly certain, sure she should go on, unsure of anything after that. [301] There was no waterfall on the Halloran land. [309] There was, however, a greenhouse behind the ruin, glass gone green with neglect, and when she pushed the door the air inside was moving. [333] Dead leaves swirled in a slow spiral around the center bed, lifted by a draft that came from below and smelled of wet stone. [357] She followed the leaves the way she had never followed anything, without a step number, and found the iron grate they circled. [379] Below it, a ladder went down into sound. [387] The cave opened wide, and at its far end a river fell out of the dark into a pool, a subterranean waterfall the admiral must have found and never told anyone about. [419] Her lantern showed a brass housing bolted to the rock beside it, the empty seat where a compass rose belonged. [439] Then the lantern went out. [444] Ilse had planned for darkness; she had spare oil in her coat. [456] She had not planned for the spray, which had soaked the oil, the matches, and the notebook with her six steps. [477] For a moment she stood in absolute black with the water roaring and understood that the admiral had not hidden the rose to be found by someone with a plan. [507] He had hidden it for someone who would improvise. [516] So she improvised. [519] She knelt and felt for the housing with the rose in one hand, letting the sound of falling water be her north, and fitted brass to brass by touch alone. [549] It clicked. [551] Somewhere above, a mechanism she had not known existed began to turn, and a thin line of daylight cracked open in the cave roof, a shutter the admiral had geared to the rose. [584] In that light she saw the wall behind the waterfall: a water clock, huge and stopped, its float jammed in the falling stream. [607] Its dial read four seventeen. [612] She laughed, alone, wet, in a cave under a dead man's garden. [624] The admiral had built a clock driven by a river, a thing no list could schedule, and she could not have said how the clocks in five houses had known when it stopped. [657] For once, not knowing did not feel like a fault in the plan. [670] It felt like part of the mechanism. [677] She waded in, freed the float, and the water clock began to move, slow and heavy, keeping time to something that had never once asked her permission. [704] When she climbed back through the swirling leaves, she left the notebook in the greenhouse to dry, then decided not to come back for it. [729] Her next job was a running clock that had started keeping strange hours. [742] She took it without a list. [748] Step one, she told the owner, was to see what it wanted. [760] She was not sure there would be a step two, and she found, uncertainly and certainly, that she could live with that. [782]
