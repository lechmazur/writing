The locksmith kept no shop, only a folding table at the Knot, where five alleys tangled into a small square that no lamp had ever reached. [26] He opened at dusk and closed when the sky was full, and nobody in the town could say what he actually did there. [49] Ines found him the way everyone did: by getting lost. [59] She had walked for an hour with an envelope in her coat, turning wherever the shadows were thickest, and when the alleys finally opened she was standing in front of him. [90] He was a small man with clean hands and a cardigan the color of dust, and he looked at her as if she were exactly on time. [117]

"You have brought something sealed," he said. [124]

She set the envelope on the table. [131] It was old, softened at the corners, and she had never once opened it since she was seventeen. [149]

"There is a lock of my hair inside," she said. [159] "Tied with a green ribbon." [164] "I cut it the night I decided not to leave." [174]

He did not ask where she had meant to go. [184] He turned the envelope over, ran a thumb along the seal, and nodded as if it had told him something. [204]

"People think I pick locks," he said. [211] "I don't." [213] "Hair remembers the head it grew on, and the head was thinking something when the scissors closed." [230] "That thought is still in there, and it is very frightened." [241]

Ines felt her face go hot. [247] The idea she had carried at seventeen had been absurd: a school for children who could not sit still, run out of a boat, moving town to town along the river. [278] She had drawn the deck plans on the back of her mother's ledgers. [291] Then her father got sick, and the river flooded, and someone laughed at exactly the wrong moment, and she had cut her hair and sealed the idea in with it so it could not embarrass her again. [328] Twenty-two years, and it still knocked. [334]

"Fear is a very good lock," the locksmith said, "because it knows the way home." [349] "Whatever I open, it will walk straight back and shut." [359] "So first we lose it." [364]

He came around the table, took her by the shoulders with startling gentleness, and turned her. [380] Once, twice, five times, until the five alleys blurred into one dark ring and she could not have said which mouth had swallowed her an hour ago. [407] Her stomach lurched. [410] He steadied her and stepped back, and it struck her how quietly he had done a thing that would have gotten anyone else slapped. [434]

"Now we wait," he said. [439]

The sky above the Knot went from bruise to ink. [449] The town's noises thinned to nothing, and in that hush Ines had the strange sense that the square was not a place at all but a hinge, and every possible life she might have lived was stacked on the far side of it, breathing. [493] The wanting was so sharp and so unlikely that she nearly laughed. [505]

"There," the locksmith said. [509]

Over the roofline to what she could no longer call the east, a single star had pricked through. [527]

"The first one always tells me which way the key turns." [538] "Tonight it says left." [542]

He slit the envelope with a thumbnail. [549] The lock of hair lay in his palm, darker than hers was now, the green ribbon gone gray. [567] He pinched the knot and turned it, left, a quarter turn, the way a man turns a key he has turned ten thousand times. [591] The ribbon fell away. [595]

Nothing happened that she could point to. [602] No light, no sound. [606] But the plans came back to her whole: the shallow draft, the chalkboard bolted to the mast, the sleeping shelves for twelve. [628] They were not embarrassing. [632] They were only unfinished. [636] Fear came looking, she felt it, but it arrived at the Knot and found five alleys and no clue which one led to her, and it stood there turning in circles, and she was already gone. [672]

"Which way do I go?" she asked. [679]

He pointed under the star. [684] "That alley comes out at the river," he said. [693] "Most of them do, eventually." [698] "Yours does tonight." [701]

She tucked the loose hair into her coat pocket and left the ribbon on his table, because a lock without a key is just a decoration, and he seemed to collect them. [733] At the alley's mouth she looked back. [740] The small man in the dust-colored cardigan was folding his table beneath a sky now crowded with stars, none of which he bothered to look at. [766] He had only needed the first. [772]
