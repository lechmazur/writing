The chronicle keeper had stopped counting seasons somewhere between the flood that took the eastern shelves and the spring the roots learned to read. [24] She knew only that the library beneath the catacombs had grown wilder while she watched, and that watching was her whole task. [46] Ferns split the spines of law codes. [53] Fungus bloomed in the margins of star charts and slowly rewrote them into something softer. [68] She wrote it all down. [73] She did not write whether it was good. [81]

That had been the first instruction, carved above the entrance by a keeper so long dead her name had gone to moss: Witness. Do not judge. [107] Ilse had found that the second half was harder, and then, across the drift of untold seasons, easier than anything. [127] Judgment needed a firm hold on how things ought to be, and she gripped reality loosely now. [144] Some mornings she was not certain whether the river had always run through the reading room or had only recently decided to. [166] She let the uncertainty be. [171] It was a kind of peace, though she could feel it crumbling at the edges the way the vaulted ceiling shed grit onto her pages. [196]

Along the river, where the current cut a black glass bank, stood her refuge: an obsidian alcove polished by a thousand years of water. [220] Sound did not enter it. [225] The river's voice stopped at the threshold as if ashamed. [235] Inside, the silence was not empty but working. [243] Torn pages she left there overnight came back whole, their words returned in an ink slightly darker than before. [262] Cuts on her hands closed. [267] She had come to think of it as regenerative silence, and she had come to fear how much she needed it. [288]

The water was rising. [292] She recorded this without alarm, the way she recorded everything, but her hand shook on the third line. [310] Within a season the lower stacks would drown. [318] Within two, the alcove. [322] The silence would fill with river and stop mending anything. [332]

She took the swan from her pocket. [339] It was older than she was, folded from a page of the first chronicle, its paper worn to the softness of cloth. [361] Every keeper had carried it and none, as far as the records showed, had understood it. [377] Ilse had unfolded it once, years ago, and found the creases made a shape that was not a swan at all. [398] Now she opened it again in the obsidian quiet and laid it flat against the black wall. [415]

The folds were a map. [420] Of course they were. [424] The long neck-crease traced the river. [430] The wing folds marked the seven galleries. [437] And the cryptic knot of creases at the tail, the part every keeper had smoothed and refolded without thinking, pointed to a place behind the alcove where there was no door. [468] Only earth. [470] Soft, root-threaded earth. [473]

She understood then that the swan was not a set of instructions to save the library. [489] It was permission to let it go. [496]

She burrowed. [498] It took what might have been days. [505] She dug with a broken shelf bracket, then with her hands, following the swan's tail-fold turn by turn, the silence following her into the dark like a patient animal. [534] Behind her the river rose and the shelves sighed and she did not look back to see whether it was a tragedy. [556]

The tunnel ended in a chamber the size of a cupped hand, dry and dark and utterly still. [574] Old bone lined it, arranged with care. [581] Not a tomb, she thought, but a seed. [589] She laid her final chronicle in the center, and the swan upon it, refolded, and sat with them. [607]

The silence here was deeper than the alcove's. [615] She felt it begin its slow work on the paper, on the bone, on her. [630] Somewhere above, water was taking the library apart and giving it back to the ground. [645] Somewhere below, or later, or in a season no one had named yet, the ground would give it back again. [665] She did not know if that was true. [673] She held the idea loosely, the way she held everything now, and found she could witness even her own ending without deciding what it meant. [698]

The peace crumbled. [701] Under it, something quieter took root. [707]
