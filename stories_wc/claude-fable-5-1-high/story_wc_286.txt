Ilse Marrow had thrown ten thousand bowls before the sky broke, and she had kept none of them. [18] Keeping, she had told her students, was a way of refusing to let clay finish becoming what it was. [37] Now she walked the scorched country west of the old river, and the country did not agree with her. [56] Here the ground held three noons at once. [64] A ridge to the north lay in late afternoon, gold and long-shadowed, while the flats beside it burned in a white midday that had lasted, by her count, eleven years. [94] Time had fractured the way a badly cooled glaze fractures, in a net of fine lines, each cell holding its own hour. [116]

She navigated by the lantern glass. [122] It had been amber once, a fisherman's storm lamp, and forty years of sun had faded it to the color of weak tea. [145] Held to the eye, it dimmed the false suns, the ones burning in borrowed years, and left only the true one, the one that still moved. [171] She followed where its light fell cleanly, where a shadow lengthened at the rate a shadow should. [188]

That was how she found the oasis, at the hour when the true sun sat low and a thread of chimney smoke rose from a hollow and, meeting cooler air, flattened into a gray lid. [223] Smoke meant a fire. [227] A fire meant someone. [231] She went down carefully, the way one lowers a pot onto a shelf. [244]

Date palms, green. [247] A stone well with its bucket still on the rope. [257] Twenty hives in two rows, white paint peeled to bare wood, and a keeper's cabin with the door hanging open on one hinge. [280] Inside, the hearth was cold. [285] Not banked, not recently dead, but cold in the way of stone that has forgotten heat, dust settled in the ash. [306] She stepped back out and looked up, and the smoke was still there, flattening, patient, coming from an evening that was not this one. [330]

So the fracture ran through here too. [337] And yet the palms were green in every year at once. [348] The well was wet. [352] The flats outside had spent eleven years burning, and this hollow had spent them being exactly what it was. [371] That was the question she had walked three hundred miles to ask. [383] Why did some places refuse to change when everything around them came apart? [396] She had no teacher left to ask, so she asked the hives. [408]

They thrummed. [410] She had not expected bees, not after eleven years of abandonment, but the sound was there, low and steady beneath the boards, a kiln at temperature. [436] She drew water from the well, and because the bucket was cracked and her hands were a potter's hands, she cupped the water in her palms and set it in the shade of the nearest hive, in the shallow of a stone. [478] It was a small thing. [483]

The thrum rose. [486] Not louder, exactly; nearer. [490] The palms leaned, a hand's width, toward the stone. [499] She sat very still and thought about sunflowers, how they turn all day toward what feeds them, and she understood that this hollow turned too, not toward light but toward the gesture that had put water in a stone. [538] Someone had done this every evening for a very long time. [549] The smoke above the cabin was the shape of that someone still doing it, in whatever year would have them. [569]

A place tended long enough grew toward its tending. [578] It bent that way even after the hands were gone, and the bending held it, the way a fired bowl holds the memory of the fingers that raised it. [607] The world had not broken evenly. [613] It had broken along the lines where nobody had cared. [623]

This was not comfortable knowledge. [628] It meant the burning flats had been lost long before the sky broke, and no potter and no glass would carry them back. [651] Ilse held that fact the way she held hot ware, firmly and without pretending it was not hot. [669]

Then she took the lantern glass from her pack and set it upright on the stone beside the water, where the last true sun came through it and lay amber, almost its old color, across the hive's dark mouth. [708] She would need another way to find the true sun tomorrow. [719] She thought she could manage; the palms would show her which way to lean. [733]

Above the cabin, in its own quiet evening, the smoke flattened and did not blow away. [749] Ilse stayed the night, and in the morning she drew water again. [761]
