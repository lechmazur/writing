Maren had forgiven her mother on a Tuesday, and by Friday she understood that forgiveness was not the same as peace. [21] The grotto held its usual shade, a cool blue dark that the sun never reached even at noon, though the cloud sea outside the mouth blazed white all the way to the horizon. [54] Below those clouds lay the vineyard where Maren had grown up, raised by strangers who were kind, and somewhere under the rows Ilse's excuse had lived for thirty years like a stone in the soil. [89] Ilse had left her newborn at the vineyard gate and climbed back up to tend the grotto, because the grotto needed a keeper and a child, she said, needed sun. [119] Maren had come up the mountain in spring to nurse the woman through her dying, meaning to be correct and nothing more. [141] Instead she had stayed through summer, spooning broth, turning the thin body every two hours, and on Tuesday, when Ilse asked, she had said yes, I forgive you, and found that she meant it. [175] Ilse died before dawn on Friday. [181]

Maren washed her, then took the shears from the ledge and began to scallop the hem of the linen shroud, cutting the slow half-moons the grotto's keepers had cut for as long as anyone remembered. [216] The edge was meant to look like waves, Ilse had told her, because nothing here was supposed to end in a straight line. [239] When the hem was done, Maren drew the antique hairpin from Ilse's white hair, a length of dark silver worn thin at the shank, its head a small hammered seed. [269] She folded the shroud closed and fastened it at the breast with the pin, as the rite required. [287] It would stay there until the body cooled, and then it would come down the mountain to be pinned into the swaddling of the next child born in the valley, and then, decades on, into another shroud. [324] Death, birth, death, the same silver passing through, so that no one in the valley was ever entirely new or entirely gone. [346]

Maren should have felt something settle. [352] She did not. [355]

So she did what she had done as a girl among the mulberries at the vineyard's edge whenever a question would not lie flat: she went outside and climbed a tree. [386] A single larch grew from the lip of the grotto, bent by wind into a shape like a hand offering something. [407] She climbed until the branches thinned and sat with her back to the trunk, her feet above the clouds, and the hush that came up from the hidden valley was the hush she remembered from harvest, when the pickers stopped talking and only the shears spoke. [453]

Where did it come from, then, contentment, if not from being right, and not from letting go of being right? [473] She had expected forgiveness to be a door, and it had turned out to be only the clearing of a doorway. [494] The larch ticked in the wind. [500] Below, the clouds moved like slow milk, and beneath them, she knew, the vines were heavy and the miller's wife was near her time. [524]

Maren thought about the hairpin passing from the dead to the newborn, and about the hands that had carried it, keeper after keeper, and it came to her plainly that Ilse had carried it too, once, thirty years ago, down through the clouds to a gate, to a swaddled girl. [574] The pin had been in Maren's own blanket. [582] Ilse had never said so, and Maren had never asked, but she was sure of it the way she was sure of the ground. [606] It did not undo the leaving. [612] It only meant that the leaving had been one gesture inside a much longer one, and that the longer gesture had not stopped. [635]

Contentment, she decided, was not a thing you found by searching your own chest for it. [651] It was the feel of the silver in your hand while you were carrying it to someone else. [669]

She climbed down, quick and sure, the way she did everything once she had chosen it. [685] In the blue dark she laid her palm on the shroud and found it cool. [700] She drew the pin free, gently, and smoothed the scalloped edge back over Ilse's breast so that it lay in even waves. [722] Someone from the valley would come up for the body; someone always did. [735] Maren banked the small fire so it would keep, because the grotto would need a keeper again and she had not yet decided whether it would be her. [763] Then she closed her fingers around the hairpin and stepped out into the white, and went down toward the vines, where a child was waiting to be born. [791]
