Iris Vane had been off the grid for eleven months when she found the first painting. [16] It hung in a bus shelter outside Halvorsen, a town she had told no one she was heading toward, and it showed the road, the shelter, and a woman with Iris's shoulders walking north. [50] Only one person alive painted shoulders like that. [58] Mara. [59]

Twelve years earlier, Mara's self-portrait had hung in the Halvorsen Lending Library, and someone had carved LIAR across its eyes with a key. [82] The librarian had left it up as a lesson in forgiveness. [93] Iris, seventeen, had studied the building the way she studied everything, mapping the paper, the varnish, the draft under the east door, and then let the feeling in her chest do the rest. [126] The papers called her the lending library arsonist for a week, then forgot her. [140] Nine years in prison gave her a word for her mind: analytically intuitive, a counselor said, as if diagnosing her. [160] Mara's letters stopped in year four. [166]

The paintings kept coming. [170] A watercolor nailed to a fence post depicted the ferry terminal. [181] A mural on a warehouse wall portrayed the black island offshore, its dead volcano split by the old archive road. [201] Each one was a place, and each place held the next. [212] Iris walked into them like rooms. [218] She did not let herself hope; she catalogued. [226] Pigment fresh. [228] Brushwork hurried. [230] Someone with Mara's hands, moving a step ahead, wanting to be followed and terrified of it. [246]

The island had once belonged to the Tessene, a people who vanished before anyone wrote them down. [263] The government had built an archive in the caldera to hold what the lava had not eaten, then abandoned it. [283] The last painting stood at the archive gate: a canvas of the door itself, left open. [299]

Inside, the air was warm from the earth. [307] Shelves of catalogued fragments ran into the dark, and the dark smelled of sulfur and turpentine. [323] On a worktable, under glass, lay a faded tapestry fragment no larger than a hand, woven in rust and ochre. [343] It was a portrait, a woman's torso with hands raised as if holding a shuttle, and where the face should have been the threads had been cut away with deliberate care. [374] Mara's handwriting labeled it: Tessene weaver, self-portrait, face removed by the artist. [386] They did this to every likeness they made. [394] Not vandalism. [396] An invitation. [398] The face was left for someone who loved them to finish. [409]

Iris stood very still. [413] Something inside her shifted like a floor of cooling rock, molten underneath. [425]

Because she remembered now. [429] Not the fire; she had never forgotten the fire. [438] The night before it, kneeling on the library steps with Mara sobbing into her coat, Iris had promised: I'll paint you back. [460] Every scratch they made, I'll paint over. [467] I'll do your face myself. [472] Then she had burned the building instead, because fire was faster than forgiveness, and the promise went wherever things go when a person decides she is not the kind who keeps them. [504]

Mara had not forgotten. [508] Mara had gone off the grid herself, into a dead volcano, and learned from a lost people how to leave a face unfinished on purpose. [533] Then she had laid a trail of her own work across a coastline so that her sister, who trusted evidence over feeling, would arrive by evidence at a feeling. [562]

At the back of the archive, a canvas leaned against the wall. [574] Mara had painted herself in it, older now, standing in this room with the caldera light behind her. [592] The face was blank primed linen. [598] A brush lay on the floor beneath it, bristles clean, and on the wall in charcoal: YOU PROMISED. [616] FINISH IT. [618]

Iris heard the footstep behind her before she turned. [627] Mara stood in the doorway with paint on her wrists and the face she had refused to give herself, waiting to see if Iris would give it back. [655]

"You were supposed to forget," Mara said, and her voice cracked down the middle like ground. [671] "I read your parole reports. [676] I know how you follow things. [682] I hated you for it." [687] "I know," Iris said. [691] "I'm here anyway." [694]

She picked up the brush. [699] She did not know how to paint; she never had. [709] But she had spent nine years memorizing a face from a photograph a guard took away, and eleven months learning its road. [731] She mixed ochre and rust, the Tessene colors, and began to portray her sister the only way she knew: precisely, measuring the distance between the eyes with her thumb, then letting the heat underneath decide where the mouth should go. [771] Under them, the earth breathed warm and kept what it had not eaten. [784]
