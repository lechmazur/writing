My father robbed the highway between Lordsburg and the border for eleven years, and he did it the way other men fish. [22] He waited, he watched, and he never raised his voice, not at the drivers and not at me. [40] He kept what he took in the ticket car of a circus train that had been left to rust on a siding since before I was born. [67] Not the cash; the cash went to rent and to the men who drove with him. [83] What he kept was the foreign paper: pesos, quetzales, a Canadian twenty, a soft green note from Nigeria that a driver had carried across three continents in his wallet. [112] "Money you can't spend is the only money that stays," he told me, smoothing a bill flat with two fingers. [132]

On the outside of every car he painted a red ring. [143] Other crews knew the sign. [148] It meant this siding, this stretch of road, this quiet, were spoken for. [161]

I left at nineteen and swore I was a tangent, a line that touches the circle once and never comes back. [182] I even named the company that. [188] Tangent built routing software for freight, the shortest path from any dock to any door, and for four years it rose the way my father's calm had taught me things could rise: without hurry, without noise. [224] Investors called me unflappable. [228] They meant it as praise, and I let them, though I knew where the polish came from. [245] It came from watching a man read a driver's hands before the driver knew what his hands would do. [264]

Each time the company cleared a milestone I drove out to the train alone. [278] I told myself I was checking on the money. [287] Really I was checking on the rings, which were fading, and on myself, who was not. [303]

The fall took eighteen months. [308] Our shortest paths were correct and nobody wanted them. [317] Drivers doubled back for a diner they liked, for a cousin in Deming, for reasons no algorithm could price. [336] A competitor built a product that let them, and the freight went with it. [350] I signed the last papers in a glass room and drove south the same afternoon, not deciding to, just finding the road already under me. [375]

I wanted to know why. [380] Why a man who had drawn a straight line through six years of his life should find himself standing again at the ticket car with the sun going down behind the elephant wagon. [413]

There was a boy inside. [418] He had the tin open and the foreign bills spread across the counter where clowns had once sold tickets, and he was sorting them by color like a child does. [448] Teo, from the trailer court, whose mother had cleaned rooms at the motel until the motel closed. [465] He did not startle. [469] He had my father's kind of stillness, or was practicing it. [480]

"Everybody says this was his," Teo said. [487] "Says the road's open now." [492]

I could have told him about prison, about the eleven years and the two that ended them. [509] I could have told him that the smartest thing I ever built was beaten by people going home the long way. [530] Boys don't hear arguments. [534] I hadn't. [536]

So I went to my truck and found the can of red enamel that had ridden under the seat for six years without my asking myself why. [563] I started at the caboose. [568] Teo followed me out and watched the first ring go on, bright over the ghost of the old one. [587] By the third car he understood what the rings said and who was saying it. [602] Not open. [604] Spoken for. [606] Held by someone who had come back and would keep coming back. [618]

"You're not going to work it," he said. [626]

"No." [627]

"Then why mark it?" [631]

"So nobody else does." [635]

He stood a long time in the copper light while the crickets started and the paint dulled from wet to matte. [656] Then he went back inside, and I heard the bills being gathered, and when he came out the tin was closed and under his arm. [681] He set it on the step of the ticket car, where it had always been, and walked toward the trailer court without looking back. [705]

I finished the last car in the near dark. [714] Then I sat where my father used to sit and took out the Nigerian note and smoothed it flat with two fingers. [736] Paths circle back, I think, because the straight ones are drawn by people who have never been anywhere. [754] The drivers knew it. [758] My father knew it, which is why he kept money that could only be carried and never spent. [776] The circle was not my failure. [782] It was the ground I had to hold. [790]
