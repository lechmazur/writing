<story>
Orrin called himself a philosopher, and the sewer did not contradict him. [12] The brick tunnels ran dry now above the drowned quarter, and he had made a home at the junction where three outflows once met. [36] Nobody else wanted it. [40] Since the plague had burned itself out, the survivors preferred the hill, the new council hall, the clean wind that blew away from the sea. [65] Orrin preferred evidence. [68]

Each morning he waded down the collapsed stair into the sunken city, where the harbor had swallowed the merchant streets and left them standing in green water to the second floor. [99] He dug through the silt with a bent spoon and a patience that irritated everyone who watched him. [117] The sediment kept records the council did not. [125]

Grey mud on top, from the years of abandonment. [134] Beneath that, a black band of ash and lime, the plague years, when they burned the dead and the pyres drifted down as grease. [158] Beneath that, sand. [161] Clean harbor sand, a hand's depth of it, laid down by a flood that had come before the sickness. [180]

This was the problem. [184] The council's story, carved already above their new door, said the sea had brought the plague, that the ships had carried it, that the old navigators with their brass instruments and their star tables had opened the gate to death. [224] Therefore the sea was finished. [229] Therefore the charts were kindling and the chronometers were scrap. [239] It was a tidy story, and Orrin distrusted tidiness the way he distrusted a dry sewer: something upstream had changed, and nobody was saying what. [264]

The layers said otherwise. [268] The flood came first. [272] The harbor wall had failed, the lower city had drowned, and the people had crowded up the hill into the granaries, and there, packed and starving, they had begun to cough. [303] The ships had been nowhere near. [309]

On the ninth day of digging, his spoon struck metal in the sand layer. [323] He rinsed it in the green water and held it to the light that came down the stairwell. [341] A cog, brass, no wider than his thumbnail, with teeth cut so fine they caught the skin. [358] He knew it at once; his father had cleaned such things for the harbor pilots. [373] An escapement wheel from a seafarer's chronometer, the kind that kept true time across an ocean so a captain could find his longitude and come home. [399] It had been lying under the ash the whole time, innocent, older than the accusation. [414]

He carried it back to the junction and sat with it for two days. [428] This was the agonizing part, and he did not enjoy it, though he had read that philosophers were supposed to. [448]

He could climb the hill and lay the cog and the layers before the council. [463] They would not thank him. [468] Their story held the town together; it gave the dead a cause and the living an enemy that could not answer back. [490] Take it from them and they would have to blame the granaries, which meant blaming each other. [507] He understood the arithmetic. [511] He simply refused to accept that a comfortable lie was the same as a load-bearing wall. [527]

Or he could say nothing, and let the sea be guilty, and let every chronometer in the town go to the smelter. [549] In one generation nobody would remember how to cross open water. [560] The town would live on the hill, safe, small, and eventually hungry, because the hill did not grow enough and the sea had always made up the difference. [588]

On the third morning he stopped agonizing, because the question had resolved itself into something plain. [604] Two stories were competing, and he could not make the council choose his. [617] But a method was not a story. [624] A method survived whoever won the argument, provided one person still knew how to do it. [640]

He went up the hill, but not to the council. [650] He went to the smelter's yard, where the confiscated instruments waited in a crate, and he bought a gutted chronometer case for the price of a fish. [677] The smelter's daughter, a sharp girl of eleven who asked too many questions to be popular, watched him fit the cog into the empty train. [702]

"That's forbidden," she said, without much conviction. [709]

"It is disapproved of," Orrin said. [715] "Those are different words, and you will want to learn the difference before you are my age." [732]

He wound the mechanism. [736] It ticked, uneven at first, then steady, a small dry voice arguing with nobody. [750]

"Come down to the sewer tomorrow," he told her. [759] "Bring paper. [761] I will show you how to read a tide, and how to read mud, and you can decide for yourself which of them is lying." [786]
