The hospice sat so high on the mountain that on most mornings the valley disappeared beneath a white floor of cloud, and the building seemed to float in its own light. [31] Maren had worked there for eleven years as its music therapist, and she had learned that the best songs were rarely the famous ones. [55] She sang about kettles. [59] She sang about the squeak of the linen cart, the way the sun crossed the third floor at four o'clock, the particular thud of Teodor's slippers hitting the tile. [88] Hymning the mundane, she called it, when anyone asked. [97] Dying people did not need to be reminded of heaven; they needed to be reminded that the world had been worth staying in. [120]

Teodor's blood work came back on a Tuesday. [128] The doctor said days, perhaps a week, and Teodor received the news the way he received everything, with a small nod, as if confirming a delivery. [154] Then he asked for Maren. [159]

"I want to do this properly," he told her. [168] He had owned a record shop for forty years and believed that endings mattered, that a side should finish clean, without a skip. [191] From the drawer beside his bed he took a jagged wedge of black vinyl, a third of a record, its label torn so that only the word "Waltz" survived. [220]

"My daughter broke it," he said. [226] "Or I did. [229] We were both holding it." [234]

Twenty-two years earlier, Ilse had wanted to leave the shop, the town, the mountain; he had wanted her to stay; the record that had been her mother's favorite had ended up between them like a rope. [270] She had taken the other pieces when she went. [279] They had not spoken since. [284]

"I want that song played at the end," Teodor said. [294] "The whole thing, no skip, and then I want to say goodbye, and I want it to be perfect. [313] Can you find a copy?" [318]

Maren turned the shard over in her hands. [326] It smelled faintly of cardboard and dust and something sweeter underneath, the smell of every record sleeve ever opened for the first time. [349] She had always thought of it privately as the scent of hope, the smell of something about to be heard. [369]

"I can find a copy," she said. [376] "But that isn't what you want." [382]

He looked at the window, at the clouds burning white below them. [394] "No," he admitted. [397]

Maren was serene by temperament, but serenity in a hospice is not passivity, and she had long ago decided that kindness was a thing you did decisively, on purpose and without delay. [429] That night she found Ilse's number through a cousin and called it. [441] She did not plead. [445] She described the room: the oxygen concentrator's steady hush, the slippers, the way Teodor still lined his pill cups up like forty-fives. [467] "He has a third of a waltz," Maren said. [476]

There was a long silence on the line, and then a sound like a drawer opening. [492]

Ilse arrived on Thursday, when the cloud floor was thick enough to walk on and the whole third floor swam in reflected light. [515] She carried two pieces of vinyl wrapped in a dish towel. [526] Teodor did not have a speech ready; the perfection he had planned dissolved the moment he saw her face, and he simply held out his shard. [552]

On the blanket over his knees they fit the three pieces together. [564] The record would never play again; the cracks ran straight through every groove. [577] But it was whole in the way that mattered, a black circle with a torn label, reunified under two pairs of hands that had once pulled it apart. [605]

Maren picked up her guitar. [610] She did not know the waltz, and she did not try to know it. [624] Instead she sang what was in the room: a daughter's coat still cold from the drive, a dish towel with a blue stripe, the hush of the machine and the hush of the clouds, the smell of old cardboard rising from a mended record. [668] Teodor's breathing found her rhythm, or she found his; in that room it was impossible to say which pulse belonged to whom. [690] Ilse laid her hand on the record. [697] Teodor laid his on hers. [702] Nothing was said that would have looked good written down, and nothing needed to be. [717]

He died two mornings later, just as the sun cleared the cloud floor and filled the room, and Maren thought afterward that it had been the most flawless goodbye she had ever witnessed, precisely because no one had performed it. [757] Ilse took the record home in its dish towel. [766] Maren went back to the third floor and sang about the linen cart, because someone else would need to hear it. [787]
