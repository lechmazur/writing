Eleven minutes under the ice had left Ilse with a light that would not leave her. [16] In every photograph taken after the resuscitation, a smear of luminance bloomed over her sternum, pooling brightest on the tarnished opera cloak clasp she had been wearing when she went through. [47] The rescuers had found her by that clasp, a brass moth glinting up through the black water, and the glow seemed to have followed it home. [73] Inside the light, if you looked long enough, there was a second face. [86] Her own, but patient. [90] Her mother called it a blessing and printed the pictures anyway. [101] Ilse called it being photographically cursed and stopped sleeping. [110]

It was Anselm who told her that fear could be tricked. [121] He had been the carnival's mentalist before the carnival died and his voice went with it, scarred down to a whisper by a fire in the ticket booth, and he lived now in the trailer behind the fence. [159] People still came to him for advice, leaning close to catch it, and the leaning made everything he said feel like a secret. [182]

"Fear only shows itself when it thinks you are hiding," he whispered. [194] "So you have to hide in the open." [202]

He gave her a dream dictionary, water-swollen, its margins crowded with his handwriting. [215] Every note contradicted the entry it sat beside. [223] Under DROWNING: the safest breath is the one you do not take. [235] Under MIRROR: whoever looks away is seen most clearly. [244] Under LIGHT: to keep it, confess it. [251]

"Read them aloud where it lives," Anselm said, and pointed his chin at the funhouse. [266] "Say the opposite of what it expects." [273] "It will lean in to correct you." [280] "Things that lean in show their throats." [287]

She went at dusk, wearing the cloak. [294] The funhouse had been shut for six years and smelled of sugar gone to vinegar, of wet plywood and something animal that had nested in the ball pit and not come out. [326] The painted clown mouth at the entrance had peeled into a grin of bare teeth, and Ilse found she could not stop looking at it, the way you cannot stop looking at a wound. [360]

Inside, the mirror maze was still standing. [367] Mold had crept behind the silvering so that each mirror bloomed with dark continents, and between the continents her reflection came back in dozens, all of them glowing faintly at the chest. [399] It was beautiful in the way spoiled fruit is beautiful, soft and sweet-smelling and wrong, and she wanted to touch it and wanted to be sick. [425]

She opened the dictionary and read. [431] "The safest breath is the one you do not take." [441]

The reflections leaned. [444] Not all at once, but the way a crowd turns toward an accident. [457]

"Whoever looks away is seen most clearly," she read, and looked straight into the nearest mirror, and the second face surfaced in the glow like something rising through ice. [486] It was hers, and it was hungry, and it was waiting for her to flinch. [501]

"To keep it, confess it." [506]

So she unbosomed herself to the mirror, the one thing she had told no one, not her mother, not the doctors who had counted the eleven minutes. [533] "I was not afraid down there." [539] "I was afraid when they brought me back." [547] "I have been afraid every day since that some part of me wanted to stay." [562]

The face in the glow leaned all the way in. [572] It had expected her to hide the wanting, and she had handed it over like a coat, and in its eagerness to seize the thing it fed on, it came too close to the surface and she saw what it was made of. [615]

Not the lake. [618] Not death, which had been quiet and had asked nothing of her. [630] It was made of the moment after, the paddles and the shouting, the brass moth catching a flashlight beam and giving her away. [653] The light had never been the lake's. [660] It was the rescue, still burning, still afraid it would be needed again. [673] Its weakness was that it needed her to keep almost dying. [684]

Ilse unhooked the clasp. [688] The cloak slid off her shoulders onto the rotten floor, and the glow in the mirrors went with it, pooling around the brass moth like a dog that has been told to stay. [721] She left it there. [725]

Outside, Anselm was waiting at the fence, and she leaned close to hear him. [739] "Well?" [740]

"It showed its throat," she said. [746] "It was only ever afraid of being finished with." [755]

The next photograph her mother took showed nothing over her heart at all, and Ilse looked at the plain dark of her own chest for a long time, and found that she could bear it. [790]
