The beacon tower at Thornsea had been dark for twelve years, which was exactly as long as the world had been quiet. [22] Ilse counted both from the same night. [29] She was the custodian now, though no one had appointed her; she had simply stayed when everyone else walked inland, and staying had become a title. [55]

Atop the tower sat the pavilion, the old lantern room, a dome of glass panes that an astronomer had designed a century before the collapse. [80] The wind off the water set every pane trembling at its own pitch, so the pavilion hummed day and night, a low chord Ilse had stopped hearing years ago and never stopped feeling. [113] Inside, on a table of salt-scarred oak, lay the prism. [123]

It had been the tower's heart once, a crystal the size of a child's torso that gathered the lamp's flame and threw it thirty miles to sea. [150] Now it was forty-one pieces. [155] Ilse had found them scattered down the tower stairs like a trail someone meant her to follow. [172]

She was blessed, or afflicted, with the backward sight; a doctor had once written retrocognitive on a form and underlined it. [193] Touch a thing and she saw where it had been. [203] Each shard she lifted gave her its history: the glassblower's breath, the keeper who polished it with his sleeve, the gull that struck it in a storm. [230] Forty shards spoke. [233] The forty-first did not. [237]

She called it the shard of hush. [244] When she held it she saw nothing, only a silence so complete it had texture, like wool pressed over the ears. [265] It frightened her, and she kept returning to it like a tongue to a broken tooth. [281]

Her father had died believing the collapse was an accident of accidents: grids failing, then water, then the long walk from cities, each disaster a coin landing tails. [309] "Coincidence," he said, "is just the universe clearing its throat." [319] Ilse could not accept that. [324] The prism had shattered the same night the world went silent. [335] She needed that to mean something, because if it did not, then her staying meant nothing either. [352]

So she tracked the stars. [357] Each clear night she lay on the pavilion floor with a tin of charcoal and mapped where they crossed the panes, pane by pane, week by week. [384] The astronomer had aligned the dome to something; the mullions leaned in a way that was not decorative. [402] By the third winter she saw it: one light, cold and white, not a fixed star but a wanderer, moved in a slow loop that would bring it to the dome's apex on a single night. [438] She worked the arithmetic four times. [444] The night was the anniversary. [449]

Twelve years to the hour after the beacon died, the wanderer would stand directly over the pavilion, its light falling straight down through the glass onto the table where the prism waited. [481]

She rebuilt it through the autumn, fitting shards with pine resin and patience. [494] She left the hush shard for last, and for last she left the center, because that was the only gap it fit. [516]

On the night, the wind dropped for the first time in memory and the pavilion's hum thinned to a single sustained note. [538] Ilse stood over the table with her hands at her sides. [549] The wanderer climbed. [552] It reached the apex, and a thread of light came down through the panes and struck the mended crystal, and the crystal took it and grew. [578]

She touched the hush shard then, and the silence opened. [588]

She saw the last keeper, twelve years gone, standing where she stood, in a night when every radio on earth had just stopped. [611] He was not panicking. [615] He had a hammer. [619] He was looking up through the dome at this same white wanderer, and he was breaking the prism deliberately, piece by piece, so that no one could use the tower until the wanderer returned and someone patient enough to gather glass was standing here to see why. [666] He had wanted a witness. [671] The hush was not emptiness; it was him holding his breath. [682]

The whole pavilion flared, rekindled. [687] Light left the prism in a flat bright blade, poured through the panes, and went out across black water for the first time in twelve years. [713]

Ilse ran to the rail. [718] Far out, where the sea met nothing, a light answered. [728] Then another, further south, a smaller flicker, as if someone had waited with a lamp and almost not lit it. [748]

She thought of thistle seed, how it goes where wind takes it and still, against all odds, some of it lands on soil. [771] Coincidence, she thought, was only a pattern seen from too close. [782] She sat down in the humming glass and watched the lights talk. [794]
