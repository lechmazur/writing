The Aurelian Hall had been a cinema before it was a concert hall, and Ines still kept the old projector's lens on her desk like a paperweight. [27] She was the night registrar, which meant she signed the musicians out after rehearsals, logged the lost gloves and the found scores, and wrote down who left last. [55] Management had added that last column in October, after a viola went missing, and the orchestra had noticed. [73] Since then nobody spoke to her. [79] They nodded, they signed, they went out through the glass forecourt into the dark, and the hush they left behind had weight. [101] Words had become unnecessary between them and her; she was a column now, not a person. [117]

But the musicians still talked to each other, and Ines discovered how in November. [131] A cellist named Dagny left a torn concert ticket in the found-items tray, and an hour later the second oboe came looking for it, took it, and left another in its place. [163] On the back, in pencil, were doodles: a spiral, three dots, a small closed door. [178] Ines told herself it was none of her business, and then she picked up the lens. [194] Under the thick glass the pencil marks swelled and sharpened, every grain of graphite distinct, and yet they meant no more than before. [217] Ambiguously clear, she thought; the lens made everything visible and nothing plain. [229]

She began to eavesdrop anyway. [234] Night after night the tickets passed through her tray, and she bent over them with the lens like a scholar over a dead language. [258] A key with a crossed-out bow: the viola, she decided, and someone's guess. [271] An eye inside a square: the desk by the door, the woman who watched. [285] That one appeared often. [289] She learned the shapes of their suspicion the way one learns weather, without being told, and it settled into her shoulders. [310]

The worst of it was that they were not wrong to wonder. [322] She had never reported anyone, but she had written the names down, and she had kept the lens on her desk, and she had read what was not hers to read. [353] Mistrust, she understood, is a thing two people build together, one silent brick each. [367]

In December it rained through the whole of a Thursday rehearsal. [378] When it stopped, the forecourt did what it always did after rain: the glass canopy dripped onto the wet flagstones, and each drop rang faintly against the panes, so that the whole crystal space seemed to whisper. [415] The musicians lingered there, as they did on such nights, reluctant to break the sound. [430] Ines carried her logbook out to them. [437]

She set it open on the low wall where Dagny sat with her cello case, and she set the lens on top of it. [461] Nobody spoke; the forecourt did their speaking. [468] Then Ines took a ticket from her pocket, one of the old cinema stubs she had found in a drawer, and drew on the back of it with her pencil. [498] An eye inside a square. [503] Beside it, a circle with a line through it, the shape of the lens, and beside that, an ear. [522] She held it out to Dagny. [528]

The cellist looked at the doodle for a long time, and then at the open logbook, every name in Ines's small hand, every time, nothing hidden, nothing beyond what they had already guessed. [561] "You read them," Dagny said, and it was the first sentence anyone had given Ines in two months. [579]

"All of them," Ines said. [584] "I understood almost nothing, but I read them." [592]

The oboist laughed, once, softly, and the glass caught the sound and gave it back. [607] Dagny picked up the lens and turned it in the wet light, and through it the drops on the canopy became enormous, trembling, perfectly clear and impossible to read. [636] "The viola was in the wrong locker," she said. [645] "Halvard found it in October." [650] "Nobody told the office because nobody trusted the office." [659]

Ines took that in, the whole absurd shape of it, two months of silence built around a locker. [677] She should have felt foolish, and instead she felt light, as if something she had been carrying up the stairs every night had been set down on the flagstones. [706]

Dagny took the pencil from her and drew on the same stub: an open door. [721] She handed the lens back, and Ines understood that she was being given it as a tool and no longer as a secret. [744] Around them the forecourt kept whispering, drop by drop, and nobody needed to say anything more. [760]
