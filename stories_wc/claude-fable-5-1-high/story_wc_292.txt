The jet stream had slipped south three winters ago, and Fen Halloran arrived at the Skerry Point weather station in the wet aftermath, when the island had stopped freezing and started to breathe. [33] The ground gave under her boots like a held breath. [43] She was a map librarian, which meant she believed, against all evidence, that a thing correctly drawn could still be corrected. [64] Her ancestor, Tobias Halloran, navigator of the Marrow Gull, had been exiled here in 1841 for refusing to sail his captain through the strait. [88] The sentence had been reversible: return with the true charts, the Admiralty wrote, and be restored. [104] He never returned, and the family maps marked him a coward in tidy brown ink. [119]

The station keeper, a woman named Orla with salt-cracked hands, met her at the door and asked what a librarian wanted with a place that measured only wind. [147] "I want to ask it a question," Fen said, and hated how her voice wobbled even as her chin stayed level. [168] That was her way, nervously confident, sure of the answer and terrified of the asking. [183]

The station was experimental in the way of things built by people who expected to be wrong. [200] Sensor masts leaned in the softened peat. [207] The floors sweated. [210] Everything Fen touched was faintly damp, and the dampness got into her thinking, so that dread did not stab but seeped, a slow sponge filling from below. [237]

Orla showed her the archive: a steel cabinet of journals dredged from the Marrow Gull's wreck when the thaw exposed her ribs the previous spring. [262] "The thaw gave up more than paper," Orla said, and set a copper nail on the desk. [279] "His coffin came up out of the bog with the ground moving. [291] We reburied him deeper. [295] This came loose." [298]

The nail was green with age and notched along its shank, seven cuts, uneven. [312] Fen held it and felt the softness of the room press in, as if the walls had learned from the peat. [333]

The journals were mostly weather. [338] Tobias had logged winds, pressures, the color of the sky at dusk, for twenty-two years of exile, in a hand that never once shook. [362] Between the columns, he had written verse. [369] Bad verse, deliberately bad, the kind of stanzas a man writes when he wants a reader to look past them. [389] Fen did not look past them. [395] She had spent her career reading margins. [402]

On the third night, with rain moving sideways across the sensor field, she laid the nail against the first stanza and took words by its notches, seven cuts, seven words. [432] The stanzas opened. [435]

The captain had wanted the strait because a fleet was waiting to be led through it, and Tobias had seen the ice building in a pattern no chart showed, a pattern that came with a southward wind that lasted years. [475] He had refused, and the Marrow Gull had gone aground anyway on a lee shore, and he had stayed on the island afterward not because the sentence held him but because someone had to keep measuring. [511] The true charts existed. [515] They were the weather columns themselves, two decades of a shifted wind, recorded by a man the world had filed under cowardice. [537]

Fen sat back and the chair sighed wetly beneath her. [547] Orla, reading over her shoulder, was quiet a long time. [557] "Our models started matching his numbers in the second year," she said finally. [570] "We thought it was coincidence. [575] We never asked who he was." [581]

The last stanza was different. [586] Decoded, it was not a record but a question, addressed to whoever came holding the nail. [602] Would you undo it, he asked, if undoing meant bringing me home to a country that only wanted me once I was right? [625]

Fen had come to reverse an exile. [632] She had the proof in her damp hands, and the Admiralty's descendants still kept an office that honored such petitions, and she could imagine the plaque. [658] But the ground here was moving, and Tobias was in it, and he had chosen the wind over the pardon. [678]

She filed the petition anyway, because the family maps needed correcting and she was, after all, a librarian. [696] But she wrote the reversal to leave him where he lay, name restored, body unmoved, exile lifted and unenforced. [715] She stayed the season to type his columns into Orla's models, and the softened floor held her weight, mostly. [734] On her last night she pressed the copper nail back into the peat above his grave, seven notches down, and asked the wind nothing, because for once she already had her answer. [766]
