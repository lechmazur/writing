Vell had kept the maps of the harmonic caves for thirty-one years, and in all that time the caves had never once been quiet. [24] Sound lived in the stone here. [30] A footstep on the lower gallery came back as a chord; a dropped chisel rang for the better part of an hour. [52] The mapkeepers before her had learned not to draw the caves with ink. [65] They read the ripple-marks instead, the fine standing waves that sound pressed into meltwater ice each winter, and copied those patterns onto slate. [88] Where the ripples ran close, the caves were loud. [97] Where they spread, a person could think. [104]

Then the energy beings arrived, and there was nowhere left to think. [116] They came down through the sinkhole in early autumn, gently luminous, drifting like lamp-oil poured into water. [133] They meant no harm that anyone could name. [141] But they were made of vibration, or fed on it, and the caves answered them the way a struck bell answers a hammer. [164] For six weeks the galleries had roared without pause. [173] The village above had moved its sick to the far fields. [184] Vell had stopped sleeping in the second week. [192]

So she went down with her slate and her lantern and the one tool the old mapkeepers had left her that she had never needed: the echo chamber stone. [221] It was a fist of hollow basalt, smooth as a river egg, with a single bored hole. [238] Hum into it and it kept the hum, gave it back a heartbeat later, exact. [253] The notes in the margins of the oldest slate said only: for the storm, find the still place first. [272]

The still place. [275] Every storm had one; she had believed that as a child watching thunderheads turn around a clear blue hole. [294] She believed it less now, wading through noise so thick it pressed on her teeth. [309] The ripple-marks were useless; the ice this winter had frozen in a frenzy, no pattern at all. [326]

But ice under strain does more than ripple. [334] It cracks. [336]

Vell knelt at the frozen sump in the third gallery and put her lantern flat against the surface. [354] Cracks ran everywhere, white threads in black glass, and everywhere they ran they pointed. [368] Sound had made them, and sound had a direction. [377] She traced the fractures with a numb finger, sketching them onto slate: long shear lines that radiated from the sinkhole, and finer ones that crossed them, and in the crossings, small unbroken islands. [410] Places where two waves met and cancelled. [417] Places the noise could not reach because the noise was fighting itself there. [430]

It took her four hours and three sumps to plot the lines back to their source, and when she stood, her knees had gone to stone. [456] The islands converged on a single point: a shelf of flowstone in the seventh gallery, hardly wider than a bed. [476] She climbed to it with the roar tearing at her, and stepped onto the shelf, and the roar did not stop. [497] But it thinned. [500] It became a thing outside her, a wall she leaned against rather than a weight she carried. [517]

Here, then. [519] She understood the old note now. [525] The stone was not meant to hold quiet; there was no quiet to hold. [539] It was meant to hold the storm, and return it one heartbeat late, from the one spot where a heartbeat's delay would set every wave against its twin. [567]

Vell set the echo chamber stone in the center of the shelf, bored hole upward, and stepped back. [585] For a moment nothing changed. [590] Then the stone drank the roar and gave it back, and the returning sound met the coming sound, and between them something opened. [613]

She would think of it afterward as a bloom of hush: a silence that unfolded petal by petal, first across the shelf, then down the gallery, then out along the shear lines she had traced, following the cracks the way water follows a groove. [657] She had not conjured it from nothing. [664] She had conjured it from the noise itself, turned back on its own throat. [678]

The energy beings drifted down into the quiet and hung there, dimmer, pulsing slow. [692] Whatever they were, they seemed to have wanted this too, and simply not known how to stop. [709]

Vell sat down on the flowstone with her back to the wall. [721] Her ears rang the way an anvil rings after the last blow, a hard, flat, beaten calm. [738] Not peace, exactly. [741] Peace was for people who had never had to hammer it out. [753] She took up her slate and, for the first time in six weeks, began to draw the new map. [772]
