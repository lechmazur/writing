The settlement of Ninefold spread across the cracked pan like a spill of iron, cabins welded from truck beds and turbine housings, and at its center Oda kept the listening shed. [31] Nobody had built a working radio since the last resource war burned the satellites out of the sky, so messages came the old way, on wax cylinders carried by traders across the waste. [64] Oda did not play them on the phonograph. [72] She read them. [75] Under a lens cut from a headlight, the groove of a cylinder is a landscape, and the people of the far settlements had learned to scratch a second text into its walls, too fine for a needle, meant only for eyes like hers. [118] She read by lamplight through shards of bottle glass, because the scratches hid from white light. [134] Amber showed the music. [138] Green showed names of the dead. [144] Blue, rarest, showed the things people could not say aloud. [154]

Vesper lay across the doorway while she worked, a bloodhound gone gray at the muzzle, ears pooled on the floor like dropped cloth. [177] For eleven years after the war she had found bodies in the rubble fields so that families could stop walking. [197] She was retired now, and Oda had promised her she would never have to smell death on purpose again. [216]

The trader came at dusk with one cylinder wrapped in a rag. [228] "From Sable Creek," he said. [233] "No name. [235] Paid in copper to reach you." [241]

Sable Creek was two hundred miles east, across salt flats where nothing walked. [254] Oda's sister had gone that way six years ago, following a rumor of rain. [268]

The cylinder was heavier than wax should be. [276] When Oda held it to the lamp, something dark hung inside it, suspended in the pale body of the thing. [296] A leaf. [298] Five-pointed, veined, curled at the edges the way leaves curl when the year turns. [312] Oda had not seen a leaf since she was nine. [322]

Vesper lifted her head. [326]

The settlement went quiet around the shed in the way it did when she read, a wild hush, wind picking at loose sheet metal, a hundred people not breathing. [355] She set the cylinder in the cradle and brought the lens down and turned it by hand, a quarter inch at a time. [378] Amber gave her nothing but a groove cut with no sound in it. [391] Green gave her nothing. [395] Blue gave her a riddle, scratched in a hand she knew from birthday cards. [409]

I stand where the ground drinks before the roots do. [419] I have grown tall and cast a shade you have never sat in. [432] Follow what I have sent and you will not need the map I could not draw. [448]

Oda read it three times. [453] Her sister had always talked like that, in pictures instead of directions, and it had driven Oda mad when they were girls. [475] Ground that drinks before roots. [480] A spring, a seep, water rising instead of falling. [489] Somewhere east of Sable Creek a tree had grown tall enough to drop leaves. [503] But two hundred miles of salt could hide a spring forever. [514]

Follow what I have sent. [519]

Oda looked at Vesper, and Vesper was already standing, nose working, reading the cylinder as Oda had, in a language older than scratches. [542] Oda had spent years watching that nose and had learned to read it the way she read wax. [560] The slow lift meant distance. [565] The flare meant water. [569] The soft whine, which she had not heard since the last body in the last rubble field, meant a person. [589] Vesper gave all three, and then she did something Oda had never seen. [602] She wagged. [604]

Oda understood then what her sister had done. [612] She had not sealed the leaf in wax to preserve it. [623] She had sealed it to preserve what clung to it: the smell of wet ground and bark and her own hands, kept airless across two hundred miles for a nose that had spent its life closing the gap between the missing and the ones who missed them. [670] The riddle was not a puzzle. [676] It was an address, written for a dog. [684]

Oda broke the wax open with her thumbnail and held the leaf under Vesper's nose, and the old hound breathed it in the way she used to breathe in a shirt before a search, filing it, making it hers. [723]

"One more," Oda said. [727] "Not a body this time. [732] I promise." [734]

Outside, the hush held. [738] Ninefold watched the woman and the dog walk east past the last welded cabin onto the salt, the leaf folded in Oda's shirt pocket against her heart, the wax left in pieces on the reading bench. [774] Vesper's nose was down and her tail was up, and two hundred miles had never smelled so close. [792]
