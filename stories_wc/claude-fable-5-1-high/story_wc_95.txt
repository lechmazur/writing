Ilse Varnhagen had fenced eleven philosophers to a standstill, and she intended to make Anselm Dorr the twelfth before the atrium's brook could carry another leaf past the door. [29] He was late. [32] The Academy settled its disputes with blades because arguments, left to themselves, only circled; Ilse had always preferred a question that arrived somewhere. [55] Sunlight fell through the glass roof in long panels, warming the moss along the water and the forty students who had crowded onto the benches to watch. [82] The Chancellor paced. [85] A porter knocked over a tray of glasses and apologized to the brook. [98]

Ilse sat on the low wall, unbuckled the leather cuff of her mechanical arm, and took a Danish tin sewing needle card from her coat. [123] The strap had frayed again where the brass met the skin of her shoulder, and waiting was the only time she ever remembered to mend it. [149] The card had come from Copenhagen with the arm itself, the maker's joke: a limb of gears, and a packet of needles to keep it decent. [175] She threaded one with her living hand and looked up, and that was when she noticed the sundial. [193]

It stood on a plinth at the brook's bend, a broad stone disc that Anselm had been painting the week before, insisting the Academy could not argue about time on an instrument that had lost its numerals. [230] He had finished only half of it. [237] Seven hour lines, crisp in fresh white, fanned across the eastern side; the western side remained bare grey. [255] And the lines were wrong. [260] Ilse had studied enough of the world to know how a dial at this latitude should spread its hours, and these angles were too tight, then too wide, then tight again, like a man breathing through an argument. [298]

She crossed the moss with the needle card still open in her hand. [311] "Doctor Varnhagen," the Chancellor said, "the floor is that way." [321] "The floor will keep," she said pleasantly, and with a tone that closed the subject like a door on a hand. [342]

The paint had dried; she could not scratch it. [351] Instead she laid tin needles along each painted line, seven bright rulers, and read how far each strayed from where an honest hour would fall. [376] Three degrees, eight, two, eight, five, one, seven. [384] Numbers, and she knew the alphabet they belonged to. [393] Fencing had eight parries, and twenty years ago two students had built a cipher on them, passing notes across lecture halls in strings of guards: tierce, octave, seconde. [421] Read in pairs, the way they had once read them, the angles made a short sentence. [437] NOT HERE. UPSTREAM. WALK UNTIL THE GLASS ENDS. [445]

Ilse stood very still while the atrium came apart around her with great decorum. [459] The Chancellor called for order; the students, sensing they might be denied blood, began to debate whether absence constituted forfeit; a heron landed in the brook and refused to leave. [489] Twenty years, she thought. [493] Twenty years of duels over the same question, whether a thing proved by force stayed proved, and Anselm had finally answered it not with an argument but with an overture, hidden in plain paint where only she would think to look. [534] The western half of the dial was blank because the rest of the sentence was hers. [550]

She gathered the needles, slid them back into their tin, and buckled the cuff of her arm with the strap still unmended. [572] "Chancellor," she said, and the room quieted, because she had said it the way she said en garde. [590] "Doctor Dorr has conceded the floor and proposed a change of venue. [602] I accept. [604] You may record that the question was settled, and that no one will be able to tell you how, which is the first honest result this Academy has produced in a generation." [636]

The Chancellor opened his mouth; she inclined her head with such perfect courtesy that he shut it again. [654] Then she took her sword, because one did not abandon a sword, and followed the brook upstream through the long glass corridor, past the heron, past the ferns, toward the far door where the panes gave way to sky. [693] Behind her the students erupted into the finest argument of the term. [705]

Ahead, where the glass ended, a man with paint on his cuffs sat on a stone waiting, holding a brush and no blade at all. [730] "You are late," Ilse said. [735] "I was early," said Anselm. [740] "By a week." [743] She sat beside him, laid the sword across her knees, and asked the question she had carried for twenty years, and for the first time it felt less like a weapon than like a road. [778]
