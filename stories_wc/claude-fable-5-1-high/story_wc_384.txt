The Chancery called it completion. [5] Ilse called it what it was: finishing a dead man's sentence in a dead man's hand. [21] Master Aurel had died with his pen lifted, the Quarry Charter three lines short of its seal, and the Chancery wanted those lines written the way the Chancery would have liked. [52]

They gave her his sanctuary to work in, because the light there was said to steady a hand. [70] It was a chapel cut into the marble quarry itself, white walls veined with gray, its roof long since fallen open to the northern sky. [95] Above, the aurora moved in slow green folds. [103] Below, water worked through the stone, and stalactites the color of old teeth dripped into basins the quarry families had carved a century ago. [127] Each drop was a small clock. [133] She had until the seal-bearer came at dawn. [141]

She laid out Aurel's things. [146] His ink. [148] His unfinished page, the last word trailing off where his heart had stopped. [161] And the two things he had left her by name: a rose thorn, long as her thumb and black with a lifetime of ink, and a small iron key that fit no lock anyone had ever found. [198]

The thorn she understood. [202] Aurel never wrote with a nib. [208] He wrote with thorns from the heritage rose his grandmother had carried out of the old country in a wet rag, a bush that had outlived three regimes and still flowered against the quarry wall. [243] A thorn splits ink differently than steel. [250] It leaves a hairline dryness at the top of every stroke, a flaw no forger with a proper pen could fake. [271] That was why they needed her. [277] She had been his student before she was the Chancery's instrument, and she had the thorn. [293]

The key she did not understand until the light changed. [303] The aurora thinned to pearl twilight, that gray-rose hour when the sky has not decided whether it is ending or beginning, and the marble took the color into itself. [332] She set the key down beside the page to free her hand, and its teeth fell across the ruled lines. [352]

They matched. [354] Every notch in the bit sat exactly where Aurel's letters leaned, the tall ascenders, the dropped tails, the slight forward tilt he had never been able to correct. [382] It was not a key to a door. [390] It was a key to him, a measure of his hand, cut in iron so that someone he trusted could carry his writing forward after his own fingers failed. [419] He had known he would not finish. [426] He had known who they would send. [433]

Ilse read the three missing lines the Chancery had drafted for her. [445] They bound the quarry families to the stone for another generation, debt passing from parent to child like a chain link welded shut. [468] She knew what Aurel had meant to write instead, because he had told her, years ago, over this same table, in this same light. [492]

Open treason would kill her and change nothing; the seal-bearer would burn the page and commission another forger with a worse thorn. [514] So she calibrated. [517] She wrote the Chancery's lines word for word in Aurel's leaning hand, the key laid along each line to keep her true. [539] But for the single clause that welded the debt shut, she set the thorn down and took up a steel nib. [560] The letters came out perfect, and perfectly wet at the top of every stroke. [574]

No seal-bearer would see it at dawn. [581] But the quarry families kept their own scribes, patient people who had studied his hand for forty years, and the first of them to lay the charter against the light would find one clause that was not his. [619] A clause proven forged is a clause struck. [627] The rest of the charter, his true charter, would stand, and the Chancery could not object without confessing what it had commissioned. [649] She had unlinked a single link, quietly, and the chain would come apart on its own. [665]

When it was done, the stalactites were still dripping, and the sky over the open roof had gone from pearl to the first thin gold. [690] She looked a long time at Aurel's last trailing word, the one his heart had cut short. [707] For a year she had hated him for leaving it, for leaving her the page and the key and the weight of it. [730] Now she saw it was not abandonment but trust, and she did not finish the word. [746] She let it stay unfinished, and forgave it, and forgave him, and a little of herself. [762]

Then she wiped the thorn clean and understood how the rose had outlasted three regimes: not by refusing the wall, but by enduring wisely, knowing exactly where to put its thorns. [793]
