The Hanging Palace of Vell dangled from nine stalactites the size of cathedral spires, its chains groaning whenever the deep sea below exhaled. [23] Prince Teodric had not slept in three days, and he looked it: soot in his hair, a torn sleeve, boots gray with the dust of conduits no one had entered in a century. [56] He was hunting for the founders' vault, and he had a hypothesis about where it was, because he had a hypothesis about everything. [79] It was the only way he knew to think. [88]

Above him, in the great halls, brass thrushes clicked through their nine permitted notes, and gear-orchids opened on the hour with a sound like coins dropped in a bowl. [117] Everyone called it beautiful. [121] Teodric had once believed them, until he read the founders' first ledger and found a single line struck through: We could not get the song right. [147] Hypothesis: the palace was born wrong. [153] Not evil, not broken, only wrong at the root, like a tune copied by someone who had never heard it. [173]

The vault was where he had guessed: behind the anchor-plate of the seventh stalactite, where the stone was warmest. [192] Inside sat a lead box, and inside the box, packed in salt, an egg. [206] Not brass. [208] Not enamel. [210] Speckled blue, light as a held breath, faintly rough under his thumb. [222] A bell-thrush egg, from a bird extinct since before the sky was lost. [235] His hands began to shake and did not stop. [244]

Hypothesis: the founders kept it because they knew what they had failed to make. [258] Hypothesis: the warmth of the stalactite had held it in some long suspension, the way the palace itself hung suspended, neither fallen nor free. [282] Hypothesis: it could still wake. [287]

He did not go to the council. [294] They would have called it a relic and put it under glass beside the other silences. [310] Instead he carried the egg against his chest back through the conduits to his workroom and began to assemble a nest. [331] He built it from the palace's own wrongness: the copper coil of a dead thrush for a heating ring, the felt lining of a clock case, a thermostat pried from the orchid bed. [364] He guessed at the temperature of a mother's breast from the ledger's weather tables. [378] He guessed at humidity from the salt. [385] He guessed, and adjusted, and guessed again, and the shaking never left his hands. [399]

On the fourth night, to feed the heating ring, he diverted steam from the main chain-warmers, and the palace answered. [419] Cooling links contracted. [422] Nine stalactites took the strain, and the whole suspended city shivered like a struck bell, dust sifting from the vaults, the brass thrushes losing their count and clicking out of time. [453] Teodric knelt over the nest with the room trembling around him and thought, half-dreaming: so this is what it costs. [473] To make one true thing, the false world must be made to shake. [486]

The egg cracked on the eleventh day. [493] He did not help it. [498] Hypothesis, whispered to himself like a prayer: the struggle is part of the making, and the founders skipped the struggle. [518] What emerged was ugly, wet, blind, and furious, a fist of down and bone that opened its beak and made a sound no mechanism in Vell had ever made. [547] It was not one of the nine notes. [555] It was not a note at all, only a hungry, wavering cry, and Teodric wept, because it was the most beautiful thing he had heard in his life. [583]

Then came the part he had not hypothesized. [591] In the halls above, the brass thrushes, jarred from their count by the quaking, had gone quiet and listening. [610] Their founders had built them to learn a song no one could teach them. [624] Now one by one, imperfectly, dragging the shape of it through their cheap little throats, they began to answer the chick. [645]

Teodric sat back against the wall with the last bell-thrush in the world cupped in his shaking hands. [663] The last. [665] There would be no second egg, no mate, no flock; when this one died, the true song would die with it, and the brass birds would be left holding only an echo of an echo. [700] It was bitterly sweet, and he knew it. [708] Bitter, because the founders' flawed beginning could not be undone; sweet, because an echo of a true thing is still truer than a perfect copy of nothing. [735]

The palace swayed on its chains. [741] The chick cried again. [745] Above, a hundred brass throats answered, a little less wrong than before, and the bedraggled prince closed his eyes and let himself, for the first time, stop hypothesizing and simply listen. [776]
