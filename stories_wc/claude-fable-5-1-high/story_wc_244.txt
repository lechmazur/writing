Nobody had Halloway's address, because Halloway did not have one. [10] She had a rucksack, a laminated map of every depot between the coast and the hills, and a fragment of moon rock the size of a thumbnail, which she kept in a film canister and showed to children who had lost their bedrooms. [53] "This came from a place with no weather," she would tell them, "so it has never once been rained on, which is more than any of us can say." [82]

When the river took Ambry, she arrived before the water had finished leaving. [95] Within a day there were pallets of blankets in the chapel, a generator humming behind the pub, and a dairy farmer three valleys over who had inexplicably agreed to lend his flatbed to strangers. [129] None of it was official. [134] Halloway worked on favors, and she wrote every favor in a notebook with a green cover, and she paid each one back before she left, usually with something she had gotten as a favor from someone else. [171] The notebook always closed balanced. [176] Then she was gone, and the town found itself with a working sewage pump and no one to thank. [195]

She joked constantly, the way some people cough, and with the same air of not being able to help it. [215] In Ambry she told the vicar that the flood had done wonders for the damp, and asked the postmistress, as she asked someone in every ruined town, what the cure for loneliness was. [248] "Company," said the postmistress. [252] "Tried it," said Halloway, already loading the flatbed. [260] "Didn't take." [262]

The postmistress, whose name was Ida, noticed that in three days Halloway had accepted nothing, not a bed, not a sandwich, not so much as a dry pair of socks. [292] The moon rock, Halloway admitted when pressed, was the only gift she had ever kept. [307] An old man in a shelter had given it to her when she was nineteen, and had died before she could work out what she owed him. [334] "So now I owe everyone," she said cheerfully, "and I settle up fast, and I leave before the interest starts." [354] Ida understood then that the balanced notebook was not generosity but a lock, and that the woman had built herself a life in which no one could ever owe her long enough to love her. [389]

You cannot post a letter to a woman with no address, but you can start a rumor. [485] Every town she had ever fixed was on the chain somewhere, and every town added a word.
