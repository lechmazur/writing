The rain had not yet decided whether to become a storm when Chaplain Ada Ferris pushed open the door of the music box repair shop on Larkin Street. [28] Inside, two hundred silent instruments waited on shelves, their lids closed, their combs holding tunes the way a held breath holds a sentence. [51] The quiet was not empty. [56] It hummed, the way a room full of clocks hums even between ticks. [69]

"You're the one who called," said the woman at the bench, not looking up. [83]

"Ferris. From Halloran Correctional." [87] Ada set her bag on the counter and drew out a bundle of gray prison-issue cloth. [103] "I need a second opinion." [108]

The woman, Marguerite, according to the card in the window, finally raised her eyes. [122] "On what, exactly?" [125]

Ada unfolded the cloth. [129] Inside lay a heart the size of a plum, made of brass and salvaged steel, its two chambers hinged and riveted, a tiny winding key set where an artery would be. [160]

"An inmate built this over four years from workshop scrap," Ada said. [172] "He comes before the parole board Thursday. [179] The board's psychologist says it's manipulation, a prop to look reformed. [190] The man himself says it's an apology. [197] I've been asked to write a letter, and I don't trust either opinion, including my own." [213]

Marguerite did not reach for it. [219] She wiped her hands first, slowly, one finger at a time, then pulled the lamp closer and adjusted the loupe on her forehead. [242] Every movement was aptly deliberate, the pace of someone who knows that a rushed hand ruins what a patient one could save. [264]

"Chaplains don't usually want second opinions," she said. [272] "Aren't you supposed to deal in the first and final one?" [283]

"I deal in what people say to God at three in the morning," Ada said. [298] "Most of it is sincere and most of it changes nothing. [309] I told him an apology that stays words isn't finished. [319] He asked what an apology looks like when it has hands. [330] Then he started building this." [335]

Marguerite lifted the heart at last. [341] She turned it under the lamp, tested the hinge with a fingernail, and listened to it the way a doctor listens to a chest. [365]

"The rivets are hand-peened. [369] The comb is cut from a hacksaw blade, tempered in something, maybe a radiator. [383] Whoever made this was trained. [388] Properly trained, years ago, by someone old-fashioned." [395]

"He was a clockmaker before," Ada said. [402] "Before a bar, a bottle, and a man who didn't get up." [414]

Marguerite's hands paused, only for a moment, and then resumed. [424] "Does it play?" [427]

"He wouldn't wind it in front of me. [435] He said it wasn't mine to hear first." [443]

Outside, the rain finally committed. [448] Thunder walked across the rooftops, and the shop's hush deepened, became a kind of vibration, two hundred combs trembling faintly in sympathy. [470] Marguerite set the heart on a velvet block and, with a jeweler's screwdriver, eased the two chambers apart. [488]

"I judge mechanisms the way I judge weather," she said, almost to herself. [501] "Not by the clouds. [505] By the way light breaks through them. [512] Anyone can build a gray sky. [518] You learn who someone is from what they let show through the gaps." [531]

"Yes." [681]
