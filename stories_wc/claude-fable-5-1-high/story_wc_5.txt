The crater had been an orchard once, before the star's warmth ran out of the ground. [16] Now the apple trees stood in rows like gray teeth, and the snow between them never softened. [33] Oda had been born down here, at the bottom of the bowl, where the winter dark lasted from November to February and the rim cut the sky into a small pale coin. [65] She made flash paper for a living, the way her grandmother Ilse had. [78] Cotton pulp, acid baths, a drying rack of sheets so thin they whispered. [91] Traders hauled it up the crater wall for stage magicians in the south, who never asked why it burned brighter than any other. [114] The answer was the dust. [119]

When the star fell, the first settlers gathered what glowed in the melt and kept it in a copper plate, an illustration plate cut for a storybook no one had finished printing. [151] Its engraved side showed a girl climbing out of a valley under a path of stars; its hollow back, soldered shut, held the last of the starlight. [178] Each generation scraped a pinch into the paper pulp and sealed the plate again. [192]

Ilse was dying in the cabin at the orchard's edge. [202] She had walked out of the crater twice in her life and back both times, and she claimed to know the route by heart. [226] She had never drawn it. [231] Now her words were gone, and only the heart was left. [242] So Oda decided to read that instead. [249]

For six days she sat by the bed with two fingers on the old woman's wrist. [265] She did not shift when her legs went numb. [274] She let the fire go to embers rather than break the count, and when frost crept across the inside of the window she watched it and did not scrape it away. [305] Beat, beat, a long pause, three quick, a beat like a held door. [318] She wrote nothing down; she learned it as Ilse had learned the trail. [331]

Then she went out to lay it in the orchard. [341] The crater was full of things people had dropped and left: pruning shears with rusted jaws, bottle glass gone violet, tin lids, a sled runner, the spoons Ilse had bent open for stirring acid. [375] Oda carried them in a sack between the dead trees and set them down one at a time. [393] A strong beat was a stone, a faint one a lid. [404] A long pause was distance, five paces of untouched snow. [414] The three quick beats, always followed by the held one, she took for a turn, and each time she laid a spoon with its bowl pointing the new way. [443]

Whether the map was true she could not know. [452] It was the only map anyone had ever made of the way out, and it ran, when she finished, from the cabin door to the foot of the eastern wall, where the trail had to begin if it began anywhere. [492] That, at least, felt like proof. [498]

On the solstice the wind stopped. [504] It did that every year at the dead center of the dark, an hour when nothing in the crater moved, not the snow, not the branches, not the smoke, which stood up from the chimney like a rope. [542] Ilse's chest went still with it. [548] Oda waited with her fingers on the wrist, and the pause went on longer than any she had counted. [567] She did not move until she was certain. [575]

Then she took the copper plate from its shelf and walked the whole map in the windless dark, stone to lid to spoon, out to the last marker at the eastern wall. [607] A map needs a seal to say it is finished. [617] She knelt, packed the snow flat with her palms, and pressed the plate into it engraved side down, holding it there until the cold burned through her gloves. [645] When she lifted it, the girl and her path of stars stood raised in the snow, pointing at the wall. [665]

Then she drew one sheet of her own flash paper from her coat and struck a match. [682] The paper went in a single white breath, and for that instant the whole orchard stood lit: every stone, every violet shard, every spoon, the long spaces between them, the gray trees throwing shadows they had not thrown in a hundred years. [724] It was the old star's light, hoarded four generations and sold a pinch at a time to strangers, spent now to show a heartbeat laid across the ground. [752] Then dark again, and no ash, because good flash paper leaves none. [764]

Oda stayed kneeling by the seal a long while. [773] When the wind came back, she would climb. [781]
