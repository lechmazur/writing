The rain quit an hour before dawn, and by the time Odile reached the breakwater the ivory stone of the harbor had gone the color of wet teeth. [28] Spray came over the wall in slow arcs and beaded on the tin box under her arm. [45] Everyone in Calder Harbor called her the ocean-spray archivist, because her archive was not a room but a ledge of salt-glazed shelves at the end of the mole, where the sea itself delivered the records. [80]

For eleven years the records had been fragments. [88] A cork stopper with a family crest burned into it. [98] A plank scratched with the words STILL EAST OF YOU. [108] A brass mouthpiece wrapped in oilcloth, then a length of tubing, then a bell dented like a struck cheek, each piece arriving months apart, each one a part of the herald's trumpet that Marek Sull had carried into exile when the council sent forty families out past the shoals. [157]

Marek had been the harbor's herald. [163] It was his trumpet that used to announce every sail, and his silence, more than any decree, that had made the exile feel permanent. [187]

Odile had never been able to plan. [194] She could not look at a blank horizon and imagine what would cross it. [208] But put a fragment in her hands and she saw, instantly and whole, the thing it belonged to. [226] The council's clerk had once called her reactively visionary, meaning it as a joke, and she had written it on the first page of her ledger because it was the truest thing anyone had said about her. [263]

So when the mouthpiece came ashore in the second year, she had seen the whole trumpet, and understood that Marek was sending it home ahead of himself, one piece at a time, so that the harbor would have to keep expecting him. [305]

The exiles' letters, when letters came, described how they had sailed: not by stars, which the fog swallowed, but by absences. [326] They steered by the dark where the Calder lighthouse should have been, by the gap in the reef where no breakers showed, by the missing weight of home behind them. [356] Odile had steered the same way, cataloguing what was not on her shelves as carefully as what was, filing the empty months alongside the planks. [381]

Three weeks ago the council had rescinded the order. [390] The exile was over. [394] But forty families scattered across a decade do not return in one fleet, and the town had been standing on the quay in the rain ever since, growing quieter each day nothing came. [427]

This morning, wedged in the kelp below her ledge, she had found the last piece. [442] A single valve, green with verdigris, its spring still stiff enough to answer her thumb. [457] She had held it and seen the sails at once: three of them, then a gap, then two more, then a long pause and a seventh, strung out over the coming weeks like the trumpet pieces had been strung out over years. [499]

Marek had staggered his return the way he staggered everything, so no single disappointment could sink the whole hope. [518]

Now she knelt on the wet stone and opened the tin box. [530] Mouthpiece, tubing, bell, and the valve, which she seated in its casing with a click that carried across the empty water. [551] Eleven years of fragments became, in her hands, an instrument. [561] That was all meaning had ever been to her: pieces admitting they were one thing. [576]

She could not play. [580] She knew this and lifted it anyway, and the first note came out cracked and flat and enormous, rolling back off the ivory warehouses so that people on the quay turned as one. [613]

They had not heard that sound in eleven years. [622] She waited, counting the way Marek had taught the town to count between his calls, then blew again. [640] Then a longer wait, and a third. [647] She was not announcing a ship. [653] There was no ship yet. [658] She was heartening the harbor, teaching it to hold its breath in intervals again, to expect the gap and trust the next note, so that when the sails came in threes and twos and ones the silence between them would feel like rhythm and not like loss. [705]

On the quay a woman began to laugh, or weep; from the mole it sounded the same. [722] Someone else took up the count aloud. [729] Odile blew a fourth note, badly, and the rain-washed stone threw it back, and far out where the reef broke the swell she saw, because the valve under her thumb had already shown her, the first white edge of a sail. [770]

She wrote nothing down. [774] For once the record could wait for the thing itself. [784]
