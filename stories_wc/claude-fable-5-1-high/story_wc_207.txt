Ida Voss kept rain in jars. [6] Not the water itself, which anyone could catch, but the smell that came before it: the breath the ground let out when the first drops struck dry stone. [34] For forty years the cement works had buried that smell under lime dust, and Ida had collected it anyway, kneeling in the quarry margins with a spatula and a labeled tin, sensibly eccentric in her gumboots and pressed blouse. [73] The town found her harmless. [78]

Then the factory shut down. [83] The kilns went cold in March, the pumps that had drained the karst for two generations fell silent, and by June the first honest rain in Ida's memory came down on clean limestone. [116] She stood in it with her eyes shut. [124] The scent was so full it frightened her. [132]

That was the season the water came back under the cliffs. [143] Boys who went looking for the old survey tunnels reported a lagoon where their fathers remembered mud, and one of them brought Ida what he had found on the ledge above it: a brass compass, its lid seized with verdigris, a name scratched inside. [187] He brought it to her because everyone knew Ida could hold a thing and tell you where it had been. [207]

She had never called it a gift. [214] Objects spoke to her palms the way weather spoke to her nose, and she had spent her life writing down what they said in the same square hand she used for her jar labels. [248] A wedding ring told her about a kitchen in Leeds. [258] A rifle stock told her about a boy who never fired it. [270] Everything, always, explained itself. [275] That was the trouble. [279]

The compass belonged to the Hallam expedition, 1911, six men who had gone into the cliffs to map the water and had not come out. [304] Ida knew this before she read the name, because the moment she thumbed the hinge and the lid cracked open, the reading came up through her wrist like cold rising from a cellar. [337] Carbide lamps. [339] Wet wool. [341] A man named Hallam saying, quite calmly, that the needle had stopped pointing anywhere. [355]

She closed the lid and the voice stopped. [363] She opened it and the voice returned. [370] Every awakening the compass held was hinged on that small brass joint, and each time it swung she got a little further into the dark with them. [397]

She went down to delve on a Tuesday, because she had errands on Wednesday. [411] She took rope, a headlamp, three empty jars, and the compass in her coat pocket, and she told the postmistress where she was going, which was the sensible thing to do. [442]

The survey tunnel dropped through a throat of pale rock and opened without warning. [456] The lagoon lay under a ceiling she could not see, black and utterly still, and the hush of it was not silence but something that hummed just below hearing, the way a struck bell keeps sounding after you can no longer say it does. [500] Water dripped somewhere and the drop went on for a long time. [512]

Ida knelt on the ledge where the boy had found the compass and did what she had come to do. [532] She opened the lid. [536]

Hallam again, the wool, the lamps, the six of them standing at this same shore while the needle turned in slow circles. [558] She waited for the rest. [563] The rest was what she always got: the cause, the fall, the drowning, the neat account that closed a life like a ledger. [586]

It did not come. [590]

The reading went to the edge of the water and stopped, not cut off, not lost, but complete, the way the last note of a hymn is complete. [618] Six men had set down their compass and walked forward into something the compass could not describe, and it had not troubled itself to try. [643]

Ida sat back on her heels. [649] Her whole life she had been handed answers whether she wanted them or not, and here at last was the place where explanations failed. [673] She had suspected such a place existed. [680] She had smelled it, almost, in that first clean rain after the factory closed: a fullness that no chemistry of soil and oil quite accounted for. [706]

She unscrewed one of her jars and held it open over the black water for a while, then capped it and did not label it. [731] She closed the compass gently, so the hinge would not be strained, and set it back on the ledge where it belonged. [753] Then she climbed out toward the ordinary rain, and told the postmistress she had found nothing, which was true, and was the whole point. [777]
