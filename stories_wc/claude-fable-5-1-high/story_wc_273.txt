The wind had been carving this encampment for ten thousand years before Ysolde pitched her tent inside it, and it went on carving while she waited for the sun to come back. [32] Sandstone hollows opened around her like the insides of shells, each one smoothed by the same patient abrasion, and in the last minutes of the eclipse they glowed the color of a healed bruise. [66] She had never seen the desert at noon without pain. [76] Her skin was the white of the paper in her lap, her eyes a pink that the sun treated as an insult, and so she tracked at dawn and dusk and slept through the hours everyone else called day. [115] The eclipse had given her four minutes of midday. [124] The drone had given her a reason to spend them angry. [135]

It hung two meters off, an artificial hummingbird no longer than her thumb, wings a blur of carbon lace, throat lit with a lens where a real bird would have kept its iridescence. [168] The institute had sent it after she stopped answering their letters. [179] They wanted her notes. [183]

Fourteen years ago a flash flood had taken her brother out of a canyon in the time it takes to inhale, and inside that inhalation Ysolde had lived what felt like an hour: every pebble in the water distinct, his hand opening finger by finger, the sky behind him a slow blue she could have named. [239] She had spent the years since trying to find out why. [250] Why time slows in crisis, why the mind stretches a single second into a room you can walk around in. [270] The institute wanted the answer for soldiers and pilots. [279] Ysolde wanted it for her brother, and she did not intend to share. [292]

So she wrote in disappearing ink. [298] Oak gall and a volatile she distilled herself; the words held for a quarter of an hour and then let go of the page, leaving nothing for a lens to read. [329] It suited her; the thing she studied was itself a kind of vanishing, and she had come to believe a truth like that should be written the way it happens. [359]

The hummingbird drifted closer, tilting its lens toward the fading lines. [370]

Then the eclipse ended. [374]

Light came back over the rim of the hollow not gradually but like a thrown thing, and the first edge of it landed on the back of her hand. [403] Pain, immediate and clean. [407] And there it was. [411] The second opened. [414] She felt the room of it assemble around her, felt the drone's wings separate from a blur into individual strokes, felt the wind stop and start and stop. [442]

She had been here before, always by accident, always with something dying. [454] This time she stayed calm inside it and looked. [463]

What she saw was that she had stopped thinking in words. [474] Nothing in her was narrating. [479] Her attention had gone the way a fox's goes when the grass moves: total, silent, unhurried, every sense pointed at the world and none pointed back at herself. [507] That was the answer, or the beginning of one. [516] Time did not slow because the body panicked. [524] It slowed because the mind, for once, went wild, and a wild mind does not count. [540] The question was whether it could be done on purpose. [550] Consciously wild. [552]

The drone darted, hunting the last legible line before it faded. [563]

Ysolde's anger did not rise; it settled, level as standing water, and with the same level hand she tipped the ink bottle over. [586] Ink ran across the sandstone in a black fan and began at once to die, the volatile lifting off it in threads. [608] The wind pushed the spill into channels, the way it had pushed the rock for ten thousand years, and for a few seconds the stone wore a map that had never existed and would not exist again. [645]

She read it the way she read sand. [653] Where the ink pooled, the air was still; where it feathered and vanished first, the wind was moving, and where the wind moved through this hollow, the hummingbird could not hold position. [685] The pattern showed her a seam of turbulence along the north wall, a gap in the drone's sight two strides wide. [706]

She did not decide to move. [712] The fox in her simply went. [718] Cloak over her burning hand, body low, she crossed the fading fan of ink while the drone bucked in the gust it could not see, and by the time it steadied, the hollow held a tent, a bottle, a blank page, and no tracker at all. In the shade of the next chamber, Ysolde let the words come back slowly, one at a time, like animals returning to water. [787] The first was her brother's name. [793]
