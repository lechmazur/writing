The parapet had lost its battlements to three centuries of weather, and what remained was a lip of wet stone that belonged to no one. [25] That was why Ines had chosen it. [32] Neither army would send a patrol up a stair that ended in mist, and neither commander would admit to wanting a place where words could be traded without banners. [61] There was no courier who could cross the valley, so she and Tobiah had agreed, two winters ago, to let the moon carry the schedule. [86] Full moon, the parapet. [90] New moon, nothing. [93] The sky did not take sides and could not be bribed. [104]

Tonight the moon was full somewhere above the fog, a pale coin she had to trust rather than see. [123] Beside her, the boy she had brought sat with his wrists tethered to an iron ring, shivering in a coat too large for him. [147] She had given him her scarf. [153] She had not given him her name, because names were the kind of thing she had learned to hold back. [173]

In her pocket she turned the drawer pull over and over. [184] It was brass gone green, shaped like a small acorn, pried from a desk they had found in the keep's one dry room at their first meeting. [211] The desk had two drawers and two pulls; Tobiah had taken the other. [224] In fog thick enough to hide a face, you showed the acorn before you showed anything else. [241]

Two moons ago, she had shown it, and then she had said too much. [254] A name, offered as reassurance: the miller who had been feeding Tobiah's people bread and rumor. [270] She had meant it as proof that her side knew and had chosen mercy. [284] Her side had not, in the end, chosen mercy. [293] The miller had hanged before the moon was half gone. [303] She had climbed the stair at the next full moon anyway, spoken her apology into the mist, and heard Tobiah's boots turn and go down without a word. [331]

That had been a month of thinking. [338] Ines thought the way other people breathed, slowly and without being asked, and the ledger in her coat had a page for every exchange and a line beneath each for what she had gotten wrong. [373] A mistake left alone was only a wound; a mistake written down and read again became a tool, and she had come to believe that was the only alchemy war allowed. [404] Under the miller's name she had written: never trade a life you do not hold. [419]

Stone scraped below. [422] The boy stiffened. [425] Ines stood, lifted the acorn into the gray, and waited. [435] For a long moment there was only wind combing the wet stone and the drip of the stair. [453] Then a second green acorn rose out of the mist, held between two fingers, and behind it the shape of Tobiah and, behind him, a woman with her hands bound in front of her. [487]

He did not say good evening, and he did not say her name. [500] His brow was furrowed the way it always was, but the lines were deeper now, cut by the same month she had spent. [523]

"One for one," he said. [528]

"One for one," she agreed. [533]

She knelt beside the boy and worked at the knot. [543] Her fingers were cold and the rope was swollen, and she took her time, because hurry was another thing written in the ledger. [566] When she untethered him, she kept one hand on his shoulder until he understood he was meant to walk forward and not run. [589] Across the stone, Tobiah loosed the woman's wrists with the same unhurried care. [602] The two prisoners passed each other in the mist without looking, the way strangers pass in a narrow hall. [621] The boy did not give back the scarf, and Ines did not ask. [634]

Tobiah stood a while longer. [639] She could see him deciding whether to speak, and she did not help him, because she had learned that too. [659]

"The miller had a daughter," he said at last. [668] "Nine years old." [671]

Ines nodded. [673] She did not say she was sorry again; she had said it once, correctly, and a second time would only be asking him for something. [698]

"I'll write her down," she said instead. [705]

He looked at her for a long, furrowed moment, and then he turned the acorn in his fingers and put it back in his coat. [730]

"Next moon," he said. [734]

It was not forgiveness. [738] But he had kept the drawer pull, and he had said the words that meant the desk still had two drawers. [759] Ines stayed on the parapet until his steps were gone, then took out the ledger and wrote in the hush the wind left behind. [783]
