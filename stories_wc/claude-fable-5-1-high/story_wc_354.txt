Teodor Ansel had been exiled for teaching a child to lose well. [12] That was how the Vell council phrased it, and he had never argued, because arguing was imprecise. [29] Twelve years ago, under a sky gone copper with eclipse, two clans had sworn a covenant at the Lodestone: whenever the moon swallowed the sun, their heirs would play one game of chess on the black rock, and the winner's clan would hold the valley's water until the sky darkened again. [80] Mira of Vell, nine years old and his student, had sat down across from the Harrow boy with a winning line memorized. [102] On the fourteenth move she had given up her queen for a knight fork that was gorgeous and entirely insufficient. [122] Vell lost the water. [126] Teodor lost his home. [130]

Now he climbed the last scree toward the Lodestone at twilight, six days before the next phase would bring the moon across the sun again. [155] His compass had begun spinning a mile back, the way every compass did here, needle circling like a thought that could not settle. [178] He did not need it. [183] He had walked this path in his mind every night for twelve years. [196]

In his coat pocket his thumb worked the basalt worry stone, a chip he had pried from the Lodestone the morning of his banishment. [220] Exile had polished it to a dark gloss, and where his thumb always came to rest there was a dent, a small hollow, as if the stone had finally agreed to remember him. [253]

He had been a thoughtfully precise man once, a man who correlated pauses with intentions. [268] His whole method as a tutor had been translating silence. [278] A child looking at a board says nothing, but the nothing has texture: a quick hush before a pawn move means confidence, a slow exhale before a capture means doubt, a held breath means the child has seen something you have not. [320] Which was why her queen sacrifice had broken him. [329] He had heard her silence that day, long and level as a held note, and he had translated it wrongly. [349] He had thought she was afraid. [355]

Someone was waiting at the Lodestone. [361] A woman sat on the black shelf where the board had once been chalked, and her back was so straight and so still that for a moment he correlated it with the wrong decade and thought: nine years old. [400] Then she turned, and she was twenty-one, and she held a chip of basalt in her own hand. [418]

"You kept one too," Mira said. [424]

It was the first thing she had said to him in twelve years, and it was not an apology, and he was glad, because an apology would have been imprecise. [454]

He sat across from her the way he had a thousand times. [466] The light was going violet. [471] Below them the Harrow fires and the Vell fires burned on opposite slopes, small and equal. [487]

"Show me," he said. [491]

She did not speak. [495] She set her stone on the shelf where the black queen had stood, and then moved it, with two fingers, to the square where she had thrown it away. [524] Then she left her hand there and was silent. [533]

He listened. [535] The silence was not the one he remembered. [543] It had never been afraid. [548] It was patient, and under the patience lay something like joy, the joy of a person who has seen a thing so beautiful she cannot bear to play the ugly line instead. [580]

He translated it slowly, the way he should have done then. [591] She had understood the covenant better than the men who wrote it. [603] A game decided by memorized openings was not a covenant; it was a coin toss with ceremony. [620] A game where a child gave up everything for one perfect idea, and lost, and both clans had to live with the difficult truth that beauty and victory were not the same thing: that was worth swearing under a darkened sun. [661] The eclipse had swallowed it. [666] It had not killed it. [671]

"They have asked me to play again," she said finally. [681] "They want me to win this time." [688]

"And you?" [690]

She pressed her thumb into the dent of her own stone, and he felt the gesture in his own hand, twelve years of the same small hollow worn by the same question. [722] Whether the difficult thing could still be made beautiful. [731]

"I want to play it properly," she said. [739] "Whatever properly costs." [742]

Teodor looked down at the two fires that had burned twelve years apart and were still, from this height, the same size. [764]

"Then we have six days," he said, and set his stone beside hers. [777]
