Vesna inked her own cards. [5] Other cartomancers bought decks stamped in a city she had never seen, but she ground onyx to dust and mixed it with rainwater and drew each figure by hand, because a fortune should cost the teller something. [42] That was the only rule she had kept. [50]

Nine days after the birth, she climbed past the tree line with a satchel on her hip and the clouds sealing shut beneath her boots. [75] The letter had come from her daughter, Ilse, whom she had not raised. [88] Four lines. [90] A girl, born at dawn, seven pounds, both of us well. [101] No invitation, no refusal, only fact, which was more than Vesna had earned. [114]

The mountain people said that lost time drifted upward like heat and collected in the high glade where the clouds could not follow, and that at midnight you could stand in it up to your knees. [150] Vesna did not believe it, and she had come anyway, because she had spent twenty years learning that belief was not the same as need. [175]

The glade opened at midnight, as if it had been waiting for the hour to arrive. [191] Silver grass. [193] A sky so close and crowded with stars that she felt them pressing on her collarbone. [209] Below, the cloud sea lay lit from within, white and heaving and utterly silent, and the beauty of it caught in her throat like a swallowed stone. [236]

She knelt and unfastened the satchel. [242]

First the tablecloth. [245] In Vesna's family, every birthday the child laid a hand flat on the cloth and someone traced around it in thread, so that a life could be read across the linen like rings in a tree. [281] Her mother's hands were there, and her own, and then six small hands of Ilse's, each a little wider than the last. [303] After the sixth, blank linen. [308] Twenty years of it. [312] Then one adult hand, stitched crooked because Vesna had been shaking when Ilse finally, last spring, let her hold the needle. [333]

Second, the saucer. [336] It came from a dolls' tea set, glazed with forget-me-nots, cracked clean across the day Vesna left, because Ilse had been pouring pretend tea for her mother and her mother had picked up a bag instead of a cup. [375] The saucer had fallen. [379] Vesna had taken it. [383] She could not, even now, say why, except that she had wanted to carry the sound it made. [401]

Third, the cards. [404] She spread them on the tablecloth, over the blank years, the onyx figures drinking the starlight until they looked wet. [424] She asked the only question she had carried up the mountain. [435] Where does it go, the time I did not spend on her? [447] Where is it kept, and can it be fetched? [456]

The cards answered the way cards do, in a grammar of hints, and Vesna found that she did not want to parse them. [479] For thirty years she had translated other people's futures into sentences they could carry home. [494] Tonight she let herself stay perplexed, willingly, the way one stays underwater a moment longer than is comfortable, and she looked instead at the glade. [519]

Nothing stood knee-deep. [522] The grass was only grass, silver because the light was silver. [533] The lost time was not here, heaped and waiting to be carried home. [546] She lifted the saucer and understood, with a plainness that hurt, where it had gone. [561]

Through the crack. [564] Twenty years had run out of that hairline like water and gone down into the clouds, which was why the clouds hung so heavy on this mountain and never lifted, and why she had felt them pulling at her ankles all the way up. [608] Repentance did not rise. [612] It was cloudbound, wet and sourceless, and you did not gather it back; you lived beneath it and it rained on you when it chose. [637]

The beauty of the sea below her was unbearable, because she knew what it was made of. [654] She gathered the cards without reading them. [661] She took the needle from the satchel's lining, and the last of the black thread, and laid her own hand on the linen beside the space where a seventh small hand should have been. [695] She could not trace herself one-handed. [701] So she set the saucer there instead, crack downward, and stitched around its rim, a small circle in the blank years, the size of a newborn's fist. [728] Not a hand. [731] A place for one. [735]

When the clouds began to lift with the dawn, she was already descending through them, the tablecloth folded against her chest, the saucer left behind in the grass for the time to keep leaking, as it would, as it must. [775] Below, a house with a new child in it, and a needle she would offer to hold. [792]
