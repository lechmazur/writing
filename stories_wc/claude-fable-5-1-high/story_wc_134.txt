The cave sang whenever water moved through it, and water always moved through it. [14] Ilse had lived inside that music for forty-one years, long enough to hear the difference between a drip landing in the north pool and one landing in the shallow basin by the entrance. [47] The crystals took each note and passed it along their edges until the whole chamber hummed like a struck glass. [67]

Pilgrims called her the clairvoyant of the singing cave, which she found generous. [80] She did not see the future so much as listen to it. [92] In the drip pools, unseen forces rolled grains of grit against calcite for centuries until they became cave pearls, small and white and smooth as knuckles. [118] Ilse would lift one, still wet, and hold it to her lamp. [130] The crystals threw a hundred shadows at once, and where those shadows overlapped on the pearl's skin, shapes gathered that were not quite pictures and not quite sound. [158] She read them the way other people read weather. [167]

What she found there was rarely grand. [174] A baker from the valley wanted to know if his shop would survive the winter; the pearl showed his hands in dough, decades of them, flour in the creases of his knuckles at eighty. [208] A widow asked whether she would love again and Ilse saw only a kitchen window, morning light, a cat that had not yet been born choosing her lap. [236] She told them these things plainly and watched their faces change. [247] That was the work, as she understood it: not prophecy but magnification, taking the small magic people already lived inside and holding it to the light until they could finally see it glow. [280]

The cave was steadily unpredictable in the way of all water. [291] The drips never stopped, but they never landed twice in the same place, and the song shifted key from hour to hour. [313] Ilse trusted that constancy of change more than she trusted anything. [324] It never occurred to her that the music might tell her something she did not want to hear. [342]

The pilgrim arrived on a wet afternoon with no lamp and no question. [355] She was young, hair cropped short, boots split at the toes, and she stood in the entrance basin without flinching at the cold. [378]

"Everyone who comes here wants something," Ilse said. [386]

"I want to listen," the girl said. [393]

So they listened together. [397]

After the pilgrim arrived, the cave changed, though it took Ilse an hour to admit it. [413] The north pool drip, which had not missed a beat in forty-one years, began to fall a fraction late. [432] Then it fell early. [436] Then two drops landed at once in a chord she had never heard, bright and low, like laughter at the bottom of a well. [460]

The girl walked to the north pool, knelt, and lifted a pearl Ilse had never taken. [476] It was larger than the others, yellowed like old ivory, the first one Ilse had ever watched begin to form, the year she came to the cave. [503] She had left it alone for four decades out of something she had never called fear. [519]

"Hold up your lamp," the girl said. [526]

Ilse's hands did it before her mind agreed. [534] The crystals caught the flame and flung it, and the shadows fell across the old pearl in layers, and in the place where they overlapped Ilse saw her own cave, empty of her. [567] Not dark. [569] The lamp still burned, and someone with cropped hair sat where she now sat, turning a wet pearl for a stranger with a question about his son. [596] She had read a thousand futures and never once thought to read the one that would inhabit this place after her. [617]

"I did not see you coming," she said, and heard how foolish it sounded from a woman with her reputation. [637]

"You never looked," the girl said, without unkindness. [645] "You were busy looking for everyone else." [652]

The drip fell again, on time now, and the chord resolved into the note Ilse had lived inside for forty-one years. [673] She understood that the unseen forces which had rolled grit into pearls had been rolling her too, patiently, toward this hour. [694] It should have felt like an ending. [701] Instead the cave seemed brighter than she had ever known it, every crystal alight, the dark between them warm as a held hand. [724] This was the magic she had spent her life magnifying for others, and she had never once turned the lamp on it herself. [747]

She set the old pearl in the girl's palm and closed the split-booted stranger's fingers around it. [764]

"Then listen well," Ilse said. [769] "It never lands the same way twice." [776]

Behind them the water kept moving, and the cave, as it always had, kept singing. [791]
