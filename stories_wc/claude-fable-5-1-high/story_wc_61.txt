Vell kept the gear because it had belonged to a grandparent it had never met. [15] It was a small brass thing, a calculating machine gear with too many teeth, and Vell had counted them once and lost the number somewhere past a thousand. [43] "It approximates infinity," the grandparent had written on the tag, in the looping script of a being who had loved measurement the way other beings loved song. [70] Vell did not love measurement. [75] Vell loved the tag. [79]

The nebula garden had no ground, only depth, and depth was a thing you could not see through. [97] Dust hung in the garden thicker than breath, tinted rose and copper where the buried stars leaned on it, and the blooms of ionized gas opened and shed their petals in slow, luminous sighs that took a decade to fall. [137] To move through it Vell sang, a low pulse cast outward, and listened for the shape of what answered. [156] Sonar was an old way, older than the gear, and the echoes came back soft, telling Vell where a petal drifted, where a cold world hid, where the gap opened that a body could cross. [191]

The gap was the point. [196] Vell's kind were transit-makers. [200] For as long as there had been records, each generation had crossed the garden once, alone, to a dark world on the far side, and each had carried light to it. [231] The great-grandparent had carried light by kindling a star. [240] The grandparent, ashamed, had sworn to do otherwise, built the calculating machine to find a gentler way, run the numbers for forty years, and kindled a star. [267] The parent had wept over the gear, promised, and kindled a star. [279] Vell had read every account. [284] Every one began with resolve and ended with fire, and the worlds on the far side were bright now, and empty. [305]

So Vell pinged, and listened, and moved. [312] A petal detached from a bloom above and began its ten-year descent, and Vell decided that the crossing would be finished before it landed on nothing. [338] That was the only deadline the garden offered. [346] The echoes returned. [349] Ahead, a world, sunless, its surface ringing back cold and close-grained like stone under frost. [364] Behind, the blooms, warm and hollow. [370] Between them, the dust, which swallowed light before it had traveled a single span. [384]

This was why the ancestors had burned. [391] Light did not carry through the garden. [398] You could not lend a distant sun to a cold world when the dust ate everything in between; you had to make a sun where the world was, close and hot, and the making took the world with it. [437] The grandparent's numbers had been right. [443] Vell turned the gear in its fingers and felt every tooth, an almost-infinity of small, patient edges. [460]

Almost. [461] Vell pinged again, harder, and this time did not listen for the shape of things but for the shape of the dust itself: its density, its lean, the slow current where the buried stars pulled it. [497] The echoes came back layered, and in the layers Vell heard something the grandparent's machine could never have computed because the machine had never been built to hear. [525] The dust was not a wall. [531] It was a lens, if you stood in the right place, if you were patient enough to let it settle around you. [553]

Vell drifted into the gap and stopped. [560] The cold world turned below. [565] The blooms shed their light behind. [571] Vell opened, the way the blooms opened, and became still, and the dust that had been nudged by a thousand years of pinging began to gather along the paths those pings had worn, thickening here and thinning there, until the garden itself bent the buried starlight through Vell and onto the frozen face below. [625] Vell set the gear into the current before it, and the dust caught on its almost-infinite teeth and spread the light evenly, a thing its maker had computed and never once thought to try. [659] No fire. [661] Only a shape, held. [665]

It would take a very long time. [672] Light this faint would need centuries to warm a stone, and Vell would need to stay exactly here, unmoving, for all of them. [695] The petal was still falling. [700] Vell understood, with a calm that felt like something it should have been afraid of, that no account would be written of this crossing, because the one who might write it was the lens. [734] Somewhere, far behind, a child of Vell's kind was being born, and would one day read the accounts, and would find only that the fourth generation had gone into the garden and not come back. [769] It would resolve to do better, as they all had. [779] Vell hoped, gently, that it would learn instead to be still. [790]
