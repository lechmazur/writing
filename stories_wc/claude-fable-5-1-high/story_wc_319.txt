The library had no door anymore, only the place where a door had been, and beyond it the frozen garden breathed its mist against the shelves. [26] The guardian crouched on the top of the card catalog and watched the lamp decide how far it would reach. [46] Every night the light stopped at a slightly different line in the fog, and every night the guardian marked the line with a strip torn from a ledger. [74] It had too many joints for a person and too few for the thing it had been at home, and it had stopped counting the winters. [100] Around its neck hung the pendant the last cultist had worn, a disc of black glass on a chain gone green. [121] The cultists had called it up through a book that no longer existed, had fed it candle smoke and promises, and had said: guard the reading room until we return, and we will open the way back. [158] They had not returned. [162] The pendant was the only thing that still smelled faintly of the place it came from, where light had no edge and inside and outside were the same word. [191]

So the guardian had begun bringing the garden in. [200] Frozen roses stood in the drawers where the catalog cards had been. [212] A birdbath sat on the librarian's desk, its ice unbroken. [222] Mist had been coaxed, armful by armful, into the stacks, until the books wore frost like fur and the aisles were a garden that had learned to read. [250] If it could not go home, it would make the outside come inside until the difference wore thin enough to slip through. [272]

The sleeper came the way sleepers always came, through the veil of sleep and out between the rose stalks, barefoot and unsurprised. [294] She was a woman from the town, the guardian thought, though in dreams people carried their ages loosely. [312] She stopped exactly at the line of ledger paper, where the lamplight gave up. [326]

"You keep moving that," she said. [332]

"The light moves it," the guardian said. [339] "I only write down where." [344]

She had been coming for eleven nights. [351] The first night it had hissed at her from the rafters, all knees and teeth, a feral thing defending a room nobody wanted. [374] She had simply sat down in the frost and waited it out, and waiting was the one language the guardian spoke fluently. [396]

"What are you waiting for?" she asked now, as she asked every night. [409]

"For the door." [412]

"There is no door." [416]

"That is why I wait." [421]

She looked at the pendant. [426] In the fog everything was clear and nothing was certain; her face was sharp, the shelves behind her were sharp, and yet the guardian could not have said whether she was ten feet away or ten years. [463]

"They told you the pendant opens it," she said. [472] "Have you tried?" [475]

The guardian had not. [479] This was the secret it had folded up under all the frost: as long as it did not try, the pendant might still work, and the door might still be somewhere in the mist, and home might still be waiting the way it was waiting. [524] To try was to know, and to know might be to lose. [536] The cultists had been certain of everything, and the guardian had watched certainty eat them one by one until only the pendant was left. [560]

"I do not know if it opens anything," the guardian said, and the words came out frosted, as if they had been kept in the drawers with the roses. [589]

"No," she agreed. [592] "You don't." [594]

She said it the way a person says the weather, and the guardian felt the not-knowing settle beside it on the catalog like a second animal, cold and patient and not unfriendly. [626] It had been trying to bring the garden inside so that inside would stop meaning anything. [642] It had never thought to bring the fog inside itself, to let the edge of the light run through its own chest and simply live on both sides of it. [672]

The guardian climbed down. [676] It crossed the strip of ledger paper for the first time in all its winters and stood in the dark beside her, where it could not see its own hands. [706] It took off the pendant and laid it on the ice of the birdbath, glass on glass. [723]

Nothing opened. [725] Nothing closed, either. [728]

"I will still wait," it told her. [735] "But I will wait the way you sat in the frost, not the way they lit their candles." [753]

The mist was thinning around her; morning was pulling at the veil from the other side. [769]

"Tomorrow, then," she said, already less there. [776]

The guardian did not move the paper. [783] It let the light go where it wanted. [791]
