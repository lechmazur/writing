The terrace had been cut into the mountain before either nation had a name, and it was cracked now in a hundred places, with thyme growing out of the seams. [30] Mara Sel had negotiated on it three times in forty years, and each time the war had resumed before the thyme finished blooming. [53] Tonight was midsummer, and the two wandering stars the Harrow called the Sisters and the Vale called the Twins were closing on each other for the first time since her last failure. [85] Both peoples believed the war, older than any living memory, had begun under such a conjunction, and both believed only such a conjunction could end it. [111] Mara did not believe it, but she had learned that belief was a material, like stone, and could be built with. [132]

She carried the ebony clipboard against her chest as she climbed the last steps. [146] It had been cut from the treaty table that split down its middle twenty-seven years ago, when a Harrow colonel had put his boot through it on hearing that the Vale had taken the corridor she had promised him would stay empty. [188] Nine hundred people had died in that corridor. [196] She had kept a piece of the table because she did not trust herself to remember without something to hold. [216]

The delegations were waiting in two clumps at opposite ends of the terrace, as they always did, twelve on each side. [237] Mara mustered them with small motions, a hand on an elbow here, a nod there, the way one gathers children who have been fighting and are not ready to admit they are tired. [270] Her gentleness was not a temperament. [276] It was a procedure, tested across four decades, the only instrument she had found that could move armed men without making them feel moved. [300]

"The Sisters will touch at the third hour," she said. [310]

"The Twins," said the Vale marshal, out of habit. [319]

"They will touch," Mara said, "and every clause will be signed before they part, or none will be." [337]

She had designed the whole night around this. [345] A treaty signed in a warm room could be renounced in a cold one; a treaty signed under the conjunction their grandmothers had prayed to would cost something to break. [375] She did not need them to believe in the stars. [385] She needed them to believe that the other side believed. [395]

The clauses went slowly. [399] Water rights, the return of the dead, the corridor. [408] Overhead the two lights crept toward each other with the indifference of things that have done this before and will do it again. [431] At the second hour the Harrow colonel's son, a colonel himself now, put down his pen. [447]

"Article nine," he said. [451] "Accountability for the corridor." [455]

"The Vale will not name a soldier," the marshal said. [465] "They were following orders that were following a map." [474]

"Then there is no article nine, and there is no treaty." [485]

Mara had known this hour would come. [492] She had drawn the map. [497] She had assured the Harrow the corridor would be watched, and she had assured the Vale it would be unguarded, and both had been true for exactly as long as it took her to leave the mountain. [534] Nobody had ever written that down. [540]

She unclipped the top sheet and turned the clipboard so both men could see the grain of the wood, the old split running through it like a river. [568] "Your father broke this table," she told the colonel. [577] "He was right to." [581]

Then she wrote her own name into article nine, as the party responsible, and dated it, and signed. [599]

The terrace went quiet enough to hear the thyme moving. [609]

"You will never negotiate again," the marshal said at last. [619] "No side will have you." [624]

"That is the price." [628]

She had turned it over for twenty-seven years, whether a confession that ended her would be worth more than a career that might, someday, do good. [654] But peace was not an idea. [660] It was a thing you had to fasten to the world with nails, and the nails were always somebody. [679] Better her than nine hundred more. [685]

Above them the two lights drew as close as they would ever come and did not quite touch. [703] They never did; that was the flaw in the myth, and everyone on the terrace knew it, and everyone signed anyway. [724]

Mara gathered the pages, squared them against the ebony, and handed the clipboard to the colonel, because it was his father's table and she had carried it long enough. [753] Then she went down the cracked steps in the dark, gently, the way she did everything, and did not look back to see whether it would hold. [780]
