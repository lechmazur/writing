The sanctuary had no sound because sound was the first thing the shifting took. [14] Ilse had learned that on the third day, when the bells in the tower opened their mouths and nothing came out, and the monks wrote on slates that this was mercy. [45] Outside, the hills folded like paper and the river ran backward some mornings. [58] Inside, the walls stayed where they were, mostly, and nobody could hear a fist land. [73]

Her brother Tomas was going to leave. [80] He had told her with his hands, the old village signs, jabbing each word: the man who burned their mother's house was in the valley, and reality or no reality, Tomas would find him. [114]

Ilse did not believe in stopping people with her body. [124] She believed in stopping them with arrangements. [131] So she began to place the music box. [139]

It was a small walnut thing, their mother's, with a brass cylinder whose pins plucked out a lullaby Tomas had not admitted to remembering since he was six. [167] One pin had snapped the night of the fire, when he threw the box against the door frame instead of throwing himself at the man with the torch. [195] That was what he could not forgive, Ilse thought: not the man, but the instant of his own hesitation, and the sister who had held his sleeve and made it longer. [226]

The first morning, the box sat on his bedroll. [235] He put it in the ash bin. [242] By noon it was on the refectory bench beside his bowl, and Ilse, three seats down, ate her bread and looked at nothing. [265] He carried it to the cliff garden and dropped it over. [276]

The cliff, that evening, was a meadow, and the box lay in the grass at his feet with dew on its lid. [298]

Ilse had not done that one. [304] She stood at the garden gate and felt the ground reconsider itself under her, and understood that the shifting was not only taking things. [328] It was returning what was needed, and it did not care who needed it. [342]

Tomas signed at her, furious, one word: Why. [350]

She took his hand, turned it palm up, and set the box in it. [364] Then she wound the key. [369]

Nothing sounded. [371] The sanctuary held its silence like water in a bowl. [381] But the cylinder turned, and the pins rose and fell, and the vibration went through the walnut into his palm, and Ilse watched his face change. [407]

You cannot hear a lullaby with your ears in this place. [418] But a body that was rocked to it for six years hears it elsewhere. [432] His shoulders knew the rhythm before his mind did; his breath slowed to the length of the phrase; his thumb, without permission, began to tap the tune against the lid. [462]

Then the gap came. [466] The place where the broken pin should have plucked and did not. [478]

His thumb tapped the missing note anyway. [485]

Ilse saw him feel it: the tune did not stop at the break. [498] It carried its own shape past the damage and came around again, whole in the body even where the brass had failed. [520]

She picked up a slate. [525] She wrote: You have been holding the broken note for eleven years. [537] Your hands just proved you do not need to. [546]

He read it twice. [550] The meadow beyond the gate became, briefly, a shoreline, then a road going nowhere, then a meadow again. [568]

She wrote more: I schemed this. [574] I put the box in your path four times. [583] The fifth time it came back on its own. [592] I think grace works that way now. [599] It does not stay where it was given. [607] It moves to where the hole is. [614]

Tomas set the box down very carefully, as if it were still capable of breaking, and signed, slowly, so she could not mistake it: You held my sleeve. [642]

Yes, she signed. [645] I would again. [648] I will never fight for you, so I will always cheat for you. [661]

He did not laugh, because there was no laughing here, but his chest moved the way it would have. [680]

He did not leave that night, or the next. [689] The valley, when Ilse checked from the tower, had become a lake, and the man in it, if he was still a man, was past any reaching. [716] She kept that to herself. [721] Some arrangements are best left to the world. [729]

The music box stayed on the sill of Tomas's cell, wound each evening, silent, its broken pin turning past its silence toward the next note. [754] Ilse, who did not believe in noise, thought it the loudest thing in the sanctuary. [769]
