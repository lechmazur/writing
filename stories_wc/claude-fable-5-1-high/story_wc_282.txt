The last of them went under in November, and Teodora Vasse stayed awake. [13] That was the arrangement. [17] Every settlement along the drowned coast kept one waker, and the wakers made maps. [31] Hers were not drawn. [35] They were recorded: the temperature of a wall, the pitch of dripping water, the smell of a passage where the tide had left kelp to rot. [61] A sensory map, the council called it, for people who would come up from hibernation with their eyes weak and their memories thin, needing to learn the cave network by hand and ear before they trusted their sight again. [100]

The council did not know about the other layer. [109] Teodora wore a thermal rig against her sternum and wrists, and the rig read her. [124] Fear pooled heat in the palms. [130] Grief cooled the throat. [134] Awe went to the face like a flush. [142] She let the rig stamp each reading onto the map of the caves, so that a sleeper waking in March would touch the rock at the third bend of the Lantern Passage and feel, through the recording, that someone had been afraid there, and had gone on. [189]

In her coat pocket she carried a stone the size of a coin, with an elm leaf pressed into it. [209] Her mother had put it in her hand the first winter Teodora was old enough to sleep alone in the hibernation hall. [231] Hold this, her mother had said, and you will not need me to be there when you wake. [249] It had worked. [252] That was the thing about such objects: they worked, and then they were supposed to be outgrown. [269] Teodora had never outgrown it. [274] She had only grown into someone who stayed awake. [283]

The caves were factually mystical. [288] She meant the phrase precisely. [293] Surveyors before the floods had measured the deep chambers and found that the sea, pushing air through the narrows, produced a standing tone just under twenty hertz, too low to hear and exactly the frequency that makes a human body feel watched. [335] The ghost of the coast had a number. [343] Knowing the number did not make the ghost leave. [352] Teodora had stood in the Choir Room with her rig flaring hot across both palms and thought: this is what I want to be for them. [378] Not the fear. [381] The thing that answers when the fear asks who is there. [392]

In February she found a chamber the old surveys did not show. [404] The storm tide had opened it, a low arch at the back of the Choir Room, and beyond the arch a pool of black water without any visible far wall. [434] Her rig read her palms at once. [441] She stepped in anyway. [445] The water was warmer than the air, and salt enough that when she leaned back it simply held her. [464] She floated. [466] The tone was strongest here. [471] She felt it in her teeth. [477] For a while the heat in her hands did not fade, and she understood that if she recorded this chamber now, every sleeper who touched the map would feel only that she had been terrified in the dark. [515] So she waited. [518] She let the water take her temperature down until she could not tell where her skin stopped. [535] The fossil in her pocket pressed against her hip, and she thought of her mother saying hold this, and she thought of how a thing meant to carry you across becomes, if you let it, a thing that keeps you on the near shore. [579]

The elm had grown here once, before the sea. [588] A leaf had fallen into mud, and the mud had become stone, and the stone had waited out an age that made human hibernation look like a blink. [616] It had outlasted what it was for. [623] She took it from her pocket and opened her hand beneath the water and let it go. [640] It did not float. [644] It went down through the dark toward whatever floor there was, and she stayed on the surface, light, her palms cooling to the temperature of the sea. [671]

When the rig read calm, she began to record. [680] Not the chamber's dimensions, which she did not know. [689] The tone in her teeth, the warmth of the water, the steadiness of a body that had come to the edge of the map and found it could rest there. [719] In March the sleepers would wake and feel their way down through the passages with their new hands. [737] At the last arch they would touch the record of a stranger floating in the dark, unafraid, and they would hear the low voice of the unknown, and it would sound like someone who had stayed.
