The grid stabilized on a Tuesday, and by Thursday the sodium lamps along Harrow Street had stopped their stuttering and settled into a steady amber that laid long shadows across the cobbles. [32] Ines Varga walked beneath them with a canvas bag against her hip and a summons in her pocket from the Laboratory of Lost Sounds. [56] For eleven years she had been the last prism-song bell ringer in the valley, until the blackouts came and the diocese sold the faceted bells for copper. [83] The bells had been cast with twelve ground faces each, so that a single strike split into a spread of overtones the way glass splits daylight, and pilgrims walking the old mine road used to say they could hear the color of the hour. [127]

The laboratory occupied a former substation, and its director, a narrow man named Teodor Okafor, met her at the door with the eagerness of someone who had waited months for the power to hold long enough to run his machines. [167] Inside, the air smelled of warm dust and solder. [176] Speakers hung from the ceiling like dark fruit, and on every bench lay the relics of vanished noises: a rotary phone, a coal stove grate, a tin of glass marbles. [206]

"We are building the pilgrimage again," Teodor said, "without the road." [217]

He explained it as they walked: the mine road had collapsed in the second winter of the blackouts, and the bells were gone, but the laboratory had recordings, schematics, the exact alloy. [249] They would model the bells in software, project the sound into a darkened hall, and let the city walk seven kilometers on a treadmill floor while the tones grew nearer. [279] An artificial pilgrimage, he called it, and he needed her ear to tell him when the model rang true. [298]

Ines had come because she wanted to know what connection was actually made of. [312] During the dark years she had felt it fray, strand by strand, and she had never learned its texture well enough to say which strand had mattered. [339]

Teodor played the model. [343] The tone bloomed from the speakers, correct in every measurable way, and it was dead. [358]

"It is the bell," Teodor insisted. [364]

"It is a bell in a room," Ines said. [373] "You have assumed the pilgrimage is the sound arriving." [382]

She reached into her canvas bag and set on the bench a small disc of thick glass, scorched at the rim and scratched into a soft haze: the lens from her grandfather's carbide lamp, the only thing he had carried on the road every spring of his life. [430]

"The last two kilometers ran through the old drift tunnel," she said. [442] "No daylight. [444] The miners lit these, and the flame hissed, and the walls dripped, and for twenty minutes you heard nothing but your own breath and the man ahead of you. [473] Then the first overtone came down the shaft, so faint you doubted it. [486] Why do you assume people walked toward the bells? [495] They walked toward the doubt ending." [501]

Teodor was quiet. [504] Then he asked what a carbide flame sounded like, and whether the drip had a pitch, and whether the breath of a stranger ahead in the dark could be recorded without a stranger. [537]

That was the moment the project began to move. [546] Each question Ines put to him knocked out a wall he had not known he had built, and each answer propelled them into the next room. [572] They rebuilt a treadmill floor with wet stone. [580] They tuned the hall so the bells would reach the walkers late and thin, filtered through a modeled shaft of rock, and only clear in the final hundred meters. [609] And Ines, who had struck real bronze for eleven years, showed them how a ringer's stroke is not a blow but a release, powerfully gentle, the rope let go at the instant the clapper wants to fall. [646] Teodor rewrote the strike model by hand until the tone stopped being dead. [659]

On the first public night, the grid held. [667] Forty people walked into the hall, and Ines walked at the back with the carbide lens held up to a single candle, so that a smear of gold trembled over the shoulders of the strangers ahead of her and threw their shadows long across the false stone. [714] The drip fell. [717] Breath went ahead of her in the dark. [725] And when the first faint overtone came down the modeled shaft, she felt forty people lean toward it at once and doubt it together, and she understood at last that this leaning was the texture she had come to find. [765]

Connection was not the bell. [770] It was the dark before it, shared. [777]
