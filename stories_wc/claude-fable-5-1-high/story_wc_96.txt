The moon clears the skyline at nine, and that is when she climbs the fence. [15] Behind it, the old Carnegie library stands roofless, its iron stacks gone the color of dried blood, its reading room open to the stars. [39] This month she is Marigold. [44] Last month she was Tallow, who wrote in short flat lines and never smiled at customers. [60] Before Tallow there was Bishop, before Bishop a woman called Nine. [71] She molts on the first of every month, shedding a name like a snake sheds skin, because the patrols keep files on people who stay the same. [98] A vagrant poet who sells verses for pennies is a nuisance. [109] A vagrant poet with a consistent voice is a movement. [119]

She carries the coffee tin under one arm. [127] It rattles with the day's earnings, forty-one cents and a button. [138] Anyone who took it from her would find pennies and the smell of stale grounds and nothing else, which is the point. [160] Idris built the false bottom. [165]

They met in the prison library at Halvern, sorting donated books into crates marked KEEP and PULP. [182] She was doing three years for sedition, which meant a poem. [193] He was doing life for something he never explained. [202] The guards wanted the KEEP crate small. [209] Idris made it small by hollowing books, sliding thin volumes inside fat ones, tucking Neruda into the spine of a tax manual. [231] Nothing is destroyed if you know where to hide it, he told her, and then he taught her to hide herself. [252] Be a different woman each month, he said. [260] Let them arrest a stranger. [265]

When she got out, the city had come apart. [274] The council had dissolved, the patrols answered to whoever paid them, and the library had been burned in a riot that nobody could name a side for. [301] Halvern's gates stood open, and Idris's cell was empty, and no one could tell her where he had gone. [320] Anarchy, people said, as if the word were a verdict. [330] Marigold, who was Bishop then, saw it differently. [338] Anarchy was raw material. [342] Nobody was guarding the ruins. [347] Nobody was guarding anything. [351]

Inside the library she sets the tin on a scorched table and pries up the false bottom with a thumbnail. [371] Beneath the pennies: a folded page of poems in four different hands, all hers, and a paper twist of seeds. [391] Every persona writes one poem for the tin before she lets it go. [404] Tallow's was about rust. [408] Bishop's was about the sound a crate makes when it closes. [419] She copies each onto a strip of card and slides it into a rusted shelf where the spine of a book would be. [442] Then she scatters the seeds in the ash between the stacks. [453]

It began as a private ritual, one woman rebuilding a library out of pennies and weeds. [469] But the strips of card multiplied faster than she could write them. [481] Customers who paid a penny for a poem in the market began leaving poems of their own in the shelves. [501] Then real books appeared, singed and water-stained, hidden through the burning years in mattresses and ovens. [517] Somebody sorted them. [520] Somebody, she noticed with a catch in her throat, had marked a crate KEEP. [534] And the seeds took. [538] Yarrow and chicory came up through the ash, then poppies, then a blue flower she never planted, so someone else was planting too. [561]

Tonight the moonlight comes down through the burned rafters in bars, and the whole ruin looks like a thing that has been broken and lit from inside. [588] Marigold writes her poem on the scorched table. [596] It is about a man who hollowed books so they would survive, and how she has hollowed herself the same way, and whether anything is left in the center. [625] She does not know the answer. [631] Tomorrow she will be someone new, and that woman will not know either. [644]

But when she goes to shelve the poem she finds a strip of card already in the slot, in handwriting she has not seen for four years. [671] It says: Nothing is destroyed. [676] Beneath it, smaller: Which one are you this month? [685] She laughs, and the sound cracks in the middle, and light gets through the crack. [700] She slides her poem in beside his. [707] Two spines where there were none. [713] Then she puts the false bottom back in the tin, drops the pennies over it, and sits down in the chicory to wait for the next stranger to arrive. [742]
