Every evening Ossian drove the empty coach down from Gloom Hill, though no one had hired him in eleven years. [20] The road was one lane wide and the sea beside it had no edge at all. [36] He kept the horses because they were old and so was he, and because the jetty at the bottom of the hill was too far to walk with a sack of seed pods on his back. [72]

The jetty had been a battlefield once. [79] Two fleets had met in the shallows above the reef, and when the guns were done the reef was white and silent, and the water that had been a garden of color became a gray field of bone. [117] Before the reef died, the two coasts had signed a concord on the jetty's planks, in a language both sides could still read then. [141] The paper went into the sea with the first broadside, and nobody had bothered to write it again. [159]

Ossian could still read it. [164] He could read the drowned tongue of the reef people too, and the merchant cant of the old fleets, and three other languages no living mouth used. [191] A coachman hears everything said behind him and repeats none of it; over sixty years the silence had filled with words. [212]

That was how he had learned the birds. [220] The terns that nested on the jetty pilings spoke the reef tongue, or something close enough that the grammar held. [240] He did not think this was magic. [247] The reef people had lived beside those birds for a thousand years and named the world in their cries; the birds had simply outlasted the naming. [273]

Tonight the twilight came down narrow and enormous, a band of copper pinched between the black hill and the black water. [294] Ossian tied the horses and carried the sack out along the planks. [306] The seed pods inside were dry as knuckles. [314] He had gathered them for eleven years from the graves on Gloom Hill, where the dead of both fleets lay in one long row because there had been no time to sort them. [347] Whatever grew from a grave he let go to seed and kept. [359] Sea-holly, thrift, a bitter vetch. [364] The pods rattled like teeth in a jar. [372]

Already the near end of the jetty bloomed. [380] Where he had worked last spring, thrift had rooted in the silt between the boards and put up pink heads, and something he did not recognize hung purple from a rusted cleat. [412] He had not planted that. [417] The birds had. [420]

He walked to where the flowers stopped and the bare gray planks began, and he waited. [436] The terns came in with the last light, scolding. [445] He listened for the word that meant rich, which in the reef tongue was the same word as buried. [464] The terns used it for water where the dead reef was breaking down and feeding fish; they used it for the pilings where the sea threw up that black rot in drifts. [496]

One bird cried it twice, circling the fourth piling from the end. [508] Ossian went there on his knees. [514] He scraped the rot from between the boards with his hands and it was warm and stank and was exactly what the pods wanted. [538] He pressed them in and covered them and spoke over them, quietly, the closing line of the concord: Let what was divided here be held in common by the ground. [568]

He did not know if the dead reef understood him. [578] He suspected it did not matter. [584] The concord was down there somewhere, pulp and ink under the white coral, and the coral was coming up as rot to feed flowers grown from soldiers, and the soldiers had been buried in one row. [620] The agreement had been kept after all, only by things that could not sign. [634]

When he stood, the tern was still circling, and it cried a word he had not heard it use before. [654] He knew it anyway. [658] In the reef tongue it was what you said when a debt was paid and both parties could go home. [678]

Ossian looked back along the jetty, that one narrow line of flowers laid across a sea with no end, and thought that it was very small and that it did not need to be larger. [713] It only needed to reach the last piling. [721] He had perhaps six springs of pods left and perhaps six springs. [733]

He untied the horses and drove the empty coach back up Gloom Hill in the dark, saying nothing, as a coachman does, with the whole sea talking behind him. [762]
