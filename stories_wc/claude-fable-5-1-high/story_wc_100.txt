Ansel Vey had buried the sea's leavings for thirty-one years, and the sea had never once said thank you. [19] The drowned came up the shingle in every season, nameless, salt-swollen, and he gave them boxes and a hymn and a plot on the hill that faced the water they had come from. [52] It never felt like rest. [57] It felt like filing. [61]

The package arrived on a Tuesday with no stamp and no sender, only his name written in a hand that had learned letters from a tombstone. [87] Inside, wrapped in oilcloth, lay a curved slab of glass as thick as his wrist, ribbed in concentric rings, cracked clean across. [109] He knew it at once. [114] It was a piece of the Harrow Point lens, the lighthouse that had gone dark the night the old town slid into the bay, before Ansel was born. [142] Under the glass was a card: Library. Lower Reading Room. They are still calling. [156]

Ansel did not decide to go. [162] He simply found that he could not put the lens down, and that once a task was in his hands he had never in his life learned how to set it back unfinished. [195] His wife, when she was alive, had called this his one gift and his whole trouble. [211]

He borrowed Tam Odell's diving bell and let the boy lower him at slack tide, the lens in a sack against his chest. [234] The old town rose out of the murk in pieces: a chimney, a church spire wearing a beard of weed, a street that ran downhill into nothing. [261] The library still had its doors. [267]

He pushed through them expecting cold and pressure, and instead stepped into a room where the water stopped at the threshold like a dog told to wait. [294] Dry air. [296] Dust. [297] Shelves that went up farther than the ceiling outside could possibly have allowed, and corridors branching off at angles his eyes refused to hold. [321] The building had not survived the drowning by staying where it was. [333] It had survived by occupying the spaces the sea did not know how to enter. [348]

And in the lower reading room, the dead were reading. [358] Not ghosts, exactly. [361] Shapes in the chairs, patient as fog, each one turned toward a broken lantern on the librarian's desk that flickered in a rhythm Ansel had heard before, tapped out on the hulls of foundering boats. [393] Three short. [395] Three long. [397] Three short. [399]

They had been signaling for a hundred years, and every body that came up the shingle to his table was someone who had heard it and swum toward the light and found only a wreck. [434] He understood, then, what had wearied him all these years. [444] It was not the digging. [449] It was that he had been answering distress calls with dirt. [460]

The lantern's housing was empty where a lens should sit. [470] Ansel unwrapped the glass. [474] The crack ran through it like a river on a map, and he thought of the old town's main street ending in nothing, and of his wife saying, you never know when to stop, Ansel, and of how she had said it as a kindness. [519]

He set the lens in place. [525] It did not fit. [529] He turned it and pressed it and did not stop, because he did not know how, and on the ninth try the glass settled with a sound like a book closing. [560]

The flicker steadied. [563] Three short, three long, three short became one long, unbroken beam, thrown through the cracked lens so that it split into two, and then, along some fold of the room he could not see, into a hundred. [600] A hundred lights, one for every chair. [607] The shapes turned their faces from the lantern to the books in their laps. [621]

A lighthouse says: here is danger, keep away, or, here is help, come. [634] This one, broken and rejoined, said something a lens had not been built to say: you are already here. [653] Stay. [654]

Ansel sat down in the only empty chair. [662] For thirty-one years quiet had run ahead of him, always one grave farther on, and he had chased it up the hill with a spade until his back was a question he no longer asked. [697] Now it sat beside him, breathing dust, and did not move. [708]

He would go up again; Tam was waiting, and the bell had air for an hour. [724] But he would come back down, and he would bring the ledger from his shop, the one with every nameless plot recorded by date and tide. [750] He would shelve it here. [755] Rest, he thought, had wandered long enough with no forwarding address. [766] It could have this one. [771] The library had room, in the places where room could not be. [783]
