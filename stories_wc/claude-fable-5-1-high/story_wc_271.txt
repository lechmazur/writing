After the last lighthouse keeper died, the light kept turning by itself, twelve seconds between sweeps, and Leyla learned to breathe to it. [23] The arboretum had bought his cottage cheap and given it to her with the job: keeper of trees on a cliff where the salt wind killed a third of everything planted. [54] She had taken the job because of that number. [63] A third was honest. [67] Nobody at the interview had promised her that everything would live. [78]

She was, the director said in his quarterly report, quietly talented: her grafts took where others failed, and she could tell a dying pine from a resting one by the way its needles held the light. [114] He did not know how she knew. [121] She knew because her body told her before her mind did, the same way it had told her, four years ago, in the seconds before her cousin's wedding tent became a crater. [153]

She had stepped out for air. [159] That was all. [162] A cold had crept up her forearms, a metal taste had filled her mouth, and she had walked to the edge of the olive grove without knowing why, holding a photograph a child had just handed her. [199] Then the sound, and then no sound at all. [208]

The photograph was still in her pocket now, crumpled to the softness of cloth. [222] Her fist had closed on it in the grove and had not opened for two days. [238] Forty-one faces around a long table, mouths open mid-laugh, a blur where the child's thumb had crossed the lens. [257] She had never tried to smooth it. [264] Flattening it would have been a kind of lie. [273]

One October dusk, after the board approved a canopy survey, a contractor arrived with a case the size of a coffin and unfolded a drone on the lawn above the cliff. [304] Nobody had told her. [308] She was in the cedar terrace with a grafting knife when the rotors started. [322]

The cold came up her forearms first. [329] Then the taste of coins. [334] Then her knees locked and the knife went still against the bark, and she understood, with the part of her that could still understand anything, that she was about to freeze. [365]

The therapist in the city had called them somatic markers. [375] Signals from the body, arriving faster than thought, sorting the world into safe and not safe before the mind could argue. [396] You cannot stop them, she had said. [403] You can only learn to read them, and then decide what they are for. [417]

Leyla read them now. [421] Cold: the body pulling blood inward, toward the heart. [430] Metal: adrenaline, nothing more. [434] Locked knees: not a trap. [439] A root. [441]

She let the freeze come, and instead of fighting it she went looking for its center. [457] There was one. [460] There always had been, a small hard place inside the paralysis where nothing moved and nothing was required to, a pearl of stillness the body had made around the grit of that afternoon. [493] She had spent years mistaking it for the wound. [502] It was the thing that had grown over the wound. [512]

Behind her, the lighthouse turned. [517] Twelve seconds. [519] She counted the sweep across the cedar trunks and breathed out on the dark. [533] The drone passed overhead, small and stupid, whining like a hair dryer, mapping leaf density for a report nobody would read twice. [555] Her knees unlocked when it was gone. [562] She finished the graft. [566]

Afterward the director found her at the cliff rail and apologized, red-faced, and asked if she wanted the survey canceled. [586] She thought about the third that died every year, and the two-thirds that did not. [601] No, she said. [604] Fly it. [606] Just tell me the day before. [612]

That was the whole of what she wanted from the world: not that the sky be empty, which it would never be, but that she be told what was coming, so her body could do its work in time. [651] Hope that needed a clean sky was not hope; it was a wedding tent. [665]

She took the photograph out and did not smooth it. [675] The forty-one faces looked up through their creases, laughing at something she would never hear. [690] The keeper who lived here before her had left one note in the cottage, taped inside a cupboard: The light does not need me. [714] That is not the same as saying nothing does. [723] She had read it a hundred times and only now heard the second sentence. [737]

Below her the sea went on hitting the rock, a third of it thrown back as spray, the rest returning. [757] The light swept over her, and twelve seconds later it came again. [769]
