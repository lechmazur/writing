<story>
Ilse Varga had written four thousand love letters and signed none of them. [13] For twenty-two years she had been the hand behind the city's confessions, the stranger who knew which baker adored which ferryman, which widow still wrote to a name carved on a bench. [45] People came to her because she kept secrets the way a pond keeps the sky: entirely, and without comment. [64] She was, her clients agreed, inevitably whimsical; she delivered drafts folded into paper boats, billed in pressed violets, and once wrote an entire proposal on the inside of a teacup. [94] But the whimsy was a lid. [100] Underneath it she was looking for something, and she had been looking since the summer she was nineteen. [118]

That summer, Teodor had shown her a thrush's nest fallen in the reeds. [131] He turned it in his palms so she could see how the twigs wound outward from the hollow, one spiral, no beginning she could find. [156] "Everything is built like this," he said. [163] "There is a thread that connects all things, and the bird knows it, and we forgot." [179] She kept a single twig from that nest. [187] She kept, later, the fact that he had gone north, and that she had let him. [203]

She kept one more thing. [208] Eleven years ago a letter had arrived through her usual intermediary, a commission from a man who wanted words for a woman he had loved once and lost by his own cowardice. [240] He described her: ink on her wrists, a habit of humming to kettles, a twig she carried in her coat. [260] Ilse read it three times. [265] Then she did what a keeper of secrets does. [274] She wrote his letter, let it be delivered to her own door, and never answered it, because she had never once written for herself and could not find the sentence to begin. [306]

This spring the city commissioned her for a public installation at the lotus temple, the white shrine that rose on its stone stem from the middle of the great lily pond. [337] She went to every living client and asked for one line. [348] Not names, she promised; only the sentence they had most needed said. [360] Almost everyone said yes. [364] Secrets, she discovered, want to be kept only until they can be kept together. [378]

She had the lines fired into glazed tiles and set them floating on cork among the lily pads, arranged so that from the temple stairs they wound outward in a single spiral, a thrush's nest four hundred feet across. [417] At the hollow, on the lowest step, she set the twig in a ring of gold wire and beside it one tile she had written herself. [443]

On opening day the sky tore. [449] The cloudburst came sideways off the hills and hammered the pond white, and Ilse stood under the temple's petal roof certain the whole nest would scatter. [475] It did not. [478] After the cloud burst and emptied itself, the water went still as a held breath. [493] Light came back through the thinning rain in bands, and every wet tile caught a different color, so the spiral lay across the pond like something a prism had confessed. [523]

People came out from under umbrellas and walked the plank paths quietly, reading. [536] No one spoke above a murmur. [542] Ilse walked too, and for the first time read the lines in order, and understood what twenty-two years had been for. [563] The baker's line and the widow's line and the ferryman's line were not four thousand letters. [579] They were one letter. [583] Every one of them, in different hands, was asking the same thing: are you still there? [599]

That was the thread. [603] Not an answer but the question, wound outward from every hollow, from every nest, from every nineteen-year-old who had not known how to write back. [628]

She reached the center as the last light struck it. [638] A man was already there, gray now at the temples, kneeling at the lowest step with the twig in his palms, turning it the way he had turned the nest. [668] He had read the notice in a northern paper. [677] He had come to see a spiral, and found his own eleven-year-old sentence three tiles from the hollow, and understood at last whose hand had written it. [704]

Now he read her tile. [709] It was the first thing she had ever written for herself, and it was only four words long. [727] It read: Still here; come back. [733]

Teodor looked up. [736] Ilse sat down on the wet step beside him, where the spiral began, or ended, and the two of them rejoined the way twigs rejoin in a nest: not by knot, but by winding. [770] Around them the pond kept its colors and its quiet, and, for once, none of her secrets. [787]
