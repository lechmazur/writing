Mara had been on a first-name basis with death for twenty-two years, which is what happens when you sit at enough bedsides at four in the morning. [27] She called it by its plain name and it called her by hers, and neither of them pretended the arrangement was friendship. [49] In the hospice she had kept a notebook of last words, not out of sentiment but out of habit, the way an engineer logs the final readings before a system fails. [80] Most last transmissions, she had noted, were not goodbyes. [89] They were position reports. [93] Water, please, or is it Tuesday, or tell him the car needs oil. [106] Jo's had been a voicemail: I'm at the pier, the water is flat as glass, see you at seven. [125] Then an aneurysm on the dock, and no seven. [134]

Three years later they found Amelia Earhart's Electra in the dark off Nikumaroro, and the world spent a week saying the word closure as if it were a place you could land. [166] Mara watched the footage of the aluminum ribs and felt nothing land. [178] Finding, she wrote in the margin of the notebook, is not the same variable as letting go. [195]

Jo had been an oceanographer, and Jo had wanted one wreck above all others: the Callander, a 1931 liner that went down off the Skerries with a floating lending library on its promenade deck, four thousand volumes nobody had ever bothered to salvage because paper is worth nothing wet. [244] Mara had learned to dive for Jo and had never used it. [256] So in October she went down alone, which every certification says not to do, and which death, who knew her, did not bother to remark on. [282]

The library was a long room tilted fifteen degrees, its brass shelf rails still holding the shapes of books that had become a gray felt of pulp. [309] Her torch made a room of the dark and the dark closed behind it. [323] Ninety years of drift had brought in lost trawl line, monofilament, kelp cord, the frayed lanyards of other people's losses, and it had all knotted itself around the librarian's desk at the far end like a nest. [360] Inside the nest, something caught the light and gave it back. [371]

She could have cut it free in a minute. [380] Instead she braced her fins against the deck and began to untangle it, thread by thread, the way she had once eased tubing from the arms of the dead, because some things you do not cut. [416] Each line she worked loose she followed to its end and let drift, and she noticed, in the quiet analytic part of her that never switched off, that the knot did not fight her. [450] It had only ever been tension held in place by other tension. [462] Remove one and the rest remembered they were free. [471]

What came out of the nest was a snow globe. [481] Some librarian's paperweight, a glass sphere on a lead base, sealed so well the sea had never gotten in, and inside it a tin lighthouse and a fall of white flakes. [512] A sealed world at the bottom of a drowned one. [522] She held it in her glove and watched the snow come down slowly, far more slowly than snow falls in air, and she knew why, because she was the kind of person who knew why. [557] Glycerin. [558] The flakes were not lighter than in any other globe; the medium was thicker. [572]

Nothing in a snow globe ever leaves. [579] The snow does not depart the lighthouse; it settles around it, and the settling is only slow because of what it has to move through. [604] She had spent three years thinking letting go was a subtraction, a thing removed from a hand. [621] It was not. [624] It was terminal velocity in a viscous medium: the same fall as anyone's, only slower, only through grief instead of air, and the fall did not end in absence but in rest. [656]

Earhart's last transmission had been a bearing, one five seven, three three seven, running north and south. [673] Not a farewell. [676] A statement of where she was, sent by someone who fully expected to be found. [691] Flat as glass, see you at seven. [698] Jo had not been leaving either; she had only been reporting her position. [711]

Mara took the slate from her thigh and wrote, in the careful print of a woman who had charted a thousand deaths, eleven fourteen, thirty-one meters, snow settles, does not leave. [742] Then she set the globe back on the librarian's desk, upright, where it had been, and watched the last flakes come to rest around the lighthouse. [768] Death, who was there, kept its own counsel, and Mara began the slow ascent, stopping at every depth required, letting the pressure leave her by degrees. [794]
