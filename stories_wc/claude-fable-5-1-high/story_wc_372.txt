The Aerie Archive stood where the mountain quit and the sky began, and for eleven winters no one but Ines Varga had climbed the ice stair to open it. [29] Below her the clouds lay in a white floor that hid every valley, and above her the library's glass dome held frost on its inside like breath that had forgotten how to leave. [62] She was records chief, which now meant she was the records. [73]

The cold had cracked the stove and silenced the bell, but it had also kept the slides. [90] Four thousand magic lantern slides filled the cedar drawers: squares of glass painted with phosphor pigment, each one a vow spoken by a librarian on the day they joined, then rendered in light so it could be projected and read by everyone who came after. [135] The founders had called them the luminous vows, and had believed that a promise made in light would never quite go dark. [157]

They were wrong, but slowly. [162] Phosphor tires. [164] Each slide, once projected, would hold its image on the wall for a while and then dim, and every year the while grew shorter, until the images lingered less like pictures than like the memory of pictures. [201] Ines had decided, the winter the last of her colleagues descended for good, that she would not let them go unmeasured. [222]

Her method was her own. [227] She set the lantern's flame, slid a vow into the brass gate, and cast it onto the frost-white wall. [246] Then she raised the prism spectroscope, a stubborn instrument she had rebuilt twice, and sampled the frequencies of the glow band by band: the violets that failed first, the greens that held, the ambers that sometimes brightened just before they died. [287] She wrote each reading in the ledger in a hand so small and even that it looked printed. [305] From the readings she could compute how many projections a slide had left, and she rationed them accordingly, the way one rations candles. [328] Only when a vow dipped below the threshold where its words could still be read did she allow herself to transcribe it. [350] It was a rule she kept gently but meticulously, because a vow copied too early became merely a sentence, and a vow copied too late became nothing at all. [379]

The hush in the dome had a quality she had come to think of as quartz: clear, cold, faintly ringing, the silence of a thing that would neither break nor bend. [410]

On the last night of the eleventh winter she reached drawer four thousand, and found a single slide unlabeled. [429] She knew it anyway, by the chip at one corner where she had dropped it, thirty-one years before, on the morning she had spoken her own vow and been too shy to hear it projected. [464] She had filed it and never returned. [471]

Her hands did not shake; she had trained them past that. [482] She warmed the glass against her wrist so the cold would not crack it, set it in the gate, and turned the flame up. [506] The wall bloomed, then steadied, then began at once to fade. [517] It had been waiting too long; there would be one projection, not a ration of them. [533]

She lifted the spectroscope out of habit, sampled the violet, found nothing, sampled the green, found a thread, and understood she was measuring the exact edge of oblivion. [561] So she set the instrument down and read instead. [570] The words were already going to memory as she looked at them, the way a dream goes when you try to tell it. [593]

I will stay until the last light is written down, her younger hand had promised, so that no one who trusted this place will have vanished unremarked. [620]

Then the amber flared, the way amber did, and the wall went white. [633]

Ines sat for a long time in the quartz hush, holding a blank slide. [647] She had not known that she had spent eleven winters keeping a promise she could not remember making. [665] That, she decided, was what the founders had meant. [674] The light did not have to last. [681] It only had to be measured once by someone who would then carry it down the stair. [698]

She opened the ledger to its final page and, in the small even hand, wrote the vow from memory, then added the reading: violet, nil; green, trace; amber, one last brightness. [729]

In the morning she wrapped the ledger in oilcloth, banked the flame, and closed the dome behind her. [747] The clouds below had not moved all winter, and she stepped down into them carrying four thousand and one promises, every one of them dark, every one of them kept. [777]
