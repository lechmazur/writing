The walnut husks had been steeping for nine days, and the jar smelled of iron and rain. [17] Ione strained the black liquid through muslin, gently thorough, pressing until the cloth gave up its last drop. [35] Twenty-two years of the town's record sat in her cabinet, written in this ink, and it had never once faded. [55] Walnut ink darkens with age. [60] That was why she had chosen it. [67]

She did not write the town's history the way the council wanted, with dates and tonnage and the names of drowned men. [89] She wrote through proximate causes. [94] The Kell boy laughed at supper because his father had come home early, because the wind turned, because the barometer on the chandler's wall had dropped at noon. [122] A moment weighed exactly as much as the chain of small things that had carried it into being, and Ione's whole vocation was to keep that weight from leaking away. [152]

Tonight she would need the ink for the jetty. [161] The council had voted that morning. [167] The old timber jetty, rotten at the pilings and furred every June with sea-pinks that no one had planted, would come down in a week. [192] Ione understood what that meant for the record. [200] Everything she had written about the jetty would change its meaning the moment the jetty ceased to exist. [218] Ink does not fade, but context does, and when context changes, every old sentence has to be read again by a stranger. [240]

She walked down at twilight with the jar in her satchel and the locket warm against her sternum. [258] The sea-pinks were fully open, thousands of them, so that the jetty looked less like a structure than like something the harbor had grown on purpose. [284] The light was the color of the ink's first steeping, thin brown going to violet. [299]

Her brother Tam was in the locket. [306] He was eleven in the photograph, squinting, one hand raised as if to block the camera. [322] She had taken it here, on this jetty, on the evening they made their covenant. [337] They had been children who understood, in the way children sometimes do, that most of life gets lost. [355] So they swore: one small thing, every day, written down, forever. [366] Tam had lasted four years before the sea took him off this same planking in a storm nobody had predicted. [386] Ione had lasted twenty-two. [390]

She sat on the last sound plank and opened the locket. [401] The photograph had yellowed, and in the failing light she saw for the first time that Tam's raised hand was not blocking anything. [424] He was pointing past her, at the pilings, at the first sea-pinks that had ever bloomed there. [441] That was the proximate cause of the covenant. [449] Not grief, not precocious wisdom, but a boy noticing flowers where no flowers should be and wanting his sister to see. [470] She had spent two decades misreading the one image she carried closest. [482] Something rose in her chest that was not quite laughter and not quite the other thing. [498]

Footsteps on the planks. [502] Mara Kell, nine years old, who had been leaving walnut husks on Ione's step all spring without explanation, stood at the edge of the blooms with a stub of pencil and a torn ledger page. [537]

"My dad says they're taking it down," Mara said. [546] "I wanted to write what it looked like." [554]

"Pencil fades," Ione said, and heard how much it sounded like Tam. [566] She uncorked the jar. [570]

The girl came and sat, and Ione showed her how to dip, how to let the ink draw up the fibers, how to write the flower before writing the fact of the jetty, because the flower came first and the record should know that. [614]

"Why walnut?" Mara asked. [618]

"Because it gets darker the longer it lives," Ione said. [628] "Most things go the other way." [634]

The sea-pinks were closing with the light. [641] Ione took the locket off, which she had not done since the funeral, and pressed it into the girl's palm. [661]

"One small thing," she said. [666] "Every day, written down." [670]

Mara looked at the boy in the photograph pointing at flowers, and then at the flowers, and understood the sentence the way children sometimes do. [695]

"Forever?" she asked. [698]

"Until the context changes," Ione said. [704] "Then someone reads it again." [709]

The girl wrote, and the ink went down brown and would go down black, and behind them the jetty held for one more night under its impossible weight of blooms. [739] Ione felt Tam's hand on her shoulder as clearly as if he had been there, which he had, which he was, which was the whole point of ink. [767]
