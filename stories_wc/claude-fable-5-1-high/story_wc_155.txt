Ilse had been famous, briefly, for a wall in the harbor quarter that looked like it was on fire at noon and underwater by dusk. [25] Now she painted bank lobbies in colors chosen by committee, and the committee always chose beige. [41] On her desk sat the only thing she still liked to look at: a fresco fragment no larger than her palm, a chip of plaster bearing half an apricot and one blue leaf. [74] A dealer had sworn it came from the orchard district, the part of the city that had walked into the fog three centuries ago and never walked back out. [103] Ilse rubbed the leaf with her thumb, the way you rub a coin for luck, and somewhere between one blink and the next the beige lobby folded shut like a book. [134]

She was standing in fog. [139] It was not the thin gray stuff that loitered around the harbor; it was fog with opinions, fog you could lean on, fog that had been accumulating for so long it had grown a kind of weather inside itself. [178] Trees stood in it, or the memory of trees: black trunks split like old boots, branches holding nothing. [196] Walls ran between them, low and crumbling, and every wall wept. [207]

"You can't paint here," she said aloud, to nobody, because she was a professional and it was the first thing a professional would notice. [231] Pigment would bead and slide. [236] Plaster would drink the damp and slump. [243]

"Nobody does," said a voice, and a woman in a keeper's oilcloth coat stepped out of a trunk that was apparently a doorway. [266] She looked like Ilse's grandmother and also somewhat like Ilse, which is how daydreams economize. [281] "We stopped painting." [284] "We started exposing." [287]

The keeper led her to a shed roofed in slate, where canisters lined the shelves like preserves. [304] Film, all of it, and all of it exposed, ruined by three hundred years of light leaking through the fog. [324]

"Ruined is one word," the keeper said. [331] "We had a hundred keepers here once, every one with a house and a lantern." [346] "When the fog came, we couldn't see each other's trees, so we signaled: this graft took, that root rotted, prune the north side." [369] "Every lantern flash went into the fog, and the fog, being wet and full of silver salts nobody understood, kept it." [390] "Then it leaked into the film, and now the film holds the whole network, everything a hundred keepers ever knew, if you have the patience to read a smear." [419]

Ilse laughed, which surprised her; it was a rusty sound, like a gate nobody had opened in a while. [438] She held a strip to the shed's one lamp and saw only fog-colored fog. [452] Then she did the thing a muralist does, which is to think about scale. [466]

The shed had a projector, brass and stubborn, because of course it did. [479] She threaded the film, aimed the lens at the thickest bank of fog between two dead trees, and lit the lamp. [500] Nothing. [501] A gray rectangle on gray. [506]

"It needs a key," the keeper said, the way people say things in dreams when the dreamer is being slow. [526]

Ilse found the fresco fragment in her coat pocket, where it had no business being, and slid it into the light between lens and fog. [551] Half an apricot and one blue leaf, thrown forty feet high. [562]

The fog recognized it. [566] That was the only way to describe what happened: the smears on the film arranged themselves around the fragment like iron filings around a magnet, and the other half of the apricot appeared, then the branch, then the tree, then the row, then a hundred lanterns blinking prune, graft, water, wait. [617] The whole district hung in the air in bloom, pink and white and impossibly loud with bees, and it was not a photograph and not a painting but the thing she had been trying to make on the harbor wall before the committees found her. [662]

"You paint with light here," the keeper said. [670] "It's the only thing that doesn't run." [677]

Ilse stood under her own display with her mouth open, feeling something she had misplaced so long ago she had stopped filing reports about it. [702] It was joy, and it was ridiculous, and it was forty feet tall. [715]

The lobby unfolded. [718] A committee member was asking whether the beige could be warmer. [729] Ilse looked at the fragment in her hand, at the blue leaf, and thought about the shed full of exposed film and a hundred keepers signaling through the dark to people they could not see, on the theory that someone was watching. [771]

"It could be pink," she said. [777] "With bees." [779]
