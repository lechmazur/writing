The fever broke a little before four, in the hour when the station's instruments were loudest and nothing else was. [20] Ilse lay under three wool blankets and listened to the anemometer tick over on the roof, counting wind the way she had been counting her own pulse for two days. [50] Through the window the sky above the ridge had begun to move, green folding into green, the aurora working its slow hands over the glacier. [75] She had been peripherally aware of it all night, the way one is aware of a person standing at a door. [96] She had also been aware of Brandt. [103] He had sat by her cot with a cold cloth and, when he thought she was asleep, he had talked. [123]

Everyone at the observatory in the valley called Brandt the Oracle. [134] He had forecast the mountain for thirty-one years and had never once been wrong, which was less a miracle than a method. [156] He never said the front would arrive at noon. [165] He said between ten and two, likely, with a chance of stalling on the western face. [181] He built error into every sentence and lived inside it. [191] A fault-tolerant oracle, the young meteorologists joked, a system with so much redundancy it could not fail because it had never committed to anything. [215] Ilse had laughed with them once. [221] Now, weak and clear-headed, she got up. [228]

The logbooks stood on the shelf above the barograph, cloth spines faded from red to the color of old brick. [248] She took down the volume for the year he had mentioned in the dark, the year she was born. [267] The pages were dense with his hand: pressure, humidity, wind, cloud base, all of it hedged, all of it bracketed with likely and probable and not excluded. [294] In the margins, though, there was something else. [302] A flower. [304] Purple saxifrage, drawn in pencil, five petals and a cushion of leaves, rendered with the patience of a man who has looked at a thing for a long time. [333] She turned the page and found another, and another. [342] The botanical illustration repeated through the whole year, always in the margin, never touching the data, as if the numbers were a country the flower was not permitted to enter. [372]

She found the entry for the sixteenth of June. [381] It was the only forecast in the book without a margin. [392] Clear through the afternoon. [396] Winds light. [398] No qualification, no likely, no bracket. [404] Below it, in a different pressure of the same hand, a name: Mari. [417] And beside the name, the first of the saxifrages, small and unsure, the petals not yet learned. [434]

Ilse touched the drawing and her thumb, still damp from the fever, smudged the graphite so that the flower blurred at its edge into the white of the page. [463] She pulled her hand back as if she had hurt it. [474]

In the dark he had said: I told her it would hold. [486] He had said it several times, to no one, to the cold cloth, to the aurora. [502] Ilse had swallowed the words the way she swallowed the tea he brought, without tasting, because she was too sick to do otherwise. [525] Now she wanted to know how much they weighed. [534] Not the facts; those were easy to reconstruct. [542] A woman on the western face, a forecast without error bars, a front that arrived early and did not stall. [562] She wanted the weight of it, the thing he had carried up and down this mountain for thirty-one years while she and the others joked that he was afraid to be wrong. [594]

He had not been afraid to be wrong. [602] He had been wrong once, absolutely, and had understood that certainty was a cliff with nothing below it. [620] So he had moved into the margins and occupied them ever since. [632] He forecast from there, and he drew from there, and the flower that grew where Mari had fallen he kept in the one place where numbers could not reach it. [662]

The door opened. [665] Brandt stood with two cups, his face gray with the night. [676] He saw the book, the page, the smudge, and his certainties, which had never swayed in public in three decades, swayed. [697] Ilse said nothing about the drawing. [703] She said, "It broke around four." [709] He nodded and set down her cup. [716] Then he took the pencil from behind his ear and, with his own thumb, softened the smudge she had made until it looked like weather. [741] Outside, the green light was thinning into dawn, and the ridge stood out against it, sharp, entirely without margin, the way only a thing that has already happened can afford to be. [773]
