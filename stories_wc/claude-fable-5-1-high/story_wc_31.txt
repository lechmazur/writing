Orrin Vale lit the pier lamps at dusk and put them out at dawn, and in between he told anyone who asked exactly what he was doing. [27] "Checking the mantles," he would say, and it was true. [37] The truth made a good wall; nobody looked behind it. [47] Behind it was Ilse. [51]

She had heard in colors. [56] Gulls were rust, she said, the foghorn a bruised violet, the tide against the pilings a slow grey-green. [74] For thirty years he asked her the color of his own voice, and for thirty years she smiled and said she would show him, not tell him, because some things could only signify, never explain. [109] Then she was gone, and in her coat pocket he found the brass skeleton key tag from the Halcyon Hotel, where she had kept the night desk when they met, and on its back she had scratched two words: the library. [150]

The Halcyon was where they had married, during a power failure. [161] With the ballroom black, he had run out and lit the pier lamps himself, and they said their vows on the boards with the whole row burning behind them. [190] Luminous vows, she called them afterward. [196] She said his voice had matched the lamps exactly. [205] He had thought it was a figure of speech. [214] The key itself was long lost; she had kept the tag because a skeleton key opened every room, and that, she said, was the only thing worth carrying a reminder of. [245]

The library was harder. [249] The town had no library anymore; the old one had been a ship, the Vireo, a steamer fitted with shelves that carried books between the islands until a storm put her down in the harbor in the year Ilse was nine. [290] She had read aboard her as a girl, every evening the ship was in port, and never said why she stopped talking about it. [314] Most people had forgotten the Vireo was there. [322] Once a year, on the night of the lowest tide, when the town gathered on the shore to release lanterns for its drowned, her upper deck broke the surface for perhaps an hour. [355] Tonight was that night. [359]

Orrin lit the last lamp at the pier's end and told the harbormaster he was going to walk the shallows and inspect the pilings. [383] True. [384] Then he climbed down the ladder into water that reached his knees, the tag closed in his fist, and began to wander. [406] He did not hurry. [410] Ilse had taught him that the way to find something is to let your feet keep the purpose while your eyes forget it. [433] So he wandered, purposefully, through a low forest of pilings toward the dark hump of the wreck, and behind him the first lanterns went up. [458]

They rose without sound. [462] Hundreds of small fires lifting off the beach and drifting out over the harbor, each one a name, each one meant to signify a person the sea had taken. [491] No one spoke. [494] The whole town stood with faces turned upward in a hush like the one that falls when a meteor crosses and everyone waits to see if it will happen again. [524]

The Vireo's door stood open where the storm had left it. [535] Inside, the water was to his waist, and the shelves ran the length of the hull, swollen books fused into soft grey bricks, spines unreadable. [560] Nothing was locked. [563] Nothing here had been locked in fifty years, and he understood, standing in it, why she had left him the tag and not the key. [588] The door had never been the problem. [595] He had. [597]

He said her name. [601] The water answered. [604] Where his voice went out across it, the surface woke in a pale green fire, a glow lifting from the flooded stacks the way heat lifts from a road. [633] Plankton, the practical part of him said; the shallows were thick with it this time of year, and it lit when disturbed. [655] He said her name again, louder, and the glow bloomed brighter, the exact green-gold of the pier lamps behind him, of the lanterns overhead, of a row of flames on the night they were married. [690]

So this was where a nine-year-old girl, reading late while the ship's bell rang, had first seen sound turn to light, and had spent the rest of her life hearing it that way. [723] She had not been speaking in figures. [730] She had been telling the truth, and letting him hide behind it until he was ready to walk around. [749]

He stood in the ruined library while the town's lanterns crossed the sky in silence, and he spoke to her, quietly, for the whole of the hour the tide allowed, watching every word he said turn to light. [787]
