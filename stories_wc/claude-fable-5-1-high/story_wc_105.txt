Ilse had always heard colors faintly, the way you hear a neighbor's radio through a wall. [16] Red was a low hum. [21] Aurora green was a whine so thin it made her back teeth ache, and it was whining now over the headland where her father's forge stood ashen and cold, its chimney a black finger under the rippling sky. [59]

She designed hearing aids for a living, small patient machines that gave people back the world one frequency at a time. [80] Her father had been her first client, deafened by forty years of hammer on anvil. [95] He had also been her first lie. [102]

For twenty years he told her that her mother had walked into the sea. [116] Last spring a letter arrived from a woman in Tromso, alive, asking after a daughter she had been forbidden to write to. [138] Ilse remembered the moment exactly, and yet she could not remember its sound. [151] The paper had rustled, the kettle had ticked, and somewhere between those two ordinary noises something inside her had given way without a single decibel to mark it. [179] That silence bothered her more than the lie. [187]

Now she was eleven weeks pregnant, and the clinic pamphlet said the cells were dividing faster than at any other point in a human life. [212] She wanted to know what trust breaking sounded like before someone new arrived who would trust her completely. [230] If she could hear it, she could recognize it, and if she could recognize it, perhaps she could stop herself from making it. [253]

So she went collecting absences. [258]

Below the headland lay the old orchard greenhouse, sunk in a hollow that the sea had claimed and half returned, its glass roof standing just above the tide line. [287] Around it the town had dragged its wrecks for a hundred years, trawlers and one iron freighter, a ring of rusted hulls that broke the wind and kept the apple trees alive long after the orchard's owners had drowned or left. [328] Ilse waded in with her recorder and held it up to the gaps. [341] Not the drip from the broken panes but the pause between drips. [353] Not the wind in the freighter's ribs but the moment it stopped and the hull held its breath. [371] The hush where bees had worked the blossoms before the salt killed the hives. [385] Each absence had a faint color to her, a bruised violet, a gray like wet ash, and she catalogued them by that. [407]

By the twelfth week she had forty-one silences and a theory. [418] Trust did not break with a crack. [425] It broke the way the orchard had broken, by subtraction, one bee at a time, until you looked up and the noise you had relied upon was simply not there. [455]

She layered the absences in her workshop, stacking gap upon gap until the file was nearly two minutes of nothing that somehow had a shape. [480] Then she took a new hearing aid, the finest she had ever built, and imprinted the shape onto its memory as a permanent low layer beneath the ordinary world. [509]

She climbed the headland at dusk. [515] Her father sat in the doorway of the forge, a small man beside a large dead thing, the anvil furred with ash, the aurora unrolling green above the chimney. [544] "You hear that?" she asked, pointing up, and he shook his head, because he had never heard the whine she heard, only seen the light. [569]

She fitted the new aid into his ear herself, her fingers remembering the exact fold of it from twenty years before. [590] For a while he heard only the wind. [598] Then his face changed, slowly, the way the sky was changing, and she knew the layer had reached him. [617]

"It's quiet," he said, and his voice cracked on it. [627] "It's the quietest thing I ever heard." [634]

"That's what it sounded like," she said. [641] "When I read the letter." [646]

He did not ask her to take it out. [655] He sat with the absence of her mother in one ear and the wind in the other, and above them the aurora hummed its thin green note that only Ilse could hear, and inside her the cells went on dividing, indifferent and miraculous, building someone who would one day be handed a memory and have to decide what to make of it. [717]

She thought of the greenhouse, battered and standing, apples still ripening behind glass inside a ring of wrecks. [735] It was not a forgiving place. [741] It was only a place that had learned what to keep. [752]

"Write to her," her father said at last, not looking at Ilse, looking at the sky he could not hear. [772]

"I will," she said, and for the first time in a year the word between them made a sound. [791]
