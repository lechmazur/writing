The amphitheater breathed twice a day, and Orla had learned to set the gates on the exhale. [17] At dusk the dormant crater cooled, the basalt tiers contracted, and warm air sighed up through the cracked vomitoria like a sleeper turning over. [41] In that slackening, the iron sluices beneath the stage could be moved by one person with a wrench and patience. [61] By noon the stone drank the sun back in and locked everything tight. [74]

She was the floodgate machinist, the third since Teodor built the works, and the lake that filled the caldera each spring answered to her alone. [99] Tonight two delegations waited on the upper tiers, not speaking to each other. [112] Below the crater, the village of Hesk needed the lake drained fast; the snowmelt had come early and the reservoir wall was weeping. [135] Open the gates past forty links, said the council, and let the water go. [149] The heritage keepers said what they always said: past forty links, the lower tiers flooded, and the lower tiers held the last intact carvings, the ones that made the building sing. [180]

Orla measured gates the way Teodor had taught, with his antique surveyor's chain, sixty-six feet of hand-forged links, one hundred exactly. [201] Every setting in the works was written in links, because he trusted the chain more than any gauge. [219] His letter, the one he left her, said: Never past forty. [230] No reason, no farewell, the handwriting as square as a survey bench. [242] The village remembered him as a man who loved iron and stone and nobody. [256]

She had believed that too, until this afternoon. [264]

She had taken the envelope from its tin to read the line again, and her thumb had caught on the flap, where a rind of old adhesive remained. [292] Teodor never used the cheap gum from the post office. [302] She scraped a flake into warm water and it dissolved amber and smelled of pine: his own resin, the tack he used to mark link positions on the chain. [331] Under her loupe, the rest of the seal was dented in a row, tiny ovals pressed into the resin while it was soft. [354] Chain links. [356] She counted them twice: fifty-two. [361]

Fifty-two links drowned the carvings and saved Hesk in a single night. [373] He had written forty on the page for the keepers, who would read it, and pressed fifty-two into the glue for the machinist, who would feel it. [400] It was the slyest tenderness she had ever encountered, a man too proud to say he loved the village, hiding permission where only calloused hands would find it. [428]

Now the stone exhaled around her, and the chain trembled as she paid it out across the stage floor, link by link, toward the gate wheel. [454] Or her hands did; in the half-dark she could not tell which. [466] Light came down through the fractured dome in coins and slivers, catching one carved face, then another, then the black water lapping at the orchestra pit. [492] Forty for the stone. [496] Fifty-two for the people. [500] Teodor had given her both numbers and left the choosing to her; she decided that was respect. [517]

But she was the third machinist, not the first, and she had spent eleven years listening to this building breathe. [537] The gates were stiff by day and slack by night. [547] Nobody had ever asked what would happen if she stopped fighting that. [559]

She set the wheel to fifty-two and did not lock it. [570] Instead she ran the chain from the wheel to the expansion joint in the proscenium wall, where the stone moved a full hand's width between noon and midnight, and shackled it there. [602] When the crater cooled and exhaled, the joint would open and the chain would drag the gates wide; Hesk would drink all night. [625] When the sun returned and the stone inhaled, the chain would go slack and the gates would ease back toward forty, and the water would climb only to the lowest step of the carvings, no further, every day, for as long as the mountain kept breathing. [671]

It would not please anyone completely. [677] The reservoir would fill more slowly than the council wanted; the lowest carvings would wear more quickly than the keepers wanted. [698] But the building would keep both promises at once, one on each breath. [711]

She stood back and watched the chain go taut in the dark as the amphitheater sighed, and the gates groaned open, and the first rush of water went down the old lava channel toward the village lights. [748] Above her the two delegations had begun, cautiously, to talk. [758] Orla found the envelope in her pocket and pressed her thumb against the seal, over the fifty-two small dents, the way you might press a hand you could not shake. [788]
