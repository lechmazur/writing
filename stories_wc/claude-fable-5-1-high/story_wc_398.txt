The last bus pulled out of Vardel station with every window lit and no one behind the glass, and Mara did not run after it. [25] She had meant to. [29] Her bag was packed and the ticket sat warm in her coat pocket. [42] Instead she stood on the platform until the taillights folded into the hills, and the station became only a bench, a timetable, and the smell of diesel thinning into the smell of leaves. [75]

In her fist was the corner of a letter. [84] She had burned the rest of it the night before, over the sink, and pulled this piece back at the last second, scorched brown along one edge, holding maybe forty words of Anton's hand. [118] She could not have said why she saved it. [127] She could not have said why she had not boarded. [137]

Above the village the vineyard terraces climbed toward a single light, and she climbed toward it because it was the only thing awake. [160] The light belonged to the old harvest pavilion, a wooden octagon with panes of ancient bottle glass that took a lantern's flame and gave back amber. [186] Inside sat Iosif, who had been the vineyard's lone sentinel for more autumns than Mara had been alive, guarding the last ripe rows from boar and starlings and the first killing frost. [218]

He did not seem surprised to see her. [226] He poured a second cup from his thermos and nodded at the bench. [239] "You are Anton's Mara," he said. [245] "I was," she said, and sat. [251]

Beyond the glass the vines were losing their leaves in the wind, gold and rust, the color of the pavilion itself. [272] She realized she was still holding the burned edge in the open and closed her hand over it, too late. [292] Iosif looked at her fist, then at the vines. [301]

"You know these are not one plant," he said. [310] "Every vine on this hill is two. [317] The roots are American, from the other side of the ocean, and they were never meant to carry a European grape. [338] Wrong soil, wrong blood, wrong century. [344] A hundred years ago a blight killed everything that grew on its own roots, and men were forced to cut two strangers and bind them at the wound." [372] He drank. [374] "The join never disappears. [378] You can feel it under the bark like a knuckle. [388] But the vine only lives because of it." [396]

"Anton would say we are the blight," Mara said. [405] "He wrote that we were two seasons that never share a month." [417] "He wrote that to you?" [422] "I burned it." [425] She opened her hand. [429] "Most of it." [432]

Iosif did not reach for the scrap. [439] He waited, the way he waited for frost, until she smoothed it on her knee and read what the fire had left. [461] Never share a month, the letter said, and below that, half eaten by the scorch: and yet I keep looking for you in every calendar. [486] She had not read that far the night before. [495] She had seen the first line and struck the match. [505]

"Two seasons," Iosif said gently, as if turning the words to the light. [518] "Autumn and spring do not share a month either. [527] But look what the vine does. [533] It spends spring becoming the thing that autumn will understand." [543] He tipped his head at the terraces. [550] "I stand here every night for a fruit I will not taste until it is wine, and by then someone else will drink it. [574] That is not a mismatch. [579] That is only two people arriving at the same table at different hours." [592]

Mara traced the scorched edge with her thumb. [600] The heat had curled the paper the way a leaf curls, inward, protecting whatever was still soft at the center. [620] For a year she had read every word Anton wrote as a verdict. [633] Now, in the amber, the same words were a question, and it was the question she had refused to hear: not whether they fit, but whether she was willing to know how the world looked from inside his season. [672]

"He wanted me to understand him," she said. [680] "Not to agree." [683] "That is the graft," Iosif said. [689] "Not agreement. [691] Only two living things learning to pass water back and forth through a scar." [705]

He turned back to the window, watch resumed, and did not press her further. [719] He had said what the vines had taught him and left her to do the rest. [735]

She sat with him until the lantern burned low and the first grey showed behind the hills. [752] The morning bus would come at six. [759] She would take it, she decided; she would still go. [769] But she took the pencil from Iosif's ledger and, on the unburned side of the scrap, began to write back. [789]
