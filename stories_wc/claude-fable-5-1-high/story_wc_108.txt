Mara Quill had a phobia for every element the studio could throw her off of. [15] Heights, fire, deep water, tight spaces: she catalogued them the way other people collected stamps. [30] It was why she was good. [36] A fearless double stops thinking; a terrified one never does. [46] Her coordinator called her firmly adaptive, which she took to mean she bent around her fear like water around a stone and kept going. [70]

For eleven years she had been the body of Celeste Marrow, the star who "did her own stunts." [88] Mara never minded the lie. [93] Then, on the last week of the shoot in a drowned fishing town, she found the insurance file. [111] Every fall, every burn, every plunge was logged under Celeste's name, sworn and notarized. [125] Mara's name was nowhere. [129] Not misfiled. [131] Absent. [132] Eleven years of her body, and legally she had never been inside any of it. [147] She was not a stunt double. [153] She was an erasure. [157]

She needed somewhere to think, so she went to the library. [168] The town's library was a corrugated-iron shed the sea had been eating for decades, roof rusted through, shelves empty, and at night the stars came in through the holes like they had a card. [202] She sat on the cold floor with two things in her lap. [214]

The first was half a seashell that Celeste's daughter, Pip, had pressed into her hand that morning. [231] "I kept the other half," Pip had said, "so it whispers to you." [244] Held to her ear, it hissed with the sound of the ocean, which was the sound of her own blood, which was the sound she heard before every water stunt. [274]

The second was Pip's cast, sawn off that afternoon after six weeks, its plaster crowded with crew signatures and, between them, rows of tiny smudged letters in Pip's hand. [303] Pip loved ciphers. [306] She had spent the summer making Mara decode lunch orders. [316] Mara had assumed the cast was more of the same. [326]

Under starlight, she worked through it. [332] The key was Pip's usual: shift every letter by the number of people who had signed that day. [350] Salt water had blurred the plaster, so Mara guessed at the gaps, adapting, filling in from context the way she filled in a fall when a rig failed. [378]

MOM SAYS MARA WILL FREEZE. [383] DOC CREW FILMS IT. [387] MOM DOES THE JUMP AFTER. [392] DONT TELL. [394]

Mara read it three times. [399] The final stunt was a forty-foot drop into black water, and a documentary crew was arriving to shoot "Celeste: Behind the Fall." [421] Celeste knew about the water. [426] Celeste had watched her shake in the wetsuit for eleven years and said nothing, and now the shaking would be the footage: the fraudulent double panicking, the brave star stepping in, a career remade in one afternoon out of Mara's worst moment. [468]

The shell hissed against her palm. [474] She waited to feel destroyed. [479] Instead something rearranged itself, quietly, like a bone setting. [488] Celeste had built a plan on the belief that Mara's fear was a weakness to be exposed. [505] But Mara had lived inside that fear for eleven years, and she knew its architecture better than anyone alive. [524] You cannot expose what someone is already holding up to the light. [536]

Morning. [537] The documentary cameras were already up on the seawall when Mara walked out in her wetsuit, not toward the rig, but toward the lenses. [561] Celeste, in an identical suit, went very still. [569]

"My name is Mara Quill," Mara said, and her voice shook, and she let it. [584] "I am the body in every stunt Celeste Marrow has ever been credited with. [598] I am afraid of heights. [603] I am afraid of deep water. [609] I am going to do this anyway, and I want you to film my hands." [624]

She held them up. [628] They trembled like a plucked wire. [634] Then she climbed. [637]

The drop took under two seconds and lasted, inside her, a full lifetime; the water closed like a fist; the ocean roared in her ears exactly as the shell had promised. [668] She surfaced. [670] The cameras were still on her, not on Celeste, because there was no story left for Celeste to step into. [690] A double who freezes is a scandal. [697] A double who names her terror and jumps into it anyway is the thing people had come to film. [716]

On the seawall Pip was waving, her thin healed arm high over her head, the other half of the shell white in her fist. [740] Mara raised hers back. [744] For eleven years she had been erased by pretending she was not afraid. [757] Trembling, certain, she had written herself back in with the one thing she had been told to hide. [775]
