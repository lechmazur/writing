The salvage crews still called it Maren's Atlas, though Ilse had drawn two-thirds of it herself. [16] She was a corridor echo cartographer, as her grandmother had been, walking the drowned districts with a clapper board and a notebook, listening for what the walls gave back. [45] After the climate shifts, sound had become the only honest surveyor. [56] Paper plans had rotted, satellites had gone dark, and the buildings themselves had swollen and sagged with forty years of wet heat. [78] A clap in a hallway told Ilse where the floor was hollow, where a stairwell had collapsed, where copper pipe still ran unbroken behind plaster. [103] The crews paid for that last kind of knowledge most of all. [115]

She came to Maren's house in the hills to close it, finally, and found the fireplace full of diaries. [134] Half-burned, most of them, their spines curled black, the fire abandoned before it finished. [148] Ilse knelt in the ash and lifted them out one by one. [160] Her grandmother's hand was small and slanted, and the surviving pages did not read like the woman on the plaques. [180] I have taught them to hear the buildings so they can strip them faster, one page said. [197] Another, scorched to a corner: every echo is a wall answering a question nobody asked it. [213] And then, three times across two volumes, underlined until the pencil tore: what do walls dream about? [230]

Ilse sat back on her heels. [236] She had spent eleven years being Maren's continuation, correcting Maren's margins, accepting Maren's commissions, and the question felt like a door in a house she thought she had fully mapped. [266]

Tucked in the gutter of the last diary was a strip of film negative, six frames, brittle as a dry leaf. [287] She carried it to the window and held it against the white sky. [300] In reversed light she could glimpse a passage swallowed in ivy, the leaves pale as bone, the shadows glowing. [319] A woman stood at its mouth with a clapper board. [329] Beside her, a child, hands pressed flat to the wall as if feeling for a pulse. [345] Ilse did not remember the day, but she recognized the passage. [356] It was in the old textile quarter, and in the Atlas it was marked as solid fill, unremarkable, not worth a crew's afternoon. [379]

She went the next morning. [384] The ivy had thickened in the decades since the photograph, a green curtain she had to part with both arms, and the air behind it was cool for the first time in her memory. [418] She stood where Maren had stood. [424] She clapped once. [427]

Most people heard an echo as one sound. [435] Ilse had always heard it as several, arriving in order, each surface confessing its distance and its material, brick blunt and plaster bright and water a soft smear underneath. [464] It was the gift that had made her useful, and she had never once used it for herself. [482]

The passage answered late. [486] Far too late for solid fill. [492] The sound went in and kept going, softened by leaves, and came back carrying the shape of a long vaulted room, a floor of standing water, the hollow hum of nothing having been taken. [526] Maren had heard this. [530] Maren, who could hear a nail behind three inches of lath, had heard this and written down a lie. [549]

Ilse walked in. [552] Ivy had come through the roof in ropes and rooted in the silt, and where it touched the walls the plaster had not crumbled but gone soft and dark, like something asleep. [584] No one had pried the pipe from these walls. [593] No one had catalogued them into parts. [600] She pressed her hands flat against the nearest one, the way the child in the negative had, and stood very still, and listened the way she never let herself listen on a job. [633] Water ticking somewhere. [636] Leaves shifting. [638] The slow creak of a structure settling into a shape no one had drawn for it. [654]

She understood then that her grandmother had not been asking a mystic's question. [667] Walls dreamed of exactly this: of being left, of being grown into, of answering no one. [683] Maren had spent her life teaching the city to interrogate its ruins, and at the end she had kept one place off the map so that one place could sleep. [713] Then she had tried to burn the evidence of her own doubt and could not finish that either. [731] The Atlas was Ilse's inheritance, and so was the lie inside it, and so, she saw now, was the choice. [751]

Back outside, she took the notebook from her bag and found the page for the textile quarter. [768] Solid fill, it said, in Maren's slanted hand. [776] Ilse initialed the entry, as she did every survey she confirmed, and let the ivy fall closed behind her. [795]
