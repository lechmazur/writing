The carousel stopped on a Tuesday, or in the third year, or this morning. [14] Ines cannot make the numbers hold still. [21] What she knows is this: when the painted horses quit turning, the lagoon quit too. [36] The tide engines under the platform had pushed water in slow bright rings for as long as anyone paid to see it, and then the power fell away, and the water lay down flat and has not moved since. [75] Below the rail there is only cloud. [82]

She scavenges because that is what the platform asks of her now. [94] Copper from the wave arms. [99] Glass from the observation deck. [104] Batteries from the kiosk where they sold disposable cameras to people who wanted proof they had stood on water in the sky. [126] One of those cameras lives in her coat. [134] The film ran out long ago, but the little ridged wheel still turns under her thumb, click, click, and she counts the clicks as days because she has nothing better to count. [166] The wheel does not agree with her. [173] Some mornings it says four hundred. [179] Some mornings it says she only just arrived. [187]

She came up to photograph the lagoon. [194] A magazine had wanted the moving water at dawn, the carousel lit behind it, and she had wanted her name under the picture badly enough to ride the freight lift with the maintenance crews. [228] Then a pump broke and she knew pumps. [236] Then the crews thinned and someone had to stay. [245] Then the wanting wore down the way the wave arms wore down, a little each cycle, until there was a smooth place where an ambition used to be. [273] She does not remember deciding any of it. [281]

So she collects other people's stories to fill in her own. [292] The platform still has voices if you know where to sit. [303] The service radio catches the evacuation lifts talking to the ground, or it did once, and she cannot say which. [323] A woman's voice says, She stayed for the pictures, poor thing. [334] A mechanic on the pipes, or the memory of one, says the horses stopped mid-stride and one girl kept riding anyway, around and around in her head. [361] A child at the rail, years ago or yesterday, asks why the water does not go anymore. [378] Ines writes the fragments on the backs of ticket stubs and keeps them in the camera box. [395]

She brings them to the water at what she believes is evening. [407] The lagoon is a mirror now, which it never was when it moved, and the woman in it is the one who rode the freight lift up. [434] Younger, or the same age, with a full roll of film and no grease under her nails. [451] Ines calls her the ghost because that is easier than calling her anything true. [465]

"I have more," Ines says, and lays the stubs on the wet lip of the basin. [481] "The woman on the radio thinks you stayed for the pictures." [492] "The mechanic thinks you never got off the ride." [501] "Pick one and I will believe it." [508]

The ghost does not speak. [513] Ghosts in still water never do; they only wait for the terms to improve. [527] This is the negotiation, and it has been going on for a Tuesday, or three years, or since this morning. [547] What Ines wants is simple: permission to stop being the person who almost did something. [562] What the ghost wants, she suspects, is the same thing from the other direction. [576]

She turns the camera wheel. [581] Click. [582]

Somewhere in the pipes the mechanic's voice, or its echo, says the girl could have gone down with the last lift and chose the horses instead. [608] Ines has not written that one down before. [616] She is not sure she heard it now. [624] She writes it anyway, and her hand is not steady, and the letters come out in the cramped shape of a thing she already knew. [649] Chose. [650] Not stranded, not forgotten, not left behind by a crew that thinned. [662] She had stood on the platform with the lift doors open and a full roll of film and thought, one more dawn, the light will be better tomorrow. [690]

The regret comes up through the stillness like a bubble from a dead pump, and it does not break; it sits there on the surface between her and the ghost. [720]

"Fine," Ines says. [723] "That one." [725]

The woman in the water does not nod, because water does not nod, but the surface trembles the way it used to when the engines were about to start. [754] It is only Ines, breathing. [759] She puts the stub in her pocket instead of the box and turns the wheel once more, and this time she lets the click be a day, the first she is willing to count. [793]
