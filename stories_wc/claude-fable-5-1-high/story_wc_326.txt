The radio static cleared a little after midnight, and the base station's voice came through the snow like someone speaking from the bottom of a well. [26] "Opal Station, confirm you are alive." [32] Marit smiled at that. [36] She had been asked to confirm it before, on paper, in triplicate, three winters ago, after a clerk at the county hospital typed her name onto a death certificate meant for the man in the other bed. [73] That man was Dr. Ansel Rook, who had driven them both off the mountain road in a whiteout, and who had not survived. [96] For nineteen days the world's records insisted otherwise. [104] Her accounts froze; her mother received a call; a funeral home sent a brochure. [118] Marit had learned, in those nineteen days, that being alive is not something the world takes on faith. [136] So she counted. [139]

"Alive," she told the radio, two fingers at her throat, sixty-one, sixty-two. [151] "Snowed in, but the generator's fine." [157] "I'm running the old drive tonight." [163]

The old drive was why she had come back. [172] Rook had spent twenty-two years under the observatory's opal dome, that milky iridescent glass the founders had chosen for reasons no one remembered, hunting a periodic signal from a dwarf star that every model said should exist. [209] He never found it. [213] His life's work lived on a failing disk in the basement, scattered across bad sectors, a digital ghost of a man who had died believing he had wasted himself. [242] Marit did not believe that. [247] She believed failure kept notes, and that someone should read them. [258]

She started the defragmenter and listened to the disk grind. [268] The program gathered Rook's fragments and laid them end to end: observation logs, calibration files, voice memos in which he cleared his throat before every sentence. [294] Slowly he came back together, seventy-four, seventy-five, a man reassembled out of what he had left behind. [311]

On the desk beside the monitor sat the only physical thing of his she had kept: a porcelain shard, blue-rimmed, from the coffee cup that had shattered against the dashboard. [341] She had found it in her coat pocket a week after the funeral that was not hers. [358] It held down a stack of his printouts, the raw light curves he had stared at until his eyes gave out. [379] Beside them sat his inkwell, because Rook had written his logs by hand first, distrusting anything that could be deleted. [399]

The disk shrieked at a bad sector. [406] Marit flinched, and her elbow caught the inkwell. [414]

Ink ran black across the printouts, pooled against the porcelain shard, split around its edge, and spread in two thin streams that thinned and branched and faded into the paper. [444] She reached for a rag and stopped. [451] The ink was not covering the light curves. [459] It was following them. [463] Wherever Rook's pencil had scored the paper, crossing out a point, the ink pooled; wherever he had left the data alone, it ran thin. [487] For perhaps ten seconds, before the paper drank it all, the spill drew a pattern she had never seen on any monitor: not in his data, but in his absences, the nights he had failed to observe, the runs he had thrown out. [530] The gaps repeated. [533] They repeated with a period. [538]

Marit's heartbeat lost its number. [543]

She sat very still while the ink dried into a shapeless stain and the defragmenter finished behind her with a small, satisfied chime. [566] Then she opened the reassembled drive and pulled every discarded run, every cloudy night, every file Rook had labeled useless. [586] The signal was there, in the failures. [593] Rook had thrown out every night the star dimmed, calling it bad seeing, thin cloud, instrument drift, and the dimming had a period of nineteen days. [619] He had been looking for a light that pulsed, and the star had been sending him a darkness that did. [639]

Marit picked up the porcelain shard and wiped the ink from its edge with her thumb. [655] Outside, the snow had stopped, and the opal dome caught the starlight and turned it faintly green, faintly rose, the way it must have looked to him on ten thousand nights when he thought nothing was happening. [692] She thought of the clerk who had killed her on paper, and of how she had spent three years proving the clerk wrong, one beat at a time. [720] Rook's paper had said failure, and it had been just as wrong. [732] Records were only records. [736] The thing itself went on quietly underneath, keeping its own count. [747]

She keyed the radio. [751] "Opal Station," she said. [755] "Tell them Rook found it." [760]

A pause, then static, then the voice again: "Say again?" [770]

Marit set two fingers to her throat, felt the steady answer there, and for the first time in three years did not bother to count it. [796]
