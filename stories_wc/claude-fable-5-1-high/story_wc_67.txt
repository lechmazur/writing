The Vanishing Point Gallery had once been a railway corridor, and it was still long enough that the far wall shrank to a gray dot where every line of the floorboards seemed to agree on a single destination. [38] Ilse worked at the near end, where the light was honest. [49] She drew the dead in charcoal, and when the drawing was done she scattered what remained of the burnt sticks along the corridor, so that the gallery floor was dusted with the residue of a hundred faces. [86]

Tobias Reyn arrived with a rocking chair and a box. [96] "She sat in this every evening," he said, setting the chair down by her easel as if it were a witness. [117] From the box he lifted a mask made from shattered porcelain shards, blue-and-white fragments of a dinner service glued into the shape of a woman's face, the cracks between them filled with gold-colored resin. [151] "Marguerite dropped the whole set the year her brother died," he said. [163] "She swept it up and made this, and after that she wore it whenever anyone came to the house." [182] "I have no photograph of her without it." [190]

Ilse turned the mask in her hands. [197] It was lighter than it looked, and the eyeholes were small, as though the wearer had wanted to see as little of the world as the world saw of her. [227] Porcelain was a strange armor; it shattered at a touch, yet Marguerite had trusted it for twenty years. [245] Safety, Ilse thought, was mostly a matter of what one agreed not to look at. [260]

"Then tell me what you saw," she said. [268] "Not what she was like." [273] "One moment." [275]

Tobias sat in the rocking chair and began to move, slowly, the way a man does when the habit belongs to someone else. [298] He gave her pieces. [302] A stairwell in their first apartment, the mask pushed up onto her hair because the strap had broken. [320] A scar through the left eyebrow. [326] The way she had looked at him when she realized her face was bare and he had not turned away, an expression he could only describe as someone stepping off a ledge and finding the floor was there. [364] It had lasted perhaps two seconds before she pulled the mask down again. [377]

It was not enough to draw a face from. [386] It never was. [389] Ilse had long ago stopped waiting for complete information; the dead did not sit, and the living remembered in fragments. [409] What she could do was hold the gaps open and draw the thing that lived in them, the two seconds rather than the twenty years. [434] She worked in charcoal because charcoal was already burnt, already past the worst of what could happen to it. [453]

For the highlights she asked his permission, then pried one small shard from the mask's temple and powdered it with a mortar until the blue glaze was gone and only white remained. [485] She lifted it with a damp brush and touched it to the paper where the light would sit in the eyes. [506] The chair creaked behind her, slower now, as Tobias ran out of things he was certain of. [523] She drew the scar. [527] She drew the mouth not quite closed, caught between fear and its opposite. [540] She drew the ledge, and she drew the floor. [549]

The rocking chair finally slowed to a stop, and in the silence it left she set down the charcoal and stepped back. [571] Tobias came to stand beside her. [577] "Is that her?" [580]

Ilse chose her words the way she chose her lines, leaving out nothing that was true and adding nothing that was not. [602] "It is the moment you gave me," she said. [611] "I never saw her, so I cannot promise you her face." [622] "But that is what she felt on the stairs, and you are the only one alive who can say whether I have it." [645]

He looked for a long time. [651] The porcelain dust in the eyes caught the window light and held it, two seconds made mineral, and Ilse felt the thing she always felt at this point and never trusted: that she had fixed something that was never meant to hold still. [694]

"Yes," Tobias said, and then, more quietly, "she looks like she thinks it's safe." [708]

"She did think so," Ilse said. [714] "For two seconds." [717] "That is the only kind there is." [724]

When he had gone, carrying the chair and the portrait and the mask with its one new gap, she gathered the spent charcoal and the last of the white powder into her palm. [757] She walked the length of the gallery toward the gray dot where the floorboards pretended to meet, and she scattered the ash there, at the point that existed only from a distance. [789]
