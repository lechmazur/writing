The laboratory had gone down with the cliff three nights ago, and the lake had swallowed it whole. [18] Wren surfaced inside it, into a pocket of air the size of a chapel, and pulled off her mask. [37] Above her, on a bent pipe, a spider had begun to spin. [49] She decided that was her clock. [55] When the web was finished, the air would be too thin to think in. [69]

The journal was where the message had said it would be, wedged behind a dead monitor, wrapped in oilcloth. [88] Beside it, two bone dice. [93] Wren knew them before she touched them. [100] The hangman's dice. [103] The warden of this place had rolled them each night to choose which patient would be taken downstairs, and he had liked to say the dice were fairer than he was. [134]

She had never been here. [139] She was sure of that, the way you are sure of a room in a dream. [155]

The journal opened to a page of letters that meant nothing, and Wren felt her mouth start to hum. [174] That was how ciphers came to her: not as grids, but as songs with the words scraped off. [192] You found the melody first, and the meaning came limping after. [203] This melody she knew. [207] It was the counting rhyme she had made up at seven, in a hospital bed, to keep from crying while the nurses did what they did. [233] Nobody else had ever heard it. [239] Nobody except the woman in the next bed, who had hummed it back to her in the dark and told her that a secret sung was a secret kept. [268]

The spider had laid its first spokes. [275] Wren read. [277]

The entries were dated by dice rolls instead of days, each one keyed to the pair of numbers the warden had thrown that night. [301] Whoever wrote them had stolen his instrument of terror and made it her metronome. [315] Three and five: I am afraid of the water rising. [325] Two and two: I am afraid he will find this book. [336] Six and one: I am afraid I will not be enough. [347] Every entry was a confession of fear, written in a hand so calm it read as defiance. [364]

Wren understood the design before she understood the words. [374] The writer had encrypted her vulnerability as strength: every weakness recorded became a letter in the key, so that the more frightened she admitted to being, the stronger the lock grew. [405] Anyone who wanted the combination would have to read a woman being honest about terror, and most men in this building would have thrown the book away in disgust before they reached the end. [439] Wren did not throw it away. [445]

The last entry was not encrypted at all. [453] The vault below the waterline holds eleven people and enough air for four days. [467] I sealed it before the cliff went. [474] I am inside with them. [479] The code is my fear, in order. [486] If you are reading this, little bird, you were always going to. [498]

Wren's hands were shaking. [502] That was fine. [505] The book had just taught her it was fine. [514]

She could not hold the sequence in her head; it was too long, and the air was going sour. [533] So she did what she had always done when words failed and someone needed to understand. [549] She stood in the knee-deep water and danced it. [558] Three steps left for the rising water, a turn for the hidden book, six slow beats for not being enough. [578] Each fear became a movement, and each movement stayed in her body where panic could not reach it. [596] The spider watched from its half-finished web. [603]

She pulled her mask back on and went down. [612]

The vault door had a dial, and the dial had numbers, and Wren's body remembered every step in the dark. [632] Left, turn, six slow beats. [637] The bolts let go with a sound like a held breath ending. [649]

Inside, in the yellow light of a single lamp, eleven faces turned toward her. [663] The twelfth did not turn, because she was already looking at the door, and had been for three days. [682] She was older than the woman in the next bed. [692] She was exactly her. [696] She did not say Wren's name. [702] She hummed four notes of a counting rhyme, and Wren hummed the next four back, and that was the whole of it. [724]

They brought the eleven up through the flooded stairwell one at a time, into the chapel of air, under the spider's finished web. [747] Wren pocketed the dice on the way out. [755] Not as a trophy. [759] As a metronome. [762]
