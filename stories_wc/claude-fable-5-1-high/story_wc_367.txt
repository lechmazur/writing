The last print newspaper had folded eleven years before Ines learned to laugh at weather. [15] She kept the final edition in a drawer beneath the lantern room, its headline gone the color of weak tea: NO FURTHER EDITIONS. [38] After that, news came only as weather, and weather came only as Bram. [51]

The lighthouse was built of green bottle glass, salvaged pane by pane from a shipwrecked cargo, and on clear nights the stars showed through its walls like sparks caught in seawater. [82] Ines lived inside it the way a flame lives inside a bulb. [94]

Every night at the ninth bell Bram climbed the stairs, already laughing. [106] He was the storm herald, and she had never asked what that meant, because the answer would have been stranger than the routine. [129] His coat dripped even in drought. [135] His boots left prints that filled with rainwater no one could see falling. [148] He sat on the coal box, poured himself her tea, and told her what the sky intended, and he laughed through all of it, gales and calms alike, as though weather were the best joke anyone had ever told him. [188]

"Nor'easter by midnight," he would say, wiping his eyes. [197] "Three fishing boats will come in blind." [204]

Then Ines would trim the wick. [210]

The wick was glass. [214] An old keeper had made it, a rod of clear crystal as thick as a thumb, standing in the oil like a stalk in water. [239] The flame burned at its foot, and the light climbed the rod without escaping, bouncing wall to wall inside the glass through total internal reflection until it poured out the polished tip and into the great lens. [276] No salt could smother it. [281] No wind could bend it. [286] Ines could steer a beam thinner than a rope across four miles of black water and lay it on a hull like a hand. [310]

That was the routine, and it was otherworldly, and it was hers. [322]

But the boats had grown fewer since the paper died. [332] Without printed tide tables or harbor notices, the fleets scattered to ports that still advertised. [347] The town below went dark one window at a time. [357] Ines began to feel she was guarding a door no one meant to use. [371]

One night in late autumn Bram arrived shaking with mirth. [381] "Clear sky," he gasped. [385] "Nothing coming at all." [389] "And you, keeper, will spend the whole night reading the news." [400] He howled at that, because there was no news. [409]

Bram's jokes had a habit she had noticed but never named: they came true sideways. [424] He had once joked that she would marry the sea, the week before the flood took her cottage and left her sleeping in the tower. [449] He never meant prophecy. [453] He was simply a creature of weather, and weather cannot help arriving. [465]

So when he left, still chuckling down the spiral, Ines opened the drawer. [478] She unfolded the last newspaper under the starlight coming green through the walls. [491] Tide tables. [493] A harbor map. [496] The old shipping schedule, sixty vessels that had once known this coast by heart. [510] Ancient shadows, she thought, and something lifted in her chest that was too dark to be hope and too bright to be anything else. [534]

She worked until the stars wheeled halfway round. [542] With a razor she cut the harbor map from the page, then the schedule, then the headline itself, until she held a lattice of paper more hole than ink. [571] She fixed it against the lens. [577] Then she trimmed the glass wick and let the trapped light climb. [589]

The beam went out over the empty sea carrying shadows: channel markers, depths, the outline of the harbor mouth, and above it all the words NO FURTHER EDITIONS, thrown enormous against the fog bank sleeping on the horizon. [627]

Ines laughed. [629] It came out of her like something breaking, and she understood at last why Bram could not stop. [647] The news was a map now. [653] The dead paper was steering. [658]

Toward dawn a single light answered from the fog, then two, then a ragged line of them, fishing boats that had seen a harbor drawn on the sky and turned toward it. [690] Ines steered each one in, laying the thin bright hand of the beam on hull after hull. [707] Behind her the east went grey, then gold, and the green walls filled with it. [722] Dawns, she had decided, were not found. [729] They were made from whatever old darkness you still had in the drawer. [742]

At the ninth bell that evening Bram came up the stairs, took one look at the paper lattice on the lens, and laughed so hard he had to sit down on the floor. [775] "Keeper," he wheezed, "you're going to be famous." [783]
