Ines had spent forty years teaching color to stay put. [10] Madder, indigo, weld: she mordanted them into wool until they could not leave, and she watched every vat the way a shepherd watches a cliff edge. [36] Then her own heart quit, and a stranger's heart was stitched into her chest, and in the eleven weeks since, people would not stop telling her things. [63]

The pharmacist described a brother drowned off Cape Ash. [72] A boy on the tram confessed to a stolen bicycle and wept. [84] Nobody had ever told Ines anything. [90] She was the watcher of the dye yard; apprentices felt her eyes and closed like anemones. [106] Now strangers opened. [109]

The donor's file was sealed, but a nurse who owed her a kindness said only this: a woman, a tea house, the Kell Valley, the rock. [135] Ines went because she needed to know what the heart had carried in with it, and why some people magnetize stories while others, like her, repel them. [162]

The Kell fills with mist before dawn, so thick the river is only a sound. [177] A ferryman told her the rock could not be found by looking; you found it by tracking sun trails. [196] When the sun cleared the eastern ridge, it burned channels through the mist, long gold corridors that shifted as the morning turned, and one of them always ended at the tea house. [228] She walked inside the light for two hours, the white walls of vapor sliding past on either side, until the corridor tilted upward and she saw it. [255]

A slab of grey stone the size of a barn hung forty feet above the valley floor, held by nothing, mist curling beneath it like breath under a door. [284] On top sat a small timber house with paper windows and a rope ladder swaying from its porch. [302] Ines, who trusted nothing she had not tested, tested every rung. [313]

An old man named Hollis met her with a pot already steeping. [325] "You have my daughter's way of standing in a doorway," he said, and did not seem surprised. [342]

Inside, the room held nothing but low tables, a stove, and one thing on the wall: a blue morpho wing pinned under glass, wide as a hand. [369] Ines knew that wing better than she knew most people. [379] There is no blue in it. [385] Grind it to powder and you get brown dust; the color is structure, ridges finer than thought that bend light and throw it back changed. [410] Tilt it and the blue slides away to violet, to nothing. [421] She had spent her life trying to bottle that and failing, and she had come to hate it a little, the way you hate a thing you cannot pin down. [451]

"Why did people tell her things?" Ines asked. [459]

Hollis poured and pointed at the floor. [466] The sun trail she had followed up the valley came in through the paper window as a slow bar of gold, creeping across the boards toward the wall. [494] "Wait for it," he said. [499]

They drank. [501] The light climbed the wall, touched the frame, and the wing went off. [514] That was the only word for it: quietly explosive, a soundless detonation of blue that filled the room and lit Hollis's face and Ines's hands and the steam rising from the cups. [546]

"She used to say the wing holds no color of its own," Hollis said. [560] "It only illuminates whatever light comes to it, and gives it back bluer." [573] "People came up that ladder grey and left thinking they had been blue all along." [588]

Ines looked at her hands in the glow. [596] She understood her own caution at last; it had been iridescent too, but turned the wrong way. [613] She had shifted her surface to whatever angle would deflect, showing a different face to every approach so that no one could get purchase, and she had called it watchfulness. [643] The dead woman had used the same structure to receive. [653] Same ridges, opposite tilt. [657]

Under Ines's ribs the borrowed heart kept its stranger's rhythm, and she felt, for the first time, that it was not repelling anything. [680] "I had a sister," she said, before she had decided to say it. [693] "She drowned in an indigo vat when we were nine, and I have watched every vat since as if watching could bring anything back." [717]

Hollis did not answer, and did not need to. [726] The sun trail moved on, the blue drained from the wing, and the mist below the rock began to thin, showing the river at last, a bright thread far down in the green. [759] Ines sat in the ordinary light and let it come to her. [771]
