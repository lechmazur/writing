The turbines had remade the hush. [6] Before them, the farmhouse had known real silence, the kind that let a floorboard speak; now the blades pushed a slow breath over the roof every four seconds, and Marris counted it the way other people counted heartbeats. [44] She had not had a heartbeat worth counting since the mandate. [55] That was not a complaint. [60] It was an inventory. [64]

For eleven years she had been a Cheer Performer for the Ministry, riding the circuit of grain towns, smiling on stages built from pallets while families practiced the approved expressions back at her. [97] Then her sister Tamsin died in the spring, and the forty-eight hours of permitted mourning passed, and on the forty-ninth hour Marris walked onstage and found her face would not move. [128] Not frowning. [130] Nothing. [131] A blank the audience could see themselves in, which the Ministry found more dangerous than tears. [147] They reassigned her to the wind farm, to a farmhouse nobody wanted under the turbines nobody could stand, and told her the fresh air would restore her. [174]

On the kitchen table sat Tamsin's bouquet, gone brown and papery, the ribbon faded to the color of dust. [193] Regulations required funeral flowers composted at the end of the mourning window, since dead things kept past their hour were considered an attitude. [216] Marris had not kept them out of defiance. [224] She had simply never felt the moment arrive when throwing them away would mean anything, and so they stayed, shedding one petal a week onto the oilcloth. [251]

What she did instead was whittle. [257] A fencepost had come down in the first storm, and she cut it into hand-lengths and sat on the porch each evening with a paring knife, and because the blankness went all the way in, she gave herself a voice to fill it. [300] The stage voice. [303] "And now," she told the empty yard, "the blade approaches the knot with the courage of a woman who has nothing scheduled." [325] "The curl falls. [328] The audience gasps. [331] The audience is a goat." [336] There was, in fact, a goat, belonging to the Halloran place across the access road. [351]

The narration was not a joke and it was not joy. [362] It was a technique she had been taught in her first year, for the towns where the harvest had failed: describe the small task grandly, and the grief underneath it becomes something you can lift. [397] Not carry. [399] Lift, briefly, like a stone you set back down. [408] She had used it on other people for eleven years and never once noticed it was a tool for herself. [428]

The Halloran boy started coming in the second month. [437] He was nine and his mother had gone into a Recalibration Center in the autumn, and he sat on the bottom step without asking and watched the knife. [465] "Behold," Marris said, in the voice, "the artist removes a splinter from her thumb, and the turbines applaud." [483] Whoosh. [484] The boy did not laugh, but his shoulders came down a quarter inch. [497] She noticed the quarter inch the way she noticed the four-second breath. [509] Data. [510]

By the fourth week he was narrating too, in a small flat copy of her cadence, announcing that he had tied his shoe and that the crowd was on its feet. [541] Marris understood exactly what she was doing. [548] She was teaching him to be lighter, and she was teaching him the Ministry's own trick, the one that made a town smile while its silos stood empty. [576] In a year he would pass any inspection. [584] Whether that was a rescue or a recruitment she could not feel strongly enough to decide, and she had stopped waiting to. [606]
