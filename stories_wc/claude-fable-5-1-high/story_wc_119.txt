Every fortune Nell Askew wrote ended with a full stop, and she had never once wondered why the printers insisted on it. [22] She wrote them at the kitchen table of the lighthouse on Sgeir Dubh, the rock her uncle Tam had kept for thirty years, twelve fortunes a day for the Golden Lantern company in Glasgow, a contract she had inherited along with the island. [65] The light had been decommissioned in 1994. [72] It still turned every night. [77] Nobody in Portmhor asked why. [82]

That was the thing about the village: everyone knew what Tam had been, the way everyone knows a hymn without remembering learning it. [105] Boats came to the rock on moonless nights. [113] Men in good coats drank at the Anchor and paid in notes too crisp for fishing money. [130] And Nell's fortunes, read aloud over prawn crackers from Oban to Inverness, kept coming true. [145] "You wrote 'The tide returns what you lent it,'" Morag at the Anchor told her, "and my Davie's boat came back off the rocks with not a scratch." [173] Nell had meant nothing by it. [179] She never meant anything by them. [185] She wrote them the way you hum, and the village treated her like a weather glass that happened to speak. [205]

The specifications turned up in Tam's sea chest, folded inside a chart of the Minch. [220] Typed, yellowed, precise: emulsion thickness, reduction ratio, how a page of text could be photographed down to a disc smaller than a printed period and fixed to paper with a touch of collodion. [253] Microdots. [254] Cold War stuff, museum stuff. [259] At the bottom, in Tam's hand: "Carrier must be innocuous, widely distributed, and always end in a full stop." [278]

She sat with that a long while. [285] Then she took her magnifier to last month's proof sheet and found, at the end of "Patience is a door that opens inward," a dot that was not ink. [314] So she was the carrier. [319] Her sentences were the envelope, and whatever men in good coats were saying to each other travelled in her punctuation. [339] Perhaps she had never been prophetic at all. [347] Perhaps she had been read by people who arranged for things to happen, and who found it tidy to let her words come true. [371]

Nell was forty-one and had spent her life as somebody's blank space. [383] She did not want a different life; she liked the rock, the gulls, the small daily craft of a sentence. [403] She wanted the same tune played in a key she had chosen. [415]

She wrote that week's twelve as usual and posted them. [425] The last read: "What everyone knows will finally be seen." [435] She did not, at the time, have a plan. [444]

The plan came on Thursday, at the Anchor, when Morag rang the bell for last orders and Nell watched the whole village drain toward the door in one slow current, down the brae, along the harbour wall, home. [482] Every night the same flow, the same forty minutes. [491] And every moonless night the boat timed its approach to the light's pattern, which Tam had set to sweep the seaward arc only, leaving the harbour dark. [518] You did not need to fight a river. [526] You only needed to move one stone. [533]

Callum rowed her back at half eleven, and she climbed the tower and unbolted the baffle plate her uncle had fitted thirty years before. [557] The beam, freed, swung the full circle. [564] Sea, rock, sea, and then the harbour, the wall, the brae, the slow procession of coats and cardigans lit up like a stage. [587] And the boat, in the wrong place at the wrong minute, caught white and naked forty yards off the slip, three men in good coats shading their eyes. [615]

Nobody on the wall shouted. [620] Nobody ran. [622] They simply stopped and looked, thirty people, and the looking was the whole of it. [637] A secret everyone knows is still a secret so long as no one has to see it. [654] Once seen, it is only a fact, and facts cannot be used. [666] The boat turned and went out. [672] It did not come again. [677]

The following week a letter arrived from Golden Lantern regretting that the contract had been reassigned. [693] Nell read it twice, then wrote her fortunes anyway, on index cards, and left them in a jar on the Anchor's bar for whoever wanted one. [719] She stopped using full stops. [724] Morag said the light looked different now, wider, and asked if Nell had done something to it. [741] "Same lamp," Nell said. [745] "Just turning all the way round." [751] The village nodded, the way you nod at a hymn you have always known and have only just heard the words to. [773]
