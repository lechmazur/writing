The first message appeared on the caretakers' walk three years after we fell out of the sky onto Quince, when we had finally stopped calling one another by our ship numbers. [31] It was chalked in white, letters as tall as my forearm: LOOK AGAIN AT THE BELL. [47] None of us had chalk. [52] None of us walked the deserted sidewalks of the old caretaker town at dawn, because the floating arches drift lowest then, grinding their undersides against the rooftops. [79] Hollis said it was a prank. [85] Mara, who had buried her sister in the first winter and had since become the mother none of us asked for, said nothing, only rubbed the white dust between her fingers and frowned. [118]

Quince is an orchard planet, or was meant to be. [128] Whoever planted it before us laid the trees in rings beneath the rock arches, iron-veined slabs that hang forty meters up, held aloft by the same field that fills our sky with the Nebula of Respite. [164] The caretakers named it that in their logs. [172] Every ninety years the planet drifts into the nebula's dust and everything sleeps: trees, insects, soil. [188] We crashed into the tail end of that sleep, into a world of bare branches and a town with no one in it. [211] Now the dust was thinning, the trees had exploded into blossom, and not one flower had set fruit. [229]

The second message came four dawns later: THE BELL IS IN THE GLASS HOUSE. [243] The third, the next morning: TOVAH, YOU KNOW WHICH ONE. [253]

I did. [255] In the collapsed greenhouse behind the caretakers' hall, among dead vines and a hundred empty chrysalis cases stuck to the glass like brown seeds, I had once found a brass watering bell, the kind meant for misting a butterfly garden. [295] I had turned it over, judged it useless, and left it. [306]

Why I trusted the chalk, I cannot fully say. [315] There is a kind of stranger you meet at the end of the world who is strangely trustworthy precisely because they want nothing from you, and the chalk felt like that. [346]

So I went back and I reexamined the bell. [355] Inside the rim, worn nearly smooth, someone had engraved a line: THEY SLEEP IN THE ARCH HOLLOWS; RING AND RAIN AT FIRST LIGHT; ALL ARCHES, ONE DAWN, OR NONE. [384]

The pollinators had not died. [389] They had gone up, into the pocked undersides of the floating stone, to wait out the nebula as everything here waits. [410] But the blossom would not wait for them. [418] Wake one arch and the butterflies would find a single ring of trees and starve in the others. [436] There were forty-one arches over the orchards. [443] There were thirty-three of us. [448]

That night I understood the chalk had never been talking to me alone. [461] Whoever wrote it could not climb, could not carry water, could not ring a bell. [476] They could only leave words where we would step on them, and hope we would do the rest together. [495]

Hollis argued. [497] The arches grind low at dawn; a person on a rope could be crushed against a roof. [514] Mara let him finish, then said, "We were thirty-three strangers who did not choose each other, and now I cannot sleep unless I hear all of you breathing." [542] "That did not happen because we were careful." [550]

We spent nine days hammering bells from wreckage hull and making rope. [562] The eight extra arches we split between the strongest climbers, who would descend one and run to the next. [581] Every dawn the chalk gave us a number: 12, 9, 5. [592] The nebula was almost gone from the sky; after it, the field would weaken and the arches would settle to the ground and seal their hollows forever. [619]

On the last morning the sidewalk said only: THANK YOU. [629] Beneath it, smaller, a name: ILSE, CARETAKER, and a date ninety years old. [642] She had gone into the sleep alone and not come out, and had spent the long dark watching strangers land on her orchard and slowly, clumsily, become the family she never had time to gather. [677]

I climbed arch seventeen with the brass bell on my hip and the rope biting my palms. [694] Below, the town was bramble and blossom and thirty-two other bodies clinging to stone. [708] When the sun's rim cut the horizon, Mara's bell rang first, then all of them, a ragged ugly chord, and we tipped our water into the hollows. [735]

Nothing, for a long breath. [740] Then the underside of the stone stirred like a field in wind, and the butterflies came down through the arches in a slow brown snow, thousands, opening orange as they warmed, and dropped into the flowers. [776]

We will not know for weeks if the fruit sets. [786] The chalk has stopped. [790] I think she is resting. [795]
