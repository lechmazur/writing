The garden hummed in the low register that night, the way it always did before the sky came loose. [19] Oda counted the hum the way other fishers counted tides. [29] She was a cool-headed woman, though not by nature; she had made herself so, letter by letter. [46] Every feeling that rose in her she transliterated, on the spot, into the garden's notation: fear became a knot three fingers from the hook, longing became a length of slack, anger the tight double hitch she never used anymore. [85] Written out that way, a feeling was only rigging. [94] Rigging could be checked. [98]

The sound wave garden lay in a stone bowl below the old rail cut, and nothing grew in it but noise. [119] Where seeds might have gone, the first gardeners had sunk iron and glass and hollowed bone, and the wind that fell into the bowl broke against them and stayed. [148] Standing waves ridged the air like furrows. [155] You could see them if the moon was right, the same way you could see a fish rising if you did not look for the fish. [181]

Overhead, the constellations were beginning to sleepwalk. [188] The Heron put one foot outside its own outline. [197] The Loom slid two stars sideways and left a gap where the shuttle should be. [212] This happened one night a year, when the patterns broke themselves and wandered, and it was the only night the goddess could be heard. [236]

Nobody in the valley remembered her name. [243] They remembered that she had sung once, standing in the rail cut when the trains still ran, and that the bowl had caught her voice and never quite let it go. [274] The echo had thinned across generations into something less than a word, a warm pressure at the back of the ear. [295] Oda's mother had heard it once and wept for a week. [306] Oda intended to catch it and keep it, so that nobody would have to weep like that again, or so that she could weep once, properly, and be done. [335] She did not know which. [340] She tied the not-knowing into a slipknot and moved on. [350]

Her line was braided horsehair, weighted with a rail spike she had pried from a rotten tie that afternoon. [369] It was pitted and cold and it rang faintly when she flicked it, a single dull note that the garden bent and doubled. [392] Good. [393] Iron held pitch. [396] Iron would sink through the shallow waves to where the old echo pooled. [409]

She could not walk straight to the center. [417] A straight path crossed every crest and node, and the pressure would deafen her before she reached the middle. [436] So she zigzagged, the way her mother had taught her, stepping into each trough at its quietest point, letting the spike swing ahead like a plumb, listening for the place where the hum dipped and the hair on her arms lay down. [478] Left, then right, then left. [483] Above her the Heron stumbled clear of its stars and trailed one wingtip through the Loom. [499]

At the center the garden went almost still. [507] She let the spike down hand over hand until she felt it touch the bottom of the sound, a soft floor like the mud under a river. [534] Then she opened her mouth and sang nothing. [542]

This was the part her mother had not managed. [551] Anyone could be quiet. [555] Voicing silence was different; it meant shaping the throat around an absence and pushing that absence out, a hollow note with edges, a cup held out. [581] It felt like calling a name she had never been given. [592] The garden leaned in. [596] Every ridge of noise in the bowl bent toward the hollow she was making, and something old and thin and warm slid along them, down the horsehair, and touched the spike. [627]

It was not a word. [632] It was the pressure her mother had described, a mouth against her ear, a lullaby with the teeth still in it, wild and gentle and asking nothing. [659] Every feeling Oda had ever tied into a knot loosened at once and she had no letters for what came up instead. [681] She did not transliterate it. [686] She let it stay in its own alphabet. [694]

The spike rang. [697] She drew it up and the echo came with it, wound in the iron the way scent winds into cloth, and she held it against her chest and did not weep, though she could have. [732] Overhead the Heron found its way back into its outline, one star at a time, and the Loom mended its gap, and the patterns were patterns again. [759]

Oda climbed out of the bowl in a straight line. [769] Nothing in the garden hurt her now. [776] She could carry the sound of a goddess going to sleep, and she meant to carry it a long way. [796]
