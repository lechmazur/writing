Every spring, when the buds swelled but had not yet dared to open, Halle climbed to the parapet. [18] It was a shelf of green stone jutting from the cliff, so thick with moss and creeping thyme that the old orchard trees seemed to grow from a cushion, and beside it the waterfall fell in one long silver breath. [58] She had pruned these trees for fifty-one years. [66] Her hands knew which cut would coax fruit and which would coax light. [79] For the orchard grew on the seam between two worlds, and the waterfall was the thread that stitched them. [98] Step through the spray at the right angle, with a living branch in hand, and you came out on the far side, where the same trees grew with different hearts. [128]

Halle had woven between them all her life, grafting a scion from here onto a rootstock from there, carrying pollen in a folded leaf, mending what each world lacked with what the other had too much of. [165]

This year the far side was failing. [172] She had seen it in autumn: the apples there ripening grey, the leaves turning not gold but the color of ash in water. [195] It was not blight. [199] It was forgetting. [202] The people beyond the falls had stopped being astonished by anything, and a world that is no longer wondered at slowly loses the will to be vivid. [229]

Only one of them still made things. [236] Her name was Pim, and she was perhaps eleven, and she was insatiably creative in the way a spring is insatiably wet. [258] She built windmills from spoons, painted birds on the undersides of tables so the floor could look up at something, carved whistles that played two notes at once. [286] But everything she made came out grey now, no matter what pigment she used, and last autumn Halle had found her sitting under a colorless tree, hands still, which was the most frightening thing she had ever seen. [324]

So Halle had waited for this exact morning, meaning to preserve the color of wonder before it drained from both worlds. [345] There is an hour in early spring when every bud on every tree holds its breath, swollen and sealed, the whole orchard poised on the edge of becoming. [373] Her grandmother had called it the hush of budding hope, and had told her that in that hush the essence of wonder was not yet spent on blossom; it was still gathered, whole, inside the wood. [409]

From her apron Halle took the heirloom seed. [417] It had come down through nine pruners before her, an apple pip dark as a wet stone, never planted. [436] A seed is a hollow made of patience. [444]

She chose the oldest tree, the one whose roots drank the spray, and found the fattest bud on the highest wand. [465] Even the waterfall seemed to fall more softly, light standing in its mist like something that had decided to stay. [485] Halle set her knife against the bark and made the cut she had made ten thousand times, but slower, and with the seed cupped beneath. [510] A single bead welled from the wound, not sap but something that caught the sun and threw back every color at once, the exact green of a first leaf, the exact pink of a first petal, the exact gold of a child seeing either for the first time. [558] The seed drank it. [562] Then it was only a pip again, warm in her palm. [573]

She took the cut wand for passage and stepped into the falls, carrying succor the size of a thumbnail. [592] Water closed over her like a held note, and she came out into grey light, onto a grey terrace, beneath trees that did not remember what they were for. [621] Pim was where she had left her. [628] Her hands were still. [632]

Halle knelt and pressed the seed into the girl's palm and folded the small fingers over it. [649] "Not to eat," she said. [654] "Not to keep." [657] "To plant." [659] "And then to make something for it while it grows." [669]

Pim looked at the seed, and the seed looked back, and in the grey of her eyes a fleck of green arrived like the first bird of the year. [698] She dug a hole with her fingers. [705] She covered it. [708] Then, because she could not help it, because she had never been able to help it, she began to build a fence around the spot out of pebbles, and the pebbles, one by one, went faintly rose. [745]

Halle did not stay to watch the rest. [753] There would be no heirloom for the pruner after her, and that was right; some things are kept only so that one day they can be given. [780] She stepped back through the water into her own bright, breathing orchard, where every bud was opening. [797]
