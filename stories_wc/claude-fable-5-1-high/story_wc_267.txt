The fortress had been painted cobalt a century ago so that lost soldiers could find it against the snow, and no one had let the color fade since. [28] Anselm thought about that every morning while shoveling the courtyard: a blue loud enough to be seen from the valley, wrapped around a house where nobody spoke. [55] He had come up the mountain in October with a throat full of ice. [69] The avalanche that buried the Grand Hotel had killed the nerve behind his voice, frozen in the hours before the dogs dug him out. [93] Dr. Vey had looked down his throat with a lamp and said the nerve would regrow, a millimeter a day, and that until it did he could carry bags. [122] So he carried bags. [126]

There were eleven in the silent choir, patients whose voices had been taken by cold or surgery or shock, and every evening they stood in the music room while old Frau Lenz played hymns and they shaped the words with their mouths. [168] It was seraphically mundane, that hour: eleven people breathing in time, the piano, the snow ticking against the windows, nothing happening and nothing asked. [192] Vey called it the mercy of the place. [200] Nothing happens here, he told the new ones, and Anselm had wanted to believe him, because he had come to learn what safety cost and had hoped the answer was only boredom. [232]

Then Maren stopped coming to choir. [238] She had been the one who mouthed the loudest, if that could be said, a woman whose daughter lay in the valley hospital and who had twice tried to walk the road in a storm. [273] Vey said she had tried a third time and succeeded, and that the mountain would answer for her now. [292] The choir absorbed this the way it absorbed everything, without a sound. [304]

Frau Lenz's bench began to wobble the same week. [313] A bolt had gone from the left leg, and she played with her knees braced, and Vey said someone had been careless. [335] Anselm did not think about it until Thursday, when he carried linens up the north tower stair, a spiral nobody used, gray with dust to his boot tops. [363] Nine steps up, the dust had been disturbed. [371] It was almost nothing, a faint smear where the gray went a shade toward cobalt, as if a sleeve that had brushed the outer wall had then brushed the stone here. [402] Above it, another. [405] Anselm set the linens down and climbed. [412]

The door at the top was locked, but bellhops carry keys, and Vey had never counted the ring. [430] Maren sat on the floor with a blanket around her and a piano bench bolt in her fist, its threads worn bright from weeks of grinding at the mortar under the window bar. [463] On the wall beside her, scratched with the bolt, was a calendar: twenty-three days. [477] She could not speak either, so she pointed to the marks, then the window, then down toward the valley, and then pressed the bolt into his palm and closed his fingers over it. [510]

Vey had not lied about the mountain. [517] He had only decided that a woman locked above the snow was better than a woman dead in it, and he had told no one, and he had let eleven people mouth hymns beneath her feet. [553] That was the cost. [557] Not boredom. [559] A silence that could not be pardoned, because it had been chosen for you, and warmed for you, and served to you with supper. [583]

Anselm stood in the doorway for a long time. [592] The bolt was cold in his hand, and he thought of the six hours under the snow at the Grand Hotel, how safe it had felt near the end, how he had almost stopped wanting to be found. [630] He went back down for his coat and hers, and for the sled the kitchen used for firewood, and he did not stop to explain anything to anyone, because he could not, and because Vey already knew everything worth explaining. [670]

At the gate Maren took the rope. [677] She looked at him once, asking. [683] He shook his head. [687] He would remain. [690] Someone had to stay who knew which step was smudged, who would be standing in the music room tonight when Vey counted his keys and looked up, whose silence was not yet finished growing back. [725]

He watched her until blue and white were the only colors left, then opened his mouth and pushed from the belly, the way Frau Lenz had taught them. [753] What came out was not a word, only a crack, a splinter of sound, but it was his, and it was the first thing on that mountain in twenty-three days that had refused to stay quiet. [789]
