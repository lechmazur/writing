The leash broke at the bottom of Harrow Lane, where the cobbles give way to gravel and the gravel gives way to fog. [23] Nell felt the old nylon give, a soft snap like a stitch letting go, and Pilot was gone up the hill before she could call his name. [50] He was fourteen and nearly blind, and he had never run from her in his life. [66]

Above the town, the ridgeway climbed into a veil of sea-mist that came in every evening and did not lift until morning. [88] Nobody from Harrow walked it after dusk. [95] Nell had not walked it at all in thirty years, not since she had been a girl with a ferry ticket in her pocket and a suitcase she never carried down to the pier. [129]

She went up anyway. [133]

The path wound like something reluctant to arrive, doubling back on itself, dipping into gorse and rising again, and the mist closed behind her step by step. [160] Below, the tide breathed against the shingle, in and out, the sound her mother used to hum along with when Nell could not sleep. [184] She called for Pilot. [188] The fog took the word and gave back nothing. [197]

Then a lantern swung out of the grey ahead of her, low and unhurried, and behind it a second, and a third. [219] Three old people in oilskins stood on the path, two women and a man, their faces creased like charts that had been folded too many times. [245] Nell knew them, in the way one knows people seen only from a distance. [259] They had guided the ferry across the strait for forty years, reading the currents when the instruments failed, and when the ferry stopped running they had simply moved their watch up here, walking the ridge every twilight with their lamps. [299]

"A dog," Nell said. [303] "Old, and half blind. [307] His leash broke." [310]

"We saw him," said the taller woman. [317] "He went straight along the crest. [323] We won't take you that way." [329]

"Why not?" [331]

"Because the crest is where the cliff is, and the fog does not tell you where the edge begins." [350] She lifted her lantern toward a side path Nell had not seen. [362] "We go around." [365]

They set off without waiting, and Nell followed because there was nothing else to do. [380] The guides did not hurry and did not stop. [389] When she lagged, the man simply slowed until she caught up, and then went on again at exactly the same pace, as steady as the tide below. [416] They traversed a hollow of bracken, crossed a stream she could hear but not see, and climbed stone steps cut by nobody remembered whom. [440]

"This is the long way," Nell said. [447]

"It is the only way that arrives," said the shorter woman. [458] "Nothing worth finding on this ridge is reached straight on. [468] You know that. [471] You have been walking straight at the pier for thirty years and never getting there." [486]

Nell stopped. [488] "How do you know about that?" [494]

The man reached into his oilskin and took out a brass ticket punch, worn smooth, the kind that bit a small star out of the paper. [520] "We punched every ticket that ever crossed," he said. [529] "We remember the ones we didn't." [535]

"That's what we keep watch for up here," the taller woman said. [547] "Not for the ferry. [551] For the things people meant to do and set down. [561] They don't vanish. [564] They wait somewhere. [567] Mostly they wait in places like this, where nobody comes unless something makes them." [581]

The path turned once more and opened onto a shelf of grass, and there was Pilot, lying with his grey muzzle on his paws as though he had been waiting for her all along. [615] Beyond him the fog thinned, and Nell saw the strait, dark silver under the last of the light, and the far shore with its scattering of windows. [642] She had not looked at it, really looked, since she was nineteen. [654]

She knelt and put her hand on the dog's warm side. [665] "You could have just shown me," she said, not to him. [676]

"No," said the man. [680] "You had to walk it. [685] That was the point." [689] He held out his hand. [694]

Nell understood, and reached into the coat she had worn for thirty winters, and found in the lining the ticket she had never thrown away. [719] The punch closed with a small clean sound, and a paper star fell into the grass. [735]

"There's a boat from Kell in the morning," the shorter woman said. [747] "The dog can come." [751]

They walked her down the winding way, three lanterns and a slow old dog, while the sea kept its rhythm against the shore, in and out, the way it had sung her to sleep when she still believed she would go. [792]
