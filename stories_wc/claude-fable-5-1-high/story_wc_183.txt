The bone marrow registry called Wren on a Tuesday to tell her she was two people. [16] Her blood carried a second genome, they said, a sister folded into her before either had a heartbeat, a twin she had absorbed and worn ever since without knowing. [45] Chimera, the counselor said gently, as if naming a monster made it smaller. [58]

Wren did not feel monstrous. [63] She felt like a house with a locked room. [72]

Her mother might have opened it, but her mother's memory was a tide now, rearranging the shore each time it went out. [94] Wren visited anyway, and asked about the pregnancy, and her mother spoke instead about the Halcyon. [110]

The Halcyon had been a ferry once, then a carnival ship, strung with bulbs and painted horses, working the bay every summer of Wren's childhood. [135] Her parents ran the Garden of Forgotten Names, an attraction below deck where visitors wrote lost things on paper leaves and hung them from wire trees. [161] "Your father hated it," her mother said one week. [170] "Your father loved it," she said the next. [178] Between the versions, Wren found a drawer with an emerald swatch card, the paint company's name faded, the color called Garden. [199] On the back, in her mother's hand: two cribs. [208]

The Halcyon had been abandoned for a decade, cut loose from its mooring by a storm nobody bothered to answer, and now it drifted in the bay, tethered only by fog. [239] Wren hired a man with a skiff who did not ask questions. [251] She climbed a rusted ladder with the swatch card in her coat. [263]

The deck was a graveyard of joy. [270] Carousel horses lay on their sides, mouths open in silent whinnies. [281] Bulbs hung dark on sagging wire. [287] The fog erased the shore in every direction, so the ship seemed both entirely free and entirely held, going nowhere at the pace of the tide. [313] Wren understood the feeling. [317] She had spent her life carrying a passenger who could not leave and would never speak. [333]

Below deck, the Garden waited. [338] The wire trees still stood, most leaves rotted to pulp, a few laminated ones surviving. [353] She read them by flashlight. [358] A dog named Biscuit. [362] A grandmother's maiden name. [366] Someone's first language. [369] And on the far tree, at child height, in her mother's handwriting, a leaf with a single word: Linnet. [388]

Wren sat down on the wet floor. [395] She had not known there was a name. [403] Her mother had never said it, not even before the forgetting, and now the forgetting had made it impossible to ask whether the silence had been grief or mercy. [432] But here it was, a bird name to match her own, hung in a garden built for exactly this. [451]

She had come expecting solace and found instead its raw material, a comfort unkindled, waiting for a match. [469] A name was not a sister. [475] A swatch card was not a room painted green. [484] Her mother's shifting stories were not a history but a set of threads, and it occurred to Wren, sitting among the dead leaves, that this was what everyone had. [513] Nobody was handed a finished past. [519] You took what threads survived and you wove. [527]

So she began, aloud, in the dark hold of a ship going nowhere. [540] Linnet would have been the loud one, she decided, because Wren had always been quiet and someone in that emerald room should have laughed. [564] Linnet would have hated the carousel and loved the fog. [574] Linnet would have hung leaves on these trees for things she had not lost yet, just to see them there. [594]

Each sentence altered the one before it. [601] The sister Wren invented at the second tree was not quite the sister she had invented at the first. [620] That was fine. [623] Her mother's memories altered too, and were no less love for it. [635]

She thought of her own body, its two blueprints tangled past separation, an error by every textbook definition. [653] And she found, to her surprise, that she felt nothing like a mistake. [666] She felt crowded in the best way, blissfully aberrant, a house whose locked room turned out to have a window. [686]

Wren took the swatch card from her coat, wrote LINNET on its blank margin, and hung it beside the leaf. [706] Then she climbed back to the deck and stood at the rail. [718] The fog had not lifted. [723] The ship had not moved. [728] But she had recaptured something, thread by thread, and it would keep changing, and she would keep telling it, which was the only way anyone had ever kept anything at all. [759]
