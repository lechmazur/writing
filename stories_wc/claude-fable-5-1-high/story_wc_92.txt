<story>
The fort of Vell Aster glowed. [6] Its walls were quarried from lamp-stone, a mineral that drank sunlight all day and gave it back after dusk, so that at twilight the whole garrison shone like a coal someone had forgotten to smother. [41] Nobody needed torches there. [45] Nobody, therefore, kept any. [49]

Merit Osk had guarded the archive locks for eleven years. [59] Her post was a stool, a ring of forty keys, and a brass plate that read LOCK SENTINEL in letters the governor had ordered polished weekly. [85] She was vigilant in the way the governor liked: she noticed everything and reported the parts he could understand. [104]

What she did not report was the sundial. [112]

It stood in the center of the courtyard, a marble disk that some painter had begun decorating two summers ago and then abandoned, or so the story went. [140] Half its face was painted with the hours in careful gilt; the other half was bare stone. [157] The governor thought it ugly. [162] Merit thought it was the most eloquent thing in Vell Aster, because the painted angles were not hours at all. [182] They were instructions. [185]

Every few weeks, a "repair" crew arrived to "finish" the sundial and left it, again, half done, but with the gilt lines shifted a degree here, a degree there. [214] Read against the shadow the gnomon threw in the walls' own glow, the angles spelled a lock number and a night. [235] Tonight they spelled seventeen and now. [241]

Lock seventeen kept the room the governor called the Ash Vault, where the fort's founders had once stored the manuscripts of a school that had stood here before the garrison. [271] The founders had called the place the cradle of sparks. [281] The governor called it a fire hazard, and he was not wrong; ideas do have that property. [298]

At twilight, the walls dimmed. [303]

This was not supposed to happen. [309] Lamp-stone did not go dark; that was the entire point of lamp-stone. [321] Yet the glow drained out of the ramparts like water from a cracked basin, and in the space of a breath, Vell Aster stood blind for the first time in its history. [353]

Guards shouted. [355] Boots ran. [357] Someone demanded torches, and someone else explained, with rising panic, why there were none. [371]

Merit did not shout. [375] She had already noted, with the calm of a woman who mends clocks for pleasure, that the painted half of the sundial had been the western half, and that the dial cast its final shadow toward lock seventeen precisely as the light failed. [418] She could not have said how the stone went dark. [428] She would tell the inquiry, truthfully, that she did not know. [439] She had simply been ready for it, the way one is ready for rain. [453]

She rose from her stool and walked the corridor by memory, counting doors with her fingertips. [469] At the seventeenth she stopped and reached into her coat for the bronze quill holder. [484]

It had belonged to the school's last master. [492] It looked like nothing: a squat bronze cup with a weighted base, the kind that sits on a desk and holds a pen upright. [516] The keys on Merit's ring were the governor's keys, and the governor's keys opened every door but this one. [535] The founders had built the seventeenth lock to take no key at all, only the hexagonal base of the holder, turned a quarter to the left, as if setting down a pen after the last word of a long letter. [575]

The lock sighed open. [579]

Inside, in the dark, the manuscripts waited: grammars, star tables, a treatise on how to argue without a sword. [598] Merit did not take them. [603] That would have been theft, and theft made noise. [612] She left the door open, pocketed the holder, and went back to her stool. [626]

Because the sundial had spelled more than seventeen and now. [636] The bare half, the unpainted half, was itself a message, and it read: not yet, but soon, and by then they will know how to read. [662]

When the light returned an hour later, as unexplained as its leaving, the governor found lock seventeen open, the shelves apparently full, and his sentinel at her post. [690] "Did you see anything?" he asked. [696]

"It was dark, sir," said Merit. [702]

He suspected intrigue in everything except sundials, so he accepted this, because it was true, and ordered the door resealed. [722] He did not notice that the young recruit who resealed it lingered a moment over a grammar, or that the cook's boy had a star table folded in his apron, or that by morning half the garrison could name the constellation over the eastern wall. [767]

The sundial stayed half painted. [772] Somebody, after all, had to finish it. [779]
