Odile Ferrant had given away eleven million dollars, four houses, and a name that once opened doors, and she had kept only the fireflies. [24] Twelve jars rode in her canvas pack, each one a small green pulse, caught in the fern gullies above the village and fed on nothing but her patience. [52] Money, she had learned, changed hands without changing anyone. [61] So she had spent her last decade giving something rarer: coincidence. [72] A widow finds a job posting pinned exactly where she stops to tie her shoe. [87] A boy who wants to be a surgeon sits beside a surgeon on a delayed ferry. [103] Nobody thanked her, because nobody knew, and that was the point. [114]

She had come to the island to arrange one more. [124] Tomas Alaka, a fisherman who mended nets and nursed a grudge, had not spoken to his son Keanu in nine years. [145] The son, Odile had discovered, was flying home tonight to photograph the conjunction: Venus, Jupiter, and a thin moon drawing together over the sea in their slow dance. [173] She had sabotaged the father's outboard motor just enough that he would be stranded at the harbor at the hour his son's taxi passed. [197] Then, as always, she needed to be elsewhere. [205]

Her detour took her down the volcano's seaward flank, where the road gave out and the black sand began. [224] The beach was the reason she had chosen this island, if she was honest. [238] Above her the crater breathed a slow orange, and beneath her feet was what that breath became: rock burned to glass, glass ground to grains, grains cool and soft as poured pepper. [270] She knelt and pushed her fingers in. [277] This was what she had spent a fortune trying to feel: the texture of transformation, the exact grain of a thing that had stopped being one thing and become another. [307] She had given everything away hoping to become someone new, and had only become someone poorer. [323]

The sea came in with a whispering hush, the sound of a thousand small stones being asked to keep a secret. [344] Odile unpacked the jars and set them in a line along the tideline, twelve green hearts beating out of rhythm, and sat down among them to watch the planets close their distance. [376]

She did not hear the villagers until they were nearly upon her. [388] Forty of them, maybe more, coming down the lava path in silence, carrying no torches. [403] An old woman at the front stopped when she saw the jars and put both hands over her mouth. [422]

"When the moon takes the two wanderers by the hand," the woman recited, barely above the surf, "a stranger with no home will sit on the black shore with the stars in glass, and the mountain will hold its breath." [462]

Odile looked at her fireflies. [467] She looked up at the crater, whose orange had dimmed, as craters sometimes do, to a sullen ember. [485]

"I only came down here to stay out of the way," she said. [498]

The old woman laughed, one soft note. [505] "That is how it always happens." [511]

They sat down around her on the warm black sand, forty strangers and one, and nobody spoke, because the prophecy had not mentioned speaking. [535] Venus slid beneath the moon's lower horn. [542] Jupiter followed, unhurried, a steady white eye. [549] The fireflies pulsed and the surf hushed and the whole beach seemed to hold a color it could not quite say aloud. [571]

Odile had orchestrated a hundred coincidences and never once been inside one. [583] It felt, she realized, exactly like the sand: something that had been one thing, and was becoming another, and did not resist. [605]

Near midnight a truck's headlights swung down the path, and two men climbed out and stood at the edge of the gathering, not touching, but close. [631] One carried a camera. [635] The other still had engine grease to the elbows. [644] Odile did not look at them for long. [652] She had learned that the moment you watch a coincidence, it becomes an arrangement, and the gift goes out of it. [673]

Instead she unscrewed the first jar. [679] The fireflies rose in a slow green scatter, and the villagers breathed out together, a sound like the sea's own. [699] She opened the second, the third, all twelve, until the last of her possessions was drifting up the volcano's dark shoulder toward the ferns it came from. [726]

The mountain, holding its breath, let it go: a low rumble, and then a brighter orange, and then quiet again. [746]

Odile pressed her palms flat into the black sand and finally understood the texture. [760] It was not smooth and it was not rough. [769] It was warm, and it was shared. [776]
