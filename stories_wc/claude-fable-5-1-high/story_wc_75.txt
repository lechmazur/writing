Teodor Halm counted the museum every morning, because a historian who stops counting has already conceded the loss. [18] The museum was one dry chamber in the grotto city of Vellmouth, and its collection was whatever the displaced had carried through the water when the valleys above emptied. [47] A loom no one living could string, a perfumer's copper still, a violin whose player lay under the rockslide, and in a velvet box the one surviving lens from a pair of opera glasses, tinted emerald, which Madame Oren had carried from the burned opera house because it was lighter than her scores. [100]

Beyond the chamber's mouth, the Cataract stood frozen, a wall of white ice three hundred feet high that sealed Vellmouth from the world each winter. [125] Every winter before this one, Teodor had welcomed the sealing. [135] This winter had begun differently, after the beekeeper's last visit. [145]

The beekeeper came down the cliff path each autumn with honey and news, the only surface man who still remembered the way. [167] In October he had set down his jars, refused payment, and said the hives were dead and the hills were quiet and he would not be coming again. [195] Then the ice closed behind him, and Vellmouth understood that when it opened, nothing would be waiting. [212]

The dread that settled over the grotto was not loud. [222] It smelled of rosewater, because the perfumer's daughter had begun burning the last of her mother's stock in the lamps, saying there was no point saving it for a spring market that would never come. [257] The sweetness got into everything, into soup and blankets and the museum's velvet, and under it Teodor tasted fear. [276]

He knew what fear did to a culture; he had catalogued it. [288] The loom had stopped being a loom the year its weaver decided teaching was pointless. [303] The perfumer had died with her recipe in her head because nobody expected to need it. [319] Teodor had spent twenty years guiding visitors past these objects, explaining provenance in a cultured murmur, and he saw now that he had been giving tours of a graveyard. [348] The arts of the valley people were not in the cases; they were in the hands of the old, and the old were burning their rosewater. [374]

So he did the one thing his own family had left him. [386] The Halms had baked initial bread at the thaw for as long as anyone could trace, one loaf for each person absent from the table, shaped into the first letter of their name so that the missing could be broken and shared and swallowed rather than merely mourned. [434] Teodor announced that this year Vellmouth would bake for its dead makers, and that no household could shape a letter until it had learned one thing the maker had known. [464] It was a historian's trick, condition upon ritual, and he was not ashamed of it. [479]

The weaver's granddaughter went to the loom because she wanted to bake a W. [493] The perfumer's daughter stopped burning stock and sat with three old women who remembered which petals her mother had bought, and by February the copper still was warm. [521] A boy who could not read music learned the violinist's one surviving tune by ear from a cook who had hummed it at the rockslide. [546]

Teodor himself took the emerald lens from its box each dawn and walked to the ice. [562] Madame Oren had taught him this before she died: held to the eye, the green glass cut the glare and showed the Cataract's interior, its old fractures, the dark veins where water still moved. [596] In March a vein widened. [601] He told no one, because hope announced too early curdles, but he told the bakers to fire the ovens. [620]

On the morning the Cataract broke, the grotto smelled of rosewater and bread, the two scents braided so closely that Teodor could no longer tell the dread from the sweetness, and he decided that was correct. [656] Water came through the wall first as a thread, then a roar, and the frozen curtain that had hidden them dropped in green-white slabs into the pool. [683] Beyond it, as the beekeeper had promised, lay nothing: empty cliffs, a path with no one on it. [701]

Behind him, forty households carried letters of bread to the long table. [713] Teodor lifted the emerald lens one last time and looked not at the falling water but back into the grotto, where the boy had begun the violinist's tune and the weaver's granddaughter held up a hand's width of true cloth. [753] Through the green glass, everything looked like a stage. [762] He set the lens down and went to break the first loaf, an H for his grandmother, who had known that a people is not what it keeps but what it does again. [795]
