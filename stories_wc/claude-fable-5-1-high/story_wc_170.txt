Nobody in Verrin had seen the mirage painter twice, which is how I knew the terrace was right. [18] It was the only place in the city that echoed, a long stone shelf above the harbor where every footstep came back to you a beat late, as if the terrace were deciding whether to agree. [54] Spring rain had just passed, and the parapet, cut from old prism glass by some architect who wanted the harbor to arrive in colors, was throwing wet bands of violet and amber across the flagstones. [89] She was standing in them. [94] I had expected someone furtive. [99] Instead she was perceptively calm, the way a heron is calm, taking in the whole terrace at once and choosing not to move. [122]

"You're the one with the mirror," she said before I had spoken. [134] Everyone in Verrin has a mirror, but she meant mine in particular, the one in my daughter's room that had shown, for eleven months, only the bed as it was: made, flat, empty. [167] Our mirrors are honest to the point of cruelty. [176] They show what is, never what could be, and the whole city has learned to dress and grieve accordingly. [195]

"She isn't dead," I said. [200] "She's across the water, in the fever house, and the mirror there tells her the same thing every morning." [219]

The painter nodded as if I had confirmed a measurement. [229] "Mirrors can be persuaded," she said. [235] "Not lied to. [238] Persuaded." [239] "They listen at dusk, in the hush before the bells, when the light is undecided and nobody is looking at them." [260]

She explained her method with the patience of someone who had explained it rarely and wanted to get it right. [280] A mirage is a picture painted on air, and air does not hold still, so the picture must be sung into place. [302] Adults sing badly and self-consciously, so she had spent years slipping lullabies into the skipping rhymes and counting songs children trade in the alleys, hiding one melody inside another the way a letter hides inside a chord. [339] "They don't know they're carrying anything," she said. [347] "That's why it works. [351] The mirrors trust children." [355]

Below us, in the lanes running down to the water, the evening game had started. [370] I knew the song; every child in Verrin knew it, a nonsense thing about a gull and a key. [389] The terrace caught it and returned it a beat late, and in the gap between the singing and the echo I heard, for the first time, the second tune underneath, slow and low, the shape a mother makes rocking. [428]

"There's a difficulty," the painter said. [434] She held up her wrist, thin as a reed. [443] "A mirage needs a pulse to flicker by, and mine has gone too faint to carry across water. [461] I'll need to borrow yours." [466]

I understood then why she was elusive, why nobody saw her twice. [478] She had been lending herself out for years, a little at a time, and there was not much left to be seen. [500]

I gave her my hand. [505] She pressed two fingers to the inside of my wrist and I felt my own heartbeat leave me the way warmth leaves a cup, not painfully, just outward. [533]

Then she began to paint. [538] She used no brush. [542] She moved her free hand through the colored bands the prism parapet was still casting, gathering violet from one, amber from another, and laid them on the damp air above the harbor with a grace that made the word seem too small. [580] Grace is also what she was extending, I thought; the mirrors had never done anything wrong, and she was not forcing them, only offering. [604]

The children's song rose and echoed, and the hidden lullaby carried the picture across the water on my borrowed pulse, and the whole terrace hummed in a dusky harmony of glass and voices and rain-smell. [639] Out over the harbor, faint as breath on a window, an image assembled: a girl sitting up in a narrow bed, hair unbrushed, laughing at something outside the frame. [668] Not a promise. [671] A possibility, offered to every mirror that was listening, including the one in the fever house that my daughter would look into at dawn. [695]

The bells began. [698] The hush broke, the light decided on night, and the mirage went out like a held note. [715] My heartbeat came back to me a little slower than it had left, as though it had learned something on the way. [737] When I looked up, the painter was gone, and the parapet was only wet glass. [752]

I did not look for her again. [759] I went home and stood in front of my daughter's mirror instead, and I sang the gull song under my breath, badly, self-consciously, listening for the tune underneath. [787]
