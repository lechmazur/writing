Wren mapped the world by what it told her hands. [10] Cliffs spoke in grit, rivers in cold, cities in the grease of a thousand doorframes. [25] Whatever language a place kept, she took it in through her palms, the way other people took in weather. [44] It was how she had learned Amara's mother tongue, the first night they held hands. [59]

Amara was two seasons gone now. [65] Wren had charted every coast on the continent and had not once charted the country of her own grief, because she did not know where to lay a first line. [95] It burned in her anyway, low and steady, a banked fire she had refused to look at directly. [113] Then she found the ring. [118]

It was platinum, heavy for its size, hidden in the false bottom of Amara's field case. [134] Inside the band, worn nearly smooth, a compass rose had been engraved. [146] But when Wren slid it on, it did not tug her north. [158] It hummed once against her knuckle, a single low pitch, and then fell quiet, as if waiting for the right hour. [179]

The right hour was the pause. [185] Every eleven nights the constellations, which wheeled and drifted like slow flocks, stopped for a span of hours. [203] Astronomers argued about why. [207] Wren only knew what the ultrasonic forest did in that stillness: its trees, which sang above hearing, stopped sliding through their pitches and each held one steady note. [235] Amara had studied that forest for years. [242] She had come home with sap in her hair and told Wren, laughing, that the trees were a choir with no conductor. [264]

So on the eleventh night Wren walked in. [272]

She could not hear the trees. [278] No one could. [281] But she could feel them, a pressure on her cheeks like sun with no heat, and when she laid a hand on the first trunk its language came up through her skin in a rush: root, water, patience, the long vowel of growing. [324] The ring answered. [327] It hummed against her finger, the same low pitch it had given at home, and the hum had a direction to it, a pull, the way a dowsing rod pulls toward water hidden under stone. [362] She let it dowse her forward. [368]

The second tree gave the ring a higher note. [377] The third stepped down. [381] By the fifth Wren understood what Amara had done. [390] The ring did not point to iron or to north; it pointed to whichever tree, in that frozen hour, held the next pitch in a sequence, and Amara had tuned it herself, tree by tree, over years of fieldwork, transposing the forest's silent voices down into something a blind woman could feel. [442] Each trunk was a note. [447] The path was a melody. [452]

Wren knew it before it finished. [458] Her hands knew it, the way they knew Amara's alphabet, because Amara had hummed it against her shoulder blades on nights when neither of them could sleep. [485] Six notes, a rest, four falling. [491] She had never asked what it was called. [499]

She was crying by the ninth tree and did not stop walking. [511] The pressure on her face grew and grew, the whole forest leaning its voice against her, and then, between one step and the next, it was gone. [538]

Silence. [539] Not the ordinary silence of a room but a true stillpoint, a place where the trees' frequencies met and cancelled, a hole in the singing where nothing pressed against her skin at all. [572] Amara had found it and had built a road to it out of the only music Wren could not mishear. [592]

The ring lay quiet. [596] There was nothing left for it to point to. [605]

Wren sat down on the moss and let the thing she had been carrying finally have its shape. [623] It was not a wave, as she had feared. [632] It was a country, and she was standing on its shore, and the coastline went on farther than any she had walked. [654] She put both palms flat on the ground and began, for the first time, to take its language in. [673] Somewhere above her the constellations held their breath a little longer. [684] She would be here, she thought, until they moved. [693] Then she would start to draw. [699]
