Every evening at Halcyon Aerie, Teodor played the same nocturne while the cloud tide came up through the roots. [19] The arbor grew from an iron-veined shelf of rock a mile above the lowlands, and twice a day the clouds rose like water, drowning the lower moorings and lifting the ships that were quick enough to cast off. [57] The rock's iron did something to compasses. [64] Every needle at the Aerie flickered, swinging between two or three norths, and a pilot who left before his needle steadied did not come back. [89]

Ilse's needle had not steadied. [94] She had gone anyway, seven years ago, on a survey ship chasing the rumor of steady ground below the clouds, and the anniversary clock she gave him that morning had stopped at the hour she cast off. [131] Its glass dome sat on the piano lid now, the brass pendulum hanging dead on its snapped wire, four balls that had once turned and turned and would not turn again. [162] He had never had it mended. [168] A mended clock would keep counting, and he did not want to know how far the count had gone. [187]

After the nocturne, when the last ship had lifted and the platform belonged to the fog, Teodor took his refuge in the station's shuttered cinema. [212] Ilse had run the projector there between voyages, and she had left one reel behind, a black-and-white travelogue of coastlines neither of them had ever seen. [238] He had watched it perhaps four hundred times before he noticed the splices. [251] Single frames, cut into the grain of the sea, too brief for the eye at speed. [267] Cranked by hand, frame by frame, they resolved into her handwriting on a slate: bearings, altitudes, a descent through the cloud floor to a valley where, she had chalked, the needle holds. [299] The last hidden frame said only: Come when you are done being brave about staying. [314]

He had known the route for three years and played the nocturne every night since. [329] The trouble was that the Aerie had cast him a part, and he had learned it too well: the pianist who stays, who plays the departing out and does not look up. [361] Fate, he had decided, was only a set list nobody remembered agreeing to. [374]

Tonight a freighter called the Marrow sat at the high mooring with her needle flickering. [389] Her captain, a square woman named Ousset, stood under the arbor's hanging roots with a compass in each fist, watching both of them lie. [413] The tide was already at the second mooring. [421] She had until the horizon invited night, and then the Marrow would be fogbound until dawn with a hold of fruit that would not last. [446]

Teodor watched her from the bench through the last verses of the day's light, and felt the old part settle on him like a coat. [471] Then he stood, lifted the dead clock under one arm, and crossed the platform. [485]

"Your needle won't hold here," he told Ousset. [493] "Nothing holds here. [496] But I have a course that does not need it to." [507]

He recited the bearings from the film, the altitudes, the descent, and watched her disbelief turn into arithmetic. [525] She was a practical woman; a route that ignored the compass was worth more to her than one that trusted it. [546]

"What do you want for it?" [552]

"Passage." [553]

"And the clock?" [556]

"There is a valley where the needle holds," he said. [566] "I intend to have it mended there." [573]

Ousset gave him ten minutes. [578] He used them at the piano. [584] The nocturne was scheduled, expected, the Aerie's own heartbeat; he did not play it. [598] He played instead the waltz Ilse had hummed while threading film, a piece no one at the station had heard, loud enough to carry down through the roots. [626] An encore nobody had asked for, after a concert that had gone on for seven years. [642] If fate kept a set list, it would have to cross something out. [655] He stopped mid-phrase, deliberately, an unfinished bar hanging over the fog, and left the lid open. [671]

The Marrow cast off as the last of the sun let go of the horizon and the cloud tide closed over the mooring rings. [695] Ousset flew by his numbers and did not look at her compasses once. [708] Teodor stood at the rail with the clock against his chest and watched the arbor sink into the white, its trees going gray, then to nothing. [734] He did not know if she was alive; he knew only that she had cut a road into the sea for him, and that a pendulum stopped at one hour for seven years was not grief anymore but cowardice under glass. [775] Below, the clouds thinned, and in Ousset's fist a needle quivered, quivered, and held. [789]
