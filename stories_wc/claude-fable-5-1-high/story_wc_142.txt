Mira Okafor's job title, printed on a laminated card the Halcyon Group had mailed her, read Culture Shock Absorber. [19] It meant she was paid to stand between the company's surveyors and the raft city of Lantern Reach, translating drum signals and barter customs so nobody wealthy felt uncomfortable. [48] It also meant she had eleven days before the privatization vote to decide which side of herself she would betray. [68]

Lantern Reach had never stopped celebrating. [74] Three generations ago, when the coast drowned, the survivors lashed their fishing boats together and held a festival to prove they were still alive, and the festival simply never ended. [104] Stilt dancers crossed the pontoon bridges at dawn; the ferris wheel, salvaged from a drowned pier, turned all night on tidal gears. [126] Every wall of every float was repainted weekly, because paint was the only thing that kept the salt from eating the wood, and because a carnival that stopped changing its face was just a shanty town waiting to sink. [165]

Halcyon called it a heritage waterfront opportunity. [172] Mira had read the plans on the company launch: the central rafts, where the oldest families lived, would become guest berths; the residents would be relocated to the outer ring, where the swells were roughest and the mooring fees would be, for the first time, fees. [218] Nobody would be evicted, the plans promised. [225] They would merely drift, one lease at a time, toward the edge. [237]

Her grandmother had given her the chip when she left for the mainland university: a wedge of gray granite no bigger than a thumb, knocked from the foundation stone of the drowned town hall on the last day anyone could stand on it. [280] Every founding family kept one. [285] The chips were the deeds of Lantern Reach, the only proof that the floating city had roots, and Halcyon's lawyers had already ruled them legally meaningless. [311] Mira kept hers in her breast pocket, a small hard weight against a company polo shirt. [327]

She was an orderly person; she liked lists, ledgers, the survey grid Halcyon had drawn over the rafts with each float numbered and assessed. [351] But she had grown up reading the Reach by intuition, the way you read weather: which walkway sagged, which family owed which, where the music thinned. [377] Staring at the grid on the fourth night, both halves of her mind arrived at the same thought at once. [397]

The grid assumed the rafts would stay where they were. [407] They never had. [410] Lantern Reach was lashed, not anchored; floats were untied and retied every season as families grew and feuds cooled. [429] If the founding families reorganized the city before the final survey, braiding their rafts into the center as one continuous structure, knotted through the ferris wheel's base, Halcyon could not parcel it. [461] You cannot lease a berth that has no edges. [470]

But she could not say so aloud; company interpreters sat in every gathering, and her contract forbade advising residents. [489] So she went to the painters. [495]

The weekly repainting was the one thing Halcyon's people admired without understanding. [507] They photographed the murals for brochures and never asked why a dancer's arm pointed east or why a painted fish had seven scales. [530] Mira sat with old Tomas, who had painted the Reach for fifty years, and drew her plan in the margins of his sketchbook: which float to which, in what order, by which tide. [563] He translated it into carnival. [568]

A mural of a granite wedge appeared on the Okafor float, a stone with seven lanterns behind it, and every founding family knew the chips were being called home. [597] Ropes painted in the old red-and-white said untie; a dancer with three ribbons said the third night's tide. [615] On the seventh morning a Halcyon surveyor complimented Mira on how vibrant the local art had become. [632] She thanked him and felt the chip against her ribs like a second heartbeat. [646]

The night of the reorganization, the carnival did not pause; it swelled. [658] Drums covered the sound of knots, stilt dancers carried mooring lines across the gaps, and by dawn the center of Lantern Reach was a single braided platform of forty-one founding floats, the ferris wheel rising from its heart. [696] Set into the wheel's base, in a ring of fresh mortar, were forty-one chips of gray granite. [713] A foundation stone at last, in the only city that had never had ground. [727]

Mira stood on the company launch when the surveyors saw it, holding her clipboard, ready to absorb their shock. [746] She knew the lawyers would find another way; she knew she would probably be fired by noon. [763] But the vote was in four days, and the grid was worthless, and behind her the carnival had begun a song she had not heard since childhood. [790]
