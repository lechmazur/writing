Ilse Marrow had recorded every storm over Vennick for nineteen years, and she did not intend to begin lying about them now. [22] The man from the council had followed her from the observatory steps to the edge of the market and then, less carefully, down Tanner's Lane toward her gate. [50] She had not hurried. [54] Hurrying would have told him she understood, and she preferred that he keep guessing. [68]

Now the courtyard held her the way it always did, small and walled and quiet, the flagstones giving back the day's last warmth. [91] Dusk came down in layers over Vennick: first gold on the ridge, then violet in the alleys, then the grey that made her rain gauge look like a candle unlit. [121] She sat on the bench beneath the dovecote and opened her ledger. [133] Barometer, thirty point one and steady. [139] Wind from the southwest, light. [144] No precipitation. [146] Sky clear. [148]

Seven weeks ago the council had closed the passes and declared Vennick a quarantine zone against the fever in the lowlands. [169] The provincial ministry had not authorized it. [176] The ministry had, in fact, ordered the passes reopened twice, and twice the council had burned the orders in the square and called it prudence. [201] Now the telegraph wire was cut, and the only thing that crossed the ridge was weather, and pigeons, and Ilse's reports of the weather. [225]

That morning Councillor Dray had asked her, pleasantly, whether the southwest wind might not be carrying lowland air, and whether a responsible recorder might not note the risk. [253] She had said the wind carried what wind carried, and she recorded only what her instruments told her. [271] He had smiled, and by afternoon she had a shadow. [281]

A wingbeat crossed the last of the light. [289] One of her birds, the grey hen with the pale bar, dropped onto the ledge of the dovecote and shook herself. [310] Ilse rose slowly, as she did everything, and lifted the bird down and unfastened the message tube from its leg. [330]

The tube was not hers. [335] Hers were brass, stamped with the observatory's mark. [343] This one was tin, dented, and sealed with red wax bearing no seal at all. [358]

She did not open it at once. [365] She turned it in the failing light and let herself be curious in the plain, credible way of a woman examining an unfamiliar object, in case the man from the council had found a place along the wall to watch. [405]

The grey hen had gone out at dawn toward the ministry station at Halden, due east over the ridge. [424] She should have returned by noon. [430] Instead she had come at dusk, from the north, with mud on her breast feathers the colour of the Skell valley clay. [452]

Ilse let the facts sit beside one another without forcing them. [463] She had learned long ago that storms did not announce themselves in a straight line; you read them by progressing laterally through the evidence, a pressure drop here, a shift of wind there, until the shape of the thing rose up on its own. [507]

So: a bird sent east returns from the north. [516] Halden had not received her, or had, and had passed her on. [528] Someone at Skell, where the ministry kept its northern post, had taken off her brass tube and put on a tin one. [550] Someone who did not want the observatory's mark seen if the bird were intercepted. [564] She intuited the rest before she broke the wax: the ministry was no longer sending orders to the council. [583] It was sending questions to her. [589]

The paper inside was thin and unsigned. [596] Your reports are the only honest word we have from Vennick. [607] Continue. [608] Change nothing. [610] We are reading the weather for what it tells us about the town. [623]

She read it twice and then held it to the stub of candle she kept for sealing wax, and watched it go. [645] She had already been doing what it asked. [653]

Across the courtyard wall a boot scraped stone, once, and was still. [665] Ilse did not look up. [670] She fed the grey hen a palmful of grain, returned to the bench, and took up her pen. [688]

There was a particular peace in this, one she had built over nineteen years of evenings and had never expected anyone to need. [711] A record kept alone, with no one to please, stayed true because there was no reason for it to be otherwise. [732] That was what solitude was for; she had thought it was a private thing, and it turned out to be a public one. [755]

She wrote the evening's entry in the same hand as always. [766] Sky clear. [768] Wind southwest, light. [771] No storm expected. [774] Beneath it, in the margin kept for birds, she added: one pigeon returned late, by the northern route. [792]
