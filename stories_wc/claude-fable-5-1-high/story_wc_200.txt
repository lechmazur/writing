Ilse Marrow collected lost souls the way other women gathered driftwood: early, alone, and without expecting thanks. [17] Echo Gorge held more of them than any place she knew, because the river had cut it so deep that the dead down there could not tell which way was up. [48] They lingered in the wet stone, repeating whatever they had never managed to say alive, and the walls threw the words back at them until the words wore smooth and meaningless. [79] Spectral confessions, her teacher had called them, and had taught Ilse to receive each one with a bowed head and a silence like a closed door. [105] Ilse had stopped bowing her head years ago. [113]

She carried the harp down the switchback path before dawn, its frame knocking softly against her hip. [130] It was a poor instrument by any honest measure. [139] A crocodile's tooth was wedged into the lowest tuning peg, jammed so deep that the string could never be brought true, and every note that crossed it came out bent, sour, a half-step wrong. [173] A harper would have wept over it. [180] But Ilse had learned that the gorge did not answer clean music. [192] Clean music echoed back as itself, useless. [199] The misaligned notes came back changed, carrying voices tangled in them, and it was only through that distortion that the dead could be heard at all. [225]

The tooth had belonged to the animal that took her father. [236] She had pried it from the ferry's rotted gunwale the autumn after, meaning to throw it in the river, and had found she could not. [261]

At the bottom the air was cold and blue, and the river ran quiet enough that she could hear her own breath. [283] The sun would rise soon, but it would rise hidden; the gorge was too deep for light to reach the floor directly, and dawn arrived only as a slow gold fire along the eastern rim, far above. [320] In that hush, the dead were closest. [327]

Ilse sat on a flat stone, settled the harp against her shoulder, and plucked the wrong string. [344] The note went out ugly and came back full of murmuring. [355]

Most of the voices she knew by now. [363] A woman who had let a fire go out on purpose. [374] A boy who had lied about a dog. [382] She had heard them for years and they had never once gone quiet, because her teacher's method had never once worked. [403] Solemn listening only confirmed what the dead already believed: that what they had done was grave, unspeakable, worth staying for. [423] What Ilse wanted to find, and was not certain existed, was the sound that would let a soul hear its own confession and find it small enough to laugh at. [453] Not mockery. [455] The other kind, the kind that comes when a weight turns out to be a shadow. [471]

She was not brave about it, exactly. [478] She was simply someone who had decided that the worst the dead could do was go on being sad, and she could bear that, since she already did. [506]

She plucked again, and this time she followed one thread of the echo, pursuing it down the gorge the way she might follow a voice through a crowded market. [535] It was a man's voice, and it was saying, over and over, that it had been the hat. [553]

Ilse stopped walking. [556]

She knew this story, or she had thought she did. [566] Her father had gone into the water saving a passenger, everyone said. [578] But the voice in the distorted note said a wind had taken his good hat, the one his wife had made, and he had leaned too far after it, and the river had been waiting. [613] Twelve years of ache, and he had died for a hat. [624]

The sound that came out of Ilse startled her. [633] It was a laugh, small and cracked, and it hurt going out, the way a splinter hurts leaving. [651] The gorge caught it and gave it back, and gave it back again, and somewhere in the third return another laugh joined hers, low and rueful and terribly familiar. [680]

"Oh, Da," she said. [684] "Your hat." [686]

The light on the rim went from gold to white. [696] The bent string hummed once more without her touching it, and the murmuring on that particular thread thinned, and thinned, and was gone. [719]

The others remained, the woman and the fire, the boy and the dog, but Ilse found she was no longer afraid of failing them. [743] She worked the tooth loose from the peg at last, turned it over in the strange indirect light, and then, after a moment, wedged it back in. [770] The harp would stay wrong. [775] It was the only way she knew to hear anyone. [785]
