Wren said it plainly, the way Ines had taught her to record a species count: "I have taken the post in the north, and I leave when the neap tides end." [31] Then the grotto was quiet, and the quiet became the whole of the night. [45] Ines did not answer at once. [51] She had learned long ago that nothing worth seeing in a tidal pool could be approached directly. [68] Lean over a pool and the shrimp vanish, the anemones close, the blenny slides under its stone. [85] You had to sit to one side and look at something else, and let the truth come back out in its own time. [108] So she looked at something else. [114]

The grotto's roof had fallen in some age before anyone kept records, and through the gap the stars stood in a rough circle, doubled in the pool at their feet. [144] The tide was out. [148] In an hour it would turn, and the pool would stop being a pool and become the sea again, as it did twice a day and had done for longer than the roof had been open. [184] Forty-one years Ines had come here with a lantern and a notebook to write down what lived in the interval. [204] People called her the chronicler, and she let them, though she knew the pools were only her method. [222] What she wanted was to know the texture of time itself, whether it was grained like the sandstone or smooth like worn glass, whether it came in layers like a barnacle's shell or all at once like a wave. [261] A tidal pool was the one place she knew where time could be watched changing its mind. [278]

Beside her on the ledge lay the jade cicada. [287] She had found it here in her first spring, wedged in a crack, green as kelp under the lantern. [306] A scholar from the city had told her what it was: a tongue amulet, carved to be laid in the mouth of the dead, because the cicada spends years buried and then climbs out singing. [341] Some ship had carried it, some grave had lost it, and the sea had set it down in the one place that was neither land nor water. [368] She had never taken it home. [374] It belonged to the between, and so did she. [383]

Wren shifted on the rock, and Ines felt the shift without looking, the way she felt weather. [400] The girl was waiting to be told she was ungrateful, or waiting to be told she was right. [418] Either answer would close her like an anemone. [426] So Ines watched the pool instead, and in the pool a hermit crab was doing what Ines had seen a thousand crabs do and never tired of. [453] It had outgrown its shell. [458] A larger one lay a hand's width off, empty, and the crab was measuring it with its legs, backing away, returning, measuring again. [481] There would be a moment, Ines knew, when it was in neither shell, soft and open to anything with teeth. [501] The crab knew it too. [506] It went anyway. [509]

And there, watching sideways, Ines saw the rest of it, not in the crab but past it, the way you see a faint star only by looking just beside it. [539] She saw Wren in the north, counting different creatures in colder water, growing a shell that fit. [556] She saw herself here with fewer years than she liked to count, the lantern getting heavier. [572] And she saw, further off and less certain, a woman with Wren's shoulders climbing down into this grotto with a notebook of her own, in some spring when Ines was not there to be thanked. [607] Whether that was foresight or only wanting, she could not have said. [619] But it had the texture she had been looking for all these years, and it was not grain or glass or layers. [641] It was the soft moment between shells. [648] Time was made entirely of that, and everything else was just where it rested. [662]

The crab pulled itself out, crossed the bare sand in a scramble she could barely follow, and drew itself into the new shell. [685] Ines picked up the jade cicada and set it in Wren's hand. [697] "Take this north," she said. [702] "It has been buried long enough." [708] Wren looked at it, and then at her, and the closed thing in her face began to open. [726] Below them the pool trembled at its rim. [734] The tide was turning, as Ines had known it would, and the conversation went on from there. [751]
