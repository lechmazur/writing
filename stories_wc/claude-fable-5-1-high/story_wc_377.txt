The bluff whispered because the wind had learned the shapes of the bells and could not stop repeating them. [19] Oren had hung forty-one of them along the cliff edge, above the cloud floor, and each winter he tuned them without a word. [42] He had no choice in that. [48] The cold took his voice every year at first frost and returned it at thaw, a bargain his body had struck long before words themselves fell out of use. [77] Nobody minded anymore. [80] Down in the valleys, people had stopped speaking a generation ago, not from loss but from abundance; they had found that a struck tone carried more honesty than a sentence, and a chord could hold what a paragraph spilled. [119] So the bell tuners became the truth-tellers, their carillons the symphonies of truth, and Oren, who was mute half the year regardless, was the most trusted of them. [147]

The girl climbed up through the clouds on the shortest day, carrying a crate packed with straw. [164] She set it down at his feet and opened it, and inside, sweating in the thin sunlight, was a fragment of iceberg the size of a child's head. [192] It was blue in a way nothing on the bluff was blue, a color that seemed to come from beneath the ice rather than off it. [218] She touched her chest, then the ice, then pointed south, toward the sea nobody on the bluff could see. [237] He understood: the last one. [242] Her grandmother had sailed out to break it off before it finished dissolving, and had died on the return, and now the girl needed someone to make the ice say what it was. [275]

Oren had spent his life keeping the bells honest and in harmony with one another. [290] A flat bell lied by omission; a sharp one flattered. [300] But honesty was not the whole of his work, and he knew it. [313] The children in the valleys had grown up on tones alone, and something had drained out of them: the particular color of wonder, the wide-eyed blue that came from meeting a thing too large to hold, and staying. [351] Truth without wonder was only accuracy. [357] He would not let the ice be merely accurate. [366]

He worked through the night, and the girl held the lantern. [377] From the cliff's iron frame he hung a cradle of copper wire and set the fragment in it, high over the cloud floor where the wind never rested. [405] Below it he arranged eleven of his smallest bells on pivoting arms, each counterweighted so a single drop of water would tip it into the next. [431] The ice would melt, because that was the truth of it. [442] But melting would be the mechanism, not the end: every drop would become motion, every motion a note, and the sculpture would play the iceberg's own dissolution back to it as music. [474] He tuned the eleven bells to the intervals he had heard in her breathing when she opened the crate. [493]

Dawn came sideways over the cloud floor and struck the fragment, and it glistened. [507] The first drop fell. [511] One bell dipped and rang, swung its weight into the second, which rang and swung into the third, and the wind caught the ringing and carried it down through the clouds to the valleys, where people stopped in the roads and lifted their faces. [555] They heard cold. [558] They heard a thing older than anyone alive, giving itself away one drop at a time, and they heard that it was beautiful while it did so, and none of that was a lie. [592]

The girl stood beside him with her hands open at her sides, the way people stood now when they had nothing to say and did not need to. [620] Her eyes had gone that blue. [626] Oren felt his own throat, locked shut by the season, and for once was grateful for it. [643] If he could have spoken, he might have tried to explain, and an explanation would have shrunk the thing to fit his mouth. [666]

The fragment would last perhaps nine days. [673] He had already begun, in his head, to tune the larger bells for what came after: the silence of the emptied cradle, which would need to be honest too, and which would need to hold, somewhere in its shape, the memory of blue. [716] The wind on the bluff whispered the new intervals back to him, learning them already. [731] He nodded to it, and to the girl, and went to fetch more wire. [745]
