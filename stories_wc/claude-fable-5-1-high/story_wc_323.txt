The lesson began, as it always did, with the chain link. [11] Oren Kel set it on the table where his students could see the rust blooming across its iron, a single oval from an anchor chain that surface divers had pulled out of the silt above the city forty years ago. [51] "This connected a boat to the bottom," he said. [60] "Now it connects us to the top." [67]

In Undermere, four kilometers beneath a dead ocean surface, navigation was a matter of pressure gradients, current maps, and sonar echoes. [88] Oren had taught it for twenty years, and he was good at it, and for twenty years it had been enough. [109]

Then the visitors came down. [114]

Three years ago, something had settled onto the poisoned land above and begun singing into the water in mathematics. [133] The city had argued for a year about whether to answer. [144] Oren had not argued. [148] He had listened, the way he listened to everything, with a curiosity he kept carefully level, neither leaning toward hope nor flinching toward fear. [172] A navigator who tilted too far in any direction ended up somewhere he had not intended. [188]

What the visitors sang about, mostly, was how they had found their way here. [202] They steered by starlight. [206] Not by stars as points, the way the old surface charts in the archive described, but by edges: the line where a star's light ended, the rim where sky met world. [237] Their whole geometry was built on boundaries. [244] And nobody in Undermere had seen a horizon in three generations. [255]

Oren picked up the link and turned it so the class could see its two edges, inner and outer. [274] "A horizon is a closed curve," he said. [282] "You stand inside it. [286] It surrounds you the way this hole surrounds my finger." [296] He slid a finger through the oval. [303] "The visitors do something we never thought to do. [312] They cut the curve and lay it flat. [320] Once the horizon is unfolded, it stops being a wall and becomes a line you can measure against." [338]

A student named Tavi asked the question Oren had been waiting for someone to ask. [353] "Why are you teaching us this? [359] There is no horizon down here." [365]

Oren set the link down. [370] The truth was that he was not teaching it. [379] He was learning it, one night at a time, hunched over the translated equations after the corridor lights dimmed, and he brought his students along because a navigator who learns alone learns crooked. [412]

"Because they asked us a question," he said. [420] "They asked where we came from. [426] Not the city. [429] Before the city. [432] And we could only answer in pressure and current, and they did not understand, because to them a place that cannot be found from the sky is not a place at all." [464]

The room was quiet. [468] Beyond the viewport the black water pressed against the glass with its patient, enormous weight. [483]

"The council has approved an expansion," Oren said. [491] "A shaft, straight up, with an observation dome at the end of it, above the waterline. [507] The air up there will kill anyone who breathes it. [517] But glass does not breathe, and neither do eyes." [526]

Tavi looked at the chain link. [532] "You want to go up." [537]

"I want to see what they see," Oren said. [546] "I want to stand where the curve closes around me and learn to open it. [561] I have spent my whole life finding my way through a place with no edges. [576] I would like, before I die, to find my way by one." [588]

He did not say the rest: that the visitors had also sung about how far they had come, and how long, and how many of them had started the voyage compared with how many had arrived. [624] That starlight was old light, most of it from things already gone. [636] That to navigate by it was to steer by the dead, and to be grateful to them. [653]

Undermere had been built by people who thought they were fleeing the sky. [666] Oren had come to believe they had only folded it up and put it away, the way you fold a chart you no longer trust. [691]

He closed his hand around the link. [698] Iron that had once held a boat still against a moving sea, and now, if he was right, would be the first thing his students remembered when they finally looked up and saw the world's edge open in front of them like a hand. [742]

"Same time tomorrow," he said. [747] "Bring something to write on. [752] We have a great deal of sky to unfold." [761]
