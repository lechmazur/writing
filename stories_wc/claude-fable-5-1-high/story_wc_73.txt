The staircase had no top and no bottom, and Idris had stopped counting steps somewhere past forty thousand. [18] She climbed because standing still was not permitted: the moment her weight settled, the step beneath her sank like a pedal and the whole spiral shifted, lifting the tread below to meet her heel. [52] Motion fed motion. [55] It was, she had decided on the first day, a perpetual motion machine, and she was the part that kept it honest. [77]

"Which is impossible," she said aloud, to nobody, for the nine hundredth time. [90] "So it has a flaw, and a flaw is a door." [101]

That was the shape of her optimism: grim, load-bearing, unornamented. [111] She did not believe she would be rescued; she believed the universe was obligated to be consistent, and inconsistency could be exploited. [133]

In her coat pocket rode two dice, one chipped at the corner where a five met a six. [151] They were the last things she had touched before the world became stairs. [164] Marguerite had rolled them across the table of a bar in Lisbon and said, "Double six and you come with me to the observatory tonight; anything else and you go home and finish your thesis like a sensible woman." [203] The dice had shown a five and a six. [212] Idris had gone home. [216] The staircase had begun outside her apartment door the next morning, and she had never learned whether Marguerite waited at the observatory, or for how long. [242]

What she wanted was not to go back. [250] She was too practical for that. [256] What she wanted was to hold the exact texture of that moment, the grain of it, the way the chipped die had wobbled on its ruined corner and settled on five when it could so easily have tipped to six. [296] A missed opportunity has a surface, she thought, and if she could describe that surface precisely enough, she would understand the mechanism that had produced it. [322] And the staircase, she was increasingly certain, was the same mechanism. [333]

So she wrote proofs on the risers with a stub of chalk. [345] Each step gave her the width of one line before it slid away under her, so she worked in fragments, one lemma per tread, climbing through her own argument. [374] The staircase was infinite in extent and infinite in energy, and she had spent months proving that both could not be true unless the system was perfectly symmetric. [402] Every step identical, every descent matched by an ascent, every input recovered without loss. [416] Symmetry was the load-bearing wall. [421] Break it, and the whole edifice would have to rupture, because a perpetual machine with a bias is a contradiction, and contradictions cannot stand. [445]

The trouble was that she had nothing asymmetric to introduce. [455] Her body was matched by the machine's response to her body; her chalk marks vanished as the steps cycled through. [475] She had considered throwing herself down the stairwell, and had rejected it, not from fear but from arithmetic: an infinite fall is just climbing in a different direction. [503] She was daring in exact proportion to what could be proven. [514]

Then, on a step she had no number for, she took the dice out to feel the chip, as she often did, and understood. [538]

A fair die is symmetric. [543] A chipped die is not. [548] It favors the face opposite its wound by some small, irreducible weight, and no proof in the world can make that weight zero. [571] It was the only truly asymmetric object she had carried in. [582] It was also, she noted with something very close to a laugh, the object that had produced the missed opportunity in the first place. [606] The same bias twice. [610]

She wrote the final line of the proof on the riser in front of her, small and fast: let the system contain one element whose expectation is not symmetric; then the sum does not conserve; then the machine does not persist. [651] Then she knelt, which the staircase hated, and set the chipped die on the step. [666] Not rolled. [668] Set, deliberately, six up, wounded corner down, the way it should have landed in Lisbon. [683]

The step tried to sink and could not decide how far. [694] She felt the hesitation travel through the spiral, up and down at once, a machine encountering the first quantity it could not balance. [717] The riser in front of her cracked along her chalk line. [728] Light came through the crack, and with it a smell she recognized: cold air, salt, the metal of a telescope dome. [749]

She did not know if Marguerite was still there. [758] She knew only that she now had the texture of it exactly: a wobble, a weight, a choice that looked like chance. [780] She pocketed the fair die, left the chipped one where it lay, and stepped through. [795]
