Ines noticed the things that arrived slowly. [7] The tide line at Pellam Bay had retreated a hand's width each spring for eleven years, and nobody on the road mentioned it. [30] The bakery's loaves had shrunk by an ounce a season. [40] Her mother's house had emptied itself one object at a time, her brother carrying out a lamp here, a box of bottles there, until the rooms went quiet in a way that Ines alone seemed to hear. [77] Everyone else adjusted. [80] That was the curse of it. [86]

Her mother had been a perfumer, and what Ines kept from the workbench was a single shard of orris root, pale as a knucklebone, cured for six years before it ever smelled of anything. [120] Violets, her mother had said, and warm dust, and underneath that, nothing. [132] Orris was a fixative. [136] It held other scents still so they would not run off. [147] A shard of hush, her mother called it, and kept it in her apron pocket for luck. [164]

Ines carried it now on her evening walks, the ones she took while the streetlights daydreamed. [180] That was the hour in April when the lamps along the bay road clicked on out of habit, glowing faintly into a sky that did not need them yet, humming to themselves like people who have forgotten what they came into the room for. [224] The water lay flat and coppery. [230]

She had a question she could not put down. [239] Why did some houses feel alive and others merely occupied? [249] The Halloran place, with its lights on and its television flickering, felt dead as a shut drawer. [266] The blue cottage at the end, vacant since winter, felt as though it were holding its breath to listen. [285]

Her theory, arrived at over many such walks, was displacement. [295] A house lived when something in it was out of place: a boot on a windowsill, a teacup on the stair, a bowl of beach stones where a bowl of beach stones had no business being. [331] The wrongness was a pulse. [336] So she had begun, quietly, to sanctify. [343] A cockle shell set on the Hallorans' porch chair. [352] A garden gnome turned to face the door instead of the road. [364] A child's mitten, found on the seawall, hung over the Petersons' gate latch like a small flag. [381] Nobody noticed, which was the point. [387] They only felt, she hoped, that their houses had drawn a breath. [399]

Tonight a boy sat on the public dock with his feet over the water, seven or eight years old, too far from any lit window to belong to it. [428] Ines stopped because she noticed him, and because noticing was what she did. [441]

"Are you waiting for someone?" she asked. [448]

"My dad's boat." [451] He did not look up. [456] "He's late, so the house is being weird." [464]

"Weird how?" [466]

He shrugged, the way children shrug when the answer is obvious. [477] "It's all awake." [480] "It does that when somebody's coming back." [487] "Then when they're back it goes normal again." [495]

He said it and forgot it in the same breath, kicking at the water, and Ines stood very still on the planks. [517] Behind her the streetlights hummed their small useless hymn. [526] The bay held the last of the sun like a coal cupped in two hands, red and silent and almost too much to look at. [551]

It was not the displacement. [556] It was the expecting. [560] A shell on a chair was only a promise that a hand would come to move it. [577] The blue cottage listened because it did not yet know no one was coming. [591] And her mother's house had gone quiet not when the lamp went, or the bottles, but on the afternoon Ines herself stopped, without deciding to, expecting her mother to walk in from the garden. [625] She had noticed everything but that. [631]

A horn sounded low across the water. [638] The boy was up and running before she could say goodbye. [649]

Ines walked the long way, past the sanctified porches, to the house that was hers now and had never once felt like it. [672] She did not turn on the lights. [679] In the kitchen she took the orris shard from her pocket and set it on the sill above the sink, where her mother had never kept it, where it had no business being. [712] Violets, and warm dust, and underneath that a hush she could finally hear the shape of. [728]

She would leave it there. [733] Not as a relic, but as something out of place, waiting for a hand. [747] Every evening she would notice it, and every evening it would ask her whether she was still expecting anyone, and she would have to answer. [772] Outside, the streetlights finally woke to their work, and the house around her, for the first time in a year, drew a breath. [795]
