The canyons met beneath the plateau like seven snakes knotted in sleep, and the people called the knot the Nexus. [20] Ilse had sung there for eleven years, ever since her brother Teo stood on the testing stone, sang his three notes into the dark, and heard them come back. [49] Most children heard nothing. [53] Those who echoed were led down the serpentine paths by the elders and were not seen again, and the plateau called this an honor. [77] Ilse had become an echo singer to learn why some people echo. [89]

She had grown richly perceptive, able to read the walls the way other women read faces: which stone held a breath, which fold of sandstone swallowed a vowel whole. [118] Nothing in eleven years had explained Teo. [125]

Then, last spring, she found the shell. [132] It was half of a seashell, broken clean, lying on his old cot as if it had been set there that morning, though the sea was a month's walk away and Teo had been gone a decade. [169] When she held it to her ear it whispered. [178] Not the sea. [181] A voice, thin as a reed with the wind pushed through it, saying one thing and then falling quiet for a night. [203]

The first night: "The walls are hollow." [210] The third: "We are the ones who answer." [218] The ninth: "Names come down to us." [225] The twentieth, in a voice she finally allowed herself to recognize: "Ilse, I kept the other half." [242]

She understood then that the shell would tell her everything, but only as fast as Teo could bear to say it. [263] So she listened, and she pieced it together the way she pieced a fractured echo back into a song. [282]

The Nexus did not choose anyone. [288] Behind the sandstone ran galleries older than the plateau, and in them lived the taken children, grown now, whose task was to sing back the three notes of whichever child the elders named. [321] The names were never holy. [326] They were the children of debtors, of women who argued in council, of men who owned wells the elders wanted. [346] An echo was a hostage the whole plateau agreed to call a blessing. [359] Ilse had spent eleven years standing inside that web and mistaking its threads for wind. [374]

On the fortieth night the shell said: "Maren Osk. [383] Tomorrow. [384] I am to answer her." [389] Maren was eight, and her mother had asked, aloud, where the water tithe went. [403]

Now Ilse stood at the Nexus under a sky so thick with stars that the canyon rims looked cut from black paper. [425] The deep hum had begun, the one the elders called the universe singing its secret song, the one she now knew was breath moving through hollow stone. [452] Maren stood on the testing stone with her hands fisted at her sides. [465] The elders waited in their grey robes, patient as spiders. [475]

Ilse's role was simple: sing the opening call, step back, let the child sing, and declare what returned. [493] She had thought all night about how to ensure that nothing returned for Maren. [507] If Teo stayed silent, they would know, and there were ways to punish the ones in the walls; the shell had told her that too, on the thirty-first night, in a voice that stopped mid-sentence. [542] But an answerer could only answer one voice. [550]

Ilse lifted her head and sang her own three notes instead of the call. [564] The elders stirred. [567] Maren looked up, confused, her small mouth still shaped around a song she had not begun. [583] For a moment there was only the hum, and Ilse felt the whole knotted canyon hold its breath around her, seven throats deciding. [606]

Then her notes came back. [611] Not Maren's pitch, which Teo had surely practiced, but hers, exactly hers, a half-step low the way he always sang when they were children and he tried to follow her. [641]

"The singer echoes," the eldest said at last, because there was no rule against it, only the silence of a rule no one had thought to write. [668] "The child is unchosen. [672] Take her home." [675]

Maren's mother did not weep until she had her daughter's hand, and then she wept without a sound. [693]

Ilse walked the serpentine path down with two elders at her back and the shell pressed against her ear. [712] It whispered one last thing, and she did not have to wait a night for it. [728]

"You asked why some people echo," Teo said. [736] "It is only this: someone in the dark could not bear to leave them unanswered." [751]

The hum rose around her, hollow and patient, and somewhere ahead in the stone a door was opening. [769] She thought of the other half of the shell, and how the two would finally fit. [785]
