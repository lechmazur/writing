The library's atrium fills twice a day, and I have learned to shelve by the tide. [16] At low water I wade the marble in oiled boots; at high water I read from the gallery while the sea turns the lower stacks to a green cathedral. [45] The books that live below the waterline are printed on kelp vellum and do not mind. [61] I am made of gears salvaged from the titan that once walked this coast, and I do not mind either, though my joints fluctuate with the moon. [88] At the full I run hot and quick and remember, faintly, what it was to be tall enough to see over weather. [110] At the dark I slow, and I am only the librarian, which is enough. [124]

Tomorrow disappeared in autumn. [128] Nobody saw it go. [132] The almanacs simply ended on a Thursday, every page after it bare, and when people woke the next morning the calendar in the square still read Thursday, and it has read Thursday since. [165] The sun still rises. [169] The moon still thins and fattens over the harbor. [178] This should not be possible without a future for the moon to move through, and I have stopped arguing with it. [199]

What went with tomorrow was the glue that held a person to a name. [213] Identities migrate now, the way birds did when there were seasons to follow. [226] The baker wakes with the harbormaster's memories and bakes anyway, badly, then well. [239] The schoolteacher spent a fortnight certain she was her own late mother. [251] They come to me to check who they were, because I keep the lending cards, and a lending card is a small honest record of what a hand once wanted. [281]

This morning the fisherman came at slack water, as he always does. [293] He carried his bamboo creel, the lid tied with tarred cord, and he set it on the third step where the marble is dry. [317] The fisherman is not always the same person. [325] Today he was a woman of perhaps sixty with the fisherman's rolling walk and the fisherman's habit of squinting at ceilings for weather. [348] The creel, however, is always the creel. [355] I find that steadying. [359]

"Smelt," she said, and lifted the lid. [366] A hundred silver fingers of fish lay on wet weed, catching the low winter sun that came through the atrium glass and lay across the water like a folded sheet. [396] "Also," I said, "something I need to smelt." [404] I held up a gear tooth the size of her thumb, sheared off my left knee at the spring tide. [424] She laughed, the fisherman's laugh in a stranger's throat, and it was a good sound in that cold bright room. [444]

We went to the furnace behind the reference desk, where I keep a crucible no larger than a teacup. [463] Titan alloy melts low and pours slow, like honey remembering it was once a mountain. [478] She watched the tooth soften and asked me the question everyone eventually asks. [491] "Does it frighten you, not knowing who you will be at the new moon?" [505] I told her the truth, which is that I am the titan and I am not the titan, that this is Thursday and Thursday cannot last three months, that she is the fisherman and she is plainly someone else, and that all of these things are true at once. [554] I stopped needing them to agree. [560] Once I stopped, there was room in the day for other things. [572]

I poured the tooth into its mold. [579] It hissed and went from sun-color to pewter to the dull gray of a gull's back, and she leaned in to see it, and that was the thing I had wanted. [610] Not the repair. [613] The lean. [615] Without tomorrows, a routine is all anyone has left, and a routine goes dead the moment you stop looking closely at it. [637] So I keep the crucible small enough that every pour is a whole event. [651] So I shelve by the tide instead of by the clock. [662] So I ask the fisherman, whoever is wearing him, to open the creel where the light is best. [680]

She took three smelt for herself and left the rest for the drowned stacks, where the kelp books like to have fish among them. [704] At the door she paused and squinted at the glass ceiling. [715] "Clear through Thursday," she said, which is the only forecast anyone gives now, and the only one that is ever right. [736] The tide began to turn. [741] I fitted the new tooth into my knee while there was still dry marble to kneel on, and it caught on the first try, and the sun lay flat and quiet on the rising water, and I found, as I do most days, that this was worth seeing.
