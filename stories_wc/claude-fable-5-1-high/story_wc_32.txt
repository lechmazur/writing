Everyone in Hallowmere agreed that Elias Varn was a hopeless alchemist, and Elias agreed most of all. [17] Four years of study and he had turned nothing into anything. [28] The guild had stopped charging him fees, which was its gentle way of saying goodbye. [43] So when his uncle the bishop died on the cathedral steps and the crozier snapped beneath him, the diocese did not send the broken staff to a silversmith. [71] They sent it to Elias, with a letter. [79] Mend it, the letter said, and carry it. [87] Nobody asked whether he wanted the miter. [94] Nobody had asked his uncle either, and his uncle had worn it for thirty years with a face like a shut door. [116] Elias had the same face. [121] He had learned it young: feel everything, show nothing, let the household call you steady. [136] Behind the door, the morning of the funeral, he had wept over a single white hellebore frozen solid in the cathedral garden, and then he had broken its stem and put it in his coat. [171] He did not fully know why. [177]

At twilight he carried both the crozier and the flower to the Nexus. [190] Every forge in the smithing quarter raked its coals into the same stone basin at day's end, and the basin never went cold. [213] Ten thousand embers breathed there under a sky gone the color of a bruise, and the alchemists used it because it was free. [236] Elias set the hellebore on a slate at the basin's rim, where the heat could reach it but not touch it. [257] Its petals were glassed over with ice, perfect and dead. [267] He meant to vivify it. [272] The texts said living silver could carry warmth into a dead thing, and the crozier's head was silver, and it was the only silver he owned. [298]

He shaved a curl from the crook with his knife, dropped it into a crucible, and pushed the crucible into the embers. [320] When it ran bright he tipped one drop onto the frozen stem. [332] The ice hissed and cleared for the width of a fingernail, then refroze. [345] Nothing lived. [347] Elias noted the result in his book without expression and shaved a second curl, larger. [362] This was how he had always worked, by successive approximation, closing on the answer in halves. [378] It was why the guild called him hopeless. [386] Alchemy wanted revelation, and Elias only ever inched. [394]

The second drop cleared the stem to the first leaf. [404] The third, from a curl the size of his thumb, thawed the leaf and left it limp and grey. [423] By the fifth attempt the crook was a hacked, ugly stub, and the flower had opened a quarter-inch and stopped, halfway between ice and rot. [448]

He sat back on his heels. [454] The letter from the diocese was in his pocket, and so was the penny his uncle had pressed on him at eleven years old, saying, For your ordination, keep it safe. [485] He had kept it safe for eight years. [493] He took it out and turned it over, and it slipped his cold fingers and dropped into the basin, and the embers took it with a small dark kiss. [522] Elias watched it go. [526]

Then he understood, all at once, what his halves had been circling. [538] He had been trying to spend a little of the crozier and keep the rest, to make a new thing without unmaking the old one. [563] That was why he had inched all his life. [572] That was the shut door. [577] The flower did not want a drop of the bishop. [587] It wanted the whole of him gone. [594]

Elias stood, lifted the crozier by its splintered shaft, and laid it lengthwise across the Nexus like a bridge. [613] The oak caught first. [617] The stub of silver slumped and ran down through the coals in bright threads, and the heat came off the basin in a wall that made the smiths at the far forges look up. [651] He fed the letter in after it. [658] Then he lifted the slate and held the hellebore over the burning staff, closer than the texts allowed, closer than sense allowed, and did not let his hand shake. [687]

The ice on the petals did not melt. [695] It brightened. [697] Light moved up the stem the way sap moves, and the grey leaf greened, and the flower opened the rest of the way inside its shell of frost, white on white, alive and cold at once. [733] It was the first thing Elias Varn had ever made. [743] It had cost him a bishop's staff, a penny, and a future, and he stood at the rim with his shut face and felt every one of them go, and let them. [775] In the morning he would tell the diocese there was nothing left to carry. [789]
