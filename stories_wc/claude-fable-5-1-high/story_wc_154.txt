The atrium misters came on at dusk, and Mere Talei watched the fog settle over the tree ferns like something exhaled. [21] Upstairs, a seven-year-old named Junior was asleep on a table, his arm open to the surgeons. [37] She had promised to stay until he woke, and she had promised something stranger: to keep the cast they cut off him. [59] It lay across her knees now, a split husk of plaster, grubby with weeks of playground and pen. [77]

Mere had interpreted for the hospital for three years, ever since she stopped being able to sleep in Bougainville. [96] Before that she had stood between men with rifles and men with clipboards, carrying words across a line that kept moving. [117] Words were heavy there. [121] One of them, translated a half-second late, had cost a boy about Junior's age his hand, and she still could not say which word. [145]

In her coat pocket she carried a fragment of brain coral from the reef at Arawa, bleached and grooved like a thought she could not finish. [171] Junior had found it that morning while she was explaining the anesthetic to his mother. [186] He turned it over with his good hand and asked, in the flat serious voice of a child who already knows the answer, whether that was where her brain went when her hands shook. [220] She had laughed, because the alternative was not laughing. [229] Then he had pressed the cast toward her and said that she should read it, because Mama could not. [248]

Now, with the anesthetic long since taken hold upstairs and the mist blurring the glass walls violet, she tilted the plaster toward the last light. [273] The notes were smudged past ordinary reading, felt-tip bleeding into sweat and soap. [286] But she had spent a war reading things that did not want to be read. [301]

Some were in English, in the round capitals of classmates: GET WELL, SORRY ABOUT THE SLIDE. [317] Others were in Tok Pisin, in a child's unsteady hand, half the letters guessed at. [332] She worked at them the way she used to work at radio static, sounding out the shapes until sense surfaced. [352]

Mama krai long nait. [356] Mama cries at night. [360]

Mi no laik slip. [364] I don't want to sleep. [369]

Dokta i katim. [372] The doctor cuts. [375]

And near the elbow, smaller, as if hidden even from himself: Anti Mere han i seksek. [391] Auntie Mere's hands shake. [395]

Mere sat very still. [399] She had thought she was the calm one in that room, the professional, the bridge. [414] The boy had been watching all of them, shrewd beneath his innocence, taking a census of everyone's fear and writing it down where he could keep an eye on it. [444] Nobody had told him that adults were supposed to be the ones who were not afraid, so he had simply recorded that they were. [468]

Around the wrist end of the cast, his mother had tied a length of red wool, knotted so many times it had become a hard bead. [494] Mere knew that knot. [498] Women in the camps had tied them on children before the trucks came, one knot for every prayer, because a knot was something you could do with your hands while you waited. [530]

She began to unknot it. [535] Her fingers were steady for once, and she noticed that, and did not trust it, and kept going anyway. [554] Each loop gave slowly, the way the reef at Arawa had given up its dead white branches to the divers who came years later to graft living fragments onto the ruin. [585] She had watched them from the beach, unable to explain to anyone why it made her weep. [602]

The wool came free in a long crimped thread. [611] She wound it around the brain coral, once for the boy with the missing hand, once for Junior, once for his mother crying in the dark, once for herself. [640] Not knots, this time; only turns, loose enough to undo. [650]

When the nurse came down to say Junior was waking and asking for her, the atrium had gone the deep blue of water at depth, and the mist hung in the ferns like the ghost of a reef. [688] Mere climbed the stairs with the cast under one arm and the coral warm in her fist. [705] She would translate the notes for his mother, all of them, including the one about her own hands. [723] That was the job, after all: to carry across, without dropping anything, what people could not say to each other. [743] And then she would put the coral in the boy's good hand and tell him yes, that was where the fear went, and look, something had grown on it. [772]
