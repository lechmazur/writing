Nobody had glided a cart before Petra did it, mostly because nobody had wanted to. [15] The workshop was a raft of brass and iron the size of a small town, and it floated on a sea that dissolved anything softer than a gear tooth. [44] Petra bolted canvas wings to a repair cart, shoved off the eastern platform, and skimmed forty feet of hissing yellow acid to reach the ballast tower before the bridge crews finished arguing. [76] The elders called it reckless. [81] Petra called it Tuesday. [85] She was not brave so much as steadily curious, the way water is steadily downhill. [100]

That curiosity was why the Council of Mainsprings gave her the kaleidoscope. [112] At the center of the workshop, under a dome of riveted plates, turned the oldest mechanism anyone knew: the yearning algorithm. [133] The founders had built it when the land was still drowning, a thicket of cams and escapements designed to compute one thing: what the workshop needed next in order to keep existing. [165] It did not print its answers. [171] It spun them into a brass tube of angled mirrors and colored glass, and the answer arrived as a bloom of fractured shapes that had to be read through the eyepiece and transliterated, symbol by symbol, into gear notation the machinists could cut. [214] Why the founders chose a kaleidoscope was a mystery. [223] Petra suspected they thought it was funny. [230]

Throughout the evolution of the workshop, the readers before her had transliterated pumps, rudders, a desalination coil, a better hinge. [250] Each generation the algorithm grew, because each new device fed back into it another set of teeth, and the shapes in the glass grew stranger. [275] The founders' machine had once yearned for buckets. [283] Now it yearned in fractals. [288]

Petra did her transliterating with a hunter's arrow tip. [297] It was flint, older than the workshop, older than the sea, the only object aboard that had come from a world with deer in it. [322] The acid could not touch it, and its edge scratched brass cleanly, so she used a sacred relic as a pencil and felt only slightly bad about it. [350]

In the spring the algorithm produced something she had never seen. [361] She put her eye to the tube and the glass turned over into a hollow curve, a lip, a hanging tongue. [382] She scratched it out tooth by tooth on a brass plate, and when the notation was finished the machinists read it and frowned. [405]

"A bell," said Old Marrow. [410] "Tuned to what?" [413]

Petra checked the ratios twice. [418] "Tuned to nothing anyone on this raft can hear." [427]

The Council debated for six days whether the algorithm had finally gone senile. [440] A machine built to want survival had asked for a thing with no use, a sound pitched for ears that did not exist aboard. [464] Petra listened to the debate the way she listened to bridge crews, and then she went and cast the bell herself. [485] It took the scrap from three broken carts and most of her reputation. [498]

She understood the algorithm better than the Council did, because she had spent years with her eye pressed against its dreams. [519] It did not want a bell for the workshop. [528] It had computed the survival of the workshop and found the answer was not in the workshop at all. [547] Somewhere out on the yellow sea there had to be other rafts, other founders' children, and whatever they had evolved into by now, the algorithm had guessed at the shape of their ears. [580]

The bell hung from the wing strut of her cart. [590] It needed a clapper that the acid spray would not eat, and there was only one such thing aboard. [609] Petra drilled the arrow tip, hung it on a wire, and told herself the hunter would have approved of aiming at something. [631]

Old Marrow found her on the eastern platform at dawn. [641] "You know it will make no sound," he said. [650]

"It will make a sound," Petra said. [657] "We just aren't the ones it's for." [664]

She shoved off. [667] The cart dropped, caught the updraft off the hot acid, and glided. [679] Behind her the flint swung against the brass and struck, and struck, and struck, and she heard nothing at all, only the hiss of the sea and the whine of the wings. [711] It was the most ridiculous thing she had ever done, and she grinned the whole way out. [728] Somewhere, she was almost certain, a machine that had spent a hundred years learning to want was finally being answered. [748] And if not, well, she had glided farther than anyone, and the bell rang on. [763]
