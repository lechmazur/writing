The orchard had been Wenna's for eleven years, and she had never once wanted it. [15] Her grandfather had left it to her the way a man leaves a debt, without asking. [31] The trees had been cursed long before either of them: every nut that fell took root by morning, and the roots did not stop at soil. [57] They climbed the cliff face, wove their branches together, and made of the old fruit rows a labyrinth that hung above the sea. [80] Travelers on the coast road kept cutting through it, because the road went around and the orchard looked like a shortcut. [101] After dark, when the frost formed, the branches closed on anything still inside and drank its warmth to last the cold. [122] Wenna had learned that the first winter, hauling out what was left of a tinker who had gone in laughing. [142]

Her grandfather's answer had been the birds. [149] He had built them from brass and clock-springs and set them flying the corridors at dusk. [165] The trees hated them. [169] Wherever a bird passed, the branches flinched from the metal and stayed bent, leaving a pressed hollow in the hedge like a thumbprint in dough, though nothing had touched them. [199] A lost traveler could follow the hollows out. [207] It had worked for thirty years, until the birds began to break, one by one, and no one was left who knew how to make them. [233]

The last one died on the shed floor that afternoon, wings still ticking. [246] Wenna stood over it with her hands in her coat pockets and did not pick it up. [263] Then she saw the lantern. [268]

It moved in the third corridor from the cliff edge, small and slow, going the wrong way. [285] The sky over the sea had gone the color of pewter, and her breath was starting to show. [303] She had perhaps two hours before the frost. [311]

Her grandfather had drawn a blueprint once. [318] She remembered it pinned above his bench, brown at the edges, and she remembered the winter she had burned it for kindling without looking, because she had been sixteen and furious at being kept. [352] What she had instead was his voice. [359] He had never explained anything, but he had talked constantly while he worked, offhandedly exact, the way some men hum. [379] Nineteen teeth on the ratchet or it stutters. [387] Left wing a finger longer than the right or it circles. [398] Never oil the neck. [402]

She swept the dead birds into a heap and began to assemble one from the parts of eight. [420] Her hands were cold and the work was ugly. [429] The body came from a bird whose head had rusted off; the head, from one whose body had been crushed. [449] She counted teeth by feel, nineteen, and filed a spare wing until it sat crooked in a way that looked wrong and, she suspected, was right. [475] The joins were bad. [479]

The heart was the problem. [484] Each bird had carried, in the hollow of its chest, a small nut from the orchard itself, drilled through and hung as a counterweight. [508] Give the tree something of its own, he had said once, or it will not flinch from the bird; it will fight it. [531] The dead birds' nuts had all split with age. [540] Wenna went out to the nearest tree with tongs, because bare skin on the bark was how the curse learned your name, and twisted a nut free from the lowest branch. [571] She drilled it, and it smelled green and bitter and alive. [582]

The lantern was in the fifth corridor now, nearer the cliff. [593] Frost had begun to feather the top rails of the hedge. [604]

She wound the spring and set the bird on the wall. [615] It fell off. [618] She set it again, and this time it lurched into the air like something thrown, one wing dragging, the whole body canted sideways, the ugliest thing she had ever made. [648] Then it found its line. [653] It swung low along the first corridor and the hedge shrank from it, branch after branch bending back and holding, a long soft groove pressed into the wall of leaves by nothing but the fear of brass. [690] The groove caught the last light and shone. [698]

Wenna watched from the shed roof as the crooked thing circled, because of course it circled, and worked inward until it reached the lantern and turned, and the lantern turned to follow. [730] The traveler came out at the coast road as the grass went white, a young man with a face like he had seen his own grave. [756] He asked her who had built the bird. [764] "Nobody," Wenna said, watching it come home over the hedge sideways, ridiculous, shining. [777] That night she found paper and wrote down nineteen, before she could forget it again. [792]
