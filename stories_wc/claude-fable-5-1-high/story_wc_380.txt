After the whale beaches, the harbor goes quiet in a way Nell has never heard. [15] The town stands on the sand at dusk and nobody speaks, because everyone knows what pulled it in. [33] A pocket of gravity drifted over the bay that morning, heavy as a fist, and the whale simply could not stay afloat beneath it. [57]

Nell lights the dock lanterns anyway. [63] It is her job, and the flames tell her where the pockets are: a lantern that burns straight up sits in ordinary weight, but one whose flame sags sideways, or lifts off the wick like a bead of oil, marks a place where gravity fluctuates. [108] Nobody has ever explained why it fluctuates. [115] The pockets come and go like weather, mysterious and unmapped. [125] Tonight the seventh lantern's flame leans hard toward the salt works, and Nell stands beneath it for a long time. [145]

She has a gap in her. [151] Three years ago she paid the clinic in the upper town to delete a memory, and she remembers the paying and the walking home and nothing of what was taken. [181] The clinic says deleted memories go nowhere; they are simply gone. [192] Nell does not believe in gone. [198] Everything in this town has weight, and everything with weight sinks toward the heavy places eventually. [214] She has spent three years trying to find where hers went. [225]

Her only instrument is the coffee. [231] Each morning she drinks it black in a white cup and watches the grounds settle, because in a town where gravity wanders, grounds do not settle randomly. [258] They slide toward whatever pocket is nearest, and the pattern they leave is a map of that morning's pull. [277] For a month now the grounds have made the same shape: six sides, a hexagon, the shape of the salt works' growing chamber. [300] This morning, the morning the whale came in, the hexagon had a dark seed at its center. [317]

She leaves the lanterns burning and walks. [324]

The salt works are shut for the night. [332] The growing chamber is a low six-sided room of brine tanks where the town's salt crystallizes on strings, and the crystals here have never grown the way crystals should. [361] Where the pockets pass through, the salt builds sideways, in spirals, in blooms, following pulls that ordinary matter cannot feel. [381] Nell has always thought this is the chamber telling the truth about the town. [395]

Inside, her lamp catches a thousand facets and the room fills with a gleaming silence, light without any sound to carry it. [417] Her boots feel wrong on the floor. [424] Each step lands heavier than the last, and by the center of the chamber she is bent nearly double, her lamp dragging her arm down. [449] The pocket that took the whale has come to rest here. [460] Of course it has. [464] The heavy things gather. [468]

In the center of the floor, the salt has grown over something. [480] It is not a crystal on a string but a mound, a crust, salt building on salt for what must have been years, in a place where the pull kept returning. [511] Nell kneels and begins to exhume it with her lantern hook. [522] The salt comes away in plates. [528] Beneath it is a tobacco tin, and inside the tin, pressed wildflowers, flat and papery and still faintly purple. [547] There is a strip of paper under them in her own handwriting. [559] It says: From the cliff path, the day you asked. [569]

Nell does not remember a cliff path. [576] She does not remember anyone asking. [582]

She sits on the salt floor with the tin in her lap and understands that this is where they go. [602] Not the memories themselves, which the clinic burned out clean, but the weight of them. [617] A memory heavy enough to pay for deleting does not stop pulling just because no one is left to feel it. [638] It drifts, and gathers, and grows salt, and drags down whales. [649] She must have buried the tin here herself, the afternoon after the clinic, hands already forgetting why. [666]

The flowers give her nothing back. [672] No face, no name, no cliff. [678] Only the pull, which she can feel now for what it is: not the town's weather, but her own. [697]

She puts the tin in her coat. [704] The weight of the chamber lifts as she stands, or seems to, as if it had only been waiting to be picked up. [727] Outside, the dock lanterns are still burning, and every flame stands straight. [739] Down on the beach the whale lies in the dark, and the town has gone home, and for the first time in three years Nell walks back along the water carrying something she cannot remember and refusing, this time, to set it down.
