The greenhouse had rusted into a kind of lace, and Ines liked it better that way. [16] Light came through the holes in the iron ribs and fell across the beds in scattered coins, and the glass that remained was green with forty years of algae. [45] She had cooked in this valley before the Reckoning burned the terraces, and she had stayed after, because someone had to know what the soil remembered. [71]

Ines could taste memory. [75] A carrot pulled from the east bed gave her the hands of the woman who had planted it, cracked at the knuckles, humming a song about a river. [103] Bread from the Council's ovens tasted of fear and long lines. [114] It was not a gift she had asked for, and she had never learned a name for it; she had learned it the way she learned everything, by burning her fingers and paying attention. [148]

The seed sat in a tin on the potting bench. [158] It was the size of a thumbnail, dark, ridged like a walnut, and it hummed against her palm the way a struck pot hums after the spoon has left it. [188] The Council called it a knowledge fruit seed, and they had spent six years searching the ruins for it, because the tree it made bore fruit that rewrote whoever ate it. [219] One bite and a farmer could recite the old chemistry. [229] One bite and a child could be made to love the Council forever. [242] They meant to grow it in the citadel and hand out slices like communion. [256]

Ines had stolen it out of a courier's satchel while serving him soup. [269] She had not planned to. [274] She had tasted the dregs he left in his bowl, and his memory rose in them: the weight of the satchel, the humming inside it, a taste like green lightning. [304] She had understood then what it was and what it would do, and her hand had simply moved. [322]

Now the sky was doing what old Tomas at the mill swore it would never do again in their lifetimes: seven planets and the thin moon dragging themselves into a single line above the ridge. [357] The seed would only wake under that line. [365] The Council knew it too, which was why their lanterns were coming up the valley road. [381]

She did not hurry. [385] Hurrying was for people who trusted a plan more than their hands. [397] She pressed the seed into the richest bed, where the algae-light was strongest, and watered it from the rain barrel, and the thing came up before the water had finished soaking in: a pale shoot, then a stem, then leaves unfurling with a sound like paper. [443] By the time the alignment locked, the tree stood as tall as her shoulder and was already budding. [461]

She took out her knife, the small one she used for shallots. [473] She had thought about this for six nights. [481] The fruit did its work through what a doctor had once called, over her soup, synaptic plasticity: the brain's habit of rebuilding itself around whatever it was fed. [509] Left alone, the tree would grow fruit dense with instruction, and instruction laid down that fast left no room for the eater. [531] But a cook knows what a doctor forgets. [539] Everything can be pruned. [543] Ruins were only ingredients nobody had cooked yet. [551]

She cut away the heaviest buds, the ones swollen with certainty. [562] She thinned the branches so each remaining fruit would have to reach for its own light. [578] She tasted a leaf and spat it out; it tasted of orders. [590] She cut again. [593] What she left was sparse, almost bare, a tree that would give fruit like a good broth gives warmth: not answers, but appetite, a brain loosened enough to learn on its own and stubborn enough to refuse. [630]

The lanterns reached the greenhouse door as the first fruit ripened, small and gold as a quince. [647] The captain was a young man with a hungry face. [657] Ines did not raise the knife. [663] She cut the fruit into eight slices on a board and offered him the first. [678]

"This isn't what you were sent for," she said. [687] "It won't make anyone obey you. [693] It won't even make you clever. [699] It'll make you want to find out." [706]

He ate it, because he was hungry, and because she was old and unafraid and had the stillness of a person who has fed thousands. [731] She watched his face change: not the blank of a rewritten mind, but the small frown of a man noticing, for the first time, that he had questions. [759]

Above the rusted ribs the planets began to drift apart. [769] Ines wiped her knife. [773] There would be more fruit, one at a time, and she would give it to whoever came, and it would be enough. [795]
