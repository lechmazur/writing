Marisol Vane had taught music for eleven years after the fever took the last of her hearing, and she had felt almost nothing for the last four. [27] Not grief, which at least had weight. [34] This was a flatness, a fog over every note her students pressed into her palms through the piano lid. [53] She graded their rhythms correctly. [58] She smiled at the correct times. [64] Inside, the needle of her did not move. [72]

She kept an actual needle in her coat pocket, a phonograph stylus snapped at the tip. [88] It had broken the evening her hearing finished leaving, mid-chorus of a Piaf record, and the last sound she remembered was that skip, that tiny scrape that never resolved. [117] She carried it the way other people carry a stopped watch. [128]

In October, during the faint rustle of turning seasons, when the maples along the river let go their first leaves, a student's father sent word about a drum maker who worked in a cave outside town. [164] He wanted Marisol to test a set of frame drums for the school. [177] She almost declined. [180] Then she thought of the fog, and how she could not remember the last time anything had frightened or delighted her, and she went. [204]

The cavern opened behind a curtain of dying ivy. [213] Inside, the limestone breathed cold. [218] Hides hung curing from iron hooks, and finished drums stood in rows like patient faces. [233] The drum maker, a broad old man named Teodor, spoke to her in slow, careful signs he had clearly learned from a book. [256] He had wrapped every drum head in felt so the cave would not ring while he worked. [273] Audibly muted, he explained, tapping one. [279] Otherwise the echoes drove him mad. [285]

She asked him how he tuned them if he could not hear them ring. [299] He grinned and pointed at her, then at himself, then at the floor. [312] Feel, he signed. [315] Same as you. [318]

On a shelf near the back sat a phonograph, its horn dented, its motor long dead. [334] Teodor cranked it by hand while a record turned, and she saw his wrist move the wrong way. [352] Backward, she signed, puzzled. [356] He nodded eagerly and set her palms flat on the largest unwrapped drum, its skin stretched over the stone lip where the cave's own hollow lay beneath. [383]

Then he cranked, and the reversed song came up through the drum into her hands. [398]

Forward music strikes and fades. [403] Backward, every note swells out of nothing and snaps shut. [413] Through the skin she felt a choir that inhaled instead of sang, drums that gathered themselves before vanishing, a voice that rose from the cave floor and was swallowed whole. [443] It was a song turned into a mirror of itself, and in the mirror was something she recognized: the shape of her own silence, played back to her as sound. [473]

She stood enthralled, unable to lift her hands. [481] Something behind her sternum that had not moved in four years moved. [493]

Teodor stopped cranking. [496] The cavern went still, but the stillness was not empty. [506] It hummed faintly through the stone, a residue of vibration, a filament of hush strung between her palms and the dark. [527] She realized that she had always believed her deafness was an absence, a hole where music had been. [545] The cave was telling her otherwise. [551] Silence was a string. [555] It could be tightened. [559] It could be plucked. [563]

She took the broken needle from her pocket and held it up. [575] Teodor frowned, then understood, and fitted it into the tone arm. [586] Blunt as it was, it dragged across the grooves with a rougher purchase, and when he reversed the record again, the floor shuddered with a heavier, more honest tremor. [615]

Marisol laughed out loud for the first time she could remember, and felt the laugh return to her through the drum, reflected off the walls, a hush mirroring a hush. [645]

She bought all twelve drums. [650]

Through November, as the leaves finished falling and the first frost stitched the schoolyard grass, she taught her students to play with felt on the heads and their hands on the rims, to feel the hidden song instead of hearing it. [691] She played them records backward on a borrowed machine and asked them to draw the shapes. [707] They drew mountains, tides, mouths closing. [713]

The broken needle stayed in the tone arm of the borrowed machine. [725] It scraped where a good one would have glided, and it reminded her every day that the sound she had lost had not stopped at a skip. [752] It had only turned around and was still coming back. [762]
