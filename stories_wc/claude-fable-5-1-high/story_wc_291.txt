The quarry held its mist like a held breath. [9] Ines Varga set her easel on the lip of the pit where, eleven years ago, a girl in a silver mask had been found among the cut stone. [37] She could not have told you the girl's face. [46] She could not tell you anyone's face; she had never been able to, not her mother's, not her own in a mirror. [68] What she knew were the parts that did not move: the distance between pupils, the set of an earlobe, the way a ribbon had been knotted. [94]

Detective Halloran arrived on the gravel path with the walk of a man who had stopped expecting anything. [112] "They took it," he said. [117] "Signed out of property Tuesday, never signed back in. [126] The ribbon fragment was the whole case, Ines. [134] Now it is a file with a number." [142]

She squeezed white and lamp black onto the palette in measured curls, the same order every time, the same distance apart. [163] Method was the only sight she trusted. [170] "You remember the fragment," she said. [176] "Frayed satin, three inches, tied in a left-handed knot." [185] "I remember the report." [189] "That is not the same thing." [195]

She had been at the masquerade that night, hired to sketch guests for a fee. [209] It was the one room in the world where she had felt equal to everyone else: no one could read a face there, so no one noticed that she could not. [240] She had drawn Marguerite Dunne twice, laughing, the silver mask tied with a ribbon that kept slipping, and beside her a man whose ribbon was tied wrong, the knot cinched from the left, its tail frayed where he chewed it. [280] Ines had drawn that too, because she drew everything that did not change. [293] It had never mattered to the investigation. [300] Witnesses saw faces; faces were what counted. [307]

Halloran stood with his hands in his coat, watching the fog move across the water at the bottom of the pit. [328] Once he had brought Marguerite's mother coffee every Friday. [337] Now he called the girl the decedent. [344] Ines had watched it happen the way she watched paint dry: slowly, irreversibly, from the edges inward. [361]

She began the portrait. [365] On the left she painted the mask as she had sketched it at the ball: silver, the ribbon looped and slipping. [386] On the right she painted the face from the morgue photograph, which she had studied not as a face but as a set of coordinates. [411] Two halves that had never occupied the same moment. [420] The living girl was all concealment; the dead one was all exposure. [432] Neither, alone, was anyone. [436]

"What is this for?" Halloran asked. [442] "You have a face in your head," she said. [451] "You can see her when you close your eyes. [460] That is a gift I do not have. [468] But you have stopped looking at it, and I have never stopped looking at the pieces. [484] I want to put them in the same place." [493]

When the two halves were laid in, she took the palette knife and, where the mask met the cheek, lanced the wet silver with one clean stroke. [520] The paint beneath welled up through the cut. [528] Skin came through the mask; the mask stayed on the skin. [539] She worked the seam until neither side could be told from the other, until it was one face wearing its own hiding. [561]

Halloran made a sound in his throat. [568] The mist thinned a little around the easel. [576] "She bit her nails," he said, unprompted. [583] "Her mother said she bit them before exams." [591] "Yes." [592] "I forgot that." [595] "You put it away," Ines said. [601] "That is different." [604]

He looked at the portrait a long time, brow drawn, and something in his stance unlocked, one vertebra at a time. [625] "The man with the ribbon," he said. [632] "The left-handed knot. [635] You told me that eleven years ago." [642] "I did." [644] "I wrote it down and never read it again." [653] He took a breath that seemed to cost him. [662] "Sergeant Pell signed out the evidence Tuesday. [669] He ties his boots the same way. [676] I have watched him do it a thousand times and seen nothing." [688]

Ines said nothing. [691] She had learned that faces lied and knots did not, and that the people who could see faces needed, sometimes, to be shown what they were not seeing. [719] The evidence was gone. [723] What remained was a portrait, a witness who could not recognize anyone, and a detective who had just remembered how. [743]

Halloran took out his phone, then put it away and simply stood with her in the hush, the two of them looking at the girl who was finally, in paint, one person. [775] "Marguerite," he said. [778] It was the first time in years he had said her name. [790]
