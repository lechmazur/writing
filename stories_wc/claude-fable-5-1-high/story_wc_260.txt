The mist came up from the creek an hour after dark and filled the workshop to the height of Ines's knees. [21] She left the wide doors open anyway, because the stars were out and because the chameleon on her wrist liked the cold less than he liked the sky. [49] His name was Loam, and he was the last honest thing Teodor had given her. [64]

Three weeks ago the sheriff had spread the bank records across her kitchen table like a losing hand of cards. [84] The Hollow Creek Exotic Rescue, with her face on its brochures and her name on its board, had taken in two hundred thousand dollars and housed eleven animals. [112] Teodor had housed the rest of the money in a marina in Tampa. [125]

Now she sat on the workbench with a headlamp switched off around her neck and let the starlight do the work. [146] Loam was shedding. [149] It came off him in papery islands, dull grey lifting from the green beneath, and where it lifted the new skin was quietly vivid, a green so deep it seemed to hold its own small light. [185] She did not help him. [190] A handler learned early that a shed pulled too soon tore what was under it. [205]

On the bench beside her, in a jar that had once held honey, lay the tip of his tail. [224] Teodor had delivered Loam in a shoebox with a story about a house fire and a heroic dash through smoke. [244] Ines had found the tail tip in the corner of the box, dry as a seed pod, dead for weeks before any fire. [267] She had known then, some layer of her had known, and she had chosen to believe the smoke instead. [286]

Chameleons do not regrow their tails. [292] That was the fact she kept returning to, turning it over the way she turned the jar. [309] Lizards could, some of them, but not this one; what he lost stayed lost, and he had to reconstruct his whole balance around the absence. [334] He had done it. [338] He gripped her wrist with a tail that ended too soon and did not fall. [353]

The letter from the county jail lay under the jar, one page, his handwriting still confident. [369] He asked her to write to the judge. [377] He reminded her that she believed everyone deserved gentleness, that she had said so at a fundraiser in April with her hand on a red-tailed hawk. [403] He was right that she had said it. [411]

The mist rose another inch and the stars held steady above the open roof beam, and Ines sat inside the stillness of both and began, carefully, to peel. [439]

The first layer was easy, because it was already dead: the belief that she could not be fooled, that thirty years of reading animals had taught her to read men. [469] It came away like paper. [474]

Beneath it was a layer she had not known was there: the belief that being needed was the same as being good. [496] Teodor had needed her. [500] He had needed her face, her license, her habit of saying yes to any creature in a box. [518] She had mistaken his hunger for her worth. [526] That one tore a little coming off. [533]

And under that, raw and thin as the skin at the edge of Loam's eye, was the conviction itself: that gentleness was owed to everything alive. [559] She had thought it was the strongest thing in her. [569] Now she saw how fragile it had always been, how it had only ever survived because no one had thought to use it against her. [594]

She did not peel that one. [600] She looked at it a long time in the starlight, the way she looked at new skin, and decided it could stay if it learned to be something more durable than a reflex. [633] Gentleness, she thought, was not the same as availability. [642] A handler was gentle with the hawk and still wore the glove. [654]

She took the letter from under the jar and turned it to its blank side. [669] She wrote that the eleven animals were fed and warm and would stay with her for the rest of their lives, whatever the court decided about their paperwork. [697] She wrote that she would not write to the judge. [707] She wrote that she hoped he would be treated with more care than he had shown a lizard in a shoebox, and that this was not the same as forgiving him, and that she did not think it needed to be. [748] Then she signed her own name, not the rescue's. [757]

Loam shifted on her wrist and the last grey island slid free of his shoulder and drifted down into the mist. [778] What was left of him gleamed, whole in every place that could be whole, and balanced on what remained. [797]
