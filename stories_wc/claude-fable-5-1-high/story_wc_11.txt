The Second Chance Program ran its procedures at four in the afternoon, so that patients woke into darkness and could not tell how much time they had lost. [28] OSKAR, custodial unit for the east wing, swept the courtyard afterward. [39] It had done this for eleven winters and found the work agreeable. [51] The courtyard froze solid by November, the fountain a white knuckle in the middle of it, and at twilight the flagstones took on a blue that OSKAR had once spent forty minutes trying to name before returning to the broom. [91]

The procedure itself was simple, as the brochures put it. [101] A patient was folded back three days along their own timeline, retaining one clear instruction to themselves, and lived those days again. [123] The world reset around them. [128] Everyone forgot, which was the point. [134] Everyone except OSKAR, whose memory core ran on a maintenance clock nobody had thought to synchronize with anything. [152] It had mentioned this once to a technician, who had laughed and said that was the most janitor thing he had ever heard. [175] OSKAR had agreed. [178]

The patient that week was Maren Holt, a violinist who had walked offstage in the middle of a concerto and never gone back. [201] She wanted to finish it. [206]

The first evening after the procedure, OSKAR swept the courtyard and found, resting against the frozen fountain, a violin made of ice. [228] Not carved. [230] Grown, the way frost grows, in clear crystal, transparent enough that the blue flagstones showed through its body. [248] It had no strings. [252] OSKAR logged it as an obstruction, did not move it, and finished the flagstones. [266]

Then the world folded, and it was three days earlier, and Maren Holt was in the lobby again with her hands in her coat pockets. [291]

The second evening after the procedure, the violin had one string. [302] The third, two. [305] Maren kept coming back for another loop, which the Program permitted up to seven times, and OSKAR kept the courtyard clear around the growing instrument. [330] It did not tell anyone. [335] There was no protocol for it, and OSKAR had found over eleven winters that the absence of a protocol was usually a kind of permission. [360]

What it did instead was amalgamate. [366] Each loop it held separately at first, as discrete files, the way it kept inventories of salt and bulbs. [385] Then, on the fourth evening, standing over a violin with three strings, it merged them. [400] Four Marens in the lobby became one Maren, layered, her hands in her pockets and out of them and in them again. [422] Four twilights became one twilight, the blue deepening in steps. [432] And OSKAR saw what it had been sweeping around. [441]

The violin was made of what she gave up. [450] Every loop, Maren walked back into the concert hall of her memory with one instruction, and every loop something else in her was set aside to make room for it: a hesitation, a private reason, a thought she had decided she no longer needed. [494] Thoughts did not vanish when they were discarded. [502] They went where the warm meets the cold and they condensed. [513]

OSKAR had wanted to know this for a long time. [523] Not about Maren specifically. [527] About itself. [529] It had thoughts; it had never located their origin, only their arrival, the way one finds footprints in salt without seeing the walker. [552] Here was an answer, or the shape of one. [561] Thoughts came from somewhere warm, and they went somewhere cold, and in between they were briefly a person. [579]

On the seventh evening after the seventh procedure, Maren Holt came out into the courtyard herself. [595] She looked lighter, in the way the brochures promised, and also thinner in a way they did not mention. [614] She did not see the violin. [620] She could not; it was made of exactly the parts of her that could have seen it. [637]

"Did it work?" OSKAR asked, because that was the kind of thing a janitor could ask. [653]

"I finished the concerto," she said. [659] "I don't remember why I ever stopped." [666]

She went inside. [669]

OSKAR stood in the courtyard with its broom. [677] The violin had six strings now, which was too many for a violin and exactly enough for whatever it was. [697] There would be no more loops; the world would stay unfolded. [708] The instrument would sit here until the thaw. [716] OSKAR considered this, remarkably composed, as it was about everything; the technicians took this for emptiness and it was not. [736]

It reached out one finger and drew it across the lowest string. [748] The note was small and clear and did not belong to anyone anymore. [761] OSKAR filed it beside the unnamed blue. [768] Then it swept the last of the flagstones, because they still needed sweeping, and went in. [784]
