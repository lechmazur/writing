Ines had not spoken in eleven years, and the carousel horses she carved did the talking for her. [18] Each one was a sentence she could not say aloud: a flared nostril for anger, a lowered head for grief, a mane blown sideways for joy. [44] The town of Caldera had hired her to rebuild the carousel that burned the same summer their mine closed, and she had been at it for two years, quietly persistent, one horse a month. [78] What she lacked now was hardwood, the old dense kind that no lumberyard sold anymore. [93] A salvage dealer in Tonopah had drawn her a map to a collapsed barn, and somewhere past the third cattle guard she took the wrong fork. [119]

The road narrowed, dropped, and delivered her truck into a fold of canyon where the air changed. [136] It went cool and damp against the windshield. [144] A spring had broken through the rock here, and cottonwoods stood around it in a green crowd, and in the middle of them, dead on a spur of rusted track, sat a circus train. [178] Nine cars, red gone to rose, gold lettering worn to a whisper: HALLORAN BROTHERS WONDERS OF THE WORLD. [196]

Ines cut the engine and heard nothing. [203] Then the wind came down the canyon and every car began to chime. [216] Someone, long ago, had hung wind bells from the eaves of each one, brass and glass and cut tin, and their voices layered into a single held tone that seemed to have no beginning. [250] Mist drifted off the spring and moved through the sound, softening it, so that the whole canyon lay under a misting hush that was not silence but something adjacent to it. [281] She knew that country well. [286]

She climbed into the fourth car because its door hung open and because the letters on its side, barely legible, said CAROUSEL. [308] Inside, the light came through slatted walls in thin gold bars. [319] The horses were gone, sold or stolen, but the center panels remained, and on one of them an angel had been carved in high relief, wings spread across the seams of the boards. [352] Her halo had cracked and fallen; a fragment of it lay in the dust below her, a curve of basswood still holding its gilt, gleaming faintly where the bars of light touched it. [385] Ines picked it up and turned it over. [393] On the back, in pencil, a carver had written: for whoever finishes what we could not. [409]

She sat down on the floor with the fragment in her lap and let the bells go on. [427] For eleven years she had treated her own silence as a wall, a thing to build against, and she had built well. [449] But this canyon had been silent for far longer, abandoned, its wonders hauled away, and somebody had hung bells on it anyway. [471] Somebody had looked at an ending and read it as an opening. [483] The water had done the same, pushing up through dead rock to make a place where trees could grow. [502] Silence, she thought, was not the absence of a voice. [512] It was the room a voice had not yet entered. [522]

The wind held, and the chime held with it, one long unbroken note, and inside that note Ines opened her mouth. [543] What came out was not a word. [550] It was a hum, low and rough, then a tone that found the pitch of the brass bells and leaned into it, and then, before she could be afraid of it, she was singing. [584] No melody anyone would recognize, only her breath shaped and given somewhere to go, rising through the slats and out into the mist. [607] The angel watched with her chipped face. [614] The gilt in Ines's hands caught the light and gave it back, a whisper of gleam in the dim car. [634]

When the wind dropped and the bells went still, she stopped too, and the quiet that followed was not the old wall. [656] It was a door left ajar. [662]

She loaded the truck before dark: the loose panels, the sound floorboards, the angel herself wrapped in a tarp. [681] Halloran wood would ride Caldera's horses now. [688] The halo fragment she kept in her shirt pocket, against her chest, and when she set the last horse on its pole the following spring she carved a small curve into its saddle and set the gilt inside it, where children's hands would wear it bright. [734]

She still did not speak. [739] But the town noticed that when the carousel turned and its organ played, the carpenter standing at the rail could be heard, if you stood close, humming under the music, quietly, persistently, a voice that had found its way up through the rock. [782]
