After the first snowmelt, the river found its old way down through the hill and into the bronze grotto, and Oda knew the burdened would follow it. [27] They always did. [30] She lit the kerosene lamp burner, a brass thing salvaged from a wreck, its glass chimney long since lost to the surf, and set it where the wall curved like a rib. [62] Without the chimney the flame leaned whichever way the cave breathed. [73] Above, through the split in the ceiling, the stars came down cold and exact onto the water. [90]

Oda was a fractal weaver. [95] The net that filled the back of the grotto had a knot inside every knot, and a smaller knot inside that, so that whatever scale you looked at, the pattern was the same. [128] She had been tying it for eleven springs. [136]

The first of them came before midnight, a fisherman with a stone in his coat pocket that he would not name. [157] She did not ask him to. [163] She only held out the scale, two shallow pans of whalebone hung from a driftwood arm, and he set the stone in one and she set salt in the other until they leveled. [196] Then she tied the salt's weight into the net as a knot, and the stone went back in his pocket, lighter by the exact measure of having been known. [225] That was the bargain. [229] She collected the weights of different burdens, not the burdens themselves, because she had learned that a weight recorded is a weight halved. [252]

A widow came, then a boy who had let a dog drown, then a woman whose burden weighed nothing at all and who wept hardest of any of them. [281] Between visitors the grotto settled into its shingled hush, the sound of the meltwater drawing back over the pebbles in overlapping layers, each retreat laid down on the last like slate on a roof. [315] Oda listened to it the way sailors' wives once ran their thumbs over scrimshaw, not seeing the carved ship, only feeling that something had been made slowly out of a long absence. [347]

Her own burden she had never weighed. [354] She could not lift it into the pan; it was not a stone she carried but the shape of her carrying, and every knot she tied to lighten someone else tied itself again, smaller, in her. [390] There was no door on the grotto. [397] The sea was open on one side and the hill on the other, and still she was trapped, boundary-lessly, the way a fish is trapped by water. [424] Wherever she went, she would be inside the pattern, because she was the pattern's smallest knot. [440]

Near dawn, with no one left on the path, she noticed what the salt had done. [456] Eleven springs of it, damp and drying and damp again, had begun to calcify the oldest knots. [473] They were going white and hard, ridged like the inside of a jaw, and the bronze wall behind them had taken the same pale crust. [498] The net was becoming bone. [503] It would hold every weight forever and give none of it back. [515]

She picked up the lamp burner to see better, and the flame, unguarded, swung toward the ceiling split and the stars. [536] It threw the net's shadow across the wall, and in shadow the pattern showed what it hid in thread: every knot opened inward. [559] She had been tying them all facing the back of the cave, facing herself. [573]

Oda turned the burner slowly in her hands, the way one turns a compass, until the flame stood steady in the draught that came down from the stars. [601] Oriented properly, it lit the net from behind, and she saw that a knot was only a knot from one side. [622] From the other it was a loop, a mouth, a way through. [634]

She did not cut anything. [639] She untied the newest knot, the weightless woman's, and retied it turned the other way, opening toward the sea. [658] It took very little. [662] Then the next, and the next, working backward toward the calcified heart of the thing, which she could not turn and did not need to. [687] Bone could stay bone. [691] It was the living edge that mattered, and which way it faced. [703]

By the time the snowmelt slackened and the stars went thin, she had turned the net's whole outer ring. [722] The pattern was still the same at every scale. [731] It simply no longer ended in her. [738] She put the burner down on the whalebone pan, where it balanced against nothing, and walked out through the shingled hush toward the water, carrying only the shape of having carried, which weighed exactly what she could bear.
