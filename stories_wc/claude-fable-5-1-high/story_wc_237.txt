The rock hung a hundred feet above the drowned valley, and the tea master called it a breath. [18] Not the rock itself, but the hour it lived in: the wet, heavy quiet between one storm's leaving and the next one's arrival. [41] Ilse knelt on the platform's slate with her sleeves rolled to the elbow, and the air pressed against her scars like a warm hand. [65] Everything dripped. [67] The iron kettle, the moss along the rock's lip, the crow that watched from the anchor chain with its feathers slicked flat. [89] Master Oda poured without looking at her. [96] "The ledger," he said. [100] "Before the wind returns." [104] The ledger lay open beside the tea tray, its last page blank, waiting for the storm-rune that would record what had passed and steady the platform for what came. [133] Ilse had been a scholar for eleven months. [141] She had copied four hundred runes onto practice bark and never once been trusted with the ledger. [158] She lifted the crow quill. [163] She had trimmed the tip herself that morning, shaving the barrel to a slant so fine it whispered against her thumbnail. [184] She dipped it in the skyglass ink and felt the trouble begin at once. [198] Skyglass ink read the pulse through the quill, and when the scribe's heart ran hard it crystallized, blooming into salt-white grit that seized the tip and cracked the stroke. [227] Already she could see it frosting the slant she had cut so carefully. [240] A rune is written on one held breath. [248] That was the first thing Oda had taught her, and the reason she had failed so often on bark. [267] Her breath did not like to be held. [275] It had been torn out of her once, when she was nine and the storm came early and the lightning walked across the valley floor and found her. [303] The scars ran from her wrists to her shoulders in branching lines, pale as the roots of something that had never grown. [325] She drew in the air of the hush, thick and green-smelling, and stopped. [338] The crystals thickened. [341] Her pulse was a fist in her throat. [349] Somewhere below the cloud floor the next storm was already turning. [360] Then she thought of her grandmother. [366] The old woman had believed all manner of nonsense. [375] That crows carried the words you spoke into them. [384] That a scar was a door the world had cut into you, and if you traced it with a feather and told it what had happened, the hurt would go out through the door and a story would come in to fill the space. [428] Ilse had been too old for it even at nine. [438] She had let her grandmother do it anyway, once, in the smoky quiet after the funeral of the boy who had been standing beside her. [463] She had not finished the telling. [469] Now, breath locked, she turned the quill and laid the dry feather end against the longest scar on her left forearm. [490] She traced it upward. [494] She could not speak, so she thought the words instead, in her grandmother's cadence: this is where the light went in. [515] This is where it left. [520] This is the part where I lived. [527] On the anchor chain the crow tilted its head, and a single wet feather came loose and fell, turning, into the white below. [550] Her pulse slowed. [553] She felt it happen the way one feels a kettle stop trembling before the boil. [568] She tipped the quill and the salt crust shed from the trimmed tip in a fine pale dust, leaving the slant clean and the ink beneath it black and fluid. [598] She wrote. [600] The storm-rune was seven strokes, and she gave it eight, because the eighth was the branching line from her wrist to her shoulder, laid down small at the rune's foot where a scribe may sign. [635] Then she let the breath go. [641] It left her in a long shaking rush, and with it went something she had carried up the anchor chain every morning for eleven months without naming. [668] The platform, which had been drifting a hand's width toward the coming wind, settled. [682] Oda set a cup in front of her. [690] He looked at the eighth stroke for a long time. [700] "That is not in the ledger's grammar," he said. [709] "No," Ilse said. [712] "It is in mine." [716] He considered this, and then he drank, and the drinking was an answer. [729] Below them the clouds began to move. [736] Ilse lifted her cup with a hand that did not shake, and in the last of the hush she could hear everything: the tea, the moss, the chain, the crow shaking rain from its wings, each small sound saturated and distinct, as though the world were holding its own breath and had decided to let her hear what it held.
