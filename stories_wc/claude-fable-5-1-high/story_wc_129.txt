The orchard grew where the sky had once fallen. [9] Its trees were crystal, root and branch, perched on the fused glass rim of a crater that still shimmered at noon like a held breath. [34] Yara came to it with a pack of cut marsh reeds on her back and nothing else worth naming. [53]

She was a sculptor of reeds, and reeds do not grow in glass. [66] That was the first thing anyone said to her. [75] The second thing was a question about her kin, and she had no answer, because her kin were mud and water and a village three seasons drowned. [102]

In the orchard town, kinship was everything. [109] Families were woven together in webs of kinship; every person was somebody's cousin, somebody's godchild, somebody's debt. [126] At the center of the orchard stood the arbor, an arch of glimmering obsidian older than the town itself, and once a year each family hung a token from it to say: we are still here, we belong. [164] Yara had no token and no family to hang it for. [175] She stayed anyway, sleeping under a crystal apple tree, because she wanted, with a hunger that shamed her, to be grafted onto something that could not drown. [202]

It was Tobin who found her, an old man with a limp and an unreasonable warmth. [218] He was boldly sympathetic in a way that embarrassed the town, sitting down beside strangers as if they were already his. [239] "Someone has been marking the trees," he said, and pointed. [249] On the glass trunk beside her, scratched fine and low, was a sign: three curved lines crossing, like a knot. [269] "Same mark on the well, the mill, the arbor itself. [279] Nobody knows who. [282] Help me find them." [286]

She should have refused. [290] Instead she followed him, and the search became a door. [300] To ask who had seen the mark, they had to knock. [311] To knock, they had to be let in. [319] The baker's daughter had seen it on a rain barrel. [329] The twins who tended the eastern rows had seen it glinting on a fence post at dawn. [346] Each conversation cost Yara a sliver of her fear and paid her back in names. [361] She learned who was married to whom, who wasn't speaking, whose grandmother still remembered the old words. [378]

Because that was the thing about the tag. [386] Tobin took her to that grandmother, blind and ancient, and traced the knot into her palm. [402] The old woman's face changed. [407] "That is a letter," she said. [413] "From before. [415] It meant tied, or bound, or family. [422] I cannot remember which. [426] Perhaps all." [428]

The town lived beyond the murmur of lost languages; the old tongues had faded to a hum nobody could quite hear anymore. [450] Yet someone was scratching a dead word for kinship onto every surface that would hold it. [466]

They never caught the tagger. [471] Yara suspected, later, that Tobin had never meant to. [480] She suspected other things too: that his limp came and went, that his fingernails were sometimes dark with glass dust, that he was too old to have no family and yet spoke of none. [514] She did not ask. [518] Some kindnesses are shaped like mysteries so that you have to walk into them. [532]

By the time the token day came, Yara knew forty names and the shape of every family's weaving. [550] She sat under her apple tree through the night and worked the reeds, soaking them in crater water so they would bend, and she made the knot. [577] Three curved lines crossing. [581] It was the only shape that made sense, larger than her hands, brittle and green and alive. [598]

She dedicated it at dawn. [603] The obsidian arbor caught the first light and turned it liquid, and she hung her reeds among the wool and copper and glass of families who had been here for generations. [634] Nobody stopped her. [637] Tobin stood at the back and did not look surprised. [647] The baker's daughter touched the knot as she passed and said, "So that's what it means." [663]

Yara stood a long time after the others had gone. [673] She was not of them, not yet, and the ache of that was bright in her, a light behind the ribs. [694] But a graft does not become the tree in a day. [705] It only has to be bound tight enough to hold until the sap agrees. [719] The arbor glimmered. [722] The crater shimmered below. [726] Somewhere in the orchard, on a trunk she had not yet passed, three lines waited, scratched by a hand that had once been as alone as hers. [753]
