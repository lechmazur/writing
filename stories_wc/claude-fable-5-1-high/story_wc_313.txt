The wind chime on the warden station porch had not sounded since noon, and Ilse Varga knew what that silence meant before she looked at the water. [27] Dead air, no ripple, and Harrow Reservoir turned into a sheet of glass that showed the drowned town of Cullen's Ford twice: once in the mud where the drought had bared it, once upside down in the still surface. [66] Three summers of vigilance had taught her every seep and hairline crack in the earthen wall, and the county paid her to walk it, log it, and keep people off the flats where the soft ground could swallow a leg to the hip. [109] Since the steeple had broken the surface in June, people had been coming. [122]

She took the truck up Midnight Ridgeway at twilight, the gravel road that ran the crest above the eastern shore, headlights off because she could still see and because lights announced her. [154] At the second fence post past the cattle guard, something pale fluttered, then did not flutter, because nothing moved in that air. [176] It was a photograph, tacked to the wood. [184] A main street, wooden storefronts, a mule cart. [192] Twenty yards on, another: a tavern with a hanging sign, the paint too faded to read. [208] Then another, and another, each one nearer the switchback trail that dropped from the ridge to the mud. [226] Ilse collected them as she went, because that was procedure, and she followed them, because that was not. [244]

The trail ended where the mud began, at the roofless shell of the tavern from the second photograph. [262] An old man sat on its doorstep with a bottle between his boots. [275]

"You're trespassing," she said. [279]

"I'm sitting on my own step," he said. [287] "Forty-one years I've been trespassed on." [293]

He lifted the bottle and she saw the label, a deep sapphire blue that the silt had spared, gold script still legible: Cullen's Ford Damson Liqueur. [319] "Made it in the back room," he said. [327] "Every bottle in the county had that blue once." [336] "Then they bought us out, moved us out, and the water came." [348] "Then the clerks went through and expunged the plats, the tax rolls, the road names, so that nobody could claim what wasn't on paper." [372]

Ilse had eleven photographs in her jacket. [379] She had not looked at the last one. [387]

"Why the trail?" she asked. [392]

"Because a story nobody follows is just an old man talking to mud," he said. [407] "I wanted somebody to walk down through it, picture by picture, and arrive here knowing what here was." [425] "It only means something if it's shared." [432] "Otherwise it's just mine, and I'm nearly done." [440]

She took out the last photograph. [446] It showed this doorstep, this tavern, and a girl of about four sitting where the old man sat now, holding a blue-labeled bottle far too big for her hands, laughing at whoever held the camera. [481] She knew the shape of the girl's jaw because she saw it in her own mirror every morning. [499]

"Your mother worked my bar," he said. [506] "You'd sit right there while she closed up." [514] "When I heard the county hired a Varga to watch the levee, I thought, well, that's either a joke or a mercy." [536]

She had no memory of it. [542] Her mother had never said the town's name, and Ilse had grown up believing they came from nowhere in particular. [562] But standing in the doorway, with the reservoir mirroring the broken walls so perfectly that the town seemed whole again, something surfaced in her that was not quite a memory and not quite an invention: the smell of damsons cooking down, a woman's voice counting coins. [608]

Procedure said: remove the trespasser, confiscate salvage, log the incident with name and time. [622] Ilse was precise about procedure. [627] She was also, in the way that had kept her in this job when others burned out shouting at tourists, precisely humane about it. [651] She helped him up by the elbow, tested each step of the trail before she let him take it, and walked him to the truck as the last light went out of the sky. [685]

In the log she wrote: one individual escorted from flats, no injury, no salvage recovered. [700] The bottle rode home in his lap. [707] The town had been expunged once by people with stamps and rulers; she would not do it a second time with a pen. [730] The photographs she kept, all eleven, and asked him to come to the station on Sunday and tell her what each one was. [753]

On the drive back the wind found the ridge again. [763] Behind them the mirror broke into a thousand moving pieces, and the town went back under, but the chime on the porch was ringing when she pulled in. [791]
