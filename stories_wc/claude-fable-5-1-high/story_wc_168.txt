The eclipse was scheduled for 11:40, and Ines Calder had never once in twenty-two years of fire been on time for anything that was not burning. [26] Tonight she was early. [30] She stood where the ridge path bent above Harlow Creek, the moon full and white over the black spines of trees the fire had left standing, and waited for the boy. [61] He was not a boy anymore. [67] Dario Reyes was nineteen and six weeks into his first season on her crew, and he climbed the switchbacks toward her with the eager, apologetic gait of someone who still thought a chainsaw could be carried politely. [104]

"You said bring nothing," he said. [110] "I brought nothing." [113]

"Good." [114]

She had brought one thing. [119] It sat in the cargo pocket of her Nomex pants, wrapped in a bandana: a porcelain box the size of a fist, white glaze gone the color of old teeth, a crack running from lid to base like a river on a map. [162] She had carried it out of the ash of a garden shed seven years ago, the morning after the Harlow Fire jumped the creek and took the left side of her face and the whole of Marcus Bell. [200] Inside it, then and now: the cardboard sleeves of six bottle rockets and a twelve-year-old's lighter shaped like a dolphin. [220]

The official report said lightning. [225] Dario's father had made sure it said lightning, with the quiet arithmetic of a man who could afford good attorneys and a bad conscience. [249] Ines had let it. [253] That was her half of the bargain, made alone in a hospital bed with her skin still weeping under the gauze: the boy would keep his childhood, and she would keep the box. [286] Innocence purchased is still innocence, she had told herself. [295] She had believed it until the day he showed up at the station with a duffel bag and his father's jaw, asking to fight fire. [320]

"Sit," she said, and he sat on the rock beside her. [331] Above them the moon's edge had begun to dull, the earth's shadow sliding over it slow as breath. [349]

"You know why crews watch the sky," she said. [358]

"Weather." [359]

"Partly. [360] Also because fire doesn't have a language, and the sky does. [371] Fire just has heat. [375] Everybody understands heat the same way, in every country, in every skin." [387]

She touched the ridge of scar tissue that ran from her temple to her collar, not for effect, but because it ached in the cold, and she had long ago stopped hiding what ached. [421] Dario looked at the scar the way the rookies all did, sideways, and then, to his credit, straight on. [440]

"Harlow," he said. [443] "You were here." [446]

"I was here." [449]

She unwrapped the bandana and set the porcelain box on the granite between them, where the failing moonlight found the crack and filled it. [473] He did not touch it. [478] She watched recognition climb his face the way flame climbs a ladder fuel, from the ground up, fast, and then the terrible stillness at the crown. [504]

"My mother's," he whispered. [508] "It was in the shed." [513]

"It was in the shed," she said. [520] "I've had it seven years. [525] I have thought about it more than I have thought about God." [537]

His hands were shaking. [541] "Are you going to turn me in?" [548]

"No." [549]

"Then why--" [551]

"Because you came to my station," she said, "and I can't teach you to read a fire if you're spending half your mind not looking at one. [578] Your father bought you a story. [584] I sold you one. [588] We both meant well and we both left you deaf. [598] You've been carrying something with no words for it, and I know exactly what that weighs, because so have I." [618]

The moon was half gone now, the color of a bruise healing. [630] Below them the burn scar stretched for miles, but in the low light she could see the pale fuzz of new growth, ceanothus and lupine, coming up through the ash in a way that did not forgive anything and did not need to. [673]

"Marcus Bell had a daughter," she said. [680] "I'm not telling you that to crush you. [688] I'm telling you because pain that stays private turns to rot, and pain that gets spoken plain turns into something else. [709] Fuel, maybe. [711] Something you can work with." [716]

He was crying without sound, which she respected. [724] "What do I do with it?" [730]

She picked up the box, and pressed it into his hands, and held them closed around it with her own, the ruined one and the whole one. [757]

"You carry it," she said. [762] "Same as me. [765] Only now you know what it is." [772]

Above them the last sliver of moon went dark, and the stars came out all at once. [789]
