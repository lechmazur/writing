The anesthesia let go of Ilse the way a tide lets go of a stone, slowly, then all at once. [20] She was in the tower room, the round one at the top of the old convent infirmary, and it was dawn. [41] The window held a sky the color of watered milk, and the last Perseid of the night drew a thin line across it and went out. [67] Nothing in the room moved after that. [74] It was the kind of hush that follows a falling thing, when the air seems to be waiting to learn whether it should be afraid. [99] Then the ivy on the windowsill said that its roots had found a crack in the mortar and were tunneling toward the greenhouse. [122] Ilse lay very still. [126]

She had come to this town to get closure, which was the word she used for it. [143] Her sister Maren had a different word. [150] Every few years Ilse arrived with a list of old grievances and a need to talk each one through to the bone, and every visit ended with something bleeding that had nearly healed. [183] This time she had not even reached Maren's door. [192] A cyclist, a curb, a splintered wrist, and now a bandage she could feel throbbing under the blanket. [210]

The ivy said the water in the greenhouse tasted of iron and of a woman who wept into the beds at night. [232] Ilse turned her head. [236] On the sill beside the ivy sat an hourglass, its glass frosted so densely by the cold that she could not see how much sand remained in either bulb. [265] She knew it. [268] It had stood on their mother's dresser, and Maren had taken it after the funeral without asking, which was item four on a list Ilse no longer needed to consult. [298] So Maren had been here. [303]

The nightstand confirmed it. [307] Ilse studied the coffee rings on its surface the way she had once watched Maren study the cross-section of a felled elm, counting years by the stains. [334] Seven rings, overlapping, the outer ones pale and the inner ones dark where a cup had been set down again and again in the same place by someone too tired to look. [366] Seven hours. [368] The whole night, then, while the meteors fell. [376]

The fern on the far shelf, which had a slower voice than the ivy, said that the weeping woman had also asked the sky for something. [402] She had asked, on every bright streak for a year, to understand what the plants were telling her, because she believed they were telling her how to stop dying. [431] Ilse felt the hush deepen into something almost like shame. [441] Maren was ill. [444] Maren had not said so, in any of the letters Ilse had answered with accusations. [459] Maren had asked the sky for a small, exact miracle, and the sky, careless as a cyclist, had dropped it into the wrong sister while she was under. [487]

The ivy did not think this was a mistake. [496] Roots, it explained, do not go where they are aimed. [506] They go where the wall is already broken. [514]

Ilse looked at her bandaged wrist and thought about scars. [524] She had always treated them as arguments to be reopened until the truth ran clear. [539] The plants treated them as text. [545] A graft line, a pruning cut, a place where a branch had been lost to frost and grown around: each was a sentence in a story that could be read only by someone willing to leave it closed. [583] She wanted, suddenly and with her whole body, to learn that reading. [595] Not to translate for Maren, but to sit beside her sister's scars and understand them without cutting. [612]

The stairwell door opened. [616] Maren stood there with an eighth cup of coffee, thinner than Ilse remembered, a soft cap pulled down over what the fern quietly confirmed was no hair at all. [645] She saw Ilse awake and set the cup down on the nightstand, in the same place, and the ring it would leave began at once to form. [672] Neither of them spoke. [676] The frosted hourglass sat between them, hiding its sand. [685]

Ilse opened her mouth to ask about item four, felt the old blade of the question against her tongue, and put it away. [708] Instead she said, "The ivy is tunneling toward your greenhouse. [718] It says the water tastes of iron. [725] It says you should stop crying into the beds, because the roots are getting the salt." [741]

Maren's hand went to the sill and found the hourglass and held it. [754] Outside, the sky finished turning from milk to pearl, and the hush that had been waiting to learn whether it should be afraid decided, for the moment, that it did not need to be. [788]
