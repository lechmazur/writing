The rain stopped an hour before the sun was due, and Wren knew it by the way the silence changed. [20] In the sanctuary, silence was not one thing. [28] There was the silence of the dormitory, thick as wool. [38] There was the silence of the scriptorium, where she spent her days inking hours onto clock faces that would never tick. [59] And there was this: the glacial hush that came after rain, when the whole mountain seemed to hold its breath and water began to move through the stone. [87] She dressed without a lamp. [92] Twenty years had taught her hands the cloth, the cord, and the small wooden box beneath her cot, and she lifted the box now and bore it against her chest the way one carries something that might wake. [130] The corridor was dark and smelled of wet slate. [139] No one else stirred; the order rose with the visible sun, and the sun would not be visible for some time yet. [161] It rose behind the glacier, always, so that dawn here was never a disc but a rumor, a gray brightening that seeped over the ice and made the world pale without ever showing its source. [196] Wren had come to love the hidden sunrise. [204] It suited a person who preferred to be felt rather than seen. [216] The forecourt opened before her. [221] Its paving was crystal, quarried from the glacier's throat by founders who had believed that silence should be beautiful, and cut with channels no wider than a finger. [249] After rain, water threaded through those channels and the crystal whispered. [260] It was the only sound the order permitted, because no human hand made it. [274] She stood at the edge and let it reach her: a thin, high, shifting murmur, like a room full of people trying not to speak. [299] The air was rich with petrichor, that scent of rain on old stone that had always, since childhood, smelled to her like a door left open somewhere behind her. [328] She knelt and set the box on the wet crystal and opened it. [341] Inside lay a bell. [345] It was no larger than a pear, bronze gone green at the lip, and she had cast it herself at nineteen in her father's foundry in the lowlands, the last thing she made before she climbed the mountain and took the vow. [387] She had meant to be a founder of bells. [396] She had meant to fill valleys with sound. [404] Then her father died, the foundry went to a cousin, and a girl who had never liked crowds discovered that she liked grief in crowds even less. [431] The sanctuary had promised no music, no speech, no visitors. [441] It had sounded, at the time, like rest. [449] It had been rest. [453] That was the difficulty. [457] She had not been unhappy here. [463] She had only carried this one thing up the mountain and never put it down, and a weight borne that long stops feeling like a weight and starts feeling like a bone. [495] She had never rung it. [500] Not once. [502] To ring it would have broken the vow, and to break the vow would have meant she had chosen wrongly, and she did not believe she had. [529] But to keep it unrung was to keep saying, every night, not yet. [542] The light was growing. [546] The glacier above the sanctuary went from black to ash to the faint blue of skimmed milk, and the whispering in the crystal rose with it, as meltwater joined rainwater in the channels. [579] Wren lifted the bell. [583] She did not swing it. [588] She lowered it, mouth down, into the widest channel, excruciatingly gentle, so that the bronze lip touched the running water and nothing else. [611] The bell did not ring. [616] But the water did. [620] Moving over the lip, it took on a tone, a single low hum beneath the crystal's whisper, not made by her hand, not forbidden, and the whole forecourt seemed to gather around it the way a held breath gathers around the moment before release. [664] She stayed there until her knees ached and the light over the ice was nearly white. [680] She understood, kneeling, that she had never abandoned the dream so much as refused to let it finish. [698] Now it had finished. [702] It had made one sound, in a place that allowed no sound, at a sunrise that showed no sun, and it had been enough. [726] She left the bell in the channel. [733] Someone would find it and wonder, and perhaps the order would let the water keep it, and perhaps not. [752] She went back to the scriptorium with empty hands and rain in her hair, sat down to ink the hours, and for the first time in twenty years the silence did not feel like something she was holding up.
