Odile called herself a sea-foam recollectionist because "memory scavenger" made people clutch their coats. [14] The work was simple and grim and she loved it. [24] Each dawn the tide shoved through the fissure at the bottom of the geode, and the foam that gathered there carried whatever the drowned and the grieving had let slip into the water. [57] She scooped it into bowls of crater glass before it could burst, because foam does not wait and neither does memory. [78] The glass had come from a meteor strike far inland, black and fused and pitted, and it did one trick that ordinary glass would not: it transliterated emotions. [106] Sorrow settled against the bowl's inner curve as a fine slanting script; guilt came out in knots; a good ordinary happiness looked like rain. [130] Odile copied the script into her ledgers, and once a season she carried the ledgers up the cliff to the village, where people paid her to know what their dead had been feeling when they went. [166]

It was cheerful work, in its way. [173] Most of the dead had been thinking about lunch. [182]

The geode itself was the trouble. [188] She lived inside a crystal the size of a chapel, cracked open by some old quake and lined with amethyst and something paler that threw the lamplight back in oil-slick colors, so that every wall seemed to be quietly deciding what it would be. [232] Passages ran off from the main chamber like the ribs of a fan. [245] When she had first come there were eleven. [253] Now there were seven. [257] A path did not collapse or fill with rubble; it simply was not there in the morning, the crystal grown smooth across the mouth of it as if the corridor had been a rumor she had started herself. [295]

And the recollections had begun to arrive with missing pieces. [305] Not smudged, not faded. [309] Missing, the way a tooth is missing, with the script running along perfectly and then a clean gap and then running along again as though nothing had happened. [337] A fisherman's last hour: cold, cold, the boat, his daughter's name, gap, salt. [350] She hated the gaps. [354] She had the odd, stubborn feeling that the gaps and the vanished paths were the same thing wearing two coats, and she wanted to know why, before the foam stopped telling her anything at all. [389]

The bird gave her the answer, or the shape of one. [400] Every autumn a single grey migrant came down the fissure to drink from the tide pool and rest a night on the ledge above her bowls. [426] It left seeds behind it, small dark ones from some country she would never see, scattered wherever it hopped. [445] This year she watched where they fell. [452] Most rolled harmlessly into cracks and did nothing. [460] But three of them, by the wall where the eighth passage had been, did not stay seeds. [477] By morning they had sunk into the crystal like stones into pudding, and around each one the amethyst had gone briefly dark, then paler, then healed. [503] The stone was soft there. [508] Only there. [510]

Odile sat on her heels and let herself conjecture, which was the recollectionist's one true skill after scooping. [528] The geode was not sealing the passages. [535] It was eating. [538] Every gap in every ledger was a mouthful that had never reached her bowls, drawn out of the foam through the walls into the crystal, and the crystal, fed on other people's last hours, grew. [573] It grew where the eating was richest, over the passages nearest the water, and it was still tender there, tender enough to take a seed the way a wound takes grit. [604] The paths disappeared because the house was full. [612] The fisherman's gap was somewhere in the wall behind her, chewed and kept. [625]

She waited to feel horror and felt, instead, a kind of hilarity. [637] She had spent six years living inside a stomach and complaining about the draft. [651] "Well," she said to the bird, which was asleep, "that is where the daughter's name went." [667] She pressed her palm to the soft place and could not swear it was not warm. [683]

In the ledger that night she recorded the fisherman's hour a second time, and in the gap she wrote: here, in the wall, three seeds. [708] It was not the name, but it was not nothing, and she had learned long ago that people will take a reason over a blank almost every time. [736] Then she went and stood in the mouth of the seventh passage, the one nearest the tide pool, and counted her steps to the end of it and back, so that when it was gone she would at least have that. [777] Forty-one steps. [779] She wrote that down too, before anything could eat it. [789]
