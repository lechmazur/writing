Wren Tolliver had played the Corvus Gorge bridge for eleven summers, and she knew its every mood. [17] Tourists called her the bridge-song fiddler because she tuned to the cables, letting the wind's hum pick the key. [36] This year the county had wrapped the whole span in white scaffold sheeting for the retrofit, and the sound had changed. [57] So had she, though she could not have said how until the hitchhiker. [70]

He stood at the switchback below the pass with a wooden camera case, an old man in a coat too heavy for August. [93] She never picked anyone up. [98] She picked him up. [102]

He said nothing for six miles, then asked her to stop at the scenic pullout above the gorge. [120] "Watch the wrap when the wind comes up," he said, looking down at the shrouded bridge. [136] "It tells where I put things." [142]

She turned to ask what things. [148] The passenger seat was empty, the door shut, no footsteps on the gravel, no figure on the road in either direction. [169] On the seat lay a flake of glass no bigger than a guitar pick, dark on one side with the tarry sheen of wet-plate collodion. [194]

She knew that sheen. [198] Her father had made such plates in a darkroom tent before he walked out when she was nine, before her mother burned the negatives and said the word never so many times it turned to ash in the house. [237] Held to the light, the flake showed a fragment of image: a small hand gripping a fiddle bow. [255]

Wren did not scream or pray. [261] She did what a bridge-song fiddler does, which is watch. [271] Eleven summers of reading crowds, gusts, and the sway of a deck under a hundred feet had made her dynamically observant, a person who tracked change rather than things. [300]

The evening wind came up the gorge at seven, as it always did. [313] The sheeting stirred. [316] Most panels stayed taut. [320] Nine of them fluttered, loose at one corner, snapping in a rhythm that was not random. [336] She sketched their positions on the back of a parking receipt: third bay, seventh, twelfth, the pattern of a grid with holes in it. [360] A secret mapped by scaffold wrap flutters is still a map. [371]

At dawn, before the crews arrived, she climbed. [379] Inside each fluttering panel, taped to the aluminum where a loosened zip tie let the wind in, was another flake of glass. [401] Nine flakes plus the one from her seat made ten. [411]

She laid them out on the picnic table at the pullout, fitting edges by feel, the way she fit phrases of a tune. [434] The plate had been a portrait, shattered on purpose into a grid of squares, and half the squares were missing. [454] What remained resolved anyway, the way a coarse image does if you stop trying to see it and let it arrive. [475] A girl on the old unwrapped bridge, fiddle at her chin. [486] At the edge, a man's shadow with a camera hood, present but not intruding. [500] Scratched into the emulsion along the bottom, letters interrupted by the gaps: I WAS WRO G. PLA ANYWAY. [518]

It was, she thought, a pixelated amnesty. [525] Forgiveness in squares, with holes where the missing pieces should be, legible only if she agreed to fill the blanks herself. [546] She had never been offered anything to fill. [554] Her mother had made sure of that; never was a complete sentence in their house. [569]

She called her mother from the pullout, where the signal was one bar and the view was everything. [587] She recounted it plainly: the switchback, the coat, the six silent miles, the empty seat, the flutters, the glass. [606] She did not editorialize, because she did not yet know what she believed. [619]

Her mother was quiet for a long time. [627] "He died in March," she finally said. [634] "A lawyer wrote. [637] I didn't tell you." [641]

Wren looked at the ten squares glowing on the table, at the empty places between them, and felt something that was not grief and not joy but a disbelief so bright it warmed her hands. [676] Whoever had ridden six miles beside her, whatever he had been, he had climbed a wrapped bridge in the dark to hide forgiveness where only she would look. [704] That was not nothing. [708] That was a door in a wall her whole life had called solid. [721]

"There are pieces missing," she told her mother. [729] "I think that's the point. [734] I think we're supposed to make the rest." [742]

That evening she carried her fiddle onto the shrouded span and tuned to the cables. [757] The nine loose panels snapped their rhythm, and she played into it, building a tune from the gaps, sculpting possibility from the ashes of never. [782] Nobody who heard it could have said what key it was in. [794]
