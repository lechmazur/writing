Odalys Ferran read every envelope twice before opening it, a habit from twenty years of brokering cargo on Quay Six, where a misread digit could send forty crates of citrus to the wrong continent. [34] This one was addressed to T. Marrow, Reading Room, Quay 6, in a hand so eager the ink had bled. [54] There was no reading room on Quay Six. [62] There was, however, a library on Quay Nine, and a nine written quickly looks like a six to a tired postal clerk. [84]

She should have forwarded it. [89] Instead she weighed it in her palm, felt something small and hard shift inside, and told herself a broker was entitled to inspect misdirected freight. [114]

The something was a brass locket. [120] Inside, a photograph no bigger than a thumbnail: a woman on a dock, hair whipped sideways, gripping a library book against her chest like a life vest. [147]

The letter was short. [151] "You will not remember me. [156] Thirty years ago I owed a fine I could not pay. [167] You left this locket on the returns cart with a note saying the fine was forgiven and that I should pass the photo on when I could. [194] I finished school because of that library. [201] I never learned who the woman was. [208] It is time she went home." [214]

Odalys, who had spent her career taking a percentage for connecting people who never met, felt an unfamiliar tightness in her throat and distrusted it immediately. [240] Still, she decided to deliver it by hand. [248]

The Quay Nine library kept strange hours in summer. [257] Its roof had been torn off in a gale a decade earlier, and rather than replace it the town had strung lanterns between the surviving beams and called it a feature. [288] On concert nights the harbor symphony played on the pier beside it, and the library stayed open until the last note, because people wandered in afterward with music still in their shoulders and wanted something to hold. [325]

Odalys arrived as the final chord dissolved over the water. [335] The wind came straight off the sea and every page in the building lifted and settled like breathing. [353] Stars showed through the beams. [358] On each table sat a brass paperweight shaped like a different fish. [370]

T. Marrow turned out to be Teodor, a narrow man with reading glasses pushed into gray hair, who was at that moment tucking a pressed strip of seaweed into a western novel. [402] "For Anselm," he said, before she asked. [409] "He runs the tug. [413] He likes cowboys but he misses the sea while he reads, so I give him a little of it back." [433] Then he looked at her salt-stiff cuffs and said, "You have been on the water all day, and you have not eaten." [455] He produced a tin of biscuits from behind the returns desk as if this were standard library procedure. [473]

She gave him the envelope. [478] He read the letter, opened the locket, and sat down on a step stool. [492] "My mother," he said. [496] "She could not read until she was thirty. [504] A dockhand taught her on his lunch breaks and never told anyone. [516] I gave the locket away because she would have wanted it working, not sitting in a drawer." [533]

Odalys thought about the dockhand. [538] She thought about Anselm on his tug, and the woman on Quay Two who mended nets for widows and charged nothing, and the night watchman who left his thermos for the boy who slept under the customs shed. [576] She knew them all. [580] A broker knows who moves what along a waterfront, including the things that never appear on a manifest. [598] None of them had ever been thanked, because none of them had ever been seen. [613]

"I want to propose something," she said, and heard her own voice come out careful, the voice she used for contracts. [634] "A ledger. [636] Not of debts. [639] I know who on this harbor gives quietly, and you know what each of them would want. [656] We route it. [659] Anonymously, the way this came." [664] She tapped the locket. [668] "A trial. [670] Three months. [672] I will audit it like anything else." [679]

Teodor was already smiling in a way that made the caution feel ridiculous. [692] "Who first?" he asked. [696] "The dockhand," she said. [700] "If he is still alive, he should know she learned." [710]

The wind turned a page somewhere behind them. [718] Odalys realized she was grinning and made a mental note to review that later, in daylight, with a clear head. [738] Then she took a biscuit, and Teodor closed the locket, and under the open beams the two of them began writing names. [760]
