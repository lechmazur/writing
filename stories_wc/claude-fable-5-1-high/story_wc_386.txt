Marit Halvorsen had spent twenty years diving wrecks and listening to what the drowned would not say aloud. [18] The dead beneath the Skjold glacier said nothing at all. [28] Forty meters down, the ferry Aurland lay on her side, her passengers locked into a silence Marit had never met in any warmer sea. [52] So they found another door. [57]

The village of Vinterfjord had carved a shrine into the glacier's face, a low chamber where the ice went blue and then black, lit by candles set in niches along the walls. [89] The flames did not seem to belong to the candles. [99] They looked borrowed, like starlight arriving long after the star had gone out. [112]

People came there to tell Marit their dreams. [120] Not dreams about the wreck; the villagers had learned quickly that she wanted the other kind. [136] A fisherman dreamed of mending nets, and a stranger in a galley apron stood behind him the whole time, clicking a lighter that would not catch. [162] A schoolteacher dreamed of her wedding, and at the back of the church a man in engineer's coveralls pressed both hands over his ears. [186] Nobody ever recognized these visitors, and nobody's dream was about them. [197] They were cameos, walk-ons, faces in a crowd, and Marit understood that this was the only aperture the frozen dead could squeeze through. [220] A sleeping mind was a window with the latch left open. [231]

She kept a ledger by candlelight, cross-referencing every appearance: who wore what, who carried what, who faced the bow and who faced the stern. [255] The lighter appeared eleven times. [260] The covered ears appeared nine. [265] And in six separate dreams, a small girl in a yellow coat stood shaking a snow globe, watching the flakes settle with the patience of someone who had all the time in the world. [299]

The shipping line's report said the Aurland struck ice because Captain Eivind Roald had taken her too close to the glacier in fog. [322] His widow, Solveig, lit the candles every evening and never asked Marit what the ledger said. [338] But a lighter that would not catch, and an engineer hiding from a sound, told a different story than fog. [358]

Marit dived on the third calm day. [365] The galley was scorched black, paint blistered, ceiling tiles sagging like wet paper. [378] Behind the galley bulkhead ran a fuel line with hasty welds, rerouted the previous winter to spare the company a proper refit. [400] Fire, then an explosion the engineer had heard coming, then a hull breach that had nothing to do with ice. [420] On the galley floor, wedged beneath an overturned bench, she found the snow globe. [434] The heat had gotten to it before the sea did. [444] Its plastic dome had slumped sideways, the little painted village inside tilted as if sliding downhill, the glitter fused into a single frozen drift. [468] She carried it up through forty meters of dark, and it felt heavier than any anchor. [484]

That night the shrine was crowded. [490] The shipping line had sent a man in a good coat to read a statement, and Solveig stood beside him because she had been asked to. [516] Marit could have exposed everything in one breath. [524] Instead she set the melted globe on the ice ledge among the candles and let the villagers look at it. [544] Then she spoke slowly, the way she always did with truths that could cut. [558] She told them about the cook who had clicked his lighter at a stove that would not light, not knowing why the air smelled wrong. [583] About the engineer who had covered his ears because he knew what a rerouted fuel line sounded like a second before it went. [606] About a captain who had turned toward the glacier on purpose, because ice was the only thing near enough to run a burning ship against. [631]

The man in the good coat stopped reading. [639] Solveig did not cry; she put one hand flat against the ice wall as though someone on the other side might feel it. [662]

Marit had not come north for the company or for the captain's name, though both would follow. [679] She had come because eighty-one people had gone into the cold with their stories unfinished, and she wanted to bring one thing back that was not ash and not grief. [709]

At the back of the shrine, a woman she had never seen before stepped forward and lifted the ruined globe in both hands. [732] "Liv's," the woman said. [736] "She bought it in the gift shop an hour out of port." [748]

Marit told her the last part, the part she had saved. [759] In six dreams, in six strangers' sleep, the girl in the yellow coat had never once looked frightened. [777] She had only stood there, shaking snow down over a small tilted village, waiting for it to settle. [795]
