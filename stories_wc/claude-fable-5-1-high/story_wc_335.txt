The library at the edge of Halloway had been forgotten so thoroughly that the town no longer sent anyone to lock it, only people to live in it. [28] Ilse had lived in it for nineteen years, cataloguing shards of broken mirrors that the council delivered in cloth sacks, each one holding some moment it had refused to let go of. [60] Now she had a boy as well. [67]

Tobin was twelve, exiled for putting his fist through the council chamber's great mirror, and he had been sent, as all exiles were, to the library to wait until he could walk home. [100] That was the whole law of Halloway's exile: it lasted exactly as long as you needed it to. [118] The road back opened once a day, at the hush when the wind came down the hill and set the empty swing in the old playground drifting, and anyone who walked it forward the whole way was simply a citizen again. [159]

Tobin had tried eleven times. [164] Eleven times Ilse had watched from the window as he reached the playground fence, felt the town's eyes on him, and began, without seeming to notice, to walk backwards. [193] He always arrived at her door heel-first, furious, and pretended he had come for the tea. [209]

"You're doing it wrong," he told her on the twelfth afternoon, watching her tip the dregs of her cup onto a saucer and lean over them with a brass protractor. [239] "Fortune-tellers look at shapes." [243]

"I am not a fortune-teller." [248] "I am an archivist, and I look at angles." [257] She did not glance up. [262] "A shard remembers what it held, but it will not tell you in words." [276] "The leaves settle at the same angles the glass broke along." [287] "Read the geometry, and you can annotate the shard honestly instead of guessing." [300] She wrote a line in the ledger in her small hard hand: Shard 4,117, cut at sixty-two degrees, holds a kitchen doorway, morning, someone leaving. [325]

"That's stupid," Tobin said, and sat down to watch her do it again. [338]

This was how it went between them: he attacked, she refused to be wounded, and neither of them said the word afraid. [360] Ilse had decided early that she would not comfort him. [370] Comfort was a hand on the back that pushed a child forward before his legs agreed; what she wanted for him was harder and slower, and it could only be given sideways. [402]

So she poured him a cup, and when he had drunk it she took the saucer from him before he could protest. [424] The leaves had settled in a long thin wedge, one point aimed at the window. [439] Ilse measured it, rose, and went to the eastern shelves, where the shards were kept in slotted trays like teeth. [459] The one she brought back was no bigger than his palm, and it held nothing but a shadow. [477] An afternoon shadow, long and gold-edged, thrown by a child on a swing at the top of its arc, legs kicked out toward whatever came next. [503]

"Council mirror," she said. [507] "It faced the playground window for forty years." [515] "This piece kept the afternoon before your fist arrived." [524]

Tobin's face went tight. [528] "That's not me." [531]

"No," Ilse agreed. [534] "It's your shadow." [537] "The shadow was doing fine." [542]

He stood, and she saw his weight shift onto his heels, saw the first backward step begin, and she did the only cruel and loving thing she knew how to do. [573] She turned her back on him and picked up her pen. [584] "Annotate it yourself," she said. [589] "Sixty-eight degrees." [591] "I'll be at the window." [596]

She heard the pen scratch for a long time. [605] Outside, the wind came down the hill, and the swing in the playground began to drift, chains creaking, the hush falling over the town like a held breath. [633] The door opened behind her and did not close. [642]

Ilse did not turn. [646] She watched a small figure cross the yard toward the fence, watched it hesitate there, and watched its shadow, thrown long and gold ahead of it by the low sun, refuse to reverse. [679] The figure followed it. [683]

When the swing had stilled and the road had closed, she went back to the ledger. [699] Under her own entry, in a handwriting that leaned hard to the left, he had written: Shard 4,118, sixty-eight degrees, holds a boy who is not scared, only checking. [728]

Ilse read it twice. [732] Then she added, beneath it, in her small hard hand: Correct. [743]

She did not write that she had once been twelve at that same fence, or that the road was still open to her every afternoon, or that she had stopped needing it the year she understood someone had to stay behind to hand the shards over. [789] Some things were not for the archive. [796]
