Aldo's mirror showed the sky and nothing else. [8] He had promised the village that his trick would reveal the heart of Grey Lake, where the old orchard spun in a vortex of ice and torn roots, and for a moment the glass had shimmered as if it meant to keep that promise. [52] Then it showed clouds. [56]

The villagers drifted home, and Wren stayed on the shore with her hood up and her hands folded, as she always stood, as if the whole of her were a door someone had forgotten to unlock. [92]

"It only shows surfaces," Aldo said, packing the glass away. [102] "Whatever is out there is under something." [109]

"I know what is under it," Wren said. [117]

She had not meant to say it aloud. [125] Twelve years she had kept the words behind her teeth, and now a failed trick had shaken one loose, and she understood, with a calm that felt like the moment before a blow, that the rest would follow whether she permitted them or not. [169]

Wren found lost things. [173] Rings, lambs, a grandmother who had wandered into the fog; the village brought her their absences and she returned them, always by the same method. [198] Begin where the thing was last seen. [205] Walk a circle. [208] Widen it. [210] Widen it again, and again, until the world gives up what it took. [223]

She had never used it on herself. [230]

She stepped onto the ice. [235] Aldo called after her; she did not turn. [243]

The lake had frozen the night her sister Maren drowned in it, and it had never thawed, and at its heart the island orchard had torn itself up by the roots and begun to spin, trunks and branches wheeling slowly in a grey funnel of air, apples still hanging on some of the boughs, twelve winters old and never fallen. [303] Wren walked toward it the way she walked toward anything dangerous: without hurry, without hope, with her shoulders squared like a soldier who has already accepted the terms. [331]

In her pocket, wrapped in oilcloth, was a single seed of redmouth, the bog plant that ate flesh and nothing else. [352] She had carried it for twelve years for reasons she had never let herself examine. [367]

The wind of the vortex took her hood. [375] Branches passed close enough to comb her hair. [383] She kept walking, and the trees parted for her the way a crowd parts for a woman carrying something heavy. [403]

At the still center stood a figure of ice. [412] It was a girl of nineteen with her mouth open on a word. [425]

Wren knew the word. [429] She had begun to say it on the shore that night, to the gathered lanterns and the faces, and had stopped, and the stopping had gone into the lake like a stone. [461]

This was what mirrors did not show. [468] A mirror gave back the guarded face, the folded hands, the door. [480] It could not show the thing behind the door because she had never opened it, not even for herself. [499]

Ice here would not melt; she had tried fire on the shore years ago and watched it die. [517] But a confession was not ice. [523] It was breath and blood, and breath and blood were flesh. [534]

She unwrapped the seed, pressed it against the frozen girl's lips, and bit her own thumb so that the redmouth would know what it was being offered. [561]

It grew fast. [564] Red tendrils went into the ice and into her hand alike, and she let them, standing straight as a spear while it fed. [587] The girl's jaw cracked. [591] Wren's own jaw moved with it, and the words came out of both of them at once, twelve years late. [611]

"I heard her call. [615] I was following a lost lamb, and I heard Maren call from the water, and I chose the lamb, because the lamb was lost and she was not, and I did not think she could be." [651]

The vortex slowed. [654] Trees settled onto the island in rings around her, the nearest first, then the next, then the next, an orchard replanting itself through expanding circles, as if the lake had been holding its breath for the last word and could finally let it out. [698] Apples fell. [700] The ice beneath her went soft and dark and became water, and she stood on the island's real ground. [719] The redmouth, sated, let go of her hand and curled into the earth to sleep. [734]

Wren looked down at what remained of the frozen girl, which was nothing, and at her own shoulders, which had been braced against the same blow for twelve years. [763] Slowly, deliberately, the way one lowers a weapon, she let them unbend. [775]

On the far shore, Aldo held up his mirror out of habit, and this time it showed her. [793]
