Oren Vale brokered lanterns the way other men brokered grief: carefully, at a distance, and never for himself. [18] He sourced them from estate sales and shipwreck auctions, matched them to buyers who wanted light with a history, and took his percentage. [41] The ultraviolet lantern in his satchel tonight was a modern thing, ugly and efficient, but the curator had paid well and asked him to deliver it in person. [69]

The Greenhouse of Lost Flora rose at the edge of the botanical park like a cathedral built for ghosts. [88] Every plant inside had been declared extinct at least once. [98] Seed vaults, herbarium scraps, and patient gene work had coaxed them back, and now they grew under glass, breathing air that had forgotten them. [122]

Oren paused at the door and breathed through his mouth. [132] It was an old habit. [137] Certain smells undid him without warning: a stranger's scarf on a tram, a bakery's back door, once an entire florist's shop that left him weeping on the curb with no idea why. [169] He had learned to move through the world with his nose half closed, the way a man with a bad knee learns stairs. [192]

Dr. Ines Halloran met him among the ferns, sleeves rolled, dirt under her nails. [206] "You're just in time," she said, taking the lantern. [215] "She opens tonight." [218]

She led him down an aisle of glass to a single potted plant on a steel table. [235] One bud, pale as candle wax, had split along its seam. [246]

"Lunaria ambryensis," Ines said. [250] "Ambry moonflower. [252] Gone since 1994, when the last valley it grew in was dammed. [264] It was moth pollinated, so the petals should carry ultraviolet nectar guides, patterns only the moths could see. [282] If your lantern shows them, we did it right." [291]

Oren nodded, but his hand had gone to his coat pocket, where a crystal perfume vial had lived for eleven years. [312] His mother's. [314] He had never uncapped it. [319] He had told himself the scent would be spoiled, that opening it would only ruin the last thing of hers that still held its shape. [344] The truth was simpler and more cowardly: he was afraid of what it might do to him. [361]

"May I try something?" he asked, and set the vial on the table beside the pot. [377]

Ines killed the overhead lights. [382] The greenhouse went blue with moon, then violet as the lantern woke. [394] The vial caught the beam and threw it back in splinters, so that shadows fell across the table like cracked glass, sharp edged and trembling. [419] Inside the crystal, a residue Oren had always taken for dust began to glow. [433] Not white, not blue, but a particular bruised silver, and it clung to the inner walls in a branching pattern, like frost, like veins. [457]

Ines made a small sound. [462] She turned the lantern to the bud. [469]

The transformation had been happening all along, slow as a held breath, and now it completed itself under the violet beam: the wax-pale petals peeled back one by one and unrolled, and across each of them ran the same bruised silver, the same branching veins, glowing exactly as the residue glowed. [520]

"That's not a copy," Ines whispered. [526] "Moonflower absolute. [528] They made perfume from it before the dam. [536] Almost no one alive has smelled it." [543]

The flower finished opening and released its scent. [551]

Oren did not have time to breathe through his mouth. [561] It came in green and cold, then warm underneath, like a coat that had been worn by someone else, and he was seven years old in a kitchen with a radio playing, and his mother was leaning down to kiss the top of his head before she left for work, and the air where she had been smelled exactly like this. [622]

He understood then. [625] The scarf on the tram, the bakery, the florist: none of them had been this. [640] They had been near misses, one note out of a chord, and some faithful animal part of him had lunged at each of them hoping and been disappointed and wept without knowing what it was weeping for. [677] He had spent eleven years cautious of the whole world's smells because the one smell he needed had been erased from it. [699]

Oren sat down on the greenhouse floor among the crystal shadows and grieved, properly, for the first time. [717]

Ines did not turn the lights back on. [725] She sat beside him and let the lantern burn. [734] Above them the moonflower held its silver open to the dark, patient, as if waiting for moths that had also been gone for thirty years. [759]

When Oren could speak again, he uncapped the vial. [768] Nothing came out but the faint memory of a memory. [778] It was enough. [781]
