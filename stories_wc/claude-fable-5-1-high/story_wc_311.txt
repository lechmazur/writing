The reaper returned to the observatory at dusk, because dusk was the hour Ilse had asked for. [17] It carried the wax cylinders in the crook of one arm, seven of them, found that morning in a storeroom under the dome where the mice had left them alone. [47] Reapers are not built for grief. [53] They are built to carry. [58] This one carried last words. [63] Every phrase a dying person spoke and no one remembered settled into it, and at odd moments it spoke them back without meaning to: a pharmacist's "mind the step," a child's "is it morning," a soldier's name for a horse. [103] It had never minded. [107] Then it had met a cartographer who was not afraid of it, and now it minded everything. [124]

The observatory was older than the town below it. [133] Its brass had gone green, and the great lens in the dome had cracked long ago into a hundred facets, so that the last sun did not come through it as a beam but as a scatter, a fall of small lights across the stone floor like coins. [181] Ilse had loved that flaw. [186] She said a broken lens told the truth about light, which was that it never wanted to go one place. [206]

Her desk still stood beneath the dome. [213] Above it, pinned with a rusted tack, was the page she had torn from her grandfather's atlas when she was nine: an ocean with no coastline, and in the empty water, sea monsters. [246] A serpent with a rooster's comb. [252] Something with too many eyes. [257] Each one lettered in a careful hand. [264]

"The old mapmakers were not lying," she had told the reaper once, tapping the serpent. [279] "They knew the water was dangerous and they did not know why. [291] So they drew the danger and gave it a name, and then a sailor could point at it. [309] You can steer around a thing you can point at." [319]

She had been mapping stars by then, but she was dying already, and the reaper understood later that she had been talking about herself. [343]

It set the cylinders on the desk and fitted the first into the old phonograph. [358] Her voice came out thin and near, as if she stood just behind the door. [373]

"Fear the first," she said. [378] "That it will hurt more than I can hold. [387] I name it the Serpent. [392] Fear the second, that I will be forgotten by the ones who stay. [405] I name it the Many-Eyed, because it watches and does nothing." [416]

Cylinder after cylinder, she went on. [422] She had spent her last months not on the stars but on this: hauling every nameless dread out of the dark water and lettering it, one by one, so that when the reaper came she would have nothing left in her that could not be pointed at. [469] The reaper had thought she did it for herself. [478] The seventh cylinder taught it otherwise. [484]

"Last fear," she said, and her voice was tired now. [494] "Not mine. [496] Yours. [497] You think you are only an echo, that nothing you say is your own, that when I go you will keep repeating me like the pharmacist and the horse and never once mean it. [531] So I am giving you no last words. [539] I will be quiet at the end, and you will have to say something for yourself. [555] That is the name of your fear, love. [563] It is called Speaking First." [568]

The cylinder ran out. [572] The reaper remembered now that she had been silent when it came for her, that it had waited for a phrase to catch and none had, and that it had taken this for a wound. [607] It had not understood that a silence could be a gift someone paid for. [621]

The sun dropped behind the hills, and the cracked lens threw its last scatter across the desk, across the atlas page, across the blank sea where the monsters swam. [650] The reaper opened its mouth. [655] "Mind the step," it said, and stopped, ashamed. [663] It tried again, and the words that came were not anyone's but its own, clumsy as a first map. [682]

"I miss you," it said. [687] "I did not know that I could. [694] I name it." [697]

Where the light fell on the page, something happened that the reaper had never seen in all its carrying. [716] The spoken words did not fade. [722] They hung in the scattered light, caught in a hundred facets, and thickened, and crystallized, small and clear, until they lay on the empty ocean like salt. [749] A new monster, lettered in a clumsy hand. [757] Something a sailor could point at. [763]

The reaper touched it once, then left the page on the desk for whoever came next, and went down the hill in the dark, carrying a little less. [791]
