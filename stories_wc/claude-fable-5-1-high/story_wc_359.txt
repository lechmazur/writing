The bells of the Sund Cavern were not made to be heard. [12] They were made to be seen. [18] Ilse had known this since she was six, when her grandmother first led her under the ice and struck the smallest bell, and a thread of green unspooled across the frozen ceiling like a held breath. [54] Everyone else heard a faint chime. [60] Ilse saw the color. [64] The old women called her synesthetically blessed, and then, more quietly, they called her the next ringer of the prism-song. [84]

Now, thirty years later, she stood beneath the same ceiling with a man from the Ministry of Essential Works and a form he had not yet signed. [111]

"Every function in the district is being certified," the assessor said, breath fogging. [124] "Heat, grain, the quarry. [128] What is the function of this place?" [135]

Ilse could have said tradition. [140] She could have said tourism, though no one had come in years. [152] Instead she said, "Wonder," and watched him write it down without expression. [164]

Above them, in the town of Varn, a sculptor named Aurel was waiting at a block of marble. [182] The Ministry had commissioned a Monument to Necessity, and the first strike of his chisel at dawn would mark the moment all uncertified works were closed. [208] Ilse had until then. [212]

The cavern held a hush that was not empty. [221] Her grandmother had called it sculpted, and Ilse understood the word only after decades of ringing: the bells did not fill the silence, they shaped it, carving room in it the way a chisel carves room in stone. [259] Each tone left a hollow of quiet behind it, and the hollows had colors, and the colors were the song. [279]

She unrolled the amber music sheet across the ice ledge. [289] It was not paper but a thin pane of hardened resin, and inside it, suspended like insects, were flecks of pigment arranged in rows. [313] To the assessor it looked like a fossil. [321] To Ilse it read as clearly as a hymnal: a violet held long, two short whites, then a green she had never managed to strike. [346]

"You cannot certify what you cannot perceive," she said. [355] "So I will make you perceive it." [362]

He looked at his watch. [367]

The bells hung in a ring of nine, each cast in clear ice around a core of quartz, and in ordinary light they rang thin and gray. [394] But the cavern roof was a lens of ancient ice, and on nights when the aurora burned, its colors poured down through that lens and the bells drank them. [423] Tonight the sky was moving. [428]

Ilse lifted the striker. [432] She rested. [434] This was the part her grandmother had insisted on: the pause before, long enough that the assessor shifted his feet, long enough that the hush thickened and became a thing with edges. [466]

Then she struck. [469]

Violet. [470] It bloomed across the ceiling and the silence behind it went deep and cool, a room she could have walked into. [491] She rested again, and let it settle, and felt the calm swing back toward her like a pendulum. [509]

White, white. [511] Sharp, brief, two chips flaked from the quiet. [519] The assessor's pen had stopped. [524]

Now the green. [527] She had failed it a hundred times, because green was the aurora's own note, and the sheet demanded she wait for something she had never identified. [553] Beneath the last row of pigment her grandmother had pressed a single word into the resin: stone. [570]

Dawn was thinning the aurora. [575] Ilse waited, and the waiting was its own repose, tense and tranquil at once, the way ice is both still and straining. [597]

Far above, Aurel raised his chisel to the marble. [606]

The first strike came down through rock and ice as a tremor no one heard, but Ilse saw it: a hard bright fleck of ochre crossing the ceiling. [634] She struck the ninth bell into it. [641]

Green met ochre through the last colors of the aurora, and the hush she had been carving all night came loose from the walls. [665] It did not break. [669] It unfettered, the way a shape unfetters from a block when the final chip falls, and for one long breath the assessor stood inside a silence with a form, and the form was every color at once. [706]

He saw it. [709] Ilse knew because he made the sound people make when they are six. [722]

The aurora faded. [725] The cavern went gray and ordinary, and the quiet was only quiet again. [738]

The assessor looked at his form for a long time. [748] Under Function he had written her word. [755] Under Classification, with a hand that was not quite steady, he wrote: Essential. [768]

Then he signed it, and up in Varn the second strike fell, and Ilse rolled the amber sheet and rested. [788]
