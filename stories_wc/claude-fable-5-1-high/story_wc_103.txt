The living bridge garden grew across the gorge in tiers, each footpath a braid of fig roots coaxed over sixty years from one cliff to the other. [27] Mireille had spent eleven of those years planting hedges along the bridges, cutting her mazes into hanging beds of box and yew. [49] She had come here for the roots. [56] A hedge maze designer learns quickly that a hedge is only a slow decision, and the root bridges were the slowest decisions she had ever seen. [82]

Then the fragment came. [86] A gardener had dug it from the anchor roots of the lowest span: a curved shard of gilded plaster, part of the halo from a saint's statue that the river had taken from a drowned chapel upstream. [123] Mireille had held it for one breath. [130] Since then she had been retrocognitively trapped: every leaf she looked at showed her all the leaves it had been, every path unrolled backward into the seedling it grew from, and she could not make the world stay at now. [170] The garden trembled with all of its own years at once. [181]

She should have hated it. [186] Instead she kept the halo fragment in her apron pocket, because in the cabin at the gorge's edge her photographs of Anouk were fading. [210] Cheap prints, bad fixer, twenty summers of damp. [218] Anouk's face was going the color of weak tea, and Mireille could no longer say whether she remembered the laugh or only remembered the photograph of it. [245] If the past was still present in the fragment, if it could be seen, then it had a surface. [264] And a surface could be touched. [270] She wanted to discover the texture of time, to run her thumb along it and know whether it was grain or weave or water, before Anouk's paper face went white. [300]

The trouble was room. [304] A hedge maze on a flat bed can hold only so many turns before the walls meet. [321] The past she was seeing did not behave like a flat bed. [333] Each moment branched, and each branch branched, and none of them ever ran parallel; they spread apart the way lines do on a saddle, gaining space the farther they went. [363] So she designed the last maze through hyperbolic geometry. [372] She trained the young roots of the middle span into a curling, frilled surface, a lettuce leaf of living wood, and set her hedges along it so that every corridor split into seven and every seven into seven more, all of it fitting because the floor itself bent away to make room. [424] It took three years. [428] The photographs kept fading. [432]

On the morning she finished, the gorge was full of mist and the bridges creaked like ships. [449] Mireille walked in with the halo fragment in her fist. [459] At the first split she saw Anouk at nineteen, hauling a sapling up the cliff path. [475] At the seventh, Anouk asleep in the cabin with dirt still on her knees. [489] The maze did not show the past as a line but as a spreading country, and Mireille walked through it shaking, half in dream, the hedges rushing green and brown and green again around her. [524] She reached out toward Anouk's sleeve. [530] Her fingers passed through it, and the halo fragment grew warm. [541] She kept walking, kept reaching, and with every failed touch a little gilt sifted from the plaster like pollen. [560]

At the center, where forty-nine thousand corridors converged on a single bench of woven root, the fragment began to disintegrate in her palm. [583] It went the way the photographs were going, not breaking but thinning, its gold and plaster loosening into a fine bright dust that the mist took. [609] And in the moment it went, she felt it. [618] Not grain, not weave, not water. [624] Time had the texture of the bench under her hands: bark grown over bark, every year a ring you could count but never climb back into, rough and warm and shut. [655] The past was not behind her. [661] She was standing on it. [666]

The hedges settled into a single green. [673] The garden held still at now for the first time in three years, and the stillness shook her worse than the visions had. [696] She walked out by the shortest path, which in her maze was also the only path that led anywhere. [715]

In the cabin she took the photographs down from the wall and looked at Anouk's tea-colored face without trying to see through it. [738] Then she carried them to the middle span and pressed them into a fold of the living wood, where the roots would grow over them the way they had grown over the halo. [771] Let the bridge keep them. [776] It was the slowest decision she knew, and the only one that went forward. [790]
