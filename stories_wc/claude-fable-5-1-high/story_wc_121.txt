The viaduct glowed after the rain, every wet stone holding the last of the sunset like a coal. [18] Mara walked its underside, boots pressed to the arches, her hair hanging toward a sky that should have been ground. [38] Gravity had let go of her the night she read the tablet, and it had never taken her back. [57]

The tablet rode in her satchel, a slab of clay older than the river below, its runes pressed by some hand that had understood a terrible thing: that a world could be written as a handful of rules and a single seed, and the rules would build the rest. [106] She had been the seed. [111]

Above her, on the proper side of the viaduct, a drop gathered on the iron rail. [127] She knew this drop. [131] She had watched it fall more times than there were stones in the arches. [145] It fell. [147]

The world cracked along the line of its falling, one timeline splintering into the many it might have been, and Mara stepped through the crack. [172]

Between the drop and the ripple there was a gap, and in the gap she was eternal. [189] Not forever, only until the drop struck the puddle on the road below and the ring spread out and the world remembered how to move. [214] Then another drop would gather, and she would be eternal again. [225] Eternity in installments. [228]

Inside the gap, the viaduct was never the same viaduct. [238] The tablet's rules took her and spun out a structure from her: arches shaped like the hallway of her mother's house, a stair that had the exact pitch of the one she had fallen down at seven, a lamp that flickered with the rhythm of a heart monitor she had sat beside for eleven days. [293] Nothing was designed. [296] Everything was generated, corridor by corridor, from whatever she carried. [306] It was a labyrinth, and the labyrinth was her, and it did not end because she did not. [324]

She had tried, in early gaps, to run. [332] Running only fed the rules more panic to build with, and the corridors had grown teeth. [348] Now she retraced. [351] She walked the underside of the generated arches, and wherever a passage split, she chose the way she had actually gone, in life, the first time. [377] The left door at the hospital, not the right one. [387] The stair she had descended without holding the rail. [396] The telephone she had let ring. [402]

Each true choice made the glow steadier. [409] Each lie she told herself about a choice made it dim. [420] That was how she knew she was close: the stones were nearly bright as noon, and the brightness had begun, at its edges, to hurt. [445]

She came at last to a place the rules had never built before. [458] A room under the central arch, and in it a table, and on the table the clay tablet, whole and unread. [479] A woman stood over it with her hand out. [488] She had the right weight. [493] Her feet were on the floor. [499]

Mara understood then what the labyrinth had been generating toward, all these installments of forever: not an exit, but this. [519] The choice before the choices. [524] The night she had wanted, more than anything, to be lifted out of her own life, and had found a thing that would do it. [549]

"Don't," Mara said, upside down above her. [556]

The woman did not look up. [562] Of course she didn't. [566] The rules only generated from what Mara carried, and Mara had never carried a version of herself that listened. [585]

So she did the only thing the labyrinth could not have predicted, because she had never done it. [603] She let go of the arch. [609]

She fell, the way everyone falls, and struck the floor beside the woman, and the tablet in her satchel broke beneath her. [631] The runes split along a line she had not written and could not read. [645] The glow of the stones went down like a lamp turned by a slow hand, from noon to dusk to the honest dark of a wet night, and in the dark she heard it. [679]

A drop, striking a puddle. [684] A ring, spreading. [687] The world remembering how to move. [693]

She lay on the road under the viaduct with the rain finished and the stones above her only stones, dull and ordinary, holding no light but what the city lent them. [724] Her satchel held only dust. [729] Her body was heavy, and it hurt, and it was hers. [740] Somewhere far off another drop was gathering, but it would only be a drop now, and she was going to lie here and watch it fall. [766]
