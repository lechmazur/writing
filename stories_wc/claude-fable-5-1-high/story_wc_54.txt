The carnival left on a Tuesday, and by Wednesday the spaceport's passenger terminal belonged to the wind again. [18] Mira Okonkwo-Reyes arrived with a clipboard, a laser level, and the title of civil engineering intern, which meant she was paid to measure what other people had already decided to destroy. [49] She was supposed to confirm the drainage was dead, the foundations were sound enough to fall predictably, and nothing of value remained. [71] Instead, she found water. [75]

It pooled in the concourse beneath the departures board, a shallow sheet reflecting the hollow ribs of the roof, and it should not have been there. [101] The mains had been capped for eleven years. [109] Mira crouched and watched the surface tremble. [116] Moving water meant a source, a gradient, an intention. [125] She followed it. [128]

The carnival had tapped the terminal's old service lines for its generators and wash stations, and someone, packing up in the dark, had left a valve open in the utility corridor. [159] Groundwater from the hillside cistern, which everyone assumed had silted shut, was creeping back through pipes that remembered their purpose. [179] Beside the valve, where a trailer jack had stood, lay a curve of brass pressed into the grit like a discarded shim. [201]

She knew it before she turned it over. [209] The memorial case in the departures hall had held Explorer Teodora Vance's sextant, the one she had carried on the first crewed transit from this very apron, back when the port had a future instead of a fence. [247] The case had been smashed years ago; the frame was gone. [258] Only this arc remained, its degree marks worn but legible, and some roustabout had used it to level a ride. [278]

Mira did not feel outrage. [283] She felt something quieter and more useful: recognition. [291] The arc was a measuring instrument. [297] It still worked. [300]

The blueprints she had pulled from the archive described a maze of intentions: three architects, two funding collapses, a council that wanted a monument and an authority that wanted a warehouse. [331] Every corridor turned where someone had changed their mind. [340] Drains ran toward fountains that were never built; conduits ended at walls. [352] Yet the first architect's intention survived underneath all the others, in the gradients themselves. [366] That architect had designed the whole terminal to drink from the hillside and carry water in a slow circuit through the concourse, so that departing passengers walked beside a moving stream before they left the ground. [402]

Mira had no flow meter and no surveying rod. [411] She had a laser level and a piece of brass graduated in degrees. [424] She set the arc against the laser line and read the slope of each channel like a navigator reading the horizon. [445] Two degrees here, half a degree there, a sump that had been backfilled by the warehouse faction and could be cleared with a shovel. [469] She cleared it. [472] She closed the carnival's valve and opened two others that had rusted half-shut, redirecting the flow away from the dead end beneath the departures board and into the original circuit. [502] Then she climbed back to the concourse and waited. [511]

The puddle drained. [514] For a long minute nothing else happened, and the terminal was only a shell where a carnival had briefly pretended to be a destination. [538] Then water rose in the channel along the east wall, clear and cold, and began to move. [555] It found the channel's old rhythm and ran the length of the hall, past the empty gates, past the memorial case with its broken glass, and turned the corner toward the apron doors exactly as a woman dead for sixty years had intended. [598]

Mira stood very still. [602] She had resurrected nothing grand. [607] She had persuaded water to go where it already wanted to go. [619] But the sound of it filled the terminal, and the terminal, for the first time in eleven years, sounded like a place people might arrive. [644]

That night she did not write the demolition report. [653] She wrote equations instead. [657] Flow rate against evaporation. [661] Cistern recharge against seasonal rainfall. [666] The cost of clearing four more sumps against the cost of hauling ten thousand tons of concrete to a landfill. [686] She knew the numbers alone would not save the building. [696] Hope was not a variable the authority's spreadsheets accepted. [705] So she worked to express it in terms they would accept: stormwater retention, cooling load, a public space that cost less to maintain than to demolish. [731] New equations, she thought, for an old intention. [739]

The brass arc lay beside her keyboard, its degrees catching the lamplight. [751] Vance had used it to find her position against the stars. [762] Mira had used it to find a gradient in the dark. [773] Outside, the carnival's tire tracks were already softening in the dust, and inside the terminal, the water kept moving. [792]
