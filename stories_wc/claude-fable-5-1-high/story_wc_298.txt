The orchard grew where the city buried its dead, and the dead did not seem to mind. [17] Roots of pear and blackthorn snaked between ribs and skulls in the catacomb galleries, lifting a jaw here, threading an eye socket there, taking what the earth had already forgiven. [47] Lamps burned low along the passages, never fully lit, never fully dark, so that the keeper worked always within the glow of half-lit corridors. [71] Her name was Wenna, and she was fiercely gentle with everything down there: the bones, the trees, and the people who came to trade. [95]

They came because of what the orchard did. [103] A fate given to the roots was a fate the roots would carry elsewhere, and a fate drawn from the fruit was a fate someone else had let go. [132] Bartered destinies, the city called it, though Wenna never used the phrase. [144] She only listened, and cut, and tied. [151]

The boy came down on a night when the lamps guttered. [162] He was nineteen, maybe, with the bright, unbalanced look of someone who had decided something on the stairs and would not be talked out of it before the bottom. [191] He held out a tarot card, or what was left of one. [203] The Tower, scorched to its lower windows, the falling figures gone to ash. [216]

"I burned it," he said. [221] "It was mine. [224] The reader said it was mine. [230] So I burned it." [234]

"And now there is a hole," Wenna said. [242]

He flinched, because it was true. [248] He had felt the hole ever since, a draft moving through him where a future should have been, and he had come to fill it the only way a reckless idealist knows how: all at once, with someone else's. [287] "Give me another. [290] Any of them. [293] I'll take the worst one on the tree." [301]

Wenna set down her knife. [306] Around them the roots creaked in their bindings; she trammeled them with netting and rope so they would not swallow the bones too quickly, so that names could still be read. [337] "You cannot barter with ash," she said. [344] "The orchard takes what is whole. [350] You brought it a wound." [355]

"Then take me." [358] He said it as if it cost nothing. [366] "Tie me in with the roots. [372] Let them have my fate and give me whatever grows." [382]

She looked at him for a long time, and something in her face went hard and soft together, like bark over a graft. [405] "No," she said. [408] "That is what you would do. [414] It is not what you need." [420]

She took his wrist, not roughly, and led him deeper, to where the pear roots wove through a shelf of skulls. [441] Every skull had a word cut small into its brow. [451] Every root had a name inlaid along its bark in copper wire, and the wire caught the lamps. [469] Scraps of paper were folded into knuckles; a child's tin ring hung from a twig. [484]

"They brought me holes too," Wenna said. [491] "Everyone does. [493] Nobody comes down here whole. [498] I cannot sew a soul closed. [504] But I have learned that a hole stays open only as long as nothing is asked to mean anything around it." [525] She touched one skull. [529] "Forgiven, that one. [532] That one, waited. [535] This root carried a widow's fate to a fisherman and he wrote to thank me. [550] I put the letter in the tree." [557]

He understood, slowly, that this was the real trade, and that she was offering it to him at a price he had not expected: patience. [582]

"Give me the card," she said. [588]

He gave it. [591] She did not feed it to the roots. [599] She pressed the burned Tower into the fork of a young blackthorn where the wood was still soft, and the bark would close over it in a season, and she took her knife and cut beneath it a single word. [639] Stayed. [640]

"Now that is what it means," she said. [648] "Not fallen. [650] Stayed. [651] You will come back and read it when you doubt." [661]

He stood in the half-light a long while, the draft still moving through him but quieter, as though it had found an edge to break against. [687] "What do I trade you?" he asked. [694]

"Come tomorrow," Wenna said, handing him a coil of rope. [704] "The roots on the east wall are getting into the children's gallery. [716] Help me hold them back." [721]

He came the next night, and the one after, and learned to tie the knots that let a tree grow without devouring what it grew from. [747] The hole in him did not close. [754] But he inlaid meaning around its edges, a name here, a word there, until it became less a wound than a window, and within the glow of the half-lit corridors he found he could live beside it.
