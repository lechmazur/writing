The tower fell on a Tuesday, and by Thursday the sky over the Adair Flats was empty of airships for the first time in Wren's life. [26] Her father called her senselessly perceptive, and she felt the absence pressing on her ears like altitude. [43] Inside the glass dome the desert roses did not notice. [53] They sat in their trays of pale sand, thick-trunked and patient, and she went down the rows at dawn telling them what she told them every morning. [80] "Hold your water. [83] Do not bloom for anyone who frightens you. [91] Forgive the heat; it does not know you." [99]

Her mother had believed that plants took on the values of whoever tended them, that a rose raised on patience would root deeper than one raised on fear. [127] Wren had no proof. [131] She only knew that in nine years, not one of them had died. [144]

Her father came home on the fifth day, walking, which meant the bounty had gone badly or the roads had. [164] She saw him from the far side of the dome, a shape bent against the glare, and read him before he reached the airlock: the drag of his left boot, the knife held bare in his fist because the sheath was gone. [206]

"The mooring tower came down on the whole port," he said, once he had drunk. [221] "Ships cut loose, dumped everything to climb. [228] Half the Flats are under sand and canvas. [236] Nobody knows who owes what to who anymore." [244]

He set the knife on the potting bench. [252] It was old and good, and along the flat of the blade one word was engraved in her grandfather's hand: QUIET. [273] It was a trade name, a promise to clients. [282] Wren had hated it since she was old enough to read. [293]

"There's paper on a woman," he said. [300] "Ran a supply skiff. [304] She's holed up in the old pump station two miles east. [315] Somebody says she cut the lines that dropped the tower." [325]

"Somebody says." [327]

"Paper's paper, Wren." [330]

She went outside while he slept. [336] The desert after the fall was a strange country. [345] Ballast lay everywhere the ships had shed it, canvas sandbags split open on the hardpan, spilling sand that was not the desert's sand but river sand, black and fine, carried from somewhere green. [378] She knelt and took a scrap of the heavy cloth, stiff with stitching, stenciled with a hull number no one would ever check again. [402] Everything up there had been made to hold something down, she thought, and now it had all let go at once. [423] That was the whole trouble. [428]

Back under the glass, in the long tranquil blaze of afternoon, she cut the scrap to size and doubled it and sewed it with waxed thread into a sheath. [457] Her hands were steady. [461] The roses watched, or did not. [467] Then she took a nail and, on the inside of the canvas where only the blade would ever touch it, she pressed the same word her grandfather had chosen, letter by careful letter. [500] Quiet. [501] Not his meaning. [504] Hers. [505]

When he woke she was waiting with it. [513]

"You lost your sheath," she said. [519]

He turned it over, ran a thumb across the hull number. [530] "Ballast," he said. [533]

"Everything that fell out here was meant to hold something steady," she said. [546] "I would like one thing to keep doing its job." [556]

He looked at the roses. [561] He had never once asked what she said to them, but he had also never once laughed. [578]

"Two miles east," he said. [583]

"With a witness who says somebody says." [590] She held out the sheath. [595] "You could go and ask her what happened," Wren said. [605] "You could listen. [608] You are very good at finding people. [615] Nobody said you have to bring them back in the same shape." [627]

He picked up the knife. [632] For a moment the engraved word caught the low sun and burned, silent, in the way it always had. [651] Then he sheathed it, and the canvas swallowed the blade to the hilt, and the word was gone, pressed against its own reflection in the dark. [677]

He went out at dusk. [682]

Wren did not follow. [686] She went down the rows instead and told the roses the evening version, the one her mother had saved for the days after storms. [710] "Something has ended. [713] Nothing you are made of has ended. [720] Root where you stand." [724]

Outside, the sky stayed empty. [729] Near midnight she heard two sets of footsteps on the gravel, unhurried, and did not look up. [746] Inside, the quiet had a shape now, and the shape was one she had chosen. [761]
