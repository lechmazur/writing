Ilse mapped places by hand. [5] Blind since birth, she built charts of texture and echo and smell for the archive, walking rooms until they lived in her fingertips. [28] Repair Bay Nine at the Kessel spaceport was the first place she had ever mapped for herself. [45] Her brother Teo had died there when a coolant line ruptured, and the port had sealed the bay and let it rot. [67] The report said "instantaneous." [71] His supervisor, Marren, said "he didn't suffer," and then said nothing else, no matter how she asked. [88] A withheld truth has a weight, Ilse believed, the way a locked room has a shape. [104] She meant to measure it. [109]

She cut the lock on the maintenance hatch and went in. [120] The bay smelled of old ethylene and pigeons. [128] Her cane found the diamond-plate floor, the drip trench, the cold foot of the gantry crane. [144] Near the ruptured line her glove closed on a shard of green glass, curved, thick, a piece of the pressure gauge that had burst with everything else. [171] She pocketed it without knowing why. [177]

She slept in the bay that night, on a tarp beside the trench, because a map should include the dark. [197] Sometime past midnight the shard in her pocket grew warm. [207] She half woke into the murmur of a dream she seemed to have had before: settling metal ticking, a voice saying her name in a corridor she could not find. [237] She put out her hand and the floor was wrong. [247] The trench had moved a full pace to her left. [257] The ethylene smell was gone. [262] She mapped it anyway, because mapping was what she knew, and when the shard cooled she was back on her tarp with the trench where she had left it. [291]

It happened again six nights later, and again six nights after that. [303] She could not summon it. [308] The shard warmed when it chose, but it chose on a cycle, like a tide that keeps its own calendar, and Ilse learned to be asleep in the bay when the tide came in. [342] Each crossing gave her a different bay. [349] In one the gauge was whole under her palm, cold and round, and the coolant line hissed faintly with life. [369] In another the gantry lay collapsed across the floor and the air tasted of a fire that had never happened in hers. [391] She understood, slowly, that she was weaving between worlds that had all held a Repair Bay Nine, and that the shard was a loose thread pulled from the same gauge in every one of them. [426]

She laid the maps over each other in her mind, and the differences began to speak. [442] Every bay had the same junction valve at the trench's head, a wheel the size of a dinner plate. [461] In the bays where the ethylene smell was faint, the wheel stood at its resting notch. [477] In the bays where it was choking, where the gauge had burst, the wheel had been turned hard to the right. [498] Turned to the right, the valve redirected the coolant surge from Bay Eight into Bay Nine. [514] Bay Eight, where the day crew ate lunch. [522] Bay Eight, where Marren's office was. [528]

On the seventh crossing the shard was heavier in her hand than it had ever been, and the dream murmured with a voice she knew. [553] "You shouldn't be here, Ilse." [558] Teo. [559] Alive in this one, standing somewhere near the valve. [568] "You turned it," she said. [573] "Marren was on the comm screaming at me to hold position and let Eight flood," Teo said. [590] "Eight had nine people in it." [596] "Nine had me." [599] "In this world you didn't turn it." [606] "In this world I did what I was told." [615] His voice cracked. [618] "Marren didn't make it out of Eight." [625] "Nobody did." [627] "Go home, Ilse." [630] "Your Marren is carrying enough." [635]

The shard cooled. [638] She lay on the tarp in her own bay, the ethylene thick, the trench where it belonged, and held the glass up in the dark and felt its weight. [667] It was a small piece of a gauge. [675] It weighed almost nothing. [679] The truth had a different weight: nine lives on one side, one on the other, and a man who had given the wrong order and been saved by his subordinate's refusal, who could not say so without admitting he had asked Teo to let them all die. [726] "He didn't suffer" had been the only thing Marren could lift. [737]

In the morning Ilse finished the map of Bay Nine, and at the head of the trench she marked the valve wheel and the direction it had turned. [765] Then she went to find Marren, not to make him say it, but to tell him she knew, and to see how much lighter he stood. [791]
