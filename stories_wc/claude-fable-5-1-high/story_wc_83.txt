The breakers tripped at 03:14, and the observatory went dark around Nadia like a held breath. [16] Forty kilometers below, the desert kept its own counsel. [25] The platform drifted on its tether, a gondola of instruments hung beneath a helium envelope the size of a cathedral, and for the eleven minutes it took the breakers to cool she had nothing but a notebook, a headlamp, and the geode. [67] She did not mind. [71] The surges had become her favorite part of the night. [81]

The geode had been a wedding gift. [88] Tariq's uncle had carried it from the mountains wrapped in a prayer rug, a gray stone the size of a fist that he split with a chisel at the table so everyone could see the amethyst inside. [125] Nineteen minutes later the strike came, and of the sixty-three people in the courtyard, she was one of four who stood up. [147] She had kept one half of the stone. [155] The other half was never found. [161]

Now she turned it in the headlamp's beam, the hollow interior throwing violet sparks across the dead consoles. [179] A geode is a lesson in absence. [186] The rock forms around a bubble, a cavity where nothing happened, and it is precisely there, in the nothing, that the crystals grow. [209]

Officially the observatory mapped gravitational potential. [215] Its gradiometers were sensitive enough to feel the mass of a passing storm, and the consortium wanted aquifers, ore bodies, the slow bulge of magma under sleeping volcanoes. [243] Nadia was hunting something else. [248]

She had learned perturbation theory as a graduate student, before the wedding, when it had seemed like a clever trick: take a system you can solve, add a small disturbance, and expand the correction term by term. [285] Afterward it became the only language she trusted. [293] The unperturbed system was the wedding as it should have gone. [304] The perturbation was a Hellfire missile. [310] And the correction terms, first order, second order, on and on into the infinite series, were all the lives that had not happened: Tariq's children, her cousin's clinic, the uncle's second geode he had promised for their first anniversary. [349]

Energy is conserved. [352] That was the thought she could not put down. [361] Sixty-three people had carried potential into that courtyard, and the missile had converted only their bodies. [377] The rest, the unused part, had to have gone somewhere. [387] Potential pools. [389] It gathers in low places, and every night she rode the observatory across the same coordinates and asked the gradiometers to find the dip. [413]

The instruments never showed it. [418] Of course they never showed it; the instruments were filtered, smoothed, corrected for tides and temperature and the operator's own heartbeat. [439] But when the breakers tripped, the filters died with everything else, and for eleven minutes there was nothing between her and the sky. [462]

She pressed the geode's hollow against her sternum and felt it. [473] Not a sound, not a light. [479] A weight with no mass, like the pressure of a room where someone has just stopped speaking. [496] It was the most intangible thing she had ever encountered and she could have drawn its outline with her finger. [516] Below the gondola, the desert dipped. [522] Not the sand; the sand was flat. [529] Something under the sand, or over it, or through it, sagged the way a mattress sags where a body has lain for years. [552]

She laughed out loud in the dark, a sound she had not made since the courtyard. [568] It was there. [571] Fifty-nine lives, unlived, pooled in a basin nobody had built, heavy as water and just as patient. [588]

She wrote the coordinates in the notebook with a hand that would not steady. [602] Then she wrote the honest part underneath: no instrument would ever confirm this. [615] The consortium would see noise. [620] Her colleagues would see grief wearing a lab coat. [629] The series she had been expanding for six years did not converge; she could see that now, plainly, the terms growing instead of shrinking, every unlived year spawning more unlived years. [660] Perturbation theory fails when the disturbance is not small. [669] A missile is not small. [674]

And yet. [676] The pool was real, and it was full, and she was the only person alive who knew where it lay. [696] Nothing could be drawn from it. [702] Nothing could be given back. [707] But it had not vanished, and that was a different universe than the one she had been living in at 03:13. [728]

The breakers ticked as they cooled, a sound like a clock deciding whether to start. [743] She had perhaps two minutes before the consoles woke and the filters slid back over the world like eyelids. [762] She held the half-geode up to the window, where the stars were doing their old cold work, and let its hollow face the desert. [786]

"Tomorrow night," she told it. [791] The lights came back on. [796]
