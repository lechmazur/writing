The fog came in at dusk the way it always did in October, low and thick, erasing the harbor one mooring at a time. [24] Teodor Rask watched it from his porch with the astronomical compass in his lap and told it, cheerfully, that it had terrible timing. [47] He had planned this night for a month. [55] Clear sky, the almanac promised; Polaris at eleven degrees over the breakwater. [67] He would walk down to the kayak launch, set the instrument on the concrete ramp, sight the star, and read true north off the brass ring the way he had done a thousand times at sea. [103] If the reading came true, he had decided, he would finally accept that the instrument had not failed four years ago, and that he had. [128] Then he would go and tell Ilse Varga so, to her face. [140]

Now there was no sky at all. [147] He laughed, because the alternative was worse, and hummed the old dirge his mother had sung over dishes, the one about drowned sailors, giving it a jaunty little swing. [176] Merrily melancholic, his wife had called him, before she left. [186]

The backlash came anyway, as it always did when he tried to move toward anything. [201] He remembered the trawler Anneke, the second mate Pavel Varga on the bow with a lamp, the fog that night thicker than this one. [225] He remembered distrusting his own fix, correcting two degrees to starboard on a hunch, the sound of the ledge opening the hull. [247] Everyone came off but Pavel. [252] Every time Teodor took one step toward Ilse, the memory shoved him three steps back into the porch chair. [271] Four years of that arithmetic. [276]

He stood up. [279] He had been waiting, he understood, for the heavens to give him permission, and the heavens had just declined. [298] A navigator without stars still had a coastline. [306] He tucked the compass under his coat and went down the steps into the grey. [321]

Along Shore Road the utility poles stood at forty-meter intervals, each one dissolving as the next appeared. [338] He had counted them a hundred times on his aimless walks; he had always gravitated toward the water and always stopped short of it. [362] Nineteen poles to the launch. [367] He touched each one as he passed, tarred wood under his palm, and called out the number like a depth sounding. [388] "Seven," he sang. [391] "Eight, and the widow's light is on." [398] It was, faintly, a yellow smear above the ramp where Ilse ran the rental shack that Pavel had built. [417]

Between poles twelve and thirteen his faith went out entirely and he stood in the road, breathing fog, ready to turn. [438] What was he bringing her, a brass antique and a confession she had not asked for? [454] Then a truck passed, headlights sweeping, and for two seconds pole thirteen blazed out of the murk like a lighthouse. [474] He laughed again, wetly, and walked on. [481]

The kayaks lay upturned on their racks like sleeping seals, and the ramp ran down into water he could hear but not see. [504] Ilse was locking the shack. [509] She saw him and did not move. [516] "You have walked past here every week for four years," she said. [528] "I know." [530] "You never came down the ramp." [536] "I was waiting for a clear night." [543] He held out the astronomical compass, its sighting arm folded, its brass beaded with fog. [558] "This gave me a true fix that night. [566] I did not trust it. [571] I trusted my gut, and my gut put us on the ledge, and Pavel went into the water because of me and not because of any instrument or any weather. [601] I have meant to say that since the inquiry. [610] I kept meaning it and not doing it. [618] Tonight I decided the meaning was worthless on its own." [628]

Ilse took the compass and turned it over. [636] Somewhere out on the water a bell buoy rang, once, patient. [647] "The inquiry cleared you," she said. [653] "The inquiry was not there." [658] She was quiet a long time, and he felt the old backlash gather behind his knees, the urge to apologize for apologizing and flee back up the poles. [686] He stayed. [688] "Pavel said you were the only navigator he ever sailed with who could sing," Ilse said finally. [705] "He said it made the fog less lonely." [713] She handed the instrument back. [718] "Keep it. [720] Come Saturday. [722] The doubles need someone in the stern who knows where north is." [734]

It was not forgiveness, exactly. [739] It was a bearing, and he had spent his whole life knowing what to do with one of those. [758] Walking home he counted the poles backward, humming the drowned sailors' song, and by pole six he noticed he had stopped swinging it. [781] He was simply singing it, the way it was written. [791]
