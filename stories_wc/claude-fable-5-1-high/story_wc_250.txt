The rain quit at dusk, and the cobalt dock at Tenpenny Lock went dark and glossy, the color of a bruise beneath deep water. [24] Oda saw the boy before he saw her, which was how she preferred all meetings; she had not spoken in eleven years, and silence goes better when you arrive first. [54] He sat on the dock's edge with his shoes above the canal and something small and bright turning in his fingers. [75] A dental mirror, she saw when she came near: a round glass no larger than a coin, angled on a steel stem. [97] She knew whose it had been. [103] Everyone on the reach had sat under Doctor Vell's lamp with their mouths open, and everyone had stood on the bank in March when the dredgers brought him up. [132] The boy did not look up. [138]

"They said you keep the lights," he said. [146]

Oda nodded, though he could not see it, and went past him to the lockhouse. [161] The lockhouse had twelve shutters, six to a side, and behind each a lamp. [175] On clear nights the barges steered by the sky, but the canal ran through low country where cloud sat on the water for weeks, and on those nights the crews steered by Oda. [208] She opened and closed the shutters in patterns the boatmen knew: three high for the gate rising, two low for shallow water, a slow diagonal to say wait, wait, the pound is not yet full. [243] It was a language with no throat in it, which suited her. [255] The boatmen called her the guardian of the reach, which she had never been able to accept or refuse. [274] She had thought of it, privately, as teaching the dark what stars were for. [288]

The boy had followed her to the door. [296]

"The night my father--" he said, and stopped, the way people stop at the edge of a canal in fog, one foot still lifted. [320]

Oda waited inside the edge of it. [327] She did not need him to finish. [334] She knew what lived in that gap because she had one of her own: a March night with no boats due, all twelve shutters closed, the reach as black as the inside of a closed mouth. [370] She had heard the water take something heavy and had thought, a log, a dog, and had not lit one lamp. [391]

"I saw him go out," the boy said to the mirror in his hand. [405] "He'd been drinking. [408] I thought--" [410]

He did not say what he thought. [417] He did not have to. [422]

Oda found that her hands, which had wanted to shake, had instead gone very calm, a calm that came from far down and rose in slow spirals, the way a thing settles in water that has finally stopped moving. [461] She could not tell him it was not his fault, and would not have, because she did not know if it was true of either of them. [488] What she could do was open a shutter. [496]

She lit the first lamp and swung the first shutter wide, and a bar of yellow fell across the cobalt boards and lay on the wet canal like a plank someone might walk on. [530] Then the second, higher. [534] Then the fifth, so the light made a shape the barges would not read as any signal, because it was not for them. [557] She beckoned, and the boy came, and she took his wrist and turned the little mirror toward the nearest lamp until it caught. [580] A point of light, no bigger than a star, went out across the water. [594]

He understood before she let go. [600] He moved the mirror and the star moved, sliding between her bars of lamplight, over them, under, and she began to work the shutters to meet it, closing one as his light crossed it, opening another ahead of him, until the two of them were weaving something on the black canal that had no name in the boatmen's code. [659] Warp and weft. [662] Lamp and mirror. [665] The water under the pattern did not move; it held the lights the way stillwater holds everything, without comment, with all its weight beneath. [689]

Somewhere down the reach a barge horn sounded, low, asking. [699] Oda closed the pattern and gave the crew their three high lamps, and the boy stood with the mirror lowered, breathing as if he had run. [725]

"Can I come back," he said. [731]

It was not quite a question, and she did not quite answer it. [744] She only left the sixth shutter open when the barge had passed, one square of light on the cobalt dock after rain, which anyone on the water could read, if they knew how to look, as here. [781]
