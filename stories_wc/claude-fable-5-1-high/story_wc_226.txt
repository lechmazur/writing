The canyon of Hollowmere did not forget a sound. [9] Every word spoken there came back, and back again, thinned but never gone, so the people who lived among its towers of bone had hammered themselves a silence the way a smith hammers a blade. [44] They called it the forged hush. [50] Ysolde had broken it more times than anyone still living. [60]

She was a glassworker, and glass had taught her the one thing the Keepers never said aloud: light that strikes a pane whole leaves it split, and nothing that has been split can be seized whole again. [97] Tomorrow at dawn her brother Tam would climb the pale spire, the tallest tower, the one that stood always in mist, and speak his truth to the mother of the boy he had killed. [131] It had been a frayed rope, a careless knot, a fall. [142] The mother had asked for the ritual, which was her right. [153] If she forgave him, the canyon would take his voice in payment, every echo of it, and seal the last one into the bone of the spire. [180] That was the price of forgiveness. [186] Everyone paid it. [189] Ysolde had decided that her brother would not. [197]

The children found her on the low ledge after moonrise, as they always did, because she was the only adult who let them shout. [221] There were eleven of them, ash-smudged and grinning, the kind of mischief that stole nothing worse than a nap. [240] "Poems tonight," she said. [244] "But we make them as we go, and we make them out of one thing only." [260] She gave them Tam's truth, plain as a nail: I tied the knot and the knot failed and Oren fell. [280] "Now bend it," she said. [285] "Each of you a different way." [291]

Little Pell went first, because Pell always went first, and rhymed knot with forgot and got the whole ledge giggling. [311] Marra made it a riddle about a rope that wanted to be a river. [325] The twins traded lines back and forth until the truth was a song about a boy who had wanted to see over the mist. [349] Ysolde listened the way she watched light move through colored panes. [360] One truth in, eleven truths out, each carrying the whole and none of them the whole alone. [377] The canyon could seize a voice. [383] She did not think it could seize a prism. [392]

"Won't it take our voices instead?" Marra asked, suddenly not giggling. [403] Ysolde had thought about this longer than about anything she had ever made. [416] "It will take something," she said. [422] "A price divided is still a price." [429] "But I think it takes a word, not a mouth, when the truth arrives in pieces." [445] Pell brightened. [447] "Can I pick which word?" [452] "Bedtime," said the twins together, and the hush broke into laughter that the canyon carried up and up and would carry for years. [475]

Before the ritual, the mist lay so thick that the pale spire seemed to float, a finger of bone pointing at nothing. [497] Tam stood at its base with the Keeper and with Oren's mother, whose face was a shut door. [515] He opened his mouth. [519] Ysolde, on the far ledge, raised her hand, and eleven children scattered along eleven shelves of rib and femur began to speak. [541]

The truth came at the spire from every direction, refracted: a rhyme, a riddle, a song, a whisper that the knot was tied in love and failed in love, a chant that Oren only wanted to see. [578] The echoes met and married and multiplied. [585] The Keeper turned in a circle, hunting one voice to bind, and found a hundred. [600] Oren's mother stood inside that storm of small voices telling her, in every shape a thing can be told, exactly how her son had died. [625] Her door of a face came open. [632] "I forgive it," she said, and the mist around the spire thinned like breath fading from glass. [649]

The canyon took its price. [654] Pell lost bedtime, exactly as requested, and was insufferable about it for a month. [668] Marra lost river and learned to say the long water instead. [679] Tam kept his voice, every echo of it. [687] Ysolde, who had spoken each verse first to show them how, who had said one word more than any child, found on the walk home that there was a shape her mouth could no longer make. [723] She tried it three times, and three times the hush swallowed it. [735] Her brother's name. [738] He heard the silence where it should have been and understood, and took her hand, and did not make her try a fourth. [761] Behind them the canyon went on repeating eleven children's laughter, and somewhere in it, very faint, the word bedtime, which no one there would ever say again. [788]
