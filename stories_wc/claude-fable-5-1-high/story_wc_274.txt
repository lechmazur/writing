The library sat under eleven meters of snow, and at minus forty even the echoes seemed to slow down before they reached her. [23] Ilse Varga had been its cryogenic archivist for twelve years, and in that time she had thawed nothing. [41] That was the job. [45] The Council had not burned the records of the years before the Quiet; burning was final, and the Council liked to keep a hedge. [69] So the truths were frozen instead, contraband in the way a knife is contraband, and Ilse and Tomas kept the temperature. [90] They did not read. [94] They did not discuss what they did not read. [103] Twelve years of that makes a silence with its own gravity, a quiet you lean into like a wall. [122]

Then drawer 4471 failed. [126] A seal cracked, frost bloomed, and Ilse did the only thing protocol allowed: she moved the contents to a thaw tray to save them. [150] Protocol did not say she had to look away while they softened. [162] On top lay a letter, handwritten, folded three times, from Councillor Aud Renner to General Peto. [178] It was a love letter first. [184] It was a confession second. [189] Between the endearments, Renner admitted that the northern towns had been sealed not because the snow was coming but because the two of them had already failed to stop it, and a failure that large needed a grave large enough to hide in. [232]

Ilse's own mother had been in the northern towns. [241] So had Tomas's. [244] Renner's granddaughter now chaired the Council in the settlement above, and Tomas still spoke of her with the careful warmth of a man who had once been invited to dinner. [274]

Ilse read the letter four times. [280] The cold made her lightheaded, or the words did; in the vault it was hard to separate the two. [299] She thought about refreezing it. [304] She thought about carrying it up the ice stairs to the settlement and handing it to the granddaughter, which would be a kind of loyalty, and about carrying it up and handing it to everyone else, which would be a kind of loyalty too. [348] Neither choice let her keep the oath and her mother both. [359] What she wanted, she realized, was smaller and harder than justice. [370] She wanted Tomas to say something. [376] Anything. [377] Twelve years beside him in this ringing cold, and they had never once asked each other what they were guarding or why they stayed. [401] If she asked now, he would answer with protocol, and she would nod, and the wall would hold. [419] The conversation had to be forced from the other side. [429]

Beneath the reading room, the old civic fiber still ran under the snow to every terminal in the settlement, dormant since the sealing, never cut because cutting it would have meant admitting there was something to cut it from. [468] Ilse rerouted the thaw-cycle power into the switching cabinet and listened to the old lines wake, a hum so low it arrived as a feeling in her teeth. [496] She scanned the letter. [500] She queued it to every sleeping screen above. [508] Her hand did not shake, which frightened her more than shaking would have. [521]

Then she went and found Tomas in the stacks and held the letter out to him without a word. [540] She watched him read. [544] She watched the names land, then the towns, then his mother behind the towns. [558] His face opened in surprise, and she understood that the next thing out of his mouth would decide her. [577] If he said refreeze it, she would refreeze it. [586] If he said we cannot, they could not. [594] She had twelve years of practice obeying his silence; she would obey his voice without even trying. [611] So she did not wait for it. [618] In the blink that followed his surprise, the one closed instant before he could choose a face, she pressed the key on the handset in her pocket. [645]

Somewhere above them, through eleven meters of snow, four hundred dark screens lit at once. [660] The vault did not change. [665] The cold did not change. [670] But the quiet did; it tilted, the way a room tilts when you stand too fast, and Ilse gripped the shelf to stay upright. [694]

Tomas opened his eyes. [698] He looked at the letter, then at the handset, then at her, and she saw him understand what she had done and that it was already done. [725] Up there, by morning, the Renner name would be a scandal and a chairwoman would be finished. [742] Down here, twelve years were finished too. [749]

"You could have asked me," he said. [756]

"You would have answered," she said. [762]

He was quiet for a long time, but it was a different quiet now, one with a question inside it, and she stood in the echoing cold and waited for him to ask. [795]
