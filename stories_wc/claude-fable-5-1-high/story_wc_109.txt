The fort on Kellen Ridge had been built to watch for ships, and now there were no ships, only weather. [20] Wind came off the sea in long shoves that made the shutters speak, and between the shoves there was a quiet Maren had learned to hate. [46] She sat at the table with her charcoal and her sheets of pressed linen, and she drew nothing, because there was nothing left to draw from. [72]

Above the ridge the sky had thinned to one star. [82] It hung low over the water, amber and patient, the last of the thousands she had charted since she was a girl. [104] The others had gone out one by one over eleven years, and each time she had inked a small cross where the light had been, until her maps were more cross than sky. [137]

What she wanted now, with a want that had grown from habit into something her sister called an obsession and Maren called a mild one, was a map for after. [167] A map of the endless night. [173] Something a person could hold in the dark and know where the cliff edge was, where the well was, where the paths ran down to the villages that would still need bread. [205] But a map needs bearings, and she was about to lose the last one. [219]

The oracle arrived with the ninth gust. [226] Maren knew her by the sound of her feet on the stair, which was the sound of pebbles shifting. [245] Everyone from the shore walked like that, but the oracle most of all, as though she carried the beach with her. [266] She was old and salt-stiff and she did not knock. [276]

"You sent for the book," she said, and set it on the linen. [289]

It was smaller than Maren remembered, a booklet no wider than her palm, bound in boards and covered in gold leaf that had gone to flakes. [315] Every oracle of the pebble shore for six generations had written in it, and every one had written in gold, because gold was the only thing on that coast that did not rust. [348] Now the letters were lifting off the pages like scales off a dead fish. [362]

"I need the prophecy of the last light," Maren said. [372] "Retell it to me. [376] Whatever it says about what comes after, I need it in my hand before that star goes." [393]

The oracle opened the booklet and turned it to the wind so the last of the gold shivered. [411] "It does not say," she said. [417] "Half the words are gone. [422] What is left is the gaps." [428]

"Then it is useless." [432]

"You were never taught to listen." [438] The oracle sat, and the pebble sound settled with her. [448] "On the shore we do not read the wave. [457] Any child can read a wave. [463] We read the hush after it, when the stones have stopped rolling and the water has not yet come back. [483] That is where the telling is. [489] I will give you this book the same way. [498] I will tell you what it does not say." [507]

And she did. [510] She did not read the words that survived; she summarized the silences between them, page by page, running a thumb across the bare places where the leaf had flaked and naming their shapes. [543] "Here the second oracle stopped writing for the length of a breath. [555] That is the well; it never went dry. [563] Here the fourth left a whole line empty. [571] That is the cliff; she was afraid of it, and she was right to be." [586]

Between gusts the fort held a quiet that was not empty but thick, the color of the star, a hush like something kept in amber. [611] Maren found her charcoal moving. [616] She was not drawing the ridge she knew; she was drawing the ridge the oracle's pauses described, and it was the same ridge, only truer, with its dangers where the silences were longest and its safeties where the gold still clung. [657]

She understood, slowly, what her obsession had been for. [666] A map of endless night could not be a map of light. [678] It had to be a map of where sound stopped, of which hush meant stone and which meant air. [697]

The star went out while the oracle was on the last page. [709] Neither of them looked up. [714]

"This page is blank," the oracle said. [721] "Every oracle left it so. [726] That silence is the longest, and it means you are meant to write in it." [741]

Maren drew on in the dark, by the feel of the linen, the wind marbling the quiet with its long grey veins, and for the first time in eleven years she did not miss the sky. [777]
