Vessa sold feelings she had made herself, and she never called them real. [13] She called them accurate. [17] The grotto where she worked had been a resin mine once, and the walls still held their amber, so that at dusk the last light came through the stone in slow gold layers and made every client look already forgiven. [57] She had been a fragrance chemist before she understood that a scent was only a feeling that had not yet admitted it. [79] The tools had carried over. [84] On the bench lay a fan of blotter petals, narrow paper tongues cut to hold one drop each, and on the newest petal the drop was grief. [111]

Oren sat on the stone stool with his hands between his knees. [123] His mother was dying in a hospital two valleys over, and he had not loved her since he was nine. [143] "I want to sit with her and mean it," he said. [154] "Not perform it. [157] Mean it." [159]

Vessa rolled a glass rod across her sleeve until the wool hissed, then held it an inch from his forearm. [179] The fine hairs lifted. [183] She read them the way other people read faces: the charge a body carried told her exactly what it was already feeling, and Oren's hairs leaned toward the rod in a flat, even field. [217] Relief, mostly. [219] Some shame about the relief. [224] No grief at all, not even the seed of one. [234]

"I can give you grief that will pass any test," she said. [246] "Your pulse, your tears, the nurses, your mother. [254] All of it will believe you. [260] But it will be a half truth. [267] Your body will grieve a woman your history does not miss." [278]

"Is that worse than nothing?" [283] She considered him with the fondness she had for all her clients, which was the fondness a clockmaker has for a clock that has stopped honestly. [309] "I don't sell worse or better," she said. [317] "I sell what holds." [321]

She was thorough in the way that had once annoyed perfume houses. [333] Every batch was tested on her own skin first, and so she drew the grief petal across the inside of her wrist and waited. [357] This was the part she never explained to clients. [366] She could read any feeling in the room down to its voltage, and she had never once been moved by one. [387] Insight without empathy was a map of a country she was not allowed to enter. [402] Each test was a small attempt at the border. [411]

The grief came up through her wrist like cold water finding a crack. [424] She named its parts as they arrived, because naming was what she was for: the weight behind the sternum, the sudden importance of small objects, the way the amber light began to look like something she was losing. [462] It was good work. [466] It was also, she noted with the same affection she gave Oren, not hers. [480]

She blotted it off with alcohol and it went the way it always went, leaving the knowledge of grief and none of the grief. [504] "It's clean," she told him. [509] "One petal under the tongue at the bedside. [517] It will take about an hour to complete." [525]

He paid and went up the mine steps into what was left of the evening. [540] Vessa did not usually follow a product past the door. [550] But thoroughness has a gravity of its own, and three days later she stood in a hospital corridor with a glass rod in her coat pocket. [576]

Through the half-open door Oren sat holding a hand that had stopped holding back. [590] She charged the rod against her sleeve and let it hang near the doorframe, reading the room at a distance. [610] The field around him had changed shape. [617] The relief was still there, and the shame, but the grief she had sold him had not sat on top of them as a layer. [642] It had subsumed them. [646] The relief had become the grief's reason; the shame had become its texture. [659] As the transformation completed, the charge settled into something she had never recorded from a counterfeit: a whole. [677]

A half truth, it turned out, was not a fixed quantity. [688] Given a real body and a real dying woman, it went looking for its other half. [704]

Oren looked up and saw her. [710] He did not seem to know whether to thank her or accuse her, and she liked him for not deciding. [730] She put the rod away. [735] Then, because she was thorough, she wrote in her small book that the formula required one ingredient she could not bottle, which was someone to sit beside. [762] On the walk back to the grotto the hairs on her own wrist stood up once, faintly, without any rod at all. [784] She noted it. [787] She did not name it yet. [793]
