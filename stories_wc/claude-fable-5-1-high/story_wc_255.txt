The furnace they called the Phoenix rose on the first morning of summer, when Ambra the glassblower fed it the ash of last year's fire and the courtyard walls began to sweat. [32] Teodor arrived the day after, carrying his enamels in a case with every pot labeled in his own hand. [51] He had painted glass in four cities and been found out in none of them. [65] The labels were the trick: cobalt, iron red, antimony yellow, each pot a word he trusted more than his eyes, which gave him a world of ash and honey and nothing between. [97]

Ambra wanted forty panes for a chapel, and he wanted them flawless. [109] Teodor watched him hold each blown sheet to the light and smash any that carried a seed bubble, then sweep the pieces into the cullet bin without a word. [137] The daughter, Pia, swept up after him. [144] She did not speak; the neighbors said she had stopped the winter her mother died, and Ambra had stopped with her, in his way. [168]

On Teodor's third morning a bundle lay on his bench: a fan of grey-green sage leaves, and in the center one flower he could not name. [194] It looked, to him, like all the others. [202] He asked the apprentice at the annealing oven what it was, lightly, as if making conversation. [218] "Poppy," the boy said. [222] "Red as a slap." [226]

Teodor looked across the courtyard and found Pia watching him with the mild, patient face of a child who has set a snare and seen the rabbit step into it. [256] He understood then that she had tested him twice already, and that her innocence was a kind of instrument she played very well. [279]

The next bundle was a proposal. [285] Rosemary, which even he knew meant remembrance, bound around a scrap of moss, and resting on the moss a sliver of brass no longer than his fingernail, notched at one end. [316] A reed tongue from a concertina. [322] He had seen the instrument itself, or its shape, under a cloth in the workshop's locked chest, the one Ambra passed without looking. [345] Pia had pried the reed loose the way she pried everything loose, quietly, and now she laid it before him like a coin. [368]

The terms were plain enough. [373] She would keep his secret and teach him what she knew of seeing; he would wake her father. [391]

Over the following weeks the lessons arrived at dawn. [400] Thyme laid beside lavender, so he would learn that two things his eyes called grey could be told apart by the nose. [422] A curled fern beside a straight reed, for line. [431] A bruised apple beside a sound one, and the bruised one placed on top. [445] He began, without the labels, to paint what he actually saw: the Phoenix's glow as a wash of pale honey, the shadows in the courtyard as deep ash, the poppy as a shape whose darkness he loved even if its color was closed to him. [490]

The forty-first pane he made for no chapel. [498] He fired it with his true colors, the wrong ones, and set it in a lead frame, and along the top rail he cut a slot the width of a fingernail. [529] The reed tongue went into the slot. [536] He hung the pane in the courtyard arch where the furnace draft breathed out every morning when the Phoenix was stoked. [557]

Ambra came out at first light with his blowpipe and stopped under the arch. [571] The pane was ugly by any rule he owned: a sky the color of a healed burn, a poppy nearly black, a seed bubble Teodor had left in the glass on purpose, catching the light like a held breath. [610] Then the draft rose, and the reed sounded one thin note, and Ambra's hand went to the wall. [628] It was the note his wife had tuned every autumn, off by a hair from true, because she liked it that way. [650]

He did not smash the pane. [656] He stood under it a long time, and when he turned, Pia was in the doorway with her broom, and behind her Teodor, with his pots unlabeled. [683]

"You can't see red," Ambra said. [689] It was not a question. [694]

"No." [695]

"Then how did you know to make it dark?" [704]

"I didn't," Teodor said. [708] "I made it the way it looks." [715]

Ambra looked back at the pane, at the bubble in it, at the wrong sky humming with his wife's off-note. [735] That evening the locked chest stood open, and the concertina lay on the bench with one reed missing, and Ambra was finding, with his big scarred fingers, whether it could still play around the gap. [770]

On Teodor's bench lay a last arrangement: a poppy, a sage leaf, and a bubbled drop of glass, placed so the flaw caught the light. [795]
