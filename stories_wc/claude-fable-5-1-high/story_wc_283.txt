The amber warehouse sat beneath the cliffs like something the canyon had swallowed and forgotten. [15] Inside, Tamsin turned hourglasses. [19] Nine of them lined the long bench, each timing a pour of pine resin as it cooled into the honeyed slabs the town sold downriver, and when one ran out she flipped it and moved to the next. [57] It was work that asked for nothing but attention, and attention was what she had. [72]

On the highest shelf, behind a curtain of dust, sat the jar. [84] Forty years ago a seer had climbed the cliffs, shouted her last words into the canyon, and died before the echo came back. [107] The canyon returned the words in a tumble, syllables folded over syllables, and old Marrow the warehouseman had caught the sound in a pickling jar and sealed the lid with resin. [138] Twice a year the council opened it. [145] Out came a rushing hiss of vowels and fear, and everyone heard what they dreaded most: the cliffs falling, the river turning, the last glass running dry. [172] Then they sealed it again and went home quieter than before. [183]

Tamsin had never heard doom in it. [190] She heard a sentence with its buttons torn off. [199] That was Marrow's phrase, from the ledger he left her. [209] Beneath the bench sat his other legacy: twelve drawers of lost buttons, each pinned to a card with a date and the place in the canyon where it was found. [239] Horn, brass, mother of pearl, bone. [245] Marrow had walked the canyon floor every dawn for fifty years, stooping for what people dropped, and he had written in the ledger: Nobody loses a button standing still. [274] She had read that line a hundred times before it struck her, the way light strikes amber and finds something inside. [295]

The day the seer spoke, the whole town had stood in the canyon. [308] Tamsin pulled the cards for that date. [315] Fifty-three buttons. [317] Fifty-three people who had clutched their coats or clawed at their collars when the words came down, scattered along the canyon floor from the narrow mouth to the wide basin where the sound would have arrived last. [354] She began to catalog them properly, not by material as Marrow had, but by distance from the cliff, the only order that might unscramble an echo. [380]

Then she took the buttons back to their owners. [389] The cooper's widow recognized her husband's brass anchor at once and, turning it in her palm, said she remembered only the word glass. [412] The schoolteacher, who had been a girl at the canyon mouth, remembered "the sand." [426] A fisherman far in the basin had heard "turn," and nothing else, and had built forty years of dread on that one syllable. [449] Nobody had heard the whole. [454] Everyone had kept a piece, sealed in themselves like a fly in resin, and each piece had grown terrible in isolation. [475]

Tamsin wrote the fragments on the cards beside the buttons and laid the cards along the bench in the order the echo would have reached them. [501] It took eleven days. [505] The hourglasses kept running; she kept turning them. [513] On the eleventh evening she read the bench from end to end, and the truth unraveled in her hands like an old knot finally given the right pull. [541]

When the sand runs out, turn the glass. [549]

That was all. [552] Not a doom but an instruction, the plainest thing a person could be told, the very thing Tamsin did nine times an hour. [575] The seer had not been prophesying an ending. [583] She had been telling the town what to do after one. [594]

Tamsin climbed for the jar. [599] She carried it out to the canyon floor, where the evening had gone so still she could hear the resin ticking as it cooled in the warehouse behind her. [628] The silence there was not empty; it was buried, the way a seed is buried, waiting for someone to dig. [648] She had never thought of herself as brave, only as quietly inspired by a dead man's habit of stooping for what others dropped. [671]

She broke the seal. [675] The echo came out scrambled, as it always had, a rush of fear-shaped sound, and rose against the cliffs, and this time she did not try to catch it. [704] It struck stone and came back, and for one clear moment fifty-three fragments met in the open air and were a sentence. [726] Then it was gone, and the canyon held only the ordinary hush of wind. [740]

In the morning she would lay the cards before the council, not as a verdict but as pieces, so they could see that it took all of them to make the words mean anything. [774] A prophecy unscrambled was only raw possibility: sand in a glass, and a hand deciding to turn it. [792]
