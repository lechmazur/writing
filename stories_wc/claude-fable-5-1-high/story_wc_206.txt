Ansel Pell mended music boxes, which mostly meant mending things that were not torn. [14] A child overwound a spring; a widow shut a lid too hard on a memory; a comb tooth went flat from being sung to instead of oiled. [41] He listened, and the box told him where it ached. [51]

So when the mayor came to say the Lantern Tower's light had been stolen by midnight, Ansel asked the only sensible question. [73] "Midnight has no hands," he said. [79] "What did the light say before it left?" [87]

Mayor Corvane did not know lights said anything. [95] He knew only that Wrenhollow had gone dark for nine nights, that the founders' song under the tower had gone flat, and that a repairman was cheaper than a wizard. [125]

They descended together at a quarter to twelve. [133]

The resonance chamber lay beneath the tower like the belly of a bell. [146] Brass ribs arched overhead, freckled orange with age. [154] A cylinder the size of a hay cart turned slow as a sleeper, its pins plucking a comb whose teeth were long as oars. [178] The song that came out was the founders' hymn, and it was wrong the way a smile is wrong when the eyes do not join in. [204]

Ansel walked the length of the comb with his palm an inch above the teeth, feeling for heat, for hairline splits. [225] Nothing was torn. [228] Every pin stood straight; the spring held its wind; the governor spun true. [241] The rust itself was healthy, the honest bloom of a machine that had told the truth about its age for three hundred years. [264]

Above the comb, cut into the wall, ran the covenant every Wrenhollow child learned before their letters. [281] LET RUST COME. [284] LET NOTHING HERE PRETEND TO BE YOUNGER THAN IT IS. [294] AND THE LIGHT SHALL STAY TETHERED. [300]

The covenant of rust. [304] Ansel had always thought it a lovely piece of nonsense until he saw the ember. [319]

It huddled in the resonator's throat, no bigger than a thumbnail, the color of the last coal in a grate. [339]

"There," he breathed. [342] "Nobody stole it. [345] It came down here to hide." [351]

"Ridiculous," said Corvane, and the ember shrank. [358]

Ansel had seen a hundred springs go slack and knew the look of a thing losing heart. [375] "Say something true," he said. [380]

"I don't take orders from a tinker." [387]

The ember steadied; that much, at least, was honest. [396]

Ansel crouched at the cylinder's heart, where a small socket sat empty as a pulled tooth. [412] Its edges were bright. [416] Something had been pried loose within the month. [424]

"The founders set a tether here," Ansel said slowly. [433] "Something that holds light in the balance between what it is and what it could be." [449]

"Old stories." [451]

The ember wilted to a pinprick. [457]

Ansel turned. [459] The mayor's watch chain caught the last of the ember's glow, and it did not glow back the way brass should. [480] It shone without a single freckle of tarnish, and a chip of dull red stone hung from it like a drop of blood that had forgotten to fall. [508]

"Nothing you own rusts anymore, does it," Ansel said. [517] "Not your chain. [520] Not your gate hinges. [524] Not the tower clock, which people have noticed looks awfully young." [535]

Corvane's hand closed over the chip. [541] "It's a sliver of the philosopher's stone," he said, and his voice went thin. [555] "The founders set it here to turn rust to brass and back, forever, so the song would never stop but never lie about its age either." [581] "I only wanted the town to look prosperous." [589]

The ember brightened, just slightly, at the confession. [597]

Overhead the tower clock began the long climb toward twelve. [607]

"Give it to me," Ansel said. [613] "There is a moment at midnight when the balance wheel hangs between one day and the next, and nothing has decided yet what it is." [638] "That's when it left. [642] That's when it can be tethered back." [649]

The mayor's fingers did not want to open, and then they did. [661]

The chip was warm and heavier than anything so small should be. [673] Ansel seated it in the socket as the eleventh chime faded, and he held his breath in the gap that followed. [694]

In that gap, the comb found its true pitch. [703] The rust on the brass ribs bloomed, then dimmed, then bloomed, breathing the way it was built to breathe. [722] The ember uncurled from the resonator's throat, stretched like a cat, and climbed the song upward, up the bell of the chamber, up the stair, up into the dark lantern where it swelled until the windows of Wrenhollow turned gold. [762]

The twelfth chime rang. [766]

Corvane stood in the returning light and looked, for the first time in a month, like a man his own age. [787]

"Nothing torn," Ansel said. [791] "Only put back." [794]
