The sky went wrong on the ninth of Thawmonth, the way it did once a generation: the sun rose in the north, thin as a coin, and the noon stars came out to watch it. [35] Ilsa Merrow was already climbing. [40] Every thief in the valley knew the story: when the sky is wrong, the clouds tear open above the Needle, and the icebound spire that the Warden of Wonders calls home can be reached by anyone fool enough to try. [80] Most thieves also knew that nobody came back rich. [89] Ilsa did not climb for riches. [95]

She had a code, inherited from her grandmother along with a set of lockpicks and a brass pocket sundial lid with no sundial under it: take only from those who hoard, never draw blood, and always leave something of equal worth behind. [137] The lid was engraved on its inside face: THE HOUR HIDES IN SHADOW. [150] She used it as a mirror for peering around corners, and as a reminder that some things are only half of what they were meant to be. [177]

In her pack, wrapped in wool, rode thirty-two chess pieces she had carved herself over three winters. [194] Each was hollow. [197] Each held a slip of paper with a secret she had gathered: how the harbor bell sounds from underneath the water, which alley in Marrow Street stays warm in January, the name of the boy who taught her to whistle and then died. [240] The Warden, the story said, kept his wonders inside a chess set. [252] Ilsa meant to swap them, piece for piece, and leave him her small world in exchange for his large one. [272]

Above the clouds the wind stopped pretending to be weather and became a wall. [286] The spire rose out of the white like a frozen scream, sheathed in ice a hand thick, and the north sun lit it the color of old teeth. [314] Ilsa checked every handhold twice and then trusted it completely; that was the only way she knew to climb. [333]

The door at the top was unlocked, which she found insulting. [344] The Warden sat at a table by a window full of daytime stars, an old man in a robe of patched felt, with a chessboard before him and one empty chair. [375]

"You brought your own pieces," he said, delighted. [383] "Nobody ever brings their own pieces." [389]

She should have run. [393] Instead she sat, because a thief who leaves before the exchange is only a burglar. [408]

He proposed the rules as if they were a hymn: they would play with mixed sets, her white against his black, and every captured piece would be opened and its secret read aloud. [441] The captor kept the piece. [446]

He laughed when he took her first pawn and read about the harbor bell, and he laughed harder when she took his knight and found inside it the exact weight of a hummingbird's heart. [480] It was not mockery. [484] He handled each slip like a relic and each joke like a prayer, and she understood that for him the game was a rite and the laughter was the liturgy. [514]

She lost her bishop and gave up the warm alley. [524] She took his rook and learned why the sky goes wrong: he unfixes it himself, once a generation, to see who climbs. [546] "The ones who come to take, the cold sends home," he said. [558] "The ones who come to trade, I play." [566]

When he took her queen, he unscrewed it and read the dead boy's name, and did not laugh. [584] He set the slip down gently and drew a ledger from beneath the board. [598] It listed names, most crossed through. [604] The last uncrossed one was Merrow. [610]

"Your grandmother lost to me on this board sixty years ago," he said. [623] "She took the lid off my sundial and left me a promise instead." [636] He pushed the ledger toward her. [642] Beside her grandmother's name, in faded ink: One of mine will come to learn. [656]

Ilsa took out the brass lid. [662] On the windowsill sat a pocket sundial, lidless, its gnomon casting a shadow that pointed at no hour at all, because under a wrong sky there is no hour to point to. [694] The lid fit with a click she felt in her teeth. [705]

"I don't know what an apprentice of wonder does," she said. [716]

"Neither do I, most mornings," said the Warden, and handed her the pen. [729] "Amend it." [731]

She crossed out learn and wrote wonder, checked the word twice the way she checked handholds, and then let go of everything she had climbed with. [757] Outside, the north sun began, slowly, to remember where it belonged. [768] Ilsa did not look for it. [774] She was opening the last of his pieces the way one opens arms to a stranger. [790]
