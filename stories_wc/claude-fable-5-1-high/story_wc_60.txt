Vesna did not believe in the fountains. [7] She believed in pipes, in pressure, in the copper valve wheel rusted shut beneath Founders' Square, and she said so to anyone who asked, which was almost no one. [36] She also glowed. [39] Faintly, the way a banked coal glows, a condition the doctors called benign and the neighbors called unsettling, so she kept lamps for the city and let people assume the light came from the oil. [74]

The geese changed that. [78] Every autumn they crossed over Meridal without landing, a ragged arrow pointed south, because a city of dry basins offers nothing to a bird. [102] This year they paused. [106] Vesna found them at dusk, hundreds settled in the cracked bowl of the Weavers' Fountain, necks folded, silent as stones. [126]

Around the rim, someone had scattered bread. [133] Not thrown, scattered, in a deliberate line that left the basin and went on down Loom Street. [150] She told herself she followed it because a trail of bread in a hungry city was a question with a practical answer. [172]

The crumbs led her fountain to fountain: the Lion, its mouth choked with leaves; the Three Sisters, headless now; the little Drinking Fountain by the school where her mother had once lifted her to the spout. [208] She could not remember the taste of it. [216] She could remember wanting to. [221]

The trail ended at Founders' Square, at the sealed grate where the Council had shut the water off forty years ago, citing cost, and then shut off the talking about it, citing peace. [254] An old woman sat on the grate with a stained glass box in her lap. [269]

"You glow," the woman said. [274]

"I know." [276]

"Good. [277] I am nearly out of candles, and the lock wants light." [288]

Her name was Ilka. [292] She had been the keeper of the waterworks before the sealing, and every clear night for forty years she had climbed to the square and marked the position of one star against the cathedral spire. [327] "Not the sun's year," she said. [333] "The stars' year, which keeps its own clock. [341] I kept its count. [345] I waited for the night the star and the spire lined up as they did the day the water stopped." [365]

Vesna wanted to say that geese do not read stars, and found she was not certain. [381]

Ilka opened the box. [385] Inside, packed in straw, lay a brass key and a valve wheel's missing spoke, and beneath them a stack of small tin plates, each stamped with a name. [413] "The Council melted the plaques off every fountain so no one would remember who built them," Ilka said. [431] "I stamped copies. [434] But a name on tin is a dead thing. [443] A name over running water is a memory that moves." [453]

Vesna did not believe that either. [459] But she believed the mechanism. [464] She could see it as clearly as if the cobbles were glass: the main below them, the great wheel seized on its stem, the seals fused by four decades of mineral crust. [496] Leverage would move it, and heat, and someone willing to be arrested. [508]

"The lock wants light," Ilka repeated, and turned the box so its colored panes faced the grate. [525]

Vesna understood then, and almost laughed. [531] The grate's lock was no enchantment but a keeper's trick, a tumbler that only aligned when a certain pattern of color fell across its face, red and blue and amber in the order of the old waterworks crest. [569] Ilka's candles had never been steady enough. [576] Vesna knelt, and let herself glow. [582]

The lock gave. [585]

Below, in the dark, she fitted the spoke to the wheel, wrapped her coat around the stem, and heated the crust with the lamp oil until it cracked like ice on a pond. [618] Then she set her weight against forty years and pushed. [628] Nothing. [629] She pushed again, and Ilka's hands joined hers, old and thin and absolutely unhurried. [643] The wheel unstuck with a sound like a held breath let go. [655]

They climbed out to a city that had not yet noticed. [666] Somewhere below Loom Street, pipes were knocking, waking, filling. [675] Ilka walked the crumb trail backward, and at each dry basin she pressed a tin plate into the stone where the plaque had been. [699] Vesna followed, lamp shuttered, lit only by herself. [707]

At the Drinking Fountain by the school, the spout coughed, spat rust, and ran clear. [722] She bent to it before she could decide not to. [732] Cold, and iron, and under the iron something sweet, and with it, whole and sudden, her mother's hands under her arms, lifting. [754]

She straightened, wet-faced, and did not argue. [761]

Behind them, in the Weavers' basin, water rose around the sleeping geese until, one by one, they woke and lifted and resumed the south, having waited exactly as long as they needed to. [794]
