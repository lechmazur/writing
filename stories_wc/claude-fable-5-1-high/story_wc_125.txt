Since the energy beings arrived, the hours had stopped agreeing with each other. [13] Clocks still ran, but a Tuesday might last a week in one town and an afternoon in the next, and nobody had learned to argue with it. [40] Wren wrote poems on flattened cardboard at highway on-ramps and traded them for rides, one verse per hundred miles. [59] She carried almost nothing: a pencil, a canteen, and a frayed coil of rope that had once been four bedsheets. [79] Dez had braided it in the dark of the Harlow county lockup, testing each knot with her teeth, and it had held exactly one woman's weight. [105] "Go," Dez had said, feeding the rope through the window bars. [116] "Write me something." [119]

Wren had never hidden any of it. [126] When a deputy outside Tulsa asked what the rope was for, she said, "Metaphor," and he laughed and let her ride on. [148] She signed every poem with a small drawing of a frayed end, and once, feeling honest, wrote THIS POEM WAS STOLEN across the top. [172] It was not that she took the escape lightly. [181] It was that guilt, worn openly, weighed less than guilt buried, and she had a long way to walk. [200] What she wanted, more than pardon, was to know what time was made of. [214] The beings did not speak, but they left patterns wherever they rested, and truckers said one settled most nights in a terra-cotta grotto south of the salt flats. [242]

She reached it under starlight, walking the last four miles on a road the maps had given up on. [261] The grotto was a hollow in red clay, its walls banded like cut sediment, and the air inside held a warmth that did not come from the day. [289] The being was there, or nearly there: a faint brightness in the clay itself, like a lamp behind a curtain. [309] Wren sat down and waited, because waiting was the one thing the new world had taught everyone. [326] Nothing happened, so she began to scratch at the wall with her thumbnail, the way she had scratched at the mortar in Harlow. [349] The top layer of clay came away in a curl, and beneath it lay a pattern: three lines braided, over and under, over and under. [374] She knew that braid. [378]

Her hands went cold, and then, because cold was useless, they went to work. [392] Layer by layer she peeled the grotto open, and each layer held the same braid, older and tighter as she went deeper, the strands pressed so close they became a single rough line. [425] The warmth grew as she dug, and with it a feeling she had no poem for: that she was not scratching clay at all but running her fingers along the grain of the last three years. [461] So this was the texture of time. [468] Not smooth, not a river; it was fibrous and frayed, three strands twisted hard against each other, the thing that happened and the two things that did not. [496]

At the deepest layer the clay was still wet. [505] The braid there was loose, barely begun, and she understood the offer without a word being spoken: press here, and the rope in Harlow would hold two. [532] Dez would come down the wall behind her. [540] And every layer above, every mile and every poem, would slough off like the curls now lying at her feet. [560] The poems she had written for Dez, all of them, would never have been written, because there would have been no one to write them to. [586]

Wren laughed, softly, the way she laughed at deputies. [595] It was the same choice, only turned over, and she had already learned that a rope built for one does not become a rope for two by wishing. [623] She took the frayed coil from her shoulder and laid it in the wet clay, pressing it gently so the braid in the wall and the braid in her hands became one line. [656] Then, in the soft top layer she had not yet touched, she wrote with her pencil the poem she had been carrying for three years and never finished. [684] It said that time is a rope and a rope is a choice and every choice frays, and that fraying is not failure but the only way a thing can be held. [716] She signed it with a frayed end. [723]

The brightness in the clay pulsed once, and the pattern in the wall shifted: the third strand, the one that had never happened, loosened and settled beside the others like a hand let go. [757] Wren walked back to the road under the same stars, lighter by one coil, and waited at the on-ramp for a Tuesday willing to carry her east, toward Harlow, where a woman was still serving time she had chosen.
