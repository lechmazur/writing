Tamsin was the last human born, and the ship remembered it better than she did. [15] The Mendicant had been welded together from eleven decommissioned hulls, and every corridor still carried the ghost-lettering of some older name: Cassiopeia, Hope Deferred, Freight Unit 9. [42] Her birth was logged in the medical bay in a hand that no longer existed, and below it, decades of blank lines. [64] Everyone who had written in that ledger was dead now, and she was sixty-one, and there would be no one after. [85]

The Door hung in the cargo hold where the old crew had dragged it, a seam of folded space the width of a coffin. [109] They had found it drifting near a dead star and believed it opened onto somewhere that still had a future. [129] They had tried to force it. [135] Cutting lasers, shaped charges, finally the mining railgun that took Petrov's arm and half the hold's atmosphere. [152] Tamsin had refused to help with any of it, and they had called her useless, and she had accepted the word the way she accepted the cold. [179] She would not strike a thing to make it open. [189] Not a door, not a man, not the universe that had decided to stop making people. [205]

The Door had never budged for violence, but it had trembled, once, when old Mireille wept against it. [223] Nobody else had noticed. [227] Tamsin had, because she understood weight better than anyone aboard. [237] Since childhood her body had answered her moods: on the rare good days she drifted an inch above the deck plates and had to hook her toes under rails, and on the bad days the ship's gravity seemed to double and she moved like something sunk. [283] The medics had called it a glitch of the womb tanks. [294] She had spent forty years trying to stay light. [303]

The lens had belonged to Mireille, a teardrop of grey glass on a cord, and Mireille had called it Kwan Yin's Tear after a goddess who heard every cry in the world and did not look away. [340] Through it, ordinary things showed their salt. [347] Tamsin had discovered this by accident, holding the Tear over a photograph of Petrov's daughter, and seeing a faint shimmer bloom across the faded paper where his tears had dried decades ago. [379] The shimmer had a shape. [384] Held near the Door, the shape pulled toward it like a filing toward a magnet. [399]

So she gathered them all. [404] Every photograph left in every dead crewmember's locker: weddings on planets that no longer existed, children who had grown old and childless aboard this same patched hull, a dog. [433] She read each one through the Tear, traced where the salt shimmered, and stitched them together along those lines with thread pulled from a decommissioned pressure suit. [460] Grief to grief, seam to seam, until the quilt of faces was large enough to wrap a person. [478] It took eleven months. [482] Her needle went through Mireille's smile, through Petrov's missing arm, through her own infant face in the only photograph anyone had bothered to take of the last child. [510]

She understood, somewhere around the seventh month, what the Door actually wanted. [522] Not force. [524] Not lightness either, though she had tried that, drifting against it on a good day and sliding off like breath off glass. [546] It wanted the thing she had spent her whole life refusing to carry. [559] Her pacifism had never been only principle. [566] It was also a way of staying weightless: never hate, never rage at the emptiness, never let the full mass of being last settle into her bones, because if she did, she might never stand again. [602] The quilt was the shadow she had been stitching in the dark, and now she had to wear it. [621]

She wrapped it around her shoulders in the ruined hold and let herself feel all of it. [638] The anger at the crew who had wasted their last years shooting at a wall. [653] The anger at herself for standing aside so cleanly while Petrov bled. [665] The grief that no one would ever again be new. [675] Her knees hit the deck. [680] She weighed more than the ship. [686] She did not strike the Door. [692] She leaned her whole impossible mass against it, palms flat, the way you lean on a friend's shut door when you have finally come to say the true thing, and she pushed. [724] The seam gave with a sound like cloth tearing. [733] Light came through, and it was not empty. [741] Tamsin felt her heels lift from the deck for the first time in eleven months, and she hooked her fingers into the quilt of the dead and let it hold her down long enough to step across. [778]
