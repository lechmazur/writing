Ilse Marrow had tested storm sirens in forty valleys, and she had never once been asked to stay the night. [20] The sanctuary of Saint Oren sat on a shelf of granite so high that the clouds gathered beneath it like sheep at a gate. [44] The wind never stopped. [48] It came through the cloister arches and hummed in the bell tower, and it had been the valley's only warning for six hundred years. [72] Now the council below had bought a siren, mounted it on the tower's north face, and hired Ilse to certify it at zero hour: midnight on the solstice, when the new warning year began. [106]

"You will want to see the rope," said Sister Hedda, who kept the tower alone. [121] Ilse did not want to see the rope. [129] She wanted to check her decibel meter and go home. [139] But she was, by long habit, a curious woman, and Hedda had watched her all afternoon crouching at the terrace edges with a thermometer and an anemometer, writing numbers in a damp notebook. [172] It was a curiosity people tended to believe, because it asked nothing of them. [185]

"You track the little weathers," Hedda said. [192]

"Microclimates," Ilse said. [195] "A siren is only as good as the air it shouts into." [207]

"Then you will understand the rope." [213]

The cathedral bell rope hung the full height of the tower, thick as Ilse's wrist, and it was frayed nearly through a yard above the floor. [239] Hedda had bound the break with cord and a pot of hide glue, which was still tacky. [256] "It will hold after the glue dries," Hedda said. [265] "Three hours, in this cold." [270]

"Nobody will need it after tonight," Ilse said, and heard how it sounded, and wished she had not. [288]

Hedda only nodded, and lit the two candles the season allowed, and went to make tea. [304]

Ilse went back out to her numbers. [311] Solstice night above the clouds was not dark so much as hushed; the snow gave back a starlight so even that it seemed to come from the ground. [339] She walked the terraces every half hour. [346] The east ledge ran two degrees warmer than the west. [356] The wind at the north face, where the siren hung, curled upward off the cliff and did not descend. [375] At the tower's open belfry, though, the air poured down in a slow cold river toward the valley, and she could hear, faint as breathing, a dog barking three thousand feet below. [407]

She wrote it down. [411] Then she wrote down what it meant, because she was honest even in her own notebook: sound went where air went. [432] The siren would scream into the updraft and spend itself on the stars. [445] The bell rode the river down. [451]

Hedda brought the tea at eleven. [457]

"Tell me how they listen," Ilse said. [464]

"Two slow strokes for ice on the pass," Hedda said. [474] "Five quick for the river rising. [480] One long toll, and then nothing, for a death." [489]

"The siren has one setting." [494]

"Yes." [495]

Ilse looked at the switch box she had wired that morning, a gray steel thing bolted to the sanctuary's oldest wall. [516] She had installed a hundred of them. [523] She had never before wondered whether anyone below could tell one alarm from another, or whether they simply learned to stop hearing it. [546]

"I am only the tester," she said. [553] "I don't decide." [556]

"No," Hedda agreed. [559] "You only tell them the truth about the air." [568]

At zero hour the candles had burned to stubs and the glue had gone hard and pale along the splice. [588] Ilse opened the switch box. [593] The council's protocol said: throw the switch, sound the siren for sixty seconds, record the reading, sign. [610] She threw it. [613]

The siren rose, a flat electric howl, and she watched her meter and watched the snow and felt the wind take the noise straight up into the enormous quiet, where it went nowhere at all. [648] At sixty seconds she switched it off. [655]

Then she did what the protocol did not say. [664] She took hold of the cathedral bell rope below the hardened splice, and she pulled: two slow strokes, so the valley would know it was only ice, only practice, only a woman testing. [697] The bronze spoke and the cold river carried it down. [707] Far below, one by one, windows lit. [714] Then the dog stopped barking, and Ilse understood that it, too, was listening. [727]

She wrote her report by the last candle. [735] Siren functional. [737] Acoustic reach at solstice conditions: negligible. [743] Recommend the council retain the bell and repair the rope properly, and that the tester's fee be applied to a new one. [765] She signed it plainly, Ilse Marrow, siren tester, and did not underline anything. [778]
