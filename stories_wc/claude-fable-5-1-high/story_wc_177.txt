Ilse's gift had always arrived late. [6] She dreamed in exact pictures, and every picture meant nothing until the thing it showed had already happened; then she would wake, page back through her journal, and find it waiting there like a bill. [41] Foresight, in hindsight only. [45] Useless, her mother had said, and mostly it was. [54] But the forest did not seem to think so. [63] For three days the deer trails had widened whenever she doubted them, closed behind her whenever she looked back, and now they ended at a wall of ivy so thick it hummed with bees. [97] Behind the ivy, concrete. [101] Behind the concrete, a steel door rusted the color of dried blood, and beyond it a bunker that the rewilded country had swallowed and half digested. [127] Roots had pried the ceiling into a grin. [135] Vines came down through the gap like slow green rain. [145] The air beneath tasted of mildew and old lightning. [154] Thunder rolled somewhere west, still miles off, but the pressure was already leaning on her ears. [170] She had come to ask the forest one question, and she had come to ask it out loud, because she was tired of being polite to a thing that pushed her around. [202] "Why me?" she said to the dripping dark. [210] "Why does a path pick its walker?" [217] The dark answered with a desk. [223] Steel, bolted to the floor, one drawer swollen shut and one drawer open. [236] Inside the open one lay an envelope, and on the envelope a red wafer seal, brittle as a communion host, embossed with a crest that moss had already begun to edit. [267] Ilse's knees went soft. [271] She knew that red disc. [276] She had drawn it eleven times in eleven years, always in the corner of a page, never understanding it, and now here was the corner of the page made real. [306] The seal had been broken before. [312] Many times, by many thumbs; the wafer was more crack than disc. [324] She lifted it and drew out a single sheet so foxed and water-eaten that it seemed less a document than a rumor of one. [348] STANDING DIRECTIVE, it said, and then the typewriting dissolved into brown. [359] HOLD THIS STATION UNTIL, and then nothing. [366] Except the nothing was crowded. [371] Around the rotted gap, in a dozen hands and a dozen inks, people had written their answers. [388] UNTIL RELIEVED, in confident pencil, struck through by the next hand. [399] UNTIL THE WAR ENDS, and under it, smaller: which one? [409] UNTIL THE TREES COME IN. [414] UNTIL SOMEONE DREAMS THE DOOR. [419] That one stopped her breath. [424] The last annotator had drawn a neat box below the gap, ruled with a straightedge, and printed inside it in tiny capitals: ONE WORD. YOUR OWN. NO PEEKING AT THE ANSWER FIRST. [456] It was precisely playful, a game with rules as exact as a surgeon's and a wink in every serif, and it was aimed at her the way a rifle is aimed. [487] She understood then what the directive was. [494] Not an order. [497] A sentence built to rot, so that it could never be finished by the people who wrote it, only by the people who arrived after, reading backward from the ruin. [527] People like her. [530] The path did not choose the strong or the wise; it chose the ones who made meaning out of what had already decayed, because that was the only kind of meaning the path had left to give. [567] Lightning whitened the vines. [571] Rain began to needle through the ceiling's grin. [579] Ilse opened her journal to last night's dream, still unmatched, still nonsense: a door standing open in a downpour, and herself walking out of it with her hands empty and her face wet. [612] By every rule she had ever lived under, she would understand that picture tomorrow, when it was too late to matter. [633] She decided, with the storm leaning on the door, to cheat. [644] Not the box's rule; hers. [649] She read the dream forward, as a promise instead of a receipt, and in that small treason transcended the only gift she had ever been given. [675] In the neat box she wrote one word, her own, in wet pencil: NOW. [689] Then she pressed the cracked wafer back onto the envelope, not to seal it but to mark that a thumb had passed, and left it in the open drawer for whoever the trails widened for next. [725] The steel door shrieked on its hinge. [732] Rain hit her face like thrown gravel, and the forest, lit green and violet by the coming storm, did not close behind her this time. [757] It simply waited, every trail open at once, as if it had never really been choosing at all, only holding, like the station, until someone walked out ahead of her own hindsight and finished the sentence for it.
