The arbor hung above the clouds on nothing anyone could see, though Nadia Reiss had spent eleven years learning to see it anyway. [23] Iron trees rose from an iron floor, and between their branches the air hummed with the lattice of magnetism that kept the whole orchard aloft. [48] The old gardeners had left no manual, only tablets. [57] Clay, wedge-marked, baked hard, stacked in the roots like fallen leaves nobody had swept. [71] Nadia translated cuneiform. [74] That was why they had sent her up, and why she had stayed. [87]

For most of those years she had not worked alone. [97] The spirit of the arbor, if that was the word, had moved among the trees as a warmth on the metal, a presence that leaned close whenever she read aloud. [127] It never spoke. [130] It corrected her by nudging a tablet an inch to the left, by cooling a branch she had misnamed. [149] She had come to think of it as a colleague who kept one desk away, always within touching distance and never touched. [171]

Three weeks ago it had gone. [177] She could not say how she knew, except that the humming had changed pitch, and the trees had begun, very slowly, to sag. [200] The lattice was unwinding. [204] Without its keeper, the arbor was forgetting how to stay up. [215]

Nadia did what she always did when frightened: she made lists. [226] She numbered the tablets, brewed tea for the two apprentices who had not yet fled downward, and sat them at the roots with soft brushes and softer instructions. [254] "Clean, do not read," she told them. [261] "Reading is my job, and I would rather you did not inherit my ghosts." [275]

The ghosts were real enough. [280] Every translator who had come to the arbor before her had wanted the same thing, and the wanting had ruined them. [301] Their notes were still here, margins crowded with the same phrase in six hands: the physics of transition. [319] How iron became lift. [323] How a seed became a tree that remembered. [331] How a mind could cross from body to orchard and back. [342] They had all wanted to know it, and one by one they had gone quiet, then strange, then down the long stair without their coats. [367] Nadia had promised herself she wanted only to read. [376] Now, with the floor tilting a degree each morning, she admitted the promise had been a fiction. [393]

The central tablet was the problem. [399] Its signs were ordinary Akkadian, a ledger of grain rations, so ordinary that six translators had set it aside as filler. [420] Nadia turned it in her hands and thought about the spirit, and about how she had learned it: not by asking, but by watching what it did with what it was given. [452] A ledger records what goes where. [458] A magnetic field is also a ledger: so much force here, so much there, every debit balanced against a credit or the whole account fails. [483]

She laid the tablet on the floor and walked the rations out along the roots, a step for each measure of barley. [505] The pattern the numbers made was the pattern of the trees. [516] It was a map of the lattice, disguised as bread. [526] One entry was missing its recipient. [532] The gap fell exactly where the spirit had liked to stand. [543]

In the crook of that tree, where no fruit had grown in eleven years, she found a single seed, dark and heavy as a bullet. [568] The tablets called it, unhelpfully, the fruit of knowing. [577] Nadia understood then what the others had reached for, and why they had fallen. [591] Eat the seed and you would know the physics of transition from the inside, the way the spirit knew it, by becoming the transition yourself. [616] You would hold the arbor up forever and never again hold anything else. [629] The spirit had not left her. [635] It had finished, after a long shift, and gone to wherever tired keepers go, leaving the seed for whoever wanted the job. [657]

She wanted it. [660] The wanting was so sharp she had to sit down. [670]

Then she did what a ledger would do. [678] A seed is an account not yet opened; a planted seed is a debt the ground agrees to carry. [697] She pressed it into the iron at the missing entry and wrote the recipient's name herself, in wedges, with a stylus of copper wire: the arbor. [723] The hum reestablished itself through her knees, uncertain, then steady. [733] Somewhere near the crook of the tree, a warmth leaned close, new and shy, a presence that would learn the tablets as she read them aloud. [759]

Nadia did not reach for it. [765] She had understood enough of transition to know which side of it she was standing on, and she found, to her surprise, that she could bear to stay there. [794]
