The heat wave broke at midnight, and by dawn the cobalt lighthouse stood cool for the first time in nineteen days, its blue paint sweating fog instead of light. [29] Ilse Varga, assistant keeper for three years and daughter of the keeper for twenty-two, climbed the spiral stairs with a vial in her fist. [53] Below her, the foghorn screamed. [58] Her father had been sounding it since the squall swallowed the horizon, every ninety seconds, a bellow so large it shook rust from the railings. [83] Tomas was out there. [87] Her brother had taken the skiff the moment the wind turned cool, laughing that the fish would rise after so much heat, promising to be back before the lamp was lit. [118] The lamp had burned all night. [124] The horn had roared all night. [130] Nothing had come home. [134]

In the lamp room Ilse set the vial on the sill and looked at it the way her grandmother had taught her to look at anything small: as if it were the only thing in the world that could hear her. [175] The old woman had distilled it the summer before she died, from the garden she kept behind the oil shed: night jasmine, tobacco flower, wet black soil, the things that only opened after dark. [209] "Everything you want hums," she had said, pressing the cork in with her thumb. [223] "Shouting scatters the hum. [227] Whispering aims it." [230] Her father called this grief talking, and had said so at the funeral, loudly, so the whole island could hear how much he disagreed with a dead woman. [258] Ilse had never argued. [262] She had simply kept the vial, and waited for a morning terrible enough to need it. [278]

The horn screamed again. [282] She felt it in her teeth, in the glass, in the bones of the tower, and she understood for the first time what her grandmother had meant: the sound was enormous and it was going nowhere, breaking against the fog like a fist against a pillow. [328] Her father was not calling Tomas. [334] He was calling the sea, the weather, God, his own helplessness, everyone who had ever failed to come back to him. [355] There was too much in it to reach anyone. [364]

Ilse pulled the cork. [368] The scent came up like a door opening onto a garden at night, and the lamp room was suddenly not a lamp room but a dark bed of earth under stars, damp and sweet and alive. [404] She breathed it, and felt her wanting begin to simplify. [414] This was what the old woman had promised: the scent did not grant anything, it only purified what you asked for, burning off the fear and the anger and the bargaining until only the true request remained. [451] Ilse felt the dross go. [456] Not come back so Father stops screaming. [463] Not come back so I am not left alone with him. [473] Not come back because you promised. [479] Only: come back. [482]

She held the cork between two fingers like a tuning fork and leaned her mouth to the cold glass. [501] "Tomas," she whispered. [504] It was not a plea. [509] It was a beckoning, a hand curling once in the dark, the way you call a child who already knows the way. [531] She felt it leave her, thin and exact, and she felt the fog not stop it. [547]

The horn screamed. [550] She whispered again. [553] Below, the screaming faltered, and then, for the first time in seven hours, it stopped, and in the silence she heard her father climbing. [577] He filled the doorway, grey with salt, and stopped when the scent reached him. [591] His face did something she had not seen since the funeral. [602] "That's her garden," he said, and his voice was so small that she barely caught it. [618]

"Come here," Ilse said. [622] She put the cork in his palm and closed his enormous fingers over it. [636] "Say his name. [639] Not loud." [641] He looked at her as though she had asked him to lift the tower. [655] Then he bent to the glass beside her, and the two of them stood shoulder to shoulder in the smell of a dead woman's garden and whispered a boy's name into the fog, and the wanting that went out of the lighthouse was so quiet and so vast that Ilse thought the whole sea must be able to hear it. [715]

The skiff came out of the white an hour later, low in the water, one oar gone. [732] Tomas was grey and shaking and alive. [739] He said he had been rowing in circles for hours, blind, and then he had smelled Grandmother's garden on the water, impossibly, miles out, and had simply turned the bow toward it. [771] Their father said nothing. [775] He put the cork back in the vial with his thumb, very gently, as if it might bruise. [793]
