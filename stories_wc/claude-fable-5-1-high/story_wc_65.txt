The obsidian spire kept no clocks, only the lake. [9] Every winter it froze from the shore inward, and every winter Ilse Varne, Cartographer of Ignorance to the Council of Hollen, walked out onto it with a kingfisher quill and a bucket of ash. [43]

Her title confused visitors. [47] Other cartographers drew what was known; she drew the edges of it, the coastlines where the Council's knowledge stopped and guesswork began. [69] The maps were unpopular and indispensable. [75]

Tonight the ice was thick enough, and tomorrow she would write the Charter. [88] Once a generation, on a single sheet of vellum, one page and no more, the Cartographer set down the rule by which Hollen would live for the next forty years. [118] It was law the moment the ink dried. [126] The page was already waiting upstairs, blank, weighted at the corners with obsidian. [139] She had not touched it. [144] She would not, until the lake had finished speaking. [153]

For three months she had used the ice as her modeling table. [165] With the kingfisher nib, honed narrow enough to cut frost, she scratched a branching tree of futures across the surface each night: harvest yields, birth counts, the price of grain in the eastern towns, the river's rate of silting. [204] Each branch carried a weight she computed from the Council's ledgers, forty years of them, and she rubbed ash into the grooves so the numbers would show black against white. [234] By morning new frost had softened the weak branches and preserved the strong ones, as if the lake itself were pruning her guesses. [257] It was not magic. [261] It was cold and arithmetic, and she trusted it more than she trusted prophecy. [275]

The Council wanted a wall. [280] They had told her so, in the spire's black hall, their breath rising in the torchlight. [296] Write that Hollen shall trade with no one across the ridge. [307] Write that the granaries shall hold three years' surplus. [316] Write certainty, they said, and we will sleep. [324]

She had modeled the wall. [329] It prevailed for eleven years in every branch, then failed in most of them, sometimes by famine, sometimes by a generation of young people who simply left. [356] Certainty, it turned out, was brittle. [362] The frost erased it every time. [368]

Now she knelt at the center of the lake with a lantern and studied the surviving branches. [385] They had something in common she had not expected. [394] Each began not with a command but with a gap: a Charter that named something Hollen did not know and set aside a portion of the granary tithe to find out. [425] What lies past the ridge. [430] Why the river silts. [434] Whether the eastern grain will grow in northern soil. [443] The branches that named an ignorance and funded it did not merely survive forty years. [458] They kept changing, and the changing was what kept them alive. [469]

She understood then what the single page was for. [478] The constraint was not a cage; it was a seedbed. [488] One sheet could not hold a plan for forty years, and every Cartographer who had tried had written a wall. [508] But one sheet could hold a question and the means to pursue it, and a question was the only thing she had ever seen grow. [533]

She turned the quill in her fingers. [540] The kingfisher was the right bird for it. [548] It lived at the boundary, diving through the mirror of the water into what could not be seen from above, and came back up with something real in its beak. [578] That was the whole of her craft, she thought: imagine hard, but return with a fish. [594]

She climbed the spire before dawn. [600] The Council would be angry; they had asked for sleep, and she would give them work. [616] But the lake had been patient with her for three months, and the ice did not lie about which futures held. [637] She had the numbers to show them, ash-black and frost-tested, and she had seen what they had not: that the only rule which lasted was the one that arranged for its own revision. [670]

The page lay under the obsidian weights, white as the lake. [681] She uncapped the ink. [685] She dipped the nib and held it a finger's width above the vellum, the drop trembling at its tip. [704] In this moment, before the first stroke, the future was still every branch at once. [719] Then it would be one. [724] She meant to make it the branch that kept branching. [734]

She lowered the nib. [738] The first word would not be a command. [746] It would be: Unknown. [750]
