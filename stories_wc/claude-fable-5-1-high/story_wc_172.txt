The thunder had been walking toward Ilse Varga all afternoon, and now it paused, the way a guest pauses at a door. [22] She stood on the salt flat that had been a lake when she was nine. [37] In her coat pocket, wrapped in a handkerchief, was the last egg of Kessler's grebe. [52]

The drought was hers. [56] She had not caused it by accident, and she had not caused it by greed, which was what confused the journalists. [77] Eleven years ago she had funded the Varga Diversion, a canal that carried the Serel River east to four towns that had never had enough water. [103] The towns named schools after her. [109] The lake, cut off from its only river, shrank for six summers and then stopped being a lake. [127] Nine fishing villages on its shore dried with it. [136] She had paid every family to leave, generously, and had felt nothing she could name. [151]

What she felt about the grebes she had never told anyone. [162] The birds had gone extinct the year she turned twenty, drowned out by motorboats and gill nets before the water ever failed. [184] She remembered their call from childhood, a doubled note like a spoon struck twice against glass, and she remembered her grandfather rowing her out at dusk to hear it. [213] Nothing of that sound survived except in her, and in the lakebed. [225]

The grebes had nested in floating colonies over the western shallows. [236] When the water left, the nests settled into the mud, and the salt came up through them and set them like plaster. [258] A hydrologist she paid well had told her, without understanding why she asked, that the crust out there was honeycombed with hollows the size of cupped hands, and that in a north wind it hummed. [293] She had built the canal for that hum. [301] Every drop of the Serel she sent east was a drop that could not dissolve it. [317]

She had never been able to find the colony. [326] The flat was forty kilometers across and the same white in every direction. [339] But that morning, walking the dead channel where the Serel used to enter, she had looked down and seen that the cracks were not random. [364] Mud shrinks along the lines it once flowed. [372] The fissures ran in a long braid where the current had been fast, and split into fine polygons where it had slowed and spread. [396] The riverbed had drawn its own map. [403] She followed it west for three hours, reading the widening cracks the way her grandfather had read the surface of the water. [425]

Now she stood where the polygons became a fan of small, regular cups, each rimmed in salt, each holding a fused litter of shell. [449] Hundreds of them. [452] The colony. [454]

Her phone had buzzed twice on the walk. [462] The engineer at the diversion headworks was polite and frightened. [472] The storm in the mountains had dropped a season of rain in a night, the canal could not take it, and if she did not authorize the release the spillway would fail and take the eastern towns with it. [511] Release meant opening the old gates. [517] Release meant the Serel coming home down this channel, following the same map she had followed, and finding the colony at the end of it. [542]

She had not answered. [546]

She considered, briefly, letting the towns drown. [553] It was not a monstrous thought to her, only an arithmetic one, and that was the thing about herself she had long ago stopped apologizing for. [579] But the arithmetic did not come out. [586] Four towns of children who could say her name against a hum only she knew how to hear. [604]

The thunder held its breath. [609]

She knelt and unwrapped the egg. [615] It was chalk-blue, lighter than it looked, bought from a bankrupt museum through three intermediaries so that no one would ask why. [637] She had slept with it on the nightstand for four years. [648] She set it in the nearest cup, among the shards of its siblings, and the salt rim held it as if it had been waiting. [673]

Then she took out the phone and said one word to the engineer. [686]

She stayed while the light went. [692] The wind came around to the north, and the crust began, faintly, to hum, a doubled note, glass struck twice. [712] She did not know whether she was hearing the flat or her grandfather's boat. [726] It did not matter. [730] Far up the dead channel, still an hour off, she could hear the other sound, the low one, the river remembering the way. [753]

She had given nine villages to silence and would give the silence back to the water, and she would let no one know that any of it had been for a bird. [785] The thunder started walking again. [790]
