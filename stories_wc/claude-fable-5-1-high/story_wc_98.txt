Nell Anvara had checked reality on Tessery Island for eleven years and had never once confirmed a miracle. [18] She had measured the ghost-lights of the eastern cove with a stopwatch and found them to be fishing lamps. [37] She had weighed the singing stones and recorded, in her neat hand, that they hummed only when the wind came from the south. [60] The council paid her for this. [66] An island that believed everything, they said, would remember nothing true. [77]

But this year the old ones were dying faster than the young were staying. [91] The ferries left full and came back light. [99] The archive shelves were gapped like a mouth losing teeth. [109] And Grandmother Tesh, on her last morning, had gripped Nell's wrist and said the ridge was blooming, and the seed would waken if someone dreamed it properly, and it must be done before the monsoon or it would be another forty years. [151]

Nell knew the story. [155] Everyone knew it, which was exactly what worried her. [164] Somewhere on the ancient ridge, among the white blooms that opened only in the sticky weeks before the rains, grew the knowledge fruit. [187] Its seed held everything the island had ever known. [196] Plant it, and the island would remember itself. [204] Lose it, and the memory went with the water. [213]

A lovely thing to believe. [218] Unverifiable, which was different from false, though she had spent eleven years pretending it was not. [234]

So she did what a reality checker does with an unverifiable claim: she gathered data. [249] The legend said the seed woke through dreams, so she went door to door with her notebook and asked people what they had dreamed since the ridge began to bloom. [279] It felt absurd and she did it rigorously, which was the only way she knew how to be. [297] Tesh had once called her empirically whimsical, and Nell had taken a year to decide it was a compliment. [316]

Forty-one dreams. [318] She tabulated them. [321] Thirty mentioned a bell that did not ring. [329] Twenty-six mentioned three stones stacked like a child's game. [338] Nineteen mentioned a woman facing the sea with her back to the dreamer, and eleven of those said she was Tesh. [359]

A dream is not evidence. [364] Forty-one dreams that agree, she wrote in the margin, are at least a pattern. [378]

There was an old bell tower on the ridge's northern shoulder, roofless since the storm of her childhood, its bell long since cracked silent. [402] She climbed at dawn with the air already thick, the clouds building purple over the strait like a held breath. [422] The ridge was in full bloom, so white it hurt, the flowers heavy and sweet and buzzing with the last bees before the rain. [446]

She found the tower. [450] She found, behind it, three flat stones stacked exactly as twenty-six sleeping strangers had described. [465] Her hands were not steady, and she noted that too. [475]

Under the stones was a clay jar, and in the jar, wrapped in oilcloth gone soft as skin, was a seed the size of her thumb, dark and ridged and utterly ordinary. [507] There was also a folded paper, and on it, in a hand she did not know, a list of dreams. [527] A bell. [529] Three stones. [531] A woman facing the sea. [536] Dated forty years before. [540]

Nell sat down in the flowers. [546]

She understood then, or thought she did, and the thought was so bright she had to squint at it. [565] No one had ever verified the knowledge fruit because the fruit was not the point. [580] Every forty years someone climbed up here with a notebook full of other people's dreams, found a seed left by the last climber, and left one for the next. [609] The island remembered itself by making someone doubt hard enough to go and check. [623]

Or perhaps the seed was exactly what Tesh said it was, and the island was about to wake. [641] She could not tell. [645] She found she did not need to. [652]

The first fat drops struck the flowers as she planted the seed in the soft ground below the tower, pressing it down with the same thumb she had used to measure it. [684] Then she took a second seed from the seedhead of a spent bloom, plain and brown, wrapped it in her own handkerchief, wrote out the forty-one dreams, and set them under the stones. [717]

Back in the archive, soaked, she opened the ledger to the page for the knowledge fruit, where eleven years ago she had written Unverified. [741] She did not cross it out. [747] Beneath it she added: Still unverified. [753] Planted anyway. [755] Check again in forty years. [760]

Outside, the monsoon came down on the ridge and the whole island, and somewhere under it, she was nearly certain, something was beginning to wake. [785]
