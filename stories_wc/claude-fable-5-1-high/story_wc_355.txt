Wren Aldous had catalogued forty-one varieties of silence, and she intended to have forty-two before the sky caught fire. [19] Her workshop squatted on the roof of a shuttered piano factory, its glass ceiling long since shattered and never replaced, so the stars hung directly over her shadowed benches like an audience that had paid nothing and expected everything. [58] Below, the whole town had gathered at the river for the midsummer fireworks, and the last vendor had stopped shouting. [78] This was the hush she had been waiting for all year: the held breath of a crowd before the first rocket, a silence with its neck craned. [105] She wrote that phrase in her ledger without thinking, the way she always described things, by instinct first and by reason never. [127]

On the bench before her lay the real reason she had climbed up here tonight: her sister Isolde's diary, its brass clasp locked, the key buried with Isolde three months ago because nobody had thought to check her pockets. [166] Anyone else would have taken a screwdriver to it and called that recovery. [179] Wren had not struck anything in twenty years, not a nail, not a match, not the man who once took her purse, and she was not about to begin with the last thing her sister had touched. [216]

"Every lock is a throat," she announced to the stars, because she had never learned to think quietly. [234] "And every throat has a note it cannot refuse." [243]

Her workshop proved it. [247] Tuning forks hung from the rafters in ranks, salvaged from the factory below; a theremin stood beside a dentist's drill she had rewired for its whine; a wall of glass jars held recordings of nothing, each labeled in her looping hand. [288] The silence of a church before the organist arrives. [297] The silence after a slammed door. [303] The silence of a hospital room where one sister lies dying and the other cannot find a single word, which she had recorded and could not bring herself to label. [333]

She clipped a contact microphone to the clasp and set a small oscillator humming. [347] The trick was patience: sweep the frequency slowly, watch the needle, listen for the moment the metal began to answer back. [368] She had opened a music box this way, and a church safe, and a rank of organ pipes rusted shut. [388] The town below stayed hushed, and the stillness pressed on her ears like water. [402] She needed it. [405] The clasp's true note would be faint as a moth, and one cheer from the riverbank would drown it. [424]

Three hundred hertz. [427] Nothing. [428] Four hundred, and the brass gave a sullen shiver, a sound she would have described, had anyone asked, as a child pretending to be asleep. [453]

Isolde had been that child. [458] Their father had been loud, and Wren had answered his loudness by becoming soft, deliberately and forever, while Isolde had answered it by becoming quiet in a way Wren had always mistaken for coldness. [492] Forty-one silences, and she had never once tried to tune to her sister's. [505]

Four hundred and twelve. [509] The needle leapt. [512] The clasp sang, thin and pure, a hairline of sound, and the tiny pin inside it danced loose, and the diary fell open on the bench as gently as a hand unclenching. [544]

Wren did not cheer. [548] She read by starlight, and the handwriting was small and careful, and the first entry she found was dated the night of their father's funeral. [573]

"Wren talks to the sky and to strangers and to broken machines," Isolde had written, "and I sit beside her with a whole chorus in my chest and cannot get one note past my teeth." [608] "She thinks I have nothing to say." [615] "I think she has never waited long enough to hear it." [626]

Wren set the book down. [631] The workshop was full of instruments for hearing what no one else heard, and she had pointed every one of them away from the person sitting closest. [658] She had made a religion of gentleness and forgotten that listening was its only sacrament. [673]

Far below, the crowd was so quiet she could hear the river. [685] She picked up her pen, found the line for number forty-two, and did not write what she had climbed up here to write. [708] Instead: "The silence of someone who is waiting to be asked." [719] Then she crossed to the wall, found the unlabeled jar, and wrote the same words on it. [736]

"I am sorry," she said aloud, to the stars, to the open diary, to the whole hushed town, because she had never learned to think quietly and did not intend to start. [768] "I am listening now." [772]

Then the first rocket tore upward, and the sky answered her with everything it had. [787]
