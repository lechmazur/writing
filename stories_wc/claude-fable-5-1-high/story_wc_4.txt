Wren Halloway kept the puzzles nobody else would keep. [9] In the back room of the Marlow town library she archived forty years of factory newsletter crosswords, riddles scratched into lunch pails, and one jigsaw of the mayor's face missing its nose. [41] She was a subtle archivist; she catalogued not the answers but the shapes of the questions. [57]

The dove gray map case arrived in a donation crate marked MISC, which in Marlow meant grief. [74] Inside, rolled tight as a secret, were thirty-one black-and-white photographs of the Ashby Wheelworks picnics, one for each summer before the boiler went. [97] The town did not speak of the boiler. [105] Eleven names were on the plaque by the gate, and the ruins behind it had rusted into something the children called the Dragon. [128]

Wren spread the photographs across her table and felt the calm that always came when a pattern started breathing. [147] Every picnic, the workers had arranged themselves in an arc. [157] Not a line, not a cluster, an arc, deliberate as a compass stroke, with the foreman's daughter always at the leftmost tip holding a wheel rim like a halo. [186] Wren traced each arc onto vellum and stacked them. [195] One summer's curve nested inside the next. [202] Thirty-one arcs, turned and layered, closed into a single enormous wheel. [213] The Wheelworks had been building a picture of itself, one picnic at a time, and no one had lived to finish it. [235]

"You were a puzzle," she told the case, perceptively calm, the way she spoke to anything that had waited a long time. [257] "Somebody meant for you to be solved." [264] She wanted, quietly and completely, to turn the town's worst summer into a masterpiece. [278]

Behind the factory ruins, in the loop of land where the rail spur used to turn, there was a field nobody farmed. [300] Wren walked it in April with the vellum in her coat. [311] The wheel fit the field exactly, as if the land had been holding its breath for it. [328]

She went to the town council with the map case under her arm and a proposal that she knew sounded ridiculous: plant lavender along the thirty-one arcs, spoke by spoke, and let Marlow walk it. [363] "Lavender," said the treasurer. [367] "Rims," said Wren, and unrolled the photographs, and the room went quiet in the specific way of people recognizing their own grandmothers. [389]

They planted in May, as the first vines began climbing the Dragon's ribs. [402] Wren assigned each arc to the families in its photograph. [412] The foreman's daughter, now seventy-two and named Dolores, took the leftmost tip and planted with a wheel rim propped beside her like an old friend who had come to watch. [442] People who had not spoken since the funerals found themselves kneeling in the same furrow, passing seedlings, arguing gently about depth. [463] Nobody said the word boiler. [468] Nobody needed to. [471]

Wren did not plant. [475] She lingered at the edges with the map case, matching living faces to silver ones, and when someone stood up too fast and turned away with wet eyes, she would drift over and ask for help reading a smudged date. [515] It was not a trick. [520] It was an archivist's kind of mercy: give a person a small question to hold and their hands will remember what to do. [543]

By July the vines had reached the factory's shoulder, green over rust, and the lavender had come up in blurred purple arcs that hummed with bees. [569] By August you could see it from the water tower. [579] A wheel, whole, tucked behind the ruins like a secret the town had finally agreed to tell itself. [597]

Dolores climbed the tower with Wren on the last hot evening, slowly, complaining the entire way with great cheer. [616] At the top she looked down for a long time. [626] "My father said the picnics were for morale," she said. [636] "He never said what he was making." [643] "Maybe he didn't know," Wren said. [649] "Puzzles usually get finished by strangers." [655]

Below them, people were still in the field, though the light was going. [668] They walked the spokes in twos and threes, pausing where their own arc met the next, lingering the way you linger at a door you have decided not to close. [698] Somewhere near the hub a child laughed, and the sound rolled outward along the rows. [713]

Wren pressed the dove gray case against her chest. [722] It was empty now; the photographs hung in the library, thirty-one arcs in a ring, with a thirty-second frame at the end that held only a photo of the field taken that morning, purple and alive. [758] She had labeled it, in her small careful hand, the way she labeled anything that had turned pain into a shape worth keeping. [781] Completed. [782]
