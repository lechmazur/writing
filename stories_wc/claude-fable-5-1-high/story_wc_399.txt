The pavilion had murmured for as long as anyone in the settlement could remember. [14] Its cloth walls were woven from the shrouds of the learned, and each evening the wind moved through them and gave back, softly, what the dead had whispered into the weave while they still breathed. [49] Ilse walked beneath it at dawn, as line walkers had always done, wearing the mask that covered the left half of her face. [72] The mask was not decoration. [77] It blinded one eye so the other would learn to trust its edges. [90]

Beneath the dunes ran the parapet. [96] No one had seen it in centuries, but everyone knew it moved, a long wall of fitted stone that slid grain by grain to keep the ground under the pavilion from wandering with the sand. [131] It could only be followed through peripheral awareness. [139] Look straight at the dune and it was only dune. [149] Let the gaze rest on the horizon and, at the corner of sight, a faint ridge of paler sand marked where the stone breathed underneath. [174] Ilse walked that ridge every morning, and every morning it was almost where she had left it. [191]

Then the prophet arrived, and the murmur began to fail. [201]

He came on foot with no followers, which surprised everyone, and he was strangely honest, which surprised them more. [220] When the elders asked what truth he carried, he said he carried none. [233] When they asked why he had come, he said he had heard that a cloth in the desert repeated the wisdom of the dead, and he wished to listen to it. [264] But people do not know how to receive a prophet quietly. [275] They filled the pavilion with questions. [281] They brought their sick and their grievances. [288] They talked over one another about what the prophet must mean, and under all that talking the cloth's murmur thinned to nothing. [310]

On the fourth morning Ilse could not find the line. [320] The pale ridge had drifted a full stride toward the well, and the stone beneath it moved in a slow, uncertain wave, like something that had lost its rhythm. [349] She understood then what the old walkers had never quite said aloud: the parapet listened. [364] It held its shape to the echo of wisdom. [373] Without the echo it had nothing to hold, and the dune would come over the pavilion within a season. [392]

She found the prophet sitting alone at the pavilion's edge, his hands open on his knees. [408] "They will not let me hear it," he said before she spoke. [420] "I tell them I have nothing to say and they take that for a riddle." [435] "You could leave," Ilse said. [440] "I could." [442] He looked at her mask without curiosity. [449] "But then I would never have heard it." [457]

There was an old practice, rarely used, that line walkers called conjuring a clearing. [471] It was not magic in the way the settlement imagined magic. [482] A walker chose a closed line, walked it once in full attention, and consecrated the ground inside it to silence. [502] People who crossed such a line did not fall quiet because they were forced. [516] They fell quiet because the quiet was already there and they did not want to be the one who broke it. [537]

"Sit in the center," Ilse told him. [544] "Do nothing. [546] Do not answer anyone." [550]

She went out into the morning and let her eye rest on the far horizon, where the sky was the color of bone. [573] At the edge of sight the pale ridge showed itself, faltering, and she stepped onto it. [589] She walked the whole circuit of the parapet around the pavilion, not looking down, dragging the heel of her staff so a shallow groove followed her through the sand. [618] Where the stone beneath had drifted, she felt it correct under her feet, small shudders settling back toward the line. [638] At the place where she had begun she stopped, knelt, and pressed her bare palm flat to the groove. [657] That was the whole of the consecration. [664] The rest belonged to the ground. [670]

Inside the line, voices dropped one at a time. [679] A man who had been shouting his question at the prophet heard himself and stopped. [694] A child was hushed, then the one who hushed her. [704] Within the span of a hundred breaths the pavilion held only wind, and the cloth began, faintly, to speak. [723]

Ilse did not go in. [728] A walker did not enter her own clearing. [736] She stood at the edge with her blind side turned to the crowd and her seeing eye on the sand, and she watched the pale ridge hold steady, grain by grain, as the murmur returned. [771]

Behind her, she heard the prophet weeping quietly, the way people do when they finally hear what they came for and it is not about them at all. [799]
