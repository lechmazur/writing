The station had been built to catch what fell. [9] Six concrete dishes on a plateau of red stone, aimed at the dark, once fed by a government that wanted to know where the meteors came from and whether any of them carried a message. [44] The government stopped asking in 1974. [50] The astronomers left in trucks. [55] Nobody came to lock the door, so Ines kept it. [65]

Her contract, typed on a form nobody had updated, said "custodian, celestial." [77] She liked that. [80] Cosmic janitor, the last radio man had called her, laughing, before he too drove down the switchbacks. [97] She swept the dust that settled on the dishes, and some of that dust, she knew, had fallen from very far away. [119] That was not nothing. [123]

Through the hush of that overlooked era, the years between the program's death and whoever would eventually come to tear the dishes out, Ines learned the sky by hand. [152] She had no training. [156] She had a broom, a logbook, and a shard of yellow lantern glass she had found in the drawer of the founder's desk, curved like a cupped palm, still smelling faintly of kerosene. [189] When she held it to her eye, the faint stars vanished and only the bright ones remained, and the falling ones, when they came, drew clean gold lines across the black. [220]

She had come up the mountain because down in the valley she had been seen too clearly. [237] A marriage had ended in a way that left her feeling skinless, and she had wanted a place where nothing looked back. [259] The plateau obliged. [262] For a year she did not speak aloud except to the logbook. [274]

Then, one August, the Perseids came, and she found she could not stop watching. [288]

She began to trace them. [293] Not with instruments; the instruments were dead, their tubes gone cold. [304] She traced them with her arm, the lantern glass held before her, following each streak from its first spark to the place where it went out, and then she wrote down where it had gone. [339] She did not know why at first. [346] It felt like the way you follow a bird with your eyes after it has passed, wanting to know it got somewhere. [368]

She noticed things. [371] The stones that came in fast and shallow burned longest. [381] The ones that came straight down flared once and were gone. [392] And every one of them, without exception, was invisible until the moment it began to be destroyed. [409]

That was the thing she kept turning over in the still nights, spiritually curious in a way she had never permitted herself in the valley, where curiosity had been a kind of weakness. [442] A meteor is a stone in the dark. [450] It has crossed distances she could not hold in her mind, cold and whole and unseen. [466] Then it touches the air, and the air takes something from it, and in the taking the stone becomes light. [486] Not in spite of the loss. [492] Because of it. [495]

She began to empathize with stones. [501] It embarrassed her, the first time, to stand on a concrete dish at three in the morning with tears on her face for a piece of iron the size of a walnut. [533] But the feeling did not go away, and she stopped trying to make it. [547] She followed each one through the yellow glass and thought: I know that. [560] I know what it is to be seen only in the moment you cannot protect yourself. [576]

The station did not change. [581] The dishes held their silence. [586] But inside Ines something began to grow in that silence, the way lichen grows on the north side of a stone, slow and certain and without any need for an audience. [617] A flourishing stillness. [620] She did not become loud. [625] She became steady. [628]

In the spring she went down to the valley for supplies, the first time in two years. [645] The woman at the store recognized her and looked, the way people look. [658] Ines let herself be looked at. [664] She felt the old heat rise, the old wish to vanish, and she stood inside it and did not go out. [685] Afterward she sat in the truck a long while with the lantern glass in her palm, warm from her pocket, and understood that she had been wrong about the sky. [715] The stars did not fall in order to be destroyed. [725] They fell because that was the direction of the world, and the burning was simply what happened to a thing brave enough to arrive. [749]

She drove back up the switchbacks before dark. [757] The dishes were waiting, open to whatever might come. [766] She climbed onto the nearest one, lay down on the cool concrete, and raised the yellow glass. [783]
