The university radio station lived in the basement of the engineering building, under pipes that sweated all winter, and Mara Voss had not slept there so much as surrendered to it. [31] Her title on the door said Overnight Programming. [39] Her title in the grant paperwork nobody renewed said celestial signal interpreter, which was a fancy way of saying she listened to the sky and told people what it meant. [69] For six weeks the sky had been lying. [77]

It started as a hum the whole town of Ashby felt in their molars around two in the morning. [96] Then it became a thing people had opinions about. [105] The east side blamed the university tower, the west side blamed the east side for being cowards, and the council meetings had begun to end with folding chairs on the floor. [136]

Mara had a different theory, and she kept it taped to the wall above the transmitter rack: three astronomical charts, hand-annotated in pencil, with a single point in Cygnus circled so many times the paper had gone soft. [174] The hum only came when that point cleared the eastern ridge. [185] It stopped when it set. [190] Nothing in the catalogs lived there. [196]

Worse, it answered. [199] Night after night she had pulled the signal off the dish on the roof and dropped it down into the audible range, and every time she changed her method, the pattern changed too, one step ahead, like a card sharp who lets you see the ace and then makes it a deuce in your hand. [254] She had stopped calling it a source. [261] She called them the tricksters, plural, because the signal had the rhythm of something laughing in more than one voice. [281]

Dean Halloran did not care about tricksters. [288] He came down the stairs at eleven with the station manager trailing him like a wet dog, and he told Mara the university would be surrendering the broadcast license to a commercial partner effective Monday. [323] The town needed a gesture, he said. [330] The tower would go dark. [335]

Mara understood the shape of it. [341] Halloran had wanted that license for years. [348] The hum was simply the first crisis convenient enough to spend. [359] Small power plays on the ground, and above them a bigger one, patient and amused, letting a town tear itself into halves over a noise it could not name. [388] She thought the tricksters were probably enjoying the dean very much. [399]

After they left she did the math she had been avoiding. [410] The hum sat at nineteen hertz, below hearing, right where the human body starts mistaking pressure for dread. [428] Nobody in Ashby heard it. [433] They only felt afraid and looked around for someone to blame. [444]

You could not outshout a thing like that. [452] But the station's tower could push subsonic frequencies too, if she bypassed the audio limiter and let the old transmitter run hot and ugly, and a wave met with its own inverse becomes, briefly, nothing. [487]

The trick was that the tricksters adapted. [494] So she would not fight them where they expected. [503] The charts gave her the moment: 1:52 a.m., when the circled point would clear the ridge and the signal, still faint, still finding its footing, would be at its most predictable. [534] She would interfere in the first ten seconds, before it had anything of hers to mimic. [550]

She spent the hour with a soldering iron and a cold cup of coffee, jumpering the limiter with wire scavenged from a dead cart machine. [575] The pipes dripped. [578] On air, a sleepy jazz record played to maybe forty listeners who had not yet picked a side. [596]

At 1:51 she watched the spectrum display, breathing through her nose. [607] At 1:52 the floor of the readout lifted, a soft green swell at nineteen hertz, and Mara threw the switch and the tower groaned against the frost and sent a wave of pure inverse dread out over the sleeping town. [647]

The swell flattened. [650] For a moment the signal stuttered, hunting, the way a voice does when it realizes it is being repeated back. [670] Then it went quiet. [674] Not gone. [676] Quiet, the way a table goes quiet when someone new sits down. [688]

Mara sat back in the chair with the burn holes in its arms and did not let herself smile all the way. [710] She had bought Ashby one night of sleep, maybe two. [720] The dean would still come Monday. [726] The tricksters would still learn. [731] But she had learned something too, sitting under the sweating pipes with her charts going soft on the wall: they could be surprised. [754] Anything that can be surprised can be beaten. [762]

She picked up the pencil, found tomorrow's rising time on the chart, and circled it once. [778]
