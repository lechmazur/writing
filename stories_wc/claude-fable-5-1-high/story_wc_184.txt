Ines Marrow had been an archivist in four departments in three years, which was either a compliment or a warning, and she had decided to treat it as a compliment. [30] When the university stopped its clock for renovation and sent her up the tower to catalogue the mechanism room, she brought labels, a thermos, and the habit of talking to whatever she was sorting. [64] "You first," she told the ledger on the workbench, and poured the tea. [77]

The room was all iron and patience: the great train of gears, the pendulum hanging still, two bell hammers cocked above her head. [100] Two hammers, one bell. [104] The second hammer swung, when the clock ran, at empty air. [115] She noted it, because a warmly methodical person notes the odd thing before deciding whether it matters. [132]

The ledger began in 1712, the year Saint Aldric's College and the Guild School were merged into the university by treaty. [153] Pressed into the first page was a diplomat's wax seal impression, red gone brown, the envoy's device still crisp: two cords twisted into one. [177] The pages after it were not accounts. [184] They were stories, in the first bell-warden's hand, under the heading Fables of Absence. [198] Each fable was about something the merger had taken away and nobody was allowed to mourn: the Guild School's night stair, its refectory, its bell. [223] The last fable was called The Door That Is Not There. [233]

Ines read it twice, then looked up. [240]

On the north wall of the mechanism room, behind the weight chains, was a door. [255] She unrolled the 1712 plans she had carried up with the ledger. [267] On the plans, the north wall was solid stone. [276] According to every drawing the university possessed, the door shouldn't exist, and had not existed for three hundred years. [295] Which was precisely when the fable said to open it. [305] "The door is not there," the bell-warden had written, "so go through it when it is not there, and the tower will have two voices again." [331]

Confusion, in Ines's experience, was just information that had not been filed yet. [344] She wanted it filed. [348]

The door was not locked, only stiff. [355] Beyond it, in a chamber the plans denied, hung a second bell, green with neglect, its hammer linkage ending in a rusted eye where a rope should have run back through the wall to the striking train. [392] The Guild School's bell. [396] Walled in, unhung from the treaty but never melted, because someone in 1712 had lost the argument and kept the metal anyway. [418]

Now the second hammer made sense. [424] The clockmaker had built the strike for two bells, as the treaty promised, and the university had quietly delivered one. [444] The rope that should have joined them was back in the mechanism room, coiled on a hook with a museum tag: Ceremonial Rope, 1712, Do Not Use. [471]

Ines lifted it down. [475] It was braided from two cords, one dyed Aldric red, one the undyed grey of the Guild, but braided badly, the strands running side by side in long separate plaits that touched only at the ends. [511] Too loose to hold in the rusted eye. [519] Too slack to pull a hammer. [525] A rope made to look like a merger and function as a refusal. [538]

She compared it to the seal impression: two cords twisted into one, each crossing the other at every turn. [557] That was the pattern the diplomat had sealed. [565] This was the pattern the tower had been given. [574]

Ines unpicked the old braid from the tail up, laying the red and the grey across her knees, and re-laid them in the diplomat's pattern, over, under, over, tightening every few inches with the flat of her thumb. [612] It took the afternoon. [616] The tea went cold and she did not mind. [625] When she finished, the rope was shorter by a hand's width and twice as strong, and the two colours could no longer be told apart at a distance. [653]

She fed it through the wall, spliced one end to the rusted eye, the other to the idle hammer's linkage, and asked the renovation crew, very politely, to release the pendulum for a single strike. [688]

Two hammers fell. [691] Two bells answered, the known one bright, the forgotten one low and hoarse, and the sound that came down over the quadrangle was neither Aldric's nor the Guild's but one chord nobody alive had heard. [726]

Ines wrote it all down in the ledger under the last fable, in plain language, with a date. [744] Then she relabelled the hook. [749] Ceremonial Rope, 1712, reconfigured. [753] In use. [755]
