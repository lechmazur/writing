The control room had been abandoned for eleven years before Ines dragged her furnace up the service road and declared it hers. [22] Nobody at the power company objected, because nobody at the power company remembered the dam still existed. [39] She liked the dead panel of dials for the same reason she liked glass: both were honest about pressure. [58] In April the reservoir began to break apart, and the sound came through the cracked windows like furniture being moved in another room. [81] That was also the month Wren started coming. [89]

Wren was twelve, lived at the bottom of the service road, and had stopped asking questions in February after a teacher laughed at one. [113] Ines knew this because Wren's mother had told her, standing in the doorway, holding the information out like something she did not know how to carry. [139] Ines had considered the problem the way she considered everything, slowly, turning it in the light. [155] A question, she decided, was a small hot thing, and a child who had been burned would not reach for one again unless the whole room was already warm. [184] So she made the room ridiculous. [190]

"Behold," she announced on the first afternoon, lifting a steel pipe from its rack, "the blowpipe, forged in a foundry in Ohio, shipped by truck, dropped once in a parking lot." [221] Wren did not smile, but she sat down on the operator's stool. [233] "Now the artisan approaches the furnace," Ines went on, approaching the furnace. [245] "Two thousand degrees. [248] Hotter than regret." [251] She gathered a glowing bulb of glass on the pipe and turned it, and turned it, narrating the turning with the gravity of a war correspondent. [277] Every mundane thing got a speech: the wetting of the newspaper pad, the sweeping of shards, the kettle. [295] If nothing in the room was too small to be announced, then nothing Wren might ask could be too small either. [316] That was the theory. [320] Ines did not say the theory aloud, because saying it would have been a kind of pressure, and she had seen what pressure did to things that were still cooling. [350]

On the third afternoon Wren found the logbook. [358] It lay under the panel where Ines had left it, a ledger of water levels and turbine speeds, and stuck inside was a leather bookmark cracked down its length like a dry riverbed. [391] It marked the final page. [396] The last operator had written the date the dam was shut down, the reading on every dial, and beneath that, in different ink: Where does the water go when it isn't needed? [428] Nobody had answered. [431] Ines had kept the bookmark there deliberately, the way she kept everything, so it would be found rather than shown. [451] Wren read the line twice and put the ledger down without a word, but she kept the bookmark, running her thumb along the crack. [475] Outside, a slab of ice the size of a truck let go of the shore with a sound like a door. [496]

On the fifth afternoon, Ines let Wren gather. [504] The bulb came out lopsided, and when Wren blew into the pipe it swelled on one side and sagged on the other, and her face closed like a hand. [533] "And here," Ines said, taking the pipe with enormous ceremony, "the artisans abandon the vessel." [548] She knocked the glowing lump into the cullet bucket, where it hissed. [560] "Not because it failed, but because they have learned what they needed from it, and it is heavy, and there is more glass in the world than anyone will ever run out of." [593] She dipped the pipe again and handed it back. [602]

Wren turned it. [605] The reservoir cracked and shifted and cracked. [612] "Why does it sag?" Wren said. [618]

It was so quiet that Ines almost narrated over it. [628] She did not. [631] "Gravity," she said, "and heat. [636] Same reason the ice is going. [642] Same reason anything lets go of a shape once it's warm enough." [654]

Wren thought about this with her whole face. [662] "Then why doesn't the pipe sag?" [668]

"Steel melts hotter." [671]

"Why?" [672]

Ines felt something in her own chest come loose, some plate of old ice she had not known was still there, floating out into open water. [698] She had built the room for the girl, but she had also, she understood now, been standing in it a long time herself. [721] "I don't know," she said, and meant it as a gift. [732] "Let's find out." [735]

Wren tucked the cracked bookmark back into the ledger and wrote her question beneath the operator's. [751] Then she picked up the pipe and, in a voice that shook only a little, said, "Behold." [768]
