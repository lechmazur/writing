On Halloran's World the orchards grew in the shade of stone arches that hung in the air without pillars. [19] The arches were veined with liftstone, the azure ore the Consortium had mined out from below for forty years before it packed up and left the shafts open like mouths. [49] Now the arches were coming down. [55] Not all at once, but a span here, a keystone there, dropping into the plum rows as rubble that had forgotten how to float. [79]

Tam Orrey shod the orchard mules and mended pruning hooks and said perhaps nine words a day. [96] When the growers met to argue about leaving, he stood at the back with his hands folded and listened. [115] He did not tell them he had been walking the fallen stone every evening, turning pieces over, feeling for the cold blue thread still buried in them. [142]

The magician came through in the last week of harvest with a wagon and a single trick. [159] He called them awakenings. [163] For a price he would drape a square of silk over a dead thing, a stalled pump or a barren tree, and when he pulled it away the thing would work again for a season. [198] He did not take money. [203] He bartered, and what he bartered for was strange: the memory of a first kiss, a man's ability to whistle, the name of a dead dog. [229]

Tam watched the silk closely. [234] It was not blue, but it caught blue, the way a wet stone catches sky. [249] On the third night he went to the wagon and asked for the silk itself. [264]

The magician laughed, then stopped laughing when he saw that Tam had not moved. [278] "It only works in the moment between understanding and confusion," the magician said. [291] "When the thing knows what it was and has not yet realized it is broken. [306] That moment is very short. [311] For a stone it might be a breath. [319] You would have to be there when it wakes." [328]

"I know where they wake," Tam said. [335]

He had worked it out from the mine maps nailed up in the Consortium's empty office: the arches were not floating free. [357] They were the tops of something, the exposed ribs of liftstone veins that ran down through the shafts into the bedrock, and each dawn a pulse of cold ran up those veins like sap. [391] The miners had cut the veins. [397] The rubble in the orchards was not dead; it was severed, and every morning for a single breath it remembered the pulse and reached for it and found nothing. [426]

The magician named his price. [431] He wanted Tam's own waking, all of them, every morning's first clear thought for the rest of his life. [450] Tam would rise each day already confused, and stay so until noon. [462]

Tam considered this for a long time, the way he considered a cracked axle before deciding where to strike. [481] Then he nodded, and the magician folded the silk into his hands, and something in Tam's head went quietly dark. [501]

He went down the Number Six shaft that same night, before the bargain could take hold. [517] He carried a sack of fallen keystone on his back, sixty pounds of it, and the silk wrapped around his forearm. [538] The Consortium's ladders were rotten, so he went slowly, testing each rung. [550] At the bottom the severed vein showed in the wall like a blue scar, and he set the rubble against it, piece by piece, fitting them by feel. [578] He was a blacksmith. [582] He knew that a broken thing joined at the right heat becomes stronger at the weld than it ever was whole. [603]

Then he draped the silk across the seam and waited for dawn. [615]

He felt it before he saw it, the cold coming up through the bedrock, and he pulled the silk away in that one breath when the stones knew themselves. [644] The rubble lit blue from inside. [650] It did not float; it fused, seam to vein, and above him, three hundred feet up through the orchard soil, he heard the low grinding of an arch settling back into the sky. [683]

One arch. [685] There were two hundred. [689]

Tam climbed out into a morning he could not quite make sense of, his own name arriving late, and stood among the plum trees until it did. [716] Then he went home, ate, slept until dark, and filled another sack. [728]

The growers noticed the arch first, then the second, then the blacksmith who now worked only afternoons and came in from the shafts each dawn with blue dust in the creases of his hands. [762] They asked him what he was doing. [769] He thought about it, deliberately, the way he did everything, and used four of his nine words. [786]

"Staying," he said. [789] "Help or don't." [792]
