The Archive of Extinct Tongues closed at dusk, but Ilse Varen had a key, a glass ink well, and roughly nine hours before Teodor's ship sailed. [26] He stood in the doorway as if the shelves might bite. [37] "Three years," he said. [41] "Three years of nothing, and now you want me to watch you work." [54]

"I want you to hear it," she said. [62] "Hearing is the only thing Vessic was ever for." [71]

She was an arcane linguist, which meant she read languages that pushed back. [84] Vessic pushed hardest. [87] Its speakers had died out four centuries ago, leaving behind a grammar with no word for lying, because a lie written in Vessic simply would not hold ink. [115] Every sentence was a promise. [120] Every promise hooked into the ones around it, clause into clause, until a page became a lattice, and a true lattice sang. [142] The old scholars had called these pages symphonies of truth. [152] Ilse had heard one, once, faintly, on her first week in the archive, and had chased that sound for six years. [173]

"You stopped writing to me," Teodor said. [180] "Not a note." [183] "Not one." [185]

"I couldn't." [187] She set the ink well on the reading table. [196] The glass was old, blown with a slight lean, and the ink inside was black shading to violet where the lamplight caught it. [219] "Six years ago I signed the archive's oath in Vessic, in this ink, so the guild would trust me with the restricted shelves. [242] I promised to speak of my work to no one outside these walls. [255] I thought it meant the work. [261] Vessic thought it meant me." [266]

She had tried to write him a hundred letters. [275] Each one had curdled on the page, the words sliding off like water off wax, because every sentence she owned was now hooked to that oath, and the oath would not let her out. [309] Silence had not been a choice. [315] It had been a knot. [320]

"So untie it," Teodor said, but his voice had lost some of its edge. [334]

"You can't untie Vessic." [338] Ilse uncapped the well. [342] "You unsnarl it. [345] You find the one thread that goes through everything and you pull it very, very gently, and you pray the rest follows." [367]

The thread was a new promise. [373] She had drafted it eleven times in her head and never once on paper, because a false draft in this ink would not merely fail; it would fold the old oath tighter and take her voice with it, perhaps for good. [414] That was the reckless part. [419] The careful part was the wording. [425]

She dipped the pen and wrote: I promise the archive I will guard what it guards. [441]

The ink held. [444] A low note woke somewhere under the table, felt more than heard. [456]

She wrote: I promise Teodor that he is not outside these walls, because he is here, and I have asked him in. [478]

The second clause hooked into the first. [485] A second note joined the low one, a third above it, and Teodor's head came up sharply. [502]

"Is that--" [504]

"Don't talk," she whispered, grinning despite herself, hand shaking, pen steady. [515] "If it stays true, it keeps singing." [522]

She wrote: I promise them both that these three promises are one promise, and I keep them together or not at all. [544]

For a moment nothing happened, and the nothing was the loudest thing she had ever heard. [560] Then the old oath, six years knotted, found the new thread running through it and turned over like a sleeper. [580] The chord bloomed. [583] It was not loud. [587] It was full, the way a room is full when every person in it is finally telling the truth, and it came from the page and from the shelves and, she realized, from the glass of the ink well itself, ringing like a struck bowl. [632]

Teodor sat down on the floor. [638] "That's what you couldn't tell me." [644]

"That's what I couldn't tell anyone." [650] She capped the well before her hands could do anything stupid to it. [663] "It only works on paper, and only in this ink, and it's the last full well left. [680] Half the guild wants to bottle it and sell it to courts. [692] The other half wants to pour it into the sea." [702]

"And you?" [704]

"I want it heard by people who can bear it." [714] She looked at him. [718] "Your ship leaves at seven." [723]

The chord had thinned to a hum, but it was still there, patient, waiting for the next true thing. [742]

"It does," Teodor said. [746] "Write down that I'll be on it, and see if the ink takes." [759]

She laughed, uncapped the well, and did not write that. [769] She wrote something else, something careful, and the room began, quietly, to sing. [782]
