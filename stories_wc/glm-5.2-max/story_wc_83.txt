Kira hadn't expected to survive. [5] The drone's targeting algorithm had calculated the wedding party's thermal signatures, classified them as an insurgent cluster, and released its payload with mechanical indifference. [29] Twenty-seven people died in the courtyard, including Maro, whose hand had been warm in hers until the moment it wasn't. [49] She'd been found at the edge of the blast radius, alive for reasons no investigation could explain, clutching a geode crystal that had been part of the table centerpiece. [78] The crystal had split open from the shockwave, revealing violet facets inside that caught the light like frozen veins. [97] She kept it. [100] Not as a memorial. [104] As a measurement. [107]

Now she worked in the Floating Observatory above the Karakum, a research platform where atmospheric monitoring equipment tracked energy anomalies across Central Asia. [130] The observatory hung at three thousand meters, tethered by carbon cables to a ground station that looked, from altitude, like a white thread stitched into brown earth. [157] For three years she'd used the facility's computational array to hunt for something she couldn't name. [173] Not revenge. [175] Not closure. [177] Something else. [179] She wanted to find where unused potential pools. [187] The twenty-seven dead at the wedding had carried futures inside them: children, arguments, reconciliations, Tuesday mornings, and all of that concentrated possibility had to go somewhere. [213] Physics demanded conservation. [216] The universe didn't waste. [220] If she could locate the reservoir, she'd know that Maro's laughter hadn't simply ceased to exist but had transformed into something she could touch. [244]

Tonight, during scheduled maintenance, the observatory's primary systems were down. [254] She had maybe forty minutes while the circuit breakers cooled before the array rebooted and the monitoring protocols locked her out again. [276] Forty minutes of unfiltered access to the raw sensor data. [286] She'd developed her own method. [291] Standard signal processing assumed clean inputs, but reality was messy. [301] Instead, she used perturbation theory: starting with the known energy signatures of the blast, then mapping how small disturbances in the surrounding field had propagated outward over time. [329] Each perturbation was a ripple from something that should have been. [340] A future interrupted. [343] The drone's algorithm had treated each signature as a data point. [354] She treated each interruption as a wound in the field that never closed. [367]

The math was brutal. [371] She worked through it on the backup terminal, fingers shaking from more than cold. [385] The geode sat beside the keyboard, its violet interior catching the screen's glow. [398] She'd discovered early on that the crystal acted as a resonance chamber. [410] When she placed it near the sensor outputs, it hummed at frequencies the equipment couldn't measure but she could feel in her teeth. [433] The effect was intangibly tangible. [438] She couldn't quantify it. [442] She couldn't ignore it. [446] Somewhere between skepticism and faith, the geode had become her instrument. [457]

The observatory groaned as wind shifted against its hull. [466] Twenty-three minutes left. [469] The perturbation map was growing. [474] Each node represented a point where the blast's energy had encountered an interrupted future and deflected. [490] The nodes weren't random. [494] They formed a pattern. [498] Kira leaned forward. [501] The pattern was a gradient, flowing toward a convergence point forty kilometers northeast, deep in the desert where nothing lived and nothing should. [524]

Where unused potential pooled. [528]

She felt it then. [532] An unfiltered awakening. [535] Not a vision or a voice. [541] A recognition. [543] The energy of twenty-seven futures hadn't dissipated into nothing. [552] It had collected. [555] It was still there, still real, still pressing against the fabric of spacetime with the weight of everything they would have done. [577] Maro's laugh. [579] His sister's graduation speech. [583] His mother's recipe for the bread she never finished baking. [593] All of it. [596] Still somewhere. [598] Still going. [600] The universe had not erased them. [606] It had shelved them, and the shelf was breaking. [615]

The crystal hummed louder. [619] Kira's hands stopped shaking. [623] She smiled, and the expression felt wrong on her face, too wide, too hungry. [637] Bleak exhilaration flooded through her. [642] She had found it. [646] The circuit breakers began their reset sequence. [653] She had seventeen minutes to record the coordinates. [661] After that, the system would purge her unauthorized work. [670] But she'd know. [673] She'd know where to go. [678] The geode pulsed once, warm as Maro's hand on the day she married him. [692] She typed faster. [695] Outside, the wind screamed against the hull, and the observatory swayed, and somewhere far below, in the dark, something that should have been nothing was calling itself a name. [724]
