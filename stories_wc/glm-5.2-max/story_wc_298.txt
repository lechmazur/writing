Vera had been called reckless since childhood, though she preferred to think of herself as an idealist who simply moved faster than doubt. [23] Her brother Elian had bartered away three years of his destiny to the Hollow Juniper in exchange for their mother's life, and now holes gaped in his soul where ambition and memory should have been. [58] The transaction had saved their mother. [64] It had emptied Elian of meaning. [70]

Vera descended into the Fosse, the catacomb orchard beneath the old city where tree roots grew thick between rib cages and vertebrae, fed by centuries of buried dead. [98] She carried a small kit: charcoal, silver dust, pine resin, and a burned tarot card pulled from the Juniper's own altar years before by her grandmother. [124] The card depicted the Star, its face blackened, its edges curled. [135] She meant to use it to complete the trammel: a binding that would restrict the Juniper's reach long enough for her to negotiate Elian's destiny back. [161]

She moved within the glow of half-lit corridors where phosphorescent moss clung to bone and root alike, everything tinted amber and shadow. [183] She paused at an arched junction where a pear tree had erupted through a stacked wall of skulls, white roots threaded like veins through empty orbital sockets. [210] She laid the burned card flat against the nearest trunk, pressing her fingertips into its curled edges until the heat of the old scorch stung her skin. [237]

She had watched her grandmother inlay meaning everywhere into artifacts: silver dust rubbed into carved letters, intention folded into the grain of wood so the object would hold what it was given. [269] Vera worked now with that same fiercely gentle attention, tracing the Star's outline with charcoal mixed with pine resin, pressing intention into each line. [293] She whispered what she needed: restriction, negotiation, return. [301]

The roots tightened. [304] The Hollow Juniper was aware of her. [311] A voice came through the wood and root, or what passed for voice in a thing that had never owned a throat. [333]

"You brought my card home," it said. [340] "But you have nothing to trade." [346]

"Only myself," Vera said. [350] "Three years of my destiny for three years of his restored." [361]

The Juniper considered. [364] In the half-lit corridor, shadows shifted like something reading the terms of a proposal. [378]

"His holes are filled with me already," it said. [387] "Patching them with your life will not remove what has grown there." [399]

"Then give me a year and a day," Vera said. [409] "Trammel yourself to that bargain: if I cannot fill him with something stronger, you take both our destinies permanently." [428]

The Juniper's laughter sounded like snapping branches. [435] "You would gamble both souls, reckless child?" [442]

"I would." [444]

The binding settled into the burned card with a sound like a held breath releasing. [459] Vera felt the trammel's weight press across her chest, a cage of time and obligation. [474] She held still until the ache found a place to rest inside her. [487] A year and a day. [492] That was how long she had to patch the holes in her brother's soul. [506]

Vera retrieved the card from the bark. [513] The old scorch remained, but silver lines now traced the Star's shape where she had inlaid her intent, and the charcoal had hardened into something that looked permanent. [541] She tucked it into her coat and turned toward the surface. [552] Behind her, roots loosened their grip on the skulls they held. [563] Above her, the corridor's greenish glow flickered once in what might have been acknowledgment. [577]

She climbed. [579] The orchard kept its silence behind her, roots and bones and the patience of bargains struck in half-light. [597] What she had bartered sat inside her like a stone that holds heat after the light goes. [614] Something that had been trembling in her went still. [623] She would need to move faster than doubt, as she always had. [635]
