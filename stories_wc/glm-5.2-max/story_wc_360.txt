In the topaz-lit workshop where he set type by hand, Maren worked beneath a sky of vanishing stars. [18] Each night another puncture of light disappeared from the heavens, and each morning he arrived to find the darkness had grown by one degree. [42]

The halo fragment sat on his composing stone, its broken edge catching the workshop's amber glow. [58] He had found it in the type case when he inherited the press, wedged between the em quads and the ligatures, as though someone had meant to hide it in the language itself. [91]

Maren set letters not to communicate but to borrow. [100] Each font carried a personality: the severe capitals of a preacher, the slanted urgency of a lover, the measured serifs of a scientist. [123] When he locked a form and pulled the proof, he became someone else entirely. [137] His own feelings had always been opaque to him, a sequence of sensations without clear origin or equation. [155] He sought the mathematics of emotion, the formula that would make feeling predictable, solvable, human. [170]

Tonight he was setting a broadside for a woman named Iola, who had come to the workshop weeping about a brother she had lost. [194] Not to death, but to distance: he had walked into the eastern fields and kept walking until the silhouette resolved into nothing. [216] She wanted words that would call him back, or at least mark his absence with sufficient weight. [233]

Maren listened to her story and felt something, but not what he should have felt. [248] The sorrow landed in him sideways, arriving as a fascination with the geometric quality of her grief: how it tapered at the edges, how it concentrated at the center, how it seemed to obey laws he could almost write down. [288]

He set the type deliberately ambiguous. [294] "He went where the sky opens" could mean dawn or death or a door. [308] "We wait in the shape of your leaving" could be accusation or invitation. [321] Every line carried two readings, each one true, neither one complete. [332] This was how he worked: through deliberate ambiguity that disoriented the reader into feeling before understanding, because understanding was often the thing that killed feeling. [357]

Iola read the proof and paused. [363] Her face shifted through three expressions in two seconds, unable to settle. [375] The ambiguity had done its work: she could not tell if the words were hers or his or her brother's, speaking from wherever he had gone. [401] That uncertainty opened something in her. [407] She began to cry differently, not from loss but from the strange sensation of feeling her own grief from the outside, as though it belonged to someone standing nearby. [436]

Transposed empathy, Maren thought. [440] That was what the halo fragment did when he held it while setting type. [454] It did not give him feeling but rotated it, showing him other people's emotions from angles they could not see themselves. [475] Iola's grief, turned forty-five degrees, became something almost like beauty: not because suffering was beautiful but because the structure of it was, the way a wound follows the grain of the skin. [507]

The last star above the workshop guttered. [514] He worked quickly now, setting a final form not for any client but for the empty sky. [531] The words were equations rendered in lead: "If absence has mass, then longing bends toward it. If love is a vector, then grief is its reflection." [557] Ambiguous by design, mathematical and emotional simultaneously. [564] He pressed the halo fragment into the margin, and it left an imprint like a sundial's shadow at an hour that did not exist. [588]

Between the vanishing stars and the total dark, the broadside cooled on the rack. [602] Iola had taken her copy and gone. [609] Somewhere in the eastern fields, perhaps her brother read words that could mean anything, and in that ambiguity found the space to feel what he had not let himself feel before. [640]

Maren cleaned the type, returning each borrowed personality to its case. [651] He remained himself, which was no one in particular: a man who understood emotion only by translating it into forms others could almost recognize. [675] The workshop went dark. [679] The mathematics held. [682]
