Taren moved among the crates like water finds cracks in stone. [11] The other dock workers had always said he was too graceful for this work, that his hands knew the shape of waves rather than rope. [36] But Taren had stayed on Eel Island long after the boats stopped coming, long after the fighting over the last high ground had burned itself to ash and silence. [65] Now violence was impossible. [69] Not forbidden, impossible, the way fire is impossible in a drowned hearth. [81] The island had been sinking for three generations, and the people who remained were too few and too tired to raise their fists against each other anymore. [108] He worked the sublittoral fringe now, the last stretch of dock that the sea hadn't claimed. [124] At low tide, the pilings stood in mud and black weed. [135] At high tide, the water rose to his thighs and he would wade between the remaining posts, salvaging what the ocean offered or taking what it was trying to steal. [165] It was in the silt beneath the deepest piling that he found the pirate's whalebone scrimshaw. [181] He'd heard of it. [185] Old Marek had spoken of a pirate captain who'd anchored here two centuries ago, a woman who'd carved her life into whalebone and left it in the island's foundation as a promise to return. [219] Taren had thought it a dock worker's legend, something to pass the dark hours. [233] But the bone was real in his hands. [241] Yellowed, heavy, etched with fine lines that showed a ship riding the back of a whale, and below that, words in a script he almost recognized. [267] The island shuddered. [270] Taren had felt it before, the slow groan of rock letting go of itself. [284] But this was different. [288] This was a sound that came through the water, not the stone. [300] A whale called from the deep. [306] The sound passed through his chest like a second heartbeat. [316] The scrimshaw vibrated in his grip, and the carved whale on its surface seemed to shift, to move, to open an eye that had been etched shut. [343] Taren understood then what the island was. [350] Not rock. [352] Not sand. [354] Something alive, something that had surfaced to breathe and was now ready to return to the dark. [371] He felt his guarded heart crack open. [378] For years he had held himself apart, moved through the dock work with precise grace but refused to let anything touch him. [400] Not the loss of the upper town. [407] Not the neighbors who'd taken the last boats and waved from the channel. [420] Not even old Marek, who'd said Taren had the soul of a dancer wasting itself on a sinking ledge. [439] He had stayed because he thought endurance was the same as love. [451] The whale called again, and the sound unlocked something he had buried beneath years of salt and labor. [469] He saw himself clearly. [473] Not a dock worker. [477] Not a man holding a sinking island together through stubbornness. [487] A body made for movement, for water, for the fluid grace that had always been his true nature and that he had punished himself for every time it surfaced. [516] To stay was to drown. [521] Not in the sea, but in the lie that he belonged to this place. [535] Taren looked at the scrimshaw. [540] The pirate woman had carved her promise into bone because bone survives. [552] But she had also carved a ship leaving. [560] She had known that love and departure could be the same act. [572] He detached the last boat from the rotted piling. [581] His hands were steady, his movement exact, every gesture as clean as a dancer's turn. [596] The island groaned again, and the water rose. [604] Taren pushed off. [607] Behind him, the sublittoral fringe disappeared beneath the waves, and he did not watch it go. [623] He held the scrimshaw against his chest and let the whale's call guide him toward open water, toward the self he had always been and had never dared to become. [653] The sea was not angry. [658] It was only honest, and Taren found that honesty felt like freedom, like the first breath after believing for years that breathing was weakness. [682] He did not look back. [687] The island was gone. [691] He was not. [694]
