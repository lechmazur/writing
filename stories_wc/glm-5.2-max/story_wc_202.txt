The alchemist's market hung above the city like a secret suspended in fog. [13] Walkways of woven chain connected rooftop to rooftop, strung between chimneys and weather vanes, and the stalls themselves seemed to breathe with the vapor of a hundred tinctures and tonics. [43] Maren had climbed here every season between childhood and now, first on her father's shoulders, then alone, always drawn to the same stall at the market's far edge where old paintings leaned against each other like sleeping relatives. [81] She was a translator. [85] Not of languages, but of the spaces between worlds. [94]

In her hands, the lighthouse lens fragment caught the gray light. [105] It was elusively tangible -- warm when she focused on it, absent when she didn't, as if it existed more in her attention than in her fingers. [131] She had found it years ago inside a painting of a storm-battered coast, tucked behind a smear of lead-white paint that had turned out to be, upon close inspection, a tiny door. [163]

That was her method. [167] The hidden details in paintings -- the dog reflected in a mirror's corner, the second signature beneath the first, the lighthouse where there should have been none -- these were the hinges between worlds. [199] Each overlooked brushstroke was a sentence in a language only she could read. [212] And she read them because someone had to. [220] The dead had histories too. [225] The forgotten had stories. [229] And those stories lived in the margins of canvases, in the paint that later restorers had covered, in the shadows that collectors had dismissed as mistakes. [255] Maren translated them to honor overlooked histories -- the servant painted out of a noble portrait, the battlefield erased for a prettier landscape, the names that had been deliberately obscured. [284]

Today she had come for a specific painting. [292] The vendor, an old alchemist with stained fingers, unfolded it from cloth: a harbor scene, unremarkable, unsigned. [309] But Maren's fragment pulsed, and the pulse meant a message waited. [320]

She held the lens fragment close to the surface and began to look. [333] Her intuition was not a feeling but a thing she could almost hold -- what she thought of as anchors of intuition, weights that kept her steady as she moved between the layers of paint and the layers of meaning beneath. [373] Without them, she would drift, lose herself in the between-worlds. [384] With them, she could read. [389]

There. [390] In the upper corner, where the sky met the harbor wall: a pattern too precise to be accidental. [408] Tiny figures, almost invisible, painted in a pigment that shifted depending on the angle of light. [424] She tilted the fragment, and the figures resolved into words -- not in any human alphabet, but in the script of the other side. [447] Her fingers trembled. [450] The message was old. [454] Older than the painting. [458] It spoke of a child who had been hidden in the frame itself, whose name had been painted over so many times that the world had forgotten it entirely. [487] But the between-worlds had not forgotten. [493]

Maren set down the fragment and reached for the edge of the canvas. [506] She had to unfasten it from its stretcher -- gently, so as not to disturb the layers -- because sometimes the real message was not in the paint but beneath it, in the fabric itself, woven into the threads by hands that had known they would be erased. [552]

As she worked, the market sounds faded. [559] The fog seemed to hold its breath. [566] Time did not stop -- it simply became irrelevant, the way it does in a dream or a memory or the moment between hearing a sound and understanding what it means. [596] This was the eternal instant, the space where translation actually happened: not in the reading, but in the gap between the reading and the knowing. [621]

She unfastened the last tack. [626] The canvas came free. [630] And there, on the reverse, in faded ink that no restorer had thought to check: a name. [647]

Maren read it aloud, softly, into the fog above the rooftops. [658] The market returned to sound. [663] The fog resumed its drift. [668] But something in the between-worlds had shifted -- a history, previously overlooked, now honored and held. [683]

She folded the painting carefully, pocketed the lens fragment, and began the long climb back down into the city, carrying another recovered name toward the light. [709]
