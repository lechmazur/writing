Vera had stolen her first portrait at seventeen--a daguerreotype of a woman bathed in amber light, taken during the last six minutes of an October sunset. [27] She'd been a golden-hour portrait thief ever since, not for money, but for something she couldn't name. [44] The portraits called to her, each one a sliver of time where light and flesh agreed to be beautiful together. [64] She was quietly savvy about it, never taking from families who'd mourn the loss. [78] She targeted estate sales and forgotten attics where portraits gathered dust in the dark. [92] What she wanted was the light inside them--the way golden hour could make a stranger look like someone you'd loved your whole life. [116]

Now she stood on the platform of Emerald Station, the last train gone, moonlight turning the iron pillars green-glass pale. [136] The golden sunset was still bleeding out at the horizon, a fleeting wound of color that wouldn't last. [154] She had maybe ten minutes before full dark. [162]

In her bag, wrapped in velvet, was the jar of captured moonbeams. [174] She'd traded three stolen portraits for it last winter in Vienna, from an old woman who swore she'd caught the light during a lunar eclipse. [199] The beams still moved inside the glass, slow as honey, faintly silver. [211] Vera didn't fully believe in it, but she carried it everywhere. [222] Luminous disbelief--that was what she lived in. [230]

The stationmaster had left flowers on the bench. [238] Roses and lilies, arranged in a pattern that looked casual but wasn't. [250] Vera's eye caught it immediately. [255] She'd learned to read codes in flower arrangements years ago, from a forger in Lisbon who claimed the language of flowers had been a spy's tool long before written ciphers. [285] Stems angled left meant danger. [290] Odd-numbered blooms meant go. [294] The white lily at the center meant a door. [303]

A door. [305]

Vera knelt beside the arrangement. [310] Her fingers hovered over the petals. [316] She traced the pattern--rose, rose, lily, rose, fern--and felt her breath catch. [330] The code said the door opens at Emerald Station, under the last light, for the one who carries moonbeams and stolen faces. [352] She had to reinterpret the message twice before it made sense. [363]

That was why she'd come. [368] Not the portraits, not the jar. [374] Those had been preparation. [378] She'd spent twelve years gathering illuminated moments--faces caught in perfect light, moonbeams trapped in glass--all to press her ear against destiny's door and hear what was on the other side. [410]

The sunset thinned to a golden thread. [417] Vera uncorked the jar. [421] The moonbeams inside stirred, then climbed out like luminous smoke, drifting toward the flower arrangement. [436] Where they touched the petals, colors shifted--roses became silver, lilies became gold, the fern unfurled into a shape like a key. [458]

She picked up the fern. [463] It was heavy, heavier than a plant should be. [472] At the far end of the platform, where the wall met the tracks, she saw what she hadn't seen before: a door set into stone, its frame covered in moss. [502]

Vera walked toward it. [506] The golden light was almost gone, just a smear on the horizon, and moonlight filled in behind it like water into a basin. [529]

She pressed her ear against the door. [536]

Behind it, she heard something. [541] Not words, not music--something older. [547] It was the sound of every portrait she'd stolen, every face frozen in golden light, all breathing at once. [566] She heard the moonbeams she'd carried, and the ones still loose in the sky. [580] She heard illuminated moments--her mother laughing in a doorway at dusk, a stranger weeping on a train, a child turning toward light. [603]

Vera pressed harder against the wood. [609] The disbelief in her chest was luminous, golden, almost unbearable. [619] She had spent her whole life stealing light, and now she understood why. [632] She had been gathering evidence that destiny existed. [640]

The fern-key turned itself in her hand. [647] The door groaned inward. [651] Light poured through the gap--not sunset, not moonrise, something older than both--and washed over her face. [669]

Vera stepped through, carrying every illuminated moment she'd stolen, finally ready to stop stealing and start seeing. [686] Behind her, the flowers rearranged themselves into a new code. [696] The door closed. [699] Moonlight filled the station, and the golden sunset was gone. [709]
