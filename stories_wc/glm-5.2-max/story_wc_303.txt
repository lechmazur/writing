Maren had always known her destiny was perforated — full of holes where other people's fates leaked through. [17] As a provenance researcher for the Velis Empire, she traced the origins of objects, and objects, she had learned, remembered everything. [21] [38] The velvet observatory at midsummer was almost unbearable. [8] [46] Its walls, draped in midnight-blue velvet, trapped the heat like a throat holding a breath. [15] [61] Maren sat at her workbench, the transparent violin made of ice crystal resting on a bed of raw silk before her. [21] [82] She had been commissioned by the Empire to authenticate it — to prove it came from the northern provinces, from the imperial mines, from the Emperor's hand. [26] [108]

But the plants told her otherwise. [6] [114] She had been accidentally telepathic with plants since childhood. [9] [123] It wasn't a gift she'd sought. [6] [129] Walking through a garden, she'd hear them murmuring — slow, gurgling thoughts about light and root and thirst. [17] [146] The observatory's ivy, creeping along the southern window, had been whispering for weeks. [13] [159] Old roots remembering. [3] [162] The ivy had been there when the violin arrived, carried by a merchant who claimed it was imperial crystal. [19] [181] The ivy remembered different soil. [5] [186] It remembered mountain air and the hands of someone who was not the Emperor. [14] [200]

Maren sipped her coffee — the third cup that morning — and tilted the cup to read the stains. [17] [217] She had learned this method from her mother, a woman who read futures in the dark rings left on porcelain. [20] [237] The stains formed patterns: a branching path, a broken circle, a shape like a seed splitting open. [17] [254] The future, as always, was ambiguous, but the coffee stains leaned toward revelation. [13] [267] They leaned toward a truth the Empire would not welcome. [10] [277]

The violin was not imperial. [5] [282] It was older. [3] [285] It belonged to a people the Empire had consumed three generations ago — the Llaoren, who had carved instruments from glacial crystal and played music that made the mountains weep. [29] [314] If Maren reported this honestly, the Empire would confiscate the violin and bury its true origin. [16] [330] If she lied, she would betray the Llaoren memory that the ivy carried in its roots. [16] [346]

She had to weigh a dream against an empire. [9] [355] The dream was this: that an object could remember where it came from, that provenance was not a matter of ownership but of belonging. [24] [379] The Empire measured provenance in ledgers and decrees. [8] [387] Maren measured it in the stories objects carried in their grain, in the memories plants soaked from the soil where those objects had rested. [24] [411]

She worked quickly, before the promise lost its glow — the promise she had made to herself when she took this profession, that she would follow the truth wherever it led, even into the mouth of power. [36] [447] The ice crystal violin sat transparent under the observatory's lamp. [10] [457] Inside it, trapped air bubbles formed a constellation that matched no imperial mine record. [14] [471] Maren photographed it. [3] [474] She documented the bubble patterns, the crystal's refraction index, the tool marks left by a chisel technique the Empire had never used. [22] [496]

Then she did something she had never done before. [9] [505] From the observatory's window box, she took a cutting of the ivy — the ivy that remembered. [16] [521] She wrapped its roots in damp moss and sealed it in a glass jar. [14] [535] She would propagate it. [4] [539] Not just the plant, but the memory it carried. [9] [548] She would root it in new soil, grow it in a place the Empire could not reach, and let it keep whispering about mountain air and Llaoren hands. [28] [576]

She wrote two reports. [4] [580] The first, for the Empire, stated that the violin's provenance was inconclusive — the crystal composition was unusual, the tool marks atypical, but the evidence was not sufficient to challenge imperial origin claims. [32] [612] It was a lie of omission, and it sat in her mouth like a stone. [15] [627] The second report — the true one — she folded into an envelope along with the ivy cutting, the photographs, and a single coffee-stained cup that told the future: a branching path, a broken circle, a seed splitting open. [37] [664] She addressed it to the Llaoren diaspora in the eastern provinces, where people still remembered the old songs and still carved small things from whatever crystal the mountains offered. [29] [693]

The velvet observatory held the midsummer heat like a held breath. [11] [704] Maren sealed the envelope and set it on the bench beside the violin. [13] [717] The ice crystal caught the lamplight and threw thin rainbows across the velvet walls. [14] [731] Her destiny had always been perforated — full of holes where choices leaked through. [13] [744] She had chosen one. [4] [748] The ache of it, dark and sweet as mulberries, settled in her chest as she watched the ivy in its jar, already turning its leaves toward the light. [28] [776]
