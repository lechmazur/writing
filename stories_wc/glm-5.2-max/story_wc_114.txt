In the third day after her fever broke, Naia woke without certainty, which for an oracle was its own kind of omen. [22]
The mountain weather station groaned around her, its instruments ticking with the small corrections of a world that would not sit still. [44]
She was a fault-tolerant oracle, trained to absorb wrong predictions without breaking, but the fever had shown her something she could not yet fit into any margin of error. [73]
For six days she had burned and dreamed in the same breath. [85]
In the dreams she had swallowed something whole, a truth that lodged in her chest like a stone, and even now, with the fever gone, she could feel its weight pressing against the underside of her ribs. [122]
She did not know what the truth was. [130]
She knew only that it was heavy and that it belonged to her. [143]
The station's windows were frosted except for one pane the wind had kept clear, and through it she could see the aurora moving over the ridge in violet and green, the colors of a grief too large for the sky to hold. [185]
She had lived here eleven months reading the future in marginal patterns, the formation of frost, the lean of weather vanes before storms, the hesitation in birdsong, and she had learned that mountains dealt not in absolutes but in swaying certainties, things that looked solid until you stood on them. [235]
She rose and crossed to the table where her sister Nouva's botanical illustration lay under a pane of glass. [254]
Nouva had drawn it forty years ago: mist-tongue, seven-petaled, a flower that existed in no field guide. [271]
Naia had kept it in a drawer until the fever, until the dream in which she had seen the illustration whole and burning in a dark valley. [298]
She removed the glass now and bent over the page. [308]
The flower's roots should have been there, beneath the stem. [318]
Nouva had rendered every other part with meticulous precision, the serrated leaves, the slight asymmetry of the petals, but the space where roots belonged held only a faint smudge of charcoal, as if she had drawn and then erased something, or as if the flower had no anchor at all. [368]
Naia pressed her thumb to the smudge. [375]
Her skin came away faint gray. [381]
She was peripherally aware of what this meant, had been aware for years, in the way one is aware of a sound just below hearing, a shape just past the edge of vision. [414]
The truth she had swallowed was this: Nouva had not drawn roots because the flower had none, and the flower had none because Nouva had invented it, and Nouva had invented it because she needed something rootless to hold what she could not say directly. [459]
Naia's method had always been to occupy margins, to read what others dismissed as noise, to find meaning in the edges. [480]
She began with the handwriting beneath the Latin name. [489]
The letters were faint, nearly erased by time, but as she angled the page toward the lamp she could make out coordinates hidden in the decorative border. [516]
Nouva had written them in the margins, trusting that no one would think to read the frame instead of the picture. [537]
This was how her sister had always spoken, by occupying margins, by placing the weight of what mattered in the space least examined. [560]
Naia copied the coordinates onto her own wrist with the charcoal dust. [572]
Outside the aurora shifted, green fading to blue, and the station's barometer clicked with a small uncertain correction. [590]
She felt the sorrow then, not her own but the sky's, as if the lights were mourning something they too had swallowed and could not name. [616]
She understood now that finding the weight of a swallowed truth did not mean learning what it was. [634]
It meant learning what it cost to hold. [642]
The coordinates pointed to a place in the valley she had never visited. [655]
She would go there. [659]
She folded the botanical illustration along its margins and placed it inside her coat. [673]
The fever had broken but the truth remained, heavy and luminous, pressing against her ribs like a second heartbeat, and she carried it down the mountain into the dark where the lights still swayed above her, sorrowing, certain of nothing but their own brief and beautiful weight. [720]
