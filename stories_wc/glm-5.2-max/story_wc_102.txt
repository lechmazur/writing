The past life had surfaced that morning, a flash of temple incense and hands reaching for someone already gone, and Lian had packed her camera obscura equipment before the memory finished settling. [32] She walked the old railway viaduct as twilight settled over the valley. [44] Wisteria had colonized the arches, cascading in violet strands that swayed in the evening air. [59] A pilgrim of sparks, that was what her teacher had called her years ago in Kunming, when she first began following light through darkened chambers, spiritually curious in a way that bordered on hunger. [93] Every pinhole she drilled, every inverted image she traced, was an act of asking what shines through what has been emptied. [114] She set the wooden box on the viaduct's worn ledge and positioned the lens toward the valley. [131] Inside, she had mounted a sheet of treated paper. [140] She sealed the light leaks with black cloth and knelt before the viewing hole. [154] The image materialized slowly: the valley inverted, the blooming arches upended like chandeliers, the sky poured beneath her feet. [173] She held up the object she had carried for seven years, what the old merchant in Lhasa had called Kwan Yin's tear, a translucent, teardrop-shaped piece of amber, warm as skin, containing a trapped air bubble that resembled a lotus seed. [276] She breathed in sharply. [280] The threads dimmed. [283] She held her breath. [287] They vanished. [289] She exhaled slowly and the threads returned, brightening as her breath lengthened, each exhalation feeding their luminance. [306] She understood then, with the foggy lucidity that sometimes visits those who have seen too much too clearly, that she was not merely observing. [330] Her breathing was the medium. [335] The amber tear translated breath into visible light-threads, and those threads traced something real. [349] They traced where unused love was stored. [356] The filaments connected the wisteria to the stone, the stone to the river below, the river to the sky. [375] They connected her to the inverted image of herself kneeling. [430] The past life memory surfaced again, not as a flash but as a long exhalation. [445] She had been a woman in a temple who loved someone she was sworn not to love, and she had carried that unexpressed devotion through decades of silence until she died. [476] She had not wasted it. [481] She had been storing it in the stone walls, in the incense smoke, in every breath beside the person she could not touch. [504] She began to breathe differently now, not the shallow rhythm of grief but the long, deliberate breath of someone who has finally found what they were looking for. [532] Each exhalation fed the threads and widened the map. [541] The amber tear grew warm, then almost hot, and the air bubble inside it seemed to pulse. [558] The image on the paper shifted. [564] The inverted valley resolved into something neither upright nor upside-down but simply whole. [577] The wisteria bloomed in both directions simultaneously, and the sky touched itself. [589] And somewhere in the valley below, a woman she had never met woke from a dream of temple incense and felt, for no reason she could name, an overwhelming sense of being loved. [640] She removed the amber tear from the aperture and held it against her chest. [654] It was the temperature of her own breath now. [663] She had not expected to heal anything. [670] She had only wanted to find where the unused love had gone. [682] But finding it and breathing with it had been the same act. [694] She packed her camera slowly, handling the paper with care. [704] The image had fixed itself permanently, though she had used no chemicals. [716] The light-threads had burned into the paper through breath alone. [726] She walked back along the viaduct, the amber tear warm in her closed fist, no longer a pilgrim of sparks but something steadier now. [750] She had found where the love was stored. [758] She had only needed to breathe into it. [766]
