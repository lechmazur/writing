Sera's memories came in fragments, scattered like seeds across the mirror world's dark soil. [14] She was a memory keeper, or had been, before the night settled over everything and refused to lift. [32] Now she tended the alchemical orchard greenhouse at the heart of the dying realm, where transmuted trees bore fruit of phosphorescent glass and bark that hummed if you pressed your ear close enough. [65] The greenhouse stood shrouded in endless night, its vaulted ceilings lost to darkness so thick that even the luminescent orchards could only push it back a few feet. [93] Sera worked by the glow of her own hands, which carried the faint residue of old light, and by the pale shimmer that filtered through the mica window set high in the eastern wall. [127] The window was her most precious possession: a sheet of mineral layers, translucent and strange, through which she could sometimes glimpse the other world, where dawn still existed. [155] The mica peeled in sheets, each layer holding a different reflection. [166] One showed a sunrise she half-remembered from childhood. [174] Another showed faces of people she had once known. [183] A third showed nothing but light, pure and absolute. [192]

She had come here to rekindle the dying realm. [201] The mirror world had once been a reflection of everything bright and living, but its source of light had faded generations ago, and now the darkness was eating the orchards from the edges inward. [235] Communities had fractured. [238] People had retreated into isolated shelters, forgetting the songs that had once connected them, forgetting that they had ever been connected at all. [261] Sera's own memory was scattered because she had given pieces of it away: traded fragments of recollection for seeds, for cuttings, for the alchemical stock that now grew in her greenhouse. [292] She could not remember her mother's face, but she could remember the warmth of a kitchen. [308] She could not remember her village's name, but she could remember the sound of singing. [323]

That sound was the key. [328] The forgotten songs, the ones the old communities had sung at planting and harvest, at birth and burial, were not merely music. [350] They were instructions. [353] Woven into their melodies were the patterns of woven dawns, a practice so old that no one alive remembered its origin. [374] Each song was a thread, and when sung together by a community, the threads wove light from nothing. [392] That was how the mirror world had once sustained itself: not through alchemy alone, but through the collective voice of its people, knitting communities together into a single instrument of creation. [423]

Sera had spent years reconstructing the songs from her scattered memories, coaxing fragments from the mica window's layered reflections, piecing together melodies the way she pieced together cuttings in her orchard. [454] She had grown the plants that the songs required: flowers whose pollen resonated at specific frequencies, fruits whose juices could be rendered into luminous ink. [479] Everything was ready. [482] Everything except the people. [486]

She stepped outside the greenhouse into the endless night and began to sing. [499] The first notes were thin and uncertain, but they carried. [509] They always carried. [512] That was the nature of the forgotten songs: they were designed to travel, to find the ears that still remembered, even if the minds had forgotten. [538] One by one, figures emerged from the darkness. [546] A woman who had been hiding in the ruins of a transmuted apple grove. [560] A child who had never seen light but whose body recognized the melody. [573] An old man who wept because the song was the first thing in years that meant something. [590]

Sera sang, and they sang, and the greenhouse behind them began to glow. [603] The alchemical orchard responded to the collective voice, its glass fruit resonating, its humming bark rising into harmony. [621] The mica window blazed, each layer splitting the emerging light into spectra that had not existed in the mirror world for centuries. [643] Sera felt the dawn weaving itself from their voices: not a single sunrise but a tapestry of them, every community's memory of morning braided together into something new. [671]

She gathered the light in her arms and swaddled it, pressing it close, the way she imagined someone had once held her. [693] It was fragile, this woven dawn, and it needed protection until it could sustain itself. [708] The warmth against her chest was almost unbearable, not because it burned, but because it felt like release. [726] She had bound herself to this task, given away her memories, accepted the scattering of her identity. [743] And in that binding, she found what she had been seeking: the freedom to let go. [759] The dawn did not need her to hold it forever. [769] It needed her to hold it long enough to breathe. [779]

She opened her arms. [783] Light spilled through the greenhouse, the orchard, the mirror world's fractured terrain. [795] Other voices began to sing. [800]
