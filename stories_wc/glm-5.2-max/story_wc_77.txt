After they pulled her from the river and forced breath back into her lungs, Margaux could not stop glowing. [19] The shimmer was not bright but sickly, a phosphorescent light that clung to her skin and made shadows seem deeper and more hungry. [42] The lingering luminance was what the other side had stamped into her, a receipt for a visit she had not wanted to make. [65] She was photographically cursed as well. [71] Every image captured of her showed not her living face but the drowned version: waterlogged skin pulled taut, the particular gray of the dead. [95] These photographs appeared on any film she touched, and even digital sensors warped in her presence. [111] She could not enter a camera's field without producing her own corpse. [123] The curse made her impossible to forget and impossible to truly look at. [136]

After the resuscitation, Margaux came to the abandoned carnival funhouse because fear had followed her from the river. [154] It sat in her chest like a second heartbeat, and she wanted to trick fear into revealing its weakness. [173] The funhouse had been closed for decades. [180] Its corridors were warped mirrors and faded portraits, and the air smelled of mildew and cotton candy: a sweetness rotted into something that pulled you forward even as your stomach turned. [211]

In the hall of mirrors, a voice found her. [220] The voice was not loud but seemed to whisper from the glass itself. [233]

"You're holding it wrong," it said. [239]

Margaux stopped. [241]

"Holding what?" [243]

"Your fear," the whispering advisor replied. [249] "You're clutching it like something precious." [255]

The advisor materialized as a distortion in the mirror, a shape that suggested a tall figure in formal wear, faceless and elegant. [277]

"You need to unbosom yourself." [282] "Not to me. [285] To it." [287]

"How?" she asked. [290]

The mirror-figure gestured along the corridor. [296] On a dusty table sat a dream dictionary, its pages yellowed and swollen with damp. [311]

"Through paradoxical notes," the advisor whispered. [317] "Look up what you fear. [322] Read the contradiction. [325] Fear cannot survive being seen from two directions at once." [335]

Margaux opened the book and searched for drowning. [343] The entry read: "To dream of drowning signifies a fear of surrender. [355] Note: those who surrender cannot drown, for they have already accepted the water. [368] The drowning one is always the one who fights." [377]

The paradox bit into her. [382] She had fought the river, had clawed at the current. [392] The fear was not the water but the fighting itself. [402] She had been afraid of her own resistance. [410]

She searched for death. [414] The entry read: "To dream of death signifies a fear of endings. [426] Note: endings require a beginning to end. [433] Fear the beginning, and you will never need to fear the end." [445]

Each entry she looked up offered its contradiction. [453] The dream dictionary was teaching her to unbosom: to strip her fears naked and watch them contradict themselves into nothing. [473] She felt the fear inside her stir and shrink. [482]

Her fingers found something at her collar: an opera cloak clasp in silver, shaped like a serpent eating its own tail. [503] She did not know where it had come from. [512] Perhaps the river had given it to her. [520] Perhaps the resuscitation had pressed it into her skin. [529] The clasp was warm, and its warmth pulsed with her light. [540]

"Show it the paradox," the advisor whispered. [546] "Let it see itself." [550]

She stood before the largest mirror. [556] Her luminance reflected back, and the mirror showed her glowing and radiant, but beside that image, the photographic curse showed her drowned and gray. [580] Two versions of her, both true, both impossible. [588]

The fear inside her fractured. [593] It could not reconcile the living light with the dead photograph. [604] It could not exist in both frames. [611] It had to choose, and in choosing, it revealed its weakness: it could only exist when she looked at it from one direction. [634]

She closed the dictionary. [638] She unclasped the serpent clasp and held it to the mirror. [649] The light bent through it, and for a moment both versions of her overlapped into something neither and both. [668] Fear had no single shape to cling to. [676]

The whispering advisor's voice faded. [681] "It's done." [683]

Margaux walked out of the funhouse into gray morning light. [693] Her glow remained, and photographs would still show her corpse. [703] But the fear was quiet now, weakened by its own contradictions. [714] She had tricked it into showing where it was fragile, and she had pressed her thumb against that spot until it gave way. [737]

The funhouse groaned behind her, repulsed by her leaving, allured by what she had taken with her. [754]
