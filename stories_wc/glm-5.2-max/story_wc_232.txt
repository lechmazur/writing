Henning had dealt in maps for thirty years, and in all that time, no chart had ever shown him the way to what he wanted most. [26] His shop on Calder Street smelled of aged vellum and pine resin, drawers overflowing with portolans and star charts, but none pointed toward the doorway of decisions, the threshold his wife, Maren, had spoken of on her last afternoon. [65] "Find it," she had said, pressing something cold and small into his palm. [78] "I'll be waiting on the other side." [85]

That was seven years ago. [90] The object was a type foundry matrix chip, an engraving template used to cast metal type, no bigger than a thumbnail. [111] Henning had examined it under magnification hundreds of times. [120] Its surface was etched with a pattern that wasn't any alphabet he recognized, closer to a topographic contour, maybe, or the whorl of a fingerprint. [145] He was empirically intuitive by nature, a man who trusted what he could observe but also believed that careful observation eventually taught the eye to see what the mind hadn't yet named. [177] The chip, he felt certain, was a key. [185] He simply hadn't found the lock. [191]

The breakthrough came in June. [196] A seller in Brindle County brought him a crate of water-damaged surveying notes, and among them Henning found a reference to a blooming cavern, a limestone chamber where, at midsummer, bioluminescent fungi opened in pale blue rosettes along the walls. [236] The notes mentioned that the cavern flooded seasonally, fed by a creek that swelled with snowmelt from the Brindle ridge. [256] More importantly, they mentioned ants. [261]

"Lantern ants," the notes read. [266] "They travel in straight lines at dusk and return at dawn. [277] Their colony is inside." [281]

Henning departed the next morning. [286] He reached the cavern entrance by midday, a crack in the hillside wide enough for one person. [303] Inside, the air was warm and wet, and the walls already glowed faintly, the fungal blooms just beginning to open in celebration of the solstice. [328] The creek ran along the cavern floor, ankle-deep but rising. [338] He could hear it pushing deeper into the mountain, a low, patient sound. [351]

He waited until dusk. [355] Then the ants came. [359]

They moved in a column along the cavern wall, each carrying a fragment of something pale, calcite, maybe, or discarded mushroom flesh. [381] Their path was deliberate, unwavering. [386] Henning followed them, stepping carefully over the creek as it widened, pressing deeper into the blooming passage. [403] The fungi brightened as he went, as if the ants were walking toward a source of light rather than away from one. [425] The chamber narrowed, then opened again, and the creek rose to his knees. [438]

The ants disappeared into a fissure at the far wall. [448] Henning knelt beside it, water tugging at his legs, and held the matrix chip to the crack. [465]

It began to glimmer. [469]

Not reflected light, something from within. [475] The etched pattern on the chip's surface illuminated, casting a faint geometry onto the stone. [490] The lines connected with the natural fractures in the rock, extending outward, forming a shape that Henning recognized at last. [510] It was a door. [514] Not a metaphor. [517] A door, outlined in pale blue light, exactly the color of the blooming fungi, exactly the color of Maren's eyes. [537]

The creek was at his waist now. [544] He could feel the current pulling, the midsummer melt surging through the mountain's bones. [558] He had maybe minutes before the passage flooded completely. [567]

Henning pressed his hand flat against the outlined stone. [576] It was warm. [579] Behind it, he thought he heard something, not a voice exactly, but the shape of a voice, the way a room holds the impression of a conversation after the people have gone. [611]

"You came," the shape said. [616] Or maybe he only felt it. [622]

"I followed the ants," he said, because it was true, and because she would have laughed. [638]

The stone didn't move. [642] But the light widened, and the creek rose, and Henning stepped forward into a promise seven years lingering, not ending, exactly, but kept at last. [667]
