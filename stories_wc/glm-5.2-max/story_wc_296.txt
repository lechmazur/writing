Thoren stood at the shore where the cursed lake had begun to move, its center pulling inward, drawing the ancient orchard into a slow, spiraling vortex. [26] His magic trick had failed minutes ago — the revelation spell meant to part illusions had instead triggered something deeper, waking the lake itself. [49] Now the trees spun in their grave of water, and among their twisting roots he glimpsed what he had come for: a figure frozen in the vortex's heart, lips parted around words that had never escaped. [85]

Thoren was a guarded druid, even among his own kind. [95] He kept his grief buttoned beneath his cloak and his doubts buried deeper than any root. [111] But he possessed one gift that had never failed him — he found lost things. [125] Not by searching directly, but through expanding circles, walking outward from a center point until the lost thing's gravity pulled him in. [147] It was a patient art, and patience was the only tenderness he allowed himself. [161]

What drove him now was a need to discover what mirrors don't show. [174] The surface of this lake had reflected lies for three hundred years. [186] Every traveler who passed saw in its glass the story of the Witch of Aven, who had drowned a village in rage and been frozen by her own curse. [215] But mirrors show only surfaces. [220] Thoren had spent his life reading what lay beneath reflections — the scars under skin, the history under dust, the truth under victory. [242]

He began to walk. [246] His first circle was tight, tracing the vortex's outer edge where orchard grass met water. [261] His second widened past the first row of spinning trees. [271] His third brought him knee-deep in lake water, the current tugging at his legs with cold fingers. [288] The fourth circle opened into the vortex proper, and now the trees groaned around him, their branches sweeping past like the arms of drowning swimmers. [313]

Warlike serenity filled him — the calm that comes when danger is so total it becomes a kind of peace. [332] He let it settle into his bones as he walked his fifth circle, deeper into the spiral. [349]

In his pack, wrapped in oilcloth, lay a flesh-eating plant seed. [360] He had carried it for six months, knowing this lake would demand payment for its secrets. [376] The seed was a vicious thing — it would germinate on contact with flesh, its roots drinking blood while its leaves unfurled to consume the rest. [401] The cursed lake had taken lives. [407] It would recognize the offering. [412]

His sixth circle brought him within arm's reach of the frozen figure. [424] He could see her clearly now — the Witch of Aven, encased in ice that was not ice but frozen grief, solidified sorrow. [446] Her mouth was open. [450] Her eyes were wide. [454] Her hands were raised, palms out — not in attack, but in the gesture of one trying to stop something. [473]

His seventh circle brought him face to face with her. [483] To reach the confession, Thoren would have to unbend. [492] Not the ice — himself. [496] His rigid certainty, his refusal to doubt the stories he had been raised on, his conviction that the witch was guilty. [517] He had come here believing he would retrieve a villain's confession. [528] But the woman before him did not look like a villain. [539] She looked like someone who had been trying to warn them. [550]

He unwrapped the flesh-eating plant seed and pressed it against the frozen surface. [563] It cracked immediately, the seed splitting open, its pale roots threading into the ice. [577] The frozen grief began to fracture, and as it did, the confession poured out — not in words, but in memory, cold and clear. [600]

She had not cursed the lake. [606] She had tried to seal it. [612] The village elders had opened something ancient beneath the water, and when it began to consume their people, they blamed her. [633] She had frozen herself to contain what they had unleashed. [643] Her confession was not of guilt, but of failure. [652]

Thoren stood in the collapsing vortex, orchard trees settling around him like soldiers standing down. [667] He had found the lost thing. [673] And what mirrors don't show, he thought, watching the lake's surface go still at last, is the difference between the one who breaks and the one who holds. [701]
