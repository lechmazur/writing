The hydraulics failed on a Tuesday, and Maren stopped trying to leave by Wednesday. [14] The signal station perched on the ridge like a broken tooth against the starlit sky. [29] Without the hydraulic lift, the lower relay doors stayed sealed, the generator stayed on emergency, and Maren stayed put. [48] She'd come up here to work the night shift, transmit weather data, drink instant coffee. [63] Simple enough. [65] But the failure stranded her in a way that felt less like entrapment and more like permission. [82] She was a patchwork navigator, not by training, but by necessity. [93] After the coastal evacuations, after the cities emptied and people scattered inland, someone had to help them find their way back. [114] Not forward. [116] Back. [117] Reverse migration, they called it. [122] People returning to flooded towns, ruined neighborhoods, places that no longer existed on any functional map. [138] The calls came at midnight. [143] Always midnight. [145] Always the same voice, or fragments of voices, Maren could never tell if it was one person breaking apart or several people stitched together by bad signal. [172] They gave her coordinates that didn't exist on current charts. [182] Landmarks that had been demolished. [187] Street names buried under reservoir silt. [193] "Third oak past the cannery," a voice said last week. [203] "The one with the carved heart." [209] "You know the one." [213] Maren didn't know. [216] But she'd built, was building, a composite map from these calls, these cryptic descriptions, these grief-soaked directions. [233] The patina bronze censer foot sat on her desk, a relic from the station's previous occupant, some kind of monk or hermit who'd burned incense to keep the silence bearable. [263] She used it now as a paperweight, holding down the edges of her growing chart, the curled corners of a territory that existed only in memory. [289] The thing fluctuated, not the map, but the signal. [298] The signal station's reach swelled and shrank with the moon phases, some interaction between the lunar pull and the damaged hydraulic system that nobody had come to fix. [326] Full moon, and she could receive calls from three hundred miles. [337] New moon, and she sat in the silence of dead air, the chart incomplete beneath the bronze foot. [355] She'd started navigating by moonlight out of necessity. [363] During full moons, she'd relay instructions back to the callers: how to find the old railroad grade, where the bridge abutments still showed above the waterline, which roads had been reclaimed by salt marsh but could still be crossed on foot. [404] During dark moons, she waited, and the geography of loss grew muddy in her hands. [419] "Can you take me to the corner of Pine and something?" a woman asked two nights ago, a full moon hanging fat and white outside the station window. [447] "There was a pharmacy there." [452] "I need to see if it's still there." [460] The pharmacy was gone. [464] Maren knew that. [467] But the woman needed to make the journey anyway, to walk through the ruins and find the foundation, to stand where the automatic doors had been and remember buying cold medicine for a child who had grown up and moved away before the waters came. [512] That was the geography Maren navigated. [518] Not streets and elevations, but the distance between what people remembered and what remained. [532] She didn't offer comfort. [536] She offered directions. [539] Turn left where the school used to be. [547] Walk until you see the water tower's shadow. [555] The route fluctuates with the moon phases, during spring tides, some paths vanish, during neap tides, others emerge. [573] She tracked it all, building the chart call by call, holding it down with the censer foot as if it might blow away. [596] Last night, a man called and asked for directions to a house he'd abandoned seven years ago. [613] "It's probably underwater," he said. [618] "Probably," Maren agreed. [621] "Can you tell me how to get there anyway?" [630] She could. [632] She did. [634] The coordinates fluctuated by moon phase, the old road surfaces shifted with the tides, but she traced the path with her finger and spoke it into the crackling receiver, and somewhere across the darkened landscape, a man began walking home. [674] The signal station hummed around her, half-broken, still functional. [683] Maren pressed the patina bronze censer foot into the corner of her map and waited for the next call. [702] The moon was waning. [706] Three more nights of good signal. [712] Then silence, then rebuilding. [716]
