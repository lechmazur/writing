The piano in Cloudhollow Station had ninety-one keys. [8] The missing one was middle C, and Serafina had stopped noticing its absence three years ago, the same year her anniversary clock stopped ticking. [32] She kept the clock on the piano's edge anyway, its hands frozen at 4:17, the moment her husband's train had departed for the lower world and never arrived. [60]

Cloudhollow was a tidal arbor suspended above the clouds, where roots hung into mist and the tide rose not with the moon but with the wind. [86] Travelers passed through on their way to more certain destinations, their compasses flickering wildly in the arbor's strange magnetism. [105] Serafina watched them from her bench, playing fragments of songs that made them pause, made them almost miss their connections. [125]

She was strategically creative in this way. [132] She couldn't make them stay, but she could make them linger. [143]

Today, though, she wasn't playing for strangers. [150] She was waiting for the Collector, a man who had been pursuing her broken anniversary clock for eighteen months. [169] He claimed it was a chronometric artifact, that its frozen mechanism held a locked frequency that could stabilize the flickering compasses. [190] He wanted to study it. [195] She wanted to keep the only thing that still measured what she'd lost. [208]

He would arrive before nightfall. [213] She had until the horizon invited night to decide what to do. [225]

When the morning tide crested, flooding the station's lower platforms with silver mist, Serafina locked the piano and climbed to the projection room. [248] Cloudhollow Station still housed its original cinema, a relic from the era when travelers waited days for the winds to settle. [269] The projector sat cold and dormant, but the film archives remained, hundreds of canisters of black-and-white reels that no one watched anymore. [291]

She'd discovered the hidden sections two years ago, spliced between frames of old travelogues. [305] Someone had cut and glued alternate footage into the reels, images of corridors and doorways that existed somewhere in the station's architecture but not on any map. [332] When she'd followed the visual clues, she'd found them: sealed passages behind the cinema walls, rooms the station had forgotten. [352]

Now she took refuge there, the broken anniversary clock tucked under her arm, the film reel's secret geography memorized like a sonata. [374] The hidden room was narrow and cold, lit only by a high window that framed the churning cloud sea. [393] She set the clock on a ledge and pressed her back against the wall. [407]

From below, she heard the Collector's train arrive. [415]

She had rehearsed this. [419] Not the hiding, but what came after. [426] She was going to surprise fate with an unplanned encore. [436]

At sunset, when the horizon began to bruise with night, she would return to the piano. [452] She would play the song her husband had requested every Friday, the one she hadn't touched since the clock stopped. [472] She would play it with ninety-one keys, and the missing C would leave a hole in the melody that no amount of technique could fill. [497]

That was the point. [501]

The Collector wanted the clock's frequency. [507] The travelers wanted their compasses to stop flickering. [515] Everyone wanted resolution. [518] But Serafina understood that some things shouldn't resolve. [526] Some frequencies were meant to stay locked. [533] Some compasses were meant to flicker, reminding their owners that direction was never guaranteed. [547]

She would play, and the broken clock would sit on the piano, and the sound would carry through the tidal arbor's strange acoustics to every platform and waiting room. [576] The Collector would find her eventually, but he would hear the music first, and he would understand that some artifacts weren't meant to be studied. [601] They were meant to be witnessed. [607]

The horizon shifted. [610] Orange bled into violet. [614] The cloud sea stilled. [618]

Serafina lifted the clock and crept back through the hidden passages, following the film reel's secret map in reverse. [637] She emerged into the station's main hall just as the first stars pricked through the darkening sky. [654]

The piano waited. [657] She sat. [659] She placed the clock where it always sat. [667] She played. [669]

The first note rang out into the inviting night, and somewhere in the arbor, a compass stopped flickering. [687] Then started again. [690] Then stopped. [692]

Serafina smiled and kept playing, the missing C a silence more eloquent than sound. [706]
