The road had been Sable's university for nine years before the sky changed. [13] She'd been sleeping in a drainage culvert off Route 14 when the energy beings arrived - great soft curtains of light that descended through the atmosphere like breath given form. [42] They didn't speak. [45] They didn't need to. [49] Their presence alone rewrote the rules. [55] Clocks stuttered. [57] Rivers ran backward for minutes at a time. [65] People aged in uneven bursts, or not at all. [74]

Sable didn't panic. [77] She'd always been ethically playful - the sort who'd steal a rich man's wallet and leave a poem in its place. [97] The arrival of beings who bent causality interested her the way a new highway interested her: as something to walk along and describe. [120] But description wasn't enough anymore. [125] She wanted to know the texture of time itself - whether it was smooth or rough, whether it had grain like wood, whether it could be frayed. [151]

The frayed coil of rope in her pack she understood as texture. [163] She'd used it three years ago to lower her brother from a prison window after he'd been sentenced for printing banned verse. [185] The rope had saved his life and ended hers in any conventional sense - she couldn't go home, couldn't settle, could only drift and write. [209] Its fibers were broken in particular places, and she'd memorized each break like a sentence with missing words. [227]

She found the grotto by accident, the way she found everything. [238] A terra-cotta hillside off a forgotten county road, split open by some ancient seismic shrug. [253] Inside: curved walls of baked clay, warm to the touch, and above, through a crack in the ceiling, a corridor of starlight. [275] The energy beings were there. [280] Three of them, pooled like luminous water in the grotto's basin, their light making the terra-cotta walls glow the color of embers. [302]

And time, inside the grotto, was wrong. [309] Sable felt it immediately - not wrong like broken, but wrong like a word in a foreign language she almost recognized. [329] She moved her hand and the motion seemed to echo, as if each gesture were a pattern being laid down in layers, one atop another, each visible for a moment before the next covered it. [364]

She sat at the edge of the basin and watched. [374] The beings pulsed. [377] She noticed the pattern: slow, fast, slow, slow, fast - like breathing, like waves, like the rhythm of walking a long road. [398] She pulled out the rope and held it. [406] The frayed sections corresponded to something. [412] She began to see it - each break in the rope aligned with a moment when the beings' pattern shifted. [431] Not coincidence. [433] The rope had been present at moments of temporal stress: the prison escape, when her brother's sentence had been read by a judge whose clock had stuttered, when the rope had borne weight at the exact instant the first energy being touched the atmosphere. [477]

She began unwinding the rope, layer by layer, letting the fibers separate. [489] Each layer revealed older breaks, older patterns. [496] The rope was a record. [501] Time had texture, and it had been recording itself in the very objects that survived temporal stress. [518]

But here was the impossible choice. [524] The beings were pulling at the grotto's walls now, peeling terra-cotta away in translucent sheets, and where they peeled, time bled - moments visible in the clay like fossils. [552] She could see yesterday, last year, her brother's face at the prison window. [565] She could reach into the wall and change a moment. [575] Pull one thread of the pattern and the rope would never fray, her brother would never be caught, she would never drift. [597] Or she could let the pattern remain. [604] Accept the texture of time as it was - rough and broken and real - and simply describe it, the way she'd always done, with passionate subtlety, without owning it. [632]

The energy beings waited. [636] They were, she realized, asking her to choose. [644] Not with words. [647] With the weight of their luminous attention. [654]

Sable looked at the rope in her hands, each frayed end a sentence she had lived. [670] She looked at the wall where her brother's face flickered in clay. [682]

She began to write. [686]
