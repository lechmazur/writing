Maren had not always been earthbound. [6]
She remembered the pull of distant suns, the way light once streamed through her like water through crystal. [24]
But that was before, and before had no place in the serpentine grotto she now called home. [41]
The grotto wound like a sleeping dragon's spine, its walls slick with moisture, its curves following no architecture but the earth's own breathing. [64]
Mist threaded through the passages each morning, born from the sea outside, and by afternoon the air hung thick and pearlescent. [85]
Maren had learned to navigate by touch, by the faint bioluminescence of mosses that clung to the stone. [103]
She was a star watcher, though the stars were denied to her here. [116]
The mist swallowed the sky. [121]
What she had instead were fragments of light trapped in earth: sea glass, tumbled smooth by centuries of tide and stone, washed into the grotto's deeper pools. [148]
She collected them. [151]
Blues, greens, the rare amber. [156]
Each piece felt like a word from a language she once spoke fluently. [169]
Her purpose had crystallized slowly, the way true purpose does. [179]
The villagers who lived above the cliffs, on the windswept grasslands, feared the dark season. [194]
They burned fires against it. [199]
But fire consumed, fire destroyed, and Maren had seen enough of destruction in her fall from the heavens. [217]
She wanted to share light without using fire. [225]
To give them something gentler, something that would not eat the world even as it pushed back the dark. [244]
The method came to her as memories resurfaced. [252]
She remembered the gem polishers of a city that no longer existed, how they had taught her to coax inner fire from stone through patience and abrasive pastes made from crushed shells. [284]
She began to work. [288]
Each evening she sat at her driftwood table and polished the sea glass with finer and finer grit, wearing away the frost of its surface until it became reflectively vibrant, a thing that caught even the faintest photon and multiplied it, sent it dancing. [332]
But sea glass alone was not enough. [339]
She needed something at the center, a nucleus of calm around which the scattered light could gather and steady. [358]
She had read of it in a waterlogged text she'd found wedged in the grotto's eastern coil: a pearl of stillness, not a true pearl but a state of being, a point of perfect internal quiet that could anchor radiance and keep it from scattering into noise. [405]
The text said such stillness could only be achieved by one who had known motion and chosen to cease. [424]
Maren weighed this knowledge as she weighed the glass in her palm. [436]
She had known motion, the terrible velocity of light across voids. [447]
She had chosen this stillness, this grotto, this mist. [456]
Perhaps she was already becoming what the text described. [465]
She worked through the dark season. [471]
She polished. [473]
She arranged the sea glass in concentric rings around a single clear piece, the one she had pressed against her chest the night she fell, the one that had cut her and healed her both. [508]
She polished that piece until it vanished into pure transparency, until it became a lens rather than an object, until looking through it was like looking through a held breath. [538]
And then, on the longest night, she carried her creation to the grotto's mouth. [552]
The mist parted, curious. [556]
The villagers had gathered on the cliff above, their fires unlit, because she had sent word. [572]
She had told them: come and see what light can be when it is not burning. [588]
She set the arrangement on a flat stone at the grotto's entrance. [600]
The moon rose. [603]
Its light touched the sea glass and traveled inward, ring by ring, until it reached the central lens, the pearl of stillness that Maren had become, in a sense, through her work. [635]
The light steadied there. [639]
It did not flicker. [643]
It did not consume. [647]
It simply existed, brilliant and calm, a small sun that asked nothing of the darkness except that it share the space. [668]
The villagers came down the path, one by one, and stood before it. [681]
No one spoke. [684]
The mist curled around their ankles like a cat. [693]
Maren watched from the grotto's shadows, and for the first time since her fall, she felt the old light move through her again, not as motion, but as warmth. [722]
She had found her way back to the stars by staying perfectly still. [735]
