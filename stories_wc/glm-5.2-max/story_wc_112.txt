Pell worked the upper gantries of the xenobiology research station, an obliging sky mechanic who kept the atmospheric sensors and observation arrays running. [23] Below him, the Vellix -- leathery-winged creatures with crystalline circulatory systems -- hung from their roosts in the observation bay, their massive wings folded and still. [47] It was within the hush of beating wings at rest that Pell did his best thinking. [63]

He had been working on something for months. [71] The magnetic nexus -- a convergence point in the planet's magnetosphere that appeared only at twilight -- had been flagged by the station's xenobiologists as potentially disruptive to the Vellix's migratory patterns. [101] But Pell saw something else in the data. [109] The nexus didn't just warp magnetic fields; it bent temporal readings in ways that conventional physics couldn't explain. [127] He believed the nexus proved that time moved sideways -- laterally, across adjacent moments -- rather than flowing forward in the neat line everyone assumed. [150]

The station hummed around him, mechanically warm despite the freezing atmosphere outside. [162] Heat exchangers and thermal regulators gave the corridors a faint, artificial warmth that contradicted the ice-blue views through every viewport. [182] It was this same contradiction that defined Pell's work: something cold revealing something warm, something rigid revealing something fluid. [201]

He had rigged the observation array to project the nexus's readings through holographic principles -- splitting the magnetic data into interference patterns that could be recombined into a three-dimensional model. [230] The holographic projection would show not just the nexus, but the temporal distortions surrounding it. [245] If he was right, the projection would reveal time folding laterally, sideways, like pages of a book being turned not forward but across. [268]

The Vellix stirred faintly. [272] Their rest period was ending. [277] Pell knew he had perhaps twenty minutes before their wing beats created enough electromagnetic interference to disrupt his readings. [296] He moved quickly but carefully, his obliging nature serving him well as he calibrated each sensor by hand rather than relying on automated routines. [320]

The twilight came. [323] Through the viewport, the planet's sun dipped below the horizon and the sky turned violet. [338] The magnetic nexus materialized in the projection field -- a shimmering knot of force lines that shouldn't have existed according to standard models. [360] Pell watched the holographic representation pulse and shift. [368]

He reached for the lever that controlled the array's sensitivity range. [379] It was an old mechanism, manual, requiring physical force. [388] He pulled the lever, and the projection expanded, revealing finer detail in the temporal distortions. [403]

There it was. [406] Time wasn't bending forward or backward. [412] It was moving sideways, slipping between adjacent realities like a current flowing parallel to the shore. [428] The nexus was a window into those lateral movements, and the Vellix -- with their crystalline circulatory systems -- had always known it. [449] Their migrations followed the sideways currents of time. [457]

Pell stood in the icy warmth of the station, watching the projection, feeling something awaken in him. [474] Not the sharp jolt of revelation, but a subdued awakening -- a quiet certainty settling into his bones. [491] The data was irrefutable. [495] The holographic model showed temporal displacement moving orthogonally to the conventional timeline. [507]

The first Vellix unfolded its wings. [513] The beating began, soft at first, then building. [521] The electromagnetic interference crept into his readings, and the holographic projection flickered. [533] But Pell had what he needed. [539] He had proven that time moved sideways. [546]

He released the lever, and the array powered down. [555] Around him, the station pulsed with its mechanically warm hum, a heartbeat of metal and circuitry that now seemed less artificial and more like a living thing breathing in the cold. [586]

The Vellix took flight, their wings filling the observation bay with sound and motion. [600] Pell watched them navigate the air with perfect precision, following currents that existed between moments, between one second and the next, sliding sideways through time as naturally as breathing. [629]

He had come to the station as a mechanic, a fixer of things, an obliging hand. [645] But in the hush of beating wings at rest, he had found something that couldn't be fixed or assembled -- only witnessed. [666] Time moved sideways, and the proof hung in the air like frost, cold and undeniable, yet somehow warm with the promise of everything that existed just beyond the edge of now. [697]
