Maret hadn't slept in eleven days when the rain finally came to the tidal island. [15] Not real rain -- not anymore, not after the water wars -- but the thin, chemical thing that streaked the sky and left trails on the basalt like veins of some dying language. [46] She followed these rain trails as a cartographer follows rivers, her wryly earnest nature refusing to let the patterns go unread. [67] They were all that remained of weather, and she painted them obsessively, dragging her easel to the island's windward coves, grinding pigment from the mineral deposits the rain left behind. [97] Her motivation was simple and desperate: to thresh meaning from experience. [108] After the wars, after the treaties, after the world reorganized itself around scarcity, Maret needed to believe that beauty still carried weight, that rain streaks on rock could tell a story worth preserving. [141] On the eleventh sleepless night, the trails led her somewhere new. [152] They braided down the eastern cliff, converging where the old causeway met bedrock -- a site archaeologists had unearthed before the wars, before funding dried up and the excavation was abandoned. [182] The rain pooled in the shallow trench, and Maret knelt beside it, her brush idle, her attention caught by what the water had exposed. [206] An amber brooch. [209] It lay in the mud like a dropped eye, and inside the resin, a trapped air bubble preserved the ghost of a flower -- petals splayed open, breathing ancient atmosphere, proof of a world that had once been green. [247] Her mother had worn a brooch like this. [255] Before the wars, before water became something people killed over and the tidal islands became refuges for those with nothing left to lose. [278] A figure rose from the rain trails behind her -- a trader, following the same paths for different reasons. [296] "Exchange," he said, and the word was not a question. [306] He had seen the brooch catch light. [313] The amber, he explained, could be analyzed for pre-war atmospheric data, locked in the resin like a time capsule -- clean air readings, proof of what had been lost. [341] Such data was worth more than the water purification tablets he held out in his palm. [357] Maret looked at the brooch, at the flower, at the rain trails braiding around her knees. [373] The haggard awe she had carried for eleven days crested then -- that exhausted wonder of someone who has seen too much but cannot stop looking, who knows the world is dying and still reaches for its beauty. [410] The amber held a flower that had breathed clean air. [420] The rain trails held minerals from a poisoned sky. [429] The tidal island held them both, for now, until the sea returned to cut the causeway. [445] "What will you give me?" she asked. [452] He withdrew a canvas from his pack, clean and unused, pre-war manufacture -- something rarer than water in the world the wars had made. [475] Maret stared at it, and for the first time in eleven days, something moved beneath her exhaustion that was not despair. [496] She thought about her mother's brooch, about the archaeologists who had unearthed this convergence site believing the past held answers, about the rain trails leading her here -- mineral paths left by a bankrupt sky, guiding her to where ancient and ruined met. [538] Meaning was what you threshed from experience: separating grain from chaff, signal from noise, the flower from the amber. [557] She made the exchange. [561] The trader took the brooch and followed the rain trails toward the mainland before the tide sealed the causeway. [580] Maret stayed on the island with her clean canvas, setting up her easel in the trench as the sea closed around the rocks. [603] She painted what she had seen -- the flower trapped in amber, its petals open, breathing air no one would breathe again. [624] When she finally laid down her brush, eleven days of insomnia caught her at once, and she slept as the tide came in, the rain trails drying on the basalt above her, their patterns saved on canvas, meaning threshed from a world that had almost forgotten how to mean.
