The Sound Wave Garden existed where music became geography. [9] Sonic frequencies shaped hedges into trembling geometries, and bass notes rippled through pools that reflected colors no eye should name. [30] Vellen had come here three nights ago, carrying memories that weren't entirely hers. [43]

She'd been Subject Nine in the Lathan Trials, seven months of injections that were supposed to isolate traumatic memories and dissolve them. [65] Instead, the compound had bound her remembrances to eleven other patients. [76] She could taste the sour metal of a stranger's childhood surgery. [87] She wept without knowing why, mourning lovers she'd never touched. [97] The scientists called it memory entanglement and offered her a second protocol to correct it. [112] She'd signed the consent forms, then walked out the fire exit before the first needle. [127] Quiet rebellion, the kind that leaves no evidence. [135]

Now she perched on the stone rim of the auric lagoon, the garden's deepest feature, where starlight dissolved into the water and emerged as gold. [160] The lagoon held every sound that had ever fallen into it, layered like sediment. [174] Musicians came to listen. [178] Vellen came because the lagoon could unweave tangled frequencies, and she was tangled. [191]

But she hadn't touched the water yet. [198]

Instead, she drummed her fingers against the stone (tap, tap, tap), a habit the other subjects had given her, or maybe she'd always had it. [223] She drummed on the pathway stones when she walked. [232] She drummed on her own knees, on doorframes, on the copper pipes that channeled sound through the garden's architecture. [251] Each percussion sent visible ripples into the air, small shocks of violet and grey. [265] The garden responded to rhythm more than melody. [273]

This was her plan: to drum the entangled memories free, to breathe life into a shared dream. [290] Not to destroy them, the way Lathan's protocol would have, severing threads indiscriminately. [303] She wanted to give them shape. [309] To arrange them into something that could hold all twelve of them, all twelve dreamers who'd been bound together by accident and chemistry. [332]

Because beneath the borrowed griefs, she'd found something the scientists hadn't catalogued. [344] Fragments from all twelve subjects that aligned like compass points: a vision of a place that none of them had ever been but all of them remembered. [371] A valley. [373] A particular quality of light. [378] The sense of being completely, impossibly held. [385]

She didn't know if it was real. [392] She didn't know if it mattered. [398]

At the hush of the universe inhaling, that breath between midnight and morning when even insects fell silent, Vellen stopped drumming. [419] The garden's ripples stilled. [423] The auric lagoon under starlight lay open, a mirror of drowned stars. [435]

She pressed both palms flat against the stone rim and leaned forward until her reflection merged with the gold. [454] The memories surged. [457] A boy's terror in a dark closet. [464] An old woman's joy at rain after drought. [472] Eleven heartbeats overlaid on hers. [477] She held them, not fighting, not sorting, just holding, the way you hold a wounded bird, with pressure enough to contain but not to crush. [502]

Then she began to drum again, this time on the surface of the water itself. [517] Each strike released a tone, deep and resonant, and with each tone a memory separated, becoming distinct, becoming whole, returning to its proper shape. [541] The lagoon blazed. [544] Colors she had no names for spiraled upward into the starless sky. [556] The garden trembled. [559]

And in the sound she was building, in the rhythm of eleven sorrows given back their boundaries, she felt the shared dream clarify. [582] The valley. [584] The light. [586] It wasn't a memory. [590] It was a possibility, assembled from twelve people who had touched each other's minds and glimpsed, for one fractured instant, what it might feel like to not be alone. [619]

Her fingers slowed. [622] The last ripple faded. [626] The lagoon dimmed to its quiet gold. [633]

Vellen sat back, breathing hard, tears running freely (hers, maybe, or someone else's), and watched the first pale thread of dawn pull itself across the sky. [659] The garden held its silence. [664]

She had not cured herself. [669] The threads would tangle again; they always did. [677] But for one held breath, the dream had existed outside her skull, audible, visible, almost touchable in the trembling air above the water. [700]

She drummed once on the stone, gently, a period at the end of a sentence. [715]

Then she waited for the universe to exhale. [723]
