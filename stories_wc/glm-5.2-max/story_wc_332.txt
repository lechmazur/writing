Edda kept the library on Thellis open from midnight until dawn, the only odd-hour librarian in the whispering archipelago. [19] Between the islands, sound traveled strangely -- a confession spoken on one shore would arrive as a murmur on the next, its meaning rearranged by wind and water. [46] The archipelago itself seemed to speak in fragments, and Edda had learned to listen. [60] She had been chasing something for three years now: a dream that visited her only once, on the night she first cataloged the Vellan Codex, then vanished before she could hold it. [92] It was a dream of a color that did not exist in waking light, a blue that held the warmth of grief and the clarity of joy simultaneously. [120] She called it the runaway dream because it would not stay caught. [132] Each morning she woke with the taste of it dissolving from her tongue. [145] Her only evidence that it had ever come at all was a watercolor palette she had found in the codex's binding -- eighteen dried cakes of pigment arranged in a circle, each one a shade she almost recognized but could not name. [186] She had tried painting with them in the ordinary way and failed. [198] The colors always dried into something muted, ordinary, wrong. [207] It was the fisher Maren who gave her the method, though unintentionally. [219] Maren brought her photographs at three in the morning, sunrise images she took from her skiff between the outer islands. [239] The light in them was not quite light as Edda had ever seen it -- it pooled and refracted as though the atmosphere itself were thinner there, more honest. [267] "You paint with light," Edda told her, and Maren laughed. [277] "Everyone paints with light," Maren said. [283] "That's what photography is." [287] But Edda understood then what she had been doing wrong. [297] She had been trying to mix the palette's colors in water when they needed to be mixed in light. [316] At four-thirty, in the hush of improbable beginnings, Edda carried the watercolor palette to the easternmost point of Thellis. [335] The archipelago whispered around her -- fragments of lullabies from Oth, a quarrel drifting up from the southern channels. [353] She set the palette on the rocks and positioned Maren's camera beside it. [366] As the sun crested the horizon, she began photographing the palette at intervals, capturing how the changing light moved through each pigment cake. [389] The first frames showed nothing unusual. [395] But as the light sharpened, something happened. [402] The pigment cakes did not merely reflect the sunrise. [411] They refracted it, split it, rearranged it. [418] In the photographs, the colors on the palette began to shift -- that impossible blue emerged between the frames, visible only when the light hit two cakes simultaneously and the camera caught what her eyes could not. [454] She was photographing the palette, but really she was photographing the gap between colors, the space where the dream lived. [474] A cipher of longing, she thought -- not a code to be broken but one to be felt, each photograph a letter in an alphabet she was teaching herself to read. [504] The dream had not run away from her. [512] It had been waiting in the palette all along, compressed into pigment, needing only the right light to vitalize it. [532] She photographed until the sun fully rose and the colors settled back into the ordinary spectrum. [548] Then she sat on the rocks and scrolled through the images. [559] There it was. [562] That blue, luminous and absent at once -- present only in the photograph, never in her hand, never in the paint itself but always in the space between the light and the pigment and the lens. [597] She understood finally that she could not possess it. [606] That was the nature of the cipher. [613] Longing was not a problem to solve but a frequency to tune toward, and the watercolor palette was not a tool for painting but an instrument for receiving. [641] She packed the palette away and walked back toward the library as the archipelago's whispers followed her, carrying fragments of a language she was only beginning to translate. [669] Tomorrow she would try again. [674] The dream would keep running. [679] She would keep chasing, not to catch it but to stay within range of its light. [695]
