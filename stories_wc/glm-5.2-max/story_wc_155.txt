Within the fold of a daydream, Sera Nour found herself walking through the ruined orchard district of Vellum Hollow, where peach trees had long since become skeletal fingers reaching through centuries of thick fog. [34] She had been a mural artist once -- not merely vivid, but luminous. [46] Her peers used pigments; she painted with light itself, mixing crushed phosphors and dawn-filtered glass dust into colors that seemed to breathe after sunset. [70] But she had stopped painting three years ago, when her daughter Maren had taken the fog-bound road to Vellum Hollow and never returned. [93] No one returned from the district. [99] The fog there was not weather but memory -- thick, ancient, swallowing. [110] Yet here she was, dreaming herself into it, hoping to rediscover the joy that had drained from her brushes and her hands and her mornings. [135] The fog tasted like overripe fruit and old stone. [144] Through its curtain, shapes emerged: collapsed archways, a dried canal, and -- her breath caught -- a wall. [160] A wall still bearing traces of color, jade and ochre, beneath the white rot of centuries. [176] Sera pressed her palm against it, and a piece detached -- a fresco fragment, no larger than her hand, showing a child's laughing face half-eaten by time. [202] The fragment grew warm. [206] Sera tilted it toward what little light filtered through the fog, and the surface shifted like exposed film -- pale negatives blooming into positive image, a sequence unfolding across the ceramic surface. [237] She saw the orchard district as it had been: children stealing peaches, merchants arguing over bushel prices, an old woman teaching a girl to mix pigment from crushed berries. [266] The images came faster, connecting -- each scene a node in a knowledge network she hadn't known existed, a lattice of memories embedded in the district's very walls. [293] Every fresco fragment, it seemed, held a piece of someone's life, and each piece knew where the others lay. [312] Sera laughed -- a startled, helpless sound that surprised her. [321] The old woman in the film was teaching the exact brushstroke Sera's own grandmother had taught her. [338] The girl in the image was laughing at a joke Sera could almost hear. [352] Joy flickered in her chest like a match struck in a dark room. [365] She followed the network. [369] Each fragment she found -- wedged in doorframes, buried under leaf mould, sealed into the canal's broken bricks -- displayed its memory when light touched it. [393] The fog seemed to thin around her as she worked, as though the district itself were exhaling. [410] She gathered fragments along the canal bank, arranged them on a flat stone, and began to paint with light across their surfaces, connecting the images with strokes of phosphor-pigment she carried -- she always carried them, even now, even in dreams. [450] Her light-strokes merged the fragments into a single continuous display: the orchard district alive again, a moving panorama projected onto the fog itself. [473] Peach blossoms swirled. [476] Children chased each other through streets that no longer existed. [486] The old woman and the girl painted a wall together, and their laughter was audible now, thin as thread but real. [507] Sera sat back on her heels, grinning through tears she didn't try to explain. [521] The pensive mirth of the moment -- this grief-adjacent gladness -- felt true in a way nothing had since Maren's disappearance. [540] She wasn't naive enough to think a daydream could return her daughter. [552] But the district's memory-network had shown her something she'd forgotten: that joy is not the absence of loss but the residue of love, preserved in fragments, waiting for light. [581] The fog pressed back in. [586] The display dimmed. [589] Sera felt the dream folding shut around her like a book closing. [601] She woke at her kitchen table, cheek against the wood, brushes and phosphor-pots arranged before her as though she'd never stopped. [622] Her hand was closed around something -- a shard of painted ceramic, warm as a living thing. [638] The child's laughing face stared up at her from the fresco fragment. [650] Sera picked up a brush, dipped it in dawnlight and crushed glass, and began to paint. [666]
