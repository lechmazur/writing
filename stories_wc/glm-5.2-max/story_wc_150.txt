Mira had spent nineteen years in the lowlands before the letters from her grandmother's grave started arriving. [17] Not letters truly, but fragments -- pottery shards pressed with her family's seal, unearthed by spring rains and carried by pilgrims to the diaspora quarter where she taught children to read. [47] Each shard bore the same image: a sundial, cracked at its base. [59] She had never seen the original, but her grandmother had described it so often that Mira recognized it the way one recognizes a face from a story. [86]

She returned to the catacomb orchard on the first morning of the excavation season, when the caretakers opened the eastern tombs and the fruit trees dropped their first blossoms onto the bone-lined paths below. [120] The orchard was ancient -- older than the exile, older than the kingdom that had caused it. [136] Fig and pomegranate roots snaked between ribs and clavicles, drawing life from the dead in an exchange so patient it looked like reverence. [159]

Mira was warmly curious by nature, a trait her students had loved and her employers had tolerated. [176] She asked questions of the caretakers, of the roots, of the bones themselves. [189] Where would a sundial base have stood in a tomb complex built before the diaspora? [204] The caretakers, used to mourners and genealogists, were not used to someone who wandered the orchard's lower passages with a lantern and a sketchbook, smiling at the strange architecture of root meeting marrow. [237]

"You're looking for something specific," said old Halem, who had been digging since before Mira was born. [254]

"My grandmother's sundial," Mira said. [259] "She said it marked the hours of the dead so they would know when to dream." [275]

Halem studied her with the careful gaze of someone who had seen many returnees leave disappointed. [291] "Most people come to find names." [297] "You want to find a clock." [303]

"I want to find what it stood on." [311]

The excavation of the tomb -- her family's tomb, the Vassari niche in the fourth catacomb -- took the better part of the season. [333] Mira worked alongside the diggers, her hands blistering, her back aching, her curiosity never dimming. [348] The tomb had been disturbed long ago, its contents scattered by looters or by the earthquake that had split the catacomb's eastern wall. [371] No sundial. [373] No base. [375] Only a stone shelf carved with the outline of where one had stood. [388]

She might have left then, her faith in the return crumbling like the limestone around her, but something in the root pattern caught her eye. [413] The thickest roots -- old, gnarled, heavy with sap -- did not grow downward. [425] They grew east, toward the collapsed passage the caretakers had sealed decades ago. [438]

Purposeful wandering, her grandmother had called it. [445] The practice of letting curiosity lead without letting it scatter. [455] Mira followed the roots through unlit corridors, over collapsed doorways, past niches where strangers' bones lay wrapped in the embrace of fig wood. [478] The roots thickened. [481] The air sweetened with fruit. [486]

She found the sundial base in a chamber the maps did not name, toppled against a wall of bone and root. [507] It was granite, heavy as a promise, cracked but whole. [517] Beside it lay the gnomon -- the shadow-casting arm -- still sharp after centuries. [529] The roots had grown around both, not crushing them but holding them, the way the orchard held everything: with patience and without distinction between the living and the dead. [558]

Mira sat with the pieces for a long time. [567] She did not weep. [571] She ran her fingers along the crack in the base and felt the grain of the stone, the chisel marks of whoever had shaped it, the smoothness where her grandmother's hands had rested. [604] Something shifted in her chest -- not the dramatic flood she had expected from a homecoming, but a cautious warmth, like the first light of morning reaching the orchard floor. [633]

She carried the pieces back through the corridors, following her own path in reverse, and set them on the stone shelf in the Vassari niche. [658] The caretakers helped her lift the base into place. [667] She fitted the gnomon into its socket. [674] The crack remained, but the sundial stood. [681]

By afternoon, the sun found the angle of the niche and cast a shadow across the dial's face. [699] The hours of the dead resumed their count. [707] Mira sat beside the tomb with her sketchbook open, warmly curious about everything still buried, her interrupted journey -- her grandmother's interrupted journey, her people's interrupted journey -- finally, quietly, continuing.
