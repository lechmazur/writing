In the lighthouse orchard perched above shifting ice floes, Maren tended apple trees that had no right surviving this far north. [21] She was a shadow-laced peacekeeper, her reputation stained by years of brokering truces between the ice-fisher clans and the keepers who controlled the beacon tax. [46] Both sides believed she belonged to the other. [54] Both were wrong. [57]

She kept a broken crystal ball fragment in her coat pocket, remnant of the first mediation she had ever failed. [77] The sphere had shattered when Torvind Olafsen hurled it against the lighthouse wall after she delivered the compromise that satisfied no one. [99] She had kept one shard, its edges worn smooth by years of her thumb worrying it during difficult conversations. [118]

The orchard branches scraped each other in the wind as Maren walked toward the vintage store occupying the lighthouse's old supply room. [140] Grelda Foss ran it, stocking salvaged mirrors, tarnished silver, and bentwood frames from abandoned settlements along the floe-line. [158] Maren came not for merchandise but for the mirrors themselves. [168] In their warped and aged surfaces, she could sometimes glimpse things differently, not through magic but through a shifted perspective that her calmly intuitive mind could work with. [196] She had learned to read conflict the way Grelda read antiques, by studying what was reflected rather than what was directly seen. [218]

Today's conflict was worse than most. [224] Young Erik Torvindsen had pulled his father's old nets across the channel, blocking the keepers' supply boats. [241] The elder keepers had responded by dimming the beacon during a storm week. [254] Two boats had cracked against the floes. [261] No one had died yet, but the next move would decide whether that held. [275]

Maren stood before the largest mirror, a tall oval with foxing around its edges, and let herself see. [293] The glass caught the orchard behind her, the ice beyond, and somewhere in the distortion she saw Erik's face not as the clans saw him, reckless and furious, but as she had seen him three winters ago, hauling his father's body from the channel after a beacon-lit rescue went wrong. [343] The keepers had dimmed the light that night too, rationing oil. [354] Torvind had died in the dark. [360] She saw Grelda's daughter, a keeper herself, weeping as she poured seal oil into the beacon reservoir, knowing the clans would hate her for the tax, knowing her own mother would call her weak if she stopped. [397]

The crystal fragment grew warm in her pocket. [405] Maren understood what she needed it to mean. [413] She had to reconstitute something broken, not the sphere but the act of seeing each other clearly. [430] The mirrors offered glimpses. [434] The shard gave her focus. [439] But she needed courage, and she had always been a courageous anomaly, the one mediator who walked toward anger instead of away from it, who treated grief as evidence rather than obstacle. [471] Her gift was not diplomacy but something harder, the ability to map empathy onto action. [486]

Erik was waiting at the channel's edge when she arrived. [496] His hands were raw from the ropes. [503] Behind him, his clansmen stood in a line. [511] Across the water, the keepers gathered on the dock, Grelda's daughter among them, her face rigid. [527]

Erik spoke first, listing grievances, his voice climbing. [535] Maren let him finish. [539] Then she asked one question. [544]

"Do you remember the night your father died?" [552]

Silence. [553] The weighted breath before reply settled over everyone, clansmen and keepers alike. [565] Maren felt it press against her chest, familiar and terrible. [575] This was the moment, not the negotiation that would follow but this breath, where everyone chose what to do with what they felt. [598]

Erik's jaw tightened. [601]

"He shouldn't have died." [605]

"No, he shouldn't have," Maren said. [611] "And Grelda's daughter shouldn't have had to decide who got the light that night." [625] "She was nineteen." [628] "She rationed oil the way the elders taught her." [637] "Both sides were following rules that got your father killed." [647]

She pulled out the crystal fragment and held it up, not as a symbol but as a fact. [665]

"This is what remains of the last peace we tried to build." [677] "Broken, but still here." [681] "I am not asking you to forgive anyone." [689] "I am asking you to stop pretending the other side isn't also grieving." [702]

Erik looked at the shard. [707] Grelda's daughter looked at Erik. [712] The breath finally broke. [716]

"My father respected your mother," Erik said across the water, his voice unsteady. [729]

"She taught me how to salt fish in your harbor," Grelda's daughter called back. [743]

Maren slipped the fragment back into her pocket and watched the first ropes come down. [758] The ice groaned beneath them all, indifferent, but the channel was opening again. [771] She would stay until it held. [777]
