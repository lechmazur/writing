Maren was the reality checker for the island of Velar, a place where the boundary between what happened and what merely seemed to happen was always sliding. [27] Her job was straightforward in title and impossible in practice: she verified which memories belonged to the island's true history and which had drifted in from dreams that had grown too vivid. [59] The islanders accepted this work with an empirically whimsical attitude, treating each verified fact as a small miracle and each debunked one as a charming joke. [85] Both responses unsettled her. [89]

She worked from the ancient ridge above the village, a spine of limestone that was, this week, in spectacular bloom. [109] Crimson flame vines and white night jasmine had overtaken the rock face, filling the air with a sweetness so dense it felt like breathing inside a bell. [136] From here she could see the whole southern bay and the dark line of clouds assembling on the horizon. [155] The monsoon would arrive within days, and when it did, the ridge would become impassable and the village would close in on itself for weeks. [180] During monsoon season, the islanders dreamed more intensely. [188] That was the problem. [192]

Velar's cultural memory lived in the collective dreams of its people. [203] Each night, the islanders added to a shared reservoir of images and stories that preserved their history, their songs, their sense of who they were. [228] But the reservoir was unstable, prone to contamination. [236] A vivid enough dream could plant a false memory that spread from sleeper to sleeper until the entire village accepted it as heritage. [259] Maren had seen it happen before: three years ago, an invented war story had infiltrated the collective memory so thoroughly that elders wept for casualties who had never existed. [288] She had spent six months separating the false grief from the real. [300]

Now something worse was creeping in. [306]

She held the knowledge fruit seed between her fingers, rolling its rough surface against her thumb. [322] The seed was the last of its kind, harvested from a tree that had grown at the convergence point of the island's dream-lines before a storm took it down a generation ago. [354] When placed under a sleeper's tongue, it allowed the interpreter to enter their dream and read its architecture from inside, to feel where the memory was true and where it buckled. [385] She had been saving it, uncertain whether the need would ever justify its use. [399] There would be no second chance. [405]

Last night, four islanders had reported the same dream: a figure standing at the edge of the sea, singing a song none of them recognized but all of them felt they had known since birth. [440] The melody carried a weight that did not belong to it, as if it were dragging a fabricated ancestry behind it. [461] If it took root in the collective memory before the monsoon sealed the village indoors, there would be no way to contain its spread. [485] The rains would amplify it, and by the time the skies cleared, the island's cultural memory would be rewritten. [504]

She had to act tonight. [509]

Maren walked down the ridge as dusk settled over the bay. [520] She would ask old Tavin to sleep with the seed beneath his tongue, since his dreams had always been the clearest channel to the collective memory. [546] Through interpreted dreams, she would trace the song back to its origin, waken the truth of it from wherever it slept, and determine whether it was a genuine fragment of forgotten heritage or something fabricated and invasive. [583] If it was real, she would let it join the island's memory. [595] If it was false, she would follow it to its source and pull it out by the roots before it could flower. [617]

The air was luminous with the last pale light, and she felt the familiar doubt that always accompanied this work, the suspicion that she was drawing lines in water. [646] What if the real and the fabricated had already grown so tangled that separating them caused more damage than leaving them alone? [668] What if the act of checking reality was itself a kind of erosion, each verification wearing away a little more of the island's texture until nothing remained but a clean, bare surface no one wanted to live on? [706]

She paused at the bottom of the path where the ridge met the village, jasmine still clinging to her sleeves. [726] The clouds on the horizon were taller now, advancing slowly, and the first breath of coming rain carried the smell of salt and disturbed earth. [751] Somewhere in the village, Tavin was already falling asleep, and the figure at the water's edge was already beginning to sing. [772]

Maren tightened her hand around the seed and walked forward into the warm dark. [786]
