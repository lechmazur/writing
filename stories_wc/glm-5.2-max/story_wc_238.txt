Marguerite Vine had spent fifteen years coaxing ghosts out of other people's houses, then writing about what they'd confessed in a column called "The Other Side." [26] When the living asked her how to handle the dead, she always answered in metaphors. [41] "A locked door only keeps out guests," she'd say. [50] "Spirits don't knock." [53] It was the only honest language she'd found for things that couldn't be said plain. [68]

But the one ghost she'd never been able to write about, or release, was her own. [84]

The Hartwell Sheet Music Archive sat at the edge of an estate where bulldozers would arrive at dawn. [102] Outside the archive's salt-bleached windows, the orchard's cherry and pear trees stood in their last hours -- unpicked, undisturbed, holding the particular hush of things about to be destroyed. [130] Marguerite had driven three hours to reach this place before the chainsaws came. [143] Not for the trees. [147] For what lived beneath them. [152]

She found Florence at the archive's long reading table, cataloging waltzes by candlelight. [165] Florence Hartwell, sixty-two, sharp-jawed, silver-haired, the woman who'd taught Marguerite everything about spirits and then stopped speaking to her eleven years ago. [187]

"You came," Florence said, without looking up. [194]

"I came to say something I should have said a long time ago." [207]

Marguerite set an antique perfume bottle on the table between them. [218] Cut glass, gilt cap, stopper sealed in green wax. [227] She'd bought it at a market in Lyon when she was twenty-three, the week she'd become Florence's apprentice. [245] She'd meant to give it as a gift. [253] Instead, she'd kept it for eleven years -- a small, perfect failure she carried from city to city, the way you carry an umbrella after the rain has already ruined you. [283]

"I'm sorry," Marguerite said. [287] "I took your methods and made them cheap. [295] I turned what you taught me into entertainment. [303] I made people's hauntings into gossip." [309]

Florence finally looked up. [313] "You think I'm angry about the column?" [320]

"Among other things." [323]

"A river doesn't resent the sea for taking its water," Florence said. [335] "It just wishes the sea had asked first." [343]

Marguerite almost smiled. [346] "Then let me ask." [350] She pulled two copper wires from her bag, connected them to a hand-crank generator, and looped them around the iron legs of the reading table. [375] Florence watched without surprise. [379] She'd invented this method herself -- induced currents to thin the barrier between worlds, to reconnect with what had been severed. [399]

"You're using my own technique to apologize to me." [408]

"A wound closes faster when you use the blade that made it," Marguerite said. [422] A metaphor. [424] She couldn't help herself -- the habit was marrow-deep. [432]

The current humming through the iron made the candle flames shiver. [443] Sheet music on the shelves behind Florence began to rustle, pages turning as if unseen hands moved through them. [462] An old piano in the corner began to hum a low sustained note, no hand on the keys, just the current vibrating through its strings. [487] The air tasted faintly of ozone and something older -- rosewater, maybe, or the memory of it. [503]

Then Marguerite felt it: the faint, unmistakable pull of her own unfinished ghost. [516] Not a spirit she'd been hired to exorcise, but the part of herself she'd abandoned when she'd run from Florence's side -- the girl who'd wanted to understand the dead, not monetize their secrets. [549] The current had reconnected her to what she'd cut off years ago. [561]

The sensation lasted twenty seconds. [566] Then the candle flames steadied, the pages went still, and the current faded. [579]

Florence picked up the perfume bottle. [585] She broke the wax seal with her thumbnail and lifted the stopper to her nose. [600] "Smells like you did at twenty-three. [606] Like you hadn't decided yet what you were willing to lose." [617]

"I know now," Marguerite said. [622] "I came because I can't carry this anymore." [630]

Outside, the orchard stood in its last hush. [638] The trees would be gone by noon, and the silence they left behind would be permanent. [654] Inside, two women sat across from each other, the air between them changed -- not healed, not restored, but moved. [673] An imperfect miracle. [676] The kind that arrives not in flashes of light but in the quiet confession that you were wrong, offered late, on the night before something beautiful is cut down. [705]

Florence set the bottle down gently. [711] She smiled -- barely, the kind of smile that knows what everything costs. [723] "Stay for the waltzes," she said. [729] "We have until morning." [733]
