No one remembered when the fountains had last flowed. [11] That was the trouble with the city of forgotten fountains—memory itself had calcified, and no one thought to question why. [29] The fountains stood at every crossing, their basins packed with dead leaves, their cherubs and lions modeling a permanent thirst. [50]

Thara was the one person who asked questions. [58] They called her a skeptic because she refused to accept the official story—that the aquifer had dried, that the pipes had rusted, that the city had simply outlived its water. [88] But she had a quality the others lacked: she was luminous. [99] Not in beauty, not in charm, but in the way she carried her doubt, like a lantern into basements no one else would enter. [121]

In autumn, the migrating geese flew south over the rooftops. [132] Thara noticed something no one else had thought to observe. [143] Every formation that crossed above the fountain in Mara Square would pause its motion, hovering for one breath, as if the air above the dry fountain pulled at their internal compass. [172] The pause of migrating geese was a small thing, three heartbeats at most, but it confirmed what Thara had long suspected: the water was still there. [198] It was locked, trapped beneath the surface like memories sealed in a bankrupt vault. [212]

For two years she watched the geese pause each autumn. [222] For two years she kept her silence, a defiant hush against the loud conclusion of the rest of the city that the fountains were permanently, utterly dead. [250]

She knew she would need sidereal patience—not patience measured in days or seasons, but in the slow time of stars. [270] She was realistically imaginative enough to accept this. [278] She could picture the water flowing again, could map the engineering of it in her mind, but she never pretended it would be fast or easy. [307]

Then the breadcrumbs appeared. [311]

Not breadcrumbs in the culinary sense. [317] She woke one October morning to find a scattering of crystallized mineral deposit along the fountain's edge, a trail leading away from Mara Square down an alley she had never noticed before. [351] Each deposit caught the light like grains of colored glass. [362]

She followed, the trail threading through the old city, past basin after dry fountain, each step pulling her deeper into a district where the buildings pressed close and the light dimmed. [394] She followed breadcrumbs through passages that seemed to have no names. [404]

At the end, she found a chamber beneath a fountain capped with iron that had been painted to look like stone. [424]

Inside the chamber was a box made of stained glass. [434] Even in the subterranean dark, it glowed, drawing light from somewhere too distant to identify. [450] The box sat at the center of a circular platform, its panels marked with brilliant scenes: the fountains running, children wading through the squares, the geese pausing not from a disturbance in the air but in recognition of something ancient calling them home. [497]

The images were memories. [502] The city's memories. [505] Every captured moment, every forgotten story, every reason the fountains had been built in the first place, preserved in the stained glass like insects locked in amber. [532]

When she touched the box, it was cold. [539] The glass panels were rigid, holding tight to what they contained. [549]

She tried to pry it open. [555] It would not give. [559]

She sat back on her heels and breathed. [566] She had to unstick time itself, to unstick the city's memory from wherever it had refused to budge. [584] She had to unstick the water from wherever it had gone to hide. [595]

She placed both hands flat on the box and waited. [604] Sidereal patience now—not anger, not force. [613] She let the idea of water fill her mind. [621] She let the memory of sound, of pressure, of flow pass through her palms. [632]

A crack. [634] Then another. [637] The panels loosened, then gave way entirely, folding outward like petals of an enormous flower. [653]

The memories did not rush. [658] They seeped upward through the stone as water returning to its old channels, filling each fountain, basin by basin, with the sound that had been locked away. [684] The cherubs spouted, the lions roared with real spray, couples danced in the cascade. [697]

Morning came. [699] Thara knelt there as the city erupted around her, laughing, dancing, and shouting at the water. [713] She alone stayed, defiantly hushed. [719] The geese flew over and did not pause. [727] They had no reason to. [732] The air above the fountain was clean and free, as a million memories, released at last, flowed outward into the city. [751]

The fountains remembered themselves. [755] So did everyone else. [760]
