The blooming jetty jutted into water that no longer nourished it. [11] After the reef died, the coral turned to bone-white rubble, and the fish drifted elsewhere, but the jetty's planks still burst with flowers, hardy salt-tolerant things that had no business thriving on dead wood over dead water. [48] The gloom-hill coachman tied his mare to the post at the jetty's entrance. [61] He had driven down from the hills every evening for three years, bringing with him a collection of dried seed pods wrapped in oilcloth. [85] Each pod was labeled in a script no living person could read, because the gloom-hill coachman spoke dead languages, had spoken them since childhood, since his grandmother whispered them into his ear on the long dark rides between settlements. [124]

He walked the jetty's length, and the twilight pressed close, the sky enormous above, the ocean endless below, but the jetty itself a narrow ribbon between them, barely wide enough for two men to walk abreast. [160] This was the feeling he lived for: the smallness of himself against the bigness of everything. [176] Narrow vastness. [178] That was what his grandmother had called it, in one of those old tongues. [192]

The birds came first, as they always did. [200] Terns and plovers and one old heron that had survived the reef's death by learning to hunt in the drainage ditches inland. [222] The coachman stood still and listened. [228] He had learned, over years, to understand bird calls, not in the way a naturalist might, cataloging songs, but in the way a farmer reads weather in the sky. [257] The terns' sharp staccato told him the soil at the jetty's far end was thinning, losing its grip. [275] The plover's low trill said the middle planks still held moisture. [286] The heron said nothing, but its silence meant the ground was ready. [298]

He unwrapped the oilcloth. [302] The seed pods were dry and light, each one a fist-sized husk from trees that no longer grew anywhere except in the gloom-hill coachman's memory and his grandmother's stories. [331] She had told him what they were for: to fertilize ground that had been used badly, ground that had drunk blood and needed to drink something else. [358] Because this jetty had been a battlefield. [365] Three hundred years ago, two nations had met here, or rather, one had waited and one had arrived by sea, and the fighting had soaked every plank. [392] The peace that came after, the concord signed on this very jetty, had been submerged by time and forgetting, buried under newer wars and older griefs. [418] A submerged concord. [421] The coachman sometimes thought he could hear it when the tide moved beneath the boards: a murmur of agreement, of ceasefire, of enough. [444]

His grandmother had given him the pods and the languages and the task. [457] "Let a garden reclaim a battlefield," she had said, in a tongue that hadn't been spoken aloud in four centuries. [477] "Grow over what was done. [482] Not to hide it. [486] To answer it." [489]

He knelt and cracked the first pod against the planks. [499] Inside: a fine dark powder, almost black, and three small seeds like teeth. [512] He worked the powder into the gaps between the boards where soil had accumulated, where the jetty's flowers had already begun the work of rooting. [537] The powder would feed what was already growing and make room for what the seeds would become. [554] He moved down the jetty, cracking pods, listening to birds, reading the wind. [567] The terns shifted their calls, a softer pattern now, and he adjusted where he placed the next seeds, tucking them into a wetter seam near the middle. [594] The heron finally spoke: one low croak that meant here, and the coachman knelt and planted. [610]

By full dark, the oilcloth was empty. [617] He stood at the jetty's end, the dead reef pale beneath the black water, and felt the narrowness of the planks beneath his boots and the vastness of the ocean pressing in. [649] Somewhere below, a submerged concord waited to surface. [657] Somewhere in the soil, seeds were already swelling. [665] He untied the mare and drove back toward gloom-hill, and behind him the jetty bloomed in the dark, answering old violence with slow, patient green. [690]
