The canyons of the Thunder-echo range held sound the way a cupped hand holds water -- briefly, then gone. [18] Sorek had walked three days through this silence, and it pressed on him with a weight that had nothing to do with sound. [41] It was avalanchingly quiet, the kind of quiet that buried a person under its own accumulation, stone by stone, breath by breath. [63] Sorek was no longer a keeper. [69] The guardianship had ended seven months ago in a candlelit vault beneath the Pale Library, when he had lost the Veth Codex to a fire that should not have been possible. [100] The elders had not punished him. [106] They had simply looked at him with expressions of practiced grief and told him his service was complete. [124] That was its own punishment. [129] He had spent every day since trying to understand how the fire had started, what he had missed, what failure of attention or judgment had let the Codex burn. [158] He was driven now by something more consuming than duty: the need to harvest insights from failure, to make the loss mean something other than waste. [184] The Thunder-echo range bordered the known edge of the mapped world, where the canyon walls were so deep and narrow that sound folded back on itself and reality wore thin. [214] Legends among the keepers held that certain places in the range allowed passage -- not to another land, but between the weave of one world and its adjacent sister. [242] Sorek had dismissed such stories during his guardianship. [250] Now he had no dismissal left in him. [258] He carried only one relic: a gilded reed flute, recovered from the same vault that had held the Codex. [277] The flute had survived the fire unmarked, which had struck no one as significant but had struck Sorek as an accusation. [298] He had kept it because it was the last thing his hands had been trusted with. [314] The canyon narrowed. [317] He began arranging driftwood as directional markers -- pieces stripped white by whatever ancient flood had carved these passages, now bone-dry and scattered along the canyon floor like fallen ribs. [346] He leaned the first piece against the canyon wall, pointing inward. [357] Then another, and another, building a path of markers that would guide him back if the weave between worlds shifted his sense of direction. [381] The driftwood was the only material the canyon offered, and he used it the way a shipwright uses what the shore provides. [403] He walked deeper. [406] The markers behind him disappeared into the avalanchingly quiet dark. [416] The air changed -- not temperature, but texture, as though he were pressing through fabric. [430] The canyon hush deepened until it was not the absence of sound but the presence of something listening. [448] He stopped where the canyon widened into a shallow amphitheater of stone. [460] The walls here were marked with patterns that were not erosion. [471] They were too deliberate, too rhythmic, like the weft threads of a loom. [484] Sorek knelt and uncased the flute from its leather wrapping. [494] His hands trembled. [497] He had never played it. [502] He did not know if it could be played by someone who had failed. [516] He lifted it to his lips and blew. [524] The note was thin and uncertain, then it deepened, and the canyon walls answered. [538] The sound did not echo -- it passed through the stone, and the stone passed it further, and the world on the other side of the weave received it. [566] Sorek felt the boundary flex. [571] He felt the fire in the vault, felt his own hands fumbling the lock, felt the moment of inattention that had let the flame reach the Codex's shelf. [599] He saw it now from the other side of the weave: a draft he had not noticed, a candle placed too near a curtain by someone else entirely. [627] He understood. [629] His failure had been real, but it had not been the failure he had condemned himself for. [646] The flute's note faded. [650] The canyon hush returned, but lighter now, as though something had been released from it. [665] Sorek gathered the flute back into its casing and turned to follow the driftwood markers home. [681] He could not unlose the Codex. [687] But he could stop losing himself to a story that was not true. [700]
