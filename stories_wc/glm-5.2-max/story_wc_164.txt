The sea-foam recollectionist hummed as she worked, her fingers leaving brief wet trails on the crystal walls of the geode. [20] The cave was iridescent, every surface catching light that had no visible source, throwing pale rainbows across her pale green skin. [41] She liked working here. [45] The acoustics were kind to her humming, and the glass walls kept the memories from leaking out too quickly. [64] She held up a shard of crater glass - formed when something from the sky hit the earth hard enough to fuse sand into clarity. [88] This piece had come from a field where two people had once argued bitterly, and the glass still held the heat of that anger, transliterated now into a faint amber glow against her palm. [122] She could read emotions through the glass the way others read words through spectacles. [136] Anger burned amber. [139] Grief pulsed a slow blue. [144] Joy sparked gold, though she encountered that one less often in her line of work. [159]

She was a recollectionist, and her job was to gather what was left behind - the emotional residue that clung to objects and places after the people who felt them had moved on or died. [193] Most of what she collected came via the scattered seeds dropped by a migrating bird that nested in the geode's upper chamber each season. [217] The bird ate seeds from across the continent, and those seeds absorbed the emotional weather of every place they'd grown. [237] When the bird passed them - dropped them, really, in its messy way across the cave floor - she would press each one against crater glass and transliterate what it held. [266]

Today she had fourteen seeds, and she was working through them with the pleasant efficiency of someone who had accepted that everything she touched was, in some way, already gone. [296] "Lovely," she murmured, as the third seed flared blue-green with a complicated tenderness that tasted like someone saying goodbye to a child at a train station. [322] "Lovely, lovely loss." [325] She recorded the transliteration in her ledger - not words exactly, but a notation system of curves and pressures that captured the shape of feeling. [349]

The cheerful gloom of her work suited her. [357] There was something genuinely delightful about recovering what had been nearly lost, even as each discovery reminded her that the original feeling was now an orphan, separated from the person who'd felt it. [390] Missing pieces were her specialty. [395] A memory without its emotion was a map without terrain. [405] An emotion without its memory was terrain without a map. [415] She could provide one half, but the other was often already fading. [427]

She worked quickly. [430] The seeds lost their charge within hours of being dropped, and the transliterations had to be captured before memory faded entirely. [451] There was always a deadline in this work, a slow dissolving that she could slow but never stop. [469]

The seventh seed made her pause. [475] It held something she couldn't immediately classify - a thin, silvery feeling like standing at a crossroads and watching one of the paths simply end. [499] Not fork. [501] Not branch. [503] End, as though the road had walked into fog and chosen not to come out. [518] She pressed it harder against the crater glass, and the glass transliterated it into a pale, flickering white she had only seen twice before. [542]

She set down her ledger and allowed herself to conjecture. [552] What if the paths didn't end? [558] What if they were being taken? [564] What if something - or someone - was collecting routes the way she collected emotions, pulling them out of the world and storing them somewhere else? [588]

The question was the reason she'd become a recollectionist in the first place. [601] Not just to save what was fading, but to discover why some paths disappear. [615] Roads that simply ceased. [619] Lives that stopped mid-sentence. [623] Relationships that dissolved without argument or announcement. [629] She had always suspected there was a pattern, and now this silver feeling in the glass suggested she might be right. [650]

She smiled, which was the expression she tended to wear when confronting the fundamental unknowability of existence. [667] "Well," she said to the geode, which hummed sympathetically. [676] "Another lovely mystery to lose." [681]

She picked up the eighth seed and kept working, because the bird would migrate again soon, and the next batch of emotional fragments would arrive whether she was ready or not, and because the paths that disappeared were worth wondering about even if she could not, in the end, follow them. [732]
