Dr. Lena Voss had always been luminous - not in the metaphorical sense, though her colleagues might argue that too - but in the way her skin caught light and held it, a phosphorescent shimmer she'd never been able to explain. [39] She was also a skeptic, which made first contact difficult. [49]

The signal had arrived three weeks ago: a repeating sequence embedded in radiation from a dead star, structured too precisely to be natural. [72] It said, in the language of chemistry and physics: "Come home." [83] That was the problem. [87] Lena didn't believe in home. [92]

She'd built her career on academic rebellion, publishing papers that dismantled the sacred cows of her field - the idea that communication across light-years would be deliberate, that intelligence would reach outward. [123] "All intelligence reaches inward," she'd written, and the academy had recoiled. [134] Now the signal seemed to prove her wrong, and she resented being right for the wrong reasons. [151]

The tower was her grandmother's, rainwashed and spring-cloaked, perched on a hill in the Marche region where the air tasted of wet stone. [174] Lena had come here to think, carrying only notebooks and a piece of fly amber her grandmother had pressed into her hand twenty years ago. [199] "Everything that leaves gets preserved in leaving," the old woman had said. [211] Lena hadn't understood then. [215] She still wasn't sure she did now. [222]

The amber sat on her desk, the fly inside frozen mid-flight, wings spread. [235] She studied it under lamplight while rain streaked the tower windows. [246] The signal's chemistry was strange - long-chain molecules arranged in patterns that mimicked neurotransmitter structures. [260] Dopamine. [261] Oxytocin. She needed to understand the chemistry of homecoming. [275] Not the poetry of it, not the sentiment - the actual biochemistry. [286] What made a body recognize a place as origin? [295] What made anything return? [299]

[316] Her grandmother had placed stone figures there decades ago - abstract shapes, now moss-softened, barely visible under wild grass. [382] She found more prints converging from different directions, all leading upward toward the tower. [396]

[592] It had come from something that had left home and preserved the memory of departure in chemistry, broadcasting it across light-years until it found minds that could decode it. [621]
