Deep beneath the mountains of a kingdom that no longer remembered its true name, the temple library of Oth-Vedros slumbered. [20] Dust covered every surface like grey snow, and the shelves stretched into darkness so complete that light itself seemed to hesitate before entering. [43] This was a place lost to time, abandoned when the old dynasty fell and a pretender donned the crown with practiced ease. [65]

In its lowest chamber, where the oldest texts crumbled into illegibility, a figure moved with deliberate patience. [82] Thessaly stood barely four feet tall, yet she carried herself with the quiet authority of someone who had once commanded armies. [103] She was the last of the Eon-Born, a diminutive titan whose small frame housed strength enough to reshape stone. [122] Her bloodline had built this library. [128] Her bloodline had ruled the kingdom above. [135] And her bloodline had been erased by a carefully constructed lie. [146]

She was regally unpretentious in her manner. [153] No crown adorned her head, no jewels weighted her fingers. [163] She wore simple grey robes, and her silver hair was cut short with the practicality of someone who had no patience for vanity. [186] The pretender who sat upon her family's throne believed the Eon-Born extinct. [198] Thessaly preferred it that way. [203] Extinction, she had learned, was merely another form of invisibility. [213] And invisibility was a weapon. [218]

For thirty years, she had worked through cryptic messages left in library books. [231] Not these books, mouldering in Oth-Vedros. [237] No. [238] She had spent decades scattering messages through every library in the kingdom above, slipping pages into volumes that scholars would eventually find, embedding clues in margins that only those who understood the old tongue could read. [274] Each message was written with iron gall ink pellets that she dissolved in water drawn from the temple's sacred well. [294] The ink aged slowly, darkening over decades, so that her oldest messages now read as though penned centuries ago. [313] They described a rune of drift, an ancient symbol that, when properly inscribed, would cause falsehoods to drift away like sediment disturbed in still water. [338]

The pretender's claim rested on a forged lineage scroll, a document proclaiming descent from the original royal house. [356] Thessaly's messages, scattered across hundreds of volumes, provided the counter-narrative. [366] Each fragment revealed one piece of the truth. [374] Together, they formed a map leading back to this library, to the genuine records buried beneath the mountains. [392]

Tonight, the constellations above the kingdom would pause in their eternal rotation. [404] Astronomers had predicted it decades in advance. [411] The pause of shifting constellations, a celestial stillness lasting exactly seven minutes, was the only window in which the rune of drift could be activated. [436] During that silence, the boundary between truth and deception grew thin enough to sunder. [450]

Thessaly knelt on the cold stone floor and placed her final iron gall ink pellet at the centre of the chamber. [471] She crushed it between her palms, mixing it with well water until it formed a dark, viscous paste. [489] With her finger, she began to trace the rune of drift upon the floor, each stroke precise, each curve loaded with meaning she had spent decades preserving. [516]

Above her, in libraries across the kingdom, her messages were being read. [528] Scholars had found them. [532] Antiquarians had puzzled over them. [537] And slowly, inevitably, the truth had spread. [544] The pretender's ministers had grown nervous. [550] Questions had been raised in the royal court. [558] The forged scroll had been subjected to scrutiny it could not withstand. [570]

As the constellations froze above, Thessaly completed the final line of the rune. [583] The ink glowed with a deep violet light, and the rune began to turn. [597] The air in the library shifted, and a sound like distant bells echoed through the corridors. [613] Above, in the throne room, the forged lineage scroll began to fade, its false ink drifting away from the parchment like smoke. [635]

The pretender stood and watched his legitimacy dissolve. [643] He did not run. [647] He did not shout. [651] He simply stood as the truth settled over the kingdom like morning light, and his reign ended not with violence but with the quiet grace of a structure that could no longer support its own weight. [687]

Thessaly remained in the library, kneeling before the fading rune. [697] She felt no triumph. [701] Only the bittersweet relief of watching something false finally fall, making room for what was real. [717]
