The shooting star tracking station perched on the edge of the known cosmos like a forgotten observatory, its domes cracked, its instruments ancient. [23] Through the hush of an overlooked era, when the universe paused between great events, Maren worked alone. [40] He was the cosmic janitor — not his title, but the truth of it. [53] He swept the observation decks, calibrated the tracking arrays, and logged each falling star that streaked across the station's field of vision. [75] Others had abandoned the station centuries ago, chasing grander discoveries elsewhere. [86] Maren stayed. [88]

He was spiritually curious by nature. [94] Where others saw data — trajectory, luminosity, decay rate — Maren saw stories. [105] Each falling star was a traveler, and he wanted to know where they had been before they burned. [123] His method was simple but patient: by tracing the path of falling stars backward through the tracking station's archived records, he mapped their origins. [147] Some came from dying nebulae. [152] Others from shattered moons. [156] A few from places the charts didn't name. [164]

One evening — if evenings existed in deep space, and Maren liked to pretend they did — a star fell differently. [183] Its trail lingered, golden and slow, unlike the sharp white streaks he usually recorded. [197] He followed its path through the old data, cross-referencing with the station's fading instruments, until he found something. [215] Among the debris field where the star had passed, caught in the station's collection net, was a shard of yellow lantern glass. [237] It was warm. [240] Not the warmth of residual heat, but something deeper, something that pulsed faintly against his palm when he picked it up. [261]

He turned it over in his hands, this fragment of something that had once held light. [277] He understood, suddenly, what it was to be a vessel for brightness and then to shatter — to scatter yourself across the dark. [299] Maren had always been drawn to broken things. [307] He had stayed at this station when everyone else left because he understood abandonment. [321] He understood what it meant to be overlooked, to be the one left behind in the quiet. [338]

He sat in the observation dome, the yellow lantern glass resting on the console, and allowed himself to feel it — the vulnerability of stars that fell, the vulnerability of a man who stayed. [371] He had spent years pretending his solitude was stoicism. [380] Now, holding the shard, he let the pretense fall away. [390]

To empathize was not weakness, he realized. [397] It was the opposite. [401] Each falling star he had traced had ended its journey not in darkness but in transformation — becoming dust, becoming glass, becoming part of something new. [426] Their vulnerability was not their end but their passage. [435]

The station was quiet. [439] The universe outside was vast and indifferent. [446] But here, in this overlooked era, in this forgotten place, something was growing. [459] Maren felt it — a flourishing stillness, not empty but full, the way a forest is full after snowfall. [477] Silence that breathes. [480] Stillness that lives. [483]

He placed the yellow lantern glass on the windowsill of the observation dome, where it caught the faint light of distant stars and cast small golden patterns on the walls. [513] It was a small thing, but it changed the room. [523] It changed him. [526]

He returned to his work. [531] The next falling star appeared on the tracker, and Maren logged its path, traced its origin, and recorded its story. [551] But now he did it differently — not as a janitor tidying data, but as a witness honoring a journey. [570]

He had come to the station to hide, perhaps. [579] To be small in a large universe. [586] But in the tracing of falling stars, in the holding of broken glass, in the admitting of his own fragility, he had found something unexpected. [611] Strength. [612] Not the kind that resists, but the kind that opens. [622] The kind that says: I am breakable, and I am still here. [634]

The stars kept falling. [638] Maren kept watching. [641] And through the hush of that overlooked era, the station — once abandoned, once silent — became a place where broken light was gathered, named, and remembered. [666]
