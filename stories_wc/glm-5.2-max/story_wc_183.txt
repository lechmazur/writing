Verity had come to call them the chimera twins, two children she discovered in the hull of the Meridian Queen, an abandoned carnival ship drifting through Silvermist Bay's endless fog. [30] The twins waited below deck with their mismatched eyes and blended skin, one bearing patches of deep umber across pale porcelain, the other wearing freckles like constellations across dark brown. [60] They were blissfully aberrant, Verity decided, for they knew nothing of the world's rigid categories and wanted none. [78]

She had found them three days ago, or perhaps three years. [89] Time aboard the Meridian Queen moved strangely, as memories alter and reshape themselves in the fog like reflections in warped carnival glass. [111] Verity remembered being a marine biologist, remembered laboratories and grant applications and the precise language of taxonomy. [128] But she also remembered a garden she had never visited, where names grew on trees like strange fruit, waiting to be plucked by those who had lost them. [156]

The emerald swatch card sat in her coat pocket, its surface worn smooth from anxious fingers. [172] She had carried it since the mainland, a sample card from a paint manufacturer that had somehow become her compass. [192] When she held it near certain walls of the ship, matching green paint would glow faintly, marking the path forward. [212] The twins had told her this, speaking in unison, their voices threading together like braided rope. [228] "The garden remembers what the world forgets," they said. [237] "Follow the green." [240]

Verity descended into the ship's former funhouse, where carnival mirrors still lined the crooked corridors. [255] Her reflection multiplied and distorted, showing versions of herself she barely recognized: a girl, an old woman, someone with scales along her jaw. [278] The emerald swatch card caught phantom light here, and ahead, a door painted the exact green of her sample pulsed once, twice. [300]

She pushed through into a corridor that should not have existed aboard any ship. [314] The walls were lined with photographs, hundreds of them, each showing a different face. [328] Names were written beneath in handwriting that shifted when observed directly. [339] This was the narrative, she realized, the threads of countless lives woven into the vessel's very bones. [356] The Meridian Queen had been a carnival ship, yes, but also something older: a repository for the forgotten. [374]

The chimera twins appeared beside her, materializing from shadows the way they always did. [388] Their four hands reached for her sleeves, guiding her deeper. [398] "You want to recapture what was taken from you," they said together. [410] Not a question. [413]

Verity's throat tightened. [416] She had come aboard the Meridian Queen seeking salvage, old equipment perhaps, anything the university could repurpose. [433] But the fog had rolled in, the ship had drifted from its mooring, and she had found the twins instead. [453] Or they had found her. [458] Now she understood the search was not accidental. [466] The garden of forgotten names was not a place but a process, and the twins were its keepers. [484]

They led her to a chamber that had once been the carousel's mechanical heart. [498] Wooden horses lay dismembered across the floor, their painted eyes still watching. [510] At the center stood a tree, impossible and real, growing from the metal deck, its leaves the same green as her swatch card. [533] Tags hung from its branches, thousands of them, each bearing a single name. [546]

"Take yours back," the twins whispered. [552]

Verity reached up. [555] Her fingers brushed the tags, and memories flooded through, not just her own but everyone's who had ever stood in this place. [577] She understood then why the ship drifted without anchor: anchors required knowing where you belonged. [592] This was trapped freedom, she thought, the liberty of the unmoored, adrift but not lost. [607]

She found her name on a tag the color of deep emerald. [619] When she pulled it free, the word dissolved into her palm, and for the first time since boarding, she felt something shift inside her. [643] Not completion. [645] There was a difference between completion and what she felt. [655] The solace was unkindled still, a warmth she could sense but not yet hold, like firelight seen through frosted glass. [675] But the twins smiled at her, their mismatched faces radiant in the tree's glow, and she knew the garden would continue growing as long as someone remembered to seek it. [705]

The fog outside pressed closer against the hull. [713] The ship drifted on through Silvermist Bay, going nowhere and everywhere at once. [726] And Verity stayed, learning the names of the lost one by one, weaving their stories into the endless thread that kept the Meridian Queen afloat. [751] Behind her, the twins hummed a melody she almost recognized, and the emerald swatch card in her pocket grew warm. [771]
