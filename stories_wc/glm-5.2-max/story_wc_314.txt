[75] Not that anyone knew that. [80] The auburn-haired alchemist had a reputation for precision, for cold logic, for results. [93] She kept the seeds in a cedar box beneath her workbench, and sometimes, late at night, she held them and remembered. [114] Tomorrow she would attempt the final transformation - the one that would turn the dew crystal into a Stone of Seeing. [134] But tonight, she needed something logic alone could not provide. [206] Vael watched them turn, all at once, as though a single thought passed through the flock. [222] The patterns they made were never random - she had charted them for years, marking each formation in her journals alongside her alchemical notes. [245] Tonight, the birds formed a shape she had seen only once before: a spiral tightening inward, then opening outward, then spiraling again. [267]

She reached for the frozen dew crystal and held it toward the sky. [280] The crystal caught the last light, and inside its structure she saw - or imagined she saw - the same spiral. [299] The same rhythm. [302] Logic told her it was coincidence, refraction, the mind finding patterns where none existed. [316] Intuition told her otherwise. [320]

[384] She thought of her mother's hands, pressing seeds into soil. [394] She thought of the first time she had watched birds move as one body and wept without understanding why. [413]

[452] Vael's breath caught. [455] She had planned every step of the transformation: the exact temperature, the precise sequence of elements, the timing calibrated to the second. [622] But tonight, she let herself feel what she had spent years pretending she did not: that the world was alive, that patterns spoke, that her mother's garden still grew in every seed she had planted. [657]

The auburn-haired alchemist, whom everyone knew as precise and unfeeling, smiled. [668] In the hidden orchard, behind the kaleidoscopic waterfall, the ancient trees bore their strange fruit, and the air smelled of lavender and sage and something older than memory. [696]

The crystal pulsed once, warm now, and waiting. [704]
