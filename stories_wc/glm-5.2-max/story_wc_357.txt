The orchard planet of Verelis wore its rock arches like a crown - hundreds of them, floating in the violet sky, defying explanation. [22] Sera had flown between them for twelve seasons, an intrepid orchard pilot tending the fruit trees that clung to the arches' undersides. [44] She knew their roots drank from mist, their branches reached for nothing, and still they bore fruit. [61] That resilience had kept the colony alive. [68] But the trees were dying now, one arch at a time, and nobody knew why. [83]

Sera was determined to discover the source of their resilience before it was gone. [97] She had one hour. [101] When the gravity inversion came - that strange monthly hour when down became up and the planet's water rose in silver columns - she would follow the flow. [127] Navigating by water flow was the only way to reach the Heart Arch, the largest formation, where the oldest trees still lived. [149] The water would lift her skiff through the arches like a hand cupping a leaf. [164]

The inversion began without warning. [169] The silence came first - that whirling silence that always preceded the shift, as if the world held its breath and spun in place. [192] Then the water rose. [196] Creeks lifted from their beds, wells overflowed upward, and Sera's skiff rose with them, her tether lines going slack as she drifted skyward. [219] She steered by the water's path, leaning into currents she couldn't see but could feel through the hull. [237] The flow carved invisible channels between the arches, and she followed them deeper into the formation than she'd ever been. [257] Trees hung above her now, their roots trailing like hair, their leaves dark against the sky. [273] Some still bore fruit. [277] Others were skeletal. [280]

Halfway to the Heart Arch, she saw the gathering. [289] A gathering of echoes - that was the only way to describe it. [301] Sound didn't behave during inversion; it pooled and swirled, collecting in pockets between the arches. [316] But here, the echoes were dense, layered with voices from every direction. [328] Not random. [330] Not chaotic. [332] They converged on a single point: a cleft in the rock where water pooled and something glinted. [349]

Sera brought the skiff close and reached into the cleft. [359] Her fingers closed around a broken arrow tip, ancient bronze-green, its edge still sharp. [373] It was warm. [376] The water around it was thick with something - not silt, not mineral. [388] She recognized the smell: the green scent of new growth. [398] The arrow tip accelerated plant growth. [404] She could see it now - the cleft was choked with root fibers, all reaching toward the fragment, all growing impossibly fast, threading through the rock like veins. [431] This was what fed the orchards. [437] The trees on the Heart Arch weren't resilient by nature; they were sustained by this fragment, their roots stretched across kilometers of stone to reach it. [463]

But the fragment was broken. [468] Cracked down the center. [472] The growth it produced was frantic, desperate, the kind of growth that preceded collapse. [486] The dying arches weren't failing because the climate had changed. [496] They were failing because the source itself was failing. [505]

Sera held the arrow tip and understood. [512] Resilience wasn't a gift; it was a borrowed thing, sustained by something older than the colony, something no one had thought to look for until it began to break. [541] The voices in the gathering of echoes seemed to agree - or perhaps they were just the memory of everyone who'd ever flown these arches, compressed into sound. [568]

The hour was ending. [572] Gravity shuddered, preparing to reassert itself. [578] The water would fall soon, and her skiff with it. [588] She pocketed the arrow tip and gripped the tiller. [597] She couldn't fix what was broken. [603] But she could bring it back, show the colony what had been holding them up all along, and maybe - maybe - find a way to mend what had been cracked before the last arch went silent. [638] The whirling silence returned as gravity reasserted itself. [646] Water crashed downward in silver sheets. [652] Sera fell with it, steering through the chaos, the broken arrow tip warm against her chest like a second heartbeat, like something that refused to stop. [678]
