The butterflies came through the valley like a breath held too long, their orange wings catching the last amber light that filtered through the ancient greenhouse glass. [27] Elise worked beneath that glass, her chisel tracing the surname Marenholtz into slate the color of a thunderhead. [18] [45] She had been a gravestone carver for nineteen years, her work fiercely meditative, and in that time she had lettered thousands of names into stone. [25] [70] But something still eluded her. [5] [75] She could carve individual sorrow with precision, could feel its weight in every hammer strike, yet the shape of collective grief, the way it accumulated across a town, a season, a generation, remained just beyond her comprehension. [21] [143] Vael had been the one to teach her that stone remembers what flesh forgets.
