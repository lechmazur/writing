The Verdant Labyrinth closed its gates at nine, but Madam Solene knew the ghost would already be tuning his voice in the orchard garden at the maze's heart. [28] She locked the ticket booth and walked the spiral path through hedges that hummed with late-summer heat. [45] She had maintained the garden for thirty years. [54] She had listened to the ghost for thirty years. [64] Tonight felt different, and the difference tasted like copper and violet light. [76] That was her blessing, or her curse. [84] She heard colors as flavors, saw sounds as weather. [93] When the ghost began to sing, she tasted oranges and saw gold leaves spiraling through a sky that should have been dark. [115] His name was Emil Darroch. [120] He had performed in the theater that once stood here before fire took it in 1923. [136] He had been a singer of some renown, and he died in the blaze after going back for a child who had already escaped through another door. [163] He did not know the child had survived. [171] He had spent a century seeking his encore, believing his final act had been failure. [186] She found him at the garden's center, where apple trees grew in rows between broken flagstones. [202] He was turning a coin between his fingers, a hand-painted tarot coin he had carried in life. [219] The World was painted on one face, the Tower on the other. [231] He flipped it, caught it, and each flip produced an echo, a sound that rippled outward and returned altered. [250] "Tonight," he said, not looking at her. [257] His voice was the rustle of old curtains and tasted faintly of rosewater. [270] "Tonight I feel it might work." [276] Solene sat on the stone bench beneath the oldest tree. [286] "What will work?" [289] He flipped the coin again. [294] The echo came back layered, and within it she heard a child laughing, then a crowd applauding, then a woman weeping. [315] The sounds did not belong to the same moment, and she understood that his existence was a fracture, a splintering of instants that refused to resolve into sequence. [343] "I have been trying to reach the night of the fire," he said. [356] "Not to change it. [360] To hear what came after. [365] To know if she lived." [370] "The child." [372] "Her name was Margaret." [376] He closed his translucent hand around the coin. [384] "Every night I produce the echoes of this place, every performance I have given, every voice that has sung here." [404] "I intertwine them, layer them, build a bridge across the break in my time." [418] "But I have never crossed." [423] Each night without answers thinned him, and he feared what would remain when the last echo failed. [441] Solene watched him. [444] She had watched him for thirty years and come to love him without ever being able to say so. [463] He was a ghost. [467] He was a voice. [471] He was the taste of longing itself, bitter-green and luminous. [481] "Let me help," she said. [486] "You cannot hear what I hear." [492] "I hear more than you think." [498] She reached out and touched the coin in his palm. [508] It was cold and warm at once, and touching it she saw his timeline like a shattered mirror, each shard holding a different reflection of the same night. [536] She saw the stage. [540] She saw the fire. [544] She saw the child running out a side door into the rain. [556] She saw Margaret grown old, Margaret with grandchildren, Margaret laughing in a garden much like this one. [573] "She lived," Solene whispered. [577] From the brink where despair had kept him, possibility rose like a note held past silence. [593] Emil's form brightened. [596] The orchard brightened. [599] The echoes he had produced for a century converged, and the fractured timeline mended along a single seam, the one thread always there but never found alone. [626] He sang. [628] He sang the encore he had never given, and Solene tasted every color of the life he had saved and every note of the one he was releasing. [656] The sound intertwined past and present, and the garden held it like cupped hands hold water. [672] When the last echo faded, the bench was empty. [681] The coin lay on the warm stone, the World face up. [692] Solene picked it up and held it to her chest, and the night tasted of honey and gold. [710]
