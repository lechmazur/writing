Queen Mirael had stopped believing in her own reign the night the dreams began. [14] Each evening brought the same vision: starlight bleeding from the heavens in pale, viscous threads, pooling at the feet of a throne she no longer recognized as hers. [42] The court astronomers called it nonsense. [48] The physicians called it exhaustion. [53] But after the final warning, a sealed letter left on her pillow bearing only the words "Climb or be climbed upon," she understood that neither diagnosis was correct. [81]

She left the palace without escort, carrying only a lantern and the cold weight of disenchantment that had settled into her bones over thirteen years of ceremonial power. [109] The ember grotto on the ridge was not marked on any royal map. [122] She had learned of it from the dreams themselves, where the bleeding starlight always drained toward a faint orange pulse on the horizon, high above the cloud line. [150]

The climb took her through the night. [157] Stone steps, ancient and uneven, carved into the mountainside by hands that predated her dynasty. [172] Her breath came thin as the air grew colder, the clouds falling away below her like a white sea retreating from shore. [194] She was a queen who commanded armies with a whisper, whose softly commanding presence could quiet a war council, yet here she was simply a woman ascending toward something she could not name. [227]

The cobalt grotto opened before her like a wound in the rock. [239] Blue light, sourceless and deep, saturated the walls. [247] The air tasted of ozone and something older, something that predated language. [259] Stalactites hung in frozen cascades, their tips glowing with that same faint orange she had seen in her dreams. [278] She set down her lantern. [283] It was useless here. [287]

The grotto was not empty. [292] A figure stood at the far wall, veiled in something that moved like smoke but held the texture of woven glass. [313] It turned, and Mirael felt the dreams surge through her waking mind: starlight bleeding, pooling, whispering. [329]

"You received the warning," the figure said. [336] Its voice was neither male nor female, neither young nor old. [347] "You came anyway." [350]

"I came because the warning was also an invitation," Mirael said. [361] Her voice held steady, softly commanding even now, even here. [371] "The dreams are yours." [375]

"Ours," the figure corrected. [379] "They have always been ours." [384] "You simply stopped hearing your half of the conversation." [393]

The figure extended a hand, and the grotto responded. [402] The orange glow in the stalactites intensified. [409] Above them, through a fissure in the rock, the sky was visible: dark, vast, and wrong. [425] The stars were not fixed. [430] They were drifting, trailing light like ink in water, bleeding starlight across the black. [444]

Mirael watched, and the dream merged fully with the waking world. [455] She understood now. [458] The bleeding was not decay. [463] It was communication. [466] The stars were speaking, had always been speaking, and the grotto was an ear pressed to the mouth of the cosmos. [487]

"Every queen before you stood in this spot," the figure said. [498] "Every queen before you chose to listen or to flee." [508] "Those who fled ruled well and died forgotten." [516] "Those who listened ruled strangely and died remembered." [524]

"Those are not appealing choices," Mirael said. [531]

"They were never meant to appeal." [537]

The starlight bled thicker now, pooling on the grotto floor in luminescent threads that crept toward her boots. [555] Wherever the light touched, the stone grew warm. [563] She could feel the dreams intensifying: not just visions now, but voices, histories, futures, all braided together in pale celestial fire. [584]

The figure stepped beside her. [589] Together, they watched the fissure above. [595] "Summer begins tonight," the figure said. [601]

Mirael understood. [603] She drew a breath, and the figure did the same, and through that shared exhale, she spotted the first fireflies of summer rising from the bleeding starlight at her feet. [633] They rose in trembling pairs, each one a fragment of the conversation between the queen and the sky. [651] They drifted upward through the fissure, carrying messages she had spent thirteen years too disenchanted to hear. [668]

The lacy menace of it settled over her like a veil. [679] This was not a gift. [684] It was a chain. [688] To listen was to be bound, to rule strangely was to never rule alone again. [703] The stars would whisper their secrets, and she would carry them, and her reign would become something she could not control but could only translate. [728]

She thought of her throne, how hollow it had become. [738]

"I will listen," she said. [743]

The figure bowed, or dissolved, or both. [750]

Mirael stood alone in the cobalt grotto above the clouds, fireflies ascending around her like a crown, starlight bleeding at her feet, knowledge settling where disenchantment had once lived. [779] She began the long climb down. [785]
