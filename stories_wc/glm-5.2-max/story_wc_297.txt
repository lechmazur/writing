The cobalt glade deepened into dusk as Sera pressed her bare feet into the soil, feeling the old roots pulse beneath her skin like a second heartbeat. [27] She was root-bound, had always been—her grandmother said the earth had claimed her the moment she was born facedown in the garden, umbilical cord tangled in witchgrass. [55] But what made her a visionary was not the binding. [65] It was what the roots showed her: threads of things yet to come, curling through the dirt like pale worms. [85]

The future had arrived early. [90] Three days ago, the silver birches at the glade's edge had begun weeping sap that glowed faintly, a phenomenon the old almanacs said wouldn't occur for another generation. [118] Sera had felt it first—a tremor in the root network, a premature flowering of something that should not yet bloom. [139] And with it came the dreams. [145] Not her dreams. [148] Everyone's. [149] She could taste them when she pressed her feet down: the merchant's dream of a dead child laughing, the smith's dream of a city burning slowly, the priest's dream of a god with no mouth. [184] They braided together somewhere beneath the glade, and Sera needed to find that intersection. [198] Not to stop it. [202] To understand it. [205]

In her hand, she held a maze topiary clipping—a single stem of yew, still green, shaped by careful pruning into a tiny corridor with walls no taller than her thumb. [236] Her grandmother had pressed it into her palm the night she died, whispering that it was a key. [254] Not to a door. [258] To a pattern. [261] "The maze is not the puzzle," she had said. [270] "The maze is the answer. [275] You just have to find where it stops being a maze." [286]

Sera was purposefully curious. [290] She had not stumbled into this. [296] She had chosen it, the way one chooses to step off a cliff believing the air will hold. [314] She knelt in the glade and lifted her shirt. [323] The scars on her ribs—three long furrows from the accident that had nearly killed her at fourteen—had been tingling since the future arrived early. [349] Her grandmother had taught her this: that wounds are not closures. [360] They are openings. [363] Linking through scars meant pressing the topiary clipping against the oldest wound and letting the roots do what roots do—find the cracks, enter, spread. [388]

She did it now. [392] The yew stem touched her scarred skin, and the glade tilted. [403] She felt them. [406] The dreams. [408] Not as images but as pressure, as weight, as the sensation of being underground and knowing the surface is just above. [429] Each dream bore the scar of its dreamer—a grief, a longing, a terror—and through her own scars, Sera could feel where they connected. [454] They did not merge. [458] They intersected, like roots from different trees meeting in the dark and growing around each other, each maintaining its own shape but changed by the contact. [484]

The intersection was here. [488] Under the glade. [491] Under her. [493]

Her heart slammed against her chest. [499] She was laughing and she was terrified. [506] The covenant came back to her then—the old promise her grandmother had spoken of, the one made between the root-bound and the dreaming earth, that when the future came early, a visionary would stand at the crossing and heed what the dreams revealed. [550] The covenant had been eclipsed for a century, overshadowed by louder magics, brighter faiths. [564] No one had kept it. [569] No one had even remembered it. [575] But the earth remembered. [579] The roots remembered. [582] And now, in the cobalt dark of the glade, with the future pressing up through the soil like fever, the covenant was unwritten no longer. [607]

She heeded. [609] She listened with her scars, with the clipping that was a maze and a map and a memory, with her root-bound feet that had never once left the earth. [638] The dreams told her what they were: not prophecies, not warnings. [649] They were the world rehearsing its next shape, trying on futures the way a body tries on pain—tentatively, then all at once. [672]

And Sera, shaking with anxious elation, understood that the intersection of dreams was not a place. [688] It was a willingness to remain. [694] She stayed in the glade until morning, feet in the soil, scars open and listening, the maze clipping warm in her palm. [716] The future had come early, but she was ready. [725] She had always been ready. [730] The roots had told her so. [736]
