On the baroque asteroid of Verdaccio, where ornate facades had been carved into raw nickel-iron by colonists with more ambition than survival instinct, Maren Solis turned the crystal vase shard in her fingers. [33] She had been a revolutionary once, before the Visible Time. [43] When temporality unfurled like a curtain drawn back, when humanity could suddenly see the layered currents of past and future coiling through the air like luminescent smoke, her cause became irrelevant overnight. [75] Who arms a rebellion when every outcome already shimmers overhead in gilded threads? [88]

Now she was an archaeologist, or at least a disputant in archaeology. [100] The asteroid had yielded a subterranean chamber predating the colonists by millennia, containing artifacts of a civilization that had flourished and vanished before humanity reached the stars. [127] The shard was one such artifact, its faceted surface catching visible time-threads and refracting them into prismatic splinters. [145] The archaeological dispute had split the research community. [153] Dr. Harvelle's faction argued the chamber was a royal tomb, the artifacts evidence of hierarchy and ceremonial burial. [171] Maren disagreed. [173] She saw something else in the arrangement of the vase fragments, in the chamber's geometry, in the deliberate placement of each broken piece. [196] Not entombment but assembly, not death but transformation. [204]

She was poetically inclined, which Harvelle considered a professional failing. [214] But poetry was pattern recognition at its most refined, and Maren saw patterns where others saw debris. [231]

The problem was proof. [235] Visible Time allowed observation of the recent past, but the truly ancient remained murky, its threads tangled and faint. [254] And temporal storms swept the asteroid belt with increasing frequency, churning the visible time into chaos. [270] During storms, mirrors had to be covered. [277] The old superstition had become practical necessity, for uncovered mirrors amplified the temporal distortions, trapping people in overlapping moments, drowning them in their own histories. [302]

Maren needed to devise a method to see through the turbulence, or rather around it. [317] She held the shard to lamplight and understood. [325] The crystal did not merely reflect visible time; it structured it. [336] The fractures created a lensing effect, sorting chaotic temporal currents into legible layers. [349] If she could reconstruct enough of the vase, she might build an instrument capable of peering into the deep past. [369]

Her motivation was not purely academic. [375] She had spent her revolutionary years dreaming of remaking civilization from the ground up, and that dream had not died but merely changed scale. [399] If she could prove the ancient civilization had been egalitarian and communal, that their ceremony was not hierarchy but collective transformation, she could offer humanity a template for its own possible future. [431] Not a tomb but a mirror, held up to what they might still become. [445]

The storms arrived before she was ready. [452] She felt the air thicken, the time-threads beginning to writhe and lash. [464] Maren moved through her study methodically, draping dark cloth over every reflective surface. [477] The mirrors had to be covered. [483] She wrapped the shard carefully and placed it in a lead-lined box where visible time could not penetrate. [501]

In the darkness between storms, she worked. [508] She devised her reconstruction from the shard's internal logic, for the angles of its breaks told her where neighboring pieces must sit, the way a line of poetry implies its rhyme. [539] She contacted other dig sites, traded for matching fragments. [548] Slowly, the vase took shape. [553]

The night she completed it, visible time parted like a veil. [564] Through the reconstructed crystal, the deep past became legible at last. [575] What Maren saw made her weep with burnished sorrow, the grief of recognition itself. [589] The ancient civilization had been everything she had imagined: communal, transformative, beautiful. [601] And it had fallen not to conquest or plague but to the quiet erosion of collective memory, the slow forgetting of who they were, piece by piece, until nothing remained but shards in a chamber on a baroque asteroid. [640]

Maren wrote her findings through the night, her prose carrying the cadence of elegy. [654] The dispute would continue and Harvelle would object, but the vase remembered what the civilization had forgotten. [671] Now Maren remembered too. [675] She would not let the world forget again. [683]
