Selia knelt beside the youngest roses, pressing her palms into the warm sand at their roots. [16] Three weeks since the tower fell, and still the sky carried the smell of burnt metal. [32] Her father would have called her a fool for this — a bounty hunter's daughter, growing flowers in the wreckage of a collapsed age. [55] But Rask had died doing what he knew, blade in hand, and she had buried him with empty hands and a promise she could not yet name. [82]

The glass dome above her hummed with afternoon thermals. [91] Outside, the dunes shifted in winds that had grown wilder since the tower's governance shattered across the desert. [109] Inside, her desert roses breathed in their rows — she had raised them to grow slowly, to extend roots deep before reaching for light, to hold water rather than spend it. [139] She spoke to them as her father had spoken to her, not with commands but with certainty: that patience was not weakness, that stillness was not surrender. [166] She inculcated them with values he had never lived long enough to learn. [179]

Selia was senselessly perceptive — her father had said so, half mocking, half afraid. [192] She could feel the dome's tension in her teeth before a panel cracked. [205] She knew a rose was dying two days before its petals curled. [217] And now, sitting in the engraved quiet of her dome — a silence she had carved deliberately into the chaos, etched into every surface and ritual — she felt the airship before she saw it. [250]

A shadow crossed the glass. [255] Then a sound like tearing cloth, and something struck the sand outside with a heavy thud. [271] She found it near the dome's eastern seal: a scrap of canvas, coarse and sand-stained, torn from an airship's ballast sandbag. [292] The ships that crossed the desert now carried mercenaries, salvagers, desperate men who had once served the tower and now served only themselves. [315] They dropped ballast to gain altitude when fired upon. [324] This scrap had fallen from a ship in trouble. [333]

She carried it back inside and held it over the youngest rose bed. [346] The fabric smelled of altitude and fear. [353] Her father's instinct would have been to track the ship, read its trajectory, find whoever had fired and sell the information. [374] That was the world he had moved through — a world of leverage and blood. [388]

Instead, she cut the scrap into narrow strips with her pruning blade, then sheathed the knife and knelt again. [407] She wrapped each strip loosely around the base of three young stems — not to constrain them but to shield them from the blowing sand that crept through the dome's imperfections. [437] The canvas would weather, soften, and eventually feed the soil. [447] Something dropped in violence would become a layer of protection. [457]

This was how she wove continuity through chaos. [465] Not by fighting it. [469] Not by fleeing it. [473] By folding it into the work. [479]

The quiet she had engraved here was not absence. [488] It was a shaped thing, deliberate as the channels she dug to guide water to root systems. [505] It held heat without burning. [510] It held grief without breaking. [515] The dome above her glowed amber as the sun lowered, and the light that reached her through the glass was a tranquil blaze — warm, steady, and utterly without rage. [544]

She touched the nearest rose's petals. [550] They were hot from the day's last light, translucent at the edges, and they did not flinch. [567]

Outside, the dunes continued their slow, senseless rearrangement. [575] Ships crossed the sky in patterns she could not predict. [585] Somewhere, someone was loading a weapon or settling a debt the way her father would have. [601] But here, in the measured air beneath the dome, the roses grew deeper. [614] The sandbag scrap would fray and vanish into the soil. [624] The quiet would hold. [628] And Selia, senselessly perceptive, tranquil in the blaze of her own making, would remain — not because the world had become safe, but because she had learned to make stillness into a shelter that nothing storming past it could touch.
