The bunker had been swallowing itself for decades, hidden in a rewilded forest, its concrete walls fissured by roots that entered as threads and thickened into arteries. [27] Vines mapped every surface in green script nobody alive could read anymore. [88]

She was, by training and survival, an interpreter. [96] Not of languages, but of dreams. [102] The bunker's former purpose, a monitoring station for something she'd never been told, had left behind a single decaying directive, its wafer seal cracked and flaking like burnt skin. [131] The wax disc bore an impression she'd memorized: a serpent consuming its own tail. [145] The directive itself had faded to near-nothing, words dissolving into the paper like sugar into rain. [161] What remained legible read only three phrases. [168] Hold the line. [171] Interpret. [172] The path chooses. [175]

That final word had driven her here, to the edge of comprehension, where foresight and hindsight merged into one blind vision. [196] Mira could only see what had already happened, events and choices already lived, but her mind framed each recollection as prophecy. [217] She called it foresight, knowing it was hindsight, because in isolation the distinction no longer mattered. [233] The past was the only future she had. [241]

Tonight, the storm approached. [245] She felt it in the vines. [251] They tightened before rain, bracing like living things that remembered drought. [262] The barometer confirmed what the forest already knew. [270] She sealed the bunker's vents, checked her water supply, and prepared for the work storms always enabled: the deep dreams. [290]

[309] During storms, her hindsight-foresight sharpened until it became something transcendent. [319] She could transcend her limits, but only through interpreted dreams, only when the atmosphere itself seemed to thin between what was and what might have been. [345]
