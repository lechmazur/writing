The lagoon had spun for centuries, a vast carousel of luminous water suspended above the clouds, and Sera had scavenged its edges for most of them. [26] Now the great wheel had stopped. [32] The kinetic lagoon hung still and fractured, its energy spent, and the silence that followed was not peace but something cracked, a fractured stillness that made every sound feel like it belonged to another era. [67] She waded through the shallows, chronologically confused about how long she had been here. [81] The lagoon's rotation had always kept time, its rhythmic pulse a clock for those who lived along its shores. [100] Without it, moments stacked unpredictably. [105] She remembered arriving yesterday, or perhaps decades ago. [113] She remembered the ambition that had brought her, to map every current and name every eddy, but that ambition had eroded to something smaller and more desperate. [140] In her hand she turned the disposable camera wheel she had found in the silt. [155] It was a simple thing, the advance mechanism from a long-dead camera, its plastic teeth still clicking forward with satisfying precision. [176] She used it as a counting device, one click for each fragment of story she overheard echoing across the still water. [197] The lagoon had absorbed voices for centuries, lovers' quarrels and merchants' bargains and children's games, and now with the motion ceased those voices surfaced like bubbles from deep water. [226] Seven clicks. [228] A woman begging someone to stay. [234] Twelve clicks. [236] A man laughing at his own joke, alone. [244] Sera collected these fragments obsessively, fitting them together like shards of a broken vessel. [258] She told herself she was preserving the lagoon's history, but the truth was simpler and uglier. [274] She was avoiding the one conversation she needed to have. [284] Her ghost waited at the lagoon's center, where the water was clearest. [296] Not a spectral apparition but a version of herself frozen in the last rotation, the Sera who had arrived full of purpose, who had believed the lagoon could be understood and charted and owned. [330] That Sera had been still moving, still spinning with ambition, while the Sera who stood here now had stopped. [349] She clicked the camera wheel. [354] Twenty-three fragments. [356] A child asking why the water moves. [363] An old woman answering that it does not, that we do. [374] The regret came suddenly, as it always did, a physical ache in her chest. [388] She had spent so long collecting other people's stories that she had let her own erode to nothing. [406] The maps she had planned to draw existed only as faint intentions. [418] The names she had meant to give the currents remained unspoken. [429] She had been a resolute scavenger, determined and focused, but she had scavenged everything except what she actually needed. [448] She waded deeper. [451] The water was warm and perfectly still, its fractured surface reflecting a sky that seemed also to have stopped. [470] Her ghost grew clearer, a silhouette of intention, a figure still rotating slightly, caught in the lagoon's last momentum. [489] The ghost's hands were open, palms upward, a gesture that could mean welcome or surrender. [504] Sera could not tell which, and the ambiguity was the cruelest part. [516] "I need to negotiate," Sera said aloud, and her voice joined the fragments echoing across the water. [533] The ghost did not speak, but Sera understood its terms. [543] She could have the ambition back, the drive, the relentless forward motion, but only if she surrendered the stillness she had found. [565] Only if she agreed to spin again without knowing when she would stop. [578] She looked at the disposable camera wheel in her hand. [588] She had clicked it thirty-one times. [594] Thirty-one fragments of other lives, other voices, other ambitions that had also eroded into the lagoon's memory. [611] Sera made her choice. [615] She turned the wheel one final time and let it fall into the water, where it sank slowly, another fragment joining the collection. [638] Then she stepped forward to meet her ghost, and the negotiation began in earnest, two versions of the same eroded soul bargaining across the fractured stillness of a lagoon that would never spin again.
