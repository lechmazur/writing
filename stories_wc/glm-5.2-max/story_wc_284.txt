The mist on Zephyr Bluff carried a silver hush as Maren approached from the old logging road. [17] She was a burned spy -- six months exposed and cut loose, her cover blown in Prague, every contact dead or denied -- and she moved with the careful silence of someone who no longer belonged anywhere. [52] She stopped at the bluff's edge where wind pushed through the mist like a gentle zephyr, bending the last remnants of winter into whispered instructions. [77] It was the tilt of the earth toward spring, and snow still accumulated in the lee of the granite outcroppings, stubborn and white against darkening stone. [103]

She carried only one thing of value: an engraved pillbox, silver and worn smooth by years in a pocket. [122] Her handler, Tomas, had given it to her on their last meeting in Vienna with the words "When you're ready." [142] The engraving was a series of tiny marks along the rim that, months ago, a former analyst in Bratislava had finally confirmed were coordinates. [166]

Maren was not reckless -- she was prudently daring. [174] She had survived twenty years of intelligence work by weighing risks carefully before leaping, and she had leaped often enough. [194] But being burned had stripped the scaffolding from her life. [204] She was left with nothing but time and the question that had driven her three hundred kilometers to this bluff in the mist: where did unused courage wait? [232]

She scanned the snow's surface. [237] There -- a small indentation, then another, a pattern that looked natural unless you had spent years reading the language Tomas had taught her. [260] Codes in accumulated snow had been their method during the coldest years of her work, the system they had never documented, never mentioned, never trusted to paper. [287] Tiny holes pressed with a walking stick's tip, melt-darkened edges, specific spacing. [299] She knelt, breath steady, and began to translate. [307]

The message was simple: the key was inside the pillbox, and a small metal building at the base of the bluff was where Tomas had gone. [333] She pried open the pillbox. [338] Inside, cushioned on a square of felt, was a brass key and a folded scrap of paper. [355] She did not read the paper immediately. [362] She sat in the silver hush and held the key in her palm, feeling its weight. [378]

Being burned had broken something inside her -- not courage exactly, but the framework courage had depended on. [395] She had operated within the agency's structure for so long that her bravery had been borrowed, reinforced by procedure and backup. [416] Without that structure, she had felt her nerve drain away, slowly, over six months of hiding in borrowed rooms. [435] She had told herself she was being careful, but the truth was simpler: she was afraid. [451]

But she had come here. [456] She had made the trip on her own, decoding and tracking and trusting nothing but the old method of snow codes and the instinct that told her Tomas would not have abandoned her without a trail. [492] That was a kind of fixing what was broken -- not a repair but a refusal to let the break be final. [513] She was not the same agent she had been before Prague, and she did not need to be. [532] She needed to be something else, something the agency had never required of her: brave without a net. [551]

The mist shifted. [554] Through the pale silver light, she could make out a structure at the base of the bluff -- small, metal-sided, half-buried in snow. [576] She stood and began picking her way down the slope, cautious on icy stone, the key cold in her fist. [596] She was prudently daring, and she was moving. [604]

At the door of the small building, she paused. [613] This was where Tomas had vouchsafed the final piece -- not merely information but trust handed freely, a secret given without obligation. [634] He could have disappeared cleanly, but instead he had left a trail for her because he believed she could follow it. [655] That belief was the real gift. [661]

The key fit. [664] The door opened to warmth, lamplight, and the face of a man she had believed she would never see again. [684]

"Hello, Maren," Tomas said, and the silver hush of the bluff seemed to fold itself around the words. [702]

She stepped inside. [705] The door closed behind her, and somewhere in her chest, unused courage woke and waited no longer. [722] She had found what she came for. [729] It had been waiting, she realized, not in some hidden place but in the act of coming, in the refusal to stay broken. [752] With morning the mist would clear and the snow would melt, and the codes would vanish as they always had, leaving no trace at all. [777] And Maren would still be standing, the architecture of her courage her own. [790]
