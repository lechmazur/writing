The umbral grotto hung above the clouds like a secret the sky kept from the world below. [17] Liora had tended its moss-veined stones for thirty cycles, watching the rebirth flowers bloom and wither and bloom again along the crystalline walls. [40] She was a caretaker, had always been one, she supposed, in every turning of the wheel. [56] She was committed to this work the way one commits to breathing, not by choice but by necessity, because without tending, the cycle itself would falter. [82] That was the grotto's deepest truth: nothing ended, only turned. [92] The dead became soil, soil became seed, seed became light. [102] She had understood this intellectually for decades. [109] But understanding and forgiving were different creatures. [116]

Her sister had stolen her name. [122] Not metaphorically, in the old way, the deep way, speaking it into the rootwaters and claiming Liora's place in the rebirth cycle. [144] For eleven years, Liora had existed as a ghost in her own life, unnamed, unmoored, watching her sister wear her future like a stolen coat. [169] She had forgiven her last winter. [175] Not because the act was forgivable, it never would be, but because carrying the weight was making her less alive, and the grotto needed her fully present to tend the turning. [206]

Now she sought something she could not name. [214] The source of contentment, perhaps. [219] A reason the rebirth flowers in the upper chamber glowed warmer than those below. [233] The grotto's single tree, an ash with roots threading through cloud and stone alike, had always been her thinking place. [253] She climbed it now, hands finding familiar bark, the vineyard hush of the place settling around her like twilight. [272] Above the cloudline, the air thinned and sweetened. [280] She settled in the crook where two branches embraced and looked down at the grotto's layout. [296]

The chambers scalloped outward from the central pool in curved shells of stone, each one a stage of the rebirth cycle. [317] She had never questioned the design before. [324] But from this height, she saw what ground-level had hidden: the scalloped chambers spiraled inward, not outward. [341] The smallest, warmest chamber, the one with the brightest flowers, sat at the very center, and it was sealed. [360]

She climbed down and walked the spiral backward, against the cycle's flow, which felt like wading through warm, slow water. [380] At the center, she found a door she had cleaned a thousand times without truly seeing. [396] A keyhole shaped like a crescent. [402]

The antique hairpin was in her pocket. [409] She had carried it since her sister's theft, the only object left behind, a tortoiseshell pin with a silver crescent head. [430] She had kept it first as evidence, then as grief, then as mere habit. [444] Now she pulled it out and turned it in her fingers. [455] The crescent head matched the keyhole exactly. [462]

She did not hesitate. [466] Liora was kindly, but she was decisive. [473] It was what made her a good caretaker. [481] She slid the hairpin into the lock and turned. [490]

The chamber opened onto a small, warm space lit by flowers growing in perfect spirals along the walls. [508] At the center sat a pool no larger than a cupped palm. [520] Its water reflected not the ceiling but something deeper: every face she had worn in every cycle, every name, every loss. [541] Her sister's face was there too, wearing Liora's stolen name, and beneath the theft, beneath the betrayal, there was fear. [561] Simple, animal fear of ceasing to exist. [568]

Liora understood then. [571] Contentment was not the absence of pain. [578] It was the moment you looked at the entire cycle, the taking, the giving, the turning, and chose to tend it anyway. [600] She knelt beside the pool and touched the water. [609] It was warm, like blood, like wine, like the last light before evening. [622] The flowers brightened. [625] They turned toward her like small, warm faces. [633]

She would climb the tree again tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after that. [649] Not because she had found an answer, but because she had found the question that made the tending worthwhile. [668] The hairpin she left in the lock. [675] Some things were meant to stay where they fit. [684]

Outside, the vineyard hush deepened, and the clouds moved like slow sheep below the grotto's edge, and the cycle turned, and turned, and turned. [708]
