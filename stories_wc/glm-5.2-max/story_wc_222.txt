The sky had been wrong for three days. [8] Not wrong like a storm coming - wrong like the sun had slipped sideways, casting shadows that pointed east at noon. [28] Above the clouds, the icebound spire of Vael caught that bent light and threw it back in fractured rainbows, and Sera watched them from the gargoyle's perch with cautious abandon, her fingers already numb. [62]

She was a thief, but a chivalrous one - she stole only from those who had stolen first. [79] Her master, old Fenn, had called her his apprentice of wonder, not because she marveled at everything but because she could find wonder's hiding place in the most guarded vault. [109] He had died last winter, and with him the knowledge of what Lord Kasta had taken from the people of the valley below: their names, written on slips of paper, sealed inside hand-carved chess pieces. [144] Kasta collected identity like others collected coin. [151] With those names, he owned them. [157]

Sera's plan was sacredly playful in its design. [165] She had spent months carving replicas - perfect matches to Kasta's ivory set, each hollowed to hold its own slip of paper with a single word: free. [191] Tonight, during Kasta's exhibition match against the visiting ambassador, she would swap them. [204] The originals, with the names inside, would go back to the valley. [216] The replicas would take their place on the board. [225]

The pocket sundial lid was her key. [232] Fenn had given it to her years ago, explaining that the spire's chess room had been built by a clockmaker, and each piece in Kasta's set locked to the board with a mechanism that responded to the sundial's calibrated edge. [272] She turned it now in her gloved hand, feeling the familiar teeth along its rim. [287]

She dropped from the gargoyle through a cracked clerestory window, landing soft on the gallery floor. [303] The chess room lay ahead, its door attended by a single guard who slept with his chin on his chest. [323] Sera moved past him the way wind moves past stone - felt but not seen. [337]

The board was mid-game, pieces frozen in their positions like a paused argument. [350] Sera began her work, pressing the sundial lid against the base of each piece to release its lock, lifting it free, and swapping it with the replica from her coat. [380] King, queen, bishop, knight - each yielded its secret weight and was replaced. [392] She could feel the names inside, curled tight as sleeping moths. [403]

She was halfway through the pawns when the sky pulsed. [413] Through the window, she saw the wrongness deepen - the sun flickered like a candle in wind, and for a moment, the stars showed through the blue. [439] The spire groaned. [442] Ice cracked along the ceiling. [447]

Sera's hands trembled, but she did not stop. [455] This was the nature of her work - to embrace the unknown with open arms, to walk into rooms that might collapse, to trust that the wonder was worth the risk. [485] Fenn had taught her that. [490] The world was ending or changing or waking up, and still, the names mattered. [504] Freedom mattered. [506]

She finished the last pawn and pressed it into place with a soft click. [520] The sundial lid went back into her pocket. [528] Behind her, the guard snored. [533] Above her, the sky tore open in a seam of gold light, and the icebound spire sang like a struck bell. [554]

Sera did not look back. [559] She climbed out through the window, the stolen pieces pressed against her heart, and descended through clouds that were beginning, for the first time in three days, to look like clouds again. [591] Whatever the sky was doing, whatever came next, the valley would have its names. [605] And Kasta would find, when he next moved his pieces, that every one of them had been amended - not stolen, but corrected, the way a wrong chord becomes right when you turn it properly. [639]

She was, after all, only an apprentice. [646] But wonder, Fenn had said, was the only craft worth practicing in a world this strange. [662]
