Aurel had collected lost souls in Echo Gorge for eleven years and never once heard one laugh. [17] The gorge was a wound in the earth where sound refused to die, folding back on itself until the simplest whisper became a cathedral of resonance. [43] In the hush of a hidden sunrise—that narrow hour when light crept over the ridge like something embarrassed to be seen—the spectral confessions began. [69] They rose from the stone in threads of unspooled voices, each one a lost soul's final regret spoken as though someone still living might hear and forgive. [96] Aurel's task was to receive them. [102] Not judge. [104] Not absolve. [106] Collect the unmoored and guide them to the threshold where they could finally pass. [120] Around Aurel's neck hung a crocodile's tooth on frayed leather—a relic from a grandmother who had healed in the marshlands before the water took her village. [147] She told Aurel the tooth was not for strength but for remembering that even the most fearsome things sometimes wept. [167] "Fear and grief live in the same jaw," she said. [177] "One bites." [179] "The other just holds on." [184] That teaching shaped everything. [188] Aurel had become modestly fearless—not through absence of dread but through having stood inside it so many times it became a known room rather than an abyss. [216] Even the way the gorge sometimes seemed to breathe, inhaling dawn and exhaling something older, had become familiar. [234] But Aurel carried another belief, quieter and riskier: that healing was not only grief's work, and that somewhere in the architecture of sorrow there had to be room for laughter. [264] She had taught that joy was not grief's opposite but its only honest companion—that a soul couldn't truly pass until it remembered how to laugh at the absurdity of clinging so hard to staying. [299] This morning, that belief would be tested. [306] Among the usual confessions—a litany of unspoken apologies, of hands not held, of words swallowed and left to sour—a different sound threaded through the gorge. [333] Distorted notes from a misaligned harp, its strings slackened by centuries of damp, somewhere deep in the southern passage, plucking something that was less melody than a wounded animal trying to hum. [365] And woven through the broken music: laughter. [372] Not joyful. [374] Not cruel. [376] It was the laughter of someone who had been weeping so long that the body couldn't tell the difference—sound turned against itself, grief wearing a terrible mask. [404] Aurel began to pursue. [408] The passage narrowed. [411] Each sour chord became a footprint, leading deeper into cold stone. [422] Aurel's fingers found the crocodile's tooth and held it—not as a talisman but as a reminder. [439] Fear and grief in the same jaw. [446] The only way to pry it open was to give it something it didn't expect. [461] The confessions grew louder, torn and raw now: "I never told her." "I was afraid of the smallness of being loved." "I laughed at a funeral once and never forgave myself." [492] That last one struck hard. [497] At the passage's end, in a hollow where the harp sat half-buried in silt, a soul shimmered—formless, trembling, caught between the music and the stone. [523] It had been here a long time, laughing and weeping in the same breath until it no longer knew which was release and which was prison. [549] Aurel knelt and ran a finger across the lowest string. [559] The note that emerged was ugly, bent, graceless. [567] "That's terrible," Aurel said, and smiled. [573] The soul paused. [576] "It is," it confessed—and something in its voice cracked, and through the crack, light entered. [592] Aurel began to tune the harp. [598] Not to perfection. [601] Just slightly less wrong. [605] Between each adjustment, Aurel spoke—not absolution, not wisdom, but honest acknowledgments of how absurd it was to carry regret so long you forgot what you were regretting. [633] The soul offered its confessions, and Aurel received them with the gravity they deserved, and then—gently, carefully—offered back the one thing no ghost expects: a small, genuine laugh. [663] Not at the pain. [667] Never at the pain. [671] But at the strange, stubborn, magnificent foolishness of holding on to a jaw that only wants to open. [689] The soul's shimmer brightened. [693] The laughter that had been grief's mask became, for one fragile moment, something that sounded almost like relief. [711] It passed. [713] Aurel sat in the hollow, the harp slightly less misaligned than before, the tooth warm against the chest, and listened as the gorge exhaled the last of the hidden sunrise. [743] And somewhere in the stone, for the first time in eleven years, something almost seemed to whisper back. [761]
