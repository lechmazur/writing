Father Elias stood in the antique music box repair shop, surrounded by the quiet ticking of a hundred small mechanisms. [20] The vibrant hush of the place felt almost sacred—not unlike the chapel where he'd served as penitentiary chaplain for twelve years. [42] In his hands, he held a clockwork heart—delicate brass chambers and tiny pistons, crafted by an inmate named Arthur Webb. [63] Arthur had built it during his eight years inside, working with scraps from the prison workshop. [79] When he'd given it to Elias, it wasn't functioning. [88] "I made it for my daughter," Arthur had said. [97] "But it don't beat no more. [104] Like me." [106] Elias had told him it was beautiful anyway. [114] He'd told Arthur that the effort mattered more than the function. [125] He'd told him forgiveness worked the same way—sometimes the mechanism jammed, but the intention counted. [141] Elias had been wrong. [145] He realized it three weeks later, when Arthur's daughter visited the prison and asked, through tears, if her father had ever mentioned her. [168] Elias had repeated what he'd told Arthur—that intention counted, that the broken heart still meant something. [185] She'd looked at him with such fury that he understood his mistake. [197] Arthur didn't need comfort. [201] He needed the heart to actually work. [208] He needed to send his daughter something that proved he could still create something whole. [223] So Elias had apologized—to Arthur, and to himself for the laziness of his consolation. [238] But an apology without action was just another broken mechanism. [248]

Now he stood in Marguerite Chen's repair shop, the air thick with the scent of aged wood and machine oil. [268] Marguerite examined the clockwork heart with aptly deliberate movements, turning it slowly under a magnifying lens. [284] "The mainspring is wound too tight," she said. [316] But I'd want a second opinion." [322] She glanced up at him. [327] "There's a retired horologist upstairs. [332] Edmund Faye. [334] He built components for music boxes for forty years. [343] If anyone can tell us what this heart needs, he can." [354]

Second opinions. [356] Elias had never sought them in his chaplaincy. [364] He'd dispensed wisdom like a vending machine, certain of his counsel. [375] Arthur's daughter had shown him how dangerous that certainty could be. [386]
