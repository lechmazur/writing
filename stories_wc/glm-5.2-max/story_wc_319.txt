The creature that guarded the Vellum Library had no name, but it had teeth, and it had hunger, and it had a patience that outlasted the frost. [27] They called it feral because it had never been tamed, though it had been made — summoned from somewhere between dimensions by a librarian who'd read the wrong book at the right time. [59] The librarian was long dead. [64] The creature remained. [67] It prowled the frozen garden that surrounded the library, where mist coiled between dead rose bushes and crystallized each branch in white. [89] The mist was permanent here, as though the air itself had forgotten how to clear. [104] The creature moved through it like a fish through water, familiar with opacity, comfortable with what it could not see. [124]

It was dimensionally homesick. [128] Not for a place it could name, but for a direction that no longer existed — a direction perpendicular to all directions, the way home used to feel when home was everywhere and nowhere. [161] This world had too many edges, too many hard lines, too many things that ended where they ended and did not continue into elsewhere. [185]

It had found the pendant three winters ago, half-buried in the frozen garden, dropped by a cultist who had come seeking forbidden knowledge and had left seeking a hospital. [214] The pendant was a small iron disc etched with spirals that did not resolve into any pattern the creature recognized from either dimension. [237] It kept the pendant because the metal was warm, always, even in the frozen garden, and warmth that comes from nowhere reminded it of somewhere. [262]

Now it waited. [265] The waiting was the work. [270] It guarded the library not by patrol but by presence, by sitting in the frozen garden and listening, by being the thing that stood between the books and the world. [300] It interiorized exteriors, as the old librarian had once called it — took the sounds of the frozen garden, the creak of ice, the whisper of mist, and pulled them inside itself, made them part of its own rhythm. [338] When it had interiorized enough of the world, it knew the world, and what it knew, it could guard. [357]

The cultist had been a violation, a gap in its knowing. [368] The pendant was a lesson: the world contained things that did not announce themselves. [382] The creature had been angry, at first, that something could exist within its territory without its awareness. [399] But anger was a hard edge, and the creature had no use for edges. [413] So it learned to make friends with uncertainty. [421]

It began to wait differently — not for threats, but for possibilities, for the shape of what might arrive rather than what had arrived. [444] It held the cultist's pendant in its paw and felt the warmth that came from nowhere and tried to love the not-knowing of where the warmth originated. [471]

Through the veil of sleep, the creature dreamed. [479] In its dreams, the frozen garden thawed, and the mist became the substance between dimensions, and the boundaries of light shifted. [500] Light, in the creature's home dimension, did not stop at surfaces. [511] It continued through and through, illuminating the interiors of things, and nothing was ever fully dark or fully bright. [530] Here, light stopped. [533] It hit the frost on the garden's roses and died, or it passed through the mist and was scattered into nothing. [554] The boundaries of light were the boundaries of this world, and the creature lived at those boundaries, feral and patient and homesick. [576]

But the pendant's warmth suggested that some things crossed boundaries. [586] Some things moved through light and dark, through here and there, through sleep and waking. [601] The creature did not understand the pendant, and it did not try. [613] Understanding was an edge, and it was learning to make friends with uncertainty instead. [627]

The mist thickened. [630] The garden remained frozen. [634] The creature waited, interiorizing the cold, the quiet, the warmth of an object it could not explain, and somewhere, beyond the boundaries of light, something that might have been home began to feel less like a direction and more like a state of being. [678] The feral guardian did not need to understand. [686] It needed only to wait, and to make room for what it could not know, and to guard the library where the books kept their knowledge in hard lines while the creature outside kept its knowing in fog and warmth and patient, patient presence. [730]
