The son of the boy who cried wolf sat in the lighthouse prison on Thornback Reef, where the sea ground against jagged rock and the copperlight of sundown turned everything amber and still. [33]
He had come here to chase hush, the silence between what happened and what people believed, the quiet that swallows truth whole. [22] [55]
His father had told the truth about the wolf. [9] [64]
No one believed him, and that became the story: a lesson about lying, not a lesson about listening. [18] [82]
The son inherited the residue of that disbelief, and now he sat in a prison cell forty miles from the nearest town, waiting for footsteps that would mean they had found the bodies. [33] [115]
Two guards lay in the lower cells, dead, their necks bent at angles that no explanation could soften. [18] [133]
He had not killed them. [5] [138]
But who would believe the son of the boy who cried wolf? [12] [150]
That was the question that had dogged him since childhood, the question that went everywhere he went, and the question that he needed to answer while there was still time. [30] [180]
He needed to thread the labyrinth of the prison's corridors and its history with his own story, to press his narrative into the stone before others could do it for him. [31] [211]
The way to do it, he knew, was through the scuffs on the narrow door left ajar for decades, the door to the old chapel, marked by generations of feet, scuffed by men seeking solace before solace became a discontinued service. [41] [252]
The scuffs were a language, a record of who had passed through and why, and he read them the way his father might have read the tracks of a wolf in soft earth, by the weight of the mark, the direction of the push, the fear encoded in the heel. [50] [302]
He followed the scuffs through the narrow door and into the chapel, a small vaulted space now used for storage. [20] [322]
Against the far wall leaned the lattice from the confessional, removed years ago when the booth was dismantled, stored here where no one would think to look for holy things in a prison warehouse. [34] [356]
The lattice sustained impossibilities. [4] [360]
That was the only way he could describe it, the wooden grid holding the memory of confessions no one had believed, secrets that were impossible but true, absolutions given and received in a place where absolution should not have existed. [40] [400]
He placed his forehead against the lattice, as others had done before him, the wood worn smooth by the gesture. [20] [420]
And he hovered there, suspended between the past and what was coming, between the copperlight streaming through the high window and the shadow of the lattice falling across his face like a net. [33] [453]
Hovering not in body but in moment, still enough to listen, steady enough to wait. [15] [468]
The bodies would be found within the hour, when the supply boat docked and the relief crew descended to the lower cells. [22] [490]
He had that long, and no longer, to thread his story into the lattice's grain, into the scuffs on the door, into the copperlight that sustained impossibilities: the wolf that came, the truth told and disbelieved, the two guards dead by someone else's hand, the son of the liar who had always told the truth. [55] [545]
He pulled back from the lattice and began to speak, not to the confessional or to God, but to the stone, which had no choice but to listen. [28] [573]
He spoke of his father, who had stood in the village square and shouted the truth until his voice cracked, and of how the village had turned away, already composing the fable that would replace the man. [37] [610]
The words came low and steady, threading the labyrinth of his own story through the prison's corridors, through the narrow door and its scuffs, through the lattice and its impossible cargo, through the copperlight repose of a lighthouse prison off a jagged reef, where the sea was always listening and the foghorn always answered. [54] [664]
Somewhere below, the dead waited in their copper silence. [9] [673]
Somewhere beyond the reef, the boat drew closer. [8] [681]
And the son of the boy who cried wolf told his story to the only witness that could not disbelieve. [20] [701]
