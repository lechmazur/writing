The ferry guides had walked the veiled ridgeway for longer than the village below could remember. [16] They were three: old Maren with her sea-glass eyes, Tomas whose hands never stopped moving, and the quiet one called Dell, who heard things others could not. [43] They carried between them a lantern, a length of knotted rope, and a ticket punch with a star stamp worn smooth by decades of marking passage. [69] They were ferry guides not because they steered boats, but because they ferried people across the hard places. [87] And tonight, after the leash breaks, they had work to do. [98]

The leash was a simple thing, a leather strap that old Hewitt used to tie his grief to the fence post outside his house. [122] Every morning he would walk out, touch it, and go about his day. [135] But this evening the strap had snapped, and now Hewitt was somewhere on the ridgeway, walking without direction, and the guides had to find him before twilight deepened to full dark. [166]

This was their vigil. [170] Not a waiting but a moving toward. [177]

They traverse the veiled ridgeway through indirect routes, taking the switchback paths that shepherds abandoned generations ago. [194] Maren said it was because straight lines frightened people who were lost. [206] Tomas said it was because the ridgeway itself refused to be hurried. [218] Dell said nothing, but tilted his head as though listening to footsteps that had not yet arrived. [235]

Their approach was gently persistent. [240] They did not call Hewitt's name. [246] They did not rush. [250] Instead, they walked with a rhythm that matched the tide below the cliffs, slow and sure, the way water finds its way through stone not by force but by patience. [280] The shoreline breathed its lullaby beneath them, and they let it set the pace. [294]

Hewitt had been carrying something for years. [301] Not grief exactly, but the fear of grief, a leash around the part of him that might feel too much. [321] His daughter had left for the coast and he had let her go without a word. [337] His wife had died and he had built a fence instead of crying. [350] The leash held it all in place. [357] And now it had broken, and everything was loose and terrifying, and he had climbed. [372]

The guides found him sitting on a flat stone where the ridgeway opened to the sea. [388] He was not crying. [392] He was not speaking. [396] He was simply still, which was worse. [403]

Maren sat beside him. [407] Tomas stood a little way off, his hands working the knotted rope into shapes that meant nothing and everything. [426] Dell crouched at the path's edge, listening. [433]

"We're not here to take you back," Maren said. [442] Her voice carried the cadence of waves on shingle. [451] "We're here to walk with you." [457]

Hewitt looked at her. [461] "I don't know where I'm going." [467]

"That's the point of the ridgeway," she said. [475] "You traverse it through indirect routes because the direct ones are the ones that broke you." [491]

She held out the ticket punch with its star stamp. [501] Hewitt stared at it. [505] The guides carried it for moments like this, not for payment but for proof. [519] A star pressed into paper meant you had crossed something. [529] It meant the passage was real, even if you could not name what you had crossed. [545]

"I haven't done anything brave," Hewitt said. [552]

"You climbed," Tomas said, his fingers still working the rope. [562] "That's something." [564]

"Everyone climbs." [566]

"Not everyone climbs after the leash breaks," Dell said. [575] It was the first thing he had spoken, and his voice was low and tidal. [590] "Most people just stand where it snapped and wait to be retied." [602]

The twilight was deepening. [606] The vigil would end soon, one way or another. [615] Maren pressed the ticket punch into Hewitt's palm. [623] It was warm from her hand. [629]

"Find where your unused courage waits," she told him. [638] "It's been sitting in you for years, Hewitt." [646] "Leashed down." [648] "Fed scraps." [650] "But it's there." [653]

He closed his fingers around the metal. [660] It was heavier than it looked. [666] Below them, the sea moved against the shore the way it always had, patient and unhurried, a lullaby sung to a coast that never quite fell asleep. [693]

Hewitt pressed the star stamp against the back of his own hand. [705] The mark was small and uneven. [711] It meant nothing to anyone except him. [718]

But he stood. [721]

And the guides walked with him, traversing the veiled ridgeway through indirect routes, back toward the village, where everything he had avoided was waiting to be finally felt. [749]
