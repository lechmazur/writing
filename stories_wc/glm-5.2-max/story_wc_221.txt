The observation deck hummed with residual energy, its chronal fields still flickering long after the shutdown had rendered every other system in the station dormant. [27] Marek sat cross-legged on the cold floor, his gilded compass rose applique catching what little light seeped through the fractured viewport. [48] The delicate golden piece had been sewn onto his father's coat, then his grandmother's before that, and now his—a family heirloom that pointed nowhere, yet had always guided him somewhere. [79] He was, by every measure the remaining colonists cared to acknowledge, a blessed pariah. [93] The temporal sickness that had claimed dozens had skipped him entirely, leaving him whole where others frayed at the edges of their own timelines. [116] They called it a gift. [121] They also stopped sitting beside him in the mess hall, stopped meeting his eyes in corridors, stopped speaking to him altogether. [140] Blessing and exile, woven together like the threads of the applique he traced with one finger. [154]

Tonight was an anniversary. [158] Not of the shutdown that had stranded them here at the edge of a collapsed wormhole, but of something older, quieter. [178] His grandmother had begun the tradition: every year, on the date her own mother had first stepped aboard a starship, the family would sketch the night sky as seen from wherever they happened to be. [211] You need to know where you are, she had told a young Marek, pressing a charcoal stick into his small hand. [233] Not where you wish you were. [239]

He opened the worn leather folio beside him and turned to a fresh page. [253] The observation deck's time dilation effects made sketching difficult—stars drifted at unnatural speeds, and constellations smeared across his vision when he looked too long. [276] He had learned to work in quick glances. [284] Glimpse, mark, look away. [289] Glimpse, mark, look away. [294] The rhythm steadied him. [298]

[332] The others kept to the habitat ring, where time moved normally. [343] Marek came here because the distortion felt less like illness and more like perspective. [356]

He had spent weeks after the shutdown in silent confusion, roving the station's corridors without purpose, unable to chart a path forward. [376] The folio had changed that. [381] When he opened it and began to draw, confusion loosened its grip. [392] Each line he committed to paper was an act of exploration—not of the sky alone, but of his own disorientation. [411] He was mapping unknowing itself, and the map was becoming his compass. [422]

[431] He glanced up, caught a brief glimpse of something shifting in its western arc—a star cluster he had never catalogued before, blooming into visibility as the wormhole's residual light bent around the station. [461] His hand moved before his mind caught up, sketching the cluster's rough position, its luminosity, the strange way it seemed to pulse in time with his own heartbeat. [486] The gilded compass rose on his chest glinted. [494] Almost without thinking, he oriented his sketch to match it—not magnetic north, not navigational north, but a truer bearing. [511] Whatever direction made sense of the chaos. [518] Whatever point he could steer toward. [524]

[629] Like all of them, here on this station, waiting to discover what the shutdown had actually been: not an ending, but a veering. [650] He would show the sketch to the others tomorrow.
