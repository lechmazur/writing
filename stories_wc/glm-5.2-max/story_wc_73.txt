Maren counted steps as she climbed, the way a prisoner counts days. [12] She had passed step ten thousand three days ago, or what felt like three days. [27] The infinite staircase spiraled upward through darkness lit only by phosphorescent veins in the walls, and it never changed, never ended, never offered a landing. [52] She climbed anyway. [55] That was the grim optimism her colleague Victor had named in her, back when they'd both stood at the bottom and debated whether to begin. [80]

She carried little. [83] A canteen that refilled itself from condensation. [90] A notebook dense with equations. [95] And a pair of dice, one chipped along the edge of its four face, that she rolled each morning to decide whether to continue. [119] She always continued. [122] The dice were not for deciding, really. [129] They were for remembering. [133]

Twenty years ago, Maren had been a mathematician at a university that no longer existed. [148] She had been working on a proof demonstrating that perpetual motion was not merely impossible but logically incoherent, a violation of topology rather than thermodynamics. [173] Victor had argued the opposite: that certain recursive structures, if embedded in physical space, could sustain motion indefinitely without energy loss. [194] The staircase was his proof, or rather the physical manifestation of it. [206] He had built it, or discovered it, or willed it into being through seventeen pages of dense topological reasoning that Maren had dismissed as elegant but unsound. [233]

She had rolled the dice that night too. [241] She had been balancedly daring in those days, willing to entertain wild ideas but only after testing them against rigor. [261] The dice came up double sixes. [267] She told Victor his proof was beautiful but wrong, and she went home. [280] Victor stepped onto the staircase alone. [286] He never came back. [290]

Now Maren climbed the same stairs, notebook in hand, rewriting his proofs, searching for the place where his mathematics became architecture. [311] She was not here to rescue him. [318] She understood that. [321] She was here because she had felt the texture of that missed opportunity, the night she chose caution over courage, and it had settled into her bones like a mineral deposit, hardening over years until she could no longer distinguish it from her own skeleton. [366] She climbed to capture that texture, to hold it up to the light of the staircase's faint glow and understand its shape. [388]

The staircase was perpetual motion made manifest. [395] Each step, once climbed, dissolved behind her into the raw material of the step ahead. [410] No energy was lost. [414] No energy was gained. [418] The system was closed and eternal. [424] Victor's mathematics had been right, and her dismissal had been the error that defined her life. [440]

On what she called day seven, though time was meaningless here, she found the rupture. [455] It appeared in her notebook before it appeared in the stairs. [466] Page forty-three: a proof she had been refining suddenly split open, revealing a contradiction she had not written. [484] Someone had added a lemma in handwriting she recognized. [493] Victor's hand. [495] Tight, slanted, unmistakable. [498]

The lemma showed that the staircase's perpetuity depended on a single variable: the presence of a second climber. [516] The structure could sustain one person's motion indefinitely only if another person's weight counterbalanced it from below. [533] Victor had not disappeared. [537] He had been waiting at the bottom, his own climb suspended, so that hers could begin. [553]

She sat on the step and rolled the dice. [562] The chipped one landed on four, its broken face catching the light differently than the others. [578] She understood now. [581] The missed opportunity had not been refusing to climb. [590] It had been refusing to climb together. [597] The staircase required partnership, and they had each tried to solve it alone. [610]

She wrote a new proof, slow and careful, demonstrating that two climbers, moving in counter-rotation, could sustain the staircase's motion while ascending. [632] She rolled the dice again. [637] Double fours. [639] Balanced. [640] Daring enough. [642]

She began to descend, and for the first time, the stairs moved with her instead of against her. [660]
