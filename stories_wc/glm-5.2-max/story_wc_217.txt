Seline came to the cobalt lagoon after every rain, a quiet shoreline poet who listened to what storms left behind rather than what they carried away. [26]
This morning the water held a burnished hush, rain-washed and barely breathing, its surface reflecting a sky caught between grey retreat and amber advance. [50]
She carried a drake's tail feather in her journal, iridescent and slightly frayed at one edge, found months ago on this same shore. [73]
She kept it not for its perfection but for the way its broken barbs caught light differently than the rest. [93]
She came here to discover beauty in the incomplete--the half-erased tide line, the broken shell spiraling inward, the poem that refused to resolve its final line. [120]
The rain had stirred sediment near the inlet where fresh water met salt, and the two flows circled each other in slow disagreement, neither willing to fully surrender. [148]
Seline watched the boundary shift and fold, warmly attentive to the way the waters argued without violence. [165]
She had been working on a series of poems she called woven dawns: mornings that arrived not all at once but in threads, one color lacing through another, never forming a whole sky. [198]
She sat on the wet stones and opened her journal. [208]
The feather slipped out and she held it against the light. [219]
The intact side shimmered green-gold; the frayed side scattered light into filaments like tiny cracks in the air. [237]
Both sides were the same feather. [243]
Near the far shore, a child flew a ragged paper kite that climbed in uncertain lurches. [259]
The wind after rain was unreliable, and the kite swung wildly, but the child leaned into the rope with complete trust. [280]
Then, as a kite string snaps, the line broke. [289]
The kite lurched, climbed once more, and began a slow curving descent toward the lagoon. [304]
The child stood frozen, hands still holding the useless handle. [314]

She walked along the shore to where the kite drifted down into the shallows. [352]
By the time she reached it, the paper was already softening, colors bleeding into the lagoon in thin ribbons. [371]
She lifted it gently--ruined and beautiful, frame cracked, tail of rags dangling--and carried it back to her stones. [391]
The child arrived at a half-run and stopped. [399]
"It's broken," he said. [403]
"I know," Seline said. [407]
"Look how the water made it new." [414]

The boy's face changed. [443]
Seline picked up the feather and held it beside the kite. [454]
"This was whole once. [458]

"Things don't stop being beautiful when they break. [486]

He touched the feather's frayed edge, then the kite's bleeding paper. [514]
The lagoon behind them still circled its two waters together--fresh into salt, clear into clouded--braiding strands of each into something that was neither and both. [541]
Seline opened her journal and began to write. [549]

She tended them by harmonizing discord--letting the broken feather and ruined kite and unresolved water speak beside each other until their separate fragmentations formed a single, coherent music. [589]
This was what she meant by woven dawns. [597]

When he finally stood, he asked, "Will you write about it?" [653]
"I already am," she said. [658]
