Elara stood at the museum's stone balcony, watching the monumental waterfall that veiled the grotto city of Karsen-Veil. [18] Since the beekeeper's last visit three weeks ago, the cascade had begun to thaw, and she could see the green valley beyond more clearly than she had in years. [47] Elara was a cultured museum guide, trained by her mother to remember every artifact's origin with historically oriented precision. [66] The museum held the rescued arts of people who had fled here when their homelands burned -- textiles, manuscripts, instruments, all nearly lost. [88] Her motivation was simple: to rescue dying arts before they vanished entirely, the way her grandmother's village had vanished. [107] The grotto city of Karsen-Veil had sheltered these displaced families for seventeen years, its caverns warm with lantern-glow and the mineral breath of the underground river that fed the waterfall above. [138] She carried an emerald-tinted opera glass lens in her pocket, a relic from the museum's display case that she had borrowed that morning. [161] Through it, the waterfall's spray looked like falling gemstones, and the valley beyond seemed impossibly close. [177] In the museum's kitchen below, dough was rising. [185] It was Tuesday, and Tuesdays meant the family custom her grandmother had carried through exile: baking bread shaped like the initials of the dead. [209] Each loaf commemorated someone lost to the displacement -- A.B., M.K., R.T. -- names pressed into living dough, given brief resurrection in warmth and yeast. [232] Elara had taught the custom to the displaced children who visited the museum, and now they came every week with new letters, new names pulled from their parents' fading memories. [262] But today the baking had a different purpose. [270] The beekeeper had brought news on his last visit: a delegation from the outside world was coming, people who claimed they wanted to document and preserve the displaced communities' arts. [300] Elara felt the rosewater dread that always came with hope -- the sweet possibility of recognition twined with the sour fear of exposure. [322] The scent of the initial-breads mingled with the mineral air, floral and warm, but underneath it ran a current of something cold. [344] What if the delegation came not to preserve but to appropriate? [355] What if their interest accelerated the dying rather than halted it? [366] She raised the emerald-tinted lens and studied the valley. [375] Through its green glass, she could make out figures approaching along the river path. [389] They moved slowly, deliberately, like people who knew they were walking toward something sacred and fragile. [405] Elara descended to the kitchen. [410] The initial-breads were cooling on stone shelves -- she had shaped the letters of the delegation's leader today, a gesture of welcome and warning. [433] Breaking bread shaped like your own name was an old custom of truce, a promise that you came in peace and would be remembered if you did not. [461] The children were already gathered in the museum hall, arranging their small artifacts on display tables. [477] A girl named Sora had embroidered her grandmother's pattern onto a scrap of salvaged silk. [492] A boy called Petar had carved a flute from river-reed, copying an instrument in the collection. [508] These were the rescued arts -- not preserved under glass, but living, breathing, passed forward. [522] Elara watched Sora adjust her embroidery on the table, the girl's fingers steady and sure, and felt the grotto's warmth press against her like a held breath waiting to be released. [553] When the delegation arrived, Elara met them at the grotto's entrance. [564] The waterfall's mist clung to their clothes as she led them through the curtain of falling water into the city's lantern-lit interior. [586] She offered the initial-breads and explained the custom. [594] The delegation's leader, a woman named Dr. Vesna, accepted her loaf with hands that trembled. [609] "This is what we hoped to find," Vesna said quietly. [619] "Not relics." [621] "Living practice." [623] Elara felt something loosen in her chest. [630] The thaw was real -- not just ice surrendering to spring, but the guardedness of displaced people softening toward a world that had once rejected them. [655] The rosewater dread did not vanish, but it sweetened into something closer to courage. [669] She guided them through the museum, speaking each artifact's history with the cultured reverence her mother had taught her. [688] The emerald-tinted lens she returned to its case, but not before one last look through it at the thinning waterfall. [708] The world beyond was no longer a threat glimpsed through green glass. [720] It was an invitation. [724] The children's bread would be ready by evening, fresh initials for new names, and the museum would stay open late. [744] Outside, the last ice broke free and the waterfall swelled with new water, and Karsen-Veil's displaced people opened their doors to the sound. [767]
