The midnight glade above clouds existed only in the breath between forgetting and remembering. [14] Vella discovered it three nights after her daughter's birth, when sleep became a country she could no longer enter and the house felt like a garment sewn too tight. [43] She was an onyx-ink cartomancer by trade, reading fortunes in the dark spill of cards, interpreting the way black ink bled into parchment like veins seeking the heart. [71] But the cards had shown her nothing useful since the baby came. [83] They showed her only the past, which she already knew too well, and the past had become a room she could not find the door out of. [110] She sought something else: to find where lost time accumulates. [120] The hours of her own childhood, the years her mother had been dying before anyone noticed, the minutes between contractions when she had been neither mother nor not-mother but something unnamed and floating. [153] That time had to go somewhere. [159] She believed it collected in high places, in the thin air where the world's noise could not follow. [177] So she climbed. [180] Above the tree line, above the fog, above the particular silence of her house where her husband slept with their daughter curled against his chest. [205] The glade welcomed her with choking beauty: moonflowers larger than her spread hand, a stream that ran silver and cold over stones that hummed, grass that bent under no wind because there was no wind here, only a stillness so complete it felt like listening. [250] Vella was willingly perplexed by what she found. [258] She had expected answers, or at least their shape. [267] Instead, she found a table. [272] It was her grandmother's table, though her grandmother had been dead for eleven years and the table had been sold to strangers when they cleared the house. [299] The birthday tablecloth lay across it, stained with generations of hand-tracings. [310] This was the family custom: every birthday, you placed your hand flat on the cloth and someone traced around it with charcoal, and the tracing stayed forever, a record of who you were at that exact size, that exact year. [350] Hands of every size layered over each other, overlapping, creating a palimpsest of reaching. [364] Her grandmother's hand at seventy, the fingers bent with arthritis. [374] Her mother's hand at six, at nineteen, at forty. [383] Her own hand at three, at seven, at sixteen. [392] And now, she realized with a pressure in her chest that was either grief or astonishment or both at once, she would need to trace her daughter's hand here too. [422] On the table sat a dolls' tea set, chipped and faded, the one her mother had played with as a girl in the back garden of a house that no longer existed. [454] Vella lifted the saucer and found the crack she remembered: a thin line running from rim to center, splitting a painted rose in half so that each petal faced the other across the fracture, never touching. [490] Her mother had cried when she broke it. [498] Her mother had cried about many small things, and Vella had spent her life trying not to. [515] She understood now that the crying had never been about the saucer. [527] She began to unfasten the cloth from the table, carefully, the way one handles anything that holds too much meaning to survive a careless grip. [552] The hand-tracings seemed to shift in the moonlight, fingers stretching and curling as though the people they belonged to were still reaching for something they had not found in life. [582] She spread the cloth flat on the grass of the glade and placed her own hand over her mother's last tracing, the one from her final birthday, when her hand had grown thin and tremulous and the charcoal line wavered like a voice trying to hold a note. [630] This was cloudbound repentance: not apology but return. [638] Not forgiveness but the willingness to stand in the presence of what you cannot change and trace it anyway, here is the proof that I was present for the beauty that nearly broke me. [672] The lost time was here. [677] In the overlap of hands across generations. [684] In the crack that split the painted rose, the fracture that made each half more precious for being incomplete. [703] In the space between her daughter's first breath and her own mother's last, a gap of only four days that felt like a door she had been trying to open her entire life, only to discover it had been open all along and she had simply been afraid to walk through. [754] Vella took the charcoal from her pocket. [761] She placed her hand flat on the cloth, beside her mother's hand, beside her grandmother's hand, beside all the hands that had reached across birthdays toward something they could not name, and she traced.
