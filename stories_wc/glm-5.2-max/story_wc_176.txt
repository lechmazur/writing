In the icebound sanctum under the aurora, where the green light moved like breath across the walls, Ildra worked the wax with technically gentle hands. [25] She was a lost wax caster by trade and by grief, the only trade that had ever made sense to her, because it understood that something must be destroyed to preserve its form. [58] She had come here after forgiving herself. [65] That had taken eleven years. [70] Eleven years since she had stood in the marketplace and watched the last street organ grind to silence, its punched cards rotting, and had done nothing. [96] The organ had played the names, every morning, the names of the district's dead, punched into cardstock so that cranks and bellows could sing them back to life. [124] When the organist died, no one had taken up the crank. [135] The cards had been sold for scrap. [142] The names had gone quiet. [147] She had been young. [151] She had not understood what was being lost. [159] Now she held a scrap of one of those cards, a fragment no larger than her thumbnail, recovered from a rag picker's tray in the lower city. [186] The holes in it were a language she almost could not read. [198] Almost. [199] But the pattern lived in her fingers the way all transcribed memory lives in those who once heard the original: incomplete, persistent, aching. [222] The sanctum had been a place of preservation for centuries. [232] Its walls were thick with ice, but at the center stood a centuries-old ceremonial rope, braided hemp and sinew, wound through with silver thread, its patterns so worn by devotion that the plaits had become sacred. [268] The rope had been used in rites of naming, each braid carrying a knot-sequence that corresponded to a sound, a syllable, a person. [291] Ildra had studied the rope for three winters. [299] She understood its grammar now. [304] And she understood that the braids could be reconfigured. [313] She set the punched card scrap beside her on the ice table and began. [327] Her fingers found the first braid, technically gentle, the way a surgeon is gentle, each movement precise and reversible until it was not. [350] She loosened the tension in a single plait and redirected it, crossing it under where it had once gone over. [370] The rope resisted, then accepted, the way old things do when they finally recognize the hands that hold them. [389] This was the method: to reconfigure the braids so that the rope's pattern matched the pattern of holes in the card. [410] The rope would become a mold, not for metal, but for sound. [422] The lost wax of her trade would be the wax of forgetting, melted away, leaving only the durable form: the names, sung again. [445] She worked through the night. [450] The aurora shifted from green to violet, casting color across the ice. [462] Her breath hung in clouds. [467] Each braid she reconfigured brought the pattern closer to completion, and as the rope changed, something else changed too: the air in the sanctum began to vibrate, faintly, as though the rope remembered the sounds its knots described. [505] By dawn, the last braid was set. [512] Ildra lit the single candle she had carried with her, the only warmth she had allowed herself, and held it to the rope. [535] The flame caught the silver thread. [541] It ran along the reconfigured braids, and where it traveled, it illuminated: each crossing, each knot, each redirected plait glowed in sequence, and the glow carried sound. [568] A hum first, then a note, then a name, Serikel, sung by no voice but by the rope itself, by the pattern, by the memory transcribed in braids and holes and silver fire. [601] Then another name. [604] Then another. [606] Ildra sat back on her heels and listened. [614] The names filled the sanctum, warm as snowmelt, and she felt something in her chest thaw, that last frozen place she had carried for eleven years, now running with water and ardor and the sound of people she had failed but would never again forget. [659] The candle burned down. [663] The aurora faded. [666] The names continued, looping through the rope's new geometry, preserved. [676]
