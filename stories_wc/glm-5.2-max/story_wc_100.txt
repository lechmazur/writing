The package arrived on the thirteenth day of the drowning season, wrapped in sailcloth and tied with the kind of knots undertakers recognize from burial at sea. [27] Maren had been the only undertaker in Glassharbor for eleven years, and in that time she had carried the dead to seventeen different resting places. [52] None lasted. [54] The sea reclaimed the cliffside crypts. [60] The marsh swallowed the graveyard on the hill. [68] Even the stone mausoleum her father built crumbled in the last quake, its occupants tumbling into black water like passengers disembarking. [89] She was tired. [92] Not the tiredness of labor but the deeper exhaustion of repetition without resolution, of being always pursued by the next body and never able to lay the last one to true rest. [124] Every grave she dug was only on loan. [132] She wanted to give rest a permanent address. [140]

Inside the package was a broken lighthouse lens. [148] Not glass but crystal, fractured clean through its center, each facet still holding a faint iridescence like something that refused to stop catching light. [172] No note. [174] No return mark. [177] Only the lens and a hand-drawn map showing a location beneath the harbor where no location should be. [195] Maren recognized the coordinates. [199] Every child in Glassharbor knew the stories of the drowned library, the city that had sunk three centuries ago when the old sea wall failed. [224] Scholars said its archives held the genealogies of every family on the coast. [237] Undertakers said it held something else: the original burial charts, the ones that marked ground the sea could never reach. [257]

She should have thrown the map away. [264] She should have set the lens on a shelf and gone back to her work. [279] But Maren had always been inadvertently stubborn, not in the way of arguments or defiance but in the way of simply continuing when others had the sense to stop. [308] It was what made her an undertaker. [315] It was what made her descend. [321]

The dive took her through a crack in the harbor floor that should not have been wide enough for a human body. [343] She passed through collapsed corridors, squeezed between shelving units still lined with waterlogged volumes, and surfaced inside an air pocket in the library's central rotunda. [368] The space should not have existed. [374] The pressure should have crushed her. [380] But there it was: a domed ceiling holding back the sea, and beneath it rows of stone alcoves, each one empty, each one waiting. [404]

She understood then what the lens was for. [412] The library had been a lighthouse once, before it was a library, before it was a tomb. [429] The lens had been its heart, sending distress signals to ships in trouble, calling them toward safety. [446] Someone wanted it returned. [450] Someone wanted to call the dead home. [457]

Over the following weeks, Maren dove again and again, working to revitalize what had been drowned. [473] She carried the broken lens down in pieces, packed in oilskin. [484] She fitted the fragments back into the ancient housing at the rotunda's peak, working by the pale glow of bioluminescent algae that clung to the stonework. [510] The cracks remained. [513] The lens would never focus a beam the way it once had. [525] But it did something else. [530] The fractured crystal caught the ambient light and scattered it, threading pale rays through the water above the library in patterns visible from the surface. [555] Not a beam. [558] A signal. [560]

The fishermen saw it first. [565] Then the harbor pilots. [569] Then the families who had lost people to the sea and never found them again. [584] The light pulsed in irregular rhythms, and those who watched long enough claimed it spelled out coordinates. [601] Not coordinates for rescue. [605] Coordinates for the dead. [609]

Bodies began appearing at the library's entrance. [616] Not placed there by human hands but deposited by currents that had carried them for years or decades, finally released. [636] Maren found them in the alcoves, as though the sea itself had decided to return what it had taken. [655] She did not question it. [660] She laid them out, recorded their names when she could find them, and let the stone hold them. [678]

The library had found its purpose again, occupying the impossible space between sea and stone, beyond the reach of tide and quake. [700] Maren still dives every morning, carrying the weight of her work down through the narrow passage. [716] She is still tired. [720] But the tiredness has changed. [725] It has the texture of something earned, something settled. [734] At night, from her window above Glassharbor, she can see the fractured light rising from beneath the water, steady as a heartbeat. [756] It does not call for rescue. [762] It simply says: here. [766] Here is where they rest. [771] Here is where they will stay. [777]
