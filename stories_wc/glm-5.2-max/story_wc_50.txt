Torven had always been a reflective locksmith, one who noticed what others missed. [13] Not the obvious things - the lock in front of him, the key in his hand - but what hovered at the edges. [34] Peripheral glimpses. [36] The way light bent differently around a deadbolt at dusk. [46] The faint vibration in a tumbler just before it caught. [56] His mother had called it peripherally focused, as if it were a condition rather than a gift. [73]

He lived now in the marble cavern beneath Essin's Ridge, where the stone had been carved smooth by water that no longer flowed. [96] The marble caught everything - torchlight, voice, the slow drip of condensation - and threw it back altered. [112] During the lull of a wounded season, when autumn had arrived broken and bloodless after the droughtfires, Torven had come here to work. [135]

The object lay on his workbench: a broken timer mechanism, brass and crystal, no larger than a fist. [153] He'd found it in the cavern's deepest chamber, half-buried in calcite deposits. [165] Something about its gear teeth - seventeen of them, arranged in no pattern he recognized - suggested it had once measured something other than minutes. [188]

He needed to reverse-engineer it. [193] Not out of curiosity alone, but because he understood, with the quiet certainty of a man who had spent decades listening to what locks said about the people who'd built them, that this device had been made to capture something. [233]

The marble helped. [236] Its reflection angles changed as light moved through the cavern's single skylight, and Torven began studying those angles at different times - dawn, noon, the blue dimness before dark. [264] He positioned the mechanism at each spot where reflections converged, watching how the marble's curved walls bent light through the crystal components. [286]

What he saw, after three weeks, was this: the mechanism wasn't a clock. [299] It was a receiver. [303]

The cavern itself was a collector. [309] When sound entered through the skylight - birdcalls, wind, the distant groan of the wounded earth - the marble walls reflected and condensed it, channeling acoustic energy toward the deepest chamber. [338] The mechanism had been designed to wind that energy into its crystalline core, storing what the old builders had called the harvest of echoes. [362]

Torven understood now. [365] The echoes weren't just sounds. [370] They were moments - the specific quality of a morning during a season that would never come again, the particular sorrow of wind through trees that were no longer there. [399] The wounded season had killed things the world wouldn't remember on its own. [412] The builders had known this. [417] They had built collectors in marble caverns because marble remembered. [427]

The mechanism was broken because its mainspring had cracked under the weight of too many captured echoes. [444] But Torven was a locksmith. [449] He understood springs. [452] He understood how to relieve tension without losing what the tension held. [464]

He worked through the season's lull, filing a new spring from tool steel, calibrating the gear train by touch. [483] The peripheral details guided him: the way condensation gathered on the crystal when he'd aligned a gear correctly, the faint harmonic that meant two components were in sympathy. [511]

On the first cold morning, he seated the last gear and the mechanism began to turn. [527]

Sound flooded the chamber - not noise but layered memory. [536] Birdsong from a spring before the fires. [543] Rain on leaves that were now ash. [550] A child's shout, preserved in stone, from a time when the season had been whole. [565]

Torven sat on the cavern floor and listened until the echoes faded into the marble's memory. [581] Then he wound the mechanism again, carefully, and placed it in the padded box where he kept his most delicate work. [602]

He would take it to the university in Essin, where they kept their instruments of reason in stone cellars that never froze. [624] They would call it an artifact. [630] They would catalogue it. [634] But they would also keep it safe, and when someone turned its gears and listened, they would hear what a season sounded like before it broke. [660]

Wonder deserved that much, he thought. [666] A place in the cellar of reason, for later, when the world remembered how to want it. [683]

He packed his tools, extinguished his lamp, and climbed toward the light. [695]
