The elusive mirage painter known only as Vael stood on the echoing terrace, where spring had softened every stone edge with moss and the air tasted of wet lilac. [29] She was perceptively calm, the way water is calm before it remembers how to move. [44] The bells would ring soon, signaling the evening curfew, and in the hush before they did, she had work that required silence. [66] The prism parapet after rain stretched before her, its crystalline railing fracturing the last daylight into spectral ribbons. [84] Vael had waited three weeks for rain to touch the parapet. [95] Without water, the prisms were dead glass. [102] With it, they sang color across the terrace walls, and that color was her medium. [117] She did not paint with pigment. [123] She painted with light that should not exist, mirages so detailed they could fool hunger, fool grief, fool a mirror. [143]

She pressed her palm flat against her chest. [151] Beneath her ribs, a borrowed heartbeat drummed, steady and foreign. [161] It belonged once to a woman named Sestra, who had died on this terrace seven years ago, whispering children's songs as she went. [184] Vael had caught the last pulse of it the way one catches a moth, not by grasping but by opening. [204] The heartbeat kept Vael alive now, but it also kept something else alive: Sestra's final wish, coded in the lullabies she had sung as she died. [230]

Vael began to hum. [234] The melody was simple, a children's song every child in the city knew, about a sparrow learning to fly. [253] But within it, between certain notes, lived instructions. [261] Sestra had been a cipher keeper, and she had woven directions into the songs the way a seamstress hides thread in a hem. [284] The first verse told Vael which prism to touch. [293] The second verse told her how to angle the light. [303] The third verse told her what to ask for. [312]

The terrace echoed with her voice, doubling it, tripling it, until the song sounded like a choir of one woman singing across time. [335] The prism parapet responded. [339] Colors bled from the wet glass and pooled on the terrace floor, forming shapes that had no business existing in stone. [360] Vael guided them with her hands, each motion an act of grace, not performance but offering. [376] She was not painting what she wanted. [383] She was painting what the mirrors refused to show. [392] Because that was Sestra's wish. [397] Every mirror in the city showed only what was: the hollow cheeks, the locked doors, the empty chairs. [415] Sestra had wanted mirrors to show what could be. [424] Not lies, but possibilities, the lives not yet chosen, the doors not yet opened. [438] She had died before she could make them. [446] The borrowed heartbeat in Vael's chest was the promise that someone else would. [459]

The coded lullaby's final notes hung in the air as Vael pressed both hands against the largest prism. [477] The light fractured, then reformed, then fractured again, and this time it did not pool on the floor. [495] It rose. [497] It moved. [499] It climbed the terrace walls and found every mirror surface in the city, every reflective window, every still puddle, every polished blade. [521] And in each one, the mirage took hold. [529]

Vael did not see what the mirrors showed. [537] She only felt the borrowed heartbeat in her chest skip once, then settle, then slow. [552] It was not fading. [556] It was finishing. [559] Sestra's heart had carried the last of her will, and now that will was done. [574] The heartbeat would remain, Vael knew, but it would be hers now, truly hers, no longer borrowed. [591] The debt had been paid in light. [598]

The bells began to ring. [603] Their sound rolled across the terrace in dusky harmony with the fading colors of the prism parapet, and the hush that had held everything broke open into evening. [631] Vael sat down on the wet stone and listened. [640] Somewhere in the city, she imagined, a child was looking into a mirror and seeing something more than a reflection. [660] Somewhere, a mirror was showing someone who they could become. [670] She hummed the last line of the lullaby, the one about the sparrow finally in the sky, and let the spring air carry it away.
