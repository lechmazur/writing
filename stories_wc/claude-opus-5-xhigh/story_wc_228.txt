Every autumn, the harbor at Cale went glass-still for three days, and the fishermen called that water the Mirror. [19] Ilse Corvan had painted it forty-one times, always from the same bollard at the end of the west quay. [38] The town loved her the way it loved its own weather vane: constantly, and from a distance. [55] She knew every hull by its patch and every mooring rope by its fray, but she had never sat in anyone's kitchen. [77] Intimately aloof, the harbormaster called her, and she had liked the phrase enough to write it on the back of a canvas. [99]

On the third night the wind swung northeast, the Mirror cracked, and the tide drew down lower than it had in fifty-five years. [122] Ilse took her lamp, her satchel, and the small brush she never used for commissions. [137] Below the cliffs, the cave network opened its throats one by one as the water fell away. [154]

She had been sixteen the first time, thin as a gull's leg, hunting mussels and outrunning her mother's voice. [173] She had taken a wrong turn then, left instead of right at a pillar shaped like a folded hand, and the wrong turn had given her the oasis. [201] Now, with her knees complaining and the lamp throwing her shadow ahead like a guide, she looked for the pillar and could not find it. [226] Salt had eaten the folded hand into a fist, then into nothing. [238] So she did what she had done at sixteen: she chose wrongly on purpose. [252] The passage narrowed, sweated, and then breathed warm air at her. [263]

The oasis was still there. [268] A freshwater seep fed a pool the color of weak tea; ferns grew in a green fringe under a chimney of daylight; the sand was dry, fine, and untouched by any tide in living memory. [303] On a shelf of rock at eye height sat the seashell. [314] She had painted it here at sixteen with three stolen tubes: the quay, the bollard, the Mirror, all of it curled into the pink hollow of a scallop no wider than her palm. [347] Then she had made a promise she told no one, not her mother, not the harbormaster, not the two men she later almost married: she would come back and find out whether the picture had been any good. [385] That secret had organized her whole life; every canvas on the west quay had been a rehearsal for this one hour. [406]

She lifted the shell. [410] The pigment was perfect. [414] No sun had reached it, no salt, no fingers; fifty-five years had simply declined to happen in that room. [433] The little quay in her hand was crude, hopeful, and truer than anything she had made since. [450] She sat down in the dry sand and laughed until the ferns trembled. [463]

Above her, the chimney of daylight turned from white to amber; the tide had begun its return. [480] She understood the arithmetic of the place at last. [489] It was a temporary eternity: an hour of forever, rented from the sea, and the rent was that you had to leave. [511]

She could carry the shell home, hang it in the gallery in town, watch it yellow in a decade of daylight. [532] Instead she took out the small brush, mixed the seep water with her last of the ultramarine, and painted a second thing on the shell's white rim: a girl with a lamp, standing at a wrong turning. [569] Then she set it back on the shelf, because the promise had been to find out, not to keep. [588]

Neve Halloran was waiting at the cave mouth with wet boots and an unrepentant face, having followed the lamp for a mile. [610] For three autumns Ilse had let the child sit on the bollard and had told her nothing at all. [629] Now, with the water rising over the sill behind them, she chose to reveal it: the left turn, the pillar that used to be a folded hand, the pool, the ferns, the shell with two paintings on it. [667]

"Why wait?" Neve asked. [671]

"Because the door only opens when the Mirror cracks and the sea forgets itself," Ilse said, "and because I wanted to be old enough to know the answer." [699]

They walked back along the quay, and Ilse felt the strange lightness of a woman with nothing left hidden. [718] She painted the Mirror for the forty-second time the next morning, and it came out crude, hopeful, and true. [737] Somewhere under the cliff, in an hour that would not end until the sea decided otherwise, a painted seashell waited for the next wrong turn. [762]
