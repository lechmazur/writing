Ansel Vey kept the light at Coble Head, and he kept other things too: a ledger of borrowed memories, forty-one jars of fog water, and the habit of talking to weather. [31] The borrowing was a family trick, not a gift. [40] Hold an object long enough, keep still enough, and whatever its owner felt while holding it would arrive in him like a tide coming through a grate. [67] It always arrived with holes in it. [73] On the last evening of March, at nautical twilight, when the sea and the sky were the same bruised violet and the horizon existed only as a rumor, Ansel saw a stranger walk out onto the opal dock. [111] They called it the opal dock because the mist made its wet planks glow milk-white and shifting, and because nobody had ever agreed on its real name. [138] The man wore a grey coat and moved without hurry. [147] At the last piling he stopped, unpinned a dried Edelweiss boutonniere from his lapel, and set it carefully on the post. [168] Then he turned toward the lighthouse, raised two fingers to his brow, held them there, and stepped off into the water. [189] Ansel ran the boat out, and the sea gave him nothing. [200] He came back with the flower. [206]

For three days he lit the lamp a full minute late, which had not happened in nineteen years. [224] He needed to investigate the gesture the way other men need water. [236] A salute, a farewell, an accusation, an instruction - it could be any of those, and the difference mattered, because Ansel had been the only witness, and a witness who misreads is worse than none. [270] So he sat with the edelweiss in his cupped palms at the top of the tower and borrowed what it held. [291] The memories came in softly persistent knocks, the way a moth works a window: a chalk-dusted mountain in early summer; the smell of a wool coat drying by a stove; a woman's hands pinning the flower to a lapel and laughing at how badly it was done. [338] And gaps. [340] Enormous, deliberate gaps. [343]

Ansel had borrowed from a hundred objects - drowned men's watches, a bosun's pipe, his own mother's thimble - and every borrowing frayed at the edges. [367] These gaps were different. [371] They had shape. [374] So he worked the only way the trick allowed, by observing gaps: not the picture, but the places where the picture had been cut away. [399] The woman's hands had no face above them. [407] The mountain had a second set of footprints, small ones, belonging to nobody. [420] In a kitchen there was a chair pulled out and a bowl set down and warm milk in it and no child anywhere in the room, though the whole memory was arranged around the absence like iron filings around a magnet. [461]

The man had given them away. [467] Ansel had heard of the brokers in the port cities, who paid well for a grief you could no longer carry, and who took the whole shape of a person to get it. [500] The stranger had sold the memory of a child to stop the pain of having lost her, and had kept only the flower, which no broker would buy. [528] But a hole cannot be mourned; it can only be fallen into. [540]

On the fourth night Ansel understood the gesture. [548] Two fingers to the brow, held, aimed at the light: not a salute to a building. [564] The stranger had walked out at nautical twilight precisely because the lamp was lit and a man was behind it. [584] He had chosen his witness, left his last unsold memory on the post, and asked, with the only word his body had, to be held by somebody. [611]

Ansel pinned the edelweiss to his own coat, where it stayed for eleven more winters. [626] He entered the man in the ledger with the others, but on a fresh page, and gave him a name: the walker of the opal dock, father of a girl who liked the mountain in early summer. [663] It was a guess, assembled entirely from empty space, and Ansel knew it. [676] It was also the truest thing he had ever written. [686] Every evening after that he lit the lamp early - a full minute early - and stood at the rail while the mist came pearling up the pilings. [712] The keepers on the mainland thought him eccentric, and he was: a man alone on a rock, wearing a dead stranger's flower, keeping records of lives he had never lived. [742] But he had learned what the light was for. [750] Not warning. [752] Witness. [753] Somebody out there in the dark, alone, needed to see that somebody else was awake and looking back. [771]
