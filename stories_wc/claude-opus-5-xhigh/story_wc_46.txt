They called Mira the earthbound star watcher, because she had given up the tower but not the sky. [18] Every low tide she walked down to the serpentine grotto, where the rock coiled inward like a sleeping eel and the mist hung so thick it seemed to have weight. [48] There, on a shelf of wet basalt, she kept her tumbler, her leathers, and her tins of grit. [66] Gem polishing was slow work for a woman with slow hands, which suited her, since she had nothing left to hurry toward. [88] The lighthouse had burned eleven years ago, and her son Elias with it, and she had sworn on the smoking stones that she would never again share light by making fire. [119] The village thought that a fine sentiment and a useless one; boats still came in at night, and rocks still waited. [140] So Mira gathered sea glass. [145] The tide brought it up the grotto's throat in drifts: bottle green, cider brown, the rare cobalt that had once been medicine jars. [168] She would weigh each piece in her palm before she chose it, testing not its heaviness but its patience, the way it held the little light there was. [196] Sea-worn glass is frosted, shy; polished glass is reflectively vibrant, throwing back whatever it is given twice as bright. [215] That was the whole of her plan: no flame, only surfaces. [226] For six years she had been setting the polished pieces into the grotto walls and up the cliff path, a slow constellation pressed into stone, angled to catch the moon. [256] Fishermen learned the pattern the way they had learned the old beam: three greens for the shoal, cobalt for the safe channel, brown where the current turned. [283] On clear nights it worked beautifully. [288] In mist, it failed, and mist was most nights. [297] She was grinding a thumb of white glass on the night the Kestrel came in blind, and as she worked the memories resurfaced the way they always did in the grotto, uninvited and exact. [331] Elias at seven, naming stars wrong on purpose to make her laugh. [343] Elias at nineteen, carrying oil up the tower stairs while she slept. [355] The sound the lantern room made when it went. [364] Her hands shook, and the glass slipped, and she sat back on the wet stone and let the shaking finish. [384] That was when she noticed the pool. [391] Deep in the grotto's last coil lay a basin the sea filled but never stirred, a pearl of stillness in all that roaring rock. [415] The mist could not settle on it. [422] And in its black face, doubled and trembling, hung the moon she could not otherwise see. [438] Water, she understood then, and not stone. [445] She hauled her tins to the basin's rim and worked until dawn, and the next night, and the next hundred nights. [466] She lined the basin floor with her brightest glass, cobalt at the center, white all around, each piece angled toward the crack in the grotto roof where the moon came through. [497] Mist rolled over the cliffs and could not touch the water that would not move. [512] The pool drank the moon and gave it back through the grotto's mouth, low across the swells, a cold green blooming that the fishermen came to call the lamp that was never lit. [545] On the first night it shone, the boy from the Kestrel, who had found harbor by luck that blind night, rowed in to see it. [570] He stood in the doorway of coiled rock with a zephyr coming off the water behind him, salt and gorse blossom, warm for the season. [595] "Does it hurt," he asked, "to make light out of what broke?" [607] Mira held up a chip of green, still rough, and let him weigh it. [621] "Somebody's bottle, once," she said. [628] "The sea does the breaking; I only do the patience." [638] She turned the chip so the moon caught it, and for a moment the boy's face was lit from below, green and gentle and utterly unburned. [664] She had not climbed a tower in eleven years, but she had never lost the sky; it came down to her now, into a pool, and stayed. [691] The boy rowed home to tell it, and the telling traveled the coast faster than any beam. [708] By spring there were three still pools glassed along that shore, and no one had struck a match to make a single one of them. [733] Mira kept polishing, humming names Elias had gotten wrong on purpose, until her hands were too slow even for slow work. [754] The mist never learned how to put out a mirror. [764]
