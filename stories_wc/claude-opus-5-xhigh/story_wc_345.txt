The chalk always came at dawn, on the deserted sidewalks of Tessel Market, where nobody lived anymore. [17] Mirren read the newest line twice, then a third time, her boots wet with orchard dew. [33] It said: WATER THE BUTTERFLY GARDEN FIRST, AND THE TREES WILL FOLLOW YOU HOME. [47] The handwriting belonged to Oso Reyn, who had been dead eleven years, and who had never once lied to her. [67] That was the strange part - not the ghost, but how strangely trustworthy the ghost had proven to be. [85]

Pell was an orchard planet riddled with floating rock arches, great stone ribs that hung above the groves like frozen waves. [106] Water came only when the nebula of respite drifted between Pell and its sun, dimming the sky to violet and letting the clouds live for forty days. [133] Forty days every six years: that was the whole harvest, the whole hope, the whole argument. [149] Oso had been the irrigation master when the first respite failed, and he had died on the arches trying to hang a siphon line alone. [174] Alone was his mistake, and he had spent eleven years chalking his apology across the empty pavement of a town that emptied because of it. [199]

Mirren was nine when the town broke apart, and the six people who took her in were strangers with nothing in common but a shared well. [225] Now, after strangers had become family, they were the Reyn Grove Collective, eight adults and a stubborn goat, and they still argued about everything. [249] This year they argued about the butterflies. [255] The pollinators had come from Old Earth stock and lived only in the walled butterfly garden at the center of the market square, and feeding them meant hauling water uphill while the trees went thirsty. [290] Kade wanted the trees; Sefa wanted the butterflies; Mirren wanted, mostly, to stop being the one who decided. [308]

So she brought them the chalk message, and then she brought them the bell. [321] The butterfly garden watering bell was a brass thing the size of a melon, cast with a wide skirt so it could be filled from the cistern and rung and rained out over the beds at once. [358] Children used to ring it at dawn, and the sound carried across nine kilometers of grove because the floating arches caught it and threw it back down. [385]

Oso's message, Mirren realized, was not about butterflies at all. [395] He wanted them to reexamine the old aqueduct survey, the one everyone had filed away as a failure. [413] They spread the brittle sheets on the garden wall while the nebula came up violet behind the arches. [431] Oso's plan had failed because it required forty hands on nine arches in a single morning, and eleven years ago there had been only his. [456] Now there were forty hands, if you counted every homestead within bell range. [468] That was what the ghost had been building all along, patient as sediment: not a warning, not a haunting, but a way to orchestrate collective effort out of people too proud to ask each other for help. [505] He could not lift a stone or open a valve; he could only write on empty sidewalks at dawn and trust that someone reading would carry the words to breakfast. [535]

They rang the bell on the fourth day of the respite. [546] Sefa filled it and swung it over the milkweed first, because Oso had asked, and the butterflies rose in a copper cloud that told every distant homestead the sound was real. [577] Then she rang it, and rang it, and the arches threw the note down the valley like a stone skipping. [597] They came with ladders and coils of hose and bramble scars on their forearms, forty three of them, arguing the whole way up. [620] By dusk the siphons hung, and water ran down through the floating arches in nine bright threads, thirty six days of rain routed into the roots of eleven thousand trees. [650]

Mirren went to the market square the next dawn, expecting thanks or instructions. [662] The chalk said only: KEEP THE GARDEN, IT IS HOW YOU HEAR EACH OTHER. [676] She never found his handwriting again. [682]

Some in the collective say he was never a ghost, only a habit of hope that the town needed a name for, and Mirren lets them say it. [710] She keeps a stub of chalk in her pocket anyway, and every dawn of every respite she writes the day's work on the empty pavement, in his slant, so the ones who come late will know where to climb. [749] The brambles still take the lower rows; the nebula still leaves. [760] But the bell hangs in the butterfly garden where anyone can reach it, and now everyone knows what it means. [780]
