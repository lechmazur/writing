The Stillness came in the winter I turned forty, and after that no fist in the world could finish its journey. [21] Knives turned in the hand, ropes slackened, and every hard word had to do the work that blood once did. [41] I am Coyle, harbor-master of Grennet, and I had built my whole life on being feared. [57] Mirren unloaded the herring boats with a grace that made the other dockers stop and watch. [73] She carried crates the way water carries light, hip and shoulder rolling, never spilling a scale, and she never once looked at the sea while she worked. [100] That was how I knew she was hiding from it. [110] In my office hung the harpoon my great-grandfather took off a hanged pirate, and socketed into its shaft was a plate of whalebone scrimshaw, scratched with an island that no chart admits. [142] Grennet folk called it Hollow Ait, and swore it rose out of the sublittoral fringe where the kelp begins, then sank again before a man could name it. [170] The scrimshaw had a habit I hated: it unlocked guarded hearts. [181] Hold it and your truest thing came up out of you like a cork. [195] Before the Stillness I used it on smugglers, and I never had to raise a hand; after the Stillness it was all I had left. [220] Mirren came for it on a night of low water. [230] She stood in my doorway, salt-wet, and said, "Give me the bone, Coyle, or I will take it." [248] "You can't take anything anymore," I said, "and neither can I, which is the only reason we are being kind to each other." [271] She laughed, and it was not a kind laugh, and I loved her for it. [286] So we fought the only way left to us. [295] She told me I hoarded the harpoon because I was frightened of a world where nobody had to obey me. [315] I told her she had spent eleven years lifting other people's fish so she would not have to ask what she was. [337] Then she put her palm flat on the scrimshaw, and the truth came up out of her like a cork. [357] Her mother had come out of the fringe and gone back into it. [370] Her own bones itched at every spring tide. [378] The grace the dockers admired was not a gift for hauling crates; it was a body remembering how to move through water. [400] And Hollow Ait did not rise on the tide at all. [411] It rose by the call of a whale from the deep, and it stayed only as long as the call held, and it was the one place where her kind could step out of their human shape and be met. [451] The pirate had carved the bone from a whale he killed mid-song, and the carving had been holding the note ever since, stopped, unanswered, keeping the island down. [479] To free it, someone had to detach the plate from the harpoon shaft that had made it a weapon. [498] "You do it," she said, "because it is your family's crime and my whole life." [513] My hands would not have managed it before the Stillness, because letting go had always looked like losing. [531] Now there was nothing to win. [537] I worked the old pins out with a marlinspike while she stood close enough that I could hear her breathing, and neither of us said a tender word, and both of us were shaking. [571] The bone came loose in my palm, warm as a living thing. [583] The note finished itself. [587] Down in the black water something enormous answered, a call so low it moved the dust in my office and the ache in my chest. [612] We ran to the end of the pier and watched Hollow Ait come up out of the kelp, black and streaming, ringed with light. [636] "Come with me," Mirren said, which was the cruelest thing she could have offered, since the island only holds what belongs to the sea. [660] "Go and be what you are," I said. "I'll keep the harbor." [672] She kissed me hard, the way you'd shove someone, and then she went off the pier without a splash. [691] I saw her stand on that shore for a moment, tall and shining and no longer graceful in any way a docker would recognize, and then the whale stopped singing and the island detached itself from the world again. [730] I still keep the scrimshaw on my desk, unmounted, and I let anyone hold it who wants to. [748] In a world where nobody can be forced, an unlocked heart is the only cargo worth moving, and every spring tide I hear her out past the kelp, arguing with me still, and loving me still, in a voice too large for any harbor to hold.
