The phoenix rose over Kesh nine years ago, and the ash has been settling ever since. [16] Anselm arrived in the glassblower's courtyard on the ninth anniversary, carrying a paintbox and one brass reed tongue prised from a concertina. [38] He was colorblind, which is why the Registry hired him. [48] Painted glass lies to ordinary eyes; ruby and emerald glazes hide the bubbles and stress-lines beneath, but Anselm saw only greys, and in grey a flaw cannot hide. [76] Kesh had slept through the burning. [82] Those who could afford it lay down in Ossa's glass cocoons before the fire and let the ash-decades pass over them like weather. [105] Waking, though, was never free. [110] Every awakening had to be bartered: a reed tuned to the sleeper's own imperfect heartbeat, and someone willing to surrender a waking of their own. [135] The fire had melted nine-tenths of the city's concertinas, and reeds could not be forged from ash. [152] Anselm's reed had been his mother's, filed slightly flat by her own thumb, and it was meant for him. [171] When the rebuilding grew too ugly to bear, he would lie down, and the reed would call him back to a finished city. [194] Ossa the glassblower set him to work cataloguing her forty-one cocoons, and did not mention the girl. [211] Tamsin was eleven, mute since the smoke, and she kept the courtyard's planters. [224] She spoke in plants. [228] On his first morning Anselm found rosemary and a sprig of yew laid across his stool: remembrance, and the dead. [248] On the second, bindweed coiled around a cracked pot beside cocoon nineteen. [260] By the fifth he understood he was being courted, argued with, and gently steered. [274] Cocoon nineteen held her mother. [279] The Registry's ledger listed the woman as unwakeable; her reed had been lost, and Ossa, who was her sister, had spent her own barter years ago on a customer who paid in coal. [312] Anselm painted the cocoons in greys and pencilled their faults in the margins: a chill-crack here, a seed bubble there. [332] Nineteen was the worst of them, and the most beautiful, a spiral of trapped bubbles running from throat to knee, like a held breath. [356] Ossa, seeing only green, had thought it flawless for thirty years. [367] Tamsin's arrangements grew subtler. [371] Sorrel for sourness when he complained about the ash; a single perfect apple blossom on the day he admitted the ash-light made his greys sing. [396] She never asked for the reed. [402] Instead she set out, morning after morning, arrangements that were slightly wrong on purpose: a lopsided posy, a fern with one frond torn, a cutting rooted crooked in the jar. [432] Each one, he had to admit, was lovelier than the symmetrical ones. [444] It was the innocently cunning argument of a child who could not speak it aloud: nothing whole is worth waiting for. [465] He tested his mother's reed against cocoon nineteen on the ninth night. [477] Held to the glass and breathed across, the reed tongue buzzed slightly flat, and the spiral of bubbles trembled in answer. [498] Two flawed things, matched. [502] His mother's thumb had filed a pitch that fit a stranger's heart better than his own. [518] Anselm sat in the courtyard until dawn, among the kilns and the crooked ferns, doing the arithmetic of barter. [537] One reed, one waking, and the price paid by whoever surrendered his own. [550] He would never sleep out the ash years now. [559] He would live in the unfinished city, in scaffolding and grey weather, and paint it. [574] At sunrise he laid the reed tongue in Tamsin's empty jar, where the crooked cutting had been. [591] Ossa wept, filed the barter into the Registry, and sounded the note. [603] The woman in cocoon nineteen took a first cracked breath and began to awaken, and the glass sang all along its spiral of faults. [627] Tamsin's answer waited for him that evening on the stool: rue, for regret, and beneath it, roots and all, a small stubborn dandelion pushing out of ash. [654] He mixed his greys and painted them both, exactly as they were. [666] He could not tell you what color the dandelion was. [676] He could tell you the exact shape of its imperfection, and that it was enough. [691]
