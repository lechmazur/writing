The fog came off the harbor every morning like a held breath, and Neve walked into it because it was the only weather that matched her. [26] She had been an aromatherapist for eleven years, and for nine months she had smelled nothing at all. [44] A fever had taken it, quietly, the way a tide takes a footprint. [57] The lamp in Aurel's water clock repair shop was the only warm thing on Cooper Street, and it reached her as a smear of amber through the glass. [85] Aurel kept the panes deliberately fogged with beeswax and steam; he claimed a clockmaker who saw the street too clearly stopped noticing people and started noticing traffic. [112] Through those occluded views the fishwives became shapes of hurry, the schoolboys became shapes of dread, and every shape asked to be guessed at, which is to say humanized. [141] Neve had learned to do the same with words. [149] In the old days clients told her what they wanted - lavender, bergamot, sweet orange - and she listened underneath, reading between lines, and found grief where they had said insomnia, and rage where they had said headaches. [185] Her ledgers were not recipe books; they were atlases that mapped emotional terrain, ridge by ridge, and the oils had only ever been the roads. [210] Without a nose, she believed, the atlas was a fraud. [220] The bell over Aurel's door made its small wet sound. [230] "You are early," he said, not looking up. [238] "The fog is early," she said. [244] On his bench lay the Marchetti clepsydra from the birthing house, gutted, its brass viscera laid out on felt like something waiting to be forgiven. [269] For sixty years the midwives had timed contractions by its drip, because a mother in labor cannot read a dial but can follow water. [293] It had stopped in September, and since then the birthing rooms had used a kitchen timer that shrieked. [311] "The regulator screw is stripped," Aurel said. [318] "Left-hand thread, 1898, nobody cuts them anymore." [325] He opened the drawer where he kept the things he did not sell. [338] Inside, on a square of flannel, lay a midwife's birthing clamp, its jaws dulled, and in its hinge a small brass screw with a left-hand thread. [364] "My mother's," he said. [368] "She delivered four hundred children with it, including me." [377] Neve understood the arithmetic of it and said nothing, because the space under his words was where he lived. [396] He turned the clamp over twice. [402] "A relic keeps one story," he said finally. [410] "A screw keeps counting." [414] He backed it out with a jeweler's driver, and his hands shook, and his voice stayed level, and Neve recognized the exact temperature of that: trembling calm. [441] It was the temperature of every woman who had ever sat in her treatment room and said, "I'm fine, I just want something for sleep." [466] The screw went into the clepsydra. [472] Water found its old channel. [477] The drip began, slow as a resting pulse, and the shop filled with a sound that Neve could hear perfectly well. [498] "Take it up the hill," Aurel said. [505] "Ilma is in her second day." [511] She carried the clock up the hill within the hush of morning fog, the town reduced to soft shapes she had to imagine into people. [536] At the birthing house the midwife took the clepsydra without ceremony and set it on the sill, and the drip began marking Ilma's minutes. [560] Ilma looked at Neve with the flat eyes of someone past politeness and said, "Tell me it smells clean in here." [581] Neve could not smell the room, so she read it instead: the untouched tea, the husband's coat folded too neatly, the fear dressed up as a question about hygiene. [610] "It smells like rain on warm stone," Neve said, "and you are not doing this badly, you are doing it slowly, which is different." [634] Ilma laughed, then wept, then breathed with the water, drip by drip, for four more hours. [650] The child arrived at eleven, ordinary and enormous, the way all of them are. [664] Walking home, Neve understood what she had been mourning and what she had not lost. [678] The oils had been roads; her work had always been the atlas, and the atlas required no nose, only the patience to unveil ordinary miracles that people carried in unmarked jars. [709] The fog was thinning over the harbor. [716] She stopped at Aurel's window, where the world was still a smear of amber and motion, and she stood a while, guessing, and mostly getting it right. [743] Inside, on the flannel, the clamp lay open-jawed and unscrewed, finally useless, finally at rest. [758]
