Tamsin Vell sorted seeds by feel, by rattle, by the small dry music each kind made in its tin. [19] Up in the ashen skyborne arbor, where the great grey tree hung its roots over the valley like a chandelier of bones, order was the only thing that kept a person from falling. [52] She had eleven trays, forty-one tins, and one speck of moonstone cabochon dust, which she kept in a folded scrap of waxed paper and did not consider frivolous. [80] The valley folk had a story about the arbor, as valley folk do. [93] They said that once each autumn came the hush where past meets prophecy: an hour when the last echo of everything that had happened brushed against the first whisper of everything that would. [126] In that hour the tree sheds leaves in a pattern, and the pattern is a sentence, and whoever reads it, the grandmothers insisted, emerges transformed always. [152] Tamsin wanted, more than warmth or company, to decode the message in falling leaves. [166] Nine autumns she had failed, because she had been trying to read the leaves where they landed. [183] This autumn she tried something her grandmother had done and nobody had bothered to remember: she built tiny monuments to forgotten things. [205] A thumb-high cairn of plum pits for the drowned mill at Ossary Ford. [218] A ring of acorn caps for the word "sennight," which nobody in the valley said anymore. [234] A splinter of slate standing upright in moss for her grandmother's name, Iselde, which she had not spoken aloud in six years. [256] The moss came up around each monument overnight, bright and wet and pleased with itself, and Tamsin found she was pleased too, which surprised her. [281] Delight, it turned out, grew like moss: in the damp, in the dark, in the cracks of a very orderly life. [302] The hush arrived on the ninth night of ash-fall, and it arrived as silence does in high places, all at once and from below. [326] The wind quit; the roots stopped their creaking; the valley below went the color of a held breath. [344] Leaves began to come down grey and slow, and they did not land on the branch-decks where Tamsin had spent nine autumns kneeling. [367] They landed on the monuments. [371] One leaf across the plum-pit cairn. [377] Two crossed over the acorn caps. [383] A long curled one hooked itself around the slate splinter and hung there, trembling, like a letter waiting to be posted. [404] Tamsin took out the waxed paper and tipped the moonstone dust speck onto her fingertip. [419] It had come off a cabochon her grandmother had polished, a blue-white pinhead of a thing that caught what nothing else caught. [441] She set it on the curled leaf and used a seed-straw to prod the leaf gently, the way you turn a page you are afraid of. [467] The speck rolled along a vein, and where it rolled the ash lifted, and letters stood up out of the grey like frost ferns on a window. [494] The mill leaf said WHAT IS TENDED; the acorn leaves said RETURNS; the slate leaf said CHANGED. [511] It was not a prophecy about kings or floods, which was what the valley had always expected. [528] It was an instruction, and it was also a description, and Tamsin understood that it had been the same sentence for a thousand autumns, waiting for somebody patient enough to build it a place to land. [564] She said Iselde out loud. [569] She said sennight, and Ossary Ford, and the names of eleven seeds she had never planted because she liked them sorted. [590] Then the hush let go, the roots creaked, and the wind came back smelling of wet stone. [607] In spring she came down the root-ladder with her tins empty and her pockets full of turned earth. [625] The valley called it the old story proving itself out, because whoever reads the leaves emerges transformed always, and here was the orderly seed sorter of the ashen arbor gone gloriously, mossily untidy. [658] Tamsin let them say it. [663] She knew the truer version: nothing had been added to her and nothing taken. [677] She had simply tended small forgotten things until they returned to her changed, which is the only way anything ever comes back. [699] Up in the arbor, in the moss, the monuments are still there, and every autumn there are more of them. [719]
</story>
