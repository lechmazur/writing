The village called Anselm the reflective locksmith, though they meant only that he was quiet. [15] He knew the truer reason: he had spent forty years learning that every lock is a mirror of the hand that made it. [38] That summer the river had come through the barley and taken the mill, and afterward the valley entered what the elders called the lull of a wounded season. [66] No planting, no threshing, no festivals; only mud drying into brick and people speaking softly, as if loudness might invite more water. [88] It was in that quiet that Anselm climbed to the marble cavern above the terraces. [103] Generations had gone up there for the harvest of echoes, the one night each autumn when the cavern gave back every sound it had swallowed all year. [130] Wedding vows, arguments, a child's first word, the last breath of an old shepherd: all of it returned at once, layered and glassy, and the village stood in the dark and wept and laughed. [164] The harvest was governed by a timer mechanism set into the cavern's throat by someone long dead, a brass thing of nested wheels that opened the stone shutters at the exact hour. [196] The flood had reached even there, and the mechanism was broken. [207] Silt in the escapement, a snapped spring, three teeth gone from the great wheel. [221] Anselm could have pried the shutters open with a bar, but echoes released early come out thin and scrambled, a noise like gravel. [244] The hour mattered; the cavern had to be full and the air cold. [257] So he decided to reverse-engineer the mechanism, working across the lull of that wounded season, mostly alone. [274] He had no drawings, no maker's name, nothing but the wheels themselves and the cavern that had taught them their rhythm. [295] What saved him was the marble. [300] The cavern walls were polished to a wet gray shine, and each morning a blade of sun came through the eastern crack and struck them, and the reflection walked the floor like a slow animal. [335] Anselm began studying the reflection angles at different times, marking the floor with chalk at dawn, at noon, at the blue end of afternoon, day after day into the cold. [365] The builder, he realized, had cut the walls to make the cavern its own clock. [380] The chalk marks converged: on one date, in one hour, every reflected blade would meet at the mechanism's face. [399] From the spacing of the marks he could count the missing teeth and calculate the tension the snapped spring had once held. [421] It helped, too, that Anselm's eyes were peripherally focused, a flaw since boyhood. [434] Look straight at a dim thing and it vanished for him; glance beside it and it bloomed. [451] The faintest reflections, the ones that told him the winter angles, only existed at the edge of his sight, so he learned to work looking slightly away, chalk in hand, head turned like a listening bird. [487] His wife asked him, once, why he gave the whole broken season to a machine that served one night a year. [508] He said he wanted to store wonder in the cellar of reason, for later. [522] Feelings rot, he told her; a flood eats a mill and a year later nobody can remember what the wheel sounded like. [544] But a mechanism understood is wonder pickled and shelved, ready to be brought up in a hard winter and opened. [564] He cut three teeth from a horseshoe blank, wound a new spring from wire meant for a granary lock, and cleaned the silt out with a feather. [591] On the night the marks predicted, the village came up with lanterns, doubting him, because the harvest had never fallen so late. [613] The wheels turned, the shutters ground open, and the marble gave back the year. [627] The flood was in it: the roar, the mill beam cracking, a woman shouting a name across the water. [646] But so was the sound of the whole valley digging, and a boy laughing at a frog, and Anselm's own chalk squeaking across marble, hundreds of mornings of it. [675] People wept, as they always did, then went down the hill lighter. [687] Anselm stayed behind and looked at the mechanism sideways, the only way he could see it well, ticking in the cold. [708] He had the whole of it now in his head: the ratios, the angles, the reason under the marvel. [727] It would keep. [730] In February, when the valley was gray and the bread was rationed and his wife's hands ached, he would go down into that cellar and bring it up and set it on the table between them, and there would be light in the room.
