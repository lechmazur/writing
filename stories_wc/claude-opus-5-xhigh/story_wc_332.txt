The archive on Vesk stood on stilts above black water, and I opened it at three in the morning and closed it when the sun cleared the reef. [28] Odd-hour librarian, the registry called me, as though the hours were the odd thing and not the islands. [46] In the Whispering Archipelago the wind moves through the hollow limestone and returns your own sentences to you a half hour later, mispronounced. [69] Nobody sleeps well here, so somebody has to shelve books at four. [81]

Selk came in on a night of no moon, carrying a watercolor palette in a tin the size of a hand. [102] She was a cartographer of tides, and she wanted the sailing logs of 1911, the year the eastern islands were charted wrong on purpose. [126] We talked until the reef went gold. [133] That is the hush of improbable beginnings: two strangers in an empty room agreeing that the world is stranger than the maps admit. [156]

She came back nineteen nights, and on the twentieth she was gone, and the tin was on the counter with a folded paper under it. [181] The paper held rows of numbers and small dry swatches of color, and no explanation at all. [198] I called it the cipher of longing, first as a joke, then because it was accurate. [214] The numbers were times. [218] The swatches were what the sky owed me at those times. [229] Selk did not paint with pigment; she painted with light, and the palette was only her key. [246] I understood it the way you understand a language you have been hearing in your sleep. [262]

So I borrowed the harbormaster's camera and began the work through sunrise photography, one dawn per line. [279] The method was simple and merciless: stand where the log entries pointed, expose the film at the listed minute, then hold the print against its swatch. [305] When the two colors agreed, that island had a letter for me. [317] When they disagreed, I rowed home with nothing but salt in my hair and waited for the wind to hand me back my own swearing. [342] It took eleven months, because a dawn cannot be repeated, only waited for. [355] Between dawns I shelved books, stamped cards, and read her logs until I could recite the fraudulent coastline by heart. [375] The library felt like a lamp left on in a house nobody lived in: a luminous absence, her chair still angled toward the tide charts. [400]

The message assembled itself slowly, one letter per island, and it was not a love note. [416] It was instructions for how to vitalize a dream that had run away from its dreamer. [432] By then my own motive had narrowed to one line: to capture a runaway dream on film, the way you would coax a lost animal into a lit doorway. [461]

Selk had dreamed, at nine years old, of an island shaped like a sleeping dog, and she had spent thirty years learning that it existed only in her. [489] The 1911 surveyors had drawn it wrong on purpose because they had dreamed it too, and could not bear to leave it off the chart. [514] A dream that runs away can be brought back to life, her cipher said, but not by the one who lost it; it needs a second witness. [541] That was why she had come to a room that is only open at odd hours, to find someone awake at the hour when things begin. [567]

On the last morning the numbers sent me east of the last charted rock, and I rowed out with the tin in my coat. [591] The final swatch was a color I had no name for, somewhere between rust and lilac. [607] At 5:41 the sun broke and the sky produced it exactly, and I pressed the shutter, and the whispering islands went quiet for the length of a breath. [635]

When the noise came back, there was a low green shape on the water where no shape had been, shaped, I will say it, like a sleeping dog. [663] Selk was standing on its beach with her hands open, thirty years late and exactly on time. [680]

I have not decided whether to file the photograph or the chart correction. [693] The archive still opens at three. [699] I keep the watercolor palette on the counter, open, in case somebody else arrives with a dream that got away from them, and needs a witness who is already awake. [729]
