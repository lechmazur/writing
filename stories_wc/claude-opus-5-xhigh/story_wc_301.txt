Three weeks after stopping the medication, the river got loud again. [11] Not louder in the ears, but in the ledger, where every eddy suddenly demanded a name. [27] I am the river's chronicler, which is a title nobody granted me and nobody has yet revoked. [44] For nine years I have logged the Sedge: flood crests, drowned sheep, bottles, boots, the exact hour the herons return. [64] Every entry carries a map reference, because a fact without a map reference is only a feeling wearing a coat. [84] I work out of Halberd Halt, a disused railway station so overgrown with ivy that the platform sign reads only ALT. [105] The ivy has pulled the canopy down at one corner and holds it up at another, which is more than I can say for myself. [130] My brother Tom went into the water at the Halberd bend on a February night, and I am the one who told the search team where to look. [158] I gave them a grid reference two squares east of the truth. [170] Digits transposed at three in the morning; forty minutes lost; the coroner said it would not have mattered. [188] The coroner is not a chronicler; he is allowed to say things like that. [202] Tom left an accordion in the ticket office, its bellows patched with silver tape in four places, each patch a different year of neglect. [226] I could not play it and could not sell it, so it sat under the timetable like an animal waiting out a winter. [249] The tablets kept me level and kept me vague. [258] Level is a fine thing for a person and a poor thing for a witness, so I stopped, carefully, the way I do everything: daringly methodical, halving the dose across six weeks, logging my sleep in the same ledger as the herons. [300] What came back was grief with all its colours turned up, and also the old compulsion to verify. [318] That compulsion is why I keep reading the station's bathroom walls. [329] The ladies' toilet at Halberd Halt has been a public confessional since 1974, and anonymous people do not bother to lie. [350] I have transcribed four hundred lines of that graffiti wisdom into an appendix: birth dates, betrayals, one recipe, a lot of arithmetic about love. [374] In April I found a new line under the cistern, in carpenter's pencil: "The river keeps nothing. SU 7431 9028. Go and see." [397] It was the correct reference for the Halberd bend, the one I had failed to give. [413] Someone knew, then. [416] Someone in this valley had stood where I stood and written the number I could not. [432] I went at dawn with the ledger, a tape measure, and, for reasons I did not examine, the accordion. [451] The bend was a green throat of alder, the water low and brown and unhurried. [466] On the bank someone had set a flat stone, and on the stone, in the same carpenter's pencil, a second line: "He was already gone at the first minute. Ask the doctor. She won't tell you unless you ask." [505] I did ask. [508] The pathologist met me in a car park and said the word "instantaneous" twice, and then said she had waited nine years for the sister to come and ask. [537] There was a carpenter's pencil behind her ear; she noticed me noticing and did not explain. [553] She did not allude to my two-square error, though everyone in the valley has read the ledger where I confessed it. [573] Instead she gave me timings, minute by minute, and let me copy them down. [587] Back at the Halt, I entered it properly: date, hour, SU 7431 9028, and the finding, which was that forty minutes had bought nothing but my own long punishment. [616] Verification is not absolution, but it is the road that runs beside it. [629] That evening I lifted the accordion out from under the timetable and worked the taped bellows until they sighed. [648] Air came through the patches anyway, thin and wrong, and I played three chords Tom used to play against the ivy and the dark. [672] It sounded like a man clearing his throat before speaking, and it did not stop hurting, and I did not want it to. [695] In the morning I took the carpenter's pencil I keep for wet days into the bathroom and added a line of my own beneath the others. [721] "Forgiveness is a place, not a feeling. SU 7431 9028. Bring the truth with you," I wrote. [738] I did not sign it. [743] The next chronicler will come along, find it, verify it, and be furious and grateful, in that order. [761] That is how the wall works, and, I am beginning to think, how people do. [776]
