The observatory sat above the clouds like a held breath, and Ilya Marek kept it. [15] For eleven years the state had called him a censored poet, as if that were a job description, and in a sense it had become one. [41] They took his lines; he learned to write in the gaps. [52] Tonight the storm came up the valley in slow blue pulses, lighting the cloud-floor from beneath so the whole world looked like a lantern with a moth in it. [81] In the darkroom under the dome he set the darkroom timer for four minutes and began his vigil. [99] He was photographing lightning, plate after plate, trying to understand the cartography of scars. [113] The burns on the sky branched exactly like the burns on his forearm, and both branched like the redaction marks that had crawled across his manuscripts for a decade. [142] If he could map one, he believed, he could map them all, and mapping was the beginning of forgiveness. [161] The timer ticked. [165] Someone knocked. [167] Nobody knocked at four thousand meters; the funicular had stopped running at dusk. [180] He pressed the timer's pause lever, and the vigil hung there, suspended mid-count, the plate still drinking the storm. [199] The woman in the doorway wore the grey coat of the Bureau of Cultural Hygiene and a face he knew from a hundred stamped rejections. [224] Vesna Halic, senior reader, the hand that had unwritten him. [234] She was soaked and furiously tranquil, the way people are when they have spent the whole climb deciding what to say. [255] "You never asked who carried them out," she said. [264] He had asked, for years, of the empty air. [273] Twice a season, a sealed envelope wedged in an old bus timetable at the abandoned halt below the treeline had carried his uncensored verses down to the coast, to presses that printed them under other names. [309] He had left them there in blind faith; something always took them. [321] "The timetable was mine," Vesna said. [327] "I chose that halt because no bus has stopped there since 1979." [339] Lightning went off beneath them, and in that white second her official expression slipped the way all masks slip when the light comes from the wrong direction. [366] He saw the shine along her jaw, the pale delta of old burn, forking down into her collar. [384] "The press fire in Kolna," she said. [391] "I was inside." [394] "I was carrying your books out, not in." [402] So the redactions had been a kind of surgery: cut the lines that would get him shot, keep the poet breathing. [423] She had spent eleven years learning to rarefy him, to thin his work to the exact air a censor's lungs could tolerate, while smuggling the full weight of it down a road no bus used. [458] "Why climb up now?" he asked. [464] "Because they closed my department this morning," she said, "and there is nobody left to hide you from." [482] She took from her coat a sealed envelope, soft with rain, and set it on the bench beside the timer. [502] Inside were the deletions: every line she had ever struck, copied out in her own hand, dated, ordered, a decade of his amputated language kept like a ledger of wounds. [532] It was, he understood, a map. [538] Not of the sky, of him: where the cuts had gone in, how deep, in what direction the scar tissue had grown a new grammar. [563] Outside, the storm walked the cloud-floor, and the dome shivered with a wonder that tasted of ozone and cold iron. [583] He released the pause lever. [588] The timer resumed its small patient ticking, taking up the vigil exactly where it had been set down, and the plate went on gathering light. [613] When it rang they developed the plate together in red light: a scar of lightning printed across the negative, branching, indifferent, exact. [635] The air up here rarefied everything, breath, voices, grudges, until only the essential remained. [649] "Read me one," Vesna said, "the way you wrote it." [659] So he opened her ledger and read the missing lines aloud into the storm, and above the clouds, for the first time in eleven years, nothing came back to strike them out. [691]
