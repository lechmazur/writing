For sixteen years Ilsa Vantor had recorded every storm that crossed Ward Nine, timed to the quarter-hour, and for sixteen years nobody had asked to read her ledgers. [28] That suited her. [31] A record kept for no reader is the purest kind; it can only be honest, and honesty practiced alone becomes a discipline rather than a performance. [57] She had come to think of it as tending a small lamp in a cellar: nobody warmed by it but the tender, and still the oil worth buying. [85]

The Ward was a rogue quarantine zone, though nobody inside used that phrase. [98] The outer authorities had lifted the cordon eleven years ago; the Sanitary Council had simply declined to notice, kept the gates chained, and continued issuing fog warnings and burial fees. [128] Letters crossed the wall by pigeon, because the wire had been cut in the first panic and never spliced. [147]

On the evening it began, Ilsa walked home from the loft on Ferrier Street and knew, three streets in, that she was being followed. [171] She did not hurry. [175] A woman of sixty-one with a satchel of barometers hurries for nobody. [187] It was only after being followed home, after she had latched her door and set the kettle, that she found a carrier pigeon's message tube on the courtyard rim, small and brass, still smelling faintly of pigeon. [224]

Her courtyard at dusk was the quietest place she knew: four walls of soft brick, a fountain reduced to a drip, doves settling in the eaves in descending layers of sound until the silence had a grain to it, like wood. [265] She sat, unscrewed the tube, and read four words in pencil: Compare the columns sideways. [280]

The boy who had followed her was standing in the archway, hat in hands, not hiding at all. [298] "Bex Almady," he said, "Council loft-clerk - I sort the birds nobody claims." [310] He was perhaps nineteen, and credibly curious - not the false curiosity of an informer fishing for a slip, but the kind that had cost him sleep. [336] She could hear it in how he asked his question: not what do your ledgers say, but why does the Council never quote them? [360]

Ilsa poured him tea and fetched the ledgers - sixty-four of them, one per season, each column ruled by her own hand. [381] She had always read them downward, storm after storm, year after year, the way a river reads itself. [399] The note asked her to read them the other way. [409] So they worked by progressing laterally: wind direction laid beside the Council's fog bulletins, humidity beside the quarantine extensions, temperature beside the burial fees, sliding across the page instead of down it. [441] Bex had brought the loft's own record of undelivered letters. [451]

By full dark, with the doves quiet and the fountain still counting, the pattern surfaced the way a fish surfaces: sideways, whole, unmistakable. [474] Every declared plague fog fell on a night of low pressure and a south wind - the wind that carried smoke down off the lime kilns at Marrow Hill and pooled it in the low streets. [509] The coughing, the burning eyes, the deaths among the old: kiln smoke, faithfully, for sixteen years. [525] There was no contagion in Ward Nine. [532] There was a wind, and a Council that had learned to bill for it. [546]

Ilsa found she was not angry, which surprised her less than it should have. [560] Anger belonged to people who had expected otherwise. [568] She had kept her columns clean for years without knowing why, and now the reason had walked in wearing a loft-clerk's coat. [590]

"Who sent the tube?" she asked. [596] Bex flushed. [598] "I could not prove anything from my side alone, so I had to intuit where the proof was hiding - and I guessed it was hiding in you, sideways." [628]

They copied the findings out that night, twelve fair copies in her small upright script. [643] The brass tube went out at first light, not over the wall to a world that had stopped caring, but three streets across it, to the kiln-men, the midwives, the gate guards' wives. [676]

It took nine months for the chains to come off the Ostler Gate, and it was not dramatic; it was lateral, one household at a time. [702] Ilsa did not attend the opening. [708] She was in her courtyard at dusk, as she had been on ten thousand evenings, logging a mild northeasterly and a pressure of 1014, while the doves came down through their layers of quiet. [742] The lamp in the cellar had turned out to be a lighthouse, but only because she had never once trimmed it for an audience. [766] Bex arrived at seven with the empty tube, and she taught him how to rule a column. [783]
