The station's walls had gone spongey, and Nell pressed a thumb into the plaster the way one tests a bruise. [20] It gave, held the dent, and slowly rose again, breathing. [30] Everything on the coast did that now, three winters after the jet stream shifted south and hung above them like a wet rope. [53] Experimental Weather Station Ivarr had been built to measure a climate that no longer existed, and so its board of adjudicators had inherited a second duty: judging the old exiles. [83]

Nell was a map librarian, which meant she was accustomed to arguing with paper that could not argue back. [102] She had come to argue with eleven damp officials instead. [112] In her satchel lay four shipwrecked journals, salvaged from the brig Cassandra Vane, their pages swollen to the thickness of bread. [133] Beside them, wrapped in oilcloth, was a copper nail she had pried from her great-grandmother's coffin with her own hands. [153]

Ingrid Aursund had been exiled in the year the jet stream first wobbled, for what the record called 'seditious forecasting.' [173] She had warned the harbor towns to move uphill, and the harbor towns had voted her onto a ship instead. [193] Exile, by statute, was reversible. [198] A descendant could stand before the board and pose one formal question; if the board could not answer it, the exile was undone, the name restored, the bones brought home. [228] Nell had rehearsed her question in the map room for six months, and she felt nervously confident, which is the only honest way to feel. [253]

The hearing room smelled of mildew and hot instruments. [262] Anemometers ticked behind the wall, measuring winds that had stopped mattering. [273] Chairman Boell, a soft man with soft hands, folded them and said she might present. [288]

'Ingrid did not stop warning us when we put her out,' Nell said. [301] 'She kept forecasting from the deck of the Cassandra Vane, through coded stanzas in her journals, because a mutinous crew would have thrown any plain chart overboard.' [328]

She laid the swollen books open. [333] The verses looked like sailor's doggerel: gulls, sweethearts, a widow counting nails. [345] But the first letters of each line gave latitudes, the refrains gave barometric pressures, and the widow's nails gave the key. [366] Nell set the copper nail on the table, and its stamped mark, CV 47, matched the tally in the fourth journal exactly. [388] The nail had come from the ship's own sheathing; the crew had used copper fastenings from the Cassandra Vane to build her coffin, out on the exile island, because they had nothing else. [421] Which meant the men who had scorned her had also buried her carefully, and had carried her journals home, and had said nothing for ninety years. [447]

Nell felt the dread of the place settle into her shoulders, spongey, patient, the same dread that lived in the walls. [468] 'Decoded, the stanzas describe the jet stream's southern collapse,' she said. [479] 'Ninety years early, from a leaking deck, in rhyme, by a woman you had already thrown away.' [496]

Boell asked, mildly, what her question was, since the statute allowed only one. [509] She had planned to ask whether Ingrid's forecast had been correct, because that answer was safe and provable and would have won. [531] But she had come to honor a bravery nobody had ever noticed, not to win a filing. [548] So she asked instead: 'Which of you, put out on that island, would have kept measuring the sky?' [566]

The anemometers ticked. [569] Water moved somewhere inside the ceiling, unhurried. [576] Eleven adjudicators looked at the copper nail, and not one of them answered, because the honest answer would have cost each of them something they had spent ninety years not saying. [607] Boell wrote reversed in the margin, and signed, and pushed the paper across the table without looking up. [625]

In spring they brought Ingrid Aursund home on a cutter, and Nell walked ahead of the box with the nail in her fist. [648] She had the journals reproduced, stanzas and translations side by side, and shelved them in the map room under Charts, Predictive. [669] The coast stayed wet; the walls kept breathing; the dread did not lift, because dread of that kind never does. [689] But schoolchildren from the uphill towns came to read the verses aloud, and they learned that someone had told the truth in code from a sinking deck. [716] Nell, who still believed paper could save a coastline, nailed the copper nail above the door, where the damp could not eat it. [739] Copper does not rust; it only greens, and goes on holding.
