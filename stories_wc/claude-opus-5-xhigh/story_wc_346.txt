The lighthouse did not appear on charts, because it stood at the place where charts run out. [17] Mira Vance climbed its stairs at dusk, as she had climbed them for eleven thousand dusks, because dusk was the only hour the building permitted. [42] She was the last doomsday clock adjuster, and the clock was waiting. [54] It filled the lantern room where a lamp should have been: brass viscera, seven concentric dials, an escapement the size of a plow. [77] Where the great lens once turned, someone long dead had mounted a pane of hush, a single sheet of silence poured and cooled like glass. [102] Sound went into it and did not come out, or came out somewhere else, in some other century, softened. [121] The pane was beautifully flawed. [126] A hairline curve ran through its center like a river seen from very high up, and Mira had learned, over decades, that the flaw was not damage but doorway. [155] Perfect silence keeps everything. [159] Cracked silence leaks. [162]

Her job had never been to stop the ending. [171] Adjusters before her had tried, winding the dials backward until the gears wept metal, and all they bought was a stutter. [192] Mira's task was smaller and stranger: to set the final chime so that what crossed the pane, across the hush of time's final ripple, would be a lullaby. [220]

She carried a broken puppet head in her coat pocket. [230] It was carved pine, painted with a girl's round cheeks, the jaw hinge snapped off in a fire that had taken the rest of the marionette and the rest of the town. [262] Her daughter Ilse had made it at nine years old, badly, gloriously, and had sung to it every night the same six-note tune, off-key on the fifth note, always. [291] That was the tune. [295] That was what Mira intended to whisper as a lullaby across a thousand lifetimes. [309] Because the clock did not measure one ending; it measured the ending, which recurs, which folds, which arrives again for every world that ever learns to build a lighthouse. [338] Somewhere a mother would be carrying a child through smoke, and Mira wanted that child to hear something other than the roar. [360]

You could not send a song through the pane directly; silence rejected melody the way water rejects oil. [378] It had to go through nested patterns. [385] Mira set the seven dials so that each cycle contained the next: the escapement's tick divided into six, the six divided again into six, down through the smallest wheel, until the whole machine was one tune wearing itself as a nest of smaller copies of itself. [431] Silence would accept that. [435] Silence has always had a weakness for recursion. [443]

Three dusks she worked, and on the third her hands shook so badly she had to brace her wrists on the frame. [465] The fifth note was the problem. [471] The mechanism kept correcting it, sliding the sour interval into something clean, because the clock had been built by people who believed the end deserved dignity. [497] Mira did not want dignity. [502] She wanted Ilse. [505]

So she took the broken puppet head and wedged it into the gear train, pine against brass, cheek against tooth, and the wheel caught on the splintered jaw hinge and limped. [536] The fifth note went flat. [541] It was wrong, and it was hers, and it was true. [552] The dials found their last position by themselves, the way a body finds sleep. [566]

Dusk held, as it always held here, bruised and enormous over a sea gone to hammered pewter, and Mira stood in it with her ruined hands and felt awe come up through her like cold water. [602] She had not saved anyone. [607]

The clock struck. [610] Nothing was heard in the lantern room, because the pane took it all, the whole nested six-note descent, and drank it, and carried it out along the flaw. [638] Mira felt the ripple pass through her sternum. [646] And then the pane did what silence does when it is finally cracked open: it vouchsafed her one thing back. [666] From a thousand directions, from smoke and flood and quiet kitchens and worlds she would never have names for, came the sound of children being sung to, flat on the fifth note, every one. [700] It was not rescue. [704] It was company. [707] Mira Vance sat down on the top step of her hidden lighthouse, put her cheek against the cooling brass, and let the last dusk hold her while the ending came in softly, the way it had never once come before.
