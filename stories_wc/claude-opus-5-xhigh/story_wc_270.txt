During the reconstruction, the council decided that the Destiny Hall would be rebuilt exactly as it had stood before the war. [21] My grandmother, who had blown up its cistern, laughed until the steam of the springs made her cough. [39] We were soaking in the third pool that evening, the hot one that smells of struck matches, where the valley's oldest women have always argued. [64] "They want the tradition back," Ilva said, "and I want the tradition forward." [77]

For nine generations, every child of the terraces was implanted at birth with a destiny: a bead of resin, seeded from the mother-vine, slipped beneath the skin at the nape, encoding one life and no other. [113] Weaver, ash-hauler, soldier, wife. [117] The Hall read the beads; the beads never argued back. [127] Grandmother had been an insurgent not because she hated the beads but because she hated who was writing them. [146] When she flooded the curing vaults, my batch of beads was still soft. [159] Mine took wrong. [162] Instead of a trade, I got the vine's own listening: I hear plants, accidentally, constantly, the way you hear neighbors through a thin wall. [186] Moss gossips. [188] Bean vines complain about wind. [193] Cedar remembers everything and forgives nothing. [199] That is why Grandmother needed me for the last thing she meant to do before her lungs finished with her. [219]

In the flooding, she had saved one thing: an embryo in amber, the mother-vine's unborn heart, sealed by the first archivists so that no generation could alter the source. [248] Whoever could unseal it could seed a new mother-vine, and a new mother-vine would mean new beads, written by hands we could see. [271] But amber that old shatters unless it is warmed in the right order: three springs, three depths, three durations, and a word spoken into each. [296] The archivists had written the sequence in a cryptic script, and hidden it where no soldier would search: on the tail of a kite. [320] Grandmother had flown it once, in the year of the barricades, as a signal; when the shelling started she cut the line and ran. [344] Twenty-two years of rain had not brought it down, and she had never found it. [359]

I found it in four days by asking. [367] The old tamarind above the fumaroles had been holding the abandoned kite in its crown since before I was born, and it was proud of it, the way trees are proud of scars. [400] It let me climb. [404] The silk had rotted to lace, but the tail was oiled linen, and the script survived: forty-one glyphs, spiraling, no spaces. [425] We decoded it in the steam, over eleven nights, Grandmother wheezing corrections. [437] The glyphs were not letters but leaf-shapes, and the key was the vine's own growth order (bud, stipule, tendril), which I knew because the beans had been teaching me all my life without meaning to. [472]

The three words, when they came clear, were not passwords. [482] They were intentions: Remember. Choose. Return. [488] The archivists had sealed the source not to freeze the tradition but to make certain that whoever reopened it would have to mean something by it. [514]

On the twelfth night we carried the amber down to the pools. [526] Cool water first, at the ankle-deep rim where the moss chatters, for the length of a hundred breaths. [544] Then the middle spring, chest-deep, milk-blue. [550] Then the third, the match-smelling one, where I held the stone under with both hands and said the last word into it while Grandmother counted. [575] The amber did not shatter. [580] It opened along an old seam, like a pod. [589] The embryo inside was the size of a rice grain and green as a new year, and it was awake, and it was screaming with joy the way seeds do, and I was the only one who could hear it. [629]

Grandmother asked what it said. [634] "It's asking who's writing," I told her. [641] She put her hand on my nape, over the bead that had failed. [654] "You are," she said. [658]

We planted it that season on the eastern terrace, in ash and warm gravel, where the vents keep the ground at blood heat. [681] The council rebuilt their Hall; let them. [688] The new vine will fruit in six years, and its beads will be blank, and every child will carry an empty destiny until the year they are old enough to write it themselves and be implanted with their own words. [728] That is still tradition; Grandmother was very clear about that. [738] She is buried at the top pool now, and the tamarind talks about her constantly, and I have stopped trying to correct it. [761] It says she is still arguing forward. [768]
