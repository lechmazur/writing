Every autumn, when the humans went down to hibernate, Ione stayed awake to map what they would miss. [18] She was a sensory map maker, the last one licensed for the Ophir coast, and her charts were nothing like the old satellite kind. [42] Hers recorded the taste of fog at low tide, the grain of basalt under a palm, the exact pitch of water leaving a cave mouth. [67] Sleepers woke in spring with six months of nothing behind their eyes, and they needed something to hold the gap open. [88] That was the work: transitional objects, the Ministry called them, small true things that carried a person from one state of being into the next. [113] Every hibernaculum bunk had a shelf for one -- a chipped cup, a braid of a dead sister's hair, a stone from a childhood beach -- and beside it, a folded sensory map for the waking. [147] Ione's own transitional object was an elm leaf fossil, palm-sized, the veins pressed into shale so finely that she could read them like a river system. [173] Elms were gone, and had been gone longer than the sleeping habit; the leaf was proof that a thing could stop existing and still be legible. [199] She kept it in her coat pocket when she went into the caves. [212]

The Ophir cave network ran nine kilometers along the coast, half of it drowned twice a day. [229] Ione worked the winter tides in a wetsuit with a thermal array strapped to her chest, heat-mapping emotions the way surveyors once mapped elevation. [253] It was not a metaphor. [258] Grief ran cool and wide across a chamber floor; awe stacked itself in vertical plumes; the specific dread of a place the sea would soon reclaim registered as a thin warm seam at the nape. [293] Her instrument read her own body's response, and her body was calibrated by twenty years of not looking away. [312] The Ministry described her maps as factually mystical, which annoyed her only because it was accurate. [328] Every figure was measured; every measurement, read aloud, sounded like scripture. [339]

There was one chamber left unmeasured, the Fifth Throat, reachable only in the eleven minutes between tides. [356] Three surveyors had tried it in the old awake-year calendars, and their notes ended mid-sentence, not from drowning but from a refusal to describe. [380] Ione wanted the Throat because she wanted to be the voice of the unknown -- not its conqueror, only its translator, the one who could say something honest about a place no one had felt. [414] On the shortest day of the hibernation, she went in. [424] The passage narrowed until she had to leave her tanks wedged at the bend and float, lungs full, letting the outgoing water carry her along the ceiling channel. [452] Floating was the only honest way to enter: no fins, no light, nothing to insist on a direction. [470] She surfaced in air that had never been breathed. [479] The array warmed against her sternum and began to draw. [489]

What it found was not fear, and not wonder, and not any of the four hundred states in her lexicon. [509] It was the temperature of a question being asked of her, steady, patient, thirty-one degrees along every wall, as if the chamber had been holding one thought for ten thousand years and would hold it for ten thousand more. [548] She understood then why the old surveyors had stopped writing. [558] To name it was to shrink it, and to leave it unnamed was to abandon everyone still asleep. [576] She took out the elm leaf fossil and held it up in the dark she could not see. [594] A leaf that had stopped existing and remained legible; a way of saying: I was here, and here is my shape, and the rest you will have to feel yourself. [624] She dictated the entry with her teeth chattering: coordinates, tide, thirty-one degrees, and one line -- The Fifth Throat is warm and it is asking. [648] She went out with the last of the ebb, thirty seconds to spare. [661]

In spring the sleepers came up slow, hands reaching for their shelves before their eyes worked. [677] Ione walked the hibernaculum rows distributing maps, and at bunk 114 a girl named Sesse, six years old, first winter down, had nothing on her shelf at all. [705] Ione set the elm leaf fossil in her palm and closed the small fingers over it. [721] She did not need it anymore. [727] Sesse asked what it was for. [733] "For the part between," Ione said. [739] She had become one -- the thing you hold while crossing from what you know into what you don't, saying steadily: it is warm in there, and it is asking, and you are allowed to answer. [774]
