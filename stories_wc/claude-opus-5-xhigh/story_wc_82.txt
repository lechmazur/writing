Nine days after the shadow detached from her daughter's body, Ilse the lantern maker climbed the stair that descends while ascending. [21] It is the only stair in the glass basilica, and everyone on the floating isle knows the joke of it: you go up, and you arrive lower than you began. [51] Ilse had built lanterns for that basilica for thirty years, and she had never once laughed. [67] Below her, terraced into the isle's belly, her tea garden hung in the wind: green rows, stone kettles, and paper lamps swinging like slow bells. [92] Travelers paid well to sleep there, because Ilse's lanterns did what no other lanterns did. [107] Hung above a sleeper's head, each lamp held a raw gem that drank the sleeper's dreams the way cloth drinks rain. [128] In the morning she polished the stone, and as the wheel took the cloudiness away, the recorded dream rose off the facets in thin colored smoke, legible as handwriting. [157] Gem polishing was the only way to read a dream, and reading cost the dream: one polish, one telling, then nothing. [178] Sela had slept under lamp seven the night she died. [188] Ilse had cut the gem free before the body cooled, and she had not polished it since. [205]

Law on the isle was old and simple. [213] When a shadow detaches from the dead, it circles the basilica until the last dream is surrendered to the glass; then the shadow can transform into light, and the light joins the walls. [246] That was why the basilica burned so brightly at dusk: it was made of everyone's endings. [262] Ilse had watched Sela's shadow ride the wind for nine nights, thin as a scorched ribbon, and each night it had passed her window and found her still awake. [291]

She was a crafty woman, and craft is only grief with somewhere to go. [305] So she cheated. [308] She polished a common quartz until it wept a stranger's dream about a horse, and she carried that up the stair in her left hand. [333] Sela's true gem she kept in her right, unspoiled, unread, hers. [344]

The stair did what it always does. [351] Twelve steps up, the light thickened; twenty steps up, she was somehow beneath the tea garden, looking through the floor of the world at her own lamps burning above her like drowned stars. [384] The Archivist waited at the top, which was the bottom, kneeling on glass in a robe the color of old tea. [405] He took the quartz, held it to the light, and set it aside without unkindness. [420] 'The glass keeps nothing you have not already let go,' he said. [432] 'The shadow circles because you are holding the string.' [441]

Ilse's fist ached around the gem. [447] She thought of the thing she truly wanted, which she had never said aloud, not even to the wheel: to fold her grief into origami cranes, small enough to hold, sharp enough to keep, a thousand of them, forever. [486] 'Then fold,' the Archivist said, and gave her paper. [495]

She sat on the glass floor of the basilica and polished Sela's gem with her sleeve, slow, and the dream came up in colored smoke, and she wrote it down as it spoke. [528] It was nothing grand: Sela at nine, sorting tea leaves, complaining about the wind, asking her mother to sit down and drink something for once. [553] The smoke thinned. [556] The gem went clear and empty and ordinary in her palm. [567]

Ilse folded the written page into cranes, one after another, until her hands knew the creases better than her mind did, and every crane was luminous with the smoke still caught in the paper. [601] It hurt exactly as much as she had hoped it would. [612]

When she carried them out onto the isle's rim, the shadow came down out of the dark and hovered, waiting. [632] She set the cranes in an empty lantern and lit the wick. [644] They caught, transformed, and went up as sparks; the shadow went up with them, unribboning into light that spread along the basilica's panes like sunrise poured into a bowl. [673] Ilse stood in that terrible brightness until she could not tell whether she was being warmed or burned. [691]

Then she went down the stair, which rose as she descended, and came out among her tea rows in the grey before dawn. [714] She kept one crane, unburned, folded from the corner of the page where Sela had asked her to sit down. [734] She set it on the stone beside the kettle. [743] Then Ilse the lantern maker sat down, in the wind, above nothing, and drank her tea while it was hot. [763]
