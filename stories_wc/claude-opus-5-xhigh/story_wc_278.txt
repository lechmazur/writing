The Laboratory of Lost Sounds sat under a dead radio station, and Bern Okonkwo let me in at eleven, whispering, because the meteors were due. [25] He said the sky went quiet during a shower, a meteor hush, all the static swept clean, and that was when his machines behaved strangely. [50] I am a constellation tattooist; I put people's dead into the sky where they can be found again. [68] Bern had emailed me because a donation arrived with a name on it that was mine. [84] The donation was my sister's antique secretary desk, walnut, with a drawer that stuck, and inside it a baby monitor from 2004. [106] Bern's shelves held the last dial tone, a demolished cathedral's bell, the cough of a whooping crane, all the sounds the world had stopped making. [131] He set the monitor on the bench beside a piece of amber with an insect inside, a gnat caught mid-wingbeat forty million years ago. [155] "A paperweight," he said, "and also the only thing in this building that has ever held a moment still." [174] The monitor's firmware was a novelty from a company that folded: it translated baby cries. [189] Hungry, wet, lonely, pain, in a small green display, an algorithm guessing at grief. [203] I was seventeen the night I babysat Junie and muted that monitor because her crying scraped something raw in me. [223] By the time I went upstairs she had wedged herself against the crib bumper and gone still and blue, and the paramedics were kind, and my sister never once blamed me, which was worse. [257] My sister died in March; her will sent the desk here, to the archive of things that no longer make sound. [278] The stuck drawer gave up nine handwritten letters, and the handwriting was mine, though I had never written them. [297] They were dated across twenty years, patient, practical, addressed to me: instructions on how to reach a night that had already happened. [319] The last letter said: during the hush, when time folds in on itself, the firmware will listen backward. [337] It said: hold the amber, because you need a tether, something that already refuses to move through time. [355] Bern did not laugh at me, because his whole life was evidence that machines keep what people abandon. [373] He said the monitor had been humming since it arrived, unplugged, and he called it haunted firmware because he had no better words. [396] At 1:40 the Leonids came, and the room went into that meteor hush, the tape hiss dropping out of every speaker at once. [419] The green display woke up. [424] I held the amber until the gnat's wing caught the light like a struck match, and the crying started, thin and furious and twenty years old. [450] It came out of a machine that had not been plugged in for a decade, and I did not mute it. [471] I listened to all of it, every ragged intake, and the display translated, word by patient word. [488] STUCK, it said, then TURN ME, then COME UP, COME UP, COME UP. [501] I did not go up the stairs; the letters had warned me that the amber holds a moment still, and a held moment cannot be rescued. [527] It can only be answered. [532] So I leaned into the parent unit and told her I was there, that I was sorry, that I had heard her the whole time and had been too young and too tired to be brave. [568] The crying changed the way weather changes, and then it stopped, and the machine, which knew only how to translate crying, translated mine. [591] HEARD, said the small green display, and held it until the meteors were done. [605] Bern archived the recording under lost sounds, though it is the only one there that had never been made before. [625] Before dawn I sat at the walnut desk and wrote nine letters in my own hand, dating them backward across twenty years, because someone had to. [651] I put them in the drawer that sticks, and I put the amber in with them, a tether for whoever comes next. [673] Then I went to my shop, mixed ink, and mapped that November sky onto the inside of my left forearm: Leo, the radiant, and one dim star that is not in any catalogue. [706] I tattoo the dead into the sky so the living can find them, and for twenty years I did it to hush the dissonance of old guilt, one constellation at a time. [738] It never worked, because a sky is not an answer. [748] A voice is. [751] Junie is twenty years dead and she was heard, and the hush that came down over me that night is the quiet kind, the kind that arrives after the light finishes falling. [783]
