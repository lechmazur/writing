The first storm had gone east, dragging its skirts of rain behind it. [13] The second was still assembling itself behind the mountain, patient as a creditor. [26] Between them lay the interval the tea masters called a breath between storms, and on the floating rock platform the air held a saturated hush, every color soaked past its edges. [57] Ilva knelt on the wet basalt and set out her tools. [68] The slab hung four spans off the cliff on three iron chains, a ceremony platform for tea, nothing more, turning slowly, the gulf below full of cloud. [95] Sena, who had kept the kettle here for forty years, poured without spilling a drop, which Ilva took as an omen and then scolded herself for taking as an omen. [125] She was a budding rune scholar, three years into the Academy, and the Academy taught that omens were arithmetic for the frightened. [147] Her right forearm had begun to glitter. [153] That was the trouble she had come here to solve: her skin crystallized when stressed, quartz plating up out of the old burn scars until her arm was a sleeve of stone she could not bend. [189] The Academy physicians called it lithic hysteria and prescribed calm. [199] Calm had not worked in three years. [206] What she wanted was not calm but a way to shed the husk, and more than that, a way to transform her scars into stories, so that the marks the fire left would mean something spoken rather than something hidden under sleeves. [248] The formal runes had failed her because they were built for stone and iron, not for a girl who was becoming both. [270] So she had decided to try the other grammar, the one she had abandoned at twelve: by revisiting youthful superstitions, the charms of her village, the rules everyone knew and nobody wrote down. [303] Count the gap between flash and thunder and the storm cannot count you. [316] Salt on the tongue before you speak a true thing. [326] Never cross moving water without holding your breath. [334] Ilva unwrapped the crow quill and checked the trimmed tip, cut short and blunt by her own knife, ugly by Academy standards, but a blunt nib presses; it does not merely draw. [366] She had trimmed it herself the night the crystal reached her elbow. [378] "You will want to be quick," Sena said, nodding at the grey far edge of the sky. [395] Ilva dipped the quill in the tea, which was the last superstition and the strangest: ink for grief, tea for telling. [416] Then she drew one long breath and held it. [425] Everything after happened during a held breath, which is the only span of time a village charm respects. [443] On the first crystal plate she wrote, in the crooked runes of her grandmother's doorframe: here is the kitchen, here is the lamp I knocked over, I was nine. [472] The plate loosened at its edge like a scab. [481] On the second: here is my brother carrying me out, here is the door that stuck. [497] Her lungs began to burn, and the burning made her afraid, and the fear made new quartz bloom across her knuckles, fast, glittering, greedy. [521] Count the gap, she thought. [526] Thunder rolled somewhere past the mountain and she counted the seconds against it, six, seven, eight, and the counting steadied her hand the way it had steadied her at nine in the smoke. [559] She wrote on the knuckles too: here is the hand that pulled the door. [573] The platform turned on its chains. [579] Sena did not breathe either, out of courtesy. [587] The quill's trimmed tip pressed the last line into the plate at Ilva's wrist, and the whole sleeve of stone cracked along the writing, seam by seam, the way ice leaves a river. [620] It slid off her arm in one hollow piece and struck the basalt, ringing. [633] She let the breath go. [638] The saturated hush broke; wind came up the gulf; the second storm arrived exactly then, as if it had been waiting for permission. [661] Underneath, her arm was pink and ridged and entirely her own. [672] The shed husk lay on the platform, a glass arm full of runes, legible from either side. [689] Sena picked it up, turned it to the light, and read the story of a fire in a kitchen twelve years gone. [711] "The Academy will want this," she said. [718] "They can have it," Ilva said, "if they let me teach the grammar." [731] Rain came across the rock in sheets, and neither of them moved to shelter, because there was tea, and it was still hot, and the next breath between storms would be a long time coming. [766]
