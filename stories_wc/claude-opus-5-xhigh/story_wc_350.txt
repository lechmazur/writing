The astronomical clock tower had been silent for eleven years, and so had the two halves of Varosszeg. [18] Ilka Sarto kept the choral library in the tower's second story, three thousand scores stacked by voice and tongue, and she kept them in harmony the way other people keep bees. [49] Hungarian anthems shared shelves with Slovak laments; nothing was filed apart, because she refused to build a silo out of paper. [70] The real silos stood at the river's edge: eight concrete cylinders of the old cooperative grain elevator, condemned, rusting, and claimed by both villages. [94] When the district finally ordered the grain elevator talks, both mayors demanded neutral ground, and the only neutral ground left was the tower nobody could wind. [120] They met at ten at night, because the day shifts could not be spared, and the tower had no electricity. [140] Ilka lit no lamps. [144] She opened the shutters instead, and the negotiation proceeded under the hush of borrowed starlight, everyone's face equally silvered, equally hard to read. [167] That was deliberate. [170] Men who cannot see each other's flags argue about grain instead of grandfathers. [183] On the table she set the only gavel she owned: a brass cuckoo clock weight, shaped like a pinecone, salvaged from her father's kitchen clock the winter he died. [212] It weighed exactly enough to be heard on oak without frightening anyone. [224] For six nights the talks went nowhere. [231] Bela's men wanted the elevator demolished and the land sold; Jozef's cousins wanted it rebuilt for their own harvest alone. [251] Each side spoke only its own language, loudly, as a form of punctuation. [264] So on the seventh night Ilka changed the rules, interspersing languages herself: she translated each sentence into the other tongue before answering it, and then required every speaker to do the same. [296] It was slow, clumsy, and humiliating for the proud. [305] It also meant no man could insult another without first learning how the insult sounded in the other's mouth. [324] By the ninth night they were laughing at each other's accents, which is a kind of peace. [341] Ilka was tender with their fumbling and merciless about the hour. [352] When a proposal ripened, she brought the pinecone down once, wrote it into the minutes, and moved on before anyone could reopen the wound. [376] Empathetically decisive, the district inspector called her in his report, and he underlined both words. [391] The plan she pushed was neither demolition nor private rebuilding. [401] Rehabilitate the structure, she argued, all eight cylinders, jointly financed, jointly weighed, with a single scale house where both villages' grain would be graded by the same honest needle. [430] And between the cylinders, catwalks. [435] She had drawn them herself on the back of a Kyrie: bridges from silos, literal ones, eight towers stitched into one working machine. [458] That was her whole motive, and she never pretended otherwise. [468] The vote came on the fourteenth night, and a cooperative charter requires every signature. [482] Twenty-one delegates raised their hands; Old Jozef, who had lost a brother to a dispute over a wagonload in 1978, kept his in his lap. [507] Ilka did not argue with him. [512] She climbed to the mechanism room with a lantern, and the delegates followed because there was nothing else to do. [532] The astronomical clock had stopped in the drought year, missing the counterweight that some scrap thief had carried off. [551] Ilka hung her father's cuckoo clock weight on the cable, added her keyring, then a delegate's pocket knife, then Bela's tobacco tin, until the brass pinecone and all their small metal lives together made the mass the gears required. [590] The escapement caught. [593] Sun and moon began to crawl along the zodiac ring, eleven years late, and at the top of the hour the bell spoke over both villages at once. [621] Nobody could tell whose bell it was. [628] Jozef listened for a long while, then said, in careful borrowed Hungarian, that a thing that ran on everyone's weight was probably worth signing. [652] Ilka struck the table to close the session, though her gavel now hung inside the clock, so she used her palm. [673] Afterward, in the library, she taught them a two-language canon she had spent fourteen nights writing, Slovak in the tenors, Hungarian in the basses, the words crossing in the middle where the harmony was. [707] They sang it badly and loudly, twenty-two men and one librarian, while the sun crawled toward morning inside a clock. [727] The catwalks went up the following spring. [734] Ilka filed the drawings under H, for harmony, which was also her joke, and nobody in either village asked her to explain it. [757]
