The tower clock in Ashvent had one law: the tide obeys nothing, so the town must. [16] Merrin Vale, its winder for thirty-one years, kept that law with the whole of his precise, ethically diligent heart. [35] Below the cliffs lay the tidal obsidian ridge, black glass shelves that surfaced twice a day and drowned twice a day, and everything the town ate came off them. [64] The bell told the gleaners when to go down and, more importantly, when to climb back. [80]

Eleven years ago, Merrin had let the clock run four minutes slow. [92] Four minutes is nothing in a kitchen and everything on wet obsidian. [104] Nine people did not climb back. [110]

He remembered it the way one remembers a burn: not as a picture but as a heat that flares when the air moves wrong. [134] Salt wind, gulls, the sound of the wrong hour. [143] Ivet Corran's boy Tomas had gone down with a rake to scrape mussels, and the rake had come back without him, its handle snapped clean at the throat. [171] Merrin kept that broken rake handle in the tower, on the ledge beside the winding key. [187]

The town had not exiled him. [193] That was the unbearable part. [198] They had voted, in the damp meeting hall, that no one else knew the tower's gears, and that a man who had broken the shared clock should be the one to mend it, daily, forever. [233] So he had invented his own sentence inside theirs. [242]

Every morning, after winding, he climbed to the pendulum well and waited for the instant the great brass bob hung still at the top of its arc -- that hairline pause where the swing has ended and not yet begun. [281] In that pause, and only then, he cut one mark into the rake handle. [295] It was a discipline, not a superstition, though after eleven years the difference had worn thin. [311] The marks were tiny inscriptions, letters no wider than a mussel's hinge, and each recorded one true thing he had done that day to restore the balance he had spent. [341] Roof patched for Ivet. [345] Nets mended, Corran, six. [349] Warned Sella off the low shelf. [355] Told Pell the truth about his boat. [362] Nothing grand, nothing that could be mistaken for payment; grand gestures were a way of buying quiet, and he had sworn off quiet. [385] Incremental absolution, he called it, when he let himself call it anything. [397] Nine lives; four minutes; one mark a day. [405] He had never once let himself calculate an end date. [415]

This morning the handle ran out of wood. [423] He turned it in the grey light and found no clean grain left, only the dense weave of eleven years, four thousand small confessions crowding the split end where the shaft had broken in Tomas's hands. [459]

The bob swung out, hung, and in that suspended breath the heat came up through him, searing, and the tower filled with reverie: black shelves shining, gulls, nine figures who never turned around, a hand raised that might have been waving or might have been asking the hour. [507] He understood, in the pause, that he had been keeping a private ledger about a public wound. [524] Balance was not his to restore alone; it was shared, or it was nothing. [538]

The bob fell back into its swing, and he went down the stairs. [551] He rang the low tide bell on time, as always, and then he did the thing he had never done: he emerged into the square while the gleaners were still gathering their baskets, and he stood on the well-step where the criers stood. [594] Ivet Corran was there, older, her hands full of rope. [604] He held up the rake handle so the light caught the inscriptions, and he read them out. [621] It took a long time. [626] Some of them were absurdly small, and someone laughed once, and the laugh broke and turned into something else. [645] When he finished he said that the wood was full, that he wanted a second winder trained, and that the tide table should be read aloud each dawn by two voices, not one, so that no single man's four minutes could ever again decide who came home. [692]

Ivet took the handle from him. [697] She did not forgive him -- she said so plainly, and he was grateful for the accuracy -- but she asked to keep it, and to add marks of her own. [726]

That afternoon Merrin polished the pendulum bob until he could see the ridge in it, black and patient, rising. [745] The clock kept perfect time, which was all it had ever been able to give. [760]
