Mira Okonjo had spent eleven years teaching machines to notice things, and now she could not stop noticing them herself. [20] As the intrusion detection specialist for Lantern Deep, she walked the tunnels an hour before dawn, when the mushroom beds were at their brightest. [44] Ten thousand caps of Panellus stipticus threw a cold green light across the racks, so soft that shadows had no edges. [65] Down here, the world glowed and did not burn. [74]

She was spatially gifted in a way the hiring panel had found unsettling; given a floor plan once, she could rotate it in her head for years. [101] Lantern Deep lived in her skull as a lattice of volumes: nine growing halls, four humidity locks, and the vast breathing skin of the place. [126] That skin was the problem. [131] The farm could not be sealed, because mushrooms are not kept safe by seals; they need airflow, moisture, exchange. [150] Every wall was a compromise, and something had been coming in through permeable barriers for three weeks. [167] Contaminant blooms in Hall Six. [172] Cobweb mold, arriving where no door had opened. [180] Her sensors watched doors, hatches, and hands, and they had watched them perfectly while the farm was quietly invaded by air. [201]

At the junction of Six and Seven she met the man with the thermos. [215] They had passed each other at 4:40 every morning for two years: he coming off the night substrate shift, she starting her rounds. [238] They had never exchanged names, only nods, the practiced courtesy of familiar strangers. [251] That morning he stopped. [255]

"You're looking for a hole," he said. [262] "There isn't one." [265]

She held up her laser pointer, thumb resting on the button. [276] It was an old tool, almost sentimental: press the button, and a red thread lit the drifting spores, turning invisible currents into something a person could read. [303] Her brother Teo had taught her that trick, years ago, in a greenhouse that no longer existed. [320] He had drowned in a flooded lock, holding a line he should have let go. [335] Since then she had been trying, in her methodical way, to understand the physics of letting go: how much force a grip stores, when release becomes survival, why the hand refuses. [366]

She pressed the button. [370] The red thread lit a slow river of spores, and the river was sliding toward Hall Six like water finding a drain. [392] The man watched it and said, "Pressure differential." [400] "The old ventilation stack -- we reversed the fans in April." [410]

In her head the lattice tilted, and she saw it whole: not a breach but a gradient, the whole farm inhaling through its pores, drawing the outside in wherever the pressure asked. [442] Eleven years of her work had assumed that security meant a boundary you could defend. [457] It didn't. [459] You could not hold a living place shut; you could only learn its breathing so well that you felt every change in it. [482] Something went up in her, quiet and total, a wildfire behind glass. [494] She would revolutionize the whole discipline with it, she thought, and the thought was not vanity but relief. [512] Detection through permeable barriers: sensors that tracked pressure, humidity, and spore drift instead of locks, a system that assumed everything leaked and listened to how. [537]

They worked until the fans were retuned, the two of them crouched over a maintenance tablet in green light, and she found out his name was Ilya, and that he had a daughter who wanted to be a diver. [576] Two years of nodding had cost them nothing and taught them nothing; twenty minutes of shared trouble had made them people. [597]

They climbed out through the north lock before the echo of morning birdsong had faded from the hillside above the farm. [618] The first thrushes were still answering each other across the wet grass. [630] Mira stood in the ordinary gray light with her thumb on the laser pointer button and understood, finally, the physics she had been chasing. [654] A grip is not strength; it is stored force, waiting to break something. [667] Letting go is not loss of contact but a change in how you carry a thing -- Teo's line, her borders, the years of nodding at a stranger she could have known. [698] She lifted her thumb. [702] The red thread vanished into the daylight, and nothing was lost, and the birds went on measuring the size of the morning. [724]
