The nib hovered a finger's width above the blank page, and Isvet let it hover. [15] Outside the obsidian spire the frozen lake lay under a sky the color of unbleached linen, and nine hundred villages waited for her to write the coming year. [43] She was the Spire's ignorance cartographer, and her maps showed nothing anybody knew. [56] Where other scribes charted rivers and grain yields, Isvet charted blanks: the depth of the lake beneath the ice, the reason the kingfishers had stopped nesting on the north shore, the thing that happened to iron in the ninth year of cold. [98] Her craft was to know exactly where knowing ended. [107]

The Almanac was not a prophecy but a promise; whatever the Spire wrote in it, the world obliged. [125] For three centuries the scribes had kept the promise modest, deriving each year through predictive modeling: ice cores, ledger sums, the migration tables, the slow arithmetic of births. [153] They wrote only what the numbers already leaned toward, because a sentence that strained too far against the evidence tore, and torn years brought famine. [178] So the Spire was groundedly imaginative by law, permitted to dream only one step past its data. [195]

It had worked, and nobody starved, and that was the trouble. [206] Isvet's maps had been shrinking for forty years. [214] Not because the realm learned more, but because it attempted less; when every harvest was written in advance, no farmer risked an untried seed, and the blanks on her charts closed like healing wounds over a splinter. [251] A realm with no ignorance left has nothing to walk toward. [262]

The nib in her hand was cut from a kingfisher feather, the last one gathered from the north shore, and the Spire had only three such nibs remaining. [290] Kingfisher quills held enough ink for eleven words; nothing else held ink at all. [304] Eleven words to write a year. [310]

Archivist Vaun had already drafted this year's eleven and laid them on the lectern in his neat hand: The winter is mild, the granaries full, the lake ice thick. [339] It was correct, it would hold, and it would make this the three hundred and fourth year in which nothing new was tried. [362]

Isvet had argued with him for a week, up and down the black stairs, while the lake groaned. [380] Her case was arithmetic, not poetry, and that was why she prevailed. [392] She showed him her own models, run the same rigorous way his were: the ice thinning two knuckles a decade, the kingfishers gone, the last three nibs, and the curve where the Spire's promise outlived the Spire's ink by nine years. [433] In nine years there would be no quill, no Almanac, and a realm that had forgotten how to guess. [452] "Then we must teach them to guess again," Vaun had said, hoarse, at the end, "before the ink runs out." [472]

So the eleven words could not describe a year; they had to build a school out of a shortage. [491] That was her whole purpose, the one she had never been able to say plainly until now: to seed innovation within a constraint. [514]

Above the page, the nib still hovered. [521] Everything was still possible in that finger's width of air, which was the only place Isvet had ever been comfortable. [541] She could not write abundance; the numbers would tear it. [551] She could not write a miracle of iron or a new bird. [563] She could write, precisely one step past the data, a lack. [574] A thin winter, a warm lake, a shortfall small enough to survive and large enough to make nine hundred villages argue about seeds. [597] Ignorance, deliberately drawn, the first new blank on her charts in forty years. [610]

The nib touched the page. [615] She wrote: The ice thins. The harvest falls one tenth. Now begin guessing. [628] Eleven words, and the ink gone, and her hand shaking as if she had lifted a mountain instead of a feather. [649]

Below, the lake gave a long crack like a door opening. [660] Vaun took the spent nib from her and set it in the reliquary beside the other two. [677] "Two more years of promising," he said. [684] "Then they promise themselves," said Isvet. [690] She unrolled a fresh sheet and began the new map, marking the northern fields in the pale wash she used for things nobody yet knew, and for the first time in her life the blank spread outward under her hand, growing, which is how a world stays alive.
