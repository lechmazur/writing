After tomorrow disappeared, the tides kept their appointments anyway. [9] Vesna Aldemar unlocked the Tidal Atrium at the hour of low water, as she had every day of the long calm winter, and the pale sun came in sideways across the flooded floor like something remembering how to be warm. [49] She was the titan-gear librarian, which meant two duties: the shelves, and the Gear. [63] The Gear stood forty feet of green brass in the atrium's heart, one tooth for each thing a person could be, and it turned by moonlight alone. [90] Its speed fluctuated with the moon phases: lazy at the dark, frantic at the full, and the catalog beneath it swelled and thinned in the same rhythm. [117]

Since tomorrow had gone out of the language, taking with it the day it named, identities had begun to migrate. [137] They left their people quietly, the way salmon leave a river, and swam the incoming tide looking for somewhere to spawn. [158] A baker in the harbor row woke without his baking; a widow woke without her grief and wept for missing it. [179] Vesna caught what she could in her father's fisherman's bamboo creel, the lid still smelling faintly of herring and rain. [199] She would kneel in six inches of cold seawater, coax a shivering self into the wicker, and carry it upstairs to be shelved and labeled and kept. [226] It was routine, and she loved it, because routine was where she had learned to keep wonder alive: the same door, the same tide, and always some impossible small passenger in the basket. [259]

But the shelved selves were dying. [265] A migration needs a destination, and the destination had always been tomorrow. [277]

Vesna understood the mechanism the night she found the gap in the Gear, a socket where one tooth had been, its edges bright as a new wound. [304] The tooth that had carried the day after today had sheared off, and without it the great wheel skipped, and the fish had nowhere upstream to go. [331] So she went down to the foundry under the west stair, where her predecessors had kept a crucible for exactly this, and she began to smelt. [357] Into the crucible went the failing selves from the creel, one by one, with her blessing and their consent, which she asked aloud each time and each time received as a small tug on the wicker. [393]

The alloy would not bind. [398] The instructions carved inside the crucible lid were older than the library and plain enough: this metal takes only by accepting contradictions. [420] So Vesna stood in the red light with the winter sun laying its thin gold along the flood, and she said the true things that could not both be true. [450] "I am my father's daughter who ran from the sea, and I am the woman who married it." [468] "This library is finished, and this library will never be finished." [479] "Nothing changes here, and everything here is changing." [487] "Every one of those selves is a stranger, and every one of them is me." [502]

The metal took. [505] It rose in the crucible the color of a low winter morning and poured smooth into the mold, and when she set the cooled tooth into the socket at moonrise, the Gear did not roar or blaze. [542] It simply caught, and turned, and kept turning, quiet as breath. [553]

The tide came in over the atrium floor and the migration went through her library like light through a window. [573] Thousands of selves ran the new channel upstream toward the restored day, and Vesna sat on the third step with her empty creel on her knees and let them pass. [603]

The baker got back his baking, though he claims it tastes of brass. [616] The widow got back her grief and thanked the sea for it. [628] Now the word for the day after this one is back in the mouths of the harbor, and the Gear runs faster at the full moon and dawdles at the dark, and Vesna opens the doors at low water with her basket on her arm. [673] Some mornings she nets a stray identity that took a wrong turn at the shallows; most mornings she nets kelp. [693] She is not disappointed either way. [699] The gap in the Gear taught her the shape of the work: you hold the ordinary and the miraculous in the same hand, you do not choose, and the wheel turns. [730] Outside, the low sun lies on the water like a hand on a sleeping animal, and the atrium fills, and empties, and fills. [753]
