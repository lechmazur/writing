The doctors gave Ilse Marren six months, and she spent the first afternoon of them pruning. [16] She was the island's caretaker, the ninth in a line of them, and the orchard would not prune itself. [35] The temple had no walls, only pillars: nine columns of pale living stone that thickened a finger's width every century, breathing so slowly that the priests had once mistaken them for architecture. [67] Inside their ring grew the orchard - eleven oaks, four quince, one obstinate fig - and between root and column ran the secret that Ilse had inherited with the keys. [95] The pillars were not stone that had learned to live; they were creatures that had learned to be stone, and they were lonely in the way of anything that outlives its own language. [128] Her predecessors had left her a botanical diary, three centuries thick, bound in sailcloth and stubbornly indexed. [145] It was, page after page, gently systematic: bud dates, frost notes, the exact hour a column had flexed when a branch was cut too near it. [171] Nobody had written the conclusion, so Ilse wrote it in her second week of dying: the pillars listen through copper. [191] The proof had been waiting in the tool shed - a coil of copper wire, and beside it a sketch of an oak branch strangled in bright metal. [218] So she climbed, at sixty-one, with a bad liver and a good ladder, and wound copper wire around the oak branches until the orchard glinted like a harness. [246] Sap warmth traveled the metal; the wire touched the columns where their skin was thinnest; and the pillars, for the first time in nine caretakers, tasted a season. [274] They answered by growing. [278] Over eight weeks the columns thickened inward and upward into arches, roofing the orchard, and the heritage board on the mainland called it vandalism. [302] Inspector Dahl came out on the mail boat with a folder and an order to halt the remodel. [320] "You are remodeling a protected monument," he said. [328] "The monument is remodeling itself," Ilse said, with the brazen calm of a woman who had already been sentenced by better authorities than his. [352] She handed him the diary and let him find it himself, because facts survive arguments and she did not have time for arguments. [375] He read for two days under the new arches. [383] The architectural secrets of the temple were not in its geometry but in its diet: every vault, every fluted pillar in the diary's drawings had been coaxed, not carved, by orchards planted deliberately close. [417] The first builders had been gardeners who negotiated. [424] "Then what stopped them?" Dahl asked. [430] "Fear," she said; "a column moved once, and somebody decided a building should not want things." [446] For four hundred years the caretakers had kept the orchard trimmed back, safe, mute - custodians of a silence. [464] Ilse's motive was plain enough to write in one line, and she wrote it: to foster interspecies respect while there was still someone alive who remembered how. [491] Respect, she explained, was not sentiment; it was accurate attention paid on a schedule the other party could feel. [510] So she taught him the schedule. [515] Copper on the third-year wood in March, never the leader; loosen the wire before it bit; log the columns' flex each dusk to the quarter-degree. [540] Ask before you cut; the pillars answered slowly, in stone, and the answer was the building. [556] By the fifth month the fig had reached the eastern column and the column had made a niche for it, the way a hand cups a match. [583] Ilse could no longer climb, so she sat in the shade of an arch that had not existed in spring and dictated while Dahl wrote. [608] He had resigned from the board and stopped calling the orchard a site. [621] On the last page she left instructions rather than farewells, because the diary was a working document, and grief is not a method. [644] Plant the tenth oak six paces west of the ninth pillar, she wrote; it will want a hundred years, and the pillars will want longer, and that is the point. [674] She died in the orchard in the sixth month, and the tenth caretaker buried her under the fig. [692] That winter the eastern column leaned - two degrees, measurable, logged - and held its lean for a hundred days, which the diary now records as the first sentence the temple ever spoke to a human on purpose.
