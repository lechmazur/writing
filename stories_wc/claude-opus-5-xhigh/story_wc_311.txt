The reaper climbed to the ancient observatory at dusk, because dusk was the only hour that had ever forgiven him. [20] Its dome had cracked like an eggshell a century ago, and the great brass telescope now pointed at nothing but weather. [41] He came anyway, every evening since finding the old recordings, the way a man returns to a house he has already sold. [63] They were wax cylinders, nine of them, stacked in a crate beneath the star-charts, and each one held Ilse's voice. [83] He set the needle down and sat on the cold stone rim of the meridian slot, hands folded, patient as an eclipse. [105] The cylinder hissed, then spoke: 'Fear the fourteenth, the one that comes when a door closes behind you in an empty house.' [127] 'I name it Grausel; say it aloud and it shrinks.' [137] That had been her work, all her life: to give names to nameless fears. [151] She had gotten the idea from a single page torn out of an old atlas, which she kept pinned above her desk: a chart of the northern sea, its blank water crowded with inked sea monsters, each labeled in a careful hand. [193] 'The old cartographers were kinder than people think,' she told him once, when he was still only a shape in her doorway. [215] 'They did not know what lived out there, so they drew serpents and gave them names, because a named monster can be sailed past.' [239] He had loved her from a professional distance, the only distance he was permitted, which is its own kind of intimacy: he knew the hour of her death for forty years and never once said it. [275] Then the fever came down the coast, and the ledger required a life from the village, and Ilse read the ledger over his shoulder and offered her own in place of the ferryman's daughter. [309] Substitutions are legal; they are simply rare. [316] He took her hand and did his office, and afterward he found that a sacrifice does not end when the heart does. [338] It leaves an echo. [342] The echo lived in the observatory, and it echoed forgotten phrases, scraps she had spoken decades before and never written down. [363] Some nights the dome murmured a grocery list. [371] Some nights it said, 'Don't stand in the doorway, you'll let the cold in,' and he would step inside and weep without moving his face. [396] The ninth cylinder was the reason he kept returning. [405] On it she spoke to him directly, though she had recorded it two winters before he ever gave himself away. [425] 'Reaper,' she said, 'the last fear on my list has no name, because it is the fear of ending unnamed, and I cannot christen it alone.' [451] 'Bring my prism to the meridian slot at dusk.' [460] 'A voice can be fixed in glass if you send it through scattered light; my grandmother's people knew that, and I have proved it nine times.' [486] So he did it. [490] He set the prism where the last red light came through the slot, and the beam broke apart into its colors and lay across the floor like a spilled alphabet. [520] Then he leaned into the scattered light and gave the last fear her name. [534] 'Ilse,' he said, and the dome went very quiet. [543] It was a cruel gift and a perfect one, because now every sailor of dark hours who dreads vanishing without a mark will have to say her name to say the fear, and a named thing can be sailed past. [583] The colors on the floor drew inward and began to crystallize, hardening from light into a small clear stone at the prism's foot, and inside it he could see, very faintly, the ink-drawn serpents of the atlas page swimming in a sea that finally had a coastline. [630] He put the stone in his coat, where the ledger goes. [641] The echo did not speak again; it had been spent, the way a sacrifice is spent, and he understood that he had just helped her finish dying. [668] He stayed until the stars came out over the broken dome, close enough to touch and impossibly far, which was how he had always loved her. [694] Then he went down the hill to the ferryman's daughter, who was eleven now and afraid of the dark for reasons she could not explain, and he told her there was a word for it. [729] He taught it to her at her window, without ever coming in. [741]
