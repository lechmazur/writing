In the orchard district, the fog had been thick for four hundred years, and the apple trees had long since turned to charcoal lace. [24] Isen the mirror maker worked there anyway, because fog is only unpolished glass, and she had never been afraid of unfinished things. [46] She was firmly grounded in the way of people who do not dream: her sleep was a plain gray shelf, and she woke from it rested and empty. [74] The astronomer came to her door on the shortest evening of the year, carrying a broken sextant in both hands like a wounded bird. [98] "My dream ran away," Halva said. [104] Isen let her in and set water to boil, since madness and grief both take tea. [120] Halva explained it plainly, which was worse. [127] For thirty years she had measured stars she could not see, computing their places from old tables, and every night she had dreamed the same country: mountains of glass, ridge behind ridge, cold and ringing, with the whole uncensored sky standing over them. [170] Three nights ago the dream had gotten up and left her, and she had felt it go, the way you feel a tooth leave. [194] It had taken her sextant's mirror with it, snapping the arc as it went, and now she could not work, or sleep, or believe in the sky at all. [223] "Dreams do not run," Isen said, "unless something on the outside calls them." [236] "The fog calls them," said Halva. [242] "It has been eating this district for centuries, and it is hungry for anything with edges." [258] Isen knew then what she was being asked to do, and she knew the price of it. [275] To capture a runaway dream you must recreate it somewhere solid, build it a better body than the fog can offer, and mirrors are the only solid thing that will hold a picture without owning it. [311] But Isen could not see the glass mountains; she had no country behind her eyes to copy. [328] So they would have to erase the boundary between them. [338] They worked upon the shimmer of twilight frost, the one hour in the orchard district when the fog stiffened into crystals and let a little distance exist. [365] Isen poured her blank at the foot of a dead pear tree, cut it to the size of the sextant's lost index mirror, and laid the broken brass arc beneath it as a spine. [399] The brass still remembered angles; a dream that had once lived by that instrument might recognize its own measurements and come home to them. [423] Then the two women knelt on either side of the cooling mirror and began the erasing, which is done through synchronized breathing and nothing else. [448] Halva breathed the four-count of her observing nights; Isen matched her, slower, then exact. [462] Frost gathered on their sleeves. [467] Somewhere in the ninth minute Isen stopped being able to say which lungs were hers. [482] And there it was, borrowed and enormous: ridge behind ridge of glass mountains, blue at the edges, striking a single note that went on and on. [508] Isen held the picture in her mind and, with her hands still and her knees firmly in the frozen mud, gave it to the mirror. [533] That was her whole gift: she was so grounded that nothing passing through her got lost on the way. [552] The fog fought. [555] It came in low over the charcoal orchard, feeling for the edges of the new thing, wanting to smear the mountains back into weather. [579] But the dream, wherever it had been wandering, heard its own ringing note and turned. [594] It arrived like weight, like a held breath released, and settled into the silvered glass, and the glass took it and did not crack. [618] Halva made a sound Isen would remember longer than the mountains. [629] They stopped breathing together, and the boundary closed, and each was one woman again. [643] Not quite. [645] That night, for the first time in forty-one years, Isen dreamed. [656] She dreamed of cold ridges she had never earned, and woke ashamed and rich, understanding that some of the crossing was permanent. [678] Halva mounted the recovered dream in the sextant's frame, filed the broken arc true, and began again to measure a sky she still could not see. [704] The fog in the orchard district thinned that winter, a little, in a ring around Isen's workshop. [721] She never claimed credit for it. [727] She only kept pouring mirrors, and kept them turned toward the place where the sky should be, and told anyone who asked that fog is only unpolished glass, and that dreams, like light, need something firmly grounded to bounce off.
