During the Unlearning, the city agreed to forget its noise the way one forgets a bad habit: slowly, and with a strange grief. [23] Ines Vero did not agree, but nobody asked her, and that was the point. [37] She was a chalk artist, whimsical by trade and temperament, and her work had always depended on the racket of people leaning over her shoulders to argue about what they saw. [68] She drew fish with umbrellas, mayors with moth wings, ladders climbing into puddles. [81] People used to shout suggestions; now they only nodded, and their nodding had a color to it, a lilac unease that Ines could taste at the back of her teeth. [111] Being socially perceptive was her only real talent; the chalk was just how she published it. [127] So she noticed what the Wardens denied: no one had ordered the silence. [140] It was an emergent hush, assembled from ten thousand small courtesies: a mother shushing a child, a vendor muffling his bell, a neighbor swallowing a joke, each person quieting for someone else's sake until the whole city held its breath and forgot why. [183] Ines wanted to find out what the hush meant, or whether meaning survived at all in such a mess. [202] In her pocket she carried a flake of tile pried from the old whispering gallery before the demolition crews muted it with foam. [225] The flake still worked. [229] Held to the ear, it repeated the last words spoken under that dome, a stranger saying, over and over, that he had loved the noise of being misunderstood. [257] That sentence was why she went down to the grotto. [267] The whispering grotto lay under the salt cliffs, and in autumn it filled with mist so thick that the lanterns looked like bruised plums. [291] Its curved walls carried any sound a hundred feet, which was why the Unlearning had made it sacred: here you could hear exactly how much you were failing to be quiet. [322] Fourteen practitioners knelt on the wet floor around a sand mandala the width of a cartwheel. [338] Since speech was gone, they conversed through the patterns in the sand mandala, laying colored grains in spirals and forks that meant agreement, grief, refusal, weather. [364] Ines watched for an hour and understood it the way she understood crowds, by tempo rather than vocabulary. [382] The mandala was arguing with itself. [388] One faction poured tight concentric rings, meaning: continue, the hush is nearly complete. [401] Another kept nudging in a small broken line at the edge, meaning: something is missing, and we cannot say what. [421] The ring-makers smoothed that line away each time, gently, the way people had been smoothing each other away for two years. [442] Ines felt the lilac unease rise off them like damp off stone. [454] She knelt, took the chalk from her satchel, and did what she had always done at the edge of an argument: she interjected. [477] On the wet rock beside the mandala she drew, fast and crooked, a fish holding an umbrella, and beneath it the broken line the ring-makers kept erasing, drawn long and unashamed. [508] Fourteen heads turned. [511] Then she pressed the tile flake against the curved wall, and the grotto did what it had been carved to do. [532] A stranger's voice, thin as onion skin, went around the whole chamber: I loved the noise of being misunderstood. [551] It said it again. [555] It said it eleven times, and on the fourth a practitioner began to weep without apologizing for the sound. [574] On the seventh, an old woman laughed, and the laugh clattered off the ceiling like dropped cutlery. [591] On the eleventh, someone said a word out loud, and it was only 'wait,' and it was enough. [609] The mandala was ruined by then. [615] Knees and elbows had dragged the spirals into smears; the colored grains ran together into a muddy purple nobody had chosen. [636] Ines crouched over the wreck, and there, in the mess, she found what she had come for. [653] The smeared grains had recorded every knee, every lean, every person who had shifted toward the voice; read that way, the ruined mandala was an honest map of fourteen people deciding to stop being polite. [688] Meaning had not been in the pattern; it had been in the disturbance. [701] She chalked that on the grotto wall in letters two feet high, misspelling 'disturbance' because her hands were shaking. [720] Nobody smoothed it away. [724] The Wardens came in spring and washed the wall, but the mist had already done its work; chalk dust had risen into it and settled on the cliff path, and for one week the whole town walked home with lilac on their shoes and argued about why. [771] Ines listened to the arguing and drew. [778]
