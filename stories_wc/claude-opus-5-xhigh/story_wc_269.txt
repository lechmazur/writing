Station Nine stands on four steel legs above the drowned crown of Vaila Atoll. [14] Nomi has kept its tide gauge for eleven years, which is long enough to feel moored and drifting at once. [34] The sea does not care that the fossil fuel ban passed twelve winters ago. [48] Heat sunk into the deep water in her childhood is still surfacing, patient as a debt, and the gauge climbs three millimeters a year regardless of anyone's virtue. [76] Every answer the ocean gives arrives late. [83] She was a coral survey diver once. [90] She was twenty-three the summer the Vaila reef went white, and she counted the dying by hand, transect by transect, while her partner Teodor logged temperature at the drop-off. [119] Her testimony is archived in the hearings that led to the ban, where the lawyers called her the bleaching witness, and the word stuck to her like salt. [147] Underwater they spoke by tapping their tanks: short and long, a code they built from an old prisoners' alphabet, rows and columns, numbers for haste. [172] On the last dive Teodor came up too fast, tapping the whole way, and he was still tapping the ladder rail when he stopped. [196] Nomi never decoded that rhythm. [201] She only learned it, the way you learn a lullaby, and for eleven years she has tapped it on railings, on mugs, on the cold flank of the tide gauge housing at slack water. [235] Grief makes a metronome of anything. [241] Above her bunk she has pinned a single atlas page, number 214, torn from her grandmother's atlas on evacuation day. [262] It shows the Vaila chain in 2019 green, and a dotted tanker lane running east, a road of ships that no longer exists. [285] The rest of the book stayed on the survey boat with Teodor. [297] That lane is the question that keeps her at the station: why do some paths disappear? [313] Shipping routes, islands, reefs, men who taught her a code. [323] There are nine monitoring stations along the drowned chain, and their keepers are a lonely, stubborn order. [340] They talk at slack tide by tapping the steel legs; the hydrophone net carries knocking better than voices. [358] Nomi taps Teodor's rhythm into the water every evening, and for years it was only a habit, a signature. [377] Then, last spring, Keeper Aduke at Station Two knocked back a question that changed the shape of Nomi's life: where did you get page 214? [402] Aduke had a torn page too, number 213, from the same battered atlas, mailed to her in a survey tube the week Teodor died, with no letter. [429] So Nomi began to assemble. [434] Knock by knock she found seven more pages held by seven more keepers, and one by one the tubes came to Station Nine over eight months of supply drops. [463] Last night she laid all nine on the floor and fitted their torn edges together. [478] Across the seams, in Teodor's cramped pencil, ran a single sentence broken into nine pieces: THE REEF IS NOT DEAD IT HAS MOVED FOLLOW THE TAPS. [504] She sat back on her heels and finally, after eleven years, translated the lullaby. [518] It was not a farewell. [523] It was numbers: a latitude, a longitude, a depth. [532] He had been ascending too fast because he had found something at the eastern drop-off and could not wait to say it. [554] The coordinates sit on the assembled pages exactly where the old tanker lane crossed a seamount, in water the atlas colored a cold, unremarkable blue. [579] Upwelling water, cold refuge, the one place heat arrives latest. [589] This morning Nomi radios Aduke and tells her to bring a boat. [601] The spawning comes on the fourth night after the full moon, the same week every year, the reef's one reliable promise; her grandmother called coral periodically eternal, dying and reassembling on a schedule older than any nation. [638] Paths disappear, Nomi thinks, because they are not lines but living things, and living things relocate while we stare at the old chart. [661] She pins page 214 back over her bunk, taps the coordinates once against the doorframe for luck, and climbs down the ladder toward the water. [686] The station stays moored behind her, gauge ticking, measuring a rise no one can stop. [701] Nomi drifts out over the vanished lane, eleven years late, exactly on time. [714]
