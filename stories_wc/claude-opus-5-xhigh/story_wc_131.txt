The library breathed in a sapphire hush an hour before dawn, and Odell breathed with it, three feet beneath the reading room floor. [23] He had lived in the crawl space for nine years, since the flood took his rooms on Kesler Street, and he had learned the building the way a doctor learns a body. [55] Ducts were arteries; the pipe chases were the long hollow bones. [66] In those chases, wedged behind steam lines and forgotten by every contractor since 1953, he had been uncovering things for years: ledgers, a child's shoe, and best of all, tape. [96] The old oral-history project had spooled the city's voices onto ferromagnetic reels, then abandoned them when the grant died, and the reels had slid down the walls like silt. [125] Odell was kindly and methodical, so he catalogued each one in pencil, wrapped it in butcher paper, and added it to his cache beneath the north stairwell. [152]

He knew the staff by their shoes and their sorrows. [162] Miss Aurelia Vance, who opened the reference desk at six, walked with a hitch and cried on Thursdays, always at the same table, always with the same folder of her mother's papers. [194] Odell had heard her tell a colleague that she could no longer remember her mother's voice, only the shape of the silence it left. [218] Three weeks ago, in the chase behind the map room, he had found a ferromagnetic tape reel fragment, snapped clean in half, labeled in faded ink: VANCE, E., BAKERY, 1961. [248] He had spliced it, threaded it onto the machine he kept oiled in his burrow, and listened once, only once, because it felt like trespassing. [273] A woman's voice, warm and quick, described the smell of her ovens at four in the morning: scorched sugar, wet flour, the cold iron of the alley door. [301] Odell understood then what he was holding, because he had his own sense memory, and it ruled him: his mother's hands smelled of turpentine, and to this day one whiff of it turned him into a boy of six. [340] A voice could do that too, he thought, and a voice on tape could do it forever. [357]

The trouble was delivery. [361] For nine years Odell had been a rumor: a caretaker who fixed what nobody reported, who left mousetraps set and radiators bled. [383] Invisibility had kept him housed, but lately it had begun to feel like cowardice, and he had started to want, badly, to become visible to those who needed him. [412]

The chance came at 5:40, when the city went dark all at once. [425] Through the grate he watched the streetlights die, then stutter, then flicker in long blue waves as the grid fought itself back to life. [449] The library's emergency lamps came up dim and cold, and in that unsteady hush Aurelia let herself in early, because the alarm panel was down and someone had to sit with the building. [482] Odell moved through the pipe chase with his reel and his hand-crank player, past the boiler, up to the panel behind the poetry stacks. [506] He had rehearsed this a hundred times in his head, methodically, the way he did everything: knock first, so she would not scream. [529]

He knocked. [531] "Miss Vance," he said to the dark, "my name is Odell Pruitt, and I live in your building, and I have something of your mother's." [556] The silence that followed had edges. [562] Then a chair scraped, and a flashlight beam found the panel, and Aurelia, white-faced, said only, "Come out slowly." [581]

He did, dust-gray and blinking, and he set the player on the reading table and cranked it, because the power could not be trusted. [605] Four minutes of scorched sugar and wet flour and cold iron filled the shadowed library, and Aurelia put both hands flat on the table as though the floor had tilted. [635] She was not hearing a document; she was six years old in a bakery, and she was crying without any Thursday to excuse it. [659] When the tape ran out, the windows had gone the color of sapphire, dawn coming up over a city still blinking its lights on and off like something waking. [688]

"There are eleven hundred more," Odell said, "behind the walls, and every one of them catalogued." [704] Aurelia wiped her face with the heel of her hand and looked at him for a long moment, the way a librarian looks at a thing that has been misshelved for years. [736] "Then we'll need a room," she said, "and a desk with your name on it." [751] The lights steadied at last, and Odell Pruitt, who had been a rumor, stood in the plain gold morning where anyone could see him. [775]
