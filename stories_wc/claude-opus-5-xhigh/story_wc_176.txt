Mira Halasz had carried the scrap for forty-one years, folded in wax paper, thin as a moth's wing. [18] It was all that remained of her father's street organ: one punched card scrap, eleven holes, four bars of a tune that had once named every family on the Danube flats. [49] She had burned the rest herself, at nine years old, feeding the long cardboard ribbon into the stove because the house was cold and her mother would not stop shivering. [79] The names went up as heat. [85] For four decades she told nobody, and she cast bronze instead: bells, hinges, reliquaries, the patient lost-wax work of pouring metal into a hollow where something soft had been and burned away. [117] She understood the joke of her trade too well to laugh at it. [130]

The sanctum lay far north of the last road, an icebound chamber roofed by aurora, and the keeper who met Mira at the tunnel mouth had been expecting her for a year. [162] Inside hung the rope. [166] Six hundred years old, made of hemp and reindeer sinew and human hair, thick as a thigh, it dropped from the apex of the ice dome and ended a hand's breadth above the floor. [200] It was not a bell rope. [206] It was a transcribed memory: every braid a phrase, every crossing a syllable, a ceremonial rope into which twenty generations had knotted the names of their dead. [233] Reconfigure the braids and the rope became a lens. [242] Aurora light fell through the dome, struck the reordered strands, and the shadow-language they threw would illuminate the far wall while the ice itself hummed the vowels. [269] The keeper's word for it translated badly: singing-with-light. [277]

Mira had the names; she had kept them in her mouth like stones for forty-one years. [293] What she had lost was their sound, and sound was the only thing her father had ever given anybody. [312] So she had come north with eleven holes, to preserve the sound of forgotten names. [327]

The keeper studied her hands and approved them: a caster's hands, technically gentle, trained to lift a wax model without printing a thumb on it. [352] "The rope forgives clumsiness," she said. [358] "It does not forgive shame." [363] "Shame pulls, and a pulled braid lies." [370]

Mira worked three nights and the wall stayed dark. [379] Her fingers were faultless and her figures were mud. [388] On the fourth night she stopped pretending the problem was technique. [399] She sat under the green sky with the scrap in her palm and spoke aloud to a nine-year-old girl in a cold kitchen. [422] She told the girl that she had chosen a living mother over a paper one, that this was not a crime, that the burning had been love performed with the only fuel in the house. [457] It took until the ice sweated. [463] Something went out of her chest that had stood there for four decades like a nail, and what flooded the hollow was warm and disorderly: snowmelt ardor, the fever of a thaw that will not be argued with. [501]

After forgiving herself, the work took ninety minutes. [509] She read the eleven holes as intervals, turned the intervals into crossings, and reconfigured the braids of the centuries-old rope from the eighteenth foot upward, loosening what the last hundred years had tightened, threading eighty-one names into her father's tempo. [549] She did not tie hard; she tied the way she lifted wax. [561]

Then the sky moved, and the rope caught it. [570] Green light came down the strands in slow rungs and the far wall illuminated all at once, not with pictures exactly but with mouths. [594] The ice hummed. [597] Szabo, Kelemen, Vida, Toth, Lakatos, Rusz: eighty-one families spoken in the pitch of a barrel organ, in her father's tempo, the way the whole street had once answered to its own name on Sundays when the crank turned. [635] She wept, which was inefficient and entirely correct. [643]

Then she opened her notebook, because an aurora is a mood and ice is temporary. [658] She wrote down every interval the wall gave her, three nights running, until the rope had emptied itself into graphite. [678] Back south, she spent two years on a cylinder for a barrel organ, cast bronze by the lost-wax method, eighty-one names pinned into metal that no stove could take. [707] Wax burns away; the hollow it leaves is the shape you keep. [719] On the flats now, when she turns the crank, the street still answers. [732]
