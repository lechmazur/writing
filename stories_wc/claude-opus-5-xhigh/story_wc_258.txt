The cryptocurrency crashes took my job in March, and my grandmother's loft came to me in April, as if the year had been dealing from a stacked deck. [28] Rain beaded the skylight the first night I slept there, each drop holding a small inverted room. [45] Sabra had been the onyx-ink cartomancer of Verrick Street for forty years, and nobody in the family could tell me what that meant. [68] They only said she drew her own cards, that she never bought a deck, that she ground her ink from onyx dust and gum arabic and something she refused to name. [99] In the loft I found nine hundred cards, a cracked slate mortar, and a cloth measuring tape. [116] The tape was the strange thing. [122] Someone had inked names along it in her hand: Okonjo at four inches, Vasquez Bakery at eleven, the shuttered Polish church at twenty-three and a half. [148] I have always been steadily curious rather than brilliant, which is why I was good at my old job and terrible at keeping it. [172] I had spent six years reverse engineering other people's models, taking a finished output apart until the assumptions fell out of it like screws. [196] So I did that to Sabra. [202] The cards had no suits I recognized. [209] Each bore a black sigil, a date, and a fragment of speech: "my brother will not come north," "the rent doubles in autumn," "we buried her with sugar in her hands." [240] For a week I assumed they were fortunes. [248] Then I laid the measuring tape across her wall map of the ward and understood that the inches were blocks. [268] The tape was a scale, and the sigils were addresses. [278] She had not been predicting anything. [284] She had been recording, surveying really, using the reading as the instrument. [296] Her method, written on the back of a laundry receipt, was to answer every question by multiplying paths. [314] A client asked whether to sell the shop; Sabra dealt not one future but six, and made the client argue against each. [336] In the arguing, the client leaked everything: who was leaving, what a cousin paid for a lease, which saint had stopped drawing a crowd. [360] Six paths yielded six times the data. [367] Nine hundred cards, forty years, one street: she had built a longitudinal census out of superstition. [383] Her motive, I decided, was the plainest one available: to map cultural patterns nobody else thought worth measuring. [401] Where did grief move when a church closed? [409] Which languages did children keep and which did they drop by twelve? [421] Sabra had answers with dates on them, and no one had ever asked her for them. [437] In June a woman named Teodora climbed my stairs, out of breath, holding a phone that showed a number that used to be money. [461] She had put her mother's savings into a coin that no longer existed, and she wanted to know if she should tell her mother. [485] I am not a cartomancer. [490] But the rain was on the skylight, and the deck was on the table, and I understood the machine now. [510] So I dealt her six paths: confide tonight, confide after the funeral of the money, never confide, confide by letter, confide by repaying it first, confide by asking her mother for help. [542] She argued against all six, and in arguing she told me about three families on Verrick who had mortgaged houses to buy tokens, and about the church basement where they had met to do it. [577] It was the same basement Sabra had marked at twenty-three and a half inches, where the Polish congregation had prayed until 1994. [599] The room kept its function: people still went there to believe something would rise. [613] That is a pattern, and I wrote it on a card in her ink, with the date. [630] Teodora chose the letter. [634] I do not know if she sent it, and Sabra's system never needed to know; the value was never in the outcome, only in the branching. [660] Nine hundred and one cards now. [665] I keep the measuring tape nailed above the door like a mezuzah, and I still cannot name the third ingredient in the ink. [688] Some inheritances stay partly sealed; that is how you know they were real. [701] Upstairs the rain finds the skylight most nights, and I sit under it with her mortar and my questions, taking the street apart to see what assumptions fall out. [730]
