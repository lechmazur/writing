The canyon echo singer was called Nera Oss, and she had spent forty years learning which stones gave a voice back. [21] Most did not. [24] Most swallowed a song and kept it, the way the dead keep secrets. [37] But at the serpentine nexus, where seven dry riverbeds coiled together under starlight, the walls answered only certain throats, and Nera had come to learn why some people echo. [66] She carried a reed flute she rarely played, because its tone was too honest, a thin blown melancholy that made men confess things they later regretted. [92] She also carried half a seashell. [98] It whispered. [100] Not constantly, and never all at once; the shell worked through progressive disclosure, giving her one new sentence each night the sky was clear, as if it had been instructed to ration itself. [133] Her brother Idris had held the other half before he vanished into the listening trade. [148] The first sentence, ten years ago, had been simple: Sister, the canyons are not stone. [163] The second: They are ledgers. [168] The third had taken a year to arrive, and it said: Some people are entries. [183] Nera was richly perceptive in the way of people who make their living from sound; she could hear a lie's small hollow, the flinch inside a laugh, the difference between a canyon that was empty and one that was merely waiting. [224] So she heard, over the years, the webs of intrigue tightening around the nexus: brokers who paid for names, cartographers who mapped which families echoed and which went silent, a guild that sold echo-rights to princes who wanted their proclamations to outlive them. [267] Every faction wanted the same thing she did, and none of them wanted her to have it. [284] She reached the nexus on the night the shell had promised, when the sky stood so clear she could hear the low sustained note the old singers spoke of, for the walls only answer while the universe hums its secret song. [325] Three men waited there with lanterns hooded, guild-marked, patient. [334] They had been reading her route for a season. [343] "Sing it," said the eldest. [348] "We only need to ensure the shell finishes." [356] She understood then, with the cold clarity of a perfect fifth, they could not do it themselves. [373] Not one of the three had ever echoed. [381] So Nera stood at the coil of the seven beds, lifted her chin, and sang the note she had held back her whole life, the one Idris used to hum when he was frightened. [415] The walls took it. [419] The shell woke in her palm and gave up its last sentence at last, and because it had rationed itself so long, she was ready to hear the whole shape of it. [451] People echo, Idris said, when they have been heard by someone who loved them; the stone only repeats what a life has already learned to repeat. [477] The nexus is where the ledger balances. [484] I am in it now. [489] Sing me out. [492] Her own voice came back off the seven walls, and it came back doubled. [506] The guildsmen fell to their knees, not from reverence but from hunger; they had spent their lives brokering something they could never be given, because no one had ever truly listened to them. [539] Nera put down her shell half beside its twin, which lay in the sand where her brother had finally stopped walking. [560] The two halves fit together like a closed mouth. [569] Then she took out the reed flute and played for the three of them, badly, honestly, until each man had said aloud the thing he had never told anyone. [598] She listened to all of it. [604] That was the whole method; there was never any other. [614] Toward dawn, when the humming thinned and the stars went the color of old paper, one of them coughed, spoke his own name, and heard the canyon give it back. [644] He wept like a man who had been found. [653] Nera walked home the long way, empty-handed, humming with the sky, and never returned to the nexus. [670] She had learned what she came to learn, which is the loneliest kind of answer: her brother had echoed because she had loved him, and no one now was left to sing her back. [704] Still, some nights, walking the reeds along the dry beds, she thought she heard the universe take up her note and carry it, patiently, on toward whoever was listening next. [734]
