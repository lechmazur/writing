The tolls at the lightning tower were paid in coin, in candor, or in wonder, and Ossa Vane preferred the third. [21] For eleven years she had sat in the gatehouse at the tower's foot, stamping manifests, weighing crates, and drinking each morning the candid draught the Charter required of keepers. [50] It made her involuntarily truthful, which the city considered a virtue and Ossa considered a weather. [66] She could not shave a number, could not say the stair was safe when the copper was singing, could not tell a merchant his figs were fine when they were sour. [97] So the routine had hardened around her like lacquer, and to keep some shine in it she had begun, years ago, to accept riddles in place of the fee. [126] Bring me a riddle I cannot solve, she told travelers, and the stair is yours. [141] Three nights after the city-wide blackout, a woman came up the road at nectar dusk. [156] The light that week was strange and sweet, the low sun pouring through unlit windows like honey through a comb, and the air smelled of orchard blossom that no one was visiting. [188] The drone beehives had gone down with the grid. [197] Ten thousand brass pollinators had dropped out of the sky in one afternoon, and although the lightning tower had been recharged by the second night, the hives had not woken up. [228] The woman carried a lacquer box, black with a red seam, cradled in both arms like a sleeping child. [247] "Marnet Ilso, hive engineer," she said. [253] "I have to reach the arbiters at the crown." [262] The arbiters had been sitting for two days at the top of the lightning collection tower, because the tower was the only building in the district with power, and they were deciding whom to hang for the failure. [300] "Toll," Ossa said. [303] Marnet set a matchbox on the ledger. [310] Inside, folded small, was a strip of hive-paper with eight lines on it, and Ossa read them twice while the copper crown above them hummed. [335] I have ten thousand daughters and never a mother; I dance a map I have not walked; feed me light and I starve; wake me and I wait. [363] "That is not a riddle," Ossa said, because she could not say otherwise. [376] "That is a confession." [380] Marnet's mouth moved, something between a smile and a wince. [390] "Solve it aloud for me," she said, "and I will show you what is in the box." [407] The drones had no mother; their dances were copied decades ago from a recorded queen, and every hive since had learned its flight maps from the archive, not the field. [437] The blackout had wiped the archive. [442] Power had returned; the map had not, and no living bee remained in the district to dance a new one. [462] "Wake me and I wait," Ossa said slowly. [470] "The hives are not broken; they are ignorant." [478] Marnet opened the lacquer box. [483] Nectar-colored light fell into it, and in the warm dark inside sat a small straw crown of comb, and on the comb, moving, were bees. [508] Living ones, kept illegally in a rooftop garden for nine years, because Marnet had never quite believed in a world pollinated only by clockwork. [532] "They will confiscate them and try me," Marnet said. [541] "Unless somebody the arbiters cannot doubt tells them what these are for." [553] Ossa understood then why the engineer had come to her gate instead of the freight lift. [569] A keeper who has drunk the candid draught cannot be accused of pleading a case. [584] She can only reveal. [588] They climbed nine hundred stairs past glass jars of stored lightning that lit the stairwell blue, and Ossa held the box because her hands were steadier. [614] At the crown, in a room of humming copper, seven arbiters asked her the only question a toll keeper is ever asked. [636] Declare the cargo. [639] So she declared it: one queen, four hundred workers, one map that had never been written down, and the reason ten thousand brass drones were standing still in the orchards. [669] She told them the drones could be taught again, but only by something alive, and that it would take a season of following bees through blossom with recorders, which was slow, expensive, and the only thing that would work. [708] Nobody in the room could argue with her, and that was the point. [721] Ossa went back down to the gatehouse before midnight. [730] She put the matchbox in the drawer with the others: hundreds of them, a life's collection of small paper wonders paid at her window. [754] In the morning the lamps would work, the manifests would come, the figs would be sour, and she would say so. [775] But out in the orchards, something small and alive was teaching the machines to dance. [790]
