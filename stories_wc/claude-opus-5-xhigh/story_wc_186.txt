The beacon had been dark for nineteen years, and Vesna had been its custodian for eighteen of them. [18] She had inherited the post from her mentor Oram, who died insisting that the collapse of civilization had been an accident of arithmetic: too many people, too little grain, no meaning in the timing. [52] Vesna disagreed, and she intended to prove it. [60] Below the tower stood the glass humming pavilion, a ring of tall panes the old astronomers had raised to catch the wind. [82] Each pane was etched with star paths, and when the night air moved through the slots, the whole structure sang a low note like a finger on a wet rim. [112] Vesna slept there most nights, because the humming kept the memories quieter. [124] She was retrocognitively blessed, which the old world would have called a gift and she called weather: touch a doorframe and see the hands that hung it, touch a bone and watch the fall. [158]

The beacon's heart was a broken crystal prism, and she had held it a thousand times. [174] Whole, it had gathered lamplight into a beam that reached the harbor forty miles down the coast; broken, it threw only a smear. [197] One wedge was missing, the piece Oram had called a shard of hush, because the flaw in its lattice drank sound. [218] Set in the prism, it silenced the wind's rattle in the lens housing; removed, it left the tower loud and blind. [239] Every spring, thistledown blew up the headland in white drifts, and every spring Vesna promised the empty sea that she would rekindle the light. [263]

This spring, a boy came up the cliff road with a sack of salvage, and among the spoons and hinges lay a sliver of glass that made no sound when she dropped it on stone. [298] Not the shard itself, but a chip off it, and when she closed her hand the vision came. [316] A woman in a soldier's coat, breaking the shard's edge against a hearthstone to make a knife, and above her a hole in a fallen roof, and through the hole seven stars in a crooked ladle. [352] Retrocognition gave her the sky but never the map. [361] So she worked the way the astronomers had: by tracking star paths across the etched panes of the pavilion, matching the ladle's angle to a date, the date to an hour, the hour to a horizon. [397] It took eleven nights. [401] The roof-hole faced a rising that only happened at one latitude on that coast, in a village called Threave, and the boy said yes, that was where his mother had died, and he had picked the pretty glass off her shelf. [442]

Oram would have called it coincidence: a scavenger's child wandering to the one door where his trinket had a name. [462] Vesna called it an argument, and she went to Threave to finish it. [475] They walked nine days through thistle fields gone loud with seed. [486] The shard was under the hearthstone, hafted in bone, still swallowing every sound around its edge. [502] The boy held it while she pried the bone away, and neither of them heard the wind for a moment, as if the world had paused to be polite. [531]

Back at the tower, the shard settled into the broken prism with a click she felt in her teeth. [550] It did not make the prism whole; it made it usable. [561] She poured the last of the seal oil, struck the flint, and the lamp caught. [576] The housing went silent, the beam went out across the water, and the harbor forty miles south answered before dawn with a fire on its mole. [602] Someone down there had kept oil too, for nineteen years, for no reason anyone could prove. [618] That was her whole case, and she rested it. [627]

Coincidences are not proofs, she wrote in the log, in the column Oram had used for weather. [644] But they are invitations, and someone must accept one before any light gets lit again. [659] The boy stayed on as her apprentice, learning the star paths, complaining about the humming until the night he admitted it helped him sleep. [683] In the pavilion, thistledown drifted through the slots and hung in the beam like sparks that had decided not to fall. [704] Vesna watched them, and let herself hope on purpose, which is harder than despair and lighter than seed. [722]
