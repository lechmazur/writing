The fans stopped four winters ago, and the silence that filled the hall was not empty but thick, a honeyed hush that Sen learned to move through slowly. [28] She was a watchful dreamer, her mother's phrase for her, meant fondly and also as warning: a girl who saw whole gardens in a crack and then, unlike most dreamers, went and got a bucket. [63] The data center had been decommissioned after the fire, its racks stripped, its roof punched open by salvage crews who took the copper and left the rain. [90] Sen came the first spring with a jar of moss blended into buttermilk and painted it onto the raised floor tiles where the water pooled. [115] Nobody gave her permission; nobody came to stop her; she was quietly proactive in the way of people who have stopped expecting permission. [138] In February she found her method. [144] Icicles hung from the severed conduit along the ceiling, and as they thawed they wrote a pattern on the dust below, not a line but a scatter of small dark coins, wider where the draft pushed, tight where the air stood still. [186] Sen chalked around every drip mark, and by March she had a map of where the building wanted to be wet. [207] She planted to the map, and the moss took, and then the ferns, and then a wild pear seedling in the crack by the loading bay door. [234]

Her sister Yeva arrived in the fifth winter, nine days after the confession went viral. [249] Yeva had been operations lead here. [255] Yeva had signed off on the cooling reports she knew were forged, and a contractor named Bo Halloran had died in the hall where the ferns were now waist-high. [284] For four years she had said nothing. [291] Then she had filmed herself at her kitchen table, said all of it in six unedited minutes, and posted it. [311] By morning eleven million people had opinions about her face. [321] She stood in the doorway of the moss garden with her hood up and her hands in her sleeves, as if the internet had followed her inside. [348]

'I didn't know you'd made it beautiful,' Yeva said. [357] 'I didn't make it beautiful,' Sen said. [364] 'I read where the water went and stayed out of its way.' [376] They walked the aisles between the dead racks, and Sen did not ask why she had waited, and Yeva did not explain, and the hush held them both the way amber holds a thing. [410]

At the pear seedling Sen crouched and took out her knife. [421] 'It fruits sour,' she said. [426] 'Wild rootstock always does.' [430] 'So I cut it, and I set a scion from Bo Halloran's pear tree into the cut, since his widow let me take three, and I bind the wound, and in two years this tree fruits his pears.' [468] 'The graft is a wound.' [474] 'It is only ever a wound.' [481] 'But it is the wound that carries the fruit.' [490]

Yeva knelt in the moss with her hood down. [499] 'You went and asked his widow,' she said. [507] 'Two years ago,' Sen said. [512] 'I wasn't waiting for you to say it out loud.' [522]

Then Sen lifted the cord from her own neck. [531] The amber pendant had been their mother's, a lozenge the color of strong tea with a small bee inside it, wings half-open, still falling after a hundred thousand years. [560] Sen proffered it on her open palm, and did not push it forward, and did not take it back. [579] 'She gave it to you,' Yeva said. [586] 'She gave it to whichever of us needed to be soft somewhere nobody could get at.' [602] Yeva took it, and the hall dripped around them, coins of water falling into moss without a sound. [620]

In April, Sen taught her to chalk the drip marks. [630] It was work for someone who could hold still and watch, and Yeva, it turned out, had been watching for four years without doing anything with it. [657] The comments kept coming; some of them were true; she read them in the honeyed hush with the bee at her throat and then went back to the chalk. [686] The graft took that summer. [691] The pears came the second year, small and gold and honest, and the sisters carried a basket of them across town and left them on Mrs. Halloran's step, with no note. [722] The tree had already said it. [728]
