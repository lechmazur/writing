The cloud burst at four, and by five the lily pond had swallowed the lower stairs of the lotus temple. [20] Mira waded out anyway, holding the twig spiral above the brown water like a lamp. [35] For eleven years she had been a love letter ghostwriter, and in a city of four million she was the quietest keeper of secrets it had. [61] Clerks, welders, a cardamom exporter, a widow who wanted one last letter to a man forty years dead: they came up her stairs and told her everything, and she gave them words they could sign. [96] Her sentences were inevitably whimsical; she could not help it. [106] Left alone, her grief always grew a small hat and went dancing. [118] That habit was how the trouble began. [125]

In the third year, a bookbinder named Ilyas hired her to write to a woman he had met at the flower market. [147] Two weeks later a botanist named Reva climbed the same stairs, blushing, and asked for help answering a bookbinder. [166] Mira said nothing, because saying anything would have broken the only rule she had. [180] For two years she wrote both halves of a courtship, arguing with herself about moths and monsoons and whether the soul had a spine. [204] They married under the temple's white petals, and she watched from the causeway with rice in her fist. [222] Then, in their fourth winter, Reva found a draft in Ilyas's coat, in handwriting she recognized from her own letters. [242] They separated in the ordinary way, without shouting, each believing they had loved a ghost. [257]

Mira stopped writing for a year. [263] In that silence a thrush built a nest on her windowsill out of shredded rejected drafts and hair-thin twigs, winding them into a tight, deliberate spiral. [289] She studied it for months. [294] Every twig came from a different tree, a different street, a different accident of wind, and yet the bird had found the thread that connected all of them: a curve. [324] That was what she wanted too, and she decided she would say it publicly or not at all. [342]

The temple council approved her proposal in March: a public art installation on the causeway, free to all, standing until the rains took it. [366] She called it The Keeper. [371] It was the thrush's spiral, enlarged: nine hundred twigs gathered from every quarter of the city, each one sleeved in a small tube of blown glass, each tube holding a single anonymous line from eleven years of letters. [409] No names, no dates, nothing anyone could trace. [417] She had kept the secrets and released only the longing. [427] Two twigs, side by side at the spiral's center, carried lines she had written for Ilyas and for Reva on the same wet afternoon. [451]

Now, after the cloudburst, she set the last two tubes into place with the water at her knees. [469] The sky cracked open into that scrubbed, aching brightness that only comes when a storm leaves in a hurry. [488] Light went through nine hundred glass tubes and came out the other side in bands of color, laying a prismatic quiet across the lily pads, and the lotus temple stood in the middle of it like a held breath. [527]

People came out to read. [532] They walked the curve slowly, bent to the glass, laughing at the whimsy in it, because whimsy was the one thing Mira could never disguise. [557] That was the point. [561] She had not summoned them; she had only made the thread visible and stepped back. [576]

Ilyas arrived at dusk, alone, with his trousers rolled. [585] Reva came from the far end of the causeway ten minutes later, following the spiral inward as everyone did. [604] They met at the center, at the two twigs, where the same hand had written to each of them about moths. [625] Mira watched from the temple steps and did not go down. [636] She could not hear what they said. [643] She saw Reva touch the glass, and she saw Ilyas say something that made Reva put a hand over her mouth. [664] Whether they would rejoin was not hers to decide, and she understood, finally, that it never had been. [682] She had only ever been the twig, borrowed from somewhere, bent into somebody else's curve. [697]

They walked back along the causeway together, not touching, matching steps. [708] Behind them the water rose another inch, and the outermost twigs began to loosen and drift, carrying their small bright sentences out among the lilies. [733] Mira let them go. [737] In the morning she would open her stairs again, and someone would climb them with a secret, and she would find the whimsical, ridiculous, exactly true word for it. [766] That, too, was a curve.
