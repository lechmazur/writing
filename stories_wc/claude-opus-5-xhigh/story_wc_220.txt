After the last resource war, the wind came across the cracked wasteland with nothing left to say. [17] Ossuary Flats answered it with the tick of cooling metal, a scrapyard settlement stitched from hulls and axles for nine miles in every direction. [41] We called that quiet the wild hush, and we worked inside it like thieves. [55] I am Wren, a groove-reader, which means I make far things near for anyone who can pay in water. [74] During the war, couriers hid letters under music, cutting secret texts into the grooves of phonograph cylinders where no censor thought to listen. [97] The trick is light. [101] Under a red lens the wax shows one script; under green, another; under blue, the confession the writer meant to bury. [122] My mother taught me to call them tinted revelations, and to never sell all three colors to the same customer. [142]

Belle sleeps under my bench, a bloodhound retired from finding bodies. [153] She found four hundred and six in the trench years, and then one morning she sat down in the ash and refused to smell another dead thing. [180] Now she smells only wax, and she is never wrong about it. [192]

The parcel came up from the south gate with a salt-trader who would not say who paid him. [210] Inside was a cylinder the color of weak tea, and beside it an autumn leaf preserved in wax, five-lobed, veined like a hand held to lamplight. [236] I have never seen a living tree. [242] People say I am intuitively descriptive, which is a polite way of saying I describe what I cannot possibly know, so I told the trader: sugar maple, north slope, cold creek, October. [274] He went pale and left without his fee. [282] Belle woke, put her jaw on the leaf, and sighed like a woman who has come home. [299]

The cylinder played a marching song under the red lens, nothing but brass and lies. [314] Green gave me a supply manifest for a depot that no longer exists. [327] Blue gave me my mother's handwriting. [333] She had walked out of the Flats eleven years ago to find whether anything still grew, and we had all agreed among ourselves that she was one of the bodies Belle never brought back. [367]

The blue text held no coordinates. [373] It held a riddle, because she trusted paper less than she trusted me: I am the only road that shortens when you speak it, I have no end but a listener, and men have died carrying me in their mouths. [413] I sat with it three nights while the wind sanded the hulls. [425] Belle answered before I did, nosing the cylinder and then the leaf, then the cylinder again. [441] The answer was the groove. [446] A spiral is a road that grows shorter the longer it is spoken, it has no end except an ear, and couriers had swallowed wax rather than surrender it. [475] So I shaved the cylinder down past all three colors, into the pale heart of it, and played what was hidden under the hiding. [499] No lens was needed there. [504]

My mother's voice, thin as wire: four hundred miles northeast, past the salt pans, a valley the war forgot, and eleven hundred trees. [527] She said she had been cutting cylinders every autumn and sending them by any hand going south, because she could not carry the valley to us, and she refused to let distance stay distance. [561] That is my trade too, and I learned it from her: to translate distance into closeness, one groove at a time. [582]

I played the last inch of it in the market at dusk, and the Flats went quiet in a way I had never heard. [606] Two hundred people holding their breath around a horn of hammered tin, listening to leaves. [621] Then someone laughed, and someone wept, and the wild hush broke open like a seed. [636]

We leave at first light with forty carts and Belle at the front, because she does not find bodies anymore. [656] She finds wax, and my mother's hands smell of it, and four hundred miles is only a spiral we have not yet spoken to the end. [682] I carry the leaf in my shirt, and when the road frightens me I hold it to the lantern and describe out loud the color I know it was: October, north slope, cold creek, coming home. [718]
