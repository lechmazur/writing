Ives brokered lanterns, which meant he spent his life selling other people light he could not use himself. [18] The good ones came from the drowned glassworks at Halder, cut so finely that their beams broke into crystal shadows on any wall, blue and violet and cold. [46] He was wistful by trade and aromatically cautious by injury. [56] Nine years ago a stranger walked past him wearing bergamot and wet wool, and he had wept in the street like a struck child, unable to say for whom. [85] Since then he breathed through his mouth in markets, kept his coat sealed in cedar, and avoided bakeries. [103] He wanted, more than he wanted comfort, to understand why some smells trigger tears. [117] That was why he took the commission from the Vault Garden, the long greenhouse for extinct plants that leaned against the north cliff like a ship run aground in glass. [147] They needed forty ultraviolet lanterns, and nobody else in the province could source them. [161] The curator, a small woman named Bel with soil under her rings, walked him down aisles of things the world had finished with: silphium, the Saint Helena olive, a fern that grew only on one burned island. [198] "Bees see in ultraviolet," she said. [204] "Our pollinators are extinct too, so we do the work ourselves, and to do it we need the flowers to show us their runways." [228] Under his lamps the pale petals lit with hidden bullseyes and stripes, an ultraviolet revealing of instructions written for eyes that no longer existed. [252] It was, Ives thought, the loneliest thing he had ever seen: a message still shouting at an empty room. [271] Bel could not pay in coin. [277] She paid in the vault's currency, and she brought it to him in a crystal perfume vial no longer than his thumb, stoppered with wax and lead. [304] Inside was the last recorded scent of the Halder moonvine, distilled the year the glassworks flooded, the year his sister drowned with them. [327] He did not open it. [332] For three months he carried it in his breast pocket while the vault's cutting from the moonvine, coaxed out of a seed bank and grafted to a living root, climbed its wire toward the ridge of the roof. [370] Bel wrote him when the bud swelled. [377] It would open once, at night, for one hour, and never again in his lifetime. [392] He arrived at midnight with his best lantern. [400] The greenhouse at that hour was all crystal shadows, the glass panes throwing lattices of violet across the beds, every leaf edged in a light that was not quite light. [430] Bel stood on a ladder with a brush of rabbit hair, ready to be the bee. [446] The bud was a green fist unclenching. [453] "Lamps," she said, and he raised his. [460] "As the transformation completes," she said, "the guides rise like a tide, and if you miss that minute you miss the map." [482] The petals peeled back, white going silver under the lamp, and the ultraviolet revealing came all at once: eleven dark rays spoking out from a black throat, a target, a runway, a hand pointing home. [517] And the scent arrived. [521] He had no time to be cautious. [528] It was bergamot and wet wool and something green beneath, and Ives sat down on the brick path and grieved. [548] Neve had worked the moonvine glasshouse before the flood; she came home every night smelling of it, and she had teased him for being a boy who cried at flowers. [578] He had forgotten her scent so thoroughly that his body had kept it instead, filed somewhere older than language, waiting nine years for a matching key. [604] That was the answer, then: tears are not caused by smells but released by them. [619] Memory does not fade evenly; it goes underground and keeps its shape, and grief waits down there, intact, patient, unpaid. [639] Above him Bel worked the brush in small circles, weeping too, professionally, without stopping. [653] When the hour closed the petals again, she climbed down, and he handed her the crystal perfume vial, still sealed. [673] "For the archive," he said. [678] "I kept it sealed because I was afraid it was all I had left of her." [694] He walked home through the dark with his lamp low, and for the first time in nine years he took the long way, past the bakeries, breathing through his nose. [724] Behind him the greenhouse held its crystal shadows, and inside, in the dark, the pollinated flower had already begun making seed. [745]
