The amphitheater breathed twice a day, and Mira Salvo had learned to work inside its exhale. [16] Warm air rose from the fissures under the stone tiers, then sank back as the caldera cooled, and the crumbling seats seemed to lift and settle like a sleeper's ribs. [46] She was the district's floodgate machinist, ingenious with gates and hopeless at politics, and in nine days the council would ask her to drown this place. [72] The reasoning was sound: the terrace farms below the volcano were failing, the aquifer had dropped, and the caldera's old theater made a perfect reservoir, forty meters deep, walls already sealed by centuries of ash. [107] The reasoning against it was also sound: the theater was the only thing the valley's people still agreed was theirs. [127] Mira had been hired to open gates, not to harmonize conflicting values, but nobody else in the district would try. [147] Her teacher, Oris Vane, had surveyed the caldera for thirty years and had died in spring, leaving her two things: an antique surveyor's chain, and an envelope with nothing inside it. [178] The chain was a hundred links of tarnished steel, brass tally markers at every tenth, and it had been repaired so many times that no two links matched. [206] The envelope had been sealed once and slit open, and it drove her half mad, because Oris never wasted anything, least of all a gesture. [231] On the sixth evening, sitting in the third tier while light came dappled through the roof's collapsed lattice, she stopped reading the chain and started reading the glue. [259] By analyzing the leftover adhesive from the once-sealed envelope -- scraping the dried flap into a spoon, warming it, testing it against the samples in her gate-repair kit -- she found gum arabic mixed with pumice dust of a grain she knew: the coarse gray sand that occurred at only one vent in the whole caldera, the north sump, a place Oris had always declared unmapped and worthless. [325] The empty envelope was not a message; it was a location. [336] That was Oris: encrypted compassion, help disguised as a puzzle, because he believed a gift you solved yourself could never be taken from you. [360] She went at dawn with the chain over her shoulder and a lamp, down the service stair behind the stage wall, into a throat of rock that exhaled at her as the building breathed. [394] The north sump was not worthless. [399] It was a natural cistern the size of the amphitheater's orchestra, dry, ash-sealed, sitting eleven meters below the lowest tier -- and she measured it with the mismatched chain, link by link, and understood the repairs. [434] The brass tally markers had been reset -- filed, restamped, wrong by exactly the offsets that turned Oris's public survey of the caldera into a false one. [460] For thirty years he had hidden a second reservoir from the council, and left her the ruler that unhid it. [480] He had hidden it, she thought, because a council in a dry year will spend a resource it can see; he wanted the valley to have both bread and its theater, and he had not lived long enough to arrange it himself. [522] Standing in that cold dark, Mira felt her hands tremble, and let them, because it was the first time since spring she had felt anything move forward. [549] At the hearing she laid the chain on the council table like a dead snake and told them the amphitheater would hold water badly -- fissures, breathing rock, an exhale that would steal a third of it to steam. [587] Then she showed them the sump, and the six gates she could build to feed it from the winter melt, and the farms drinking by the second summer. [615] Someone asked why Vane had never told anyone, and Mira said he had told one person, in a way only she could read -- a slyly heartfelt bequest, an empty envelope and a lying chain, trusting her to want the answer enough to earn it. [659] The vote was six to five, which in that valley counted as harmony. [672] She began the gates in autumn, working through the afternoon exhale, sun coming down in shifting patches through the broken lattice while she cut seats for the sluice mouths -- careful, mostly reversible, three of them shaped to double as drains. [712] The chain she hung on the stage wall, uncorrected, with the offsets engraved on a plate beneath it, so the next machinist would have to solve something too. [740] It was, she decided, the kindest sort of instruction: not the answer, but the trembling certainty that one existed. [759]
