The nursery sat in a bowl of hills that filled with weather every August, and Marisol Vega had spent eleven years calling that month her enemy. [26] Storm season meant staking, tarping, tying, and above all, notes. [36] She kept a ledger for every bed, because she had not opened a nursery to sell geraniums; she had opened one to study plant behaviors, the way other people studied birds. [67] Her mentor, Dr. Amadi, had taught her that a plant is a slow animal with excellent manners. [84] He had also taught her, circuitously, that control is a kind of blindfold, though he never said it in a sentence you could quote. [108] His letters arrived like maps of a country he refused to name: three paragraphs about linden trees, one about his knees, then a question that made her put down her tea. [139] Circuitously sincere, she called him, and she had learned the dialect. [150] In his last letter he had folded a linden leaf, heart-shaped and dry as paper money, and written: What grows when you are not watching it grow? [177] Marisol had used the leaf as a bookmark in her ledger and had not answered. [192] Then he died in March, and the answer became homework she could not turn in. [207]

The subject of her longest experiment was a moonflower vine on the east trellis, twelve years old, sprawling, mossy at the joints, and stubbornly unwilling to bloom. [234] Every spring she pruned it to her design, tied its arms to the lattice at the angles her notes prescribed, and every autumn it gave her leaves and no flowers. [264] She had begun to suspect the vine was not failing but refusing. [276]

The August storm came at two in the morning with a sound like furniture being dragged across the sky. [295] By dawn the east trellis lay flat in the mud, its cedar slats snapped into a bad handwriting of angles, the moss on it swollen and green as a held breath. [326] The vine was intact. [330] It had let go of the frame somewhere in the worst hour and slid whole into the wet grass, coiled like something asleep. [353] Marisol knelt in the mossy wreckage after the rain, ledger under her arm, and understood that she was being asked a question by a plant. [378]

She could reconstruct the trellis exactly. [384] She had the original drawing, the same cedar supplier, the measurements in her own careful hand. [400] Instead she spent three days doing something that felt like malpractice. [411] She rebuilt the frame around the shape the vine had chosen for itself when it fell -- following its slump, its westward lean, the low loop where it had cradled itself against the storm. [444] She tied nothing. [447] She only gave it wood where it already wanted wood, and moss where the moss had come back, and then she made herself walk away, which was the hardest labor of the year. [480]

Her apprentice, Tomas, asked what the new method was called. [490] 'Observation by releasing control,' she said, and heard Amadi laughing somewhere in the syllables. [504]

The rest of storm season she wrote less and looked more. [515] The vine climbed the new frame the way water finds a drain, unhurried, and in the third week of September it set buds -- forty-one of them, fat as knuckles. [544] Marisol watched the first one open at dusk and felt her own reluctance crack somewhere behind her sternum. [562] She had spent eleven years insisting that a scientist's job was to hold the world still enough to read. [581] Now she was becoming a different kind of person against her own preference, and it hurt the way a shell must hurt from the inside. [606]

Metamorphosis, she wrote in the ledger, is rarely volunteered. [615] It is sustained. [618] She turned to the page where the linden leaf lay, its veins still holding their small stubborn architecture, and finally wrote her reply beneath his question. [644] Everything, she answered, and then, because he had taught her the long way around: Me, apparently. [660]

In October she sold the moonflower cuttings with a card that said: Give it a frame, not a rule. [679] Customers thought it was a marketing line. [685] Tomas knew better, and pruned nothing that year, and the nursery in its bowl of hills went on being changed by weather, which was, Marisol had decided, the only honest way to grow anything. [719]
