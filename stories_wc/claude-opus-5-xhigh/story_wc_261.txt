The anesthesia let go of me at dawn, in the quiet tower, and the first voice I heard was the wisteria's. [21] It did not use words. [26] It pushed a feeling into me the way a root pushes through mortar: patient, blind, certain. [42] Doctor Anwe had warned me about strange sensations after surgery. [52] She had not warned me about being accidentally telepathic with plants. [63] The tower held that meteor hush you get in the minute after a bright thing crosses the sky, when everyone is still looking up and nobody dares speak. [91] I had come here for closure, which is a polite word for what I actually do. [107] For nine years I have made a living opening other people's wounds -- asking widows the question they buried, driving to towns where a family agreed never to say a name again. [138] I tell them a scar is only a story nobody has learned to read yet. [153] I came to Hesper's tower to learn to read them properly, and I arrived eleven days after she died. [172] Her students left me the keys, the wisteria, and a frosted hourglass. [184] The glass wore a permanent rime, inside, and the sand stood frozen in mid-fall, a grain suspended in the throat of it. [206] Hesper's note said only: a miracle came to the wrong address. [217]

My sister Enye died at nineteen of the same growth that Doctor Anwe cut out of my side yesterday afternoon. [237] Mine came out clean. [241] Mine was benign, absurdly, impossibly, after four specialists agreed it was not. [253] That is the displaced miracle: the thing our mother prayed for over Enye's bed arrived twenty-two years late, in the wrong body, carrying a side effect nobody ordered. [281] So I sat up in Hesper's cot with the drain still taped to my ribs, and I listened to a vine. [302] It wanted to show me the dark. [309] Its roots tunnel out from the tower's foundation, under the lane, under the churchyard wall, and they have been tunneling for sixty years. [332] They know the shape of every box down there, and one of them is Enye's. [347] The wisteria does not grieve; it simply keeps. [355]

Before I could bear that, I did what I always do first, which is read the room. [372] Hesper's desk was a map of coffee rings, and coffee rings are honest, layered like tree rings, brown over brown. [392] By examining coffee rings I can date a vigil: how many cups, how long they sat cooling, which nights she did not sleep. [415] Eleven rings crowded one page of her journal, overlapping like a chain of moons, and under the last one she had written a date and the word GRAFT. [443] It was the night Enye died. [449] The hourglass frosted that night too -- Hesper had traced the rime with a fingernail and dated it in the margin. [469] She had caught something that was falling and held it, the way you hold a coal you cannot put down, and she held it for twenty-two years until her hands failed. [500] Then it went to the nearest open wound, which was mine, on an operating table two hundred miles away. [519]

The wisteria showed me her scar next, because that is what it had been waiting to say. [536] Low on the main stem, a knuckle of old callus where Hesper had grafted a cutting the year Enye was born. [557] I put my thumb on it, and I read it: not words, but the whole account -- the wound made on purpose, the two lives pressed together, the seam that held because something was given up. [592] That is how scars read. [597] Not as accusations. [600] As inventories of what a thing survived and what it paid. [611] I have opened a hundred strangers' wounds looking for that sentence, and it was on my own ribs the whole time, under gauze, four hours old. [637]

Dawn came up grey through the tower's east window and touched the hourglass. [650] The rime went to water. [655] The suspended grain dropped, and the rest followed, a small brown whisper in that meteor hush, and it ran out in ninety seconds -- twenty-two years compressed into a minute and a half, which is all the time such things ever needed. [696] I did not go down to the churchyard. [704] The roots had already told me everything a shovel could. [714] Instead I made coffee, set the cup down on a clean page, and let it leave the first ring of my tenancy. [736] Then I wrote to the nine families whose wounds I had opened and never closed, and I offered them the only thing I had learned: your scar is not the record of what was taken. [771] It is the record of the graft that held. [780] Outside, the wisteria turned its blind attention back to the dark, keeping. [792]
