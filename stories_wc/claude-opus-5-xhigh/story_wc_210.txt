The last wish was granted at moonset, and by dawn the cove was empty. [14] Mireva watched the final family push their skiff past the reef, their paper lantern still burning on the stern like a coal that had forgotten it was paper. [42] She had been the guild's alchemist; now she was simply the woman left behind, deserted by seven apprentices, three masters, and the whole apparatus of belief that had made Lanthe Cove famous. [74]

For two hundred years, pilgrims had floated lanterns on the black water here, and the water had answered. [92] Each granted wish cost the cove a small, unnoticed thing: a variety of pear, a fishing knot, the name of a grandmother, the melody of a work song. [120] By the end there were nine hundred lanterns and no one who remembered how to mend a net. [138] The last wish, spoken by a tired fisherman, was simply to be spared any more wishing. [154] The water obliged, and the cove emptied, because there was nothing left in it to hold anyone. [171]

What remained in Mireva's workroom was an amber bottle with no opening. [183] It was the residue of the wish-engine, cooled and sealed by its own working: a fist-sized lozenge of gold-brown glass with a whole town suspended inside it, blurred, mid-gesture, warm to the touch. [216] Hold it to your ear and you could hear the market at noon. [229] Hold it long enough and you would sit down on the floor and never get up, which was how the masters had found old Peleg, smiling, three days gone. [258] That was the bottle's nature: it offered the cove back exactly as it had been, and it offered nothing else, and it could not be opened because there was nothing in it to pour. [292] Mireva set it face-down under a copper bowl and did not look at it for a year. [309]

She rejected the bottle for the same reason she had rejected the guild's last offer of passage to the mainland: comfort that asked nothing of her was a lie she could not afford. [342] She intended something harder - to conceive a civilization, not restore one. [353] And she would do it the only way left to her: through working memory, the alchemist's oldest discipline, holding many volatile things at once without letting any of them settle into a fixed form. [387] Writing would have been easier and would have been fatal; ink fixes a town as it was, and what the cove needed was a town as it might be. [416]

So she walked the empty streets each morning and rehearsed. [426] Ninety-four households. [428] Ten trades. [430] Four wells, one of them poisoned by the tannery in a year nobody had written down. [446] She held them all in her head at once, turning them like tumblers, and each morning she changed one thing on purpose: moved the tannery downwind, gave the pear its old bitterness back, took the lantern festival out of the calendar entirely. [488] If the arrangement still stood by evening, she kept it. [498] If it collapsed, she let it collapse and started again, and this was the difference between her work and a wish. [519] A wish never collapses; it simply keeps you. [527]

People came back the third spring, as she had calculated they would - first a mule-driver named Tass, who wanted somewhere no tax collector had a map of, then a family of net-menders, then a widow with a boat and a grudge. [568] They found the cove swept, the wells marked, the poisoned one capped in stone, and a woman who could recite where everything went and why. [593] She did not tell them about the lanterns. [601]

Tass found the amber bottle anyway, under its copper bowl, and held it up to the afternoon light with the delight of a man who has found treasure. [629] 'There's a town in here,' he said. [636] 'There was,' Mireva said, and took it from him, and walked it out to the end of the pier where the water still remembered how to answer. [663]

She did not throw it in. [669] Drowning it would have made it a legend, and legends are where new lanterns come from. [685] Instead she set it on a post in the middle of the new market, in full sun, where anyone could pick it up and listen for a slow count of ten while she stood beside them with her hand on their sleeve. [727] Ten was enough to hear the market at noon. [736] Eleven was enough to want it. [742]

The charts still call the place Paper-Lantern Cove, and the children ask why, and Mireva tells them the truth, which is long and unflattering and takes all evening, and which she makes them repeat back to her from memory. [781] It is not a comfortable story, which is why it holds. [792]
