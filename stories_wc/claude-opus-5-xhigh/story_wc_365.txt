Ilse Vantry made maps of how places felt. [8] Not their distances, but their sensations: the copper taste of recycled air, the way certain corridors pressed against the collarbone, the pitch at which a floor plate would sing. [37] The Archive of Vanished Places paid her in ration credits and silence, which suited her, because she had come to the abandoned spaceport repair bay for reasons the Archive did not need to know. [71] Bay Nine smelled of cold rust and old lubricant, of hydraulic fluid that had dried into amber scabs across the gantry. [92] Her sister Nell had walked out of this bay eleven years ago and had not walked back in. [110] Their mother had said only that Nell was lost, and had said it in the flat voice people use to cap a well. [133] Ilse had spent a decade learning to weigh things by feel, and she wanted, more than food or sleep, to discover the weight of that withheld truth. [160]

The green glass shard came from Nell's cracked helmet visor, a curved sliver the color of bottle bottoms. [178] It was cyclically spontaneous, and Ilse had mapped that too: three unpredictable wakings, then a long dead stretch, then three more, the pattern arriving like weather that obeyed a calendar nobody had published. [211] When it woke, it hummed against her sternum, and the humming only worked in the murmur of half-remembered dreams. [230] She had to lie down on the bay's gantry mesh and let herself drift to that thin layer where a dream is still a rumor, and there the shard would take her weaving between worlds. [265] Travel through parallel universes, she had learned, is not a leap but a stitch; you go where the fabric already touches. [286] So she always arrived in Bay Nine, somewhere. [294]

In the fourth world the bay was flooded to the catwalks and tasted of salt and iron. [311] In the ninth, the spaceport still ran, and freighters shook dust from the rafters, and a woman with Nell's jaw sold noodles to mechanics and did not know her. [340] In the seventeenth, the bay had burned, and the reactor beneath it had taken the whole port with it, and the air was still bitter after eleven years. [368] That was the map that made her stop and sit down on a scorched crate. [383] Seventeen worlds, and in sixteen of them the port stood; in the one where it did not, Nell had never gone into the reactor housing. [408]

Ilse had been hunting a sister. [414] She should have been hunting a document. [421] On the next waking of the shard she chose to redirect the stitch: not toward Nell, but toward the version of their mother's kitchen where the letter had never gone into the incinerator. [454] It took nine attempts, because the shard's cycle gave her only three tries at a time and each drift dumped her somewhere adjacent and wrong: a kitchen with no table, a kitchen with two mothers, a kitchen where she herself had been the one lost. [499] On the ninth she found the letter under a tin of chicory, and because she was a sensory map maker before she was a grieving woman, she weighed it in her palm before she read it. [535]

Nine grams. [537] Two folded sheets, a thumbprint of engine grease, and the words: the coolant loop is failing, they will not evacuate the district, I can hold the redirect valve open by hand for maybe an hour, do not tell Ilse I chose this. [579] Their mother had obeyed, and the obeying had weighed nine grams for eleven years, and it had bent her voice flat. [600]

Ilse woke on the gantry mesh in her own bay, in her own world, with the shard cold and quiet and the taste of chicory on her tongue. [628] She filed her report to the Archive that night. [637] Under textures she wrote: rust, cold hydraulic amber, mesh printing its grid into the shoulder blades. [653] Under sounds: one loose panel, wind, and something underneath the floor that had not been allowed to be a monument. [673] Under weight she wrote nine grams, and let the Archive wonder. [684]

Then she walked to the coastal ward where her mother sat with the lamps off, and she did not accuse her. [705] She said the district is still there, and so are eleven thousand people, and I know what she chose, and you can put it down now. [731] Her mother's hands opened, and the sound she made was small, and it did not need mapping. [748] The shard stayed silent for a long while after that, then woke again, as it always would, cyclically and without warning. [769] Ilse let it hum, and slept, and did not go. [779]
