The Ministry of Corrections hired me because I looked like a person who would never matter. [16] For six years I sponged names off ledgers, glued in the revised histories, and said thank you when the supervisor called me diligent. [39] He never noticed that I saved the sponges. [47] Wrung out at night into a jar, the ink gave back everything the state had wanted gone: Ilse Karmody, dockworkers' delegate, deleted; the Third Bread Riot, revised into a parade; my brother Teo, revised into never. [83] I am not brave in the way the posters mean. [93] I am carefully resourceful, which is the sort of courage that survives audits. [106] My most valuable possession is a brass key stamped Meridian Telephone and Telegraph, a company the censors erased so thoroughly that its property no longer legally exists. [133] Which means the forty-one derelict phone booths in this city are invisible: unlisted, unpatrolled, unowned. [148] The key opens their service panels, and behind each panel is a hollow where a folded page can wait for a week without anyone thinking a thought about it. [177] That is our whole network: coded freedom, one dead booth at a time. [190]

We were to gather at the auric lagoon on the ninth night, when the tide turns the shallows the color of poured gold and the starlight makes even the guards' lamps look poor. [223] No one writes directions to the lagoon. [230] The route is read from the city itself, at dusk, in puddles. [242] When the correctors painted out the old street names, they only reached as high as a ladder allowed; the true names remain on the upper cornices, upside down, and rain gives them back. [275] Walk the wet quarter at the hour the sun sits low, and the reflections spell a map no archive holds: Karmody Row, Ferryman's Cut, Lantern Steps down to the water. [305]

On the ninth evening the plans unraveled before I reached the second puddle. [318] Vasek, our printer, was taken at four o'clock with the courier list in his coat. [333] By six, three booths had been welded shut, and a van sat at the Lantern Steps with its engine idling like a patient animal. [357] Everyone senior to me went to ground, which they were right to do, and which left the ninth night to the clerk nobody had ever bothered to fear. [385] I did not go to the lagoon by the Lantern Steps. [396] I read the puddles backward. [401] A reflected map has no preferred direction; the same water that shows you the way down shows you the culvert that comes up under the fish sheds, if you are willing to be patient and filthy. [437] I carried the jar of recovered ink, forty-one pages, and the key. [449]

The lagoon at midnight was exactly as the old songs claimed, gold under starlight, a bowl of quiet light with eleven people standing in it, waiting to be arrested. [478] They expected Vasek's press and a proclamation. [485] I got them out through the culvert and gave them a lesson in glue and geography instead. [502] Tyranny here is not a boot; it is a shrug, a clerk stamping RECONCILED and going to lunch, and I had decided long ago that someone had to hold the line against that. [535] So I told them how to hold it. [543] Each of them took a page and a booth number. [553] Each page was a single erased life, copied in the state's own recovered ink: name, trade, the true date, the true crime. [575] The key went around the circle, one panel at a time, over nine nights. [589]

Nothing exploded; nobody shouted. [593] The pages simply began to reappear where the deletions had been, pasted inside the glass of dead booths on every corner of the city, in the state's own hand, unremovable without admitting that the booths were there at all. [632] People stopped to read them, because a phone booth is a thing a person leans into, alone, out of the rain. [653] By the second month the correctors were issuing denials, and a denial is a confession with better grammar. [671] They arrested a printer, two dockworkers, and a man who sold umbrellas, and they never once searched my desk drawer, where the jar sat beside the sponges and the glue. [701]

Yesterday my supervisor complained that some vandal keeps putting up the name of a woman called Ilse Karmody, and asked whether I had ever heard of her. [728] I said no, and thanked him for the correction, and went out at dusk to check the puddles. [746] Teo's page goes up tonight, at the booth on Ferryman's Cut, under the same gold water and the same stars that were there before anyone thought to revise them. [775]
