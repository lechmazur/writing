The bell tuner arrived in Corvane Hollow the autumn the last roof caved in, carrying nothing but an ebony tuning fork and a canvas roll of felt hammers. [28] I was the only one left to meet him, and I was only there to sell the place. [46] He stopped under the ancient archway at the head of Miller Street, where the cobblestones begin, and he listened for a long time to nothing. [71] Nothing was what Corvane had instead of weather now: a wild hush, the kind that makes your ears invent things. [91] "The arch is a fifth flat," he said. [99] I told him arches do not have pitches. [107] He struck the ebony fork against his knee and held it to the keystone, and the old granite hummed back, sour and low, like a man remembering an argument. [136] I have written down what he told me, because I still do not entirely understand it, and understanding may not be required. [158]

Corvane Hollow had eleven bells once, one for each family that laid stone here, and they were tuned not to a scale but to each other. [184] That, he said, was the shared balance: eleven notes leaning on one another so that no single house could ring alone and be right. [208] When the quarry closed, the families sold their bells one at a time, each sale a small reasonable thing, and the town went out of tune before it went out of people. [240] He had been walking to towns like mine for forty years, always arriving before the collapse. [256] Not after, he insisted; after is masonry, and masonry is nobody's music. [268] The arch would come down within a year, he judged, and the arch was the town's throat. [285]

Then he explained how he intended to rebuild Corvane Hollow, and I laughed at him, and he waited, infinitely patient, until I stopped. [308] Stone is set by vibration, he said. [315] Cobblestones knit tight under regular footfall and heave loose under none; mortar cures to the rhythm it is given; a street that is walked is a street that holds. [344] So he would rebuild the town via the rhythm of footsteps on cobblestones, and he would begin with mine. [363]

Every morning that winter he set me walking from the archway to the dry fountain and back while he crouched with the fork pressed to the stones, feeling for the beat that made the ground answer. [399] Slower, he would say, or, a half-step wider, and I obeyed because I had nothing better and because the hush was easier to stand with a sound in it. [428] By February the loose stones near the gutter had settled flush. [439] I stopped telling myself it was frost. [446]

In March a roofer named Delia came looking for salvage slate, heard two sets of boots keeping time on Miller Street, and stayed a week for reasons she could not name. [477] The tuner gave her a rhythm of her own, a limping three against my four, and the two together made the cobbles ring like a struck rim. [504] Walkers are cheaper than masons, he said, and they last longer. [515] By midsummer there were nine of us: Delia's brother, two sisters who had sold their grandmother's bell in 1961 and wanted to hear what they had done, a man who simply liked the arch. [549] Nine walkers, nine rhythms, and the tuner moving among us with the ebony fork like a man setting a table. [569] He never explained why nine was not enough, and we did not ask; we understood that he was restoring a balance that had to be shared or it was not balance at all. [602]

On the last night of August the keystone dropped a quarter inch and stopped, and the whole arch spoke one clean note over our heads, and every one of us stood still. [634] The tuner touched the fork to the granite, listened, and said the arch was a true fifth now, and would hold as long as we kept walking. [661]

He left in September for a valley town whose mill had shut in spring. [675] I asked him, at the archway, what he actually was. [685] He said he tuned bells. [690] I said there were no bells in Corvane Hollow. [699] He said there were eleven, and one of them was me, and then he walked off down the cobbles keeping perfect time with himself. [723]

We are eleven now, counting the baby, who has no rhythm yet. [735] Mornings, we walk. [738] The hush is still out there past the last streetlamp, wild and waiting, and we hold it off with our feet. [759]
