Every autumn the town let its grief go upward, and Ilse Varga stayed below to light the pier. [18] She worked the late shift, walking the boards with her taper while the sky lanterns rose over the harbor like slow orange planets. [41] Above them, the Perseid remnant scratched thin silver lines, and the crowd went quiet in that particular way, a meteor hush, as if noise might knock the stars loose. [70]

Ilse could not hear the hush. [76] She had lost the high registers at nine, the year the Coriander went down in the shallows off the breakwater with her mother's choir aboard. [101] What remained of hearing was pressure and color: a bell struck brass-yellow behind her eyes, a gull's cry a scrape of white. [123] She wanted, more than anything, to discover the color of her mother's voice. [136]

When the pier keeper asked why she took the worst shift, she told the truth: the lamps needed lighting, and no one else would do it. [162] It was true, and it hid everything; she had learned young that a fact, offered plainly, makes a fine door to stand behind. [185] In her coat pocket she carried a brass hotel skeleton key tag stamped CORIANDER - LIBRARY DECK C, salvaged the week after the wreck and never surrendered. [211]

The lantern release lasted one hour. [217] That hour was the only time the harbor patrol watched the sky instead of the water. [233] She lit the last lamp, hung her taper, and went off the ladder in her diving skin with a hand light strapped to her wrist. [258]

The Coriander lay eleven meters down, tilted, her decks silted like an unmade bed. [272] Ilse had never found the library by chart or count; the old lamplighter who trained her said wrecks refuse arithmetic and yield only to wandering purposefully, which meant swimming with a question rather than a route. [308] So she held her question - what color was she singing? - and let the corridors answer. [323] Doorways offered themselves, and a stairwell bent the way her question bent. [335] The skeleton key still hung from the tag, and it turned the Deck C lock the way a name fits a face. [357]

Inside was the forgotten library: nine cases of swollen books, a reading table bolted down, brass rails furred with rust. [377] Nobody dove here anymore; the town preferred its grief airborne. [387] On the table, under a cap of silt, lay the ship's guest book. [400] She had opened it a dozen nights and found only sodden ink. [412] Tonight her light caught something new. [418] The pages had begun to bloom. [424]

Some patient colony had taken to the old ink, and the writing came up in cold green fire - the passengers' vows, made the last evening, when the choir sang and everyone promised something to someone. [459] Luminous vows, line after line. [464] I will learn the cello; I will forgive my brother; I will come home before the feast. [481] Near the bottom, in a hand she knew better than her own: Vera Varga - I vow to teach Ilse that sound has a color, and that mine is the green of a lamp seen through rain. [517]

The glow in the page was exactly that green. [526] Ilse stayed until her air went thin, one gloved finger under the line, learning it. [541] She did not take the book; paper that has waited this long deserves to stay where it was patient. [560] She locked the door behind her, pocketed the tag, and rose through the lanterns' reflections. [575]

On the pier, the keeper asked where she had been. [585] Swimming, she said, which was true, and which was a door. [596] Then she opened it. [600] My mother's voice was green, she told him, and the lamps I light every night are meant to signify that - a green vow, kept, burning where the water can see it. [631] The keeper looked at the sky, where the last lanterns were going out one by one, and said nothing, which was the correct answer. [655]

Before the week was out, she had fitted every lamp on the pier with green glass. [671] Sailors complained that it looked like nothing else on the coast. [682] That was the point. [686] Below, in the dark of Deck C, the vows kept their slow burning, and Ilse, walking her boards in the meteor hush, finally knew what the harbor sounded like. [715] It sounded green. [718]
