The fever broke at three in the morning, and the mountain let me hear it go. [16] Sweat cooled on my neck; outside, the anemometer cups turned in a slow, undecided circle, the way certainties sway before they fall. [38] Kirn Ridge Station is two rooms and a radio mast, nine thousand feet above the nearest apology. [55] I had been delirious for four days, and in that time I had not once told anyone what I had done. [76] Mira's bunk was still made. [81]

In the corner, under a tarp that hummed, sat the oracle. [92] The Survey called it a fault-tolerant oracle: a prediction engine built to keep working while it broke, rerouting thought through whatever cells still answered. [116] Half of it had failed in the twenty years since anyone climbed up to service it, and it had simply grown politer about being wrong. [141] It answered by occupying margins. [146] Its printing arm was long dead; what remained was a fine stylus that could only mark the blank edges of a page laid open on its plate, and it was peripherally aware in the same fashion - it perceived nothing you looked at directly, only what stood at the edge of your attention. [198]

Mira used to feed it seed catalogues and laugh at its forecasts. [210] I fed it my mother's botanical illustration. [217] It was a plate torn from her field atlas: a spring gentian in graphite and blue wash, root system drawn with the same care as the flower, wide clean margins on every side. [250] She had drawn it the winter she taught me that honest work means writing down the number you actually see. [270]

Five days earlier I had transcribed the pressure trace wrong - a four for a nine, tired, certain - and the forecast I sent down the valley said the slope would hold. [300] Mira went out at dawn to swap the ridge sensor, and the cornice came down on her at ten past seven. [321] When the rescue team reached us, I was already burning with fever, and my original tape - the one with the true figure in my own hand - was in my mouth. [351] I swallowed it while they were still stamping snow off their boots. [363]

So when the fever broke, I did not want comfort; I wanted to know the weight of a swallowed truth, in grams, the way I would want the weight of any object I had to carry. [399] I laid the gentian on the plate, closed the hatch, and looked hard at the flower so that the question would sit at the edge of things where the oracle could see it. [432]

The stylus began in the left margin, patient as frost. [442] PAPER TAPE, 0.9 GRAMS, it wrote. [448] Then, down the outer edge, beside the root: CONFIDENCE 61 PERCENT. [459] It never gave certainties; it gave numbers that swayed, and it wrote what it could not verify in smaller letters, so you would know which of its bones were broken. [489]

I asked again, differently, without speaking, the way you ask a mountain. [501] The stylus crossed the top margin: THE PAPER WEIGHS WHAT IT WEIGHS; THE REST IS WHAT THE VALLEY WOULD HAVE DONE WITH IT, AND I CANNOT WEIGH THE UNDONE. [530] In the bottom margin, smaller and hedged, it added: I AM ONLY EDGES, AND YOU ARE THE PART THAT MUST SPEAK. [551]

I took the page out with both hands and my thumb came down wet across the gentian's petals. [569] The graphite lifted and spread; I had put a smudge through my mother's best flower, a grey bruise where the blue had been. [592] I stood there a long time, and I understood that this was the size of it: not a number, but a mark you cannot rub out and cannot stop touching. [622]

Outside, the sky had begun to do the thing Mira called the green tide - aurora sliding down the north in slow curtains, lighting the snow the color of pond water, beautiful and unbearably indifferent. [656] She used to wake me for it, every time. [665]

I opened the radio at 05:40 and raised the valley station. [676] I said my name and my post number, and then I said that the forecast of the fourteenth was wrong because I had copied the figure wrong, and that Mira Sandal walked out on my mistake. [712] I said there was a tape and that I had destroyed it, and I told them how. [729] The valley operator asked me to repeat it slowly, and I did, and my voice did not sway. [747]

Behind me the stylus was moving again, hedging some new small probability into the margins of a flower nobody would ever print again. [770] I did not turn to look. [776] It only ever wrote for people who were looking somewhere else. [787]
