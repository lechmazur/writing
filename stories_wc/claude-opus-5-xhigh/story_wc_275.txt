After the tower fell, the desert learned a new silence, and my roses learned it with me. [17] I am Mira Halle, and I keep the glass-domed farm at Ashkettle Spring, four hundred bushes of desert rose under a sky of curved panes. [42] At noon the light comes down as a tranquil blaze, heat without violence, and the petals hold it the way a good listener holds a confession. [68] My father hunted people for money before the tower fell, and after, when there was no law to sell them to, he kept hunting anyway. [93] He calls me senselessly perceptive. [98] He means that I notice everything and use none of it: the tremor in his knife hand, the second set of bootprints outside the airlock, the way a bush droops for three days after a man shouts near it. [137] He is wrong about the use. [143] I have never struck a living thing, and I do not intend to begin; that is not softness but a discipline, like watering late. [167] A rose is a record. [172] Grandmother taught me that you can inculcate a plant with values the way you inculcate a child, by repetition and by consequence, and that the cuttings carry the habit forward. [202] Row Seven is patience: I water it late, always, and always eventually. [214] Row Twelve is mercy, thinned by hand so no stem is choked out. [227] Row One is quiet, and nothing loud has happened in Row One for eleven years. [242] I label the rows with scrap cut from an airship ballast sandbag, the stiff tarred canvas that outlasts everything, and I burn the words into it with a hot nail. [272] The tag on Row One is my best work: an engraved quiet, five letters pressed deep enough to read with a thumb in the dark. [297] That is the point of writing on ballast: when the words are gone, the weight remains. [313] My father came through the airlock at the hour of the blaze, dragging a boy of maybe sixteen by a wrist rope. [335] Behind him the storm was standing up off the flats like a wall being built in a hurry. [353] "Water," he said, "and the floor until it passes." [362] His knife was already out, because his knife is always already out. [374] "Sheathe it," I said. [378] "He bit me twice." [382] "Sheathe it, or stand in the storm." [389] He looked at Row One. [394] He knows what the rows are; he helped my grandmother dig the first trench before he decided that the world paid better than roses. [418] The blade went into the leather with a sound like a held breath, and eleven years stayed unbroken. [436] We sat while the sand scoured the dome, three of us and four hundred witnesses. [451] I cut the boy's rope, which my father allowed, because he is tired and because inside the dome he becomes the man who dug the trench. [477] "He stole a cistern pump," my father said. [485] "From whom?" [487] He had no answer, because the tower fell and took the answers with it; there is no one left who owns a pump, only people who hold one. [515] This is what I am for. [521] Not to stop the chaos, which I cannot, but to weave continuity through it, one green thread at a time. [541] Somebody has to keep something alive that remembers how to behave. [552] By dusk the storm thinned and the light went low and gold, still blazing, still tranquil. [568] I took two cuttings from Row One and potted them in tins. [580] From the sandbag scrap I cut two tags and burned the word into each, deep, and wired them to the stems. [601] One I gave to the boy, with water and a direction. [612] One I gave to my father. [618] "It will die in my saddlebag," he said. [626] "Then it will have taught you something about what you carry," I said, and he laughed and did not argue, which is how he apologizes. [651] He went out into the cooling dark with his knife sheathed, and I noticed, senselessly, that he took the long road, the one that avoids the boy's tracks. [679] Perception is not senseless when you plant what you see. [689] In some year I will not live to see, a stranger will find a rose growing in a ruin, thriving in a place where nothing loud has happened for a very long time. [722] There will be a scrap of tarred canvas at its root, and on it, still legible to a thumb, the engraved quiet. [744] They will not know my name, and that is continuity enough. [755]
