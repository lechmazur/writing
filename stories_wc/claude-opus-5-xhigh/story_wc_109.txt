The last storm of the year broke itself on our ridge fort, and I counted the stars through the gaps it tore in the roof. [25] Nine, then four, then one. [30] I am Vesh, keeper of the fort's cold library, and I have been mildly obsessed with the dimming for eleven years. [51] Mildly, because obsession that shouts gets sent down the mountain; mine only whispers, and whispering is permitted here. [69] My whole purpose, written in the fort's charter in ink older than my grandmother, is to draw a map of an endless night. [92] A map needs landmarks, and when the stars go out there is nothing left to survey but silence. [110] That is why I sent for the pebble-shore oracle. [119] She came up the switchbacks in the tail of the storm, an old woman with wet gray hair and a sack of stones from the beach where she works. [148] Down there she does not read palms or entrails. [157] She reads the pauses between waves, and charges two coins to tell you what the pause meant. [174] Sailors call it fraud. [178] I call it the only surveying instrument left in the world, because the oracle works by summarizing silence. [196] I showed her the booklet. [201] It is small enough to hide in a fist, bound in gold leaf that flakes off onto everything I own, so that my sleeves and knuckles glitter like a poor man's idea of wealth. [235] Twelve pages remained blank. [239] "You want the dark charted," she said, turning the flaking gold leaf booklet over, "but the dark has no coastlines." [259] "It has silences," I said. [264] "Each one different, the way each bay is different." [273] She laughed, and then she stopped laughing, because the wind quit all at once. [287] We went to the parapet together. [292] One star remained in the dimming sky, low over the sea, the color of a candle seen through a shut hand. [313] The storm had gone elsewhere to be a storm, leaving our storm-tossed ridge fort dripping. [328] What settled in its place was not quiet but something thicker, a marbled hush, veined with faint sounds the way stone is veined with paler stone: a goat bell four valleys off, my own blood, the sea folding. [366] The oracle set her stones along the wall, one pebble for each silence she intended to name. [383] Then she began, and I wrote. [389] "This first silence is a held breath," she said, "and it summarizes in six words: the world waits, unsure of permission." [410] I inked it on page one and drew a small bay around it. [423] "This one is older," she said, moving a second pebble to the wall, "the hush of amber, sap that closed over a fly and kept it looking surprised for nine thousand years." [455] "Nothing in it moves, but nothing in it is lost." [465] That was the sentence that saved us, though neither of us knew it then. [478] I named the place on the page the ambered hush and gave it a wide harbor, since it seemed a thing a person might shelter in. [504] We worked until the last star thinned to a rumor. [514] Eleven pages filled: the hush after an argument, the hush of snow deciding, the hush of a fort whose garrison has been dead two hundred years and never told. [543] One page left, and the star went out. [551] Then came the silence I had feared for eleven years, and it had no shape at all. [568] It was not a bay or a harbor. [576] It was the whole sheet of the page. [584] "Summarize that," I whispered, and gold flakes came off the cover into the dark like the last of the stars. [604] The oracle took my hand and put her final pebble in it. [616] "Two words," she said, "still here." [622] I wrote them on the twelfth page, and the map was done: not endless after all, only large, and now bounded on every side by someone who had been there to say so. [655] Every night since, in the fort's black kitchen, I retell it to the children the sailors send up the mountain. [675] I read them the twelve silences and let them hold the booklet until their palms are gold. [692] They ask whether the stars will come back. [700] I tell them the truth, which the oracle taught me on a ridge in a marbled hush: I do not know, but nothing in the amber is lost, and we are the ones who kept looking surprised. [737] Then I turn the page, and we listen, and I write down what they hear. [752]
