The last of the lending libraries burned in April, and by June the woman who lit it had vanished from every grid I knew how to search. [27] Corin had been my friend for thirty years, and I still could not decide whether she was a criminal or a keeper. [49] The prosecutors called her the lending library arsonist; I called her the only honest person in the antiquities trade. [68] Those little roadside libraries were not libraries at all, but drop boxes for looted Ashuun tablets, and she burned the boxes only after emptying them. [93] She left me a trail instead of a letter, because she knew I read pictures better than words. [111] First came a mural on a grain silo outside Halth: two girls kneeling at a loom, their hands stained the same red. [133] Then a mosaic set into a bus shelter, then a lintel carved above a shepherd's door, then a kiln-fired bowl sold cheap in a market at Ves. [160] Each work repeated one wrong detail, and I am analytically intuitive enough to trust the itch before I can prove it. [181] The wrong details were letters in the old Ashuun syllabary, drawn as flaws: a snapped warp thread, a tessera turned backward, a chip in a rim. [207] Eleven pieces, eleven syllables, and the hidden message assembled itself on the back of a ferry ticket: COME WHERE THE MOUNTAIN KEEPS ITS BREATH. [231] I knew the place, because we had found it together at nineteen. [243] Under the south flank of Kesse there is a lava tube the Ashuun swept and shelved and filled with their memory: the last archive of a civilization that let itself be forgotten on purpose. [277] Our fathers were the men who first cut into it with saws. [289] We were nineteen and ashamed, and we promised each other, hands on the loom, that we would put back everything they took. [311] Corin kept the promise for thirty years; I got a museum salary and forgot it. [326] So I went off the grid myself: no phone, no cards, a mule track up through steam vents and yellow moss. [347] The mountain breathed warm as an animal against my palms. [357] Inside, the tube was lit by her lamps, and the shelves were full again. [371] Tablet by tablet, crate by crate, she had carried back what the drop boxes held, and she had labeled every return in her small furious hand. [397] At the end of the gallery, propped on the old loom, was her self-portrait. [411] She had slashed it. [415] Someone might have called it vandalism and left it at that, but I sat with the canvas on my knees for an hour, and the cuts were deliberate, a script of blade strokes across her painted mouth and throat. [454] She had refused to portray herself as innocent; she had let the knife say what her face could not. [473] Read as syllabary, the slashes said: ONE THING IS STILL MISSING, AND YOU HAVE IT. [488] I did. [490] In my coat, wrapped in linen, was the faded tapestry fragment my father had cut from the great loom-cloth the summer I turned twelve. [514] Fourteen threads wide, ochre gone to dust-pink, it had hung framed above my desk for twenty years while I wrote grant proposals about stewardship. [538] I found the gap in the cloth on the loom, and the fragment slid home like a bone into its socket. [559] Where it joined, the woven figures completed a line no scholar had ever read: WE ARE NOT LOST, ONLY KEPT. [579] Corin came out of the dark then, thinner than her wanted posters, smelling of smoke that would never wash out. [599] She did not embrace me. [604] She said, "Thirty years I waited for you to remember," and her voice shook with something hotter and more careful than anger, the tenderness of a thing that has been under pressure so long it can only come out as fire. [645] "You could have written," I said. [651] "You would have filed it," she said, and that was true, and we both laughed until the laughing turned into the other thing. [674] We worked three weeks in the warm dark, shelving, mending, listing. [685] She would not come down with me; there are seven charges waiting in three countries, and she says the archive needs a doorkeeper more than the courts need a defendant. [715] I brought down the slashed self-portrait, and it hangs now where the tapestry fragment used to hang. [732] Visitors ask why I keep a ruined painting of a criminal. [743] I tell them it is not ruined; it is written, and I am learning to read it aloud. [761]
