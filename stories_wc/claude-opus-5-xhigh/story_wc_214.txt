The Quiet Interval had no historians, which suited Ovel, because it meant nobody came to check his logs. [18] His title, unchanged since the founding charter, was cosmic janitor, third class, Tessellin Ridge, the last shooting star tracking station on a moon nobody named anymore. [44] His work was simple: sweep the ash that meteors left on the dome, oil the tracking gears, and record every fall in a ledger that no office had opened in ninety years. [76] The empires that built him a job had burned out; what remained was hush, and mountains, and the slow polite falling of stars. [99] Ovel was spiritually curious in the way of men who work alone with a broom and a sky, and he had read the scriptures of four dead faiths without finding an answer. [131] He kept, on the sill of the observation slot, a disc of yellow lantern glass, the last piece of his mother's signal lamp. [154] She had used it to guide freighters home through the dust, and she had told him that light which hides itself saves nobody. [177] He had spent forty years disagreeing with her. [185]

The falls began behaving oddly in the second month of the long night. [198] Meteors are supposed to arrive like weather, thoughtless and scattered, but these came in triads, always three, always angling toward the same dark quadrant beyond the ridge. [225] Ovel did what a janitor could do, which was to keep a ledger honestly, and by tracing the path of falling stars across sixty pages he found the shape of an intention. [257] The triads were not falling at his moon; they were falling past it, deliberately shallow, the way a person waves an arm slowly so as not to frighten a horse. [287] Somebody was dropping their dead ships into the atmosphere to ask, without words, whether anything down here still had eyes. [307] He knew the arithmetic of that gesture, because he had made it himself, every evening for four decades, sweeping a dome that no one would inspect. [333] To empathize, he understood then, is not to pity from a safe distance; it is to admit you have stood in the same posture. [357]

Answering was the hard part. [362] The station's beam still worked, a stubborn old thing, but protocol from the burned empires forbade illumination: a lit station is a mapped station, and a mapped station is a target. [393] Ovel had obeyed that protocol through wars, through famine convoys, through the death of his mother on a freighter that missed the harbor by nine kilometers in the dust. [422] Obedience had kept him alive and had kept him small. [432] He wanted, at the end, to find strength in vulnerability instead, to learn whether the thing he had called cowardice all his life was simply an unused muscle. [460]

So on the night the ledger predicted the next triad, he climbed the gantry with his broom, his ledger, and the yellow lantern glass. [484] He cleaned the lens for the last time, out of habit and out of love. [499] Then he fitted the yellow disc over the beam, because white light says machine and yellow light says hearth, and he wanted whoever was out there to know the difference. [529]

The triad came at moonrise, three long amber scratches down the dark. [541] Ovel threw the switch. [545] Warm light poured out of the ridge and up, unarmored, honest, a small yellow word in a language of ash. [565] He stood in the open slot and let himself be seen, and he found that his hands were shaking, and that the shaking was not fear alone. [592]

Nothing struck him. [595] No weapon, no rescue, no voice. [601] What arrived instead, over the following hours, was stillness of a kind he had never met: not the empty hush of an overlooked era, but a stillness that had somebody in it. [633] The triads stopped, and in their place, at the edge of the tracking screen, three faint steady lights held position and did not fall. [657] They stayed for eleven nights, keeping their distance the way strangers do at a wake, and each night Ovel lit the yellow glass and swept his dome and wrote it down. [688] Nothing more happened, and everything did. [694] He had expected revelation with teeth in it, some primal thunder, but what came was primal in a quieter way, elegant as a bone: two lonely things agreeing to remain visible to each other. [728]

On the twelfth night the lights moved off, and Ovel did not grieve much, because they had left him something growing. [749] He kept the beam lit after that, every night, a flourishing stillness on a nameless moon, and he understood at last what his mother had meant. [775] Light that hides itself saves nobody, least of all the one holding it. [788]
