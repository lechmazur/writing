Halloran Vane had buried the drowned for thirty-one years, and the sea had returned nearly all of them. [18] Storms unstitched his hillside graveyard; coffins surfaced in the tide pools like bad debts. [32] He was weary in a way sleep could not reach, because he wanted only one thing: to give rest a permanent address. [54]

The package arrived on a Tuesday, wrapped in oilcloth, bearing no postmark and no sender. [69] Inside lay a wedge of a broken lighthouse lens, one third of a Fresnel eye, its prisms chipped and clouded. [89] Halloran held it to the window out of idle curiosity, and the harbor filled with lights. [105] Hundreds of them, low and green, blinking in patterns he had learned as a boy on the lifeboat crew: distress signals. [126] They came from under the water, from every drowned soul he had ever failed to keep buried, and they were all spelling the same word: WHERE. [152]

He should have thrown the lens into the fire. [161] Instead he carried it down to the churchyard well, because Halloran Vane was inadvertently stubborn; he never decided to persist, he simply failed, again and again, to stop. [189]

Through the lens the well was not a well but a staircase, and the staircase went down farther than the hill was tall. [212] That was the lens's gift and its cruelty: it let a man occupy impossible spaces, a lung of air inside a fathom of ocean, a corridor inside a stone. [241] He walked for an hour and arrived, dry-shoed, in the forgotten library of drowned Marrow-under-Wave, a city no chart remembered. [261]

Water stood in the aisles like green glass, holding itself back from the shelves out of old habit. [279] Eels slept in the card catalog. [285] At the library's heart stood a lighthouse, drowned to its collar, its lantern room dark and missing exactly one wedge of lens. [307] A brass plate at its door explained the arrangement in the flat language of clerks. [322] Every soul taken by the sea was to be catalogued here, given a shelf, a number, a permanent address; the lamp above would then signal the answer, and the signaling dead would go quiet and stay. [358] But the last librarian had drowned mid-entry, three centuries ago, mid-name, and the lamp had gone out, and the sea had been shouting WHERE ever since. [384]

Halloran understood then why the graves kept opening at home: the dead were not restless, they were unaddressed, and they had been sending the lens out into the world like a bottled note until it found somebody weary enough to answer. [425]

He set down his own ledger, the one with thirty-one years of names in it, and began to copy. [444] The work was enormous and it revitalized him the way cold water revitalizes a fainting man: unkindly, completely. [462] He wedged himself into places no body should fit, the seam behind the stacks, the inch between one shelf and the next, because the library obeyed the lens and made room wherever he insisted there was room. [499] Grethe Sandhal, drowned 1911, shelf nine, third from the aisle. [509] The boy with no name from the wreck of the Corvid, shelf twelve, and Halloran wrote "Beloved, unnamed" beside it, since an address needs a resident. [535]

Above him, one by one, the green lights stopped asking. [545] Stillness came down the aisles toward him, and he found he was afraid of it. [560] He had chased quiet his whole life and never once considered what it would want from him when it caught up. [581]

When the ledger held every name, he climbed the drowned lighthouse and fitted the broken wedge into the lantern's empty socket. [602] It would never turn true again; a third of its light would always leak away into the dark. [620] Still, it lit, and it spelled out across the seabed the only reply the drowned had ever wanted: HERE. [639]

He meant to stay one night, to be sure the lamp held. [651] That was the trouble with him; he had never once managed to leave a thing half-tended. [667]

Up in the world the hillside graveyard settled, its coffins staying where they were put, and the townspeople said the undertaker had gone off somewhere to rest at last. [696] They were not wrong. [700] The last entry in the ledger is in his own hand, unhurried, the letters of a man who has finally stopped being followed: Halloran Vane, keeper, shelf one, at home. [730]
