Rain came to Fort Qaliya once in nine years, and it left the walls sturdy and brittle at once, like a crust of salt. [24] Numa the cook went out at dawn to read the water. [35] In the courtyard hollows, the runoff had settled into pools, and pools, she knew, were archives. [51] Her grandmother had taught her to analyze tidal pool formations on the western coast: the ring of salt, the drowned grains, the way a pool's rim recorded whatever had drunk from it. [83] Here in the desert the same laws held, because the desert had once been a sea, and old seas do not forget their manners. [107] She crouched, tilted her head, and traced the spiral of grit in the third basin. [122] It pointed south-southwest, past the granary, toward the coral icebound pavilion beneath the dunes. [136] The pavilion was older than the fortress and colder than any well. [148] Fossil coral had grown into a roof there, and the sea's last breath had frozen under it and never left. [168]

Numa cooked, and Numa tasted. [173] Every ingredient carried the memory of the hands that had raised it, and every eater left a memory in the spoon. [194] That was why the garrison had bought her contract: soldiers who cannot sleep are soldiers who cannot hold a wall. [214] Her work was to sandpaper rough memories. [220] She would stew barley with dune thyme, ladle it into a man's bowl, and taste from his spoon the burr of what he had done at the oasis nine years ago. [251] The memory stayed; only its edges came away, and men wept less and marched better. [266] The edges went into her. [271]

Remorse, once stored, does not evaporate. [277] It sat in her like grit in a mill, and it made her dangerous to touch. [293] Grief in her was contagious through skin; a brush of her wrist could hand a stranger somebody else's worst hour, whole and hot. [316] So she wore gloves of oiled linen and served with long spoons and had not embraced anyone in eleven years. [336] Once every nine years, when the rain came, she went to empty herself. [349]

Captain Idris found her at the granary gate with her satchel and her gloves already gray with dust. [367] "The men dreamed it again last night," he said. [376] Nobody in the fortress ever described the dream aloud; describing it would make it a fact. [392] But they all knew its shape: a village well, a torch, an order given and obeyed. [408] "It always comes back when it rains," Numa said. [417] "Water remembers first." [420]

She walked out at the close of that unspoken dream, while the last sleepers were still turning, and the wet sand held her boot prints like a ledger. [448] The dune had shifted; the rain had peeled it back, and coral showed through like a knuckle. [465] Inside, the air bit. [469] Ice sheeted the coral columns, and inside the ice hung nine years of stored remorse, layered like varnish, each layer a season of other men's shame. [495] She had learned the trick from her grandmother too: cold holds what heat sets loose. [510]

Numa peeled off her gloves, laid both palms on the frozen column, and let the pavilion subsume what she carried. [530] It went out of her in a long shudder, nine years of burrs and splinters, and the ice took it and grew a little thicker. [555] She stood there a long while, hollow as a rinsed pot, and honest enough to admit what she was. [574] Not a healer. [577] A woman who sanded the guilt off men so they could do it again in nine years and sleep. [596] The pavilion was sturdy; it was also brittle, and one day it would crack, and everything stored would run out into the sand at once. [621]

So she reached back into the ice and took one layer with her, the oldest: the well, the torch, the order. [642] It burned in her chest all the way home across the steaming flats. [655] Captain Idris was waiting at the gate with his hand out for her satchel, as he always was. [673] Numa gave him her bare hand instead. [680] He went white, then wet-eyed, then still. [687] "You'll want to write it down," she said, "before the rain dries." [699] Then she went into the kitchen, lit the fire, and began a stew that would not smooth anything at all. [719]
