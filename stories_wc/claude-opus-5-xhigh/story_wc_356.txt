Maren climbed to Ilsevane on the ninth day, and the ladder swayed as if the forest were breathing. [18] The village hung in the crowns of the salt-cedars, a mysterious knot of bridges and lantern-baskets that no map admitted, and it never stopped moving. [43] Below, the tide worked the roots; above, the wildflower boxes breathed a hush so soft that Maren heard her own pulse. [64] She was an herbalist, ambitious in the way of people who have been told they are talented too early. [83] She had come to grow the one plant no lowland garden could hold: a corpse flower, from a single seed the size of a thumbnail. [108] The seed had arrived wrapped in brown paper, and Maren, obeying a family custom older than her name, had unfolded the wrapping and turned it inside out. [135] Her grandmother had doodled there, as all their family doodled on wrapping-paper interiors: pictures for the receiver's eyes alone, ink hidden against the gift. [159] This one showed a slack sea with no arrows on it, a beetle with a comic mustache, and a cradle rocking on a branch. [183] Maren had laughed, folded it into her journal, and thought no more of it than she thought of weather. [202]

The villagers of Ilsevane gave her a growing-basket on the eastern bough and asked only that she journalize what her nose told her. [225] They knew, somehow, that she was olfactorily sensitive to the point of affliction: rain three hours out, fever in a child, rot in a rope. [250] So she wrote each evening by lanternlight, and each evening the pages complained of the same thing: the wildflowers were thinning. [271] Fewer beetles came up from the marsh; fewer seeds set; the hush was becoming an emptiness. [287]

Maren asked when she should plant, and the eldest bridge-keeper answered: at the turning of the tide's thought. [305] It was their phrase for the slack minute between ebb and flood, when the water below forgets its direction and the whole village stands still. [330] Ilsevane lived for that minute; it was why they had built high in a moving thing. [346] They had come to cultivate serenity within motion, and the slack tide was their teacher's single word. [363] Maren wanted to be famous for a monster bloom; she noted, dryly, that this was not the same ambition. [382]

The night before planting she opened her journal and the folded wrapping fell out, and she looked at the doodle properly for the first time. [407] A slack sea with no arrows: the turning of the tide's thought. [419] A beetle: the marsh carrion-beetles that the corpse flower's stench calls up in clouds. [433] A cradle on a branch: Ilsevane, and what a stink might carry to it. [447] Her grandmother had never explained; that was the custom's whole point, an undercurrent of wisdom running beneath the wrapping, felt before it is understood. [471] The old woman had not sent a trophy; she had sent medicine for a village she would never see, disguised as a gift for a granddaughter's pride. [498]

Maren planted at slack water, when the bridges hung quiet and every rope stopped complaining. [513] It took four years. [517] She stayed, because ambition, once it has been shown a use, is patient. [530] She learned to grind bark for the fevers, to smell a fraying knot before it frayed, to stand on a swaying plank without bracing. [554] She journalized every scent of the growing thing, and the pages became a book the village kept. [571]

When the bloom finally opened, it opened at the turn of the tide, and it smelled like everything Maren had ever feared. [593] Her sensitive nose wept; she did not leave the basket. [603] She breathed the rot the way the village breathed the sway, and found the stillness under it. [620] By dusk the beetles came, a low green thunder out of the marsh, and by summer's end the wildflower boxes were loud with seed. [644] The hush returned, fuller than before. [650]

Maren wrapped the season's first seeds in brown paper for the children of the eastern bough. [666] On the inside of each sheet she doodled: a slack sea, a mustached beetle, a cradle on a branch. [685] She explained nothing. [688] They would smell it out in their own good time. [698]
