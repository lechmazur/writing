The mist came up off the river at midnight and pooled in the workshop like something poured. [17] Through the open roof panels, Ilse could see stars, hard and clean above the fog. [32] She had not turned on the lamps. [39] The terrariums glowed on their own, quietly vivid, twenty small green worlds humming with heat cable and mist. [57] Eight hours ago, the inspector had said the word fraud in the front office, and Ilse had felt the floor of her life shift a half inch to the left. [87]

Now she stood at the bench with a jeweler's loupe and the chameleon's tail tip pinched between her fingers. [106] It had come away three weeks earlier, shed in a curl no longer than her thumbnail, and she had saved it out of habit. [130] Under magnification the truth sat there, patient as a fossil: three layers. [142] Ordinary brown skin at the base. [148] A film of cosmetic resin. [153] Above that, the famous cobalt of the Kavari veiled chameleon, the species Dorian had rediscovered six years ago in front of every camera in the country. [179]

Dorian, who had taught her to hold a python so it never learned to fear hands. [195] Dorian, who had cried at a hedgehog's funeral. [203]

She set the tail tip down on white paper and did not move for a long while. [220] The mist held the light from the terrariums and turned the whole room into a shimmering stillness, and in that stillness she tried to reconstruct him. [246] Not the man on the posters. [252] The actual sequence of choices. [257] The sanctuary going broke in its second winter. [265] The donors who wanted a miracle and would not pay for mere survival. [278] One dyed animal, then a breeding program, then a whole invented lineage, each lie a thin coat over the last, until the truth could only be found the way she had found it, by shedding layers. [314] The chameleon itself had confessed, patiently, molt by molt. [323]

Ilse was thirty-four years old and gentle by trade; she had spent a decade learning that fear in an animal is only a question asked too fast. [350] She had believed, absolutely, in three things: that the Kavari existed, that Dorian was good, and that she herself could tell the difference between kindness and being useful to someone. [380] Convictions, she was learning, are fragile in exactly the way glass is fragile: fine until struck, then everywhere at once. [400]

Dorian came in at two in the morning, smelling of rain, and stood in the doorway where the mist parted around him. [422]

'They'll take the animals,' he said. [428]

'Not if the transfer paperwork is clean,' Ilse said. [437] 'I started it tonight.' [441] 'Eighty-one animals, sixty of them mine to place.' [449] 'Kestrel Rise will take the reptiles.' [455]

He exhaled, and she watched relief begin in his shoulders, and she made herself finish. [470]

'And the tail tip goes to the inspector at nine.' [480]

The stillness in the room did not break; it only deepened, the way water does when something heavy goes into it. [501]

'You could just let it go,' he said. [509] 'You love them.' [512] 'I gave you them.' [516]

'You did,' she agreed. [520]

That was the trap, and it had held her for eleven hours: compassion pulling one way, self-respect the other, as though she had to choose which of them to betray. [550] The answer, when it came, was not a compromise but a shape: she would carry both, one in each hand, and set them down in different rooms. [577] Compassion belonged to the animals and to the man's leaving: she would drive him to the station herself, and she would not tell the reporters what he had cried about. [607] Self-respect belonged to the evidence. [612]

'I can't be the last coat of paint,' she said. [622]

Dorian looked at the terrariums for a long time, and then at the small brown chameleon in the nearest one, which had shed its whole false self over three weeks and was, underneath, an ordinary animal, alive, breathing, unbothered by the stars. [664]

'Brown,' he said, and it sounded like grief and like relief at once. [677]

'It was always brown,' Ilse said. [683] 'It was always enough.' [687]

They worked until dawn, side by side, rebuilding the records from scratch, real dates, real species, real numbers, because the animals would need a truthful ledger to travel with, whatever became of the men and women who had written the false one. [729] The mist thinned. [732] The stars went out one by one, politely, like guests. [742] At nine o'clock she handed over a paper envelope the size of a stamp, and her hand was steady, and she was sorry, and she did it anyway. [770]
