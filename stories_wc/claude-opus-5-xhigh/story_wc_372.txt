The Library of Rime hangs nine miles above the cloud floor, and nothing inside it has ever raised its voice. [20] It is a quartz hush up there, a silence with facets, and Anselm Vey, orderly records chief, has learned to work inside it the way a diver works inside pressure. [50] His duty is simple to state and cruel to perform: he must measure the luminous vows before they go dark. [70] Every promise sworn under witness leaves a residue of light, and the Library collects it the way a glacier collects seasons. [91] Each residue is fixed onto a magic lantern slide, thin as a fingernail, filed in drawers of frozen brass. [110] The slides do not last. [115] An oath keeps its glow only while illusions fade to memory, which is the long dusk between believing a thing and merely recalling that you once did. [142] After that, the slide is clear glass, and no lamp on earth can pull an image from it. [160] So Vey works gently meticulous, hands wrapped in wool, breath turned aside so it will not fog the plates. [179] He does not read the vows; reading burns them. [188] He takes them by sampling frequencies, drawing a wire comb across the slide's edge until it sings, then writing the pitch in his ledger in a hand so even it looks printed. [220] A vow humming at four hundred cycles is usually a marriage. [231] Six hundred is a debt forgiven. [237] Above nine hundred lie the thin desperate promises people make at bedsides, and those he samples last, because they ring longest and hurt most. [261] The work has a purpose he can say aloud only when the lamps are low: to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion. [284] A frequency is not the promise, but it is a shape the promise made, and shapes can be rebuilt. [303] In the spring, apprentices in the lower vaults will take his numbers and strike them on glass bells, and something will stand up in the air again -- not the vow, but its posture. [336]

On the two hundredth day of his forty-first winter, Vey opened Drawer Ninety and found a slide with his own name scratched on the tab. [361] The glow in it was nearly gone, a dim amber, the color of a window seen from a road at a great distance. [384] He had no memory of swearing anything. [391] That was not unusual up here; the Library's cold keeps records and eats recollection, which is why its chiefs are chosen for tidiness rather than sentiment. [417] He set the slide in the cradle. [424] He warmed the comb between his palms so the metal would not shock the glass. [439] Then he drew it across the edge, once, and listened. [449] Two tones came up together, which never happens: four hundred and twelve, and above it a faint eleven-hundred, bedside pitch, the sound of a promise made to someone leaving. [478] A marriage and a deathbed in the same breath. [487] He wrote both numbers down. [492] His hand stayed even; his hands always did. [500]

Then, against every rule of the vaults, he lit the lantern and pushed the slide into its throat. [518] Reading burns them, yes, but a slide this faint would be blank by the equinox anyway, and he had spent his life measuring what he refused to look at. [547] On the frost of the north wall, an illusion unfolded: a woman in a grey coat, snow on her shoulders, a small warm room, a hand held between two hands. [577] He heard himself, young, saying he would keep every word she ever gave him, even after he stopped being able to feel them. [600] Then the image thinned, the way all of them do, and the wall was only frost. [616] The slide came out clear as water. [623]

Vey sat in the quartz hush for a long while. [633] He had not gotten her back; nobody ever does. [642] But the ledger held two numbers now, four hundred and twelve and eleven hundred, and numbers are patient. [660] In the spring the apprentices would strike them on their bells, not knowing whose, and for eight seconds the air in the lower vaults would stand a certain way. [689] That is all meaning is, at the edge of oblivion: a posture kept by strangers. [704] He closed Drawer Ninety, gentle as always, and moved to Ninety-one, where four hundred slides were still burning quietly, waiting to be counted before the dark took them too. [733]
