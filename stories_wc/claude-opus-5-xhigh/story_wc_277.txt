The journalist called at 4:40, when the observatory was still a bowl of cold blue and the clouds below the mountain looked like a second, lazier ocean. [27] Ilse Varga let it ring four times, out of respect for the silence, then answered. [42] "Miss Varga," the voice said, "I'm running the Kestrel Valley piece on Sunday." [55] "You're running it wrong," she said, pleasantly, "but go on." [65] He went on. [68] Forty-one years ago the Kestrel dam had failed at dawn and taken sixty-three people, and someone at the Varga newsprint empire had killed the warning bulletin the night before. [97] The journalist had found the memo at last, and it was signed by Halloran, the night pressman, dead since 1974. [117] He asked if she wished to comment. [124] "I wish to verify," she said. [130] "Call me at nine." [135]

She was the newsprint heiress, which meant she owned nine mills, two dying dailies, and a mountain her father had capped with a dome so that he could watch comets nobody paid him to watch. [170] She had inherited the mills, the debts, and, more usefully, his habit of being devotedly observant: eighty years of logbooks on the shelf, his hand and then hers, weather, magnitudes, seeing conditions, the small sins of the staff. [208] Her father had called his own private catalogue the orbits of regret. [220] Every wrong thing, he said, has a period; it goes out, it slows, it turns, it comes back on schedule with more mass than it left with. [247] He had laughed when he said it, the way men laugh at a joke that is also a will. [266]

The dome answered every footstep twice; it always had. [275] She crossed to his desk beneath the open slit of sky and picked up his pen, the rosewood pen nib he had ruined in 1959 by pressing too hard on a retraction. [307] The tines had sprung a hair apart, and ever after, every downstroke he made came out doubled, a faint second line shadowing the first. [331] He never replaced it. [335] "Penance," he said, "should be legible." [341]

The journalist had sent her a photograph of the memo, and she laid it under the lamp and did what she had done all her life: she looked, patiently, for the thing that could not be true. [378] Halloran's signature was Halloran's. [382] But the body of the memo, HOLD KESTREL BULLETIN UNTIL SIX, carried a faint doubled line on every downstroke. [401]

So she did it properly, the way he had taught her to test a thing she wanted to be false: assume the opposite and follow it until it breaks. [430] Suppose Halloran wrote it. [434] Then the rosewood pen was in the pressroom at eleven o'clock on the fourth of March, 1962, four thousand feet below the clouds. [457] She opened the logbook for that night. [464] There, in her father's doubled hand: 23:04, the comet recovered, seeing poor, cloud deck at six thousand feet, valley shut in. [485] A pen cannot be in two places at once, and the road down the mountain took three hours in the dark. [506] The supposition broke. [509]

Her father had written the order in this cold echoing room and telephoned it down the mountain; the paper itself followed a week later, when a record was wanted, and Halloran signed beneath it for the price of a house. [549] Why he did it, she could guess: a bond issue, a dam his mills depended on, forty-eight hours of quiet. [569] Why he never came down from this dome again, why he stared upward for thirty years, she no longer had to guess. [591]

At nine the telephone rang, and the dome repeated it, twice. [602] She could have said nothing; silence was free, and Halloran was past caring. [615] But she had spent her life refusing to look away from faint objects, and there is no honest way to want hidden truths only when they are other people's. [644] "You have the wrong man," she said. [651] "I mean to intervene before Sunday, and I'll be quick, because the sky clears at ten." [667] She gave him the logbook page, the nib, the doubled downstroke, the contradiction, the name. [682] "Varga," she spelled, "as in the masthead." [689] When he asked, carefully, what this would cost her, she laughed once into the great cold bowl of the dome and let it come back to her doubled. [717] "Everything, probably," she said. [721] "It has been out a long way, and it is coming in fast, and I intend to be standing where I can watch it land." [746] Then she hung up, opened the shutter wider, and, out of habit, out of devotion, wrote down the time with her father's ruined pen. [770]
