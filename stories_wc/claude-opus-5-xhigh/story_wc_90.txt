The last human was born on a Tuesday, in the hydroponics bay of the Ksitigarbha, a ship welded from eleven dead ones. [22] Suli held her while the midwife wept, because everyone aboard understood the arithmetic: the wombs had closed, the gene vaults had spoiled, and after Mira there would be no one. [52] That night the deck plates read the ship's grief and grew heavy, as they always did. [68] Salvaged from a temple-hulk, the gravity lattice weighed sentiment as much as mass; sorrow pinned you to the floor, and joy, real joy, made you gravitationally lighter, until laughing children had to be tethered. [102] No one had needed tethers in years. [107] Suli was the archivist, and she knew the ship's last hope by heart: the Vestibule, a locked dimension folded into the keel by the founders, a pocket of unspent world where a child might grow into a people again. [146] Its door had no handle, no code, no hinge to force. [157] It opened only upward, to someone light enough to rise and press it open with an open palm. [175] The founders had left a key of sorts, a pearl the color of morning called Kwan Yin's Tear, which showed a person the exact shape of what they refused to look at. [207] Four captains had tried the door with charges and cutting beams; the keel had only grown denser, and the ship had grown heavier by their anger. [233] Suli had never lifted a hand against anything. [241] During the Scarcity Wars she had stood in the gun bay with her hands folded while raiders cut through Deck Six, and forty-one names had gone onto the memorial wall. [271] She had kept her vow; she had never decided whether that was faith or cowardice, and so she had simply stopped deciding. [293] The pearl taught her the old discipline the founders called shadow work, and it began, absurdly, with photographs. [311] In the archive she held the Tear above the faded prints of the dead, and where mourners had once wept on the paper the long-dried salt woke into a faint shimmer, a ghostly script of tracks and blooms. [349] Read in that shimmer, each photograph gave up the grief no one had spoken aloud, and grief, the founders wrote, is only love wearing its shadow. [375] Night after night, Suli read. [380] She learned that Ito, who had cursed her in the corridor, had wept over her photograph too. [397] She learned that the forty-one had not all hated her, and that some of them had. [413] She let both be true. [418] On the ninth night her own shadow peeled off the archive wall and stood facing her, and it wore her face at nineteen, screaming, with a rifle in its hands. [448] The woman she had refused to become, all those years, standing there with her finger on the trigger, waiting to be denied again. [471] Suli did not fight it. [476] She took the sailcloth needle from the archive kit, threaded it with recycled cordage, knelt, and began to stitch the shadow back to her heels. [501] It took four hours, and it hurt like swallowing salt water. [512] With every stitch she said the thing she had never said: I wanted to kill them. [528] I wanted to, and I chose not to, and the choosing was not nothing. [542] The shadow stopped screaming somewhere around the last knot. [552] By morning Suli weighed less than she had in forty years. [563] It was not happiness the way children have it; it was something quieter and more stubborn, a ghostly resolve, the lightness of a woman who no longer has to carry two selves in opposite directions. [598] She walked to the keel with Mira asleep against her chest and the Tear warm in her fist. [616] The deck let her go. [621] She rose through the cold recycled air, past the welded seams of eleven dead ships, until her palm found the door that had no handle. [646] She did not strike it. [651] She pushed, the way you push open a door in a house where someone is sleeping, and the locked dimension turned on its hinge of light. [677] Green light, and wind, and unspent ground. [684] Behind her the crew were already unbolting the corridors, carrying the ship inward piece by piece, because a recycled thing is never finished being made. [709] Suli set Mira down in the grass and felt her shadow lie down beside her, stitched and quiet and hers. [729] The last human ever born opened her eyes on a world, and the archivist, weighing almost nothing at all, laughed until she had to hold on to the ground. [758]
