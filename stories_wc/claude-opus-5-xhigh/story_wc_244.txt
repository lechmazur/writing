The rumor arrived before the water did, which is how you knew it was true. [15] A shepherd told a bus driver that the reservoir was groaning; the bus driver told a radio host it was singing; the radio host told forty thousand people that the valley was already gone. [49] By the time the story changed like that, we had eleven minutes to climb. [63] Afterward, we counted ourselves on the platform of the old railway station, where ivy had eaten the timetable board thirty years ago and nobody had come to argue. [91] Ninety-one of us, six goats, no insulin, and a great deal of politeness. [104]

That is what the valley is famous for: polite anarchy. [114] No mayor, no permits, no locks; everyone says please, and then does exactly as they like. [130] I keep the Ledger, because someone volunteered me and it seemed rude to refuse. [144] The Ledger is not money. [149] It records who is bound to whom, and every entry says the same thing in different handwriting: what you were given, you owe forward to a stranger. [176] Reciprocity is the only law we have ever managed to keep, mostly because it cannot be enforced. [193]

On the second night I decided to summon Ondine Marek. [203] You cannot call a disaster-relief logistician who has no office, no agency, and no surname that stays spelled the same twice. [224] You cannot write to her. [229] You can only lie beautifully enough that the lie travels, because she does not follow requests; she follows rumor chains, and she arrives at the exact moment when the story changes. [260]

So I told a trucker that a fragment of moon rock was buried under the ivy station, left by an astronaut's widow, worth a hospital. [285] The trucker told a customs clerk, who told a nun, who told a smuggler of secondhand oxygen, and somewhere in that improbable chain the lie mutated into the shape she listens for. [317]

She came on the third afternoon, walking the dead rails, dragging two crates and a wheeled pallet of cold-chain boxes. [337] "Somebody ordered a hospital," she said. [343] "I brought insulin, tarps, and forty kilos of lentils, which is a hospital if you squint and are extremely brave." [363]

She joked for six straight hours. [369] She joked while she splinted a boy's wrist, while she rigged a rain catchment from the station's rotted awning, while she catalyzed our ninety-one useless hands into a supply line that unloaded both crates in nineteen minutes. [406] It was desperate, that jocularity. [411] Every punchline landed a half-second early, the way a man whistles in a tunnel to keep from hearing the tunnel. [431]

At dusk she asked to see the Ledger, and read it the way other people read letters from home. [450] Then she put a grey pebble on the page. [459] "Your rumor was half true," she said. [466] "That's the annoying thing about rumors; they keep guessing right." [476]

It was a fragment of moon rock, four grams, scuffed, absurd. [487] A geologist in a Kamchatka shelter had pressed it into her hand nine years earlier, after she pulled him out of ash, and died two days later saying: pay it forward, but only to someone who can carry it. [526] "Nine years of debt," she said, grinning. [533] "Do you know what an unpayable debt makes you?" [542]

I said no. [545] "Alone," she said. [548] "I have fed two hundred thousand people and I have never once been owed anything, because I leave before the thank-you." [569] She had spent nine years looking for the cure for loneliness in the logistics of other people's ruin, and she had it backwards, and she knew it, and knowing had not helped. [601]

So I did the rudest thing in the valley's history. [611] I refused the rock. [615] I wrote her name in the Ledger instead, on the receiving side, and under it: one roof, one supper, one seat at the platform table, owed by ninety-one people, indefinitely, non-transferable. [646] "You can't put me in there," she said, and her voice did something the jokes had been guarding. [664] "We are bound by reciprocity, not by permission," I said. [674] "Please stay; we will do exactly as we like about it." [685]

She stayed eleven days, which for her was a geological era. [696] The station has a clinic now, and a seed store, and a rumor department, which is three teenagers and a radio. [717] When floods come elsewhere, we start the chains ourselves, and she moves along them like current along wire. [735] The moon rock sits in the ivy under the timetable board, uncollected, still hers. [749] She says she is coming back for it, always laughing, always in that early, half-second way. [765] That is the point of leaving something behind: it makes the story change, and then it makes you return. [784]
