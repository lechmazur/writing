Mira Vance had taught music for nineteen years without hearing a single note of it. [15] She read her students through her palms, through the floorboards, through the way a cello's ribs shivered against her sternum. [35] Then her brother Teo drowned in April, and by the following autumn she had gone flat inside, a woman conducting from behind glass. [58] The grief had not hurt; that was the terror of it. [69] Emotional numbness, her doctor called it, and Mira decided she would go find something strong enough to break it. [88]

The drum maker's cavern lay two hours above the valley, where a limestone throat swallowed a creek and gave back nothing. [109] Old Bandi had made drums there since before Mira was born, curing goatskin on frames of ash, because the cave's air, he said, was audibly muted -- it ate echoes, so a drumhead tuned inside it would ring true anywhere else in the world. [152] Mira came during the faint rustle of turning seasons, when the beeches let go all at once and the mountain sounded, to those who could hear, like paper being crumpled far away. [184] She felt only the cold. [189]

Bandi did not shout at her, the way hearing people did. [200] He wrote on a slate, and when he set her hand on the wall she understood that he lived inside a hush that mirrored her own -- his by geology, hers by birth, both of them tenants of the same enormous quiet. [241]

In the deepest chamber stood a phonograph, brass horn green with damp. [253] Its needle was broken, snapped to a stub, and Bandi had never replaced it. [267] He wound the crank anyway, laid the ruined stylus on a wax cylinder, and turned the cylinder the wrong way, backward, by hand. [290] No sound came out of the horn -- not for him, not for anyone. [303] But the broken needle, dragged in reverse, sent something up the machine's iron spine and into the stone floor: a thread so fine that Mira, kneeling, mistook it at first for her own pulse. [337]

Bandi's slate said: a filament of hush. [344] Backward, he explained, a song loses its words and its bragging and keeps only its shape -- the rise, the ache, the leaning -- and that shape travels through rock better than any melody. [376]

He turned the cylinder again, and Mira laid her forehead on the floor. [389] It was a lullaby run in reverse, she learned later, one Teo used to hum badly while gutting fish. [408] Reversed, it arrived without his voice attached, and so it slipped past the glass. [422] Mira wept for forty minutes on a cave floor in November, and every second of it was a relief. [441]

She stayed three weeks. [445] Bandi taught her to stretch skin over ash; she taught him fingerspelling, and to feel the difference between a fourth and a fifth in his jaw. [471] When she came down, she carried a low drum and the broken needle in her coat pocket, because Bandi insisted the flaw was the instrument. [496]

Her students at the district school were the ones nobody wanted: the bored, the loud, three who were hard of hearing, one who had stopped speaking after a fire. [525] In December she set the drum on the classroom floor, mounted the stub of needle on a spindle rig, and played them backward recorded songs -- hymns, pop tunes, a marching band -- felt through their shoes and their palms and their teeth. [566] It should not have worked. [571] Instead it seemed to enthrall them. [577] Twenty-two children lay flat on cheap linoleum with their eyes shut, guessing which song was hiding inside the shape, and the room went so still that the principal opened the door twice to check that it was not empty. [616] The boy who had stopped speaking wrote her a note: it feels like someone remembering me. [632]

Mira kept the needle broken. [637] Each year now, when the beeches let go and the seasons rustle over the valley, she takes her older students up to the cavern, where Bandi's drums hang curing in the dark. [669] She has them lie down and be quiet, which they resist, and then stop resisting. [684] The silence in there mirrors the silence she was born into, and she has learned what she teaches: that hush is not absence but a medium, thin as a filament, strong enough to carry a brother's crooked lullaby down through rock and shoe leather into a numb heart, backward, which is the only direction grief will let a song travel home. [745]
