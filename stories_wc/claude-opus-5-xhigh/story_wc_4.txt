Odile Marchetti was the town's subtle puzzle archivist, which meant she catalogued the things people could not solve out loud. [20] Her office was a former payroll shed at the edge of the Halvern works, and her only filing cabinet was a dove gray map case with eleven shallow drawers. [49] Ten drawers held the town's black-and-white photographs. [56] The eleventh held nothing yet, and that was the puzzle. [66]

Eight years earlier the boiler house had come apart at four in the morning, taking nine workers and the whole reason for the town. [90] Since then people had grieved separately and expertly, the way one learns a trade. [104] Odile was perceptively calm about it; she did not tell anyone to move on, she simply asked them for pictures. [124]

She had a theory she guarded like a small animal: that grief could be sorted via arcs in black-and-white photographs. [144] Every photograph the town gave her contained a curve. [152] The swing of a crane cable in 1961. [160] A shot-put boy's throw at the school field, 1974. [169] Smoke leaving a stack; a bride's veil caught crosswise in wind; the long sag of a clothesline behind the Petrosyan house. [190] Odile traced each arc onto tissue paper, and the tissues, stacked, began to argue with one another beautifully. [208] They wanted to become one continuous line. [214]

Behind the rusting ruins of the works, where the loading yard ended, there was a lavender field tucked out of sight, planted the first spring after the accident by Mrs. Petrosyan, who had lost a son and needed something to do with her hands. [258] Nobody had asked her to; nobody had stopped her either. [267] By the eighth summer the field ran to the fence line, and the ruins above it were changing as vines climbed the brick, softening the stacks into green columns. [296] That was Odile's deadline, though she told the council it was an art project. [310]

She wanted to transmute the town's pain into a masterpiece before the vines finished the job and made the ruins merely pretty. [332] Pretty was not the same as answered. [338]

In June she carried the dove gray map case down the loading ramp and set it on a bench between the rows. [360] She had cut the north wall's surviving windows out of her plan, because the arcs needed sky. [377] Then she invited the town to help her hang glass. [387]

The plan was absurd and she presented it without apology: forty-one panes of window glass salvaged from the works, each painted with a single arc lifted from a single photograph, hung in the empty frames of the north wall. [426] From anywhere else it would look like scribble. [434] From the bench in the lavender field, at about seven in the evening, the forty-one arcs would line up into one unbroken curve running the length of the wall. [463]

It took nine weeks. [467] Men who had not spoken since the inquest held ladders for each other. [480] Mrs. Petrosyan painted the clothesline arc herself and would not let anyone see her do it. [496] A retired safety inspector brought a photograph he had never shown anyone, of steam venting at a wrong angle three days before. [518] Odile put it in the eleventh drawer, thanked him, and painted its arc into the middle of the wall where nobody could avoid it. [542] That, she thought, was the difference between a monument and a masterpiece: a masterpiece keeps the evidence. [559]

They lit nothing on the first evening of September; they only came and sat. [573] At seven the low sun came through the north wall and the scribble resolved, arc catching arc, crane to shot-put to veil to steam to clothesline, one long line lifting from the left and coming down gently on the right, like a breath let out. [618] Somebody laughed, which is what earnest people do when they are about to weep. [632] Two hundred people stayed until the light failed, and then they lingered anyway, in lavender up to their knees, under vines that had almost reached the roofline. [659]

Odile sat with the dove gray map case on her lap, ten drawers full, the eleventh finally holding something. [678] She had solved nothing, strictly speaking. [683] Nine men were still dead and the works would never reopen. [694] But forty-one separate arcs had agreed to be one arc, and the people who owned them had stood on ladders together to prove it. [719] She wrote the date on a card, and beneath it, in her small archivist's hand: collective healing, in progress, method unclear, results visible at seven. [744] Then she let herself linger too, which for her was the hardest part of the work. [760]
