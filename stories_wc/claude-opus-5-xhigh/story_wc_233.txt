The dreams came every night for a year, and every night they ended the same way: a cave of cobalt stone floating above a floor of clouds, and light leaking out of the sky like blood from a slit sleeve. [40] Queen Sevrine had stopped telling her council about them. [49] They had begun to look at her the way farmers look at a well going brackish. [65] So she went instead to Halda, last keeper of the mountain, to learn what the dreams wanted; Halda's hut was sewn over with lichen fine as lace, and Halda gave her the final warning. [99]

"Three times I have told you to sleep with iron under your pillow and let the dream rot," Halda said. [119] "This is the third. After the final warning there is only the climb, and the climb is not a thing you come back from whole." [144]

Sevrine had not been whole since her mother's funeral, so she took a rope and a lantern and left before matins. [165] Wren, who lit the palace lamps and asked no permission for anything, followed her up the switchbacks with a second rope. [186] "Go back," the queen said. [191] "You'll want someone to carry the light," Wren said, and that settled it. [204]

By dusk they reached the ember grotto on the ridge, where pilgrims for six hundred years had turned around. [223] Its floor was a bed of coals that never went out and never spread, breathing orange under a ceiling black with old smoke. [246] Sevrine warmed her hands and understood, with the flat calm of a woman reading her own death warrant, that she had seen this room in the dream too, always just before the cobalt one. [280]

They rested on the lip of the ridge with their legs hanging over the valley. [295] The heat at their backs, the cold on their faces; a shared exhale, and in the meadow far below, the first fireflies of summer came up out of the grass, hundreds at once, as if the two of them had breathed them into being. [339] "Fireflies mean the wound is open," Sevrine said. [347] She had not known she knew that until she said it. [358]

Above the ember grotto the path narrowed to handholds, and then there was no path at all, only rock and the lacy white frost that grew in patterns like veils and tightened, hour by hour, around their wrists and ankles. [398] It was beautiful and it wanted them dead, and Sevrine climbed anyway. [410]

They came through the cloud floor at moonset into cobalt. [420] The grotto was exactly as the dream had built it, blue walls glassy as a kingfisher's back, clouds lapping at the threshold like a tide. [445] And at the back of it, a tear in the sky itself, no longer than a forearm, bleeding starlight in slow bright drops that hissed when they struck the stone. [475] Beneath the tear lay a crown Sevrine had buried nineteen years ago. [487] Her mother's. [489]

The dream had never been a prophecy; it had been a summons, and now she could read it whole. [508] The queens of her line had held the sky closed with their enchantment - the borrowed glamour that made subjects kneel, harvests come, borders hold - and her mother had spent hers to the last thread, and died thin as paper, and the seam had begun to give. [554] The dreams were the mountain calling for the next payment. [564]

Wren held up the lantern, though there was no need for it here. [577] "You'll be nothing," she said. [582] "They won't kneel; you'll be a woman in a wet cloak." [593]

Sevrine put her palm against the tear. [600] It drank. [602] It took the glamour out of her the way a wick takes oil, and she felt her own grandeur go: the weight of every room she had ever entered, the hush, the certainty. [635] The starlight slowed, thickened, and closed, and the frost lace on the walls loosened its grip and slid away like a dropped shawl. [658]

"Rope," Sevrine said, softly commanding, and Wren obeyed before she thought to wonder why - because the voice had never been the enchantment. [680] That had always been hers. [685]

They went down past the ember grotto at sunrise, and Sevrine felt no dread of the descent, only her knees and the ordinary ache of a body. [712] The council would not kneel again; she would have to argue, and be doubted, and win people one at a time. [733] In the meadow the fireflies were still up, and she stopped to watch them without any dream underneath the watching. [753]

"Halda said you wouldn't come back whole," Wren said. [762] "She was wrong about the word," said the disenchanted queen. [772] "I came back exactly whole. [777] I only came back small." [782]
