The last dictionary came off the press at midnight, and by dawn the library had stopped breathing. [17] Anselm Vey felt it the moment he unlocked the reading room: a suspended hush, thick as held water, the sound of a world that had agreed to stop inventing. [46] He was seventy-one, a reticent man who had spent forty years defining other people's words and almost none of his own. [67] The Commission of Settled Speech had summoned him to testify at seven. [79] Shadow lay in the aisles like silt, and the high windows held only the gray promise of morning. [97] Anselm did not light the lamps. [103] He went instead to the ladder beneath the chandelier, climbed until the brass rungs complained, and reached into the dusty tiers of glass. [126] One crystal came loose in his palm, a teardrop the length of his thumb, warm from nothing at all. [145] He had learned about it by accident, thirty years back, when a student dropped a book and the chandelier answered her gasp. [167] The glass was sonically reflective in a way no glass should be: it drank the small noises of the room and gave them back later, out of order, patient as a seed. [199] For three decades he had listened to it the way other men fished. [212] He pressed the crystal to his ear and the library began to speak through fractured sequences. [228] A syllable from 1974. [232] A cough from the war years. [238] Half of a girl's laugh, and then, three heartbeats later, its beginning. [250] Nothing arrived whole, and that, Anselm had decided long ago, was the point. [263] Words did not enter the world whole either. [271] They came in pieces, mispronunciations, jokes, errors repeated until they hardened, and he had spent his life trying to decode the pattern of growth hidden in the noise. [299] The Commission held that language had reached its full extent. [309] Ninety-one thousand entries, the final volume bound in green, and after this, they said, no additions; a settled tongue for a settled age. [332] Anselm had signed the proofs himself, and his hand had shaken, and he had said nothing, because saying nothing was the only skill he had ever perfected. [359] But at dawn, on the ladder, with the crystal warming in his fist, he felt the old childish thrill he had never managed to outgrow. [384] Forty years of dust and small salaries had weathered him without once teaching him not to wonder. [401] He listened harder, and the crystal gave him yesterday. [410] A boy at the map table, six or seven, describing the shape a river made where it met the sea. [430] "Fingery," the boy said, meaning the way the water split into hands, and the crystal returned the word twice, backwards, then whole. [452] It was not in the green volume. [459] It never would be, and yet it existed, and Anselm had heard it born. [473] At seven the Commission convened in the shadowed reading room because the hearing rooms were being converted to storage. [492] Nine members, green volumes squared before them, asking the last lexicographer whether the record was complete. [508] Anselm stood, and the hush came down over the room like a lid. [521] He was not a speaker; he had never once in his life won an argument aloud. [537] So he set the crystal on the oak rail, in the light coming up through the east windows, and asked the Commission to listen. [561] For a long minute it gave them nothing but the room's own held breath, reflected back. [577] Then, in pieces, out of sequence, it testified. [585] A cough, a turning page, a girl's laugh arriving before its start. [597] And then a boy's voice, small and certain, saying a word that nine bound volumes did not contain. [615] It said the word three times, in fragments, then entire: fingery, fingery, fingery. [628] Somebody laughed - one clerk, quickly stifled - and that laugh went into the glass to be given back some other dawn. [648] Anselm watched the chairwoman's pen hover, then move, then hover. [658] The pattern of growth, he wanted to say, is not a line; it is a river coming apart into hands. [678] You may print the last dictionary; you cannot print the last word. [690] He said none of it. [695] He only picked up the crystal, held it a moment in the strengthening light, and asked, very quietly, for a blank page at the back of the green volume. [724] They gave him one. [728] It was not a victory, and he was old enough to know it, and young enough still to write the boy's word at the top in his small careful hand. [758] Then he climbed the ladder and put the crystal back, where it could go on listening. [774]
