Before privatization, the Bobbin was nobody's property and everybody's home. [10] Nadia Quill met every newcomer at the gangplank, because that was the job the raft council had invented for her: culture shock absorber. [33] She took the blow of the place so the strangers wouldn't drown in it. [47] The carnival never stopped; it had not stopped in ninety years. [58] Drums ran on shifts, stilt-walkers slept in harnesses, and the fry-oil smoke hung over the lashed rafts like a permanent low cloud. [80] Newcomers arrived pale and shaking, and Nadia walked them through the noise until their hands unclenched. [96] She was orderly the way water is orderly: she found the pattern first and the reason later. [113] That intuition was why the Marrow Consortium hired her too. [123] They paid her in hard currency to soften the Bobbin for its buyers, and she took the money, and she kept the council's key around her neck, and she told herself both loyalties were one. [158] On the key's ring hung the foundation stone chip. [167] It was a thumb-sized flake of gray granite, struck from the mainland pier where the first raft was tied, and by the old charter no sale of the Bobbin was legal unless the chip sat on the contract. [205] In a city of rope and wood, it was the only stone, and it meant the same thing stone always means. [226] The Consortium's surveyors came in spring with clipboards and drone-kites. [236] They assigned every raft a fixed address, and to every address a value, and to every value a future rent. [256] Already the mainland money was arriving: a cocktail barge where the net-menders had worked, arrivals who wanted the carnival without the carnival's people. [279] Nadia read the schedule at their table, smiling, and saw the eviction of nine hundred people written in tidy columns. [299] Fighting was hopeless; the Bobbin had no guns and no lawyers. [310] But the survey depended on the rafts staying where they were, and rafts, by their nature, do not. [328] So Nadia decided to reorganize the city. [335] She could not say it aloud. [341] The Consortium had bought her voice, and one whisper would end her, and worse, would end the plan. [359] So she used the walls. [364] Every hull on the Bobbin was painted: spray-saints, kraken jokes, memorials to the drowned, all of it layered forty years deep. [385] Nadia had spent a decade explaining that art to bewildered arrivals, which meant she knew its grammar better than anyone alive. [406] A saint's left hand meant a mooring line; the number of fingers gave the night; the color of the halo gave the new neighbor. [430] She painted at dawn, in the vandal hour, when even the carnival dozed. [443] Others answered on other hulls, corrections and confirmations, a conversation nine hundred people could read and no surveyor could. [462] It took eleven nights. [466] The bakery raft went to the eastern rim where the bread smell would reach the arrivals dock. [483] The loud stages moved to windward; the sick and the old drifted inward to the quiet pool at the city's heart. [504] Nothing was destroyed; everything was somewhere else. [511] The Bobbin worked better afterward, which Nadia had not planned and could not help. [525] When the Consortium's men returned, their map described a city that no longer existed. [539] Nadia stood beside them at the rail, absorbing their shock as she had absorbed a thousand others, explaining gently that the Bobbin moves, that it has always moved, that this is simply what it is. [574] The lead surveyor asked whether she had known. [582] She said she had suspected, which was true, and he wrote it down, and the valuation had to begin again. [602] The charter's renewal window closed first. [608] That bought a year, maybe two. [614] It was not victory, and Nadia never pretended otherwise. [623] The Consortium kept paying her, because a liaison who understood the rafts was suddenly worth more, and she kept taking the money, because a bought insider is still an insider. [653] Two allegiances, one throat, no rest. [659] But on the last night of the reorganizing, she had pried the foundation stone chip off her key ring and set it into wet pitch on the new keel-block at the city's quiet heart. [693] Above it she painted a saint with both hands open, six fingers, halo the gray of granite. [710] Anyone raised on the Bobbin could read it: the seal is ours now, all of ours, and to sell this place you must first cut out its heart. [738] The drums kept going, as they always had, and the newcomers kept coming, frightened and hopeful, and Nadia met them at the gangplank and taught them how to read the walls. [769]
