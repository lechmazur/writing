The ghosts arrived in the third summer after the sky learned to show them, and Ijo Marek, who had not spoken aloud in nine years, was astonished. [27] He had chosen the oasis precisely because nothing came here anymore. [38] Beyond its ring of dead tamarisks the wasteland lay scorched flat, glass in places, ash in others, and the hundred hives of Havvah's apiary stood empty as abandoned mailboxes. [67] He kept the well sweet, the smoker oiled, the frames scraped, because a promise does not care whether anyone is left to witness it. [91]

The first ghost was Naima. [96] She came at dusk along the irrigation channel, walking the way she always had, unhurried, tranquilly perceptive, taking in the state of the yard before she took in him. [125]

The dead, everyone had learned by then, could be seen but not heard. [138] Their mouths moved on nothing. [143] So they spoke with the only grammar left to them: position, patience, and gaze. [157] Ijo, who had spent nine years learning silence, found he could read her through contextual clues, the way a beekeeper reads a hive he cannot open in the rain. [186]

She stood at hive twelve, then at the well, then at the shelf in the shed where the stained glass box sat under its cloth. [211] That was the whole sentence, and it took her four days to say it. [225]

The box had been their idea together, back when the oasis still hummed and the covenant was only a joke between two stubborn people. [249] Certain things, they had agreed, lose their weight the moment they are said out loud. [264] So the unsaid things were written, folded, and sealed in a box of stained glass panels - amber, rose, a green like wet leaves - and the box was never to be opened by either of them. [299]

That was one strand. [303] The second was older: the bargain Havvah's family had struck with the bees, water and shade forever in exchange for the honey, a debt inherited by whoever held the deed. [333] The third was the one Ijo had made alone, over her grave, in a wind full of ash: that he would stay until the bees came back. [360] Three promises, knotted at one point, and the knot was him. [371]

On the fifth evening she stood in the shed doorway and simply looked at the box, and he understood the tangle at last. [394] She did not want the box opened; she wanted it given. [405] Hive twelve, the well, the box: an instruction, in the order the work had to be done. [422]

He scrubbed hive twelve until the old comb inside smelled of wax again. [435] He drew water and filled the stone trough beneath it, because a swarm looking for a home reads a place the way he read her: by what is offered, not by what is announced. [469] Then he carried the stained glass box out into the last of the light. [483] One lead seam had loosened over the years, leaving a slot no wider than a bee's back. [500] He wrote nine years onto one small page - the argument he never finished, the apology he never gave, the name he never said to her face - and posted it through the slot without reading it back. [536] The weight stayed inside, undiminished, which was the entire point of the covenant. [549]

He set the box in hive twelve, closed the lid, and sat down in the dust to wait, and Naima sat beside him in the way of the visible dead, casting no shadow, disturbing no dust. [585]

At noon the next day a scout came in off the wasteland, one bee, ragged, from some wild colony that had outlasted the burning in a rock crevice forty miles east. [616] It drank at the trough, entered hive twelve, walked the rose panel of the box, and left the way scouts leave, carrying an address. [640] Ijo was astonished all over again, and he laughed, and no sound came out, which felt correct. [657]

Naima stood, brushing off a skirt that could not gather dust, and gave him the long tranquil gaze she had always used instead of goodbye. [682] All three strands lay loose at once: the words kept their weight, the debt was paid in the only currency left, and the bees had come back, which meant he was free to go. [716]

He did not go. [720] He mouthed something at her, slowly, in the grammar of the dead, and she read it the way he had read her - by the water, the wax, the sealed glass, everything he had built instead of speaking. [757] Then the light went amber, and rose, and green as wet leaves, and she was gone, and the oasis began to hum. [779]
