The fort of Anneal burned green at twilight, its walls furred with lumen-moss, and everyone below in the valley called it beautiful because calling it a cage was punishable. [29] Mira Sallow painted the courtyard sundial, one wedge a week, and had done so for two years. [46] The Warden thought her slow. [51] She was, in fact, calmly inventive, which is slower than genius and much harder to catch. [67] Half the dial's face remained bare stone; the painted half held numerals, hatch marks, and hour-lines whose angles were wrong by exactly the amounts that meant something. [94] Anyone who had learned to read the coded angles in a half-painted sundial could stand in the courtyard at noon, squint like a bored laborer, and receive an instruction. [123] This was the whole of Mira's intrigue: geometry, patience, and a brush. [135]

Under the fort lay the cradle of sparks, a stone basin where the first fire of the mountain still turned over in its sleep, throwing off seeds of light. [164] One seed, cupped in clay, would light a family's table for a lifetime. [177] The Warden sold the light back to the valley by the hour and kept the cradle behind a lock with nine tumblers. [199] The lock's three keys were not keys but slender bronze rods, and they stood upright in a bronze quill holder on the Warden's desk, disguised as pens because arrogance loves a joke. [231] Guarding both desk and door was the vigilant lock sentinel, Kesh, who had not slept a full night in thirty years and could hear a tumbler settle through two feet of granite. [263]

Then, on the evening of the autumn accounting, the fort went dark. [275] Not dimmed but dark, moss gone ash-grey, lanterns guttering, the whole luminous fort swallowed by its own twilight. [293] Nobody could explain it. [297] The engineers shouted at the basin; the basin sulked. [306] Mira had a private theory, that a fire caged for a century eventually declines to perform, but theories were not her trade tonight. [329] Three days earlier she had painted the eleven-o'clock line two degrees shallow, which meant: when the light fails, come to the cradle empty-handed and wait. [354] She had known it would fail, because she had been measuring the cradle's pulse in the moss for a year, and the pulse had been shortening. [380]

So she walked the black corridors with her paint kit, being a woman with a legitimate errand, and found Kesh standing at the lock door with a candle. [408] Behind her, in the dark hall, thirty people breathed and said nothing. [420]

"Painting at night," Kesh said. [425]

"Studying," Mira said. [428]

The sentinel turned the candle so its light fell on Mira's face, then, oddly, away. [443]

"Your eleven-o'clock line is two degrees shallow," Kesh said. [452] "Your nine is three." [456] "I have watched that dial every day for two years, and I am paid to notice angles." [473]

Mira's heart went very quiet, in the way of a held breath that has decided not to become a scream. [493] Then the sentinel reached into her coat and brought out the bronze quill holder, three rods standing in it like a small dry bouquet. [517]

"The Warden believes I am incapable of arithmetic," she said, "and that a woman who never sleeps must never think." [537] "My daughter's house in the valley is billed nine coppers an hour for light my own hands guard." [555]

The nine tumblers took Kesh forty seconds; she had been rehearsing them in her head for years. [572] The cradle lay below, a basin of grey stone with one weak coal turning in it, and around the rim the thirty valley folk knelt and lifted clay cups. [601] Mira had worked out the trick of it months ago: the fire did not want feeding, it wanted spreading, and it brightened as it was divided. [627] Thirty seeds left the basin, and the coal came up gold, and nobody cheered. [641] That was the agreement: to spark a silent revolution, you must be quieter than the men counting coins upstairs. [660] They went out through the drains, thirty cupped lights under thirty coats. [672]

By the time the fort's moss flushed green again at dawn, the Warden's ledgers still balanced and his lock was still locked, and in the valley thirty windows glowed that had never glowed before. [706] He blamed the blackout on damp. [712] Mira went back to the courtyard and painted another wedge, slowly, badly, two degrees off. [727] Kesh watched her from the wall, vigilant as ever, and said nothing at all, and the saying of nothing had a shape to it, the way a wink has a shape. [758] Six hundred windows to go. [763]
