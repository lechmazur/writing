The Hush took the world's noise on a Tuesday in February, and by Thursday Amaya had learned that drowning is quiet either way. [23] The ferry had capsized in the third hour of it, hull splitting without a sound, passengers shouting into air that had forgotten how to carry a shout. [50] She had come up through black water with her father's tuning fork case clamped in her teeth, and nine others had not come up at all. [76] She had been the one at the rail who saw the floe coming and opened her mouth to warn them, which in the new world was the same as saying nothing. [107] Eleven years later she taught composition in the winterglass conservatory above that same lake. [121] In February the building stood on the frozen shore like a lantern, pale green light in every pane, and nothing inside it made a sound. [146] Winterglass was the one good invention of the silent decade: lake ice fused with silica and seeded with pigment, panes that read the heat of a hand and answered in color. [177] Press your palm to a wall and the wall bloomed you outward: anger in orange spirals, patience in long green bands. [198] Heat-mapping emotions had begun as medicine, a way for doctors to triage patients who could no longer describe their pain; the conservatory had made it a language. [225] Amaya's students, sixteen of them, ranged from six to sixty and all were silently expressive in the way survivors become: eyebrows, wrists, the tilt of a shoulder doing the work of a whole orchestra. [259]

What Amaya wanted, and had wanted since the water, was to translate silence into music: not pictures, not pretty weather on a wall, but pitch and interval and the terrible arithmetic of a chord resolving. [294] The obstacle was the Standard Lexicon, calibrated in the second year by a committee of physicians who had lived through the Hush without losing anyone. [319] Their lexicon filed grief under fear: same red flare at the sternum, same label. [333] Every student who tried to write about the drowned came out sounding frightened, and every piece went sour in the middle, and Amaya had petitioned four times to rectify the entry. [364] Four times the Bureau had written back: insufficient data. [373]

So on the eleventh anniversary she opened the tuning fork case for the first time since the lake. [391] Inside, in green baize, lay seven brass forks, still bright, still true. [403] Struck, they vibrated exactly as they always had, because the Hush had never touched metal, only the air's willingness to relay it. [425] The conservatory's museum case called such objects unshaken marvels: bells, whistles, forks, glass harmonicas, all functioning perfectly, all mute. [444] Children pressed their wrists to them and felt A above middle C climb the bone of the arm. [462]

Amaya took the forks and her sixteen students to the calibration plate in the north transept, where the winterglass was thickest and the Bureau's sensors still logged every reading. [491] She put her palm down and let herself go back into the water. [504] Fear had been the first three minutes, and the plate showed it: red, fast, jagged, spending itself. [521] Then came the eleven years afterward, and the plate went somewhere the Lexicon had no word for: cool, wide, slow, a blue that did not flare but spread, holding its shape the way a held note holds. [558] Grief was not hot. [562] Grief was patient. [565] One by one her students laid their hands beside hers and gave the plate their own dead, and the blue widened until the whole north wall was one long cold chord. [596] Amaya struck the forks against the plate's brass rim, one after another, and the glass, reading heat and vibration together, hung pitches inside the color. [621] D, F, A, and then the low C that made it ache and made it move. [637] Nobody heard it. [640] Everybody felt it, through the floor, through the wrist bones, through the soles of sixteen pairs of boots. [658]

The Bureau's sensors logged four hundred and twelve seconds of a reading they could not file, submitted by seventeen bodies at once. [680] In spring the Standard Lexicon issued its first amendment: entry 41, grief, cool spectrum, distinct from fear, notated as a sustained interval rather than a flare. [706] Amaya's students wrote nine pieces that year, one for each passenger who had not come up, and none of them went sour in the middle. [731] She kept the case open on her desk after that, lid up, forks bright, silent, and perfectly in tune. [750]
