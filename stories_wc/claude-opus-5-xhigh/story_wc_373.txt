Under the Bellweather pier, where the pilings had gone soft as old bread, the market opened only when the tide allowed it. [22] Nell came down the ladder with two jugs of her father's corn liquor roped across her back, and the boards overhead breathed tar and salt. [47] She was fifteen, and she had been a moonshine runner's daughter long enough to know that the safest rooms in the world are the illegal ones. [73] Down here the pirate market kept its own weather beneath the rotting boards: lanterns swinging on nails, oysters roasting on a shovel blade, a fiddler tuning through the drip. [102] Nobody used names, because from the lighting of the first lantern until sunrise the market held a truce of hush. [122] No face remembered, no debt collected, no word carried up the ladder. [134] Nell paid her way with her father's antique watch fob, a brass anchor on a broken chain, laid on Mother Chine's palm and lifted back again. [160] The watch was long gone; the fob remained, and that was the whole lesson of the pier. [177] It was a comforting place, if you did not think too hard about the water rising under the floor. [196]

The stranger sat by the fiddler with a machine on his knees, a horn like a lily and a cylinder of wax turning under a needle. [222] He called himself a collector of sounds and paid in coin for songs, which is a fine trade to claim. [242] Nell watched him for an hour and saw that he never wound the machine for music. [258] He wound it when old Pell got low and weepy, when the coopers argued over a shorted barrel, when a woman spoke of the man who had left her holding a bill. [290] He had come to capture the sound of trust breaking, and he meant to carry it up the ladder in a box. [312] A judge could not be brought down here, but a cylinder could be brought to a judge. [329] The hush protected the market only so long as nothing was recorded, and wax remembers what men agree to forget. [349]

Nell could not accuse him aloud without breaking the hush herself, and she could not smash the machine, because a fight under the pier would end the truce for everyone. [379] So she sat down in front of the lily horn and offered to sell him her father's routes. [397] His hands shook a little, the way a man's do when the thing he wants walks up on its own legs. [418] He asked how many cylinders she thought her father was worth. [429]

All of them, it turned out, because Nell thwarted him through false starts. [442] She began, and coughed. [446] She began again, and gave the wrong creek, and corrected herself, which spoiled the cut. [461] She started too far back, with her grandmother's kitchen, and had to be stopped. [475] She sneezed at the crucial name. [481] She laughed at nothing, twice. [486] Each ruined cylinder had to be shaved smooth or set aside, and the stranger's case held only eleven. [504] Her voice stayed curiously steadfast through all of it, patient and warm and helpful, apologizing, promising to get it right on the next one. [528] That was the cruelty of it, and the kindness: she never once refused him. [542]

The fiddler understood by the fourth cylinder and began to play louder, badly, over her worst mistakes. [559] Old Pell wandered close and dropped a bucket. [567] Mother Chine sent over a cup of broth for the poor nervous girl, and steam is death to wax. [586] Nobody said a word about any of it, because that is what a truce of hush is for. [604]

At the eleventh cylinder the tide had turned and the pilings were running with gray light. [620] Sunrise ended the hush, and after sunrise every man under the pier was free to remember a face. [638] The stranger packed his machine fast, with his lily horn under his arm like a stolen flower. [655] At the ladder he turned and asked her, quietly, why she had not simply lied to him. [672] "A lie plays back like a lie," she said. [681] "A false start plays back like a girl who is trying her best." [694] He looked at the brass anchor swinging at her waist and offered to buy it, the way men do when they want to leave with something. [720] Nell closed her hand around the fob, which had outlasted its watch, its chain, and now him. [737] Then she climbed up into the light, where her father waited with the truck idling, and told him nothing at all. [758] There was nothing to tell: the hush had held, and a thing that holds makes no sound at all. [777]
