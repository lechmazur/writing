They call me the penny poet, because a penny is what a poem costs, and because a penny is what I own. [22] I keep no house, only a feral loft beneath the dunes, the last attic of a drowned village that the sand swallowed politely, a hundred years ago. [49] Marram grass grows through the floorboards; gulls nest in the rafters and argue about me. [64] It is a lonesome splendor, and it suits my trade. [74] My trade is capturing sounds in glass jars. [82] Any fool can write a word down; a word down is a corpse. [95] I catch the living sound, the breath around it, and I stopper it with wax. [110] The trick is the hour: at dawn's earliest whistle, when the wind first finds the broken cannery pipe on the point, the air goes thin enough to hold a voice. [140] Miss anything by an hour and you jar only sand and hush. [152]

The boy Teo came up the dune path in October with a music box under his coat. [169] He paid me a penny for a poem, then asked whether I took commissions in sound. [185] The music box was a forgotten thing, tarnished, its comb bent, its cylinder furred with green. [201] It had belonged to his great-grandmother, Mira Salt, who was ninety-four and dying in a room above the ferry office. [221] She spoke the old dune tongue, the drowned village's speech, and Teo spoke none of it. [237] "She sings to me," he said, "and I nod like a liar." [249]

I knew that nod. [253] I gave it to my own grandmother for eleven years, sitting on her bed with my boots on, waiting for her to finish so I could go out. [281] She died with a whole language in her mouth and no one to hand it to, and I have been gleaning ever since. [304] Gleaners take what the harvest left; I take what the dying leave lying in the air. [320] It is the only redemption I am owed, and I intend to collect it jar by jar. [337]

So we worked. [340] Fourteen mornings I sat by Mira Salt's window with my crate of jars, and at dawn's earliest whistle I opened them one at a time. [365] She gave me her words for fog, for grief, for the sound a boat makes when it wants to leave. [385] She gave me her mother's counting rhyme and the nine names of wind. [398] Her voice was positively wistful, thin as tide-lace, and it filled each jar like smoke that had learned a melody. [418]

Teo mended the music box with a watchmaker's screwdriver and a great deal of swearing. [433] When it played at last, Mira wept and sang over it in the old tongue, and I caught both together, tune and words in one jar, which is the rarest catch there is. [466]

Raw sound sours, though. [470] Left alone in glass it turns to a hornet-hum of static and regret, so you must sweeten it. [488] I use a thumbnail of dune honey, dropped in before the wax; the bees here work the sea-holly and the wild rose, and their sugar keeps a voice tender for a hundred years. [521]

Mira died on a Tuesday, in the middle of a sentence about her brother's boat. [536] I sealed the half-sentence anyway. [541] Teo climbed the dune that night and found me among two hundred jars set out on the sanded floor, each one glowing faintly, the way old sound does. [569] "Teach me to open them," he said. [576]

That was the whole of what I had ever wanted: not to keep the jars, but to become the translator between generations, the hinge between a woman who is gone and a boy who is not. [612] We began with the counting rhyme, because children's words are the easiest doors. [625] By spring he knew the nine names of wind and could say fog three ways. [640] He winds the music box each dawn now, up here in the loft, and answers it in his great-grandmother's tongue while the gulls complain overhead. [665] I charge him nothing. [669] A poem costs a penny, but this was never poetry; this was gleaning, and gleaning is what the field forgives you for. [691]

Some mornings, when the cannery pipe whistles and the sand shifts under the floor, I open one jar of my own, the smallest, the one I filled last of all, on the beach at low tide, saying my grandmother's name into the glass. [734] It is only my voice in there, sweetened, saying I am sorry, I am listening now. [750] It is enough. [753]
