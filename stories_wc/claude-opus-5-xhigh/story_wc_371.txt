The bakery on Kestrel Lane vanished on a Tuesday, and by Thursday nobody but me remembered the smell. [18] That is my affliction: I am a noticer of creeping normality, the woman who cannot let a missing thing settle quietly into never-having-been. [41] Everyone else adjusts by degrees, the way you adjust to a room going dark. [55] I kept the last loaf. [60] It sits in my satchel now, hard as a stone saint's foot, wrapped in the brown paper that still carries a printed wheat sheaf and a name the registry says was never licensed. [93] I carry it the way other people carry relics, because I have decided that the sacred lives in ordinary things or it lives nowhere at all. [119]

The tea house opens only after the birds fly south. [129] The reason is practical rather than poetic: the wardens come each autumn to net the starlings, and when the flocks leave, the wardens leave with them, and the overgrown courtyard belongs to us until spring. [164] You reach it through a gap in the palace wall where I planted a curtain of hops four springs ago. [184] Guerrilla gardening is how we built the place and how we keep it. [197] We hid the door behind blackthorn, we grew potatoes in the fountain basin, and each seed we pressed into that cracked marble was a small argument that a city is not only what the ministry prints about it. [235]

Inside, under a roof of salvaged greenhouse glass, Ossip pours tea for nine or ten of us who remember inconvenient things. [256] He is heuristically gentle. [260] I mean that his kindness follows rules he worked out in advance, the way a careful man draws a map before a fog: never ask a newcomer's real name, always serve the oldest guest first, never let anyone leave without something to eat. [303] I used to find it charming. [309]

Last night he asked me to stay after the others had gone, and then he uncloaked. [325] Under the gardener's oilskin he wore the grey ministry coat, collar tabs and all, the uniform of the Bureau of Orderly Records. [347] "I signed the Kestrel Lane order," he said. [355] "Nineteen shops, one bakery -- I wrote the paragraph that made them never have existed." [369]

The kettle ticked as it cooled. [375] I thought of every cup he had poured me, every rule of his gentleness, and understood it as a ledger he had been keeping against himself. [401] He had built a tea house for the people he erased. [412] "Can you forgive it?" he asked, and I noticed he did not say absolve. [426]

I am the wrong woman to ask, because I am the only one still counting. [441] So I did what I know how to do with ordinary things. [453] I unwrapped the loaf from the vanished bakery, the last physical proof that Kestrel Lane had ever fed anyone, and I set it on the table between us. [481] It was too hard to break by hand, so he fetched the pruning saw we use on the blackthorn, and we cut it into rough coins and soaked them in tea until they could be chewed. [517] It tasted of dust and caraway and four years of waiting. [528]

"This is all I have," I told him. [536] "Not forgiveness -- a meal, and a witness, and work." [545] That is what I can give: a tenuous absolution, renewable each morning, revoked if he ever stops. [562]

This morning we were out before light with a sack of rye seed and the crumbs of the loaf, digging the long bed where the coronation steps used to be. [592] He works with the same rule-bound tenderness he pours tea with, and I have stopped finding that charming and started finding it useful. [615] Next autumn, after the birds fly south again, there will be a small illegal harvest in a palace courtyard, and Ossip will bake it in the clay oven we are building from the fountain's rubble. [650] The bread will not be a monument, and the ministry will not notice, and nobody will call it holy. [669] But I will smell it, and I will remember Kestrel Lane, and the smell will not be allowed to slip away by degrees. [692] That is the whole of my religion, and it needs no cathedral, only a noticer, a gardener, and something ordinary enough to be worth saving. [717]
