The drift-ice marina moved a little every hour, so Mirren Vask had learned to love things that refused to hold still. [21] Her azure coat had gone stiff with salt, and the seams smelled of her own body, which she considered honest. [41] As lighthouse engineer she was paid to keep a light burning; unofficially, she was paid to keep a song from breaking. [62] Beneath the tower's plinth, in a socket of green stone, lay the Reliquary of Saint Ilva - a sacred artifact that hummed at forty-one hertz, low as a heartbeat heard through a wall. [94] Mirren had never seen it, only felt it in her teeth on still nights. [108] She was, as her old chief had written in her file, logically emotional: she grieved in decibels, loved in tolerances, and could not tell you why she wept at a well-tuned bell except that its error margin was zero. [147] That evening the ice came apart in slabs the color of butter, because the sun was setting and the sun here set fast. [170] Nine minutes of gold, she had measured it once, and then blue nothing until spring. [185] Into that fleeting gold slid the salvage cutter Adamant, grinding past the marina's ice-bollards and the three fishing boats frozen in for winter, six men on deck with a resonance drill lashed like a cannon. [220] They did not want the tower; they wanted the hum under it. [232] Mirren went down the ladder with her satchel and her heart going like a bilge pump. [248] In the satchel was the crypt keeper's key: not a key for locks but a forked rod of black bronze, hand-worn, used for centuries by the keepers of Ilva's crypt to strike a stone and grant the dead sonic absolution. [288] The doctrine was simple and physical: a soul clenched around its guilt could be loosened if you found its exact frequency and sang it back, until the tightness let go and the body it had haunted lay quiet. [326] Mirren had always read it as engineering wearing vestments. [335] The foreman, a courteous man with frostbite scars, offered her a share. [347] "It's a bone in a box," he said, "and nobody prays here anymore." [360] "It's a tuned mass," Mirren said, "and if you shatter its housing at the wrong pitch, the resonance will crack this whole floe and drown all of us before the light is gone." [393] That was true, and it was also the least of her reasons. [405] They started the drill anyway. [410] It came up through the ice as a flat, mean note, and she felt it in her fillings, her sternum, the wet parts of her. [435] Blocking it was hopeless; a barricade of one woman against six is arithmetic. [448] So she adapted, the way ice adapts, by giving the pressure somewhere to go. [462] She climbed to the lamp room and unclamped the fog bell's governor, then sat with the crypt keeper's key against her lower lip, listening to the drill's beat frequency against the reliquary's forty-one hertz. [496] Vibrational matching was the whole of the old rite: find the thing's true note, meet it, and it will answer you as flesh answers flesh. [521] She struck the key on the bell's lip and turned the governor until the great bronze mouth was singing not at the drill but with the stone beneath it. [550] The reliquary woke. [553] Forty-one hertz doubled, and the two voices locked, and the whole floe became one instrument, and the drill's mean note was swallowed the way a shout is swallowed by a choir. [584] Down on the deck the men went to their knees, not from piety but because the sound went into their bodies and took the tension out, guilt by guilt, the way heat takes a knot from a rope. [622] The foreman wept and could not have told you why, and Mirren, who could have, wept with him. [640] Absolution, she thought, holding the singing key with both blistering hands: not forgiveness handed down, only a body finally permitted to stop clenching. [663] When the gold went out of the sky the note went with it, nine minutes exactly. [679] The Adamant cut its engines and drifted off toward open water without asking anything more of the ice. [697] Mirren lay on the lamp room floor in her azure coat, sweating, ears ringing, absurdly alive. [713] The reliquary hummed on beneath her, unbroken, and she understood that she had not saved it so much as introduced it. [734] In the log she wrote the frequency, the duration, the names of six men. [748] Then, because she was an engineer, she wrote the tolerance; and because she was also a mourner, she wrote: it held.
