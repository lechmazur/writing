My aunts called me chronically curious, which in our family was a diagnosis rather than a compliment. [17] Decades later I still test every story with my hands, because a hands-on folklorist learns what tape recorders never catch. [37] That is why, at three in the morning, I am kneeling in salt mud inside the sunken orchard greenhouse ringed by shipwrecks. [59] The wrecks arrived the winter the sea took the seawall: five trawlers and a dredger, shoved up onto the orchard's rise, rusting there into a crooked ring around the drowned glasshouse. [90] Locals call them the fence. [95] I call them a warning I ignored for fifty years. [105] Inside, the apple trees stand dead in brackish water, and along their roots the fairy ring mushroom still comes up, small and buff and stubbornly patterned. [131]

The summer I was eleven, my brother Tomas and I ate stolen apples in here and slept in the toolroom on sacks that smelled of creosote. [157] In September we walked home with three weeks scrubbed out of us both. [170] Our mother thought we were lying; the doctor thought we were concussed; I thought, even then, that we had been robbed. [191] Tomas died in March, and the last thing he asked me was what happened that August, as if I had been keeping it from him out of spite. [219] So I have come to recover stolen memories, mine and his, while there is still someone alive to hold them. [239]

Here is the method, and I would defend it before any faculty panel that dared invite me. [256] Fairy rings are supposed to be circles, but every ring I have measured in this orchard is an arc with pieces missing. [278] For forty years I copied those arcs into notebooks, and one night in a hotel in Galway the pattern turned over in my head: they were star maps. [306] The mushrooms mark constellations as they hung over this coast in the years of the thefts, and each place a fungus refuses to grow is a star put out. [335] You can detect a stolen memory by studying broken constellations: find the gap, date the sky, and you have the year, the hour, and the name of what was taken. [365] Our August sits in the shoulder of the Hunter, one missing star above the toolroom door. [381]

The old tellers, all of them, whatever they disagreed about, agreed that the door opens when habits break. [399] So I built one to break. [405] Every night since 1974, at ten past three - the hour my mother died - I have woken and drunk black tea from her dented thermos, on ferries, in hostels, in the corridor where Tomas was dying. [440] Eighteen thousand days of tea, so that tonight the not-drinking would ring like a dropped plate. [456] At ten past three I pour the whole thermos into the mud instead. [469] Then I take from my coat the fairy ring mushroom I cut from the ring that came up unbidden on my brother's grave, and press it into the gap in the arc, under the empty shoulder of the Hunter. [508]

The glass overhead goes bright, though there is no moon. [518] It comes back the way cold water comes back to a stopped pipe: all at once, hard, and tasting of iron. [539] Tomas at eleven, sunburnt, laughing at a wasp in a windfall apple. [551] The greenhouse whole and hot, glass sweating, tomatoes gone feral along the benches. [564] The keeper of the orchard, whose face I will not describe, kneeling to mend a ring of mushrooms we had scuffed through with our bare heels. [590] She said we had broken a door, and that she would take the three weeks as payment, and be fair about it. [612] She was fair, in her way, and she warned us that the sea would come for the orchard once the last of us forgot it. [637] It did. [639]

Now the memory is mine again, crisp as the first bite of a Bramley in August, and it costs me nothing to hold it except everything it reminds me of. [669] I write it down by torchlight, exactly, because Tomas asked and because the sea is coming back over the rise in twenty minutes. [692] Then I set the empty thermos upright in the mud between two mushrooms, lid off, the way you leave a cup out for someone expected. [717] Habits break, and something always comes through the gap; I have been curious my whole life about which of us it would be. [740] Above me, through the rust and the salt-blind glass, the Hunter has both shoulders. [754]
