Mira Kell had thirteen phobias, and she had fallen through every one of them for money. [16] Water, heights, fire, small rooms, the sound of a door locking behind her: the studio called her firmly adaptive, which meant she trembled and did it anyway. [43] She doubled for Verity Lune, who never trembled at anything. [53] The truth arrived on a child's arm. [60] Theo, eight years old, an extra who had broken his wrist when a rigged balcony gave early, wore a plaster cast that the crew had covered in signatures. [88] Verity's assistant had signed it too, and beside her name she had scrawled columns of tiny smudged numbers, because nobody searches a child. [111] Mira kept the cast after the doctor cut it away, and spent four nights decrypting the smudged notes with a penlight. [132] The cipher was simple, a shifting alphabet keyed to shoot dates, the kind of thing a bored assistant invents and trusts too much. [155] SUBJECT M: redaction four stable, fear conditioning on schedule, Lune's face healed well. [168] Mira sat on the bathroom floor and understood that she was not the double. [182] She was Verity Lune, and the woman wearing her face had spent three years installing her phobias, one surgery, one drug, one drowning at a time, so that the original would stay frightened, obedient, and useful for the dangerous work. [222] A betrayal that thorough does not break a person; it remakes her. [234] Mira had a trembling certainty now, the way a bridge hums before it holds. [248] In her kit she kept half a seashell, cracked lengthwise, that whispered. [260] The pair had been props in a film about lovers separated by an ocean; speak near one and the other whispers it back, faint as surf, wherever it lies. [289] Mira had given the other half to Verity as a wrap gift, back when she believed they were friends. [308] Verity had never stopped carrying it, because thieves love souvenirs. [318] The last night of the shoot they filmed in the old Ironvale library, a roofless ruin where rusted steel shelves stood in rows under open stars, and rain had eaten the books to pulp. [352] Mira was to fall sixty feet into a black water tank in the basement stacks, at night, in a locked harness: three phobias in one shot, and Verity had written it herself. [384] The plan was obvious once you knew: the frightened double drowns, the notes on the cast go to landfill, and the face keeps working. [408] So Mira did not fight, because fighting was Verity's game; she wanted instead to prove that the trembling built into her was not weakness but leverage. [434] An hour before the shot she gave her seashell half to Theo's mother in the catering tent and told her to hold it to her ear beside the producer and the two officers who worked night shoots for traffic. [473] Then she climbed to the rig and let Verity find her shaking, which took no acting at all. [491] "I can't do the water," Mira said, and her voice cracked exactly as it needed to. [507] "I'm afraid of everything you gave me." [514] Under the stars, among the rusted shelves, Verity smiled a smile Mira had once practiced in mirrors, stolen and worn thin by three years of use. [540] "You'll do it," she said, "because I built you frightened, and frightened is what you are." [556] "I took the name, the face, the redactions, and the fear went in easiest of all." [572] In the catering tent, a small cracked shell whispered every word into a mother's ear while four people leaned close. [592] Mira had not outwitted her with courage; she had outwitted her with the one thing Verity never thought worth stealing, the willingness to be seen afraid. [618] Vulnerability was the lever; it had opened her, and it opened Verity wider. [631] They took Verity at the tank ladder, still holding the seashell half that had confessed for her. [648] Mira did the fall anyway, hours later, for herself. [657] She shook the whole way down, sixty feet of cold air between rusted shelves and colder stars, and she hit the black water and came up whispering her own name. [687] She gave Theo his cast back, signed at last in her own hand, the one thing no one had learned to forge. [709] She still had thirteen phobias, and she was certain she would keep them, because they were evidence, and because a body that admits its terror is the hardest thing in the world to own. [743]
