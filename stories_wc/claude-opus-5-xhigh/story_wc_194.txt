The lawyers would arrive at dawn, and by noon the Vantor dynasty would be a footnote, so Ilsa spent the first yawn of evening breaking into her grandmother's reading room. [30] The lock gave with an apologetic click, as if it had been waiting sixty years for someone impatient enough. [49] Inside, everything was sepia: the varnished shelves, the tobacco-stained maps, the light leaning through the west window in long golden shadows that made the dust look like suspended honey. [78] Mirek followed her in, carrying the lamp. [85] He had served the house since before her birth and had not spoken a word since a fever took his voice at nineteen; he was, her grandmother used to say, eloquently silent, which was the highest compliment the old woman ever paid anyone. [128] Ilsa set her inheritance on the reading table: a gramophone crank handle, brass, worn bright at the grip, willed to her with no gramophone to fit it. [155] For eleven years she had assumed it was a joke about winding things down. [169] "She sealed this room the week my father died," Ilsa said. [180] "Everyone says grief. I want the other reason." [188] Mirek tilted the lamp toward the far wall, and she understood he was not helping her see; he was helping her hear. [210] The room had no corners. [215] Its ends curved like the inside of a shell, the shelves cut short of the plaster so the walls could breathe. [236] Ilsa, who had spent her exile years tuning organs in provincial churches, knew what she was standing in. [254] A resonator. [256] She found the socket at the base of the third shelf, a square hole she had mistaken all her life for a knothole, and the crank handle slid home with the sweetness of a thing coming back to its own body. [297] She turned it. [300] Somewhere under the floor a flywheel woke, and a tuning cylinder already mounted there filled the room with a low, patient tone that did not travel so much as stand. [330] Ilsa felt it in her teeth, in the soles of her feet, and then she saw it: the honey-dust sifting out of the air and settling in stripes across the floorboards, gathering where the sound lay still. [367] Through standing waves, the room was drawing its own map. [377] Nodes, evenly spaced, marching toward the shell-curve of the west wall - and one stripe that broke the pattern, bending where no physics allowed, because something hollow lay beneath the boards. [407] Mirek was already kneeling there, palm flat, his face asking a question she could answer without words. [424] That was how it had always worked between them, a bridge of intuition thrown across the gap where speech should be, and she trusted it more than any contract her family had ever signed. [458] The board lifted. [461] Beneath it, in a lead-lined box, lay four wax cylinders and a deed. [474] The mandrel below the floor took a second cylinder as easily as the first, and when Ilsa cranked again, her grandmother's voice rose out of the whole room at once, the curved walls speaking with one throat. [511] "If you are hearing this," the old woman said, "you have the ear I hoped for, and you deserve the truth I could not print." [536] The Vantor claim to the river valley was forged in 1841, and every rent collected since had been theft dressed in a seal. [559] The real deed, the one under the floor, named eleven tenant families. [571] Her grandmother had sealed the room rather than destroy the evidence, and had sent Ilsa a crank handle instead of a warning, betting that a girl who loved resonance would one day come looking. [605] The cylinder hissed to its end. [611] Ilsa sat back on her heels in the last of the golden shadows and let herself muse, the way tuners do, on how long a wrong note can hold if the room is built to keep it. [648] Then she stood. [651] The lawyers had come to dissolve a dynasty; they would find its ender waiting with a deed that made dissolution the smaller half of the business. [677] Mirek picked up the crank handle, polished it once on his sleeve, and set it in her palm - a whole speech, delivered. [699] She would keep it, she decided, the family's one honest heirloom: the handle that had made the house confess. [718]
