The clouds began their migration on the third day after the fire, long gray herds sliding west above the dunes, and Mira Vashti walked beneath them with a jar of black sand in her coat. [35] She was still in her station boots; she had not slept. [46] Behind her, the island's only fire brigade was hosing down what was left of the Ferreira orchard, and every hand on the pump knew whose daughter she was. [74] Twenty years ago her father had burned four houses and a chapel, and the town had never agreed on whether the fire had been in his blood or only in his hands. [106] This fire was not his, he had been dead nine years, and the black sand in the jar was scraped from the volcanic beach where she had let his ashes go. [137] The boy who had started this one was waiting for her at the top of the dune, thirteen years old, ash on his eyebrows, hands shaking. [163] Teo Ferreira had burned his family's prunings in a wind he was too young to read, and by dawn the fire had taken his grandfather's fig trees, the oldest on the island. [195] "They are saying I did it on purpose," he said. [205] "They will say it for years," Mira answered. [213] "Come down to the lagoon with me anyway." [221]

The obsidian lagoon lay beneath the dunes like a held breath, a bowl of old volcanic glass filled with rainwater and seawater and long ropes of kelp that drifted without hurry, turning, untangling, turning again. [256] Nobody came here in the burning season except Mira, who kept a nursery of grafted stock on the black terrace above the water. [279] She knelt, opened the jar, and worked the black sand into a bed of soil, gracefully alert as always, her head tilted the way it tilted at a smoke alarm three streets off. [312] From her coat she took a bundle of scion wood, green sticks she had cut that morning from the one fig limb the fire had spared. [338] "Your grandfather's trees are not gone," she said. "Give me your knife." [350] She showed him how to cut the rootstock at a slant, how to shave the scion to match, how to bind the two wounds face to face so they would graft, wood to wood, stranger to stranger. [387] "The join always scars," she said. "That ridge is the strongest part of the tree." [402] "Bind it, water it, and in three years you will prove to this island that scars can sing." [420]

Teo looked at the thing swinging from her throat: a foreign coin, drilled through and strung on red thread, worn smooth as a fingernail. [444] "My father made it," she said, before he could ask. [454] "He drilled a coin from a country he never saw, put it on red thread, and told me to wear it so I would remember that a thing can be pierced and still hold." [488] "He was an arsonist, Teo; he burned the chapel your grandfather was married in." [502] "I visited him every month for eleven years, and the town has never forgiven me for that either." [520] "Then why the fire brigade?" Teo said. [527] "Because someone in this family had to learn the other half of the work," she said, and she pressed his thumb over the graft to hold it still. [555]

The clouds went over them in their slow herds, shadow and light, shadow and light, and the kelp turned in the lagoon, and for a while neither of them said anything, and the peace was the drifting kind, the kind that asks nothing. [598] By dusk they had forty grafts standing in black volcanic sand, each one bound with red thread cut from the same spool as her coin's. [623]

Every spring after that, while the clouds migrated, Mira brought whoever the island had decided to blame that year down to the lagoon and put a knife in their hand. [653] Her compassion was not gentle; it did not bend, and it did not let go. [668] Teo Ferreira is fifty now, and his orchard above the dunes is famous for figs that come in early, on trees with a ridge of scar tissue at the knee of every trunk. [701] When the sea wind comes off the lagoon those ridges catch it and the whole grove hums, low, like a held note. [723] He tells visitors that a woman with a drilled coin at her throat taught him to prove that scars can sing, and that she is buried in black sand at the water's edge, where the clouds still pass over her, going west. [765]
