The eclipse ended at the ninth bell, and the wind-carved encampment held its breath to see who was left. [19] Vess came up through the flutes of sandstone with her hood thrown back, letting the returning sun burn her the way it always did. [43] An albino tracker learns early that the desert will not negotiate; she had simply stopped asking it to. [61] Above her right shoulder the artificial hummingbird drone kept station, wings a smear of noise, its glass eye turning slow circles over the tents. [85] The tents were empty. [89] Thirty-one of the Doctrine had gone in under the shadow, and, as always, they had left their ink behind. [108] It lay in a wide black star on the swept floor of the meeting hollow, already thinning at the edges, already beginning to disappear. [132] Disappearing ink, the scholars in the low cities called it, and laughed, because they had never watched what it did in the last seconds of its life. [159] Vess knelt at the rim of the stain and did not touch it. [172] Her anger was a cool, level thing by now, worn smooth like a riverstone, the kind of fury you can carry a hundred miles without spilling. [198] Two years ago her sister Ilat had knelt exactly here. [208] "Watch the pattern," Ilat had written in her last letter, in ordinary ink that stayed. [223] "It only holds for a heartbeat, but a heartbeat is not a fixed measure." [237] That was the whole doctrine, and the whole reason Vess had spent two years chasing it: to discover why time slows in crisis. [260] Everyone knows the falling man has an hour to inspect the ground. [272] The Doctrine had worked out what that hour is for. [282] She sent the drone down low. [288] It had cost her a year's tracking fees and a finger's worth of frostbite in the northern passes, and it was worth both, because it saw nine hundred frames to her one. [320] On the little screen strapped to her forearm, the dying stain unfolded in slow honesty. [335] As the ink surrendered itself to the air it made lattices, branching corridors of pale between the black, ephemeral patterns in spilled ink that lasted a fifth of a second and were, unmistakably, a doorway. [370] She had known that for a month. [377] What she had not known, until she watched the recordings from the eclipse itself, was the rest of the method. [397] The Doctrine did not send their people through calmly. [406] They frightened them. [409] They put out the sun, and cut the tent ropes, and screamed of the world ending, and in that engineered terror the mind opened its throttle and the fifth of a second became long enough to step into. [447] Time does not slow for the crisis, Vess thought; the crisis is only the excuse the body has been waiting for. [468] Underneath the trained animal there is a wild one who has always been able to see this fast. [486] That was where the anger lived, and the calm sat beside it like a dog. [501] They had used fear on her sister like a crowbar when a key existed. [515] Vess had spent eleven years making herself consciously wild -- sleeping without walls, eating what she killed, unlearning the flinch that domestication puts between a creature and its own speed -- and she had done it deliberately, on purpose, for this hour. [555] The Doctrine's stain was nearly gone, so she took the stolen flask from her belt and spilled a fresh star of her own across the swept stone. [582] Her pale eyes, ruined by noon and excellent in dusk, had one gift left to give, and the eclipse's long half-light still pooled in the hollow. [608] She did not scream, or cut a rope, or pray. [618] She simply stopped holding herself back. [624] It came up out of her like something surfacing: the flat, unhurried readiness of a hunting animal, and the world obligingly thickened. [646] The drone's wings separated into individual strokes. [653] A grain of sand crossed the air in front of her, turning. [665] And the ink, dying at her feet, spread its pale corridors wide and held them open, patient as a held breath. [686] Vess said her sister's name, once, without heat. [694] Then she stepped down into the pattern and was gone before the wind finished its next turn through the flutes of stone. [716] The hummingbird hung above the place for a long while, recording nine hundred frames a second of clean, dry, empty rock. [737] Then, obedient to its last instruction, it turned south toward the low cities, carrying the answer to anyone patient enough to slow down and look. [762]
