Sera held her summer-quill above the ledger and mourned the morning before it had finished happening. [16] That was the flaw the Remembrancy had noted in her file: preemptively nostalgic, prone to grieving things that were still in the room. [39] They had appointed her anyway, because no one else wanted the summer term, and the summer term was the only one that mattered. [62]

Once in a generation the fog below the observatory thickened until it went solid, a white floor you could stand on, and the villages of the lower valleys climbed up to be remembered. [95] They arrived in the storm season, so the observatory always received them stormlit, lightning walking the cloud plain like a slow surveyor. [117] And when the rain stopped, the crystal forecourt whispered. [126]

That was the purpose of the place. [133] Wet quartz gave back what had been spoken on it: oaths, arguments, lullabies, the names of the drowned, four hundred years of a people talking. [158] The archivist's task was not to transcribe it all, which was impossible, but to sculpt from the flood a shape the valleys could recognize as themselves. [184] Identity, her predecessor had written, is experience with the edges chosen. [195]

On the third night, Sera found the gap. [203] Every memory the crystal offered ran backward smoothly to the year of the Ash Winter and then simply stopped, as though the people had begun mid-sentence. [229] No founding, no first fire, no reason for the climb into the mountains. [242]

So she went out among the monuments with a chalked cord and did the old, unfashionable work: examining shadow lengths at monuments, noon by noon, matching each stone's angle to the season it had been raised. [278] The builders had set them by sun, not by whim; a shadow could be read like a date. [296] Eleven monuments agreed with the crystal. [302] The twelfth, a blank pillar at the forecourt's northern lip, threw a shadow that belonged to a solstice ninety years earlier than any story anyone had ever told her. [331] Someone had stood here, long before the Ash Winter, and had wanted the date kept and the deed forgotten. [350]

Sera spent two nights on conjecture, which the Remembrancy permitted only in the margins and only in grey ink. [369] The valleys below shared no language with the coast, yet their oldest lullaby used coastal words for salt and sail. [389] They had come from somewhere and had chosen, deliberately and together, to arrive without a past. [405] Perhaps they had fled something shameful; perhaps they had done it. [416]

On the last morning the delegates filled the forecourt, six hundred people from nine villages, waiting for the reading that would tell them who they were for the next thirty years. [447] Rain had just ended. [451] The quartz was murmuring under their boots, and the hush over them deepened as she climbed the reading-stair, deepened again as she opened the ledger, until the loudest thing on the mountain was the drip of water from the eaves. [491]

She could give them the clean version. [498] Twelve monuments, four hundred years, a people born of the Ash Winter and tempered by it. [514] It would hold for a generation, and she would be dead before the seam split. [529]

Instead she read the eleven certainties first, plainly, so they would be believed. [542] Then she read the twelfth stone: its shadow, its date, and her grey-ink guess that their ancestors had crossed the fog carrying a silence on purpose. [568] "I do not know what they buried," she said. [577] "I know they buried it together, and that is also a thing you are." [591]

Nobody shouted. [593] The hush went further down, the way water goes into sand, and Sera understood that they were not angry but busy, six hundred people quietly resetting themselves around a hole. [623] An old woman from Tarn asked whether the missing years could be looked for. [637] Sera said yes, and felt the future memory of this exact minute arrive in her chest fully formed, already precious, already gone. [659]

By evening the fog had begun to soften; the delegates went down before the floor could fail. [676] Sera stayed to dry the quartz with linen, a courtesy the old archivists observed, and listened to the whispers thin out into weather. [699] Her term ended at the equinox. [705] She wrote the last line of the ledger in grey, because it was a conjecture and she had promised to mark them: They will come back for the rest of themselves. [736]
