Vesna was the last prism-song bell ringer, and the Registry of Necessary Things had given her one night to prove she was not decoration. [24] The proof would be delivered in an ice cavern under the Kolva headland, where nine glass bells hung from a spine of blue ice. [48] She had been synesthetically blessed since birth, which meant that every tone she heard arrived wearing a color, and every color hummed. [70] Auditors called this a defect; her teachers had called it the whole reason the bells were built here at all. [90] The nine bells were cut prisms; struck, each threw a note and a bar of split light, and only a ringer who could match the two was called prism-song. [119] The Registry's charge was blunt: to certify wonder as essential, or see the caverns sealed and the bells melted for window glass. [141]

Auditor Pell arrived at dusk with a ledger, a lantern, and the patient face of a man who had never once been surprised. [164] "Show me the instrument," he said, and she showed him the amber music sheet. [178] It was a palm-wide slab of fossil resin, blank as honey in lamplight, holding a drowned moth and nothing else. [198] Pell wrote: no notation observed. [203] "Wait for the sky," Vesna said. [209]

At the ninth hour the aurora came over the headland, and the cavern roof, forty feet of clear ice, became a lens. [231] Green light poured down and struck the amber, and through the colors of the aurora the score woke inside the resin: staves, rests, and a long spiral of notes coiling around the moth. [264] Red light showed the bass line, violet the ornaments; no single hue gave the whole, so the music had to be read as the sky shifted, one breath at a time. [295] Pell stopped writing. [298]

The score had one instruction that Vesna had spent eleven years learning to obey: begin at the moment a sculptor's chisel first strikes marble. [322] Far above, in the workshop on the cliff, Anselm was blocking out a figure for the harbor, and the first strike of any commission carries a sound nothing else carries -- a dry, bright crack that travels through stone and sea-cliff alike. [363] Vesna heard it as a white spike, and she pulled. [373] The pull did not ring anything; it unfettered the bells, releasing nine frost-locked clappers at once so the cavern itself could decide when they spoke. [398]

Then came the part the Registry had no column for. [408] The bells swung in slow opposition, and their tones cancelled one another in the middle of the chamber, folding sound into a shape with edges. [433] This was the sculpted hush, the reason the cavern had been carved: a silence you could walk into, cut to human size, warm as a held breath. [460] Pell stepped into it because everyone does. [467] Inside, the oscillation went on around him -- swell, ebb, swell -- a repose that never settled, rocking like a boat at a mooring. [489] He said later that he heard his mother's kitchen, and the sea under the ice, and something with no name that he had been keeping down since he was nine. [519] Vesna, watching colors bloom off the ice -- rose, ash, a green like new hay -- kept the swing steady with her heels, because a hush this size must be held, not struck. [550]

When the aurora thinned, the notes sank back into the amber and the bells came to rest, and the cavern was only a cold room again. [576] Pell climbed out with wet cheeks and an unusable ledger. [586] "It is not measurable," he said. [592] "No," Vesna agreed, "but you will still walk home differently, and that is the finding." [607] He wrote it in the margin, where findings are honest: Essential; certified; see the moth. [622] The Registry disputed the wording for a year and lost. [631] Vesna still climbs down at the ninth hour, and up on the cliff a young apprentice, told nothing about bells, lifts a chisel and waits for the sky to turn green before her first strike. [666]
