The tide left its gossip in the shallows, and Mirren waded out to collect it before memory fades. [18] Foam holds whatever the water overheard: last thoughts, old arguments, the shape of a road nobody drives anymore. [36] As a sea-foam recollectionist, she had eleven minutes from froth to nothing, which she considered generous. [52] She carried the day's harvest up the shingle in a copper bowl, humming a funeral tune with a bounce in it. [73]

Her workroom was an iridescent cave inside a giant crystal geode, split open by some ancient temper and never properly closed. [94] Amethyst teeth threw purple light on her ledgers; the kettle steamed cheerfully beside a wall of names. [111] She wrote with a shard of crater glass, green as bottle-bottom, dug from the pit where a star had once misjudged its landing. [134] The glass transliterates emotions: dipped in foam, it renders grief as a long slanted stroke, guilt as a knot, homesickness as a small door left ajar. [160] Mirren had filled nine hundred pages and never once needed to guess. [172]

That evening the foam came broken. [177] A boy's walk home from the herring sheds arrived with its middle torn out: the sheds, then nothing, then his mother's kitchen. [199] The road between had missing pieces, and so, she discovered, did the cliff itself. [213] She climbed out at dusk and found gorse where a lane had run for two centuries, unbroken, unremarkable, gone. [232]

This was not new to her, only newly close. [241] Her own way home had vanished when she was nineteen; she had walked the coast for a week and never found the turning, and had built her life inside a rock instead. [273] She wanted, with the mild ferocity of a woman who has made peace with almost everything, to discover why some paths disappear. [295]

Foam could not tell her; foam only remembered what people remembered. [306] So she turned to the only witness that kept its accounts elsewhere. [318] Every autumn a single godwit crossed the bay and roosted on the geode's cracked lip, and every autumn Mirren catalogued the seeds it dropped. [342] A bird eats where it stands; its droppings are an itinerary. [353] She had learned to read the migrating bird's route via the scattered seeds dropped in the crystal's damp seams: sea-rocket from the northern spits, then saltmarsh grass, then a burr that grew only along one hedged lane above the herring sheds. [394] This year the burr was absent, and in its place lay three seeds of a plant that had no business existing anywhere: a black wheel of a thing, faintly warm. [424]

Mirren allowed herself conjecture, which she had always thought a slightly indecent word, like undressing in public. [441] Suppose, she wrote, that a path is not ground but agreement. [452] Suppose the world keeps only the ways that are walked and remembered, and suppose something else grows in the vacancy, seeded from wherever forgotten things go. [478] The godwit, flying the old line out of habit, had passed over the vacancy and eaten what it found there. [498]

She planted one black seed in a cup of shingle and set it under the amethyst. [514] It came up in four days as a thin stalk hung with pale bells, and inside each bell, small as a fish's eye, was a piece of road. [542] Gravel, a gate-latch, the smell of tar and hawthorn. [551] Mirren laughed until the geode rang, then cried, and the crater glass wrote both down with the same stroke, because in her ledger joy and mourning had always shared a letter. [582]

She understood then what her nine hundred pages were: not a record of the drowned but a mooring rope for the world, and thin, and short. [608] The boy's lane could be saved if he walked it while the bells still held it, before memory faded past recovery. [629]

She wrapped the stalk in wet linen, filled a flask, and stopped at the door. [644] On the shelf sat the two remaining black seeds. [652] One for the boy's road. [657] One, she thought, for a turning above a village she could no longer picture, though she could still smell its bread. [678] She wrote her own name in the ledger at last, first line of a fresh page, in the knotted glyph the glass gave for hope, which was nearly identical to the one for dread. [712] Then she went out, singing something dreadful and jaunty, to walk both roads back into the world. [729]
