The Bureau of Fortune measured luck in cracks. [8] Every laborer on the Ashgrove estate carried a glass sphere the size of a plum stone, and each time luck was spent the glass took a hairline fracture; when the sphere clouded over entirely, the laborer was let go as a liability. [50] Ovid Marsh, the estate's withered vine dresser, had been let go three times in ten years and had never once left. [71] He was seventy-one, bent as a pruned cordon, and wisely pragmatic about metaphysics: he did not argue with the Bureau, he simply mended what it broke. [97]

The mending was the crime. [102] It began with his marbles. [107] Long before luck had a unit of measure, Ovid had kept a collection of marbles, cracked, one for every small victory in the maze. [131] The garden maze at Ashgrove ran nine hedged rings deep, yew and hornbeam and, along the innermost wall, a two-hundred-year-old grapevine that no one else could coax into fruit. [160] When a graft took, when a frost passed over the espaliered pears, when a canker retreated, Ovid commemorated the victory elaborately: he rang the brass bell at the gate, poured two thimbles of cider, one for himself and one for the roots, spoke the vine's name aloud, and then tapped a marble with the heel of his trowel so that a star bloomed in the glass. [226] Each crack was a triumph, not a loss. [234] That was the whole of his theology, and it fit his hand better than the Bureau's. [250]

The Bureau's spheres, conveniently, looked exactly like marbles. [258] So for ten years Ovid had run a quiet fraud. [268] Each audit eve he boiled resin from his own prunings, filled the fractures in his official sphere, and set it in gravel to cure; at dawn he would polish it with lambswool until it shone like new luck. [306] Three times an assessor had held his sphere to the light, found it clear, and struck the dismissal from the ledger. [327]

The fourth assessor was a young woman named Wren, and she came in October, when the maze was doing both things at once. [350] The outer rings were withering, hornbeam going the color of weak tea, while the inner rings put out a late absurd flush of roses, blooming as though nothing had been arranged about winter. [383] Wren entered at the gate with her tally-scale and did not come out for two hours. [399] Ovid found her in the fifth ring, turned around twice, her boots gray with yew dust. [415]

"The map is wrong," she said. [421]

"The map is old," he said. [427] "I moved the sixth turning nine years ago, when the beech died." [439]

"You moved it and told nobody?" [445]

"I told the garden," Ovid said, and led her out by the shortest way, past a hedge that seemed to open where he expected it. [470]

In the potting shed she asked for his sphere. [479] He gave her the mended one, and then, because he was seventy-one and tired of dawn polishing, he gave her the jar of marbles too. [504]

"Resin," he said, tapping the sphere. [510] "Grape prunings, boiled down." [514] "Nine fractures under there." [518] "By your arithmetic I have been unlucky since the year the beech died, and by your rules I should have been off the estate before you learned to read a ledger." [549]

Wren turned the sphere, and the gold line of resin caught the lamp. [562] "Why confess now?" [565]

"Because you were lost and I was not," he said. [575] "That is my whole argument." [580] "Gardens know their gardeners." [584] "Your glass measures what happens to a man." [592] "It cannot measure what a hedge does when it hears his step." [604]

He set a bowl of grapes on the bench, black and heavy, from a vine two centuries old that had been declared barren by three previous assessors. [631] Wren ate one. [634] Then she wrote in her ledger, slowly, that the Ashgrove sphere had been tampered with, and that the tampering was hereby entered as evidence of yield rather than deficit, the vine dresser's fortune being demonstrably located outside his person and inside nine rings of hedge. [679] It was a deception mended into a truth, which is the only honest way to keep one. [696] She kept the sphere for the Bureau's cabinet, and left him the marbles. [709]

That evening Ovid rang the brass bell at the gate, poured two thimbles of cider, one for himself and one for the roots, named the audit aloud as though it were a graft that had taken, and tapped a fresh marble with his trowel until a small star bloomed inside it. [760] The outer hedges went on withering; the roses went on blooming; he polished the new crack until it shone, and set it with the others. [785]
