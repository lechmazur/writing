The Coghouse turned all night on its pontoons, a workshop of gears and cogs afloat on an acid sea, and Ilma Vess could not decide whether she was a healer or a liar. [33] Below the grating, the Verdigris chewed at the stilts with a patient green appetite. [47] It had eaten Anselm four hundred years ago: streets, archives, hymnals, the names of everyone's grandmothers. [63] What the sea did not dissolve it pitted, and what it pitted it forgot. [77] On her bench lay eleven shards of Saint Kell, dredged up last winter by salvagers who had wanted coin and gotten prophecy instead. [100] The statue was centuries old and faceless now, its carved robes sanded to blank stone. [115] Ilma was an alchemist of the mended sort, and mending has one law: gold in the seam, and true intention in the gold. [138] Kintsugi intentions could not be faked; a wish poured crooked would set crooked, and the relic would narrate nothing but static and grief. [161] Mended honestly, an object spoke aloud, narrating its whole life in order to anyone standing near. [177] That was why she had taken the commission: to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion, one seam at a time. [198] Anselm's descendants lived on rafts and remembered nothing. [206] "She quilts memories together," the raft-folk said of her, half in hope, half in warning. [221] The trouble was that eleven shards make ten thousand wrong arrangements, and the carving offered no guide. [238] She found her guide in the damage itself, by matching the pockmarks on the centuries-old statue: the acid had drilled its pits before the saint broke, so the pit-fields ran across the fractures like constellations cut in half. [276] Align two half-constellations and you have found a true joint. [286] Ten nights she matched them, and on the eleventh she saw the flaw she had been dreading. [303] A chip was missing from the saint's throat, no bigger than a thumbnail, and without a throat the statue would never speak. [325] The gold would only set in the one interval the craft allowed: between breaths held underwater, when the world hesitates and intention has nowhere to hide. [351] So she filled the neutralizing tank, lowered the shards, drew breath, and sank. [364] Down there the Coghouse ticked through the water like a heart heard from inside a chest. [380] Ten seams took ten held breaths, and each one cost her something she had to mean. [396] For the throat she had nothing, until she remembered the key ring in her apron. [411] On it hung a key ring charm, a tin quail her sister Doro had carried down into the Verdigris the day the salvage line parted. [436] The acid had pitted the quail too, and its pits, Ilma saw with a cold, ringing clarity, matched the pockmarks at the saint's broken throat. [461] The sea signs its work the same way in every century; that was the terrible mercy of it. [479] Here was her uncertainty, whole and awake: to give the quail was to hand Doro's last warmth into a stranger's throat, and she did not know if that was mending or theft. [511] She held her last breath and sank anyway, because uncertainty is not the same as refusal. [527] In the green dark she pressed the charm into the gap, laid the gold, and quilted the memories together: Doro's, hers, the drowned city's, one seam. [553] I want it to mean something, she thought, which is the only intention the gold has ever accepted. [571] Then her lungs failed her and she came up coughing into the lamplight. [584] The saint stood on the bench, veined with gold like a map of rivers, and it began to narrate. [603] It spoke in Doro's voice. [608] It gave the founding, the four hundred sinking years, the name of every grandmother the sea had swallowed, and then it said, in the small dry tone Doro used for jokes, that the quail had always wanted a better job. [648] Outside, the raft-folk gathered along the gunwales to listen, three hundred faces lit acid-green, learning who they were. [666] Ilma sat on the wet grating with the empty key ring in her fist and did not stop the sound. [686] The Verdigris went on chewing the stilts, patient as ever, and for the first time she was not uncertain about what she was for. [710]
