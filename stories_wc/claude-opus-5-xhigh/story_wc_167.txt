Mira Oyelade had been the harbor glazier of Pearl Bay for nineteen years, and she still cut every pane as though someone she loved would be looking through it. [29] The deed lay on her bench beneath a lump of leaded scrap, waiting for her signature, and it was written in the smooth municipal language she had long ago learned to distrust. [61] Mira read between lines the way other people read weather; the clause about "structural rationalization" meant the prism atrium would come down before the dredgers arrived. [87] The atrium stood at the harbor's blunt end, an eight-sided glass room her teacher Teodor had raised from ship salvage, and at dawn it filled with mist that the panes broke into slow, drifting color. [122] Nobody in the council could say what it was for. [131] That, Teodor used to say, was precisely its function. [140]

Pearl Bay kept its accounts twice a year, at the noctilucent reckonings, when the high summer clouds burned silver past midnight and the fleet masters tallied what the town would keep and what it would let go. [177] This year the ledger had a hole in it the size of a harbor, because the channel was silting and the dredging would cost everything the town had not already spent. [208] Councilwoman Ferris had been kind about it, which was how Mira knew the decision was already made. [225] Glass is sentiment, Ferris had said; depth is survival. [234]

So on the last night before everything shifted, Mira walked up the shore path to the freshwater pond behind the chandlery, where Teodor had taught her to commune. [262] It was not a mystical practice, or not only that. [272] You threw a stone, and you read the patterns in the rings, and in the way the circles crossed and doubled back off the reeds you saw what your own thinking had been doing all week without telling you. [311] Above her the noctilucent clouds came out, pale as the inside of a shell, and she threw. [328] The first ring went wide and clean, and the second and third came slower behind it, and where they struck the bank they returned, and the returning rings interfered with the outgoing ones until the whole pond was a lattice. [368] Mira watched a long time. [373] She had been arguing the atrium as beauty, and beauty loses to depth every time; the rings told her she had been throwing her stone at the wrong part of the pond. [405]

By three in the morning she was back in the atrium with her cutter and her lead, and she did not make anything grand. [429] She reset nine panes, changing only their angles, so that the low first light would not spill uselessly across the floor. [450] Then she carried in the ordinary things: a net half-mended, a crate of gutted herring, the ledger itself, a loaf, a coil of tarred rope, her own cracked whetstone. [479]

At dawn she sent the harbor boy to fetch the council. [490] Pearl Bay at dawn is a bowl of milk with boats in it, and the mist came off the water and into the atrium as it always did. [518] Then the sun found her nine new angles. [526] Light came through the mist in defined columns and lay down deliberately, one bar on the mended net so that every knot threw a shadow twice its size, one on the herring so the scales went from grey to bronze, one across the open ledger, where the numbers stood up black and legible. [579] Nobody said anything for a while. [584]

"That is the dredging figure," Mira said, pointing at the lit page. [596] "It is correct, and I am not arguing with it." [606] "I am telling you what this room is for, and it is not sentiment: it is the only place in town where you can see the work." [633]

Ferris crouched by the crate of herring for a long moment, the way a woman does when she is recalculating. [653] She was a practical person, and Mira had finally spoken to her in the only language she trusted: a return on light. [675] The atrium was not saved that morning, exactly. [683] It was moved, pane by numbered pane, to the head of the new dredged channel, where the fleet would pass it going out and coming back, and where Ferris insisted the reckonings be held from then on, in glass, over the ledger, at dawn. [727] Mira signed the deed with the amendment written in her own plain hand, and she thought of Teodor, and of stones, and of how a ring does not stop at the bank. [759] It comes back through you, complicated and clear. [767]
