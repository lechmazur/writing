Corwin Ashe sold maps of places that had changed their minds. [11] His shop sat above the creek in Hollis Bend, and for eleven years it had been the quietest business on the street. [33] Customers came twice a month; the ants came daily. [42] Nella had loved the ants. [47] She used to say that Corwin was empirically intuitive, which was her polite way of noting that he measured everything twice and then did whatever his ribs told him. [76] She had also left him a promise, unpaid, three summers old. [87] "When the creek rises at midsummer," she had said, "I'll take you to the blooming cavern, and you'll finally stop hunting for the doorway of decisions on paper." [115] The doorway of decisions was Corwin's private obsession, a phrase he had found inked on a 1790s surveyor's chart of the valley, beside a spur of limestone nobody had mapped since. [146] The surveyor had drawn a small glyph there: a letter that existed in no alphabet Corwin knew. [163] Nella, who had worked her whole life in her father's type foundry, had recognized it in half a second. [182] She brought him a matrix chip from the foundry's discard bin, a thumb-sized brass block with the same letter sunk into its face, and told him it was a private mark her grandfather cut for a client who mapped caves. [222] Then she died in February, and the promise stayed in the shop like a draft under a door. [240]

This midsummer, the creek rose. [245] Corwin closed early, put the matrix chip in his coat pocket, and walked out to the limestone spur with the surveyor's chart rolled under his arm. [271] He had come here nine times before and found only briars, seeps, and the polite indifference of rock. [289] The tenth time, he stopped measuring and watched the ants. [299] They were streaming up the slope in a thread, each one carrying a crumb of pale grain, and they were not going toward the water like everything else in a rising valley. [331] Nella's rule for finding anything underground had been simple: when the creek rises, follow the ants home, because ants keep drier houses than surveyors. [355] So he followed them. [359] The thread crossed a fallen beech, turned twice against the grain of the hill, and slid into a crack behind a curtain of wild grape that Corwin had walked past nine times. [391] The crack breathed cold air on his face. [398]

Inside, past a squeeze that cost him a shirt button and most of his dignity, the passage opened. [416] The cavern was blooming. [420] Midsummer water, pushed up through the limestone by the swollen creek, had filled a hundred shallow basins, and out of them grew crystals in flat white petals, opening as the level climbed. [452] Where his lamp touched them they gave back a slow glimmer, passing it basin to basin until the whole chamber seemed to be thinking. [476] At the far wall, the passage split in two. [485] Between the openings, cut into the stone at the height of a kneeling man, was the letter from the chart. [505] Corwin took out the matrix chip and held it beside the carving. [517] Same hand, same slight lean, the same odd hook on the crossbar: a caver's signature, cast in brass by a foundry man for the friend who went into the dark. [547]

This was the doorway of decisions: not an oracle, not a threshold to anywhere else, just the place where a surveyor two centuries ago had to choose left or right and wanted the choice remembered. [582] Corwin laughed, and the glimmer answered him from every basin. [592] He had spent three years believing Nella meant to give him an answer. [605] She had only meant to show him that a decision was a room you could stand in, briefly, with light coming off the walls. [629]

The water was still rising; it was already over his boot soles. [641] He did not take either passage. [647] He set the matrix chip on the ledge beneath the carving, letter out, where the next person following ants home would find it, and he went back through the squeeze while there was still air to squeeze through. [685] Above, the valley smelled of wet hay and the creek was loud and brown. [699]

In the morning he drew a new map, the first he had made instead of sold, and marked the crack behind the grape with a letter of his own invention. [729] He hung it in the window facing the street, where people walking to the water could see it, and then he propped the shop door open. [755] The promise had been kept, he understood; he had simply arrived late. [767] By noon a stranger stood in the doorway, pointing at the window and asking what the letter meant. [785]
