The giant napped four times a year, and the city of Anverath did its living in the gaps. [18] Mira Vell, natural history illustrator, poled her flat skiff into the rooftop canal system that wove between the towers, her portfolio strapped flat against her spine. [44] Beneath her the aqueducts hummed; above her, the sky was the color of wet slate. [59] In the bow sat a wicker cage, and inside it, silent, the last echo-thrush. [73] Her sister Nell had raised the bird from a fledgling before the flood took her under the Low Locks, and it had not sung since. [98] Mira had spent eleven years drawing the giant: the moss-scaled knuckles, the ear canal where herons nested, the great slow bellows of its ribs. [122] Natural history was patience, and patience had taught her the giant's breathing was a phrase of music that never finished. [142] Each nap was five breaths climbing toward resolution and a sixth that swerved aside, an interrupted cadence, and the unfinished chord was what kept the giant sleeping. [169] When the phrase at last resolved, the giant would stand, and the canals would empty down the towers like poured wine. [190] She had until then. [194] She meant to find the place where echoes are born. [204] Old lockkeepers called it the Cistern, the loop at the canal system's heart where every sound the city ever made came home to be re-spoken. [229] If Nell's voice survived anywhere, it survived there. [237] Around Mira's neck hung the lockkeeper's fee: a brass infinity symbol pendant, warm as a held coin. [254] It sculpts time itself, the old woman had said, pressing it into her palm, but only by borrowing time - every minute you stretch here is a minute taken out of your later. [287] Mira had no particular use for her later. [295] She poled north across the giant's nap, hour after hour, past dye-works roofs where the water ran violet and bridge-markets folding their awnings. [318] Twice she thumbed the pendant when the light thinned, and twice the afternoon widened around her like a lens, the sun hanging stubborn above the tower-tops while her own knuckles went papery and knobbed. [352] She drew as she traveled, because that was how she thought. [363] Plate ninety-one, the thrush, drawn from life with its beak shut. [374] The Cistern announced itself before she saw it. [382] The canals here stopped weaving and began to loop, two great rings crossing at a single lock - the same shape as the pendant, she realized, or the pendant the same shape as this. [416] Water ran the figure eight forever, and in the crossing place the giant's held breath came up through the stone as a chord with its top note missing. [444] Every echo in Anverath was born in that missing note - a sound leaning forever toward an ending it was never given. [466] Mira tied off the skiff and listened. [473] Fishmongers, bell-ropes, a boy counting stairs, her own pole knocking the gunwale an hour ago - and then, thin and unmistakable, Nell calling her name up the Low Locks, the way she had before the water closed. [510] It repeated, and it would always repeat, because nothing here was ever allowed to finish. [525] The thrush stirred. [528] Mira understood then what the bird had been waiting eleven years to hear: not a beginning, but the note that would end the phrase. [552] To uncage it was to give the chord its resolution: the echoes would be released, Nell's voice among them, spoken once and never again. [576] And the giant would wake. [581] She thumbed the pendant one last time and bought herself a long, borrowed hour, paying it out of whatever years she had left, and she spent every minute of it drawing. [612] Plate one hundred: the Cistern, the double ring, the water going around and around with nowhere to arrive. [630] Then she opened the cage. [635] The thrush went up in a shatter of wings and sang one note, and the chord closed around it like a hand. [657] All of Anverath spoke at once - every bell, every argument, every drowned girl calling - and then, for the first time in the city's history, was quiet. [685] Beneath the towers, something enormous took a sixth breath and let it go. [698] The canals began to drain. [703] Mira sat in her emptying skiff with her portfolio dry against her spine, old now, out of borrowed time, listening to the silence that meant the phrase was finished at last. [734] She had one page left, and she did not draw the giant standing; she drew the empty cage, and left the last line open. [758]
