The orchard had been dead for nine winters, and Sena had knelt in it for eleven hours. [17] Frost had grown up over her knees like a second pair of trousers. [30] She did not brush it off; brushing cost motion, and motion cost heat, and she had budgeted both to the gram. [51] The crater walls rose black around her, a mile of old volcano holding the cold in its cup, and above them the sky had begun to close. [78] Occultation night. [80] For one hundred and forty seconds, the dark body would pass across the great star, and the world would hold its breath: no wind in the dead branches, no dogs, no smoke rising straight or crooked. [116] Sena needed that stillness the way a surgeon needs a steady table. [128] She was a flash-paper alchemist, the last one licensed in the northern districts, and her trade was paper that burned to nothing: no ash, no smoke, no residue to smudge a message or a sky. [163] In her satchel lay ten envelopes of it, each one holding a thimble of safekept starlight: light gathered in mirrors during the good years, folded, fed, and kept quiet in the dark. [195] Starlight will keep for a century if you seal it properly. [206] She had sealed each envelope herself, with wax and with the only die she owned: a storybook illustration plate, copper, engraved with a girl asleep under an apple tree. [235] The plate had come from her father's print shop, which was now under forty feet of ice at the crater's east rim, along with the print shop's street and the street's town. [267] Between the withered trees she had spent the whole short day laying out patterns from rubbish. [283] Bottle caps, bent nails, the bowls of broken spoons, tin lids, a hundred fragments of window glass, all of it set edge up in the snow so that each piece would catch light and throw it. [319] The patterns were not decoration. [324] They were hearts. [327] For three weeks Sena had gone down into the cellars with her stethoscope and listened to the sleepers who remained: forty-one of them, in nine shelters, breathing slow under the frozen orchard. [359] She had counted every pulse and written it down, and then she had done the harder thing, which was to translate heartbeats into maps. [383] A fast heart became a tight spiral of caps; a slow, strong one, a long line of glass with wide gaps; a child's flutter, a ring of nails no wider than a hat. [416] Rhythm made into shape; shape laid where the body lay. [426] The relief flyers came over twice a year and saw only white. [438] This time they would see forty-one hearts beating in light, and they would know which drifts to dig, and in what order. [460] The star narrowed to a blade, then to a bead. [470] The wind lay down in the branches and stopped. [479] Sena rose, and her knees cracked like the ice they had become, and she did not hurry, because hurry ruins geometry. [500] She walked the rows and set each envelope at the center of its pattern, and she broke the seals in order, oldest sleeper first. [524] The flash paper took the cold air and went, each sheet a single soundless bloom, and the starlight it had held for a hundred years stood up out of the snow. [555] No smoke; that was the whole point of her trade. [565] Glass and tin and nails caught it and passed it along, and the patterns filled, one after another, until the dead orchard lay covered in slow bright figures, pulsing at the rates of the people beneath them. [602] Nothing moved but the light. [607] The tenth envelope was for herself. [613] She had no shelter and no one to listen to her chest, so she had felt her own pulse at the wrist for an hour and laid its pattern last, out in the open where she stood. [650] For its center piece, she had nothing left but the copper plate. [662] She looked at the girl asleep under the apple tree, the picture her father had printed ten thousand times, and then she set it face up in the snow and lit the last envelope over it. [698] The copper took the starlight and held it and gave it back, and for a hundred and forty seconds the orchard had one bright orchard inside it, a girl and a tree, printed in light on the floor of a crater. [739] Then the star came out from behind the dark body, and the world breathed, and the wind moved in the branches again. [761] High above the rim, a small engine changed its pitch and began to circle. [775] Sena knelt again in her own pattern and waited without moving, the one thing she had always been good at. [795]
