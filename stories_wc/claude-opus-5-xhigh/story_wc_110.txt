The rain stopped at the third bell, and the forecourt began to whisper. [13] Mira Vance, the quiet night registrar of the Hall of Ledgers, waited under the arch until the sound settled into its usual resonant hush. [36] Wet quartz did that: it held the day's voices in its seams and let them out slowly, like heat from a stone. [58] That was why nobody spoke anymore. [63] In the years after words became unnecessary, the town of Cantrell learned to trade in ink instead. [80] They wrote on the only paper the flood had spared, three crates of tickets for a concert that never happened, and because the tickets were small, the writing became cryptic doodles: a bird for a promise, a broken circle for a debt, three dots for I am afraid. [128] Mira filed them all. [132] It was her office to receive each ticket at the night window, stamp it, and shelve it in the dark where no lamp could fade the ink. [159] It was not her office to read them. [167] She read them anyway, with a lens she had pried from the ruined projector in the concert hall, a thick disc of glass that made the smallest doodle swell into something ambiguously clear. [200] Under the lens, a mark was always legible and never certain. [211] A crescent could be a moon, a scythe, a woman turning her face away. [225] She also eavesdropped, which was worse. [231] After every rain she knelt on the forecourt and put her ear to the crystal, and the stone gave back fragments of the day: a cough, a name, Councilor Halloran's boots, the small dry sound of a man laughing at someone. [272] She told herself she listened to protect the ledger's honesty. [282] By midwinter the town believed the registrar sold secrets. [291] The doodles told her so. [296] A ticket came through the window bearing an eye inside a padlock; another showed a hand with a coin in it and, beneath, the tower of the Hall. [324] Halloran's own ticket, filed that same week, drew the Hall with its windows crossed out. [339] Mistrust in a silent town has no argument to break it; it simply thickens, like ice from the edges in. [359] Mira understood that ice thaws only from the outside in, under light, and that she could not answer suspicion with more secrecy. [381] If mistrust was ice, she would thaw it with glass. [391] She set the projector lens in a tin collar, put a whale-oil flame behind it, and aimed the whole contraption at the north wall of the forecourt, which after rain shone like a page held up to a window. [430] Then she rang the third bell herself, twice, which in Cantrell meant come and see. [445] They came in the drizzle-end darkness, thirty, then eighty, standing in that resonant hush the wet crystal made, their breath collecting under the arches. [469] Mira did not speak, because words had been unnecessary for eleven years and she had no wish to seem to plead. [490] She slid a ticket into the beam. [497] Her own. [499] On the wall, twenty feet high, appeared an ear pressed to a floor, and beside it a lens, and beneath both a broken circle: I listened, I magnified, I owe. [529] The hush changed shape. [533] She fed the beam a second ticket, then a third, and did not stop: every doodle she had ever read in secret, blown huge and trembling on the crystal for its author and its subject and every neighbor to see at once. [575] The eye in the padlock. [580] The hand with the coin. [585] Halloran's crossed-out windows. [588] Nothing was hidden, including her sin of knowing. [596] The lens did what it always did, enlarging without deciding, so the crowd had to agree together what a crescent meant, and to agree they had to lean close. [625] Halloran pushed forward with his stick and stood in the beam, his shadow swallowing his own accusation. [642] He held up a fresh ticket, and Mira slid it into the light: a bird, and under the bird, the Hall with its windows open. [667] A promise, transparent, in front of eighty witnesses. [675] Others began to pass their tickets forward. [682] By the fourth bell the wall was a moving crowd of birds and circles and dots, and the crystal underfoot, warmed by so many bodies, gave back the day's murmurs, so that the forecourt whispered and shone at once. [721] Nobody eavesdropped, because there was nothing left to overhear. [730] Mira felt the thing she had not expected: not forgiveness, exactly, but weightlessness, the plain relief of a woman freed by her own honesty. [754] Near dawn, Halloran turned to her, worked his jaw, and said aloud, in a cracked and rusty voice, 'Thank you.' [774] The forecourt caught it, and held it, and gave it back all morning to anyone who knelt to listen. [793]
