The tea house floated because the rock beneath it wanted to, and nobody in the valley had ever asked it for a better reason. [24] I climbed the rope stair with my hand pressed to my sternum, where the wire and the scar still argued. [44] Six weeks after receiving the donor heart, I had begun to suspect I was carrying somebody else's appetite for the world. [65] Mist moved through the floor slats like something browsing. [74] Ona Reeve, the watchful dye master, did not look up when I sat down. [88] She was watching a slat of sunlight crawl across her table toward a shallow bowl of water. [105] "Wait," she said, "it will illuminate in a minute, and then you may ask." [119] In the bowl lay a single blue morpho wing, colorless as ash in the shade. [134] The sunlight reached it and the wing detonated into blue. [144] Not painted blue, but a blue shouting from inside the water, so bright that the mist above the bowl went the color of a held breath. [170] "There is no pigment in that wing," she said, "none at all." [182] "The color is architecture." [186] "Scales stacked so exactly that they cancel every wavelength but the one that stops a bird mid-dive." [203]

I had come up the valley for exactly this: iridescent caution, the thing she was famous for teaching. [221] Her dyes did not warn with skulls or red bands. [231] They warned the way the morpho warns, by being too beautiful to approach without thinking. [246] "You want something," she said, "so sit closer to the vat, since I have to watch it anyway." [264] The indigo vat was quietly explosive: a fermentation that could turn to acid ruin in an hour if the sun angle changed and cooled it. [289] That was why she worked by tracking sun trails. [298] The rock turned slowly on its own axis, one lazy rotation a day, so the light came in from a different window every hour. [322] She had chalked the paths on the floorboards over forty years, pale arcs like the ribs of some enormous fish. [342]

I told her about the surgery. [348] I told her the name of my donor, because everyone in three valleys knew it: Sennen Kadge, who had walked into a flooded mine and come out carrying two men, who had lost a boat and a fortune, whose funeral had needed a hillside. [392] "I am a tax clerk," I said, "and nothing has ever happened to me." [406] "I came to learn why some people magnetize stories and some of us stand three feet away, holding their coats." [426]

Ona dipped a skein into the vat, and it came out green, ugly, wrong. [440] She hung it in the sun trail and said nothing for a long time. [454] The mist rose from the gorge below and wandered among the tables, and two farmers upstairs laughed at something, and the yarn began, very slowly, to turn blue. [482] "Air does that," she said. [487] "Green is the dye's real color." [493] "Blue is only what happens when it meets the world." [503]

"Sennen Kadge came here twice," she added. [510] "He was frightened of water his whole life." [518] "He went into that mine because he could not think of a way to stand still, and standing still is most of what a life is." [544] "Stories collect on people who cannot stop moving." [552] "That is not a virtue; it is a structure, like the scales on that wing." [567] She turned the bowl so the wing went gray again, then bright, then gray. [581] "Iridescent caution," she said. [585] "Admire the flash, but do not envy the wing." [594] "It is dead, and it is still the loveliest thing in this house." [607]

I stayed until the rock had turned enough to put the sun on the western tables. [623] She taught me to read the chalk arcs, and to test the vat with the back of my wrist, and to keep my own heart's borrowed engine steady while I waited for a color to finish deciding. [660] Going down the rope stair at dusk, I understood that I had come up asking to be magnetic and was going down having been given something quieter: a trail to track, a vat to watch. [695] The valley filled with mist the color of that first green. [706] Inside me, Sennen's heart kept up its restless knocking, and I let it knock. [720] Somewhere above me the tea house lamps came on and illuminated nothing but themselves, floating, patient, and enough. [738]
