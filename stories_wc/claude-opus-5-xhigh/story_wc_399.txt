Sefra was a half-masked line walker: the mask covered the right side of her face, because the sand only tells the truth to an eye that is not trying to see it. [32] Beneath the dunes ran the kinetic parapet, a wall of jointed stone that shifted as the desert breathed, and she read it through peripheral awareness alone. [58] Look straight at a line and it locks; glance past it and it sings. [72] Where the parapet rose, the sand fled, and a clearing opened: a floor of swept slate, walls of standing wind. [92] These were the conjured clearings, and in them the desert went quiet enough for words to be kept. [110] The oldest of them had been consecrated nine hundred years ago and roofed over, and it was called the murmuring pavilion. [131] Sefra had walked its perimeter every dawn since she was eleven, listening to it hold the voices of the dead. [151] Under the dome, every sentence ever spoken inside still turned, thinned to a murmur, layered like silt. [168] You could not make out single words anymore, only the shape of counsel: patience, mercy, do not dig where the line is thin. [191] That shape was the whole inheritance of her people, and her work was to preserve the echo of wisdom, nothing more. [212]

The trouble began after the prophet arrived. [219] She came in from the salt flats on foot, unmasked, both eyes bare, which meant she was either holy or a fool. [241] Law was simple: a prophet's words must be consecrated in a clearing of their own, raised fresh from the parapet. [261] But the parapet had only so much motion in it, and every new clearing borrowed strength from the stretch that held up the pavilion's dome. [286] Raise one, and the dome would settle a finger's width, and a century of murmurs would be pressed out of it like breath from a lung. [312]

Sefra took the prophet walking along the buried line, both of them silent, the wind moving in that low whisperfield calm the dunes keep at midday. [338] "What will you say," Sefra asked at last, "that is worth a hundred years of our grandmothers?" [355]

The prophet was strangely honest. [360] She did not claim a vision, or a flood, or a god at her shoulder. [375] "Nothing," she said. [378] "I have walked eleven hundred miles to ask a question, and I do not have an answer to trade for it." [399]

Sefra kept her bare eye on the horizon and let the woman sit at the edge of her sight, where truth lives. [421] There she saw no tremor of performance, no rehearsed grief. [431] "Ask it," she said. [435]

"Your pavilion holds nine hundred years of counsel," the prophet said, "and none of it can be understood. [453] Why do you keep it?" [458]

Sefra had no answer either, and that was the first honest thing she had felt in years. [475]

That night she walked the line by feel, mask toward the dunes, and found a hollow where the parapet lay shallow and eager. [498] She asked it up: not a hall, not a court, only a clearing the width of two seated women. [517] Slate rose. [519] Sand fled. [521] Far off, she heard the dome settle its finger's width. [531] What it pressed out was the newest silt, the top hundred years: the caretakers' murmurs, her own included, all of them repeating counsel they had never once questioned. [559] She let them go the way you let go of an echo of an echo. [574]

At dawn she consecrated the small clearing with sand and water and the old words, and then she sat the prophet down and had her ask the question again, aloud, so the slate could keep it. [610] Nine hundred years of answers had gone soft as felt; a question would stay sharp, because anyone who heard it would have to work. [634] That, Sefra decided, was how you preserve the echo of wisdom: not by hoarding the sound, but by keeping something alive that makes people listen. [659]

The prophet walked on west that afternoon, still unmasked, still with nothing to sell. [673] Sefra went back to her line, and by evening she could hear, at the very edge of hearing, a new murmur turning under the little roofless dome of wind: why do you keep it, why do you keep it, why. [713] She let it sit in the corner of her attention, where the desert puts everything it means. [730]
