Teodor Vance had been a stray philosopher for eleven years, which is to say he asked questions that no city would pay to hear. [24] He slept in the mechanical rainforest, under brass poplars whose leaves had been hammered thin as moth wings. [42] He woke, most mornings, nowhere near the place he had lain down. [54] This had started the night the sixth sense awakened, and he had never learned to be sorry about it. [73] The sixth sense arrived the way weather arrives: everywhere, unevenly, without permission. [85] Afterward, people could feel each other thinking, and the feeling had a sound - the hum of insight, a low golden chord that answered your question before you had finished shaping it. [116] Cities loved it. [119] Nobody guessed wrong anymore, and nobody wandered, and within a year no one could remember the last thought they had grown themselves. [141] Teodor wanted to rediscover the wild frontier of his own mind, and so he walked out into the only wilderness left, which was manufactured. [165]

The rainforest had been built by engineers who wanted a lung for the world and then, satisfied, went home to die. [186] It had kept growing without them: copper ferns, iron bamboo, glass willows, canopy pumps sweating warm rain onto oiled soil. [206] In there the hum was loudest, and it jammed his sense of direction as thoroughly as fog jams a lamp. [226] So he navigated by the sound of wind through different trees. [237] Brass poplars rang like small bells struck with felt. [246] Iron bamboo clicked in triplets, a dry applause. [254] Glass willows sighed, and the sigh went flat where the ground turned to plate steel. [269] Eleven years of listening had taught him to hear a mile of forest the way a reader hears a sentence. [289]

He needed the skill, because his body traveled while he slept. [300] Not sleepwalking: going. [303] He would lie down beside a pumping station and wake in a gully of gear-fruit twenty miles east, dew on his coat, no footprints anywhere. [328] He had a theory, the way stray philosophers have theories, which is fondly and without evidence: sleep let the sixth sense carry him toward whatever question was hungriest. [356]

In his pocket he kept a small gear, thirteen teeth, brass gone green at the hub. [372] It had been in his fist the first morning after the sixth sense awakened, and he had never found the machine it belonged to. [396] He carried it as a philosopher carries a premise he cannot discard. [408]

In the eleventh year the wind changed. [414] He woke at dawn to a sound he had never catalogued: wind through leaves of woven cable, a sound like a held breath being let out slowly. [441] He followed it uphill for two days, eating rainwater and rust-sweet fruit, and found the heart tree. [458] It stood ninety feet of open ironwork, cables braided up its trunk like tendons, and the hum poured off it in visible shimmer. [481] The engineers had not built a lung, or not only a lung; they had built the thing that had awakened the sixth sense, and it had a governor at its base, and the governor was missing one small gear. [520] Thirteen teeth. [522]

He sat down in the warm rain and let the arithmetic unravel him. [535] His sleeping self had come here on the first night, before there was any hum to follow, and had taken the gear, and the machine had been running ungoverned ever since, shouting its answers into every skull on earth. [574] He had spent eleven years hunting the frontier he himself had paved. [586] This is the trouble with probable miracles: given enough forest and enough sleep, the improbable is only patient. [604]

He could have smashed the governor. [609] Instead he cleaned the green off the hub with his thumb, knelt, and seated the small gear on its spindle. [629] The hum did not stop. [634] It unraveled - dropped from one golden chord into a million separate threads, each too faint to answer anybody, each just loud enough to be a question. [660] Across the world, that morning, people woke up not knowing things, and some of them wept, and some of them began, badly, to think. [684]

Teodor slept at the foot of the heart tree, and in the morning he was exactly where he had lain down. [705] The wind came through cable leaves and brass poplars and iron bamboo, three languages at once, and he understood none of it. [727] He lay there a long while, grinning like a man handed back his own unmapped country, and began to guess. [747]
