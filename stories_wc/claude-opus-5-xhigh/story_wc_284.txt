At the tilt of the earth toward spring, Mira Halloway climbed Zephyr Bluff to write her last letter in the snow. [21] The mist came off the water in slow silver sheets, and the whole headland lay in a hush so complete she could hear the drip of thaw under the drifts. [51] Three winters ago the service had burned her. [59] A courier died in Trieste, a cipher wheel went missing, and the file that explained both had her handwriting in the margins. [81] They had not shot her; they had simply stopped answering, which for a woman like Mira was a colder verdict. [101] So she had come here, to a caretaker's cottage above the fjord, and had kept the one protocol nobody had thought to revoke. [124] Codes in accumulated snow. [128] Her old handler, Anselm, had invented it in the war: tramp letters into the deep drifts of a north face, ten paces to a character, and let the mail plane read them at dawn. [162] No plane had answered in three years. [169] She wrote anyway, all winter, because the writing was the only thing left that was hers. [185]

In her coat pocket she carried an engraved pillbox, silver, thin as a communion wafer. [200] Anselm had given it to her the night she went across, with the capsule inside and two words cut into the lid: COURAGE KEEPS. [224] He meant that courage stores well, that it waits in the dark like preserves in a cellar until the year needs it. [246] She had never swallowed the capsule, and that unspent dose became the question that governed her: where does unused courage wait, and what is it for? [272]

This morning the answer was climbing the bluff behind her. [282] The girl was perhaps twenty-two, with a ferry ticket in her glove and mud to the knee, and she stopped at a prudent distance before she spoke. [309] "I have been reading your snow since November," she said. [319] "From the lighthouse across the water, with his old glass." [329] "I am Ilka Sund, and Anselm was my grandfather." [338]

Mira felt the hush go thinner, the way ice does. [348] She had spelled out coordinates, names, a whole indictment, in letters forty feet tall, and a girl with a spyglass had copied every one of them into a notebook. [377]

"Then you know the network still runs the Trieste route," Mira said. [389] "Two couriers a month into a channel the other side has been reading since before you could vote." [407]

"I know," Ilka said, "because I carry for them." [416]

That was the whole reason Mira had kept writing: not to clear her name, but to reach whoever was still walking into the trap. [440] Something was broken, and she was the only one who knew which piece. [453] She told the girl the name. [459] She had held it for three years, refusing to vouchsafe it to anyone who could not act on it, and she gave it now the way a surgeon gives a diagnosis: flatly, with the date, the office, the bank. [498] Then she pressed the pillbox into Ilka's glove. [506] The capsule was gone from it; in its place lay a strip of microfilm, curled like a watch spring. [525] "The engraving will get you through the door," Mira said. [535] "He gave the same box to four of us; show it in Bergen and they will believe the film." [554]

Ilka did not promise anything, which Mira liked; she only asked which hour was safest for the crossing, and then chose the earlier, riskier boat because it left before the harbor office opened. [587] Prudently daring: Anselm's phrase for the ones who lived. [596]

When the girl was gone down the path, Mira looked at her winter's work. [610] The thaw had already taken the first three characters; by evening the whole message would be grass and gravel, and no one flying over would ever know that a bluff had spoken. [642] It did not matter now. [647] The words had come off the snow into a notebook, and out of the notebook they would go into a room with a door and a lock and consequences. [676]

The zephyr came up warm off the water, moving the mist in long silver folds, and the bluff kept its hush. [697] Mira stood in it with empty pockets and understood, finally, where unused courage waits: not in a silver box, and not in the one who hoards it, but in the hands of whoever is young enough to spend it. [736] She went down to fix the cottage gutter, which had been broken since October. [750] There was a great deal of water coming, and she meant to be ready for it. [766]
