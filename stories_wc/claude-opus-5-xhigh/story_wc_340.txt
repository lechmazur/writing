The elevator in the icebound spire climbs ten floors, and Casimir has ten floors to decide what to tell the heir. [21] He is a servant, and today he is an ascending servant in both senses: the cage lifts him, and the household has voted him steward. [46] In his hands he carries the airship captain's goggles and the captain's diary, which nobody has ever read. [64] Floor one. [66] Captain Ilse Varga died eleven days ago, three thousand meters up, with frost on her lashes and her hand on the wheel, and Casimir, who served her tea for nineteen years, finds that he mourns her the way one mourns weather: thoroughly, and without expecting an answer. [113] Floor two. [115] The diary is written in a cipher of ink that shows nothing under lamplight, only faint gray scratches, like the tracks of a bird that thought better of landing. [144] Casimir has held it to candles, to the stove, to the blue glare of the glacier below, and learned nothing. [164] Floor three. [166] He was always warmly inquisitive about her, in the permitted manner of servants, noting which songs she hummed and which griefs sent her out to polish the brass rail until her palms blackened. [199] Floor four. [201] The goggles are the reason he understands, at last. [210] She wore them at altitude, and their lenses are not glass but stacked crystal, cut to break the white glare of cloud into ribbons. [234] The elevator lamp swings; light falls through the goggles and scatters across the page; and the gray scratches unfold into her handwriting, whole sentences blooming and fading as the ribbons move. [265] Only through scattered light will the cipher speak, which is exactly like her. [278] Floor five. [280] Third of Thaw, he reads: lost Petrov to the ice off the north face, and beneath that, the joy, she wrote, was that he laughed twice at my bad joke about the barometer on his last morning. [317] Floor six. [319] Every entry is built the same way: a wound, then a rummaging beneath the wound, then something small and warm dragged up out of it and pinned to the page like a specimen. [352] It was, he sees, a discipline; she had set herself to seek out the joy beneath anguish the way other captains set themselves to sound a channel, methodically, in bad weather, for the use of whoever came after. [390] Floor seven. [392] Wintering at the spire, the entry says, three of the crew frostbitten and the fuel rationed; the joy beneath is Casimir, who brings tea without being asked and never once asks me how I am, which is a mercy I cannot pay back. [435] He reads it three times before the ribbons slide off the page. [447] Floor eight. [449] Outside the cage, through the shaft's narrow slots, the cloud floor lies white and unbroken, and the spire's ice sheath groans as it always groans at this hour. [477] He does not weep, because he is not certain he is entitled to, and because he has an heir to meet in two floors. [501] Floor nine. [503] The last entry is nine words long, dated the morning of her final ascent: the joy beneath is that someone will decode this. [526] She left the goggles to him in her will; the household thought it a sentimental scrap, a trinket for the tea man. [548] It was an instruction. [552] Floor ten. [554] The doors open on the observation room, where the heir, nineteen and stiff in her mother's coat, stands waiting with the ledgers of an estate she does not want. [583] Casimir bows, sets down the diary, and puts the goggles into her hands. [596] Hold them to the window, he tells her, and turn them until the light breaks. [611] She does, and the pages unfold for her, and Casimir steps back to the tea service, which is where he has always done his loving, at a distance of one polite meter, warmly, without asking anything in return. [649] The girl reads, and her face does what faces do when anguish is shown its underside. [665] Behind her, the clouds go gold. [671] Steward, she says at last, without looking up, what was she like? [683] He pours two cups, because the answer will take a while, and because it is the sort of question he has been waiting nineteen years for someone to ask. [712]
