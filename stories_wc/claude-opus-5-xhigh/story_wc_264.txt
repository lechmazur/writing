After the last forest burns, the freight still runs. [9] Sable rides car nine, the one with no windows and no name stenciled on its flank. [25] She is the dreamless freight guard, hired for exactly that: a skull the cargo cannot get into. [42] The cargo is the divergent archive, and it dreams for a living. [54] Nine crates of cold lattice, each holding every version of the world that almost happened. [69] Sleepers who guard it wake up remembering harvests that never came, brothers who were never born, and by morning they have argued the branches into a single dull line. [98] Sable's head is a clean vault, so the archive stays plural inside it. [111] The Ministry of One Account pays her by the mile, and never asks how she sleeps without dreaming. [129] She sleeps like a stopped clock. [134] She carries one, actually: her mother's chess clock, frozen at midnight, the hour the last canopy went up in a sound like applause. [157] Her mother was an archivist before the Ministry decided that history should be a corridor and not a delta. [176] She taught Sable that the archive is held together through wave functions, that its crates are only a polite fiction, that the thing itself is occasionally everywhere. [203] Pin it down in one place, one story, and the rest of it dies quietly. [218] The second hand of the chess clock is the pin. [227] Start it, and the superposition collapses into whatever the Ministry has written down. [240]

On the fourth night the train slows at Kilometer Ash, and Sable slides the door open to look at the impossible thing. [262] A glowing dune in bloom. [267] Somewhere under the sand a seed vault cracked open decades ago, and now the whole slope is furred with pale luminous grass, thistles that hum, saplings the height of her knee throwing blue light on the ash. [304] The Ministry maps call it contamination. [310] Her mother called it proof that the world keeps spare copies of itself. [323] The engineer, Odell, walks back along the gravel with the delivery order crumpled in his fist. [339] "Terminus tomorrow," Odell says, "and they want the archive collapsed before unloading, one account, clean as a corridor." [357] Sable looks past him at the dune, at nine crates of unlit corners waiting to be told they never existed. [377] Then she does the only thing her mother ever taught her that was not physics. [392] She charms him. [395] Not with a spell, only with the old lullaby the archivists sang to keep the ordering of the shelves in their heads, the one that breaks in the middle and starts again wrong, deliberately, so that no two singers ever finish it the same way. [440] Odell's mother sang it too; every mother did, before the corridor. [451] He stands in the blue grass with his fist slowly opening and he does not stop her when she starts unbolting crate one. [474]

The archive is not a book. [480] It is a fog of maybes, and it wants a place to settle, and a stopped clock is a terrible place, and a Ministry gate is worse. [507] So Sable carries the crates out onto the glowing dune in bloom and opens them one by one against the humming thistles. [529] Light goes out of them like breath in cold air, into the roots, into the sand, into the unlit corners of the ash flats where nothing has been told what to become. [561] Every branch takes a seed; every seed takes a branch. [571] This is what she was always for, she thinks, not guarding but planting: to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners, where a delta can still open. [598]

At the end she takes out the chess clock and sets it on the sand, midnight still holding, and she does not press the plunger. [623] Let the hour the forests burned stay unfinished. [631] Let it be a move no one has answered yet. [641] The train leaves without its freight, and Odell will report a derailment, a fire, some corridor-shaped lie. [658] Sable stays on the slope until dawn, humming the broken half of the lullaby, waiting. [673] And that night, for the first time in her life, the dreamless freight guard dreams. [688] She dreams nine forests at once, all of them true, none of them decided. [702]
