They appointed me the end of history witness because I could not lie and could not hurry. [17] My only equipment was a softcover dictionary, swollen by rain, its spine soft as a moth's wing. [34] For eleven thousand years I recorded endings: the last war, the last harvest, the last child born under a sun that still bothered to rise. [59] Then the universe hiccuped. [63] Everything I am about to record happened across that hiccup, in a gap narrower than a heartbeat and wider than the eleven thousand years behind me. [89] It was not a collapse or a bang, only a stutter, a skipped syllable in the long sentence of things, and in that gap I was set down somewhere that should not have existed. [123] An ancient temple library, lost in time, its shelves curling outward in a spiral from a single black tile at the center. [145] The hush there was not empty; it resounded, the way a struck bell resounds after the sound has left it. [165] Two figures waited on the black tile. [172] They called themselves the Keeper of Causes and the Keeper of Consequences, and they had been arguing, they said, since before the first star condensed. [197] "You are the witness," said Causes, "so you will preside." [207] The procedure was spiral arbitration: the disputants walk the spiral outward from the center, and whatever they can still agree on when they reach the last shelf becomes law. [236] The trick, Consequences explained, is that you must journey inward while walking out. [249] Every step away from the center must be matched by a step toward your own worst honesty, or the spiral simply returns you to the tile, and you begin again. [279] I asked what they were disputing. [285] Causes said the universe was a machine, and grief in it was only friction. [299] Consequences said the universe was a story, and grief was its only proof of reading. [314] Both had been told, at their making, that they would be reconciled by someone who had watched an ending and taken notes. [336] I opened the dictionary. [340] It is a wanderingly exact book: it never gives you the word you want, but it gives you, eventually, the word that is true. [364] Look up machine and you are sent to make; look up story and you are sent, by a long detour through storey and stair, to make again. [391] We walked. [393] Outward past shelves of clay tablets, outward past scrolls, outward past books whose languages had not been invented in any century I had witnessed. [417] Inward, I went too. [421] I admitted, on the third turn, that I had not been a neutral witness. [435] I had loved a city and written its fall as though a stone had fallen, and the lie of that flatness had been my only comfort. [461] The spiral let me keep walking. [467] Causes confessed that he feared being blamed. [474] Consequences confessed that she feared being unnecessary. [481] Twice the tile pulled us back, because a confession offered as decoration is not a confession. [497] The third attempt held. [501] At the last shelf, my task was to unify their claims into one sentence that neither could deny, and I had the dictionary, and the dictionary had, as always, wandered somewhere exact. [533] Under illusion it did not say falsehood. [540] It said: a thing seen from only one side. [549] That was the whole of it, the thing I had come the length of a dying cosmos to unravel. [568] The illusions were never lies; they were profiles. [576] So I wrote, in the margin of the last page, the only law I could defend: the universe is a machine that means what it does, and a story that must be maintained. [609] Grief is the friction of being read. [616] Causes wept, which was friction; Consequences laughed, which was proof; the hush took both sounds and gave them back enlarged. [636] Then the hiccup finished. [640] The library folded back into whatever fold of time keeps it, and I was returned to my post at the end of history, on a cold plain, under a sun deciding whether to bother. [674] The dictionary was in my hands, still swollen, one page newly inked. [686] I am told endings need witnesses. [692] I have come to think they need translators more, and that the difference between the two is a spiral you can only walk outward, telling the truth the whole way in. [723] The sun rose. [726] I wrote it down. [730]
