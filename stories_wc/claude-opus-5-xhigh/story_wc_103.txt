They called me the hedge maze designer, though by then I had stopped designing anything a person could walk out of. [21] The commission came from the living bridge garden at Nongriat, where the ficus roots are trained across the gorge for two hundred years until they become a bridge, and then a garden, and then a habit the forest cannot break. [61] I was hired to weave a maze into the far bank: hornbeam, tea shrub, wild orange. [77] I accepted because of the halo fragment. [83] A boy pulled it from a root-knot the monsoon had split open, a curved shard of gilded copper, engraved with rays, snapped from some Portuguese saint who had drowned in the gorge three centuries before. [118] When I held it to my eye, I saw the saint's hands. [130] Then his boat. [133] Then the rain of 1698, quaking down through leaves that had not yet been born. [148]

You should understand that I have been retrocognitively trapped since childhood. [159] I do not see the future; I see nothing else but the past, pressing in at every angle, and I cannot get out. [182] A doorway holds every hand that ever touched it. [191] Most people call this memory and forgive it. [199] For me it is weather. [204] So I photographed everything, obsessively, to pin the past flat where it could not shake. [219]

That was the year the photographs began fading. [227] Not yellowing, not curling - emptying. [232] The bridge went first, then the boy, then my own hands at the edge of the frame, until I held rectangles of grey nothing that smelled faintly of fixer. [261] The halo fragment was drinking them. [267] I did not resent it. [272] I had spent forty years wanting one thing only: to discover the texture of time - not its length, which everyone measures, but its grain, its weave, whether it is felt or gauze or bark. [306] Photographs were only ever a bad hypothesis. [313] The maze was the better one. [319]

I built it through hyperbolic geometry. [325] On a flat lawn, a maze has a fixed acreage and a fixed number of choices, but a surface of constant negative curvature has more room the further you go from its center. [358] Roots are willing to be curved. [364] For eleven years I trained hornbeam and ficus over frames bent to saddle shapes, seven hedges meeting where six should fit, corridors that widened as they receded, angles that summed to less than they had any right to. [402] The gardeners of the living bridge garden understood me before the mathematicians did. [415] They had been growing a bridge for two centuries; they knew that a shape can be a long argument with a river. [437]

When the maze closed over itself, I walked in at dusk with the halo fragment in my fist. [455] Inside, the past had somewhere to spread. [462] That was the whole design: give retrocognition more surface than the present has, and it stops pressing. [479] The gorge unrolled beside me in every year at once, and none of them crowded. [494] I saw the saint go under, unhurried. [501] I saw the first gardener bend the first root, a woman with orange dust on her knuckles, humming. [519] I saw myself at nine, drowning in a doorway. [528]

And I reached out and touched it - the grain. [537] Time is not felt, and it is not gauze. [546] It is nap, like velvet: smooth one way, resisting the other, warm, alive with a quaking reverie that hums under the fingers the way the bridge hums when someone crosses at the far end. [580]

The halo fragment began to disintegrate in my hand. [589] It went the way the photographs had gone, gilding to powder to nothing, its rays uncounting themselves. [606] It had been a lens, and a lens is only borrowed sight. [618]

The maze remains. [621] The gardeners feed it; children lose themselves in it and come out laughing, having noticed nothing except that the walls lean strangely and the wild orange is sweeter deep in. [651] I keep my empty photographs in a tin at the entrance, and I am no longer trapped, only occupied. [670] A designer of mazes is not supposed to want an exit. [681] She is supposed to want a place large enough to hold what is coming toward her from behind. [699] Every evening I walk the negatively curved corridors and the past walks beside me, no longer shoving, and somewhere behind us the saint goes under again, unhurried, gold flaking off his head like pollen. [733] I have touched the texture of time, and it is kind to the fingers, and it does not hold still. [753]
