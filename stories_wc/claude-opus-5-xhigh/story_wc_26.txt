They called her the dusk noticer, though her contract said Observer of Threshold Light. [14] Ilsa Veyle had sharp eyes and forty years of practice, and she could name the minute the sky turned from gold to violet without glancing at a clock. [42] This mattered, because dreams thickened at dusk, and the Cirrus Athenaeum floated high enough above the clouds that dusk arrived late and lasted long. [66] The library bloomed all year: wisteria threaded through the stacks, moonflowers unspooling along the catalog rails, roots drinking cloudwater from clay gutters. [88] It had been built in the first decade after dreams became shareable, when nobody yet knew that a dream, once copied out of a skull, keeps growing. [115] The archive stored them as silent footage, grainy and gray, because sound never survived the transfer. [131]

Ilsa noticed the shadow on a Thursday. [138] A child in the eastern provinces had submitted a dream of a footrace, and in the eleventh second a shadow crossed the track that belonged to no runner. [166] It moved against the light, softly deliberate, like someone pacing a room they had measured a thousand times. [184] She knew that gait. [188]

Proof took her eleven nights, and she found it the only way the Athenaeum allowed: by comparing silent video footage from decades apart. [211] On the left screen: the child's footrace, last week. [220] On the right: reel 0031, submitted forty-one years ago by her brother Ordec, seventeen years old, dreaming of the Ashen River where they had grown up. [246] Frame by frame the shadows matched: the same hitch in the left shoulder, the same habit of stopping to look back. [267] Ordec had died at twenty-two, but his shadow had come unbound from him, and it had been walking through other people's dreams ever since, unlisted, uncatalogued, free. [294]

In reel 0031 the boy knelt at the water and lifted out a river stone, flat and gray and veined with quartz, and held it up to the sun as though it were a verdict. [329] Ilsa had that stone. [333] It sat in her desk drawer, wrapped in wool; he had given it to her the week before he enlisted, and said, absurdly, seriously, This is what hope looks like, keep its shape for me. [368] She had kept it, and she had kept his reel, and for forty-one years she had told herself that preservation was a kind of love. [393]

The shadow began appearing in three dreams a night, then nine. [404] It was searching. [407] The Athenaeum's board wanted it excised: an unbound shadow contaminates the record, they wrote, and the record is all we have. [428] Ilsa read the order at the hour she knew best, when the clouds below went the color of struck brass and the moonflowers opened one by one like small decisions. [458] Then she carried the stone up to the seed gallery, where the library's vines were spliced into every outgoing dream, the way old librarians had once pressed flowers into books. [488]

The shadow was waiting between two arbors, thin as spilled ink, looking back over its left shoulder. [505] "I kept it," she said, though sound never survived here either, and she set the river stone on the moss where the shadow could reach. [530] It bent, and lifted, and held the stone up as the boy had; and then it began to metamorphose. [549] Darkness went granular, then green: a stem, a spiral of leaves, a white flower opening with the others, its pollen already drifting toward the splicing looms. [575] By morning it would be in ten thousand sleeping heads: a boy at a river, holding up the shape of hope. [596]

Ilsa logged the anomaly as resolved and did not mention the stone, which was gone, or the drawer, which was empty, or the forty-one years she had spent guarding an object when she might have been passing it on. [635] The regret was luminous; it lit her from the inside like the last of the sun on the cloud-floor. [654] She stayed at the window until the violet minute came, and noticed it, and wrote it down. [671] Somebody had to keep noticing the exact moment the light let go. [683]
