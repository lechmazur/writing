Mira Vosk had explored forty churches and entered the vaults of none. [12] The Cadastral Office called it cowardice; she called it being prudently adventurous, which was the only kind of adventurous that lived long enough to file a report. [39] Narrow stone made her lungs shrink to the size of walnuts, so she had learned to read the dark from outside it. [61] She read it through the reverberation of a lonely church organ. [72]

Saint Alrun's stood empty during the fallow period, its fields resting, its village waiting on her. [88] In nine weeks the sowing would begin, and the sowing needed water, and the water needed the old sluice that the mason Ledda Corvi had buried under the church three centuries ago. [120] Corvi's master plan survived in the parish chest, beautiful and useless: forty pages of arcs and hatchings whose legend, the key that told what each mark meant, had rotted to lace. [151] Mira had come to uncover a vital key lost to time, and she meant to do it without crawling. [170]

The method was simple physics dressed as devotion. [178] She weighted the bellows, held down a pedal note, and let the low D pour into the crypt stair like poured lead. [200] Voids sang back. [203] Sealed rooms hummed. [206] Rubble swallowed the sound and returned nothing. [213] From the loft she could sketch the bones of a building she never had to enter. [229]

For six nights the crypt lied to her. [236] The east passage answered at thirty feet, then forty-one, then twenty-nine. [247] Echoes arrived from the wrong quarter; a chamber she had fixed beneath the font reported itself, the following evening, beneath the porch. [269] She checked her instruments twice and her sanity once, and then she wrote the true word in her field book: mutiny. [290]

Spatial mutiny: the refusal of a space to be held by the grid laid over it. [306] Surveyors whispered of it the way sailors whisper of reefs that move. [318] The Office denied it existed. [323] Mira, who had spent her life at the edge of rooms rather than inside them, felt something close to kinship. [343] Corvi, she suspected, had not built a crypt so much as a lock, and the lock had been taught to squirm. [364]

On the seventh night she went down. [371] Not far, only to where the crossing tower had collapsed in some forgotten winter, opening the vaults to the sky. [391] Four passages met there under a ragged oculus of broken ribs: a shadowed nexus under starlight, cold and open, the one place below ground where her chest did not tighten. [421]

She had left the pedal weighted above. [428] The low D came down the stairwell and filled the nexus, and the dust on the flagstones gathered itself into ridges, standing waves pale as frost, four arcs converging on a single point. [461] The sight transfixed her so completely that she forgot, for a whole minute, that she was underground. [478]

At the convergence, worn smooth by no foot, was a dimple in the stone the width of a needle. [497] Mira took out her drafting compass, the one she used for blueprints, and set its point into the hole. [516] It seated with a click she felt in her teeth. [526] Corvi had drilled the center of her arcs into the floor itself; every mark on all forty pages had been swung from this one pivot. [551] The key was not a word or a legend. [559] It was a place. [563]

With the compass point fixed, Mira laid the vellum beneath it, transfixed the sheet, and swung the arcs where Corvi had swung them. [586] The lying passages stopped lying. [591] Thirty feet became thirty feet. [597] The sluice gate stood two rods north, under the porch, exactly where the crypt had once claimed a chamber to be and then denied it. [622] The mutiny had not been chaos; it had been a sentry, turning strangers in circles until one of them was patient enough, and frightened enough, to listen from a distance. [652]

She filed the sluice, the depth, the bearing; the village would drink and sow. [666] She did not file the nexus. [672] In the margin of her report, where the Office demanded a clean grid, she wrote: Below the crossing the space keeps its own counsel, and I have left it there. [702] It was a small mutiny of her own, and it made her wistful in a way she could not name: she was thirty-nine, afraid of every corridor she had ever measured, and only now, in a roofless room under the stars, had she been shown what her instruments were for. [752] Before she climbed out, she pulled the compass point from the stone, and the dust arcs let go, and above her the organ's last breath faded out of the pipes like something forgiven. [785]
