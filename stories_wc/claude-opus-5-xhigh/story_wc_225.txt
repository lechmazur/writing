The village of Sarn had been holding its breath for nineteen years. [12] Bellamy Ord walked in through the frozen rain, which parted around him like beadwork on a loom. [29] Nothing here moved except him: not the miller's dog mid-leap, not the smoke standing upright above the chimneys, not the girl at the well with her mouth open on a word she had not finished. [64] Above the hills, thunder had been rolling toward Sarn since the year he was exiled, and it had never arrived. [84]

He had been a chess tutor once, the best in the capital, until he taught a child to sacrifice everything for a beautiful finish. [108] Her name was Ilse. [112] She was eleven, and she was a weaver's daughter, and he had told her that a piece which cannot be spent is not a piece but a cage. [140] When soldiers came up the valley road, she spent the whole village. [152] The weavers of Sarn had always known the woven hush, a stitch-pattern that could hold a moment the way a knot holds a rope; Ilse used it on everything she loved, and stopped the war at her doorstep by stopping her doorstep. [194]

The court blamed the tutor for the lesson. [202] They took his boards, his students, and his name, and sent him east with a single gift from the queen's own workshop: a platinum labyrinth the size of a plum, a maze of hollow channels with one steel bead sealed inside. [243] It had been meant as mockery - a puzzle for a man who had run out of games - and it became the only thing that let him think. [270] He fidgets productively; his hands need a small labor before his mind will consent to a large one. [288] For nineteen years, rolling that bead through platinum corridors, he had studied the one thing the court left him: a scholar's copy of the hush-charts, and the rumor that every woven hush carries its own undoing in the border of the last cloth the weaver touched. [334]

Ilse's last cloth hung in the meeting hall, and he found it by noon of a day that was not a day. [356] The tapestry showed the valley, the soldiers, the small figure at the well. [369] Its border was a chaos of frayed threads, hundreds of loose ends, and among them - he was certain - the sequence that would release her. [393]

So he sat on the cold flags and began to winnow. [404] Decoding frayed threads is not unlike reading a board: most moves are noise, and the whole art is discarding them without pride. [426] He sorted by dye lot, by twist, by the direction of wear, laying aside eleven threads, then nine, then four. [446] The labyrinth clicked in his left hand while his right hand worked, bead sliding, turning, doubling back. [463] Three threads remained: a red, a green, and a grey no thicker than a hair. [478]

The charts were clear about the rest. [485] A hush could only be cut during the lull of distant thunder, in the gap between the roll and its echo, and in Sarn that gap had been standing open for nineteen years like a held door. [522] He could pull one thread only; the others would knot the pattern shut forever. [536]

Red was the soldiers' dye - undo the hush there and the valley would resume with the war still walking up the road. [558] Green was the fields, the safe choice, the slow choice: the village would wake in a spring nineteen years late, with no enemy left alive to meet it. [586] Grey was the figure at the well. [593] Pull grey and Ilse alone would step free, this hour, into his arms - and Sarn would stay stopped, and he would have spent a village to save one beautiful piece. [623] That was the lesson he had taught her. [631]

He sat with the grey thread between his fingers for a long time, not agonizing - he was past that - but resting, the way a fierce thing rests before it acts. [661] Forgiving himself, he had come to understand, was not a feeling he could argue his way into. [678] It was a move he had to make and then keep making. [690]

He set the grey thread down, uncut, and drew the green. [701]

The hush let go with a sound like a held breath released across a whole valley. [717] Thunder finished its long sentence. [722] Rain fell properly. [725] The dog landed. [728] The girl at the well finished her word, which was a name, and it was his. [744] She was still eleven, and he was old, and she would have to learn the endgame from someone gentler. [763] He put the platinum labyrinth in her hand, bead rattling toward its center, and let her keep it. [781] Then he walked out into the moving rain. [789]
