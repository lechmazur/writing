Ilo Vantry had been expelled from three academies, which he liked to call a diversified education. [16] Now, in the second year after thoughts became taxable, he taught grammar in a greenhouse that smelled of wet bark and beetle frass. [39] The greenhouse belonged to Dr. Marisol Ochre, entomologist, who had lost her university post when the Revenue Bureau ruled that classification counted as productive cognition and billed her for every moth she had ever named. [74] She kept the beetles anyway, and she kept Ilo, mostly because he made her laugh while the assessors' drones hummed over the roof glass. [98] Twice a week, eleven people crowded between the pupation cages for the club. [111] They called it a gardening society, because that was true, and because the truth was the best hiding place left. [131] The language they built had no sound; sound could be recorded, and recordings could be assessed. [147] Instead it had rows. [151] Ilo taught them to read a garden bed like a loom: the warp was the furrow, the weft was the species, and meaning lived in the interval between one seedling and the next. [184] Their key was a gourd rattle seed - one dried seed from the club's founding gourd, drilled and strung on a red thread, passed hand to hand each meeting. [212] Whoever held it set the week's spacing, and the spacing set the cipher, so the language could never be frozen into a dictionary the Bureau might tax. [239] The method was simple and slow: join community gardens, take the worst plots, and plant message patterns where the drones saw only vegetables. [262] In eight months the club had members in nineteen gardens across the river districts, and a sentence could take a season to finish. [285] Ilo was candidly cryptic about his reasons, which annoyed everyone who wanted a manifesto. [299] "I am not saving anybody," he said cheerfully, hands full of soil. [311] "I am standing guard over one small fire that happens to be mine." [324] Marisol understood that better than the manifesto crowd did; she had a spark of defiance of her own, and she watered it with mealworms and patience. [350] The trouble arrived in October, all at once, the way trouble prefers. [362] An auditor named Pell filed for a survey of the river gardens; a member in the Kestrel Street plot confessed under interview levy; and the club's longest message - three hundred beds long, begun in spring - was two weeks from legibility. [402] Everything converged on the greenhouse at nine that night: eleven members, one auditor's notice, and a hailstorm that turned the glass into a drum. [426] They argued in the dark, which was itself a taxable act if the meters could hear thinking, and the meters could. [447] Someone wanted to plow the gardens. [453] Someone wanted to finish the message and be arrested famously. [463] Ilo took the gourd rattle seed off its red thread, dropped it into an empty specimen jar with forty of its cousins, and shook. [487] The rattle came out uneven, stumbling, nothing like a code. [497] "New spacing," he said. [501] "Random. Nobody learns it, nobody can sell it, nobody can confess it." [513] The plan he laid out was a small, ugly mercy: they would warp the three hundred beds overnight, shifting each interval by the jar's nonsense, until the sentence they had grown all summer collapsed into ordinary cabbage. [550] Pell's survey would find nineteen tidy allotments and no grammar at all. [562] Marisol asked what they lost. [567] "A sentence," Ilo said. [571] "We keep the tongue." [575] They worked until four in the morning in hail-melt and mud, nineteen gardens moving in the same crooked rhythm, and by dawn the message was gone and the club was not. [606] The auditor came, measured, and found a lot of cabbage. [616] He also found, pressed into the mud of the Kestrel Street plot, a gourd seed on a red thread - one of the forty from the jar, indistinguishable now, left there by someone as insolence - and because he could not name it, he wrote nothing down and paid no tax on the wondering. [668] Back in the greenhouse, Marisol pinned a new label to an unnamed moth and left the species line blank, which was, Ilo told her, a perfect sentence. [695] Outside, in nineteen gardens, eleven people were already planting spring. [705]
