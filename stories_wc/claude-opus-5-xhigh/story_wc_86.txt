The fortress at Kell belonged to no one, which was why both armies trusted it. [15] Mira Adescu climbed its storm-worn parapet an hour before moonrise, windblown mist stinging her face, one glove flat on the stone. [36] Mist came off the river in slow sheets, erasing the valley, the roads, the two camps that hated each other four miles apart. [59] Up here there was only the furrowed hush of two prisoner-exchange negotiators waiting to be certain of the sky. [78] Colonel Teodor Vas was already there, hunched in his gray coat, hands cupped around nothing. [93] They had learned, after the radios failed and then were jammed and then were mistrusted entirely, to synchronize through moon phases. [114] First quarter for talks; full moon for exchanges; the dark of the moon for burials, which neither side needed to negotiate. [135] Tonight the moon was full, and behind them in the courtyard sat forty-one men in ropes, waiting to be untethered at the same instant as forty-one men in a barn across the valley. [168] The same instant mattered. [172] Three years ago it had not been the same instant, and Mira had signed the list that made the difference. [192] She had trusted a clock instead of the sky, released her forty men first, and watched Vas's side keep theirs three days longer, until nine of them stopped being prisoners in the only way left. [227] Vas had apologized last spring, on this parapet, in a voice like a door closing. [242] Mira had not forgiven him, and they both lived in the long room between those two events, furnishing it as best they could. [265] "You brought it," he said. [270] She opened her hand. [274] In it lay an antique drawer pull, brass, shaped like a wheat sheaf, worn smooth on the underside by a century of thumbs. [297] It had come off the desk in the requisitioned schoolhouse where she signed that ruinous list; she had pulled the drawer too hard, and the little sheaf had come away in her fist while a boy's name was still wet on the page. [340] She had carried it since, not as a relic of guilt but as a tool. [355] Mira was uncommonly thoughtful, a habit built out of the wreck: she had made one fast decision once, and every decision since had been slow. [380] "Show me the protocol again," Vas said, though he knew it; he wanted it said aloud, the way frightened men want prayers. [402] "When the moon clears the mist and touches the top of the east tower, your sergeant cuts, my sergeant cuts," she said. [424] "No clocks, no couriers, no trust required, only a thing both of us can see." [439] "And the sheaf?" [442] "Goes into the hinge of the courtyard gate, so it cannot be shut from either side while men are still crossing." [463] She turned the brass over once. [468] "There is one honest use for a mistake," she said, "and that is to recycle it into something the next fool can lean on." [492] "I file them down into rules that will hold even when the men holding them are cowards." [509] Vas looked at the mist for a while. [516] "Neutral ground is a strange thing," he said. [524] "Down there I would have you shot; up here I lend you my knife." [538] "That is not hypocrisy," Mira said. [544] "That is architecture." [547] "We built a place with no advantage in it, and now it holds eighty-two lives." [562] The mist thinned from the bottom up, the way it always did after storms, and the moon came out low and enormous and indifferent. [586] It climbed the east tower stone by stone while nobody spoke. [597] When light touched the broken merlon at the top, both negotiators raised a lantern, and below them ropes fell away in two courtyards at once. [622] Men walked toward each other through the mist and did not know why they were unafraid. [638] Mira wedged the wheat sheaf into the gate hinge and left it there, giving up the last of her evidence, the last thing she had to be angry with. [667] "Next full moon," Vas said, and then hesitated, the way a man does at a threshold. [683] "Next full moon," Mira answered, which was not forgiveness, but was the shape forgiveness would take if it ever came: showing up, on time, by the same light. [711] She went down the stairs into the wind, thinking, as she always did, about what she would do differently, and writing it down before she could forget. [738]
