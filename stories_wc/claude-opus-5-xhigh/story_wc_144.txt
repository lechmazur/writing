The depot's mist came in off the harbor every morning like a slow tide, softening the iron rails until they hummed rather than shone. [24] Odile liked that hour best, because the fog put everyone else on her footing. [38] Her repair shop occupied the depot's old parcel office, three walls of drawers and a workbench worn to a shallow bowl by forty years of her elbows. [65] She had gone blind at thirty-one and had overseen the shop ever since, which the guild found strange until they watched her work. [88] Teo, her apprentice, said customers came for the calm as much as the craft; something in her stillness pulled people through the door like a magnet under a table pulling nails. [119]

At four that morning the harbor pilot arrived, hat dripping, carrying a cedar box. [133] Inside lay an artificial hummingbird drone, its wings folded wrong, one lens starred with a crack. [149] "The Verity lifts anchor at six," the pilot said. [158] "Maiden voyage, four hundred souls, and the shoal channel has shifted twice since the survey." [173]

Odile already knew the bird's purpose: it flew ahead of a hull, tasting current and depth, and sent back a stream of feedback in clicks that a pilot's glove translated into a heading. [206] Broken, it would still chatter, but the chatter would mean nothing, and nothing was worse than silence to a man steering into fog. [229] Four hundred souls; she felt the number settle in her chest like a held breath. [244] She set her palms on the box and let herself be unhurried. [256] "Two hours," she said. [260] "Bring the lamp and fill the trough." [267]

The depot had water everywhere: a cracked drinking fountain, the locomotive trough, and the shallow rain basins under the platform's broken glass. [289] Odile had built her practice on those water features, because water bends light in ways a lens can be measured against. [310] She could not see the bending. [316] Teo could, and she had spent six years teaching him to say exactly what he saw. [332] "Hold the lens a thumb's width under the surface and tell me where the light goes." [348] "The lamp line breaks into two," Teo said. [356] "One stays straight; the other curves left and dies against the trough wall." [369]

So the crack ran shallow through the outer coating only, and the sensor beneath it was sound. [386] That mattered enormously; a dead sensor she could not have replaced before dawn, but a lying one she could correct. [406] She worked by touch, splinting the left wing spar with a sliver of clock brass and gluing a patch of lacquered silk over the torn membrane. [432] The patch had to weigh less than a grain of rice, or the bird would fly a slow curve and report the sea it never crossed. [458] She weighed it on the seed balance her father had left her, and was satisfied. [473]

Then came the lens, and the true problem. [481] She filled the fountain's bowl, floated the mended coating film on the water, and had Teo call out the angle of the lamp's reflection while she counted the drip beat with her fingertips. [514] Nine degrees off, then five, then one; she shaved the resin between each reading until Teo said the two lines lay on top of each other like a single wire. [544] "Now the glove," she said. [549]

The pilot gave it over, and she wore it while Teo hovered the hummingbird above the rain basins. [567] The clicks arrived in her palm, and she listened the way she listened to everything now: to decode feedback into direction. [588] Three clicks meant deeper water to starboard; a stutter meant the bird doubted itself. [602] It stuttered twice over the second basin, so she adjusted the gain by a quarter turn, and after that the little machine spoke plainly. [626]

At ten minutes to six, the harbor bell rang and the depot shook as the Verity's anchor chain came up, link by link, out of the water. [653] Odile closed the cedar box and pushed it across the bench without hurry. [666] "Fly her low," she said. [671] "She'll tell you the truth for six hours; after that the silk stretches and she'll start flattering you." [689] The pilot ran; the mist swallowed him at the platform's end. [700]

Teo stood at the door listening to the ship's horn open across the harbor, a long clean note with no fear in it. [723] "How do you stay so calm?" he asked. [731] Odile wiped the resin from her thumbnail. [738] "Panic is noise," she said, "and I have only so much room for listening." [752] Then she asked him to fill the trough again, because the six-fifteen freight would bring a lantern with a warped mirror, and she wanted the light ready. [779]
