They gave me the ranger cabin at nine thousand feet because no one else wanted a fire lookout who slept fourteen hours a day. [24] I am a lucid dream chronicler, which is a grand phrase for a woman with a birch bark scroll and a jar of oak gall ink, writing down where she goes while her body lies still under three wool blankets. [64] The Forest Service pays me in firewood and silence. [73] My work, as I understand it, is to distill chaos into narrative. [85] The scroll is eleven feet long now, wound around a peeled aspen dowel, and it is chronologically confused, and so am I, in a way I stopped apologizing for years ago. [116] Entry forty sits before entry twelve. [122] There is a passage about my mother's funeral written in handwriting I did not have until last winter. [140] Dreams do not queue. [144] Three nights ago, or three years, the scroll will not say, I went walking in the sleep country below the cabin and found the old miners' cemetery where the aspens go copper. [176] My name was cut into the fourth stone. [184] Understand that I have watched a whale swim up a staircase and I have negotiated, at length, with a door. [204] So I did not scream. [209] I knelt, read the dates, and could not make the arithmetic work, and that was what unsettled me: not death, but bookkeeping. [231] Since then something has come loose in me, and after living with it a while I learned its name: feral nostalgia. [252] Not the soft kind that hums old songs on the porch, but the kind that paces, that came into the cabin like a starved animal and began eating my old entries. [283] Because in the early scroll, the girl writing those pages could soar. [295] At nineteen she stepped off a ridge in a dream and went up like smoke, no effort in it, no technique, nothing to it at all. [321] I have to work for it now: running, flapping, bargaining with the air, and most nights I only skim the treetops. [342] So I got jealous of a nineteen-year-old who no longer exists, and jealousy of your own past selves is a wound that will not close, because there is nobody left to blame and nobody left to forgive. [379] Then I made the envy a method. [386] Each night before sleep I read a page of her until my chest went hot, and I carried that heat down into the dark and burned it like lamp oil. [416] I hunted my past selves. [421] I found the nineteen-year-old on the ridge above Marmot Creek, barefoot in snow, laughing at nothing. [437] I asked her how she did it. [444] She said, you stop asking permission, which is the most useless and most accurate instruction I have ever been given. [464] Then she stepped off the rim and soared, and I went after her furious, and fury turns out to have lift in it. [487] Up there, higher than the cabin's stovepipe smoke, I finally understood the stone. [500] The two dates were not my birth and my death; they were the first and last entries on the scroll. [520] Every night I go under, some version of me is buried in that copper cemetery, and I have been living off the estates of the dead without ever holding a funeral. [551] So I am doing the job properly at last. [559] Coffee, the stove ticking, the scroll unrolled the length of the cabin floor with river rocks holding its corners down, and me numbering the entries. [584] Where the birch is blank between them I write the bridges, the dull connective tissue that turns a heap of miracles into a story somebody could follow. [611] It is tedious. [614] Wonder is mostly tedious, once you have logged enough of it. [625] But an ordered life can be handed to somebody; a heap cannot. [637] Last night I did not read her pages. [645] I walked to the rim, asked nobody, and rose maybe sixty feet, badly, hauling myself over the copper aspens until I could see the whole cemetery at once. [673] Fourth stone from the left. [678] I saluted it, the way rangers salute the fire they could not stop. [691] Then I flew home to the woman writing this, who will be buried by morning, and whose name I intend to get right on the bark before she goes. [720]
