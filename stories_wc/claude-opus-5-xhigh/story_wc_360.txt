The topaz workshop stood on the ridge where the sky had begun to lose count of itself. [17] Its walls were slabs of raw topaz, thin enough to glow when the press-lamp burned behind them, and its roof was open to the stars. [42] I came there in the season when stars were vanishing, one every ninth night, quietly, like names being struck from a ledger. [64] The letterpress operator was called Ottoline Vasc, and she never answered a question the way it was asked. [82] "Why do the stars go out?" I asked, and she said, "Because something is reading them." [98] I had come to learn the mathematics of emotion. [106] That was my whole ambition, unembarrassed: I wanted feeling reduced to ratio, grief expressed as a proportion I could carry in a pocket, love as a stable constant. [134] I needed this because I had no feelings of my own that I trusted. [147] Since childhood I had habitually borrowed personalities: my mother's flat courage, a ferryman's laugh, the exact pause a schoolmaster used before saying no. [170] Wearing others, I could pass as a person; alone, I was arithmetic without numbers. [184] Ottoline set me to washing rollers and sorting slugs of lead, and for eleven nights she taught me nothing. [203] On the twelfth she unwrapped the halo fragment. [211] It was a curved sliver of something like mother-of-pearl gone metallic, no longer than my thumb, and it did not so much shine as make the surrounding air admit it had been dim. [244] "Broken off a saint, or a satellite, or a bad idea," she said. [257] "The provenance is unimportant; the property is not." [265] Locked into the chase among the type, the fragment did something to printed language. [279] A page pulled with it in the forme transposed empathy. [289] That was her word, transposed, as a musician moves a melody into a key the throat can survive. [307] A reader would feel, with total accuracy, an emotion belonging to a stranger -- but shifted, arriving attached to the wrong object. [328] A widow's ache would land on a doorknob; a soldier's terror would settle into the smell of rain. [346] "Why the wrong object?" I said. [352] "Because the right one is defended," Ottoline answered. [360] Her method was ambiguity, deliberate and surgical. [367] She composed lines that could be read three ways, and she chose the wording so that no reading won. [386] The purpose was to disorient: a mind kept slightly off its footing cannot hold up its usual shields, and the transposed feeling walks in through the side door. [414] "You want the mathematics of emotion," she said, inking the roller. [425] "Here is your theorem: feeling is conserved, and transferable, and it will never sit still where it is put." [444] For a season I worked her lucid turbulence: clear-headed nights of ink and wind, every idea sharp and nothing steady. [464] We printed small ambiguous cards and left them in villages, and people wept at hinges and forgave each other over nothing. [485] I borrowed Ottoline too, of course -- her flat vowels, her habit of pausing over a comma as if it were a cliff. [507] She noticed, and did not scold me. [514] "Between vanishings there is a gap where the sky stops counting," she said. [527] "Set your own line in it." [533] The eighteenth star went out at moonrise, and I had until the next ninth night. [548] I composed slowly, in nobody's voice but the one I had never used. [561] The line was about my brother, who drowned in a reservoir when I was nine, and it was deliberately ambiguous -- it could mean he was still swimming, or that the water was still holding him, or that I was the water. [602] I locked the halo fragment beside the type, pulled one impression, and read it myself. [617] The grief that arrived was mine, and it did not arrive where I expected. [631] It landed on the topaz walls, on the smell of solvent, on Ottoline's crooked thumb resting on the lever -- everywhere except the boy. [654] That was the answer, and it was not a ratio. [664] Emotion has no clean arithmetic; it has a remainder, and the remainder is where you can bear to put it. [684] I set the press down and found I was standing in my own posture, borrowing no one. [701] Above us another star thinned, hesitated, and stayed. [709] Ottoline looked up at it, and for once she said something that could only mean one thing. [726] "Good," she said. [729]
