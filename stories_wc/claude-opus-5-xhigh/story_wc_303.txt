The velvet observatory had been sewn shut against the sun for ninety years, and Ilse Varga arrived at midsummer to open it. [22] As a provenance researcher for the Aldringen Collection, she had unstitched worse rooms. [35] Inside, mulberry-colored drapes hung from the dome's ribs, and a young mulberry tree had cracked the marble floor to reach the shuttered eye of the telescope. [61] The tree spoke to her immediately, the way plants had spoken to her since the greenhouse fire, unasked and unstoppable. [81] It did not use words; it used an ache, low and purple, a grief with a taste. [98] The object she had come to authenticate lay in a lead-lined case on the meridian bench: a violin of transparent ice crystal, complete with strings of drawn frost. [126] It did not melt. [131] It glowed faintly, a green-white promise, and the collection's telegram had been explicit: certify it before the promise loses its glow, or the sale collapses. [156] The papers were stranger than the instrument. [162] Instead of a signed pedigree, the maker had left a long card of stiff vellum punched with holes, a perforated destiny meant to be fed through the violin's own scroll. [192] Each hole was a night the instrument was permitted to sound, and Ilse counted them twice: forty-one holes, forty of them stopped with wax. [216] One night remained, and it was tonight. [223] She set her thermos on the ledger while she worked, because her mentor Todor had taught her the only forecasting she trusted: read the future in coffee stains. [251] The rings the cup left were maps of what a document wanted to become. [265] Tonight the stain came out doubled, two overlapping circles, one thin and dark, one wide and pale, and between them a bridge of drying brown. [290] Two futures, Todor would have said, and the bridge is the choice you are already standing on. [307] The mulberry pressed its ache against her ribs until she understood what it was showing her. [323] The violin was not carved. [328] It had been grown, cultured on a graft in the tree's own heartwood by a maker who had loved frost and orchards equally, and it could still propagate. [356] Played on its last permitted night, at the exact hour of midsummer, the violin would seed the tree with a hundred crystal buds, and the orchard would begin again. [385] Played, it would also shatter. [390] Unplayed, and shipped intact, it would anchor the Aldringen Collection's winter auction and buy the empire another century of marble and silence. [412] This was the work, then, and it was not cataloguing: to weigh a dream against an empire, alone, with the glow already thinning. [435] Ilse had spent eleven years proving that objects belonged to the people with the best lawyers. [451] She had never once been asked what an object belonged to. [462] At eleven o'clock the drapes went the color of bruised fruit, and she cut the wax from the last hole with her scalpel. [485] She fed the perforated card through the scroll, and the holes caught the strings like a music box catching its comb. [506] She was not a violinist; she only had to draw the bow once and let destiny do the fingering. [525] The note that came out was thin, cold, and enormous. [535] The mulberry drank it. [539] Ilse felt the tree's ache turn over into something she had no adult word for, only the child's word: yes. [559] Frost ran up the trunk in veins. [566] Buds formed, transparent and hard, ringing faintly against one another in the draft from the opening shutter. [583] The violin came apart in her hands like a held breath, and the glow went out of it, into the tree. [604] In the morning she wrote her report in a clean hand. [615] Provenance: grown, not made; owner: the orchard; disposition: propagated, twelve cuttings taken and packed in sawdust for distribution to any conservatory that asks. [638] She signed it, resigned in the same sentence, and left the thermos on the sill for whoever came to shout at her. [660] The last stain it made was a single circle, unbroken, and she did not need Todor to translate that one. [680] Behind her, the mulberry ached on, purple and satisfied, and the empire, for once, was the thing left holding an empty case. [702]
