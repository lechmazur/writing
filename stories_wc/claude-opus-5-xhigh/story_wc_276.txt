The sound wave garden woke at dusk, its copper reeds humming in rows, its glass troughs trembling with the shapes of old noise. [23] Sabe walked it the way her mother taught her, in a zigzag, cutting across the nodes where a footstep could bloom into thunder. [46] Straight lines were for people who did not intend to catch anything. [58] She was known along the terraces as the cool-headed fisher, and she had earned it the dull way, by not flinching. [79] Grief and greed both leak into the water as ripples, and echoes will not come to a rippled pond. [98] Tonight the sky was loose. [103] It happened once a generation, when patterns break themselves: the constellations went sleepwalking, the Hound drifting out of its harness, the Ladle spilling stars in a slow zigzag toward the horizon. [134] While the figures wandered, the tethers that held old sounds to their sources went slack, and lost voices surfaced in the garden's troughs like carp. [159] Sabe wanted one voice only: the echo of the goddess Ennu, who sang the world to sleep and then drowned in the delta with the railway that carried her pilgrims. [189] From that railway her mother had salvaged a single rail spike, iron gone soft-brown, and used it all her life as a plumb and a striker. [215] Her mother had invented the alphabet; Sabe transliterates emotions into it, turning a fisher's dread or joy into ink so the water stays still. [239] Eleven hundred glyphs, one for each shade of feeling, and one blank space left at the end. [256] Her mother had died before finding a sign for whatever it is Ennu's lullaby does to a listener. [274] So Sabe came to finish the book. [281] She reached the last terrace, where the reeds grew tall and detuned, and knelt at the trough of black water that the garden's builders had aimed at the sky. [310] She lowered the rail spike on a wire until it hung a hand's width above the surface. [327] An echo cannot be baited with sound, since sound is what an echo already is; it can only be answered. [347] You catch it by voicing silence. [353] That is harder than it sounds. [359] Silence must be pushed out of the chest with the full weight of a shout, lips shaped, throat working, nothing coming, until the air around the mouth bends. [387] Sabe voiced silence nine times. [392] On the ninth, the water climbed the spike. [400] It came up the wire in a thin thread and rang the iron, and the iron gave back a note that was not iron's. [424] It was a lullaby, and it was feral. [432] Something in it licked its young and something in it warned off the dark; it had teeth and it had milk. [453] Above her, the sleepwalking constellations stopped drifting and hung still, listening, the way animals do. [468] Sabe's hands stayed steady on the wire, but her eyes ran, and she noted that too - dread and joy braided together, no existing glyph for it. [494] She had brought a wax tablet, and she wrote as the echo circled the trough, transliterating the thing the song did to her: a downstroke for the ache, a hook for the wildness, a broken line for the part that wanted to be eaten. [538] The glyph came out crooked, three strokes doubling back on themselves, like her own path through the garden. [556] It meant, as near as words can manage, the love that is also hunger, and the letting go that follows. [576] Then Sabe had to decide. [581] A caught echo can be sealed in a jar to sing forever, thinning year by year, and the house that keeps it never sleeps. [605] Her mother had wanted the glyph, not the goddess. [614] So Sabe drew the spike up out of the water, closed her mouth, and let the silence be only silence. [634] Unanswered, the echo went back down the wire, into the trough, into the delta, into the slack sky. [652] The constellations shivered and resumed their sleepwalking, the Hound finding its harness by morning, the Ladle upright again as if it had never spilled. [676] Sabe walked home along the terraces, calm as ever, the tablet warm against her ribs, humming nothing at all. [695] The reeds bent behind her, in a pattern no one had taught them. [708]
