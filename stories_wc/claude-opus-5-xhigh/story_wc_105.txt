The orchard drowned when I was nine, and my mother said the sea had only borrowed it. [17] Sixty years later I still work beneath its glass, in the sunken greenhouse where the pear trees lean like old men listening. [39] Nine shipwrecks ring the rim above us, and their rust hums faintly to me, because I hear colors: a thin whistle from orange, a cello note from green. [67] It made me a good designer of hearing aids and a poor liar, since faces flush and flushing sings. [86] For forty years I sold devices that flattered the world; now I make only one kind, and I make it wrong on purpose. [109] My aids do not amplify. [114] They imprint. [116] Each diaphragm is annealed at the ashen forge on the shingle above the glass, where the aurora comes down green and enormous and sets the whole beach ringing like a struck bowl, and where ash from the old orchard's burnt prunings takes an impression the way wax does. [164] Press a sound into that ash while it cools and the sound stays; wear the aid and the sound arrives inside your skull as memory, not noise. [191] I have imprinted my mother's kettle, the bees of 1974, the pump house before the dike gave way. [209] People pay for those, and they weep, and I learned early what memory weighs: give a man his father's cough and he cannot lift his arms for an hour. [238] But I never sold the sound I actually wanted, because I could not find it. [253] I wanted the sound of trust breaking. [260] Forty-one years ago my sister Reva stood where the loading rails end and let a salvage man ask her whether the orchard could be sold. [285] She did not say no. [290] She said nothing for half a second, and in that half second something in me came apart, and I have spent my life trying to record it. [317] You cannot record a break; I tried hammers, ice, glaciers, the tearing of green wood. [332] All of them were events, and trust does not break as an event. [345] So I changed method and began working by collecting absences. [355] I record rooms just after someone leaves, the held rest inside a hymn, the notch in a sentence where a name should have gone. [379] Absences imprint beautifully. [382] They are the only thing ash truly remembers. [390] This spring the surgeon gave me a season, and the same week I grafted the last living scion from the last pear tree onto young rootstock in the warm end of the greenhouse. [423] A graft takes six weeks to knit, and everything I have left happens while cells divide - theirs joining, mine going wrong - so I stopped sleeping and worked. [450] In week four Reva came down the ladder. [458] She is eighty-one and deaf now, thirty years of cutting hulls apart in the yard above, which is its own kind of justice and its own kind of grief. [487] She wanted an aid. [491] Of course she did; everyone along this coast knows what the old woman in the drowned orchard makes. [509] I had one blank diaphragm left, cooling in ash under an aurora so bright it hummed. [525] I could give her the bees, or the kettle, or our mother saying her name. [540] Or I could give her the half second. [548] I had it at last - collected from ten thousand rooms, filed down, true - the shape of a silence where a no should be. [571] I imprinted it. [574] I set the aid in her ear at the forge, with the shipwrecks groaning their brown notes below us and the sky ringing green. [598] Reva heard the absence, and her face went the color that whistles. [610] "I was afraid he would take it anyway," she said. [620] "I said nothing so he would not know we had no money left." [633] Forty-one years of an absence, and inside it a whole sentence I had never once considered. [649] That is what memory does, if you let it stay unopened: it hardens into a shape and you build your life against it, and the shape is not the thing. [679] The graft took in week six. [685] Reva wears the absence still; she says it is the only thing she can hear that does not hurt. [704] We sit under the battered glass among the ribs of ships, in a marvel nobody built on purpose, and we wait for the pears. [728] My cells divide the wrong way, the graft's the right way, and the sea still has not returned what it borrowed. [749] I no longer need it to. [755]
