The lake over Dome Nine held its breath every June, and Marisol Vey had come to hold hers with it. [20] She was a landscape astrologer, trained to read valleys the way others read constellations: ridge as spine, riverbend as ecliptic, every drowned street a fixed star waiting for someone to name it. [52] The valley beneath her had been named once, then flooded, then forgotten, and she had been born in it. [71] In her chest pocket she carried a burnt feather, a heron's primary, scorched at the tip by the last bonfire her people ever lit on dry ground. [98] Odalis met her at the moon pool with a lantern and an apology. [111] "The grid died in April," he said, "so I have been patching it with fishing line and stubbornness." [129] He was softly ingenious in the way of men who have been alone with broken machines for years: he had rewired the dome's failing lamps to a bellows, so that light came only when something breathed. [165] Below them, the facility spread out in shadowseams: corridors half-silted, glass ribs, the old sign for PROJECT LUMINOUS RESPITE still legible under forty years of algae. [191] The researchers had been breeding a colony that flashed on a firefly's cadence, hoping to give sleepless cities a light that rested when people did. [216] Then the dam closed, the valley filled, and the colony went feral in the dark. [231] Marisol had come because the colony surfaced only once a year, on the night the water warmed past nineteen degrees, and it would sink again at sunrise. [258] One night to chart it; one night, Odalis said, to captivate it. [270] The old logs described the method plainly and impossibly: the colony synchronized to breath, and could be led into pattern by divers exhaling together. [294] They went down at midnight through water the color of steeped tea. [306] Marisol worked her charts against the ruin, and the ruin answered. [317] The drowned crossroads sat where her mother's kitchen had stood; the dome's spine ran east, toward the ridge her grandfather called the Ladle. [340] She had spent twenty years mapping other people's places, hoping one of them would teach her the rhythm of belonging, and none had, because she had never stayed long enough to learn a rhythm at all. [376] At two in the morning they found the colony in the flooded atrium, a low dull smear on the ceiling glass, sullen as banked coals. [401] Odalis worked the bellows; nothing came. [407] Marisol took out the burnt feather and turned it in the lanternlight, and understood what a chart could not tell her. [428] The night before the gates opened, the village had burned its harvest and its bad furniture on the levee, and a heron had crossed the smoke, and everyone had breathed out at once when the first fireflies of summer rose over the wet grass. [472] That shared exhale was the last thing her people had done together, and she had kept the feather because she could not keep the breath. [497] "Not the bellows," she told Odalis, "us." [504] They counted three, and exhaled together. [510] The bubbles went up in one braided column, and the ceiling answered with a green so gentle it did not seem to be light at all, more like permission. [539] They breathed again, and again, and the colony learned them. [549] Pulse, dark, pulse: the cadence of a June field, moving down the corridors ahead of them, lighting the drowned crossroads, the kitchen, the sign, the whole forgotten grid in soft rolling waves. [581] For four hours the city was luminous, and it was, exactly as the researchers had promised, a respite. [599] Marisol stopped charting. [602] There was nothing to fix in place; the light only existed while they kept time with it, and keeping time, she found, was the whole of the lesson. [630] At sunrise the colony sank, taking the glow down into the silt for another year. [645] They surfaced into a pink lake and sat on the pontoon without speaking, and Marisol let the burnt feather go onto the water, where it floated above her mother's kitchen. [675] "Same time next June," Odalis said, already sketching a better bellows on his palm. [689] She said yes before she could talk herself out of it. [700] A year was a rhythm; a return was a kind of address. [712] Over the shoreline reeds, a few late fireflies were still going out one by one, and she and Odalis breathed out together, without planning to, and watched them until the last one dimmed. [745]
