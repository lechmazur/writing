The anomaly hung four thousand kilometers below the station like a thumbprint pressed into glass, and everything Mira Osei knew about time she had learned by watching light limp around it. [31] She was the station's celestial signal interpreter, which meant she sat in a warm room and translated the gravity-bent whispers that arrived fourteen months late. [56] It also meant she could answer them. [63] That was the part nobody on Earth liked to say plainly: the anomaly's throat swallowed a signal and coughed it out into the past, and so the station's true function was editing history. [96] Not grandly. [98] You could not unsay a death or unwrite a war. [108] You could only work through contingent relationships: shift a shipping manifest, delay a requisition, change which of two names sat first on a duty roster, and let the world rearrange itself downstream. [140] Mira had a requisition to change. [146] Fourteen months ago she had ordered seed for the station's hydroponic ring and typed one wrong catalog number, and instead of amaranth the crates had come full of bitter vetch. [176] Ilya, who had taught her the grammar of lensed light, had laughed and planted it anyway. [192] Then, six weeks later, he had gone out to reseat a dish antenna and the tether had failed, and now she lived in the time after planting the wrong seeds, tending a crop she had never wanted, eating the salt-bitter greens of her own mistake. [237] The two facts had no causal thread between them, and that was exactly why she thought she could pull one. [257] Correct the catalog number, and the amaranth arrives; the amaranth needs different lamp mounts; the lamp mounts need a maintenance window; the maintenance window eats the afternoon that Ilya spent outside. [288] Probably. [289] She was, as he used to accuse her of being, confidently uncertain: sure of her method, unsure of her world. [309] Protocol required the edit be handwritten first, on paper, in the sender's own hand, because the review board believed that a slow hand thinks. [333] So Mira took Ilya's pen out of the drawer. [342] It was a dried-up fountain pen with an exotic nib, a left-oblique thing ground for a hand that wrote at a slant, and she had bent the nib herself, hurling it at a bulkhead the week after the funeral broadcast. [382] Now she had to unbend it. [388] She worked the tines apart with a brass shim under the desk lamp, breathing carefully, the way she had learned to breathe when reading a signal at the edge of noise. [419] Two hours to make it write again. [426] Ink, water, patience; a slow hand thinks. [433] What it thought about was the vetch. [440] Bitter vetch has to be threshed, and threshing is stupid, patient work: you beat the stalks, you winnow the chaff, you keep the small hard grain. [466] Ilya had built the flail out of a broken antenna strut and called it the most honest machine on the station. [487] She had spent fourteen months at that flail, and somewhere in the rhythm of it she had stopped wanting to be forgiven and started wanting only to thresh meaning from experience, to beat the whole bitter year until something small and hard and edible fell out. [533] Something had. [535] Here was the trouble with contingent relationships: they ran both directions. [546] The woman who could write the edit was a woman made by the mistake. [560] Correct the catalog number and the crates hold amaranth, the maintenance window opens, Ilya comes in for supper, and the woman at this desk never learns to unbend anything, never threshes, never sits up at 0300 with a resurrected pen, sure of her method and unsure of her world. [609] She would not be erased, exactly. [615] She would be unearned. [619] Mira dipped the nib and wrote, in Ilya's slant, the only message she could send that no board would ever have to review, because it changed nothing at all: Plant them anyway. [651] She fed the page into the transmitter and let the anomaly swallow it, and fourteen months upstream the words printed out beside a crate of wrong seed, where Ilya found them, read them aloud over her shoulder, and laughed. [658] So that was where he had gotten it. [666] The history she had been living was already edited, and she had been the editor, and the edit had been mercy. [687] It was a strange comfort, the kind that arrives sideways, like light around a heavy thing. [703] She capped the pen, went down to the ring, and picked up the flail. [717]
