Corvin Ashe arrived at the Vell twilight barge canal with eleven dead legends behind him and one antique pocket sundial in his coat. [23] He was an errant legend seeker in both senses: he wandered, and he had erred. [38] Eight years earlier he had published a monograph proving the Vell bell existed, citing a radio broadcast as his source, and the monograph had been the first brick in a wall that eventually fell on him. [74] Now he came not to prove the legend but to understand the architecture of lies: how a false thing is built, what carries its weight, why it does not simply collapse. [105]

The canal ran nine miles through peat flats, and the barges only moved at twilight, when the lock-keepers charged half rate. [126] At that hour the water went the color of tea and lamp oil, and the salvage crews worked by feel. [146] Corvin hired on with them. [151] The foreman, a thoughtfully clear woman named Junia Wray, explained the work in three sentences and never repeated herself. [170] 'You cannot see the bottom,' she said, 'so you learn it by training instincts, not eyes.' [186] For a season she made him drag a bare hook along the silt until his wrists could tell brass from bone from bedplate. [209] He learned that mud lies too, and that it lies consistently, which is what makes it readable. [226]

Every morning at five the canal radio read the Registry of Lost Things, forty years of it, in the voice of Ambrose Dole. [249] Dole had been the first to describe the bell, in 1961, and every teller since had borrowed his adjectives. [268] Corvin listened each dawn with the pocket sundial in his palm, because the sundial had been Dole's. [285] It had come to him at an auction of the dead announcer's effects: brass, hinged, engraved FOR THE HOURS YOU CANNOT SEE. [307] Corvin had taken it for a joke about radio, or about faith. [319] It was useless on the canal, where the sun set before the work began. [333]

By midwinter he had mapped the lie's foundations, and they were civic. [345] The bell story brought dredgers, and dredging kept the channel open; the open channel kept the barges, the barges kept the lock fees, the lock fees kept the radio tower lit. [376] Dole had not built a rumor; he had built a load-bearing arch, and a whole valley stood inside it. [395] Junia had known for years. [400] 'Everyone past sixty knows,' she said, 'and nobody says it, because the saying would sink us.' [416]

On the ninth of March the station closed. [424] Corvin woke at five to no Registry, no carrier hum, nothing. [435] He had never heard the canal by the silence of morning radio, and the silence had a shape. [453] He went out onto the towpath with the sundial open in his hand, and the low sun came over the peat and threw the gnomon's shadow across the dial. [482] Someone had scratched a second line into the brass, faint as a hair, and at that hour of that week the shadow lay exactly along it. [508] He turned the dial until the line pointed off across the water, and it pointed at mooring stone forty-one. [527] This was hindsight clarity of the cruelest kind: the instrument had been telling him for two years, and he had needed the dark to teach him how to read the light. [558]

He went down at noon with Junia holding the line. [568] His hands found it in four minutes, because his hands had been trained: a bell, bronze, small as a bucket, wired to a stone with a length of brass sundial chain. [599] Dole had hung it there himself, in 1961, where the current would knock it on winter nights. [616] The legend was a lie, and the lie had a hinge, and the hinge was a man who wanted his valley to keep breathing. [640]

Corvin surfaced into the flat white afternoon, and felt the serene upheaval of a life reorganizing itself without panic. [659] He could publish, and be right, and be the man who unroofed a canal. [673] Instead he did what salvage is for. [680] They raised the bell, cleaned it, and hung it on the dead radio tower where the wind could reach it. [700] Corvin wrote one page for the parish record, thoughtfully clear, naming Dole as the bell's founder and the canal as its keeper, and let the readers do their own arithmetic. [730]

The bell rings at twilight now, when the barges move, and nobody who hears it is being deceived. [748] Corvin still carries the sundial, still useless after dusk, and that is precisely why he keeps it. [765] Some things can only be read afterward, in the morning, by someone who has learned to wait. [782]
