Mirela Vosk kept the shards of everyone the town had thrown away. [12] She was the shard-mirror archivist, and her collection filled a forgotten library under the playground hill, where the shelves had swollen shut with damp and nobody came to complain about dust. [43] The boy arrived at the hush, when the empty playground swing above her drifted in the breeze and the whole valley seemed to hold one breath. [69] That was the only hour exile could be argued. [78] "You're late," she said, though he was not. [86] "They took my shadow," he said. [92] "They took your afternoon shadow and sealed it in glass, which is the polite way this town says get out," Mirela answered, and set a kettle on the burner without asking whether he wanted tea. [127] He held out the shard: a slice of mirror no longer than his forearm, and inside it his shadow lay stretched at four o'clock, knees bent, elbow crooked, as if forever leaning against a wall that had been demolished. [166] "Name," she said. [169] "Tobin Ail, and I broke the alderman's window." [177] "On purpose?" [179] "His son was inside it laughing at my sister." [188] "Good aim, poor arithmetic," Mirela said. [194] "Exile here is reversible, boy, but only if somebody annotates the record properly, and only within nine hushes." [212] She poured the tea, made him drink it, then took the cup and turned it slowly under her lamp. [231] Reading fortunes was a fraud, she told him; reading a person by analyzing the geometry of tea leaves was mathematics. [251] How a hand trembles, how long a mouth hesitates before swallowing, how heat and fear scatter sediment - the leaves settled into angles, and angles did not lie about a boy's odds. [282] "Three clusters, an acute wedge, one stray at the rim," she muttered. [294] "You'll survive the winter and you'll be insufferable about it." [304] Then, without meaning to, she began to walk backwards along the aisle, heels finding the gaps between crates, spine straight, eyes on him. [327] She always walked backwards when nervous; forty years of it had taught her the library's floor by memory. [345] "You're frightened," Tobin said. [349] "I'm irritated," she said, "and you will sit down." [358]

For eight hushes she taught him, and taught him badly on purpose. [370] She would not write his annotation for him. [378] She handed him the ledger, the pen, the cold facts of the alderman's window, and told him his sentences were mush, his grammar cowardly, his self-pity audible from the street. [408] When he cried she gave him more tea and said nothing kind, and when he slept she covered him with her own coat. [431] He learned what she wanted him to learn: that nobody was coming, and that this was survivable. [448] On the ninth hush the swing overhead began its slow drift and the shard on the table brightened. [466] Tobin wrote: "The window was between me and my sister, and I would break it again and pay for the glass." [487] The shadow in the glass unbent its elbow, stepped down off the wall that no longer existed, and slid across the floor to his heels. [512] "Adequate," Mirela said, which from her was a coronation. [521]

Tobin looked at the shelf behind her, at one shard set apart, its shadow small and young and sitting on a swing. [543] "Nine hushes," he said, "and yours is forty years past, so who annotates you?" [557] "Nobody, and that is the arrangement." [563] She began to move backwards toward the stacks and caught herself, furious at her own feet. [579] "They exiled a girl for reading fortunes in a magistrate's cup," she said. [592] "Nobody argued for her, so she came down here and became useful." [604] "Somebody has to be waiting when the next one falls through that door with a shard in his fist." [623] "That's not resilience; that's a hole you crawled into," Tobin said. [634] "Go home, Tobin Ail," she said, "before the hush ends and the town forgets it forgave you." [651] He went up the stairs into ordinary daylight, and the swing was still moving, and he did not look back. [671]

Later, sweeping, Mirela found her ledger open at her own page, forty years untouched, and beneath the sentence of exile, in a boy's stubborn hand: "She was provoked, she stayed anyway, and this should be argued at every hush until somebody listens." [713] The shard on the shelf did not open. [721] It was nine hushes too late, and nine hushes is the law. [733] Still, the small shadow inside it had turned its face toward the door, and Mirela Vosk, who did not weep, walked backwards the whole length of the library twice before she could bring herself to annotate the margin: "Received, filed, argument continuing."
