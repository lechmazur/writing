Snow fell on the silver courtyard the way ash falls on a sleeping city: politely, thoroughly, without apology. [18] Vesna crossed it barefoot, because the Collectors listened for boots, and she had trained her soles to be silent as held breath. [40] She was a courtesan of the Ninth House, and in nine years no client had touched her. [57] They came for the other thing she sold: the bodily poetic, a language of wrist and shoulder and turned chin, whole stanzas spoken without a single harvestable word. [85] In the Republic of Quiet, that was nearly treason. [94] Every evening at dusk the Choir began the harvest of echoes. [105] Copper funnels on every rooftop drew in the day's leftover sound, the arguments and laughter and small percussion of grief, and pressed all of it into the lullaby that poured from the civic bells until dawn. [141] People slept well. [144] People woke agreeable. [147] People forgot, gently, whatever they had once wanted enough to shout about. [159] The serenity of the city was total, and it was the most alarming thing Vesna had ever known. [177] Her mother's voice had gone into the funnels eleven winters ago. [188] Vesna still heard fragments of it in the bells, sanded smooth, folded into the chorus that told the district to rest, rest, everything is arranged. [213] She had decided, without drama, to get it back. [222] At the courtyard's center stood the prism sanctuary, a lantern-sized cage of cut glass that the old astronomers had left behind. [243] Under starlight it did what no funnel could: it split a captured sound into its true colors and let it live again, unsanded, exactly as first spoken. [270] The Choir had never destroyed it, because it could not be opened by force, only unlocked by untangling threads. [289] Its lock was a knot of nine silk strands, and the astronomers had built it so that only patient hands, hands that wanted nothing quickly, could persuade it open. [318] Nine years of chastity, it turned out, was excellent training. [328] She knelt in the snow and began. [335] The Collectors patrolled the arcades, hooded, their listening horns turning like sunflowers. [347] They could hear a whisper through a wall, a heartbeat through a coat. [360] But there was one interval they could not enter, a seam in the fabric of listening: the space between sigh and smile. [382] In that half-second the body makes no sound at all, and the horns register only warmth. [398] Vesna had spent her career living in that seam, and she worked the knot in installments: sigh, loosen, smile, wait. [418] Snow gathered on her shoulders like a second, whiter gown. [428] The first strand came free between a sigh and a smile, and the sanctuary hummed one clean note of blue. [448] By the fifth strand she was shaking, and shaking made noise, so she turned the shaking into dance. [466] A Collector paused at the gate and watched a woman kneeling in a silver courtyard, performing something graceful and meaningless, and moved on. [489] Nothing is safer in the Republic of Quiet than beauty that says nothing. [502] The ninth strand slipped loose at midnight. [509] The prism opened like a flower made of knives, and starlight fell through it, and the courtyard filled with color that had been sound. [533] Her mother's voice came out whole. [539] It was not a lullaby. [544] It was an argument, unfinished, furious and laughing, about the price of bread and the cowardice of neighbors and the necessity of saying true things out loud. [571] Vesna listened to all of it, twice. [578] Then she did the thing she had come to do. [588] She turned the open prism toward the copper funnels and let it sing. [601] The harvest ran backward for eleven seconds. [608] Across four districts the bells stuttered, and instead of rest, rest, everything is arranged, the city heard a dead woman shouting about bread. [631] Ten thousand sleepers woke with their own unfinished arguments in their mouths. [643] Somewhere a window opened, and someone answered her. [651] The Collectors came running, of course, all their horns swiveling toward the silver courtyard. [665] Vesna stood, brushed the snow from her knees, and arranged her body into its last poem: spine straight, palms open, chin lifted the way her mother's had been lifted. [694] It said, plainly, to anyone who had learned to read: I defy the lullaby, and so may you. [712] They took her quietly. [716] They always did everything quietly. [721] But they could not gather the strands back into a knot, and no one in the Republic ever learned how to close a prism, only how to open one. [750] By spring, the bells had learned nine new voices, and none of them were sleepy. [765]
