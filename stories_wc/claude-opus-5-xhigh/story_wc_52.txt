The kettle in Marta's loft took four minutes to boil, and she had long ago decided that four minutes was exactly as much grief as any woman needed in a day. [31] Rain beaded the skylight above her workbench, each drop holding a coin of gray light before sliding off to join the gutter. [53] Here and gone, here and gone; the roof kept saying it, so she did not have to. [70] She set the kettle on the ring, struck the match, and picked up the ash wood carving blank she had refused to touch for eleven months. [96] Bela had cut it himself from the tree behind the winter barns, sanded the ends smooth, and left it on the shelf like a promise. [121] Then a lion named Pasha, who had loved him for nine years, forgot for four seconds that he loved him. [141] The circus paid for the funeral and called Marta the lion tamer's widow in the programme, as though it were a job title, which by then it was. [169] She had inherited his tools, his ledger, and his amber. [179] The amber was the size of a plum, rust-colored, with a wasp inside it, wings spread, caught mid-decision forty million years ago. [201] Bela used to hold it up to the lamp before every show and say that this was the only honest way to keep anything: through crystallized time, whole and useless and yours. [233] Marta held it to the lamp now while the kettle began to tick. [246] Inside the ledger, in her own hand, was the work she had set herself after the funeral: to harvest lessons from loss, one line at a time, because she could not bear for so much pain to yield nothing but pain. [287] Lesson one: a cage does not contain a lion, only your belief about the lion. [302] Lesson six: love is not a method of safety. [311] Lesson nineteen: keep the stool between you and the thing you adore. [323] The neighbor downstairs, who heard her hammering at odd hours, once called her hauntingly practical. [338] Marta had thanked her for it and meant it. [347]

The knock came at the third minute. [354] Ilka stood on the stairs with rain in her hair and a contract in her fist, seventeen years old, wanting to be told she was right. [380] The company had offered her the cats. [387] "They want an answer tonight," Ilka said. [394] "Everyone says I should ask you, because you'll talk me out of it." [407] Marta looked at the skylight, at the beads shivering and letting go. [419] She thought about the four seconds, and about the nine years before them, and how the ledger had never once, in all its lines, told her that Bela should have chosen otherwise. [451] That was the lesson she had been avoiding for eleven months, and the blank in her hand had been waiting to hold it. [474] "Sit down," she said. [478] "The water's nearly on." [482]

She took up the small knife and began to work the ash while the kettle climbed toward its whistle. [501] Four minutes was not enough to carve a lion, so she carved what fit: a wasp, wings spread, mid-decision. [520] The curls of pale wood fell into her lap and smelled faintly of the winter barns. [536] "I won't talk you out of it," Marta said. [545] "I'll tell you three things instead, and you'll write them down." [556] "Never let the cage do your thinking; never turn your back on what loves you, because love forgets; and give yourself four minutes a day to be wrecked, then get up and feed them." [590] Ilka wrote them on the back of the contract. [599] "That's all?" she asked. [603] "It's not a warning," Marta said. [609] "It's an inheritance." [612]

The kettle screamed, and she lifted it, and the steam went up through the rust-colored light under the skylight like something being released. [635] She poured two cups, and her four minutes were over, and she found she was not wrecked. [652] She set the little ash wasp in Ilka's palm beside the teacup. [664] "It won't keep you safe," she said, "it will only remind you that everything worth standing near is here and gone, and that you were there for the here." [693] Ilka closed her hand around it. [699] Outside, the rain thinned; the drops on the glass grew slow and fat and let go one by one, and Marta drank her tea in the rust-hued peace of a room that had finally learned what it could contain. [738] The amber sat on the sill, holding its one small moment, and the loft held the rest. [755]
