The county keeps its foster care file room in the basement of the old courthouse, where the pipes sweat and the paper drinks. [23] I have been its archivist for nineteen years. [31] People assume I stayed because I love order. [39] I stayed because paper does not leave in the night, and because at nineteen I signed a surrender form in a room like this one and have never been permitted to learn what became of the boy. [76] A broken heart is a filing problem you cannot solve: one sealed document, another state, no standing. [93] So I magnify small kindnesses instead. [99] It is the only work this room permits. [107]

Theo Marsh came down my stairs three weeks after the emancipation, twenty years old, carrying a records request and a coffee cup he never drank from. [133] Aged out, released, unclaimed by any adult in the state's meaning of the word. [147] He set something on my counter before he said hello: a bronze infantryman's button, green in the grooves, its eagle worn nearly smooth. [170] "It was sewn into the hem of the blanket I arrived in," he said. [184] "Nobody in twenty years could tell me whose it was." [194]

I answered him the way I have learned to answer, tactfully hopeful, which down here is the only honest register: the button's first history is probably beyond recovery, but its second history lives on my shelves, and that one I could show him today. [238]

His file was four inches thick and eight placements long. [248] Before I pulled it, I made him look at the shelf. [259] Nineteen years down here teaches you to read the institution through accumulated dust patterns. [273] Dust settles evenly over paper nobody wants; where a folder is drawn out and pushed back, it leaves a pale rectangle, and if that happens once a year for a decade the rectangle stays clean while the shelf around it grays into felt. [316] Theo's rectangle was the cleanest on the row. [324] Someone had been visiting him for years, and I had known the pattern long before I knew his face. [343]

The sign-out card named her. [348] R. Okonkwo, caseworker, retired in 2015, coming back every February ninth, his intake date, until last winter. [365] Each visit she added something small enough that no one audited it: a photograph, a school drawing, a note in blue ink. [387] And in nine different hands across sixteen years, the button. [397]

Intake, 2004: infant arrived with one uniform button, hem of blanket, do not discard. [411] 2009: sewed button back onto liner, green thread, he holds it to sleep. [424] 2013: button confiscated at school as contraband, retrieved same day by this worker. [437] 2018: informed new placement that the button is his property, not a toy, not to be traded. [454] Theo read them standing, then sat down like a man whose knees had lost an argument. [470]

There was one more thing, filed flat in the oversize drawer where hearing exhibits go to be forgotten. [488] I brought it to the table and asked him to hold one end while I unfurled it: six feet of butcher paper, pencil marks climbing it like a ladder. [517] A growth chart from a foster house on Delaney Street, entered as evidence in 2016 by a woman defending her license, showing every child she had ever measured against her kitchen doorframe. [549] Theo appeared four times, eleven and twelve years old, and beside the fourth mark she had written: T. still growing, still ours, whatever the county says. [575]

He asked whether Mrs. Okonkwo knew where the button came from. [586] I said probably not, that some histories stay unknown no matter how carefully you examine them, and that the examining is not therefore wasted. [610] Then I wrote down the address of the care home on Fifth, because she is eighty-one and alive and has stopped being able to manage my stairs. [637]

Before he left, Theo asked if he could add a document. [648] Nobody has ever asked me that. [654] He wrote his new address on a half sheet in blue ink, and under it: if anyone comes looking, I am findable now. [677] I stamped it, logged it, and did not reshelve the folder. [688] It sits on my desk in the lamplight, gathering nothing. [698]

Tomorrow I file my own request again, to the sealed room in the other state, and they will refuse me again, tactfully. [720] Still, I have learned what a basement can prove. [729] Not where a person comes from; only every hand that refused to lose him. [743]
