Everyone new to the staircase asks which way leads out, and I tell them the truth: both directions, forever. [19] That is not despair. [23] It is the first line of a proof. [31] I have been surveying these steps since Tomas let go of my hand, or rather since I did not take his, which is the distinction my whole life now rests on. [62] He stood on a landing that had a door in it -- the only door I have ever seen here -- and he said, "Now, Iris, now," and I said, "Wait, let me measure it." [95] The door closed on an empty frame of light, and the steps resumed their patient upward grinding, and I have been measuring ever since. [119]

What I want is not escape, exactly. [126] I want to hold the texture of that missed moment in my hands -- the grain of it, the weight, the exact temperature of the second I chose arithmetic over my brother. [157] You cannot mourn what you cannot describe. [164] So I describe it through mathematical proofs, which are the only instruments that survive down here. [180] Paper rots, lamps drink themselves dry, but a theorem stays sharp forever. [192]

The staircase is a perpetual motion machine, and I am its fuel. [204] Every riser you climb gives the mechanism a little of your heat; the steps unspool below you and re-spool above, so the labor returns to itself and nothing is ever spent but you. [237] That is the trap and also the flaw, because true perpetual motion is a lie, and every lie has a seam. [258]

I carry a pair of dice, one of them chipped at a corner where Tomas dropped it on the landing. [278] For years I rolled them to choose my direction, up on evens, down on odds, refusing to let hope steer me. [299] Ten thousand rolls later I noticed the chipped die favored the four by a hair -- a bias of about one part in ninety. [322] A flawed object cannot be fair, and a flawless machine cannot exist. [334] That sentence took me nine years to prove, and the proof is beautiful, and it condemns this place. [352]

Here is the shape of it, stripped of notation. [361] If the stair conserves nothing and returns everything, its curvature must be constant; if its curvature is constant, then the landing where the door appeared cannot recur; but I have counted landings, and they recur, thirteen thousand steps apart, with the same scuff of Tomas's boot on the ninth riser. [411] Contradiction. [412] Therefore the stair repeats imperfectly, and where imperfection accumulates, a rupture is not merely possible but obligatory. [429]

The others here call me grim. [435] I say the machine is cruel, the climb is endless, my brother is gone, and none of that changes the arithmetic: the seam exists, and I will find it. [464] Grim, yes; hopeless, no. [468] I have learned to be balancedly daring -- to wager exactly as much as a proof will support and not one riser more. [490] Wild men here have leapt over the banister into the white nothing and simply appeared four flights up, screaming. [509] Cowards sit and become part of the masonry. [517] I calculate, then I risk. [522]

Yesterday the error term in my count crossed the threshold, and I walked to the landing at step 1,703,000, where the scuff waited, and I knelt and set the chipped die on the ninth riser with the four facing up. [562] A biased object introduced into a system that cannot tolerate bias: that is the whole method, one flaw offered to another. [583]

The stair shuddered like a struck bell. [590] The seam opened along the wall with a sound like paper tearing underwater, and through it came not daylight but a smell -- cold stone, lamp oil, wool damp from rain. [620] It was the exact air of the landing where he said now. [632] I put my hand into the rupture and felt it entirely: the grain, the weight, the temperature of a chance declining to wait for me. [657]

It was not a door. [662] It was a description, which is what I asked for, and I have never been able to want a thing without measuring it first. [686]

So: the staircase is infinite, and I remain inside it, and the seam is already knitting shut. [703] But it opened. [706] A perpetual machine ruptured because one small chipped thing refused to be fair, and I am keeping the other die. [726] Tomorrow I start the second proof, the one about the door, and I tell the newcomers what I now believe with my whole grim heart: both directions, forever -- until they aren't. [757]
