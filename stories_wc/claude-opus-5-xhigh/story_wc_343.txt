The airship Perihelion was ninety-one years old, and she complained about it in every joint. [15] Her envelope was patched silk, her gondola varnished pine, and her cargo deck still wore the chalk hopscotch board that some long-grown ship's child had drawn there, dusty and half-scuffed, which nobody would sweep away. [50] Teodora, apprentice to the Guild's last living map-maker, swept around it twice a day and studied it the rest of the time. [72] She was diligently curious in the way that annoyed no one and worried everyone. [86] The crew loved her; they simply could not follow her. [96] That was the shape of her life aboard: eleven friends, four meals a day, songs after the evening trim, and absolutely nobody to talk to about the hopscotch board. [125]

It had numbers where numbers belonged, one through nine, but under the chalk dust someone had inked small marks beside each square: a crescent, a stag, a bell. [153] Teodora had been noting them for a year, square by square, in the back of her sketchbook. [170] The marks were not decoration; they were a sequence, and sequences want somewhere to go. [185]

The key came from the ship's library, from a polyglot's phrasebook that had belonged to Halbrecht, the cartographer who vanished from this very deck in her grandmother's time. [213] Nine languages, nine hundred phrases, and only one dog-ear. [222] The dog-ear was the whole message. [228] Folded to the page of nautical courtesies, its crease ran across a line in Rhennish: "We beg pardon, we cannot come to the light." [252] The point of the fold aimed at a marginal note in Halbrecht's hand: a crescent, a stag, a bell. [271]

So the board was a map, and Halbrecht had left it low to the deck where only a child, or a sweeper, would ever read it. [297] The crescent was Crescent Fen; the stag was the antlered ridge above Voss; the bell was Bellmouth, where the river went underground. [319] Nine squares, nine bearings, hopped in order, converging on an unnamed hollow in the chalk downs. [335] Teodora wanted, more than sleep or company, to chart the next great discovery, and here it was waiting under her broom. [356]

She brought it to Captain Ilve at dawn, in October, amid the starling murmurations that made the sky over the downs look like ink poured into water. [383] Ilve listened the way she always did, kindly and from a great distance. [396] Then Teodora pointed, because the birds were doing her arguing for her. [408] Every flock that year had bent around one hollow, as though the ground there were a hot stone. [426]

They descended at noon, valves hissing, the Perihelion's old shadow sliding down the grass. [440] In the hollow lay a lens of still water, and beneath the water lay light. [455] Not reflected light: a stranded luminance, cold and green-gold, pooled in the chalk like something that had come down long ago and could not get back up. [482] It pulsed slowly, in nines. [487] On the bank sat Halbrecht's brass theodolite, green with sixty years of weather, and her boots, and nothing else. [506]

Teodora understood the dog-ear then. [511] "We cannot come to the light" was not a refusal; it was a warning folded in half, the politest sentence Halbrecht could find in nine languages. [537] So Teodora did not go down to it. [545] She stood on the rim with eleven friends breathing behind her, and she took out her pen, and she made her note. [567] Latitude, longitude, depth, color, period of pulse, and the birds' avoidance radius, measured in wingbeats. [582] Beneath it she wrote the hopscotch sequence, plainly, so the next apprentice would not need a broom. [599]

Ilve read over her shoulder and said, at last, from no distance at all, "Show me the bit about the bell." [620] They talked until the sun went orange, all of them, crowding the rim. [633] The Perihelion lifted at dusk, groaning, and the starlings closed over the hollow behind her like a shutter. [651]

The chart went into the Guild's great atlas that winter under a name Teodora chose carefully: Halbrecht's Patience. [669] And on the cargo deck, the hopscotch board stayed exactly where it was, dust and all, because Teodora had begun adding squares of her own. [694] Ten, eleven, twelve: a crescent, a stag, a bell, and then a shutter of birds. [709] The crew hopped it, badly, laughing, and for the first time she did not mind being followed slowly. [727]
