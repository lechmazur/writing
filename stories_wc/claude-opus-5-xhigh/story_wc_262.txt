The Alhambra was to be gutted on Monday, so on Sunday night I let the crystal gazer into the projection booth. [21] Her name was Vesna, and she came up the iron stairs without touching the rail, as if she had memorized them from the street. [45] She was spatially imaginative in a way I have never seen since; she could stand in a lobby and describe the shape of a room three floors above her, including the water stain. [78] Outside, thunder moved over the roof like furniture being shifted in an upstairs apartment, hushed by four feet of Spanish tile. [99] I had asked her here because of Ida Kellerman, who ran this booth for thirty-one years and died in it, sitting up, the projector still turning. [125] In her locker I had found ninety-four goodbye letters, all addressed to a man named Aurelio Vance, all stamped, none mailed. [146] And I had found the film strip: six frames of a young man on a fire escape, spliced from nothing, belonging to no picture I could name. [173] Vesna did not use a crystal ball for this, though she carried one in a sock, warm as an egg. [193] "Glass is for looking forward," she said. "Objects are for looking back. This is psychometry; I read with my hands." [213] She asked me to hum something, anything, to keep the room from listening too hard. [228] So I gave her the only song in my head, a thing Ida used to sing at the rewind bench, and found that the words had outlived the melody. [257] Blue hours, blue door, I'm no good at leaving -- the lyrics went hunting through the booth for a tune to live in, and everything that happened next happened inside that search. [288] Vesna took the film strip between her palms and did not squeeze it, only closed her hands and held them at her sternum, the way you incubate an egg or a grudge. [320] "It has to warm," she said. "Cold things lie." [329] Thunder again, low, polite, like an audience that has agreed not to cough. [342] When she spoke, she spoke in floor plans. [350] "There's a hollow behind you," she said. "Not the wall -- the wall's twin. Two skins with a gap of maybe nine inches, and the gap runs the length of the booth." [381] I told her the booth was built double for fire, back when nitrate stock could turn a palace into a chimney in ninety seconds. [405] "Then she chose well," Vesna said, and told me what her hands were getting. [419] Aurelio Vance had been a soldier, then a projectionist's apprentice, then a name on a ship's manifest; he had asked Ida to come with him in 1951 and she had stayed to keep her mother. [454] The six frames were her own: she had filmed him on the fire escape with a borrowed camera and cut the frames out of the rest of her life. [483] Every night, before the feature, she spliced them into the leader where no audience would notice, and every night she wrote him a letter that ended the same way, and every night she did not send it. [520] "Where does she put it?" Vesna asked, and I understood she had been asking that question her whole working life. [540] That was why she took jobs like mine, in condemned buildings, for whatever was in the register: she wanted to find where unused love is stored. [566] She had buried a husband at twenty-six and had never once believed that what she still felt for him had simply evaporated. [588] "It goes somewhere," she said. "Somebody's holding it. I want the address." [600] We pried the access panel by the rewind bench and reached into the nine inches Ida had chosen. [618] The gap was packed, floor to shoulder height, with film cans and letters, forty years of both, warm from the arc lamp on the other side of the skin. [647] Nine inches wide, sixty feet long, insulated, dark: an incubator. [657] Vesna knelt in it and laughed, and the laugh cracked in the middle. [670] "This is it," she said. "This is where. Not heaven. A wall in a movie house." [686] Outside, the thunder let go at last, and even that arrived softened, a sound like applause heard through a curtain. [706] We threaded the six frames onto the machine and ran them for an empty house, over and over, a young man on a fire escape lifting his hand. [734] And somewhere in the fourth pass the tune came back to me and the words dropped into it like a key into a lock, and I sang it, badly, and Vesna wrote the address of the wall in her book, and the search was over. [779] On Monday they gutted the Alhambra; I kept the letters, and I finally mailed one. [794]
