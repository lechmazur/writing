After numbers changed meaning, nobody trusted a clock again. [9] Seven could mean a season, or a stone, or the smell of rain on gravel, depending on who read it. [29] So the valley hired a timekeeper, and the valley hired Mira, because Mira was already living on borrowed time. [48] Doctors had given her a number once, back when numbers meant things, and she had outlived it by three winters. [68] Her office was the emerald silo at the edge of the Halloran farm, painted that green in a hopeful decade and never repainted. [91] Inside, she kept time the only way left: by texture. [101] A candle burning down one finger-width was a morning. [110] Barley settling in the bins made a dry whisper that meant an hour had gone somewhere. [126] She wanted, more than she wanted extra years, to understand the texture of time passing, the grain of it under a thumb. [148]

In the spring the Halloran boys dug up the old time capsule, and it came out of the mud damaged. [168] The seal had failed decades ago. [173] Letters had fused into a gray brick; photographs were pale ghosts of themselves; a cassette had swollen like a drowned thing. [194] Only two objects survived: a cremation urn fragment, curved and chalk-white, and a stack of newspaper clippings. [211] The clippings were garage sale listings, twenty-nine of them, all from the same seller. [225] Mira read them in the silo by candlelight, and by the fourth she understood that someone had been embedding messages in garage sale item descriptions. [250] "Brass birdcage, no bird, hinge sings at dusk." [258] "Child's globe, ocean rubbed away where a hand kept returning." [268] "Urn, broken, will not sell, ask me why." [276]

The first letters of the listings, read in order, spelled a name she knew from the town register: Teodor Vance, timekeeper, deceased. [298] He had held her post before the capsule was buried, and he had been the one to bury it. [317] The last listing, dated the week he died, described "a fragment of my wife, priced at whatever you can carry, buyer must rediscover the rest." [342]

Mira spent the summer doing exactly that. [348] She walked to every address in the clippings, farmhouse after farmhouse, and asked what people had bought from the old man with the folding tables. [373] They remembered him better than they remembered the year. [382] Each buyer had received a piece of the same urn, wrapped in newsprint, with a line of writing on the inside of the paper. [406] Twenty-nine lines, twenty-nine houses, one sentence broken and scattered on purpose, because Teodor had known the capsule would rot and had wanted a message that lived in people instead. [435]

By autumn Mira had the whole thing on a single sheet, and she carried the pieces of the urn back to the emerald silo in a barley sack. [463] The valley had begun to follow her, quietly, the way people follow anticipated mysteries: the Halloran boys, the woman who sold eggs, the doctor who had once given her a number. [494] They stood in the green dark while she fitted the shards together on the floor. [509] The urn did not hold ashes; it had held them once, and Teodor had emptied it into the field before he broke it. [532] The barley that whispered her hours had grown out of his wife. [544] The sentence, reassembled, read: TIME IS NOT COUNTED, IT IS HANDED FROM ONE PAIR OF HANDS TO THE NEXT, SO KEEP GIVING IT AWAY. [568]

Mira laughed, and the laugh surprised her, because she had spent three borrowed winters bracing for an answer with more arithmetic in it. [591] Standing there with the shard warm in her palm, she felt herself go suddenly timeless, not deathless, only unhooked, a woman no longer subtracting. [615] That was the texture she had wanted: not sand, not ticking, but weight passed hand to hand, and warm on arrival. [636]

She gave the pieces back, one to each family, and told them to sell them someday at their own tables, wrapped in newsprint, with a line inside. [663] The valley keeps time by that now. [670] Nobody asks what year it is; they ask who is holding the fragment, and how long since it moved. [689] Mira died in the silo two harvests later, with the candle a finger-width down, and the barley whispering. [707] The new timekeeper, a Halloran boy grown tall, buried a fresh capsule that same week, and drilled a hole in the bottom of it on purpose. [733] Let it rot, he told the valley; the message will be out walking. [746]
