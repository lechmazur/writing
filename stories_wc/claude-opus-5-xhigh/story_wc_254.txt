The ban lifted at dusk, and Vesna walked out onto the frozen lake for the first time in eleven years. [20] Behind her rose the cobalt atrium, its glass walls holding the last blue of the sky like water in cupped hands. [41] Inside, the humming quartz colonnade had begun to warm, twelve pillars ringing a floor of black ice, each one singing a note too low to name. [67] She was a cloud-shadow poet, the last one licensed before the Assembly outlawed the craft. [82] Her art needed only three things: cloud, low sun, and a surface patient enough to hold a moving shadow. [101] She had spent her youth composing on the lake's white page, arranging her body and her mirrors so that the shadows of passing clouds fell into stanzas. [128] The Assembly had not banned the poems for what they said, but for what they did. [144] Cloud-shadow verse worked through dissolving boundaries, and a reader who stood too long inside it stopped feeling where her skin ended and the weather began. [169] Officials called this trespass; Vesna had always called it the point. [180]

Tomas, the atrium's young archivist, met her at the threshold with a lantern and an apology. [196] Moths had come in with the thaw crews and now circled his light, and the whole room held that lantern-moth hush, soft, powdery, expectant. [220] "The Assembly will permit one performance," he said, "if you paraphrase it first." [233] They wanted the poem rendered into plain prose, filed, and read aloud beforehand, so that no listener would be surprised into dissolving. [255] Vesna laughed, and the colonnade shifted its hum a quarter tone, the way it did when someone told it something true. [276] She was aesthetically driven and never pretended otherwise; she had gone to prison over a comma of shadow, over the width of one gray edge. [301] A paraphrase would keep her free and keep the poem harmless, which amounted to the same murder twice. [319] Still, she took Tomas's pen. [324]

For three hours she wrote in the cobalt atrium while the lake groaned under the moon, and every sentence she made was a small betrayal she performed carefully, the way one washes a body. [358] "This poem describes a woman becoming indistinguishable from a cloud," she wrote, and hated it, and kept it. [376]

At dawn the Assembly's witnesses arrived in gray coats and sat on the benches ringing the black-ice floor. [394] Tomas read the paraphrase aloud in a flat clerk's voice, and it was, she admitted, accurate and completely dead. [413] Then the sun came up over the eastern ridge at the shallow angle she had waited eleven years for. [432] Light entered the quartz colonnade and split, and the pillars threw their hum into the air as visible ripples. [451] Outside, a low bank of cloud crossed the frozen lake, and its shadow slid through the atrium's glass and onto the floor. [473] Vesna stepped into the shadow and began. [479]

She used no words; she moved, and the shadow moved, and the twelve notes bent around her like reeds. [498] The witnesses had been prepared for the paraphrase; nothing prepares anyone for the thing itself. [513] One by one they felt the edges of themselves go soft, felt the cold of the lake arrive in their own knees, felt the cloud's slow thought pass through their chests. [544] A woman in a gray coat put her hand flat on the ice and wept without knowing why. [562]

In the middle of it Vesna felt what she had come for: a tremor of certainty, small and physical, starting in the soles of her feet. [588] It came partly from the quartz and partly from the ice and mostly from herself, and it said: go further. [608] There was a line the old license had drawn: a poet composed outside the ring of pillars and never entered it, because the inner circle belonged to the colonnade alone. [638] Her whole life had been the wish to stride through boundaries once thought sacred, and here was the last of them, twelve pillars wide. [662] She walked in. [665]

The hum closed over her head like water, and the cloud-shadow followed, and for a moment there was no Vesna in the atrium at all, only weather thinking in a ring of stone. [698] Then the cloud passed, the shadow slid off toward the far shore, and she was standing among the pillars with frost in her eyebrows, whole, ordinary, and permitted. [726]

Tomas came to her with the filed paraphrase in his hand and, without ceremony, tore it in half. [744] "It was wrong," he said. [749] "I know," Vesna said, "but it got me through the door." [760] Behind them the colonnade hummed on, patient as a held breath, and the moths settled on the cold blue glass to wait for the next cloud. [786]
