Vesna the clairvoyant had read the futures of nine hundred travelers, and never once her own. [16] She lived in the Singing Deep, a crystal cave that hummed whenever water touched it, and water touched it always. [36] The drip from the high vault was steadily unpredictable: the same weight of water every hour, never on the same beat twice. [58] That was why the cave's music could not be memorized, and why the unseen forces loved it. [75] They entered through overlapping shadows, in the instant when two lantern shadows crossed and made a doorway of darkness. [94] Vesna kept only one lamp for that reason, though the crystals gave her a bright darkness of their own, a glow like moonlight trapped in salt. [120] In a rimstone basin at the cave's heart lay the cave pearl, round and white, grown grain by grain over four hundred years of restless dripping. [146] It was not a prophecy stone. [152] Held in the palm, it did something humbler and stranger: it magnified everyday magic. [166] A candle lit beside it warmed a whole chamber; a lullaby sung over it knitted a fever shut; bread rose twice as tall. [189] Vesna had spent her life on that work, because she distrusted miracles and believed the world was already sufficiently strange, only too quiet about it. [214]

She foresaw the pilgrim three winters before he came. [223] She saw the mud on his boots, the split in his walking staff, the coin he would offer and the question he would ask about his drowned brother. [251] After the pilgrim arrived, everything she had seen happened exactly as seen, and then it kept going. [268] He set down his lantern beside hers. [275] Two lights, two shadows, one doorway. [281] "Don't," Vesna said, too late, and the crystals changed key. [291] The cave's hum dropped into a chord she had never heard in forty years of listening, and something stood in the overlap where the shadows agreed. [317] It had no face; it had a shape like a person remembered badly. [330] The pilgrim did not flinch, which told her he had come for this and not for her. [347]

"You never saw us," the shape said, in the voice of the drip, steady and unguessable. [363] "Because we are what does the seeing." [370] Vesna understood then why her own future had always been a blank wall. [383] She had been the window, and no window sees itself. [393] The unseen forces had lodged in her since girlhood, feeding her the mud on strangers' boots, and in exchange they had waited for a body that would consent. [421]

"The pearl is empty," the pilgrim said gently. [429] "Four hundred years of growing, and nothing to inhabit it." [439] "My brother drowned because I had no small magic to spare that night, and no oracle would waste a prophecy on a fisherman." [462] "You wanted to magnify everyday magic; the pearl can carry a voice, and a voice in the crystal reaches every village the river touches." [486] "But it needs a seer to sit inside it forever, and I could not ask, so I brought the ones who could." [508]

Vesna laughed, and the laugh came out bright in the dark, which is the only way anything comes out down there. [529] She had not seen this coming, and she found, to her surprise, that she was grateful. [545] A future she could not read was the first honest gift anyone had brought her. [560] She picked up the cave pearl, warm as a held egg, and stepped into the seam where the two shadows crossed. [581] The chord resolved. [584]

Afterward the pilgrim carried the pearl out and set it in the shallows below the falls, where the water is steadily unpredictable and the cave's song leaks downstream. [612] Now the fishermen say their nets mend a little faster, their lamps burn a little longer, their fevers break by morning. [633] Nobody prophesies anymore; nobody needs to. [639] And on nights when two lanterns pass on the towpath and their shadows briefly agree, a woman's voice comes up out of the pearl, cheerful and unhurried, telling whoever is nearest something small and useful and true. [676] She never says what will happen. [682] She only says what is already there, waiting to be made larger. [694]
