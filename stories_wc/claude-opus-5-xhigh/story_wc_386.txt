The ferry went down in the fjord on a windless night, and the company said the helmsman was drunk. [19] Sivert Aune had not been drunk; he had been dead of a heart attack for eleven minutes before the hull touched rock, and only I knew it, because he told me himself. [51] The dead are poor witnesses at an inquiry, so I learned long ago to weave truth carefully, thread by thread, until even the living could hold it. [78] I am a wreck-diver by trade and a ghost whisperer by accident. [90] Fifty feet down, portholes become windows of connection: I press my mask to the glass and the drowned press back, and for a moment we share one small circle of borrowed light. [122] They cannot shout. [125] They can only appear, briefly, in the dreams of strangers who never met them. [139] Cameo appearances, I call them, walk-on parts: the woman in the yellow raincoat crossing the back of somebody's dream about a school exam. [162] For nine months I collected them, by mail, by telephone, by sitting in fishermen's kitchens with a notebook while their wives described faces they had no reason to invent. [191] Analyzing cameo appearances in strangers' dreams is not science, but it is arithmetic of a kind. [207] Ninety-one passengers died. [210] Ninety-one faces recurred, in dreams reported from four countries. [219] Sixty-three of them carried the same detail: smoke, and a smell of burning plastic, before any water at all. [238] That was the thread I needed, and I followed it back into the cold. [252]

The wreck lies on its starboard side beneath the glacier's snout, where the meltwater keeps the visibility at the edge of nothing. [274] In the aft lounge I found what the fire had left: a child's snow globe, melted out of round, its glass slumped like a closed eyelid, the plastic base fused to the carpet. [307] Water does not melt glass. [312] The company had sworn there was no fire. [320] I cut the globe free and carried it up through eighty feet of green dark, the one pure thing I ever salvaged from that wreckage: a small dead world a girl named Kirsten had once shaken to make it snow. [360] She appeared in nineteen dreams, always in the doorway, always pointing down the corridor toward the engine room. [378]

Above the fjord there is a shrine carved into the glacier ice, three centuries old, kept by nobody and visited by everyone who has lost someone to that water. [407] You crawl in, light a candle, and the blue walls take the flame and pass it along like gossip until the whole chamber glows with light it did not make. [437] Borrowed starlight, the old women call it. [444] That is where I laid out my case, on the ice altar: ninety-one names, nineteen dreams of a girl pointing, sixty-three noses that smelled burning plastic, and one melted snow globe. [475]

The inquiry lawyer came up the moraine in city shoes because I would not meet him anywhere else. [493] I wanted him kneeling in a place where the dead are the landlords. [506] He said dreams were not admissible. [512] I agreed, and told him dreams were only the map; the globe was the ground. [527] Nineteen dreams had told me where to cut a hole in the hull, and the hole exposed a switchboard blackened for four meters in every direction, wiring the company had certified new eight days before departure. [563] Expose the wiring and you expose the invoice; expose the invoice and you expose the man who signed it. [582]

The lawyer looked a long time at the slumped glass, then at the candle, then at me. [599] "Sivert Aune," I said, "was steering a burning ship with a stopped heart." [612] "His widow has been spat at in the co-op for two years." [624] He took the globe in both hands, the way you take something from a child, and said he would need the notebooks too. [647]

The ruling came in autumn: negligence, fine, a name given back. [658] Kirsten's mother asked for nothing from the settlement except the globe, and I carried it to her myself. [676] She set it on the windowsill, where it cannot snow anymore, where it only catches whatever light the evening lends it. [697] I still dive. [700] I still crawl into the ice and light my candle and wait at that cold window for the ones who have something to say. [724] They come as they always come, borrowing our sleep the way ice borrows starlight, asking only that someone carry the small clean thing up into the air. [751]
