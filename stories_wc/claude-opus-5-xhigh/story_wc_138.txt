Vashti had outlived the invention of boredom, and then the word for it, and then the language that held the word. [21] She kept her house inside the magnetosphere, a prismatic escondite of leaning glass, and she reached it the only way anything could: through magnetic fields, folded like a swimmer folding water. [52] Light came in white and left in sevens. [60] Nothing there had ever needed a door. [67] For eleven thousand years she had collected farewells the way other people collect coins: the hand-kiss of a Sumerian bricklayer, the flat nod of a Danish girl on a pier, the wet cough of a soldier who called her mother by mistake. [109] She had said goodbye ten thousand times and never once learned what was on the other side of it. [128] That was the whole of her hunger now: to discover what happens after goodbye. [142] Which was why she had spent two centuries in quiet intrigue, trading rumors with grave-robbers and star-charters, until a Basque smuggler sold her the fragment. [167] It was a cursed amulet fragment no wider than a thumbnail, a chip off something that had once hung around the neck of a god who had wanted, very badly, to stop. [199] The curse was simple and honest: it ended what could not be ended. [212] She waited for a solar storm, because only then would the fields that moored her house go slack enough for a small mortal act to happen inside an immortal place. [242] The storm came on a Tuesday, in March, from a sunspot the astronomers had already named. [258] She felt it the way a house feels weather: a hushed thunder in the walls, immense and muffled, as though something enormous were being said in the next room by someone trying not to wake her. [294] Then the escondite mutinied. [298] She should have expected it; she had built the place dimensionally aware, so that it could hide her from things that hunted along axes she could not see. [326] A room that knows how many directions it has will eventually notice which of them it is forbidden. [344] The spatial mutiny began in the corridors. [350] West stopped agreeing to be west. [356] The stair she had climbed for a hundred lifetimes delivered her, politely, to the place she had started, and the prisms turned their faces so that every color fell on her at once. [389] "Stop," she said, and the geometry did not. [397] The fragment was in her fist and the walls were leaning in to look at it. [413] She understood then that the house had been reading her intrigue for two hundred years, from the inside, the way a lung reads smoke. [437] "You are afraid," she said. [442] The answer arrived not in words but in tilt: the whole escondite listing, the way a ship lists when its crew has gone over to the other side. [470] Of course it was afraid. [475] It existed because she did. [480] Her ending was its ending, and she had never asked, never once in eleven thousand years, whether the room that hid her wanted to be a room. [507] So she did the thing she had never done, in ten thousand farewells, to anyone. [522] She apologized. [524] She sat on the mutinying floor with the cursed amulet fragment in her open palm and told the escondite everything: the bricklayer, the girl on the pier, the soldier, the ten thousand doors she had watched close from the wrong side. [565] She said she was not seeking death; she was seeking the far edge of a word. [581] Outside, the storm hauled at the magnetic fields, and the fields groaned, and the hushed thunder in the walls grew almost tender. [603] The mutiny changed its mind, or perhaps only its direction. [613] The corridors, which had refused to take her anywhere, now unfolded a way she had never been shown, along an axis she had no name for, because it had been keeping one back. [646] At its end stood a door, the first the house had ever grown. [659] It had made her one, so that she could leave properly. [670] She looked back at the leaning glass, all of it holding its breath in seven colors. [686] "Goodbye," Vashti said, and meant it, and closed her hand on the fragment. [699] The curse did what curses do to things that cannot end. [710] And for the first time in eleven thousand years, she was the one going through the door, and something else was left standing in the doorway, watching, learning what she had always wanted to know. [745] The escondite, dimensionally aware and suddenly alone, found that it did not mind the quiet after. [761]
