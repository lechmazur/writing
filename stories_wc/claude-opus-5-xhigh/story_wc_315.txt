Mira Ostrelan had earned her nickname the way most watchers earned theirs: by finding shapes in the dark that no one else believed were there. [25] The midnight pattern seeker, they called her, half joking, and the joke had followed her up the switchbacks to Arbor Nine. [46] Arbor Nine was an orchard on a ridge, planted three centuries ago by monks who wanted pears that tasted of cold, and it sat above the cloud deck like a raft on a white sea. [81] At night the clouds swallowed every sound from the valley, so that even the shutting of her cabin door came back to her strangely, a soundless echo, a thing felt in the teeth rather than heard. [117] That silence was why the listening post had been built here, and why the burden of it was hers alone. [137] Forty villages in the valley below kept their lamps dark or lit them, slept or ran, according to what Mira reported between midnight and dawn. [162] She was attentively inventive by necessity: she had strung pear-wood slats into a comb that hummed at particular engine pitches, floated a needle in a saucer to read vibration through the rock, and taught herself to count propeller beats against the tick of her own pulse. [208] Patterns were not superstition up here; they were the only evidence she would ever get. [223]

At 0100 the set gave her nothing but hiss, the mountain's usual grudge. [236] After the radio static cleared, at fourteen minutes past, a voice came through so clean it seemed to be standing in the room. [259] It gave the call sign Kestrel Four, said it was inbound wounded, and asked her to light the landing beacon in the upper meadow. [283] Mira had already logged the aircraft twenty minutes earlier as friendly, three lights in the old recognition order, and her pencil mark sat there in the ledger like a promise. [313] Now the pear-wood comb was humming on the wrong tooth. [323] Kestrel engines synchronized in six beats; this one ran in five, patient and slightly lame, and the lameness did not match a wound but a make. [349]

She sat with her hands flat on the table and understood that she would have to revise the entry. [368] A revision was not a small thing. [375] The network held together on the plain fact that watchers did not guess, did not embroider, and did not contradict themselves; one bad correction and Sector would begin weighing her reports against everyone else's, and the whole fragile web of trust would slacken. [418] If she was wrong, forty villages would learn it in fire. [429] The cipher tables for corrections were kept in her head, because paper could be taken, and under pressure her head had gone blank and white as the cloud outside. [458]

So she reached for the buckle. [464] Two winters ago a pilot named Anselm had come down through the orchard, his canopy snagged in the tallest pear, and she had cut him free and kept the parachute buckle he pressed into her palm as thanks. [502] He had taught her the drill that winter: hold something familiar, let the hand run the sequence, and stop asking the mind for help. [526] Every night since, she had tapped the correction cipher against the buckle's tongue before sleep, a thousand repetitions, remembering through muscle memory what fear could erase from thought. [554] Her thumb found the tongue now, and her fingers walked the key without consulting her at all. [571]

REVISE 0120: earlier friendly classification withdrawn, engine sync five not six, request for beacon unverified, beacon remains dark. [589] She added, because honesty was the whole of her value, one certainty: confidence eight in ten. [605] Sector acknowledged in nine seconds. [610] The voice on the other channel asked again for the beacon, warmer now, using her first name, which no pilot in the corridor had ever been given. [637] Mira held the buckle and said nothing. [644] The five-beat drone passed over the arbor, circled twice above the clouds, and went east without dropping anything. [662] The trees shook a little; the sound came back off the cloud floor thinned to nothing, the way everything did up here. [684]

At dawn the message came that a decoy had worked three valleys west, where a watcher had lit his beacon. [704] Mira did not feel vindicated; she felt the weight settle back onto her shoulders, heavier for having been right, because being right once meant they would believe her next time, and next time she might not hear the difference. [743] She copied the revision into the ledger in her neatest hand, wrote the confidence figure beside it, and set the buckle on the table where her fingers could reach it. [773] Then she began the night's practice again, tapping, while the pears ripened in the cold above the clouds. [791]
