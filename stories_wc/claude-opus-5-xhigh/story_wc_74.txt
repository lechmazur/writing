Nadia had not slept properly in eleven years, so she had learned to make the night useful. [17] Her rooftop greenhouse sat above the ninth floor of a building the color of old bandages, and the city below it breathed smog the way a sick man breathes. [46] Inside the glass, under grow-lamps and dust, she raised saffron crocuses, and every autumn she plucked the red threads with tweezers and gave them away. [71] The greenhouse smelled of that: warm earth and a saffron quiet that made even the sirens sound far away. [90] She painted there, too. [94] Not landscapes, not portraits: letters. [99]

Eleven years ago, at sixteen, she had sat crying on a night bus with a stolen coat in her lap, and a stranger had sat down beside her. [127] He was an old calligrapher with ink under his nails, and he did not ask what she had done. [146] He gave her his brush, a slim thing tipped with a pale crescent of bone that he called the moon-tip, and he taught her one word. [172] The word was from his mother's language, which had nine remaining speakers and no alphabet anyone printed. [189] It meant, roughly, I saw you and did not walk past. [200] He wrote it for her on a receipt, said a language only lives if a second mouth carries it, and got off at the depot. [225] She never learned his name. [230]

The receipt faded years ago, and with it most of the shapes. [242] So Nadia became methodically hopeful about recovering them. [250] She kept a ledger, numbered every attempt, and worked only under a waxing moon's glow, because that was the one light that worked. [273] Smog swallowed stars, but a fattening moon still pushed a thin silver through the glass, and when it fell across her hands she slipped into daydreams. [299] In them she was on the bus again, sixteen and snot-nosed, watching the old man's wrist move. [316] Moonlight roused the memory the way water roused a dry seed; sleeplessness had made her mind a wide, patient room where such things could be replayed. [342]

Attempt four hundred and six recovered the downstroke. [350] Attempt five hundred and ninety gave her the little hook beneath it, like a hand cupped under a chin. [369] Her neighbors thought she was painting nonsense; she told them she was proving something. [383] Strangers remember kindness, she said, and lost languages are only kindness that nobody bothered to carry. [399]

On the ninth night of the waxing moon, attempt seven hundred and eleven, the word came whole. [416] She loaded the moon-tipped brush, and her hand moved without consulting her, and there it was on the paper: crescent, downstroke, cupped hook. [439] She wept, quietly, into the crocuses. [445] Then, because proof requires a witness, she carried the wet paper down nine flights and taped it inside the elevator. [465]

For three days nobody said anything. [471] On the fourth, at four in the morning, someone knocked on the greenhouse door. [485] It was Mrs. Ferhat from the seventh floor, in slippers, holding the paper she had peeled off the elevator wall. [505] Her hands shook. [508] My mother wrote this on my school lunches, she said. [518] I have not seen it in fifty years. [526]

They sat among the crocuses until the sky went the color of weak tea, and Mrs. Ferhat gave her four more words: bread, snow, forgive, and the name of the plant her mother had grown on a windowsill in a village that no longer existed. [571] Nadia numbered them in her ledger. [577]

By the full moon there were nine of them on the roof: the widower with the accordion, the twins from 4B, the night-shift nurse who had never once been thanked. [607] None of them spoke the language. [613] All of them, it turned out, had a stranger to account for -- a coat, a bus fare, a hand on an elbow at a curb -- and each one had kept the debt like a seed in a pocket. [651] Nadia handed the moon-tipped brush around and let them copy the word badly, one after another, ink on their knuckles. [671]

Below the glass the city coughed and glowed orange. [680] Above it, in the saffron quiet, a dead language was being roused nine mouths at a time. [697] She still did not sleep. [702] She no longer minded; the nights had somewhere to go. [712]
