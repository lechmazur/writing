The energy beings arrived in the season of thaw, and after that the harmonic caves never stopped singing. [18] Sela Vant was the ripple-mark mapkeeper of the third gallery, and her work had once been slow and pleasant. [37] She had walked the tunnels with chalk and calipers, recording the frozen ripples the ancient meltwater left in the walls, because those ripples told a walker which passages would hum and which would howl. [71] Now every passage howled. [75] The beings were not cruel. [80] They were gently luminous, drifting through the ice like lamps under water, and they seemed to mean nothing at all by the noise they made. [105] But their bodies rang. [109] Wherever they passed, the cave's resonance doubled and doubled again, until the village in the fourth gallery could not sleep, and old Marrow the smith went deaf in a week, and infants screamed in a pitch that matched the walls. [149] The council asked Sela for a map to silence. [158] She told them there was no such place, and then she went looking anyway. [172] Her grandmother had left her an echo chamber stone: a fist of grey basalt, hollowed, with seven holes bored through its shell. [194] Held to a singing wall, it gathered the noise inside itself and gave back, faintly, the shape of the room that had made it. [218] Sela carried it for eleven days through galleries she had mapped and galleries she had not. [234] She slept badly and ate cold fish and hammered iron pegs into the ice to mark her way, and the hammering was the only sound she loved, because it was hers. [265] What she wanted was not peace forever. [272] She wanted, precisely and only, to find the quiet in the storm - one room, one pocket, where a child could sleep while the world went on ringing. [299] She found it by examining crack patterns in ice. [308] Ice cracks the way it is spoken to. [316] A wall struck once shows a star; a wall sung at for a season shows feathering, fine plumes that fan along the grain of the sound. [342] For eleven days Sela crouched with her lamp against the walls, sketching fractures, and she saw the plumes all bending, everywhere, in one direction - as if the whole mountain were leaning away from something. [376] At the center of that leaning she found a chamber with no cracks at all. [391] The old maps called it nothing. [397] Her grandmother's marginal notes called it the bloom of hush. [407] It was not silent when she entered. [414] It was full of the same shrieking as everywhere else, but the ice there was flawless, which meant the shrieking had never been able to take hold. [441] The room's shape refused it. [446] Sela set the echo chamber stone in the middle of the floor and listened to what it gave back, and it gave back a note she could not hear - a pressure, a held breath, the room's true voice waiting to be let out. [489] To conjure that voice she had to strike the chamber correctly, and correctly meant everywhere at once. [506] So she brought the village. [511] Thirty people with hammers, spaced along the walls where the ripple-marks told her the ice was truest, striking on her count. [532] They hammered for six hours. [537] It was brutal, ugly work - arms dead, ears ringing - and the energy beings came to watch, gathering in bright knots against the ceiling. [560] Then, in the sixth hour, the noise folded. [568] The blows had beaten a standing pattern into the air, and every incoming shriek met its own reflection and was cancelled, and the chamber flowered open into silence. [596] Sela sat down on the floor with her hammer across her knees. [608] This was the shape of their peace from now on: not given, not found, but hammered - a repose that had to be re-struck every dawn by whoever could still lift an arm. [640] She wrote it on the map that way. [647] In the new quiet she could hear one thing only: the beings above her, and they were not ringing. [666] They had come down close to the floor, and they hung there, still, folded in on themselves like sleeping birds. [686] Sela understood, then, what she had not put on any map. [697] The beings had not come to the harmonic caves to sing. [708] They had come the way she had come, dragging their terrible noise behind them, looking for one room in the world where it would finally stop. [734] She let them stay. [738] In the morning she taught them to hold hammers. [747]
