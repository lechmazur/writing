The exile ended on a Tuesday, and the rain stopped an hour later, as if the two had been holding each other up. [23] Sela came down to the ivory harbor after rain because that was when the walls spoke clearest. [40] Wet, the bleached coral quays turned the color of a lit lamp, and every salt crust she had spent twelve years reading stood out like ink. [66] She was the ocean-spray archivist, the last one left behind when the fleet was driven out, and her office had been the tide. [89] Spray wrote on the harbor's white stone; she copied what it wrote. [101] Storms, drownings, the years the herring failed - all of it legible in mineral, if you were patient. [118] But the exiles' twelve years had left no mark on her walls at all. [132] That was the hole in the record, and she had promised herself she would fill it. [148]

The first boats warped in at noon, low and patched, carrying fewer people than had left. [164] Nobody sounded a horn, because the herald had died in the third winter on a coast with no name. [183] His daughter handed Sela the only piece of him that had survived the crossings: a herald's trumpet valve, brass gone green, still oiled. [206] It moved under her thumb with a small wet click, like a knuckle, like a heartbeat with its music stripped away. [227]

Sela had never been able to see anything in advance. [237] She was reactively visionary: nothing came to her until something touched her, and then the whole shape arrived at once. [257] The valve clicked, and she saw the archive she had to make. [269] Not a chronicle of the exile - they had lived that, and would not say it straight - but a chart of navigated absences. [291] Every returning family had steered by what was missing: a father, a harbor, a star behind cloud, a well that had gone brackish. [314] Absence had been their instrument, and Sela meant to make an instrument of it too. [329]

She learned quickly that she could not simply ask. [338] Grief closed like a shell under a steady hand. [347] So she worked through staggered intervals: two questions at first light, two at slack water, two after the lamps were lit, then a day of nothing. [373] The gaps did the work. [378] People came back to her on the off days with the things they had not meant to say. [396] A woman described the eleven nights she rowed by the smell of a burning island she never saw. [414] A boy, born out there, drew the harbor from his mother's descriptions and got the lighthouse wrong in a way Sela decided to keep. [438] Fragments, all of it, and her motivation never changed: to compile fragments into meaning, since meaning was the only cargo the exile had not confiscated. [463]

She copied each account onto the white wall in salt paste, in the same script the spray used, so the harbor itself would remember. [487] By the ninth week the wall ran the length of the customs arch, and people had begun to read it aloud to each other without being asked. [514] Still, reading was not enough to hearten a town that flinched at every sail on the horizon. [531]

So Sela mounted the herald's trumpet valve on a brass post at the harbor mouth, where the stone was whitest. [551] It made no note by itself; it only clicked, small and dry, a sound the whole basin somehow carried. [570] Each evening, one returnee pressed it - once for every year gone, with a breath of silence between - and the harbor listened to the pattern of the pauses. [597] Twelve clicks for those who had gone the whole way; three for the boy; none, one long held silence, for the dead, and everyone stood for that one. [625] It was not music and it was not mourning. [634] It was a roll call the shape of a route. [643]

Sela wrote the last line of the wall on a night when the rain came and went, leaving the ivory harbor lamplit and streaming. [667] She had no vision of what the town would become; she never did, until it touched her. [684] But when the evening click came, staggered and patient across the water, she felt the shape arrive: a people who could count their losses in order and still put out to sea in the morning. [719] She pressed her thumb to the valve, once, for the twelve years she had spent alone with the walls. [738] The harbor answered the way it always had, in salt and echo, and she wrote that down too. [756]
