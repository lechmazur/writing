The grotto had lost its roof to an earthquake nine hundred years before Mei-shan was born, so she did her counting under stars. [23] She was the tidal-pool chronicler of Bitter Anchor Cove, which meant that twice a day she knelt on wet rock with a wax tablet and wrote down what the water had left behind. [56] The grotto itself was older than the cove's name, its walls pocked with offering niches worn shallow by salt, its pools lying in the stone floor like spilled coins. [85] The village paid her in salt fish and considered the work harmless. [97] They were wrong about that. [102] Mei-shan was not cataloguing animals; she was trying to discover the texture of time, and the only place time showed its grain was in the transition periods, the hour of slack water when the sea could not decide which way to be. [144]

Her brother Ru came down the cliff path at dusk with his boots in his hand and waited until she had finished the eastern pools. [169] Then he put the jade cicada into her palm, still warm from his fist. [183] It was their mother's tongue-jade, carved for the mouth of a corpse, bought forty years early because their mother liked to be ready. [206] "She is asking for you," Ru said. [213] "The physician gives her a month." [219] "Will you come inland with me tonight?" [226]

And then the pause, which is where this story happens. [236]

Mei-shan did not answer, because she never answered anything head-on. [246] Her whole method was the tangential approach, the sidelong look, learned from the stars over the broken roof: a faint star vanishes if you stare at it and returns the instant you glance a little to its left. [284] So she looked at the pool instead of her brother. [294] The tide stood at its turning, that quarter hour when the cove holds still and the water in the rock basin lies flat as poured glass. [320] A green crab, half out of its old shell, hung in the shadow of a mussel, neither armored nor free. [340] She had written that crab down eleven times across two summers and knew its scars; she was warmly observant of it the way other people are of a neighbor's children. [370] Molting was the crab's slack water: the only hour it could grow, and the only hour anything could eat it. [390] In the pool's rim she could see reflected stars and, among them, the pale movement of her brother's face waiting for her. [412]

Looking away like that, she could foresee the shape of the next month with a clarity that direct thought had never given her. [435] She saw the inland house with its shuttered windows, the bed, the basin, the long unmeasured hours. [452] She saw herself refusing, too, and the shape that made: forty more years of tablets, and one pool she would never be able to look at again. [479] The cicada in her hand had been carved by someone who understood her trade better than she did. [497] A cicada spends seventeen years in the dark and one summer singing, and the jade is placed on the tongue not to honor an ending but to mark a molt. [527] Her mother had bought hers forty years early, which meant her mother had been watching her own transition come on the way Mei-shan watched the pools: not with dread, exactly, but with attention. [560] That was the thing she had been refusing to look at directly for eleven years, and here it was in the corner of her eye, perfectly clear. [587]

The water in the basin shivered. [592] Somewhere below the ledge the sea had chosen; the flood had begun; the crab pulled itself free of the shell and became, for a few soft hours, entirely new. [621]

Mei-shan closed her hand on the jade and stood. [630] "Let me get the tablets," she said. [637] Ru's shoulders dropped. [640] "You are not bringing your work." [646] "I am bringing my work," she said. [653] "She is a transition period." [658] "Nobody will write her down but me." [665]

They climbed the cliff path with the stars swinging over the open grotto behind them, and the pools filled, and the record went on. [689] The pause had lasted exactly one turning of the tide. [699] In it she had learned the texture of time, and it was this: cool, faintly veined, shaped like a cicada, and warming in a closed hand. [725]
