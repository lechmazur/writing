The lighthouse had stopped warning ships a long time ago, so it warned Mira instead. [15] She climbed to the forgotten library on its fourth landing every evening, a crestfallen portrait artist carrying a satchel of brushes she no longer trusted. [40] Salt had eaten the shelves; the books had swollen shut like drowned mouths. [53] Only one thing in the room still worked, and it was broken. [65] The tarot card box sat on the reading table with its hinge snapped and its inlay cracked into a hundred small countries. [87] Inside, the cards had come apart into fragments, and the fragments would not agree. [101] A wrist from the Star, a lantern from the Hermit, half a face she recognized and could not name. [120] That was the trouble with the world since the shipwreck: it arrived in pieces, and the pieces belonged to different stories. [141] Her brother Ilan had gone down with the ferry, and she had been commissioned by their mother to paint him from memory. [163] Memory, it turned out, was the one client who kept changing the brief. [176] Eleven attempts hung along the wall, eleven strangers wearing fragments of his mouth. [189] The keeper's widow, who had given her the key, had asked what good it did to keep trying. [207] Mira had answered her, because she always answered. [215] People threw rhetorical questions like stones into a well and walked off before the splash; Mira climbed down after them. [235] What good did it do? [240] It kept a face from dissolving, which was the only job she had ever been given. [256]

The library had a habit she had learned not to argue with. [268] Each night, when the walls closed in, the round room narrowed by the width of a shelf. [285] The stone came inward like a fist slowly making itself, and the air went thin and bright. [302] On the first night she had fled upward toward the lamp room, into the last of the light. [320] Up there the fragments only glittered harder and told her less. [331] Tonight she meant to go the other way, to descend into the necessary darkness beneath the tide line, where the lighthouse kept its foundations and its lost things. [359] She took the broken box with her. [365]

The stairs turned tight, the walls pressing her shoulders, and the last of the lamp's glow followed her down like something reluctant to be left alone. [391] In the cellar the sea breathed through the stones, and the dark was not empty. [406] It was full of threads. [411] They hung from the ceiling in a snarled curtain, faintly luminescent, each one aching with a small stubborn light, the way a bruise remembers the blow. [437] She understood the box then. [442] Someone had not shuffled a deck; someone had cut the strands that held one moment to the next, and the pictures had spilled. [465]

Mira sat on the wet floor and began working, not by painting, but by untangling threads. [481] Each knot she loosened gave her back a true fragment: Ilan's thumbnail split from a hammer, the gap where his laugh started, the way his hairline argued with itself. [510] Threads she could not free she left alone, because some of them were not hers. [525] The walls kept closing, and she kept sorting. [533] By the hour the sea began to gray, she had enough. [544]

She did not paint him whole, because he had never been whole, and neither had she. [560] Instead she used the cracked lid as a frame and redesigned the portrait the way the box had taught her: a mosaic of true pieces, laid in the fractures, with the gaps left honest and dark. [596] The threads she had freed she wove into the seams, and they held their small glow. [612] When she carried it up, the library had opened again to its full circle, as if satisfied. [629] The eleven strangers on the wall looked like what they were: guesses. [641]

Her mother would ask, at the door, whether this was supposed to be a comfort. [656] Mira had her answer ready, since she never let a question stand unanswered simply because it was asked to wound. [676] No, it was not a comfort; it was a likeness, and a likeness was what the dead were owed. [695] She locked the library, left the key with the widow, and did not go back. [710] Ships still passed without guidance, and the tower stood dark above the water. [723] But sometimes, on the worst nights, sailors reported a faint light low in the rock, patient and unmoving, the color of something remembered on purpose. [748]
