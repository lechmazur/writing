The medium's houseboat sat at the heart of the mangrove labyrinth, in a green corridor no chart admitted existed. [19] I poled the skiff through roots like fingers, and the spirits sang me in. [33] They sing everything here: tide tables, drowned hymns, the names of people who never wrote back. [49] In my coat I carried nine unsent letters to my sister, and a piece of stone. [65] The stone was a gargoyle's rainspout shard, snapped from the cathedral gutter the night Rosalind and I stopped speaking. [84] It fell between us in the rain, and I picked it up instead of saying the thing I meant. [103] Ondine Marchetti opened her door before I knocked. [111] "You brought the wrong grief," she said, "which is the only kind that travels." [125] Her cabin windows were furred with condensation, and words were surfacing on them the way bruises surface on skin. [144] That is how the dead talk to her: fingertip letters in fog, gone by morning. [159] I set the shard on her table and told her what I wanted. [172] Not my sister's ghost -- Rosalind was alive, in a hospice two hundred miles north, refusing visitors. [188] I wanted the moment before deciding. [194] Not the argument, not the silence after, but the hairline instant in which I could still have chosen otherwise. [213] Ondine turned the shard over. [218] "Limestone from a mouth that spat rain for four hundred years," she said, "and it heals in moonlight." [236] She was right; I had watched its fracture close a little every clear night, the chip knitting like bone. [255] "When it is whole," she said, "the night it broke will be gone from it, and you will have nothing left to stand in." [279] We waited for moonrise with the letters between us, unopened, addressed in nine different handwritings of the same coward. [298] Outside, the mangrove spirits changed key. [304] Ondine said they were tuning themselves to the shard, and that I should not speak while she worked. [322] She pressed the stone to the cold glass and began to evoke the night it fell. [338] The condensation crawled. [341] Letters formed, dissolved, reformed. [345] First the cathedral downpour, written out in water: the gutter, the crack, the stone coming down between our shoes. [364] Then Rosalind's face, in fog, mouth open in the shape she made before she decided to be cruel. [382] And there it was: the space where an apology waits unspoken, wide as a held breath, and I was standing inside it. [404] Time did not run there. [409] I could have stayed a year in that grey pause, rehearsing. [420] The spirits sang louder, and I understood, finally, what they had always been singing. [434] Every unsent word ever written on this coast, kept warm, kept useless. [446] They were not a choir. [451] They were a hoard. [455] "You can live in the moment before," Ondine said, and her voice had a fierce tenderness in it, like a hand pulling a child off a road. [482] "People do." [484] "They build houses in it." [489] "That is what this swamp is made of." [497] I looked at my nine letters and saw them for what they were: nine rooms I had furnished so I would never have to arrive. [522] The moon came through the roots and struck the shard, and the fracture closed the rest of the way with a sound like a lock. [547] The window cleared. [550] The moment before was gone, healed shut, and I was left with only the moment I was in. [568] I found I was not sorry. [574] I was terrified, which is different, and better. [582] I asked Ondine for a pen. [588] She gave me none. [592] "Not that," she said. [596] "Paper is where apologies go to be safe." [604] So I burned the nine letters in her stove, and the spirits outside went briefly, horribly quiet, the way a crowd does when someone jumps. [629] Then they sang something new, which is rare in a place that only collects. [643] I poled out at dawn with the healed shard in my pocket, whole and ordinary, a piece of a mouth that had spent four centuries letting water go. [671] Two hundred miles north, Rosalind was refusing visitors. [679] I intended to be refused in person. [686] The last thing Ondine told me, standing in her doorway with fog rewriting itself behind her, was that the dead do not regret what they said. [712] They regret the drafts. [716]
