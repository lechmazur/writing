The Ember Nexus at twilight was less a place than an agreement: nine forge-flues from the Ironward district met above the reservoir, and for eleven minutes the city's spent heat hung there, red and undecided. [35] Yara Sennick had climbed the scaffold every evening of that autumn, because a pocket-sun alchemist cannot make light out of nothing; she can only persuade light that has already worked hard to work once more. [70] Her tools were absurd and exact: a brass caliper, a jar of iron dew, and the columella curl of a nautilus shell, sawn from the animal's central column, a spiral no wider than her thumb. [105] Light poured into that curl and had to come out the same aperture it entered, which meant it could only escape by returning differently: wound tighter, slowed, folded into itself until it weighed something. [139] That was the theory she had defended, badly, to the Luminary Board: nine hundred and four pocket-suns, each of them bright, each of them thin as rumor. [166] She did not want brightness. [171] She wanted to capture the density of presence, the particular heaviness of someone being in a room with you, so that a miner's widow could set a sun on the table and feel occupied rather than merely lit. [209]

Teodor came up the ladder at the sixth minute, carrying bread he had forgotten to eat. [225] He was a bell-founder, and the only person who had never once called her scientifically romantic as an insult. [244] 'Palette,' he said, because he had learned her vocabulary the way friends do, by repetition and mockery. [261] She unrolled it: not pigments, but a palette of resolve, nine small wells of intention that she mixed before every attempt. [282] Stubbornness, grief, curiosity, spite, tenderness, patience, hunger, apology, and one she had never labeled. [296] The color of a pocket-sun, she had proven twice and published once, depends less on the fuel than on what the maker has decided while feeding it. [323]

Twilight thickened; the flues drew; she worked with the directed spontaneity of someone who has planned exactly how to improvise. [343] Caliper, then dew, then the shell held at the angle the heat wanted rather than the angle her notes prescribed. [363] Teodor leaned close, and in the manner of a man who has been carrying something up nine flights of ladder, he whispered. [385] The foundry was sold; he was going to the salt coast in the morning; he had known for three weeks and had not been able to say it in daylight. [415]

The whisper lasted perhaps four seconds. [421] Inside those four seconds the shell went heavy in her hand. [432] She had measured hundreds of failures and knew the feel of light merely circulating; this was light arriving somewhere and refusing to leave. [455] The spiral filled with a color she had no name for, the unlabeled well, and it weighed, by the scaffold scale, one and one-eighth grams: eight times any sun she had ever wound. [488]

She understood, then, that she had been asking the wrong question for four years. [502] Presence is not a quantity of person; it is the moment a person hands you something they cannot take back. [522] A secret whispered between friends is the densest event the human body produces, and it had been standing at her elbow, unmeasured, all autumn. [546]

'You are crying into the apparatus,' Teodor said. [554] 'I am revaluing,' she said, and meant it literally. [563] Nine hundred and four suns sat in her cabinet, catalogued as failures; every one had been wound on an evening when nothing was at stake, and each would have to be revalued as an honest record of a room with no one in it. [607] That was a finding, not a defeat. [614]

She pressed the shell into his palm before the eleven minutes closed. [626] 'It will go out,' she warned, 'unless you come back and tell me something true.' [641] 'That is not physics,' he said. [647] 'It is exactly physics,' she said. 'The light has to return differently or it cannot leave at all.' [665]

He went down the ladder holding a small heavy glow against his chest, and the Ironward flues guttered, and the reservoir turned the ordinary gray of water at night. [694] Yara stayed on the scaffold with her palette open and finally labeled the ninth well, in ink, with the date and the word 'told.' [718] It would need testing, of course, many times, and she already knew what returning differently would cost her. [736]
