The bookstore filled Mount Anselm the way music fills a cathedral, and Ilse Vandermolen had spent eleven years learning to read its silences. [23] As the shop's arcane linguist, she catalogued words that had never been spoken aloud, and the mountain paid her in stairwells. [44] Nine miles of shelving spiraled through the granite, and somewhere inside them customers went missing every season. [61] Ilse led the rescue missions. [66] She was carefully resourceful in the way of people who have buried someone: she carried chalk, dried apricots, a lantern with four wicks, and her sister's botanical diary. [94] Margit had been the shop's herbalist, and her diary pressed ferns and violets flat between descriptions of every plant that grew on the mountain's flanks. [119] It also had eleven blank pages, and Ilse had built her whole method on absences like those. [136] You found lost people by researching gaps: the aisle numbers no map admitted, the years no ledger claimed, the notes a symphony left out. [160] The mountain, she believed, was an unheard symphony, and the missing were its rests. [174] Margit had vanished chasing the same rumor Ilse now chased: the Library of Unlearned Lessons, where every lesson a person failed to learn was shelved, waiting. [200]

Then the time traveler arrived, and the acoustics of everything changed. [211] He came up through the Geology mezzanine on a Tuesday, wearing a coat cut in no century Ilse could name, and asked for a book that would not be written for two hundred years. [245] The clerks laughed. [247] Ilse listened to his grammar instead. [253] He conjugated verbs into tenses her field had only theorized, and every sentence he spoke had a hole in it, a place where a word should have been. [281]

"You are missing something," she said. [287]

"A name," he admitted. [291] "I traded it to get here, and now I cannot ask for what I came for." [307]

So Ilse made a bargain: she would speak his missing name into the record, if he would tell her when Margit had actually disappeared. [331] He studied the botanical diary and turned to the fourth blank page. [343]

"She isn't gone," he said. [348] "She is late." [351]

The blank pages, he explained, were not empty; they were entries Margit had not yet lived long enough to write. [371] The Library of Unlearned Lessons kept its debtors that way, holding them in the pause before a note sounds. [390] It had taken Margit as collateral against a lesson she refused to learn, which was that she could not save everyone. [411]

They descended together for six days. [417] Ilse mapped the route the only way she trusted, by researching gaps: she compared the shop's four contradictory indexes and walked wherever all four agreed there was nothing. [445] She rationed the apricots, spliced her chalk with soot to make it last, and burned one wick at a time. [465] On the sixth day the stairs ended at a door with no hinges, and behind it the shelves held books with uncut pages, spines uncracked, every one of them a lesson nobody had ever finished. [500] Margit sat among them with a violet in her hand, still fresh, mid-sentence in a book she could not close. [520]

The Registrar of that place was a voice like an orchestra tuning, and it refused Ilse's claim; a debt was a debt. [542] So Ilse did what an arcane linguist is for, and moved to renegotiate. [555] The contract, she pointed out, was written in the old subjunctive of intent, and its clause on collateral had a gap where the word "permanently" should have stood. [583] She offered a substitution: the time traveler's forfeited name, a word the Library had never shelved, in exchange for the herbalist. [604] The traveler nodded; he had come, he said, precisely to spend it. [616] He had read, in a century that did not exist yet, about a woman who ran rescue missions into a mountain and never lost anyone twice. [642]

The Registrar accepted, and the note the mountain had been holding for eleven years finally sounded. [658] Nobody in the shop above heard it, but the chandeliers swung and the dust came down in chords. [676] Margit closed her book, learned her lesson late, and wrote the fourth entry on the walk up. [693] Ilse kept the diary anyway, seven pages still blank. [702] Gaps, she had learned, are simply the parts of the music that have not been played. [718] The nameless man stayed on as a clerk in Geology, and when new customers went missing, he carried the second lantern. [739]
