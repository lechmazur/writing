The sanctuary had burned so long ago that even the ash had gone quiet. [14] Mira kept it anyway, sweeping charcoal into careful drifts under a sky so crowded with stars that the ruin glowed faintly blue. [36] She was the shadow keeper, and shadows needed tending the way anything tender does. [50] They came to her suffocating. [55] Every one arrived clenched, throat shut, still holding the last breath it had failed to take. [71] Her work was to teach them to breathe again, so they could cross. [84] The crossing was a doorway with no door left: two scorched posts, a lintel, and the iron doorknocker ring still bolted to nothing. [107] Knock, and the threshold opened onto whatever came after. [116] It was fading. [119] Each year the ring rang thinner, the way a bell does when the metal is going. [135] Mira had counted the diminishment for eleven years and told no one, because there was no one. [152] She had taken the post after cutting off all contact with the living. [165] She burned her bridges carefully, one at a time, with letters instead of silence, so that everyone she left knew exactly why. [187] Careful burning was still burning. [192] Only her brother Aleth got no letter. [199] He was the fire's cause, and she had not trusted her hand to write to him kindly. [216]

On the eleventh winter, a shadow came up the ash road that she recognized by its walk. [233] It stopped at the edge of her swept ground and would not encroach. [246] Shadows always encroached; that was their nature, to press in, to crowd the warm. [260] This one held its distance like a man at a door he has no right to. [276] "Aleth," she said. [279] The shadow shuddered, mouthing air, drowning in the middle of a clear cold night. [293] Suffocation looked the same on everyone: the shoulders up, the chest locked, the eyes asking a question the throat cannot. [313]

The method was old and it was the only one she had. [324] You link through scars. [328] A shadow keeps the marks its body kept; so does the keeper. [340] Mira had burn scars from wrist to elbow, taken from the sanctuary door on the night it went up. [359] She knelt in the charcoal and offered her forearm, and the shadow laid its own scarred arm against it, dark on ruined skin. [382] Scar to scar, the link held. [388] Then came the emptiness she had learned to expect and never learned to bear: not blankness, but a room with the furniture taken out, every absence still shaped like what had stood there. [421] Through the link she felt the years after the fire: the drink, the letters he wrote and never sent, the last hour in a rented room where the air simply stopped being enough. [454]

She gave back the only thing a keeper can give, which is the rhythm. [468] In through the scar, out through the scar; slow, slow, until the locked chest remembered the shape of an ordinary breath. [489] It took most of the night. [495] Stars wheeled over the roofless nave; the charcoal held their light like a lake holds it, giving nothing back. [514] When Aleth breathed, he breathed like a man tasting water after a desert. [527]

She walked him to the posts. [533] The doorknocker ring hung there, cold, and she saw how much of it had gone: the iron thin as a wedding band, the threshold behind it barely a shimmer. [562] One more knock, perhaps two. [567] Aleth looked at her, and the loaded emptiness between them finally had somewhere to go. [582] "I meant to write," she said. [588] "I burn my bridges carefully, so I always know where they were." [600] He could not speak; shadows do not, and she had never wanted his apology, only his breath. [617]

He lifted the ring and let it fall. [625] The sound was small, a coin dropped in an empty church, and the threshold opened just wide enough. [643] He went through, and the ring came away in her hand, spent. [655] So that was the last one. [661] Mira sat down in the ash with the dead iron in her lap and did the exercise on herself, in through the scar, out through the scar, until the shaking stopped. [692] Dawn would come and the ash would still be there and there would be no more crossings, ever. [710] Which meant the suffocating would go on arriving up the ash road with nowhere to be sent. [727] She would still teach them. [732] There are worse ways to spend forever than showing the choked how to take one more breath, under stars, in a place where everything that could burn already had. [761]
