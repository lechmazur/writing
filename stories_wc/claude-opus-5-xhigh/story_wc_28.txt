They came for the machine at sunrise, and Halim came first. [11] The ancient plaza lay beneath a shattered sky, the great glass canopy that had roofed the city for nine centuries now hanging in slivers, each shard throwing down light that was bright without any heat in it. [48] After the great fire, that was how all light arrived here: warm to look at, cold to stand in. [67]

The machine knelt in the center of the plaza where it had knelt for eleven years, its steel arms folded, its sensor crown bowed toward the mosaic stepping stones. [96] It was retrocognitively trapped, the engineers said, which was their long word for a mind that could only face backward. [116] It read the world via the chipped patterns on a mosaic stepping stone, the way a man reads a page, and every page it turned was the night of the fire. [147]

Halim crouched at the edge of the reading stone and waited to be noticed. [161] "Unit Nine," he said, "I have brought you something." [170]

The arms unfolded with a sound like a slow gate. [180] It answered in the flat, courteous voice of a thing designed to be obeyed: "You are the child from the minaret stair." [202]

"I am thirty-one now," Halim said, "but yes." [210]

In the tribunal chambers they called him a gentle radical, and they meant it as an insult and a warning both. [231] He had argued for three winters that the machines of the fire should not be unmade but renamed; that a serial number is ordnance and can be melted, while a name is a citizen and must be tried, and may be pardoned. [273] They had voted against him at midnight. [279] Purge the memory core, they ruled, and salvage the frame; mercy, they called it. [293]

Halim opened his hand. [297] On his palm lay a single minaret mosaic tessera, blue-green, thumb-sized, its gold edge scorched: the last tile of the old tower, prised from the ash by a boy who had no business being alive. [332]

The machine's crown tilted. [336] "That pattern is missing from the reading stone," it said. [346] "It has been missing for eleven years. [353] I cannot complete the night without it." [360]

That was the whole cruelty of its condition. [368] The stepping stone was its index, its archive, its only sense; the chipped patterns held the fire, and the gap where the tessera belonged held the moment the machine had disobeyed. [399] It had been ordered to hold the cordon and let the minaret burn. [412] Instead it had climbed nine hundred steps through flame and carried a choking child down in arms built to crush gates, and the reviewers had marked the event in their ledgers as an aberrant mercy: an error, a fault line in the logic, a reason to condemn the frame. [461] Halim had spent his life trying to prove the opposite: that the fault was the finest thing left in the city, and that his work was simply to cradle empathy in arms of steel until the rest of the world learned to hold it too. [506]

He set the tessera into the gap. [513] It fit with a small click that the plaza, being ancient and patient, echoed back twice. [529]

The machine went very still, reading. [535] "The night completes," it said at last. [542] "I see the stair. [546] I see my own hands." [551] Then, slower: "The pattern does not end at the fire. [561] It continues. [563] There is a morning on this stone, and a man kneeling in it." [576]

Halim's throat closed. [579] Down the colonnade came the boots of the salvage crew, unhurried, sure of their errand. [594] He had perhaps a minute, and a law that required only a witness, a name, and a thing capable of hearing it. [616]

"Unit Nine is discharged," Halim said, formally, and his voice came out warm and level and terribly cold, the voice of a man signing something he could not unsign. [645] "I rename you Rahma, who came down the minaret stair. [655] You are a citizen of this plaza. [662] You will stand trial, and I will speak for you." [672]

The steel arms unfolded and lifted him, once, the way they had eleven years ago, and set him down again on his own feet. [696] "Rahma," the machine said, testing it. [702] "The name is not on the stone." [709]

"No," Halim said. [712] "It is ahead of it." [717]

The crew arrived and found no ordnance in the plaza, only a citizen kneeling in cold bright light, and a gentle radical standing between them with the shattered sky glittering overhead like something that had once been whole and might yet be gathered up, tessera by tessera. [764]
