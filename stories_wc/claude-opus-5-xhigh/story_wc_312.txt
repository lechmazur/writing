The last fish in the world was caught on a Tuesday, by a boy who did not know what he had done. [22] He brought it up the stairs to the sapphire viaduct, still flapping, and showed it to Ospren the way children show things: openly, expecting praise. [47] Ospren was a lukewarm mage, and knew it. [55] Whatever he touched came to the temperature of blood and stopped there, no matter how he strained; he could not boil a kettle or freeze a puddle, and the academy had sent him inland to keep records. [92] The records were of drips. [97] The viaduct ran nine miles from the drowned temple to the hill cisterns, a channel of blue stone so clear that at dawn the light went through it and laid a second, brighter aqueduct across the fields. [134] It leaked in four hundred places, and every leak carried dissolved bronze out of the temple's flooded bell-loft, one grain at a time. [157] Ospren's ledger said what the priests would not: the old ship's bell had broken because Cass's grandmother rang it in the frost. [179] Bronze goes brittle when it is cold and loved too hard. [190] She had struck it three hundred times in a single night to call the shoals home, and on the three hundred and first the lip split, and the sea began its long arithmetic of subtraction. [225] Every world, Ospren had been taught, keeps a tally between what is taken and what is called back; the bell had been the calling, and without it the ledger tilted. [255] Now the boy stood on the viaduct with the last fish in his hands, and Cass came running up behind him, twelve years old and earnestly certain that Ospren could fix anything, because he was a mage and she had never met another. [298] "Ring it again," she said. [303] "It has no tongue and half a mouth," he said. [313] "It cannot be rung. [317] It can only be rebuilt." [322] No forge remained; the temple was under sixty feet of water, and bronze poured hot cracks as surely as bronze struck cold. [344] But Ospren had eleven years of drip counts, and drips are a kind of casting done slowly. [361] Where the viaduct wept over the cistern mouth, the mineral had already grown a knuckle of bronze the size of a fist, ringing faintly when the wind moved. [389] Grow it in the shape of a bell and it would be a bell. [403] The difficulty was that the thickness of a bell's wall decides its note, and thickness, in a drip, is time. [423] Too fast and the metal laid down porous and dead; too slow and the mouth would close before the crown thickened. [444] The rate had to be held between four and five drops a minute for as long as it took, and only one thing on the hill could hold water at exactly the temperature where bronze consented to settle without souring. [484] Blood-warm. [485] Lukewarm. [486] Ospren laughed, and then sat down on the blue stone and cried a little, and Cass, who did not understand, patted his shoulder. [509] They put the fish in the cistern first, because Cass insisted, and because it was a hen heavy with roe, and a tally needs something left on the other side of it. [541] The rebuilding took two years, four months, and nine days. [551] Ospren slept beside the seep with his hand in the channel, warming nine gallons to the temperature of himself, waking every hour to count drops against a candle marked in sixths. [582] Cass built the clay former, broke it, built it again, and gradually stopped believing that mages fixed things quickly, which Ospren considered the most useful thing he ever taught her. [612] They hung the new bell at dawn on the day the drips stopped, in the archway where the sapphire threw its second aqueduct out across the fields. [639] It was not beautiful; it was grey-green and pitted, a ship's bell grown rather than cast, still bearing the old bell's cracked lip fused into its crown like a scar it had swallowed. [672] Cass reached for the rope. [677] "Once," Ospren said. [680] "Gently. [681] Devotion is what breaks these things." [687] She struck it once, softly, and the note went out along the water and down the nine miles and into the sea, low and warm and unhurried. [714] Nothing happened for eleven days. [719] Then the cistern fish, which had grown fat and stupid and numerous, went down the outflow toward the ocean in a silver rope, and by summer the boats came back, and nobody thanked the lukewarm mage, because nobody could believe that a thing so slow had done it. [767] Ospren went back to his ledger, and counted, and was content. [778]
