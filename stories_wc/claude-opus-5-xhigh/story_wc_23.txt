They called her the patchwork navigator because her charts were sewn from other people's scraps. [15] Bus schedules, hymn margins, a butcher's wrapping paper with a river drawn in blood-thin pencil. [30] Vesna had worked the starlit signal station on Kobalt Ridge for nine years, and for eight of them the dish had turned. [52] Then the hydraulics failed, and the great white ear froze facing the eastern pass, as if listening for one thing only. [73] After the hydraulics failed, the ministry stopped sending fuel, and Vesna stopped pretending she worked for anyone but the returning. [93]

Because they were returning. [97] Forty years the valley had emptied westward - trains, then trucks, then rafts - and now the current had reversed. [115] Reverse migration, the old word for birds who fly the wrong way on purpose, though nobody in the convoys thought it was wrong. [138] They came home to houses that were now cellars, to orchards gone to willow, to a border that shifted with the season. [160]

The route was the problem. [165] The only safe corridor ran along the drowned rail cut, and its width fluctuates with moon phases: at full moon the reservoir swelled and swallowed the ballast; at new moon a person could walk it dry-shod, eleven kilometers, in a single night. [207] So Vesna had to build something the frozen dish could not do: a chain of small relays down the ridge, mast to mast, powered by scavenged panels and the wind. [237] She built them out of bed frames and irrigation pipe, and to plumb each mast true she hung her one inheritance on a length of fishing line: the patina bronze censer foot from her mother's church, a clawed green lion's paw, all that the fire had left. [284] It weighed exactly enough to be honest. [291]

Her mother had walked west in 1983 and died in a rented room above a laundromat, and Vesna had gone with her, and come back alone at thirty-one. [319] Everyone asked why she stayed on a ridge with a broken machine. [331] She never said the true answer, which was that she wanted to navigate the geography of loss - to give it contour lines and elevations and safe passes, so that it could be crossed instead of merely suffered. [368]

The intelligence came through cryptic phone calls at midnight. [377] The station's copper landline still worked, a fossil the ministry had forgotten to disconnect, and every night at twelve it rang. [398] Voices she would never meet gave her fragments and hung up: 'Nine geese at the mill, none at the weir.' [418] 'The bread is warm on the third shelf.' [426] 'Aunt Milica's knee hurts, so the water is high.' [435] It took her two years to learn the grammar: geese meant patrols, shelves meant checkpoints, and any mention of a body's ache meant the reservoir was rising toward the full moon. [466] She stitched each fragment onto her patchwork chart with red thread and dated it, and by dawn she knew where the corridor was walkable and where it would drown a child. [497]

On the last night of March a caller broke protocol. [507] 'Four hundred of us at the pass,' a man said, plainly, exhausted. [519] 'Two days of food.' [523] 'Which way?' [525]

The moon was three days from full; the rail cut would be waist-deep by Thursday. [540] Vesna took the censer foot off its line, weighed it in her palm, and told him the thing she had been building toward for nine years. [566] 'Not the cut.' [569] 'Come over the ridge, past the frozen dish.' [577] 'I have lit it.' [581]

She had wired every relay mast with a single bulb, and that night she switched them on, eleven small stars descending the ridge in a line no map had ever shown. [612] From the station door she watched them come up out of the dark, four hundred people carrying suitcases and grandmothers, and the sky above the dish was so thick with stars that the station earned its name twice over. [651] Someone had a censer, actual and whole, swinging smoke that smelled of her mother's Sunday coat. [667]

She did not weep until an old woman asked her, in the valley's oldest accent, whether the church still stood, and Vesna put the bronze paw into her hands and said, 'This much of it.' [702] It was not comfort exactly. [707] It was the rawer thing that comfort is built from: the knowledge that the route existed, that it had been surveyed by someone who had also lost the place she was standing in. [740] At midnight the phone rang again, and Vesna picked it up with smoke in her hair, ready to write down the next fragment, ready for the next moon. [768]
