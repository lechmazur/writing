Nobody in the ravine could lie anymore, which is why the orchard needed me to be its liar. [18] I could not, of course; the empathy virus had taken that from all of us. [33] It had come through the border markets four winters ago, and it did not kill; it simply opened every face into every other face, so that a spoken falsehood arrived in the listener's chest as a nail. [70] We became involuntarily truthful, and we learned that a village of perfect honesty is a village where no one sleeps. [90] My name is Sabra Vey, and I am a truffle forager, which means I am rational by trade: I dig where the evidence says to dig. [116] The orchard hangs in the Kettle Ravine on eleven cables, baskets of soil and oak and old apple stock swinging over the water, and when the storms come up the gorge the whole grove tilts like a slow bell. [155] Under those swinging roots I find black truffles the size of fists, and that is why the traders climb down to us at all. [179] I traded eleven kilos to a man from the quarantine coast for a smuggled relic: an antique glass eye, blue as a gas flame, cold, with a hairline crack across the iris. [211] He had lifted it from a sealed museum, and he wept while he handed it over, because he could not pretend he was not robbing the dead. [238] I wanted it because of the textbooks. [245] In the ravine library, under the drip buckets, we keep the discredited shelf: mesmerism, humoral balance, phrenology, a 1907 tract on ocular sympathetics that claims contagion of feeling passes along the axis of the gaze and can be occluded by a false eye. [288] Rumored cures in discredited books are usually nothing; but nothing is where a rational person begins, because a claim you can test is worth more than a comfort you cannot. [318] So I tested it on myself first, in the drying shed, with Tomas holding the lamp. [334] I held the glass eye over my right socket, met his gaze through it, and said: the truffles this year are poor. [356] They were not poor; they were the best in six years; and Tomas flinched, but only a little, the way you flinch at distant thunder rather than the strike. [385] We repeated it forty times over three storms, with different speakers, different lies, a control group blindfolded, and the numbers held: the glass occluded roughly two thirds of the transmission. [415] The old tract was wrong about souls and right about eyes, which is how most wrongness works. [432] I did not do this so we could deceive each other. [443] I did it to cultivate psychological safety, which is a phrase from another discredited book, a manual for factory teams, and it means: a place where a person can be wrong out loud without being flayed. [479] Since the virus, no child in the ravine had told a hopeful exaggeration; no widow had said I am fine; no picker had guessed at a harvest number and been allowed to be wrong. [513] We had honesty and no mercy, and the cables were the only things in the gorge still holding. [531] So we built the Mercy Frame: the glass eye set in a brass ring on a post at the center of the hanging orchard, at speaking height. [558] You stand behind it, and whoever listens must look at you through the blue crack, and inside that ring you are permitted to be uncertain, to be gentle, to be partly untrue. [590] Elsa Barrow used it first, and told her son that his father had died quickly. [605] He felt a far-off ache and nothing more, and he slept that night, and the storm came up the ravine and the whole orchard swung and no one screamed. [634] I keep the ledger of it, because someone rational should. [644] Line one: the relic is stolen, and I stole it, and I would do it again. [660] Line two: it is glass and paint and a dead stranger's socket, and all it does is narrow a gaze; the mercy is ours, and we could have chosen it without any relic at all. [695] Line three: I tell the children this, standing behind the ring, and they hear it as thunder far off, and they believe me anyway. [719] That is the shape grace takes now: cracked, secondhand, bolted to a post, swinging over the water while the wind tries the cables one by one. [745]
