The gondola operator's name was Ilva Rask, and her whole world was in transit. [14] Every eleven minutes her cab swung out from the cliff station and crossed the gorge to the drifting prism terrace, a raft of tilted glass panels moored in the updraft like a chandelier that had learned to float. [52] Below, the valley railway ran its four daily trains, and each one dragged a whistle behind it up the rock walls. [73] Ilva worked for the terrace because of what happened in the quiet that follows a train whistle's echo. [91]

The terrace was a sanatorium of sorts, though its founders preferred 'listening house.' [104] People came who could no longer bear noise: veterans of the tunnel blasting crews, mourners, a violinist whose hands shook at applause. [126] They lay on the prism panels while the glass split the afternoon into slow bands of color, and they were promised silence. [148] Ilva's job, officially, was monitoring noise levels: a needle gauge bolted beside her brake lever, a logbook, a rule that any reading above twenty decibels on the terrace must be reported and cured. [181] She was fluidly logical about it, the way water is logical: she found the cause, then found the shape of the cause, then poured herself around it until it changed. [211] A rattling gutter she packed with felt; a guest who wept at dawn she moved to the eastern panel, where the wind hushed under him like a hand. [239]

Her reason was older than the terrace. [246] Nine years ago her brother Teodor had been a brakeman on the valley line, and the last sound she heard him make was the whistle at the mouth of the north tunnel, two long blasts, the signal for 'all well.' [286] Then the echo went up the walls, and thinned, and in the quiet that followed there was a sound of stone that was not a sound at all, only an absence arriving. [318] She had hated silence ever since, and hatred, being useless, had turned into a project: to teach silence how to comfort. [339]

She kept Teodor's storm glass vial on a cord at her throat: a finger of sealed glass, camphor and saltpeter suspended in spirit, the brakeman's cheap barometer. [366] When pressure fell, the crystals catalyzed into white ferns, and Teodor used to say a man could read tomorrow off his own chest. [389] On the third of October, crossing at 4:12, Ilva looked down and saw the vial filled with ferns to the stopper. [410] Her needle gauge told the same story in its own language: eleven decibels, the lowest reading in her nine years, the gorge holding its breath before a squall. [438]

The terrace, moored in an updraft that was about to become a downdraft, would be flung against the north wall within the hour unless it was hauled two hundred meters upwind on its winch cables. [473] Six crew could not turn those winches; forty hands could, and forty hands only work together if they can hear each other. [495] Warden Verrin met her at the landing rail and said the guests had paid for silence and would have it. [515] Ilva did not argue with the rule; as always, she looked for its shape and poured herself around it. [534] The rule forbade noise above twenty decibels; it said nothing about the sound a room makes when everyone in it breathes on the same count. [559] She went panel to panel, knelt, and asked each guest for one thing: to pull on a cable and to breathe out, hard, on the beat she gave them with her hand. [591]

The violinist stood first, and that one movement catalyzed the rest of the terrace. [605] Then the blasting crews, who knew winch work in their shoulders, and then the mourners, who knew about pulling. [624] Forty people hauled the drifting prism terrace upwind through a falling sky, and the loudest thing on it was forty exhalations, which the gauge recorded at nineteen. [651] It was a vaulting hush, the kind that lifts a ceiling: glass singing faintly, color sliding across their arms, no one speaking, everyone heard. [675] The squall came through beneath them and hit the north wall where the terrace had been. [691]

At 5:40 the freight ran the valley, and its whistle came up the rock as it always did, two long blasts, thinning. [713] Ilva stood in the quiet that follows a train whistle's echo, waiting for the old absence to arrive, and it did not. [735] The silence had forty breaths in it, and the ferns were already dissolving in the vial at her throat, and everyone on the terrace was in transit toward something kinder. [765] She wrote nineteen in the logbook and, under remarks, the word comfort. [777]
