Elgin Mott was the meekest man on Pomarel, and everyone agreed the planet deserved better. [15] He apologized to doors. [19] He apologized to the anvil when he struck it. [28] But between understanding and confusion -- the blue hour when Pomarel's small sun dropped behind the floating rock arches and the orchards forgot their own names -- Elgin became something else. [57]

The arches were the mine's fault. [63] Three generations back, the company had cored out the gravity-veins beneath the orchards, and when the veins ruptured, whole hillsides tore loose and hung there, upside-down and blossoming, tethered to nothing. [94] Below them the ground went sour, the roots went quiet, and the settlers learned to call the rubble home. [113] Elgin's grandmother had called it worse things. [120]

The magician came through in Elgin's twelfth year, a shabby conjurer working the harvest fairs, and when he died of the coughing sickness he left behind one length of azure silk, twelve feet of it, light as breath. [158] It was not a trick. [163] Drawn across a sleeping person's face during the blue hour, the silk lifted an awakening out of them -- one small waking, the kind of clarity a mind makes when it first opens its eyes -- and held it, shivering, in the weave. [204]

Elgin learned what to do with them by accident, and then on purpose. [217] He bartered. [219] A hinge for an awakening. [224] A plow blade for three. [229] The neighbors thought it a harmless eccentricity, paying the shy smith in something they had plenty of and could not spend. [250] They woke a little slower the next morning; that was all. [261]

Elgin carried the loaded silk down the abandoned mine shafts. [271] This was the deliberate part, the part he had worked out over nine years with a mason's patience. [289] The old shafts ran under every dead orchard on the plateau, and in their walls the severed root-ends still hung, grey and mummified, waiting for something to tell them to try again. [321] An awakening would do it. [326] He would find a root, wrap it in the azure silk, and wait while the borrowed waking bled out of the weave and into the wood. [352]

Nothing happened quickly. [355] Nothing on Pomarel ever had. [360] But in the fourth year a shaft ceiling cracked and a white shoot came through it, and Elgin sat down in the dark and wept, quietly, so as not to bother anyone. [392]

The trouble arrived with the assessor. [398] The company still owned the mineral rights, and a survey drone had photographed green where green had been declared impossible. [418] The assessor stood in Elgin's smithy with a sealed order to flood the shafts and re-core the plateau. [436] "You have no standing," she said, not unkindly. [444] Elgin looked at his boots. [449] He apologized. [451] Then he asked her to come back at the blue hour, and, because meek men are easy to indulge, she did. [472]

The whole settlement was waiting at the mouth of Shaft Eleven. [483] Under the hanging hillsides, in that light that turns everything the color of a held breath, ninety-one people had come to be paid nothing and give something anyway. [511] They lay down in the sour dirt in rows. [520] Elgin walked the rows with the silk, and it grew heavy as a wet sail, and it grew blue -- not the dusk's blue, a fiercer one, the blue of a forge at the exact moment the iron decides to bend. [560]

He went down alone. [564] He wrapped every root-end he could reach, ninety-one of them, and came up at dawn with an empty cloth and torn hands. [586]

It took eleven days. [590] The assessor stayed, because she was curious, and because the settlement fed her. [603] On the eleventh morning the plateau split along a hundred green seams, and the plateau's dead orchards stood up out of the rubble in a single slow shrug of stone and blossom. [635] You cannot flood a shaft that a root has claimed; the order specified dead ground. [650] The assessor read her own paperwork twice, filed it as inapplicable, and left. [663]

Elgin went back to the forge. [669] He still apologized to the anvil. [675] But every dusk, in the hour between understanding and confusion, he folded the azure silk over his arm and walked out under the floating arches, quietly determined, looking for the next thing that only needed to be woken and told, patiently, that rubble is only soil that has not been asked yet. [727]
