They called her a lost soul collector, though Mira preferred the older word: ferrywoman without a boat. [17] Her work happened in the hush of a hidden sunrise, when light crawled the rim of Echo Gorge but had not yet fallen in. [41] In that gray half hour the gorge held its breath, and the dead could be heard. [57] She carried two things down the switchback trail: a harp that had never been tuned correctly, and a crocodile's tooth on a leather cord. [81] The tooth had belonged to her brother Ansel, who drowned laughing. [92] That detail mattered more than anyone in the guild would admit. [103] The old collectors worked with bells and solemn faces, gathering only the grief of the unresolved, and their clients rose from the ritual hollowed out, polished, dull. [130] Mira had watched a widow leave such a ceremony unable to remember her husband's terrible jokes. [146] So she set out to integrate laughter into healing, which the guild called heresy and she called arithmetic. [164] A soul is not only its wounds. [171]

Today's client waited at the gorge floor, a stonemason named Odell whose daughter had died in the spring flood. [190] He had not slept in ninety nights. [197] "I want her taken out of me," he said. [206] "The remembering is a stone in my chest." [214]

"I do not take," Mira said. [220] "I let her speak, and then you decide what to keep." [231]

She knelt, propped the harp against a slab of limestone, and began. [243] A properly tuned instrument would have been useless here. [252] The dead do not answer harmony; they answer the wrong note, the sour interval, the sound of something almost right. [272] It was through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp that Mira could pursue a soul across the water-scoured stone, following it the way a hunter follows a limp in the snow. [304] She played badly on purpose, and the gorge answered. [313]

Spectral confessions came up out of the rock like heat. [323] Every soul that had passed through Echo Gorge had left its unsaid things layered in the walls, and the crooked music loosened them. [346] A soldier admitted he had run. [352] A midwife admitted she had loved the wrong sister for forty years. [364] Mira let them pass; she was modestly fearless, unbothered by ghosts and thoroughly frightened of failing the living. [382]

Then a small voice arrived, and Odell made a sound like a door giving way. [397] The girl confessed first to the ordinary crimes of the dead: she had been afraid, she had wanted her father, she had let go of the rope. [424] Odell bent double. [427] Mira kept playing, and she pushed the harp further out of true, twisting the peg until the string whined. [446] This was the part the guild had never learned, because the guild stopped at sorrow. [461] Go further and the confessions turn, and the dead begin to admit what delighted them. [476]

The girl confessed that she had once put a frog in her father's boot and watched him hop the length of the yard. [499] She confessed she had stolen his chisel to carve a rude face into the church step. [515] She confessed that his singing was awful and that she had never told him. [529]

Odell laughed. [531] It came out of him cracked and horrified, half a sob, and then it came again, cleaner. [548] The gorge took the sound and gave it back a dozen times, and for a moment the walls seemed lit from inside, that luminous ache that Mira could never describe to anyone who had not heard it. [585]

Above them, the hidden sun finally cleared the rim. [594] The voices thinned out like frost. [600]

Mira untied the crocodile's tooth and set it in Odell's palm. [611] "My brother pulled this from a river beast and told the story wrong for years," she said. [628] "He drowned in that same river, and I could not say his name for two winters." [644] "Then I remembered the lying, and I laughed, and the name came back." [657]

Odell closed his hand around the tooth. [664] He asked what he owed. [669] "Tell someone the frog," Mira said, and shouldered the harp, deliberately, gloriously out of tune, and started up the trail before the day could grow warm. [695] Behind her, in the brightening gorge, a man was still laughing, and somewhere in the stone his daughter's confession settled in among the others, waiting for the next wrong note to set it free.
