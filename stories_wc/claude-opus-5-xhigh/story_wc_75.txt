The beekeeper came every thaw for forty years, and then he did not. [13] After the beekeeper's last visit, we counted the wax in the storerooms of Vressel and understood that the mountain had decided something about us. [37] I am Ilva Renn, guide of the Museum of Vanishing Trades, and I have spent my life explaining our city to people who arrived in it with nothing. [65] Vressel is a grotto city, hung in the limestone throat behind the Sorrow, a waterfall so monumental that its noise is our weather and its spray is our rain. [94] Three generations of displaced persons have made homes in its balconies: glassblowers from Ostreu, lace-cutters, ferrymen without a river, opera singers whose theater lies under a lake. [121] I am, my colleagues say, hopelessly historically oriented; I can date a spoon by its tarnish and I weep at inventories. [142] My favorite object is a single emerald-tinted opera glass lens, ground in Ostreu the year before the flooding, all that survives of a craft that once dressed the world's eyes in green. [174] Visitors hold it up and the grotto turns to summer meadow; children laugh; I explain, in my cultured murmur, that the tint was made with a copper recipe nobody alive can reproduce. [206] That sentence used to feel elegant. [212] Now it feels like an accusation. [218]

The winter after the beekeeper's last visit, the Sorrow froze. [228] It froze from the lip downward, thickening into a green-white wall, and our doorway to the world became a vault. [248] The air went stale and sweet. [254] My mother's family had a custom of baking bread shaped like initials, and that winter the whole city took it up, so the stale air smelled of rosewater and scorched flour: a rosewater dread, genteel and unbearable, the smell of people being polite while calculating how long the lamp oil would last. [306] The custom is simple: when a family loses a place, it bakes the initials of its dead and its living into loaves, so that the letters of an emptied language stay in the mouth. [340] You eat your grandmother's name. [345] You do not forget how it is written. [353]

The dough wanted honey, and honey had come up the switchback path each spring on the beekeeper's mule, and the beekeeper was dead of old age in a village we could no longer reach. [387] We used his last combs for the dough, and then burned the museum's wax for light: anatomical models, archive seals, votives from the drowned chapel. [412] I signed the deaccession orders myself, weeping at my own inventory, because a museum is a promise to the future and the future had begun to shiver. [439]

In the ninth week the council asked me for the cabinets. [450] Kiln-dry Ostreu walnut, two centuries old, enough to build a fire against the ice wall and thaw a channel wide enough for a boat. [474] I said no for one night. [480] I spent it in the dark gallery with the emerald lens against my eye, looking at glass I could not save and craftsmen I could not resurrect, and I understood at last what I had been doing wrong for thirty years. [521] I had been rescuing dying arts by embalming them. [530] The lens was safe and the recipe was gone; the lace was in a case and the fingers that knew it were washing dishes in the lower terraces. [558] Only the bread had survived three displacements, because nobody had ever put it behind glass. [573]

At dawn I gave them the cabinets. [580] We burned two hundred years of joinery for eleven days, and the ice wept, and on the twelfth day the thaw opened a green channel and a boat went out for grain and came back with grain. [617] It also brought two hives, bought from the beekeeper's granddaughter, who wanted to know whether we still made the letter-bread. [637]

The museum now has no cabinets. [643] It has ovens. [646] The glassblowers' grandchildren grind lenses on the old wheels, and last month a girl named Teodora Baum brought me a disc of green glass, cloudy, honest, wrong by two shades. [676] I held it beside the emerald-tinted opera glass lens and told her, in my cultured murmur, that she had four years of failures ahead of her. [702] She brought me bread the next morning: two loaves, T and B, glazed with new honey, still smelling faintly of rosewater. [723] I ate her name. [727] I did not forget how it was written. [735] The Sorrow roars again, and it will freeze again, and I have stopped calling that dread; I call it a season, and we know now how to thaw it. [764]
