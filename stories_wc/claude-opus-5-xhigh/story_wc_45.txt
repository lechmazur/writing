They found the substitute body on a Tuesday, in the millrace, and I refused to believe it for twelve years. [20] It was alder wood, wax-skinned, dressed in my nephew's coat, and it had my nephew's face until the sun got to it. [42] The village said fairies; I said drowning, and grief, and a carver's cruel joke. [56] My sister said I had let him break, once, and never mended him right, and I forgave her that before she finished saying it. [80] Forgiveness is cheap for me; certainty is what I hoard. [90] I am a bone-setter, the fourth in my line, and I keep my workshop in the secret garden behind the yew maze at Aldermere. [114] You must walk the hedge maze to reach it, seven turns, and the seventh smells of quince. [131] Inside, my grandmother's rhymes hang beside my own inventions: steamed ash splints, brass ratchets, a traction frame I built from a cider press. [154] And overhead, the lamps. [158] Every bone I set wrong has a lamp: a small glass jar, a wick, a name scratched in the base. [178] I hang them on wires in patterns, and I light them when I need to remember what a mistake taught me. [199] My mother called it vanity; I call it turning mistakes into constellations, because a scatter of failures is unbearable and a shape is not. [223] The boy came through the maze in October, alone, which no child has managed in my lifetime. [240] He was twelve or thereabouts, wrist swollen blue, and he held it out to me like a bill to be paid. [261] "You set my collarbone when I was six," he said. [271] He was shrewdly innocent, the way barter children are: he knew exactly what his story was worth and offered it plainly, without a flicker of pleading. [297] I told him to sit and I began the work I trust: thumb along the radius, the small honest conversation between hand and bone. [321] Bone setting is not belief; it is listening. [329] I pulled, felt the ends kiss and seat, and splinted him in steamed ash with a brass buckle of my own design. [351] Then, because my hands are older than my opinions, they went to his collarbone. [365] Under the skin lay a callus I knew the way I know my own signature: the ridge of a break I had set myself, twelve years ago, on a squalling six-year-old named Tomas. [398] That is where the cleft opened in my certainty, and it has not closed since. [413] He gave me the tooth then, without my asking. [422] It was a baby tooth on a red thread, small as a rice grain, and it was not enamel; it was something like frost that had learned to hold a shape. [453] "They pulled my own the first winter," he said, "and grew me this one instead; it is the changeling's baby tooth, and it aches when I am lied to." [482] I do not believe in changelings. [487] I believe in calluses, and in a tooth that would not scratch with my steel probe, and in a boy who yearned so plainly for his mother's kitchen that the yearning came off him like heat. [523] I did not promise him he was Tomas. [530] I walked him back through the seven turns to my sister's door and stood in the lane while she looked at his face, and then at his splint, and then at me. [562] She had buried a wax boy in consecrated ground and been told for twelve years by me that wood is wood. [583] "I was wrong about something," I said, which is the nearest thing to an apology my trade allows. [601] She forgave me before I finished saying it, which is how I learned where I got the habit. [619] That night I went back through the maze and blew glass for a new lamp. [634] I sealed the changeling's baby tooth into the base where the name goes, because the mistake was mine and it deserves a proper marker. [658] Then I hung it high, off at an angle from the others, well outside my tidy old shapes. [676] A constellation, my grandmother used to say, is only a mistake in perspective that people agreed to love. [694] The boy comes on Thursdays to have the splint checked, and stays to work the bellows. [710] He asks what each lamp is for; I tell him the truth, one bad set at a time, and he listens like a boy learning a trade. [737] I still do not believe in fairies. [744] But I have stopped mistaking the edge of my knowledge for the edge of the world, and on clear nights, in a garden no one can find without seven turns and the smell of quince, I light every lamp I own.
