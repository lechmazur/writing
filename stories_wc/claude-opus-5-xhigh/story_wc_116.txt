The chronicle keeper had not spoken aloud in eleven years, and the library was healthier for it. [17] Down in the catacombs of lost knowledge, where fig roots had cracked the shelves and moss had eaten the spines of ten thousand certainties, silence did the work of gardeners. [47] Ivrel called it regenerative silence: vellum rotting into loam, loam feeding seedlings whose leaves came up veined with sentences nobody had written twice. [70] She read them, copied them, and said nothing. [78] Her only correspondence crossed the dark across the drift of untold seasons, a single origami swan, well-worn, its paper soft as skin. [100] Teodor sent it from the obsidian refuge along the river, a hut of black volcanic glass he had built where the ferry landing used to be, and the messages were never ink. [132] They lived via cryptic folds: a crease doubled at the throat meant the barley failed; a wing turned inward meant soldiers on the north road; a tail pressed flat and reopened meant the peace still held, barely. [169] Every refolding left a scar, and the scars were the language. [180] Ivrel gripped reality loosely now. [185] Some mornings she set out two cups and asked her apprentice, dead forty years, whether the cistern had frozen. [204] It did not trouble her much; a chronicle keeper is a lens, not a judge, and a lens may be scratched and still pass light. [229] In the autumn of the ninth name of the city above, a girl burrowed in through the collapsed ossuary, dragging soil with her elbows. [253] Her name was Wren, and her village was ash. [262] She had heard that something underneath the city remembered everything, and she wanted a page. [277] "Write that the garrison did it," she said. [285] "Write that they were wrong." [290] Ivrel wrote: In the ninth autumn, the garrison burned Ellwater; a girl came down with soil under her nails and asked for justice. [313] Then she stopped. [316] Wren read it upside down by lichen-light and began to shout, and the shouting shook dust from the arches, and Ivrel let her, because shouting was also change and she had sworn to witness change without judgment. [353] "You are a coward," Wren said, and Ivrel wrote that down too. [365] The swan came three nights later, carried by a river-boy who would not descend past the third stair. [383] Ivrel unfolded it in her lap and read the new creases with her thumbs. [397] The river had risen; the obsidian refuge stood in four feet of water; the wing was folded all the way under, which in all their years of private grammar had never meant anything but a body. [433] Teodor had folded his own death and sent it downstream to be witnessed. [446] Ivrel sat a long while in the crumbling peace of the stacks, where roots ticked as they grew and something small burrowed behind the atlases. [471] Then she found Wren asleep against a shelf of drowned law books and shook her awake. [487] She put the swan in the girl's hands and, for the first time in eleven years, used her voice. [506] It came out like a hinge. [512] "Throat is hunger," she said, "wing is soldiers, wing tucked under is a death, and a tail flattened means the peace is still holding, barely." [537] "Everything else you must invent, and someone downstream must learn it." [548] "Why me?" Wren asked. [552] "Because you are angry," Ivrel said, "and anger keeps a record warm; I have gone too quiet to hold a season straight." [574] "But you will not write that they were wrong," Wren said. [585] "I will write that they burned it," Ivrel said, "which will outlive any verdict I could give it." [603] Wren climbed out through the ossuary at dawn with the swan buttoned inside her shirt. [618] Later, from the obsidian refuge she bailed dry and roofed with river willow, she folded a crease of her own devising and sent the bird down to a stranger. [647] It meant: a keeper has died, the catacombs are closed, and the words are going into the loam to come up again as leaves. [671] Ivrel had asked for nothing else. [676] She had lain down among the seedling sentences, gripping the world so loosely by then that she could not have said which season it was, only that it was turning. [706] Above her, the peace crumbled on schedule; below her, in the dark, the silence went patiently on making the next library out of the last. [731]
