Odile kept a ledger of small drifts, because nobody else in Bell Harbor would admit the town was changing. [19] The ferry left four minutes later each spring; the herons came in fours instead of forties; the bay, once loud with hulls and gulls, had gone quiet by degrees so patient that her neighbors called it peace. [56] She called it something else, and wrote it down. [65] Her father had been a perfumer, and he taught her that the nose forgets a smell it lives inside; she had extended the lesson to everything. [91]

That spring the ledger held one entry she could not resolve: why some houses feel alive. [107] Her mother's house did not, anymore. [113] It had once: the door breathed, the stairs held opinions, the kitchen argued with itself at night. [130] Then Vera died in February, and by April the rooms had gone smooth as a stopped clock. [147]

Odile searched for the mechanism the way she searched for everything, by comparison. [160] She visited the Ferreiras, whose house was gloriously alive, and inventoried it: a boot in the bookcase, a teaspoon in a coat pocket, a hairbrush asleep on the piano, a novel face-down and abandoned mid-sentence. [195] She visited the Larkins, whose house felt embalmed, and found nothing out of place at all. [211] "A living house," she told the Ferreira boy while he trailed her to the gate, "is just a house full of interruptions." [233] The boy repeated it to his mother, who repeated it at the market, and the line went around the bay as something inadvertently profound, which embarrassed Odile, since she had meant it as data. [267]

Data, though, has consequences. [271] Her mother's house had no interruptions because Odile had spent February putting everything back. [285] Every displaced object Vera had left mid-life, she had returned to its proper drawer: the reading glasses, the thimble, the half-empty tin of cocoa, the shears from the garden bench. [315] She had tidied the woman out of her own rooms. [325]

And in the top drawer of the hall chest, wrapped in waxed paper, sat the shard. [341] It was orris root, a broken piece of iris rhizome her father had cured for three years, and it smelled of violets and old wood and the inside of a held breath. [373] Perfumers use it as a fixative: on its own it says little, but it stops other scents from fleeing, holds them in place for years. [398] Vera had kept it by her bed through the last long winter, and Odile understood, standing in the hallway with the drawer open, what she had actually filed away. [427]

A shard of hush. [431] Not silence, which is empty, but hush, which is silence with someone in it, fixed and preserved and unable to fade. [452] She had been sanctifying grief instead of the woman, and the house had held its breath to match. [470]

So she began, in the hour before dawn, while the streetlights daydreamed over the water and the bay lay flat and pink and unbothered. [494] She put the thimble in her coat pocket. [502] She set the reading glasses on the windowsill, folded, as if Vera had just gone to see who was at the door. [524] She carried the shears back out to the garden bench and left them there, rusting, mid-task. [540] She opened a book and laid it face-down on the arm of the chair, and it felt less like a lie than a sentence she was allowed to finish later. [570] Displacement by displacement, she sanctified the rooms, and the stairs began, faintly, to hold opinions again. [586]

The shard she took down to the shore. [594] She had meant to throw it in, but orris root is iris, and iris wants dirt, so she knelt at the bed under the kitchen window where Vera's flags came up every May and pushed the shard down among them. [634] Hush is only a fixative; given soil, it goes back to being a flower. [648]

The sun came up the way it does in a quiet bay in spring, a hushed blaze, all that fire and not one sound. [672] The streetlights blinked off, one by one, dreaming done. [681] Odile opened the ledger and did not write down a drift. [692] She wrote: the herons are still four, the ferry is still late, and I have stopped putting things away. [711] Then she left the pen on the page, cap off, in the middle of the line. [727]
