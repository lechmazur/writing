The rebellion had not died; it had merely sat down in the dust to catch its breath. [17] Nine months of ceasefire had abandoned the Ceyla spaceport to rust and unfinished intentions. [31] In repair bay six, cranes hung like sleeping herons over the gutted hull of the Marrow Lark, and Ivet Sarn walked its catwalks barefoot. The stones stood a hundred meters beyond the bay's open wall: eleven grey megaliths, older than the colony charter, older than the word colony. [146] The port's founders had built their landing fields around them because the ground refused to be leveled, and because the stones sang. [168] Not loudly. [170] A hum you felt in the arches of your feet, a low pressure that rose and fell with the sun. [263] Twelve ships waited in the outer dark for a single flare from Ceyla, and twelve crews had begun to suspect the flare would never come. [288] Ondro's diagnosis was blunt: the coil needed an outside resonance to reignite, a clean tone at eleven hertz, and every generator in the bay was scrap. [314] "Eleven hertz," Ivet repeated, and laughed at nothing in a way that made him step back. [330] "Eleven stones." [332] "The circle peaks in the margin of daylight, that last thin minute when the sun is half-drowned." [349] "I have felt it eight hundred times." [407] She needed the mess to mean something; she needed one line to run from the stones through her feet to the sky and hold.
