The last bus coughed out of the village station at 9:41, and Mira stood on the platform and let it go. [21] When the taillights thinned into the mist, the station became what it truly was: a bench, a timetable, and a bulb full of dying moths. [46] She had a ticket to the city in one pocket and, in the other, the burned edge of a love letter. [67] Rather than wait nine hours on that bench, she climbed the hill road toward the light she had watched all her childhood: the amber pavilion above the vineyard, glowing like a lantern set down in the middle of autumn. [106] Anselm was awake, as he was every night of harvest. [116] The estate paid him to walk the rows against frost and thieves, and to sound a bell if either came; the village called him the sentinel and left him alone. [146] He did not seem surprised to see her. [154] 'The pavilion is always open after the final bus,' he said, pouring tea the color of the walls. [172] 'People who miss it on purpose usually need somebody to disagree with.' [184] She showed him the scrap then, because he had not asked. [195] A triangle of paper, brown at the curl, three words surviving in the middle: 'even the rain.' [212] 'His?' Anselm asked. [215] 'Mine,' Mira said. [218] 'I wrote it to Tomas in April and burned it in the stove, and then I put my hand in and took back the corner.' [243] Her aunts had a word for her and Tomas: mismatched. [253] Wrong village, wrong prayer, wrong money, wrong everything, while the man waiting for her in the city matched her on every line of the ledger. [278] Anselm set down his cup, took the lamp, and walked her out into the rows, where the leaves had gone the same yellow as the pavilion glass. [305] He crouched and showed her the knot low on a trunk, a lump of scar tissue. [321] 'Every vine on this hill is two plants that were never meant to meet,' he said. [337] 'American root, French wood, married with a knife because the well-matched ones all died of the louse a hundred years ago.' [358] 'The graft holds because each one needs what the other cannot make.' [370] He was gently persuasive in the way of men who have decided never to argue: he only kept drawing parallels and letting her walk into them. [396] The field blend at the top of the slope, he said, was four grapes that ripened at four different weeks and quarreled in the vat for a year before they agreed to be wine. [430] The matched rows, the tidy monoculture on the eastern flat, made something correct that nobody remembered drinking. [447] The mismatches, he said, were the ones destiny kept. [456] Then he told her about his wife, dead eleven years, a woman from three hundred kilometers of different weather. [475] 'For two years we mistranslated each other every single day,' he said. [487] 'Then I learned to taste what she tasted, and she learned to stand where I stood, and that labor is the only thing I have ever been proud of.' [516] That, he admitted, was why he kept the lamp lit and the door unlatched: not for the vines, which could survive a thief, but to nurture in the odd stranger the habit of standing inside another person's weather. [554] He had a phrase for it, borrowed from a battered book under the bench: intersubjective understanding, which he pronounced carefully, like the name of a grape. [580] Near three in the morning, with the lamp low, Mira held the burned edge to the light and rediscovered the rest. [601] She had memorized her own letter without meaning to, and the sentences came back in order, one clause at a time, until she was speaking them aloud to a stranger and an unlit hillside: that she wanted his ordinary hours, his bad singing, even the rain of his miserable coastal town. [652] She did not cry, and Anselm did not look at her; that was the courtesy of the place. [670] 'I could still marry the matched one,' she said. 'It would be a calm life.' [685] 'It would,' he agreed, and said nothing else, which was the most persuasive thing he did all night. [703] At five-twenty the first bus of the morning came up from the coast road, going the other way. [721] Mira left her city ticket folded under the teacup, weighted like a note, and went down the hill through the yellow rows with the scrap of paper flat against her palm. [752] Anselm rang no bell. [756] There had been no frost. [761] He washed the two cups, wrote the date in his book, and added a line he had written before for others: another graft, taken. [785]
