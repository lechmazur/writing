Eleven months after first contact, the committee finished building a homecoming. [11] They called it an artificial pilgrimage, which Ines Vaquero thought was at least honest. [25] Three kilometers of overgrown sculpture path through the old foundry park, ending at a rainwashed tower, all of it engineered to make a stranded creature feel that it had returned to somewhere it had never been. [61] Ines had been hired to measure whether it worked, partly because the panel needed someone who would say if it hadn't. [82] She was, the press insisted, the luminous skeptic, and both halves were literal. [95] Two years earlier, without approval, she had tagged her own blood with a phosphor that bound to the exact cascade of oxytocin and dopamine that fires when a person walks through a door they grew up walking through. [133] The ethics board called it academically rebellious; her supervisor called it worse; the papers called it beautiful, since her wrists now glowed faintly whenever she felt at home. [161] They had not glowed in four years. [167] She wanted to understand the chemistry of homecoming the way a locksmith wants to understand a lock: from the inside, with the housing off. [191]

The Guest could not go home. [197] Its vessel had arrived spent, and the direction it had come from no longer contained anything to return to; the astronomers had checked twice and then stopped talking about it. [227] So the committee proposed to build the feeling instead: a route, a threshold, a relic, a rite. [244] Ines argued for four hours that you cannot manufacture arrival, then wrote the measurement protocol anyway. [260]

Spring came in soft and gray, and the park drank it. [271] Rain stood in the palms of the bronzes, a nine-foot mother, a fist, an arch of welded ribs, all of them gone green-black and patient under the wet, holding the kind of silence Ines had privately started calling bronze quiet. [311] The Guest walked ahead of the pilgrims because the design required it, and it left a trail nobody had planned. [331] Its feet shed a fine pale dust, ceramic and dry, the last of the world it had launched from, and the rain could not touch it. [357] For three kilometers the pilgrims converged on the tower via dusty footprints left on an overgrown sculpture path, following a dead planet pressed into wet moss. [383] Ines came last, with her sleeves pushed up, watching her own wrists like a hostile referee. [399]

In her coat pocket she carried the control: a lozenge of fly amber, forty million years old, her mother's, the one thing from the house in Tarragona that had not been sold. [431] Real memory, real object, real loss; if the phosphor did not answer to that, it would not answer to a committee's staircase. [453]

The tower had been a foundry chimney once, and the designers had left the water stains alone, which was the only decision Ines admired. [477] Inside, the rite was simple: each pilgrim climbed, said a name of a place they had loved, and set down something carried. [499] Ines climbed with her instruments and her skepticism intact through eighty-one steps of dripping brick. [514]

At the top, in the doorway of the light, the Guest was waiting for her, dust still falling from it. [534] It had not glowed either, in any wavelength the panel could read, which the panel was calling a null result and Ines was calling grief. [559] She took out the amber and held it up in the gray window light, because it was the only thing she had to offer, and because she wanted a witness for the control. [592]

The Guest looked a long time at the fly inside it, wings open, caught two wingbeats from wherever it had been going. [614] Then it said, through the slow translator at its throat, the first sentence it had ever offered unprompted: 'It is still flying.' [636]

Ines heard her mother's kitchen in it, the whole ruined house, the resin of the pines above Tarragona still running down bark somewhere, sap that had not finished being sap. [666] Her wrists lit. [669] Faint, greenish, unmistakable, the phosphor answering a threshold she had walked through in a chimney in a foreign spring, with dust on her boots and a stranger beside her. [698] The measurement was ruined and the finding was enormous: homecoming was not a place remembered, it was a sentence somebody said to you that made your carrying make sense. [727] She set the fly amber down on the sill, which was not in the protocol. [742] Below them the bronzes stood in the rain, holding their handfuls of water, and the pale trail on the path was already softening, and nobody in the tower said anything at all for a while. [777]
