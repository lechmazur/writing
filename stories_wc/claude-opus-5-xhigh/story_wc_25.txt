The geomagnetic storm arrived at dusk, and every satellite over the port city went blind at once. [17] Nadia had been waiting nine years for the sky to do that. [29] She was the labyrinth's maintenance worker, which meant she knew the stack of forty thousand shipping containers the way a surgeon knows a rib cage. [54] Twelve tiers high in places, the labyrinth grew and unmade itself hourly as the gantry cranes shuffled boxes, so no digital map ever stayed true for more than a shift. [84] Nadia's maps were folded paper, eleven of them, each creased into sixteenths so a single flick of the wrist could bring any corridor to the surface. [110] She scored the folds with the milled rim of a zodiac coin, a brass Year of the Goat piece her predecessor, Barsad, had pressed into her palm before he retired into the fog. [143] He left her two things: the coin, and a small key with no apparent lock. [158]

Dockmaster Renke ran the yard by loudspeaker. [164] He believed volume was authority, and he had spent a decade bolting horns to the light towers until the labyrinth boomed with his corrections and swallowed them in echo. [193] Nadia had told him once, quietly, that whispers travel farther than shouts in a steel maze. [209] He had laughed hard enough to be heard on three tiers. [220]

So she issued a challenge, and she made it look like a whim. [233] In truth she had rehearsed the whim for two years, waiting for a night when the aurora would drown the radios and Renke's horns would be the only voice left. [263] The stake was the Goat coin against Renke's horns: if she won, the loudspeakers came down. [279]

The test was simple, because that night an actual person was actually lost. [291] A customs trainee named Pell had walked into Block D at four o'clock to verify seals, and when the storm hit, the electronic gate locks froze shut and the yard's tracking tags turned to static. [326] Renke bellowed instructions through the horns for forty minutes. [335] The echoes came back off eight thousand corrugated walls, arrived at Pell as a smear of noise, and turned him in circles. [357]

Nadia climbed instead. [360] She had one gift beyond the maps, and it was this: she identified people by their walks. [377] Steel decks transmit gait like a signature - Renke's heel-first stamp, the crane oilers' scuffing shuffle, Barsad's old uneven tread. [396] Pell walked in nervous double-taps, a hesitation between each pair, and she caught the pattern ringing up a ladder rail from somewhere below Row 19. [421] That gave her a quadrant. [426] The folded maps gave her the spine. [433]

Barsad's key had shamed her for nine years, until she stopped hunting for a keyhole and started reading his maps as a question rather than an answer. [460] Every sheet, folded, put the same crease at the same spot: the derelict grain terminal buried under Block D, and its network of pneumatic speaking tubes, sealed since 1974. [489] The key fit no lock because it was not a key for a door; its shank was a square drive for a valve stem, and the valves lived under the deck plates, unmarked. [522] She pried up a plate at the junction her creases had been pointing at all along, seated the key, and turned. [543] The tube exhaled fifty years of dust. [550]

Above her the aurora ran green over the cranes, and the horns kept roaring nonsense. [565] Nadia put her mouth to the brass and whispered. [573] "Pell, stand still," she said, barely voicing it. [581] The tube carried the whisper under two blocks of containers and let it out of a rusted bell-mouth six meters from his boots, undistorted, intimate, like a hand on the shoulder. [612] His double-tap stopped. [615] "Left hand on the blue container, nine paces, then turn where you feel the wind." [630]

Eleven minutes later Pell came out at the fuel gate, shaking, unharmed. [642] Renke arrived at a stamp she heard four rows off. [652] She held out the zodiac coin anyway, because the challenge had been for the coin, and a bet is a bet. [673] Renke looked at the aurora, at the trainee, at the dead speakers hanging off the towers like fruit nobody wanted. [693] "Keep it," he said, and he said it quietly, which was how she knew she had won. [710] By morning the crews were down the towers with wrenches, and Nadia was scoring a new fold across Block D with the Goat's worn rim, mapping tubes for the next storm. [741]
