Nine hundred feet above the Foundry District, on the sky-bridge where the wind sang through cable-harps, Wren played her violin for coins that never came. [25] The city below her ran on schedule, gear-driven and gray, and its citizens had forgotten that sound could mean anything besides warning. [47] In her coat pocket she carried a locust shell, split down the back, hollow as a held breath. [65] Her brother Ilo had pressed it into her palm the night he vanished, saying only that a thing can leave its own body behind and still be itself. [93] She had known what he meant for years; she had not understood it until the orchard. [109]

The enchanted orchard lay past the last wall, the only green left in the province, and it was policed by living statues. [131] Marble wardens with pear-blossom crowns, they walked the rows at intervals, and their hands were heavy enough to crush a wrist. [152] Wren had gone there in autumn to see beauty before the Bureau harvested it into pigment and preservative. [170] She had played, quietly, and one statue stopped. [178] Not to seize her -- to listen, with its stone head tilted like a man remembering a name. [195] That was when Wren learned the wardens were not carvings at all, but people the Bureau had set in stone and tuned to the Metronome. [220] Their hearts still beat, sixty strikes a minute, obedient as clocks. [231] You could not talk to them; the stone let nothing in through the ears. [245] But a violin bowed resonantly soft, below hearing, is not heard at all -- it is felt, in the chest, where the rhythm lives. [268]

So she practiced on the sky-bridge all winter, signaling through heart rhythms: two beats, a pause, three beats, the pattern Ilo used to drum on the table before he laughed. [298] The bridge cables carried her low notes down into the Foundry, and men on the floor felt something stir and could not name it. [322] That gap between knowing and understanding, she decided, was the only door left unlocked in the world. [339]

In spring she went back with a spade and the locust shell. [351] The plan was small and impossible: uproot one sapling -- the youngest quince, no taller than her ribs -- and carry it into the gray city, and plant it in the cracked plaza where the Metronome stood. [386] Beauty could not be argued into a mechanical world; it had to be smuggled in and left to root. [405]

The orchard smelled of rust and blossom, tarnished wonder, magic kept alive on ledger paper. [420] Wren knelt, set the shell on a stone so it faced her like an audience, and began to play. [439] Two beats. [441] A pause. [443] Three. [444] Six wardens came down the row and stopped, and she felt their stone chests take up the pattern, one after another, like lamps catching. [468] They knew the rhythm; they did not yet understand that it was not the Bureau's. [483] In that gap she dug. [488] The spade went in easily, as if the orchard wanted the child gone from it, and the roots came up pale and wriggling and whole. [513]

When she stood, the nearest warden's face had cracked from brow to jaw. [526] Marble sheeted off him in plates, and beneath it was a man, thin, gray-lipped, breathing at his own speed for the first time in nine years. [552] He looked at the split husk on the stone, then at her, and drummed two beats on his chest, a pause, three. [574] Ilo. [575] He had gone to see the orchard too, and had been kept. [587] The other five were breaking free behind him, stone falling like slag from a mold, and the orchard filled with the terrible soft noise of people learning to move again. [617]

They carried the quince between them through the wall, into the plaza, and pried up the paving stones at the Metronome's feet while Wren played and the city stood watching with its mouth open. [651] Nobody stopped them; nobody had a procedure for this. [660] The sapling looked absurd there, crooked, half-dead, a green scribble against iron. [672] It looked like the beginning of something anyway. [680] Wren left the locust shell in the soil at its base, because a hollow thing, kept, teaches the next one how to split. [703] The Bureau would come, but the roots were already under the stone. [715] Then she climbed back to the sky-bridge, where the wind was already learning her tune, and played resonantly soft into the cables until dawn, two beats, a pause, three, for everyone below who knew and had not yet understood.
