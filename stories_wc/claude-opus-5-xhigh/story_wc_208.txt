The GPS died at the causeway, and Ivo Renner sat listening to the wipers argue with the rain. [18] Beyond the windshield, six offshore turbines turned in the mist, and their hum came through the glass like a lullaby sung by something enormous and patient. [44] He had come to survey a ruin he could no longer find. [56] The road ended in shingle; the shingle ended in an archway of grey stone, half-drowned, storm-tossed, with the sea slamming through its throat. [79] Under it, a woman in a yellow oilskin was tying driftwood into a shape he did not understand. [97]

"You're the engineer," she shouted over the surf, without turning around. [108] "Satellites quit on you?" she asked, thrusting a spar of bleached oak at him. [122] Her name was Sanna Voll, she sculpted driftwood, and she had been rebuilding the arch for eleven years. [140] Not the stone, since she was no mason, but the idea of it. [153] Every winter the sea took a course of blocks; every spring she wove the gap with driftwood lattices, simple triangles lashed with tarred rope, so that whoever came after her would have something to build against. [189]

"Complexity needs a scaffold," she said, "and a scaffold should be stupid simple, or nobody can copy it." [207] Ivo, who had a doctorate and a laptop full of dead maps, admitted he did not follow, and she seemed pleased, being perpetually learning herself and glad of company in it. [238]

She took him up the shingle to a hut that smelled of pitch and kelp, where the shelves held a masonry manual, a knot book, and notebooks in three different hands. [269] Then she lit a paraffin lamp behind a small square of painted glass. [282] It was a magic lantern slide, hand-tinted, dredged from a wreck by her grandfather: the same archway, whole, a lantern hung in its keystone, nine figures beneath it holding timber. [312] The slide had a second layer, a slip of glass that slid across, and when Sanna pushed it the nine figures became nine others in different coats, standing in the same places, holding the same timber. [348]

"A dissolving view," Ivo said, recognizing the trick, and then he stopped recognizing anything, because the coats belonged to three separate centuries. [370] The slide worked through layered meanings: one picture said here is the arch, and the two together said here is the arch, kept. [393] Sanna called the people in it her allies, though every one of them was dead. [408] "Timewoven alliances," she said, laughing at how grand it sounded, and she did not take it back. [425] Wreckers had robbed the arch of stone in 1780; a fisherman had propped it with spars in 1849; her grandfather had wound steel cable through it in 1961. [453] Each had had to reimagine the arch as whatever his century could afford, and each had left the next pair of hands a hint. [477]

Ivo had come to condemn the structure; his report was half written already in the language of loads and liabilities. [497] That night the storm rose, the turbine hum climbed a note, and he stood in the doorway in a reverie, watching the lantern beam wobble across wet stone. [525] By three in the morning the sea had pulled four blocks off the north pier, and Sanna was out in the spray with rope in her teeth, spirited as a gull in a gale. [559] He went to help, because there was nobody else, and because her triangles were, he saw, exactly right. [577] They lashed spars until dawn came grey and flat and the surf lay down like a dog. [594]

He wrote his report on the third day, and it was not the report he had planned. [611] It recommended nothing clever: a plain steel jig, four bolts, drawings any welder could read, and a copy of those drawings sealed in the keystone for whoever arrives in 2120. [641] Simple things, to scaffold a complexity neither of them would live to see finished. [655]

Sanna carved a tenth figure from the bleached oak and lashed it under the arch, its face turned toward the turbines. [676] "You're on the slide now," she told him, and pushed the second layer across. [690] He drove out along the causeway with the GPS still stubbornly blank, and did not need it, because he knew the road now by the hum. [716]
