The sanctuary had no walls, only prisms: forty panes of leaded glass set upright in the headland grass, splitting starlight into ribbons that lay across the ground like unwoven thread. [30] Mireia came at moonless midnight, as the keeper's letter had instructed, carrying a tapestry needle and a shovel. [48] She restored broken masks for a living, and she was, the village agreed, broken herself. [63] Under the central prism the turf was soft, and she began to exhume what the village had buried there in 1608. [84] It was not a body. [89] It was a fisherman's net, tarred and salt-stiff, folded like a sleeping animal, and every one of its four hundred knots had a name. [113] The keeper, Anselm, held the lantern while she lifted it free. [124] "Listen," he said, and she understood that he meant the opposite. [135] The net accumulates silence. [139] Three centuries of drowned men had their last words tied into it, each unspoken syllable pulled into a knot, and the knots had interlaced into a single hush so dense that the crickets stopped within ten paces of it. [178] That hush was why the spirits could not settle. [187] A soul with an unsaid thing in it walks; the village had known that since the first storm, and had chosen to bury the problem under glass rather than solve it. [218] Mireia had come to ground them. [224] She sat cross-legged in a band of split starlight and slid the tapestry needle into the first knot. [242] The needle was blunt-tipped, made for passing between warp and weft without tearing, and that was the whole art: a knot of silence must be opened, not cut. [270] It would not move. [274] "Silence only loosens for silence spent," Anselm said. [282] "You must be telling something true the whole while, and it must cost you." [296] So she told her secret, and the telling and the unknotting kept exact time, as if the net were listening with all four hundred ears. [321] Nine years ago her brother Tems had brought her the sea-blessing mask, the old one with the cormorant beak, cracked clean through the left cheek. [346] She had glued it with hide glue and pride, and she had known, holding it up to the window, that the join was weak. [370] She said nothing, because she wanted her work worn at the prow on the feast day, wanted the whole harbour to see it. [393] The mask split in the squall; Tems reached for it; the boat went over. [407] The first knot opened as she finished, and something went out of the air like a held breath released. [426] One of the prisms rang faintly, a single clear note, and the grass under it lay down. [443] That was one man grounded, three hundred and ninety-nine to go. [454] She had only the one secret. [460] "Then tell it again," Anselm said, "and each time leave less of yourself out." [474] So she did. [477] Across the sharing of that one secret, told and retold, she learned what the village had been protecting: not the dead, but itself. [500] Each retelling gave up more: that she had heard the join crack in her hands the night before the feast, that she had varnished over the sound, that she had let the harbour call her a master for nine years. [540] Knot after knot gave way, and the hush thinned, and the crickets came back one field at a time. [559] By the hour before dawn her hands were bleeding and her mind was perfectly clear, and she could not have slept if the sea had asked her to. [587] This was the lucid unrest the old restorers wrote about: you do not finish such work, you only reach the last knot you can honestly open. [613] Three knots remained when the starlight went grey. [621] Mireia laid the needle down and took the cormorant mask from her satchel, still cracked, never mended, carried for nine years. [642] She set it in the empty pit and filled the pit in. [654] "And the three?" Anselm asked. [659] "Mine," she said. [662] "I am not drowned yet, and I can carry three." [672] She carried the net down to the harbour and hung it in the boat sheds where the living could see it, three knots and all. [697] Silence you can see is not the same as silence you have buried. [710] Behind her, on the headland, forty prisms stood empty of shadow, and the grass lay flat in three hundred and ninety-seven small circles, and not one of them was hers. [740]
