After the oasis dried, the postal service of Verdigris folded in a single afternoon, and Ondrea kept writing anyway. [19] She was a gilded-leaf calligrapher, the last one licensed under the old glass, and her medium was fussy. [37] Gilded leaves take ink beautifully, hold it for a century, and wilt near dishonesty. [51] Merchants used to hire her for contracts, standing sweating in her stall while the leaf under their statement curled brown at the edges. [74] Now nobody paid for honesty; the greenhouse city had bigger problems, chiefly the sky. [88] Above the panes hung a yellow lid of poison, and beneath it Verdigris sprawled for forty miles of ferns, condensation ladders, and misting pipes that sang at dawn. [116] Ondrea had come to the city as a girl with her sister Mieke, back when the oasis still fed the outer cisterns and letters still traveled. [142] Mieke had gone out with the last water survey and never come back, and every week for nine years Ondrea had written to her. [166] The letters left her hands and dissolved into the system, and what she wanted, more than reunion, more than proof of a grave, was to find where lost conversations go. [196]

The floorboards of her stall gave her the answer, badly. [206] Prying up a warped plank to chase a leak, she found a nest of paper shreds, hand-torn, packed tight against the damp. [228] Under them lay a fragment of zinc-etched postal plate, the kind the old routing house used to print delivery maps. [248] The stall had belonged to a postmaster before her, a man named Auben who had died apologizing to strangers. [267]

For eleven nights she worked by piecing together the shredded treasure map, laying shreds on wet glass under a lamp and matching fern-ink strokes edge to edge. [294] Whoever drew it had called it a treasure map in earnest, with a compass rose and a crooked X, though the treasure was words. [318] The zinc fragment fit the map's blank corner exactly, its etched grid supplying the coordinates the drawing had left off: Cistern Gallery Twelve, beneath the dry oasis basin. [346]

She walked out through the western locks at first light, mask sealed, sixteen miles across cracked salt where a lake had been. [368] The gallery door was unlocked; nobody steals from the dead. [378] Inside, in dim green light, stood the treasure: forty thousand undelivered letters, shelved and labeled, breathing faintly with mildew. [397] This was where lost conversations went. [402] Auben had carried them out here when the routing house burned, sorted them alone for a decade, and shredded the map afterward, because he could not bear to be asked why he had never delivered them. [438]

She found her own bundle in the M drawer, nine years of Thursdays, unopened because the recipient had never been found. [459] And then a thinner bundle addressed to her, in Mieke's slanted hand, postmarked from a relay station eight years ago. [479] She sat on the floor of the cistern and read, and then made herself reread, slowly, because the first pass had been all noise in her ears. [506] Mieke was alive at the writing, working a windmill station in the northern flats, and she was not coming back. [526] She wrote it plainly: I am not brave enough for the domes, I hated the glass, I love you, do not wait. [548] Four small honest lines, worth more than nine years of Ondrea's careful, beautiful, evasive Thursdays. [563] She understood, sitting there, why her own letters had never wilted a single leaf: she had never written anything true enough to test. [586] She had written weather and prices and I am fine. [596]

Back in Verdigris she did not petition the council or publish Auben's crime. [609] She hired three couriers with her gold-leaf savings and began delivering, forty thousand letters, one district at a time. [628] At every door she said the plain thing first: this is late by years, and nobody here will pretend otherwise. [648] People wept, or swore, or shut the door, and the gilded leaf she carried in her breast pocket stayed green through all of it. [672] That was the whole of her trade now, and it turned out to be enough: candor handed over in small moments, at thresholds, under a poisoned sky. [699] She kept the zinc fragment on her desk as a paperweight. [710] And once a year, on the Thursday the oasis had finally gone dry, she wrote north to a windmill station, four lines only, all of them true. [737]
