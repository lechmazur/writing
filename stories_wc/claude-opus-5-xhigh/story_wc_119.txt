The pub emptied at eleven, and after last orders Nell walked down to the jetty where the tide was already turning. [21] She had written four hundred fortunes that week, and one of them had told her to come. [38] Nell wrote for the Ho Family Cookie Company, sixty slips a day, paid by the hundred. [54] Her lines were praised in newspapers as accidentally prophetic: a woman in Leeds read "the yellow door is not locked" an hour before her house burned, and lived. [82] Nell had never believed in it, because she knew exactly how little she meant by any of it. [100] She had wanted to be a composer once, and she still described the job to strangers as a way to transpose her life into a new key while she waited for the real music to start. [136] She was thirty-eight; the waiting had become the life. [145] The slip in her own cookie that night, printed in the company's typeface, was a line she had never written: THE KEEPER IS TIRED. COME BEFORE THE LAMP TURNS TWICE. [175] Everyone in the village knew about the lighthouse on the rocky island, which was the definition of an open secret: the light had been automated in 1988, and it still went dark and bright in patterns no chart explained. [214] Fishermen called him the Keeper and left him tobacco, and nobody asked, because asking would have made it a real secret and then someone would have had to do something about it. [246] She rowed across on the slack water and climbed the iron stair. [258] The lamp room smelled of paraffin and old paper, and the man in the chair had her mother's nose. [277] "Uncle Teo," she said, and it came out flat, the way you greet a familiar enigma you have been circling your whole life. [300] He showed her the wall first, as if that were the polite thing. [313] Pinned above the workbench were spycraft microdot film specs, yellowed and typed: reduction ratio 200:1, emulsion grain, focal length, developing times in seconds, all of it dated 1961 and stamped in a language she did not read. [350] Beside them sat a camera the size of a thumb and a hundred of her own fortune slips. [368] "The period at the end of your sentences," Teo said, "is not always a period." [383] For twenty-two years he had bought her slips from the printer, photographed documents down to specks of film, and glued them into her punctuation. [407] He did not need couriers, he explained; he only needed to orchestrate the ordinary traffic of cookies, choosing which crate went to which restaurant, which table, which city. [435] He worked by directing flow: the lamp told the boats when to sail, the boats told the wholesaler what to load, and nothing ever had to be hidden, only moved. [465] "And the prophecies?" she asked. [470] He shrugged, and she understood, and the understanding took the floor out from under her. [485] A recipient had to know a slip was live, so he made her sentences come true: a yellow door unlocked, a debt paid, a ship rerouted. [511] Her accidents had been arranged, and her whole small fame was a signature he had forged in the world itself. [531] "I am tired," he said, "and the people I served are dead, and the ones who took over do not deserve you." [553] He wanted her to keep the light, or end it; either way, he said, she should choose while the lamp still turned. [575] Nell sat on the cold step until the beam came round twice, and thought about all the years she had called herself a composer in waiting. [601] She had been writing for an orchestra of ten thousand strangers all along, and she had never once heard the piece. [622] In the morning she rowed back with the microdot specs in her coat and the little camera in her fist. [642] She did not burn them, and she did not use them for anyone else's purposes either. [658] That week the Ho Family Cookie Company shipped four hundred of her slips, and not one of them carried a microdot; they carried plain sentences, names and dates and account numbers, a bar apiece. [692] She had learned the trick from him: nothing needs hiding if you control where it goes. [708] Ten thousand diners cracked open her sentences over their bills, laughed, pocketed them, read them aloud. [724] By Friday the newspapers were printing the whole tune, and calling it, again, uncanny. [738] The lamp on the island went dark that same night, in no pattern at all. [753] Nell wrote one last fortune before she quit, and it was the only one she ever meant: THE SECRET WAS ALWAYS OPEN. NOW PLAY IT LOUD. [779]
