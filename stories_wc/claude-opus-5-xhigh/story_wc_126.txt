After the rainforest burned, the world came down with a chronic hush. [12] Ash sifted into the air and thickened it, and sound began to die three steps from its own mouth. [31] Bells went dumb; arguments ended early; mothers learned to sing with their hands. [44] In Echo Harbor, where the cliffs had once thrown a fisherman's whistle back to him fourfold, Neve kept playing anyway. [64] She was acoustically perceptive in the way her family had always been, able to hear the grain of a room, the seam where a note stopped being welcome. [92] The hush, she said, was not empty; it was a held breath, and every held breath wants ending. [110] Her grandmother Ilse had believed the same, and had done something about it. [123]

On the last night before the air went gray, Ilse coaxed the harbor's final true echo into a mendicant's bowl, a shallow beggar's dish of hammered brass that had passed down the family for six generations, and bound it there with a knot of bow-hair. [168] Held to the ear, the bowl still murmured: gulls, rigging, a girl laughing at something nobody wrote down. [186] An echo cannot live in a bowl forever, Ilse warned her. [197] It must be unbound somewhere with ribs enough to keep it. [208]

There was only one such place left, three miles down the coast where the land had slumped into the sea. [228] The orchard greenhouse had gone under with its pear trees still espaliered against the glass, and the shallows around it were ringed by shipwrecks, six hulls that had mistaken its dome for open water. [262] Glass, water, and the curved bones of ships: a room built by accident for listening. [277] At the lowest tide of autumn, the dome surfaced like a great cracked lantern, and Neve went out to it with the bowl and her violin. [303]

Her family came with her, because there was a tradition to keep. [315] Every year they built a bonfire of wreck timber and told one story around it, and the rule, older than anyone could explain, was that no one finished it. [344] Her uncle would carry the tale to the edge of a cliff and hand it off; her cousin would take it three sentences further and stop mid-breath; the last speaker always left a door standing open. [380] As a child Neve had found this maddening. [388] As a woman who had spent nine years playing into deadening air, she understood it as the family's whole theology: you learn to love a thing before it is done, or you never love anything at all. [425]

That night the fire went up on the sandbar between two wrecks, and the smoke smelled faintly of the rainforest, as everything did now. [449] They told the story of a beggar who traded his bowl for a single note. [464] They got as far as the beggar standing at the water's edge, deciding, and then, one by one, they stopped. [484]

Neve climbed down through the broken pane into the greenhouse, waist-deep, ash-water cold around her ribs. [500] Pear leaves, pickled and silver, hung in the water like sheet music left out in rain. [516] She listened the way her family listened, and found the room's grain: glass overhead, oak hulls beyond, a floor of silt that would swallow anything careless. [542] Then she set the mendicant's bowl on a shelf of fallen brickwork and pulled the knot of bow-hair loose. [561]

The echo came out small, no louder than a moth. [571] It touched the glass and came back doubled. [579] It touched the hulls and came back sixfold, and the greenhouse remembered what a room is for. [596]

Neve raised the violin and played into it. [604] She did not play a finished thing. [611] She played eleven bars of a melody her grandmother had hummed and never named, and when the eleventh bar ended she lifted the bow and let the wrecks and the glass carry it on without her. [647] Above, on the sandbar, her family heard it come up through the water and the fire's crackle: a sound that arrived, and stayed, and kept arriving. [673]

The hush did not lift from the world. [681] It never did; the forest was ash and would be ash for a hundred years. [696] But there was one room on that coast where sound still had somewhere to go, and word travelled, and people came down the cliff path with instruments and grievances and dead relatives' names to say aloud. [732] Neve left the bowl on its brick shelf, empty, right side up, the way a beggar's bowl waits. [750] It was, she thought, a magnificently simple arrangement: give the world a place to be heard, and it will fill the bowl. [772] Someone else, some year, would finish the beggar's story. [781] She hoped they wouldn't. [785]
