Mira wrote fortunes for a cookie factory, and her supervisor complained that nobody understood them. [15] "Your childhood is still transmitting; adjust the antenna." [23] That one had been recalled from four thousand cookies in a single afternoon. [36] She was not being cryptic for sport. [43] She was chronologically homesick, which is a condition without a bus route: she missed 1994, not Ohio. [60] She missed a certain hour of a certain August when the world had held its breath and she had heard, underneath the cicadas, an orchestra that did not exist. [89] Every fortune she typed was a message to herself, breadcrumbs pointing back toward that sound. [104]

The glass spire stood at the north edge of the frozen lake, a decommissioned observation tower nobody bothered to lock. [124] Mira climbed it at three in the morning with a thermos, a notebook, and a segment of nautilus shell in her coat pocket. [147] She had chosen the spire because glass does not argue with sound, and because from its top the lake's mirror was flawless. [169] Her father had sawed the shell in half when she was six, and she had kept the half with the chambers, thirty-four rooms spiraling smaller and smaller toward the beginning. [199]

The method had come to her slowly, the way a tune arrives before its words. [214] She would map her childhood memories onto the chambers, one per room, largest to smallest, and read the spiral as a score. [236] Chamber one: the sprinkler ticking across the driveway. [244] Chamber nine: her mother's cough, three floors down, at two in the morning. [257] Chamber twenty-two: the freight train that made the windows hum a minor third. [270] Each memory had a pitch, and the spiral gave her their intervals, and intervals were all a conductor really needed. [290]

She did not want to compose. [296] She wanted to conduct the symphonies nobody had ever heard, the ones that lived in the gaps between remembered sounds, and she wanted one of them to answer her. [325]

Working had to happen before exhaustion hit; after that the mapping went sentimental, and sentiment was noise. [342] So she wrote fast, hunched in the spire's cold throat of glass, the lake below her a black plate polished by wind. [364] By chamber thirty she was shaking. [370] By thirty-three she had recovered the August hour itself: the porch, the held breath, the cicadas stopping all at once. [390] That was the sound she had been chasing for twenty years: not music, but an upturned hush, a silence tilted like a listening face. [414] Chamber thirty-four was too small for a memory. [422] It was the size of a rest. [429]

Then the light began to dawn. [435] It came grey, then rose, then gold along the ridge, and the ice took the whole sky and gave it back. [456] For four or five minutes there was no shoreline anywhere, no up and no down: a mirrored horizon, cloud beneath cloud, sun beneath sun, and Mira suspended in her glass needle at the seam. [490]

Warming, the lake began to speak. [495] Ice sings when the sun leans on it: long groans, whip-cracks, a rising whine like a bow drawn across a wire. [516] It arrived in the intervals her spiral had predicted. [525] Minor third, then the leap, then the long-held rest she had found in the smallest chamber. [541]

Mira stood, put down her notebook, and lifted both hands. [551] She could not make the lake sing. [558] But she could shape it, hold a fermata in her palm, cut off a phrase with a closed fist, and the ice obeyed, or seemed to, which for a conductor is the same discipline. [592] She conducted until her arms burned and the mirrored horizon broke into ordinary morning, shoreline resuming its old argument with the sky. [614]

Then exhaustion hit, all at once, the way it always does, and she sat down laughing on the cold steel floor. [635] She was not homesick. [639] For the first time since 1994, she was exactly on time. [650]

That Monday she turned in eighty new fortunes, and her supervisor read the first one twice. [666] "The horizon is a hinge; go stand on it at dawn." [677] Nobody will understand it, he said. [683] Somebody will, she said, and slipped the nautilus segment into her pocket, where thirty-four small rooms went on holding their breath. [704]
