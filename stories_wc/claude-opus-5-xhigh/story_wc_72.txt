The garden at the heart of the drowned city had been forgotten for two hundred years, and it moved anyway. [20] Anemone-choked hedges swayed; the coral roses opened and shut like sleeping mouths; a stone fountain still pushed silt in a slow, patient plume. [43] Ilse swam through with her lamp low, distrusting how eagerly the dead place mimicked breathing. [58] The statue stood at the garden's center where the guidebooks had promised, a woman in physician's robes, algae in the folds, one hand out as if offering. [85] You are late, said the statue, inside Ilse's skull. [94] Three months since Doctor Vaunt had broken two centuries of silence into the dreams of a sonar technician, and the tribunal upstairs still wanted proof it could hold in its hands. [125] That was why Ilse had been sent: she was the apothecary's great-great-granddaughter, and she was certain. [141] Certain that her ancestor had poisoned the Sovereign, certain that the flood gates had opened in reprisal, certain that guilt ran in her like a color in glass. [169] Certainty is a wonderful lamp and a terrible window, Vaunt said. [180] Her voice arrived incisively kind, the way a good surgeon's hands are kind, cutting exactly where it would heal. [199] The reliquary sat behind the statue, a coral-crusted box whose lock had been keyed to a pulse that stopped two centuries ago. [221] It could not be opened; it could only be gotten into through structural weakness, and Vaunt showed her where. [240] A seam in the mortar, eaten thin by boring worms, wide enough for a wire. [255] Ilse began to thread her monofilament through it, hook first, feeling for the shape she had been taught to hate. [275] The revelations came as she worked, one unlocking the next, the way silt slides when you disturb its lowest layer. [295] First: the Sovereign's heart had been failing for a year, and your ancestor's foxglove was the only thing that steadied it. [316] The wire snagged, and Ilse's hand shook. [323] Second: I was her physician, and I raised the dose myself, past what the apothecary wrote, because the Sovereign asked me to. [345] Third: she asked because the seawall was failing, and she had spent thirty years insisting it could not. [363] A Sovereign cannot recant without unmaking every law she signed; but a Sovereign's death empties a city into the inland shrines for forty days of mourning. [389] Eleven thousand people walked out through the hill gate behind her coffin. [401] The water came on the ninth day. [407] Ilse's hook found glass. [411] She drew it out through the seam with the slowness of someone defusing her own life, and the lamp lit a small ampoule stained violet-brown where foxglove had steeped too long against the walls. [445] It was half full. [449] Fourth, said Vaunt, and for the first time the voice in Ilse's head hesitated. [463] Half was for her. [467] Half was for me. [471] The council reached me before I could drink, and they did not want a martyr, so they gave me stone and left the apothecary to the rope. [498] They set my mind in my own memorial with my hand still out, still offering, and told me to keep the secret. [520] Two hundred years is long enough to learn that a secret keeps you, not the reverse. [536] Ilse hung in the cold with the ampoule in her fist, waiting for absolution and not getting it. [554] Your ancestor mixed a medicine, Vaunt said, and I made it a death, and a dying woman made it a rescue, and the council made it a crime. [582] The same violet glass, four true things. [589] I did not speak to unburden myself; I spoke to let a little nuance into your certainty, because certainty is what drowned this city, and it will drown yours. [618] Above, the tribunal wanted a verdict; Ilse would bring them a dose instead, and the arithmetic of what a dose means. [639] She tied her line to the statue's offered wrist before she rose, so the divers who came after would find the way. [661] Behind her the garden went on with its borrowed animation, coral mouths opening, dead hedges bowing in the current, the fountain lifting its slow gray flower. [687] And the statue, who had been kind in the only way stone can be kind, thought after her, one last cascade: Ask them what they are certain of. [715]
