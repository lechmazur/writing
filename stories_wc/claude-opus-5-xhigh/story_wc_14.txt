After the town below went silent, Miyo still opened her shutters an hour before sunrise. [15] She was the last gilded-leaf stationer on the mountaintop, and the mountaintop was a blooming labyrinth of bonsai forests, hedged and pruned by three centuries of patient hands. [43] Plum trees no taller than her forearm flowered in gravel trays; maples the size of teacups held autumn in April. [63] The paths between them wound so intricately that visitors used to arrive at her door by accident, which had always been good for business. [87] Now no one came. [91]

Her trade was invented histories. [96] When the archives in the valley burned, families climbed to her with nothing and left with everything: deeds, birth records, letters from grandfathers who had never learned to write, all of it laid on rag paper and finished at the corners with hammered gold. [140] She was not a liar; she was earnestly studious, and she believed that a forged page could only hold if its era was correct down to the grain. [168] So she had spent forty years learning to collect the textures of different times. [182] Iron gall ink that bit like winter; the soft nap of mulberry sheets pressed in a wet decade; the particular way old gold refracts a candle into two dull coppers instead of one white star. [217]

And time itself, which she measured with the only instrument she trusted: the fluorescent lamp above her bench. [235] Clocks lied on the mountain, sun and shadow were ruled by fog, but the lamp hummed, and its hum answered the valley's dam. [258] Every dawn for four decades she had charted that faint hum in a battered notepad, a pitch and a wobble and a date, and the notepad had become the truest history on the mountain. [292] When the hum sagged flat, the looms had stopped early; when it climbed, a wedding somewhere had every kitchen burning at once. [314] By listening, she could tell you what year a paper had been made in, and in what mood. [332]

For eleven mornings now, the lamp had hummed one clean, dead note. [344] Nobody in the valley was drawing power. [351] She wrote the note down anyway, eleven times, in the same small hand. [364]

On the twelfth morning a girl came out of the bonsai maze with wet shoes and a rice sack. [383] She was perhaps nine, and she had walked up the switchbacks alone. [395] "They said you make papers," the girl said. [403] "I need one that says who I am." [411]

Miyo set the kettle on and did not ask about the valley, because the lamp had already told her. [430] The girl's name was Sora; her family's records had gone with the rest, and she had no one left to swear she had a grandmother. [455] "Did you?" Miyo asked. [459] "I don't remember her," Sora said. [465] "I want her anyway." [469]

That was the whole of it, and Miyo felt the old heat rise behind her ribs and let none of it reach her face. [493] She chose a sheet from 1934, a dry, thin year that took ink cleanly. [507] She ground her own ink. [512] Then she wrote a grandmother into being: Sora's grandmother had kept the tea stall at the third switchback, had a laugh like a gate hinge, and could name every tree in the labyrinth by its trunk. [548] None of it was true, and all of it was possible, which in Miyo's trade were nearly the same country. [568] She gilded the corners last, breathing shallowly so the leaf would not lift, and the lamp's dead note held steady above her like a held breath. [594]

When she carried the page to the window, the sun came through the bonsai and the gold refracted it into a hundred small fires across the girl's hands. [622] Sora did not cry, and neither did Miyo. [630] "Learn it," Miyo said. [634] "A history only stays real if somebody keeps saying it out loud." [646]

The girl stayed. [649] She turned out to be a careful student, in the way of children who have decided to survive. [667] Before dawn she sat with the battered notepad on her knees, charting the hum while Miyo slept, and she learned to hear the difference between silence and absence. [695]

In March the note wavered. [700] It bent upward, a hair sharp, and then wobbled the way it used to when the valley's kitchens woke. [719] Sora wrote it down, pitch and wobble and date, and ran to the door. [733] Someone was coming up through the flowering maze, lost, as visitors always were. [746] Miyo laid out a sheet, and said, quietly, that they had better begin. [759]
