The year concepts became physical, Nell Arreola was three hundred feet underground, tending mushrooms that glowed like spilled moonlight. [19] The farm filled an abandoned submarine pen, and bioluminescent light came up blue-green through the shadowseams of the rock, so that walking the rows felt like walking inside a held breath. [50] She had come here after the collapse, when the company that had promised her a downline and a lake house dissolved into subpoenas. [73] Nell survived the pyramid scheme the way you survive weather, by losing the roof. [87] Her sister Adaeze, whom she had recruited with the words I promise this will make us rich, lost more. [106] When concepts began taking bodies, that promise arrived at Nell's door as a lump of graphite the size of a fist, warm and heavy and unkeepable. [132] It pressed on whatever she set it on, including her chest at night. [145] The texts started in the second week, arriving at three in the morning from numbers with too many digits. [164] NELL ths is NELL, one said, letters dropping out like teeth: y0ur promis is qu4ntum, it exists in all of us at once. [187] Other Nells wrote from dimensions where she had never signed the contract, where Adaeze had signed instead, where the lake house was real and empty. [212] The messages glitched badly, dropping vowels and whole clauses, but the shape of the news came through: a quantum promise cannot be broken and cannot be kept, only collapsed, and it collapses when one version of the promiser volunteers to carry the pressure of all the rest. [259] None of them volunteered. [263] Nell understood, because she had been the kind of person who once sold hope in tiers, and she knew what it cost to stand at the bottom. [290] One correspondent, though, signed herself N3, and N3 was virtuously mischievous in a way Nell recognized from her own better days. [311] dnt argu w/ th physics, N3 wrote, ch3at it. [320] Graphite is carbon, N3 said, and carbon under enough pressure becomes diamond, so dont carry the pressure, translate it. [339] The submarine pen still had its dry dock, and in the dock's scrap heap lay the pressure hull of a decommissioned research sub, portholes and all. [365] Nell had scavenged from it for years -- shelving, pipe, lamp housings for the mushroom beds -- and she had always left the portholes alone, because they were the only windows she had. [396] Now she wanted one. [400] She worked three nights by mushroom light, unseating the glass ring, and the last fastener fought her the longest: a single submarine porthole bolt, thumb-thick, salt-welded, older than her debts. [430] When it finally turned, she laughed out loud in the glowing dark, and the mushrooms seemed to lean toward the sound. [451] The plan was mischief of the honest kind. [459] She would seal the graphite promise inside the porthole assembly, sink it in the flooded pen where the water went down two hundred meters into the hill, and let the sea do the translating. [493] But the promise still wanted a volunteer, and N3 admitted it plainly: sm1 has to say YES out loud. [512] So Nell stood on the dock at the seam of light and shadow, holding a fist of her own worst sentence, and volunteered. [535] She said it the way she wished she had said the original promise -- slowly, with the fine print read aloud -- I will carry this, all of it, from every one of me. [567] The pressure came in all at once, from a thousand dimensions of unkept intention, and it was exactly as bad as she had feared. [591] It also had nowhere to go but into the porthole, into the graphite, into the bolt she tightened with both hands while her ribs sang. [616] She dropped it into the black water and sat down among the mushrooms, breathing. [630] Eleven days later she winched up a porthole full of water and one small hard star, and her phone buzzed with a message that arrived clean, no glitches, from a number with the right number of digits. [667] It was Adaeze, asking whether the farm needed help. [676] Nell wrote back that it did, that the pay was terrible, that there was no downline and never would be, only rows of light and a diamond in a coffee can to sell when the roof needed fixing. [714] Then she went back to her mushrooms, glowing quietly in the shadowseams, and kept the bolt in her pocket, where it pressed just enough to remind her. [741]
