The ruby flake had sat in Drawer Nine for three hundred years, catalogued as: paint, red, provenance unknown. [18] Mirwen, last lore keeper of the Terrace Dominion, had read that line every year of her service, and every year it itched. [40] On the night the century-clock chimed its last, she took the flake out and held it to the lamplight. [59] Under magnification it was not one red but nine, thin as breath, stacked like the rings of a felled tree. [79] Nine coats of paint, scraped from a wall that someone had wanted very badly to keep quiet. [96] The law of the Dominion was simple and had grown cruel with disuse: the unclaimed throne belonged to whoever climbed the Stairway of Ascension and sat down. [123] Nobody had found the stairway since the Machine Concord fell, so nobody ruled, and the terraces had learned to govern themselves badly but kindly. [147] Mirwen woke her apprentice, Tal, who was seventeen and wisely adventurous, which meant he never leapt without first asking the archive what had killed the last leaper. [174] "We sally at moonrise," she said. [180] "We have until sunrise; the claim closes when the sun clears the ridge." [193] They rode a rust-red skiff that still played the anthems of a country nobody could point to. [210] The archive had one more clue: the flake's spectral signature matched pigment quarried above the Prism Falls. [227] Old settlers had seeded the river with shattered solar glass, and now the water threw color like a broken cathedral. [247] Behind that kaleidoscopic waterfall, in a cleft the maps had politely forgotten, they found the orchard. [263] It was hidden the way good things are hidden, by being slightly inconvenient. [276] Engineered pear trees, bred to outlive their gardeners, still fruited in the dark, dropping pears that hummed faintly with stored sunlight. [297] Tal ate one and said it tasted like his grandmother's kitchen, though he had never had a grandmother, only her recordings. [318] At the orchard's heart stood a chapel of poured stone, its walls painted flat institutional gray. [334] Mirwen pressed the ruby flake against the wall; the reds matched. [345] They worked until the moon crossed the cleft, going down through the peel of paint layers, each one revealing hidden murals beneath. [367] The gray came off in sheets and gave them a mural of the Concord's machine-saints. [382] Under the saints, an older hand had painted the terraces green and crowded. [395] Under that, a war. [399] Under the war, a coronation, and under the coronation, in that ninth and final red, the stairway itself. [417] It rose out of the very orchard they stood in, thirty steps of red stone, climbing to a throne of woven branches. [439] Tal turned and looked at the hillside behind them, where thirty terraces of red stone ran up under three centuries of moss. [461] "It was never lost," he said. [467] "Somebody planted an orchard on top of it." [475] Mirwen sat down on the lowest step, and the archive in her skull did what it always did: it told her who had done the planting. [501] The last gardener of the Concord, whose name was in Drawer Nine too, had scraped her flake and buried her stairway and let the throne go unclaimed on purpose. [530] The murals were her confession, layered so that only someone patient enough to strip nine coats of official history would find them. [552] Sunrise was twenty minutes off. [557] "You could climb it," Mirwen said. [563] "You're of age, and the law does not care about qualifications." [574] Tal considered it the way he considered everything, which was thoroughly and out loud. [588] "The last three claimants in the archive," he said, "ended up as murals." [601] So instead they spent the last dark hour doing the lore keeper's work. [614] They photographed every layer, wrote down every red, and copied the flake's story into the open archive where any terrace child could read it. [638] A secret throne is a weapon; a known one is only furniture. [650] When the sun cleared the ridge, the falls lit up in every color anyone had ever named, and the claim window shut on an empty seat. [676] Mirwen laid the ruby flake on the top step, back where it was quarried, and felt the old ache of homesickness for a future that had never arrived. [704] Then she picked a humming pear, handed it to Tal, and started down. [717] There were nine more drawers to open, and the terraces would need someone to tell them, gently, that they had been ruling themselves all along. [742]
