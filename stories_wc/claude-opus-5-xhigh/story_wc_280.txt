The Ambrosine Theatre closed for good on a Tuesday, and I stayed behind to count the last of the till. [20] Eleven dollars and a hand-painted tarot coin. [27] Somebody had paid for a balcony seat with the Wheel of Fortune, its rim gold-leafed, its spokes painted in a blue I could taste. [51] That is how I am; my grandmother called me synesthetically blessed, which sounded better than what my teachers called it. [71] Cellos come to me as wet slate. [78] Applause is cinnamon. [81] I had not tasted cinnamon in two years. [89] The coin was warm, and it kept turning itself over in my palm, which is why I went out the stage door instead of the front. [115] Behind the Ambrosine stands a hedge maze that the founders planted in 1898 to entertain patrons at intermission. [133] Nobody trims it now. [137] The coin pulled left, left, right, and the walls opened onto an orchard I had never seen in twenty-two years of sweeping that lot. [161] Apple trees stood in a ring, and they were doing all four seasons at once. [176] Blossom on one bough, frost on the next, windfall rotting sweet beneath a branch already budding. [192] A fractured timeline, the ghost told me later, though he said it the way other men say a cracked rib: an old injury he had learned to work around. [221] He was standing under the largest tree in evening dress forty years out of fashion, half-transparent, listening. [238] "Bellamy Vance," he said. "Tenor." [243] "I died in the wings on closing night, 1961, waiting for a curtain call that the fire took instead." [262] He had been waiting for his encore ever since, and the orchard was where he practiced. [278] The garden works by producing echoes, he explained. [286] Sing a phrase, and the hedge throws it back a season late; sing it twice, and 1898 answers you, and 1961, and next spring. [310] The echoes intertwine, and where they cross, a listener from one year can hear a singer from another. [328] "That is my audience," Bellamy said. "Scattered. I need someone alive to hold the middle note steady." [345] I told him I had stopped singing when my mother died, that I had run the box office instead, and that the box office was now a padlock. [373] He turned the tarot coin faceup on a root and said that this was precisely the point. [390] A ghost cannot make anything new; he can only lure possibility from the brink of despair, which is where possibility likes to stand. [413] So I sang. [416] The first phrase came out cracked, the color of dishwater. [426] The second came back to me from the hedge as my mother's alto, because she had sung in this orchard too, and had never told me. [452] That is the danger of the place: nostalgia rises in it like water in a lock, and you either float or drown. [474] I heard the 1898 patrons rustling their programs in the boughs. [485] I heard a girl practicing scales who was, unmistakably, me at nine. [497] Bellamy came in beneath all of it with the aria he had died holding. [511] I held the middle note. [516] The seasons in the orchard shivered and lined up, blossom to blossom, and for eight bars the timeline was whole. [536] Then it broke again, gently, the way a held breath goes. [547] The applause arrived from every direction and every decade, and it tasted like cinnamon, and I sat down in the wet grass and wept. [571] Bellamy bowed until he was only a smell of pomade and a warm patch of air. [587] "Again tomorrow?" I asked the orchard. [593] The hedge threw the question back at me in my own voice, three seasons late, which I chose to read as yes. [615] In the morning I put the tarot coin in the window of the padlocked box office, faceup, the Wheel turning. [635] Then I called the bank, and the fire marshal, and a soprano I had gone to school with. [653] We are selling tickets to the orchard now, twelve at a time, after the business closes. [669] The maze lets in whoever the coin likes. [677] People come out of the hedge holding their hats and asking who was singing, and I tell them the truth: a man still owed his encore, and everyone he ever lost, and the rest of us, all of it intertwined. [717] Nobody believes me until the second chorus. [724]
