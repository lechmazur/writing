The question hung in the lantern room, and Nen Ashgrove did not answer it yet. [15] Below the Marrow Light, the orchard clung to the cliff in rows of frost-apples, and below the orchard the ice floes shifted and ground against each other like slow teeth. [45] Harrow Sill had asked her, in front of both delegations: "Peacekeeper, do we cut the ice bridge and let the floe-riders starve out there, or do we open the orchard to them?" [77] A weighted breath sat between the question and the reply, and Nen, who was a shadow-laced peacekeeper, knew how to live inside it. [100] The Concord had sewn shadow into her veins so that she could step sideways out of a moment, into any reflecting surface within forty miles. [125] It had also, supposedly, cut the feeling out of her; she was the courageous anomaly, the one peacekeeper in whom empathy had survived the stitching. [150] Her mentors had used the word anomaly like a diagnosis. [160] She used it like a compass. [166] In her palm lay a broken crystal ball fragment, no larger than a plum pit, cold as the floes. [185] Whole, the orb had been the Concord's empathy engine: it let a peacekeeper stand inside another person's fear and come back knowing exactly what to do with it. [213] Shattered in the riots at Kelp Harbor, it was six shards scattered across three decades of junk. [230] She had spent years hunting them via glimpses in vintage store mirrors. [242] Old mirrors kept residue; a foxed silver glass in a secondhand shop would hold, faintly, every object that had ever passed before it. [265] Five shards she had recovered that way, one by one, across eleven years, and the sixth had stayed hidden. [284] So now, in the stretched second before her answer, she went looking. [296] The lens of the lighthouse was polished brass and glass; she leaned into its curve and came out in Pell's Second Chances on the harbor road, among tarnished wardrobe mirrors and hand glasses. [329] She did not search frantically; she had never learned to hurry. [340] Calmly intuitive, she let her attention drift the way water finds a crack, and the twelfth mirror showed her a glimpse: a boy's hand, a green scarf, a bright chip of crystal traded for two apples. [376] The scarf was floe-rider weave. [381] The apples were orchard stock from the Marrow Light itself. [391] She followed the glimpse through three more mirrors -- a barber's glass, a shop window, a black pane above the ice -- and found the boy in a tent lashed to a floe that was cracking under him. [427] He wore the sixth shard on a string at his throat, because it caught the light and his sister liked it. [448] Nen stepped out of the black pane into the tent's small warmth, and the boy did not scream, which told her everything about how tired his people were. [476] "I need to reconstitute something," she said. [483] "I will trade for it." [488] The boy asked what it did, and she told him the truth: it would let her feel what he felt, and then she would have to act on it. [517] He gave it over without haggling, which cost her more than any bargain would have. [532] In the tent, on a crate, she laid the six pieces together. [544] They did not fuse so much as remember each other, seams sealing like ice healing over a wound, and the orb woke. [566] It did not show her the future; the Concord had never built a thing that could. [582] It showed her the inside of people, all of them at once: the boy's hunger, his sister's cough, Harrow Sill's terror of a winter with three hundred extra mouths, the orchardmen's memory of the last raid. [618] Empathy alone was a paralysis; she had learned that at Kelp Harbor, standing in everyone's grief and doing nothing. [637] Her whole purpose, the thing the stitching had failed to burn out, was to map empathy onto action -- to turn feeling into a plan a frightened council could vote on. [667] The orb gave her the shape of one. [674] She stepped back through the black pane into the lantern room, and the weighted breath released. [690] "Neither," Nen said. [693] "Keep the bridge, and I will keep it -- I will stand on it myself, one peacekeeper wide, and pass them across in tens." [716] "They pick the orchard's lowest three rows, which you were going to lose to the frost anyway, and their sealers feed your children through the dark months." [743] Harrow Sill stared at the orb in her hand, whole and faintly warm. [756] "You were gone a second," he said. [763] "I was gone eleven years," said the anomaly, and she went down through the orchard to open the gate. [782]
