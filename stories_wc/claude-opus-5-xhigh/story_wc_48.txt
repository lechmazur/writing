Mirren wrote the last line of her ninth novel and did not let the page fall. [16] She had learned, years ago, that inside the whisper of closing pages there is a doorway, and it stays open only as long as the paper is still moving. [45] So she held the sheet half-turned, breathed the swirling hush that rose from the spine, and stepped through. [63] The feral nexus lay where it always lay, a crossroads of black water and bramble, drowned in mist, where every road she had ever abandoned came snarling back together. [92] Foxes with human patience watched from the reeds. [100] The river ran the wrong way here, uphill, hauling its cargo of unfinished sentences toward the place where stories are unmade. [121] Mirren had come to walk against the current, as she had walked against it for thirty years. [138] In her fist she carried the spiral seashell fragment her brother had pressed on her the summer before he drowned, a broken whorl no bigger than a thumbnail, its inner chambers still counting outward toward something unfinished. [175] It was the only object that traveled with her between the world and the pages, and it had a use. [195] Each hope she gathered went into the spiral like a coin into a slot, and the shell held more than a shell should. [218] That was the bargain she had made and the bargain she had come to break. [233] For thirty years she had entered her own books through ephemeral cameo appearances, the ferrywoman with two lines, the nurse in the corridor, the stranger who hands over a match and is never mentioned again, and in those seconds she had asked each of her characters the same question. [282] What do you hope for? [287] She had done it to collect the shapes of different hopes, because her own had one shape only, and it was the shape of a boy going under. [315] A soldier hoped for rain. [320] A cartographer hoped to find one blank space left on earth. [331] A dying gardener hoped only that the frost would hold off one more week. [345] Each hope went into the shell, and each time she felt less like a woman and more like a jar. [365] Tonight the nexus was crowded. [370] They stood in the mist at the water's edge, all of them, lucidly watchful, and they knew her face because she had lent it to a hundred bit parts. [399] The ferry-boy from her third book spoke first, since he had been waiting the longest. [414] "You take our hopes," he said, "and you never let us keep them." [427] It was true. [430] She ended every novel a half inch before the thing her people wanted, because she did not believe in arrivals, because a boy had gone under and no one had arrived. [461] The last page she had just written was the same, the woman on the pier, the ship not yet in. [481] Behind her the doorway thinned; the page was still turning, and pages do not wait. [496] Mirren waded into the black water, upstream, the mist parting around her shoulders in a hush that swirled and closed. [516] The current shoved at her knees with the whole weight of every ending she had refused. [532] She went to the middle of the ford and opened her hand. [544] "I came to collect," she said, "and I have collected enough to know the shape of my own." [562] Then she did the thing she had never done: she solemnized them. [574] One by one she spoke each hope aloud over the water like vows at an altar: rain, blank space, one week without frost, a ship; and as each was named the shell gave it back, a small bright chamber emptying, and the named hope went downstream to the book it belonged to. [626] The fragment cracked along its last curve and dissolved into grit. [637] Her brother's shell, spent at last, and she found she could bear it. [650] The foxes went back into the reeds; the crossroads loosened; the mist began to drift downhill, the right way. [669] Mirren climbed out on the far bank and stepped back through the narrowing whisper as the page came down. [688] At her desk, in the ordinary lamplight, she was soaked to the thigh and shivering, and the last line of her ninth novel had changed. [713] The woman on the pier was still watching, and now something low and slow was coming in out of the fog. [734] Mirren wrote three more words, and let the page fall. [744]
