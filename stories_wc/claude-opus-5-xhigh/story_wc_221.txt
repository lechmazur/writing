The drive died on a Tuesday, and after the shutdown the stars stopped smearing. [14] For sixty years the Ambit had run at nine-tenths light, and the time dilation observation deck had been the only place aboard where you could see what that cost us. [44] Now the deck held nothing but echoed silence, my footsteps returning to me a half-beat late, as if the room itself were still deciding whether I had walked. [72] That lateness was mine, not the room's. [78] When the field collapsed, I was the one inside it, and it let me out temporally stretched: my seconds run long, my minutes longer, so that a conversation with me feels to others like wading upstream. [114] The chaplains called me blessed. [119] Everyone else called me the reason the drive failed, and both names amounted to the same thing: doors that shut politely, meals left outside my hatch, a pariah with a halo. [150] I came to the deck anyway, on the anniversary, because that is what my family does. [166] Every year on the date my great-grandmother boarded, we sketch the night sky. [179] Not photographs, which the ship's instruments could make better; sketches, in pencil, by hand, because a hand admits what it does not understand. [202] I have ninety-one of them, four generations deep, bound in a case my great-grandmother lined with a quilt panel. [221] Stitched to the panel is a gilded compass rose applique, thread gone brittle, the north point longer than the rest. [241] She sewed it on Earth, where north meant something. [250] Aboard, it was a joke: we had no north, only forward. [261] After the shutdown, we did not even have forward. [270] Six hundred people drifting, the star charts useless because sixty years of relativistic distortion had been baked into every reference the navigation core owned, and the core had burned with the field. [302] Confusion had settled over the Ambit like dust, and dust, left alone, becomes despair. [316] I spread the sketches across the deck floor in order of year. [328] Ninety-one hand-drawn skies, each one wrong in the particular way its artist was wrong, and that was the point: the errors were human and random, while the drift underneath was not. [359] Generation by generation, the constellations my great-grandmother drew had crawled forward, squeezed toward the bow by our speed. [377] Ninety-one frames of a slow film nobody had thought to watch. [388] It took eleven days, and because my seconds run long I lived every one of them twice over. [406] But the stretch was also the gift the chaplains meant. [416] Where the others saw a shimmer at the edge of the viewport, I could hold a single instant open long enough to glimpse the true structure inside it: one point in that whole smeared sky that had not moved across four generations of pencil. [460] Not a star. [463] A radio-loud galactic core, ancient and so far off that our speed could not smear it, sitting exactly where my great-grandmother's brittle gold north point happened to lie when I laid the quilt panel over her first sketch. [501] Coincidence, probably. [503] I have decided to call it inheritance. [509] I brought it to the council in the echoing dark of the deck, where they had to come to me, because no one would sit in a room with me for an hour. [542] I told them we had a true north again -- not a direction home, but a fixed thing to measure ourselves against, which is all north has ever been. [571] From one fixed thing you can build a heading. [580] From a heading, a destination. [585] The old woman from Hydroponics asked what we should do with a heading now that home was sixty years and half a lifetime behind us. [610] I said we could stop being lost and start being explorers, which is the same set of facts held a different way. [632] They voted before the shift bell. [638] We are under sail again, at a tenth of our old speed, toward a yellow star that my long seconds may yet stretch far enough to reach. [665] They call me Navigator now, and still nobody eats with me; blessing and exile were always the same word aboard this ship, and I have stopped waiting for that to change. [696] The applique is mounted above the chart table, its long gold point aimed at nothing anyone can see without me. [716] Next anniversary I will come back to this deck, into its long patient silence, and sketch the sky as my family does. [738] The pencil will be honest about what I cannot understand. [748] Somebody's great-grandchild will need that, four generations on, when the ship is confused again and needs to be told which way is true. [771]
