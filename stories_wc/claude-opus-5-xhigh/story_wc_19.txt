My father built rooms that ended. [6] He measured air by the lungful, sealed doors with wax and iron, and taught me that survival is arithmetic: subtract the world, divide the fear, keep what fits. [34] I could not breathe in them. [40] A bunker builder's claustrophobic child is a joke the universe tells slowly, and I was its punchline for eleven years, panicking in the very shelters that were meant to save me. [71] So I learned to collect silences instead. [78] They have flavors, if you are patient. [85] The silence after an argument is metallic, like biting a spoon; the silence of a sleeping dog is warm bread; the silence inside a sealed bunker at three in the morning tastes of chalk and swallowed screaming. [122] I kept them in a tiny porcelain doll my mother left me, hollow as a whistle, with a chip in her painted mouth where the silences went in. [150] The trick, she had told me once, is preservation by limiting exposure: uncork a silence too often and it goes flat, ordinary, air. [173] So I rationed. [176]

Once a month, for one hour, gravity inverts. [184] The old people call it the Turning; children call it falling up. [196] My father spent every Turning belowground, strapped to a cot, counting his sealed rooms while the sky did its impossible thing without him. [219] I spent it climbing. [223]

Above the clouds there is a trellis. [230] Nobody knows who wove it, only that it is xenial: it exists to host. [244] Vines lace it into a lattice wide as a county, and every crossing holds a cup, a cushion, a bowl of something sweet for whoever arrives. [270] It has no walls, no ceiling, no end I ever found; my lungs opened there like hands. [287] At its highest crossing grows the gilded hush. [295] I had tasted every silence I could reach on the ground, and none of them were that. [312] It is a silence you can hold: intangibly tangible, like the weight of a name you have not said aloud yet. [333]

On my twelfth Turning I climbed for it. [341] The horizon tipped, my hair rose, and the ground let go of me the way a held breath lets go. [361] I fell upward through the cloud deck with the doll fisted against my chest, and the world stretched, contracted, stretched. [381] The trellis caught me gently, as hosts do. [389] There was tea steaming upside down in a cup that did not spill, and a cushion still warm from some earlier guest. [411] At the highest crossing, the gilded hush hung in a pod of light, and beside it was a small bare socket of soil, waiting. [435] Guest-gift for host-gift; the oldest arithmetic, and kinder than my father's. [446] I understood I had to plant my collection. [454] Every silence I had rationed and hoarded, every flavor kept fresh by limiting exposure, all of it poured out through the chip in the doll's mouth into the soil. [483] It hurt like emptying a lung. [489]

Then the soil began to sprout. [495] A green thread rose, thickened, unfurled leaves the color of old paper, and produced one seed-pod heavy and cool in my palm. [517] The gilded hush, grown from everything I had ever been quiet inside of. [530] I tasted it once, while the hour thinned. [538] It tasted like a room with no walls. [546]

Gravity righted itself and set me down in the yard, and I went straight down the ladder, into the chalk-and-screaming dark where my father lay strapped to his cot. [575] He was shaking; he always shakes, after. [582] I put the seed-pod in his hand and told him to hold still and breathe. [597] The hush opened in that sealed room like a held door, and I watched the walls stop mattering to him. [617] "What is it?" he asked. [622] "Somebody's hospitality," I said. [626]

We opened it one minute a day, no more, so its flavor would keep. [640] He has not sealed a door since. [647] He builds trellises now, small ones, in the yard: crossings and cups and bowls of something sweet, for whoever arrives. [667] The porcelain doll sits empty on the shelf, mouth chipped and patient. [679] I am learning the last flavor, the one my mother never named: the silence of a small space that no longer frightens me, stretching where it used to contract. [708]
