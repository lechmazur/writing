The breakers tripped at 03:14, exactly as Amira had planned, and the floating observatory went dark above the cloud deck. [20] She had eleven minutes while the circuit breakers cooled: eleven minutes of silence in which the great mirror hung idle and nothing on board could lie to her. [48]

Amira Halabi unclipped the geode from her collar and set it in the cradle where the calibration lamp should have been. [69] It was half a geode, actually, sawn open, purple teeth around a hollow. [82] Tariq had given her the other half at the henna table, an hour before the sky opened. [99] The drone had been hunting someone else; the official report used the word "proximate." [113] Forty-one guests, one groom, and a courtyard of unused potential. [123] That phrase was hers, not the report's, and it had taken her nine years to turn it into mathematics. [142]

The idea was simple enough to fit on a napkin and heretical enough to get her removed from three faculties. [162] Every system that is interrupted before it finishes carries a remainder. [173] Work through perturbation theory: treat the annihilated future as a small term added to the equation of the present, then solve order by order for what the term leaves behind. [203] First order gave her grief, which everyone already knew about. [213] Second order gave her a field: faint, curl-free, pooling in low places like meltwater. [227] Third order told her where to look, and that was why she had spent her severance on a berth aboard Kestrel Station, a balloon-slung observatory drifting the stratosphere at forty kilometers, hunting for the places where unused potential pools. [266]

Up here the air was too thin to smear the signal, and the station's own machinery was the only noise left. [287] Hence the breakers. [290] Hence the theft of eleven minutes, three nights a week, while the crew slept and the coils cooled. [308]

Tonight she went further. [312] She reached into the detector chain and pulled the bandpass filters, all six, the ones that scrubbed everything the instrument was not expecting to find. [337] Filters are humane. [340] They protect you from the raw sky. [347] She wanted the unfiltered version. [352]

The geode woke first. [356] Its hollow filled with a light she could not name -- not glow, not glare, more like the sensation of a name being spoken in a room you have just left. [386] Intangibly tangible: her hand passed through it and came back warm. [397]

The screens, running on battery, drew the field she had predicted nine years ago on the back of a hospital form. [418] It pooled, exactly as meltwater pools. [424] It pooled over Aleppo and over Dresden and over a beach in Sicily; it pooled over a hospital in Kunduz and a schoolyard she did not recognize; it lay in a thin bright sheet across half the Sahel. [462] Wherever a thing had been stopped mid-becoming, the remainder had gathered, and it was enormous, and no one had ever built an instrument stupid enough to look. [489]

Amira laughed, and the laugh came out wrong, too bright for the room. [502] This was the joy she had been warned about: the exhilaration of standing in a ruin and finding the ruin was load-bearing. [524] Nothing she was seeing would bring Tariq to the table with his sleeves rolled. [538] None of it was mercy. [543] It was only true, and true was the first thing anyone had offered her since the courtyard. [560]

At minute nine the geode's light steadied, and something in it took the shape of a question. [577] She understood it the way you understand weather. [585] The pooled potential wanted a channel. [591] It had been hunted by nothing, measured by nothing, spent on nothing, and now here was a woman with a mirror and eleven minutes. [615]

She thought about the drone operator, whoever he was, who had gone home that evening. [630] She thought about how easily a hunt reverses. [637] Then she turned the mirror away from the earth and pointed it out, into the cold, where there was nothing at all to interrupt. [661]

"Not a weapon," she told the geode. [668] "A drain." [670]

Somewhere below, a relay clicked; the breakers had cooled. [679] In ninety seconds the station would wake, the filters would be missed, and a very polite man from the consortium would ask her to explain herself. [705] Amira copied the night's data to three drives, hid one in the ballast hopper, and put the geode back against her collarbone, where it went on being warm and being nothing. [736] Then she sat in the dark and watched the sun come up over the cloud deck, grinning like a woman who has been handed the world's largest bill and, at last, an address. [769]
