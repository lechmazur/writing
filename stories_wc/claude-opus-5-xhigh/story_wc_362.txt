<story>The quartz arbor stood at the top of the hill, twelve pillars of milk-white stone, and in December the mist never fully left it. [24] Sabine Reyes met me there at seven, holding a thermos in both hands like a small animal. [41] I had come to expose her. [47] My sister Odile had died on the coast road in October, and within a week this woman had written to me, offering to commune with what remained. [74] Not with Odile, she was careful to say — with her belongings. [85] I am a materials engineer, firmly grounded, and I test claims by breaking them. [99] So I had brought a lie in my coat pocket: a glass prism, ordered from a lab catalog, identical to the one Odile hung from her rearview mirror. [127] The real one was in my other pocket, wrapped in a handkerchief. [139] "Objects absorb emotions," she said, when I asked what exactly she intended to prove. [153] "Grief soaks into things the way smoke soaks into wool," she said. [165] "I have filled nine notebooks, and I would like one of them to survive me as evidence." [182] I handed her the decoy. [187] She was an attentive mystic; that was the first thing I got wrong about her. [202] She did not close her eyes or hum. [210] She watched my hands, my jaw, the way my breath came shallow when she turned the prism toward the pale light between the pillars. [234] "This one is new," she said, "and nobody has loved it yet." [246] I told her that guessing was easy, and she agreed, which unsettled me more than argument would have. [264] "I invent perhaps half of what I say," she admitted. [274] "The trick is that the invented half must be true, or it does no work at all." [291] Truthful lies, she called them, and asked me to drive her down to the impound yard. [307] Odile's car sat in the third row, front end folded, glass in the seams of the seats. [324] The morning had frozen, and Sabine did not touch the doors. [335] She read the driver's side through the patterns in the frost on the car window, the way a doctor reads an X-ray. [357] "Feathers here, and clear channels there," she murmured. [365] "The heater ran hard for a long while before it stopped, so the cabin was warm." [381] "She was not cold, and she was not driving away from anyone." [393] That is when my assumptions shattered. [399] I had spent nine weeks certain that Odile left our last argument and drove north to be rid of me. [419] But the coast road curls; north becomes east becomes home. [429] She had turned around, and she had been coming back, and the frost only confirmed what the odometer and the warm heater had been saying all along to anyone attentive enough to look. [462] Sabine had gone to the yard the week before; she told me so without flinching. [477] She had read the police report, the mileage, the direction of the skid. [490] "Then why the frost, why the arbor, why any of it?" I asked. [503] "Because you would not have believed a spreadsheet from a stranger," she said. [516] "You needed the hill, and the mist, and a woman who seemed to know things she could not know." [535] We drove back up to the quartz arbor, and I took the real prism out of my handkerchief. [553] It was chipped at one corner, and it smelled faintly of the pine air freshener she never replaced. [571] Sabine held it, and this time she was quiet for a long minute, and something in the arbor changed. [590] It was not silence arriving; the silence had been there all morning. [602] It was a shifted hush, the way a room's quiet changes weight when someone finally stops pretending. [619] "I have nothing for you from this," she said, "because it is glass, and glass is only glass." [637] Nine notebooks, and that was her verdict: she had set out to prove that objects absorb emotions, and had proved only that people do, and that they will pay to hear otherwise. [669] She gave the prism back, and my hand was shaking, and it fell against the base of a pillar and broke into a fistful of bright pieces. [696] I knelt in the wet grass and picked them up, and the mist held the little rainbows a moment longer than it should have. [720] Sabine wrote something in her notebook and did not tell me what. [732] I did not ask, because I already knew the shape of it: that she was a liar, and that she had given me back my sister turning the car around, which was the truest thing anyone had told me since October. [773] I keep the pieces in a jar on the sill, where the sun finds them. [788]</story>
