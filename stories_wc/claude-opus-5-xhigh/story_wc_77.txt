They pulled me out of the flooded funhouse after eleven minutes, and the paramedics called it a resuscitation, though nobody agreed on what exactly had been resuscitated. [27] Since that night I have been photographically cursed. [35] Every picture taken of me develops into the same image: a corridor of tilted mirrors under green water, and my own face somewhere in it, smiling in a way I have never managed while awake. [70] The second curse was the whispering advisor. [77] It came back with me, the way a smell follows you out of a cellar, and it began at once to give me counsel. [101] Do not go upstairs tonight; do not trust the man with the blue van; do not sleep with the window unlatched. [122] Its advice was always correct, and following it made my world shrink, room by room, until I lived in a single chair. [144] The advisor left only one thing behind each night: an opera cloak clasp on my pillow, black enamel shaped into a moth, warm as a swallowed coin. [171] I understood then that fear had a body, or at least a wardrobe, and anything that wears a clasp can be undone. [193] So I set out to trick fear into revealing its weakness. [204] My method was a dream dictionary I bought for a dollar, its margins now crowded with paradoxical notes in my own hand. [226] Water: you are drowning, therefore you are safe. [234] Mirror: nobody watches you, therefore you are seen. [242] Moth: the light is a lie, therefore follow it. [251] The advisor could not resist annotating them; a counselor must counsel, that is its nature, and every night it whispered corrections into my sleep. [275] Each correction contradicted the last, and contradiction, I had guessed, is the one meal fear cannot digest. [292] It grew louder, sloppier, hungrier to be heard, and at last it told me to come back to the pier. [312] The abandoned carnival funhouse still smelled of burnt sugar and standing water, a sweetness so rotten it pulled me forward by the teeth. [335] Painted clowns peeled in long strips like sunburn. [343] The tilt-floor sagged, the mirror maze had gone furred with algae, and everything in that place was hideous and beautiful in the same breath, which is exactly how I knew fear had furnished it. [377] I had brought the dictionary, an instant camera, and the moth clasp pinned at my breast, holding shut a moth-eaten opera cloak I had found in the prop trailer. [406] In the mirror maze the whispering advisor finally showed me its outline, a tall smear of not-quite-dark, leaning close, mouth at my ear. [429] Read, it said. [432] I read the water entry aloud and it whispered that drowning was death; I read it again and it whispered that drowning was safety; I read it a third time and it said both at once, in two voices, and the mirrors began to sweat. [477] Then I did the thing it had never advised, because it could not: I unbosomed myself. [493] I told it about the eleven minutes. [500] I told it I had gone into the funhouse that night on purpose, that I had wanted the green water, that I had been tired in a way I had never confessed to my sister or my doctor or the dark. [541] As I spoke I unpinned the clasp, and the cloak fell open, and the whisper faltered because a secret spoken aloud is a room with the door taken off its hinges. [572] It had nothing left to threaten me with. [580] Fear's weakness, it turns out, is that it can only speak in a room where something is being kept. [599] I raised the camera and took its picture. [607] The flash went off in that drowned corridor and left its lingering luminance on every mirror, a slow white ghost that would not fade, and the advisor was caught inside the glow like a moth under a glass. [645] It whispered once more, asking me to look away. [654] I did not. [657] The photograph, when it developed, showed no green corridor at all. [668] It showed a woman in an open cloak, standing in a ruined funhouse, lit from inside, alone. [685] I keep it on my wall, and the curse has changed its terms: every picture of me now glows faintly, for hours, as if the eleven minutes are still leaking out. [716] The moth clasp sits in a dish by the door, unfastened, and no one whispers to me anymore. [734] I go upstairs whenever I like, and I sleep with the window open. [747] Some nights the light on my skin takes an hour to die, and I lie there letting it, the way you let a good thing be strange. [774]
