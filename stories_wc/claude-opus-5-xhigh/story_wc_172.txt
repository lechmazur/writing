The Meret River had been dead for nine years, and Corvin Ash had killed it with paperwork. [17] I know, because I drafted most of it. [25] Upstream permits, aquifer leases, a diversion canal financed by his own foundation and named, without irony, the Mercy Channel. [44] The same foundation trucked in water for the villages it had emptied, and the villages blessed him for it. [63] That is the arithmetic of an amoral philanthropist: take a hundred, give back ten, and be adored for the ten. [83] I only learned why on the last morning, out on the salt flat, with thunder walking the horizon like something patient. [104]

The flat went white to every edge of the world, cracked into plates the size of doors. [121] Corvin walked ahead of me with a surveyor's rod, reading the ground the way other men read scripture. [139] "The cracks are a map," he said. [146] Mud remembers current; when a riverbed dries, it splits along the old channels, and the fractures repeat the shape of what once flowed there. [170] Nine years of manufactured drought had drawn him a chart no satellite could: every vanished creek, every seep, every shallow lagoon the Meret used to braid across this basin. [199] He was following the cracks to a place the water had hidden for a century. [214]

We found it at noon: a limestone shelf, tide-marked, honeycombed with old nest scrapes. [228] The Meret stilt-crane had bred here, ten thousand pairs, until the last one was shot for a museum drawer in 1912. [249] Salt had sealed the shelf like wax over a letter. [259] Corvin knelt and worked one free with a dental pick and a camel-hair brush: an extinct bird's egg, whole, the color of weak tea, freckled at the blunt end. [288] I had priced his collection for insurance once; an intact one would fetch more than the Mercy Channel cost to dig. [309] "Hold it," he said, and I did, and it weighed almost nothing, and something inside it shifted with a dry whisper. [330]

Then he told me about his grandmother's wax cylinders. [339] She had recorded the stilt-cranes in 1909 with a horn and a hand-cranked machine, and the cylinders had rotted to grease in a cellar before he was born. [367] All that survived was a page of her notes describing the call: two rising notes and a long one, like a question answered slowly. [391] He had spent forty years and a river to preserve the echo of extinct birds, and he had never once said so out loud to anyone but me, on a salt flat, where there was nobody else to hear. [430] Secretly sentimental men are the most dangerous kind; they will drain a basin for a sound. [446]

I had already betrayed him. [450] Three days earlier I had sent the crack-map coordinates to a broker in Zurich, because I wanted the shelf catalogued by scientists before it went into a private vault, and because a broker pays for what a scientist only thanks you for. [492] By afternoon there were two aircraft on the western pan, and Corvin watched them settle through the heat shimmer without any expression I could name. [517]

The storm was still an hour out, the thunder folding over itself far away. [531] "The rains would have taken three weeks to fill the basin," he said, in the lull between one roll and the next. [553] "The gates will take forty minutes." [559] He made the call on the satellite phone in front of me, unhurried, the way he had signed away the Meret nine years before. [583] Release the reservoir, all of it, at once. [591]

The flood came across the flat in a brown sheet, no higher than a man's knee and faster than the buyers could run to their aircraft, and it dissolved the cracks as it went, the map unwriting itself, channel by channel, the way a sentence goes under an eraser. [640] The shelf went under with ten thousand eggs in it, and every one of them is still there, and no one alive but me knows where. [666]

We stood on the limestone rise while the water came, and he held the one egg against his coat like a man shielding a match. [691] Then he blew across the pinhole he had drilled in the blunt end, and the shell answered: one low note, held, faint as breath in a bottle. [718] It was not the call. [723] It was only the room the call had once been kept in, and he listened to it with his eyes shut, and the thunder came in over the top of it and took it away. [758]

He gave the egg to me before the boats came, and told me to sell it, and I have not. [778] Some nights I blow across it anyway, and the empty room answers. [790]
