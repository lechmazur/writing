After reality became negotiable, the librarians of the glass orchard voted for a perfect noon, and got it. [18] For ninety years the sun hung at one angle over the swirling sea of clouds, and the glass orchard library floated in it without aging, without fruiting, without falling. [47] I was hired to keep the Concordance, which is to say I was hired to make sure nothing happened twice, or once. [69] The trees are glass, root to crown, and their leaves are pages; when a book ripens it hangs heavy and clear, and you read it by holding it against the cloud-light. [100] Under the perfect noon, nothing ripened. [105]

The letterpress operator lived in the press house at the orchard's western edge, and nobody could tell me his name, only that he answered to knocking three times. [133] He spoke in set lines, as if his mouth were a composing stick: "Frost first. Then furniture. Then the truth, backwards." [154] Every dawn -- and dawn, under a fixed noon, is a thing you must go looking for -- he walked the rows reading patterns in morning frost. [179] Frost still came to the glass leaves; it was the one habit the vote had failed to negotiate away. [198] He copied the ferns and feathers of it into his notebook, then set them in metal type, letter by letter, and printed them on paper the color of weather. [227] "The frost is the orchard talking in its sleep," he said. [238] "I am learning the grammar so I can answer." [247]

I reported him, of course. [252] The Concordance is a perfect system, and a perfect system has no tolerance for a man who prints things nobody has agreed to. [275] The committee wrote back that he was a harmless hobbyist and that ink destabilizes nothing. [290] He heard about the report and did not mind. [298] "A crack is a small thing," he told me, inking the rollers. [310] "But a crack destabilizes perfect systems, and that is the entire purpose of cracks." [324]

In the press house he kept a green bottle stoppered with wax, and inside it, he said, was the whisper of the last woman who died here before the vote. [354] She had whispered one sentence into the glass, and he had corked it, and he had spent ninety years trying to set that sentence in type without breaking it. [383] The frost, he explained, was her handwriting. [390]

What he wanted was not revenge and not reversal. [399] He wanted to compress eternity into a moment -- to take the ninety flat years and press them, as a press presses, into one line of type dense enough to be felt. [430] "Eternity is only a moment that refuses to close," he said. [441] "Give it an ending and it becomes a life." [450]

On the day the frost came in a pattern he recognized -- a long spiral closing on itself, like a signature -- he asked me to hold the bottle. [477] I was the Keeper of the Concordance; I should have refused. [488] But I had been twenty-nine for ninety years, and I wanted, very badly, to be thirty. [504]

He set the line in the composing stick and would not let me read it. [519] Then he took the bottle, broke the wax with his thumbnail, and held the mouth of it over the inked type. [540] The whisper came out slowly, the way honey leaves a jar. [551] It settled into the letters, and the letters drank it, and the platen came down. [566]

The rupture began at the press and ran outward along the frost lines, as if the frost had been a diagram of where the world was thinnest. [593] Noon broke like a dropped pane. [599] And then the rustling began. [604] Ten thousand glass leaves turned on their stems at once -- the rustling awakenings, the orchard coming out of sleep -- and the sound went across the cloud sea like applause, like rain, like a held breath let go. [641] Books ripened and fell and smashed and were read from their pieces. [653] Afternoon arrived, then evening, then, God help us, the first winter. [664]

The Concordance is useless now, so I have taken up other work. [676] The operator died in the third autumn, which he had planned for and seemed to enjoy. [692] He left me the press, the notebook, and the empty green bottle. [704] I have the proof sheet too, the only one, and I read it every morning while I copy the frost. [724] The line says: Wake up, love, the light is moving again. [735] It took him ninety years and all of eternity to set eight words, and they lasted a second, and they were enough. [757]
