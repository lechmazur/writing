The eclipse had begun, and Mara Vance climbed the moonlit ridge path with a cracked porcelain box under her arm. [20] Behind her, Teo Ruiz labored, nineteen years old, boots too new, breath too fast. [34] "Slow your lungs," she said, "the mountain is not going anywhere." [45] Twenty years on the hotshot crews had taught her that panic burns oxygen faster than flame does. [62] Her scars caught the dimming light: the ropey seam down her jaw, the glove-shaped gloss on her right hand. [81] Teo had been staring at them all season, the way rookies do, wanting the story and afraid of it. [100] Tonight, during the slow dance of celestial bodies, she meant to give it to him. [115]

They stopped where the path opened onto the scar of Cedar Gap, forty thousand acres grown back thin and silver. [135] Mara set the box on a granite shelf and turned its cracked glaze toward the moon until it threw a pale coin of light onto Teo's boots. [162] "My mother kept thread in this," she said. [170] "Now I keep a matchbook in it." [177] She opened the lid with the gently skilled fingers that had splinted a dozen broken ankles on this same ridge. [197] Inside lay a paper matchbook, scorched at one corner, from a diner that had closed before Teo was born. [216]

"I signed a statement when I was seventeen saying I lit the Cedar Gap fire," Mara said. [233] "I hadn't." [235] "My brother Ansel did, burning trash behind our cabin in August with a west wind rising." [251] He was twenty-two and on parole; I was a minor with a clean record and a scholarship nobody expected me to use. [273] Our father did the arithmetic at the kitchen table and I agreed to it, and that is how a family bargains away a daughter's innocence for a son's freedom. [302]

Teo said nothing, which she respected. [308] The moon's edge went the color of old rust. [317] "A rancher named Delgado died in that fire," she said, "trying to move his horses." [332] "I carried his name the way you carry a hot ember in a glove: not burning, exactly, just always there." [352] "Ansel drank himself into the ground by thirty." [360] "The bargain bought him nine years he did not want." [370]

For a long time she had believed the debt could only be paid in labor: sixteen-hour shifts, cold camps, the ache of a pulaski in blistered hands. [397] Then a burnover on the Kettle Divide took the skin off her jaw and left her three weeks in a bed with nothing to do but think. [424] By reflecting deeply, hour after hour, on what she had agreed to at that kitchen table, she found the shape of the only useful thing left in it. [452] Guilt kept privately was only weather; taught, it became a map. [463] So she took the rookie classes nobody wanted, and every June she stood in front of nineteen-year-olds and tried to translate her pain into a universal language: wind, slope, fuel, consequence. [494]

"Why me?" Teo asked at last. [500] "Because on the Antelope burn you lied about your lookout position to keep Ramirez out of trouble," she said, "and I saw the arithmetic starting in your head." [528] "Small bargain, small innocence." [532] "They compound." [534]

The shadow closed over the moon and the ridge went the deep blue of a bruise, and Mara let the silence work on him. [558] She had learned that you cannot enlighten anyone by shouting; you set a small clear light where they can reach it and then step back. [583] "Tell the truth on your next report," she said. [592] "Even the small one." [596] "Especially the small one." [600]

Teo picked up the porcelain box, ran his thumb along the crack, and closed it carefully, the way you handle something that has already broken once and held anyway. [629] "Does it help," he asked, "saying it out loud?" [638] "No," Mara said, and then, because he deserved the whole hard softness of it, "and yes." [654]

They sat until the shadow slid off and the light came back over the regrown pines, thin, silver, insistent. [673] Somewhere below, the descendants of Delgado's horses were grazing on land that had burned and come back, which was not forgiveness but was something. [697] Mara stood, knees complaining, and started down the moonlit ridge path with the box under her arm and the boy behind her, walking slower now, learning the language. [725]
