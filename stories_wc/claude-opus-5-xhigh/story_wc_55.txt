Ansel Vire had kept the hours of the Sanctuary of Coldreach for sixty-one years, and in all that time he had written the word None more often than his own name. [31] The register had three columns: Hour, Sound, Method. [39] In a soundless sanctuary, the last two were a formality, but formality was the whole of his craft. [57] He was a clock scribe, which meant he lived alone in the gallery above the frozen nave, inking each hour as the water-clock let it go, and speaking to no one for weeks at a stretch without once feeling lonely. [97]

The glacier pressed against the north wall like a patient animal, and its cold made a hush so complete that novices wept in their first month, mistaking it for the absence of God. [130] Ansel had learned to call that glacial hush a kind of listening. [142] Still, he kept a wooden box beneath his cot, and in the box lay the clapper of the great bell. [162]

He had been eleven when the elders voted for perpetual silence, eleven and already promised the bell-rope, already dreaming of the morning he would bear the bronze tongue up the tower stair and give the valley its first true note in a century. [205] Instead they gave him a pen. [211] He had taken the clapper the night the tower was sealed, and had never decided whether that was theft or grief. [232]

Rain came to Coldreach perhaps twice a year, when the wind swung south and the ice sweated. [249] It came that Thirdmonth night, and Ansel lay awake smelling it through the shutters: wet stone, old moss, the mineral ghost of every rain he had ever failed to walk out into. [281] Petrichor, the old texts called it, and every time it arrived he was eleven again. [296] At the fourth hour he took the box and went down. [307]

The sanctuary's forecourt was paved in tablets of pale crystal quarried from under the glacier, and after rain it whispered. [327] Water slid into the hairline seams, air fled the stone, and the whole yard breathed a note too thin to name, the only sound the elders had never forbidden, because no hand made it. [361] In the register, the crystal whispering forecourt after rain had its own line, and beside it, in Ansel's excruciatingly gentle hand, sixty-one years of the same entry: Method: None. [390]

He stood at the edge of it with the box in his arms and waited for the hidden sunrise. [409] Three days each spring, the sun rose behind the glacier's shoulder and struck the courtyard through a crevasse, so that light arrived in the valley before it arrived anywhere else, secretly, seen by whoever happened to be awake. [447] In the hush of that hidden sunrise, with the stone still wet, Ansel knelt and opened the box. [465]

He had rehearsed the other plan for six decades: the sealed door, the crowbar, the stair, the rope, one stroke before they stopped him. [489] He was old enough now to know what that stroke would be: a boy's note, forced, and gone. [507] So he did the other thing instead. [514] He lifted the bronze clapper, heavier than memory, cold as the wall it had slept against, and laid it in the shallow drain channel where the rainwater ran between the crystal tablets. [546]

The water found it, folded around it, and the whisper changed. [557] It deepened, took a rim of bronze into itself, and became, for as long as the rain lasted, a bell. [577] Not loud. [579] Nothing a valley would wake to. [585] But the sound was there, and it was his, and no hand had made it. [600]

He sat back on his heels in the light of the hidden sunrise and let himself bear it: the dream he had abandoned, arriving anyway, sixty-one years late and in the wrong shape, which was the only shape it could ever have taken. [643] The hush closed over the courtyard again as the stone dried. [654]

Ansel climbed to the gallery, opened the register, and wrote the hour. [666] Under Sound he wrote, for the first time in his life, Bell. [678] Under Method he wrote None, and meant it as praise. [688] Then he left the clapper where it lay, for the next rain, and for the scribe after him. [706] The smell of wet stone stayed in his sleeves all day, and he did not wash it out. [724]
