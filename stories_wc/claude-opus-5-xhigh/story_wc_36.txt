The guardianship ended at dawn, and by noon Mira Kesh was walking back into the country that had taken it from her. [22] Nobody stopped her. [25] A stripped keeper is only a woman with a permit to grieve, and the Thunder-echo range does not check papers. [45] The range earned its name honestly: a hundred red canyons cut so deep and narrow that a single dropped stone came back at you nine times, each echo louder than the last. [77] But between echoes the silence was total, an avalanchingly quiet that pressed on the ears like held breath, like ten thousand tons of snow deciding. [102] Mira had loved that hush once. [108]

She carried three things: water, a coil of red cord, and a case of oiled elkhide the length of her forearm. [129] The council had voted to seal the flute in the vault at Sern, and she had walked out with it instead, which was theft, and which was also the only honest thing left to do. [164]

By afternoon she reached the dry bed of the Hallow, where floods left driftwood bleached to bone. [181] Here she knelt and began arranging driftwood as directional markers, forked branches laid in arrows, each pointing back toward the mouth of the canyon. [205] She placed them every forty paces, and she counted aloud, because on the other side of the weave the canyon runs mirrored, and a keeper who trusts her memory dies in it. [237]

Tomas had trusted his memory. [242] That was the failure, or part of it; the rest was hers. [254] Eleven months ago she had taken her apprentice through, on a survey, with no markers at all because she had crossed forty times and grown proud of the crossing. [283] She came back alone. [287] The inquiry had wanted contrition, and she had given it, and contrition had bought Tomas nothing. [303] What she wanted now was different and colder: to harvest insights from failure, to walk the exact route of her error and write down what it had cost, step by step, so the next keeper would inherit a map instead of a warning. [346] Grief was useless; data was not. [352]

At the ninth marker the canyon narrowed to a slot two shoulders wide, and the hush deepened until she could hear the blood in her own throat. [379] This was the place. [383] She sat, unwound the cord, and began to uncase the relic. [394] The gilded reed flute came out of the elkhide the way a wasp comes out of a fold of cloth: light, dry, faintly humming, older than the language of the people who had gilded it. [429] Gold leaf over marsh reed, six holes, and a mouthpiece worn concave by a hundred keepers' lips. [446] It did not open doors. [451] It wove: three notes into the stone's own echo, and the echo came back braided, and where the braid crossed itself a person could step sideways out of one world and into the loose weft of another. [488] Only the Thunder-echo range answered it; only these canyons echoed nine times, which was how many crossings the braid required. [508]

She played. [510] Three notes went out, and in the pause before the first return she felt the old pride rise, the same warm certainty that had killed Tomas, and she took the flute from her mouth. [544] She set one last driftwood arrow in the throat of the slot, pointing out, before she let the braid finish. [564] Then the ninth echo arrived, and the world went sideways. [574]

The far canyon was the same canyon, reversed, lit by a sun she could not find. [590] Forty paces on, she found the survey satchel, and inside it Tomas's notebook, swollen and legible. [606] He had lasted four days. [611] He had built markers out of his own splintered walking staff, seven of them, all pointing the wrong way, because he had not known the mirror reverses left and right but not up and down. [646] She had never taught him that. [652]

She copied every page, kneeling in the reversed hush, her hands steady, her face wet. [667] Grief and data, it turned out, were not enemies; they were the same animal wearing two coats. [684] She walked out along his arrows, reading each one backward, and at the slot her own driftwood was waiting. [703] She played the braid a second time and came home into the canyon hush of her own world at dusk. [723]

At Sern she surrendered the flute, the notebook, and forty pages titled Errors of the Forty-First Crossing, and she did not ask for the guardianship back. [749] The next keeper is a girl of nineteen who has never crossed. [761] Mira teaches her driftwood first, and pride last, and the silence in the classroom is the good kind. [779]
