The locksmith kept her shop where three roads folded into one, a shadowed nexus under starlight that mapmakers refused to name. [21] She was small, gray-aproned, and unassumingly bold, the sort of woman who would pick the lock on a king's grief and hand him the key without charging. [48] Nobody knew her name; the sign above her door simply read OPENINGS. [60]

Elin came at dusk in autumn, carrying an envelope she had carried for nine years. [75] Inside it lay a lock of hair tied with a ribbon, and a single folded page she had never let anyone read. [97] "I don't need a door opened," Elin said. "I need this opened." [109] The locksmith turned the envelope over, feeling the small weight of the hair shift like a sleeping animal. [127] "Envelopes are the hardest locks," she said. "Paper holds a secret better than iron, because paper asks nothing of you." [147]

Elin explained in pieces, the way people confess. [155] The page held a bridge: nine spans across the Vare gorge, a crossing everyone in the valley called impossible. [174] She had drawn it at twenty-two, for a man named Tomas who lived on the far rim and who died before the ink dried. [198] The ribbon had been his; the hair was her own, cut the night of the funeral and sealed with the drawing, as if grief were a kind of glue. [227] "If I show it and it stands, he still won't cross it," Elin said. "If I show it and it falls, I killed people for a man who is already dead." [258]

That, the locksmith thought, was the true lock: fear with an idea sealed behind it, humming like a wasp in a jar. [280] She led Elin outside, where the hush of cosmic possibility lies across the crossroads between sunset and full dark. [299] "My craft is simple," the locksmith said. "I wait for the first star, and I let it tell me which way the lock turns." [323] Elin almost laughed, and then the star arrived, low and west, over the black lip of the gorge road. [342] "West," the locksmith said. "The hint is always a direction, and the direction is always the one you have been refusing." [363]

Then she took Elin by the shoulders and turned her, three times, four, until the roads swapped places and the sky slid sideways. [386] It was a crude trick and she knew it: to disorient a person is to unhook fear from its landmarks, briefly, the way you jiggle a stuck bolt. [414] "Now," she said. "Point west." [419] Elin, dizzy, pointed at the star without thinking, and her arm went straight down the gorge road toward the valley council house. [441] "There," said the locksmith. "Your hand knew. It's your mouth that's bolted." [453]

She slit the envelope with a thumbnail, and the lock of hair fell into Elin's palm, still holding the curve of a younger head. [477] The drawing came out clean, uncreased, nine spans of arithmetic and improbable longing. [490] "I could have opened it myself," Elin whispered. [498] "Everyone can," the locksmith said. "That is why I keep the shop where the roads confuse people. A little disorientation, a little starlight, and folks find the courage they walked in with." [530]

Elin asked what the fee was. [536] "The envelope," the locksmith said, and Elin gave it over, empty, its seal broken like a shell. [553] Inside the shop, the walls were papered with hundreds of them, slit and emptied, a museum of secrets that had finally been let out to weather. [579] Only one hung sealed, high in a corner, gone yellow, addressed in the locksmith's own hand. [595] Elin looked at it, and did not ask, and the locksmith was grateful. [608]

The bridge took eleven years and cost more arguments than money. [619] It stands; nine spans; the sixth one whistles in a north wind, which the valley has decided to call singing. [639] Elin walks it most evenings, ribbon in her pocket, the hair long since given to the wind above the gorge. [659] She still watches for the first star, and it still tells her nothing she did not already know, which is the whole of the trick. [684]

The shop at the nexus is closed now. [692] Travelers say the locksmith went west on the night the last span was set, taking one yellowed envelope with her, unopened still, carried the way you carry a lamp you have not yet decided to light. [728] Elin hopes she crossed the bridge. [734]
