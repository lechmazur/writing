The heat wave broke at three in the morning, and the rock exhaled. [13] Ilse felt it through the soles of her boots, the long shudder of stone letting go of a week of fever. [34] Above her, the lamp room of the cobalt lighthouse hummed the way a glass rim hums under a wet finger. [54] That hum was the whole trouble. [60] The lens at Coilbrae did not answer light; it answered vibrations of intent, the pitch of what a keeper truly meant, and it threw that meaning out over eleven miles of water. [92] Sten, the head keeper, had spent the whole heat wave shouting into it. [105] Come back, come back, come back, until his voice tore and his intent tore with it, splintering into a hundred jagged wants: fear, guilt, pride, the ugly private wish to be forgiven. [137] The lens had scattered all of it, and the fishing boat carrying his daughter Mairi had not come home. [156] Six days now. [159] Ilse was only the assistant keeper, nineteen, with salt in the creases of her knuckles, and she had an argument she had never dared to make out loud. [187] Whispers travel farther than shouts. [192] Not through air, through the lens. [198] A shout is a crowd of intentions elbowing each other; a whisper is one intention, narrow as a needle, and the glass can carry a needle forever. [225] Sten had laughed at her once, gently, which was worse than cruelty. [237] In the lee of the tower grew Aoife's garden, planted by Sten's wife before the fever took her: nicotiana, moonflower, night-scented stock, all of them stupid with perfume after dark. [267] Aoife had distilled that scent into a single blue vial and taught Ilse the one thing about it that mattered: it purifies intentions. [290] Breathe it, and everything crooked in what you mean burns off like mist, and only the clean thing is left. [310] Ilse had carried the vial in her apron since Tuesday, thumbing the vial cork loose and tight, loose and tight, unable to decide whether using it would be faith or theft. [341] At dawn the tower turned the color it was named for, cobalt catching the first light like a struck bell. [361] Sten came down the stair with his hands shaking and his throat ruined. [374] "I have nothing left to give it," he rasped. [383] "Then give me the lamp," Ilse said. [390] He looked at her for a long moment, this girl who had scrubbed his lenses and buried his wife's seed packets in dry ground and never once raised her voice at him, and he stood aside. [426] Up in the lamp room the air was hot glass and old brass. [439] Ilse pulled the cork. [443] The garden came out of the vial whole, not a smell so much as a place: a night in July with the sea quiet and Aoife laughing somewhere behind the stock flowers, and it went through Ilse like cold water through a rusted pipe. [487] Everything crooked in her burned off. [493] The wanting to be right went first, then the wanting Sten to see she was right, then the small mean pride of being the assistant who knew better. [521] What was left was so simple it hurt: come home, Mairi. [532] She put her mouth close to the glass and whispered it. [543] The lens took the whisper the way a tuning fork takes a note. [556] No flare, no thunder, only a single clean vibration going out flat across the water, carrying one meaning and nothing else, a needle threading eleven miles of morning. [584] Sten climbed up behind her, and she saw him understand. [594] All week he had been trying to drag his daughter home by the collar. [608] You cannot drag anyone across eleven miles; you can only beckon. [619] And out there, Ilse knew, in a swamped boat with a broken rudder and no compass, the crew would not hear a sound. [642] They would only smell it, impossibly, miles from any shore: a garden at night, jasmine and moonflower and wet stock, the exact scent of a house where someone is still waiting up. [674] And they would turn the bow toward it, because that is what such a smell does to the human heart. [694] The sun came up full and the tower burned blue. [704] Ilse stood with her hand flat on the warm glass, whispering the same two words over and over, screaming them inside her chest where nobody could hear, and beside her Sten wept without making any noise at all. [742] At half past six, the fog off the point moved, and a mast came through it. [758] Ilse did not shout. [762] She set the vial cork gently back in its throat, saving what was left of the garden for the next boat, the next dark, the next impossible small thing that needed saying quietly. [795]
