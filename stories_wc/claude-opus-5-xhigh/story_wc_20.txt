The search party disbanded on the ninth day of rain, and Ilse Marrow walked back alone into the foundry to weigh what was left. [24] Water ran down the brick flues and pooled black around the cold crucibles, and the whole building smelled of wet iron and ash. [47] She was a balanced alchemist, which meant nothing she did was ever free. [60] Her trade was factually mystical: every transmutation obeyed arithmetic, and every arithmetic left a bruise. [75] Lead into silver cost a memory of exact mass; a healed bone cost an hour of someone's sleep. [93] Tobin, her apprentice, had learned the ledger before he learned the tongs, and he had gone into the north flue eleven days ago to scrape a slag sample she had asked for. [125] The flue went further than any map allowed. [133]

On her bench sat the scale, and on the scale sat the zodiac coin. [147] Twelve signs ringed its edge, worn shallow by four generations of assayers, and it was the only counterweight in the world heavy enough to balance a name. [174] Names had mass, and the mass was simply the number of mouths still willing to say them. [191] She wrote TOBIN ASH on a paper slip, set it in the left pan, and watched the coin tip it up like a leaf. [215] Nine days ago his name had outweighed the coin twice over; now the searchers had folded their lanterns and gone home to say other words at supper. [242]

This was what she had wanted, in the awful way of wanting: to understand the weight of names, to hold the exact figure of how much a person is loved. [272] Now she had the figure, and it was small, and the rain kept washing the yard outside as if trying to rinse him off the town's memory faster. [300]

A balanced alchemist could not make weight from nothing. [309] She could only move it. [314] So she did what her guild forbade and began exploring limits, pushing the scale past its rated load to see where the arithmetic frayed. [338] She weighed the names of the dead, which weigh a little forever. [350] She weighed the name of the foundry itself, and the coin cracked clean across Libra. [365]

She sat with the two halves for an hour while the gutters sang. [378] Then she lit the small furnace and did the only honest repair she knew. [392] Kintsugi intentions: mend the break with gold, declare the break, and let the seam carry the purpose of the mending. [412] Into the molten gold she spoke her intention plainly, because a vague intention makes a brittle seam: I sent him, I want him back, I will pay. [439] The coin came out of the mold heavier than before, its gold vein glowing faintly, and the scale accepted it as valid currency. [462]

Ilse understood then what she had built: a pledge, and a pledge can be redeemed. [477] She wrote ILSE MARROW on a slip and set it in the right pan, opposite the boy's. [494] Her name was heavy; she had forty years of neighbors and creditors and students in it. [510] She pressed the mended coin down onto her own slip and said the transfer aloud, all of it, the whole weight, to redeem him. [534] The pans swung, leveled, and held. [540]

Somewhere in the north flue a boy who had been forgotten into thinness became solid enough to be cold, and to be furious about it, and to crawl. [568] He reached the foundry floor at dawn, soaked, coughing soot, shouting for someone whose name would not come. [586] The bench was tidy, the scale empty, and the zodiac coin lay in a puddle of rainwater with its gold seam still warm. [609]

Tobin carried it to the assay office and to the guild and to every door on the hill, asking whose intention this was, and nobody could tell him. [637] But the seam glowed a little whenever he spoke to it, and he found he could not stop speaking to it. [658] So he did the arithmetic himself, the way she had taught him before he could hold tongs, and he understood he was standing in the debt of someone who had spent everything and left the receipt gilded and visible on purpose. [699]

He kept the foundry running for fifty years, and taught every apprentice the first rule in a voice that shone with regret: name what you broke, then fill the crack with gold. [731] And on the last shelf, wrapped in oiled cloth, he kept a slip of paper he could never read aloud, blank as rain, waiting for a mouth heavy enough to lift it. [763]
