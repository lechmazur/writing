The last petal dropped from the old rosebush at dusk, and Ilse Marek stopped pretending she had time. [18] The bush had grown against the platform of Vesna Halt since before the Unification, and it had outlasted the trains, the timetable, and every minister who commissioned her work. [47] Ivy had swallowed the station house, prying the letters off the sign until only VESN remained, which she had always thought fitting. [69] Fifty years ago she and Tomas Bek had agreed on the signal: when the last petal of that rosebush dropped, whoever still lived would come. [94] He came at full dark, leaning on a cane, smelling of turpentine and gold size. [109] "You look like a woman about to confess," he said. [119] "I am a historian," Ilse said, "and confession is a genre I invented." [132] The pamphleteers had called her the fictional historian, and she had never argued, because they were right. [149] In the ashes of the civil war she had written the Chronicle of Vesna, and Tomas had illuminated its centerpiece: a letter from a saint who never breathed, urging her warring sons to hold one bread between them. [187] The illuminated letter, gold-leafed and craquelured with tea and patience, had stopped a second war. [202] That was the argument, anyway: noble lies for a nation too young to survive its own facts. [219] "You want the letter back," Tomas said. [226] "I want what we buried under it." [233] Beneath the far platform lay the inspection pit, where in the winter of the amnesty they had dumped nine crates of testimony: the depositions, the village rolls, the names of who shot whom and on whose order. [270] They had covered the crates with sacks of their own forged copies, and Ilse had joked, grimly, that truth now lay in a pit of lies. [296] She had come to rescue it. [302] "It's rotted," he said. [306] "Vellum in a dry pit under a stone platform does not rot," she said, "and you know it, because you made the vellum." [329] Tomas sat on a bench that the ivy had half-adopted, and she watched him weigh fifty years. [346] "If it comes out, everything we prevented comes back," he said, "the feuds, the reprisals, somebody's grandson with a list." [366] She did not tell him he was heartless, because he wasn't, and she did not weep, because weeping had never moved him. [388] Ilse had been called cold all her life; in fact she was emotionally rational, which meant she let her grief choose the destination and her reason choose the road. [417] So she argued the only way that had ever worked on him: by drawing parallels. [432] She reminded him of the Sable Empire, which had forged a saint's genealogy to justify its taxes, and then, three generations later, forged a heresy to justify its gallows. [461] She reminded him of the Mirovec republics, where an invented treaty kept peace for forty years and then licensed an annexation, because a state taught that its founding document is unquestionable will not question the next one. [498] "And last month the Interior Ministry quoted our letter in the order that closed the Krasna schools, writing 'one bread between us' to explain why those children will now learn in our language only," she said. [534] Tomas closed his eyes. [538] "A lie that saves a generation is a weapon the next generation inherits," she said, "and we did not only build the peace, we built the argument for the pit, and someone else will fill it." [574] The ivy ticked in the wind like a clock nobody wound. [585] "You always did convince me by comparison, and I always hated it," he said, "so bring the crowbar." [603] It took them until moonset to lift the platform slab, and the pit breathed out cold lime dust. [621] The sacks of forgeries had gone to pulp; the crates beneath them had not. [635] Tomas held the lamp while Ilse lifted out the first deposition and read aloud a farmer's account of the level crossing in April, in a voice that did not shake, because she had decided beforehand that it would not. [674] They worked the rest of the night to recapture what they had drowned, crate by crate, up the ladder and onto the ivy-cracked platform, until nine boxes of the country's real memory sat where passengers had once waited. [712] At dawn Tomas took the illuminated letter from his coat, the original, gold still bright as a wet coin. [731] "Publish it with the rest, as what it is," he said, "my hand, your words, our reasons." [748] Ilse laid the forgery on top of the testimony, the beautiful lie crowning the ugly facts, and thought that this, at last, was an honest arrangement. [774] Below them, the bare rosebush let the wind through, having nothing left to drop. [788]
