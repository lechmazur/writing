The library slept the way old animals sleep, twitching in its stacks, and Vess kept watch through the veil of sleep because that was when the boundaries thinned. [28] She was not a librarian. [33] She was the thing the librarians had left behind when the last of them walked out into ordinary weather: a guardian gone feral, nails split from climbing shelves, teeth kept sharp on the spines of books nobody would miss. [72] She slept in the frozen garden, which was not outside. [82] Nothing at the library was outside anymore. [89] The old builders had practiced interiorizing exteriors: they took a hillside, a canal, an orchard in October, and folded each one into a room, so that the collection held landscapes the way other libraries held maps. [125] The garden had been interiorized mid-frost, mist and all, and it had stayed that way for two hundred years: black hedges in white breath, a sundial with no sun, fog that never lifted and never quite settled. [162] Vess loved it and hated it, because fog was uncertainty made visible, and uncertainty was the one visitor she could not chase off. [185] She was dimensionally homesick. [189] It was a real ailment, listed in the medical stacks between dropsy and dysentery: the sickness of a body pulled through a door into a world whose light has the wrong edges. [221] In her home dimension, light did not fall off gradually; it ended, clean as a coastline, and you always knew where you stood. [244] Here, lamplight leaked. [247] Here, everything was a maybe with a glow around it. [257] So she waited. [260] For sixty years she had waited for the door to reopen, and waiting had become a kind of religion with a congregation of one. [284] The cultist came on a Thursday, through the delivery gate, smelling of tallow. [297] He belonged to an order that worshipped the boundaries of light - the exact line where a candle's reach failed - and he had come to steal the garden's sundial, believing it marked the last honest edge in the world. [335] Vess took him down among the hedges without much ceremony. [345] She did not kill him; she was feral, not cruel, and besides, he was the first person she had spoken to in a decade. [369] She took his pendant instead: a small brass lamp, cold, with a hairline crack of true dark running through the glass. [390] "It shows you the edge," he said, from the frost, sounding almost kind. [403] "Whatever you carry it toward, it tells you where that thing stops." [415] She let him limp away and kept the lamp, and that night, through the veil of sleep, she carried it into the fog. [438] The pendant woke as she walked, throwing a pale ring around her boots, and at the ring's rim the mist turned to a wall of clean black - a boundary, sharp as home. [470] She wept, briefly and badly, the way animals do. [479] Then she understood the trap. [484] Inside the ring there was no fog and no question, and also nothing else: no hedges, no sundial, no library breathing in its sleep around her. [510] Certainty was a very small room. [516] She thought about the builders, who had never conquered a landscape; they had only invited one in and asked it to keep its weather. [540] Interiorizing an exterior meant loving it enough to let it stay strange indoors. [553] So Vess did the difficult thing. [559] She hung the cultist's pendant on the sundial's gnomon, opened the little cracked door of it, and let the boundary go slack until the pale ring widened, thinned, and dissolved into the general grey. [593] The fog came in close, familiar as a dog. [602] "All right," she said to it, "stay." [609] That was the beginning of the friendship: a feral woman and an uncertainty, keeping house in a garden that would never finish freezing. [632] She stopped waiting for the door and began, instead, to interiorize her own exterior - to fold the remembered coastline of her old light into a room behind her ribs, mist-edged, imprecise, hers. [664] It was not accurate. [668] It was inhabitable. [671] The homesickness did not leave; it simply learned to sit down. [682] Some nights, deep in the library's dreaming, a reader wanders in through the veil of sleep and finds her there, lucid in the fog, giving directions she is not sure of. [713] She tells them the truth: that every shelf ends somewhere, that the lamp on the sundial marks the last honest edge, and that they are welcome to sit and wait with her until the mist decides what it wants to be. [754] It never does. [757] That is the point. [761]
