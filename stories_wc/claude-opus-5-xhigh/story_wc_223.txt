The dome had no king under it, and would not have one for a year. [15] In that interregnum the city measured time by shadow instead of reigns. [27] Each morning the oculus threw a soft-edged disc of half-light onto the marble, and each morning it landed a hand's width west of where it had been, so the citizens called it the nomadic penumbra and treated it like a guest. [68] Ilsa followed it. [71]

She had been a crisis actor once, paid four coppers a drill to lie in rubble that had been trucked in that morning and to scream on cue for the wardens. [102] Then the granary district fell for real, and she lay in rubble nobody had trucked anywhere, and no warden came for eleven hours. [125] What she remembered afterward was not the noise. [133] It was the man who kept dabbing his neighbor's forehead with a sleeve, long after the neighbor had stopped needing it. [154] It was a girl who counted aloud to keep the dust honest. [166] The chronicles recorded the tonnage of fallen stone and the names of three magistrates. [180] They recorded no sleeves. [184] So Ilsa decided to become the historian of small gestures, a post no one had ever funded. [201]

The archive sat in the whispering gallery that ringed the dome's drum, where a word breathed against the stone at one end arrived intact at the other, sixty paces away. [231] Steward Vashti kept it. [235] Vashti was benevolently ruthless, a woman who would feed you and then take the one thing you did not want to give. [257] She gave Ilsa bread, a stool, and one condition. [266] "The edict of the interregnum forbids speaking the dead aloud under this dome," she said. [281] "Factions harvest grief." [284] "Say a name in this gallery and some claimant owns it by dusk." [297]

Ilsa looked at the floor and understood that she would have to write without sound. [312] The gallery floor was laid in pale hexagons, the penumbra crossed them slowly, and the dust of the fallen district still came in on everyone's boots. [338] So she walked. [341] She developed a pattern of walking that spelled words: long strokes for the uprights, tight arcs for the bellies of letters, a pause and a scuff for the space between. [371] From the rotunda below it looked like grief pacing. [380] From the drum above, where the copyists leaned on the rail, it read: A MAN DRIED A FACE THAT NO LONGER SWEATED. [402] The copyists wrote it down, because copyists cannot help themselves. [412]

Sixty days of this. [416] The penumbra migrated, and her letters had to migrate with it, since only inside that half-light did the dust show at all. [438] She came to love the thing: a wandering brightness with a soft absence at its heart, moving on before anyone could own it. [461]

In the council chamber below, the regents played on a chessboard missing all the pawns. [476] They had lost them in the collapse and refused replacements, saying the game moved faster this way. [493] One night Ilsa carried the board up to the gallery and set it in the penumbra's path. [510] Then she walked, and the copyists read: SIXTEEN SMALL ONES ARE MISSING FROM EVERY ACCOUNT. [525]

Vashti came at dawn with the great codex of the interregnum under her arm. [539] "You want these inside the record," she said, and began to unbind it, cutting the spine cords with a fruit knife until four hundred pages lay loose. [566] "Now your price." [569] She held out the actor's contract Ilsa had signed years ago, the one naming her a paid pretender in eleven drills. [590] "It goes in too," Vashti said. [596] "Otherwise the claimants will use it to unmake everything you have written, and I will not spend a year's work protecting one woman's pride." [620]

Ilsa's hands shook. [623] To be filed forever as the fake who became real was a cruelty dressed as kindness, which is exactly what it was. [645] She laid the contract on the loose pages anyway. [654] Then, because the gallery would carry it sixty paces intact, she leaned to the stone and whispered not a name but a gesture: "He kept dabbing." [680] No edict covered that. [684]

Through the summer she carved sixteen pawns from granary timber, one for each gesture her feet had spelled. [702] The half-light moved on west, taking her letters with it, leaving the floor bright and blank. [718] By then the codex held them, and when the new king was crowned in autumn he was handed a book with no spine and sixteen wooden pawns loose in the box beside it. [751] He read the first loose page standing up, and then, they say, he dried his own face with his sleeve. [771]
