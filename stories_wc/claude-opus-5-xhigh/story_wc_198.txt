Every autumn since the flood, Nils carried his saxophone up the hill to the prism atrium in mist, arriving before the sun could burn the valley clean. [27] The town called him the morning fog saxophonist, half fondly, half the way you name a weather pattern you cannot stop. [48] He was neither, really; he was a keeper, the last of the Vested Hush. [62] Their office was old: nine men and women in grey wool vests, sworn to keep one sentence from ever being finished aloud. [84] The sentence was not words. [89] In 1911 a girl named Oona Bell had climbed down into the radiant grotto in mist beneath the hill and come back up singing twelve notes, in an order no one had heard before, and she said the notes were a prophecy spoken in code, and that the code would keep. [140] It kept. [142] The grotto was a freak of geology, a bowl of white calcite that threw light back like a lit lamp and held sound the way amber holds a fly. [171] Whatever melody entered it whole came out true; the town had proof it did not like to discuss, three drowned streets' worth. [193] So the Hush was founded, and its method was strange, and Nils had inherited it from his grandmother along with her vest and her reeds. [218] You did not silence the twelve notes by refusing to play them; refusal only stored them, and stored things ripen. [238] You hushed a prophecy through negative space: you played everything around it. [250] Every neighboring pitch, every rest given breath, until the twelve notes existed only as the shape of an absence, a keyhole with nothing behind it. [275] It had to be done as dewdrops form, in that narrow half hour when the mist condenses on the atrium's glass and the grotto below opens its ear. [303] The prism atrium had been built over the grotto's mouth for exactly this: a hundred angled panes that split the grey light into ribbons and, more usefully, split sound, so that a careful player could aim silence. [340] Nils unlatched the door, set his case on the wet bench, and listened to the hill breathe. [357] The fog was quietly vivid that morning, not grey at all but faintly rose where the sun leaned on it, and the prisms threw those colors down the throat of the earth in slow bands. [392] He remembered his grandmother's hands on the same keys, her ring clicking, the smell of cork grease and cold stone, and the ache of that memory was the ache of the whole valley: everyone here missed something that had never quite happened. [434] Then he began. [437] He played the notes on either side of the forbidden ones, long and patient, letting the horn go breathy at the edges. [459] The grotto answered with its lamp-white glow, brightening whenever he came near the shape of the code, dimming when he veered away, and he steered by that light like a man rowing a fogbound lake. [494] Halfway through, a girl's voice came up out of the stone. [505] It was a whisper, and it was Oona Bell's, and it had not aged a day since 1911. [523] Finish it, she said, so gently, so familiarly, the way an old friend suggests one more song. [540] That was the trap his grandmother had warned him about: the code did not want to be understood, only completed, and it would use anything you loved to ask. [569] Nils kept his lips on the reed and did not answer with words, because words were how it got in. [589] He answered with everything else. [593] Dewdrops beaded on the panes and slid, and each one bent a ribbon of rose light, and he played to the drops as they fell, filling every gap around the twelve notes until the code stood in the middle of his music like a hole cut in cloth. [641] The whisper thinned. [644] The glow went ordinary, then went out. [651] When the last dewdrop dropped from the lowest pane, the grotto was only a wet white room under a hill, and the twelve notes had nowhere left to stand. [680] Nils lowered the horn and found his cheeks wet, and could not have said whether it was mist or grief for a prophecy he had just made impossible. [708] That was the cost the Hush never printed in its charter: to hush a thing, you must love its shape well enough to trace it, and afterward you miss it. [738] He packed his case, buttoned the grey vest over his chest, and went down through the fog toward the bakery lights. [759] Next autumn he would climb again, as dewdrops formed, and play the world around the silence one more time. [778]
