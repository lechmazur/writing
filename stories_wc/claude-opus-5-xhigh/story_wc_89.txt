The orchard had not seen a sunrise in nine hundred years, and the blossoms did not mind. [17] Under permanent moonlight the pear trees bloomed white and blue, their petals held as still as held breath, and Maren walked beneath them with a brass censer swinging from her wrist. [48] She was the last beast whisperer of the Moon-Held Grove, and three nights ago the phoenix had risen. [66] She had knelt in the ash and watched the heap shudder, and a wet new bird had climbed out of it, shaking itself dry under the unmoving moon. [94] It had looked at her the way a bird looks at a stone. [107] Fifty years of bond had burned with the body. [116] Verhal had slept against her ribs every night of those fifty years, and it was Verhal who had taught her the tongues of foxes and moths and the slow gossip of bears. [148] A whisperer is only the second half of a conversation; she had lost the first. [163]

In her breast pocket she carried a strip of paper, a fortune from a cookie, pressed on her decades ago by a spice merchant who had crossed the grove and refused to explain himself. [197] WHAT RETURNS WILL NOT BE WHAT IT WAS; NOR WILL THE ONE WHO WAITS. [211] She had laughed at it once, over tea, with a phoenix asleep in her lap. [226] Tonight her hands were paradoxically steady, the way hands go when the grief is too large to fit inside them. [246]

The books in the orchard's stone hut said a bond could be conjured back, but only via the scent of rare spices, because memory lives in the nose, older than language and older than death. [281] She had spent forty years gathering them: black cardamom smoked over dragon-coal, resin from a tree that grew in a drowned city, saffron from crocuses that flowered once in a human lifetime, and a thumbnail of the true cinnamon that is said to smell like the inside of grief. [330] She struck flint over the censer and the smoke went up in a thin blue rope. [346] The orchard leaned in. [350] Above the blossoms the sky stood open all the way down, star after star, cold and enormous and entirely indifferent, and beneath all that indifference there was one bird in a pear tree and one woman holding a censer, and the distance between them was the only distance that mattered. [400]

The phoenix came down branch by branch. [407] It put its beak into the smoke and made a small interrogative chirr, the sound Verhal had made every morning on waking, and Maren's throat closed. [433] Then her own body began to answer. [440] Her fingernails thickened and darkened. [445] Quills pushed up under her collarbones with a sound like rain starting. [457] Her bones went light, hollow, wrong. [463] The books had warned her about this in one dry line: a bond across death cannot be carried by one side alone, and something in the living must cross partway to meet what has come back. [499]

She did not want it. [504] She wanted her friend, not wings; she wanted her wooden house and her two ordinary hands and the foxes coming to the door at moonrise to complain about the badgers. [534] She reached to smother the censer, and her hand had feathers on it, and she stopped. [550] Nor will the one who waits. [556] The merchant had not sold her a prophecy; he had sold her a warning she had needed forty years to read. [577]

She let the smoke burn. [582] The phoenix landed on her changed wrist, heavier than any bird should be, warm as a banked sun, and pressed its head up under her jaw. [608] She said his old name and he did not know it. [619] That was all right. [623] The bond took anyway, rough and unfinished and new, a rope spliced from two different fibers, and it held. [642]

The change stopped where it stopped. [648] She kept her hands, mostly; she kept her name; she gained feathers along both forearms, eyes that could see the heat of a mouse under snow, and a throat shaped to make sounds no human throat should make. [686] In the morning that would not come, she began teaching the new bird the tongues that the old bird had taught her. [708] He learned fast, and badly, and laughed at the moths. [718] Above them the stars went on being vast and uninterested, which she no longer minded, because vastness had turned out to be the only proper room for something this small: two creatures in a moonlit orchard, learning each other's names again. [759]
