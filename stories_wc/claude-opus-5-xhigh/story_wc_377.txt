After words became unnecessary, Marekh climbed to the bluff anyway, carrying a hammer she no longer needed to explain. [19] The whispering bluff stood above the clouds, a shelf of black stone where the wind arrived already sorted into tones. [39] She was the last harmonious bell tuner, and the bells up here were the last that still disagreed with anyone. [59] Below the cloudline, the Concord passed meaning hand to hand, thought to thought, exact as coins. [75] Nothing was misunderstood anymore, and nothing was wondered at either. [85] Marekh had watched the color go out of people the way dye goes out of cloth: slowly, then all at once, leaving them accurate and gray. [111] She had come up here to preserve the color of wonder, and she had eleven days of autumn left to do it. [133]

Marekh was seasonally mute. [137] Every winter since childhood her throat had closed like a shutter, opening again only when the thaw came up the cliff face. [159] In the old days that had been a hardship; now it was merely quaint, since nobody spoke at all. [178] But she needed her voice to tune. [185] A tuner sings the note she wants and files the bronze until the bell agrees with her. [202] Come the first frost, she would be a sculptor of silence, unable to test a single hum. [219]

The apprentice the Concord had sent, a narrow boy named Ost, sat on the ledge with his knees up, radiating patience she had not asked for. [245] He had never heard a lie, so he had never heard a truth; both required a gap between saying and meaning, and the Concord had closed that gap forever. [274] Marekh showed him the crate instead of explaining. [282] Inside, packed in sawdust and its own slow weeping, lay the iceberg fragment: a wedge of glacier older than speech, hauled up the cliff road by six oxen and a great deal of stubbornness. [316] It held bubbles of air that no lung had ever borrowed. [327] Ost touched it and flinched, and for one unguarded instant his mind gave off something unsorted, something the Concord had no coin for. [350] Marekh nodded, satisfied: wonder, still in there, buried like the air in the ice. [364]

Her plan was not a bell but an instrument that would play itself through kinetic sculpture: eleven bronze vanes hung on a spindle, each catching the updraft, each swinging a clapper against a tuned tongue of metal. [401] At its heart, the fragment, mounted to split the low sun. [412] The bluff's wind never repeated itself, so the machine would never repeat a phrase; it would say only what the air actually was, note by note. [438] Symphonies of truth, her old teacher had called such things, back when truth needed instruments. [453] She worked with muted vigor, the way an old body works when it has one thing left to finish: no wasted motion, no shouting, only the file's steady rasp and the hammer's small definite verdicts. [488] Ost learned to hold the vanes steady. [495]

Autumn went faster than the arithmetic allowed. [502] On the ninth day the frost came early, crawling up out of the valley, and Marekh woke with a shutter in her throat and three vanes still untuned. [530] The fragment, meanwhile, was dying; a hand's width of it had gone to water in the crate, and its bubbles rose and fled. [553] She could not sing the notes. [559] So she did the thing tuners are taught never to do: she pressed the bronze to her collarbone and tuned by bone, feeling the beat frequencies stumble and then smooth, filing blind against a note she could only remember. [598] Ost watched, and did not offer her the Concord's easy thought-speech, because he had begun to understand that some things must cost something. [621]

They set the fragment at dawn. [627] It was smaller now, fitted into a bronze cradle at the spindle's crown, and when the sun came over the cloud-plain the ice began to glisten and throw its held colors down the vanes. [661] The updraft took the eleven vanes. [667] The bluff, which had only ever whispered, now spoke. [676] It was not a tune anyone had written. [684] It was the wind's exact shape rendered in bronze, unrepeatable, wrong-footed, gorgeous, and the colors moved with it so that the sound seemed to have a hue. [711]

Ost stood up with his hands open. [718] He could not transmit what he felt; there was no coin for it, no exact meaning to hand across. [737] He would have to bring people up the cliff road and let the machine do it, one wind at a time, forever. [759] Marekh, mute until spring, leaned on her hammer and listened to the last of the old ice singing itself away, water beading down the bronze like applause. [786] She did not mind the silence.
