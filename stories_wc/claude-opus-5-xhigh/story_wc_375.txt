The last echo of laughter faded up the throat of the grotto at nine minutes past six, and Yelena let her charcoal go still. [24] She had drawn eleven portraits that day, tourists in rented parkas, each of them grinning under the blue-white chandeliers of gypsum. [45] She would remember all eleven faces in forty years, the way she remembered every face she had ever set down. [65] It was not a talent she had asked for; it was more like a tenancy. [80] Her uncle Bastian had called such things inescapable callings, and he had said it the way other men said "sentence." [100] For thirty years he had run the Aurora Grotto as a show cave: colored floodlights, a recorded harp, a joke about the stalactites holding tight. [125] Before that it had been the Corvo mine, and before that it had been nothing but crystal growing in the dark, patient as grief. [149] He had died in April, and he had left her the cave, the folding stool, and a sentence she could not stop turning over: "Rectify the entrance painting." [177] The entrance painting was the only thing Bastian had ever hung where people could see it - a wide oil of the grotto at false dawn, the crystals throwing off rose and green light like a low aurora. [214] Tourists photographed it. [217] He never spoke about it. [222] Now she lifted it off its two iron pins, and the cold breathed out at her from a cavity cut into the limestone. [245] A broken combination lock lay in the dust at the bottom, its dial smashed flat, still threaded through a hasp it could no longer hold. [270] She understood it at once, the way she understood faces. [280] He had locked the niche, forgotten the numbers or refused them, and then broken the lock open with a hammer - not to get in, but so that someone after him could. [311] Inside were seven canvases, rolled, smelling of linseed and stone. [321] She spread them on the walkway under the floodlights and felt the floor of her chest give way. [339] Seven men in miner's canvas, painted with a tenderness she had never once seen in her uncle's face - and every head above the collar left blank, primed white, waiting. [368] The collapse had been in 1994. [374] Yelena was six, and it had been family day at the mine mouth, and she had been given her first stick of charcoal and a pad, and she had drawn the men because they laughed at her and held still. [414] Bastian had come up out of the shaft that afternoon; the seven had not. [428] He had painted them for twenty years and could never find their faces again, because grief had stoppered him and memory was not his gift. [453] It was hers. [456] That was the rectification he meant: not to repair his painting, but to finish what it had been hiding, and to put the right faces back on the right shoulders. [486] She worked in the grotto every evening that summer, after the last visitor's laughter went thin and folded itself away up the tunnel. [509] She used charcoal first, blocking each face out of the dark of her own head: Dolniak with his crooked bridge of nose; the Ferrer brothers, one broad, one narrower by a knuckle; Ames, who had asked her to make him handsome and she had, badly. [554] Her fingers went black to the second joint and stayed black; the crystal dust and the charcoal made a grey paste in her cuticles that no soap touched. [582] Then oil, thinly, learning his palette by copying the hands he had already finished. [596] She had spent twenty years drawing strangers for eight dollars a head, telling herself that was all her gift was for. [617] Something stoppered in her since that family day came uncorked, and it came out slowly, under pressure, the way anything does that has been held that long. [644] In September she hung all seven along the walkway where the floodlights turned the gypsum rose and green, and she rehung Bastian's false dawn at the end of the row, last, where it belonged. [678] Visitors came, and laughed less, and stood longer. [686] Old women from the valley put their fingers on the canvas and said names out loud. [702] Yelena kept the broken lock on the ticket table, where people asked about it, and she told them the truth: that some things are locked by the people who most want them opened. [735] Every night, after the last echo of laughter faded, she stayed down there alone with her stool and her black fingers, adding, correcting, remembering. [759] It was not a thing she could put down. [768] It never had been. [772]
