The plague ended in the third week of spring, and within days the Reclamation Guild arrived to explain what it had meant. [22] Doret watched them from the mouth of the storm drain, which was, she would tell anyone who asked, the only honest architecture left in Old Vess. [48] Everything else had gone down into the mud when the harbor wall failed: temples, counting houses, the observatory with its brass meridian. [70] The sewers, built by people who expected water, survived. [79] She lived in them without apology and with excellent posture. [89] She called herself a philosopher, on the strength of four salvaged books; the fisher families called her the drain woman. [109] The Guild's herald read the official account aloud to the fisher families camped on the ridge. [125] The city had drowned because it had grown proud; the plague had come as chastening; the plague had lifted because the Guild's star-priests had prayed the correct sequence in the correct order. [157] Doret climbed up out of the drain, mud to the knee, and said, pleasantly, that this was a beautiful story and every load-bearing part of it was false. [185] She was not a shouter. [189] She had learned that a flat voice cuts deeper than a raised one, and she used it like a blade she kept oiled. [212] Her evidence was in her hands, and in the wall of the cut she had spent two years digging behind her cistern. [234] She had gone down through sedimentary layers the way a scholar goes through a library: silt from the flood, then a black band of ash from the shipyard fire, then oyster shell, then the pale clay of the old quay. [274] Plague bones lay above the flood silt. [281] The city had drowned first and sickened after, which meant the drowning could not have been punishment for a sickness that had not yet happened. [306] Order matters, she said. [310] Mud keeps order better than men do. [317] From her coat she drew the thing she had found in the pale clay: a cog the width of a thumbnail, cut in brass, thirty-one teeth, from a seafarer's chronometer. [347] The Guild taught that longitude was a gift of prayer and starlight, granted to the devout. [363] The cog said otherwise. [367] It said that men and women in Old Vess had once carried the harbor's hour with them across open ocean in a box of oiled brass, and had known where they were by arithmetic. [401] The Guild had not banned the chronometers. [408] It had simply stopped teaching how to cut the teeth. [418] Within a generation the method was a rumor, and the Guild owned the sky. [432] The herald asked, reasonably enough, why a woman living in a sewer should be believed over an institution. [450] Doret said she should not be believed at all. [458] She said the cut was open, the layers were numbered, and anyone with a lamp and a spare afternoon could go down and check her, which was precisely the difference between her account and his. [493] That was her whole rebellion, and she thought it a rational one: she did not ask to be trusted. [512] Afterward, in the lamplit dark, she agonized. [519] Not over the herald, who was only a mouth, but over the thing she wanted, which was dangerous. [537] She wanted to be right in public. [543] A person who wants that will start shaping evidence to fit the shape of her enemy's error, and then she will have built a rival story instead of preserving anything. [573] Two stories fighting produce a winner. [579] A method produces successors. [583] So she did the harder thing. [589] In the morning she found the fisher girl who had followed her down the cut three times without being invited, and gave her a file, a scrap of brass, and the cog to copy. [623] Thirty-one teeth, evenly spaced, no mercy for the eighth one. [633] The girl's first three attempts were rubbish, and Doret told her so in the same calm, abrasive voice she used on heralds, because a file does not care about feelings and neither does the sea. [668] The fourth was passable. [672] By autumn there were nine files working in the drain, and the Guild's herald had gone inland to a city with drier history. [695] Doret kept the original cog in a jar of oil and did not tell anyone it was sacred, because it was not. [717] It was a sample. [721] The sacred part, if there was one, was the file and the counting and the willingness to go down through the mud and look. [745] Old Vess stayed sunken. [749] Its ruins made a fine, patient archive, and above them the new boats went out carrying the harbor's hour in their bellies, knowing exactly where they were. [776]
