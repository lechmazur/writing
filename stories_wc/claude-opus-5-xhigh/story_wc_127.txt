The amber grotto held the last of the light the way a jar holds honey, thick and unhurried. [18] Mirren liked dusk best, because at dusk her customers lied more beautifully. [30] She sold counterfeit emotions: sorrow cut with rosewood, courage thinned to a whisper, devotion that lasted exactly as long as the buyer's evening. [53] Every vial she poured was a half truth, which she considered the only honest product on the market, since a whole truth cannot be diluted and a whole lie cannot be worn. [85] She was professionally thorough about it. [91] She logged every batch, tested each accord on her own wrist, and never sold a feeling she had not first taken apart note by note and then reassembled. [119] What she could not do was feel any of them. [128] Instead she had insight, which is not the same thing and is easier to bill for. [144] The grotto gave her that. [149] Fossil resin covers the walls in warm bulges, and resin, rubbed, remembers electricity; the Greeks named amber elektron for exactly this reason. [171] When a customer stood in the cave and spoke, Mirren dragged her palm along the amber and read the static that leapt to her skin: the crackle of a man exaggerating, the flat calm of a woman who had already decided to leave. [214] Charge does not lie, though people do, and she loved her customers with the affectionate detachment of a lighthouse keeper counting ships. [236] Yorick Sabel came at dusk, as she had asked, carrying a small tin. [249] He was a fragrance chemist, sixty years old, and inside the tin lay a single blotter petal, teardrop shaped, dipped once and dried. [272] 'This is the hour my wife died,' he said, 'distilled the way I would distill jasmine.' [288] He wanted copies to sell to mourners who could not reach their own grief, and he wanted the original kept safe, and he wanted Mirren, the best forger in the district, to do the work. [323] She said yes, which was half true. [330] She would make his copies; she had no intention of leaving the original intact. [344] For eleven years she had wanted one thing, and it was not money. [357] She wanted to fuse insight with empathy, to feel what the static told her, because a woman who understands everything and feels nothing is only a very good instrument. [386] The trade said it could be done once: take a whole, undiluted emotion and let it subsume you. [404] Not wear it, but be entered by it, overwritten, remade. [414] No dealer she knew had risked it, since counterfeiters survive by never touching the genuine. [429] She set the petal on the amber ledge and worked through her procedure, thorough to the end: ninety one notes named, weighed, sketched in her ledger, the copies drawn off into six vials while the light in the grotto went from honey to rust. [473] Yorick sat on a stone and talked about his wife's hands. [484] Mirren brushed the wall and the static told her the truth beneath his voice: he was not selling grief to help mourners, he was selling it to get rid of it, and he could not say so out loud. [523] Two half truths, then, sitting in an amber cave at dusk, each politely holding the other's coat. [540] When the copies were sealed she lifted the original petal and pressed it to her mouth like a communion wafer. [560] It came in through the roof of her palate, and it was not sadness; it was a kitchen at four in the morning, a woman's weight going out of a hand, forty years contracting to a single held breath. [599] Her hands shook, her eyes ran, and every fact she had ever read off the amber walls came back and landed, this time, with weight. [624] As the transformation completed she understood that she had not gained a feeling; she had gained the ability to be inconvenienced by other people. [648] Yorick was watching her with the frightened face of a man who has just handed over his only relic. [667] 'The copies took,' she said, and that was true. [676] 'The original is spent,' she said, and that was also true, and she did not tell him where it had gone, because he would have wanted it back and she could not have given it, and because grief he could not carry was now being carried in a cave by someone who had begged for the chance. [733] Then she did something she had never done in eleven years of trade: she sat beside him on the cold stone, put her hand on his sleeve, and said nothing at all. [765] The amber crackled faintly under her palm, reporting only what she already felt. [778]
