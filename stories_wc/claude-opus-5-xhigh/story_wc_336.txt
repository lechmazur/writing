Upon the hush of budding hope, when the orchard's buds swelled but had not yet dared to open, Nessa climbed to the verdant parapet beside the waterfall. [27] Her mentor was already there, shears in hand, ankle-deep in moss. [38] Old Ismay had pruned these trees for sixty years, and her wisdom was a subtractive kind: the orchard grew along the parapet's stone rim like a green sentence she had written by erasing. [71] Below them, the waterfall fell through a seam in the air, and where its mist turned, the other world showed: grey streets, grey rain, people walking with their faces closed. [101] It had been greying for a decade. [108] "They have forgotten the color of wonder," Nessa said. [117] "Not forgotten," Ismay said, "mislaid." [122] "There is a difference, and the difference is our work." [132] Nessa was insatiably creative, and it was her worst habit and her only gift. [146] In four seasons as apprentice she had grafted pears that chimed, coaxed a plum to bear fruit shaped like small blue lanterns, and invented nine varieties nobody had asked for. [176] "Let me carry a whole tree across," she begged. [185] "A hundred trees, an entire orchard, and let them see." [195] Ismay set down her shears. [200] "Weaving between worlds is not carrying," she said. [208] "A thread goes through the needle's eye or it does not go at all." [222] The seam admitted only what was small and true. [231] That was why the pruners had always sent seeds. [240] And it was why, on that morning, Ismay meant to give away the last heirloom seed in the orchard's keeping. [260] It lay in a linen fold, brown and unremarkable, wrinkled like a sleeping ear. [274] Nessa had expected something luminous. [279] "It looks like nothing," she said. [285] "It looks like enough," said Ismay. [291] She explained then what Nessa had never been told: that the seed was not one tree's child but the whole orchard's, made by capturing essence. [316] Each spring, every pruning cut released a little of what a tree meant to be, and the pruner's craft was to gather that, breath by breath, into a single kernel. [346] Sixty years of cuttings were folded in the linen: the chiming pear, the lantern plum, the apples her own teacher had loved. [368] "You want to make," Ismay said. [374] "But making is also choosing what to leave out." [383] "I have cut away ten thousand branches to preserve the color of wonder in something a hand can close around." [403] The waterfall's roar softened, as it did each year at the turning, until the parapet held a radiant hush, light standing in the spray like something patient. [430] In that quiet the seam opened wide enough for a thread. [441] "Your hands are steadier than mine now," Ismay said, and held out the linen. [455] Nessa took it and stepped into the mist, and weaving between worlds felt exactly like being pruned - everything unnecessary fell away, and what remained was small and awake. [483] She came out in a grey courtyard between grey buildings, where a strip of dirt held cigarette ends and a dead sapling in a wire cage. [509] A child sat on the step with her chin on her fists, waiting for nothing in particular. [526] Nessa knelt, made a hole with two fingers, and set the seed in it. [540] "What is it?" the child asked. [546] "Succor," Nessa said, because that was the old word her teacher used - help that arrives before you can name your want. [567] "It will want watering." [571] "It will want twenty years." [576] The child considered the dirt, then the strange woman, then the dirt again, and something in her face opened like a bud. [598] She went for water without being asked. [605] Back on the parapet, Ismay was pruning again, because the orchard had already begun making the next seed and would need help choosing. [628] Nessa picked up the second shears. [634] She was still insatiably creative; she would invent a thousand things yet. [646] But she understood now that the greatest of them would fit in a linen fold, and that the wonder she carried across was not hers to keep, only to hand over, wet-fingered, to a child on a step. [684] The waterfall took up its roar again, and the buds, given permission, began to open. [699]
