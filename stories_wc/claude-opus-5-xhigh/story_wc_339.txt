Three weeks after the world found Amelia Earhart's plane, the world stopped looking at us. [15] The cameras went to the Electra's coral-furred wing, and our little expedition kept its funding only because a foundation in Lisbon liked the word "library." [40] So I went down alone to the SS Verity, six hundred meters under a flat gray sea, to catalogue a room nobody had read in ninety-eight years. [67] Anselm came with me, as he does. [73] We have been on first-name terms since the winter my sister Nell's trawler went down and I spent four minutes clinically dead in a decompression chamber, arguing with him about the ethics of return. [107] He is not dramatic. [111] He is patient, and he reads over my shoulder. [120]

The Verity's first-class library survived because the sea took her gently: she settled upright in the mud, and the mahogany doors held. [142] Inside, the shelves stood in their ranks like a congregation, the books swollen to bricks of pulp, the reading lamps still angled toward chairs that had waited a century for someone to sit down. [176] I am quietly analytic by temperament and by training; I measure things in order to stop feeling them, and I have never once succeeded. [200]

In my thigh pocket I carried Nell's snow globe, a cheap Reykjavik souvenir with a plastic puffin inside. [218] For six years I had been timing its flakes. [227] Glycerin, Stokes drag, terminal velocity: the white grains fall at a rate you can predict to three decimal places, and they never quite land, because the smallest ones are stirred forever by the water's own trembling. [263] I told my colleagues it was a calibration hobby. [271] What I actually wanted was the physics of letting go: a law, an equation, some coefficient that would tell me how long grief takes to settle and whether it ever does. [302]

On the fourth dive I found the sampler. [309] It hung in a shallow display case beside the atlases, embroidered linen, and the case's seal had held just enough that the threads survived as threads. [335] No flowers, no alphabet, no pious verse - only rows of knots and dashes in black silk, worked by someone in a hurry. [357] I brought it up in a pressurized case, and for eleven nights, in the ship's lab, I read it by untangling threads. [379] Under the microscope, with a tungsten needle, I separated each knot from its neighbor and noted its length: short, short, long. [400] Morse, stitched by a passenger named Alma Whitcombe, who according to the manifest had no paper and, being deaf, had learned code from her son the wireless operator. [428] She had sat in the wireless room with her hand on his key and copied what he sent while the Verity went down by the bow. [454] No shore station ever logged it; the storm ate the signal. [465] So the last transmission of the SS Verity existed nowhere in the world except in linen, in the dark, at six hundred meters. [488]

It read: SOS SOS SS VERITY FOUNDERING ENGINE ROOM DARK BOATS AWAY WE ARE NOT AFRAID. [504] Then, in looser stitching, larger, as if the hand had steadied into something other than fear: MOTHER SAYS LETTING GO IS NOT A FORCE IT IS THE ABSENCE OF ONE. [534] I sat with that for a long time in a lab that smelled of brine and acetone. [551] Six years of glycerin and drag coefficients, and a deaf woman in 1927 had solved it with her hand on her son's key. [574] Holding on is work; it costs muscle and heat and attention. [585] Letting go is what happens when you stop paying. [594] Anselm read it over my shoulder and said nothing, which is how he agrees. [608]

On the final dive of the season I went back to the library. [621] I set Nell's snow globe on the empty shelf where the sampler had hung, and the puffin looked out at the congregation of ruined books. [646] Beside it I left a note on waterproof paper, in pencil, for whoever comes next in whatever century they come. [666] It said: Settling time forty-one seconds, and after that the snow is not falling and no one is holding it. [686] Alma Whitcombe, cabin 12, was right. [692] Then I shook the globe once, so that the flakes would be moving when I left them, and swam out through the mahogany doors. [716] Anselm stayed at my elbow through the long ascent, the way an old friend walks you to a gate. [735] At the last decompression stop he let go of my wrist without being asked, and I noted, because noting is what I do, that it cost him nothing at all. [765] The surface was gray, and bright, and full of air. [775]
