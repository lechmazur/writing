The constellations had stopped moving, the way they did once every ninety years, and in that pause Ilyari went into the ultrasonic forest to finish her last map. [28] She had been blind since birth, which the cartographers' guild had called a disqualification until they saw her charts. [47] Ilyari read the world through her skin, which absorbed languages the way dry cloth takes water. [63] One palm on a stranger's wrist and she carried his grammar for a week; one hand on a hull and she spoke the dialect of its shipwrights. [90] The forest spoke too, though above the range of ears. [100] Its trees sang in ultrasound, chirping at each other across the black hours, and their song entered Ilyari's forearms as heat and syntax. [123] She had mapped its ridges, its four rivers, its shivering thickets of resonant birch. [137] What she had never mapped was the stillpoint. [145] Every voice in the forest, the guild believed, cancelled somewhere: one clearing where the waves met their opposites and silence stood like a held breath. [170] It moved as the trees grew, and could only be found while the sky was still, when the forest's song locked into a single unchanging chord. [196] Ilyari wanted it for a reason she had not written in her commission papers. [210] Her brother Sev had gone into the forest eleven years ago to dowse for the stillpoint and had not come out. [231] Grief, she had learned, was the only country she could not chart; she knew its weather and not its borders. [251] So she came to navigate the one depth that had no coastline. [263] On her index finger she wore Sev's platinum compass ring, its needle suspended in a bead of oil so small that only a fingertip could read it. [290] Platinum, he used to say, does not lie to the cold. [301] She used it to dowse, holding her hand loose and letting the ring drift until the metal warmed against one direction. [322] The ring pulled her east, over root and frost, into a stand of trees whose bark hummed under her palms in a language of numbers. [347] There she found the first note. [353] Sev had carved it into the trunk: a whole note, cut deep, ringed by his initials. [369] Forty paces on, the ring warmed again, and her fingers read a second carving, a third, a rising phrase written across the forest in wood. [394] He had left her a trail of musical notes, knowing she would come, knowing she read with her hands. [413] She hummed them as she walked, low in her throat, a quiet blaze building behind her ribs. [430] By the twelfth tree the phrase had become a melody she had never heard and somehow already knew: their mother's lullaby, transposed, turned inside out. [455] The forest heard her singing it. [461] The trees answered above hearing, and the answer came into her skin as words she had no mouth for, an old grammar of pressure and interval. [487] Follow, it said, or something near it. [494] She followed, and the humming in her arms thinned, then faded, then stopped. [507] Ilyari stood in a clearing where nothing spoke. [515] No pressure on her cheeks, no syntax in her wrists, no borrowed tongues crowding her blood. [531] For the first time in her life she was only herself, untranslated. [543] The stillpoint. [545] Sev's coat lay folded at the center, weighted with stones, and under it his last map, drawn in raised dots for her fingers. [568] It charted no ground. [572] It was a map of what he had felt going in: the shallows of missing her, the trench where their mother's death still sat, and, at the deepest sounding, a small marked place labelled here it is quiet enough to hear yourself love someone. [616] He had not come out because he had stayed to finish it, and the forest, in the end, had kept him the way it kept its silence. [643] Ilyari knelt and wept without sound, because sound did not exist there, and the burning in her chest stayed low and steady and did not consume her. [670] Above, the constellations shifted, and their pause ended. [678] The chord broke; the forest resumed its arguing; the stillpoint began to drift away from her like a boat unmoored. [698] She took the coat, the map, and one carved chip of bark, and walked out with the melody still in her hands. [720] The guild wanted coordinates. [724] She gave them a song instead, and told them to sing it and feel where they went quiet. [742] Then she went home and began the only chart that had ever mattered, drawn in raised dots: the uncharted depths, sounded at last, with the platinum ring warm on her finger and pointing, always, toward the still place in herself.
