The harbor went luminous a little after ten, the way it did on clear nights, moonlight lying flat and bright across the water like spilled milk. [26] Marisol watched it from her fire escape, four flights up, where she grew what the alley would allow. [44] Beans in coffee tins, sorrel, a fig no taller than her knee, and the old rosebush her mother had carried up those stairs in a bucket thirty years ago. [73] Everything lived inside a cage of iron and chicken wire, which was, she thought, the only honest way to keep something tender in a city like this. [100] The alley never got sun, so she had learned to steal light instead. [113] Bits of mirror, a dented kettle lid, foil taped to broomsticks, all angled to reflect light into dark corners where the seedlings sulked. [136] The best of them hung at her throat on a boot lace: a single tooth broken from her father's lockmaster's bitting gauge, steel, notched, polished by forty years of thumbs. [166] Teo, who was eleven and lived below, called it her lucky flake. [178] She called it a ruler. [183] "Measure, don't guess," her father used to say, and she had spent a life trying to instill that in anyone who would sit still long enough to hear it. [212]

Tonight the rosebush held one petal. [218] It had bloomed late and stubbornly, as it always did, and her father's rule had been simple: when the final petal drops from the old rosebush, you row out. [247] He had died in March with that instruction and a brass tag in her hand, and she had waited eight months, wistfully pragmatic as ever, because grief keeps but seedlings don't. [278]

The petal let go at 10:40. [284] A quivering hush came over the alley then, the kind that arrives when a gull stops mid-cry and the ferry horn hasn't yet answered, and Marisol felt it in her teeth. [315] She knocked on Teo's window on the way down. [324]

They took the skiff from the fish dock, and she made him row, correcting him until he found the rhythm of oars in water, that patient two-count that is mostly waiting. [355] "Long pull, then let her run," Marisol said. [363] "Same as watering; too much force and you only splash." [373]

The moon laid a road across the harbor and they rowed up it toward the old range light on the seawall, dark since the port cut its budget. [401] Her father had fitted that lamp house with its lock, forty years back, and had never told the port he kept a second keyway. [425] The brass tag was the blank. [431] Marisol had filed it over the summer, checking each cut against the notches of the gauge tooth, because a key is only a measured lie a lock agrees to believe. [461] It turned on the third try. [467]

Inside the lamp house, dry as a drawer, sat his gauge with its missing tooth, a can of lamp oil, a wick, and a note in pencil. [494] Rose cuttings wrapped in wet newspaper lay beside them, still green at the heel. [508] The note said only: keep the lamp lit, keep her cuttings going, and don't guess. [523]

Teo trimmed the wick while she poured, and when the flame caught, the glass threw a long yellow blade across the water, into the black notch under the coal pier where nothing had been visible for years. [560] Marisol did not cry, which surprised him. [567] She was already counting cuttings against pot space, dividing eleven by four. [579]

"Take the tooth," she said, unknotting the boot lace and hanging it on him. [593] "Your job is the north wall, angling it under the stairs where the light dies." [608] He held it up, and the harbor's shine slid along the notches like water finding a channel. [625] They rowed home with the lamp burning behind them, keeping the two-count, saying nothing, and the hush over the harbor stopped quivering and simply held. [650]

In the morning she planted the cuttings behind the chicken wire, tender things caged and guarded, aimed at every scrap of borrowed light she owned. [675] Teo's mirror-work on the north wall was terrible for a month, then it wasn't. [689] Three took root by spring, which was, she noted, better odds than the lock had given her. [706] She told him nothing about it, which was its own kind of praise. [719]
