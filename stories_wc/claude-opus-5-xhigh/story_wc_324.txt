The Bureau gave me a clipboard, a lanyard, and the ethics of a plumber: trace the leak, note who got wet, move on. [23] Contact tracer for hearts, third district, eleven years. [31] When someone dies, love does not simply stop; it keeps traveling, and somebody has to knock on the doors it reaches. [52] Teodor Halas died on a Tuesday, and by Thursday twilight I was standing in the mist-clad atrium of Almond Court with his hearing aid in my palm. [79] The fountain there breathes fog up the six galleries every evening, so the building seems to hold its breath with you. [100] The aid was decades old, beige, cracked at the hinge, the kind that whistled in church. [116] Old devices are the best registers we have. [124] A machine pressed to a man's skull for forty years learns which voices made his pulse jump, and it keeps those pulses the way a stone keeps heat. [152] My work is done by tracking faint pulses in a decades-old hearing aid, one name at a time, and it is only possible in the twenty minutes when the dead call. [183] Between the last of the sun and the first true dark, the recently dead lean on anything that once carried a living voice. [206] You do not hear words. [211] You hear taps, like a finger on a windowpane, and each rhythm belongs to somebody he loved. [228] I logged four contacts in nine minutes: a daughter in Ostend, a chess partner on the second gallery, a nurse, and a man who had not spoken to Teodor since 1998. [259] Then came a fifth pulse, and it was wrong. [268] It did not tap. [272] It scraped, fast and shallow, like something running out. [281] Protocol says a distorted pulse is interference and should be marked unresolved. [293] I have been written up three times for being persistently curious, which my supervisor spells "slow." [309] So I sat on the fountain rim and worked the scraping pulse against the last photograph in Teodor's file. [328] We collect a last photograph from every decedent, because people point their cameras at what still matters to them. [347] His was taken eleven days before the end, from this exact rim: the atrium at twilight, mist to the fourth floor, and up in the fifth gallery one lit window with a pale hand flat against the glass. [385] Nobody photographs a hand unless they have been watching it for a while. [398] Flat 5F. [502] She had fallen on Tuesday evening, the same hour he died, and had spent two days crossing her own floor toward the door. [525] The paramedics wanted to know how anyone had found her, and I did not have language for it.
