They said the fire took everything, but fire is only a fast kind of hunger, and hunger leaves crumbs. [19] Mira Solvay woke in the almshouse with her hands wrapped like two white lanterns and one thing saved: a strip of birch bark, charcoal-marked, scorched brown at both ends. [48] Alder's last ledger strip. [52] For nine years he had run charcoal through the canyon to feed her furnace, twelve sacks a month, untaxed, and she had pretended not to know. [78] She was a luminous glass artist; the town excused a great deal for the sake of her windows, which drank daylight and gave it back blue at midnight. [106] The excusing stopped the night two men bolted her studio door and lit the coal store. [122] Alder broke the window to get her out and did not come back through it. [137]

After surviving the attempt, she did what she had always done with heat: she adapted. [152] Steadily adaptive, that was how her old master had described her, meaning she never fought the fire, only learned its temper and leaned. [175] So she leaned toward the canyon. [181]

The strip made no sense as accounting. [188] The numbers were too small for sacks and too regular for coin: a mark, a gap, three marks, a longer gap, and along the charred underside, pressed in with an ember by a man dying of smoke, four words she could not stop reading -- ALL OF IT YOURS. [236] A scorched testament, the magistrate's clerk called it, and refused to enter it as evidence of anything. [253]

So Mira went to retrace the route herself. [261] She walked it the way he had, at night, because charcoal men move under stars and the canyon is quiet then, quiet in the particular way of a struck bell three rooms away. [294] The walls held a drifting resonance, her boots coming back to her a half second late, sand hissing down the slickrock like a slow exhale. [319] She counted her steps against the marks and found the strip was not a map of distances. [336] It was a map of pauses. [342] Each gap was a place Alder had set down his load and waited -- for a patrol, for moonset, for nothing at all. [364] Nine gaps, nine rests, and she had never once asked him what he did in them. [380]

The finding came with the frost. [386] At first light the canyon floor furred over white, and where the ground was hollow the frost came up thin and ringed, because buried air breathes warmer than buried stone. [416] Mira had spent twenty years reading annealing colors; patterns in morning frost were only another kind of temperature made visible. [436] At the third pause she knelt in a bare gray ring and dug up a crate. [452] Inside, packed in wool, were eleven glass panels. [460] Her glass -- her cullet, her failures, the flawed sheets she threw out the studio's back door for years. [478] He had gathered them, and in the pauses, by candle, with a nail, he had scratched his accounts into them. [498] Dates, names, sums, and the excise officer who took a cut of every sack and later wanted her furnace cold and her windows gone. [522]

Scratched glass holds nothing to the eye by day. [531] But hers was seeded with river ash and salt, and at midnight the panels came up blue, and every line he had cut stood black and unarguable inside the light. [561] She sat with them until dawn under a sky so thick with stars it seemed to hum. [578]

She had gone into the canyon to name the men who burned her, and that would happen; the clerk could refuse bark, but not eleven panels of glowing testimony. [607] But what she carried out was smaller and heavier: proof that the pauses were the work. [623] All her life she had believed the making happened in the blowing, the gather, the roaring mouth of the furnace, and that the waiting between was dead time to be endured. [654] Glass knows better; glass is only saved in the slow cooling, and a piece rushed out of the annealer will crack for no reason anyone can see. [681] Alder had understood, sitting nine times a night in the dark with a nail, needing to discover purpose in the pauses and finding it: her name, her furnace, everything he could not say aloud, filed patiently into her own discarded glass. [722]

She kept the ledger strip in her collar after that, ash-black and unreadable, the shorthand of a man's silences. [741] And when she built the new furnace, she built the annealer first, twice as long as it needed to be. [761] Let the cooling take its time. [767]
