In the year the Guild finished teaching machines to imagine, Grell Ammidon was still the worst mage in the northern registry. [21] He had failed the fire trials twice, slept through weather-binding, and mastered exactly two workings in forty years. [39] The first was mist walking, a porter's trick for crossing cloud on borrowed footing. [53] The second was lingering luminance: light that stayed a while after its source was gone, the way a kitchen holds warmth. [74] Neither had ever earned him a commission. [81] Now nothing earned anyone commissions. [86] The Ideation Engine, housed in an icebound spire above the clouds, wrote nine thousand original spells a day and named them all better than a human would have. [114] So when the summons came, Grell assumed it was a clerical error. [126] He strapped his grandmother's garden folly keystone to his back anyway, because he never traveled without it, and stepped off the cliff at Harrow Ferry into the mist. [154]

Mist walking is mostly patience. [159] You persuade the fog that your weight is a rumor, and you do not look down for nine hours. [178] The spire rose out of the cloudfloor like a nail of glass, sheathed in ice that never melted because the Engine's heat went entirely into thinking. [204]

Warden Isaura met him at the ninth landing, holding a lamp she clearly did not need. [220] The whole tower blazed, and yet the light landed on nothing; it was brilliant dimness, a glare with no glow in it. [242] "You're the one who does the leftover light," she said. [252] "It's a novice charm," Grell said. [258] "It's the only working the Engine cannot copy." [266]

She led him up through halls of humming brass. [275] The Engine could compose a hymn for a language not yet invented, she explained, but it measured light as arrival and departure, and it did not believe in the part that stays. [307] "Prove it exists," she said, "and the Guild keeps one room where people still make things." [323]

The summit chamber had a broken arch at its center, an old ceremonial thing the builders had never finished. [342] Its crown was empty. [346] Grell laughed, which he had not done in some years. [356]

He knelt, worked the buckles loose, and began to unstrap the keystone from his back. [371] It was gray, mossy, entirely unmagical to look at, cut for a folly: one of those garden arches that hold up nothing at all. [395] That was the secret his grandmother had told him while shelling peas. [407] A stone that has never been asked to bear a load accommodates impossibilities; it has no idea what is too heavy. [428] She had grown beans on that arch, and every summer evening the sun went down behind it and left the mortar glowing for an hour after. [454] Grell had spent forty years trying to say why that mattered, and failing every examination that asked for something grander. [474] He had only ever wanted to find the sacred in the simple, and the Guild had graded that as a lack of ambition. [497]

He set the keystone into the arch's crown. [505] It fit the way an answer fits. [512] Then he asked Isaura to douse her lamp, and he worked his one small charm on it. [529] The flame died. [532] The dark came in, and inside the dark the arch held a soft amber lingering, exactly the color of a kitchen at the end of a day. [559] Not bright. [561] It was the first thing in that tower anyone had been able to see by. [576]

Below them, the Engine's brass fell quiet, listening for a value it could not take. [591] It could generate a thousand better lights before breakfast. [600] It could not generate an hour of a dead woman's summer. [611]

Isaura sat down on the cold floor and stayed there a long while. [624] "How long does it last?" she asked. [631] "About an hour," Grell said. [636] "Then someone has to come and do it again." [645] That was the argument, and it won. [652]

They kept the summit room unautomated, and the registry's worst mage was given a title he thought was a joke until he understood it: Keeper of the Lingering. [680] Once an hour he relit nothing at all, and the arch that held nothing held it. [696] Apprentices began mist walking up to watch. [703] They came for the trick and stayed for the hour, and some of them went home afterward and planted beans. [723]
