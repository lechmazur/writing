The grotto sat above the clouds, and its shadows never quite left, which was why the old maps called it umbral and the vintners below called it nothing at all. [30] Mira had been its caretaker for forty-one years. [38] The work was simple and endless: gather the souls that drifted up from the world like ash going the wrong way, wrap each one in a vine leaf, scallop the leaf's edge with her thumbnail so the wind could catch the notches, and let it fall back down through the cloud floor to be born again. [94] An unscalloped leaf dropped straight and dumb and came back up empty; a scalloped one spun, and spinning was how a soul learned which life it wanted. [121] She had never told anyone this, because there was no one up here to tell. [136]

Last autumn a soul had come up that she knew. [146] Souls arrive faceless, but they carry the one object they could not put down, and this one carried an antique hairpin of blackened silver, shaped like a wren mid-landing. [175] It had been her mother's, then hers, then her daughter Sena's, and it had been in Sena's hair on the night Adner of the low terraces had come drunk into the pressing shed with a cooper's mallet. [212] He had kept the hairpin for thirty years and had not been able to put it down, and so it came up the sky with him. [238] Mira had held that soul a long while in the cold dark of the grotto. [253] Then she had wrapped it, scalloped it carefully, more carefully than most, and let it go. [269] She kept the hairpin. [273]

People say forgiveness is a door, and beyond it is peace. [284] What Mira found beyond it was a plain room with the furniture taken out. [298] For a year afterward she went about her work in a hush like the hush of a vineyard at the hour before harvest, when the pickers have not yet come and every vine is holding still. [334] She had given away the worst thing she owned and expected to feel light; instead she felt like a jug waiting. [355]

So she set herself a task, the way practical women do: she would find the source of contentment, locate it as one locates a spring, and drink. [382] She thought best in trees. [387] There was one cedar at the grotto's mouth, ancient and lightning-split, and she climbed it most evenings, because thinking on the ground went in circles while thinking twenty feet up went in a line. [421] From the high branches she could see the cloud floor going gold, and the souls coming up through it in slow bright numbers. [444] She examined every contented person she had ever known and found no common ingredient. [458]

On the ninetieth evening, while she was still up in the cedar, someone came up the goat path. [476] Nobody came up the goat path. [481] It was a girl of nineteen or so from the terraces, wind-burned, seven months pregnant, carrying a basket of grapes as an offering to whatever she thought lived here. [510] "My grandmother said a woman up here mends things," the girl said to the empty grotto. [526] "I have nothing to give the child but debts and a father who ran." [540]

Mira came down out of the cedar the way she had come down out of it for forty years, and the girl did not scream, which said something for her. [570] Mira looked at her for the length of three breaths and then did what she had always done best, which was decide, kindly and at once. [596] She took the wren hairpin from her collar and pinned it into the girl's hair. [611] "It is silver, it is old, and it is yours," she said. [623] "Sell it if you must." [628] "Don't tell me which." [632]

The girl went down the path in the last light, and Mira stood at the grotto mouth with her hands empty and felt, without warning, the jug fill. [660] There was no spring to find. [666] Contentment had no source; it had a direction, the way the leaves had one, and it only ran when something was handed downward and let go. [692] She had forgiven Adner and felt nothing because forgiveness is only the door; the giving is the room. [710]

That night the souls came up thick, and she wrapped and scalloped and dropped them one by one into the gold, and somewhere below, a girl walked home through the vines with a wren in her hair, carrying a soul that would need a name. [755] Mira climbed the cedar to watch her go. [763] It was enough; it kept being enough. [770]
