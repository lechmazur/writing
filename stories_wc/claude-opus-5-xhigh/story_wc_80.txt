The drawbridge went up at seven, and for eleven minutes the sluices below Ashe Basin closed, and the water over the drowned village dropped a hand's width. [27] Mira Vance had built her whole summer around those eleven minutes. [38] She was the shore surveyor for Hollend and Dey, and the firm paid her to be exact, unsentimental, and quick. [58] She was exact. [61] The rest she kept where the firm could not see it, banked and burning, the way a stove keeps a fire behind iron. [84] Her orders were simple: certify the pavilion as debris so the dredgers could take it out in March. [102] The pavilion stood in nine feet of green water at the old village common, an octagon of limestone piers with the roof long gone, its dance floor silted to the ankles of its columns. [136] When the level dropped, the tops of the piers broke the surface like knuckles, and the last of the sun lay on them, a hushed blaze that made no sound at all. [168] She rowed out alone, as always, with her level rod, her slate, and her father's antique traveling inkwell in her coat pocket. [190] It was a brass cube the size of a plum, hinged, with a screw cap and a felt sleeve, made for a surveyor who expected to write in weather. [219] Her father had carried it on the dam works in 1961, and then he had gone into the water below the spillway and not come out, and the inkwell came home in a canvas bag without him. [256] That was the first absence, and she had learned to carry it the way she carried the inkwell: closed, capped, always in the pocket. [280] The masons of the basin had a custom she had chased for years through parish records and pension rolls: whenever they patched a foundation, they sealed a note inside the repair. [311] Not blessings. [313] Accounts. [314] What they had hoped to build and had not. [323] That was the work Mira wanted, more than grade stakes and cross-sections: to become the archeologist of abandoned dreams, the one who kept the ledger of things people had meant to make. [355] On the ninth evening, with the drawbridge up and the water at its lowest, she found the patch. [373] The northeast pier had been repaired twice, in two different mortars, one grey and coarse, one pale and fine, laced together like fingers. [396] She had to wrest the loose block free with her rod, bracing her boot against the pier, and it came away with a sound like a jar unsealing. [424] Inside were two lead tubes. [429] The first held a note from 1908, signed E. Hault, mason: he had meant the pavilion to be the porch of a dance hall for the whole valley, and he wrote that his son had gone to sea in April and sent nothing since, and that he was laying the piers wide anyway, for a building he no longer believed in. [490] The second was her father's. [495] Same custom, fifty-three years later, in the ink she knew from her mother's recipe box: he had patched Hault's patch while the basin was drained for the dam, and he wrote that he was sorry to be drowning a man's unbuilt hall, and that he had a daughter who liked to measure things, and that if the water ever went down again he hoped somebody would come and read this. [565] Mira sat back on her heels in the boat and did not weep, because she never did. [582] Her hands shook so badly she had to set the tubes down on the thwart. [597] Two men had stood at this same stone with the same small hole in their lives, and neither had known the other, and the absences had grown together in the mortar like roots. [630] She unscrewed the traveling inkwell. [635] On the back of her survey slip she wrote, in a hand steadier than her hands: Pier NE, sound. Structure historic. Not debris. [658] Then, on a second slip, she wrote her own note: her name, the date, the two men's names, and one line that said she had come and read it and would keep the ledger from here. [694] She rolled all three notes into the tubes, wedged them back, and packed the block with fresh mortar from her repair kit, because a surveyor of the basin carried mortar the way she carried ink. [729] Downstream the drawbridge came down; the sluices opened; the water rose over the knuckles of the piers and put the blaze out. [751] Mira rowed back to file the report that would cost her the job and start the work. [768]
