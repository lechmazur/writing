The flood took the Teatro San Ferro on a Tuesday, and by Friday Mira was the only one who still came. [21] For eleven years she had played viola in the orchestra pit, that trench between the audience and the story, and she had learned to love in-between spaces. [48] Now the pit held four feet of green water, and the house electricians had strung kerosene lanterns from the box rails, so the whole ruined hall flickered like a held breath. [79] She waded in wearing her brother's fishing waders, hunting for her music stand. [92] What she found instead, floating in the horn players' corner, was an antique opera program. [107] It was dated 1911, printed for a premiere of Ansaldo's Le Voci Sommerse, an opera the Teatro had never staged and no library owned. [131] The water had gotten into it, and the water had done something strange. [144] Where the ink had bled through the pages, the stains had bloomed into images: a lantern, a stair, a hand with two fingers folded down, a prompter's box seen from below. [175] Mira had been lately inspired in a way she distrusted -- after nine dry years she had begun waking at four to write music again -- and so she did not tell herself the images were accidents. [210]

Old Ferri, the last stagehand, told her the rest while they bailed: Ansaldo had conducted from this pit and died in it, of a stroke, mid-rehearsal, with the chord unstruck and his baton still up. [245] The orchestra, Ferri said, had never played that chord, and the house had never quite stopped waiting for it. [264] So Mira stayed after the bailing crews left, amid the hush of unstruck chords, to unravel the code a ghost had left in ruined paper. [289]

The images were fingerings. [293] Two fingers folded meant a stopped string; the lantern's height marked position on the neck; the stair meant descend. [312] She played what the stains told her to play, standing thigh-deep, her viola tucked high and dry against her jaw. [332] The water carried the sound oddly, and something answered. [341] Not a voice. [344] A pressure in the air after each phrase, the way a rehearsal pianist leans in to say you're late without saying it. [366] It went on for three nights, a wordless conversation conducted in intervals and silences: she offered a phrase, the hall corrected her, she tried again. [391] The corrections built a melody, and the melody, she realized, spelled out a path in the pit floor: fourth board from the prompter's box, the one that had always buzzed under the cellos. [424]

On the fourth morning the restoration company arrived to pump the pit dry and pour a concrete slab over the underfloor. [445] Mira asked for an hour and was told the schedule did not have one. [459] So she disrupted them. [463] She dragged the intake hose out of the water, coiled it around a lantern hook eight feet up, and stood on the conductor's podium with her viola like a woman defending a bridge. [496] The foreman shouted for twenty minutes. [502] Ferri, God bless him, produced a crowbar and the union rulebook, and between the two of them they bought the hour. [523]

Under the fourth board, in a zinc box soldered shut against exactly this kind of water, lay the full score of Le Voci Sommerse in Ansaldo's hand, three hundred pages dry as a promise. [557] The last page ended mid-bar, on the chord he had never given the downbeat for. [572] Beneath it, in pencil: For whoever is still listening in the space between. [585]

Mira did not play the chord that morning; it was not hers to play, and anyway it needed sixty musicians. [605] She let the pumps run. [610]

Fourteen months later the Teatro reopened with the premiere, ninety-nine years late, and Mira sat where she had always sat, in the trench between the seats and the stage, waiting through the long rest before the end. [647] The program in the lobby case still showed its water stains, and no one but her read them as instructions. [667] When the conductor's arm came down and the chord finally sounded, the hall did something Mira had never heard a hall do: it stopped waiting. [692] In the pause afterward, before the applause broke, she felt the pressure in the air lean in one last time, close as a whisper, and she answered it the only way the two of them had ever spoken: she played nothing at all, perfectly in time. [738]
