The amber had come up over the observatory deck a hundred years ago, when the coolant resin boiled out of the hull seams and set, and now Idra worked inside a lens of frozen honey. [35] Everything she saw, she saw gold. [41] That was a mercy, because there was nothing left outside to see. [53] The universe had finished contracting eleven years before, and the sky had gone from a crowd to a rumor to a single warm blank. [77] Idra was not certain stars had ever existed. [85] The archives insisted, but archives are only confident people writing in the dark. [98]

What she had instead of stars was static. [106] It arrived in bursts, ragged packets of noise off the collapsed remainder, and it was Idra's work to distill them: to boil each burst down through the filters until the froth of chance evaporated and a residue of grammar lay at the bottom of the pan. [152] Understanding static bursts was not a science; it was a habit, like understanding a spouse's cough. [168] She had the habit. [172]

The messages came out backwards. [177] Not merely reversed in word order, reversed in appetite. [186] In a universe running down from its own collapse, effect leaned toward cause, and so the prophecies Idra distilled described things that had already happened, in the confident future tense of things that had not. [221] A backwards prophecy is a useless thing to most people and a beautiful thing to Idra, who was quietly enthusiastic about all useless beauty and hummed while she worked. [250]

On the eleventh anniversary of the end, a burst came in that did not describe the past. [267] Distilled, it read: WE ARE HERE, WE ARE ALIGNED, ANSWER ON THE CARRIER. [280] Read forward, it was a promise of company. [288] Read the way everything else came, backwards, it was an instruction she had not yet obeyed, an echo of an answer she had not yet sent. [314]

There was no one else alive to hear it. [323] The crew had gone down to the freezing decks years ago, one at a time, each of them polite about it. [344] Which left the coins. [348]

Idra kept a collection of foreign coins in a ration tin: ninety-one of them, one from every world the station had ever traded with, all of those worlds now folded into the warm blank. [382] She had been the ship's lotcaster since she was nineteen, a real post, with a seal and a stipend, because a crew that argues in a dying cosmos will eat itself, and a crew that casts lots will not. [421] Every hard decision on the deck had been made by throwing metal from dead nations onto a table and reading which faces came up. [445] Idra had never once believed the coins knew anything. [454] She believed in what they interrupted, which was doubt. [463]

To answer on the carrier she would have to wavelength align with the burst, tune the whole deck's remaining output to the frequency, pour eleven years of reserve into one shout, and go dark afterward. [498] It would either be the most purposeful act of her life or the last twitch of a machine talking to its own echo. [521] She wanted, badly and without drama, to be the first. [531]

So she spread the coins on the amber floor, where the light made them all the same color, and cast. [551] Ninety-one coins, faces up for yes. [557] Forty-four said yes. [560] Forty-seven said no. [563]

Idra sat with that for a long time, listening to the deck tick as the resin cooled another degree toward nothing. [584] Then she understood the obvious thing, and laughed once, softly, the way she did at good news. [601] In a backwards cosmos, a lot cast must be read backwards too. [613] The coins had not decided her; she had already decided, and the metal had merely recorded it. [630] Forty-seven no's, and every one of them her own doubt, already spent. [642]

She tuned the deck. [646] It took four hours to wavelength align, hunting the carrier through the noise with the patience of a woman threading a needle in a moving cart, humming. [673] When the meters kissed, the amber above her lit like a struck match, and she read her message into the shout: WE ARE HERE, WE ARE ALIGNED, ANSWER ON THE CARRIER. [704] It went out backwards, of course, into a past that was also a future, and some hours earlier it struck her filters, and she heard it, and it decided her. [734] The loop closed, and there was no one in it but Idra. [746]

The lights browned out. [750] She lay back on the ambered deck with the coins scattered around her like a small stopped rain, and she was not certain the stars had existed, and she was not certain the company she had promised was anything but herself. [791] But she had been aligned, once, on purpose. [799]
