Nerea painted mirages, and a mirage must be finished before the light changes. [13] That was why the city called her elusive; she arrived where rain had just stopped, worked while the puddles held the sky, and was gone before anyone could ask her name. [44] Tonight she came to the echoing terrace above the Hall of Reflections, where spring had loosened the plum trees and the prism parapet, still beaded from the afternoon storm, threw ladders of color along the stone. [80] She had one hour: the hush before the evening bells, when the city held its breath and glass, briefly, could be taught. [102] Wren was waiting at the parapet, nine years old, holding the tin whistle Nerea had given her a year ago. [122] "They all learned it," Wren said. [128] "Every yard in the low town." [134] A year of work, that whistle: Nerea had smuggled her instructions into the city the only way glass would accept them, through coded lullabies in children's songs. [161] Mirrors do not read and cannot be argued with, but they are patient listeners, and what is sung near them often enough they eventually believe. [186] Three doors, four doors, who knows what the morning wore - nonsense to a mother rocking a cradle, a grammar of branching to the polished silver on the wall. [214] Tomorrow the Hall's great mirror would be turned on Tobin, the baker's boy who had taken a purse in winter, and it would show the magistrates what it always showed: one life, fixed, a thief at twelve and a thief at fifty. [256] Then the salt works, which nobody left. [263] Nerea did not intend to break the mirror or the law. [274] She intended to persuade the mirrors to show possibilities - to make them capable of the one thing the city had forgotten how to grant, which was grace. [301] "You're sure?" she asked, and Wren nodded, and this was the part Nerea hated. [315] A mirage holds only as long as a heart beats behind it, and Nerea's had stopped years ago, the price of learning to paint what was not there. [343] So she worked on a borrowed heartbeat, and tonight it was Wren's: the girl set her palm against Nerea's ribs and lent her the drum of it, freely, for the length of the hush. [377] When the bells rang, the loan ended; a borrowed heartbeat kept past the bells becomes a stolen one, and Nerea had promised herself she would never be that. [405] She began. [407] She painted with rainwater off the prism parapet, dragging light across the terrace air in long dusky chords - amber, then the violet under it, then the green that lives at the bottom of wet stone. [442] Below, in the low town, the lullabies started, yard after yard, the code she had planted coming back to her in harmony, and the terrace caught the voices and threw them at the Hall's high windows. [478] Nerea, who was perceptively calm in the way of people who have watched their own hands from a distance, listened for the moment the glass began to agree. [506] It came as a softening, a sound like frost giving up a window. [519] Into that softness she painted Tobin: not innocent, not condemned, but four boys at once - one at the salt pans, one at an oven, one at a ship's rail, one grown grey and ordinary with flour on his sleeves. [558] The great mirror below took the shape and held it, learning, the way a child learns a tune before it learns the words. [581] Wren's heart labored in Nerea's chest, faster, thinner. [589] The mirage wanted more; mirages always do. [596] She could have held it another hour and taught every mirror in the city, and the cost would have been small and quiet and named Wren. [622] The first bell struck. [626] Nerea lifted her hand off the light and gave the heartbeat back, an act of grace no magistrate would record, since Wren was the only one watching. [653] The mirage went out like a held note released. [661] In the morning the great mirror showed the magistrates four Tobins, and they argued until noon, and then, having no precedent for a boy who was several things at once, they sent him back to the ovens. [698] Nerea was three towns away by then, waiting for rain. [708] But in the low town the children kept singing the lullaby, because children keep what they are given, and every year the mirrors of that city grew a little worse at certainty and a little better at doors. [746] Wren, grown, taught it to her own daughter, and kept her heartbeat for herself, and considered that fair. [764]
