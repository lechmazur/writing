The gate rusted shut in the ninth year of drought, and Ashaya stopped being a city with two halves. [19] The market shell drifted east on its own turtle until the bridge chains hung straight down into the sea, and the boatyards went with it. [44] Mira kept working. [47] She was the last spiral-shell cartographer in the upper wards, and hers was a patient trade: she carved the coastlines of a moving world into the whorls of great sea snails, so that the map turned as the water turned. [87] Nobody bought maps anymore. [91] The turtles that carried Ashaya had gone slow and dreamy, the light on the tiled roofs was the color of bleached bone, and the city had agreed without any vote to stop looking at the horizon. [127] Mira looked. [129]

Out there stood the silent watchers, nine turtles taller than Ashaya's, motionless for two generations, their backs crusted with the white ruins of cities that no longer sent up smoke. [159] Four years ago her sister Ines had taken eleven boats and a year of salted fish out to question them, because someone had to ask why they had stopped. [188] Ines had gone with her reed whistle at her lip; Mira still wore the twin of it, cut from the same reed, on a cord at her throat. [216] The expedition did not come back, and the council said the sea had eaten it, and moved on to the water rationing. [238]

Mira did not argue. [242] She was quietly driven, which is slower and far more dangerous than loud grief. [256] Every dawn she climbed down the ladders to the shell rim, where the turtle's scutes met the water, and copied their growth-lines onto paper. [280] Shell grows the way speech does, in repetitions with small deliberate errors. [292] The old pilots had always steered through pattern languages -- three notes and a pause for turn, a rising trill for sound the depth, a doubled note for the interrogative -- and a turtle answered in the ridges it laid down that season. [333] Mira could read a year of weather in a hand's width of horn, and she was looking for something that was not weather. [356]

Three months after the gate rusted shut, she found the crack. [367] It ran a finger deep along the eastern rim scute, edges still pale, and inside it lay four years of buried growth that the sun had never touched. [395] The ridges were pilots' grammar. [400] Not weather, not tide: sentences, laid down season after season by a turtle that had heard something through the water and remembered it in horn. [425] Turtles hear one another across a hundred leagues; the sea carries a groan better than air carries a shout. [444] Somewhere out there, for four years, someone had been whistling the same message into a watcher's ear, and the watcher had passed it along the deep, and Ashaya's turtle had written it down in the only ink it owned. [483]

It took Mira eleven days to read it, kneeling in noon light so white it felt like a held breath. [503] Six words, in the steering grammar, in Ines's stubborn rhythm: they are waiting for an answer. [519]

She carried it to the council house and they heard her out under the fans. [534] Then the harbormaster reminded her that the boatyards had drifted east with the market shell, that the gate was a red scab of iron no hammer would open, and that a city on a dying turtle had no business asking the horizon questions. [577] Nobody knew what the question had been, he said, and that was mercy. [590]

Mira agreed with him, thanked him, and went down the ladders at dusk with the reed whistle warm in her fist. [611] She did not have an answer; she had never had one. [622] What she had was the doubled note, the interrogative, the oldest thing in the pilots' language, and she set her lips to the reed and asked the silent watchers what they were waiting to hear. [657] The sound was thin, ridiculous, a reed against nine miles of red water. [670] She whistled it until the sun went down and her mouth tasted of blood. [684]

Ashaya's turtle answered first: a groan that came up through the shell into her teeth, into the ladders, into the water jars in ten thousand kitchens. [710] The city leaned. [713] The eastern rim scute cracked wider, a sound like a door opening. [725] Roof tiles slid; somewhere a bell fell and rang once on its way down. [739] Out on the horizon, for the first time in two generations, one of the watchers lifted its head. [757] And across the water, faint and doubled and stubbornly in rhythm, a reed whistle answered her. [773]
