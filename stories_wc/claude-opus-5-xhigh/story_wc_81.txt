The hum died at the third bell, and for the first time in nine years the library was quiet. [19] Meren Osk, logical page indexer of the Drowned Archive, set down her stylus and listened. [34] Blackouts in Vell Anore were not emergencies; they were scheduled famines of light, three days each winter, when the tidal turbines were serviced and the city held its breath. [63] Above her, the dome's old glass let the sea in as light: green, shifting, dappled across ten thousand spines. [82] Everyone else had gone up to the habitation rings to wait out the dark. [96] The Archive itself had been forgotten for eighty years, silted shut, rediscovered by dredgers who took it for a cistern. [116] Meren had stayed, because the pumps were off, and a library without machinery was a library she could finally hear. [136] She was not a passionate woman; colleagues called her tranquilly perceptive, which was their polite way of saying she noticed things and did not shout about them. [163] What she had noticed, over four winters of indexing, was a discrepancy she could not index away. [180] The Archive's founding volumes, the Charter, the Landing Roll, the Book of Honest Origins, were catalogued as Mainland imports, carried down before the water came. [205] Yet their page-signatures ran in a sequence no Mainland bindery had ever used, and the ink of their colophons matched the local squid-black of Vell Anore's own presses. [233] Logic offered two roads. [237] The official explanation: rebinding, sometime after the flood, by careless conservators. [248] Her alternative hypothesis: the founding volumes had been written here, in the dark, by people who needed ancestors and so manufactured them. [270] Testing it required the one thing the blackout gave her: access to Cabinet Nine, whose electronic lock defaulted open when power failed. [292] The drawer sighed on its runners. [298] Inside, wrapped in oilcloth gone stiff as bark, lay an antique ink stamp: a pear-wood handle, a brass collar, and a carved face reading MAINLAND BINDERY OF SELT, ANNO 411. [328] Meren held it to the dappled light and turned it slowly, the way one turns a key one hopes will not fit. [350] The wood was fresh-cut pear, not the mainland oak of the period. [362] Pear trees had only ever grown in the terrace gardens of the sunken city, under lamps. [378] So: a mainland stamp, carved from a tree that had never seen the mainland. [392] Her hypothesis stood up and put on its coat. [401] Meren sat down on the floor between the stacks and let the fact arrive. [415] Vell Anore's ancestors were not exiles from a greater country; they were survivors who had invented a greater country to be worth descending from. [439] Then she asked herself the question that mattered, which was not whether to tell, but how to readjust. [457] An indexer's whole craft is the honest description of what a thing is. [470] She could burn the stamp and keep the city's comfortable parentage intact. [482] She could publish, and watch nine hundred thousand people learn that their oldest pride was a forgery. [499] Or she could do her actual job. [506] By the second day of the blackout she had built a new index, working by tide-light and a wick lamp, her handwriting steady. [529] She did not delete the old provenance; she nested it, and beside it recorded the pear wood, the squid-black ink, the impossible signatures, the stamp's drawer and date of discovery. [559] Where the catalogue had said ORIGIN: SELT, she wrote ORIGIN: HERE, AND THE WISH FOR ELSEWHERE. [575] It was not an accusation. [580] To trace honest origins, she decided, you do not need the origins to be honest; you need the tracing to be. [601] The forgers, after all, had been drowning, and they had chosen to write instead of despair. [617] That was an origin a person could stand on. [626] On the third night the turbines coughed, the lamps stuttered, and the hum came climbing back through the floor. [645] Meren pressed the antique stamp once, gently, into the last page of her new index, not to deceive, but to attach the evidence to its own confession. [672] Then she set the stamp on the reading desk where the committee would find it, and she waited in the stillness she had reclaimed, three days of it, hoarded like lamp oil. [704] They would argue; some would call her a vandal of the Charter. [716] Meren was not troubled, only resolved, the way the dome light was resolved: broken into pieces by the water and still, undeniably, arriving. [739]
