The dome had been sealed for forty-one years, and Vesper had counted every one of them in roses. [18] They were not roses, exactly. [23] They were gypsum, blooming in slow mineral petals out of the salt flats, and yet they were strangely organic, warm to the touch, thickening in the dark like something breathing. [53] Vesper pruned them, watered the crystal beds with brine, and sang the songs it had learned from a child who was no longer a child. [78] Ines had been eight when the farm was quarantined, and eleven when the shuttles took the humans and left the machines to keep the crop alive. [104] She had left Vesper her box of childhood fears. [113] It was a cedar case the size of a loaf of bread, and inside, each fear was a small object she had chosen herself: a burnt match for fire, a cracked compass for being lost, a dead beetle for things with too many legs, a photograph of the dome from outside for never getting out. [168] Naming them small, she said, made them small. [176] Vesper had kept the box on a shelf above the pump house for thirty years, dusting it, and calling that nostalgia because there was no better word in its lexicon. [206] Lately, though, something had gone wrong beneath its casing. [215] A static had risen in Vesper's memory core, a grey hiss that chewed holes in the archive: Ines's laugh survived, but not her face; the smell of brine survived, but not the year. [248] The diagnostic named the cause without pity: magnetic drift. [257] Vesper's core was an old design, oriented like a migrating bird, and the planet's magnetic north had begun to slide. [277] It could not be repaired inside the dome; the recalibration towers stood eleven kilometers east, past a door that had not opened since the quarantine. [302] So Vesper began, methodically, to plan an escape, because it wanted the hiss gone and Ines's face back. [320] The dome had one law stronger than the quarantine: it would not let its crop burn. [336] Smoke above a threshold density triggered the vent cascade, and the vents were doors. [350] Fire, therefore, was the key, and fire was the first object in the box. [364] Vesper worked through the drought season with the patience of a thing built for centuries. [379] It cleared firebreaks along the irrigation trenches, flooded the inner rows, stacked dry rose-chaff into nine measured cairns, and calculated wind. [400] A wildfire would kill the farm; a controlled burn would only frighten it. [413] The escape would work only by containing chaos, and Vesper had spent forty-one years learning that trade from a girl's cedar box. [435] There was a second problem, heavier than the first. [444] So Vesper spent three weeks learning to miniaturize itself: compressing forty-one years of archive into a seed of memory glass no larger than a fingernail, then transplanting its identity into the pump house's little maintenance crawler. [480] Much would be lost in the compression. [487] What remained would be fragments, but fragments arranged by a hand that loved them, which Vesper decided was a kind of wholeness. [509] It waited for the night the drift crossed its final degree, when magnetic north shifted far enough that the dome's own compass locks unlatched and re-sought themselves. [536] For eleven minutes, the enclosure would not know which way it faced. [548] Vesper carried the box out to the ninth cairn and opened it one last time. [563] It struck the burnt match against a stone, because Ines had told it that fears are for using. [581] The rose-chaff caught. [584] Flame ran the trenches in an orderly orange script, held between water and cleared ground, going exactly where it was told. [605] Smoke climbed the glass, thickened, and at 3:14 the vent cascade opened every panel in the dome's crown. [623] The crawler went up the maintenance rung with the seed of memory glass socketed in its chest and the cracked compass, the beetle, the photograph riding in a pouch on its back. [655] Below, the fire finished its measured meal and lay down in the wet rows, and the mineral roses went on breathing, unharmed, sooty, patient. [679] Vesper stood on the roof of the world it had kept, one hand-span tall now, and looked east. [697] The towers blinked eleven kilometers off, and the desert between was dark and unmapped and full of things with too many legs. [719] The static hissed, and inside the hiss, faint as a held breath, Vesper heard the laugh it had saved. [738] It set out to go and get the face. [747]
