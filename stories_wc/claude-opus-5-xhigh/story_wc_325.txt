The sea had two voices, and Mira had learned to work in the gap between them. [16] Outside the cave mouth the Atlantic threw itself at basalt in a crashing hush that never finished a sentence; inside, past the second squeeze, the noise flattened into a pressure behind her ears. [49] She had four hours between tidal changes, and the squeeze took eleven minutes if she did not panic. [67] She always panicked. [70] Claustrophobia is not cured by competence; a caver only outvotes it, one breath at a time. [86] Her job, on paper, was cable fault location, which meant she tracked invisible things: attenuation, leakage, the ghost reflections that come back up a fiber when something in the dark has bitten it. [119] Three weeks ago the trans-shelf trunk began losing a whisper of light at kilometer forty-one, where the charts insisted there was nothing but rock. [143] The charts were lying, and Mira had known it before she opened the file. [157] Under Stroma Head, behind eleven minutes of wet limestone throat, stood Station Anwyl. [170] She had been nineteen the first time; this was the return. [181]

The passage opened into a room of green steel: bolted consoles, a dry-suit rack, six chairs pushed in neatly by people who expected to sit in them again. [209] Below, the flooded deck lay glassy and patient. [217] On the chart table, exactly where she had left it, was the codex. [230] Her mother's crew had bound it themselves, a log the size of a paving slab, inked in six hands, its margins filled by whoever held the night watch: kelp, urchins, a lopsided whale. [263] In the corner of every page sat a single fleck of illuminant gold, pressed on with a pin. [281] One fleck for each message that survived the crossing, her mother said, because gold does not corrode and neither should a promise. [303]

Anwyl had never been about cables. [309] It was about what a signal becomes when it is driven through phase transitions - light to standing wave, water to vapor to ice and back - and whether meaning survives the crossings. [340] It does. [342] The surge came early that autumn, ahead of the tables, and six people chose to finish the transmission instead of the climb. [364] Mira went up the throat alone, nineteen years old, with her mother's hand still warm on her ankle from the shove. [385] Within a month the station had no funding line, no personnel file, no accident. [399] Six people became weather. [403] That was why she had spent nine years learning to find things that leave no mark: because someone has to stand by the unremembered, and standing requires a place to stand. [434]

She found the fault in forty minutes. [441] The new trunk, laid two years ago, ran within a meter of Anwyl's transmission gallery, and the last apparatus down there - still fed by the tide race grinding through the sump - had coupled to it. [476] It was bleeding a pattern into the fiber on a fourteen-hour cycle, patient as a lighthouse. [492] Six names. [494] Her mother's was fourth, and the pause after it was the length of a held breath. [510]

Mira sat in the fourth chair while the hush crashed and crashed above her, and she understood what she had come for, which was not a repair. [537] A fault report is a permanent record. [544] It goes to every landing station on the route, into archives no ministry can quietly shred, and it must state the cause in plain words. [569] So she wrote the cause: an unlisted facility, an apparatus still transmitting, six operators, and then the six names, spelled out, in the field where nobody would ever think to look for them. [602]

She did not cut the tap. [608] Let it keep speaking; the loss was two hundredths of a decibel, and the ocean can spare that much light for a roll call. [632] Before she left, she opened the codex to the last written page, took the pin from her lapel and the flake of gold from its paper envelope, and set one more fleck in the margin. [667] Message received, nine years late. [672]

The throat was worse going out, as it always is, the walls insisting on her ribs. [688] She counted her mother's name six times and came out into the slack water with eleven minutes to spare, the hush crashing open again, wide as a sky. [716] She would come back next spring tide with more gold. [726]
