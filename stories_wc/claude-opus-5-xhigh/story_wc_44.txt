The observatory on the floating isle of Kell was nocturnal by law, because after spoons bent reality, daylight could not be trusted to stay where it was put. [28] Yeva, a budding rune scholar with three months of study and one dead teacher, climbed the brass stair at dusk as she always did. [52] She was quietly deliberate in everything, which her teacher had called her only talent and her best one. [70] In her pocket she carried the clockwork automaton key: a stub of grey iron with nine teeth, warm even in winter. [91] Nine teeth, nine sleepers. [95] Beneath the observatory floor, in a vault of chalked circles, stood nine automatons frozen halfway out of sleep - hands lifted, mouths open, eyes half-lit. [119] Her teacher had named them sealed awakenings and forbidden her to touch them, and then he had died on a Tuesday, leaving her the key. [144] The Bending had ruined her craft before she learned it. [154] When the spoons went soft in every kitchen from the mainland to the drifting isles, curving space the way they had always curved light, every closed figure in the world came loose at its joins. [189] Runes failed. [191] Wards leaked. [193] Bridges forgot to be bridges. [198] So she had to relearn everything her teacher taught, from the first stroke, by closing loops. [214] That was the new law of the world: a rune held only if its line returned exactly to the point where it began, with no gap wider than a hair. [244] She practiced for a year on the observatory slate, thousands of loops, until her hand could come home in the dark. [265] She was not practicing to repair fences. [272] She was practicing to find where shadows go at noon. [282] On Kell's Isle, at the stroke of twelve, every shadow left. [293] Not shortened, not thinned - gone, wholly, so that the towers stood in light like objects painted on glass. [311] Her teacher had shuttered the observatory against noon and never explained. [322] The night she finally drew a perfect loop, she went down to the vault and fitted the clockwork automaton key into the first sleeper's back. [347] It did not wind. [351] The seal on its chest was an unclosed rune; her teacher had left the last hair of the line undrawn, on purpose, nine times. [375] She closed the first loop with chalk, and turned the key. [386] The automaton finished the gesture it had begun before she was born, drew a breath it did not need, and cast a shadow across the vault floor - a long human shadow, moving on its own. [421] That was where the shadows went. [427] The isle floated because someone had taken its weight and locked it inside nine bodies; at noon, when the sun stood directly overhead, the shadows of everything on it were drawn down into the sleepers, and the isle, unburdened, stayed up. [468] The awakened one spoke in her teacher's cadence, because he had built it: wake all nine, and the weight comes back. [489] The isle would come down. [494] Slowly, it said, if she closed the loops in order and let each shadow settle before the next; all at once, and Kell's Isle would fall like a dropped plate. [524] She thought about it for eleven nights, which was her way. [535] The isle was beautiful and it was also a debt, held aloft by nine interrupted lives and a village that had never wondered why its noon was empty. [563] She warned the village first, in writing, and gave them a season to move their goats and grandmothers to the low meadows. [585] Then she woke them one at a time, a month apart, closing each loop with the same patient hand she had spent a year training. [610] The isle sank through the clouds by degrees, and the automatons taught themselves to walk on ground that no longer drifted. [631] On the last day, the ninth loop closed under her thumb, and Kell's Isle came to rest in a shallow bay with a sound like a spoon set down on a table. [663] At noon the next day she opened the observatory shutters for the first time in its history. [680] Light came in, and behind every chair and every automaton and behind her own quiet, deliberate body, a shadow lay down on the floor and stayed. [706] She wrote it in the log, closed the loop of the final letter, and went out to see the isle's shadow on the water. [730]
