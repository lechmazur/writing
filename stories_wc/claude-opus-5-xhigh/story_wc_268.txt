The last titan in the world stood three feet tall and swept her own floors. [15] Ombra had been larger once, in the age when temples were built to fit her kind, but the rune of drift had taken her measure along with the library's. [44] The rune was cut into the threshold stone, a single spiral that let the temple slip sideways out of the years, and it had kept the Concordance of Blood safe for nine centuries while empires burned above. [81] It had also kept Ombra. [86] Visitors came only across the pause of shifting constellations, the held breath between one arrangement of stars and the next, when the drift loosened and lost roads found the temple door. [117]

The girl arrived on the third such pause Ombra had counted, mud to the knee, a court scribe's inkhorn empty at her belt. [140] Ombra did not greet her, because a titan's voice unmakes a traveler's memory of the road home, and the girl would need that road. [164] So she watched from the stacks, silent, and learned the girl's name from the flyleaf she signed: Ilya, once of the royal chancery. [187] Ilya read genealogies for eleven days and wept over one page in particular, and Ombra understood that she had come to find out whether the man wearing the crown had any right to it. [221]

Ombra knew the answer; she had shelved it herself, the winter the pretender's grandfather bribed a priest to insert a line of ancestry in ink that had no gall in it. [252] Lampblack fades brown in nine hundred years; iron gall bites the page and stays black forever. [268] That was the whole proof, and it could not be spoken, only shown. [281]

In the reliquary drawer Ombra kept one iron gall ink pellet, hard as a peppercorn, the last of the temple's stores. [302] She dissolved a shaving of it in rainwater and began to write in the margins of the books Ilya returned to each morning. [325] COMPARE THE BROWN LINE WITH THE BLACK ONES, said the first note, in a hand small and plain. [343] THE PRIEST WHO WROTE IT IS BURIED UNDER THE SEVENTH FLAGSTONE, said the second, three days later, in a book of drainage plans. [366] Ilya answered on the endpapers, and for a season they spoke only through cryptic messages left in library books, a conversation conducted at the speed of shelving. [393] WHO ARE YOU, Ilya wrote. [398] THE LIBRARIAN, Ombra wrote back, which was true, and enough. [408] Ilya guessed anyway, and left a bowl of her own supper on the reading desk without ceremony, as one leaves bread for a neighbor. [432] Ombra ate it, and was grateful, and swept the floor beneath the desk that night: regally unpretentious, the last of a race that had never needed thrones because it had never doubted itself. [465]

The hard note came last, written with the pellet's final grain. [476] NOTHING LEAVES A DRIFTING HOUSE, it said. [483] CARRY THAT PAGE ACROSS THE THRESHOLD AND NINE HUNDRED YEARS WILL CLAIM IT IN YOUR HANDS. [499] I MUST SUNDER THE RUNE FIRST. [505] THEN THE TEMPLE WILL BE IN THE WORLD AGAIN, AND THE WORLD WILL COLLECT WHAT IT IS OWED. [523] Ilya wrote back one word, low on the page, pressed hard: NO. [535]

The constellations were already sliding when the diminutive titan took the mason's chisel from the tool press and knelt at the threshold stone. [558] She struck the spiral once and the spiral answered. [567] Time came in like weather. [572] It came gently, which she had not expected: dust settling rather than falling, the roof beams bowing like courtiers, the ten thousand scrolls going to powder in a long soft exhalation, shelf by patient shelf. [607] Ombra grew as she died, or seemed to; whatever the drift had folded away came back, and for one breath she filled the nave to its ceiling, huge and unhurried. [637] She had time to push Ilya out the door, and to push the Concordance out after her. [654]

On the step, Ilya opened the great book with shaking hands. [665] It had aged all at once and honestly, brittle and brown at the edges, and there, in the tenth generation, one line sat pale as tea beside forty lines still black as the night they were written. [702] Iron gall keeps its word. [707]

The crown came off a liar's head that autumn, in a hall full of shouting, and the historians credited the archivist Ilya, who never corrected them except once a year. [737] On the pause between constellations she would walk to the low green mound where a temple had been, and leave a bowl of supper, and a book with a blank margin, in case. [770] The margins stayed empty; she kept the habit anyway. [779]
