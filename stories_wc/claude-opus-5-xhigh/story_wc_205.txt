They called the asteroid Perdita Magna, and its builders had carved it baroque throughout: scrollwork corridors, gilded volutes, stone angels whose trumpets doubled as air vents. [26] Casimir Vry had helped storm it once, forty years ago, in the name of a republic that lasted nine months. [46] Now he swept its floors and catalogued its dead. [55] This was after time became visible, when the light of every hour settled as a fine bronze silt on windowsills, so that a room could be dated by the depth of its shining dust. [89] Archaeology had become simple arithmetic, which was precisely why the dispute cut so deep. [103] The object at the center of it lay on Vry's workbench: a shard of crystal vase, thumb-sized, its edge still bright enough to draw blood. [128] Doctor Ileana Sarn, sent from the Directorate with three assistants and no patience, said the shard proved the asteroid's first inhabitants had been a garrison culture: austere, militarized, drinking from cut glass looted off passing freighters. [164] Vry disagreed. [166] He had spent eleven years devising another reading, and he intended to reimagine an entire civilization out of that single flake of light. [189] In his monograph the vase was ceremonial, filled each solstice with water traded at ruinous cost, poured out for guests who owned nothing. [212] A generous people, he argued, a people who had built angels into their ventilation. [226] Sarn read his draft in one sitting and handed it back with a single comment: "You are poetically inclined, Citizen Vry, and that is a disease in our profession." [255] She was not wrong. [259] He wrote verses on the backs of inventory slips, and he wanted the dead of Perdita Magna to be worth descending from, because his own republic had left him nothing else to inherit. [292] The storms settled it. [296] Twice a year the asteroid crossed the wake of the old shipping lane, and the visible hours came loose, blowing through the corridors in slow bronze gusts. [323] Every caretaker learned the same rule on the first day: covering mirrors during storms is not superstition but hygiene. [342] Loose time pools in glass, and a mirror left bare will show you the room as it was, hour after hour, running backward until you cannot bear it. [370] Vry had draped the palace's forty-one mirrors every season for two decades, muttering his own lines as he worked. [389] That night, with the monograph due to the Directorate at dawn, he left one uncovered. [404] It was the tall pier glass in the Hall of Trumpets, where the shard had been found. [421] He wanted, just once, to see his generous people pour their water. [433] The storm came at the third watch and the glass began to unspool. [446] Centuries thinned away like breath off a window: Sarn's assistants, his own younger back, the long empty decades, the barricades. [466] And then the hall was full of lamps, and forty men in mismatched uniforms were laughing, and one of them was Casimir Vry at twenty-three, drunk on the ninth month of the republic. [499] He watched himself lift a crystal vase from the governor's cabinet, hold it up as a trophy, and drop it on the marble to make the men cheer. [527] The mirror held the moment a long while before letting it go. [539] In the morning Vry covered the pier glass, washed, and went to find Sarn in the refectory. [556] "The shard is forty years old," he said. [564] "It is mine." [567] "I broke it myself, for applause, in the ninth month." [577] Sarn set down her cup. [582] She did not gloat, which he found harder to forgive than gloating. [594] "Then we have no garrison and no gentle people," she said. [605] "We have an empty hall and a dust depth of nine hundred years." [618] "We have the angels in the vents," Vry said. [627] That spring they devised a joint report, careful, unbeautiful, and honest, that described what could be measured and named what could not. [649] Vry appended one page of verse, over Sarn's objection, and she let it stand because it was labeled clearly as invention. [670] It described a people who poured water out for strangers, and it ended by admitting that no such people had ever lived there, and that someone ought to. [698] He kept the shard in his pocket after that, the last relic of a defunct revolutionary and the only civilization he had actually made. [722] Its edges wore smooth against the cloth, the way things do, and each storm season he went out with his ladder and his sheets and covered the mirrors, gently, one by one, like closing the eyes of the dead.
