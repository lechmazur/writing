The Selene Lunar Observatory sat above the clouds, so that on most nights the world below was only a white sea and we were the only shore. [27] I came at nineteen to photograph the moon, and stayed for the woman who kept our cameras alive. [45] Her name was Ose Vantu, and the astronomers called her the sage, half in mockery, until their shutters failed. [64] She repaired cameras the way a physician sets bone: slowly, listening, refusing to hurry the thing she held. [82] In her workshop hung a broken sidereal clock whose second hand would not stop spinning, around and around, a silver blur that never rested. [106] Fix it, I said once. [111] "It is fixed," she answered. "It tells me the only true thing: that nothing holds still." [127]

That autumn, comet Ferran-Achebe came out of the dark, and the tables said it would pass once and never return; its orbit was open, its speed past escape velocity, a visitor with no second visit in it. [164] Every plate we exposed would be the only record humanity would ever have. [177] We were a lunar station; our patient work was mapping a face that shows the same side forever, which is why the comet unsettled us all. [203] That is what drew me to the mountain in the first place: to harness the ephemeral, to press onto glass a light that would otherwise be lost the instant it arrived. [234] Ose understood that hunger better than I did, and distrusted it more. [246]

The tremors began in the week before perihelion. [254] The mountain was old but not dead, and when foundations shake, an observatory learns what it truly is: a heavy glass eye balanced on a pier of stone. [282] A hairline crack ran up the pier of the great astrograph; the drive gears jumped their teeth; the tracking clock died with a sound like a swallowed cough. [310] Without the clock, the stars would smear across the plate, and the comet would leave us with nothing but blur. [330] The director wanted a replacement drive shipped up from the coast; it would arrive four days after the comet had gone. [351]

Ose took the housing apart on her bench and said we would restructure it: not repair, restructure, throw away the ruined governor and rebuild the drive around what still worked. [381] She scavenged the spinning hand's clock for its escapement, the mainspring from a field camera, a worm gear from the dome shutter. [403] For three days the workshop smelled of oil and cold brass. [414] The hard part was calibration, and here she did the thing the astronomers never believed. [429] Ose was going deaf to voices but not to small sounds. [440] She kept a dry poplar leaf from the valley in a tin, and she calibrated the new drive by the sound of a leaf falling: she would climb the ladder, release the leaf into the still air of the dome, and count the ticks of the escapement until the whisper of its landing on the floor. [496] The same leaf, the same height, the same windless dark: nine and a half seconds, changelessly constant, over and over, a metronome made of something dead and weightless. [524] "You want to trap the fleeting," she said, tightening a screw by feel. "So use the fleeting. It is the only honest ruler." [547]

On the night of the pass, the mountain shivered twice, small shrugs, and dust fell through the beam of my lamp like slow snow. [571] Ose sat on a stool with her palm flat against the rebuilt drive, feeling for wobble the way you feel a fever. [593] I opened the shutter for twenty-two minutes while the comet crossed Cygnus, a smear of green with a tail like breath on glass. [616] The stars came out of the developing bath as points, not streaks. [628] We had it, held it, the one and only image of a thing that would never come back. [646] I remember laughing, and then not laughing, because holding it made the losing of it real. [662]

Ose died in the spring, in the valley, among the poplars. [673] I keep her clock on my desk with its hand still spinning, wound each Sunday, and I keep the plate in a light-tight sleeve I open perhaps once a year. [703] The comet is out past the planets now, still climbing, changeless in its leaving. [717] Everyone I loved on that mountain has reached their own escape velocity, and the observatory itself is closed, its dome white above a white sea. [742] But when the floor of my house shakes with a passing truck, I drop a leaf in the hall and listen, and for nine and a half seconds nothing in the world is lost. [776]
