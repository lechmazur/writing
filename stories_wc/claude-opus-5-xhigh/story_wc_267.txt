The snow had been falling on the cobalt fortress for nine days, and for nine days no one had spoken. [20] Ivor carried the luggage anyway. [25] That was the work of a silent-choir bellhop: brass polished, boots dried, ledgers stamped, all of it seraphically mundane, a hymn sung with hands instead of throat. [52] The choir had no voices; it had gestures, and the fortress had rules. [65] Above the north wall hung a shelf of snow the size of a cathedral, and the mountain, everyone said, listened. [85] One shout could bring it down on two hundred refugees. [95] So they whispered; better still, they did not. [103]

Eleven nights ago, someone had knocked at the gate. [112] Ivor was the one who heard it, and he had run for the tower bell -- the one signal permitted, a single muffled stroke that meant a soul waits outside -- and found the clapper hanging loose, its pin snapped clean. [151] By the time he had hauled the gate wheel himself, bare-handed, in the dark, the knocking had stopped. [169] They found her at dawn, folded against the hinge, and they buried her in the snowdrift because the ground was iron. [191] That was the unpardonable silence, and Ivor had lived inside it since. [203]

His hands were healing now, badly. [209] The infirmarian called it nerve regeneration and said the burning meant the flesh was arguing its way back to feeling. [229] It arrived as sparks under the skin, as ants, as a slow silver wire drawn through each fingertip whenever he lifted a suitcase. [252] He welcomed it, because pain was a kind of question, and he had one: he wanted to understand the cost of safety, item by item, as if it could be ledgered. [283]

The truth came on the fourth day of thaw-hope, when a guest asked him to steady the piano bench in the frozen ballroom. [306] The bench wobbled because its left leg had lost a bolt: soft brass, square-headed, exactly the width of the pin he had carried in his pocket since the burial. [335] The pin in his pocket was not steel either. [344] Someone had pulled the bell's steel pin, set a piano bench bolt in its place, and trusted soft brass to shear at the first stroke -- sabotage dressed as ordinary wear. [374]

He found the climber the way a bellhop finds anything, via the slightest discoloration on a dusty spiral staircase: a blue-green smudge at the third turn, verdigris wiped from a glove that had gripped brass. [409] Only two people in the fortress handled brass daily: Ivor, and Steward Hallis, who kept the keys. [426]

Hallis was in the pantry, counting beans against days, which is the only honest way to count either. [444] Ivor set the sheared bolt on the shelf between them. [454] The choir's sign for why is a palm turned up and opened slowly, like a book, and Ivor made it twice. [475] Hallis did not pretend. [479] "The bell rings, the gate opens, the gate wheel screams," he whispered, so low it was mostly breath. [497] "Last winter it screamed, and the shelf came down on the east wing." [510] "Forty-one people." [512] "I could not vote on it every night, so I removed the vote." [525] "I made mercy impossible, and then nobody had to be guilty." [536]

Ivor's fingers burned; the silver wire drew itself through them, and he understood that this was the ledger he had asked for. [558] Safety cost one woman folded against a hinge, and it cost a man who would rather be a coward in private than a judge in the open. [585] He could carry the bolt to the prior, and Hallis would be put out into the snow, and the fortress would have its second unpardonable silence before spring. [613] Instead, he chose to remain. [618]

That night he set the steel pin back into the clapper and hammered it home with his ruined palm, and the pain sang. [641] He fixed the piano bench too, with an iron nail, because a bench that wobbles is a bench nobody sits on. [662] Then he chalked the watch-board in the block letters the choir uses for wordless orders: THE BELL WORKS. [680] WHOEVER HEARS A KNOCK MUST RING IT, AND WE WILL ANSWER TOGETHER. [692] Hallis read it and did not erase it, which Ivor decided to count as a hymn. [708]

Outside, the cobalt-glazed walls went on holding their tons of snow, and the mountain went on listening, and somewhere under the drift a woman waited for the ground to soften. [738] Ivor stood in the cold with his hands full of needles, feeling everything, and waited for the next knock. [757]
