The path above the clouds smelled of cold sugar, and Ilse climbed it with her daughter's birthday tablecloth knotted around her shoulders like a cape. [25] Nine years of ink hands lay against her back. [34] That was the custom: every birthday, each of them pressed a palm to the linen and traced it, so the cloth grew crowded with the family's slow enlargement. [62] Her husband's broad hand, her daughter Mim's hand opening year by year like a fern. [77] And four years where Ilse's outline simply did not appear. [87] After the birth something had unlatched in her, and she had spent those years in a grey room in her own house, awake but not present, hearing Mim's laughter through a wall of wool. [121] She had come to find where lost time accumulates. [130]

The glade at the top was midnight and would always be midnight. [142] Below the rim, cloud lay in a floor of moonlit fleece; above, stars hung so thick and near that breathing felt like theft. [165] The beauty was thin and it choked her; she stood with a hand at her throat, weeping without deciding to. [185]

The cartomancer sat on a stone with an inkpot black as a beetle's back. [199] She made her cards as she went, onyx ink on blank rectangles, drawing whatever the seeker had brought up the mountain. [220] Her fingers were stained to the second knuckle. [228]

"You'll want the cloth off," she said. [235] "Unfasten it; I read on flat things." [242]

Ilse unfastened the knot at her collarbone and spread nine birthdays over the stone. [256] From her pocket she took the payment she had chosen: a saucer from Mim's dolls' tea set, no wider than a coin, with a crack running clean across it. [285] She had cracked it herself, in the grey years, when Mim set the little table beside her bed and asked her to pour. [308] Ilse had pushed it away, too hard, and the sound of that small breaking had been the loudest thing in four silent years. [331]

The cartomancer turned the saucer over and looked pleased, the way a mason is pleased by a good stone. [350] "Everyone who climbs here brings something with a crack in it," she said. [363] "That is the toll; now watch." [369]

She drew three cards. [373] The first was a room with no door, and Ilse recognized her own wallpaper. [387] The second was a child's hand, traced, with nothing beside it. [398] The third was this glade, and the clouds beneath it, and Ilse understood before it was said. [415]

"Lost time does not vanish," the cartomancer said. [423] "It rises. [425] It gathers up here, it condenses, it becomes all that." [435] She gestured at the fleece below, silver, endless. [443] "Every hour anyone ever spent shut in a grey room. [453] It is the sorriest and prettiest thing in the world, and it holds nothing you can carry down." [471]

The mountain folk had a name for it that Ilse had heard only in songs: cloudbound repentance, the penance of those who go on regretting where regret can be seen. [501] "Some seekers stay," the cartomancer said. [507] "They sit at the rim demanding to know why the years took them. [520] They want the reason unfastened like a buckle, and the reason never comes, and by winter they are cloud themselves. [540] The ones who go home," she added, "go home willingly perplexed." [551]

Ilse looked at the four blank birthdays on the linen and felt the old question rise: where did I go, whose fault was it, how many hours exactly. [579] The clouds below would happily take the counting off her, forever. [590] So she chose not to know. [596] She let the mystery stand in her like an unopened room, and it was strangely light to carry. [614]

She asked for the inkpot instead. [620] On the linen, in the four empty squares, she traced her hand four times, dated them, and did not apologize on the cloth. [643] Apology, she thought, was something you did downstairs, at a table, pouring. [655]

The cartomancer kept the saucer and set it among a hundred other cracked tolls glinting on the stone. [673] Ilse knotted the tablecloth back around her shoulders and went down through the cold, sweet, strangling brightness. [690]

Mim turned ten in April. [695] They spread the cloth, and Mim found the four new hands and looked up, and Ilse said only that she had been away and had come back. [722] That summer they mended the dolls' tea set with a new saucer, plain white, and it never matched the others. [742] Mim liked it best. [746] And on days when the sky went low and silver over the valley, Ilse would look up, willingly perplexed, and pour. [767]
