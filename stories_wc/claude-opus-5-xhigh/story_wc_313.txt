The wind chime on the ridge fence had rung for eleven years, and when it stopped, Orla Vance knew the reservoir was giving the town back. [26] In the drought weeks the valley air went still and stayed still, so the silence itself was a gauge. [45] She walked Midnight Ridgeway at twilight, as she did every evening, gauge book under her arm, checking the levee crest for boils and seepage. [69] Twenty-two years a levee warden: her commendation called her precisely humane, which meant she measured seepage to the millimeter and still knocked on doors softly when she had to wake a family at three in the morning. [106]

Tonight the mirrored hush of the drawdown pool held the ridge upside down, and pegged along the fence wire were photographs. [127] Seven of them, spaced a stride apart, curling in the damp. [138] She knew the hand that had hung them. [146] Silas Renn had kept the tavern in Cardew before the dam, and since his stroke he had no words left, only pictures; he had been leaving them for her all week, a trail she had refused to follow. [184] She followed it now. [188]

The first photograph showed a street of low stone houses with a chapel at the end. [204] The second, a bar counter crowded with bottles. [212] The third, a girl of eight holding a wind chime of blue glass, mouth open in a shout. [230] Orla stopped there a long while, because she did not remember being that girl, and she had lived her whole adult life beside the water that had drowned her childhood. [260] Grief is an efficient clerk; it will expunge a whole year to keep you working. [275] The county had done its own expunging: when Cardew went under, the reservoir authority struck the village from the maps and let the roster of its two hundred names lapse out of the record, as though ninety households had merely been water all along. [319]

The last four photographs led down the spillway steps into the mud. [331] Orla went down with her lamp, boots sucking, into the resurfaced town. [343] It was all there and all wrong: doorways silted to the lintel, the chapel a stump, garden walls furred grey like something asleep underwater, which it had been. [371] The final photograph was pinned to a beam where the tavern's sign had hung, and it showed a cellar hatch. [391] She found the hatch under a foot of silt and levered it up with her gauge rod. [408] Inside, wedged in a stone niche above the flood line, stood one bottle, dry, its sapphire-hued liqueur label still legible: Renn's Gentian, Cardew, and a heron rising. [435]

She turned it, and the memory came back whole, the way a name comes back. [450] The last night in the tavern, the year she was eight. [461] Every family signing the back of a label in pencil, two hundred names, so that the town would exist somewhere the authority could not reach. [486] Silas pressing the blue glass chime into her hands and telling her she was the ringer: hang it on the ridge, and whenever it sounds, gather whoever is near and tell them one true thing about Cardew. [523] She had hung it. [527] She had never once gathered anyone. [533] She had become the warden instead, the one who watched the water that had taken her mother, and watching had been easier than speaking. [557]

Now she turned the bottle to the lamp and read the pencil, faint but there: Vance, Renn, Aldworth, Coyne, all of them, the record the county had let go. [586] She climbed the ridgeway with the bottle inside her coat. [596] The chime hung silent in the dead air, and she reached up and struck it herself, three times, hard. [615] Then she went along Midnight Ridgeway knocking on doors softly, the way she did at three in the morning, telling people the water was down and to come up to the fence. [647]

They came: Coyne's grandson, the Aldworth sisters, a dozen incomers who had only ever known the lake and its good fishing, and Silas in his chair, wordless, watching her with terrible patience. [679] She opened the bottle, poured a thimble for everyone into whatever they had brought, and held up the sapphire label so the names caught the lamplight. [705] Below them the valley lay in its mirrored hush, town and sky doubled in the shallows. [721] "One true thing each," she said. [727] "I'll start." [729]

The rain would come in autumn and the water would rise and cover Cardew again; she had read the gauges, she knew it precisely. [753] But a story told out loud between forty people cannot be drowned, and cannot be expunged, and by the time the chime rang next it would belong to all of them. [784]
