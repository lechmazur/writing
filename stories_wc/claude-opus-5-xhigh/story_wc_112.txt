The quillers came down at twilight, as they always did, folding their wings over the magnetic nexus like hands over a coal. [22] Anselm watched them from the gantry of Station Ferrous-Nine, a xenobiology research station bolted to a ridge of iron sand, and counted the minutes until the hush. [49] He was the station's sky mechanic, which meant he was obliging by trade: he kept the survey balloons patched, the mast cables tuned, and everyone's requests in a list he never refused. [81] Dr. Ilse Varn was on the list twice tonight. [90] "Twenty-two minutes," she said, arriving without greeting, breath smoking in the cold. [102] "That is the whole window: from the moment their wings settle to the first subdued awakening." [118] Anselm nodded, flexing his left hand, the prosthetic one, which ran mechanically warm in weather like this and which she had built for him three winters ago without ever once calling it a gift. [152] Everything about Varn was like that: icy warmth, a stove behind a shut iron door. [167]

The quillers were why the station existed, and the nexus was why the quillers existed. [182] Where the ridge's magnetism converged, the field braided itself into a standing knot that the animals slept inside, and when they woke they woke wrong: the young stirring first, then the eldest, then the ones between, as if morning arrived in a shuffled deck. [226] Varn had spent four years on that shuffle, and it had cost her every friend on the station, because she used it to argue something no one wanted to hear: that time does not move forward at all, but sideways. [266] "Through holographic principles," she had told the review board, "a volume is only ever a surface's rumor." [283] "The nexus is a surface." [288] "Every hour of those animals' lives is written on it side by side, and they sleep with their faces against the page." [310] The board had cut her funding; Anselm had lent her his winch, his interferometer, and his silence. [327]

Now they crossed the sand together, into the hush of beating wings at rest. [341] It was a specific quiet, that hush - not silence but the memory of noise, hundreds of vast wings folded and ticking as they cooled. [365] Her array stood at the knot's edge: a ring of field coils, and a single stiff brass lever that would collapse them into phase. [389] The lever had frozen shut in last week's storm. [398] "I cannot move it," Varn said, and Anselm heard what it cost her to say so. [414] He set his warm hand against the ice, waited, and then leaned his whole weight into the arm until the metal gave with a sound like a bone deciding. [443]

The coils sang. [446] Above the sleeping quillers the air went grainy, then bright, and the nexus unrolled itself. [461] Anselm had expected a graph. [466] What hung over the ridge was a plain: every quiller that had ever roosted there, standing shoulder to shoulder in a field with no horizon, hatchlings beside skeletons beside mothers mid-flight, all of them present, none of them past. [505] They were not asleep in time; they were lying down across it. [517]

Varn did not shout. [521] She stood with her recorder running and her jaw locked, and Anselm understood that this was her awakening too, and that it would be subdued, because being right had never been the warm part. [555] At the plain's near edge, small and unremarkable among the wings, stood two human figures at a lever. [573] One of them had a hand that glowed faintly with borrowed heat. [585] They were still there, sideways from now, permanent as a word already written. [598]

The first quiller shifted; the hush thinned; the plain folded shut. [609] "Twenty-one minutes," Varn said, unsteadily. [614] "You obliged me." [617] "I usually do," he said, and offered his warm hand, and she took it before the cold could argue. [636] Behind them the ridge began to murmur as the herd came up by ones and twos, out of order, unhurried, waking into a morning they had already had. [664] Anselm added the lever to his maintenance list, under tomorrow, which he now understood to mean beside. [681]
