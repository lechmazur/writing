The grotto woke only at night, when starlight fell through the cracked dome and the lodestone walls began to hum. [20] That hum held the platform up: one flat slab of black rock, floating a handspan above the pond, turning slowly like a leaf that had forgotten to sink. [48] Above the rim, sycamores stood in their long hush, and I knelt there to meditate as I had knelt for three years, waiting for my teacher to lie to me kindly. [79]

Ven Adu was a boy that season. [86] When I first came to him he had been ancient, ink-stained, famous in three provinces as the researcher who aged backwards, and he had never once explained the condition he was supposed to be studying. [121] He weaves truth carefully, the villagers said, meaning he withheld most of it. [134]

"Tonight you read the water yourself," he said, and his voice had gone high and sweet, a child's voice, which frightened me more than the drop of years. [162] He put a smooth stone in my palm. [170] The method was old and simple: throw, then enter the trance, and let the rings tell you what they know. [190] Water remembers in circles; a skilled reader can follow the spacing of the rings the way a scribe follows lines, and the pond under a magnetic grotto remembers more than most. [221]

"What am I looking for?" I asked. [228]

"The lesson," he said. [232] "I teach vanishing so that something may appear, and in three years you have never asked me what." [250]

I threw the stone. [254] The rings went out, silver under starlight, and the trance took me the way sleep takes the exhausted. [272]

Through the trance the circles slowed and opened, and each one was a year. [286] The outermost ring held tonight: a boy of twelve on a floating slab, and a woman kneeling across from him. [306] The next held an old man teaching her to unmake her own outline, and the next an older man still, and the next a child running along this shore with a stone in her fist. [341] That child threw it, and the grotto's hum caught her, and she went out like a lamp. [358] I watched the old man wade in after her. [367] He came out carrying nothing, and he was younger by a decade. [379]

The rings kept turning, and I understood, all at once and far too late, what he had been withholding through every patient season of my apprenticeship. [405] I was not his student. [410] I was the thing he was trying to bring back. [420]

Twenty-six years ago I vanished in this water, and he had spent every year since paying himself backwards into the past to reach me. [444] The lessons were not lessons. [449] They were rungs. [452] A vanished thing cannot simply be pulled into the light; it has to be taught how to appear, and the only grammar it still understands is the grammar of vanishing. [482] So he had taught me to dissolve, night after night, until dissolving made an outline, and the outline learned to hold a hand, a voice, a name. [509] He had told me nothing because knowing too early would have made me a ghost who knew she was a ghost, and ghosts who know do not learn. [537]

I came out of the trance with my knees aching against real stone. [550] Aching. [551] Three years ago I could not have felt it. [560]

Across the slab, Ven Adu had become a small child, asleep, one fist curled, and the lodestone walls hummed him gently around and around above the water. [587] He had spent his whole span to finish the sentence he had begun the night he waded in, and he would not remember the ending. [612] I lifted him. [615] He was warm, and my arms were solid enough to hold him, which was the whole of his research, proven at last. [637]

I carried him up the root-stair into the sycamore hush, past the trees standing in their patient quiet, and I did not look back at the pond, because the last ring had shown me one more thing. [674] A woman on a floating rock, decades from now, weaving the truth carefully for a boy who does not yet know he was ever lost. [699] I will withhold it as long as he needs. [708] That is the lesson, and it is delivered late on purpose. [719]
