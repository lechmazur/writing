The road to Puerto Ceniza ended in a hush. [9] Mireya Solano heard it before she saw the sea: the whispering hush of small waves dragging black sand back and forth, like a congregation not yet ready to speak. [38] She had not meant to come here. [45] The bus to the capital had broken an axle, and the driver's cousin offered a detour along the coast road, and Mireya, who had learned long ago that detours were the only honest roads, said yes. [81] Above the village, Mount Aguairo breathed a slow orange breath, and above the mountain the moon was sliding toward the sun's dark shoulder. [104] People had come from three islands for the eclipse, and they stood on the beach in their thousands, waiting, murmuring, a vibrant hush. [127]

Mireya owned one bag. [131] In it were eleven jars. [136] Once she had owned a shipping company, then a foundation, then nothing, because she had given the foundation away too, piece by piece, to people who needed it more than her ledgers did. [169] What she kept was her bottled lightning bug collection - eleven jars, each labeled with a year, each holding a few living sparks she caught, released, and caught again, because what she collected was not the insects but the light. [208] She had spent her life trying to understand the texture of transformation - how a grub becomes a lantern, how a fortune becomes a school, how stone becomes sand fine enough to run through a child's fingers. [244] Giving was how she got her hands on the seam. [254]

An old woman selling boiled corn told her the island's prophecy without being asked, because that is what old women do during the slow dance of celestial bodies. [282] "When the moon eats the sun, a stranger with stars in her hands will walk the black beach, and Aguairo will keep its fire, and the child who was lost will be found." [315] Mireya laughed and bought corn she could not pay for; the woman waved the coin away. [331]

She did not believe in prophecies. [337] She believed in what she had done all her adult life, which was to orchestrate coincidences: to seat the widow beside the man who needed a cook, to leave a jar of fireflies where a grieving child would find it and think the world had winked at her. [385] Charity embarrassed people; coincidence let them keep their dignity. [394]

At twenty minutes to noon the light went strange, thin and coppery, and the beach's murmur died into the deepest quiet Mireya had ever stood inside. [420] Ten thousand people, and nothing but the whispering hush of water on volcanic grit. [434] Which is how she heard the crying. [440] It came from the lava bench north of the crowd, where the cliffs made a pocket of shade, and it was thin and hoarse, a child who had been calling for a long time to people who could not hear over their own celebration. [484] Mireya went, because she was closest, and because she was nobody, and nobody is free to move. [501]

The boy had fallen into a collapsed lava tube, up to his chest in old ash, one ankle wrong. [520] It was dark down there in a way that noon had no business being. [534] So she opened her jars. [539] Eleven years of light went up out of the glass and hung in the eclipse-dusk, confused and glorious, and by their green pulsing she found the boy's hands. [567] She got him out before the sun returned. [575]

The crowd, turning, saw a stranger climb out of the mountain's black flank with stars leaking from her fists and a lost child on her hip. [601] Aguairo, which had grumbled all week, went quiet that afternoon and kept its fire for another eleven years. [619] Nobody could be talked out of the prophecy after that. [629] Mireya tried, once, and gave it up; a woman who had spent decades arranging coincidences could hardly object to being one. [650]

They fed her, and housed her in the corn seller's back room, and asked nothing, and this was the strangest gift of her life: to be on the receiving end of somebody else's careful accident. [685] In the evenings she walked the black beach with the empty jars, catching nothing, because she had finally felt the texture she wanted. [708] Transformation was not a flash. [713] It was the sand under her feet, a mountain patiently becoming small enough to hold; it was a hush that was not emptiness but ten thousand people breathing at once. [743] It was a grub in the dark, deciding to be a lamp. [755] The boy, whose name was Teo, learned to catch fireflies with two fingers and no fear, and he was the one who took the jars when she died, and he refilled them every year. [789]
