They called me Subject Nine, but the nurses called me the drummer, because I could not stop drumming on everything. [20] Bedrails, IV poles, the ceramic lip of the sink, my own collarbone at three in the morning. [37] It was the only defiance the clinic could not confiscate, a quietly rebellious pulse under their schedules and consent forms. [57] I was the experimental treatment guinea pig for Project Cantata, and I had signed everything they put in front of me, because Ilse had signed it first. [84] The therapy braided two patients' recollections together -- entangled memories, the doctors said, so that a mind losing its own past could borrow scaffolding from another. [109] Ilse was losing hers to a disease with a long Latin name. [121] I had a healthy hippocampus and nothing much to do with it. [133] They performed the entanglement in the sound wave garden on the roof of the east wing. [149] Nothing grew there from soil. [154] The garden was built of standing tones: resonance columns that flowered into visible ripples, chimes tuned to the frequency of a human sigh, beds of gravel that hissed like surf when the speakers beneath them woke. [190] At its center lay the auric lagoon under starlight, a shallow basin of gold-suspension fluid that held sound the way water holds a stone's ring. [215] Strike a note near it and the surface braided itself into rings of light that did not fade for hours. [235] That was where the machines read us. [242] Ilse liked to perch on the lagoon's rim with her heels tucked under her, a small heron of a woman, seventy-one and unimpressed by doctors. [267] She had been a percussionist once; that was why they paired us. [279] Compatible rhythm signatures, the intake form said, as if souls came with time signatures printed on them. [296] Maybe they do. [299] The sessions happened at the hush of the universe inhaling -- the technicians' phrase for the ninety seconds before dawn when the city's grid load bottomed out and the garden went silent enough to hear the lagoon breathing. [336] In that gap, they said, the mind was porous. [345] What they did not tell us was that entanglement runs both ways. [357] By the fourth session I remembered a rehearsal hall in Rotterdam I had never entered, the smell of rosin and radiator dust, a conductor's baton snapping. [383] By the seventh, Ilse hummed a lullaby my mother had invented, badly, in a language Ilse did not speak. [402] The doctors called that contamination and adjusted the dosage. [411] Ilse called it a shared dream and asked me to help her breathe life into it before the disease finished its work. [433] Her plan was simple and against every protocol. [441] The lagoon kept whatever it was given. [448] If we played her life into it during the hush -- every rhythm she still owned, everything I had absorbed of her -- the gold would hold the pattern, and the pattern would hold her. [481] Not a recording, she insisted. [486] A living thing that we both stood inside. [494] So we did it on the last night, while the aide charted vitals downstairs. [508] I drummed on everything the garden offered: the resonance columns, the gravel beds, the chime frames, the aluminum rail, the flat of my palms on the lagoon's rim until they stung. [539] Ilse kept the pulse with two spoons, and when the pulse slipped away from her, I found it and handed it back, because by then I remembered her hands better than my own. [572] At the hush the garden fell dead quiet, and the lagoon took it all. [586] Gold rings rose and did not settle. [593] They stacked into a slow green-violet shimmer over the water, starlight caught and re-spent, the whole roof lit like a low aurora built out of one woman's seventy-one years. [622] Ilse watched it and cried without any sound at all, which is the only grief the garden could not amplify. [642] She died in March. [646] The clinic shut down Project Cantata, citing contamination. [654] They drained most of the lagoon, but an inch of gold still lies in the basin, and the roof access lock is old. [677] So I climb up before dawn and perch where she used to, and I drum. [692] At the hush, the thin gold still answers -- rings, then that soft aurora, weaker each month, sorrowful in the way northern light is sorrowful, beautiful because it is leaving. [721] I do not know anymore which rehearsal halls are mine. [731] I have stopped trying to sort us out. [739] The nurses were right about me: I drum on everything, on bus windows and coffee lids and my own collarbone, keeping a stranger's tempo alive in a borrowed body. [768] It is not a cure, and it was never allowed. [778] It is only a dream two people are still standing inside. [789]
