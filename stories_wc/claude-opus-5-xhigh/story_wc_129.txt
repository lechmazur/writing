Ilve arrived at the rim settlement with a bundle of marsh reeds on her back and nobody to speak her name. [21] She had walked here from the fen country, through years beyond the murmur of lost languages, where the old words lay under the water like sunken boats that no one remembered how to raise. [55] In the fens she had been a marsh-reed sculptor, weaving herons and lanterns and cradles for people who paid in bread and never once asked her to stay. [83]

The crystalline orchard perched at the edge of the shimmering crater, and it hurt to look at. [100] Trees of grown quartz stood in rows, fruiting small prisms that broke the low sun into colors, and beneath them the crater floor glowed pale as a held breath. [129] At the orchard's heart stood the glimmering obsidian arbor, black glass arched over black glass, the only dark thing in all that light. [152]

Every household at the rim kept a tree, and every tree bore names cut into its trunk, and the names ran together in lines that Ilve came to understand as webs of kinship: this cousin grafted to that widow, this orphan grafted to that boatwright. [197] She wanted, with an ache she could not put down, to graft herself onto them. [212] So she asked the orchard-keeper how it was done. [221] "You dedicate something to the arbor," the keeper said, "but first you have to find the tag." [238]

There was an artist at the rim whom nobody had ever caught working, who left a mark, a knot of interlaced lines, somewhere new each season. [264] Find the tag and you could dedicate; that was the law, older than the settlement's oldest ledger. [281]

Dov found her on the third morning, sitting under a quartz tree with her reeds gone limp from dew. [300] He was fourteen and boldly sympathetic in the way of people who have not yet learned that kindness can cost, and he simply announced that he would help her look. [330] "I have hunted it four seasons," he said, "and I am very bad at it, but two are better." [349]

They searched together for a month. [355] They searched the underside of the cistern bridge, the crawlspace behind the glassblower's kiln, the rain-scoured backs of the fruit crates, the sills of every window that faced the glow. [385] Along the way Dov's aunts fed her twice a day, and the glassblower asked her to weave a reed screen for his window, and a woman named Hessa asked her to show the children how a heron's neck is made from a single bent stalk. [430] Ilve kept her eyes on the walls and did not notice, at first, how many doors had opened. [448]

They did not find the tag. [454] On the last night of the fruiting, when the prisms rang in the wind like small bells, Ilve sat down beneath the obsidian arbor and wept quietly at the luminescent ache of a place that had let her in everywhere except in. [496] Dov crawled under with her, out of habit by now, and put his hand flat against the glass overhead. [515] "Ilve," he said. [518]

The tag was there on the arbor's inner curve, where the black glass caught the orchard's light and gave it back. [539] It was enormous. [542] Hundreds of lines, incised thin as hair, looping and crossing and holding, a knot with no beginning, every strand joined to the strands beside it. [567] Beneath the newest line someone had scratched a date from the previous spring. [580] "It is not one artist," Ilve said. [587] "No," said Dov. [590] "It is everyone who got in. [596] They add a line, and then they never tell." [605]

She understood then that the search had never been for a mark. [617] It was for the month of asking and being fed, of hunting under bridges with a boy who had chosen her before she was anybody's. [642]

She worked until dawn. [646] What she dedicated to the arbor was a lattice of marsh reed, green and living, bent into a knot that copied the one above it and then continued it, one new strand carrying her name and one carrying Dov's. [685] She lashed it into the obsidian's crook so that as the reeds dried they would tighten and grip, the way a graft takes. [708]

In the morning the keeper cut ILVE into a young quartz tree, and beside it the names of the aunts, the glassblower, Hessa, and Dov. [733] Ilve looked out at the crater glowing beyond the rows, that old wound turned to light, and went to find something new to weave. [757] She told nobody about the tag. [763] She had a secret now, and people to keep it with. [774]
