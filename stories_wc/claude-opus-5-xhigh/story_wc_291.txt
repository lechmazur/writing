Mira Kesh could not recognize her own mother's face, but she could draw yours from a stranger's stammering. [18] The police called it a gift; she called it arithmetic. [28] Faces meant nothing to her, so she never flinched from the contradictions inside them. [42] Cold cases came to her desk in gray boxes, and she opened them the same way every time: index cards, a numbered grid, one witness per card, no exceptions. [71] Where two witnesses disagreed, other artists averaged; Mira did the opposite. [82] She drew both claims at full strength and searched for the single face that could honestly hold them, building her likenesses by unifying opposites. [106]

Case 88-114 was the Ledgerstone quarry girl, missing twenty-seven years. [116] The file held one physical item: a two-inch fragment of black ribbon, cut from a masquerade mask, found snagged on the fence above the water. [141] Mira had measured it, sketched it, and recorded the weave as 4 over 1, twill, with a scorched notch near the tear. [163] Three days later the evidence locker logged the envelope out to a signature nobody could read, and the ribbon was gone. [184] After the evidence disappeared, the department's interest thinned to a furrowed hush: sympathetic brows, lowered voices, no budget. [202] Mira kept working, because a stolen ribbon proved something a present ribbon never had: someone alive still cared where it pointed. [223]

Her cards pointed at Ellis Vane, quarry foreman, sixteen years old in 1998, the boy who reported the ribbon and then said nothing else for twenty-seven years. [250] Witnesses split on him exactly as they always did on the guilty and the grieving. [265] Half remembered a gentle choirboy; half remembered a cold thing in a doorway. [278] So Mira drew the choirboy and the cold thing on one sheet, pressing them together until the paper gave her a man who had been kind once and had spent decades not feeling it. [312] She was not trying to convict him. [319] She was trying to awaken dormant empathy, because a man who cannot feel will not confess, and a man who feels cannot stay silent. [343] She mailed him the portrait with a note: Thursday, six, the old face of the quarry. [359]

Thursday came up drowned in mist. [365] The shadowed quarry held it like a bowl holds milk, gray on gray, the cut walls leaning in, the water below flat and patient. [389] Mira arrived early, methodical as ever, and set her boots on the ledge where the fence had once stood. [408] Ellis Vane came out of the fog at six exactly, carrying a rusted drill rod like a lance. [426] She could not have named his features if he had held a lamp to them, but she knew the shape of his shoulders from her own drawing. [453]

'You took the ribbon from the locker,' she said. [462] 'My brother took it,' he said. [468] 'He wears a badge, and I let him, and I have been letting him since I was sixteen.' [486] His voice had the furrowed hush of a man reading his own headstone. [499] 'Your drawing,' he said. [503] 'How did you know I was both of those people?' [513] 'Because nobody is only one,' Mira said. [520]

Then she asked him where the mask was, and he lanced the silt at the water's edge with the rod, three careful times, the way you probe a wound you already know the depth of. [555] On the third pass the rod snagged and brought up a biscuit tin, and inside the tin lay a masquerade mask with one ribbon cut short. [581] Mira knelt and matched it against her notes from memory: 4 over 1, twill, a scorched notch at the tear. [601] The stolen fragment hardly mattered now, because two initials were inked inside the mask's rim, and they were not Ellis Vane's. [622]

'Say her name,' Mira said, because a name is where feeling starts. [634] 'Ada Riel,' he said, and then he wept, twenty-seven years late, into the mist. [648] Mira could not read the grief on his face; she read it in his hands, which she had drawn correctly weeks before ever meeting them. [673] She wrote the time on a card, 6:41, and clipped it to the tin. [687]

The sergeant was arrested in September, and the quarry file moved from the gray boxes to the shelf of closed things. [708] Mira went back to her grid, one witness per card, no exceptions. [720] She still could not pick her own mother out of a crowd. [732] But she had learned the only likeness that ever solved anything: hold the choirboy and the cold thing in the same frame, press until they agree, and wait for the man inside to feel the pressure.
