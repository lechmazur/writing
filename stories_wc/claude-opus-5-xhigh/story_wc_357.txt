The Upturn came at dusk, and Wren Sabo cut her engines to listen to nothing. [15] On Casilda, gravity inverted for one hour each season, and during that hour the wind whirled without sound. [33] Leaves rose like startled birds, dust spiraled up the flanks of the floating rock arches, and none of it made a whisper. [55] She had been Casilda's orchard pilot for eleven years, and the whirling silence still made her chest ache. [73]

Below her skiff, the terraced apple and quince groves hung upside down over an unfathomable sky. [89] Most of them were dying. [94] The blight had come three winters ago and eaten the heartwood out of half the planet's trees, and the harvest ships had stopped arriving. [118] Only the Ninth Grove had survived, and no one could say why. [130] Wren intended to find the source of that resilience before the last orchards went to ash. [146]

She could not do it by instrument. [153] During the inversion, compasses spun and altimeters lied; the only honest thing on Casilda was water. [169] The irrigation channels emptied themselves upward in slow silver ribbons, and every ribbon ran toward whatever fed it. [187] No other pilot on Casilda would fly the Upturn; Wren had flown eleven. [200] So she navigated by water flow, threading her skiff between the arches, keeping the brightest ribbon off her left wing. [220]

In her coat pocket she carried a broken arrow tip. [230] It was her grandmother's, dug out of the Ninth Grove's soil the year the colony landed, and it was not an arrow tip at all. [255] It was a shard of something older: a black, fluted wedge that hummed when wet, and that made seedlings in a cup leap a hand's height overnight. [282] Grandmother Sabo had called it a curiosity and refused to let the botanists cut it. [297] Wren had come to believe it was a fragment of something whole, and that the rest of it was still up there, somewhere in the arches, accelerating plant growth in one stubborn grove. [330]

Forty minutes of inversion remained. [335] The ribbon she followed braided with two others and climbed toward the largest arch on Casilda, the crescent the settlers called the Yoke. [358] Water poured into its underside and did not come out. [368] Wren took the skiff in through the wet mouth of it, lights on, hull scraping wildflowers that grew in the dark. [389]

Inside was a chamber the size of a cathedral, and the chamber was full of voices. [405] She heard her grandmother first. [410] Then a hundred others, overlapping, none of them louder than breath: pruning songs, curses at frost, a lullaby in a language the colony had stopped speaking. [436] The walls were fluted black stone, the same stuff as the arrow tip, and they were wet, and the wetness carried sound the way copper carries current. [463] A gathering of echoes, a century of them, folded into rock that had been listening since before the landing. [482]

Wren cut the engine and let the skiff drift, and in the whirling silence outside and the murmuring inside, she began to discover what she had come for. [510] The stone did not merely record. [516] It answered. [518] Each echo returned to the groves through the water, and the water carried instruction the way her grandmother's shard carried it: grow, hold, thicken, endure. [543] The Ninth Grove drank from a spring that ran out of the Yoke. [556] The Ninth Grove had been sung to for a hundred years, and the arch had been giving the songs back. [576] The source of resilience was not a machine buried in the rock. [588] It was attention, stored. [592]

Wren took out the broken arrow tip and held it in the falling water at the chamber's heart. [610] It hummed, and the echoes bent toward it, and she understood that the shard was a splinter of this whole listening place, a fragment that had kept faith with one old woman's voice. [643]

Eleven minutes left. [646] She recorded the chamber's coordinates and the flow map, then leaned into the rushing dark and spoke. [663] She named the dying groves one by one, Second, Fourth, Sixth, Twelfth, and told the stone where their channels ran, and asked it to send the songs down those too. [693] Then she added her own voice to the gathering, because that was the only payment the place accepted. [711]

The hour turned. [714] Gravity came back like a hand on her shoulder, the ribbons of water fell, and sound returned to Casilda all at once: wind, leaves, the tick of her cooling engine. [744] Wren flew home along the new channels she would have to dig by spring. [758] In her pocket the broken tip was still warm, still humming, and in the Ninth Grove below her the quinces were putting out leaves in the dark, fast, as if someone had told them it was safe.
