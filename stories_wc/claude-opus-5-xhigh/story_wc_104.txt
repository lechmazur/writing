The arrow came over the wall on the last Tuesday of the dry season, and it did not come to kill anyone. [22] It stuck in the tin roof of the ring-toss booth, quivering, and I climbed up to pull it free because I was twelve and the carnival was mine. [50] The fletching was strange: not feathers but husks, three stiff blades of millet leaf glued in a spiral, the kind of millet that grows where nothing grows. [77] On the shaft, in pencil: PRIZES. PIANO BENCH. [85]

The prize redemption center was the only building at Halloway's Amusement Park that still had a door. [102] Inside, the shelves still held their dusty inventory of things nobody had wanted enough to win: chalk dogs, a bald teddy bear, harmonicas green with corrosion, each shelf stenciled with a number and a ticket price. [138] The upright piano had been left to rot by the door, and in its bench, under a lid of cracked vinyl, lay nine keys. [162] Not piano keys. [165] Door keys, brass and iron, laid out on the felt in a pattern that made no sense until I understood it was a keyboard. [189] Each key rested on a note, each note stood for a letter, and read in the order of the keys' lengths they spelled FOURTEEN LIFT. [214] Shelf fourteen lifted out on hidden hinges, and beneath the redemption counter, down a ladder, was the vault. [232] Six hundred jars, labeled in a careful hand, sealed with wax and ash: sorghum, cowpea, tepary bean, amaranth, bitter melon, teff - seeds bred and rebred for soil that had forgotten rain. [263] A card wired to the ladder said, in the arrow's pencil: When the wall comes down, carry them east. [282]

Everyone in the dry district knew about Idris Vane, though nobody said his name near the checkpoints. [299] He had been the Authority's best plant breeder until he began smuggling his own varieties out to the farms the wall had cut off from the aquifer. [326] They did not execute him; they chained him to a labor gang on the wall itself, which was, they thought, a joke he would have to live inside. [354] For nine years he was patiently strategic: he learned the demolition schedules, sent seed out in the water-boy's bucket one jar at a time, and taught that boy to shoot. [384]

The wall came down in October, not from rebellion but from arithmetic - the aquifer had failed on both sides, and the Authority wanted the stone for the new pumping road. [414] They blasted the north span at dawn, and the dust went up like a gray sail, and for one hour there was no wall and no line and no guards who could see ten feet. [449] I had eleven friends waiting in the prize redemption center with flour sacks, and we filled them jar by jar. [469] What we had not planned for was the salvage crew. [479] They came through the fairgrounds at seven with a loader, to scrape the carnival into the road bed, vault and all, and they would have reached the counter before our third sack was full. [513]

Idris impeded them. [516] He walked out of the dust in his leg irons, unhurried, and fed the slack of his chain into the loader's drive sprocket, and then he sat down in the mud. [547] The machine seized. [550] The foreman screamed at him for forty minutes about property and about the cost of a sprocket, and Idris answered every question politely, wrongly, and at great length. [578] It was the most staggering grace I have ever seen: a man who could not walk fifty feet buying us a whole morning, one sentence at a time. [606]

We carried six hundred jars east through the gap, past the stumps of the wall, into the fields where the soil cracks into plates you can lift like dinner plates. [636] He had not sent us to save the seeds; jars keep. [647] He had sent us to plant resilience in dry soil, which is the only place resilience can be proved. [666] Sorghum in the Halloway lots. [671] Cowpea along the ditches. [675] Teff where the wall's shadow had kept the ground cool and strange. [687]

Two seasons later the tepary beans came up in ground that had grown nothing since my mother was a girl, and the Authority sent agronomists to ask, respectfully, where such varieties had come from. [721] They released Idris in the spring, because a man's seeds were feeding the road crews and it had become embarrassing to keep him. [744] He walked out to my father's field, still limping from the irons, and knelt and put his hand flat on a stand of millet, the same millet whose leaves he had glued to an arrow to point a boy toward a piano bench. [787] "Good," he said. [790] "Now you know how long it takes." [797]
