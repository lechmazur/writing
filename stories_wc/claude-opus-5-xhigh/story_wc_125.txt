They came the summer the wheat forgot to ripen, and afterward nobody measured years the same way. [17] Meret, who drifted, still measured them in footsteps. [25] She was a roadside poet, which meant she owned two shirts, a stub of chalk, and a frayed coil of rope she never explained. [49] For a bowl of soup she would write your grief on a wall; for two, she would make it rhyme. [69]

The energy beings found her on the coast road in the third autumn of their arrival. [85] They looked like heat above stone, like the shimmer that lies about water. [98] They spoke by warming the air inside her ear, and what they said was: teach us the texture of time. [118] They had everything else. [122] They could hold a thousand years the way she held a pebble, all at once, no before, no after, and it had begun to feel to them like holding nothing at all. [154] They wanted grain, weight, the drag of an hour. [163]

She led them to the terra-cotta grotto above the salt flats, because a poet performs where the acoustics are honest. [183] It was an old kiln cave, its walls lined with fired clay tiles the color of a healing wound, and through its broken roof the starlight came down in cold handfuls. [214] The grotto had been a prison once - the province kept its poets there - and Meret knew the room by its smell before her eyes adjusted. [239]

"Sit," she said to the shimmer, "or do whatever you do instead." [251] She uncoiled the rope across the tiles. [258] "This is a lesson in layers," she said. [266] "Patience is the whole curriculum." [271]

She began uncovering layers: the outer sheath, gray and furred with use; beneath it three strands, each one twisted from many; beneath those, fibers pale as tendon. [298] "Salt from my hands, eleven years ago, in this room," she said. [310] "Here, where it's thin, is where the rope ran over the lip of that window while a man's weight went down it." [332] "Time is not a length," she said. [339] "It is a slow wearing." [344]

The beings brightened; the tiles took the light and gave it back the color of blood in a lamp. [363] They asked whose weight. [367] So she told it plainly, because plainness is the harder art: two prisoners, one rope too short, one guard's rotation of nine minutes. [390] Tovin had gone first because she pushed him. [398] The rope gave at the ninth minute; he fell, and they took him in the yard, and hanged him for the escape she had authored, and pardoned her for the poem she recanted. [431] "Every choice I have made since has been made of that nine minutes," she said. [446]

Then the beings offered her the thing she had known they would eventually offer, because power always wants to pay. [466] They could unwear the rope. [471] They could put the strands back, and the salt, and the nine minutes, and she could choose again - go first herself, or hold the rope, or never write the poem at all. [503] One restoration only; whatever grew from it would be hers to live inside. [516]

She laughed, and the laugh had grief in it and mischief on top, the way a good wine has both fruit and iron. [539] "A game first," she said, because she had never been able to resist making a moral into a wager. [561] She knelt and began to pattern the loosened strands across the terra-cotta floor: a long braid for the road years, three knots for the three times she had nearly confessed, a loop left open at the end. [598] "Find the knot that is the choice," she said. [607]

The shimmer moved over the pattern for what she guessed was an hour, and she watched them learn to guess wrong, then wrong again, then to hesitate. [634] Hesitation, she thought, is the first true taste of duration. [644] At last they said: all of them are the choice. [654] "Good," she said. [657] "You've earned the lesson, so I'll pay you with my refusal." [668]

If she took the mend, the rope would be whole and smooth and would mean nothing, and they would have learned that time is a thing that can be rinsed off. [699] Tovin would live in a corrected world where no one had ever had to choose him, and the choosing was the last true thing she owned of him. [727] "Leave the rope frayed," she said. [733]

She walked out at dawn, road-dust rising pink off the salt, and left the pattern lying on the clay for them to read as long as they liked. [761] Behind her, in the grotto, the shimmer had begun, very slightly, to dim at its edges - wearing, at last, like everything that has somewhere to be. [787]
