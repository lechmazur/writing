The rain had stopped, and the old viaduct glowed like a wire pulled from a furnace. [16] Isa stood on the parapet with her heels over nothing, and nothing did not mind. [31] She was a gravity defier, which is a polite way of saying she had fallen once and never finished. [50] Between the drop and the ripple, she lived. [58] A single bead of water had left the iron railing above her nine years ago, by her count, and had not yet touched the puddle below; in that suspended sliver she was periodically eternal, granted forever in installments, each one ending the instant the ripple opened. [104] Then the tablet would hum, and the installment would begin again. [115]

The tablet was clay, ancient, cracked across one corner, its runes cut by a thumb that had been dust for four thousand years. [138] She had stolen it from a museum crate the same night she climbed the viaduct, believing it was a map. [158] It was not a map. [163] It was an engine. [167] The runes did not describe a labyrinth; they built one, fresh each time, through procedural generation - a handful of rules chewing on her own memories and spitting out corridors. [196] Every run, the walls were made of her: the kitchen with the burnt saucepan, the stairwell where her brother said the unforgivable thing, the clinic corridor with its bright and darkening light. [228] She had entered it four hundred and six times to conquer the labyrinth of self, and four hundred and six times the labyrinth had conquered her, politely, by boring her into surrender. [260]

Tonight she noticed the crack. [265] Not in the clay - in the air. [272] The viaduct's glow forked, and where it forked she saw the other Isas. [285] Fractured timelines, hairline versions of the same night: one Isa already falling, one climbing down, one holding the tablet out over the drop as if to let it go, one lying on the wet stones with her eyes open and the rain still deciding. [329] Each of them held a tablet, and each tablet was generating a different maze, and none of them could win, because the labyrinth was not one labyrinth. [356] It was four hundred and six of them, splitting like the glow. [368]

So she stopped trying to solve it and began, instead, to retrace. [380] Backward through the generated corridors, not hunting the exit but the seed - the first rule, the thing the runes had been fed. [402] She walked the walls of herself in reverse, past the clinic, past the stairwell, past the kitchen, past a birthday with too many candles, past a shallow river where her brother taught her to skip stones. [438] And there, in the oldest chamber, she found the seed, and it was not a memory at all. [456] It was a sentence she had said out loud on the parapet, nine years and one instant ago: I want to stop being the thing that happens to me. [485] The tablet had taken her at her word and given her a forever with no consequences in it. [503] Nothing could happen to her here; nothing could happen at all. [514] That was the maze: not walls, but the refusal to let the ripple open. [528]

She came back up into the glowing viaduct with rain-light on her face and the other Isas watching, hairline-thin, waiting to be chosen. [551] Gravity waited too, patient as a dog. [558] She thought about how brightness always darkens somewhere, and how she had spent an age standing inside the last bright second so she would never have to meet the next one. [589] She turned the tablet over in her hands, feeling the runes rearrange themselves, hungry for another seed, another version, another eternity in installments. [612] Then she set it down on the parapet, stepped off the ledge onto the wet stone of the walkway - inward, not outward - and let her weight come back into her like water filling a boot. [647]

The glow dimmed as the timelines folded shut, one Isa at a time, the brightness darkening down to a single ordinary woman on a single ordinary bridge. [674] Above her, the bead of water finished its nine-year journey. [684] It struck the puddle. [688] The ripple opened, and kept opening, and she stood in it, mortal and moving, and walked home the long way to tell her brother she was alive. [715]
