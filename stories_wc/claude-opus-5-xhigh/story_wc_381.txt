Ferrin had not spoken a word in nine days, which was exactly how the work required her. [17] Her haven sat in the elbow of a quiet brook, a roofless ring of standing stones that gathered moonlight the way a bowl gathers rain. [42] She worked only at night, because night was when the village came to whisper. [56] They came one at a time, downstream, and knelt and told the water things they could tell nothing else: debts, betrayals, the names of people they loved wrongly. [84] The brook carried their secrets past her stones, and Ferrin, a summoner by trade, had learned to hear the shape of the pauses between them. [109] Nine days ago, a courier had left a paper crane on the flattest stone. [123] It was folded from the last page of the Oracle of Sill, who had died mid-sentence, and along one wing ran a line of cipher in rust-colored ink. [151] Ferrin knew the code; every summoner did. [158] It was the old ledger-cant used for prophecies too dangerous to say plainly, because a prophecy spoken aloud in any tongue at all becomes a debt the world must pay. [188] She had read the crane's origami wing once, silently, and then had not spoken since. [203] The prophecy concerned the brook, and a fire, and the village asleep on both banks; the details do not matter now, and that is the point. [229] A prophecy needs a mouth. [234] Deny it one, and it will find another; the courier's own lips had been sewn shut, and still the crane had traveled three hundred miles. [259] So Ferrin had decided to hush it instead. [267] Summoning is naming, and every name she had ever used was a sound. [280] But there exists one listener whose name contains no sound whatsoever, and it can be called only by mapping silence: by charting, night after night, the exact intervals where the whispering stops. [312] For nine nights she had sat with chalk and slate, marking the gaps between confessions the way a cartographer marks coastline. [333] Tonight the map was finished. [338] She lit her fire first, as ritual demanded, and it came up strange, as she had hoped: a tranquil blaze, tall and orange and utterly without crackle, burning like a held breath. [370] Then she laid the slate in the grass and walked its coastline with her fingertips, touching each gap in order. [390] The brook kept flowing; a woman upstream was confessing something about her brother. [403] Ferrin let the sounds flow past her and attended only to the holes in them, and on the eleventh gap the listener arrived. [426] It did not step into the ring so much as the ring admitted it had always been standing there. [445] It was terrestrially cosmic: a shape of wet loam and root-hair and beetle-shell, and inside that shape, visible through it, the slow turning of galaxies, as though someone had dug a hole in a garden and found the night sky at the bottom. [488] It smelled of rain on stone. [494] Ferrin held out the crane. [499] She could not ask; asking would require a voice, and a voice would spend the prophecy. [515] So she offered, and let the offering be the whole of her argument. [528] The listener took the crane in fingers like tilled earth and unfolded it. [541] One wing came open, then the other, then the long creased spine, until the Oracle's last page lay flat as a field. [563] The cipher was still there. [568] The listener pressed its palm across the wing's old fold, and smoothed it, and went on smoothing. [585] What left the paper was not the ink but the readability of the ink: the letters remained, and stopped meaning. [605] A smoothed hush came off the page in rings, the way warmth comes off a stone at dusk, and it moved outward through the haven and over both banks. [634] Upstream, the woman confessing about her brother stopped mid-word, and could not remember why she had come, and went home lighter. [655] The fire stood tall and said nothing. [661] The listener set the blank page in the water, where it turned and drifted and came apart. [678] Then it was gone, and the ring was only stones again. [689] Ferrin sat down beside her tranquil blaze and discovered the cost, which she had suspected and accepted in advance. [708] She had carried the prophecy in her head for nine days, and now she could not find it there. [724] She also could not find her mother's name, or the word for the bird the crane had been folded to resemble. [745] Hush takes what is nearest. [750] She was solitary; there was nobody to explain this to, which was, she thought, why the work had come to her and not to someone else. [776] So she listened to the brook flow, and let it be a brook, and nothing more. [792]
