Every dusk, Ivet climbed the spiral of the opal library above the clouds and began to light the lamps. [19] She was the glass-orchid caretaker, and she was gently systematic about it, wick by wick, west wing to east. [38] The orchids were not alive, exactly; they were blown from opal dust and hung on brass stems, and each one held a name. [61] When lamplight touched them, they warmed, they flexed, and they sang the name aloud in the voice of whoever had last spoken it. [84] That was the whole purpose of the library: to preserve the sound of forgotten names. [99] Her route ran across the museum of extinct feelings, because the two collections had been built into each other centuries ago, when people still climbed the stairs. [126] Now nobody did. [129] The halls were deserted marvels: a hall of ombrance, the sorrow felt for a stranger's unlived life; a hall of thrist, the joy of being correctly named by an enemy; cabinets of feelings nobody had needed for two hundred years. [169] Ivet lit them anyway, all of them, in order, because a marvel unattended becomes only weather. [185] In the last room, where foxglove grew up through the floor seams and nodded its speckled purple bells at her, hung the wind chime. [209] It was the oldest instrument in the collection: nine bamboo tubes, one for every caretaker who had died in service, so that the keepers were kept too. [236] The ninth tube was cracked. [241] It had cracked on the night Ivet's mother died, and Ivet, who had been sixteen and furious at the wind, had slammed the shutters so hard the chime swung into the frame. [273] For forty years the ninth tube had made no sound at all, and the name inside it had gone unspoken: her mother's name, Seliane. [297] Glass could be re-blown and brass re-hung, but bamboo would not take a tuning by hand; the tubes had been voiced, the old manuals said, by the echo of a long-forgotten song. [329] Ivet had spent four decades hunting the song, cataloguing every fragment of melody in the ledgers, and had found only titles and silences. [352] That was her particular grief, and it had a flavor she could name because it was catalogued in the room she stood in: foxglove regret, the ache for a thing you broke while trying to shut out the cold. [391] It was the last feeling in the museum still in use, and she was its only remaining specimen. [409] On the night the cloud-floor turned to glass with frost, Ivet climbed the last stair, lit the last lamp, and heard the library answer her. [434] She had been humming, as she always hummed on her rounds, a shapeless six-note thing with no name. [452] The opal dome caught it, folded it, and sent it back down doubled, and every orchid in the west wing opened at once. [475] Ivet stood very still, understanding, forty years late, that she had been carrying the song in her mouth the whole time. [496] Seliane had hummed it while she worked, wick by wick, west wing to east, and had never once said what it was called. [519] A forgotten song is not a lost one, Ivet thought; it only needs a room shaped like a listening ear. [539] She carried the cracked tube to the center of the dome, held it up, and sang. [555] The echo came back around her in a slow ring, pressing the bamboo from every side at once, and she felt the crack draw closed the way frost draws closed on a windowpane. [588] When she hung it back on its string and let the night wind through the shutters, the ninth tube spoke. [608] Seliane, it said, in Seliane's own voice, because Ivet had been the last to speak it. [624] Ivet wept a little, in the manner of the exhibits around her, and then she did the sensible thing: she took down the ledger and wrote the song out, note by note, and beside it the title she chose, Rounds. [664] There were nine tubes and no tenth; there was no apprentice on the stairs, and no visitor had come up through the clouds since she was a girl. [692] So she left the ledger open on the reading desk, under the brightest lamp, where anyone climbing out of the weather would see it first. [717] Then she went back to the beginning of the west wing, and, because there was still an hour of dark left, she began to light the lamps again, name by name, for whoever might be listening. [753]
