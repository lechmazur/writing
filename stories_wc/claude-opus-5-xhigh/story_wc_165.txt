Mira called it a trade, not a trick: she made wonder happen by ordering randomness with plain arithmetic. [18] The tidal arcade suited her. [23] Twice a night the sea climbed the colonnade's lower steps, emptied the promenade of loiterers, then withdrew and gave it back, so the crowd arrived in tides of its own. [53] Between acts, while the water hissed over mosaic and the stars came out hard and countable above the open roof, Mira did her real work. [78] She kept ledgers. [81] Footfall by quarter hour, coin-drop rates by weather, which arch echoed best when the tide was at nine feet. [100] Before the arcade, she had been a statistician for the harbor authority, and before that a child who could see eight moves ahead and no reason to enjoy any of them. [131] Numbers had made her life a corridor. [138] So she left the corridor and took the numbers with her, because she had decided her purpose was to serenade monotony with creativity, and monotony, she knew, kept excellent records. [168] Tonight's target was the man in the gray coat. [177] He crossed the arcade at 9:14 every evening, seven minutes after the shipping ledgers closed, and he never once looked up. [198] He was lightly attentive in the way of a man who has learned that most things do not concern him: he registered the world, tipped nothing, corrected nothing, and walked on. [229] Mira had watched him for eleven nights, and she recognized the walk. [241] It was her old walk. [246] Her tool was a bishop, ivory, chipped at the mitre, salvaged from a set she had broken at fourteen after beating her father and feeling nothing. [272] A bishop moves only on the diagonal; it cannot reach half the board, ever, no matter how patient it is. [292] That, she thought, was worth handing to a man who had stopped looking up. [306] She could not simply give it to him. [314] A gift is an obligation; a coincidence is an invitation. [324] So she built one. [328] At 9:02 she set her fiddle case in the third arch, where the receding tide left a shining strip of wet mosaic exactly two paces wide. [354] Walkers avoided the wet; the dry channel funneled them along the seaward columns. [367] At 9:06 she paid a boy two coins to fly a paper gull on a string from the upper balustrade, because eyes follow motion. [391] At 9:11 she began a tune in a key that the ninth arch amplified, and she let it stop mid-phrase, unresolved, because an unfinished cadence turns a head faster than a shout. [423] And at 9:13 she placed the bishop upright on the balustrade at the exact height of an averted gaze, where starlight came through the arch and struck the ivory white. [453] This was her craft, the thing she had left the harbor authority to practice: orchestrated serendipity, luck manufactured to the tolerances of a bridge. [477] Wilderness, calculated. [479] He came through at 9:14. [484] The gull twitched overhead; the cadence broke; his chin lifted the necessary inch; and the bishop caught him. [502] He stopped. [504] Mira did not look at him, because being watched turns a gift into a trap. [519] She heard him lift the piece and turn it, and heard the small human sound a person makes when something happens to them for the first time in years. [548] Then he did what her ledgers had not predicted. [557] He set the bishop back down on the balustrade, took a stub of pencil, and wrote on the stone beneath it: WHITE TO MOVE. [581] Then he walked on, and only at the fourth column did he glance up at the stars. [598] The tide came in and took the pencil marks off the stone; the second act filled the arcade with warm, ordinary people who tipped well in fair weather. [626] Mira played badly and did not care. [633] She had spent eleven nights engineering a stranger's astonishment and had received her own instead, unplanned, unbudgeted, arriving from a direction no bishop could travel. [658] The next evening she came early and chalked a board on the dry mosaic of the third arch, sixty-four squares, and set the chipped bishop on its diagonal. [686] At 9:14 the gray coat stopped without any gull, any broken cadence, any arithmetic at all. [702] He crouched, considered, and moved a chalk pawn with his thumb. [713] "It floods at eleven," he said. [719] "Then we play fast," Mira said, "and tomorrow the board is new." [731] Above them the stars held their fixed positions, which she had always found reassuring and now found merely useful: a grid to improvise against. [755] The water was already whispering at the lower steps, counting down her interval, and Mira, between acts, was exactly where she meant to be. [779]
