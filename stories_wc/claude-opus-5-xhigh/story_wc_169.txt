The grotto breathed twice an hour, and Ines had learned to breathe with it. [14] Tide-polished walls curved above her like the inside of a shell that had given up on pearls. [31] She was a logical dreamer, which meant she wrote her hopes as equations and refused to fudge the constants. [50] Nine years ago her balloon had come down in this cove, and the sea had kept her brother Tobias and returned only the altitude gauge. [75] The brass gauge hung now from a spike of rock, salt-fogged, its needle trembling at numbers no one in the village could account for. [99] It did not measure the grotto's air. [106] It measured how high a possibility had climbed. [114] Ines had proved this the patient way, by sending up trial balloons. [126] Each was a palm-sized bladder of oiled silk, warmed by a thimble of lamp oil, and each carried a different arrangement of decorations: three cowries and a blue bead; four cowries in a diamond; a spiral of gull down and green glass. [168] Creating patterns with decorations was not superstition; it was notation. [178] The grotto read the patterns the way a lock reads a key, and the gauge answered. [194] Diamonds of cowrie lifted the needle eleven feet, then stalled. [204] Spirals rose fast and fell faster. [210] Lucidly watchful, she filled a ledger with altitudes and shell-counts, and slowly the numbers stopped being numbers and became a grammar. [231] What the grotto did, she came to understand, was hold the same nine years across parallel timelines, stacked like pages in a wet book. [255] In some pages the fishing fleet had not rotted at anchor. [266] In some, Tobias walked the quay with a limp and a bad joke. [279] A trial balloon, released with the right notation, could climb into one of those pages and come back carrying its weather. [300] The village above was dying quietly, which is how villages usually do it. [313] Ines wanted to revitalize it, and she was too honest to promise anything she could not derive. [330] That was her whole motive: to puzzle out new equations for hope, ones that would hold when tested, because the old ones had failed and left people ashamed of having believed them. [362] On the autumn spring tide, the grotto rose to her knees and she launched the eighty-first balloon. [379] Its decorations were arranged from her own error columns: two cowries, one bead, a gap where she had always placed a third shell out of habit. [405] The gap was the discovery: hope required an absence to expand into. [417] The balloon went up through the blowhole and the needle climbed past eleven, past forty, past the little brass stop Tobias had once filed down for fun. [444] Then the grotto turned over. [449] It was not violence; it was serene upheaval, the way a sleeper turns and the whole bed becomes a different country. [470] Water stood still and lucid around her waist. [478] Light came from below, from every page at once, and she saw the stacked years fan open like a hand of cards. [500] In one, she was drowning; in one, she was old and laughing; in one, she had never come back to this coast at all. [524] The logical dreamer in her did not choose the sweetest page, because choosing was not the operation available. [542] What she could do was write the notation on the grotto wall in shell and glass, large enough for anyone to copy, small enough to test. [568] So she did, patiently, while the water held its breath. [578] When the tide fell, the grotto was only a grotto again, and the gauge hung dead at zero, its needle finally still. [600] Ines climbed the goat path with wet boots and a ledger that now ended in a proof. [617] She did not tell the village that Tobias was alive somewhere; that would have been a lie in her timeline and cruelty in theirs. [641] She told them about trial balloons. [647] She showed the children how to fold silk, how to set cowries with a deliberate gap, how to read a needle without flinching. [670] By spring, fourteen households were launching small hopes and recording where they failed, and the failures were interesting enough to keep people awake and talking. [695] Two boats were repaired for no better reason than that the arithmetic of the grotto suggested they might be needed. [715] That, Ines wrote in the ledger's last margin, is how a place is revitalized: not by a miracle, but by a testable ascent, repeated. [739] She kept the gauge on her windowsill, lucidly watchful for the day its needle stirred again. [755] Some mornings, when the sea was flat and the light came in low, she was almost sure it did.
