The orchard had eleven hours left, and Mirela Vance had promised a ghost she would not be late. [18] She parked behind the conservatory, where the sheet music archive slumped under fifty years of damp, and carried her equipment in through a door that had forgotten how to lock. [48] Her column ran Thursdays, page nine: "Whispers and Wormwood," three hundred words of who left whom and which councilman's house had gone cold in the corners. [74] Gossip and exorcism were the same trade, she liked to say: both were the art of getting something unsaid to come out into the light. [99]

Delia Okonjo was waiting between the shelves, holding the antique perfume bottle in both hands like a bird she had caught by accident. [122] "They start cutting at dawn," Delia said. [129] "The whole orchard, forty trees, and she is in every one of them." [142]

Mirela set down her case and began unspooling copper wire along the archive's iron shelving, because ghosts do not answer voices; they answer through induced currents, the way a struck bell answers another bell across a room. [179] The archive was the right place for it. [187] Every folio in the room held silence pressed flat and waiting, ten thousand songs nobody had played since the war, and silence like that carried a charge. [214]

"Tell me about her," Mirela said, clipping a lead to the shelf marked ORATORIOS, D-H. [229] "My sister Ruth kept the orchard while I kept the accounts, and in 1994 I sold her half of it without asking." [251] Delia turned the perfume bottle so the lamplight moved inside it, amber and cut glass, still holding an inch of something that smelled of bergamot and rot. [278] "She died angry, I never said sorry, and I want to say it before the trees go." [295]

Mirela had heard eight hundred versions of that sentence, and she never once believed the dead cared about the apology as much as the living did. [321] That was fine; the debt was still owed. [329]

"Understand the machine before you hope at it," Mirela said, setting the bottle in the coil's center. [346] "A ghost is a wet match. [352] You can strike it, and you will get smoke, maybe a spark, but do not order dinner by that light." [372]

Delia almost smiled. [375] "You always talk sideways." [379] "Straight lines are for people selling something," Mirela said, and threw the switch. [392]

The current went around the room and the room went strange. [403] Dust lifted off the folios and hung there, glittering, refusing to fall. [415] Somewhere in the stacks a page turned itself, then another, a paper wind moving down the aisles. [432] The perfume bottle rang like a wineglass, and the smell of bergamot went through the archive as if a woman had just walked past. [456]

"Ruth," Delia said. [459] Nothing spoke. [461] What came instead was music: the coil hummed at sixty cycles, and inside the hum, thin and out of tune, a hymn Mirela did not know and Delia plainly did. [491] "That's the one she hummed in the trees," Delia whispered. [501] "Ruth, listen, I sold the land and I have been ashamed for thirty years." [515]

The hymn faltered, dropped a semitone, and kept going, and Mirela watched the needle on her meter climb and stick. [535] This was the part she never printed in the column: it does not work the way people want. [553] Miracles arrive imperfect, secondhand, a little bent, like a letter delivered to the right street and the wrong door. [572] Ruth did not speak; the dead rarely learn new tricks, and Ruth had never been one to answer while she was still angry. [595] But the hymn went on, and it was a door left open, and Delia talked into it for forty minutes while the dust hung and the copper warmed. [623] She apologized badly, repeated herself, cried, and said the true thing last, the way people do. [639]

At 5:40 the current dropped, the dust came down all at once like snow giving up, and the archive was only a cold room full of unplayed songs. [667] "Did she hear me," Delia asked, not really a question. [677] Mirela wound the copper back onto its spool. [685] "You cannot reconnect a rope by staring at both ends," she said. [697] "You tie a knot, and the knot is ugly, and it holds." [709]

Outside, in the hush before the saws, the orchard stood in fog with its forty trees breathing. [726] Delia walked out to meet the crew with the perfume bottle open in her fist, letting the last inch of bergamot go into the cold air, and Mirela wrote it down for Thursday, page nine, three hundred words, most of them true. [768]
