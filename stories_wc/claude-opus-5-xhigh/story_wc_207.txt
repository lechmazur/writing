For eleven years the cement factory breathed white dust over the valley, and Marta Vey could not smell the rain. [20] Petrichor, she explained to anyone patient enough, is not a smell but a message: oils from dry stone, bacteria waking, water arriving. [42] Kiln dust flattened all of it into chalk. [50] Then, after the factory shut down, the first September storm came through and the valley exhaled something she had waited her whole adult life to bottle. [76] She was, the town agreed, sensibly eccentric. [83] Steel-toed boots, a laminated tide chart, a ledger with columns for temperature, wind, and dust load; also two hundred amber vials of rain-smell, each labeled with the hour and the stone it rose from. [117] The vials were inventory. [121] Her real work was the compass. [126]

It had belonged to the Rothsay expedition, nine people who walked into the limestone cliffs in 1931 and never walked out; a farmer found the compass forty years later in a sinkhole, brass lid still hinged, needle still trembling. [165] Marta read objects with her hands. [171] Psychometry, her aunt had called it, and taught her the discipline of it: dampen the fingertips, breathe out, let the object speak before you decide what it should say. [200] Most things confessed easily. [204] A doorknob gave her forty years of hurried hands; a factory glove gave heat, boredom, and a man thinking about his daughter's arithmetic homework. [228] The compass gave her one image only, and it never resolved: black water with a light under it, and the sensation of waking up inside another waking. [255]

That was what she wanted. [260] Marta had spent her life explaining smells to people who preferred not to ask, and she had grown tired of explanations that worked. [283] She wanted to find the place where explanations fail, and stand in it, and not be afraid. [300]

The quarry gates hung open now, chained by nobody, and the pumps that had kept the lower galleries dry were off. [321] Water had come back into the hill the way blood comes back into a slept-on arm. [337] She went in on a Tuesday, sensibly: helmet, two lamps, a whistle, a note on her kitchen table giving her route and her return hour. [362] She carried the compass in her palm, not her pocket, because it steered better than magnetism allowed. [379]

To delve is to trade one kind of silence for another. [390] Above, the valley silence was an absence of machines; below, past the crumbled adit and the old ladder and the crawl the miners had called the Hinge, the silence was a resonant hush, a held note in the throat of the rock. [432] Every drip arrived with its own small chord. [440]

The subterranean lagoon opened beneath the cliffs like a room that had been waiting politely. [455] Water so clear her lamp found the floor nine meters down; flowstone the color of tea and bone; and under the surface, faintly, a green glow that was probably algae and probably was not. [489] The air smelled of petrichor, absurdly, impossibly, here where rain had never fallen. [502]

She knelt, wet her fingers in the lagoon, and put both hands flat on the stone lip. [519] The reading came sideways, all nine at once. [527] The Rothsay party had reached this shore in November 1931 with three lamps and no rope, and they had not drowned and had not starved. [552] They had sat down in the hush and gone through a series of hinged awakenings - each one a door swinging off the last, each waking revealing that the previous waking had been sleep - until the ninth opened onto something with no hinge on the far side. [598]

Marta felt the first door move in her, and understood the mechanism at once: this was what the compass had been trembling toward for ninety years. [624] She could describe the second awakening; the third she could only compare to a smell. [639] At the fourth, language sat down at the shore and refused to go further, and Marta, laughing without sound, let it stay. [661] She had found it: the place where explanations fail. [670] It was not empty and it was not kind and it was not unkind. [684] It was attentive. [687]

But she had also written a return hour on her kitchen table, and being sensibly eccentric is mostly a matter of keeping promises to your own handwriting. [714] So at the fifth door she stopped, and closed it the way you close a lid on brass hinges, gently, without locking it. [737] She left the lost expedition compass on the flowstone, needle finally still, pointing at the water. [753]

Above, rain had started on the dry cliffs. [761] She stood in it a long while before she thought to open a vial, and then she didn't. [779] Some things you carry unlabeled. [784]
