After the static shock, we could not hear each other anymore. [11] It happened in the studio, of all places, with the shutter open and forty seconds left on the exposure. [30] Tomas reached for my wrist, our fingers snapped blue, and the warm room inside my skull where he had lived for six years went dark. [55] The telepathy pill recall notice arrived by mail the next morning, printed on paper thin enough to see through. [74] Coelis, batch 40 through 61, withdrawn voluntarily; users may experience abrupt link termination and phantom sensation. [90] Tomas read it twice and laughed, because he had never believed in the pills to begin with. [107] He was my affectionate skeptic, the man who kissed my knuckles while explaining that what we called telepathy was probably just two people paying ferocious attention. [133] I had always argued with him. [138] Now I needed him to be right. [145]

My light painting studio is a black box on the fourth floor, twelve feet of nothing but tripods, blackout cloth, and a shelf of little lights. [171] I paint with them: I open the shutter, walk into the dark, and draw. [185] For six years I had drawn what Tomas thought, tracing the shapes his mind handed me, and the gallery called it invention. [207] It was transcription. [210]

So we sat on the studio floor with the recall notice between us and began to devise a replacement. [229] Tomas proposed we work through somatic markers, which was his own field before he taught high school biology. [247] Every thought, he said, drags a body with it: dread tightens the left jaw, tenderness warms the sternum, a lie cools the fingertips. [270] If we could catalogue those, we would not need a pill to read each other. [285] We would need only patience, and a very careful notation. [295] That was my part. [299]

I keep a muscovite geode sliver in a felt pouch, split from a rock my mother cracked open in Ontario when I was nine. [323] Mica peels into sheets thinner than a fingernail, and when you tape one over a penlight it does not blur the beam; it divides it, again and again, into a fractally precise fan of hairline rays. [359] Branch, branchlet, branchlet of branchlet, all the way down to threads the camera can barely hold. [375] I had never found a use for it. [383] Now I had one. [387]

Each night we ran the same protocol. [393] Tomas sat in the dark and remembered something specific, and reported only the body: heat behind the ears, a hollow under the ribs, the left heel pressing down. [421] I opened the shutter and walked the penlight through the air, one branch per marker, mica splitting each line into its smaller truths. [444] By the fortieth night the darkroom wall held eighty prints, and the prints had begun to rhyme. [461] Grief bent left, wanting climbed, and shame folded in on itself like a fern. [475] The photographs were a map, and what they mapped were hidden pathways: not the ones the pill had bulldozed open, but the older, dimmer ones underneath, the ones we had used before we ever swallowed anything. [511] My whole aim was to illuminate them, literally, one long exposure at a time. [525]

On the sixty-second night I did not tell him what I was remembering. [538] I only sat, and let my face do whatever it did. [549] Tomas took the penlight from me, taped the mica over it himself, and drew in the dark for forty seconds. [569] The print came up as a low, wide arc with three roots and a break in the middle: my mother's kitchen, the rock, the hammer coming down. [596] He had never been given that memory. [603] He had only ever watched me. [609] He set the print down and kissed my knuckles, which is his way of conceding nothing and everything at once. [629] Attention, he said, and I let him have it. [638]

We are hanging ninety of them in April. [646] I titled the series Willowglass, after the color the studio window goes at dusk, green and thin and holding light it cannot quite keep. [670] That is the honest name for what we have: not the old certainty, nothing so solid as a pill. [689] A breakable brightness we have to keep making by hand, in the dark, forty seconds at a time.
