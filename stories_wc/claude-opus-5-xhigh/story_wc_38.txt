The lab had been sunk on purpose, and Wren had always known which purpose. [14] She swam down the throat of the stairwell with a lamp strapped to her wrist, past desks bolted to the floor, past chairs drifting like patient dogs. [41] In the highest corner of Ward Nine, where a bubble of stale air still clung to the ceiling, a spider had begun a web. [65] Wren surfaced into that pocket, wiped her face, and decided she would be finished before the spider was. [83] On the ledge sat a tin box, and inside it the hangman's dice: six bone cubes, each face a letter, worn smooth by the overseer's thumbs. [109] Every evening the overseer had rolled them, and the letters that landed upward chose which subject was walked to the pressure tanks. [131] Wren knew the dice the way one knows a childhood song: without deciding to. [145] Beside the box lay a journal wrapped in oilcloth, and when she opened it her chest went strange and warm, the way a stranger's face does when it turns out to be kin. [178] The entries were coded, of course. [184] Wren was the Navy's best code breaker because she did not read ciphers, she sang them; she hummed frequency into rhythm until the letters fell into meter and confessed. [213] This cipher answered her in her own cadence. [221] First entry: I am writing my fear down so that it becomes a lock, and I am the only key. [241] Second: they can drown a girl who screams, but they cannot open a girl who has made her trembling into arithmetic. [262] Third: the key is not written, it is danced. [271] Wren remembered, then, in the way a body remembers water. [281] There had been a subject in Ward Nine who would not speak to the overseer, not one word in fourteen months, and who explained everything she knew through dance: a turn of the wrist for a vowel, a stamped heel for a repetition, a slow spiral for the shift of the wheel. [333] The others had learned it from her, watching in the exercise yard, calling it stretching. [348] That was how the plan had traveled the ward without ever crossing a page: via coded journal entries for the parts that had to keep, and dance for the key that could not be confiscated. [383] The subject's name had been Wren. [389] They had pulled her out of the flood with her lungs full and her memory gone, and the Navy, admiring her ear, had given her a uniform and a new life and never told her where the ear came from. [429] Now she stood chest-deep in her own handwriting. [437] She danced it there on the narrow shelf of the ventilation gallery, hips and wrists moving in the lamp's small light, and the cipher opened like a door that had only been leaning shut. [471] The keyword, danced first and written never, was one word long: liberate. [483] The final entries were a manifest: eleven names, eleven transfer papers, eleven facilities inland where the overseer had sold the subjects he had not drowned, each one still listed as deceased. [514] Dead girls could be kept forever; that had always been the arithmetic. [526] Wren read the eleven names twice, and did not weep, and did not shout. [540] The girl who wrote this journal had built her fear into a strongbox so that someone steady could carry it out, and Wren intended to be steady. [567] She copied the manifest into her waterproof slate in plain, unlovely letters, because plain letters were what a tribunal could read. [588] Then she took up the hangman's dice. [595] There was an old, hot temptation to roll them once, to give the overseer's own game a chance to name him. [616] She did not roll. [620] She put the six bones in her pocket as evidence, uncast, refusing the last thing he had wanted from her, which was a wager. [644] It was not a triumphant feeling; it was calmer than that, and harder to break. [659] Above her, the spider had finished the first spiral of its web and started the second. [675] Wren tucked the journal against her ribs, filled her lungs, and went back down into the flooded stairwell to swim eleven women out of the register of the dead. [704] On the ascent she hummed the old cadence, one hand keeping time against the rail, and the water carried it up ahead of her like a rumor of release. [733]
