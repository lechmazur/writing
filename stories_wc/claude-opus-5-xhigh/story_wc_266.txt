The leash broke on the pier at four o'clock, and Bell went into the fog like a thrown stone. [19] For eleven years that strap had been the shape of Mara's evenings; now it hung from her fist, frayed and light. [40] The ferry guides found her still standing there an hour later, watching the veiled ridgeway rise out of the water like a spine. [63] They were sisters, Ossa and Del, wise about water, and they knew every submerged yard of that long drowned road. [83] "Dogs follow the ridge," Del said, "because it is the only dry thing out there." [98] "Then take me across," Mara said. [104] Ossa produced her ticket punch, a brass thing with a star-shaped die, and held it up so Mara could see the tiny points. [127] "A star means you intend to come back," she said. [137] "A plain hole means you don't know yet." [145] Mara looked at the frayed leash and asked for the plain hole. [157] The sisters did not argue; they were gently persistent instead, the way water is. [171] All the way out, they asked her small questions and waited a long time for each answer. [188] The ferry did not traverse the channel in a straight line. [199] It went through indirect routes, looping around sunken orchards and the roofs of the old toll houses, because the direct way was all snags and stone. [225] Del steered by humming, a low shoreline lullaby that matched the slap of water on the hull; when the pitch changed, she turned. [248] "Everything worth reaching," Ossa said, "is reached sideways." [256] Mara told them about Bell then: the arthritic hips, the eleven years, the way she had built her life narrow enough to hold a dog's schedule and nothing else. [285] She had not left the harbor town since the flood. [295] "Then you are not only looking for a dog," Del said. [306] "You are looking for where your unused courage has been waiting." [317] Mara laughed, and the sound came out wrong, and she stopped. [328] They put her ashore at the ridge's high shoulder, where mist lay in the grass like fleece and the light was already going amber. [352] "We keep the vigil at twilight," Ossa said, unshuttering a lamp. [363] "Anything lost on the ridgeway comes toward a light between the last sun and the first star." [380] "But you must call, and you must not leave, and the calling is the hard part." [396] It was. [398] Mara's voice came out thin as thread the first time, swallowed by all that grey water and grass. [416] The sisters did not call for her. [423] They only stood at her shoulders and hummed, leaving the whole silence for her to fill. [439] So she filled it. [443] She called until her throat scraped, until the amber went violet, until the far shore was only a rumor and the lamp was the only warm thing on the ridge. [473] She called into places she had not spoken to in years. [484] Somewhere in that hour she understood that the courage had never been elsewhere; it had simply been coiled, unused, waiting at the end of a leash she had been holding with both hands. [517] Bell came out of the fog at the first star, wet to the belly, limping, insulted, entirely alive. [535] She had followed the ridge the way the ferry did, sideways, around the drowned orchards. [550] Mara sat down in the wet grass and let the dog put a cold nose against her neck, and she cried the way you cry when nothing bad has happened. [580] On the crossing back, Ossa held out the punch. [589] "Star," Mara said. [592] "Two, if you have them." [597] "I want to come out here again, in daylight, and go further." [609] Del laughed and cut two stars into the card, and the ferry went home the long way, humming, as it always did. [631] Mara held the leash in her lap, broken and useless, and did not throw it overboard. [647] Some things you keep because they mark the hour the world got wider. [660]
