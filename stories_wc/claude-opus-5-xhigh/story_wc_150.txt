The bus left me at a crossroads that my grandmother had described only once, and wrongly. [16] She had fled Sarnovo at nineteen and never finished the sentence that would have brought her home. [33] I am the returnee, the one the family sent back with her ashes and her arguments. [49] For three weeks I found the village by purposeful wandering, asking after olive presses and old names, refusing maps because maps only show what someone already believed. [76] Sarnovo turned out to be nine houses, a dry fountain, and a warden named Petar who was warmly curious about my shoes. [98] He asked where they had walked, and when I said four countries, he nodded as though shoes were a kind of scripture. [120] Then he took me down into the orchard. [128]

It lies beneath the hill: a catacomb orchard, where fig and quince trees were planted centuries ago in the ossuary's open galleries, and their roots snake between the bones. [157] Light falls through shafts in the ceiling in slow coins. [167] A femur is cradled in a root like a finger in a ring. [180] I stood there a long time, unwilling to breathe too loudly, enchanted and cautious in equal measure, the way you are with something that might still be holy. [208]

Petar said the pilgrims used to stop here. [216] Sarnovo sat on the road to the coast, and the shrine above the orchard was where travelers broke their journeys, ate figs from the dead, and slept. [243] The war rerouted the road, the pilgrims stopped coming, and the village stopped believing anything would arrive. [260] He wanted help with the tomb in the eighth gallery. [270]

Excavating it took eleven days, and those eleven days are the whole of what I have to tell. [288] On the first day we lifted silt and quince leaves and found the lip of a limestone slab. [306] On the fourth we found the ribs of a woman who had been buried with a walking staff and a purse of foreign coins. [330] A pilgrim, then, whose journey had been interrupted permanently, and whom the village had kept anyway. [346] On the seventh day my trowel struck something square. [355] It was a sundial base, a drum of pale stone with a socket at its center and hours cut around the rim in a script Petar could not read. [384] The gnomon was gone, so the dial had been telling no time at all for six hundred years. [402]

I turned it over and my hands went cold. [410] Cut into the underside was a mason's mark: a fish inside a wheel. [423] My grandmother wore that mark on a pendant she never explained, and she was buried in it, four countries away. [443] Her family had cut stone here; the dial was, in the only sense that matters, hers. [459]

I had come to Sarnovo out of duty and an old, embarrassed hunger. [472] I had stopped believing in inheritance, in continuity, in any story that claimed a person could be from somewhere. [491] But faith is not restored by argument; it is restored by weight in the hands. [506]

On the eleventh day we closed the tomb and carried the base up into the light. [522] We decided to rebuild the shrine. [528] Not restore, Petar insisted, since nobody remembered the shape of it; rebuild, using what the orchard gave us. [546] We set the sundial base on a plinth of rubble at the crossroads where the bus had left me. [565] For the gnomon I used my grandmother's pendant chain wrapped around an iron nail from her trunk, angled north as she never was. [588]

The shadow fell across the eleventh hour and moved, and the nine houses came out to watch it move. [607] Someone laughed the way people laugh when they are frightened of hoping. [619] Petar brought figs from the orchard, which is to say from the dead, and we ate them at the crossroads like travelers. [641]

I am staying through the winter. [647] I want to learn the script on the rim, and I want to see whether anyone stops. [664] My grandmother's journey out was interrupted; mine in was interrupted too, by eleven days and a stone. [681] That is the thing nobody tells you about interrupted journeys: the interruption is where the orchard grows. [698] Roots do not travel in straight lines; they wander with purpose, and they find the water, and they hold the bones together. [720]
