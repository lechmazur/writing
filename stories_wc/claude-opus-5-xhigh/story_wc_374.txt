Grandmother's compass ring was tin, and it never once pointed north. [11] She wore it anyway, all through the years when the sky was only weather and weather was only weather. [30] I wear it now on the bluff, where the lavender goes purple-black under the aurora, and I still do not believe in it. [53] Belief is not my job. [58] My job is taste. [62] They seeded my tongue at nineteen and my skin at twenty, so I read the air by flavor and glow while I do it, a walking lantern the survey crews call the luminous skeptic. [96] The glow was supposed to be a courtesy to my colleagues; mostly it means moths love me and no one can lie to me in the dark. [123]

Tonight the forests hold their elections, and I am here to settle an argument with myself. [139] Every seven years the mycelium tallies its vote -- which seeds travel, which stands drink first, which ridge gets given up to fire -- and every seven years the tally leaks into the air as taste. [173] I taste it as iron, then apricot, then something like the rind of an old coin. [189] That is not superstition; that is chemistry with an accent. [199]

What I do not accept, what I have refused for eleven years of field notes, is Ovie. [216] Ovie carries weather. [220] He walks into a dry valley and within an hour there is drizzle in his eyelashes; he sits down to grieve and hail comes to sit with him. [248] There are perhaps four hundred like him worldwide, and the agency files them under Anomalous Personal Climate, which is a way of saying we would rather not learn why. [277] I want to learn why. [282]

So I asked the readers. [287] Three hundred of us, tongues seeded, scattered across two continents, each reporting nothing but flavor shifts: salt at the delta, green pepper on the tundra, burnt sugar in the pine belt at 4 a.m. [321] No one of us knows a direction. [328] But lay the reports over each other and a needle appears out of nowhere, made of nothing but small opinions about taste -- an emergent compass, pointing. [354] For three weeks it has pointed here: a lavender bluff above a drowned town, on the night of the elections. [374]

Ovie arrives at midnight with rain walking behind him like a dog. [386] He is not alone. [390] They come up the switchbacks in ones and twos, thirty-one of them, weather-carriers from four countries, none of whom were told to converge and all of whom knew the date. [420] Above us the aurora comes down lower than any instrument says it should, green folding into the lavender until the hillside looks terrestrially cosmic, dirt and starlight sharing one bed. [450] My tongue floods with the taste of rain on hot slate, and then with something older, something I have not tasted since I was small: my grandmother's kitchen, the metal tang of a tin ring pressed to my lip while she told me the ground was thinking. [497]

"You tasted us," Ovie says. [502]

"I tasted a pattern," I say, because I am still me. [513] "Patterns don't have reasons." [517]

"This one does," he says, and kneels, and puts his hand flat in the lavender. [532] Thirty-one others kneel with him, and the rain stops all at once, as if a room had gone quiet to hear a name. [555]

Here is what I understood, standing above them with my skin throwing its useless light: the forests have no mouth, so they grew one out of us. [582] The carriers are not cursed and not blessed; they are ballots that walk. [595] Every drought a person has grieved through, every flood that took a town like the one under this water, gets written into their weather and carried to the count. [624] People who carry weather are people the ground trusts to remember accurately. [636] The vote needs witnesses; witnesses need bodies; bodies pick up habits, and one habit is rain. [652]

I write it down. [656] I do not write down what happened to the ring, because the agency would ask for it. [673] At 3:14 a.m., while thirty-two hands lay flat on a purple hill and the aurora leaned in like a grandmother over a cradle, the tin needle in that cheap little ring swung off its dead spot and held, steady, pointing at the kneeling crowd. [717] Not north. [719] Them. [720]

I have a hundred sober explanations and I will publish one of them, and I will keep the other thing too, the thing that is not science and not quite faith: an old woman's tin promise, finally coming true one century late, in a future she would have found gaudy and beautiful. [772] Then the taste changed to apricot again, and the forests, having decided something, sent everyone home. [788]
