The Vanishing Point Gallery was not a gallery so much as a corridor that lied about its length. [18] Its walls leaned inward by a hair, its floorboards narrowed, and every visitor swore the far end held a door. [38] There was no door, only a black square of painted wood and, behind it, the sea wind. [55] Ilse rented the place for a coin a month because nobody else wanted a room that swallowed perspective. [73] She was the town's charcoal portrait ash scatterer, and I was her apprentice, which meant I carried the tray. [92] The work was simple to describe and terrible to learn. [102] When someone was dying, we came and drew them, and when they had gone, we burned the drawing. [120] The point was never the picture. [126] The point was to crystallize a fleeting emotion - one instant of peace, if we could find it - so that what we scattered was not merely a person but a moment worth keeping. [158] Ilse wore a mask made from shattered porcelain shards, wired together with silver thread. [172] The shards came from her mother's teacups, broken the night the fishing fleet did not come home. [189] She said a dying person should not have to look at a stranger's whole face; fragments are kinder, because fragments let you see whoever you need to see. [217] That was the first lesson: comfort arrives through incomplete information. [227] The second lesson was that Ilse never lied. [235] She weaves truth carefully, the way a mender weaves a net, leaving holes exactly where the water should pass through. [255] In November we were called to old Wenna Sarl, who had been rocking in the same chair for forty years, waiting for her son Emrys to come up the road from the harbor. [288] Emrys had drowned in the same winter that broke the teacups. [299] Everyone in town knew, and everyone in town had decided, by a silence so old it felt like law, that Wenna would not be told. [324] So she lived inside the mirage of safety we had built for her, a harbor with no water in it. [344] I set out the charcoal; Ilse set out her face of broken cups. [357] Wenna looked up at the mask and smiled, and I understood that she was seeing her mother, or the sea calmed, or something I would never be told. [385] "Did a boat come in?" she asked. [392] "A boat came in at Harnesk this morning," Ilse said, "with the tide, and men walked up the quay in the light." [414] All of it was true. [419] None of it was Emrys. [424] Wenna's face opened - not joy exactly, but the loosening of a knot held for forty years - and Ilse's hand moved before I could breathe. [448] Charcoal went across the paper in six strokes, and there it was: an old woman hearing footsteps on a quay that did not concern her. [473] It was the most beautiful thing I have ever been ashamed of. [485] The rocking went on a while after that, slower, the runners ticking like a clock deciding to retire. [503] Ilse knelt beside her and held the mask where Wenna could keep it in view. [518] And then, as the old rocking chair finally slows to a stop - I still see it in the present tense, I cannot help it - Ilse said, "Begin." [545] We burned the portrait on the copper tray, and the paper curled into a black flower. [561] I powdered the char in the mortar until it was finer than flour, until the woman and the moment were one grey weightless thing. [585] Then we carried her to the gallery. [592] You scatter at the vanishing point because that is the only place in town where a straight line ends in nothing you can name, and the sea wind takes what you give it. [625] The ash went out through the black square in a slow grey ribbon, and for a moment - I swear to this - it held the shape of a woman listening. [654] Ilse gave me the mask that night. [661] "You will want to tell them everything," she said, "and you will think that is respect." [677] "It is only the wish to be forgiven while they can still do it." [691] I wear the shards now, and I have learned what she meant: safety is a mirage, and a mirage is still water to a woman dying of thirst. [719] I do not lie to the dying. [726] I only choose which true things to carry up the stairs, and I hold my broken face where they can see it, and I keep my hand ready for the six strokes that will outlast us both. [763]
