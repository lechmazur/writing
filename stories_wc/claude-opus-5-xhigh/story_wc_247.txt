The harbor glowed the way it did only in October, plankton lighting the water from beneath so the moored boats seemed to float on a low green fire. [28] Mira had come back for it, and for the Vigil, and for nobody in particular, because there was nobody left who would know her face. [53] Thirty years is long enough for a town to close over a name like water over a dropped stone. [72] She walked the quay with her grandfather's camera hanging at her chest, an old black Rolleiflex with no film in it and no film to be had. [99] She used it anyway, kneeling here, crouching there, framing the space between two hulls, the alley behind the salt warehouse, the dead pocket under the bell tower stairs. [127] She was mapping silence. [131] Deaf since a fever at six, she had learned young that silence is not flat; it has rooms, corners, thresholds, places where a shout goes to die and places where a whisper carries like a thrown rope. [168] The camera helped her hold each place still long enough to write it down. [182] Ovid found her at the third berth, exactly as he had found her at nine years old. [199] "You are still stealing pictures of nothing," he whispered, because whispering was all his ruined throat had allowed him for forty years, and because he had been the Council's advisor so long that quiet had become his instrument of power. [239] She answered in sign, her hands unhurried, and he read them the way he always had, lips moving slightly, translating her into his own hoarse tongue. [265] Not nothing, she told him: the shape of it. [274] Tomorrow night the Vigil would hold the harbor mute from moonrise to moonset, no bell, no voices, seventy drowned names honored by an absence. [298] Mira intended to disrupt it. [302] She had spent three decades in other cities building a notation that turned handshapes into phrasing, sign into score, and she had come home to have it played in the only hall she trusted: the harbor itself. [339] Her map told her where to put the players: two horns in the dead pocket, cellos on the fish steps, a clarinet on the coal barge where sound skated flat across the water. [372] Scattered by her charts, their separate lines would meet only at the middle of the basin, where she would stand, and where the chord would arrive as a pressure in her ribs. [404] Ovid listened with his head tilted, an old man who had built a career on advising against exactly this sort of thing. [426] "They will call it desecration," he whispered. [433] She signed back that a silence you cannot leave is not a tribute, it is a wall, and that she meant to translate it, not break it. [460] He was quiet for a while, and then he told her he would find her nineteen musicians. [477] He did. [479] It is easy to forget how persuasive a whisper is when the whisperer has spent a lifetime being the last person in the room to speak. [505] They came for her, too, though none of them knew her name; she was simply a small grey woman who explained a hurricane of a piece with her hands and made every one of them want to be inside it. [545] At moonrise the harbor went silent, and the water lit up green, and the town stood along the rails with their hats off. [568] Mira waded to the sandbar at the center of the basin, luminous to the knees, and raised her hands. [587] She conducted in sign: not beats, but words, the drowned names she had learned as a girl, each name a handshape and each handshape a cue. [613] From the dead pocket, a horn; from the fish steps, the cellos, arriving late by design, the delay written into her map. [635] The sound crossed the basin along paths she had charted with a camera that had never taken a picture, and it met above the sandbar, and it fell on her like weather. [667] She could not hear it. [672] She felt it in her sternum, in the water, in the glowing rings her shaking hands sent out across the green. [693] On the rails, people were weeping and also laughing, which is the correct response to being given back seventy names in a language you did not know you had lost. [723] Nobody stopped it. [726] Afterward, while the players broke down their stands, the last man alive who remembered her name went along the quay, leaning toward one ear, then the next. [753] "Her name is Mira," he whispered, over and over, until the whisper had gone all the way down the rail and come back to her as a word she could read on a hundred moving mouths. [789] She lifted the empty camera and took the picture anyway. [799]
