In the mirror world, dawn is not given; it is woven. [11] I know this because I keep the loom, and because I have forgotten nearly everything else. [27] They call me the scattered memory keeper, which is a courteous way of saying my mind leaks. [44] What I hold, I hold in pieces, and the pieces live outside me: in jars, in bark, in the throats of strangers. [66]

The orchard is an alchemical greenhouse shrouded in endless night, and it is dying. [80] Under its copper ribs grow the dawn-trees, whose fruit, when ripe, unspools into morning. [94] Sixty years ago the last fruit fell unripe, and the realm has been running on remembered light ever since. [113] Light so thin the rivers have gone grey and the children are born quiet. [127]

There is one window in the greenhouse, a great pane of mica, and it is the whole of my inheritance. [147] Mica does not shatter; it peels, layer from layer, thin as a held breath. [161] My predecessor taught me that each layer holds one dawn, pressed flat, waiting for a song to lift it. [180] Not any song. [184] Dawns wake only through forgotten songs, the ones no one sings on purpose anymore. [198] That was the flaw in the design, and the mercy of it. [210]

So I went out into the dark villages with an empty basket and a leaking mind. [226] I could not simply ask; a song you can name is a song you have not forgotten. [243] Instead I sat in kitchens and let people work. [252] A fisher hummed while mending nets, four notes, no words, learned from a grandmother whose face he had lost. [271] A miller's wife sang nonsense over dough, and three houses down a widower sang the same nonsense to his dog. [291] Neither knew the other had it. [297] I brought them together, and they argued about the second verse for an hour, and by the end they were family. [318] This is the thing no one told me about forgotten songs: a shared one knits communities together faster than any law or harvest. [341] Sixty-one villages, three years, and a rope of melody running between all of them. [355]

They followed me back to the greenhouse because they wanted to see what their humming was for. [372] Under the copper ribs, among trees whose leaves had gone the color of old tin, they sang. [389] The mica window began to peel. [395] Layer after layer lifted away like sheets from a bed, and each sheet was a morning: rose, then wheat, then a green so young it hurt. [421] We caught them as they came and wove them, hand over hand, into a cloth of woven dawns. [439] The fisher's four notes made the warp. [446] The nonsense from the mill made the weft. [454]

At the orchard's center stood the youngest tree, no taller than a child, its roots hardly holding. [471] I knelt and used the cloth to swaddle it, trunk to lowest branch, the way you bind a newborn so it does not startle itself awake. [497] To rekindle a dying realm you must first give it something warm enough to sleep in. [513]

The price was written in the first ledger, and I had read it long ago, back when I could still keep a thing without setting it down. [540] The keeper's scattered memories are the fuel. [547] Every jar, every carved bark, every borrowed throat: the cloth drank them all. [560] I felt sixty years go, gently, like a boat untied. [570] I had been bound to that window since I was nine years old. [583] Being emptied did not feel like theft. [589] It felt like release. [593]

The sapling took the warmth and pushed one bud, and the bud opened, and light came out of it the way water comes out of rock: unreasonably, all at once. [623] The endless night broke over the mirror world in pieces, village by village, along the rope of melody. [641]

I do not remember my name now. [647] The fisher tells me it was Wren, and I believe him, because he is the sort who mends things. [666] They have taught me the four notes, and I hum them while I water the young trees. [683] The mica is down to its final layer, one dawn thick, and someone else will have to learn the loom. [703] I am not worried. [707] Yesterday I heard a child singing something in the orchard that nobody had taught her, and I could not place it, and that is exactly how it starts. [735]
