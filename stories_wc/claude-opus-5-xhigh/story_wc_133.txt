Nobody on Saltmere slept well after the water wars, but I made a profession of it. [16] I am a painter, which on a tidal island means grinding pigment from ochre clay and stretching canvas out of drowned men's sails. [39] I am also an insomniac, so I am awake for the two hours each night when the causeway bares itself, black and glistening, like a spine. [65] Sleep is where other people thresh meaning from experience; I have to do it standing up, with a brush. [84]

For nine years I painted the same three minutes of the war and got no further into them. [102] My sister Odile is in those three minutes, holding the intake valve at Cormack's pump while the mainland boys came up the slip. [125] All she left me was an amber brooch with a mayfly inside it, caught mid-flight forty million years ago and still, somehow, in a hurry. [150]

Rain came in October, the first real rain in three years. [161] Everyone else ran out with buckets and tarps; I went out with a lantern, because rain does something on Saltmere that no living surveyor can do. [187] It finds the old works. [192] Water on a hillside is not random; it sinks where the ground has been disturbed, and it runs where somebody once made it run. [216] So I mapped the island by following rain trails, on my knees, all night, drawing the silver threads as they braided down the shoulder of Kettle Hill. [243]

By dawn they had shown me a thing that made me sit down in the mud: three separate trails converging on one flat stone above the sheep fold. [271] Not converging downhill, the way water should, but inward: three channels cut by hands, drawing water from three catchments into one throat. [293] A convergence, unearthed by one night's weather after being swallowed by turf for centuries. [307] The monks who kept the island before the lighthouse had built a cistern, and the water wars had never found it because the wars only knew how to look for pumps. [338]

I could not lift the stone alone, and I could not ask the warden, who would sell the yield to the mainland by Friday. [362] So I went to Berthe Cormack, who is eighty-one, wryly earnest about everything including her own greed, and who owns the last winch on the island. [388] "You want my winch, my nephews, and my silence," she said. [399] "That is three things, and you are a woman who owns two brushes and a dead sister's jewellery." [417]

The exchange took four seconds and nine years. [425] I put the amber brooch in her palm; she closed her hand, and did not gloat, which I have decided to remember as kindness. [449] It hurt precisely as much as I expected, which is the trouble with hoarding a grief: you learn its exact weight and nothing else. [473]

They lifted the capstone at low tide on a Tuesday. [483] Cold air came up first, smelling of wet slate and pennies. [494] Then the lantern went down on its rope and the light spread out across water, forty feet of it, still and black and absolutely sweet. [519] Berthe's nephew drank from his cupped hands and then he sat back on his heels and wept, and none of us said anything about it. [544]

I have been awake for two days and I am not tired, only full of a haggard awe I do not know where to put. [569] Here is what I keep turning over. [576] The monks dug three channels into one because they knew a single source cannot be trusted, and that meaning, like water, has to be gathered from more than one weather. [606] For nine years I ran one channel, three minutes wide, and wondered why nothing filled. [621]

Last night I primed a new canvas and painted the convergence instead: three silver threads, one dark throat, and the mud where I sat down. [646] Odile is in it somewhere, the way the mayfly was in the amber: held, hurrying, no longer mine to keep. [666] Berthe wears the brooch to chapel and tells everyone it came off a duchess. [680] I tell everyone the rain drew me a map, which is truer and gets believed less. [696]

Tonight the tide is coming in over the causeway and the island is an island again, and I still cannot sleep. [717] But I am awake differently now, the way a cistern is awake, waiting for the next weather to come and tell me something I could not have guessed. [745]
