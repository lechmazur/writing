The canyon woke the way a held breath wakes: a forged hush, hammered thin over towers of bone. [18] Vesna climbed the ninth tower before dawn, her satchel clinking with glass. [30] Below, the Well of Once waited, and above it, far off across the gorge, the pale spire in mist held the Confessor's lamp. [53] By law, a killer earned forgiveness only one way: speak the truth once into the Well, then let the canyon swallow it forever. [76] That was the price of forgiveness here -- silence, permanent and total, purchased with the one sentence the wronged most needed to hear. [98] Vesna had killed her brother Enno, though not the way the tribunal wrote it. [112] She had pulled the pin from the bone bridge to spite their father, and Enno, laughing, had stepped onto it anyway. [133] The tribunal called it an accident of maintenance and fined the ropewrights; the ropewrights were still paying. [150] So the truth was hers alone, and the ritual would take it, and everyone else would keep the lie. [169] She was an artist, and artists are defiant by trade; she had spent nine years cutting prisms from tower-bone and river glass, learning how one beam becomes twelve. [197] Her plan was the same trick in another medium: to refract a single truth into many, so no Well could drink it dry. [220] For three weeks before the ritual, she had held impromptu poetry sessions in the lamp-galleries of the bone towers. [239] Nothing announced, nothing written: she would sit among the salt-haulers at dusk and offer a line, and someone would answer it. [260] "A pin," she would say, and wait. [267] "A pin, a bridge, a boy who trusted both," a haulier would answer, not knowing what he carried. [285] The canyon of endless echoes did the rest, repeating verses down its throat until children in the tenth tower sang them while gutting fish. [309] Tam, her apprentice, found her out first. [316] He was nine and innocently mischievous, the sort of boy who stole her glass offcuts to blind goats with sunlight, and he had a listener's ear. [342] "Those poems are all one story," he said, swinging his legs over four hundred feet of nothing, "and it's yours." [362] "Then help me finish it," Vesna said. [369] At dawn the Confessor came down from the pale spire, boat-shaped hat, lamp swinging, and the towers filled with witnesses. [389] The hush they made was not peace; it was forged, beaten into shape by generations who preferred tidy stories to true ones. [411] "Speak once," the Confessor said, "and be forgiven, and be silent after." [423] Vesna knelt at the Well and spoke her truth plainly: she had pulled the pin, and Enno had died of her spite. [445] Then Tam, high in the ninth gallery, tilted the great prism she had spent nine years cutting. [462] Dawn struck it and split, and twelve blades of colored light fell across twelve galleries. [477] That was the signal, and the poems came back. [486] A pin, a bridge, a boy who trusted both -- from the salt-haulers, from the fish-children, from the ropewrights who had paid nine years for someone else's crime. [513] The canyon took the truth and could not bury it; it had been refracted, and echoes do not forget what a thousand throats keep giving back. [539] The Confessor's lamp shook. [543] "You have broken the rite," he said. [550] "There is no forgiveness for you now." [557] "I know the price," Vesna answered. [563] "I only refused to let others pay it." [571] They exiled her at noon, which she had expected, and the ropewrights' fine was struck from the ledgers, which she had hoped. [593] Tam walked her to the canyon mouth carrying the prism, still innocently pleased with himself, still stealing offcuts. [611] Behind them the towers of bone were already murmuring her brother's name in twelve tunes, wrong in the rhythm, right in the fact. [634] "Will they forgive you?" Tam asked. [640] "Someday, maybe, when they've heard it enough to mean it," she said. [652] "Forgiveness that costs nothing to say is only a lock on a door." [665] Above the mist, the pale spire dimmed as the Confessor put out his lamp, and Vesna went out into the flat, echoless world, carrying nothing she needed to hide. [694]
