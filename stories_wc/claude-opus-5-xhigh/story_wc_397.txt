The ferry left me at the drift-ice marina in the blue hour, and the harbor clinked like a drawer of spoons. [21] I had come to die on a Thursday. [29] The clinic called people like me guests; the town called us euthanasia tourists, and the woman at the pier office said it without cruelty, the way you name weather. [58] Since the aneurysm I grip reality loosely, the way a child holds a balloon string while looking at something else. [78] After waking up changed, I could not always tell which room I was in, or which decade, but I could hear better than anyone alive. [103] That was why I brought the parasol tip. [111] My grandmother's lace parasol burned with her house in the earthquake; only the brass tip came out of the ash, a little spike blackened at one edge. [138] She used to tap it against things, listening; she said every ending announces itself first as a change in the pitch of ordinary noise. [162] I had spent forty years trying to learn that sound, and I had learned it only through disaster relief volunteering: the hush inside a collapsed school before the beam gives, the tick of drying mud. [197] Now my own ending was scheduled, and I wanted to hear it coming, so I would not be surprised in the one moment I had rehearsed my whole life. [226]

The storm arrived Tuesday. [230] A surge shoved the drift ice into the marina and the floes climbed the docks like animals, snapping mooring lines, folding the fuel jetty in half. [256] By morning nine boats were under and the harbormaster stood on the seawall with a bullhorn asking anyone with hands to come. [278] The clinic postponed all appointments. [282] The counselor, apologizing, called it an unavoidable delay; I thought of it as a fold in patience, the way you fold cloth to make two distant parts of it touch. [312] My grandmother folded lace like that, and now my waiting folded: the woman queuing for death stood beside the girl queuing for water in the smoke, forty years apart, touching. [342]

They gave me a boat hook and a net and put me on the skiff with a teenager named Osku. [362] We were to trawl the slush between the pontoons for anything that mattered: gas cans, life jackets, a dog, a person. [383] Osku talked to keep me anchored. [389] He learned within an hour that I drifted, that I would call him by my brother's name, so he began saying the date every few minutes like a lighthouse. [418] In return I taught him what my grandmother taught me. [428] I made him take off his glove and press the parasol tip to the hull, and I asked what he heard. [449] Ice, he said. [452] Listen for the pitch dropping, I told him; ice about to give sounds like a question being answered. [470]

On the third day we found the Vartiainen boy under an upturned dinghy in a pocket of air, blue-lipped and furious with cold. [493] I heard him before Osku saw him: a small wrong note in the knocking of the floes, a sound with a person inside it. [517] That was the fortieth life, if you count generously, that the tapping has given back. [532]

The clinic reopened on the eleventh day. [539] I sat in the pale room with the papers and the kind doctor, and the wind came off the pack ice and pressed the window like a hand asking to be let in. [572] I am not afraid of dying; I was afraid of being taken while my attention was elsewhere, the balloon string slipping. [593] But in eleven days on the water I had heard my ending clearly, and it did not sound like the ice. [614] It sounded like a boy saying the date out loud, and it was still a long way off. [632] I told the doctor I would like to fold my patience once more. [645] She did not argue; she wrote a new date in spring and said the door stays open, which is the only mercy this town sells. [670]

Outside, the marina was clinking again, spoons in a drawer, and Osku was scraping the pontoons with a shovel. [689] I gave him the parasol tip. [695] He will need it; the ice comes every year, and someone has to stand on the dock and listen for the note that means hurry. [720] The wind off the floes is not warm, but it carries; it took the sound of the harbor and laid it against my ear, patient, unhurried, like a hand smoothing lace. [751] Spring, then. [753] I will hear it coming. [758]
