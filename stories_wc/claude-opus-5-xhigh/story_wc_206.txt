Osric repaired music boxes, and the ones he loved best were not broken at all. [15] Widows brought him cylinders that played perfectly and wept anyway, and he learned the craft the town called mending what isn't torn: listening for the flaw inside a whole thing. [45] His daughter Wren, born on the stroke of midnight, saw the world in gray tin. [60] Midnight had kept her light the way a magpie keeps a coin, and Osric meant to reclaim it. [78]

Under the clock tower lay the resonance chamber, a stone egg lined with brass tines, where the founders had struck their bargain. [100] They called it a covenant of rust: let the great mechanism corrode untouched, and midnight would take only one hour, never the dawn. [123] Nobody had oiled a gear in four hundred years. [132] Nobody had asked what midnight was collecting all that while. [142]

In his apron pocket Osric carried his grandmother's inheritance, a chip from the philosopher's stone no bigger than a fingernail and warm as a sleeping bee. [168] It did not make gold. [173] It did something rarer: the chip wilts near dishonesty, curling like a scorched petal whenever a lie is spoken within arm's reach. [195] He had tested it on merchants, on priests, and once, shamefully, on Wren, who said she did not mind the gray. [216] The chip shriveled to a husk and took three days to unfold. [228]

On the year's last night the tower's two weights hung level, and for one long minute the whole mechanism rested in the balance, neither ticking forward nor falling back. [257] That minute was the only door. [263] Osric climbed down with a coil of copper wire, a jeweler's comb, and the chip. [278] The chamber smelled of iron gone soft, of four centuries breathing. [289] Along the walls the brass tines shivered with stored tinkersong, four hundred years of confiscated mornings humming like a hive too polite to swarm. [313] And he saw them, the lights: pale coins of dawn, thousands of them, held in the rust like fish in ice. [334]

The mechanism was not broken. [339] That was the trouble: it ran flawlessly, faithfully, and every night it ran, it stole. [354] So Osric did what he did best. [361] He set the chip against the great escape wheel and tethered it there with copper wire, twisting the strands until they sang. [383] Then he recited the covenant aloud, word for word, as the founders had carved it above the door. [401] "Let the iron rust, and midnight will take only the hour." [412] The chip blackened, shrank, curled inward like a leaf on a griddle. [424]

The covenant was a lie. [429] He had suspected it for years, but hearing the little stone confess it made his hands shake on the wire. [449] The founders had not bargained; they had surrendered, then dressed the surrender in poetry so their grandchildren would never scrape the rust away. [472] Rust was the lock, and the key withheld from it was a true sentence. [486]

So he mended what was not torn. [493] He broke no tooth, bent no spring, filed nothing. [502] He simply spoke into the resonance chamber, which magnifies any small honest sound: "Midnight, you were never owed the dawn; we were only afraid of you." [528] The tines took the sentence and multiplied it a thousandfold. [538] The chip uncurled, greening at its edges like cress after rain. [549] Rust came off the wheels in red snow, and the tethered coins of morning shook loose one by one, and the chamber filled with a chiming so bright it ached in his teeth. [582] The weights slipped, the minute ended, the year turned. [591]

Osric came up at dawn -- a long dawn, an extravagant, unrationed dawn -- with rust in his hair and the chip cool and whole in his pocket. [617] Wren stood at the workshop window crying, unable to say why, except that the sky was doing something it had never done. [639] "Blue," he told her. [643] She said the word back to him carefully, the way a music box repairman turns a new cylinder to hear its first true tune. [667]
