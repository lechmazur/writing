The wandering fireworks designer arrived at the Blooming Fortress an hour before dusk, chanting her measures as she walked. [19] One part sulfur, two parts patience, three parts nerve. [28] The chant kept her pace even and her hands calm, and it filtered the world down to what mattered: powder, wind, and the shape of the sky. [55] She had come because of a note in a mosaic. [65] Six weeks earlier, a letter reached her in the salt-flat towns, wrapped around a rubbing of the fortress courtyard floor, where someone had pried loose the old tiles and reset them into words: COME MAKE US LOOK UP. [103] Nobody signed it. [106]

The fortress was named for the vines that had taken it: wisteria through the arrow slits, jasmine drowning the parapets, roses in the dry moat where the drawbridge chains had rusted to lace. [139] At dusk it smelled like a wedding and looked like a grave. [151] The town below it had lost its harvest twice and its river once, and grief there had acquired weight, a downward pull she recognized. [175] Her whole trade, she believed, existed to defy the gravity of hopelessness, and unlike sermons, fireworks could prove it. [194]

A fire spinner met her at the gate: a lean man named Rooke, who had made the mosaic note. [213] "I pried up forty tiles," he admitted, "and the mayor fined me for each one." [228] "Then we had better make it worth forty," she said. [238]

Her problem was practical and nearly fatal to the whole endeavor: the fortress magazine was damp, her stars had caked, and her sieves had been stolen in a border town. [268] She spent the last hour of light teaching two children to filter her black powder through a stretched square of Rooke's spare silk, tapping the frame in rhythm with her chant so they would not tap too hard. [306] Serious work, she told them, is the best game there is, as long as you respect the part that can kill you. [328]

The second problem was fire. [333] Her portfire had drowned in the same river that drowned the town's fields. [346] Rooke offered his kerosene rag knot, the one he wore at his hip like a fist of cloth, wound tight and soaked and knotted so it burned slow and would not shed flaming threads while it spun. [383] She turned it over in her palms, delighted. [391] "This knot is better engineering than anything in my kit," she said. [403] "You made a lamp that dances." [409]

Then the sky closed. [413] Cloud came in low and lidded the fortress, and by full dark there was not one star above the parapets, not a single pinhole. [437] The crowd's murmur thinned into the hush that comes with a starless night, when people stop expecting anything of the sky. [458] She had waited years for a canvas that black. [467]

She could not see her own mortar racks, so she worked the way she always did in the end, through ambient awareness: the cool of stone against her left arm telling her where the wall stood, the jasmine breathing stronger when the breeze turned east, the fifteen-count of echo off the keep that told her exactly how high a shell would be at its burst. [532] She chanted her way down the line of mortars, touching each fuse, and Rooke walked behind her spinning the knot so she would have light where her hands were and darkness everywhere else. [565]

The first shell went up without a sound anyone remembered, and broke into a slow green chrysanthemum that hung, and hung, and refused to fall. [590] She had built the whole show upward: nothing that drooped, nothing that wept, every star climbing after its burst. [609] She had spelled something too, though it took the town a moment to read it: nine shells in sequence, low and bright over the courtyard, each bursting in a cluster of white pinpricks arranged like tiles. [645] It was the mosaic note again, lifted off the floor and set into the empty sky, and this time the sky answered it. [668] Somebody laughed the way people laugh when they have been holding still for a year. [683]

Afterward the mayor paid the forty fines back in copper, which Rooke spent on kerosene, which struck them both as the correct arithmetic. [706] She left before the vines were wet with morning, chanting her measures, her pack lighter by nine shells and heavier by one gift. [729] Rooke had made her a knot of her own. [738] One part sulfur, two parts patience, three parts nerve; and, she added now, a fourth part of somebody willing to hold the light steady while you work. [765]
