Prince Ilve had not washed in eleven days, and the suspended palace, anchored to its giant stalactites by nine brass chains, no longer cared. [24] Its servants were pistons. [28] Its courtiers were counterweights, and its music was the slow grinding of gears in the rock above. [45] He had inherited a kingdom of mechanisms and a single unhatched thing. [57] The egg sat in his lap, warm only where his hands had been, speckled gray, heavier than stone: the last egg of the vault-thrush, a bird extinct for two hundred years. [88] His grandmother had kept it in oil under glass, calling it a relic. [101] Ilve called it a possibility. [106] The machines had been built to preserve the old world after the surface froze, and they had preserved it the way a jaw preserves bread. [131] Somewhere in the first design there had been an error - a flawed genesis, the engineers' own word, scratched into a plate Ilve found behind the throne - and everything grown since had come out gray, efficient, and mute. [168] No moss, no moths, no song. [174] He wanted to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world, and he had eleven days of failure under his fingernails to show for it. [197]

The incubator was his own doing. [203] He had stripped the ballroom clock, the wine-warmers, and three of his father's ceremonial lungs to assemble it: a copper nest, a bellows, a thermostat of mercury and hope. [232] Removing the ballroom clock had cost the palace its steadiest counterweight. [243] Since then the floors quaked every hour, a slow shudder that traveled up the chains and set the chandeliers whispering. [263] He worked inside that quaking reverie, half-dreaming, hypothesizing about everything. [273] Perhaps the shell needed the cold first, then heat, as the old winters had given it. [289] Perhaps it needed sound; birds heard their mothers through the shell, so he sang, badly, the only song he knew. [309] Perhaps the oil had sealed the pores and the creature inside had drowned two centuries ago, in which case he was a bedraggled prince serenading a stone. [336] He hypothesized that hope was a kind of engineering, and that all engineering began in error. [352]

On the twelfth day the mercury climbed too high. [361] The bellows had jammed with grit from the ceiling, and by the time Ilve smelled the heat the copper nest was glowing faintly at its rim. [387] He tore it open with bare hands and burned four fingers to the bone-white shine that would scar. [405] The egg was cracked. [409] Not broken - cracked from within, a jagged seam running its length, and he sat with it through the discovery, hour after hour, as the seam widened by the width of a hair. [441] The palace shuddered on schedule; the chains groaned; he did not move. [453]

What came out was wrong. [458] One wing was a crumpled fan of quills; one eye never opened; the beak was crossed like badly shut scissors. [478] Two hundred years of oil, or the heat, or the flaw already sleeping in the shell - he would never know which. [499] It lay in his scarred palm, wet, shivering, ugly, alive. [509] Then it opened its crossed beak and made a sound. [519] It was not the song the histories had promised. [528] It was thin and off-pitch and it cracked at the top of every rise, and it was bitterly sweet, and it was the first thing in that cavern in two centuries that no one had designed. [564]

Ilve wept, which the machines recorded as a coolant leak. [574] He understood, finally, what his engineers had confessed on their plate. [585] Nothing beautiful had ever begun clean. [591] A flawed genesis was not a curse; it was the only door a living thing could come through, and the machines had spent two hundred years sealing every door they found. [622]

He carried the crooked bird to the great window where the stalactites went down into dark. [638] Below, the seed-vaults hummed, ten thousand species held perfect and asleep, waiting for conditions that would never be certified safe. [658] He had a bellows, a mercury thermostat, four ruined fingers, and a hypothesis. [671] It would take the rest of his life to assemble enough incubators, and most of what he woke would come out crooked. [693] Crooked things sang. [696] Behind him the palace quaked again, and the chandeliers whispered, and the bird on his shoulder answered them badly, and he began. [718]
