The rain had just left the islet, and every leaf was still shedding it, so the whole hill ticked like a clock winding down. [24] Wren climbed it with the compass in her coat and her mother's lace folded flat against her ribs. [42] At the top, under a roof of woven palm, the bonsai tutor was pruning a juniper no taller than a teapot. [63] His name was Ollan Bey, and he spoke the way the sea breathes: slowly, rhythmically placid, one clause per exhale. [83] "You have come," he said, "for the tides of remembrance." [93] "I've come to ask what color an apology is," Wren said, "when it was never said out loud." [111] He set down the shears. [116] Nine years after the severance, most people had stopped asking such questions; the state had cut the memory-lines between the islands and the mainland to end the feuds, and it had worked, in the way an amputation works. [154] Wren could no longer recall her brother Tolm's face, only that she had refused him the last word he asked for, and that he had drowned three days later with the refusal still standing between them like a wall. [193] She laid the lace on his workbench. [200] It was antique, hand-worked, the color of old teeth, and it had hung in her mother's window for forty years pretending to be nothing but decoration. [226] Ollan traced the pattern with a fingernail, taking his time, reading it the way other men read charts. [244] "The women of your family," he said, "hid the old rites in their needlework, because thread was never searched." [263] "Here is the hour, here is the ground, here is the gesture," he said, translating knots into words as if each one cost him something. [288] The hour was slack water after rain; the ground was the tidepool below his terrace; the gesture was to diffuse one drop of a person's grief into standing seawater and wait for the color to rise. [324] For that, the lace insisted, one needed a needle that pointed at the drowned. [338] Wren took out the compass. [343] It had come off the Vespertine, a ghost ship that still ran her old route at night with no crew aboard and no lamp lit, and its needle had forgotten north entirely. [375] It pointed instead wherever the tides of remembrance were running, which was why the harbor authority had wanted it burned. [395] "It has been swinging toward this islet for a month," she said. [407] Ollan nodded, unhurried, and led her down the wet steps, and the breeze came up off the water and pushed the rain out of the trees onto both of them. [437] The tidepool lay flat as a held breath. [445] She set the compass on the stone rim; the needle steadied and stopped trembling for the first time since she had found it. [468] "Now the drop," Ollan said, and pricked her thumb with a grafting knife, so slowly that she had time to change her mind and did not. [494] The blood went into the pool and did not stay red. [505] It opened outward in slow feathers and turned, and what came up through the water was blue -- a cold, luminous cerulean, the exact shade of a sky that has decided not to speak. [538] Wren knelt and put her hands in it and remembered Tolm's face, all at once, the way a door gives. [558] She said the word she had refused him. [566] The pool kept the blue for as long as it took her to say it twice, then let the color thin away into the general sea, which is where such words must go. [599] When she looked up, Ollan was holding one of his junipers, and its needles, she saw, were tipped with the same blue. [621] "I came here after the severance too," he said, "with a lace of my own, and a daughter I had not answered." [643] "The trees keep the color," he added, after a while. [653] "That is all I teach: how to keep a thing small enough to carry." [667] He gave her a cutting, a seed pot, and no schedule. [678] Wren left the compass on the rim of the pool for whoever climbed the hill next, because the Vespertine's needle had done its one job and she no longer needed to be told where remembering was. [714] Going down, she walked in the tutor's rhythm without meaning to, and the wet islet ticked behind her, and the cutting in her hand was already, at the tips, faintly blue. [745]
