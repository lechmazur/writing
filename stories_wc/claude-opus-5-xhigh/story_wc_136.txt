Noor Halabi mapped bus routes for the city, and she mapped them beautifully. [13] Her supervisors wanted timetables; she gave them timetables braided with hand-inked compass roses, headways calculated to the second and shaded like dusk. [35] Analytical and artistic in equal measure, she believed a route was an argument, and a beautiful argument was harder to refuse. [56] Her brother Emil had been a conductor on the Ambrose Street line before the tunnels flooded and the line was struck from every map but hers. [82] He used to dare her to find the fortress of unspoken truths. [94] It was a children's game at first: a place, he said, where everything people swallowed instead of saying was stored under guard. [116] He died in March, and the dare stayed alive. [125] In his effects she found a ticket punch, brass and worn smooth, its jaws cut in the shape of a small five-pointed star. [148] It punched nothing. [151] Paper closed behind its bite like water; card stock came away unmarked, though for a moment she would see a star of light where the hole should have been. [180] The other conductors had called it the phantom punch and refused to touch it. [194] Noor, who trusted measurement, measured instead. [200] For nine months she surveyed the streets above the drowned line, and the city began answering her in cracks. [219] Frost split the pavement into arrowheads. [225] Each one pointed the way the next would appear, and when she plotted them on tracing paper the sequence formed a route no engineer had ever drawn: a spiral tightening toward the sealed head house on Ambrose Street. [263] The final arrow lay under her own front step, which she chose to read as an invitation rather than an accusation. [284] She went on the last night of December, arriving at eleven fifty, because unraveling a mystery deserves punctuality. [302] Emil had always insisted the door would only open upon the hour that divides bad habits and new starts. [321] The padlock was rusted through and gave at a touch, which she noted in her field book, because she noted everything. [342] Below the stairs the flooded platform had drained to a skin of water, and mist stood in the air like held breath. [364] Above her the ceiling opened into a starlit vault, gold leaf constellations laid across blue tile by some long-dead artisan who had also been an engineer of sorts. [392] The mist caught their light and held it, so that the stars seemed to hang at shoulder height. [410] This is the fortress, she thought, and felt her certainty arrive trembling, the way a bridge trembles under a load it can still carry. [434] There was no guard, only a conductor's stand with a slot worn in it, and beside the slot a blank card. [455] She understood the fare at once. [461] The punch would take no paper; it took the thing you had never said. [475] Somewhere above, a church bell began the twelve strokes. [484] She set the card in the slot, closed the brass jaws, and spoke into the mist. [500] "You called me the night the tunnels flooded and I let it ring," she said. [515] "I was tired of your voice asking for help I had already drawn out for you, in ink, three times." [535] "I have mapped every route out of that sentence for nine months, and there is none." [551] The punch bit down, and this time it left a hole. [562] A star of gold light stood in the card, and through the mist the vault answered, thousands of small punched stars flaring at once, every one of them a truth someone had carried down here and finally set down. [601] She heard Emil laugh, brief and unsurprised, the laugh of a man who wins a dare he had wanted to lose. [622] Then the bell finished, the stars cooled to gold leaf, and the mist was only mist. [638] Noor climbed out into the first hour of January with the punched card in her coat and her field book open. [659] In the morning she filed a proposal to reopen the Ambrose Street line, drawn in her usual two hands: the ledger of grades and headways, and, along the margin, a vault of stars. [692] The committee asked what evidence she had that anyone would ride it. [704] She laid the ticket punch on the table, still trembling, still certain, and told them to follow the cracks. [723]
