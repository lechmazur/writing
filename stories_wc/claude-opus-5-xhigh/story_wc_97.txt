The station clung to the tidal arbor above the clouds, where silver branches drank the mist and let it go again twice a day. [24] Ilse played the platform piano there, a wistful station pianist with sleeves worn thin at the elbows, and every traveler who passed said she looked like someone waiting. [52] She was. [55] On the lid of the piano sat a broken anniversary clock, its hands stopped at 6:41, the minute the skiff Verity left the arbor and did not come back. [84] Marek had given it to her on their second anniversary; it had broken on their third, and she had never let anyone mend it, because a stopped clock keeps its promise. [115]

Pilots came to the arbor for refuge when their compasses began flickering. [127] Everyone knew what flickering meant: fate had not finished writing that route, and any needle that trembles is a route still open to argument. [151] Ilse had argued for eleven years, in the only language she had, which was music. [166]

The archivist Bela brought her the reel in autumn, wrapped in oilcloth, breathing hard from the stairs. [183] It was the old black-and-white film reel of the arbor's dedication, harmless, official, dull. [197] "Hold it to the lamp," he said. [204] Between the frames of ribbon-cutting and applause were hidden sections, spliced in by someone with a razor and a grudge: nine seconds of the Verity, adrift in a white harbor, and Marek's face turned up toward a sky that had no weather in it. [248] "The lost aren't gone," Bela whispered. "They're filed." [256] Fate, it turned out, kept its overflow in the gaps between pictures, and the arbor's high tide of cloud was the one hour those gaps could be entered. [284]

Ilse was strategically creative, and she had eleven years of studying the station's rhythms to spend. [300] She did not beg the harbormaster. [306] She looked at the timetable instead. [312] The evening program was fixed, printed, sanctioned - three nocturnes, then silence for the departure bell - and printed things are what fate reads. [334] An encore is not printed. [339] An encore is the one thing no schedule can anticipate, and Ilse intended to surprise fate with an unplanned encore long enough to walk through a nine-second hole in a film. [370]

She rigged the projector to the piano's music rack so the reel would advance with her pedal. [387] She bribed the lamp-boy with a month of wages. [396] She told Bela to keep the compasses in the waiting room under a cloth, so no one would see them flickering and call an inspector. [421] Then she waited until the horizon invited night, which at the arbor happens all at once, the cloud-tide standing high and gold and level with the roots. [448]

She played the three nocturnes exactly as printed. [456] The travelers rose; the bell hung ready in the stationmaster's hand; and Ilse, without standing, without announcement, began a fourth piece that existed nowhere on paper. [482] It was the waltz from her third anniversary, the night the clock broke. [495]

The projector stuttered awake. [499] On the platform wall the dedication flickered, ribbons, hats, applause - and then the seam opened, white as a held breath, and the nine seconds stretched because nobody had told them when to stop. [532] She jammed the sustain pedal down with the broken clock, left the chord ringing, and stepped sideways into the light. [552]

The white harbor smelled of nothing. [558] The Verity lay tilted in it, and Marek turned, eleven years younger and not surprised at all, as though he had simply been listening for the wrong end of a song. [589] "You're late," he said. [593] "I'm extra," she said, and took his wrist. [601]

The chord was still fading when they came out onto the platform. [613] Under its cloth in the waiting room, every compass steadied and pointed, for the first time in eleven years, at the piano. [635] The stationmaster rang his bell too late and never admitted it. [646] Bela wrote nothing down. [650] Ilse lifted the broken anniversary clock off the pedal, and its hands, jarred loose at last, moved one minute forward to 6:42, which was not forgiveness and not a repair, only proof that a thing can start again after it has stopped. [692] They stayed on the platform while the cloud-tide went out beneath them, holding hands like two people who had rehearsed nothing, and the arbor kept them, as it kept every traveler whose route was still open to argument. [730]
