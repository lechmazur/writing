The city above the Archive of Combinations seemed asleep, but Sella Venn felt a latent blaze under every wet stone.  As the prism-minded archivist, she catalogued no object by itself, only the angles where one thing altered another.  That gift had cost her the lower stacks after she insisted her mother had vanished into an unindexed margin.  It had also given her the motive no censor understood: to find where forgotten combinations hide.  By decree, the Ministry of Plain Use would burn pairings that did not serve tax, bread, or war.  Its clerks called a remedy paired with a poison wasteful, a lullaby paired with a lock seditious, and a name paired with a river nonsense.  Sella knew erasure drove meanings into seams, breath stains, and apparently idle hands.  On the evening before the furnaces, she stole a rain ledger whose pages smelled of iron and pondweed.  In its outer margin, beneath a list of drowned bells, crouched a manuscript margin doodle no larger than a thumbnail.  The manuscript margin doodle was clandestinely luminous, not bright enough for inspectors, yet to her split gaze it burned.  At first it showed a coiled snake, six upward drops, and a door drawn as two mismatched lines.  She held a prism over the ink, and each stroke broke into a different warning color.  The founders had named such buried alarms seeds of vigilance, because they woke only when knowledge was threatened.  Years earlier, Sella had dismissed them as romantic dust; now the dust trembled toward the cellar stairs.  The warning was a map of relations: coil to coil, drip to ceiling, door to pulse.  It led beneath the archive to a serpentine grotto in mist, where old conduits exhaled unpaired memories.  The ledger named the only opening in a cramped hand: when gravity inverts for an hour, upward drops become keys.  Once each year, mineral tides under the grotto pulled against the moon, and loose stones fell toward the ceiling.  A locked grate barred the descent, stamped with the sentence of her exile: inspect above, never reenter.  During a childhood fever she had been carried from those caves, but fragments of her breathing had stayed below.  To recover the vault, she had to reenter that feverish path and admit she had never left it whole.  She could not force the grate; she had to answer the conditions that made it hold.  The founders opened sealed places through threshold effects, arranging heat, moisture, light, and weight until a border forgot its side.  At dawn she set a lamp behind blue glass, breathed on the grate, and pressed the damp ledger to iron.  Where condensation met ink, warmth met stone, and prism light met mist, three thin borders shivered.  The coiled snake straightened, the door lines leaned together, and the grate unlocked without a key.  Sella stepped down as water climbed from the floor and the ceiling began to shed its cold rain upward.  The serpentine grotto in mist held slow coils of vapor that switched her footsteps between roof and floor.  She moved through threshold effects, choosing each boundary by the color it returned to her prism.  A red seam meant hot stone and retreat; a green one meant moss, water, and a floor lasting seconds.  Above her, crews lit the first furnace, and heat crawled down the cracks like a cautious animal.  The latent blaze was no longer an intuition; it had begun testing doors.  Sella felt the old temptation to classify danger, to make fear clean by dividing it into hues.  Instead she let fear remain whole, hot and smoky, and followed the luminous grain toward a blank wall.  The blankness was a threshold, composed of dripping stone, held breath, and the fever she no longer resisted.  She exhaled as her mother had once taught apprentices in dark rooms: once for the room, once for themselves.  The wall forgot its loyalty, opened as a seam of light, and admitted her to a chamber with no shelves.  Inside waited no treasure, only a low table of dark seeds whose hidden glow matched the margin doodle.  Sella understood then that her mother had not died below but had become a threshold keeper.  Forgotten combinations hid in watchers, not shelves.  Each seed carried an urge to notice the next unlikely pairing.  Sella scattered them through warm margins; clerks copied odd pairings by morning.  The furnace fire found no single obedient fuel and banked low.  The latent blaze had keepers in every margin.
