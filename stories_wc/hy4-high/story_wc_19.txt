On the xenial trellis above the clouds, everyone knew Mara as the bunker builder's claustrophobic child. 
Her father riveted refuges beneath the guest vines, tighter each year, so travelers could hide from the sky. 
He had begun after Mara's mother fell upward into a white calm and never returned. 
Mara could not breathe beneath a ceiling, so she slept on open rails with her feet toward the stars. 
The gardeners called that ache stretching contracted, because distance invited her outward while walls urged her to fold. 
Her only inheritance was a tiny porcelain doll with a hairline crack across its hollow belly. 
Her mother had used it to taste pauses, and Mara learned to collect the flavors of different silences. 
Apology left iron, a lullaby's end left milk and thunder, and a crowded welcome left pear peel. 
Such flavors vanished if she held a door open too long, so she kept them by limiting exposure. 
One breath for the crack, one turn of the doll, then darkness, and the taste stayed distinct. 
Her father called this a child's game, though he oiled the shutters before every seasonal inversion. 
Once each year, when gravity inverts for an hour, rain climbs, bells swing downward, and guest vines turn their leaves toward the sun below. 
At the still center of that hour, the trellis made the gilded hush. 
It was intangibly tangible, a warm coin in the palm that no scale could weigh. 
Gold lay on every rail, yet the source was courtesy, strangers choosing not to speak while the world turned over. 
Bunker builders prized it as sealant, because a refuge lined with gilded hush made panic unable to echo. 
Mara wanted it for another reason, since every guest brought a separate quiet and the rarest flavor needed welcome. 
On the morning of the inversion, her father locked the storm shutters open and rolled a copper vat beneath the central joint. 
He meant to gather the whole hour at once, pour it into a refuge, and keep Mara there whenever the sky grew dangerous. 
She understood the love inside the plan and feared the room inside the love. 
She also knew one long exposure would bleach every flavor into a tasteless slab. 
Before the first raindrop rose, Mara strapped herself to a line and took the tiny porcelain doll from its pouch. 
The servant corridor to the central joint was no wider than a coffin, and her lungs recognized it at once. 
She entered by limiting exposure, not to sound now, but to narrowness: three inches, a pause, three more. 
The trellis had never seemed less xenial, yet the wood warmed under her palms as if making room for a guest. 
Behind her, her father shouted, and the shout fell upward, taking his anger with it. 
Ahead, travelers from cloud skiffs hung from their belts, mouths closed, each holding a different silence. 
When the hour opened fully, the gilded hush began to sprout along the rails in threads no wider than breath. 
In the copper vat below, those threads were dragged toward one blank shining surface. 
Mara reached the joint, clipped her strap to a ring, and set the crack of the doll against a seam. 
She did not open it wide or offer the hour everything it wanted. 
She tipped the crack for one traveler, shut it for one heartbeat, then tipped it again, and let each silence enter alone. 
Through the seam, fine roots of hush sprouted outward, lacing every guest's quiet to the next rather than sealing the rails. 
The doll grew warm, then heavy in the intangibly tangible way she had learned to trust. 
Her father reached her as gravity began to settle, his hand hovering over the doll as if over a door. 
He could have crushed it, but he saw the catalog trembling there: iron, milk, pear peel, rain, apology, welcome. 
Mara waited for the ceiling of his old fear to close over them both. 
Instead, he unclipped her strap first, because she had gone pale inside the coffin corridor. 
He closed the copper vat and eased the shutters down one handwidth at a time. 
By the last minute, the gilded hush lay across the trellis as many flavors, not one wall. 
Later, Mara added his silence to the doll, and it tasted of a door left carefully ajar.
