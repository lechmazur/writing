The opal observatory under snow wakes only after the radio static clears. 
The old staff called this hour midnight solace, when instruments confessed mistakes without shame. 
Mara Vale, a death certificate error survivor, waits beside the dead receiver with her mitten closed around a porcelain shard. 
She counts heartbeats constantly, a habit born when a registrar handed her a certified copy of her own death. 
The clerk had confused two cot numbers after the avalanche, and the correction office had burned in a spring flood. 
For six years, doors demanded a document she could not produce, so she answered with the drum beneath her ribs. 
She has climbed back to fetch wisdom from failure, because the council will seal the observatory unless the avalanche forecast can be explained. 
The forecast survives as a digital ghost, a reconstruction of Ilya Sen from telemetry, console notes, and transmissions lost in the storm. 
Ilya had been her teacher, and he had died insisting that a failed model was still a kind of testimony. 
His voice waits in drives shattered by the storm, each memory assigned to the wrong sector. 
The service code says Mara must defragment the record if she wants one clean answer. 
The manual gives one method, through ephemeral patterns in spilled ink, because the observatory floor is cut from conductive opal. 
Ferric ink from the chart plotter briefly lights charge trails left by broken files, then vanishes into opal. 
Only a living rhythm can hold the order long enough to read it, and the ghost has lost every clock but hers. 
Mara kneels, opens the ink reservoir with the porcelain shard, and lets a black spill cross the pale slabs. 
The shard is the edge of the cup she broke when the registrar said there was no legal person left to appeal. 
She has carried it not as a charm but as evidence that brittle things can still cut a way open. 
Now the ink feathers into circles, hatch marks, and sudden branches that fade before their shapes can be named. 
Mara starts her count aloud, one beat for each bright knot, two for each gap, keeping only pulse and pattern. 
The receiver clicks, warmer than applause, and a voice assembled from old warnings asks for the next sector. 
She laughs once, not because anything is funny, but because even as a digital ghost Ilya still gives homework. 
Each spill shows a failure: an ignored pressure drop, a delayed relay, a clerk's neat entry mistaking silence for death. 
The patterns tell her where to join the sectors, and her steady heartbeats tell the ghost when to answer. 
Cluster by cluster, she begins to defragment not only Ilya's last night but the official story that followed it. 
At the center of the recovered record lies a transmission buried under static, sent after the avalanche had already stopped. 
Ilya had written that Mara's pulse was visible on the rescue monitor, weak but unmistakable, and that her name must not be added to the dead. 
The signal had arrived during the worst hiss, so the clerk trusted the cot list and discarded the only human correction. 
Mara feels anger rise and pass through her like weather across glass. 
The wisdom is no grand equation but a smaller instruction: keep the irregular signal, because the tidy record may be the error. 
She sends the recovered packet to the council along with six years of pulse logs, timestamped by her own counting. 
The display answers that the seal is delayed, and the observatory settles with an almost animal relief. 
Ilya's voice thins as the defragmentation finishes, leaving one sentence he must have repeated while waiting in the broken drives. 
He says that a failure is only useless when no one returns to listen. 
Mara places the porcelain shard beside the darkening stain, no longer needing to carry the edge that opened her old injury. 
Snow presses softly against the opal dome, and the cleared radio band holds a hush kinder than any verdict. 
She still counts heartbeats constantly, but the numbers have stopped sounding like evidence and started sounding like company. 
Before dawn, she sits in midnight solace with an honest failure, a delayed correction, and a life her pulse keeps spelling.
