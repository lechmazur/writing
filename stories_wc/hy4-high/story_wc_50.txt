Across the lull of a wounded season, with orchards frosted and bells muted, Mara Venn came each evening to the marble cavern.  She was the town's reflective locksmith, a name earned by mirrors, long silences, and keys cut from the edges.  A forge accident had left her peripherally focused, more alert to a rim of light than to the bright center.  That gift suited the marble cavern, where the harvest of echoes depended on angles no one faced directly.  Each year people spoke laughter, names, and small brave questions into the eastern throat, and the stone returned them at one appointed hour.  A collar of mirrors and shutters opened then, guiding each echo into a labeled lead jar before it scattered into useless tunnels.  She wanted to store wonder in the cellar of reason, for later.  Her cellar was a dry recess under the north stair, ruled by cataloged keys, numbered drawers, and jars waiting in straw.  She trusted hinges and ledgers because grief had taken enough rooms, and reason owed astonishment one cool shelf.  Since the siege, the harvest had failed, because a shell had shaken loose the broken timer mechanism above the north arch.  Its hands stopped when the guns began, and its smallest escapement lay like gray sand.  No drawing survived, and the old keeper had died trying to read the missing gear by candlelight.  The elders asked Mara to reverse-engineer the broken timer mechanism before the thaw ended the lull and unclaimed echoes faded.  She tried ordinary clockwork first, fitting teeth to wheels and listening for one honest click.  The cavern refused ordinary time, bending lamps in its veins and returning footsteps from places not yet crossed.  So Mara began studying reflection angles at different times, setting black glass along the walls and chalking each pale return.  Dawn threw a thin bar from the east vein onto the collar.  Noon split the bar toward a hidden pin.  Under cloud she found a softer angle that no central dial could show.  After moonrise, while apprentices stared ahead, her peripherally focused gaze caught a gleam at the rim.  The gleam returned at the dead bell's old intervals, as though absence kept time.  Mara understood that the mechanism had never counted hours alone; it counted how light struck marble after a voice was given to it.  The sequence was a lock whose tumblers were sunrise, weather, season, memory, and the angle of a listening wall.  Once she saw the lock, she could reverse-engineer it without forcing it to resemble a clock.  She filed a new escape wheel with nineteen uneven teeth, each copied from a chalk mark made at another hour.  She reset the shutters to open outward at the edges first, leaving the center dark until the echoes had gathered.  Then she slept beside the broken timer mechanism through another week of the lull, waking whenever frost ticked from the roof.  On the last evening, the wounded season seemed to hold its breath under bandages of snow.  Mara set the repaired collar, lowered her lamp, and listened for the town she had been trying to preserve.  The first shutter opened like a key turning in a sleepwalker's dream.  Every mirror caught a rim of light, and the cavern filled not with noise but with arrivals.  A baker laughed before dawn, a child asked why pears are green, and a widow said yes on a day she had feared.  Orchard bees returned, rain pattered on a school roof, and Mara's mother hummed as she once had beside the forge.  Mara did not weep until the last echo reached the lead jar, because tenderness had always come to her from the side.  She labeled the jar by angle, date, gear tooth, and weather, then carried it to the cellar of reason.  There, among measured drawers, wonder waited without demanding to be understood before anyone was ready.  By morning the thaw had begun, and people came asking whether the harvest could be repeated for the names of the dead.  Mara said the lock would open again when light, loss, and patience agreed, which was the truest schedule she knew.  She left the scar on the mechanism uncovered, a reminder that even reason needs a wound to enter by.  Then the reflective locksmith locked the cellar door and kept the key where she could find it later.
