In the eleventh week after the energy beings arrive, Nera reaches the terra-cotta grotto under starlight with dust on her mouth and a question she refuses to simplify. 
She is a drifting roadside poet, paid in water and endings, and she has walked three provinces to discover the texture of time. 
At ditch shrines and bus wrecks she writes last lines for people who cannot carry them, listening until a life shows its weave. 
The energy beings have made clocks unnecessary and hunger exact: they shine above reservoirs, offer towns impossible choices, and leave each hour with a visible grain. 
For a well, a village gives up the day its dead are named; for rain, a child forgets her mother's face. 
Since the arrivals, every abandoned prison has been folded into a choice chamber, and every opened cell demands one cherished hour in payment. 
Most people call these the impossible choices, because the terms are polished clean of mercy. 
Nera calls them badly written, and a bad line can be revised only by someone willing to touch it. 
Nera is ethically playful: she turns patrol slogans into rhymes that smuggle maps, but never spends a stranger's hunger for a joke. 
Against her ribs she carries the frayed coil of rope from a prison escape, taken from the wall where her brother Arek went down and did not come back the same. 
That escape happened before the arrivals, when prisons had iron and keys, and twelve prisoners followed Arek down the rope before the guards smashed the floodlights. 
Arek returned later as a rumor in a glass cell, one of the fugitives the energy beings now keep between a heartbeat and its echo. 
She has come because the road says that here time keeps an underside. 
She works by uncovering layers, peeling old stains with her thumbnail, because the road has taught her that certainty is usually paint. 
The rope has layers of lime, tar, blue thread from a uniform, salt from hands, and a child's brown hair braided into the final knot. 
Under each layer she finds a smaller decision: who went first, who coughed, who lied about fear, who tied the knot so it could be undone from below. 
Because the coil crossed a wall between a sentence and air, it has kept one minute unspent. 
In the grotto, starlight enters through a chimney crack and lies across the fired floor like cold silk. 
The walls are terra-cotta ribs washed with ocher, kiln smoke, and older scratches made by people hiding before there were roads. 
At the mouth, the energy beings gather as patient weather, neither cruel nor kind, merely exact. 
They give Nera two choices, and both are complete. 
She may spend the rope's last saved minute to feel the exact second Arek cleared the wall, and know time at last, or she may spend it opening Arek's glass cell and the eleven others like it. 
Then she begins to pattern, not to decorate, matching the rope's freed strands to the rings of clay around a drain hole. 
One strand for the dead, one for the living, one for the guards who were afraid, and a gap where her own wanting can stand without being fed. 
The energy beings brighten, interested despite themselves, because play with rules is the only language they have learned to respect. 
Nera kneels closer and, by uncovering layers of the grotto floor, scrapes away a newer patch of red wash with the edge of a coin. 
Beneath it lies a thumbprint, then a tally, then the faint outline of a hand teaching someone else how to wait. 
The discovery is small enough to miss and large enough to change the arithmetic. 
Every rescue, the beings have said, demands one cherished hour; they have never said the hour must be famous, or that only one person may have cherished it. 
In the strata under the red wash, centuries of fugitives have left an hour nobody counted: the hour of waiting quietly while a stranger learns the knot. 
Such an hour is cherished by many, owned by none, and rough as a shared sleeve. 
She offers that shared hour through the patterned rope; its texture passes into her palms like a worn hem, not a trophy. 
The beings accept, glass cells exhale, and twelve fugitives step into starlight with dust on their sleeves. 
Arek looks at Nera, not unchanged but present, and she leaves him the gap in the pattern. 
She has not conquered time; she has touched the layer it uses to hold people together.
