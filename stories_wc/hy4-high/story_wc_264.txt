In the year after the last forest burns, the rails shine with powdered leaf, the trains move at dusk, and maps keep a black margin where orchards used to be. 
I am the dreamless freight guard, certified awake by surgeons who took my nights so no thief could enter me while the cargo slept. 
Because my watch is routed through wave functions, I am occasionally everywhere along the cars: a hand on the brake, a cheek against cold glass, a breath in the thirteenth crate. 
The cargo is called the divergent archive, not books but failed branches sealed in leaden cells: rivers turned north, children missed trains, choices history declined. 
The Bureau wants one official past, and guards are told to carry, never garden, which is why I keep my reason folded under my tongue. 
My reason is stubborn: to cultivate the sparks in the unlit corners, where children see by touch. 
At dusk the convoy stops on the glowing dune in bloom, where sand lilies open like little lamps and drink old lightning under ash. 
A fractured lullaby climbs from the engine, hush, then a missing beat, then hush again. 
The manual said the action was charm, not force, and the method was through wave functions. 
I charm through wave functions by offering a lock every possible song and asking one to stay. 
In the thirteenth crate waits a chess clock frozen at midnight, its brass faces aimed at each other. 
Its buttons are older than the Bureau, and both hands stand at twelve like children refusing bed. 
If one side ticks, the divergent archive collapses into the branch that burned; if the clock stays frozen, denied branches lend sparks to the dark towns beyond the dune. 
The lead inspector arrives with three clerks, a warm warrant, and an argument that hungry people need one countable harvest. 
I answer with a nod that belongs to no dream and offends no law. 
Then I charm the couplings with the broken lullaby my mother used for fevers. 
The melody is thin: sleep, little ash, sleep; but the crates hear the intervals between the words. 
Through wave functions, each possible note touches each possible lock, and the locks consider mercy before obedience. 
My body stays in the cab, but my attention flowers across the train, occasionally everywhere and never whole. 
I feel the inspector press the left button of the chess clock frozen at midnight; the first tick falls like a stone into milk. 
I cannot stop her hand, so I divide my watch among ten thousand refused mornings. 
In one, the last forest rains itself green; in another, my mother keeps singing past the fever; in another, I dream. 
The branches are too brief to live, but each gives off a spark hotter than shame. 
I send them outward through wave functions, not as answers, but as seeds small enough for unlit corners. 
They settle in cellars, under bridges, inside ovens gone cold, beneath the eyelids of wakeful children. 
Each spark says begin again, and asks for water, a name, a patient hand. 
The inspector hears them too, because the dune brightens with every borrowed dawn, and her clerks forget which past they came to certify. 
She presses the right button, perhaps to choose nothing, perhaps to silence the lullaby. 
The two ticks meet at twelve and cancel, leaving the chess clock frozen at midnight after all. 
The divergent archive does not become a single history; it scatters into tasks, cradles, recipes, patrols. 
The freight empties, my distributed eyes close one by one, and I return to the cab smaller than myself, still the dreamless freight guard, but no longer ashamed of the empty place. 
There is room there now for tending, which is not sleep and is almost a dream. 
By morning, the glowing dune in bloom is full of people carrying basins, wire, bread, and questions; we plant sparks as the fractured lullaby loses a beat, then finds it in smaller hands.
