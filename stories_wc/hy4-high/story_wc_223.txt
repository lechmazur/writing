During an interregnum, the ministers summoned Mara Venn to the whispering gallery beneath the empty throne.  The king had died without an heir, and for nine days no seal was valid, not even one from the Ministry of Consolation.  By custom, the crown could be claimed only after the nomadic penumbra crossed the whispering gallery, because every oath hidden in the city gathered there.  The penumbra was a traveling half shadow cast by palace mirrors from the crownless throne, visiting a different quarter each afternoon.  During an interregnum, such wandering light made law pause, and anything spoken under it was believed to be the city's true voice.  Lord Serrin meant to supply that voice with one obedient woman.  Mara Venn was a crisis actor hired for rehearsals of fire and flood, and a real crisis survivor pulled from the North Gate collapse with two broken ribs.  At North Gate, while chronicles praised the governor, she remembered a mason lowering a lamp, a baker offering a cup, and a nurse tying her shoe before lifting strangers.  She wanted to become the historian of small gestures, because the great chronicles always arrived after the hands that had saved people were cold.  Survival had made her benevolently ruthless; she mourned a torn sleeve and burned the lie that tore it.  The Ministry of Consolation had hired her to enact public grief for a ceremony no public was allowed to attend.  In the whispering gallery, one trained sob, placed at the focal stone, would return from the curved walls as a multitude.  Serrin had given her a script of sighs, upraised hands, and a chorus begging him to keep order until a regent was chosen.  She accepted the paper, because refusal at the door would have left the gallery to a smoother actor.  The whispering gallery had been built to magnify rulers, but that day it held a luminous absence, bright with everyone who had been removed.  Dustless benches shone, royal chairs wore white covers, and the air seemed rinsed of breath.  Near the focal stone stood a chessboard missing all the pawns, its ranks occupied only by kings, queens, bishops, knights, and rooks.  Each morning ministers moved the tall pieces while clerks noted the people displaced.  Mara saw empty pawn squares numbered in blue ink, matching sounding niches around the wall.  They marked where common witnesses should have stood when the penumbra crossed.  Serrin noticed her looking and said pawns had never mattered in succession.  Mara smiled with the calm face she had worn at North Gate and said nothing.  The only method left was a pattern of walking that spells words, a rescue sign older than the ministry.  At North Gate, smoke had made shouting dangerous, so guides traced letters with their bodies for survivors who could read a turning shoulder.  The resonant slate floor carried such steps as plainly as speech.  Mara decided to unbind the city from rehearsed consent before the shadow reached the focal stone.  She had come to unbind one voice from a lie wearing a thousand voices.  Outside, the nomadic penumbra entered the western arcade, turning the guards' faces into gentle smudges.  Clerks lifted their pens, and Serrin stepped back from the focal stone to give her the stage.  Mara began to unbind the oath by taking the first empty pawn square in her worn rehearsal shoes.  Her heel sounded once, her toe answered twice, and the curve of the wall completed a letter no hand had written.  Her first circuit wrote NO, and the second wrote CROWN, while Serrin stared at the floor as if the letters were ants.  The next circuit wrote WE WERE HERE, and the gallery carried each word to the opposite wall in a voice made of many throats.  All those throats were absent, and that was the mercy of it.  The pattern did not summon ghosts; it loosened the acoustic knots by which her single breath had been tied to a forged crowd.  Serrin's chorus returned to him as one dry cough, and the clerks lowered their pens.  No regent was acclaimed when the shadow crossed, because the law found no public consent to magnify.  In the luminous absence, the nomadic penumbra moved on, leaving the tall pieces with no one to command.  Mara opened her ledger and wrote the words Historian of small gestures.  Then she erased Serrin's title but kept the tremble in his hand, because frightened gestures also belonged to history.  At last she left the pawns out.
