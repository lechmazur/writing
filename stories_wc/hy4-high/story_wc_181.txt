Mara Venn, the bewildered curator, reached an astral orchard blossoming on a lonely asteroid with three minutes of authority left.  The accession ledger in her glove was warm, and her commission existed between the first and last page and ended when the last page settled.  She had come to inquire about a pistachio shell cameo blank, the smallest object ever to threaten the Institute.  Its label, in her own future handwriting, read, Do not carve me until you understand what a descendant owes.  Mara wanted to pursue forbidden knowledge, but told herself cataloguing was not theft.  Silver petals and gold fruit filled the dark with brightened disquiet, as if sunrise had become a warning.  Because revelation favored enclosed water and embarrassment, the Institute's method for impossible catalogues was having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms.  Mara had mocked that rule, yet she entered the orchard bathroom when the cameo refused to answer.  It was a tiled asteroid closet with a starry mirror and orbiting beads of water.  As she washed her hands, the first breakthrough arrived with the recycler's hiss.  Inheritance here was not parent to child but nonlinear inheritance, a storm in which the unborn rained heirlooms backward.  That made the blank simply complicated: someone unborn had sent it to prevent a fatal portrait.  Mara laughed once, then stopped; the mirror answered in an old woman's voice.  The voice said, You inherited my warning before I inherited your bones.  The woman might be her daughter, her student's daughter, or no kin except through ideas.  The uncertainty frightened her more than the vault beneath the orchard, where the Institute kept questions no mind could hold.  Mara's mother had lost her name there, and Mara had spent thirty years mistaking curiosity for justice.  She left with the pistachio shell cameo blank cupped in her palm, smelling faintly of salt and almonds.  Outside, each tree bore tiny frames instead of blossoms, each holding a face for its proper century.  A paper gardener bowed and asked for permission from the last page.  Mara offered the authority in her glove and admitted it was running out.  The gardener pointed to a blackroot tree with branches bowing toward the ground.  At its base, a brass door opened onto stairs of light.  The stairs reached the vault, which ended in a second bathroom humming with forbidden plumbing.  Mara sat on the closed lid because her knees had begun to bargain.  The second breakthrough came while a drop of water crawled up the tile against gravity.  Forbidden knowledge was hidden not because it was dangerous, she saw, but because timing made knowers ancestors of consequences.  Her revenge would not free her mother; it would name the dead before their acts had finished echoing.  She remembered her mother labeling shells, and how a blank label could be mercy.  Back in the orchard, the ledger's last page had begun to glow.  The paper gardener offered a diamond stylus; one carved line would reveal the vault's index to her bloodline.  All she needed was a portrait, a name, and the courage to call discovery inheritance.  The offer glittered with brightened disquiet, lovely enough to make refusal feel like cowardice.  Mara wanted the index, her mother's name, and proof that ignorance had been a wound inflicted by cowards.  Instead, she stopped to inquire whether an heir could refuse a bequest before deciding whether to exist.  The paper face creased with relief, and the frames opened, dropping unclaimed faces into grass.  Mara understood the third breakthrough, late for any bathroom but no less philosophical.  A blank heirloom could protect the unborn from an ancestor's hunger for answers.  She labeled it: retained, uncarved, inherited from an open future, returnable by anyone.  The last page turned.  The orchard folded into a white seed, and Mara woke in the reading room with salt on her fingers.  Her report was simply complicated; the directors hated it because it disclosed no forbidden knowledge and prevented theft.  In the margin, future handwriting had added, Thank you for leaving me unwritten.  Mara smiled, bewildered still, and filed the pistachio shell cameo blank under Not Yet.
