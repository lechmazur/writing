The forgotten underwater temple receives Nera without a sound, its black doors folded inward like hands.  Her lamp opens a lonesome splendor over drowned mosaics, silver weed, and fish like unspent prayers.  In the ship log she is listed as distress signal sender, not diver, not daughter.  Above her, the storm swallows her final call, and the tender drifts beyond cable range.  The gauge at her wrist gives her eighteen minutes, then a red margin, then nothing.  No one will come; she has spent her life sending the emergencies of others.  At her throat, under the suit collar, hangs a crystal perfume vial.  It belonged to her mother, Saira, whose last transmission ended here in nine seconds of perfect silence.  The institute called that silence equipment failure, but Nera heard an act.  Nera has come to harness the ephemeral.  She wants the vanished rite, the nine seconds, the breath her mother chose not to waste.  The priests named its treasure the gathered hush.  It is not absence, but centuries of unsaid farewells caught beneath the dome.  Old divers say the gathered hush touches memories more surely than hands touch skin.  Saira wrote that a crystal vessel could hold it for one tide, if sealed before bubbles broke the stillness.  Thus the vial is not sentiment but apparatus, ground from quartz and threaded with blue gold.  Nera rehearsed the motions in dry rooms, but rehearsal never included this hush watching from every niche.  The temple seems less abandoned than patient, keeping its lonesome splendor for whoever arrives late.  She moves past altars furred with coral and gods whose faces have been smoothed into mercy.  The central chamber waits beyond a curtain of braided bronze cords and eelgrass.  At its threshold the water becomes so quiet that her breathing feels ill mannered.  The outer doors begin their slow shrug, and she crosses the nave as time runs short.  The gauge gives her twelve minutes, then nine, each number smaller than the space it leaves for regret.  On the altar lies a shallow basin shaped like an ear, its rim polished by centuries of foreheads.  This is where the gathered hush thickens, drawing near listeners rather than fleeing them.  Nera opens the vial and immediately tastes bergamot, salt, and a kitchen from twenty years ago.  The memory is not a picture but a pressure at the back of her eyes.  Saira is there, sealing a cracked jar, telling her that fragile things deserve exact hands.  Nera almost smiles, and the almost costs one bubble.  The bubble climbs; the hush recoils, leaving the vial cold and empty.  Her tether catches in the bronze cords at that exact moment.  A hard pull pins her shoulder and winds the air hose through three ancient knots.  She must unsnarl it before the inner shell closes, or the rite will be lost with her.  The only way to unsnarl it is by overriding instinct.  Instinct orders a yank, a kick, a fierce ascent toward air and weather.  Every obedience would shower bubbles through the chamber and tear the gathered hush into noise.  By overriding instinct, she makes herself limp and feeds slack into the knot.  Because the hush touches memories, it shows her the last descent of Saira.  Her mother is not calling for rescue in those nine seconds.  She is holding the transmitter away from her lips so the temple can keep listening.  Saira spent her final pulse on silence spacious enough for another grief.  Nera understands that to harness the ephemeral is to give it direction, not a cage.  She slips loose the last knot by feeding slack where her hands ache to pull.  The hush returns, pouring into the crystal perfume vial like scent into warm cloth.  She seals the stopper with exact hands while the gauge blinks down to three minutes.  No rescue fits inside those minutes, but one pulse remains in the emergency unit at her belt.  Once, being the distress signal sender meant spending the last pulse on herself.  Now she sets the vial against the quartz plate of the unit and lets the gathered hush answer that old frequency.  The pulse carries no coordinates, only a kitchen, a calm descent, and nine seconds held open.  Somewhere above, a receiver trained on storms will mark it as interference and save it anyway.  Nera settles beside the basin shaped like an ear while the lamps dim into lonesome splendor.
