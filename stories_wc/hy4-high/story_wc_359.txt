At the moment a sculptor's chisel first strikes marble, the ice cavern beneath the marble court must answer. 
That answer is not a sound but the sculpted hush the Council dismissed as waste. 
If clean, the Council will certify wonder as essential and reopen the quarry. 
If fractured, my sister Mira loses her first block and her apprenticeship. 
I stand below as the last prism-song bell ringer, holding an amber music sheet. 
The sheet was from Mother; her carved swallows were cut from civic doorways. 
I once testified that her birds held no roof or warmth. 
The Ledger Fathers made my sentence law, and she stopped carving for pay. 
At night she carved soap swallows, small to hide, bright to shame me. 
When she died, Mira took her chisels, and I took a silence with edges. 
The sculpted hush is that silence shaped until useful men hear beyond use. 
I have come to certify wonder as essential and repay what my careful words broke. 
The law demands the hush arrive at the moment a sculptor's chisel first strikes marble. 
An early breath sounds like evasion; a late one like mockery. 
Above, Mira waits while councillors clutch blank certificates. 
Below, blue walls hold the aurora in ribs, quiet, restless, quiet. 
The old instructions name that pulse oscillating repose, and the cavern obeys. 
I am synesthetically blessed, hearing green as a third, violet as warning. 
The gift makes bells legible as colors, but doubt sings in several keys. 
The Council called it sickness until no clerk could manufacture their verdict. 
Only through the colors of the aurora can I unfetter the hush frozen in the oldest ice. 
The amber music sheet shows no staff or notes by common lamplight. 
When the aurora strengthens, red veins become low bronze tones. 
Green opens into handbells, blue into glass, silver into breath between strokes. 
Mother copied the score when statues began with listening, not calculation. 
Her last margin says, not to hurry the hush. 
I want to hurry, because the shaft carries every cough. 
Then ice ticks, and the cavern returns to wider calm. 
I set the amber music sheet on a frozen prism and touch the first rope. 
It does not move until green finds its matching strand. 
When I pull, the bell gives no ring, only absence shaped like a bowl. 
That absence is the first cut of the hush, and my fear settles. 
Violet comes next, warning of a flaw in marble or perhaps in me. 
I remember Mira at fourteen, carving soap swallows with the smallest chisel. 
She never asked if the bird was useful before she gave it wings. 
I let violet become the bell that throws a note away from itself. 
Another absence joins the first, beveling silence until it has a face. 
The cavern sways between labor and rest, and I stop resisting its oscillating repose. 
Above, Mira shifts her mallet, a dry whisper in my bones. 
The councillors still, knowing a first blow cannot be repeated. 
I read crimson through the colors of the aurora and see no command, only permission. 
The sheet warms as if Mother has laid her palms over mine. 
I do not pull to prove skill or buy cheap forgiveness. 
I pull because Mira deserves a city where a carved bird can be necessary. 
The ropes rise together, each following a color now a tone. 
Their silence unfurls through the shaft, layered, exact, strangely tender. 
At the exact stroke, the sculpted hush meets marble before sound can claim it. 
The chisel stops, held by a quiet carved rather than empty. 
For one long instant no ledger moves; even the aurora rests between colors. 
Then a councillor laughs, not in scorn, but as a man surprised by air. 
Blank certificates remain folded because the hush itself has become the proof. 
When Mira lifts the chisel, the groove holds a patient emptiness ready for wings. 
I understand that wonder was never the ornament; it is the usable pause that lets first things begin. 
In the ice cavern, colored light settles over the amber music sheet, and I hear nothing missing. 
The bells hang still; the city above begins to breathe.
