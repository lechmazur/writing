Under borrowed starlight, Nera Venn, a wreck-diver ghost whisperer, kneels in the candle-lit shrine carved into glacier ice and warms a melted snow globe between her palms.  Its glass has slumped into a crescent, but a bubble of clear water still holds a miniature silver bell.  Candles line seven blue niches, and villagers call those panes windows of connection, where grief passes as a dream.  The register lists each dream without dates; its first page reads Timeframe: None, because the drowned refuse clocks.  For that reason Nera asks not when a ghost knocked, but who stood beside the glass.  She has come north to salvage something pure from the wreckage of the Lark, which broke beneath this glacier after its beacon went dark.  Divers brought up coins, rosaries, and a salt-scrubbed captain's log; Nera brought up the toy and voices tangled in it.  The port council says Captain Eder mistook the channel and drowned the steerage families, but the dead whisper around it like wind.  Nera weaves truth carefully, never pulling one pale thread hard enough to tear the rest.  She intends to expose the hand that closed the beacon, yet carries no accusation that cuts the innocent praying here.  Only by analyzing cameo appearances in strangers' dreams can she find it without forcing the dead to speak plainly.  A baker dreams of a gloved hand hooding a lantern; a midwife dreams of that glove buttoning a child's coat; a tax collector dreams of the coat in curved glass.  None of them sailed on the Lark, yet each slept near someone tied to the council's rescue fund by bread, cloth, or coins.  Nera maps the dreams on ice in candle soot, each beside the pane where its dreamer sat.  The pattern is no sequence but a web of favors, debts, borrowed rooms, and bonds.  Every cameo is a window viewed from a different bed, and every window frames the same gloved pilot, Orrin Sable.  Declared lost with the ship, he lives beneath a pilgrim's hood and keeps this shrine's wicks trimmed.  Nera knows this because the melted snow globe can turn his reflection into a cameo beneath the seventh candle.  The object belonged to Lio, a cabin boy carrying it for his sister with clean paper folded inside its base.  When the Lark split, Lio pressed the globe to a porthole so a child saw one small bell instead of black water.  That tenderness is the pure thing Nera wants, not a verdict that drowns the living as well.  Sable enters with oil and the patient smile of a rehearsed mourner.  He asks whether the ghosts have forgiven Captain Eder; his glove rests beside the lantern in the soot drawing.  Nera does not answer quickly; she sets the melted snow globe on the central sill, taps its bell, and lights the seventh candle behind it.  The warped glass catches flame, splits it into seven pale stars, and throws each onto a pane where a dreamer sat.  The blue ice holds tiny scenes: a hooded lantern, a coat buttoned wrong, a twice-counted coin, Lio's hand at the porthole.  The windows of connection do not accuse; they let separated people see they have always shared one room.  Sable's smile folds; his hand hides the crescent scar where a signal lantern burned through his glove.  Nera names what the dream cameos have woven; because she weaves truth carefully, she lets him add the last thread.  The baker, midwife, tax collector, and Eder's daughter stand in the doorway, drawn by that bell's ringing.  Sable sees them and says the council paid him to darken the beacon, wreck the Lark, and blame Captain Eder.  He adds that Eder had refused their insurance scheme, and the woven pattern leaves no loose end to dispute.  He says Lio held the cabin latch open until the last boat left, singing so trapped children counted breaths instead of waves.  Nera warms the softened base and eases out Lio's paper, dry beneath wax despite salt.  It bears eight careful names and the line, Keep the bell ringing for whoever comes next.
