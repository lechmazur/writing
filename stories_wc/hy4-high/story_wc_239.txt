I was the end of history witness, appointed to stand at the final instant of each cosmos and certify that nothing remained hidden. 
I took the post to unravel cosmic illusions, because every apocalypse I certified arrived in flattering veils: heroic suns, obedient clocks, and the claim that an observer could remain outside the observed. 
At the last ending, those veils hung so thickly that even extinction seemed a ceremony performed for my benefit. 
Then the universe stopped between one vanishing beat and the next, and a door of black stone opened where no direction had been. 
No appeal could last longer than the pause, so I had to work across a universe's hiccup, from a canceled star to an unbreathed law. 
Beyond the door waited the ancient temple library lost in time, erased from calendars before calendars learned to lie. 
Its pillars held records of earlier conjurations, and its shelves circled inward like grooves of a held breath. 
The air maintained a resounding hush, not silence but the pressure of annulled voices listening for one accurate word. 
Dead librarians had named me wanderingly exact, since I drifted through impossible stacks yet found the one shelf, page, and syllable required. 
That gift mattered now, because the stairs went down only if I left them, and the central chamber could be reached by journeying inward while walking out. 
At each outward threshold I surrendered an assumption: survival proved no merit; memory granted no possession; distance offered no innocence. 
When I admitted that I had needed the universe to be a spectacle, the last corridor uncurled and let me pass. 
The arbitration chamber was a round cell with no judges, only a desk, two vacant chairs, and a softcover dictionary bound in gray cloth. 
On its first page, in a hand like dried rain, was the case: Appearances against the Unborn, disputing the meaning of beginning. 
Through Appearances, the universe argued that a beginning meant a time before loss, so every future world could deny its ending. 
The Unborn argued that a beginning meant raw possibility, so nothing inherited would ever be honest. 
The procedure was spiral arbitration: every circuit around the desk forced the contested word to shed one lie and return thinner, older, harder to flatter. 
I opened the softcover dictionary at beginning, read that it was an entrance without a creditor, and heard the shelves shiver at the fraud. 
On the first spiral the word lost soon, because even time had been a costume sewn over change. 
On the second it lost clean, because every created thing carried dust from what had failed before it. 
On the third it lost elsewhere, since no world begins in a place untouched by the hands that name it. 
With each circuit I felt my office defined, and I understood that the last illusion was neither star, clock, nor heaven. 
It was the tidy distance between a witness and the history he was paid to watch. 
Appearances asked how an ending could be certified by someone who had never entered it; the Unborn asked how a beginning could be trusted to someone who had never been one. 
Both questions were accurate, and accuracy had been the temptation I mistook for duty. 
To close the case I had to unify the claims, not by choosing a side but by giving the dictionary a definition that left my separateness no shelter. 
In the margin I wrote beginning: ending accepted without disguise, and knew at once that the sentence also described me. 
The arbitration spiral tightened once around the word, once around the room, and once around the official self that had wanted truth without cost. 
Then the gray cover warmed under my palm, as if the book had waited for a hand willing to be corrected. 
The shelves ceased circling, the chairs filled with dust, and the resounding hush let me hear the universe relinquish its mirrors. 
Stars, clocks, heavens, and the untouched observer in me loosened together, and the cosmic illusions I had chased fell away in relief. 
Outside, the hiccup ended; inside, the ancient temple library lost in time folded its corridors into the single line I had written. 
The new cosmos began as a hush that did not need me apart from it; history and witness kept one name.
