The skyborne grotto under aurora held a radiant hush, as if light had been taught to enter a sickroom without waking anyone.  Mara Venn climbed from the rope ladder with her mortuary case, while the city below went dark window by window.  She had been its beautician mortician, licensed to powder cold cheeks, close stubborn eyelids, and make departure gentle enough for the living to bear.  That trade had taught her that a final expression was not a lie but a shelter, built from exact attention.  She learned the lesson fully after painting one impossible warm shade beneath her sister Lio's gray mouth.  Her slate carried only Timeframe: None, for no clock in the upper winds survived a storm of light; without a schedule, the instant was all she had.  She had come to capture one last spark before oblivion.  Not for herself, but for the nurseries below, where children would inherit a sky with no memory of green.  The old pilots called this blue hollow a threshold of stillness, where motion could be persuaded to set down its instruments.  They said that beyond that pause lay the first quiet of unmaking, where one unspent gleam might still be gathered.  Mara did not believe pilots, but she believed in pauses, having watched a pulse hesitate between beats and grief hesitate before a farewell.  She unpacked brushes, translucent powders, a needle, a brass receiver, and an emerald chip cut from a drowned beacon lens.  The chip held a green depth like a forest behaving politely underwater, and her task was to infuse it with the spark.  The grotto was full of aurora static, a fine white hiss caught in ice and released whenever the walls warmed by a breath.  Her method was finding patterns in static, learned from failing pulses, storm radios, and the decisions color makes before it leaves skin.  Most ears heard weather; Mara heard the stubborn arrangement of things about to vanish.  She held the receiver above the emerald chip and turned its dial until the hiss broke into separate grains.  Where a grain returned too regularly to be wind, she touched green powder to the frost; where the hiss opened into shaped silence, she left bare ice.  The marks around the chip resembled a face in repose, not painted younger than truth, only relieved of panic.  Her face in the wall stayed lucidly watchful, neither brave nor broken, simply awake to every least alteration.  The threshold opened only when a person stopped forcing loss into a living shape, so Mara let even the memory of Lio stop tugging at her sleeve.  Her breath slowed, the powder settled, and the hiss grew smaller, until the cave seemed less a place than a held note.  At the threshold of stillness she felt no doorway, only a courteous lessening, as if the world stepped aside for one bright thing.  A spark appeared above the emerald chip, no bigger than the glint in a living eye.  It did not dance or flare; it simply consented to be seen.  Mara understood that oblivion was not a mouth but a tidy room, and the spark could leave one lamp burning in its corridor.  She lifted the chip with tweezers, held it beneath the glint, and opened the receiver's gate so the patterned hiss could pour through.  The static entered green stone, found the lines she had drawn, and curled around the spark like thread around a button.  Lucidly watchful, she waited until hiss and glint shared one rhythm, then pressed a brush once, softly, to infuse the emerald chip.  Light settled into the gem without noise, the way the correct blush settles when a face has been understood at last.  The aurora went white, then transparent, and the city below gave one answering gleam from its dark windows.  The grotto began to loosen, its blue walls shedding motes that rose as if released from long duty.  On the slate beneath Timeframe: None, she wrote Delivered with light, then closed the case against her ribs.  She did not hurry, because hurry would have broken the radiant hush that held the chip warm through leather and cloth.  The skyborne grotto under aurora dissolved behind her, yet the stillness came along, not as absence but as attention.  Mara carried the emerald chip down toward the children while the last spark kept its small green promise before oblivion.
