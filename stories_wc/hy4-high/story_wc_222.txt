Only when the sky is wrong did the icebound spire above the clouds ring its frozen bells.  Stars crawled under noon; the moon rose from the world's floor.  Canons called it error; cloud villages called it birth.  Corin Vale climbed the last ladder with cautious abandon, measuring each rung before he laughed at the drop.  He was a chivalrous thief, so he bowed to the locked door and left a polite receipt for its seal.  Under his tongue lay a stolen key; in his sleeve slept a pocket sundial lid.  As an apprentice of wonder, he had been taught that marvel must be handled like a sleeping bird.  He wanted only to embrace the unknown with open arms, not cage it in another tidy chart.  That wish had brought him here because the Charter of Permitted Hours was about to be sealed.  At the thirteenth bell, the canons would freeze this unfamiliar heaven into the old map.  Springs below would keep their ordained courses, and every unimagined rain would be declared illegal.  Corin had come to amend that decree before frost could make it holy.  The spire was a needle of blue ice above every weather.  Inside, warm lamps burned behind rime, and the Board of Hours waited beneath a dome of black glass.  It was a chessboard large enough for priests to walk on, each square a different depth of winter.  To amend the Charter, the rule permitted one method: swapping hand-carved chess pieces with secrets inside.  Long ago, the founders made law sacredly playful, believing honesty survived best when dressed as a game.  A secret placed on an open square became evidence, and evidence could amend the Charter.  The canons, however, had stopped inviting petitioners from the clouds.  They locked the offered pieces in the Vault of Unheard Things.  Corin had stolen three, each thumb-high and heavier than a lie.  He left courteous copies; theft without restitution was merely cruelty.  Now the ceremonial game stood set, white bishops of certainty against black rooks of rain.  The High Canon wore a mitre of frost and the smile of a man who had never lost.  Corin, disguised as a page with a silver bell, kept his hands folded and his breathing shallow.  The wrong sky leaned through the dome, turning shadows into questions.  He drew out the pocket sundial lid and let it rest upon the board rail.  Its notch cast two shadows, one toward noon, one toward an unborn hour.  Mira, his teacher, had called the second shadow wonder's first rude gesture.  Where it fell, hidden ink warmed on cedar, and Corin could read which secrets had been freely given.  One rook held a farmer's map of rain that fell upward.  One knight held the High Canon's private note admitting that his daughter had named a new star.  One bishop held a mother's question, copied beside her child's empty bed: why must heaven never revise itself?  Any piece could break the map; only the gentlest could open a future.  At the twelfth bell, canons began their moves, laughing as if laughter were a lock.  Bell in hand, Corin switched a silver pawn for a bishop from his sleeve.  He did it by swapping hand-carved chess pieces with secrets inside, spilling nothing aloud.  The second shadow chose the mother's bishop, not the canon's shame.  He set it on an open square; the ice chimed like a shy bell.  The High Canon saw the switch and lifted a white-gloved hand.  Corin bowed, confessed the theft, and offered the stolen seal back with both palms.  The founders' rule protected the hand that placed evidence, even when the hand was a thief's.  The game could not continue until the charter was amended; the square had spoken.  With the pocket sundial lid, he cut one new line through frost-bright parchment.  The new line read: the sky may be otherwise.  Mirrors shuddered; the wrong sky steadied without becoming familiar, and rain climbed upward from the clouds.  Below, windows opened to an unpromised season.  Corin returned the copies, bowed, and took the ladder with cautious abandon.  He had amended the law, carrying no certainty, only a lid warm as a small sun.  The apprentice of wonder kept his arms open; the unknown came laughing through.
