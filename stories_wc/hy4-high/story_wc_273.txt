After the eclipse ends, light returns to the wind-carved encampment like a blade laid flat on stone. 
Rhu, the albino tracker, waits in the lee trench, smoked lenses over pale eyes and serene anger beneath them. 
She has come to discover why time slows in crisis, because a mudslide once held her brother between breaths for nine counted days. 
The healers called it mercy, the governors called it order, and Rhu learned to call it theft. 
In the council hollow, clerks copy every family name in disappearing ink beneath the cut loess walls. 
By decree, any name that fades from the page also fades from ration lines, wells, memory, and the right to remain. 
Rhu does not shout at the clerks, because her anger is too clean for noise and too old for haste. 
Instead she opens a leather case and releases her artificial hummingbird drone, whose glass wings blur against the returning glare. 
It is thumb-small, bright enough to shame the sun, and consciously wild after years of her refusing to command it. 
She had trained it to choose unlikely eddies, since expected trails lead only to what authority counts. 
A belly vial holds the same disappearing ink, thinned with meltwater until it stains for a few heartbeats. 
When the drone tilts, its needle beak drops ink on the trench floor, and wind pulls it into a frayed map. 
The ink vanishes as it moves, so the map is only a sequence of losses that the tracker has learned to read. 
She keeps her own mind consciously wild, letting instinct outrun fear without accepting fear's orders. 
The wind had carved the encampment into ribs and scallops, and Rhu knows each groove by the note it makes when sand crosses it. 
A false note means an unregistered foot, a broken peg, or a storm arriving before the clerks can finish their census. 
The eclipse had chilled the cliffs for three hours, and now the first full sun splits a reservoir of grit from the eastern shelf. 
A peg snaps, a water jar tilts toward a child, and the whole camp enters the long moment that Rhu has hunted across half her life. 
Dust hangs in bright coins, screams soften into threads, and the clerks freeze with their pens above the names. 
Only Rhu moves, not because she is outside time, but because she refuses to spend the crisis begging it to choose one ending. 
The drone moves with her, sipping the stalled air and spilling ink in quick dots along the nearest groove. 
Each dot disappears, but the place where it had been pulls the next dot sideways, until a lattice of brief absences opens under the falling jar. 
Rhu reads the lattice and understands why time slows in crisis: fear clutches every possible rescue and every possible death, so the world must weigh them all before any hand can close. 
Time is not kind or cruel, only crowded, and disappearing ink is the only substance that can show the crowd because it leaves by the same door that possibility uses. 
With that knowledge, her serene anger finds its use, not as revenge against the clerks but as a route for everyone they intended to erase. 
She whistles once, and the consciously wild drone abandons its dots, sweeps low, and empties the vial across the council floor. 
Wind catches the flood before it can dry, combing it into ephemeral patterns in spilled ink that curl around tent ropes, bread ovens, water jars, and the clerks' ledgers. 
The patterns are not merely signs; they are the unchosen futures of the crisis, written only where ink agrees to vanish. 
Rhu calls to the families in a level voice, telling them to step toward what is missing and not toward what remains. 
They obey because her certainty is calm, and because the slowed dust has already shown them their own frozen fear. 
One by one, the families disappear through ephemeral patterns in spilled ink, crossing the brief shapes while the world is still weighing other outcomes. 
To the clerks, when time resumes, the census will show empty columns, and the wind-carved encampment will be only a rumor of clean stone. 
Rhu goes last, carrying her brother's name in her mouth and no ledger's permission in her hand. 
Behind her the artificial hummingbird drone folds its wings and lets the last pattern erase even the bright fact of its flight. 
In the unrecorded country beyond the ink, time no longer needs to slow, and her anger finally rests.
