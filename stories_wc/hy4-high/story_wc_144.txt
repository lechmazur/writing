The morning narrows as an anchor lifts for a ship's maiden voyage beyond the seaward gates of the mist-coated rail depot. 
Within the long shed, benches and rails hold a magnetic calm, the kind that draws shouting down to breathing. 
Mara Vale, a heartfelt artisan, keeps the repair shop in the old ticket office, where every broken thing is received like a guest. 
She oversees while blind, and no one in the depot calls it courage anymore, because her attention has made it ordinary. 
Shallow inspection basins, rain chains, and fountain lips form water features along the tracks, built long ago to cool axles and calibrate signal lamps. 
Selenium cups beside them turn changing brightness into current, feeding selenium keys beneath her fingers, so she reads a beam by pressure, pitch, and warmth. 
Her apprentices know this discipline as examining light refraction through water features, though Mara says it is simply listening to what water does with light. 
Long before the harbor learned her name, she lost her sight when a freight signal exploded in fog, and shards reached her before the warning did. 
Since then her deepest motive has been to decode feedback into direction, to make scattered clicks and bruises tell a living person which way to move. 
On this morning, the Meridian waits at the quay with her brass compass steady but useless, because the overnight storm dragged a shoal into the charted channel. 
Only the artificial hummingbird drone sent ahead at dawn carried the new current in its wing strain, prism eye, and stubborn little memory drum. 
It has returned to the depot cracked, folded, and silent, dropped by Joss, a harbor pilot whose hands will not stop trembling. 
Joss sets it on the felt mat and says that the anchor chain is already singing upward and that the captain will trust no chart unless the bird speaks. 
Mara does not answer at once, because haste always arrives loud, and loud things must be made gentle before they can be heard. 
She asks for the room to quiet, and the apprentices lower their tools as if a conductor had drawn them into one breath. 
The magnetic calm around her bench is not softness but a field, and everyone, even Joss, leans toward it. 
With two fingertips she finds a torn wing membrane, a bent tail, and, under the left rotor, a hairline break in the prism. 
The break matters more than the wreck, because every stored glint in the drum has been tilted by that flaw. 
Mara chooses to patch the prism first, not the wing, and her apprentice Nia brings sail varnish, a sliver of lens glass, and heated silk thread. 
Joss opens his mouth to protest, then closes it, since Mara has already begun measuring the fracture against the humming heartbeat of the drone. 
The patch is small enough to be absurd, a clear crescent laid over the crack with a brush made from one white hair. 
To test it, Mara places the bird above the nearest basin, starts the signal lamp, and lets the beam bend through ripples, droplets, and the patched glass. 
The selenium keys tap a crooked scale under her left hand, then hesitate at the third note, where a true prism would have struck clean. 
She tilts the fountain lip by one finger width, and the water features split the light into a second band that presses a colder key. 
That pressure gives her the tilt of the error, and from the memory drum she draws the feedback in pulses of wing load, compass drag, and echo delay. 
Nia reads the numbers aloud as Mara subtracts the prism error, and the pattern resolves into a safe bearing, east by north from the bell buoy. 
There is still the torn wing, and if the bird cannot hover, the mast lamp will have no steady shutter to copy. 
She patches the membrane with silk, warms the bent tail between her palms, and sets the rotor trembling until it sounds like a bee in clover. 
Joss reaches for the drone, but Mara keeps one finger on its back, waiting for the note that tells her the machine is no longer guessing. 
The note comes, steady and round, and Mara loads the bearing into the memory drum as a direction the bridge watch can read. 
Joss runs to the signal mast with the artificial hummingbird drone cupped in his palms, and the shutter lamp stutters the bearing before the anchor clears the water. 
The Meridian turns, and Mara resets her basin for the next broken promise.
