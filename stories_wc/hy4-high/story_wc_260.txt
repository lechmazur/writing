Only after the con is revealed does Mara Venn return to the misty workshop under stars, where fog softens the benches and accusations still cling to the glass. 
The shimmering stillness is the held breath before a verdict. 
As the gentle wildlife handler for the traveling menagerie, Mara reads fear in scales and small refusals. 
Her fragile convictions were simple: an animal must never be made to lie, and a person who feeds strays cannot be wholly cruel. 
That belief had kept Ivo beside her, though he sold blessings and hid the ledger. 
Tonight the ledger and oracle cage share the floor, relics of a fraud that charged grief for painted colors. 
Mara had opened the cage before the aldermen because the chameleon's tail tip tightened whenever Ivo pressed the pedal under the table. 
Under velvet moss, a wire, clamp, and dyed shutters turned pain into prophecy. 
The crowd left, Ivo bowed his hands, and Mara kept the key to the feed shed. 
She has stayed to balance self-respect and compassion before dawn turns hesitation into judgment. 
The magistrate will ask what was taken, who knew, and how the trick worked. 
Mara must reconstruct the fraud by shedding layers, because a bare accusation would make Ivo monster or martyr. 
Ivo had taught her: remove only what gives way, then lay pieces in the order they served. 
On oilcloth lies a thin shed from the chameleon's tail tip, curled like a question. 
It is quietly vivid, green at the seam and blue where copper dust entered the clamp. 
Mara lifts the painted moon first; pinholes appear in stars the villagers had paid to consult. 
Beneath it, amber glass carries donors' names matched to shutters, while the ledger supplies amounts. 
She aligns the glass with the shed skin and sees that the clamp struck exactly where the blue stain blooms. 
The animal had chosen no sinner; it had answered a wire with the only grammar a tail knows. 
She peels velvet, glue, moss, and mirrored strip that made the chameleon appear holy. 
Under it lies a sleeve holding dead names from burial notices, payments, and Ivo's note that mercy means silence. 
This layer hurts most; it proves he watched grief enter and chose the shade that opened purses. 
Mara's first conviction stays upright; the second bends flat beneath those names. 
She remembers Ivo stealing bread for a lame fox and warming a lizard in his sleeve. 
She also remembers widows counting coins while shutters clicked, and that memory refuses to let tenderness become erasure. 
Ivo waits in the feed shed, held not by hatred but by the villagers' demand that he face morning. 
Mara could slip him the key and call it mercy, but every donor's name would follow her into every cage. 
She could give the magistrate the ledger and call it justice, but that would forget the hands that steadied hers over a splint. 
The shed door creaks when she takes him tea, and he does not ask her to lie. 
He says only that the chameleon hated the blue shutter, and Mara believes him because its skin has said so. 
She learns compassion is not a curtain over evidence; it is a lamp low enough for shame to look up. 
Back inside, mist beads on starry nails while Mara writes two pages. 
The first lists each mechanism, payment, witness, and injury, including clamp marks on the chameleon's tail tip. 
The second asks for public repayment, a ban from animal acts, and no stocks, because punishment without repair leaves widows unseen. 
She wraps the evidence in oilcloth and crosses out Ivo's note that mercy means silence. 
Before dawn, she carries the pages to the magistrate and returns with the cup. 
Ivo will answer for the con beside the people he cheated, not beneath them. 
Mara opens the small cage and sets the chameleon on a warm branch beneath the paling stars. 
Its tail, no longer wired, curls through mist with a color quietly vivid enough to shame every painted moon. 
Her fragile convictions do not harden into certainty; they settle like scales finding a branch, still breakable, still useful. 
In the misty workshop under stars, shimmering stillness returns, not as pardon, but as room for truth and mercy to breathe.
