After the sixth sense awakens, the Mechanical rainforest stops being a place and becomes a question. 
For Ivo, whose awakening came late, the question had teeth. 
The gate registers called him a Stray philosopher because he had no school and asked why the trees dreamed. 
At the Conservatory he had mapped legal thoughts until a notebook from his old teacher ruined him. 
It read: To rediscover the wild frontier of your mind, stop mapping and listen. 
He had come to the Mechanical rainforest only to obey that impossible instruction. 
The forest had taught him to expect probable miracles, outcomes so exact that people mistook preparation for magic. 
Copper palms breathed mist, ironwood leaves chimed, and nickel vines ticked under scheduled rain. 
The sixth sense did not show him futures; it let him hear a mechanism deciding what it was for. 
The hum of insight was not a voice but the interval before a question and a hidden structure agreed. 
After his own awakening, he followed that hum as other people followed roads. 
When the Sense Registry examined him as a boy, the stamp had named his oddity in three words: Translocates during sleep. 
Each dawn he woke beneath a different canopy, dew on his sleeves, and after shame faded he called the condition tuition. 
The sound of wind through different trees became his compass, because ironwood chimed, palms hissed, vines clicked, and bamboo valves made a soft vowel. 
Comparing those voices told him where sleep had dropped him and which hidden river of gears ran below. 
Three weeks after the sixth sense awakened in him, a small gear appeared in his closed fist. 
It was warm, thumbnail-wide, with one tooth bent into a question mark. 
When he held it near his ear, the hum of insight sharpened until he could taste tin and thunder. 
He understood that the small gear had fallen from the buried Sleep Engine, which braided dreams into approved routes. 
His nightly displacements had never been pure wandering; they had been lessons in a cage he could not see. 
Returned to its old socket, it would fix every sleeper on counted paths; carried to the root, it might make the routes wild again. 
He spent the day listening past piston bark, condenser flowers, and herons of folded tin, matching the gear's tick to gaps in the wind. 
Near dusk, the ironwood grew silent, the palms hissed without moving, and the bamboo valves uttered a vowel no map contained. 
Ivo lay beneath that contradiction, set the small gear on his tongue, and let his old condition carry him. 
When he woke, he was kneeling inside a hollow trunk whose roots were braided around a turning shaft. 
The shaft hummed with the same insight, but here it sounded frightened by its own obedience. 
Each root strand carried a tree voice knotted to the others, so no dream could choose a new direction. 
A brass plaque offered Repair and Obey, but older hands had scratched both away. 
Ivo chose one command, Unravel, and began to unravel the braid, strand by audible strand. 
The root-knot resisted, then loosened, because a knot made of separate songs cannot survive being heard as separate songs. 
As strands parted, ironwood chimed alone, palms hissed alone, vines clicked alone, and bamboo valves opened into silence. 
His awakened sense held those differences without blending them, and the hum of insight became a chord wide enough to stand in. 
In that chord he saw that the teacher's line had been an address rather than a command. 
To rediscover the wild frontier of your mind was not to travel beyond the last tree, but to let one unmapped difference remain unmapped. 
He set the small gear into the loosened end, a seed for paths that would grow without permission. 
Its bent tooth caught, the shaft missed one beat, and every tree in the Mechanical rainforest found a different note of wind. 
Probable miracles gathered in the condensation rain at dawn, and Ivo stayed where he was, carrying the frontier with him through the rainforest.
