After surviving the attempt, Mara carried her last warm pane into the quiet canyon under stars and waited for the ringing in her ears to become useful. 
By dawn she would learn who had tried to seal her inside the annealing shed, but first she needed the cold to finish what fire had begun. 
As a luminous glass artist, she made windows that held starlight the way a held breath holds a name, and the town had trusted those windows to guide travelers along the rim. 
Her steadily adaptive hands knew when to feed a furnace, when to let a bubble rise, and when to stand still while a glowing sheet chose its shape. 
The attempt had broken that knowledge: the shed door had been barred, the vents stuffed with pitch, and her newest pane set to cool too fast so it would burst inward. 
She had gone through the half cooled glass because the glowing color showed a seam of thinner light, and the canyon received her with snow, ash, and a cut along her ribs. 
In her bandaged palm lay a charcoal smuggler's ledger strip, no longer than a willow leaf, scorched at both ends and stiff with resin. 
Oren had pressed it into her hand before the fire, a charcoal runner who supplied forbidden fuel to her kiln and warmer stones to the families the council forgot. 
The bailiff called him a thief; the widows called him a schedule; Mara had called him a friend only after he was gone. 
Now the strip was a scorched testament, one last statement written in tally strokes, soot, and deliberate blanks where names should have been. 
She had come to the canyon to discover purpose in the pauses, because the blanks appeared where Oren had waited, and waiting had kept several people alive. 
At the annealing shed, every pause had once meant patience, a courtesy to cooling glass; after the fire, each pause sounded like an accusation she had survived. 
The stars made no answer, but the canyon held a drifting resonance, the low tone her panes gave when a boot changed the pressure of the air. 
When the first pale light loosened the shadows, she set the warm pane flat on a slab and watched the cold bloom over it. 
Frost did not settle evenly where feet had packed the sand, where coals had spilled, or where a body had stood long enough to lend the ground its heat. 
Mara read patterns in morning frost the way she read stress lines in glass, following silver ferns, bare circles, and thin arrows of rime. 
With that map, she began to retrace the night from the shed to the ledge, stepping carefully so her own boots would not invent new meanings. 
The first bare circle lay by the vent, where someone had crouched to pack pitch and had then remained still while the furnace breathed. 
A second bare circle, smaller and rimmed with ash, lay outside the door; Oren had stood there with a water jar, waiting for a pause in the guard's rounds. 
Between them, the frost kept a braided track of quick steps and one long hesitation, as if fear and decision had crossed the same patch of sand. 
Mara held the ledger strip beside the mark, and the third blank on the strip matched the length of that hesitation. 
Oren had recorded time not with numbers but with gaps between charcoal notches, each gap measured by how long frost would later refuse the hidden warmth. 
He had built a code for the canyon winter, because paper names could condemn people and ink could be washed, while warmth in sand waited for dawn. 
Her steadily adaptive mind changed, turning blame into shelter. 
The strip said Oren used each gap to free families, hide lamp glass, loosen Mara's bar, and wet the pitch. 
Its last blank was a white oval where Oren chose not to run. 
The scorched testament accused the bailiff, who had killed Oren, then sealed the shed to erase witness and record. 
Ash crescent prints doubled back at dawn; Mara waited with the warm pane. 
He came to inspect the frost; she struck the glass once. 
Star salt woke in ledger blanks, a drifting resonance across the walls. 
The dark bands returned the pauses he had tried to erase. 
She offered the council road, then left one beat of quiet, and he walked unchased. 
Mara set the scorched testament into a new pane, gaps intact. 
Travelers stopped there; her survival became a steadily adaptive practice answerable to the next breath.
