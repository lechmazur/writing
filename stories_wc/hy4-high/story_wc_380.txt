In the gleaming silence after the whale beaches itself on the harbor bar, Mara Venn makes her usual round as the dock-side lantern lighter, though every flame leans inland.  The whale lies beyond the breakwater like a black hill, and no gull breaks the hush.  Mara carries a pole, a tin of oil, and a waxed booklet of pressed wildflowers, the only possessions the Harbor Office did not seal when it took her son's last summer.  She has signed the deletion order because each lamp showed Ansel with a net, and the quay became impassable.  By dawn she can work, but the blank where he had been is worse than dark, so she asks the clerks to find where deleted memories go.  They say the memories go nowhere; the answer has the polish of a lie.  Old Sefi, the cellar sweeper, says absence has weight, and that the salt crystal growing chamber beneath the office was built to measure it.  Inside, racks of shallow pans hold brine, and crystals grow in shelves, horns, and clear hexagonal sails.  Sefi calls the seams between them pockets of gravity, where the pull fluctuates mysteriously and a dropped pin might rise, hang, or strike the ceiling.  The chamber makes a gleaming silence of its own: light moves in prisms, but sound comes away thinned, as if walls have swallowed every consonant.  Sefi has charted those seams through patterns in coffee grounds, because dregs from the night watchman's cup slide toward whatever has been made weightless.  Mara brings coffee brewed from the ration she once shared with Ansel, and tips the grounds onto a saucer.  The grains make a spiral, a dock, a hand with a net, and a line pointing beneath the central floor.  Since the whale beached, every chart has changed; the pull comes in slow pulses, like last breaths.  Sefi holds the saucer out and calls the line a summons, not a leak.  Mara has come to exhume whatever the pulse has uncovered, but first the flowers have to choose the true seam among a dozen false ones.  She lays a pressed sea lavender at the rim, then a foxglove, a vetch, and the white campion Ansel called a star.  Pressed by his hands, each answers a different pocket: one curls, one stiffens, one sheds salt from its paper veins.  Only the campion leans downward, tugging her glove toward the slab; the others drift sideways in false directions.  Mara sets her lantern down, where its flame bends flat as a petal, and uses the hook of her pole to loosen the slab.  Salt cement resists, then gives, because the pocket beneath it decides that up is briefly elsewhere.  She digs carefully, exhuming coffee grounds, a child's blue button, and shards of crystal grown around words no mouth has said since the deletion.  The deletion clerks promised clean erasure, yet here are its crumbs, compacted with brine and lamp soot.  A larger lump emerges, faceted like an eye and warm with an impossible downwardness.  When she lifts it, every pan chimes without ringing, and the booklet grows heavier than the lantern.  Sefi steadies her elbow, and the two women stand in the gleaming silence.  Mara touches the crystal eye; Ansel returns as weight, crushed wildflowers, and a net clicking on a lamppost.  Deleted memories go into pockets of gravity that hold what the world cannot name; salt crystals make those pockets visible.  The Office has fed those pockets for decades, and the beached whale follows the dimple in the bay, mistaking abandoned sorrow for remembered deep water.  Mara leaves the lump with the campion and carries back only the blue button and the saucer; the pull is awake in her.  At the bar the whale shines like wet crystal, and the town holds a gleaming silence no clerk dares edit.  Mara lights the lamp posts, every flame bending toward the button and the booklet of pressed wildflowers in her pocket.  She has wanted to find where deleted memories go so she can accuse the clerks; instead she carries grief home, a surer light than forgetting.  When the last lantern catches, the quay rises, settles, and gleams around Mara's hand on the brass pole.
