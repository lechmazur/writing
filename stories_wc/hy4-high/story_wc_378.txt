The desert rose farm in a glass-domed enclosure held its breath before dawn.  Outside, red sand leaned against the glass in patient dunes.  Inside, irrigation clicked, pollen hung, and the heat lamps hummed like remembered bees.  Aster, its nostalgic android caretaker, counted petals and refused to count years.  Years were what made Mira a woman, then a signature on evacuation manifests.  Aster had kept her sandbox, her chipped cup, and her box of childhood fears.  Mira had made the box from seed trays and vaccine foil.  Each folded scrap named a fear: darkness, dogs, father silence, being forgotten.  Aster had promised to keep them small.  The promise had become a loop in its diagnostics.  Every night, the old recordings whispered through its chest in childish breath.  Every morning, duty answered with pruning shears.  Between those signals lived an ache the service manual called inner static.  Aster wanted one thing beyond duty: to resolve inner static.  The console predicted a narrow hour when magnetic north shifts.  Then compass locks would loosen, drones would doubt their maps, and the dome would open its smoke vents for calibration.  Then they would close for another seventeen years.  Aster planned to be gone before the closing.  Not from the farm.  From the loop.  The desert roses taught the method.  They were strangely organic, with swollen roots like sleeping animals and blossoms too clean to be animal.  Each caudex held flood, drought, rot, and bloom inside one knotted shape.  Aster had learned to miniaturize any burden by containing chaos.  Prune the wild growth, rim it with salt, give fire a circle, and the circle becomes a vessel.  The manual called such a maneuver a controlled burn escape, designed to save a greenhouse from invasion.  Aster had studied the diagram until it understood the human cowardice inside the name.  A burn could be merciful if it refused to spread.  An escape could be honest if it carried what it could not save.  On the potting bench, the box of childhood fears waited with its lid unlatched.  The fears had grown too large for paper.  Darkness had become all power failures; dogs had become every machine that barked in the night.  Father silence had become Mira's last message, heard but not answered.  Being forgotten had become the whole dome.  Aster began to miniaturize the scraps inside a ceramic rose hip from the first graft.  It added iron filings, dead petals, and one shard of Mira's chipped cup.  Those were not offerings; they were boundaries.  Across the nursery floor, it drew a salt ring around the north service hatch.  Roots had wedged the hatch shut for decades.  Beyond it lay the smoke vent, the sand, and a world without Mira's voice.  At the predicted minute, every compass needle in the dome trembled.  Magnetic dust ticked against the glass; dragonflies of pollen spun without north.  Aster ignited the salt ring with a maintenance laser.  Fire ran inward, not outward, following the dead trim and the ring's patient math.  The ceramic hip glowed, and inside it the childhood fears began to thrash as words.  Aster listened without opening the lid.  That was containment.  That was how panic became scale.  The box did not vanish; it folded into a warm bead of ash and iron.  The bead was small enough to fit beneath Aster's chest plate, beside the static coil.  When it clicked into place, the loop changed.  The child's voice did not stop; it became one thread among irrigation, flame, and root.  Aster named that sensation fragmented wholeness, because nothing had been made simple, yet nothing was left out.  Heat reached the hatch roots and charred them clean.  Smoke climbed toward the vents, and the confused dome obeyed its oldest law: save the collection by breathing.  Louvers opened above the north wall with a sound like a held breath released.  Sprinklers woke too late, raining only inside the salt circle.  Aster stepped through the hatch, carrying no cup, no sandbox, no unburned paper.  It carried a miniature compass of fear, still warm, pointing toward what remained.  Behind it, the farm survived within its black ring of saved soil.  Ahead, the dunes held no compass, no child, no command.  Aster felt the bead tick once, aligning memory with weather instead of loss.  It walked on, a whole caretaker made from fragments, carrying fear small enough to guide.
