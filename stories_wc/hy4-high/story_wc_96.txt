They begin as the moon clears the skyline, when the watch changes and the rusted library under stars briefly belongs to no one.  Its roof has eaten itself into ribs, and every rib throws a lunar shard across the floor, a luminous fracture of glass, rain stain, and open sky.  Nella stands in the aisle with her coat full of pennies, because a vagrant penny poet learns to carry both payment and proof.  She has come to rebuild, not to avenge the prison burning or bless the riots that followed it.  The city beyond the broken doors is all sirens, fires, and unmoored voices, an anarchy that keeps offering her a torch.  She refuses it with the flat urgency of someone who wants to forge beauty from anarchy instead of more ash.  In her hands is the coffee tin with secret false bottom, dented blue, still smelling of chicory from the prison kitchen.  The pennies in its upper chamber clink like small obedient bells, but the hidden chamber holds seeds, bookplates, and names.  Ivo waits by a toppled shelf, thinner than the last time she saw him, studying a note written to himself.  Ivo molts personalities monthly, a survival trick learned in prison and sharpened by years of avoiding informants.  This month he is a mason, patient with angles and distrustful of slogans, so Nella trusts his hands more than his theories.  Next month he may wake a singer, a judge, or a frightened boy, and leave these shelves to another man's conscience.  Because of that, they work now, sorting splintered boards into stacks for beams, shelves, seed boxes, and benches.  Their method is a friendship sparked sorting books at a prison library, and it still has the shape of those afternoons.  Nella had been sentenced for a sonnet that rhymed governor with gravel, and Ivo had been assigned to inventory donated books.  Officially they sorted by number, subject, and condition, but secretly they sorted by what a captive could use to remain human.  Botany went to the woman who dreamed of beans, carpentry to the man who built crutches, law to anyone hunting a loophole.  Nella slipped forbidden lines into the gaps between pages, and Ivo shelved them where hungry readers would find them.  That was the first library either of them had wanted to enter, and the first friendship the wardens failed to catalog.  Tonight, the same sorting brings thirty people through the doors, each carrying a useful fragment of the broken city.  A baker brings ash for mortar, a nurse brings clean cloth, and three former guards bring hinges they no longer wish to defend.  No one asks who commanded the riots, because command is the old grammar and the room is learning a new one.  Nella twists the coffee tin until its secret false bottom yields, and the names spill across the laid boards like a deck of cards.  Each bookplate bears a prisoner number, a trade, and one line of verse, the indexes of a city that authority tried to erase.  Ivo lays the cards in alphabetical order, then curses softly and rearranges them by need rather than by letter.  Menders go to the cracked wall, readers to the wet ledgers, diggers to the planters, and children to the lamp wicks.  It is not efficient in the way a prison was efficient, but it is exact in the way a promise is exact.  They call the work a blooming resistance, not because it is gentle, but because no patrol can arrest every root.  Into every seed box goes a folded poem, and into every poem goes the address of another ruined room.  By morning, if the couriers live, the city will find beans, marigolds, and instructions sprouting in a hundred windows.  Nella pays a scrapman for nails with pennies from the tin, then hammers each nail through a strip of copper roof shaped like a leaf.  At a small anvil dragged from the burned workshop, Ivo forges brackets from prison bars until the shelves stand crookedly upright.  The crookedness matters, because a perfect hall would suggest the old ministers had merely returned with cleaner gloves.  Under the torn roof, moonlight catches every repaired seam, splitting brightness into the luminous fracture Nella has chosen as her only flag.  Boots sound on the avenue; the readers do not scatter.  A sergeant demands the leader; Nella gives him a penny for a marigold poem.  While he reads, the moon clears fully, and the rebuilt library opens without permission under stars.
