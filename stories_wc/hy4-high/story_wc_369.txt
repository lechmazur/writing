At the moment a pin is heard in a silent corridor, Mara Lint stops pretending the tower is only a building.  The pin is one of thirty kept in her cuff for marking hairline cracks in the labyrinth plaster.  As the labyrinth maintenance worker, she carries chalk, lamp oil, and a broom whose straws have been replaced twice.  In the stormlit tower by moonlight, such tools are not less useful than astrolabes, and usually less trouble.  She filed reports on drains that sang and doors that forgave themselves by dawn, the mundane miracles of a repaired place.  Tonight the storm has hushed every hinge, rat, and visitor, so the dropped pin makes a small, respectable thunder.  That sound is the clock she has waited for, because the corridors will stay honest for exactly one breath.  The city pays the tower to convert grief into quantified sorrow, a single number that can be stamped, taxed, and forgotten.  Mourners sleep in the lower passages, leave their dreams in the mortar, and depart lighter by so many measures.  Only a sweeper knows how much dust a measure leaves, or how often a number sweeps badly.  This monthly total is absurdly high, enough sorrow for a famine, though the town has suffered only rain and a failed bakery.  Mara cares because, nine years ago, her brother Ivo was entered as eleven measures and then as absent.  The clerk said the tower had relieved him, but his grafting knife remained leaning against the central yew.  Since then she has kept the night shift to discover what trees witness, not because trees speak, but because they keep appointments.  They were present when Ivo entered, when the old astronomer argued with heaven, and when every official hand let go.  The astronomer's broken sextant lies in her toolbox among washers, string, and labels for doors that no longer exist.  Its cracked brass heals in moonlight, closing thin as a scab, though dawn always reopens the old mistake.  The astronomer had tried to measure what the yews saw, believing each ring held the angle of a human farewell.  He broke the instrument when its sighting arm refused to rise above the roots, and the town turned his failure into an office.  Her copy of the maintenance manual gives a plainer verb for tonight: deconstruct.  The approved method is by mapping scattered dreams, which sounds mystical until one has to kneel with a chalk line and cold knees.  Each dream has a household address, a usual weight of dust, and a direction it drifts when thunder presses the stones.  She learned them by sweeping, not studying glass charts upstairs.  Now, with the pin still ringing on the flagstone, she deconstructs the swollen total into crumbs of sleep.  She sets the fallen pin at the first junction, lays the sextant beside it, and waits for lightning to uncover the moon.  When the cloud opens, moonlight pours through the storm windows, and the crack in the brass draws itself shut.  For one breath the sextant is whole enough to aim downward, where its mirrors catch the bright tracks of dreams in mortar dust.  Mara pins each track with a scrap label: wet flour from the failed baker, two cups from a widow, a blue marble from a child.  These are mundane miracles, ordinary objects made visible only because nobody has yet decided what they mean.  By mapping scattered dreams across the corridor, she finds the shape of a queue, not a famine, gathered around one unfinished goodbye.  At its center lies Ivo's pear bark dream, still smelling of sap, folded around the same green grafting knife.  He had followed a grieving crowd into the roots, trying to save their particular stories from becoming one obedient sum.  The tower, built to ease burdens, had mistaken his rescue for clutter and filed him with the round number.  Mara understands then that a quantified sorrow can be accurate and still be a lie told for convenience.  She turns the mended arc toward the central yew, and the dream map opens like a work order written in rings.  This is what trees witness: hands oiling hinges, people returning for lost cups, and the stubborn order made after sorrow is counted.  Mara releases the total, labels every dream with its owner, and lets Ivo's name stand in the margin where a measure had been.  By dawn the sextant will crack again, the moonlight gone, but the chalked map will be there for the clerks to trip over.
