The floating observatory hangs above the cloud deck, eight mirrors aimed at a sky no nation admits owning. 
Below, the city that authorized the drone strike at Mara's wedding sleeps under black rain. 
Mara is listed as a Drone strike wedding survivor in the crew manifest, a clerk's phrase stamped beside her burns. 
She keeps the geode crystal from the altar in a pocket sewn over her ribs. 
Ilyan chose it as a joke and vow, rough outside, star bright inside, like their expected marriage. 
The strike cracked it, fused her veil to shrapnel, and left no body to bury. 
Since then, she has studied instruments that measure absence without calling it grief. 
The observatory sells residual probability forecasts to generals who want cleaner wars. 
To find where unused potential pools, Mara has come here before those men convert the waste into targets. 
She believes the futures burned out of Ilyan, the guests, and herself have weight, location, and season. 
Layered filters trim any signal too human to monetize, except during a power shutdown. 
Engineers call that forbidden interval the Unfiltered awakening, because raw possibility enters the sensors unshielded. 
It is classed as a failure state, yet Mara has waited three years to cause one. 
Tonight, her sabotage trips the main array by reflecting a pulse through the geode crystal. 
Alarms die, mirrors freeze, and the breaker vault ticks as copper busbars shed heat. 
While circuit breakers cool, emergency coils run the station and the safety filters cannot reboot. 
She has nine minutes, perhaps less if the night wind tilts the helium envelopes. 
"Seal the chapel bay," the watch officer says, unaware that she is already inside it. 
Her hunt begins with a breath held between grief and calculation. 
She does not hunt a culprit or ghost; she hunts the reservoir made by choices that violence cancelled. 
To locate a thing intangibly tangible, she has only mathematics and the wounded crystal. 
Through perturbation theory, she turns tiny distortions into questions and reads the remainders the telescopes cannot hide. 
A mirror nudged one millimeter should shift a quasar by a predictable fraction. 
If that fraction grows or shrinks, something unseen has leaned back. 
Clamped in a brass armature, the geode crystal supplies each nudge with a clean chime. 
Mara changes ballast pressure, sends the chime through the array, and watches residuals shiver. 
She follows them down the observatory spine while the hatch rings behind her. 
The navigation remainder points under the chapel bay, but the spectral result curves inward, as if distance has folded. 
A third appears in maintenance glass, where her reflection wears a veil of smoke. 
Bleak exhilaration rises in her, the guilty thrill of being alive inside a catastrophe she chose. 
She laughs because the equations answer faster than fear can object. 
The officer shouts that the breakers have six minutes left; she increases each perturbation by a hair's width. 
The array shows no point, only a convergence of missing outcomes. 
Unused proposals, abandoned children, unbaked bread, and Ilyan's unheard joke gather around one cold focus. 
Intangibly tangible, the focus presses on the crystal yet leaves no mark, heavy as weather and intimate as a hand. 
Mara understands why the filters exist, because the Unfiltered awakening does not resurrect the dead. 
It lets the living feel the shape of what they were spared and permitted to steal. 
The focus lies in the observatory's shadow, where ballast holds the station above the city. 
That is where unused potential pools, under instruments built to turn loss into targeting data. 
She crawls into the ballast bowl; the air tastes of hot metal, rain, and old flowers, crowded with the unarrived. 
Her solution fixes the reservoir inside a handwidth of cracked stone. 
Discharge could wake the grieving city with stolen futures; sealing would preserve clean forecasts. 
The choice should crush her, but bleak exhilaration steadies her hands. 
She survived a wedding by accident; now she can choose the blast radius. 
Mara opens the crystal's crack and lets the stored chime answer the pooled field. 
As the breakers finish cooling, the Unfiltered awakening passes through the observatory like dawn through a wound. 
Mirrors turn, lightning crawls along dead rails, and the city wakes to unbearable doors. 
Mara feels Ilyan beside her, not returned, not consoled, no longer an absence someone else can sell. 
The hunt ends with sirens, cold metal, and her heart outrunning a drone. 
She sits in ballast dark, holding the empty geode crystal, smiling with bleak exhilaration.
