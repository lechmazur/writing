71 years after the last human is born, the recycled spaceship Mercy of Scraps drifted past a star with no name.  Mira had been that child, and now her hair was the color of unlit corridors.  No nursery remained aboard; each cradle had been unwoven into filters, scriptorium racks, and quiet drawers of photographs.  Every bulkhead came from something that had already failed elsewhere, yet the ship held together like a patient argument.  Its vents repeated old voices at night, not because of ghosts, the engineers said, but because reused metals remembered pressure.  In the 72nd year, every remaining duty had narrowed to one desire: to push open a locked dimension.  The founders had sealed the Quiet after the wars, fearing that any new world would inherit the old one's appetite.  They left a lock that answered neither rank nor weapons, only a full accounting of what frightened people called virtue.  Mira had grown into a steadfast pacifist because she distrusted the cleanliness of heroic answers.  When Captain Ivo proposed a mass driver against the seam, she disabled the firing circuit with a sleeping child's gentleness.  He called her mercy cowardice; she answered by offering him the wrench and standing between him and the panel.  Yet behind her calm she carried a private heat, a rage at being born as a census line and a tombstone.  The ship's oldest protocol named the necessary labor shadow work, and Mira had always skipped her own chapter.  To pass the lock, each operator had to speak the harm concealed beneath every cherished refusal.  On the recycled spaceship, that was practical engineering, since hidden force bent the patched gravity plates first.  Mira had inherited a colony adaptation and was gravitationally lighter when happy, a joke of her vanished generation.  At funerals her boots held fast; during rare music she rose toward the ceiling until attendants tugged her down.  The gene had been meant for low gravity labor, but it made joy a weather inside her bones.  The only trustworthy map to the Quiet came via the faint shimmer of long-dried tears on faded photographs.  Generations had wept over pictures of children, Earth, and doors no camera could quite explain, leaving salt in tiny constellations.  The object that read those constellations was catalogued as kwan yin's tear, a clear bead from a broken shrine.  Under its lens, tear tracks became coordinates; held wrongly, they became merely dust and all the dead turned silent.  Mira had learned to stitch the coordinates across the photographs with copper thread drawn from a nursery mobile.  Each stitch joined grief to grief without cutting the faces, because the lock rejected any map that wasted a person.  On the final evening, Ivo brought the mass driver's key again, but the panel was a garden of cool wires.  Mira did not argue with him; she asked him to sit among the photographs and witness what she had refused to know.  Then she began the shadow work aloud, naming the wars that financed the hull and the births forbidden to spare resources.  She named her relief that no daughter would inherit her famous date, and her jealousy of the unborn dead.  She named the pleasure she had taken in saying no to Ivo, though she had dressed it as principle.  Ivo looked away first, and the old voices in the vents stopped pretending to be pipes.  The photographs warmed under kwan yin's tear, and every salt line rose like a small patient moon.  Mira set the first stitch with hands that shook, then found that honesty had made them steady.  One by one, she drew the copper thread through pinholes, across old cheeks, over oceans the color of ash.  The hull answered with a low sigh, and a line of darkness opened above the central rail.  Only the last stitch remained, above her reach.  Mira almost laughed at the absurd mercy of it, and the small happiness loosened her from the deck.  Being gravitationally lighter when happy, she floated up with the terrible gentleness of a leaf in church light.  Ivo caught her boot, not to stop her, but to lend his weight, and the dead voices steadied the thread.  With ghostly resolve, Mira passed kwan yin's tear through the final pinhole and pushed the seam open.  No explosion followed; the Quiet widened like a held breath released by someone who had waited centuries to forgive.  No armies waited inside, only weaponless truth.
