During the lull on a store's very first opening day, Mara, the clean slate vendor, listened to the glass orchard library floating upon a swirling sea of clouds creak around her little shop. She had opened the shop to find the place where stories begin, not merely to sell untouched tablets to strangers. The orchard had been built according to integrated fragmentation: each glass branch held a broken clause, each translucent fruit a paused chapter, and the whole could be read only from a careful distance. Glass, cloud, ledger, breath. Mara kept her attention among all four, mindfully scattered so that no shard could pretend to be alone. Under glimmering parole, figures released from finished books could walk the library while their borrowed light lasted. A parolee who found a true beginning kept the light; one who failed returned to the final page that owned them. Mara understood the contract because she had been erased from her own first page. Since then, blankness felt less like emptiness than a door with its hinges removed. On her counter lay one object she did not advertise: bottled moonlight in a square vial stopped with gray wax. Pour a drop across a slate, and every missing thing casts a measurable shadow. That was her method, by quantifying absence, and it had paid for the awning and three stools. The lull widened, quiet as a held breath. Cloud shadows turned beneath the floor; glass leaves chimed without wind; the bottled moonlight shone only inward. A boy stepped between the stools, holding a paper crown folded from an ending. His light was thin, the color of skimmed milk, and his shadow had already started quoting the last page. Mara recognized his name from a jar of unasked questions, where it had been represented by a space. He set both palms on the counter. He did not ask for a beginning; he asked whether the store had been open long enough to be trusted. Mara almost laughed, because the ink on her ledger was still wet. Instead, she heard the hinge in his question and understood that trust was the first absence. She uncorked the vial. Moonlight pooled across a new slate, refusing to brighten what was there and silvering precisely what was not. Mara became still by becoming multiple: one ear to the boy's breathing, one to the chimes, one to the cloud sea, one to her own old gap. She counted by quantifying absence: no lullaby, no doorstep, no mother's hand, no named fear, no first lie, no witness who had stayed. Each omission made a clean edge; together they formed the shape of a question. Not where do I come from, but who will answer if I speak. The number was not large, which frightened her more than any enormous sum. One. One shared silence stood between an unfinished boy and a story. The Court's inspectors would arrive with the closing bell, and a false answer would cancel every glimmering parole on the branch. Mara took her steel stylus, meant for prices, and prepared to incise the count. Her hand stopped. If she wrote the discovery as a location, she could sell coordinates, buy back her own first page, and end her parole in privacy. If she wrote it as a debt, every slate in the shop would have to answer the boy. Glass, cloud, ledger, breath. The fragments did not cancel her longing; they composed it. She looked at the boy, whose paper crown had begun to sweat light. She chose the debt. With one stroke she used the stylus to incise a small open circle, not a zero, beside the measured absence. The bottled moonlight cracked into every blank slate on the shelves. Chimes answered chimes; leaves leaned toward gaps; the cloud sea made a road beneath the floor. Mara saw the place where stories begin, and it was not a room, a root, a star, or a fountain. It was the interval where one missing thing trusted another missing thing to make an edge. The inspectors arrived as bells of glass. They found no coordinates, no marketable Origin, no private rescue, only circles and witnesses. Because a witness is what parole requires, the Court had no sentence left to pronounce. The boy spoke first, a question with room for Mara inside it. By closing, the shop's first day had become a common beginning.
