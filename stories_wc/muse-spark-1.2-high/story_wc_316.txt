<story>Kael had survived ego death three years earlier on a salt flat, and nothing since had felt entirely real. [19] He came to the floating rock dojo because the master there promised not healing but honest collision. [36] The dojo hung above the Pacific, a slab of basalt tethered by old prayer cables, slowly rotating against the wind. [56] Inside, Kael trained beside his own clone, a younger copy named Joren who had been grown to replace him after the ego death was misdiagnosed as brain death. [84] The clone custody battle had been filed by the company that owned the growth vat, claiming Joren was property, while Kael's sister Mara claimed kinship and filed for guardianship. [113] Kael wanted neither to own nor to abandon Joren, only to understand why he could not feel grief or love for either outcome. [136] His motivation was simple and desperate, to defeat emotional numbness before it made him permanently hollow. [152] The master, an old woman called Sensei Ayu, spoke in a tone none could easily place, neither kind nor cruel, and that flatness slices through pretense faster than any lecture. [182] She taught through complementary opposites, pairing stillness with sudden strikes, silence with shouting, softness with hardness until the student stopped choosing one over the other. [207] Kael and Joren were paired this way, instructed to mirror each other for hours until their separate histories seemed absurd. [227] While history rewrites itself outside, the news feeds stuttered as the maritime border accords were edited retroactively, and people remembered different wars. [249] The dojo's radio crackled with revised anthems, and students paused to check whether their own memories still matched the official record. [270] Ayu ignored it all and pointed to a shelf where a brain in a jar labeled duplicate floated in clear fluid, its stem trailing wires. [295] She said it was Kael's original scan template, grown as a backup and never implanted, kept as evidence in the custody case. [317] Joren stared at the jar and asked if that made him the duplicate or Kael, and Kael had no answer that felt true. [340] Ayu told them the question was a trap built from language, and only the body could answer it without lying. [360] That evening she set them a final exercise, to spar not to win but to vivify each other, to bring the other's dormant nerves back into presence. [387] They bowed, and the rock shifted under their feet as a high jet stream pushed the dojo into a slow spin. [408] Joren attacked first, fast and linear, and Kael answered with circular deflection, each style revealing what the other lacked. [427] Ayu called out to trade, so Kael was forced into aggression and Joren into yielding, both clumsy with the unfamiliar shape. [448] Kael felt irritation rise, then shame, then a strange bright anger that his numbness had hidden for years. [466] Joren took a hard palm to the chest and fell, laughing and coughing, his eyes suddenly wet. [483] Kael knelt beside him, frightened, and felt his own face break without permission into tears he had not produced since before the ego death. [507] The feeling sliced sideways through his careful detachment, not healing but exposing the raw tissue underneath. [523] Joren gripped his forearm and whispered that he did not want to be owned, only witnessed, and Kael whispered back that he understood. [546] Above them the jar glowed faintly as history settled again outside, the broadcast announcing that the custody law had always protected clones as persons. [570] Mara's voice came through the dojo radio, saying the court had ruled Joren could choose, and Joren chose to stay the winter and learn. [594] Kael lifted the brain in a jar labeled duplicate from the shelf and carried it to the edge of the floating rock, where the wind was strongest. [621] He did not smash it or worship it, he simply opened the seal and poured the fluid into the sky, letting the tissue dissolve into mist and light. [649] Joren watched and did not flinch, and for the first time Kael saw his clone not as a reflection but as a brother, opposite and necessary.
