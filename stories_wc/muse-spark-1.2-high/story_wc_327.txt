<story>The copper-veined storyteller was called Jesh because the metal that laced his forearms caught the sun when he gestured. [19] He lived among the towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes where wind never settled and every word returned altered. [41] The towers were not built but grown from the skeletons of leviathans that had beached there centuries before the city died. [62] Jesh was quietly articulate, choosing each word as if laying a thin stone across a river. [78] His people spoke Kethra, a language that described texture before meaning, and it was dying with his generation. [96] Across the death of a language, Jesh had watched one speaker after another fall silent until only his niece Lina remained half-fluent. [118] He wanted to translate texture into sound so that Kethra might be heard even after its last throat forgot how to shape it. [141] For years he had carved bone flutes and stretched skins, but no instrument could hold the grain of stone or the nap of moss. [165] In the deepest cut of the canyon lay a tunnel of echoes, a smooth bore where the leviathan's spine had once rested. [187] Any sound made inside it returned not once but dozens of times, each echo thinner and older, as if the tunnel remembered every voice since its forming. [214] The elders said the flawed diamond rumored to be cursed could anchor those echoes if placed at the tunnel's center. [234] It had been pulled from the canyon wall, clouded by a black feather at its heart, and every keeper before Jesh had lost their voice or their sleep. [262] Jesh did not fear the curse because his language was already a curse of disappearing. [277] He carried the diamond to the tunnel and set it on a pedestal of polished bone, where it caught the thin light and threw it in fractured spokes. [305] Yet the diamond alone only screamed, amplifying his words into a harsh chorus that hurt the ears and lost all texture. [326] He needed to harness the canyon's endless reverberation and give it form, not volume. [340] For three seasons he watched the sky, learning to read meaning through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight. [361] The starlings above the canyon moved as one body, tightening and loosening, rippling over bone towers like ink in water. [381] Jesh saw how each turn held information about wind shear and distance, and how the whole flock translated air texture into motion. [403] He realized his task was not to invent a new instrument but to copy that translation, to let pattern become grammar. [424] He restrung the bone flutes with copper wire drawn from his own veins, the metal humming faintly when wind passed through it. [446] He placed the flutes along the tunnel of echoes, each tuned to a different roughness, from river-smooth to canyon-rough. [465] When the flock passed overhead, their shadow fell through the tunnel mouth, and their motion triggered the wires to vibrate. [485] The flawed diamond at the center caught those vibrations and, instead of screaming, began to sort them into layered echoes. [505] Each echo now carried a different texture, and Jesh could finally hear Kethra spoken not by a mouth but by the canyon itself. [528] His tone as he listened was none, neither joyful nor mournful, but flat with attention, as if he had become an empty bowl for sound. [553] Lina came that winter, coughing from dust, and Jesh led her into the tunnel to hear what he had harnessed. [573] She closed her eyes as the echoes layered, and she felt the word for smooth-wet-stone become sound without needing to speak it. [595] Tears cut lines through dust on her face, and for the first time in years she answered in Kethra without hesitation. [616] The diamond at the center pulsed once and then cleared, the black feather within it dissolving as if its curse had been a weight of unused voices. [643] Outside the tunnel the flock turned again, their pattern endless, and the canyon of endless echoes repeated the new language back to the sky. [667] Jesh knew he would not live to see Kethra fully return, but he had made a place where texture could live as sound, waiting for throats to learn it again. [697]</story>
