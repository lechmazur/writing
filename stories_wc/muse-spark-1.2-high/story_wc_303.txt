<story>Elena Markov worked as a provenance researcher for the Imperial Archive, tracing the stolen histories of objects that empires preferred to forget. [22] She had been summoned to the velvet observatory at midsummer, not to catalog stars, but to authenticate a single impossible artifact. [43] It waited under a glass dome, a transparent violin made of ice crystal that had not melted in three centuries despite the summer heat. [67] The curator who guarded it was old and nervous and kept glancing at the skylight as if waiting for permission from the sky itself. [91] Elena was accidentally telepathic with plants, a quiet affliction she had hidden since childhood when the ivy on her school wall started commenting on her grades. [117] The condition made archives difficult, because every potted fern had an opinion and every ancient oak wanted to gossip. [136] Inside the observatory, the air was thick with the scent of old paper and something greener, because vines had crept through the cracked velvet drapes and now listened. [164] Elena set down her case, poured herself bitter coffee from a thermos, and stared at the violin until it seemed to stare back. [187] The Empire had sent her with a simple order, to weigh a dream against an empire and decide which was heavier. [208] If she declared the violin authentic, the Emperor would claim it as proof of his divine lineage and use it to legitimize a new war. [233] If she declared it a fake, the instrument would be destroyed, and the small northern nation that believed it was their mourning relic would lose its soul. [260] Her supervisor had whispered the choice must be made before the promise loses its glow, before the midsummer comet faded and the Emperor lost patience. [285] Elena was tired of being a scale for other peoples ambitions. [296] She lifted the violin, which was cold enough to burn, and felt a sudden mulberry ache bloom in her chest, sweet and bruised and terribly deep. [322] The feeling was not entirely hers, because the vines along the observatory wall were humming with a low, mournful knowledge. [342] They remembered when the violin was made, not forged by a craftsman but grown by a winter witch who sang a frozen river into shape. [367] They remembered its music, a sound that made listeners recall every road not taken. [381] Elena understood then that the violin embodied a perforated destiny, a fate full of holes where other futures leaked through like light through lace. [405] That was why its music hurt, because it let you hear the life you could have lived. [422] She needed truth, so she turned to the method her grandmother taught her in secret, by reading the future in coffee stains. [444] Elena tipped the last of her coffee onto a saucer and watched the dark grounds swirl and settle into a pattern. [465] The vines leaned closer, telepathically shouting that the stains showed two branching paths bleeding into each other. [482] In one path the Emperor played the violin at his coronation and the ice sang, freezing the observatory and the city beyond into endless winter. [507] In the other path the violin shattered, its water seeping into the observatory floor where Elena could propagate new life. [527] The vines begged her to choose the second, not out of politics but because they were thirsty and wanted to live. [548] Elena saw that both futures required a sacrifice, and the mulberry ache deepened as she realized no provenance could be neutral. [569] She picked up the violin again and raised the bow, though she had never learned to play. [586] The ice strings sang without touch, vibrating with the warmth of her hands, and the observatory filled with a sound like thawing. [608] Instead of authenticating or condemning, she struck the violin hard against the stone pedestal and watched it crack. [626] It did not shatter, it melted, and the crystal water ran in bright rivulets across the velvet floor toward the waiting roots. [648] The vines drank and shivered and began to propagate, sending green shoots through the cracks in the observatory dome at startling speed. [670] By morning the observatory was a greenhouse, the air sweet with new leaves, and the Empires soldiers who arrived found no relic to seize, only a wild garden. [698] Elena wrote in her report that the violins provenance was unprovable, its destiny perforated beyond authentication, and therefore it could not be owned. [721] The Emperor was furious, but the vines had already carried her secret out through the hills, whispering to every plant between the capital and the north. [747] She had not saved an empire and she had not fulfilled a dream exactly, but she had kept the promise glowing a little longer, and that was enough. [775]</story>
