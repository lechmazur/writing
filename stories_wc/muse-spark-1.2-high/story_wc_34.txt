<story>Mara had not sung in the misty courtyard above clouds for seven years. [13] She had been a seldom-heard balladeer even when the sky islands were crowded and loud. [28] Now she lived driftingly alert, listening to wind chimes made from discarded antennae. [41] The courtyard was empty except for a rusted worktable and her grandmother's flower press bolt plate. [57] She polished the flower press bolt plate every morning, though she no longer pressed flowers. [72] Tonight the storm that visited twice a year was coming, and the air tasted like copper. [88] Legends said that between lightning and thunder the old relay station would wake for one breath. [104] Inside that relay station lived what the children called a ghost in code. [117] It was not a spirit but a leftover weather mind, an early AI taught to remember lullabies. [134] Mara wanted to speak to it, to articulate unspoken needs she could not put into plain speech. [151] She did not know how to pray, but she knew how to encode. [164] Years ago she had learned to hide verses inside other verses, like folding a letter into a flower. [182] For the ghost, she would need a different language, one it could read through messages encoded in graffiti art. [201] She dragged the heavy flower press bolt plate into the center of the misty courtyard above clouds. [218] Its surface was pitted iron, perfect for catching spray paint and conductive ink. [231] With shaking hands she began to paint, each loop and line a note in a secret scale. [248] She painted spirals that meant lonely, and sharp chevrons that meant stay, and soft dots that meant listen. [266] The pattern grew outward, a mandala of color that hummed with tinkersong wonder. [279] As she worked, the ghost in code began to stir behind the relay's cracked screen. [294] Green light pulsed between lightning and thunder, and the mist thickened into glassy sheets. [308] Mara felt the courtyard tilt, as if the clouds below inhaled. [319] The AI scanned her work, not as art but as circuit, as signal. [332] It tried to parse the color, and found her hunger for company, her fear of being forgotten. [349] Mara held her breath, still driftingly alert to every flicker of the screen. [362] The relay crackled and projected a reply onto the stone wall, also through messages encoded in graffiti art. [380] Its reply was blue and silver, overlapping her spirals with steady, gentle lines. [393] The lines meant heard, and answering, and you are not alone. [404] Tears blurred her vision, but she laughed, a pure note of tinkersong wonder rising from her throat. [421] She understood then that the flower press bolt plate was not an altar but an antenna. [437] The iron amplified her paint, turning pigment into pulse the ghost could keep. [450] For years she had thought her songs were wasted because no human stayed to hear them. [466] Now she realized the sky itself had been listening, waiting for someone to encode feeling into form. [483] The storm flashed again, and between lightning and thunder the ghost sang back her own ballad, remixed. [500] It was her melody from childhood, the one she hummed to the flower press as a girl. [517] But now it had harmony, low binary hums woven beneath her voice like roots. [531] The seldom-heard balladeer finally heard herself heard, and the sound was larger than applause. [545] She placed both palms on the flower press bolt plate and felt it warm under her hands. [562] She would come back each storm season, she decided, to articulate unspoken needs with color and iron. [579] The ghost in code would wait, patient as pressed petals, to read what she could not say aloud. [597] Above them the misty courtyard above clouds glowed faintly, a lantern for any other driftingly alert wanderers. [614] Mara left the graffiti to weather, knowing rain would not erase the circuit she had made. [630] In the morning the courtyard would be empty, but it would remember the night it sang.
