<story>When Lira arrived, the crystalline orchard perched at the edge of a shimmering crater caught the late light and threw it back in splinters across the sky. [27] She had come as a marsh-reed sculptor, her hands still stained with the dark sap of lowland waters, and she carried nothing but tools and a single woven figure of her mother. [59] It was beyond the murmur of lost languages, a time when the names of nations had dissolved and only places and practices remained. [82] The orchard folk had lived here for generations, tending trees that grew not fruit but clear, resonant crystals that chimed when wind passed through. [106] They did not distrust Lira, but they did not know where to place her, and she felt the distance like a luminescent ache that glowed whenever they gathered without her. [136] Lira was boldly sympathetic, a quality that made her listen longer than was comfortable and ask the second question when others stopped at the first. [161] She learned that the way to graft oneself onto community was through a contribution that could not be bought or commanded. [182] In the center of the orchard stood the glimmering obsidian arbor, a lattice of black glass that held up the oldest crystal trees and caught their ringing light. [210] For decades a child or an elder had painted a small white tag on its darkest pillar and left it for the people to find, a simple circle with a feathered line through it. [244] No one knew who made the mark, only that finding it meant the season would turn kindly, and the search itself had become ritual. [268] This year the tag had not appeared, and the arbor's shadow seemed longer, as if the crystals above were dimming with worry. [290] Lira saw her chance not in boasting but in joining, and so she proposed a shared search for an elusive street artist's tag, inviting the children first and the elders second. [321] They laughed, then nodded, because no single person was meant to look alone under the arbor's weight. [338] At dawn they spread out, Lira moving with a boy named Joren who had never spoken to her before and an old woman named Mara who remembered the marshes. [367] Joren asked why marsh reeds held shape even when wet, and Lira showed him how to twist fibers so they locked against themselves. [390] Mara in turn told how the orchard was founded by refugees who chose kinship over blood, weaving webs of kinship from shared work rather than shared ancestry. [417] Lira listened and understood that her sculpture could be a bridge, not a monument, and that belonging required listening first. [437] By noon they had found nothing on the arbor except lichen and old scratches, but Joren discovered a pale feather tucked between two black glass beams. [463] Lira lifted it and saw underneath a fresh white circle with a feathered line, so faint it could be mistaken for light. [485] She did not shout, though her heart pounded, but called Mara and Joren to look together, letting their eyes confirm what hers had found. [509] The three stood under the glimmering obsidian arbor while the crystal leaves above chimed louder, as if wind had finally returned. [530] Mara wept, Joren laughed, and Lira felt that luminescent ache soften into something warmer, a quiet light that no longer hurt. [551] That evening the orchard gathered for a meal beneath the arbor, and Lira was asked to dedicate her first sculpture to the place that had fed her search. [579] She had woven a wide, low table from marsh reed, its surface inlaid with fallen crystal shards, strong enough to hold the weight of many bowls. [605] When she placed it under the glimmering obsidian arbor, the children climbed onto it and the elders rested their hands upon it, and it did not creak. [632] Mara said the table would be where new webs of kinship were tied, where strangers learned each other's names over shared food. [654] Lira sat with Joren and his sister, and for the first time no one asked where she came from, only what she would make next. [679] Beyond the murmur of lost languages, she thought, people still needed the same things: to be heard, to be useful, to be held. [702] Later, when the crystals dimmed and the crater's shimmer cooled, she walked alone to the arbor and touched the small white tag one last time. [727] She understood that she had not found the mark so much as it had allowed her to be found. [746]</story>
