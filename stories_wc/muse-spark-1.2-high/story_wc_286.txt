<story>The potter came across the wasteland with empty hands and a mind like still water. [15] She had been a zen-minded potter for twenty years, shaping clay until the clay shaped her breathing. [32] Her village had vanished after the fracture, not destroyed but smeared across fractured timelines where yesterday and tomorrow bled together. [52] She did not travel to find what she lost, but to understand why some places resist change when all else unravels. [73] The maps called it an abandoned beekeeping oasis in a scorched wasteland, a green thumbprint that should not exist. [92] No one who left the trade routes returned with a clear story of it. [106] She navigated by following sun patterns, reading the arc of light across salt and the slow migration of shadows between dunes. [127] Where others used compasses that spun wildly near the fractures, she trusted the sun to tell true time. [145] After three days the air turned sweet, a lie in the wasteland. [157] She crested a ridge as chimney smoke flattens against a low sky, and saw the oasis below. [174] Even with no fire lit, a thin grey veil hung level over the hollow, as if the air itself remembered winter. [211] Palm trees and hardy lavender held a pocket of shade where the earth should have been cracked dust. [229] And in the center stood a clay-walled hut with its kiln cold and its door open. [279] Inside the hut, dust lay thick except for a single clear circle on the worktable. [294] There rested a piece of faded lantern glass, its edge sea smooth and its surface clouded by years of handling. [335] Yet the disc did not stay fixed to the sun patterns; it drifted toward her hand, toward the gentleness in her pulse. [457] The oasis had not resisted change through stubbornness, but through consistent kindness that taught time to stay coherent. [497] In one, the hives burned; in another, the well ran dry; in this one, the lavender still lived because someone had watered it yesterday and tomorrow. [523] She lifted the faded lantern glass again and set it in the window where the sun could pass through it. [587] With sharp gentleness she cleaned the kiln, wedged fresh clay she found sealed in oilcloth, and centered it on the wheel. [608] She did not throw a pot to keep, but a shallow bowl to offer, its curve wide enough to catch light and water. [631] As she worked, her breathing evened, and the thrum of the bees evened with it, a shared rhythm holding the fracture at bay. [654] When the bowl was finished she placed the faded lantern glass inside it, letting the sun pass through both. [673] The combined light spread across the oasis, and for an instant every timeline overlapped into a single steady present. [692] She understood at last why some places resist change: they are held by repeated small kindnesses that time learns to orbit. [713] She would not stay, for the oasis did not need a master, only a visitor who remembered how to tend.
