<story>The lore keeper had not been born when the city learned to remember for itself. [15] Now the towers hummed with archived summers and voices of people who never died, only uploaded. [31] He tended the Archive of Unfinished Things, cataloging holograms of childhood toys and half written songs with a reverence that felt like futuristic nostalgia. [55] He was wisely adventurous, old enough to know which locked doors should stay locked and young enough to try them anyway. [76] For twenty years the central dais in the Council Spire had stood empty, holding the unclaimed throne that no algorithm and no elder dared to occupy. [102] The lore keeper believed the throne was not a seat of power but a listening post left by the founders, waiting for someone who could hear the past without trying to edit it. [135] To prove it he needed to find the stairway of ascension, a mythic route the builders supposedly walked when they first seeded the sky. [159] The only clue was a sliver of crystallized code no larger than a thumbnail, a ruby flake that pulsed faintly when brought near forgotten places. [184] Its light grew stronger each time he passed the maintenance corridor on level nine, where the municipal painters had covered the walls for a century. [209] Tonight, with the Council in recess and the drones dimmed, he decided to sally into that forgotten spine of the city. [230] He had until sunrise before the watch systems rebooted and his absence would be logged as desertion. [247] He pried at the corner of the corridor where the paint had bubbled, and began working through the peel of paint layers revealing hidden murals that no living eye had seen. [278] Beneath cream and grey and industrial green lay a fresco of trees heavy with light, a river of glass, and children with unmasked faces laughing in an orchard. [306] Further along, the mural showed the waterfall that fed that orchard, its water refracting the sun into impossible colors. [325] The ruby flake in his palm burned suddenly cold, projecting a thin line of coordinates onto the concrete. [343] He followed them down a service ladder, beyond the foundation mesh, to a place where the city's hum faded and real water could be heard. [368] There he found it, the hidden orchard behind a kaleidoscopic waterfall that fell not from a river but from the city's own recycled atmosphere, catching starlight and throwing it back in ribbons. [400] The orchard was impossible, apple trees growing from hydroponic soil under artificial auroras, their leaves rustling with a sound no speaker could replicate. [423] He understood at once why the founders had painted over it, for a place so tender would have been optimized out of existence in the early efficiency drives. [451] In the center of the orchard stood a trunk wider than any tower column, its bark etched with names, and within its hollow a spiral of light rose upward. [480] It was not stairs at all but a slow ascent of light and root and memory, the stairway of ascension made for those who remembered how to climb patiently. [509] The lore keeper stepped onto the first riser and felt the city's archived summers flood his mind, not as data but as scent and weight and warmth. [536] Each step lifted him higher through the canopy, past the waterfall's veil, until the orchard and the tower were visible together, one reflecting the other. [561] He realized the unclaimed throne had never been unclaimed at all, it had been reserved for whoever could find this path and choose to return to carry others. [589] At the top, dawn was breaking, a thin true light that cut through the kaleidoscopic spray and laid gold across the real leaves. [612] He had no desire to sit on a throne, only to keep the way open, so he pressed the ruby flake into the bark where it bloomed into a seed of light for the next wisely adventurous soul.
