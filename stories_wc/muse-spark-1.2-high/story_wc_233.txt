<story>After the final warning, the kingdom did not burn but held its breath. [13] The disenchanted queen stood on her balcony and watched the smoke thin without relief. [27] For three months she had woken at the same hour from the same dream. [41] In it, a child version of herself stood before a cobalt grotto above clouds, holding a bowl of light that would not stay still. [65] The light was bleeding starlight, spilling over her small hands and staining the stone. [79] She wanted to unravel the mystery of recurring dreams before the dream began to unravel her. [95] Her advisors called it grief, her priests called it prophecy, and both bored her. [109] She was softly commanding, even when tired, and so no one argued when she ordered the maps brought out. [128] The oldest map marked an ember grotto on the ridge beyond the northern pass, a place the shepherds said held fire in winter without fuel. [153] The lines matched the stone arch from her dream. [162] She left alone, without crown or guard, wearing a plain cloak that smelled of cedar. [177] She had to climb through thinning air where the mountain forgot to be kind. [191] The path was narrow and stitched with frost that glittered like a warning written in lace. [207] There was a lacy menace to the beauty, as if the mountain offered its hand only to see if she would take it. [230] The priests had said after the final warning no one should ascend before spring. [244] She went anyway, because faith had become too heavy to carry downhill. [256] Halfway up, the wind found a hollow and made a sound like a shared exhale spotting the first fireflies of summer. [277] She remembered doing just that as a girl with her sister, before crowns and wars had chosen them. [295] The memory steadied her feet. [300] At the ridge, the air cleared and she saw it. [310] The ember grotto on the ridge was not myth but a low mouth of stone pulsing faint orange against blue dusk. [331] Inside, the walls were not stone at all but a cobalt grotto above clouds, lit from within by something restless. [351] The sky had dropped into the rock. [358] In its center, on a pedestal of unmelted ice, sat the same bowl from her dream, and it was bleeding starlight in slow, thick drips onto the floor. [386] Each drop hissed and became a small white moth that vanished into the cobalt. [400] She understood then that the dreams were not warnings but memories of a promise she had made and forgotten. [419] As a child, she had been brought here by the mountain priestess to learn how to hold the light without burning. [440] She had failed, and the priestess had sealed the grotto until she was ready to try again. [457] The disenchanted queen knelt, though her knees ached, and placed her hands around the bowl. [472] The bleeding starlight burned cold at first, then warm, as if recognizing her. [485] It did not spill now. [490] It rose, steadied, and settled into a calm, even glow that painted her face in silver. [506] For the first time in months, her mind was quiet without being empty. [519] She would not rebuild the kingdom with prophecy or with grief, but with this small, held light carried down the mountain. [540] She stood, cradling the bowl, and began the careful climb down where the air grew kind again. [557] Behind her, the ember grotto on the ridge dimmed but did not go dark, waiting as it always had. [576] Above, the cobalt grotto above clouds held its own sky, and below, the world waited for a queen who had finally remembered how to carry fire without being consumed. [605]</story>
