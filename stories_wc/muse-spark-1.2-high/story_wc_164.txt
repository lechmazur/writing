<story>Mara was a sea-foam recollectionist who collected other peoples yesterdays the way children collect shells. [15] She lived at the edge of the salt flats where the wind hummed a cheerful gloom over everything, bright enough to sing to and sad enough to be honest. [44] Her gift was strange: she transliterates emotions the way other people transliterate languages, turning a grief into a color or a joy into a temperature. [69] For years she had tried to discover why some paths disappear, why two brothers walking the same road remember two different roads entirely. [92] The elders said it was simply forgetting, but Mara knew it was something more precise, something like missing pieces from a map that had been folded wrong. [119] One dusk a courier brought her a shard of crater glass, still warm from the desert where a meteor had struck a hundred years before. [144] Hold this before memory fades, the courier whispered, because the man who owned it is losing his last road home. [164] Mara pressed the glass to her forehead and felt not a memory but an absence, a smooth place where a memory should have been. [188] She decided to conjecture the shape of what was gone, to build a scaffolding around the empty space until it revealed its outline. [211] To do that, she would need to travel to the archive, which was not a library but an iridescent cave inside a giant crystal geode buried beneath the salt. [240] No one went there cheerfully, but Mara did, humming all the way down the spiral stair, because cheerful gloom was her natural weather. [263] Inside, the walls caught her lantern and threw back a thousand amber and violet lights that moved without wind. [282] Each crystal held a memory left by someone who had walked a path and found it gone on the return. [302] Mara unwrapped the crater glass and set it on the central stone, where it began to vibrate with all the missing pieces clustered around it. [327] She closed her eyes and let her talent work, letting the glass transliterates emotions that had no words anymore, turning loss into the precise shade of blue found deep under ice. [358] The glass showed her a boy and his sister on a coastal trail, laughing, and then only the boy returning alone, certain his sister had never been there. [386] Another flash: a caravan that followed via the scattered seeds dropped by a migrating bird, green shoots marking a safe way across the white flats. [411] The seeds had sprouted one spring and not the next, and when the path vanished, the caravan swore the ground had lied to them. [435] Mara understood then that memories were not stored like books but like trails, and trails need tending or they overgrow. [455] People stopped walking certain stories, stopped telling them via the scattered seeds dropped by a migrating bird or by any other kindness, and the untold paths thinned into air. [484] The crater glass grew hotter, showing her the missing pieces were not accidents but choices, quiet neglects layered together. [503] A mother deciding not to speak of her hometown, a soldier deciding a shortcut was too dangerous to mark, a child deciding a game was foolish. [529] Each small absence took a stone from the road until finally the road could not hold weight. [546] Mara laughed, not because it was funny, but because the truth was so gently terrible that cheerful gloom was the only honest response. [569] She placed her hands on the geode wall and let it transliterates emotions back into memory, feeding warmth to each cold crystal. [591] Before memory fades entirely, she whispered to the crater glass, you must remember for us what we have stopped carrying. [611] The glass cooled and cleared, and in its reflection Mara saw not the cave but a long line of footprints leading outward, waiting to be walked again. [638] She left the iridescent cave inside a giant crystal geode carrying the glass carefully, knowing she could not rebuild every path, only invite people to replant them. [665] Outside, dawn was turning the flats pink, and the migrating birds were already high, a few seeds tumbling from their beaks onto the salt. [689] Mara watched one seed land, and where it touched, the white crust seemed less empty, as if a path had just remembered itself. [712]</story>
