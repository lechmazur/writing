<story>The feral library guardian had not spoken in nine years, not since the stacks swallowed the last librarian and left her to tend the margins alone. [26] She was dimensionally homesick, though she had never left the building, because the library she remembered had warm lamps and wooden ladders, not this endless vault where corridors folded into cold geometries. [58] To cope she practiced interiorizing exteriors, memorizing the cracked courtyard and the distant hills and then rebuilding them in her mind as shelves she could patrol. [84] It was through the veil of sleep that she first heard the garden calling, a faint chiming like pages turning under ice. [106] She followed the sound past the restricted archive, past the chained atlases, until a door she had never catalogued appeared between two pillars. [129] Beyond it lay a frozen garden in mist, its hedges white with frost and its statues half-buried in snow that never melted. [151] The air held a foggy lucidity, as if every snowflake were a thought made visible and held suspended for her inspection. [172] In the center of the garden stood a stone plinth, and upon it rested a cultist's pendant, a dull iron disc etched with a sun that had too many rays. [202] She recognized the symbol from a forbidden index: it marked those who studied the boundaries of light, not where light ended, but where it became something else entirely. [230] The pendant was cold enough to burn, and when she touched it she saw, for an instant, the library as it had been before the folding, and then as it might be after. [263] A voice behind her said, Do not take it lightly. [273] She turned to find a boy in a patched coat, his breath clouding, his feet leaving no prints in the snow. [294] He claimed he had been sent by the same cult, but had abandoned their rites when he realized they feared what they worshipped. [317] I came here to make friends with uncertainty, he said, because certainty had made my masters cruel. [334] The guardian, who had guarded books but never people, felt a sudden kinship and also a sharp suspicion. [352] She asked why he waited in a place where nothing grew. [363] He answered that he had learned to wait not as idleness but as attention, letting the frost write its slow language upon him. [386] If you stare too long at the boundaries of light, he warned, you will see only your own longing reflected, so you must look sideways, with kindness. [413] She understood then that the pendant was not a key but a lens, and that interiorizing exteriors had prepared her to use it without being consumed. [439] Together they lifted the cultist's pendant toward the low winter sun, which hung diffused and patient behind the mist. [458] Light passed through the etched rays and scattered across the frozen garden in mist, projecting not shadows but doorways faintly outlined in snow. [481] Each doorway showed a different library, one burning, one flooded, one filled with children reading aloud, one empty and silent like her own. [504] The boy asked which one she would choose, and she realized the choice was not about choosing a home but about choosing how to belong. [529] Being dimensionally homesick, she whispered, means every place is both strange and familiar, so I will carry the garden inside me instead of searching for the perfect door. [557] She pressed the pendant back onto the plinth, and the projected doorways folded back into ordinary light, leaving only the hush of snow. [580] The boy smiled, and for the first time since her watch began, the feral library guardian felt less like a guard dog and more like a host. [607] We can wait together, she offered, and tend this place until it thaws, or until someone else needs its foggy lucidity. [628] He nodded and sat on the plinth's edge, and she curled nearby, her old feral alertness softening into a companionable vigil. [649] Through the veil of sleep that night, travelers in distant stacks dreamed of a warm light in a frozen garden, and woke feeling less alone. [674]</story>
