<story>Mira had worked as a celestial signal interpreter for twelve years before the sky went quiet. [16] Her job was fantasy practicality, listening to static for messages from dead stars and logging them like weather reports. [35] When the signals stopped, the guild sent her to the abandoned radio tower city suspended over a gorge to investigate. [55] She arrived during lunar perigee, when the moon hung so close it made the cables hum. [71] The city was a lattice of rusted antennas and empty dishes, swaying gently above the black chasm, serenely electric with residual charge. [93] Mira carried only a field kit and a hollywood star fragment she had found in the archive, a dull brass piece etched with a name no one remembered. [121] Her mentor had always told her to find the beginning in every ending, and she believed an ending was exactly what the silence was. [145] She climbed the central tower, boots ringing on the grid, and saw that the great receiver dish was cracked down its center. [167] Inside the crack, wedged like a splinter, was a shard of hush, a physical silence that swallowed sound around it. [187] She had been taught that silence was an absence, but this was a presence, heavy and cold against her gloved fingers. [208] Every assumption she had about signals told her to amplify the void, to fill it with power. [225] She realized she would have to tune the city itself, not the receiver, by discarding assumptions about how listening worked. [245] For decades the towers had broadcast outward, but perhaps they were meant to resonate inward, to catch what fell between transmissions. [266] Mira set the hollywood star fragment into the crack beside a shard of hush, and the two fragments did not touch but sang. [289] The brass warmed, and the hush softened, creating a narrow band of audible air between them. [305] She connected her field kit to the tower cables and let the moon's gravity during lunar perigee pull tension through the lines. [327] The abandoned radio tower city suspended over a gorge began to vibrate, each tower a string on an impossible instrument. [347] Mira closed her eyes and listened practically, as any good celestial signal interpreter would, for pattern rather than meaning. [366] The hush was not empty after all, but folded, containing the first broadcast ever sent from the city fifty years earlier, trapped in its own echo. [392] She understood then that every ending preserves its beginning like a seed, and to find the beginning in every ending one must listen backward. [416] With careful hands she adjusted the tension, letting the serenely electric air do most of the work, and the folded signal unfolded. [438] A child's voice crackled through, reciting a station identification that had never been recorded, clear and small. [455] It was the city's first word, caught and held for decades inside a shard of hush until someone knew how to tune for it. [479] Tears came to her eyes, but she logged the event with fantasy practicality, noting frequency, duration, and the strange warmth of the brass. [502] She left the hollywood star fragment in place, a marker for future interpreters that would turn a dead monument into a listening instrument. [525] As she descended, the wind through the gorge carried the newly released signal outward, finally allowing the ending to complete its circle and become a beginning again. [552] She did not feel heroic, only useful, which was how she preferred her miracles. [566] Behind her the towers kept humming, no longer abandoned but patient, waiting for the next listener who knew that silence could be held. [589] Mira walked the swaying bridge back to the mainland, the moon huge above her, and felt the quiet in her kit settle into something like companionship.
