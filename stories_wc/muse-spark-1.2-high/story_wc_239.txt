<story>I am the end of history witness, and I have no name left to give. [15] When the last cities quieted and the sky folded shut, I remained because someone had to remember. [32] My task was simple and impossible: to unravel cosmic illusions that had convinced every age it was the final one. [52] For that purpose I sought the ancient temple library lost in time, which the old maps marked only as the place where dust learns to read. [78] Its location was not on any road, but was found by journeying inward while walking out, a method the monks called the leaving return. [102] I walked for what felt like across a universe's hiccup, a single skipped breath in which stars stuttered and centuries slipped past unnoticed. [125] When I arrived, the temple stood intact, its doors open to a wind that had not blown in ten thousand years. [146] Inside, the hall was held in a resounding hush, the kind of silence that seemed to echo louder than any bell. [167] Light fell through broken clerestories onto endless shelves, each book perfect, none decayed, as if time had forgotten to take them. [188] At the central table, beneath a disc of still air, lay a single softcover dictionary with its spine cracked and its margins crowded with handwriting. [213] It was not a book of definitions but of corrections, each entry attempting to fix the lies that language had told the cosmos. [236] The handwriting was mine, though I had never written it, and the ink was still wet. [252] I understood then that another witness had been here before me, or would be after, and that we were the same person looped. [275] On the first page a note read: To unify what was broken, you must learn spiral arbitration, the art of judging without choosing a side. [300] I had expected a weapon or a key, but spiral arbitration was a way of thinking, a circle that never closed. [321] It taught that truth and falsehood were not opposites but successive turns of the same stair. [337] I turned to a random entry, and the dictionary fell open to the word ETERNITY, defined as a misprint for entreaty. [358] The margin beside it said: We begged the universe to end, so it pretended to. [373] I laughed, and the sound was swallowed by the resounding hush until it returned as understanding. [389] My steps became wanderingly exact from then on, each footfall deliberate yet without destination, as if the floor itself were correcting my path. [412] I walked the aisles journeying inward while walking out, moving deeper into the library while my mind moved outward toward the void beyond history. [436] With every shelf I passed, another illusion peeled away: that time was linear, that endings were final, that witnessing was passive. [457] The dictionary grew heavier in my hands, not with weight but with accrual, as if each definition I read added a life I had lived. [482] Near the far wall I found a low dais where two chairs faced each other, both empty, both indented as if recently vacated. [505] I sat in one and waited for the other to be filled by myself. [519] The air between the chairs thickened, and I felt spiral arbitration begin within me, not as judgment but as balance. [539] I saw the universe not as a story with a last page, but as a sentence that inhales, hiccups, and continues. [560] To unravel cosmic illusions is not to destroy them, I realized, but to see through them while still letting them be useful. [582] The dictionary opened on its own to a blank page at the end, and a pen made of light rested there. [603] I was to write the next definition, not for a word, but for the witness who would come after me. [623] I wrote: WITNESS - one who holds the pause between endings so that beginning can remember how to begin. [642] As the ink dried, the second chair was no longer empty, for I could feel my future self listening in the resounding hush. [665] Our purpose was finally one: to unify the broken archive of existence not into a single truth, but into a single attention. [687] I left the softcover dictionary open on the table, knowing the library would keep it wanderingly exact until the next hiccup came. [709] Then I journeyed inward while walking out, stepping through the doors that were also mirrors, carrying the hush with me. [729]</story>
