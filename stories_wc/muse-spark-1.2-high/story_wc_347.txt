<story>Mara had not asked to inherit the cursed orchard, but the trees had been left to her anyway. [18] Beyond the orchard the land fell away into a cliffside labyrinth patrolled by mechanical birds whose brass wings clicked against the sea wind. [41] The orchard itself bore fruit that withered overnight, and the villagers said the curse would spread if the labyrinth ever went dark. [63] Mara spent her days pruning dead branches with a graceless beauty, her movements awkward yet tender as if she were apologizing to each limb. [87] She did not love the work, but she understood that someone had to keep the path open. [104] For a hundred years the labyrinth had served as the only safe route between the harbor and the high town, and now without a keeper travelers vanished in its turns. [134] Her grandmother, the previous caretaker, had left a single note tucked inside a hollow trunk. [149] Find the forgotten blueprint before the night frost forms, it said, or the lights will die. [165] Mara had laughed at first, because there were no lights left in the labyrinth save for the dull glow of the mechanical birds' eyes. [189] On the second morning she found the small nut lodged in the roots of the oldest tree, humming faintly as if something inside were awake. [214] When she cracked it, not with her hands but by leaving impressions without touching, the shell shivered and projected a lattice of light onto the bark. [240] The light was a map, offhandedly exact in every line, showing the labyrinth not as it stood but as it was meant to be. [264] It was the forgotten blueprint, drawn by the first engineers who had built the cliffside passages to guide lost travelers to safety. [286] The blueprint showed lantern nests at every junction, and a central generator beneath the orchard that had long since been buried. [307] Mara understood then why her grandmother had called her reluctant, for the task was not to tend trees but to remember. [328] She carried the nut to the labyrinth entrance as the afternoon light thinned and the wind began to smell like frost. [349] The mechanical birds circled above, their lenses turning to watch her with soft, indifferent chirps. [364] According to the blueprint, the generator could be revived if she could assemble its scattered parts, each hidden at a different turn. [386] She moved through the stone corridors letting the nut hover ahead, leaving impressions without touching the walls yet marking them with light wherever the old lantern mounts had been. [415] At the first junction she found a copper coil tangled in ivy, offhandedly exact in its spiral, as if it had waited to be placed. [440] At the second she uncovered a glass lens, at the third a set of gears that still smelled faintly of oil. [461] The work demanded patience she did not think she possessed, but each recovered piece made the nut glow brighter. [480] By late dusk she reached the heart of the labyrinth, a hollowed chamber under the cliff where the air was cold enough to see her breath. [506] Before the night frost forms the generator must be whole, her grandmother's note had warned, and she could already see crystals beginning to lace the stones. [532] Mara knelt and began to assemble the parts on the blueprint's projection, her hands clumsy but certain in the graceless beauty of the moment. [556] The coil fit into the lens housing with a soft click, the gears meshed, and the nut settled into the center like a seed returning to soil. [583] Light surged through the labyrinth in a warm, uneven pulse, filling each junction where she had left impressions without touching. [603] The mechanical birds steadied in the air, their eyes brightening from dull amber to clear white as they resumed their old patrol patterns. [626] From the high town she heard voices, lost travelers who had been circling the cliff edge, now turning toward the new light. [648] Mara did not feel heroic, only tired and oddly rooted, as if the orchard's curse had finally let go of her ankles. [670] She walked back through the illuminated passages, and behind her the trees on the cliff unfurled buds that would not wither by morning. [693]</story>
