<story>Elara had been the bewildered curator of the Archive of Withered Things for eleven years, and she still did not know why the Archive had chosen her. [27] The Archive was not a building but a library pressed between the first and last page of a book that had never been bound, where every story began and ended at once. [59] She had come to pursue forbidden knowledge, the kind that the founders had sealed away for being too true to be taught. [81] On her desk that morning lay a small pistachio shell cameo blank, pale green and uncarved, humming faintly as if a portrait were trying to remember its own face. [110] No record described it, no donor claimed it, and the catalog simply listed its origin as simply complicated, which had never been a useful classification until now. [137] She leaned closer, and the blank did not reflect her but refracted her into three faint selves: a child placing the shell on her tongue, an old woman carving it, and herself now, bewildered between them. [173] It was nonlinear inheritance, she realized, not a bloodline running forward but a memory falling backward and forward at once, grandchildren gifting heirlooms to grandparents. [198] The sensation filled her with a brightened disquiet, as if sunlight had passed through cold water and settled in her chest, lovely and profoundly wrong. [223] Elara did what curators are rarely trained to do: she decided to inquire of the object instead of about it. [243] She whispered, Who are you waiting for? [250] The cameo blank pulsed, and a door behind the shelves of pressed moths exhaled open, revealing not another aisle but the sharp vacuum and bloom of the astral orchard blossoming on a lonely asteroid. [284] Elara stepped through and felt gravity loosen its promise, her boots touching dust that smelled of paper and pollen while comet light filtered through branches that grew without ever having been planted. [316] Each tree was an ancestor she had not yet met, each fruit a pistachio hanging heavy, its shell already split to reveal the cameo blank inside, waiting to be carved by hands that had not yet been born. [354] She understood then that the orchard was the Archive's margin, where inheritance did not descend but orbited, returning again and again until someone was brave enough to claim it out of order. [386] A wind moved through the blossoms, and with it came the faint sound of running water, which Elara recognized as the old staff bathroom sink that had never quite stopped dripping since the Archive was founded. [422] She had always done her clearest thinking there, having philosophical breakthroughs in bathrooms where the echo made her thoughts sound less like her own and more like advice. [450] Standing beneath the flowering void, with orchard petals falling like torn pages around her shoulders, she finally understood the method in her madness and let the dripping guide her. [479] I am not inheriting you, she said to the blank, you are inheriting me, and if I carve you now I will be giving my grandmother her face. [507] The brightened disquiet sharpened into purpose, not fear but the bright ache of touching a forbidden truth that wanted to be touched. [529] She knelt, took the small tool from her pocket, and began to carve, not a likeness of herself but of the old woman she had seen in the refraction, whose eyes she now knew as her own future. [567] As the shell dust fell onto asteroid soil, a new tree shivered into bloom behind her, its bark fresh and its fruit already hollow, and Elara felt the loop close not as an ending but as a remembered beginning. [606] When she stepped back into the Archive, the catalog entry had rewritten itself, now reading simply: Returned on time, and the bathroom sink at last had stopped dripping. [634]</story>
