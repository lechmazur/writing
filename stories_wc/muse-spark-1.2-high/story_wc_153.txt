<story>Mara had not spoken to her sister in three years, not since the storm took their mother's house and left them arguing over what could not be saved. [28] She came to the edge of the mangrove labyrinth inhabited by singing spirits because the old maps said a cryptic medium lived there who could hear the dead without disturbing them. [59] Mara carried nothing but a canvas bag holding unsent letters she had written to her sister every month and never mailed, and a gargoyle's rainspout shard her mother had pried from their ruined chapel as a keepsake. [96] She did not want forgiveness yet; she lived in the space where an apology waits unspoken, that tight breath before words change everything. [119] Her purpose was simple and desperate: to find the moment before deciding whether to send the letters or burn them and finally let go. [143] The cryptic medium was not what she expected, a barefoot woman in her sixties mending nets with hands that seemed to know the tide's language. [168] You brought something that still holds rain, the medium said, nodding to the bag. [182] Mara showed her the gargoyle's rainspout shard, its limestone mouth still stained with rust and lichen, useless now except as weight. [203] The medium turned it over and said it heals in moonlight, that gargoyles were carved not to frighten but to carry water away from fragile stone, and that some burdens do the same. [236] Night fell quickly in the mangroves, and the singing spirits began, low voices that rose like mist between the roots. [256] Mara had hoped the medium would evoke her mother, but the woman shook her head and said the dead are not parrots to be called on command. [283] Instead she led Mara deeper into the labyrinth to a hut where the air was cool and the windows had never been cleaned, so thick the world outside blurred. [312] Here people learn to listen, the medium whispered, because grief speaks best through messages in condensation on windows, where breath becomes writing and then vanishes. [337] Mara sat as moonlight poured through the mangroves and laid the unsent letters in a circle around the shard, which indeed began to pale and smooth as if the night were washing it. [370] With fierce tenderness the medium placed a hand on Mara's shoulder and told her to breathe until the glass clouded. [390] Mara breathed, and the windows bloomed with fog, and slowly words formed not in her handwriting but in her sister's, or her mother's, or perhaps her own from a future she had not yet chosen. [425] Do you remember how we hid in the pantry during hurricanes and made shadows with flashlights, one message read, and Mara choked out a laugh that was also a sob. [455] Another line appeared: I kept your letters even though you never sent them, I felt them anyway, and Mara realized the spirits were not singing for the dead but for the living who had forgotten how to listen. [493] She understood then that the gargoyle's rainspout shard had never been about drainage, but about choosing what water to carry and what to let fall, just as she must choose what words to keep. [527] The medium left her alone in that hush, and Mara pressed her palm to the cold glass, watching her own breath rewrite the messages until only one remained: I am sorry I left you alone with her anger. [565] It was the sentence she had needed to give, not receive, and it appeared in her own handwriting this time, trembling. [586] At dawn she gathered the letters, and outside the hut the shard lay fully healed, its carved lip whole again in the pale light, no longer a fragment but a small, patient mouth. [619] Mara walked back through the mangrove labyrinth inhabited by singing spirits, but now she heard them as lullabies, fierce and soft at once, guiding her feet between the roots. [648] She did not yet know if her sister would answer, but she knew which moment she had been trying to find, the one before deciding when love still has time to choose its shape. [682]</story>
