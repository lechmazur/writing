<story>Mara had been an onyx-ink cartomancer since she was old enough to hold a brush steady. [16] Her cards were not paper but thin sheets of soot-black glass that drank ink and remembered every line she painted. [36] After the birth of her son, she found she could no longer read them. [50] The futures she inked blurred at the edges, as if the hours she lost to sleepless nights had taken her sight with them. [73] She told herself she wanted to find where lost time accumulates, not for power but for apology. [90] That was her cloudbound repentance, a vow to climb above the world that had kept her grounded in guilt. [109] The old mountain paths spoke of a midnight glade above clouds where time pooled like mist between the roots of sky-pines. [130] She left her son sleeping in his woven cradle and followed the stone stairs that unraveled into fog. [148] At the summit the air thinned into a choking beauty that made her lungs ache and her eyes water with wonder. [169] The glade was there, exactly as promised, a circle of silver grass suspended on a bedrock of cloud that held without falling. [191] Mara stepped onto it and felt the cold breathe up through her boots, willingly perplexed by how solid the softness could be. [213] In the center of the glade stood a low stone table set for two, as if waiting for children who had never grown. [236] On it rested a dolls' tea set saucer crack running faint as a hairline from rim to center, still holding a shallow pool of night dew. [262] She knelt, and the memory that cracked the saucer returned to her not as a card but as a sound. [282] Her mother was laughing the day Mara had dropped the real set while pretending to read fortunes for her dolls at age six. [305] She recalled a family custom of tracing hands on birthday tablecloths, each year her outline growing larger in ink around the stains. [327] Her mother had traced hers even that year, laughing and saying a crack only means something was loved enough to be used. [349] Mara understood then that she had not lost time at all but spent it, poured it like tea into her son. [370] Yet the glade demanded she answer why she had come, why repentance needed a place above the world. [388] She pulled her satchel forward and tried to unfasten the leather straps that bound her glass cards, her fingers clumsy from cold. [410] The knot had swollen with moisture and she had to breathe on her hands before the buckle would give. [429] When it opened she spread the black sheets in a circle and painted a new spread not of futures but of past hours. [452] She painted the traced hands, the cracked saucer, the cradle, the sleepless nights, each stroke accepting rather than predicting. [471] The ink bled and settled, and the clouds beneath the glade shivered and rose to meet the images. [489] For a moment she saw time itself, not as a line but as an accumulation, dew collecting in cracked saucers and handprints on cloth. [513] It was not lost, only gathered in places too small and too common to be called sacred until you climbed high enough to look down. [538] The choking beauty returned, tighter now, as tears filled her throat and she realized repentance had been the wrong word for love. [560] She gathered her glass cards, now dry and clear, and left the dolls' tea set exactly as it was for the next climber. [583] The descent through clouds felt warmer than the ascent, each step untying the fear that she had failed by pausing her art. [605] At home her son woke and reached for her hand, and she pressed his palm to paper and traced it, starting a new border for the tablecloth. [632] Outside, above the peaks, the midnight glade waited empty, its cracked saucer still catching dew for whoever needed to remember. [652]</story>
