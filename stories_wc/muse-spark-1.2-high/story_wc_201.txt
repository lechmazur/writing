<story>After the anesthesia wears off, Lena wakes to a cold that is not temperature but absence. [16] The ultraviolet forest stands around her, its leaves filtering a light she can feel on her teeth more than see with her eyes. [39] She is an enhanced interrogation survivor, and that fact is written not in her memory but in her nervous system. [59] They had made her proprioceptively enhanced so she would never lose herself in a room without doors. [76] Now she can feel every joint angle, every muscle fiber, the exact position of her hands even with her eyes closed. [97] It was supposed to make her easier to break, and then easier to put back together. [113] She sits up too fast and her body remembers before her mind does. [126] Her breath shortens, her shoulders lock, her hands claw at empty air, remembering through trauma responses what her conscious mind cannot yet retrieve. [149] The doctors had warned her that this would happen, that recall would come in spasms and flinches first. [167] In the pocket of her field jacket she finds a pewter mask strap, cold and dull, with her own initials scratched on the buckle. [191] She does not remember packing it, but her fingers know its weight and close around it without thought. [209] The strap belongs to the mask they used for ultraviolet penance, a ritual the wardens called healing and the patients called exposure. [231] For hours they placed you under violet light until your skin learned guilt as a kind of luminescence. [249] She had volunteered for it, not from faith but to discover the mathematics of mercy, to learn if forgiveness had a quantifiable limit. [272] If mercy could be measured, perhaps it could be earned, and if earned, perhaps she could be absolved for what she revealed under interrogation. [296] She had given them names, not because pain broke her but because the enhancement made every second of resistance echo forever in her body. [320] Now the forest is both alien and familiar, which makes her feel displaced rooted, a stranger who knows the soil by taste. [342] The trees are not Earth trees, yet their roots hold her as if she had grown here. [359] She tries to stand, and must brace one hand against a trunk whose bark pulses faintly with stored light. [378] The touch steadies her, and a new memory surfaces not as image but as posture. [393] Her spine curls, her chin tucks, her shoulders rise to her ears, the exact position she held in the chair. [413] By holding the shape, she remembers the question they asked, and the answer she gave. [428] It was not betrayal, it was a trade she calculated would save the younger detainee in the next cell. [447] The mathematics of mercy, she realizes, is not about how much pain you can endure but how precisely you allot harm. [468] She had been trying to solve for mercy as if it were a zero sum equation, when it was a distribution problem. [490] Another tremor runs through her, and she lets her body complete the response instead of fighting it. [507] Her knees fold, her forehead presses to the moss that glows soft violet, and she breathes until the shaking passes. [527] When it does, the forest seems less like a sentence and more like a sanctuary. [542] She slips the pewter mask strap around her wrist, not as penance but as a reminder that light can reveal without burning. [564] She will walk out of these trees proprioceptively aware of every step, every root underfoot, every choice that led here. [584] With each step she will carry both the displacement and the root, the exile and the belonging, and know they are not opposites. [607]</story>
