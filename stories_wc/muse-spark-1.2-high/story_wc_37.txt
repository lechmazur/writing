<story>Elara had not been born so much as scattered, a scattered memory keeper whose self existed in fragments left inside other people's minds. [23] In the mirror world she could walk between those fragments as if stepping between islands in a dark sea. [42] There the sky never turned, for the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night held the last living light of the realm. [64] Its trees grew not fruit but bottled seasons, each glass orb humming with a suspended sunrise that never reached the soil. [85] Elara tended it because her purpose was simple and absolute, to rekindle a dying realm before its final ember cooled. [105] The orchard was failing, its roots drinking darkness instead of water. [116] She alone remembered that mornings could be made, not just awaited, as woven dawns stitched from attention and care. [135] That knowledge knits communities together, or it had once, when villages sang the light into being each morning. [153] Now no one remembered the songs, except as an ache behind the teeth when night pressed too long. [171] Elara collected those aches the way others collected rainwater, storing them in jars beneath the mica window at the greenhouse heart. [192] The mica window was not glass but pressed light, thin sheets of mineral and memory that could filter a tune into color. [214] Behind it hung the last loom, its warp threads spun from dusk and silver root. [229] To use it, Elara had to gather the scattered pieces of herself, traveling in the mirror world from mind to mind. [250] In the house of the baker, she found her own laughter, left there years ago when she taught him a kneading song. [272] In the child's attic, she recovered her curiosity, folded small as a paper crane. [286] Each retrieval made her more whole and more heavy, bound by every tether she reclaimed. [301] The binding was necessary, because only a bound release could power the loom without tearing it apart. [318] When she had gathered enough of herself to remember her full name, she returned to the greenhouse and built a fire of quiet. [341] One by one, she opened the jars beneath the mica window and let the forgotten aches spill out as sound. [361] She sang them, not with her voice alone but through forgotten songs that lived in her marrow, melodies villages had used to call the sun. [386] As each song passed through the mica window, it separated into threads of peach, amber, and pale gold. [404] She caught the threads and fed them to the loom, where woven dawns began to take shape row by patient row. [425] The pattern she wove was not a picture but a promise, a memory of morning that had never yet happened. [445] Outside, the alchemical orchard shivered, its bottled seasons rattling as the night pressed harder, as if jealous. [462] Elara's hands bled where the warp cut them, but the loom drank that too, threading her scattered life into the cloth. [483] When the cloth was long enough to swaddle a child, she lifted it, glowing and warm as fresh bread. [502] She carried it to the center tree, an ancient pear whose bark had turned to iron in the endless night, and wrapped the trunk gently. [527] The tree shuddered and then sighed, sap rising like sunrise through its veins for the first time in centuries. [546] Light spilled from the woven cloth into the roots and then outward, threading through the soil to every other tree. [566] Each bottled season caught the glow and amplified it, until the entire alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night became a lantern. [588] Beyond the mica walls, the mirror world flickered, and in the waking world people lifted their faces without knowing why. [608] In the baker's house, the baker remembered a tune he had not hummed since childhood and began to sing without words. [629] The child in the attic unfolded a paper crane and found it was a map to morning. [646] Elara felt herself scattering again, not from loss but from distribution, her memories knitting into every lit mind. [664] The bound release held, the loom's tension easing as her self thinned into light, freed precisely because she had chosen to be tied. [687] Dawn did not break all at once but was woven, thread by thread, until night learned how to hold it. [707]</story>
