<story>Mara had not set foot in Kalin Valley since her grandparents carried her away as a child, and when she returned as a woman she barely recognized the hills that had haunted her dreams. [34] She came not for nostalgia but because the letters from her aunt had stopped, and the village council had written that the old orchard was finally to be cleared. [63] The orchard was unlike any Mara remembered, for it had grown above the ancient catacombs where tree roots snaked between bones in a quiet, tangled embrace. [89] The elders called it the catacomb orchard, and they warned her not to linger there after dusk. [106] Mara, warmly curious by nature, listened politely and then ignored them, carrying her notebook and her grandfather's brass compass into the shade. [128] She had been hired to help the archaeology team, but her true purpose was private, to restore lost faith in a lineage she had spent her life doubting. [156] As a diaspora returnee, she had grown up on stories rather than soil, and none of the stories had ever felt entirely true. [179] Her grandfather claimed their family were Keepers of the Sundial, guardians of time for travelers who passed through the valley. [199] No one in the city believed him, and after his death Mara found his journal filled with sketches of a stone sundial base buried somewhere among the roots and the dead. [230] The excavation of the central tomb would take weeks, and Mara arranged to be present across excavating a tomb, volunteering to log each layer of earth and bone. [258] The head archaeologist, Dr. Liora, was skeptical of legends but welcomed an extra pair of careful hands. [275] On the first day they uncovered woven shrouds and olive pits, evidence of interrupted journeys where merchants and pilgrims had been laid to rest before reaching their destinations. [303] Mara felt a cautious enchantment settle over her as she worked, as if the air itself were holding its breath to see what she would find. [329] She did not dig in straight lines, but through purposeful wandering, letting the thickest roots guide her between niches where the stones had shifted. [353] The other workers laughed at her method until she began to find things they had missed, small carved beads and fragments of a dial's bronze gnomon. [379] By the third day her hands ached and her notebook was damp with earth, yet she felt closer to the story that had eluded her childhood. [405] On the fourth afternoon, beneath a fig root thick as her wrist and curled around a child's skull, she struck stone that did not sound like the catacomb wall. [434] She brushed away the soil and revealed the circular edge of a sundial base, its carved hour marks still sharp despite centuries of darkness. [458] Mara's breath caught, for the base was exactly as her grandfather had drawn it, with a hollow center for the gnomon and inscriptions in the old valley script. [486] Dr. Liora knelt beside her, eyes wide, and whispered that no such instrument had ever been recorded in a burial site. [507] Mara traced the inscription with her fingertip and read aloud the words she had last heard at her grandfather's knee. [527] It read, We mark time not to count it, but to guide those who were interrupted. [543] She understood then that the Keepers had not measured days for the living, but kept light for the dead whose journeys had ended too soon. [568] Tears stung her eyes, not from grief but from recognition, as if a doubt she had carried for decades finally softened. [589] Mara asked permission to rebuild the sundial, not as a museum piece but as it once stood, to honor the faith her family had forgotten. [614] Dr. Liora hesitated, then agreed, moved by the artifact's perfect preservation and Mara's quiet conviction. [629] Across the remaining days of the excavation they lifted the base together, cleaned its face, and set a new bronze gnomon forged by the village smith from Mara's grandfather's sketch. [659] When the sun broke through the orchard canopy, light fell precisely along the noon mark, and for a moment the tangled roots and bones seemed less like a grave than a garden. [691] The workers gathered in silence, feeling the same cautious enchantment that had guided Mara, as if time itself had paused to be noticed. [714] Mara placed her grandfather's compass upon the sundial base and watched its needle tremble, no longer a tool for leaving, but for belonging. [737] That evening she wrote in her notebook not as an outsider recording relics, but as a Keeper continuing the record. [757]</story>
