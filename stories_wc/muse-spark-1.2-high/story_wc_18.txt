<story>Mira was a mirror maker who had spent forty years polishing truth until it shone without flattery. [17] She was firmly grounded in a way that made apprentices trust her hands more than their own eyes. [35] Her workshop stood at the edge of the ruined orchard district lost in centuries of thick fog, where apple trees grew as twisted black skeletons. [60] No road led in and none led out, for the fog ate distance and time alike. [76] Upon the shimmer of twilight frost, when the whole district turned silver and still, Mira saw her chance. [94] For three nights a runaway dream had slipped through her house, a bright thread she had first seen sleeping in her own unfinished mirror. [118] It was not hers, but her dead sister's childhood memory of sunlight on an orchard that had never been ruined. [138] The dream did not want to be remembered, and each night it ran further into the fog between the trees. [158] Mira understood her motivation with the clarity of glass: to capture a runaway dream before it dissolved and took her sister with it forever. [182] She could not chase it with nets or prayers; dreams obey only the surfaces that are made to hold them. [202] So she decided she must recreate the orchard as it had been before the fog, not as wood but as light. [223] On her workbench lay the astronomer's broken sextant, left by a traveler who died trying to measure stars through fog that never cleared. [246] Its mirrors were cracked and its arc was bent, useless for navigation but perfect for catching light that did not yet exist. [268] Mira set the sextant at the heart of a new mirror frame made from the orchard's own frozen branches. [287] The world that night felt like glass mountains, vast and transparent and sharp enough to cut, where every breath hung visible and fragile in the cold. [313] She knew that glass breaks if forced, and so she would not force the dream. [328] Instead she waited until the frost thickened and the fog exhaled, then opened the shutters to the ruined trees. [347] The runaway dream returned, flickering like a pale bird between the glassy branches, curious and afraid. [363] Mira did not move toward it, but began to breathe, slow and deliberate, inviting the cold air deep into her lungs. [384] Through synchronized breathing she matched the rhythm of the fog itself, which rose and fell like a sleeping giant. [403] The dream, caught in that shared tide, began to breathe with her, its light pulsing in time with her chest. [423] In that moment the central work began: erasing boundaries between what was reflected and what was real. [440] The mirror with the sextant at its center did not show Mira's face, but showed the orchard whole, blossoming, sunlit, impossibly present. [462] It showed not a reflection but a door, and the fog that had divided memory from the world grew thin as paper. [484] The runaway dream hovered before the glass, no longer running, seeing at last a place it could land without breaking. [504] Mira remained firmly grounded, feet on the cold floor, hands steady, even as her eyes filled with tears that did not fall. [526] If she reached out too fast the glass mountains of this fragile moment would shatter, so she only breathed. [545] The dream touched the mirror and sank into it like light into water, and the sextant trembled and held. [564] All at once the orchard outside remembered itself; buds cracked the frost and the fog lifted a hand's breadth. [583] Within the mirror, her sister ran laughing through blossoms that would never rot, and the dream was safe because it had been given a world. [608] Mira had not trapped the dream; she had given it a home by dissolving the wall between longing and presence. [628] The glass mountains softened into glass valleys, clear and calm, reflecting only what was true. [643] Dawn never came to the district, but the twilight frost held, and Mira kept breathing with the mirror long after. [663] She would tend this orchard of light until the fog learned to guard it rather than hide it. [681]</story>
