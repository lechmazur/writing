<story>The withdrawn knight had not spoken his name in seven years, since the fall of the northern citadel left him without a banner to serve. [25] He made his home in the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night, where glass panes held back a sky that never remembered dawn. [49] He tended trees that grew not fruit but ideas, and he did it to capture the shape of possibility before it withered on the branch. [74] The work was factually mystical, for each blossom contained a theorem and each root drank from buried starlight. [92] Pruning them required care through forgotten patterns his mother, a hedge witch, had taught him as a child. [110] Inside the potting shed, tucked beneath a cracked terracotta pot, he found an abandoned marionette theater program dated from the year the citadel fell. [134] It listed acts never performed, names of wooden actors and the strings that moved them, and on its back someone had scribbled a map of his greenhouse. [161] At the bottom, in faded ink, was a murmured allegiance, a vow whispered by puppeteers to their puppets that they would never be left hanging. [186] He read it aloud as reality shifts began to ripple the greenhouse air, turning the glass orchard into a hall of mirrors. [208] Each tree reflected a different life he might have lived, one where he remained a knight, one where he became a father, one where he never left the citadel at all. [239] He reached for the nearest branch, his gauntleted hand trembling, and had to unsnap the old leather strap at his wrist to feel the bark with bare skin. [267] The contact sent a current of shivering bliss through him, cold and exquisite, as if joy itself had been stored in ice. [289] In that instant the greenhouse breathed, and the puppet strings drawn on the program lifted into the air as thin lines of light. [312] They tied themselves gently to his fingers, his elbows, his heart, not to control him but to remind him how movement once felt. [335] The knight remembered his withdrawal had not been peace but paralysis, a long silence after his oath had been broken by commanders he trusted. [359] He had told himself he was protecting the last living ideas, but he had been hiding from choosing among them. [379] Now the trees offered futures that asked for a decision, and the light strings tugged him softly toward the one where he knelt and planted something new. [406] A voice came not from outside but from the wood itself, repeating the murmured promise he had just read, asking if he would keep it. [431] The knight understood that allegiance was not to a lord or a city but to the possibility that he could still act with care. [455] He answered, his voice rusted from disuse, that he would tend not only what grew but what might grow. [474] As reality shifts steadied, the mirrored futures folded like pages, and one branch lowered a single luminous fruit into his open palm. [496] Inside it was not a theorem but a seed, warm and humming, containing no answer only the permission to begin. [516] He pressed it into the dark soil of the central bed through forgotten patterns of sowing that made the earth remember spring. [538] The greenhouse shuddered, and for the first time in seven years the endless night outside the glass thinned to a deep indigo. [560] A faint line of coming dawn touched the horizon, not yet day but the promise that night could be kept from being eternal. [583] The light strings dissolved, leaving his hands free, and the abandoned program curled at the edges and turned to compost where it lay. [606] The withdrawn knight no longer felt withdrawn, but widened, as if his armor had finally become a greenhouse too, holding life instead of hiding it. [631] He filled a tin watering can and poured, feeling the old shivering bliss return, now not strange but earned, a cold fire that made him smile through tears. [659] Outside, the night remained, but inside, a small green shoot broke the soil, reaching with factually mystical certainty toward whatever light he would give it. [684]</story>
