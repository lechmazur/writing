<story>My grandmother called him the diminutive titan, though the temple records named him only as Keeper. [16] He stood no taller than a child, yet his shoulders bore the weight of the ancient temple library lost in time as if it were a cloak. [43] The library had no doors and no windows, only endless shelves carved from starlit basalt that breathed faintly when no one watched. [65] I was twelve when the queen died and her second son, Cael, took the crown without the rite of verification. [85] My aunt, who served as archivist, whispered that we had reason to unmask a pretender to the throne, because the true heir should have borne the birthmark of ash. [114] She was regally unpretentious, mending her own robes and eating with the novices, and that is why I trusted her. [134] She told me the Keeper alone could confirm lineage, but he had not spoken in a generation, waiting across the pause of shifting constellations for a signal. [161] That signal was a rune of drift, a mark that appeared only when the temple's slow calendar completed its silent turn. [182] No one knew how to draw it, and the library gave no index for it. [197] So my aunt began communicating with the Keeper through cryptic messages left in library books, tucking folded slips between pages on genealogy and law. [221] She used an iron gall ink pellet dissolved in rainwater, because its dark stain could not be erased and would endure for centuries. [244] The Keeper read each note at night, his small hands careful, his eyes older than the stone. [261] For months he answered nothing, and the pretender Cael grew bolder, levying taxes and melting the queen's silver seal. [280] Then on the night the constellations stalled, as they do once every eighty years, the air in the library turned perfectly still. [302] The Keeper climbed the central ladder, lifted a massive ledger above his head, and with a soft breath made it hover. [323] I watched from the shadows, my heart hammering, as a faint light traced itself upon the ledger's margin. [341] It was a rune of drift reforming, not drawn but remembered by the paper itself. [356] The Keeper touched the rune and whispered that the moment had come to sunder the false lineage from the true. [376] He asked me to bring him the registry of births, which lay chained beneath the altar of quiet. [394] I obeyed, dragging the iron-bound book across the floor with both arms, while Cael's guards patrolled the outer cloister unaware. [414] The Keeper opened the registry and pressed his thumb to the empty space beside Cael's name, where a birthmark should have been recorded. [437] Nothing appeared, and that absence was proof, but proof means little without witness. [450] So the Keeper taught me to copy the rune, mixing my ink from the same iron gall ink pellet, and to place it where all scholars would see. [478] We worked through cryptic messages left in library books no longer as secrets but as invitations, each margin now bearing the faint glow of drift. [503] By dawn scholars began to arrive for the equinox census, filtering into the ancient temple library lost in time as if summoned by a dream. [528] They saw the glowing rune hovering above the registry and fell silent, understanding its old law without explanation. [546] Cael entered last, confident and draped in stolen crimson, and demanded to know what trick had been played. [564] The Keeper, still diminutive, stepped onto the registry and seemed to grow without changing size, his presence filling the hall. [584] With quiet grace he asked Cael to place his hand upon the rune and to let it read the truth of his blood. [607] Cael laughed and obeyed, believing no ink could judge him, but the rune flared and rejected him with a soft, terrible light. [629] No violence followed, only a graceful collapse of his claim, as courtiers stepped back and the air itself withdrew its permission. [650] The birth registry shivered, and beneath Cael's false entry a line dissolved, revealing the name of the true heir, a daughter thought lost at sea. [675] My aunt wept, regally unpretentious even in tears, and bowed not to power but to continuity. [691] The Keeper closed the book, dissolved the last iron gall ink pellet in water, and watched the dark spread like night returning to its proper place. [717] Outside the constellations resumed their slow turning, and the temple settled, its task complete for another age. [734]</story>
