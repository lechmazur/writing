<story>Mira was the Orderly seed sorter of the Ashen skyborne arbor, and her days were measured by the quiet click of seeds against lacquered trays. [25] The arbor hung above the valleys like a low grey cloud tethered by ancient roots, its branches heavy with hush and lichen. [47] During the hush where past meets prophecy, the leaves began to loosen, although no wind had stirred. [64] Mira watched them fall and felt a soft ache of purpose, for she wanted To decode the message in falling leaves before they crumbled to ash. [90] Regional folklore claimed each falling leaf carried a syllable of the arbor's memory, a sentence the tree wrote only once a century. [112] Mira believed it with a Mossy delight that made even tedious sorting feel like a game. [128] She worked beneath a vaulted bough where a single Moonstone cabochon dust speck had lodged in the bark, catching light like a captured star. [152] The speck had been there since her apprenticeship, and the elders said it marked the heart of the arbor. [171] Though she was orderly and patient, Mira possessed a secret Method: Building tiny monuments to forgotten things from husks, bark curls, and broken twigs. [195] Each monument honored something the arbor had lost, a bird's nest abandoned last spring, a withered blossom, a name carved and worn away. [218] She believed that to remember was to invite the arbor to speak more clearly. [232] On the morning the hush deepened, a leaf whirled down and landed on her sorting tray, its veins glowing faintly amber. [253] She did not immediately Prod it, though her fingertips itched, because the sorting rites demanded stillness first. [270] Instead she watched its trembling, and as it settled she saw the amber veins rearrange into a jagged symbol she half remembered. [292] It was the old sign for listen, a shape from Regional folklore that mothers traced on children's palms during storm nights. [313] Mira felt the Mossy delight rise again, damp and bright as spring moss after rain, and she carefully set her tweezers down. [335] With a breath she used the tweezers to Prod the leaf's edge, hoping to turn it without tearing its delicate lattice. [356] The leaf shivered and the Moonstone cabochon dust speck above her flared suddenly, scattering pale light across the tray. [375] In that light the leaf's symbol brightened, then softened, and the leaf itself Emerges transformed always when touched by moonstone. [395] Its green edge curled into a tight scroll no larger than a thumbnail, revealing a second, smaller leaf hidden inside. [415] Mira unfolded it with infinite care, and there, pressed between the layers, lay a line of tiny, perfect script like seed writing. [437] It read, Remember the ground below, and she understood the arbor was not asking to be decoded but to be answered. [458] For generations the Ashen skyborne arbor had drifted, severed from the earth, and the folklore had forgotten that it once rooted in soil. [481] Mira's monuments had been rehearsals for remembering, and now the arbor asked her to build one larger than all before. [501] She gathered fallen leaves, seed husks, and bark, and on her tray she formed a minute forest floor, complete with tiny roots and pebble mountains. [526] She placed the curled leaf at its center like a prophet, still glowing from the moonstone light. [543] The Mossy delight in her chest swelled, not giddy but deep and rooted, like joy that grows in shade. [562] When she breathed upon the tiny monument, the speck of moonstone above pulsed and cast its light down once more. [582] The leaves on the arbor shivered in unison, and a rain of amber-veined leaves descended, each bearing a syllable she could now read. [605] She would sort them not by size or color but by story, and in sorting she would give the skyborne arbor a path home. [629]</story>
