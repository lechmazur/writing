<story>The reflective locksmith named Mara had not opened a door in three years. [13] She lived above her old shop and spent her days listening to the town clock that no longer kept time. [33] Across the lull of a wounded season, when the mines had closed and the river ran low, the town fell silent enough to hear dust settle. [59] Mara told herself she was done with tumblers and keys, but she could not ignore the letter that came on foot from the cavern keepers. [84] They asked her to look at a door that had never needed a key, deep inside the marble cavern beyond the quarry. [106] She went because the invitation mentioned a broken timer mechanism and she had never been able to leave a broken thing alone. [128] The marble cavern was colder than she expected, its walls veined white and gray and polished by water over centuries. [148] At its heart stood a plain iron door set into the stone, with no handle and no keyhole, only a shallow dish of mirrored glass at its center. [176] The keepers said the door had opened once a year for generations, always at the same hour on the same day, and now it did not. [202] Behind it, they believed, was the harvest of echoes, the cavern's memory of every sound ever made inside it. [221] Mara was peripherally focused, the kind of person who noticed how light moved at the edge of vision while pretending to look straight ahead. [245] She did not try to pick a lock that was not there, instead she sat and watched the mirrored dish. [265] For three days she practiced her method, studying reflection angles at different times as the sun shifted through a narrow shaft overhead. [287] At noon the light struck the dish and scattered, at dusk it pooled, and just before dawn it made a thin perfect line across the marble. [313] She began to understand that the door was not locked but timed, and its timer had failed. [330] The broken timer mechanism lay behind the glass, a nest of copper gears and hair springs frozen with verdigris and dust. [351] No one had built it to be repaired, only to be precise, and precision had been its only virtue. [370] Mara decided to reverse-engineer the mechanism without touching it, by mapping what it should have done from what the light still did. [392] Her motivation was not payment or praise, but something quieter, to store wonder in the cellar of reason, for later. [412] She had learned long ago that wonder fades if you spend it all at once, and reason needs a cellar to keep things cool and dark until you need them. [442] On the fourth morning she saw it, a second reflection that should not have been there, flickering on the ceiling of the marble cavern when the shaft was still dim. [472] It was an echo of light, arriving a fraction late, as if the cavern itself remembered yesterday's noon. [490] She realized the harvest of echoes was not a metaphor but a function, the cavern storing sound and light and releasing them when the timer allowed the door to open. [520] The broken timer mechanism had not just sealed the door, it had dammed the cavern's memory, and pressure was building behind it. [542] If she forced the door, the stored echoes would burst out at once and be lost as noise. [560] If she waited and rebuilt the timing from light alone, she might coax the door to release them slowly, one year at a time as intended. [586] She worked peripherally still, not staring at the dish but letting its changes catch her eye while she cleaned and oiled what she could reach without breaking the glass. [615] She fashioned a new regulator from a spare mainspring and a shard of mirror, calibrating it by the angles she had recorded. [637] When she set it trembling beside the dish, the old gears shivered and began to turn again, taught by the new rhythm. [659] The door did not swing open but sighed, and a soft tone filled the marble cavern, the first echo of a child's laugh from a century before. [686] Then another, a hymn, a hammer strike, a whispered promise, each emerging clear and separate instead of colliding. [704] The keepers wept, but Mara the reflective locksmith only smiled, for she had not restored a door but tuned an instrument. [725] She left the cavern before the cycle finished, knowing wonder is best carried out in small portions and saved. [744]</story>
