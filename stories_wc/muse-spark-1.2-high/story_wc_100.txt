<story>After the mysterious package arrives, Silas does not open it for three days. [13] He is a weary undertaker, and he has learned that unopened things stay quiet longer. [28] It sits on his kitchen table, wrapped in oilcloth and tied with tarred twine, humming faintly when the house is still. [49] On the fourth morning, he cuts the twine and finds inside a broken lighthouse lens, heavy as a drowned moon. [69] With it comes a water stained card that reads only FOR THE LIBRARY THAT WAITED. [84] Silas knows no library, but the lens smells of salt and deep water, and that night he dreams of shelves stretching into dark. [107] In the dream, distress signals throb through the water like slow heartbeats, light trying to speak through cracked glass. [126] He wakes to find his small house filled with a chased stillness, as if quiet itself were being hunted through the rooms. [148] The lens on the table pulses once, weakly, and he understands it is answering something far below. [165] Silas is inadvertently stubborn, the kind of man who keeps mending a fence long after the field is empty. [184] So instead of reburying the lens in his drawer, he carries it to the shore where his father's old diving suit hangs in the shed. [209] He has not dived in twenty years, not since he traded currents for coffins, but his hands remember the seals and valves. [231] By noon he is descending, the broken lighthouse lens strapped to his chest like a second heart. [248] The sea takes him without resistance, water occupying impossible spaces inside his ears, his suit, his thoughts. [265] Light thins, pressure builds, and then the slope drops away to reveal the forgotten underwater city library, its dome cracked open like an eye. [289] It should have been crushed, buried, erased, yet it stands intact, columns wrapped in kelp, doors open to the current. [309] Silas lands on the silted steps, and the chased stillness returns, deeper here, a silence that flees even as it holds. [330] Inside, books remain on shelves, preserved by cold and pressure, their pages swollen but not rotted, titles still legible through film. [351] He brushes silt from a ledger and reads names, dates, causes, a catalog of the drowned whom the sea never returned. [372] At the center of the great hall, a pedestal waits empty, ringed with dead lamps and a hollow shaped exactly for his lens. [395] The distress signals grow stronger now, not sound but light flickering weakly from the broken lighthouse lens against his chest. [415] Silas understands then that this place was a lighthouse once, before it sank, before the coast changed, and the library kept its light. [438] For decades it has been trying to revitalize its beam, to call the lost home, but with shattered glass it could only stutter. [461] He wants to give rest a permanent address, not a shifting grave at sea but a shelf, a name, a place that does not move. [486] That has been his work on land, measuring bodies for boxes that stay put while families learn to stay still themselves. [507] Here, the same work waits, larger and unfinished. [515] With trembling gloves he lifts the broken lighthouse lens and sets it into the pedestal, though its fractures catch and resist. [536] He is weary, his air thin, his knees aching against the stone, yet his inadvertently stubborn hands do not pull away. [557] The lens flares, not bright but true, and light occupies impossible spaces again, threading through cracks, through water, through the stacks. [578] One by one, the dead lamps around it kindle, and the library reveals itself not as ruin but as beacon. [598] Shelves glow, water clears, and on each book a name now shines faintly, distress signals finally answered with location. [617] Silas sinks to his knees as light steadies, and the chased stillness at last stops running and settles over him like dust. [639] He knows he cannot stay, his air is nearly gone, but he has left something that will outlast his breath. [659] He ascends slowly, light shrinking behind him, yet still visible, still holding, a permanent address for rest glowing in the deep. [680] When he breaks the surface, the sun is low, and he floats on his back, breathing hard, watched from below by a light that will never again go dark. [709]</story>
