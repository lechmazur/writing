<story>Elara had been a lost soul collector for forty years, though she never liked the title. [16] She did not cage or claim, she listened, and she escorted those who had lingered too long because no one had believed their story. [40] In the hush of a hidden sunrise, when the world held its breath between night and day, she arrived at echo gorge with her harp on her back. [68] The gorge was a great wound in the earth where every sound returned changed, as if the stone itself were trying to remember. [91] She was modestly fearless, not because she felt no fear, but because she carried it gently and kept walking anyway. [111] Her purpose there was not merely to guide the dead, but to integrate laughter into healing, a lesson her mentor had taught her with his last breath. [138] He had told her that grief without laughter hardened into stone, and that even the lost needed to remember how to smile before they could leave. [164] The souls in echo gorge were different, bound by spectral confessions that repeated endlessly against the cliffs. [181] They whispered the things they had never dared to say alive, apologies and lies and small cruelties, and the gorge echoed them back until they became a prison. [209] Elara could not simply speak to them, she had to reach them through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp, the only music that could match their broken cadence. [238] She tuned the harp imperfectly on purpose, loosening the third and seventh strings until their tones wavered like voices on the edge of tears. [262] Around her neck hung a crocodile's tooth, smooth and yellowed, given to her by a child soul she had once helped cross. [284] The child had said it was for biting through knots, and Elara had kept it since as a charm for untangling what would not unwind. [309] That morning she had come to pursue one soul in particular, a woman named Mara who had died laughing and then spent a decade regretting it. [335] Mara had choked on laughter at her sister's funeral and believed her mirth had damned her, so she silenced herself forever after. [357] Elara found her huddled near the gorge's coldest wall, her spectral confessions looping in a hoarse whisper that the stone threw back a hundred times. [382] I laughed, I laughed, the echo sobbed, turning a single moment of helpless human overflow into an eternal accusation. [401] Elara knelt and did not offer pity, for pity would have only polished the echo. [416] Instead she plucked her harp, sending through the distorted notes of a misaligned harp a melody that stumbled and cracked in perfect sympathy. [439] The notes did not soothe, they recognized, and Mara looked up, startled to be heard rather than judged. [457] Elara then told her own truth, that she too had once laughed at the wrong moment, and that the sound still lived in her like a luminous ache. [485] It was bright and it hurt, like sun on a bruise, and it meant she was alive enough to hold both sorrow and joy at once. [511] For a moment Mara was silent, and the gorge, so used to repeating, fell quiet enough to listen. [529] Elara lifted the crocodile's tooth and pressed it into Mara's translucent palm, asking her to bite through the knot she had tied around her own heart. [555] Mara closed her fingers, and a low, rusted chuckle escaped her, not joyful but honest, the first air she had let herself breathe in years. [580] The sound struck the walls of echo gorge and came back changed, lighter this time, as if the stone had finally learned a new word. [605] Other souls turned toward the sound, their own spectral confessions faltering as they heard laughter returned without mockery. [623] Elara began to play again, steadier now, and urged them not to forget their regrets but to let laughter sit beside them while they healed. [648] One by one their whispers softened, and the gorge filled with a strange, uneven chorus of sighs and small, startled laughs. [669] Mara stood, no longer pursued by her own echo, and Elara walked beside her toward the pale light spilling over the rim in the hush of a hidden sunrise. [698] At the edge, Mara paused and handed back the crocodile's tooth, saying she no longer needed to bite through anything. [718] Elara smiled and kept the tooth, knowing another knot would come, and that to integrate laughter into healing was not a single crossing but a practice. [744] She watched Mara step into the light and dissolve like mist, her last sound not a confession but a clear, modest laugh. [766] Elara remained modestly fearless in the dawn, tuning her harp again for the next soul who believed they were beyond forgiveness. [787] Behind her echo gorge waited, luminous and aching, for the next song. [799]</story>
