<story>After the last echo of laughter fades in the empty plaza above, Mara remains alone at her easel. [18] She is a charcoal-fingered portrait sketch artist whose hands are always stained black at the tips. [34] For ten years she has drawn strangers for coins, capturing their faces while leaving her own canvases blank. [52] She is mnemonically gifted, able to recall every face she has ever drawn with perfect and painful clarity. [70] That gift feels less like talent and more like a burden, because no face ever truly leaves her. [88] Tonight a child she sketched earlier returns and slips a folded note into her palm without a word. [106] The note contains only an address and a charcoal smudge in the shape of a crystal. [122] She knows she should crumple it and go home, but she feels one of those inescapable callings pulling at her chest. [143] The address leads her to the old municipal building, abandoned since the quake that cracked its foundation. [160] Inside, behind the custodian's desk, she finds a narrow stairwell descending into darkness. [173] She follows it down until the air turns cool and the walls begin to glitter. [188] The stairwell opens into a subterranean crystal cave where light refracts in pale auroral colors. [203] For a moment she stands breathless, overwhelmed by an auroral sorrow that makes her eyes sting with light and grief. [223] In the center of the cave someone has placed an easel, a blank canvas, and a small wooden box. [242] The box is secured by a broken combination lock whose dial spins loosely without catching. [257] Mara kneels and pries the box open with her charcoal stained fingernail, finding inside a single iron key. [275] Etched on the key are the words Paint what you have never allowed yourself to see. [291] On the far wall of the cave hangs a large painting draped in dust, its colors dimmed by years. [310] She lifts the cloth and sees a portrait of herself as a young girl, laughing with a brush in her hand. [331] She has no memory of this painting, yet her mnemonically gifted mind insists she must have seen it once. [350] Behind the canvas she notices the wall is hollow, and a faint draft moves the painting slightly. [367] Using the iron key she lifts the painting with something behind it revealing a shallow niche cut into crystal. [386] Inside the niche rests a stack of her own abandoned sketches, the ones she buried years ago to pursue portrait work. [407] They are wild and unfinished, full of crystal caves and impossible lights she had dreamed as a child. [425] She understands now that she did not come here to find a treasure but to rectify an old betrayal of herself. [446] Her motivation had always been to uncork suppressed creativity, but fear had taught her to sketch others instead of herself. [466] The inescapable callings she felt were not from the child or the note but from those buried drawings demanding air. [486] With trembling fingers she takes her charcoal and begins to draw directly onto the cave wall, not a face but a vista. [508] The crystal catches each stroke and throws it back as light, turning her lines into a soft aurora around her. [528] Tears come, but they are not bitter, they are suffused with that same auroral sorrow she felt on entering. [547] She draws until her fingers ache and the broken combination lock lies forgotten beside the wooden box. [564] When she finally steps back, the cave is no longer dark but alive with her own imagined world rendered in charcoal and crystal light. [588] She knows no one may ever see this gallery, yet she no longer needs a watcher to validate its truth. [608] The child's note had not been an invitation but a reminder, left by her own younger self in a forgotten pocket. [629] Mara leaves the key in the niche and climbs back toward the plaza, her hands still black but her chest finally light. [651] Above, the night air is quiet, and she listens for any echo, hearing only her own steady breathing. [669] She will return to her easel tomorrow, but she will no longer draw only the faces of strangers. [687] For the first time she understands that creation is not a performance for coins but a calling she can no longer escape. [709]</story>
