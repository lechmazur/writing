<story>After the tower falls, Mara stood alone in the hush that followed the dust. [14] She was the bounty hunter's pacifist daughter, and the valley knew her not for a gun but for her hands. [34] Her home was a desert rose farm in a glass-domed enclosure that caught the brutal sun and softened it. [53] She had chosen to weave continuity through chaos by tending life where others saw only barrenness. [69] At noon the dome filled with a tranquil blaze, light so intense it felt like silence made visible. [87] In that brightness she found an engraved quiet, as if peace had been etched into the air itself. [105] The roses were senselessly perceptive, turning their heads before she entered and opening before she watered. [121] It was not magic but her patient work of inculcating plants with values, speaking to each cutting of patience and endurance. [142] Her father, the bounty hunter, had taught her how to track and how to kill, but never how to stay. [162] When the tower fell he rode out to claim bounties in the collapse, leaving her the dome and a warning. [182] Among his abandoned gear she found an airship ballast sandbag scrap, canvas torn and leaking fine white sand. [200] She did not throw it away but used it to sheathe the roots of a failing rose, wrapping the burlap gently around the stem. [224] The plant steadied, as if it understood the gift of weight and protection. [237] Outside the dome the world churned, caravans raiding and settlements burning where the tower's law had vanished. [254] Inside, she kept rows of desert roses blooming in defiance, pink and white against the rust colored waste. [272] People came to trade, expecting the bounty hunter's heir to be hard, and left surprised by her soft refusal to fight. [293] One mercenary laughed and asked what use a pacifist was after the tower falls. [307] She answered that someone must keep the water clean and the seeds alive while others decided who owned the sky. [327] He did not understand, but he left a coin on the soil and took a cutting, holding it like it might break. [349] That winter the glass cracked in a storm, and cold poured through a jagged seam. [364] She patched it with the last of the airship ballast sandbag scrap and sealed the edges with resin from the roses. [385] The repair held, and the roses pressed their blooms against the patched canvas as if thanking it. [402] In spring her father returned, dust covered and empty handed, his bounty coat torn and his rifle gone. [420] He stared at the desert rose farm in a glass-domed enclosure and seemed not to recognize his daughter among the flowers. [441] You stayed, he said, as if the idea had never occurred to him. [454] She poured him tea brewed from rose petals and told him the dome had taught her how to listen. [473] He sat among the senselessly perceptive blooms, and for the first time she saw him notice how they leaned toward kindness. [494] Without a word he took his old knife and helped her sheathe the young stems against the wind, his hands clumsy but careful. [517] She watched him learn the slowness of inculcating plants with values, that patience could be planted as surely as seed. [537] At dusk the dome held again its engraved quiet, and even the weary hunter seemed to breathe within its lines. [557] He finally understood her need to weave continuity through chaos, not by hunting men but by keeping one place whole. [577] Outside the storm still threatened, but inside the tranquil blaze of late sun made the sand glow like gold. [596] She knew the tower would not rise again, but the roses would, season after season, because she remained. [614]</story>
