<story>After the procedure, Mara could not remember why she had volunteered to forget. [13] The clinic called her a clean slate seeker with dirty past, a label printed neatly on her discharge papers as if it were a diagnosis. [38] She remembered nothing of the fires she had set in the port district, but she felt their ash in her lungs anyway. [60] Her therapist had warned her that erasure was not absolution, only a labyrinth of choices with the map torn out. [80] Mara took the warning as an itinerary. [87] She began her journey the next morning, following a classified ad her former self had circled in red ink before the procedure. [109] The ad was not what it seemed, not a job offer but a cipher, and she decoded its true destination by reading between newspaper lines. [134] Between the lines about weather and stock prices, a faint message glowed when she held the paper to the light: FIND THE LAKE BELOW THE MILL. [160] She had dismissed such tricks as paranoid fantasy before, but now she stared with jaded wonder at how carefully her old self had planned for her new self. [188] In her backpack she carried only two things: a thermos and a baseball signed by a time traveler. [206] The ball was delicately robust, its leather scuffed soft at the seams but its stitching still tight enough to cut a finger. [228] The signature across it read 'You will need this to prove you were here JT 2047' in faded blue ink. [248] She had no idea who JT was, yet the ball felt heavier than it should have, as if weighted with borrowed years. [270] The mill stood abandoned at the edge of town, its wheel still turning slowly in a creek that smelled of rust. [291] Inside, behind a collapsed wall, a narrow shaft dropped straight down into darkness, and cold air breathed up from it like a question. [314] Mara clipped her lamp to her chest and descended, each rung slick with mineral sweat. [329] Fifty feet down the shaft opened into a cavern so vast her light could not find its walls, until the water below answered with its own light. [356] There lay the underground bioluminescent lake, its surface perfectly still and alive with drifting constellations of blue-green fire. [374] She stood at the shore and felt, for the first time since waking after the procedure, something like reverence. [393] It was jaded wonder in its purest form, the awe of someone who had forgotten how to be amazed but remembered the shape of it. [418] At the center of the lake a small stone island rose, and on it sat a man who had not been there a moment before. [443] He was old, delicately robust in the way of desert trees, his skin lined but his eyes clear and unblinking. [463] He held out his hand, and Mara understood that he was waiting for the baseball. [478] She waded into the shallows, the glowing water lapping at her ankles and sending ripples of light up her legs. [498] How long have you been here, she asked. [506] Long enough to watch you make the same choice three times, he said, his voice echoing off the wet stone. [526] This was the fourth loop, and he was tired of watching her leave the lake without learning anything. [544] Mara frowned and said she did not understand, that after the procedure she had been told she would finally be free of repetition. [567] Freedom is not forgetting what you did, he said, it is remembering why you did it and choosing differently. [586] He explained that her dirty past was not the arson itself but the coldness that let her light the match without imagining the families inside. [611] She had come here not to hide but, as her old self had hoped, to kindle empathy where none remains, and the lake was the trial for it. [639] The lake showed you not your reflection but theirs, he said, and Mara looked down to see faces ripple across the glowing water. [662] A child coughing in smoke, a mother clutching a doorway, a fireman kneeling, all of them caught in the amber of her memory. [685] Tears cut through the grime on her face that she had not known was still there, and she clutched the baseball until her knuckles whitened. [710] The old man gently took the ball, pressed it into her palm again, and told her to leave it on the island as a promise. [735] If you return it to the world, you return yourself, he whispered. [747] Mara set the baseball signed by a time traveler on the cold stone, felt its weight leave her, and waded back toward the shore with empty, steady hands. [775]</story>
