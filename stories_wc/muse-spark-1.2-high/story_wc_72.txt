<story>Mara had spent eleven years diving the trench because her grandmother swore a city slept below the silt. [98] Mara approached and the statue spoke without moving, her voice blooming directly inside Mara's mind. [113] I was the gardener, the statue told her, and I have waited for someone who could hear. [130] Mara was incisively kind in the way of people who have been hurt and choose precision over pity. [148] She did not gasp or worship, she simply knelt and asked what it needed. [162] The statue answered that she guarded the city's last seed, kept in a foxglove-stained glass ampoule between her palms. [181] It is not a plant, the gardener said, but a memory of how we lived before we chose certainty over curiosity. [202] For generations we believed the surface was poison, that the sky would burn us, that safety was staying sealed. [221] Mara felt the weight of that certainty and understood her own, that her grandmother had been a liar to make childhood magical. [243] She came here to welcome nuance into a certainty, to learn whether a myth could be true without being literal. [263] The gardener felt that motive and approved, unfolding her telepathic library after revealing the secret of the city's end. [282] We did not drown, she showed Mara, we sealed ourselves in, convinced the ocean was safer than the unknown. [331] Mara wept, not from sadness but from recognition, and the salt of her tears mingled with the sea. [349] You can still choose differently, the gardener whispered, but the ampoule will not open for force. [487] With a whisper the ampoule cracked along its weakness and released not dust but light. [502] The light contained pollen, data, and music, a blueprint for a flower that could filter salt and store air. [521] It spiraled outward, seeding the dead trellises where the polyps caught it and began to glow. [537] The gardener's telepathic voice brightened with something like laughter, and Mara felt the city exhale after centuries of holding breath. [557] You understood, the statue said, that truth is not a lock but a trellis for nuance to climb. [575] Mara nodded, tears buoying her cheeks, and promised to carry a new ampoule to the surface. [591] She would not claim she found Atlantis, she would say she found a garden that needed gardeners. [608] The statue's hands, empty now, slowly opened fully, as if finally allowed to let go. [623] Mara pressed her forehead to the basalt, thanking her without words, mind to mind.
