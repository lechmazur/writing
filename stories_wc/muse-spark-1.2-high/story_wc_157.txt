<story>In the year after thoughts became taxable, Mara learned to think in static. [13] The Revenue Auditors did not read minds so much as they billed them, scanning for spikes of undeclared invention. [32] Most people paid in silence, filing their dreams like receipts. [42] Mara had been a charming rogue scholar before the Tax, the kind who stole library books only to return them annotated. [63] Now she curated whispers in an entomologist's greenhouse on the edge of District Nine, where the glass sweat and the beetles hummed louder than the drones. [89] The greenhouse belonged to Dr. Ellin, who had died and left behind a forest of milkweed and a ledger of insect dreams. [111] It was the perfect cover for the secret language club Mara had founded, because nobody audited photosynthesis. [128] There were six of them: a tram driver, a soup vendor, a child who never spoke, two former linguists, and Mara herself. [150] They met at dusk and spoke candidly cryptic, a dialect they invented to hide meaning inside plain talk. [168] If an Auditor asked, a sentence like the marigolds are late this year could mean the checkpoint on Fifth is watched tonight. [190] Mara's motive was not revolution in the grand sense, but something smaller and sharper: to stand guard over your own spark of defiance. [213] She did not want to overthrow the system so much as keep one part of herself untaxed. [230] The Auditors had learned to flag gardens, so the club needed a new method. [244] Mara proposed joining community gardens to plant message patterns across the city, each plot a syllable in their hidden lexicon. [264] A row of beans followed by a gap of carrots meant meet, a spiral of basil meant safe, and sunflowers on the north edge meant run. [290] The city saw beautification, the Auditors saw taxable hobby yields, but the club saw sentences blooming. [306] To initiate a new member, Mara used a gourd rattle seed, heirloom and hollow, that rattled only when shaken at the right rhythm. [329] She would place it in their palm and say this is not a seed, which in their dialect meant do you consent to be unreadable. [354] The child, Lian, took it and shook it three times, perfectly, and was welcomed. [368] For months the converging chaos worked, the gardens spreading like quiet gossip while the Auditors drowned in paperwork. [386] Then the Auditors deployed Lumen Moths, bioengineered listeners that could warp sound and taste the electric charge of a thought before it was spoken. [410] One evening the greenhouse air warped as a moth slipped through a cracked pane, its wings throwing audit light across the basil. [432] Lian froze with the gourd rattle seed in her fist, the rattle suddenly loud as a bell. [449] Mara spoke quickly, candidly cryptic, telling Lian to water the wilted mint, which meant hold still and think of nothing. [469] The moth hovered, confused by the lack of taxable spike, and then latched onto the gourd rattle seed instead, drawn to its dry hollow resonance. [494] Beetles rose from the milkweed in a cloud, their wings thrumming in the frequency the moths hated. [511] The moth shuddered, its light flickering, and Mara smashed it with Ellin's ledger, the old paper exhaling dust and dead latin. [532] Silence returned, except for the converging chaos outside as sirens rose and fell and the community gardens began to signal all at once. [555] Someone had triggered the emergency pattern, sunflowers folding inward citywide, beans spelling distress in every plot they had planted. [574] Mara realized the Auditors were scanning the gardens now, decoding growth the way they once decoded dreams. [591] We need to answer, she said, not with hiding but with abundance. [603] She led the club into the greenhouse's heart, where she poured every remaining gourd rattle seed onto the soil and told them to plant nonsense. [628] They planted spirals that meant nothing, rows that contradicted themselves, beautiful illiterate gardens that would flood the audit with static. [648] The city bloomed into glorious incomprehensibility, a converging chaos that protected every small spark behind its noise.
