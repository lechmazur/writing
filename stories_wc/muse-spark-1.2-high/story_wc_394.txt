<story>Mara was a Pioneering cart glider in a world that had not yet invented roads. [15] She had built her Gears-and-cogs workshop floating on an acid sea because solid ground was too predictable for testing flight. [35] Throughout the evolution of her craft, from wooden sleds to canvas wings stretched over bone, she had chased a single impossible note. [57] Her purpose, plain and stubborn, was To ring a bell no one hears yet. [71] It was the kind of goal that inspired Irreverent awe in anyone who heard it, half laugh and half prayer. [91] She was Steadily curious by nature, the kind of person who took apart clocks just to see if time could be improved. [113] To guide her designs, she had written a Yearning algorithm, a messy, self-editing code that learned not what flight was but what flight wanted to be. [139] The core of the algorithm was housed in a small brass case tipped with a Hunter's arrow tip, a relic from the shore scavengers who swore it never missed. [168] The tip served as an antenna, twitching toward longing the way iron moves toward a magnet. [184] Each morning she would launch her cart from the workshop's high rail, glide over the hissing sea, and let the algorithm taste the wind. [208] The problem was that the algorithm spoke only in numbers, and Mara needed to Transliterate its yearning into wood and wind and muscle. [231] Her solution was embarrassingly analog. [236] She fed the data Through the images in a kaleidoscope bolted to the glider's nose, watching numbers turn into shifting geometries of light. [259] When the sea boiled and threw up yellow steam, the kaleidoscope bloomed with sharp triangles, and she knew to tighten the left wing. [282] When the wind went still and the acid lay flat as glass, it showed soft spirals, telling her to let the cart fall and trust the updraft that was not yet there. [314] Throughout the evolution of the sea itself, which grew more acidic and more restless each year, her workshop learned to flex and breathe like a living thing. [341] The gears sang, the cogs shivered, and the pontoons drank the spray without corroding, because she had taught metal to forgive. [362] Still, something was missing. [366] The Yearning algorithm kept circling a frequency she could not build, a tone so high and clear it existed only as absence. [388] She realized the bell she wanted To ring a bell no one hears yet was not a bell at all but the sound air makes when it finally decides to hold you. [420] On the last day of the storm season, the acid sea turned the color of old brass and the wind stopped as if holding its breath. [446] Mara climbed into her newest cart, lighter than any before, its frame humming with the Hunter's arrow tip at its heart. [467] She was Steadily curious until the very end, checking straps, whispering to the algorithm, grinning at the sea as if it were a worthy opponent. [492] She launched. [494] The cart dropped, caught nothing, dropped again, and then Through the images in a kaleidoscope she saw it, a perfect, expanding circle where light became lift. [520] She needed to Transliterate that circle with her body, leaning not against gravity but with it, as if asking a question politely. [542] The Yearning algorithm shivered and for the first time stopped calculating, because wanting had become having. [558] The cart glider rose. [562] Not fast, not high at first, but steadily, as if the air had finally remembered her name. [579] Below her, the Gears-and-cogs workshop floating on an acid sea rang all its loose bells in sympathy, though no hand had touched them. [602] She laughed, a sound full of Irreverent awe, because the universe had made a loophole just for her and she had slipped through it. [626] Far above the hissing plain, where no one could hear it yet, a clear tone hung in the cold air, the bell she had promised herself. [652] She did not need to land. [658] The Pioneering cart glider had learned that falling and flying are the same motion seen Through the images in a kaleidoscope, only the frame changes. [683]</story>
