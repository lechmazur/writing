<story>Mara had not expected to be awake after the universe contracts, yet the ambered observatory deck held her like an insect in honeyed light. [24] She was the cast lots decision maker for the Arkives, a role she inherited when choice itself became too heavy for consensus algorithms. [47] Her only tools were a collection of foreign coins from dead Earths, each stamped with kings and atoms no longer extant. [68] She was quietly enthusiastic even at the end of everything, a temperament that made solitude bearable and ritual precise. [87] Each morning, if morning could be called the slow dimming of the contracted cosmos, she would distill the overnight coolant into drinking water and listening fluid. [113] The array outside the dome no longer tracked stars, because there were no stars, only the memory of their light trapped in amber. [136] Instead it caught static, endless static bursts that hissed and popped from the cooling void like breath through a broken speaker. [157] Understanding static bursts was the only science left, and Mara believed that within the noise lay a message folded inside itself. [178] The motivation that kept her calibrating the dish each cycle was simple and absolute: to wavelength align with purpose before silence became permanent. [201] On the seven thousandth cycle she found it, a pattern repeating every 89 seconds, too regular for entropy. [219] It was a backwards prophecy, a sentence spoken by the universe before the bounce, now playing in reverse as time unwound. [240] When played forward it predicted its own discovery, and when played backward it described the contraction she had just survived. [260] Mara felt the particular pressure of stellar doubt, not fear that the stars were gone but fear that they had never meant anything to begin with. [286] If the prophecy was true, she was meant to answer it, not merely hear it. [301] The ambered observatory deck preserved more than air; it preserved the last quantum entanglement switch that could broadcast a reply. [321] But the switch required a choice of frequency, and her dish could only be tuned once before its amber sealed capacitors failed. [343] She could not calculate the correct wavelength, and calculation had failed the Arkives before, which was why they created a cast lots decision maker at all. [369] Mara spread her collection of foreign coins on the console, letting the amber light catch their worn edges. [387] A euro with a faded map, a yen with a hole, a Martian sol with a cratered face, each a world that had cast its own lots and lost. [416] She assigned each coin to a possible frequency band, whispering their names like prayers while the static bursts pulsed behind her. [437] Her hands trembled, but her manner remained quietly enthusiastic, as if the act of choosing could itself be a form of hope. [459] She would distill the choice down to a single toss, not from superstition but from the understanding that uncertainty was the only honest map of the new cosmos. [487] Her years of understanding static bursts had taught her that noise and signal were lovers, and only surrender could separate them. [508] She closed her eyes, cast the coins, and let them spin and settle across the ambered observatory deck. [526] One coin rolled off the console and hovered, caught in a flaw of amber where time had thickened and refused to flow. [548] It was the smallest coin, a tin cent from a nation that had drowned before the first launch, and it pointed to the lowest band. [573] Mara laughed, a short bright sound that the amber seemed to hold and soften. [587] She tuned the dish to that low wavelength, feeling for a moment the old stellar doubt loosen its grip on her chest. [609] The switch hummed, the amber crackled, and the backwards prophecy suddenly ran forward through the speakers in a clear human voice. [630] It said: When you hear this, cast again. [638] Mara understood then that purpose was not a single alignment but the willingness to keep choosing, even after the universe contracts and remakes itself. [662] She would have to wavelength align with purpose again and again, each time with less certainty and more love. [681] Outside, the amber light finally began to thin, and for the first time since the contraction, something like a star pricked the void. [704]</story>
