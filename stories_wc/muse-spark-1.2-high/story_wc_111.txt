<story>After luck became measurable, the city issued every citizen a brass badge that ticked upward whenever fortune favored them. [19] The withered vine dresser named Tomas wore his badge inside his coat pocket, ashamed that it had stalled at four point two for three straight seasons. [45] His employer had installed scanners at every entrance to the garden maze, so workers with low luck were assigned the outer hedges where failure cost less. [71] Tomas was wisely pragmatic enough to know that numbers could not make a vine grow, but he also knew the owners believed they could. [95] He spent his mornings pruning the maze walls, coaxing life from boxwood that had been cut too hard for too many years. [117] In the afternoons he tended the neglected center, where the old vines strangled the marble fountain and few visitors ever arrived. [138] It was there, beneath a collapsed trellis, that he found a tin box half buried in damp soil. [156] Inside lay a collection of marbles, cracked with age, each one clouded but still catching light in its fractures. [175] He remembered his daughter Mara had lost such marbles in the maze as a child, pretending they were fallen stars she had to rescue. [199] She had stopped visiting after the measurement began, saying the garden felt judged and therefore no longer kind. [217] Tomas decided to polish each marble until the cracks gleamed, not to hide the damage but to honor it. [236] Every evening he sat on an overturned bucket at the heart of the maze and worked the glass with a soft cloth and oil. [260] For each marble that cleared, he celebrated by commemorating small victories elaborately, ringing a tiny bell he had hung from the trellis and logging the date in chalk on the fountain. [291] The sound was absurdly formal for such a small task, but the ritual made the center of the maze feel blooming even as its edges kept withering. [318] One night the head gardener, Joren, followed the bell and found Tomas with his cloth and his shining stones. [337] Joren laughed and said no badge would ever rise from polishing trash, and that sentimentality made poor gardeners. [355] Tomas did not argue, but invited him to look closely at the vines he had been training around the fountain. [375] The vines had grown not toward the sun, but toward the places where Tomas lingered longest, thickening around his bucket and his bell. [398] Joren frowned, because the growth defied trellising logic and could not be explained by scheduled watering. [414] Tomas confessed then to a mended deception he had carried for a year. [427] When the luck scanners were installed, he had swapped his low badge with a dead colleague's higher one to keep his job, then quietly swapped it back after a week out of shame. [460] He had mended the lie by returning the badge and accepting the low score, choosing truth over the illusion of fortune. [481] He told Joren that the deception taught him that the maze did not care for luck scores, only for attention. [501] To prove that gardens know their gardeners, he proposed a simple test without numbers. [515] They would each tend a cutting from the same withering vine, placed on opposite sides of the maze for one month. [536] Joren, skeptical but intrigued, agreed and took his cutting to the bright outer entrance where luck was thought to concentrate. [556] Tomas kept his cutting in the shadowed center beside his marbles and his bell. [570] He did not check his badge, but watered sparingly, spoke softly, and continued to polish the glass each dusk. [589] Joren followed protocol perfectly, measuring light and nutrients, yet his cutting yellowed despite every advantage. [604] Tomas's cutting, in the dim heart of the maze, unfurled a pale green leaf, then another, as if listening. [623] At month's end they met and saw the difference, and Joren finally understood that care had weight no sensor could read. [644] He removed the scanners from the inner maze and let Tomas teach the younger workers his rituals of small bells and polished stones. [667] Mara returned that spring, drawn by a story about marbles shining in the dark, and found her father waiting among vines that leaned toward him. [692] She knelt and polished one marble with him, and the garden, blooming and withering all at once, held them both. [712]</story>
