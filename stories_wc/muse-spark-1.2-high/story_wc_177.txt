<story>Foresight Hindsight Only had not been born so much as designated, a child raised to read futures in weather and pasts in ruins. [23] She lived in the vine-draped bunker hidden in a rewilded forest, where concrete had softened under moss and birch roots split the old blast doors. [48] The bunker had been built to endure centuries, but its purpose had thinned into a decaying directive humming from a dead command panel. [71] RETAIN HOLD OBSERVE, the panel flickered, though there was nothing left to retain and no one left to report to. [91] When the storm approaches, her elders had taught her, the forest holds its breath and the bunker remembers what it was made for. [114] Tonight the barometer fell like a stone, and the pines beyond the clearing bent low as if listening. [132] Foresight Hindsight Only felt the pressure change in her teeth and knew she had hours before the sky broke. [151] Her task, self-assigned and stubborn, was to discover why some paths choose their walkers, why the forest opened for some feet and tangled for others. [176] The old maps in the bunker showed three trails converging on the bunker, but only one had ever stayed clear. [196] She had walked each of them, and each had walked her differently. [208] On the steel desk lay her inheritance, a tin box containing a single wafer seal, still pale and unbroken. [227] It was not wax but pressed starch and salt, a relic from when orders were sealed against moisture and tampering. [247] The decaying directive had once required every dream report to be sealed with one and filed before dawn. [265] She worked through interpreted dreams, not because she believed they predicted the future, but because they revealed the shape of the walker. [287] The villagers who sometimes traded at the forest edge called her method precisely playful, a game of seriousness that made children laugh and elders uneasy. [312] She would ask a dreamer to describe a crossing, then rearrange the details until the true path emerged like a watermark. [333] Last night she had dreamed herself, of a hallway that kept extending and a door that breathed. [350] In the dream she pressed the wafer seal to the door and it dissolved, showing woods on the other side that were not her woods. [375] She woke with the taste of salt on her tongue and an understanding that the bunker was not shelter but filter. [396] The storm was close enough now that the vines on the bunker shivered each time the wind tried the ridge. [416] Ethereal grit filled the air, that particular forest light before rain where dust hangs like mist yet every pine needle looks cut from glass. [440] She took the wafer seal and walked to the blast doors, where the three paths met under the watch of old warning signs. [463] The first path, beaten hard by habit, smelled of rust and obedience. [475] The second, half-swallowed by brambles, still held the footprints of those who had turned back. [490] The third was barely a path at all, a thinning in the ferns where deer passed, and it pulled at her ankles. [512] To transcend the bunker and its decaying directive, she understood, she would have to let a path choose her rather than choosing it. [535] She knelt where the three met, placed the wafer seal on damp earth, and whispered her dream aloud as the wind rose. [557] Rain began to fall in cold lines, melting the wafer seal into the soil until its circle blurred and vanished. [577] For a breath the forest did not decide, and she felt suspended between foresight and hindsight, belonging to neither. [596] Then the third path, the one no one tended, brightened faintly as rain struck its ferns, and she felt its quiet invitation close around her feet. [622] She stood, no longer precisely playful but fully awake, and stepped forward as thunder cracked the ridge behind her. [641] The storm broke over the bunker, but she was already under the ferns, walking where the path pulled and the forest finally exhaled. [664]</story>
