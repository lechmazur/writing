<story>After fever breaks, Eli could finally hear the wind again through the thin walls of the mountain weather station. [64] She was the station's fault-tolerant oracle, an old system built to keep speaking even when its sensors failed, and Eli had grown used to her soft, correcting voice. [92] You are awake, she said, and your temperature is nominal. [102] Eli sat up slowly, his throat raw from a swallowed truth he had carried for two winters without ever giving it shape. [124] He had come to this peak to find the weight of a swallowed truth, to learn whether guilt or love had been heavier in the choice that cost his sister her life. [231] On the steel table beside his cot lay the single thing he had brought for her, a botanical illustration of alpine gentian his sister had painted the summer before she died. [292] Mara spoke then, not with data but with the gentle redundancy she used when certainty failed, her voice layering predictions over gaps. [369] I told her the pass was safe, he whispered, and the words hung in the cold air like frost. [496] The station creaked in the wind, holding its vigil, and the night breathed around them, sorrowful and luminous and patient. [516] In that thin, shared air between man and oracle, the swallowed truth no longer sank but settled, with a weight he could finally bear to name. [542] Morning would come late and blue over the ridge, and he would be ready to walk in it.
