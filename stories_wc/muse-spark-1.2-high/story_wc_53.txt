<story>After the carousel halts, the kinetic lagoon above the clouds forgets how to breathe. [14] Mara had tended its rim for eleven years, a resolute scavenger who pulled salvage from the slow-moving glass water. [33] The lagoon was not water at all but a suspended field of dense air and light that rippled when touched and held lost things aloft. [58] When the great wind carousel that drove its currents finally stalled, everything within it stilled into a fractured stillness. [77] Tools hung midair. [80] Gulls froze between wingbeats like paper cutouts. [87] Even sound seemed to have weight, falling straight down instead of traveling. [99] Mara was chronologically confused, though she would never use those words for it herself. [113] She remembered her childhood as if it had happened yesterday and yesterday as if it had happened to someone else decades ago. [135] The doctors on the lower stations called it altitude sickness, but Mara knew it was the lagoon keeping all times at once. [157] She had come here young with eroded ambitions already thinning at the edges. [170] Once she meant to chart the unmapped currents between continents, to put her name on a true map. [188] Now she only wanted enough salvage to trade for passage back down, and even that want felt worn smooth. [207] Regret had settled in her chest after she left her brother on the last lift, promising to return before winter. [227] He had waved, she had not looked back, and the carousel had not yet halted then. [243] She told herself she was collecting fragments of overheard stories to map safe paths through the debris, but that was only half true. [266] By collecting fragments of overheard stories from stranded pilots, cloud farmers, and lost children, she stitched together a history she could stand inside. [289] Every story held a detail about the ghost that followed her, though no one else ever saw it. [307] It was not a haunting but a doubled image of herself at nineteen, stubborn and bright-eyed, still believing she would be a cartographer. [330] Mara needed to negotiate with one's own ghost, because the younger version held the only memory of why she had promised to leave and return. [355] If she could make the ghost forgive her, perhaps time would soften and start moving again. [371] In the pocket of a downed weather balloon she found a disposable camera wheel, its plastic yellowed and its film count stuck at twenty seven. [396] She had not seen one since childhood, and the simple ratchet of its advance made her hands remember purpose. [415] She lifted it and turned the wheel, though the lagoon's fractured stillness swallowed the click. [430] Instead of a click, the lagoon showed her an image: her brother's face on the day she left, not angry but puzzled. [452] She turned the wheel again. [457] She saw herself at twelve, tracing maps on the kitchen table while her brother traced her maps with a finger and laughed. [479] Another turn, and she saw the carousel turning above the lagoon before it halted, its vast sails beating the sky into motion. [501] The wheel was not taking pictures but releasing those fragments of overheard stories she had been gathering, each click spending one she had saved. [525] With each advance, the ghost beside her grew clearer, younger no longer but her exact contemporary, tired and resolute. [544] You kept the promise the wrong way, the ghost said, not unkindly, as if finishing a story Mara had been telling incorrectly. [566] You thought returning meant coming back as someone great, but he only needed you to come back at all. [585] Mara felt the regret that had been a stone in her chest crack, not vanish, but become something she could carry and set down. [609] She turned the disposable camera wheel one last time and the lagoon shuddered, the fractured stillness giving way to a single, clean breath of wind. [634] The suspended tools trembled and then drifted, the gulls completed their wingbeats, and far above, the carousel groaned and inched forward. [655] The ghost smiled and was no longer separate, stepping into Mara until there was only one tired, resolute woman holding an empty camera. [678] Mara slipped the wheel into her pack, not as salvage but as proof that some eroded ambitions can be ground down into something like a compass. [704] She began walking toward the lower moorings, where the lifts still ran, each step now in the right order, one after the other. [727]</story>
