<story>The bedraggled prince had not been born to rule, but to maintain the suspended palace anchored to giant stalactites that hung above the chasm like a rusted chandelier. [28] For three generations his family had replaced wonder with gears, turning clouds into steam and birdsong into whistles, until the world below became entirely mechanical. [53] He alone remembered that beauty had once grown wild, and he woke each morning to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world that had forgotten how to bloom. [80] His hands were stained with oil, his robes torn, and his mind drifted in a ceaseless quaking reverie where memory and machinery blurred together. [104] In that state he spent his nights hypothesizing about everything, from why the stalactites wept mineral water to why no bird had flown through the cavern in a hundred years. [134] The palace archives, vast as a cathedral, held one forbidden vault he had never dared to enter until the tremors began. [155] Through the discovery of a hairline crack in the lowest stalactite, water had begun to leak in a rhythm that sounded almost like a heartbeat. [180] He followed the sound down iron catwalks, past sleeping gearworks, until he found the vault door sweating with condensation. [199] Inside, on a pedestal of uncorroded glass, rested the extinct bird's egg, pale blue and veined with faint gold, humming faintly despite centuries of silence. [224] The plaque beneath it read only two words: flawed genesis. [234] His great grandfathers journal nearby explained that the bird had been engineered to sing without end, but its song had been too beautiful and its body too fragile to survive the smog. [266] The egg was left as a warning, a promise that creation without humility would curdle, yet to the prince it looked bitterly sweet, like hope preserved in sorrow. [294] He lifted it, and the palace shuddered, for the massive stalactites groaned under a sudden shift of weight and wind. [314] In that trembling moment he understood his purpose was not to preserve the gears but to assemble something new from their ruins. [336] He carried the egg to his workshop, where half built wings and copper nests lay scattered among blueprints of extinct forests. [357] There he worked for forty nights, soldering feathers from memory, calibrating warmth with stolen sunlight trapped in lenses. [375] He did not simply build an incubator, he composed a lullaby of heat and vibration, hypothesizing again and again until the egg pulsed in time with the dripping stalactites. [404] On the final night, a crack appeared, thin as a hair, and a beak of soft amber pushed through, breathing the stale mechanical air for the first time. [432] The hatchling was blind, featherless, and impossibly loud, its first note shattering a pane of glass and startling the gearworks into silence. [454] The prince wept, not from sadness but from the sheer, overwhelming release of his long quaking reverie into reality. [473] Its song was not perfect, it wavered and broke, for it carried the flaw of its genesis, yet that flaw made it alive in a way no machine could imitate. [503] The stalactites trembled again, but this time not from decay, for the sound resonated through them and loosened centuries of dust into glittering falls. [527] Workers and servants climbed from below, drawn by a music none of them had ever heard, covering their ears and then uncovering them to listen better. [553] The prince held the bird aloft, his tattered cloak billowing, no longer a custodian of rust but a father to something fragile and future bound. [578] He understood at last that beauty did not need to be reborn flawless, only to be remembered and tended through the cracking of a single egg. [604] Outside, the mechanical winds still turned, but beneath them a new, unsteady song was learning how to stay alive. [623] And the suspended palace anchored to giant stalactites, for the first time in an age, did not feel like a tomb but like a nest. [648]</story>
