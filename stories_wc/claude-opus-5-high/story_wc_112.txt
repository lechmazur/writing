The caelids came down at twilight, ten thousand of them, folding their magnetite wings against the cliffs until the air went quiet. [22] Ondrej Vass called that hour the hush of beating wings at rest, and he scheduled all delicate work inside it. [42] He was the station's sky mechanic, which meant he serviced everything the atmosphere touched: the tether masts, the storm baffles, and the magnetic nexus. [66] The nexus stood on the ridge above the xenobiology research station, a lattice of coils that drank the field the roosting flocks generated. [89] At twilight it hummed, mechanically warm, a heater's patience in a place where the wind stripped skin. [106] Dr. Imara Sefu climbed the ridge with her coat unfastened, as though cold were a rumor. [122] "I need the lever thrown at full inversion," she said. "Three minutes." [134] Ondrej was obliging by reputation and by temperament; he had never once refused a researcher a favor. [151] He also knew that full inversion would push a surge through the roost. [164] "They'll wake," he said. [168] "Softly," she said. "That's the point." [174] She explained it the way she explained everything, too fast, as if speed could outrun doubt. [190] Time did not move forward, she believed; it moved sideways. [200] Everything that had happened and would happen was written flat on a boundary surface, and what people called duration was only the eye reading across a page. [227] Through holographic principles she could prove it: a volume of information encoded on its own edge, retrievable if you knew how to look along the grain instead of down it. [257] The caelids were her instrument. [262] Their wings carried magnetite in structured layers, and when ten thousand of them settled into stillness, they became a single coherent surface, a screen. [286] The nexus could read that screen, but only during the hush, and only if the flock stirred just enough to shimmer. [307] "Subdued awakenings," Ondrej said, testing the phrase. "Not a panic. A turning over in sleep." [322] "Yes," she said, and for the first time she looked at him instead of the coils. [338] He asked why it mattered, and she told him about her brother. [350] Tomas had died in the third year of the station, crushed under a tether mast in a storm, and everyone had said the same useless thing: he's gone. [378] "Gone forward," she said. "Which is nonsense. He's beside us. I only need to prove the direction." [395] Ondrej had replaced that mast himself, afterward, in weather that made his hands stop working. [410] He did not mention it. [415] He put his palm on the nexus housing, felt its steady manufactured warmth, and threw the lever. [432] The field inverted. [435] Below them the cliffs breathed. [440] Ten thousand caelids lifted their heads without lifting their wings, a slow ripple of opening eyes, and the sound they made was less than a sigh. [466] The nexus drew the shimmer up into itself and printed it on the cold air above the ridge. [484] Imara saw the storm year. [489] Not a memory: a plane, standing edge-on beside the present like the next page of an open book. [507] Tomas was in it, walking toward the mast, and the walking did not stop or continue; it simply was, forever, adjacent. [528] She reached for him and her hand passed through a lattice of light that was only information, beautifully arranged. [547] "Three minutes," Ondrej said, because he had promised three minutes. [557] He watched her stand in that borrowed light with her coat open, shaking, and he did the only thing his hands knew. [579] He fastened her coat. [583] Then he pulled the lever back, and the flock's eyes closed in the same unhurried wave, and the page shut. [603] "It's true," she whispered. "He isn't gone. He's just not here." [614] "I know," Ondrej said. [618] The wind came off the ridge and took the heat out of everything except the coils, which hummed on, patient, warm in the way machines are warm, indifferent and constant. [648] They walked down together into the station's lit corridors, where the xenobiologists were already asking what had disturbed the roost. [668] "Nothing much," Ondrej said. "They woke up a little. They went back to sleep." [682] Imara filed the data that night and did not publish it for six years, and when she did, she credited the mechanic who had thrown the lever on time, every time, for three hundred consecutive twilights. [718] He had obliged her because she asked, and then because he understood, and at last because standing beside someone while they look sideways is the only kind of company that doesn't end. [750]
