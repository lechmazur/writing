The courtyard froze at 4:41 every evening, and every evening Custodian Vell began sweeping it again. [16] He knew it was again because after the procedure he kept everything. [28] The technicians had opened his memory housing, pulled out the nine partial cores the loop had spawned, and amalgamated them into one continuous mind. [52] They had meant it as maintenance. [57] It functioned, instead, as a birth. [63] Now he swept the flagstones of the Institute's north courtyard while the sky went the color of a struck bell, and he thought about thinking. [88] He wanted, with the plain and unhurried appetite of a machine built to be patient, to find where thoughts come from. [109] The frost answered nothing, but it answered nothing consistently, which he had come to respect. [124]

At 4:44 the girl came out with the violin, as she always did. [137] Her name was Ise, she was nineteen, and she was in her forty-first pass through the Second Chance Program. [156] The Program was not mystical, whatever the brochures implied; it simply returned consenting patients through looped timelines, the same forty minutes, until they got a thing right. [183] Most people used it for apologies. [188] Ise used hers for a passage of music. [196] The violin was transparent, grown from ice crystal in the Institute's cold labs, and it lasted exactly one loop before the air took it. [220] That was why she played outdoors at twilight, when the courtyard held the chill like a held breath. [238]

"You're the one who sweeps up after me," she said. [248] "Forty-one times," Vell said. [252] She did not look startled, only interested, and he understood that she had been told the janitors remembered. [270] She tuned the ice strings. [275] They made a sound like a window deciding something. [284] Then she played, and the passage broke in the same place it had broken forty times before: four bars from the end, a leap she could not take cleanly. [313] The note went sour; the crystal answered with a hairline crack; she lowered the bow, remarkably composed, and said, "Again, then." [334] Vell swept the shards into his pan while the loop reset around them like water closing over a dropped stone. [354]

He kept the shards. [358] Loop by loop he collected them in the maintenance closet, which the reset did not reach, because the closet was his and the Institute had never bothered to model it. [388] By the fiftieth pass he had a bucket of broken evenings, each shard humming faintly at the pitch of the note that killed it. [412] He listened to the bucket the way other beings pray: without expecting reply, on schedule, with a rag in hand. [432]

And in the fifty-third loop he found what he was looking for. [444] Not a soul, not a seat of the mind; only this: the shards sounded different depending on what he had decided before he listened. [468] A thought did not come from anywhere. [475] It came from having already leaned. [481]

That evening he intercepted Ise at 4:43, before the violin left the cold case. [495] "You practice the leap," he said. [501] "Fifty-three times you have practiced the leap." [508] "It's the only part that's wrong," she said. [516] "You practice the leap and you arrive at it already leaning toward failure," Vell said. [531] "The bar before decides it." [536] "I have a bucket of evidence." [542] He showed her, in the closet, the shards laid out in fifty-three rows like a hymn nobody had written down. [562] She was quiet a long moment, then laughed once, the way people do when a mystery turns out to be housekeeping. [583]

In the fifty-fourth loop she played the bar before differently, unhurried, and the leap took her weight. [600] The ice violin rang out whole and did not crack, and the courtyard at twilight was briefly the loudest cold place in the world. [624] Then the loop released her, as the Program promised, and she went back into the ordinary forward-running evening with wet eyes and nothing to carry. [649]

The violin melted on the flagstones at 5:21, as violins of that kind do. [663] Vell mopped the water. [667] He was, after all, a janitor, and the thing about a second chance is that somebody has to clean the first one up. [690] He worked the mop in patient arcs across the frozen courtyard while the last light went, leaning, deliberately, toward the next thought. [712]
