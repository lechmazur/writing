Odell kept his prophecies in chalk because chalk forgives. [9] He was the junkyard prophet of Sable Reach, and everyone on the coast knew the rule: he remembered backward perfectly and forgot forward completely. [33] Tell him what would happen tomorrow and he would lose it by noon; tell him what happened in 1974 and he could name the weather, the tide, the color of your mother's coat. [66] The doctors in Bellport had a phrase for it, precognitively forgetful, which he liked because it sounded like a job. [86] I came back to Sable Reach in October, when the fog comes in like something owed. [102] I found him in the junkyard, sitting inside a gutted station wagon with a piece of plain chalk in his fist, writing on the door panel. [128] FIND THE GARDEN, it said, and under that, ASK INES. [138] "You're Ines," he said, not asking. [144] "I wrote your name before I forgot why." [152]

The garden was my mother's work, and his. [160] Twenty years of salvaged rebar and bicycle wheels and bedspring lace, bent into armatures and planted on the bluff so the fog would snag on them and become, for an hour each morning, shapes. [194] A fog sculpture garden, she called it, the only gallery that empties itself by nine. [209] He could not locate it anymore. [215] He knew it existed because the past told him so; he simply could not hold the route, because routes are future things. [237] "Every morning I start walking and every morning I end up here," he said. [251]

In the wagon's back seat sat a milk crate of videotapes, labeled in my mother's hand, each with a year: 1981, 1988, 1996, 2003. [275] She had recorded her own voice once a year, reading the same tide table aloud, trying to capture the sound of growing older. [298] "You can't hear it in a day," she used to say, "only across twenty." [312] None of them had ever been watched. [319] That was the point, he told me: a watched tape becomes a memory, and memory, for him, was a trap with no exit. [342] He lived amid the hush of old videotapes left unwatched, and that hush was the closest thing he had to a future. [364]

But the crate was one tape short. [371] The last one, 2011, the year she died, she had carried up to the garden to record with the fog around her, and she had never come back down with it. [402] He wanted it found before he forgot he wanted it. [412]

So we made him a map he could not lose. [422] Chalk washes; paper blows; but the beach keeps what you press into it. [435] We spent two days arranging driftwood as directional markers, spine-boards and gray ribs of old hulls, each one angled up the bluff path like a sentence read by the feet. [465] He would not remember deciding to follow them tomorrow, but the wood would be there, saying north, saying up, saying keep going. [487]

On the third morning he walked it alone and found the garden. [499] I met him there at eight, in the hour when the armatures were still wearing their fog: a heron of rebar with a white throat, a woman of bedsprings whose hair kept forming and letting go. [535] The camera bag was under the woman, half sunk, the tape inside it swollen and green. [551] Ruined. [552] He held it a long time. [558] "Good," he said finally, and I understood that he meant it. [569] Unwatched forever now, it stayed a sound he could still be walking toward. [582] Clinging to echoes was not his weakness; it was his only direction. [594]

He took out the chalk and wrote on the flank of the bedspring woman: SHE WAS HERE. [611] The fog would erase it by noon, and he would not remember writing it, and he would write it again tomorrow, having walked the driftwood up. [637]

I bought a recorder in town that afternoon. [645] Once a year now I read the tide table aloud into it, on the bluff, at eight, while the shapes come and go. [668] I do not play them back. [674] I am waiting for the twenty years to pass, so that the sound of growing older will be there whole, the way the sea is there whole, arriving and taking back, arriving and taking back. [709]
