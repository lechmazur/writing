The orchard had not seen a sunrise in eleven years. [10] Orrin Vale kept it that way on purpose, sealing the greenhouse glass with lampblack and lead so the alchemical trees inside would ripen only under lantern and star. [38] Outside, the hush of winter-cracked fields pressed against the panes like a held breath. [52] Inside, mercury pears swung on black boughs, and every one of them was singing. [66] Orrin called the singing resonance. [71] Strike the right note near a ripening fruit and its metals woke, aligning, brightening, giving up their medicine. [89] He had spent a decade searching for the note, and had found instead a bead. [104] It was carved stone, gray as a wasp nest, small enough to hide under his tongue, and it had been turned up by the plow in the field where his grandmother said a bride was buried. [140] When he warmed it in his palm, it hummed. [149] When it hummed, she came. [154] Never for long. [157] She arrived the way a face arrives in a window you are walking past: an ashen veil, a gray hem, a mouth already leaving. [181] Ephemeral cameo appearances, he thought, as though his life were a play she refused to act in. [198] Three seconds at the cistern; two at the grafting bench; once, a whole half minute at the door of the seed vault, long enough for her to point. [226] He dug where she pointed and found the first of the black boughs, dormant, and woke it with the bead. [246] That was how the orchard grew: her hauntings were instructions. [256] The village said he had let the dead ghost him, and they were half right; it was he who had been ghosted, over and over, for eleven years. [284] On the longest night he finally learned the note. [293] He set the bead against a copper tuning fork, struck it, and the greenhouse answered. [308] Every fruit rang at once, and the ringing woke more than metal. [320] It woke her whole. [324] She stood among the boughs in full, veil gone ashen-white with frost, and she did not flicker. [341] "Ilsabet," he said, and the name arrived in his mouth from a life he had never lived. [358] "You remember," she said. [362] "Good, because I have very little time left to be a coward in." [375] She told it plainly, the way one confesses to a priest one has already outlived. [390] Four hundred years ago they were to be married, and four hundred years ago the fever came down off the fields and took the village by the throat. [418] She had been the one with the herbs, the still, the patient hands. [431] "I made you a lovingly venomous cup," she said, "because the fever's ending was worse than mine." [448] "You drank it smiling." [452] "Then I drank the rest." [457] "They buried me in the field with my wedding bead, and buried you anywhere, and I have owed you a lifetime ever since." [480] Orrin looked at the orchard she had spent eleven years pointing him toward, three seconds at a time. [498] Mercury pears for the lungs, wintergold apples for the blood, and in the seed vault the cure for the fever that still slept under the frozen ground, waiting for a thaw. [529] "A debt across lifetimes," he said. [535] "Paid in fruit." [538] "Paid in nights," she corrected. [543] "The sun would have burned me out of this glass the first morning." [556] "You kept it dark for me." [562] He had not known that; he had thought it was the trees. [574] It was, he supposed, both, which is how debts get paid: sideways, and by accident, and completely. [591] The bead had gone quiet in his hand; its resonance had been hers all along, and she was spending the last of it standing still. [616] "Open the windows at dawn," she said. [623] "The seeds need light now." [628] "So do you." [631] He wept, and he was also, absurdly, glad, the two feelings braided so tightly he could not tell which one was making the sound. [655] She kissed him once, and it tasted faintly of bitter almonds and apple skin, a kiss four centuries late and not one degree cooler. [679] Then the note faded out of the glass, and she went with it, the way a held breath goes. [698] At dawn Orrin scraped the lampblack from the eastern panes. [708] Light came over the winter-cracked fields, hushed and enormous, and every tree in the orchard rang once, all together, like a village of bells learning it had been saved. [737] He buried the bead where the plow had found it, and planted a pear tree over it, and left the greenhouse door open behind him. [762]
