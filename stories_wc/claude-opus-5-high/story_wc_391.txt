They called Neve the vigilant horizon mapper, though her only instruments were chalk, a brass ring, and a patience she had not been born with. [25] At twenty she had wanted to burn the ore company's offices; at thirty she had learned to sculpt patience from impatience, the way the valley wind sculpts basalt. [53] The sculpting had taken eleven winters, and it was not finished. [64] Her post was the basalt sanctuary above the lake, six black columns and a roof of slate, built by people whose names had gone out like coals. [91] From there she could see the whole southern rim, and the magnetic viaduct striding across it on iron legs. [110] The viaduct carried the company's ore cradles humming a handspan above their rails, and it never stopped, and because it never stopped, neither did the drivers, nor the oxen yoked to the feeder sledges below. [145]

Neve mapped horizons for the company: sightlines, weather, the exact hour the sun cleared the eastern scarp. [162] She was good at it, softly purposeful, never late, never loud, and so no one wondered why she also tracked moon paths. [184] The viaduct had one weakness, and Neve had spent eleven years measuring it. [197] After rain, the magnetic viaduct sweated; water crept into the coil housings, and the cradles sank until their bellies scraped the rails. [219] Warm rain meant an hour's delay; rain followed by hard frost meant the whole line seized, and the company's engineers needed three days and a furnace train to free it. [249] Rain and frost together came rarely, and never by accident of guessing. [261] But the wet winds off the southern sea came in with the moon's low path, and the killing frosts came with its high one, and by tracking moon paths across the sanctuary's six columns, season after season, Neve had learned to name the night when both would meet. [309]

The other sign was the lake. [315] The frosts that seized the viaduct were the same frosts that closed the water, so the plan had a rule: nothing happens until after the lake freezes over. [343] Eleven years of that rule had made the drivers into something the company could not read: a patient revolution, unarmed, unhurried, wearing its anger like a coat buttoned to the throat. [374]

Young Ferrik had come up to the sanctuary twice that autumn, wanting fire. [387] "One torch," he said, "and the coil houses go." [396] "And they rebuild in a month, and hang nine of us," Neve said, "and the valley learns that we are children." [417] She gave him the chalk and made him draw the moon's track himself, until his hand stopped shaking. [435] That was the work, she thought: not putting the fire out, but teaching it to wait for its own night. [455]

The night came in the second week of the freeze. [465] Rain fell all afternoon, warm and heavy off the southern sea; by dusk the wind swung north and the cold came down like a lid. [490] Below the sanctuary the lake lay white and locked, and out on the southern rim the magnetic viaduct after rain began to groan as its ice grew. [517] At the ninth hour the humming stopped. [524]

Neve lit one lamp in the sanctuary, set it between two columns where every sledge road in the valley could see it, and sat down. [549] Below, in the dark, three hundred drivers stepped to their teams and began to unyoke. [564] It made almost no sound: leather, a buckle, the breath of an ox understanding it was free. [581] They walked the oxen out across the frozen lake, which was the one road the company owned no gate upon, and the ice held them all. [607] Three days, the engineers would need, and by then the valley's whole labor would be standing on the far shore, wanting terms. [629]

Neve stayed at her post, mapping the horizon as she was paid to do. [643] She watched the lamplight run along the lake in a thin bright seam, and she let herself feel, for one measured minute, the thing she had spent eleven years grinding down into a tool. [677] Then she put it away again, marked the hour in chalk, and began the next map. [693] Above the scarp the moon climbed its high winter path, exactly where she had said it would be. [711]
