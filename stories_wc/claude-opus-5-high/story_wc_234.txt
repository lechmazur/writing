The Ember Nexus was a crossing of six forge-roads, and at twilight all six exhaled the same orange breath. [19] Mira Thale worked there because nowhere else in the city held so much unspent heat. [34] She was a pocket-sun alchemist, which meant she sold light small enough to carry and warm enough to matter. [53] Dockhands bought her suns for night shifts; widows bought them for windowsills. [65] Her ledger called this trade honest, and her heart called it insufficient. [77] What she actually wanted was to capture the density of presence: the difference between a room with a friend in it and a room remembering one. [103] Osei found her at the eastern brazier, shaking a vial against the last of the sky. [119] He had been her assistant for four years, which is to say her only real audience. [135] "I'm leaving in the spring," he said, close to her ear, the way you say a thing you have not told anyone. [157] "The Riverworks want me." [161] "Don't congratulate me yet." [165] In the moment that secret passed between them, Mira's hands kept working, and the vial changed color. [182] Not from heat. [185] From attention. [187] She had spent eleven years mixing what she called a palette of resolve, pigments keyed not to wavelength but to the human decision behind a light being lit. [215] Dull ochre for duty. [219] Hard white for spite. [223] A blue she had never once produced on purpose, which her notes labeled "staying." [237] The vial in her hand had gone that blue. [246] "Hold still," she told him, which was ridiculous, and he held still anyway. [259] This was how she worked: directed spontaneity, a plan loose enough to let the world finish it. [276] She reached into her apron for the nautilus shell columella curl she had carried since she was nineteen, the inner spiral of a shell, stripped of its chambers. [304] It was the only object she knew that held its own history as geometry: every place the animal had been, still there, only tighter. [328] She wound the blue into the curl and let the nexus fire take it. [342] The result sat in her palm: a pocket-sun the size of a plum, and inside it, unmistakably, Osei. [360] Not his face. [363] His weight in a room. [368] Osei looked at it for a long time. [376] "That's the most scientifically romantic thing anyone has ever done to me," he said, and then, quieter, "and I don't want it." [398] She had expected gratitude, so the refusal took a moment to fit. [410] "You'd keep me on a shelf," he said. [418] "You'd have me always, and you'd never have to miss me, and missing me is the part that would tell you what I was worth." [443] The nexus dimmed toward full dark; the six roads went from orange to the color of cooling iron. [461] Mira understood, in the way you understand a proof you have been avoiding. [474] Presence is not density. [478] Presence is the pressure of possible absence. [485] To hold a person perfectly is to revalue them at zero. [496] So she did the only honest experiment left. [504] She crushed the plum-sized sun against the brazier lip and unwound the columella curl clean. [519] Then she gave Osei the empty shell. [526] "Spring, then," she said. [530] "And after, come back by a different road each time." [540] He laughed, because the Nexus had six roads and he was a literal man. [554] But he understood the method she was proposing, which was not sentiment but procedure: a person is revalued by returning differently, so that no arrival can be mistaken for the last one. [586] He came back in the autumn by the salt road, and she did not know him for a half second, and that half second was worth more than any sun she had ever sold. [620] She wrote it in the ledger under a new pigment, and left the column for its name blank, because the blue was no longer called staying. [646] He kept the shell in his coat, spiral outward, and it stayed empty, and that was the point. [664]
