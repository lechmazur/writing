They called Wren the mouse of Ward Nine, which was exactly the cover she needed. [15] She was small, quiet, and carefully resourceful, the sort of girl the censors filed under harmless and forgot. [33] That forgetting was her only weapon, and she sharpened it nightly. [44] In the Ministry of Continuity, where she swept floors, histories were revised the way other cities repaired roads. [62] A strike became a festival; a massacre became a weather event; a poet became a clerical error. [79] Nobody protested, because protest required caring, and caring had been quietly taxed out of the population. [95]

Wren's mother had been a splicer for Halcyon Telephone, dead eleven years, company defunct nine. [110] Her inheritance was a brass phone booth key, stamped with a bird and the words SERVICE ACCESS. [127] The booths still stood on eleven corners, glass fogged, coin slots welded, useless to anyone who lacked the key. [146] Inside each, beneath the floor plate, Halcyon's splicers had hidden the city's unrevised record: call logs, names, dates, the true weight of things. [169] Wren's work was simple and slow: steal a page from the Ministry, compare, copy, restore. [184] She told herself she was only holding the line against the tyranny of indifference, one corrected line at a time. [204]

Then the plan unraveled, as plans do, on an ordinary Thursday in the rain. [218] Inspectors sealed six booths before noon and posted a notice declaring Halcyon Telephone had never existed. [234] Her contact, Ovid, was arrested at the tram yard with her ledger in his coat. [249] By dusk she had no route, no ally, no instructions except the ones her mother had left in a nursery rhyme. [270] Walk the water upside down, the rhyme said, and the gold will show you home. [285]

So she knelt at a gutter puddle and looked, not at the street, but at its reflection. [302] In the inverted city, the Ministry's new towers vanished, and older rooflines she had never seen stood against the orange sky. [323] Puddles, it turned out, had never been updated. [331] She moved from puddle to puddle as the light failed, and each one offered a fragment of the true map. [351] A drowned stair here, a bricked arch there, an alley the censors had smoothed away. [366] She carried chalk, a candle stub, wire, and three stolen crackers, because being careful meant being ready to be hungry. [386]

The map led her past the last unsealed booth, where she used the key one final time. [403] The floor plate lifted; the papers were gone; a single line was scratched in the metal beneath. [420] MOVED TO THE LAGOON, it read, and under it, a bird. [431]

The auric lagoon lay past the salt flats, outside every official chart, a shallow basin where mineral silt glowed faintly gold. [452] She reached it near midnight, under starlight so thick the water held two skies. [466] On the shore, forty people waited with crates, lamps hooded, speaking in the old switchboard code Halcyon had trained them in. [487] Clicks and pauses, a language of coded freedom that sounded like nothing and meant everything. [502] They were the splicers' children, and they had been quietly emptying the booths for a year. [518] They had assumed Wren too small to trust, and had not told her. [531]

'You underestimated me,' she said, and held up the key, the only one that still opened Booth Eleven. [549] Booth Eleven held the master index, without which their crates were forty thousand orphan pages. [564] So they listened to the mouse of Ward Nine. [573] She told them the plan: not a newspaper, not a bomb, but a reappearance. [587] Each erased name would reappear where it had lived, chalked on the doorstep, small enough to ignore and impossible to unsee. [608] Indifference could survive an argument; it could not survive a neighbor's name under its own window. [624]

They worked until the gold faded and the stars went out one by one. [638] At dawn Wren waded into the lagoon and dropped the brass key into the glowing silt. [654] The booths were finished; the histories were free; the key had become evidence. [666]

Three weeks later, the Ministry issued a correction, the first in its existence, admitting a small clerical error regarding a poet. [687] It was four lines long and nobody signed it. [696] Wren read it twice, then knelt at a puddle to check the reflection, which now showed the Ministry's towers slightly shorter than before. [719] She tapped two fingers on the wet stone, a click and a pause, and went to work. [736]
