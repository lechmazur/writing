Nerin went barefoot because the stones told her things her boots could not. [13] The repair bay had been abandoned for nine years, its roof peeled back by a hull fire nobody stayed to fight, and through that wound she watched the sky. [42] She watched it with vintage opera glasses, brass and mother-of-pearl, looted from a theater on Kell before the theater became a barracks. [64] They magnified badly and beautifully; stars swam in them like drowned lanterns. [76] The rebellion was in its second season of lull. [85] No sieges, no broadcasts, only the slow rot of waiting: fighters scattered to farm work, codes expired, the Ministry calling it peace and meaning it as forgetting. [112] Nerin had stayed because someone had to keep the beacon's ledger, and because she wanted, stubbornly, to discover meaning in the mess. [134] Twelve thousand dead, three cities freed and lost again, and a sky full of ships that no longer came. [153] Meaning, she believed, was not given; it was measured, like parallax. [164]

Under the repair bay's cracked floor stood the old circle. [174] The engineers who built the spaceport had not removed the nineteen standing stones; they had poured the deck around them, so that grey shoulders of granite rose between the gantries like patient shepherds. [207] Barefoot, she could feel them hum. [213] The hum was not mystical, or not only; the stones sat on a seam of piezoelectric rock, and at certain hours they sang a low, steady current into whatever touched them. [244] The hour was now, in the margin of daylight, when the sun had gone but the air still held its light like water in a cupped hand. [271]

Sokel arrived while she was measuring, climbing through the wound in the roof rather than the open door, which told her everything about him. [295] He was seventeen and awkwardly confident, the way people are when they have memorized a speech they do not yet deserve. [316] "You are the stargazer," he said, and did not wait for an answer. [329] "I have come to make you reignite the beacon." [338]

She lowered the opera glasses. [343] The beacon was the spaceport's old approach laser, a column of coherent light that could be seen from orbit, and nine years ago it had been the signal that the rising was real. [376] "Its cells are dead," she said. [382] "The grid is dead, the cells are dust, and every generator was stripped for scrap in the first winter." [401]

"Then use the stones," Sokel said, as though he had invented them. [413] He had heard a rumor; he had walked six days on it. [425] That was the whole of his plan, and she found she could not despise it, because it was also the arithmetic she had been doing for two years without permitting herself the conclusion. [458] Nineteen stones, one steady hum, a capacitor bank she had rewound by hand, and a laser that needed four seconds of power to speak to orbit. [484]

They worked until the last of the light, clamping copper braid to nineteen granite shoulders while the hum climbed into their teeth. [506] Sokel talked the whole time about the speech he would give when the fleet answered. [521] Nerin let him, because confidence, even borrowed and badly worn, is a kind of fuel, and they were short of fuel. [542]

At the true edge of dusk the capacitors filled, and the bay filled with a sound like a cathedral clearing its throat. [564] Thunderous, and yet Nerin felt only serenity, standing on stone that had outlasted every empire that ever paved it. [583] The beacon caught, stuttered, and then stood up: a white column through the broken roof, straight as a decision. [602]

She raised the opera glasses and watched the beam thin into the dark, and at the top of it, small and certain, three points of light changed course. [630] "Is that them?" Sokel asked, his voice finally the age it was. [642] "It is somebody," she said, and handed him the glasses, because meaning is not a thing you find alone; it is a measurement two people agree on. [669] Below them the stones went on humming, unbothered, having seen fires lit and fires lost and lit again. [687] Nerin curled her toes against the granite and let the current run up through her like an answer. [705]
