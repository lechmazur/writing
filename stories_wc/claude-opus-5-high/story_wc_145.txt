The atlas editor arrived at the outpost with two pencils, one grudge, and no intention of admiring the view. [19] Mira Vahl had spent eleven years correcting other people's rivers, and now three of them had conspired against her. [38] The Sedj, the Kolva, and the Little Anar all claimed the same headwater on plate forty-one, which was impossible, and impossible things did not belong in a national atlas. [67] So she had come north to the rusted outpost by the frozen lake, where the last surveyor to touch those rivers had died of a quiet winter. [94] The outpost was a tin box on stilts, orange with rot, and the lake beyond it lay so flat and white that it seemed less frozen than merely paused. [123] Nothing moved. [125] Mira distrusted the stillness the way she distrusted round numbers. [135]

Ostrow's things were still there, arranged with the tidiness of a man who expected to come back: a kettle, a brass theodolite, and a phrasebook swollen with damp. [163] He had been a polyglot, famously so, able to ask for directions in nine languages and follow them in none. [183] One page of the phrasebook carried a dog-ear, pressed so often that the fold had gone soft as cloth. [202] Mira opened to it and found a single line, repeated in six tongues: Where do the rivers begin? [220] Beneath the last translation, in pencil: Ask the window, not the herders. [232]

She took this for a dying man's joke until the second evening, when she lit the stove and her breath began to bloom on the glass. [258] The condensation did not gather evenly. [263] It beaded around scratches, hundreds of them, hair-fine, invisible dry: Ostrow had scored his survey into the windowpane, and fog made it legible. [286] Mira stood with her cup cooling in her hand and read the water of an entire province out of her own breathing. [308]

The pattern of condensation on the glass showed the three valleys converging, not diverging - and it showed why. [326] The lake was not the mouth of anything. [334] It was the source of all three, a singular convergence held under the ice, one basin bleeding out in three directions through channels no summer boat could find. [362] Plate forty-one was not wrong; it was simply too polite to say so. [375]

The trouble was that Mira could not herald anything from a tin box. [388] Ostrow's falcon carried the mail - a blunt-headed grey bird that flew the ninety miles to the railhead and back, and which had been gone four days when she arrived. [417] So she waited, which was the worst of it. [426] The lake lay tranquil in a way that worried her more each hour, because tranquility on that scale meant pressure somewhere, water going somewhere, and she now knew precisely where. [456] Downstream of the eastern channel, four hundred families had built a town on the assumption that their river came from the mountains and would behave like one. [483] A lake-fed river does not behave like one. [491]

She traced the glass every night, copying the scratches by fog before the stove dried them, and she did not hurry. [512] Hurry made errors, and Ostrow had hurried once, on new ice, in a season when the lake was quietly emptying itself. [533]

On the ninth day the falcon returned, ragged and indignant, and landed on the theodolite as though it owned it. [553] After the falcon returned, Mira allowed herself eleven minutes to be pleased, then wrote three dispatches in a hand so small the bird would not notice the weight. [581] One to her press, correcting plate forty-one. [588] One to the provincial engineer, in plain words, about the eastern channel and the town that trusted it. [606] One to Ostrow's sister, copying the dog-eared line and its answer, because a question asked in six languages deserves a reply in at least one. [631] She was subtly confident about all three, which is to say she did not gloat, and did not need to. [651] The falcon went out over the white lake, and Mira watched it shrink, and behind her the window fogged again, patiently redrawing the place where the rivers began. [679]
