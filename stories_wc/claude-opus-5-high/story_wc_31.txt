On the pier's late shift, Odalys lit the lamps in order, and the order was a sentence only she could read. [21] Seven flames to signify safe water, five to signify a lie she had not finished telling. [37] Tonight she lit five. [41] Below the boardwalk the tide had gone out farther than the almanac allowed, and the town was busy with paper and fire. [63] It was the night of the sky lantern release, when everyone wrote a promise on rice paper and let the heat carry it up. [87] Odalys had never written one. [92] She had gone deaf in her left ear at eleven, in the storm that took the Amaranth down, and ever since she had wanted one thing: to discover the color of sound. [124] Half a sound, she had learned, is a shape without its shadow; she wanted to see what the missing half looked like. [146] The Amaranth lay four hundred yards out, keel up in the mud, and at this tide her aft companionway sat above the waterline like an open mouth. [173] Odalys walked out to her by wandering purposefully, the way she did everything: drifting along the sandbar, stopping to study shells, arriving exactly where she had meant to arrive. [202] Anyone watching would have said she was strolling. [210] She had always hidden behind the truth: she was strolling, and she was also going to the library. [228] The Amaranth had carried the county's lending collection to the islands, and when she went down the books went with her, sealed in the purser's iron cabin. [255] Nobody had come for them in fourteen years. [263] Inside, the air was cold and still, and the ceiling of the forgotten library glowed. [278] The mold that ate the bindings had made phosphorescence of the ink, so every margin note, every inscription, every promise a sailor had ever written to someone on shore burned faint green in the dark. [313] Luminous vows, her mother had called them, back when she still talked about the wreck. [328] Odalys had been coming here since she was fifteen, reading other people's oaths, waiting for one of them to be loud enough to have a color. [354] In her pocket she carried the reason: a brass hotel skeleton key tag, Hotel Meridian, Room 9, hooked to a ring of nothing. [377] Her father had left it on the kitchen table the morning of the storm, and her mother had said, truthfully, that he had gone to work. [403] He had worked on the Amaranth. [409] He had also kept a room ashore. [416] Tonight Odalys went to the shelf she had saved for last, the purser's ledger, and found her father's handwriting in the glow. [438] Not a manifest entry. [442] A vow, written sideways across the last page as the water came in: he was returning the key that night, he wrote, and coming home. [467] She sat with it a long time, the hush around her enormous, the kind of hush that comes when a meteor crosses and everyone forgets to speak. [494] Then, through the hull, she heard the town: a low collective sigh as the first lanterns went up. [512] It arrived in her good ear as sound and in her deaf ear as nothing, and in the nothing, at last, she saw it. [536] The ceiling had answered the sigh. [542] Every luminous vow pulsed brighter at once, a wash of green climbing the beams like breath on cold glass. [561] The color of sound, it turned out, was the color of everything anyone had ever promised and not been able to keep. [583] She waded back along the sandbar while the sky filled with slow orange. [596] On the pier she lit the two lamps she had left dark, and the sentence closed, and it was true. [616] She kept the key tag but bought a lantern from the boy at the rail, and wrote on it the only vow she could guarantee: that she would tell her mother what the glow had said. [652] Seven flames, she thought, watching it rise: safe water. [661] Nothing left to signify but arrival. [667]
