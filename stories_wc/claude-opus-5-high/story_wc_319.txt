Vellum had been the library's guardian for nine hundred winters, and she still slept under the shelves like an animal. [20] She had claws for cutting pages free of ice and teeth for the rats that ate the indexes. [38] What she did not have was a country. [45] The library had swallowed her world, as it swallowed everything, by the old method: interiorizing exteriors. [61] Carry a lamp into a place, stand inside its circle until dawn, and the place becomes a room. [79] Walls grow where the light stopped. [85] That is the whole trick of the boundaries of light: whatever the lamp touches, the library keeps. [102]

Beyond the last door lay the frozen garden in mist. [112] It had drifted in from her lost dimension the way a seed drifts, arriving one morning against the threshold: black hedges furred with rime, a sundial, nine fig trees dead in their pots. [145] She knew the sundial's chip. [150] She had made it, as a child, with a thrown stone. [161]

Every night for a year she had carried the lamp to the threshold and failed to step out. [179] Interiorize the garden and she would have her home forever, indoors, catalogued, safe, and utterly finished. [195] Leave it outside and the mist might take it back, and take with it the last road home. [213] So she did the third thing, which was to wait. [223]

In the reading room she kept a cultist's pendant on a nail above her nest of blankets. [240] It belonged to a man who had come forty years ago to steal an atlas of unmade places. [258] She had cornered him among the stacks, and instead of fighting he had sat down and talked until dawn. [277] His order worshipped the door that is never described, he said, and their discipline was simple: to make friends with uncertainty. [298] He left the pendant as payment for the atlas and walked into the mist, and the mist did not give him back. [320] The pendant was cold brass, a ring with nothing in the middle. [332]

Being dimensionally homesick is not like ordinary grief. [340] Ordinary grief knows where the grave is. [347] Hers was a compass needle spinning in a room with no north, and it spun hardest at night, through the veil of sleep. [370] That was when the cultist came to her, as he had come every winter since he vanished. [387] Dreams in the library had a foggy lucidity to them; you could not see far, but everything within arm's reach was painfully exact. [410] He sat on the frost-white sundial, wearing the same wet coat. [421]

"You are still holding the lamp," he observed. [429]

"If I set it down, the garden goes back to being weather," she said. [443]

"If you lift it, the garden goes back to being furniture." [454] He turned the dial's shadow with one finger. [462] "There is a third position. Hold it at the edge." [472]

She woke with her jaw aching and the pendant already in her fist. [485] Outside, the mist had thinned enough to show the hedges and no farther. [498] Vellum walked to the threshold, knelt, and set the lamp down exactly on the doorstone, so that half its circle fell inside the library and half fell on frozen grass. [528] She stayed there while the night went out. [536]

At dawn the walls tried to grow and could not decide where. [548] What the library made instead was a doorway with no door: an arch of shelving that ended in weather, its books dusted with real snow. [573] The garden was neither kept nor lost. [580] It was a room that could still change its mind. [590]

Vellum hung the cultist's pendant on the arch, where its empty ring framed the mist. [605] She did not stop being homesick. [611] But she found she could sit in the doorway with her tail curled over her feet, half-lit, patient as a hinge, and call the not-knowing a companion rather than a wound. [642]

Some mornings the fig trees were there. [649] Some mornings there was only fog, and a smell of figs, which was its own kind of proof. [667] She waited, and the waiting was no longer a punishment. [677] It was the shape of hospitality. [683]
