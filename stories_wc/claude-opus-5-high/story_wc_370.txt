The invitation had been in Ansel's coat pocket so long it had gone soft as cloth, its creases gray with handling. [21] He smoothed it on the night desk of the Municipal Reading Room and read it for the four-hundredth time. [40] "Midnight, the third Thursday; bring the error." [47] It was eleven fifty-one. [51]

Nobody ever looked for the Order of the Nested Leaf, because the Order had never bothered to hide. [69] Its library was the public one: forty thousand volumes on open shelves, free to any citizen with a card, and among them, catalogued honestly, the eleven hundred books that mattered. [99] You found them by knowing the shelfmarks; you knew the shelfmarks by being told; and the telling was the only door. [120]

Ansel had been the one who told people, once. [129] Now he lived in two rooms above a laundry and spoke mostly to books, because people came into him too easily. [150] A stranger's grief would sit down in his chest and stay a week. [163] He had learned to love the world from a distance, the way one warms a room by leaving the stove door open in the next one. [189]

The error was thirty years old and four words long. [200] As a young copyist he had transcribed the Order's founding maxim from a water-damaged page, and where the original read "Stay, or leave," his pen had written "Stay while leaving." [230] Three hundred copies went out before anyone compared them to the source. [242] By then the mistake had grown roots. [249] Members had built a practice on it: to remain inside a life while releasing your grip on it, to serve a town you had already given away, to keep the door of yourself open both directions at once. [287] The elders called it corruption and struck his name from the roll; the members kept living by it anyway. [306]

He had taken the night custodian's post the next week, dusting shelves he was forbidden to interpret. [323] Thirty years of staying while leaving, which was either penance or proof, and he had never been able to decide which. [344]

The invitation had come in March from Lorne Aday, who had been a boy when Ansel was cast out and was now the Order's archivist. [369] Ansel had carried it nine months without answering, because answering meant being seen. [382]

At eleven fifty-four he put down his cloth and went to the northeast corner, where the shelves made a blind angle behind the atlas cases. [407] It was the only place in the building a man could hide, and he had used it for thirty years to watch the Order meet without joining them. [435]

Tonight there were eleven of them at the long table, and Lorne Aday was reading aloud from the corrupted copy. [455] "We have used a wrong sentence for three decades," Lorne said, "and it has made us kinder than the right one did." [477] "I move that we stop calling it an error and start asking what it knows." [492]

That was the whole reason for the invitation: not forgiveness, which Ansel did not want, but examination. [509] They wanted to unearth the wisdom in his mistake, and they could not do it without the man who had made it. [531]

Ansel stood in the blind angle with the crumpled paper in his fist and felt the room the way he always felt rooms. [554] Lorne's fear of being laughed at; the old bookbinder's arthritis; a young woman's held breath. [569] Usually such things drove him out into the street. [578] Tonight they arrived and simply stayed, nested one inside another like the paradoxes on the shelves, and there was room for all of them. [602] Something in him had gotten larger instead of thinner. [611]

The clock's minute hand came within arm's reach of midnight. [621] He understood, standing there, that his sentence had not been a mistake he made but a mistake he was: a man who had loved people by standing outside their light, and had thereby kept it burning. [657] The wisdom was not in the words. [664] It was in the thirty years. [670]

He stepped out from behind the atlas cases. [678] Eleven faces turned, and he did not flinch, because he was not entering; he was simply admitting he had never left. [699] "I brought the error," he said, and laid the crumpled invitation on the table, face up, beside the corrupted copy. [719] Midnight went by underneath them like water under a bridge, and nobody moved to mark it. [735] Lorne pulled out the chair that had been empty for thirty years, and Ansel sat down in the enormous quiet, at last close enough to be of use. [763]
