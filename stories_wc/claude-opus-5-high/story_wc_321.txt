Ilo had lived aboard the commuter ferry Marigold for nine years, which made him, as the captain liked to say, a permanent transient. [23] He slept in a bunk under the wheelhouse, ate at the galley counter, and loved the boat with the ardent, unreasonable devotion of a man who had chosen a thing that never stayed still. [57] The 5:40 crossing was ordinary until the clocks began running sideways. [68] Ilo noticed it first in the galley clock: the hands no longer swept downward but drifted horizontally, east to west, as if time had decided to travel rather than pass. [98] Then the fog closed, the engines went quiet, and the Marigold slid into a grotto that had no business existing in a harbor six fathoms deep. [124] The walls were pale and translucent, and above them, impossibly, hung starlight -- whole constellations burning through stone. [141] Spectral shapes moved along the ledges: other deckhands, other passengers, other Marigolds, all faint as breath on glass. [159] Forty-one commuters looked up from their phones and discovered they had no signal, no shore, and no hour. [177] Ilo, who was resiliently positive in the way that annoyed pessimists and saved lives, put on the kettle. [195] Then he went to the radio. [201] Static, mostly -- but inside the static there were rhythms, a hiss that rose and fell in threes, then fives, then threes again. [223] He had heard that pattern before: it was the wake interval the Marigold made rounding the north mole, the one he had felt in his knees twice a day for nine years. [255] An old woman in a conductor's coat came up beside him holding an empty hourglass. [270] No sand, no dust, only two clear bulbs and a throat of glass between them. [285] "I am Vesna," she said, "and I have been riding the 5:40 for forty years, and I built this grotto to merge the timelines." [309] She explained it plainly: every crossing spawned a slightly different voyage, and she had spent decades learning to fold them together via interconnected patterns -- tide cycles, engine harmonics, the turning of the constellations overhead. [343] The hourglass was her instrument and her proof; it held nothing because, in the merged place, no grain needed to fall. [364] Her husband had drowned on one crossing and survived on another, and she wanted a world where both were true. [384] "Nobody leaves," she said, almost kindly, "and everyone you ever were stays with you." [398] Ilo looked at the ledges and understood the vanishing: the missing ferries of harbor legend were all here, moored in a permanence they had never asked for. [425] He thought about the forty-one commuters with dinners to reheat and children at daycare. [439] He did not argue with her, because arguing was slow and he was, above all, a deckhand: he fixed things with his hands. [462] Patterns worked both ways, he reasoned; if she had woven the timelines together out of rhythms, a rhythm could unpick them. [483] So he keyed the radio and began transmitting the only sequence he knew by heart: the Marigold's docking signal, three short, two long, three short, the north-mole wake. [511] He sent it in time with the water slapping the hull, and then with the pulse of the starlight, matching pattern to pattern until the grotto began to hum. [540] The spectral crews on the ledges heard a working boat calling home and, one by one, took up the rhythm -- bells, boots, palms on railings. [565] A merged thing cannot hold if its parts insist on leaving. [576] The walls thinned. [579] Vesna's hourglass filled -- not with sand, but with light, running sideways and then, finally, down. [594] She wept, and Ilo held her arm while the fog let go. [606] "Ride tomorrow's 5:40 with me," he said, "same boat, different water -- the closest anyone gets to having both." [624] The Marigold docked at 6:02, twenty-two minutes late, which the harbor office logged as engine trouble. [640] Ilo hosed the deck, coiled the lines, and hung the empty hourglass in the wheelhouse, where it stayed empty again by morning. [662] He liked it that way. [667] It meant the next crossing had not happened yet. [676]
