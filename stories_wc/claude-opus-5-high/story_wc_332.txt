The library on Sarn opened at three in the morning and closed when the sun cleared the water. [18] Ilo had kept those odd hours for nine years, because the archipelago's fishermen needed charts before they sailed, and because the dark suited a man who had stopped expecting company. [48] On the islands they said the wind spoke through the basalt flutes of the cliffs; the charts called them the Whispering Archipelago, and the whispering was real enough at four a.m. [79] It was in that hush of improbable beginnings, when nothing had happened yet and anything still might, that Ilo found the watercolor palette in the returns slot. [106] Bel had used it for eleven summers, painting the same horizon from the same bench, and three weeks ago she had taken the mail boat out and not come back. [136] The note taped inside the lid said only: you always wanted the key, so here is the lock. [154] The tin held twenty-four wells, and Bel had scraped every printed name away and re-mixed the pigments herself, arranging them in an order that was not the order of any spectrum Ilo knew. [187] He understood, after a week of staring, that it was a cipher of longing: each well was a morning she had loved, laid down in sequence, and the sequence was a sentence. [219] Bel had never painted objects. [224] She painted with light; that was her phrase when tourists asked, and she meant it literally, chasing the exact minute the sun struck the water, mixing until the paper held that minute. [256] So Ilo bought a secondhand camera and began matching the wells to the sky. [270] Through sunrise photography he could measure what no eye could hold: he shot the same eastern water every morning, and compared each frame's color against the tin under the reading lamp. [301] A well of bruised violet matched the fourth of October, when fog sat low over Kett Island. [318] A thin acid green matched a morning after storms, when the shallows over the reef turned the light upward. [337] He learned the archipelago's twenty-four moods and wrote each date beside each well, and when he read the dates in order they traced a course: island to island, north up the chain, ending at Var, the last rock before open ocean. [378] Bel had been rehearsing a departure for eleven years. [387] She had told him once about a runaway dream, the kind that leaves before you can write it down, and how she woke at four each morning to try to catch it. [419] Ilo, who had spent nine years shelving other people's voyages, had assumed she was speaking of paint. [436] He took the mail boat north in November. [444] Var held a lighthouse, a goat, and a keeper's cottage with a table by the window, and on the table a stack of paper weighted with a stone. [472] Every sheet was a sunrise, unfinished, because she had run out of the colors she had mixed on Sarn. [491] Bel was not there. [495] The keeper said a woman had wintered over and gone on in September, west, on a freighter, and had left word that anyone who came looking would know what to do. [526] Ilo sat at her table until the window went gray, and then pale, and then gold. [542] The room filled with a luminous absence, the shape of her cut out of the light. [558] He understood that the cipher had never been a map to Bel. [570] It was an instruction: look, every morning, at the thing that will not stay. [584] He set the camera on the sill, opened the palette, and began to vitalize the unfinished sheets, one by one, with the colors he had learned from his own photographs of a sky she had never seen. [621] Outside, the cliffs whispered, and the sun came up over water that had no other side. [637] He would keep the odd hours, he decided, and the library too; but every dawn from now on would be his own, and he would spend it painting with light. [667] The dream had run; that was its nature. [675] Capturing it, he finally saw, meant beginning again each morning, in that hush where nothing had happened yet. [693]
