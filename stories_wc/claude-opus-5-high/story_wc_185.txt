The energy beings had been singing for nine days when Sela climbed down into the harmonic caves with her hammer and her maps. [23] She was the ripple-mark mapkeeper, the last of four, and her work had always been quiet work. [40] Before the arrival, she had charted the frozen ripples in the cave walls, the old water-marks that told where meltwater had run a thousand summers ago. [66] Now the walls rang. [70] Every chamber in the range had begun to hum in sympathy with the visitors, who hovered above the glacier like gently luminous jellyfish, patient and enormous and utterly deaf to pleading. [101] They meant no harm; that was the worst of it. [111] Their voices simply were, and the caves took those voices and multiplied them until the village above could not sleep, could not think, could not hear a child crying in the next room. [144] Old Bregan had walked onto the ice to escape the sound and had not walked back. [160] Sela's mother had taught her that a mapkeeper's calm was hammered, not born: struck into shape, cooled, struck again. [179] She held that repose now like a tool in her fist. [190] The archives said there had once been a way to conjure a bloom of hush, a spreading flower of silence that could open inside a mountain and hold. [218] It required the echo chamber stone, and the stone required a tuning, and no one living knew the tuning. [237] Sela had the stone in her pack: a fist of grey basalt, hollowed, no heavier than bread. [254] Strike it wrong and it swallowed one note; strike it right and it could swallow a season of noise. [273] The tuning was the problem, and the ice was the answer, though it took her four days to understand that. [293] Mapkeepers read ripples; ripples were only pressure written down. [302] So was a crack. [306] Sela spent those days on her belly on the glacier, examining crack patterns in the ice where the beings' song had gone into it. [330] Every frequency fractured ice in its own signature: the low tones in long feathered splits, the high ones in rosettes fine as frost on a window. [356] She traced them, copied them, and built from them a map of the visitors' voice, chord by chord. [374] Her ears bled twice. [378] She packed them with wool and kept drawing. [386] Now, in the deepest chamber, where the storm of sound was a physical pressure against her teeth, she laid the map on the floor and set the echo chamber stone at its center. [419] The chamber's walls were her own ripple-marks, charted in her girlhood, curving like the inside of an ear. [437] She had always suspected the old mapkeepers had not been surveyors at all, but instrument makers. [453] She lifted her hammer. [457] Eleven strikes, the map said. [462] Eleven strikes, in the intervals the cracks had taught her, each one an answer to something the beings were saying. [482] Not a refusal. [485] A reply. [487] She struck. [489] The first blow did nothing she could hear. [497] The fourth made the hum stumble. [503] By the seventh the stone had begun to glow, gently luminous itself now, the same cold color as the things in the sky, and Sela understood that the visitors were listening. [534] On the eleventh strike the hush bloomed. [541] It opened outward from the stone in slow petals, and everywhere a petal passed, sound simply stopped being repeated. [560] The song did not end; the caves stopped answering it. [570] Sela sat in the middle of it with her hammer across her knees, and heard her own breath for the first time in nine days. [595] That was all she had wanted, from the first night of the singing: not to silence the visitors, only to find the quiet in the storm. [621] Above her, the village would be sleeping. [628] The bloom would last a season, maybe two; the ice would keep cracking, and the cracks would keep writing down what the visitors said. [652] Sela was already thinking of the next map. [660] She would need an apprentice, someone with steady hands and a mother's patience, someone who could be taught that calm is a thing you make and remake under the hammer. [690] In the silence she had conjured, she rolled up her charts, slid the warm stone into her pack, and began the long climb toward the light. [716]
