The terrace had been old when the war was young. [10] Three hundred summers of siege had chipped its balustrades, and weeds grew from the cracks like green stitches holding the stone together. [32] Iyera Sold thought it was the most beautiful ruin she had ever been asked to save. [48] She arrived at midsummer with an ebony clipboard under her arm and forty-one clauses she had written herself. [66] The clipboard was older than the clauses. [73] It had been her mother's, and her mother had brokered nothing but grain prices, but the wood took ink-stains like scars and refused to warp. [98] Iyera had learned to be the same. [105]

By law of the old rite, a treaty ending a forever war could only bind during the slow dance of celestial bodies, when the red wanderer crossed the face of the summer star. [138] That crossing would last nine minutes. [144] Words spoken outside those nine minutes were only words; words spoken inside them were, by every tradition both armies still believed in, unbreakable. [167] Iyera did not believe in the stars. [174] She believed in what men would do because of them, which was a harder and more useful faith. [192]

Her task that afternoon was to muster eleven delegates who had spent their lives learning to kill one another's cousins. [212] She did it the way she did everything, operatively gentle: tea poured with her own hands, a chair angled toward the view, a question asked so softly that refusing it felt like cruelty. [245] Gentleness was not her nature; it was her instrument, honed the way a sapper hones a blade. [262] By the eighth hour she had them seated. [270] Then the Coil's Highmarshal laid one finger on her clipboard. [280]

"Add a clause," said Highmarshal Veth. [286] "The Ashfall of Mere Hollow. Your name. Your hand on the order." [298] "Nine thousand dead in one night, and the rite requires the guilty to be spoken aloud, or the stars hear nothing." [319]

It was true, and it was also a trap, and it was also, Iyera admitted, simple justice. [336] She had been twenty-six and certain, and the wind had carried the ash forty miles. [351] Every treaty she had drafted since was an apology written in the only language that mattered, which was procedure. [370] She had told herself for nineteen years that ideals meant nothing until they were anchored in practice: in customs posts, in prisoner registries, in who repaired which bridge. [398] Now the anchor wanted her body for ballast. [406]

"Speak it aloud in the nine minutes," Veth said, "and the Coil signs. Refuse, and we go home and the war is four hundred years old." [432]

Iyera's own generals understood immediately what the clause cost. [441] A confession inside the rite was a conviction; the Coil could demand her extradition, and her own Ascendancy would grant it to keep the grain routes open. [468] They told her to stall. [473] She poured Veth more tea instead, and asked him the name of his youngest niece. [488] He would not give it, which told her he had one. [499]

The light went bronze, then the colour of a healing bruise, and the astronomers on the terrace's western steps began to call the approach. [523] Iyera wrote clause forty-two herself, in her own hand, on the last clean page. [537] She added one line beneath it that Veth had not asked for: that the guilty party would serve her sentence at Mere Hollow, rebuilding it, for as many years as she had left. [570] Redemption, she had decided long ago, was not a feeling you were granted but a bill you paid in instalments, in mortar and timber and the patience of people who hated you. [602]

The red wanderer touched the summer star. [609] In the nine minutes she read all forty-two clauses aloud, fast and clear, her voice breaking only once, at the number nine thousand. [632] Veth signed with a soldier's blunt strokes and did not look at her. [645] When the crossing ended the delegates sat in the dark not knowing what to do with their hands. [663]

Iyera set the ebony clipboard on the balustrade, in a crack where a weed had made its home, and left it there. [685] She would not need it where she was going; the work there would be done with a trowel. [703] Veth caught her at the stair and said, "Sarrin. My niece is called Sarrin." [717] It was, Iyera thought, the first clause of the peace, and the only one not written down. [734]
