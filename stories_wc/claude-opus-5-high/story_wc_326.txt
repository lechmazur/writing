The state had buried me on paper three winters ago, a clerk's slipped digit turning Ilse Varga, living, into Ilse Varga, deceased. [22] Since that morning I have counted my heartbeats, every one, a tally against the paperwork. [37] By the night the snow closed the road to the opal observatory, I was at two hundred nineteen million, give or take the hours I slept badly. [64] The dome is milk-white stone shot through with blue and orange fire, and under a meter of snow it glows like a lamp buried in wool. [90] I came for my sister's failures. [96] Nadja had spent eleven years here trying to fly a solar sail on nothing but sunlight and stubbornness, and she had failed eleven times, and then she had died of a stroke at thirty-nine while the instruments were still recording. [136] My own launch window opens in April, my own sail folded in a crate in Riga, and I did not want to repeat a single one of her eleven mistakes. [166] She had left me a way to ask. [174] In her last year Nadja had fed everything she was into the observatory's old array: her logs, her voice, her handwriting, her sixteen notebooks of what-went-wrong. [200] She called it her ghost and laughed about it. [209] The array is thirty years old and its memory is a landfill, and a ghost scattered across ten thousand fragments cannot speak in sentences. [233] It has to be defragmented first, pulled together piece by piece, and that takes the whole clean bandwidth of the mountain link. [255] The link only comes clear at midnight, when the ionosphere settles and the radio static finally clears off the relay. [275] So I waited, thumb on the porcelain shard in my coat pocket, counting. [288] The shard came from the cup Nadja threw at the wall after the eighth failure, the only time I ever saw her give up for an evening. [315] She swept up the pieces herself at two in the morning and kept one on her desk as a reminder that the world breaks and the work continues. [343] At 11:58 the static thinned to a hiss, then to nothing, and the dome went very quiet under its snow. [363] I started the defragment and listened to the drives work like something breathing. [376] Nadja had never trusted screens for the important things. [385] Her interface was a flatbed plotter she had rebuilt to weep ink onto a glass tray, so that the array answered by spilling and the answer lived in the pattern before it ran together. [419] You read it fast or you lose it; every word was a thing that existed for nine seconds. [437] The first spill, at 12:14, made only a smeared lattice I could not parse. [451] The second, twenty minutes later, resolved into her cramped capitals: WHO IS COUNTING. [464] I said my name aloud to a room full of instruments, and added the number, because it is the only proof I carry that the certificate is wrong. [492] Ink bloomed: ILSE. STOP COUNTING. START WITH FAILURE FOUR. [501] Then, in the last legible seconds before the tray flooded, the numbers: boom mass, hinge tolerance, the exact angle at which her sail had creased and torn in vacuum. [530] I copied for three hours. [535] Eleven failures came out of that tray in order, each one spilled and gone, each one wisdom that had cost her a year. [558] Near four in the morning the spills stopped making words and began making something like a signature, over and over, fading. [579] The array had given me everything it had been holding together, and defragmented or not, a ghost is only as durable as its last good sector. [605] I did not restart it. [610] I sat under the opal dome while the snow settled and the ink dried into a black continent on the glass, and for the first time in three winters I lost count. [642] It did not frighten me the way I expected. [651] I put the porcelain shard on the desk where she used to keep it, squared to the edge, and I went to bed at dawn with eleven years of somebody else's mistakes in my notebook and a launch in April. [691] The state thinks I am dead and my sister is not alive, and still, somehow, between midnight and morning, the two of us got the work done. [718] That is a kind of company, and it was enough.
