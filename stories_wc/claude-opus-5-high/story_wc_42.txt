The tremor came at moonrise, and the observatory's floor rang like a struck bell. [14] We stood above the clouds at the Selene Lunar Observatory, where the sea of vapor below hid the world and made the sky the only country. [40] When the foundations shake, Director Palo liked to say, you learn what you actually believe in. [56] I believed in the plate camera, and the tremor had broken it. [68] Its shutter stuttered; its mirror had slipped a hair out of true; and in the clock room the sidereal hand had begun to spin. [92] It would not stop. [96] Round and round it went, a thin brass finger devouring hours, mocking the one thing an observatory requires: time that is changelessly constant. [119] In eleven hours the Moon would pass before Zeta Tauri, an occultation we had waited four years to photograph, and we could not even say what second it was. [148] So we sent the cable car down through the cloud for Ilse Kovar. [161] She repaired cameras in a shop at the foot of the mountain, and she was old enough that the mountain seemed young beside her. [185] Her eyes had gone milky years ago. [192] She worked by ear. [196] Palo met her at the door with apologies and a list; she waved both away and asked for silence. [215] From her coat she took a dry maple leaf, held it at shoulder height in the dome's great hollow, and let it fall. [238] We heard nothing. [241] She heard the room. [245] "Your mirror sits four arcminutes low and the shutter spring is cracked at the third coil," she said, and she was right about both. [269] By the sound of a leaf falling she could map a room the way we mapped craters. [286] She worked through the afternoon while the cloud sea turned copper beneath us. [299] She did not repair the shutter so much as restructure it, taking the cracked spring out entirely and rebuilding the mechanism around a counterweight and a length of piano wire. [329] "Springs remember being bent," she said. [335] "Weight only remembers falling." [339] At dusk I brought her to the spinning hand, expecting her to stop it. [353] She laid two fingers on the case, listened, and laughed softly. [364] "The escapement is fine," she said. [370] "The mountain is still moving." [375] "Your clock is telling the truth about a thing you would rather not measure." [389] I thought of the pendulum drift, the aftershocks running under us like slow surf, and understood that the spinning hand was a seismograph we had never meant to build. [418] We let it spin, and timed the occultation by radio pips from the coast instead. [433] Near midnight the Moon came up hard and white over the cloud, and Ilse asked me to describe it. [452] I told her about the ashen seas, the terminator's serrated edge, the way the light seemed to ache. [470] "Luminous," she said. [473] "Everything leaving is luminous." [477] She told me her husband had built this dome's first camera; she told me the Moon recedes from us by the width of a thumbnail each year. [504] Not fast enough to reach escape velocity, never fast enough to be free of us, and never again as close as it was this minute. [529] "That is why we make photographs," she said. [537] "Not to keep things." [541] "To harness the ephemeral long enough to look at it." [551] At twelve forty-one the star touched the lunar limb and vanished. [562] Her counterweight fell; the new shutter opened and closed with a sound like a leaf landing. [578] The plate held it: four seconds of a star going out behind a world that is always leaving. [596] Under our feet the mountain shuddered again, and in the clock room the brass hand went round and round, faithful to the only thing it could still report. [624] Ilse went down the mountain at dawn. [631] I keep the plate in the dark, and I take it out when I need proof that something ephemeral once agreed to hold still for us. [657]
