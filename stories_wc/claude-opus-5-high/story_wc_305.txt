The grotto hung above the clouds like a held breath, and its shadow had the hush of a vineyard at dusk. [21] Mira had been its caretaker for sixty-one years, and in that time she had learned every sound the place could make: the drip, the vine-creak, the sigh of souls turning over in their sleep. [55] Grapes grew here in the umbral cool, without sun, dark and small, fed by a spring nobody had ever traced. [75] The work was simple and endless. [80] Each soul arrived as a seed no bigger than a grape pip, and Mira threw a bowl for it from grey grotto clay, then scalloped the rim with her thumbnail, wave after wave, so the soul would wake remembering that an ending can have a pleasant shape. [127] When the bowl dried, the soul slept in it a season, and then she tipped it over the ledge into the cloud, and it went down to be born. [156] She was kindly, and she was decisive; the two were the same muscle in her, and it had never failed her until the night the pip came with a name she knew. [188] Bram. [189] Sixty-two years ago he had drowned her sister Enna in the millrace over a debt of nine coins, and Mira had climbed the mountain out of grief and stayed to tend the dead because she could not bear the living. [229] Last winter, old and blind and rattling, he had come up the path to ask for nothing, and she had forgiven him anyway, out loud, twice, until he believed it. [259] He died on the ledge with his head in her lap. [270] Forgiving the unforgivable had not made her content, which was the thing that troubled her now. [286] She had expected contentment to arrive like weather, and instead she felt only the quiet of a job finished, and the same old hunger to know where the quiet came from. [317] So she did what she had always done when a thought was too large for the floor of the grotto: she climbed. [339] The fig tree grew crooked from a crack in the grotto's roof, half in shadow, half in the blazing white above the clouds, and Mira went up it hand over hand, slow, sixty-one years' practice in her knees. [377] Height had always sorted her thinking; on the ground a problem was a wall, and from a branch it was a map. [399] In her pocket rode Enna's hairpin, antique even then, silver worked into a wren with one garnet eye, the only thing the millrace had given back. [425] She had carried it for six decades the way other people carry a grudge, which is to say: daily, and without deciding to. [448] From the high fork she could see the whole grotto laid out beneath her, terraces of vine, and the spring. [468] She had never seen its source from below, because from below it was hidden behind the midden where she stacked the emptied bowls. [491] From the tree it was plain: the water rose out of the bowls themselves, sixty-one years of them, every scalloped rim beading and running, feeding the vines that fed the shade that kept the souls asleep. [527] The source of contentment was downstream of the work, not upstream of it. [539] There was nothing to find; there was only the next bowl. [550] Mira laughed once, a dry sound in the vineyard hush, and came down. [563] She threw Bram's bowl that evening and did not hurry the rim. [575] Scallop after scallop, she pressed the clay with the flat of the wren's silver body, so his ending carried the shape of Enna's name without ever being told it. [604] Then she set the hairpin in the bowl beside the seed. [615] It was hers to keep and hers to spend, and she had decided. [628] When the season turned she carried the bowl to the ledge, where the cloud lay flat and gold. [646] 'Go on,' she said, kindly, and tipped it. [654] The seed went down to be a child somewhere, in some valley of vines, and the hairpin went with it to be found in the dirt by that child's small hand. [685] The empty bowl she set on the midden, where it began, almost at once, to weep its bright thread into the spring. [707] Mira washed her hands in it, and drank, and went to look at the clay. [722] Another pip was already waiting in the dark, nameless, patient, turning in its sleep. [736] She rolled up her sleeves. [741]
