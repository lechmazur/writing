The egg cracked at midnight, and Mira set down her ledger of seeds. [13] For eleven winters she had waited for that sound. [22] Her grandmother's rule had been exact: the spiral may be reopened only after the egg hatches. [38] The atrium stood icebound around her, its glass panes furred with frost, its dead orange trees holding up chandeliers of ice. [59] Above the shattered dome, starlight came down clean and cold, the only light the world had left to give. [78] Mira was an heirloom seed saver, the last of four generations who had kept the old varieties alive in clay jars and paper folds. [102] Glass Gem corn, Cherokee beans, a tomato named after a drowned town. [114] She had also inherited the vortex. [120] It was not a place but a habit of the blood: a slow funnel her family could open in the air, a spinning throat that gathered heat and order out of the surrounding chill. [154] Her great-grandmother had used it to ripen wheat in a famine year. [166] Her grandmother had used it once, to hold a fever back from a child, and had never spoken of the price. [187] The vortex worked by decreasing entropy in a small sphere, and the universe, being an honest bookkeeper, took the difference from elsewhere. [209] It took it from the user's own remaining hours. [218] That was the inheritance: order now, borrowed time later, and no ledger to tell you the balance. [235] In the seed vault behind her, the snowfinch chick shivered out of its shell, wet and furious and alive. [254] Its mother had died in the frost, and Mira had carried the egg against her ribs for three weeks. [273] The hatching was the sign her grandmother had promised: proof that something in the world still wanted to begin. [292] From her coat pocket Mira drew an oak acorn cap, small as a thimble, polished by four generations of thumbs. [312] It was the family's only instrument. [317] You filled it with seed, set it on the floor, and the vortex would gather around whatever the cap held, no more and no less. [342] Her grandmother had called it a mercy, that the cap was so small. [355] Mira filled it with white runner beans, the last of that line, nine seeds that no other jar on earth still carried. [377] They had gone dormant past waking; eleven winters had sealed them shut. [389] To reopen a seed is not gardening. [396] It is argument with physics. [401] She knelt on the frozen tiles and set the cap down in the center of the atrium, under the open dome, under the stars. [425] Then she breathed the way her grandmother had taught her, and felt herself become furiously tranquil - a stillness with a hurricane inside it, a calm that cost everything to hold. [455] The air above the acorn cap began to turn. [463] Frost lifted from the tiles in a slow helix. [472] Warmth came from nowhere and everywhere, molecules choosing order the way water chooses downhill. [486] Inside the spinning column the nine beans swelled, split, and pushed out pale radicles, groping for soil that had been dead since before the long winter. [512] Mira felt the cost arrive. [517] It was not pain. [521] It was a thinning, as if some years had been shaved off the far end of her and spent here, and she understood at last why she had really waited eleven winters. [553] Not out of fear. [557] She had wanted to discover the texture of borrowed time - to feel what it was made of when she finally spent it. [579] It was smooth, she found, and warm, and faintly sweet, like the inside of a bean. [595] It felt like giving, not losing. [601] When the helix collapsed, nine seedlings stood in a ring of thawed earth, and the atrium smelled of green for the first time in a decade. [627] Mira sat back on her heels, laughing with a wild serenity she had no words for, older by some unknown number of years and entirely unafraid. [653] The snowfinch chick cried from the vault, demanding the world. [663] She picked up the oak acorn cap, dried it on her sleeve, and put it back in her pocket for whoever came next. [686] Then she wrote in her ledger, under the date: nine white runners, woken; one finch, hatched; cost unrecorded, paid gladly. [706] Above her the stars wheeled their own slow vortex, patient bookkeepers, and Mira went to find something for the bird to eat. [728]
