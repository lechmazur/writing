Rain came to Qasr Silt once a decade, and it came the night the commander stopped sleeping. [17] By dawn the fortress smelled of wet clay and rust, its sandstone drinking so fast the walls ticked like cooling iron. [38] Ammar, who cooked for the garrison, took a lamp and went down. [50] Beneath the dunes lay the coral icebound pavilion, a chamber of fossil reef the old sea had left behind, kept so cold by its own depth that frost furred the branches. [81] In its alcoves stood the jars of stored remorse. [90] Every soldier who could not carry a thing alone brought it here, wept it into brine, and sealed it under wax. [111] Ammar's work was to cook it back into them, gentled. [121] He had been born tasting memory the way other cooks taste salt. [133] A spoonful of someone's grief told him its year, its weather, the color of the door it happened behind. [152] His talent had a cost the garrison understood only as a rule: do not touch the chef with bare hands. [172] Remorse was emotionally contagious through touch, and Ammar held too much of it to be safe skin. [189] He wore linen gloves boiled each morning, and he ate alone. [200] The rain had done something rare. [205] Water had found the old reef and pooled in its cups, and Ammar crouched a long while analyzing tidal pool formations that had not filled since before the fortress had a name. [237] He read them like recipes. [242] Where the water lay still, silt settled in rings; where it swirled, the grit stayed suspended and scoured the coral smooth. [263] That was his whole method, and had been for eleven years: to sandpaper rough memories, you did not remove them, you kept them turning in slow water until the edges wore down. [295] Broth did it, and time, and patience. [302] The commander's jar sat apart, on the highest shelf, unclaimed for three years. [315] She had never told anyone what was in it. [324] She had simply stopped eating at the long table, stopped removing her own gloves, and begun walking the ramparts until the sky went gray. [348] Now the rain had cracked her open, and she came down the stairs behind him with her lamp shaking. [367] "Cook it," she said. [371] "I keep dreaming the well raid and I have never once said it aloud." [385] He took the jar down with tongs. [392] The wax broke like a knuckle. [398] Ammar tasted a single bead of the brine on the back of his gloved finger, through the cloth, which was all he ever allowed himself. [423] It was not the raid. [428] It was the order she had given afterward, to seal the cistern while a child was still inside it, because forty men were dying of thirst above. [455] The taste was sturdy and brittle at once, like a rusted hinge that still holds a door. [472] He built the broth over three hours: reef water, bone, bitter melon, the long slow turning he had learned from the pools. [494] But the memory would not wear. [500] It kept its edge no matter how he stirred, because she had never spoken it, and a thing unspoken has no surface for the water to catch. [527] There was one other way, and he had sworn off it twice. [539] A taster could subsume a memory outright, take it in whole and let it live in him instead. [557] "Tell me the dream, all of it," he said, "and then give me your hand." [572] She talked until the lamp guttered, and at the close of that unspoken dream she pulled off her glove and laid her palm in his bare one. [599] The cistern came into him like cold water into a boot. [610] He held it, and held it, and did not let go until her hand had gone warm and slack and stupid with relief. [633] In the morning the commander ate at the long table with the garrison, ungloved, laughing at something a corporal said. [653] Ammar carried a new jar down to the pavilion and set it on the highest shelf. [669] He spoke the child's name aloud to the frost-furred coral, so that the memory would have a surface, so that the slow water would have something to catch. [697] Then he went up to cook breakfast, gloves boiled, edges turning, waiting for the rain that would come in ten years. [718]
