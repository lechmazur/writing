The Marigold Opera House closed on a Tuesday, and by Friday the auctioneers had stripped it down to its echoes. [20] Ilse Verrand stayed after the business closed, because she had nowhere else her strange gift made sense. [37] Synesthetically blessed since childhood, she heard chords as colors: B-flat was wet slate, a sung vowel could be apricot. [56] In the ruined auditorium she heard only grey. [64] So she went out back, past the loading dock, into the hedge maze the founders had planted in 1898. [83] The maze took nine wrong turns and one humming note to solve; she had learned years ago to follow the sound that tasted like plums. [108] At its center lay the mystic orchard garden, twelve quince trees in a ring, their fruit glowing faintly like held breath. [129] Rosalind Kite was waiting, as she had waited for ninety years. [140] The ghost wore a beaded gown the color of nothing at all, and when she spoke, Ilse saw carnelian. [159] "They are selling the building," Rosalind said, "and I have not had my encore." [173] She had died mid-aria in 1934, one breath from the final note, and the applause had never come. [191] The orchard remembered, though, because the orchard kept a fractured timeline in its roots. [205] Every quince held a different night of the Marigold: 1902, 1934, 1961, a Tuesday that had not happened yet. [224] The shards did not touch; that was the trouble. [233] Rosalind's last aria lived in one fruit and her audience in another, and no ghost could reach across the gap alone. [254] "You can intertwine them," the ghost said. "Your ears braid what mine only hear." [268] From her glove Rosalind produced a hand-painted tarot coin, brass, its face bearing the Fool in flaking gold leaf. [287] A stagehand had painted it for her the week she debuted, promising it would return whatever was thrown away. [306] "Strike it against the trees," Rosalind said. "The coin works by producing echoes." [319] Ilse struck the first quince branch and heard 1902 ring out, plum-purple, a chorus of shipwrights laughing in the gallery. [339] The echo hung in the air like a rope of colored smoke. [351] She struck the second, and 1961 answered in copper: a boy's cough, a violin tuning, someone's mother weeping at the interval. [372] Nostalgia came up through the soles of her feet, warm and unbearable, and she understood what the garden was for. [392] It had been planted to lure possibility from the brink of despair - to keep one good night alive when all the others died. [415] Ilse had come to the orchard the night her own voice failed her, eleven years back, and left able to bear the morning. [438] She owed the trees a debt she had never named. [448] Tonight the debt had a face and a name and one unfinished song. [461] So she went around the ring, striking each branch, catching every echo by its color and knotting it to the next. [482] Slate to apricot, apricot to carnelian, carnelian to the green of a hall that has just gone quiet. [500] The braid held. [503] Ninety years of Marigold audiences stood together in the orchard, half-transparent, shoulder to shoulder across decades that had never met. [523] Rosalind stepped into the ring of quince trees and finished her aria. [535] The last note was silver, and it did not waver. [545] The applause, when it came, shook fruit from the branches. [555] Somewhere beneath it, Ilse heard the Marigold itself exhale, a sound like dust settling. [569] Ilse clapped until her palms stung, and she was weeping, and she did not know for which of them. [588] When the sound faded, Rosalind was a thinning shape holding out the tarot coin. [602] "The maze will let you back in," she said, "as long as somebody still needs a last night to hold onto." [623] Then she was gone, and the orchard smelled of quince and rosin. [635] In the morning the auctioneers found the hedge maze impassable, grown shut overnight, and marked the land unsellable. [653] Ilse kept the coin in her coat pocket, where it sang faintly, the color of a curtain about to rise. [673]
