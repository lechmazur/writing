Ilen had not slept in eleven years, which was why the shell trusted her with its nights. [17] The gargantuan shell lay on its side across the reef, a spiral of nacre big enough to ensconce two feuding cities in its whorls, and it sang whenever the tide pushed air through its chambers. [52] It sang badly. [55] The wind that lived inside the shell was off-key by centuries of habit, half a tone flat in the low chambers, sharp in the high, and the Marrowside and the Lanternside had gone to war over what that wind meant. [95] Ilen thought it meant nothing, which was the most dangerous opinion on the reef. [109]

She kept one possession that was not a poem: a galaxy postcard, warped and salt-furred, that had drifted down through the shell's mouth generations before her birth. [136] On its face swam a spiral of white fire, labeled in a dead surface language; on its back, in faded ink, someone had written, Wish you were here. [164] Ilen had spent her sleepless nights deciding that the galaxy was only a bigger shell, and that somebody out there was also awake, also listening to a badly tuned wind. [194] That was the thought she meant to turn into a weapon. [205]

Every hundred and nine years the reef corals spawned at once, and the water filled with pink snow, and both cities put down their spears for eight nights. [233] They called it the blossom armistice, and it had always been a pause, never a peace -- a held breath across centuries of longing, after which everyone resumed killing with the flowers still in their hair. [268] Ilen intended, shyly, to make this one permanent. [276]

She was not brave. [280] She stammered at market, apologized to doorways, and had never once read a poem aloud to a crowd. [298] But sleeplessness had made her patient in the way water is patient, and everyone who tried to stop her discovered, too late, that she was shyly unstoppable. [325]

Her method was small and took nine years. [333] Coral folk like Ilen carried green in their skin; they fed on light, and the old teaching said a poet's true work was to photosynthesize pain into beauty, to take the grief the reef produced in such abundance and turn it into something that could feed a child. [381] Ilen took the teaching literally. [385] Each night she climbed to the apex chamber, where the shell narrowed to a whisper, and she ensconced herself in the nacre curl with her postcard propped against the wall. [415] There she collected the names of the dead from both sides -- she had asked every widow on the reef, blushing, apologizing, refusing to leave -- and she set those names into half-sung lullabies, pitched deliberately flat in the low chambers and sharp in the high, so that the off-key wind would carry them true. [468] The shell's flaw became her instrument. [473] Every night, in both cities, mothers heard a song drifting from the walls that contained their own lost names braided with the enemy's, and could not tell where one grief ended and the other began. [508] Children learned the lullabies before they learned which side they were on. [520]

When the spawning came, the pink snow rose, and the eight nights of the blossom armistice began with the usual grim courtesy. [542] On the ninth morning, the generals went to the shell's mouth to take up their spears, and found them garlanded by children who were singing, off-key, the names of everyone the spears had ever taken. [577] You cannot kill a man whose mother's name your daughter sings to sleep. [590] It is not that anyone forgave. [596] It is that the arithmetic had quietly changed. [604]

They found Ilen in the apex chamber that evening, asleep for the first time in twenty years, her cheek against the galaxy postcard. [627] She woke to an argument about what to call the new season and laughed until she had to sit down. [647] Nobody on the reef had ever heard her laugh. [656] That is the joy of shadowed places, reef-joy: it arrives sideways, in green half-light, tasting of salt and survival, and it is never innocent. [680]

Later she mended the postcard's corners and hung it in the shell's mouth where the wind could see it. [699] Wish you were here, it said, to whatever sleepless thing was listening out there in the bigger shell. [717] Ilen kept writing the lullabies anyway, flat in the low chambers, sharp in the high, because an armistice is only a held breath, and somebody has to keep singing until it becomes ordinary air. [751]
