The dead call at twilight, when the light goes thin enough to let them through, and it is my job to trace who they touched. [25] I am a contact tracer for hearts, Bureau of Unfinished Attachments, badge number forty-one. [39] We do not stop the calls; we follow them, and we assure the living that what they heard was real. [59] That word matters more than people think, because an unassured grief goes looking for someone to sell it comfort. [78] My instrument is a decades-old hearing aid, beige as a bandage, that belonged to my grandmother Rosalia. [95] Analog devices keep faint pulses the way a room keeps smoke, and by tracking those pulses I can tell whose voice came through, how long it lingered, and which ear it went hunting for. [129] Rosalia went deaf slowly and died lonely, and I believe I do this work because nobody traced the calls she must have made. [152]

On Thursday the pulses led me to the Verrey Home for the Aged, to its glass atrium, mist-clad at twilight because the heating pipes sweat in October. [179] The ferns dripped. [182] A piano nobody played stood with its lid down under a reeling hush, the kind of quiet that seems to be still recovering from a noise you missed. [210] Teodora Balan waited for me in a wheelchair with her hands folded over a photograph. [225] It was the last photograph ever taken of her husband Emil: a bus station, a raincoat, a paper cup of coffee raised toward whoever held the camera. [252] "He called on Tuesday, and nobody here believes me," she said. [263]

I held the hearing aid near her right ear and read the residue. [276] Emil's pulse was there, thirty-one seconds of it, thin and warm and unmistakably his own, and I told her so. [296] She cried the way people cry when they have been calling themselves crazy for three days. [312]

That should have been the whole file. [318] But I am persistently curious, which my supervisor writes on my reviews as a flaw, and the device kept catching a second pulse under Emil's, off-rhythm, mechanical, repeating. [346] The dead do not repeat themselves. [351]

I walked the atrium with the hearing aid cupped in my palm like a compass, through the mist, past the dripping ferns, until the pulse sharpened beside the piano. [380] Taped under the bench, inside a hollow where the sheet music should go, sat a recorder no bigger than a soap bar, its light blinking. [405] It had been listening for weeks. [411]

Later the police explained what I had already guessed in the wet twilight: a voice-cloning ring, harvesting the speech of the old and the lonely, then phoning their children from the mouths of their parents to ask for money. [450] Forty-eight residents had been recorded. [455] The night nurse who planted the recorders had chosen Verrey because half the hall wore hearing aids and would never notice a hum. [478]

I want to be honest: I was not there to catch anyone. [490] I was there to assure one woman about thirty-one seconds of her husband. [503] The heroism was incidental, a side effect of not letting a strange rhythm go unexplained, and I have stopped apologizing for that. [525]

The Bureau gave me a commendation and a new caseload. [535] I kept the assignment anyway, because the two jobs turned out to be one job, which is to protect vulnerable voices: the ones coming in from wherever Emil was calling, and the ones going out from rooms where nobody checks under the piano bench. [579]

Teodora died in February. [583] She left me the last photograph, Emil with his coffee cup raised, and on the back in a shaking hand: "You believed me first." [607] I keep it in the case with Rosalia's hearing aid, the two of them together, evidence and instrument. [625]

At twilight I go where the pulses go. [633] Sometimes it is only a husband saying goodbye thirty years late. [644] Sometimes, underneath, something is blinking in the dark, and somebody has to be curious enough to kneel down and look. [664]
