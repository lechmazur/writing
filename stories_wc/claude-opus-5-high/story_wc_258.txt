After the cryptocurrency crashes, the fortune-tellers get busy. [8] I learned this in my grandmother's rain-beaded skylight loft, taking coats from men who had lost eight figures and wanted someone to tell them why. [33] Grandmother Vesna was an onyx-ink cartomancer, and she refused every one of them. [46] She had drawn her own deck at nineteen, ninety cards inked in black onyx dust ground into linseed oil, and she would not read for anyone who asked about money. [76] "The cards are not a clock," she said. [84] "They are a census." [88] That was the whole of her teaching, and she died with the rest of it inside her, leaving me the loft, the deck, and a tailor's measuring tape coiled in the card box like a sleeping snake. [125]

I am steadily curious rather than brilliant, which is a slower engine but it runs all night. [142] So I began reverse engineering her. [148] I photographed every card, catalogued every figure, and noticed what nobody else had: the images repeated at intervals, and the intervals were measured. [171]

The measuring tape explained itself in the second week. [180] Vesna had not laid her spreads in the usual rows; she had placed the cards at precise distances, forty-one centimeters, then thirteen, then eighty-nine, and the distances were part of the sentence. [212] A reading was a map. [217] Under the skylight, with the rain beading and sliding and beading again, I taped the tape to the floor and began to work out what territory she had been mapping. [247]

It was us. [250] Not futures, but patterns. [254] Her ninety cards were categories of human behavior, and her spacings were the recorded gaps between a rumor and a riot, a fashion and its funeral, a promise and its price. [285] My grandmother had spent sixty years building an instrument to map cultural patterns, and she had disguised it as superstition so that people would keep telling her the truth. [314] A client will confide to a cartomancer what he would never admit to a survey. [329] That was the data collection. [334]

The mechanism took me longer. [339] Vesna had not read by drawing one card and declaring it fate; she had worked by multiplying paths, dealing out every branch a situation allowed, then measuring which branches crowded together. [370] Where the paths bunched, the future was dense, and dense things fall. [382]

I tried it on the crash. [388] I dealt the 2019 spread the way she would have, ninety cards fanned into four hundred branches across the floorboards, tape stretched from the Fool of Coins to the Drowned Chorus: ninety-one centimeters. [421] The same distance she had written in her 1997 notebook, and the same distance in the 1929 chart her own grandmother had left her. [445] The mystery was inherited, and so was the answer: manias do not vary, only their vocabulary does. [462] In 1929 the cards for it were railways and radio; in 2021 they were tokens and coins that existed only as arithmetic. [484] The spacing never moved. [488]

I understood, finally, why she had refused the ruined men at my door. [501] They had come for absolution, and the deck had none; it only recorded, patiently, that they had behaved exactly as their great-grandfathers behaved, and that someone had written the interval down in advance. [534]

Last night a young woman climbed my stairs with rain in her hair and a phone full of losses. [553] I did not tell her what would happen to her money. [564] I laid the tape along the floor, dealt her every branch I could see, and asked her to tell me, honestly, what she had believed in April, and what everyone around her had believed. [598] She talked for two hours while the skylight beaded over. [608] I wrote down the distances. [613]

The deck is ninety-one cards now. [619] I drew the newest one myself, in onyx ink, and named it the Ledger of Air. [635] Someone in 2093 will deal it, measure it, and know exactly how far we stood from the edge. [653]
