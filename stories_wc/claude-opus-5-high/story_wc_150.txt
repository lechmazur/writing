The bus broke down twice before Adela reached the village, which felt, she decided, like an omen written in the local dialect of delay. [24] Her grandparents had left this valley sixty years ago on a journey that was never finished, only abandoned somewhere between a port and a paperwork office. [50] Adela had come to finish something, though she could not yet say what. [63] The dig was already underway when she arrived: a tomb under the orchard, opened one careful centimeter a day. [82] They called the place the catacomb orchard, and it deserved the name. [94] Fig and quince trees grew from the old burial terraces, their roots snaking between bones like patient fingers through a drawer of spoons. [117] Nobody harvested the fruit anymore. [121] The village had stopped believing in the orchard's saint around the same time it stopped believing that anyone who left would return. [143] Adela was warmly curious about all of it, which the diggers found suspicious and then, by the second week, useful. [163] She asked questions no archaeologist bothered with: who swept these steps, who sang here, who was in the middle of arriving when the road closed. [188]

On the ninth day of the excavation, a trowel struck stone that was not tomb. [203] It was a sundial base, a drum of limestone with a socket at its center where the gnomon had been snapped away. [225] Around the rim, worn but legible, ran a ring of hour marks and, between them, small carved sandals. [243] The site director shrugged; Roman, reused, out of context, not her problem. [255] Adela knelt beside it for an hour with the cautious delight of someone handling a sleeping animal. [272]

The old woman who brought the diggers bread explained the sandals. [283] Pilgrims used to stop here, she said, on the long walk to the coast shrine. [298] They read the hour, ate a fig, and left a sandal-mark scratched into the stone to mark the journey they had interrupted and promised to resume. [324] Most never did, the old woman added, but the dial kept their time anyway. [338]

That was when Adela decided to rebuild it. [346] She had no plan, no permit, and no gnomon, so she did the only thing the valley seemed to permit: she wandered, on purpose. [370] Each morning before the dig resumed she walked a different terrace, asking after old iron. [385] The wandering was its own excavation. [391] A goatherd showed her a boundary stone with the same sandal mark. [403] A widow in the upper hamlet sold her a bent iron rod that had held a vine for forty years and, before that, nobody remembered what. [429] A retired schoolteacher, delighted, calculated the valley's latitude for her on the back of a seed packet. [446] By the time the tomb's lid came off, half the village knew what she was doing, and several had opinions about the angle. [469]

The tomb held a woman of about forty, buried with a walking staff and a sealed jar of coins from three countries. [491] A pilgrim, then, who had reached this orchard and gone no further. [503] Adela stood at the lip of the trench for a long time, thinking of her grandmother's suitcase, still packed, still in a hallway in another country. [529] She had grown up among people who spoke of return the way other people speak of heaven: constantly, and without directions. [550] Faith, she thought, is just the belief that an interrupted journey is still a journey. [565]

They set the sundial base upright at the orchard's edge on the last day of the excavation. [582] The schoolteacher checked the angle twice. [588] The goatherd tamped the earth. [593] The widow's rod, filed and set in lead, threw a thin clean shadow across the eleventh hour, and the whole crowd made a small sound, half laugh, half something older. [623] Nobody called it a miracle. [628] Nobody quite called it nothing, either. [634]

Adela scratched a sandal into the rim with a nail, small, beside the others. [648] Then she went down into the orchard, where the roots held the bones like a hand around a coin, and picked a fig, and ate it. [674] It was the first one taken from that ground in sixty years, and it was very good. [691] She would leave in the spring, she thought, and come back, and leave again. [705] The dial would keep her time either way. [713]
