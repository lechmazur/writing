The observatory hung above the cloud deck on a spine of black rock, and for nineteen years Yara Sendt was its only tenant. [23] Lightning walked the ridges every evening like a landlord checking the locks. [35] She had stopped naming the days after the goat died, so every evening was Tuesday. [50] She had been sent up to catalogue starlight; she stayed to build a dawn. [64] The dawn required ancient shadows, and those she had in abundance. [75] Beneath the dome, cut into the mountain, stood the cobalt glass portico: twelve panes salvaged from a drowned temple, each one holding the smoked silhouette of something that had stood in front of it two thousand years ago. [113] Priests, or kings, or ordinary farmers - the glass did not say. [124] It only said: here is a shape, and shapes are what people follow. [137]

Yara's instrument was a salvaged shortwave rig held together with solder and superstition. [150] She could not broadcast a sermon; the storms ate whole sentences. [161] So she learned to speak in shards, seeding the valley through fragmented radio frequencies, a phrase here at four in the morning, a half-line there between weather bulletins. [189] She made them lyrically prophetic on purpose - 'the lamp that eats its own wick', 'a country with two hearts and one throat' - because static forgives poetry and murders prose. [218]

Someone down there was listening. [222] His name was Beno Arkady, a cannery clerk with a tin ear for music and a perfect ear for God. [242] He wrote her fragments on cigarette papers for six years, arranged them, and began to unfold them in the market square as one continuous revelation. [267]

Yara watched his rise the only way she could: as changes in the noise. [281] Year one, the pirate stations mocked him. [288] Year three, they quoted him. [293] Year five, the state broadcaster hired him a choir, and the militias painted her cobalt shapes on their trucks, and Yara understood that she had lost control of her own vocabulary. [324] This was the flaw she had never modeled: variant allegiance. [334] A shape means whatever the follower needs; a prophecy loyal to everyone is loyal to no one. [351] Beno preached her new dawn to grain co-ops and to gun-runners in the same week, and both believed him, and both were right. [374]

In the nineteenth year he climbed to her. [382] He arrived through the storm in a good coat and bad boots, forty-one years old, with the calm of a man who has been agreed with too often. [410] She fed him lentils and let him stand in the portico while the lightning fired the panes blue. [428]

'You are the voice,' he said, not quite a question. [438]

'I am the antenna,' Yara said. [444] 'The voice is two thousand years dead and cannot be subpoenaed.' [455]

He laughed, and for a moment they were simply two lonely people at altitude. [469] Then he asked her to come down and confirm him, to stand beside him on a balcony and let the shadows be his. [492] She thought about it honestly for eleven minutes, which was longer than she had thought about anything in a decade. [512] Then she said no, because a dawn with an owner is just a lamp. [526]

At sunrise she showed him the trick. [533] The clouds below opened, light came in level through the cobalt glass, and the twelve ancient silhouettes threw themselves enormous across the valley, six miles wide, cast on vapor, unreadable and undeniable. [566] Every faction would see its own founder in them by noon. [577]

'That is my sermon,' she said. [583] 'It cannot be owned, and it cannot be obeyed, and it will outlast every man who tries.' [600]

Beno Arkady went down the mountain and preached for nine more years, and his church split into four churches, and all four kept the blue shapes. [626] Yara stayed above the clouds, mending solder, broadcasting her broken lines into the weather. [640] She had wanted disciples once. [645] She settled for listeners, which is a lonelier and more durable thing. [657]
