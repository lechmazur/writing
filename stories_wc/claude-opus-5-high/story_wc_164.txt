Miren worked the tideline at dawn, scooping sea foam into jars before the sun could drink it. [17] A sea-foam recollectionist earns little and mourns often, which is why she hummed while she worked. [33] Foam holds the last thoughts of everything the water takes: boats, gulls, boys. [46] Her tool was a shard of crater glass, green-black, dredged from the old impact bay, and through it she transliterates emotions into letters she can read aloud. [73] Grief becomes a long consonant; hope comes out as a vowel that will not close. [88]

Eleven months ago her brother Hale took the cliff road to the orchard villages and the road took him. [107] Not a landslide, not bandits; the road simply stopped existing, and nobody in town could agree it ever had. [126] This happens more than people admit, and Miren wanted to discover why some paths disappear. [141]

Her only evidence was a foam memory of a tern, drowned in a squall, whose small mind still held a flight over green hills that no map contained. [169] Birds remember routes the way stones remember heat, and a migrating bird carries the shape of a place in its gut. [190] So she tracked the tern's flock inland via the scattered seeds dropped by a migrating bird, a dotted line of orchard pits and thistle husks leading up the cliffs. [219]

Where the seeds ended, the cliff had a seam in it, and the seam opened. [234] Inside was an iridescent cave inside a giant crystal geode, walls furred with violet spines that threw her lamplight back in every color at once. [259] It smelled of rain on hot rock. [266] Laid across the floor, like tools in a workshop, were missing pieces. [278] A stile with no fence, three flagstones, a ferry bell, a signpost with its arm snapped off. [295] And at the center, a low cairn of shoes. [304]

Miren sat down among them and made her conjecture out loud, because conjecture spoken is harder to lose than conjecture held. [325] A road is not dirt, she said; a road is agreement. [336] It exists while enough people remember walking it, and when the last rememberer stops, the geode takes the leftovers and keeps them here, pretty and useless, like a magpie's hoard. [366] The cliff road had gone because the orchard villages had emptied and the last old drover who loved it had died in spring. [389] Hale had been on it when the agreement failed, and he had fallen through the gap, into the workshop, into the hoard. [411] She found his boots in the cairn and laughed, which is the noise grief makes when it is also relief. [431]

Then she got to work, because a road can be re-agreed, but only before memory fades. [447] She had one jar of foam left, and in it the tern's flight; she had the crater glass; she had her own eleven months of remembering, thinning like a tide going out. [479] She poured the foam over the broken signpost and read the tern's memory through the glass, transliterating the bird's blunt joy into letters, then scratching them into the stone with the shard. [511] Joy, it turns out, is legible: a wide open vowel, hills, hills, hills. [524] Her own grief she wrote beneath it, the long consonant, the boy's name, the smell of orchard smoke he brought home on his coat. [548] Two rememberers, one dead bird and one tired woman, arguing the road back into being. [563]

The geode did not like it. [569] The spines dimmed, the flagstones shuddered, and somewhere behind the light Hale coughed, the way a sleeper coughs. [587] Miren kept humming while she wrote, cheerful in the gloom, because she could feel the cost arriving. [604] Every letter she carved left her head a little emptier; the road was taking its payment in her. [622]

When the seam widened into daylight and a rutted track lay under her feet, she walked out with her brother's hand in hers and could not, for the life of her, recall his name. [656] He told her, and she wrote it down twice, on paper and on glass. [670] They went home along the road, and she taught it to everyone she met, the way you teach a song, so the agreement would hold. [695]

Miren still gathers foam at dawn, jars and jars of other people's last thoughts, and she keeps one empty jar on the shelf, labeled in her own transliterated hand: WHAT I PAID. [727] She does not know what is missing, only that something is, and that it was worth it. [744] So she hums. [747]
