My commission reads Reality Checker, Third Island Circuit, and for eleven years I have disappointed everyone who hired me. [19] I measured the weeping statue of Hanu and found a cracked cistern above it. [33] I weighed the fish that fell from clouds and found gulls. [44] So when the elders of Sehan summoned me to the ancient ridge in bloom, six days before the monsoon arrived, I brought my calipers and my doubt. [71] The ridge blooms only in that narrow window, when the ghost-orchids open like small lamps and the whole spine of the island smells of cut pears. [97]

Mother Dehua met me at the summit shrine with a seed in a clay thimble. [112] "The knowledge fruit seed," she said. [118] "Last of the old stock. We must waken it before the rains, or Sehan forgets itself." [134] I asked, reasonably, what forgetting had to do with horticulture. [144] She explained that the tree grown from such a seed holds the island's collective memory: every name, every boundary song, every recipe for salting shark. [169] The last tree burned forty years ago, when the survey ships came and the archive with it. [186] Since then the old people have been the only library, and the old people are going. [202]

"It germinates one way," she said. "Through interpreted dreams." [211] Sleepers lie on the ridge; dream-readers listen at dawn and speak the dreams back as story; the seed, they claim, drinks the telling. [234] I wrote it down in the column my superiors hate, the one I label empirically whimsical: claims that are absurd and yet keep producing results I cannot fault. [262]

My verdict mattered more than my opinion. [268] If I certified the ritual as living practice, the ridge stayed protected land; if I did not, the quarry concession took the whole spine before the next dry season. [297] That, I understood, was why they had sent for a skeptic: only a skeptic's stamp would hold in the capital. [317]

For five nights I audited the practice. [324] Forty-one sleepers, eleven dream-readers, a ledger of everything anyone said before sleeping and after waking. [339] The dreams were ordinary at first: eels, weddings, a bicycle with no chain. [352] But the readers did not interpret them as prophecy. [361] They interpreted them as inventory. [366] A boy dreamed of a woman shelling beans on a stone with a notch in it, and old Pran said, that is the boundary stone of the Sikot family, notched in the year of the red tide, and here is the song that goes with it. [412] Then he sang it, and four people who had never heard it sang the second verse. [428]

That is the thing I could not fault. [436] The dreams were unremarkable; the interpretation was a retrieval system. [446] Ninety people sleeping on a ridge, and one hundred and six pieces of knowledge surfaced in five nights, each confirmed by at least two mouths that had not spoken to each other. [478]

On the sixth night, with the sky going green at the edges, Mother Dehua asked me to sleep. [496] "You have written everyone's dream but your own," she said. "That is not a full survey." [512] She had me, and she knew it. [519] I lay among the orchids with the clay thimble by my ear. [531] I dreamed of my grandmother's kitchen in Latu, and a blue tin I had forgotten, and the way she said my name with the second syllable long. [558] At dawn Pran listened, then told me my own family's evacuation route from the survey years, road by road, which no one had ever told me and which I had somehow described. [590]

I do not know what woke in me then. [599] The seed split that morning. [604] I can offer explanations: warmth, humidity, the first monsoon pressure dropping. [615] I wrote them all down, and then I wrote my verdict. [626] Living practice, continuous, protective of cultural memory; ridge to remain unquarried; mechanism unproven. [639] I signed it as the rain came over the water like a grey wall. [653]

Eleven years I have been paid to extinguish lamps. [662] On the ridge I learned that a lamp can be a fact. [674] I still doubt the tree. [679] But I planted it, and I go back every year before the rains to sleep there, and to say the names I owe. [702]
