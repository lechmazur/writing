The dome over the forgotten garden had held three hundred years, and Ysolt found the flaw in her first ten minutes. [21] Kelp had learned to grow in rows here, obedient to gardeners long dead, and the coral had taken the shapes of pear trees. [44] In the center stood a statue of a man with his palms turned up, as if testing rain that would never come again. [67] You are Marta's granddaughter, the statue said, without sound. [76] Ysolt's regulator hissed; her hand went to the pouch at her hip, where a small glass ampoule lay, its walls stained violet-brown by a century of foxglove tincture. [104] I am Halven, he went on, and I have been waiting for that bottle since before your grandmother learned to swim. [125] Then he told her the secret, all of it, in the time it took her to exhale. [142] The city had not been murdered. [148] It had been flooded on purpose, by its own council, to smother a fire in the grain vaults that carried a sickness in its smoke. [173] The Gardeners, whom Ysolt had been raised to hate with the clean, portable hatred of a single sentence -- they drowned Aithra to keep their secret -- had opened the sluices themselves and then stayed behind to hold the valves. [211] After revealing the secret, Halven was quiet, letting her float in it. [223] That was the first revelation, and it pulled the next one after it like a knot sliding down a line. [243] If the Gardeners had stayed, then someone had made them able to stay; the tincture, Halven explained, slowed a heart until stone could take the body, patient as frost. [272] If the tincture had made them, then someone had carried it, dose by dose, down the long stairs -- and that someone had been Marta, sixteen years old, apprenticed to the poison garden. [304] And if Marta had carried it, then the one ampoule she kept was the one she had failed to deliver. [324] Mine, said Halven. [327] She ran out of air in the passage, he said, and I told her to go up, and she went, and she never forgave either of us. [354] So she gave you a monster to hate instead of a girl to pity. [368] Ysolt had expected the dead to be cruel or serene. [378] Halven was neither; he was incisively kind, cutting exactly where the rot was and no wider, the way a good surgeon or a good gardener cuts. [404] Your grandmother was not lying, he added; she was only being certain, which is cheaper. [419] Ysolt had come down with a certainty of her own, and she had come to break it, though she had not admitted that to herself until now. [446] She wanted room in the story for a frightened girl on a stair, for men who opened sluices onto their own homes, for a stone man who had waited without bitterness. [477] She wanted the nuance let in, even if it cost her the comfort of the sentence. [493] The dose still had to reach him. [500] Stone has no mouth, but stone has flaws, Halven said, and mine runs from my collarbone to my heart -- a hairline fracture from the year the dome shifted. [528] Ysolt drew the waxed thread from her repair kit, wet it with the tincture, and worked it into the crack, feeding it down through the structural weakness a finger's width at a time. [561] It was slow work, threading a wick into a man. [571] Twice the line snagged; twice his voice steadied her hands, describing the garden as it had been -- pear trees, real ones, and lanterns strung above the rows. [598] When the last of the violet liquid had gone down the thread, the lifeless thing began, briefly, to animate. [617] His fingers closed; silt lifted from his shoulders in a slow gray wing; the marble face found, for perhaps four seconds, an expression. [640] Tell them we were both, he said. [647] Then the garden was only a garden again, and the statue only stone, and the kelp went on bowing in its obedient rows. [670] Ysolt surfaced an hour later with an empty ampoule and a longer sentence than the one she had been taught. [690] It began the same way and ended differently, and it took much longer to say. [705] That, she decided, was how you could tell it was true. [716]
