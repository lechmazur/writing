The elevator in the icebound spire climbs ten floors, and Wren has learned to spend them well. [17] She is a servant, second class, ascending; the ledger of the house says so in dry ink, and so does the brass plate on the lift that marks her permitted range. [48] Yesterday she was allowed only to the seventh floor. [57] Today, because Captain Isolde Verrane fell out of the sky three weeks ago and somebody must sort her effects, Wren is allowed to the tenth. [82] The spire stands above the clouds, and its outer walls are sheathed in ice so old it has gone blue as a vein. [105] Light comes into the lift shaft through that ice, broken and scattered, so that riders travel in a slow drift of pale colors. [128] Wren carries two things: the captain's diary, locked in gibberish, and the captain's goggles. [142] The goggles were an airship captain's, of course, smoked glass ground in eleven facets to keep hail and glare from a pilot's eyes. [165] Wren discovered, on the third floor of an earlier ascent, that the diary was not gibberish at all. [183] She had been holding both objects when a rung of ice-light struck the lenses, and the scattered beams fell across the page, and the false letters began to unfold into true ones. [215] The captain had written in a cipher of interference: only light broken at those eleven angles could resolve it. [234] It is an extravagant way to keep a secret, and Wren has come to think it was less about secrecy than about wanting to be found by someone patient. [263]

Floor one. [265] The household below is in full mourning, and mourning in this house is a kind of weather: grey, total, humorless. [285] Wren mourns too, but sideways. [290] She was warmly inquisitive by nature even as a child, the sort who asked cooks what they dreamed about, and she cannot grieve a woman she never finished asking questions of. [321]

Floor three. [323] She tilts the goggles, and a stripe of blue light comes apart into a dozen thinner stripes, and the page for the fourteenth of Frost unfolds. [349] "Lost Pellan to the cold at dawn," the captain wrote, "and I record here that on Tuesday he laughed at my poor joke about the barometer, because if I keep only the debts the ledger will kill me." [387] This is the whole method of the diary, Wren has decided: a woman going down through her own anguish on purpose, with a lamp, to seek out the joy buried under it. [419]

Floor five. [421] The steward expects Wren to hand over the diary unread, and the steward will get it, eventually. [438] Wren is not a thief; she is only slower than she has to be. [452]

Floor seven, her old ceiling, sliding past with no more ceremony than a rung on a ladder. [469] She notes the promotion the way she notes weather in another country: it is good, it is real, and it is happening slightly beside her. [494]

Floor eight. [496] The last entry, written nine days before the crash, resolves in the scattered light slowly, as if reluctant. [514] "To whoever has my goggles," it reads, "you will have worked for this, so I will not waste your eyes: the joy is not under the grief like treasure under a floor, it is inside it, the way heat is inside a fever, and you must hold the whole thing to get any of it." [569]

Floor nine. [571] Wren closes the book and finds she is smiling, which would look monstrous downstairs and is perfectly correct up here. [591] She loved the captain about as much as one can love a person glimpsed from a stairwell, which is to say truly, and from a distance, and without any claim. [621]

Floor ten. [623] The doors unfold onto a white room where the cold comes through the window glass like a held note, and the clouds below are a floor she could almost cross. [653] Wren sets the diary on the steward's table, exactly as ordered, and keeps the goggles, exactly as not. [671] Without the eleven facets, the pages are noise again, and the house will bury its captain in mystery and grey, and that is their business. [696] Wren starts her own list on the ride down: ten floors, blue light, a dead woman's good advice, and one bad joke about a barometer that she intends to repeat. [726] The elevator descends, and the ice lets the light through in pieces, which is the only way it ever comes. [746]
