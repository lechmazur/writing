Ket had skimmed stones in the Canyon of Endless Echoes for forty years, and she had never once left it. [20] She did not need to. [25] The towers of bone rose around her like the fingers of a buried giant, pale and porous, and every stone she threw came back to her as sound. [53] A harmonic stone skimmer learns the canyon the way a blind woman learns a house: by the noise her touch makes in it. [76] Skip a flat shale across the dust flats and the towers answer in chords. [90] Learn the chords, and you know where the water is, where the sink-pits gape, where the wind has gnawed a new arch since spring. [114] Her teacher, old Sabb, had called it navigating without moving, and had said it was the only honest way to travel. [135]

Ket was historically reverent, which in the canyon meant something practical. [146] She did not disturb the layered dust without cause. [155] Centuries had settled there in bands, ash over silt over ash, each layer the record of a year somebody had survived. [176] When she cut a skimming lane, she cut it shallow, and she read what her stone uncovered before she smoothed it back. [198]

That was how she found the arrowhead. [205] It lay eleven hands down, in the black band that the old skimmers called the Silent Year. [222] A stone arrowhead, chert, knapped with a serration pattern no living hand in the canyon still taught. [239] Ket sat back on her heels to appraise it, turning it against the light, weighing it, tapping it with a fingernail to hear its grain. [264] It rang wrong. [267] Not the dull click of a dead flake, but a thin sustained note, as though the thing had been struck long ago and had never finished ringing. [294]

Sabb had warned her about this, once, in the tone he reserved for things he half believed. [311] Somewhere in the canyon, he said, there was a pulse left wandering. [323] A sound that had entered the bone towers and found no wall to die against, so it went on turning corners, thinning, hunting an ear. [348] The arrow that made it had been loosed in the Silent Year, and had missed. [363]

Ket could not hear the wandering pulse; it was too old and too faint for any human ear. [381] But the canyon swifts could. [386] They nested in the tower sockets by the thousand, and they flew by sound the way she skimmed by sound, and a flock will bend around a pressure it does not like. [418] So she stopped throwing stones and began to watch the birds. [429]

For three seasons she sat at the same ledge with the arrowhead in her palm and tracked the patterns in the flock: the little hollow that opened in the evening swarm, the shiver that ran down a wing of ten thousand bodies, always at dusk, always drifting one tower west of where it had been the year before. [487] The pulse was still circling. [492] She mapped its orbit on a slab of shale, and the map was the canyon entire, every chamber and chimney and drowned gallery she had never walked. [519] The wandering sound had been everywhere she had not. [528] It had been navigating without moving for six hundred years, and she had only to sit still enough to be taught. [549]

Her knees went bad in the fourth season. [557] Her eyes clouded at the edges. [563] She kept the ledge. [567] Weathered resolve was the only kind the canyon respected; everything soft here was dust by the second century. [585] When apprentices climbed to her ledge to ask why she no longer skimmed, she handed them the shale and said: throw nothing. [607] Watch the birds. [610]

In her last winter the flock's hollow passed directly over her ledge, and the swifts split around her like water around a post. [633] Ket held the arrowhead up into the gap. [641] She could not hear the pulse, but she felt the chert warm and the note in it rise to meet its own six-hundred-year-old shout. [665] Then the arrowhead went quiet in her hand, the way a thing goes quiet when it has finally arrived. [684]

The towers stood over her, patient, full of holes. [693] She smoothed the dust back over her lane one last time, out of reverence, and because the next skimmer would need the record unspoiled. [717] Then she sat, and did not move, and knew the whole canyon. [729]
