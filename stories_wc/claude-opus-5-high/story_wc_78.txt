At the blue hour, when the sea turned the color of a held breath, the orchard began to drift again. [20] It was a circle of driftwood lashed with cable and tar, forty trees deep in salt-fed soil, and it had no home but the current. [45] Mira Halsey, the leeward tide prophet, stood at the downwind rail with a xylophone mallet in her fist. [63] She had been given the title as a joke by the fishers of Corrow, who said she could only foretell weather that had already passed her by. [90] She kept the title anyway, because leeward is where you learn what the wind has done. [106]

Overhead, the clouds were doing the thing she hated: mackerel scales folding into anvils, the sign of a squall that would arrive with teeth. [130] She interpreted cloud movements the way other people read faces, and this sky was grinding its jaw. [147]

Three days earlier a floating reef of tangled kelp and lost netting had caught the orchard's leeward arc and would not let go. [170] The grove-keepers had sawed at it, cursed it, prayed at it. [181] Mira struck it with her mallet instead. [187] That was her whole philosophy, the one nobody had asked for: turn obstacles into instruments, because an obstacle you can play is an obstacle you understand. [213]

The kelp mass rang. [217] Not like a bell, but like a room full of wet rope waking up, and under that a low sustained hum where the netting had knotted itself around a sunken spar. [248] She sat down on a crate of unripe salt-pears and began to sketch the resonance. [263] This was the craft her mother had taught her: strike a thing, listen, and draw the shape the sound wants to be. [285] A drawn tone could be measured, halved, doubled. [292] On the chalkboard hung between two pear trees, the kelp reef came out as a branching thicket of lines, each strand splitting into two smaller strands at the same angle, again and again. [325] Fractal generating, her mother had called it, the way a single rule repeated at every scale could fill a whole sea with structure. [348] If the reef's tangle obeyed one rule, then one rule could untangle it. [361]

Mira worked through the blue hour with the mallet, striking and sketching, striking and sketching. [376] The sound she got back was never still; it climbed, doubled back, harmonized with itself in ninths and elevenths, a restless euphony that made the grove-keepers stop pruning to listen. [406]

Old Bassett came up behind her, salt in his beard. [416] "The squall will break us against that thing," he said. [426] "It will," Mira agreed, "unless we let it play us instead." [437] She showed him the chalkboard. [442] The branching lines had a seed: one knot, wrapped three times around the spar, from which every other knot in the reef descended. [465] Cut the seed knot and the tangle would unravel outward at every scale, like a chord releasing. [482] But she did not want it gone. [489] She wanted it moved. [493]

They dove in the last of the light and cut the seed knot, and the reef came apart into long ribbed sheets that the crew hauled up and lashed along the orchard's leeward arc, spar and netting and all. [532]

When the squall came it hit that arc first, and the arc sang. [545] Wind through the ribbed sheets, water through the netting, the spar humming its low sustained note under all of it, the same restless euphony Mira had sketched, now loud enough to feel in the teeth. [580] The sheets bled the squall's force sideways; the orchard heeled, shuddered, and held. [593] Nobody lost a tree. [597]

Afterward, in the gray wrung-out morning, Bassett asked what they should call the thing. [611] Mira laid her xylophone mallet flat against the spar, the way an admiral lays a sword on a shoulder. [630] "I dub thee the Leeward Voice," she said, and struck it once. [642] The note went out across the water and did not come back, which she took as a good sign. [661]

By the next blue hour she was already at the rail again, reading a new sky, mallet in hand. [680] The clouds were stacking into towers in the southwest, and the orchard was drifting toward them, as it always would. [700] Mira sharpened her chalk. [704] Somewhere out there was the next obstacle, and she intended to learn its tuning before it learned hers. [722]
