The library stood above the clouds, and the clouds never came up to visit. [14] Frost had sealed its windows into panes of pale quartz, and the hush inside was the hush of quartz too: dense, patient, faintly ringing. [38] Anselm Vire, records chief, walked its aisles at the same hour each morning, straightening slides that had shifted by a hair. [59] He was gently meticulous, a man who dusted with a sable brush and apologized to the glass when he set it down too hard. [83] The collection was not books. [88] It was vows. [91] When two people promised each other something they meant with their whole spine, the promise left a residue of light, and the light could be caught on a magic lantern slide. [122] Ten thousand such slides lined the frozen shelves, each luminous vow still faintly burning, each one dimming. [139] That was the trouble Anselm had inherited: light does not keep. [150] A vow held in glass lasts perhaps ninety years, and then the image thins, colors leaking one by one, until the slide is only a window onto nothing. [178] His predecessors had simply logged the losses. [185] Anselm refused to preside over a graveyard. [192] So he built the harp. [197] It was an instrument of brass combs and wire, and it worked by sampling frequencies: you slid a dying vow into its throat, and the harp would measure the light that remained, band by band, red through violet, counting how many times each hue trembled per second. [244] From those numbers Anselm could reconstruct, in ink, what the image had shown and what the promise had meant. [263] Illusion became memory; memory could be copied, and copies could be kept. [275] He worked, therefore, only in the narrow season while illusions faded to memory, when a slide was dim enough to be legible to the harp but not yet blank. [304] Twelve slides a day, no more; haste bruised the light. [314] He transcribed a fisherman's oath to return, a midwife's promise to a stranger's child, a treaty sworn between two villages over a shared well. [338] None of the people were alive. [344] That, he had decided long ago, was precisely the point: to conjure meaning from the edge of oblivion, since meaning at the center is never in danger. [371] In the ninth winter of his tenure he reached shelf forty-one, drawer nine, slide four. [386] The catalogue card, in his own younger handwriting, read: Vire, A. and Sorrel, M. -- mutual, unwitnessed, 1/1. [403] He had filed it himself, forty years earlier, on the day Mira Sorrel left the mountain and did not come back. [424] Anselm held the slide to the window light. [432] The image was nearly gone: two figures on a stair of ice, a smear of gold where a lamp had been. [453] He could not remember what they had promised. [461] He remembered the cold of the stair and the particular way she pronounced his name, but the words themselves had gone the way of the colors. [487] This, he understood, was the reason he had built the harp, and he had never once admitted it to himself. [507] He set the slide in the throat and let the combs sample it. [520] The harp rang once, a thin sound that the quartz hush swallowed whole. [533] Then it gave him numbers, and the numbers were wrong. [543] The violet band was strong -- stronger than any slide he had ever measured -- and violet meant a promise still being kept. [564] A vow does not dim from age alone, he had written in his own manual; it dims as it is fulfilled or forgotten. [587] He worked the calculation twice, gently, meticulously, and both times the transcription came out the same. [603] We will keep what the world lets go. [611] That was all. [614] Eight words, sworn at twenty-two on an icy stair, and he had spent four decades keeping them without knowing their shape -- a thousand strangers' promises hauled out of the dark and set down in ink. [649] Mira had kept them too, somewhere below the clouds; the violet said so. [662] Anselm copied the line onto a card in his smallest hand, dated it, and filed it under V. [680] Then he returned the slide to drawer nine, because a record belongs where the catalogue says it belongs, even when it is your own heart on the glass. [708] Outside, the clouds lay flat and bright as a floor. [718] There were nine thousand slides left, and he was seventy-one years old, and he would not finish. [735] He picked up the sable brush, apologized to the drawer, and began on slide five. [750]
