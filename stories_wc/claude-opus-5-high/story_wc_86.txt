The parapet at Vell's Reach belonged to no country, which was the only reason anyone would stand on it. [19] Storm-worn, windblown, wrapped in mist that came off the estuary like breath off a sleeping animal, it had been a customs house once, then a gun platform, then nothing. [48] Now it was neutral territory, and Idris Kane, prisoner exchange negotiator for the Ashfall Accord, climbed its stairs eleven times a year. [70] Eleven, because neither side trusted couriers, telegraphs, or each other's clocks; they synchronized through moon phases instead. [87] First quarter meant a name list. [93] Full moon meant bodies, living ones, walked up out of the dark. [105] Last quarter meant grievances, and the new moon meant nothing at all, which both delegations had come to treasure. [124]

Kane was uncommonly thoughtful, which his superiors considered a defect. [134] He kept a ledger of every error he had ever made at that railing, not to punish himself but to recycle mistakes into wisdom, the way the estuary villages melted down shell casings into door hinges. [170] Mistake forty-one was the worst: three winters ago he had trusted a spoken count over a written one, and eleven men had been untethered from the exchange boat's line and drifted out into the fog while the two delegations argued about arithmetic. [212] Four bodies were recovered. [216] Seven were not. [219]

Tonight was a last quarter moon, grievance night, and the mist was thick enough to taste. [235] Commissioner Vera Anselm came up the north stair alone, as the protocol Kane had written required, and the two of them stood in the furrowed hush that always settled here, where speaking loudly felt like breaking something. [272] She set an object on the stone between them: an antique drawer pull, brass, shaped like a lion's mouth holding a ring. [294] "It came off the desk in the harbor office," she said, "the desk where you and I counted wrong." [313]

Kane had apologized for it two years ago, in writing, in front of witnesses, and she had never answered. [332] They had been living ever since in the narrow country between apology and forgiveness, which was its own kind of neutral territory, and colder than this one. [359]

"A brother of one of the seven sent it to me," Anselm said. [372] "He wanted me to throw it in the water." [381]

Kane picked it up. [385] It was heavier than it looked, and the ring still turned. [396]

"Then we should not throw it," he said. [404] "We should make it useful." [409]

He explained the thing he had spent nine moons turning over. [420] At every full moon, the pull would be fixed to the parapet gate, and no prisoner could be untethered from the boat line until both negotiators had, together, turned the ring and spoken the count aloud in the same breath. [460] One hand each. [463] No aides, no shouted numbers down a fogged stairwell. [472]

"A ritual," Anselm said, not quite scornfully. [479]

"A hinge," Kane said. [483] "Made from a casing." [487]

The mist moved past them in slow ropes. [495] Below, invisible, the estuary made the sound of something patient. [505] Anselm took out her own ledger, which Kane had not known she kept, and wrote the clause in her small upright hand, and then read it back to him twice, because that was the lesson both of them had bought at a price of seven men. [551]

"I am not forgiving you tonight," she said. [559]

"I know," Kane said. [563] "I would not trust it if you did." [571]

She almost smiled, and went down the north stair, and the hush closed over the place like water over a dropped stone. [593]

At the next full moon Kane arrived early and drilled a hole in the old gate himself. [610] The brass caught the fog-light; the lion's mouth held its ring; the boat came up out of the mist with nineteen men aboard. [633] Two hands turned the ring together. [639] Two voices said nineteen. [643] Then, and only then, did Kane lean over the railing and untether the line, and nineteen men walked up the storm-worn stair into a country that had agreed, for one night, to be nobody's. [677] Anselm did not say she forgave him, then or later, but every full moon she put her hand on the brass beside his, and Kane came to understand that this was what forgiveness looked like while it was still being built. [718]
