They called Oren the whispering advisor because the sea had taken his voice and left him only the husk of it. [21] For thirty years he had stood at the harbormaster's elbow and murmured which ships to trust, and the harbormaster had never once asked him to speak up. [48] Mira could not hear him at all, which was why they became friends. [61] She was deaf from a fever at four, and quietly charismatic in the way of people who make you lean in. [82] When Mira signed, the fishermen stopped mending nets to watch her hands the way they watched weather. [99] Oren learned sign language at sixty-two so that someone in the world would finally understand him the first time. [118]

Our harbor is luminous by moonlight; the plankton wake and the water turns the color of a held breath. [137] Every autumn the town rows out for the Night of No Names, when we sing the old Requiem for everyone who has reached the year when no one remembers you. [167] It is a dirge in four heavy parts, and Mira hated it. [179] She could feel it through the hull as a dull, self-pitying thud, and she told me it lied about the dead. [200] The dead were not quiet, she signed once; they were silent, and silence has a shape. [216]

Her grandfather's vintage camera had no film in it and had not for a decade; she carried it anyway, on a cracked strap, like a pair of borrowed eyes. [245] Through the viewfinder she framed things and counted: the gap between a gull's cry and its echo off the sea wall, the pause a mother left before answering a child. [275] The shutter clicked on nothing, and the click was a metronome only she could feel in her thumb. [293] This was her method: she was mapping silence, ruling the harbor into frames and writing down how long each emptiness lasted. [314] Three years of notebooks, all rests and no notes, because her whole ambition was to translate silence into music. [333]

Oren was the one who told her to steal the Night of No Names. [347] He whispered it, then signed it so there could be no mistake: disrupt them, because a requiem for the forgotten should not be loud. [371] So we did. [374]

On the night the water lit up, forty boats drifted in a ring with lanterns hooded, and Mira stood in the prow of the pilot boat with the film-less camera around her neck. [407] When the choir drew breath for the first heavy chord, she raised her hands. [421] She did not sign words. [426] She conducted. [428] From her notebooks she had built a score out of the harbor's own pauses, and she gave each singer a silence to hold and each rower an oar-stroke to place at its edge. [461]

What we made, out there on the glowing water, was mostly nothing: a hum cut off, a dip of wood, eleven seconds of plankton light and wind, then one low note from old Marta who had buried three brothers. [500] It was the most crowded quiet I have ever stood inside. [511] People wept and people grinned, and nobody could explain why both at once. [524] Mira called it joyful sorrow and said the dead had always sounded like that, if you stopped shouting over them. [544]

The harbormaster was furious for about a minute. [552] Then Oren leaned to his elbow and murmured something nobody else heard, and the old man sat down in the stern and put his hat over his face. [580]

We have sung Mira's version every autumn since. [588] She died in March, in a room that smelled of tar and apples, signing to the last. [605] Oren keeps the camera now, and he still lifts it sometimes and clicks the empty shutter at the water, counting. [625] He says a year will come when no one remembers her, and that this is not a tragedy but the point. [646] A name goes; the shape it left behind stays, and we have learned how to hold that shape for eleven seconds under the moon. [670] Come out with us in October, when the harbor is luminous and the lanterns are hooded, and you will hear her. [691]
