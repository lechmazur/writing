After the energy beings arrived, nobody in the valley farmed, and nobody in the valley slept well. [17] They hung above the ridges like slow lightning that had learned patience. [29] They did not speak, and they did not kill; they only offered choices, and the choices were impossible. [47] Into that valley came a drifting roadside poet named Vasha, who carried a satchel, a tin cup, and a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape. [74] The rope was eleven years old and smelled of lime and iron. [86] She had gone down it; her brother had held the top, because only one weight could be trusted to the window bars. [108] That had been her first impossible choice, and it had taught her that such choices leave a texture, like a thumbprint pressed into wet clay. [133] Ever since, she had wandered the roads with one ambition she never wrote into her poems: to discover the texture of time. [155] The villagers told her the beings held court in the terra-cotta grotto above the olive terraces, and that petitioners went in at dusk and came out changed or not at all. [186] She climbed at moonrise and arrived under starlight. [194] The grotto was a throat of baked orange clay, ribbed and glazed, open at the crown so the stars stood directly overhead like nails in a lid. [221] Three of the beings waited inside, pulsing, and they gave her their question without sound. [236] The valley's well could be healed, or the valley's blight could be lifted, but not both; choose, and speak the choice in a pattern we can read. [263] Vasha laughed, which surprised them, and she apologized for laughing, which surprised them more. [277] She was ethically playful by trade; a poet who begs supper from strangers learns to take right and wrong seriously without ever taking them solemnly. [302] So she unwound the frayed rope and began to pattern it across the grotto floor: a knot for the well, a loop for the blight, and between them a long, deliberate slackness. [334] "That slack is my answer," she said, "come back in a year and I will have made one choice bearable." [354] The beings brightened, displeased, and the clay walls warmed against her back. [366] It was then, with her palm flat on the glaze, that she felt a ridge under the surface. [384] She worked at it with her thumbnail, and the answer came the only way answers ever came to her, by uncovering layers. [406] Beneath the top glaze lay an older glaze, and pressed into it was a rope pattern - a knot, a loop, and a span of slack. [431] Beneath that, another layer, another rope, another refusal to choose cleanly. [442] Nine layers down, the impressions grew crude, made with sinew and reed, and still they said the same thing in the same stubborn grammar. [466] The grotto was not a courtroom. [472] It was a record, and every petitioner before her had been fired into it like slipware in a kiln. [491] This was the texture she had walked eleven years to touch: time was not a line but a wall of clay, each impossible choice a layer, the newest one soft, all the old ones holding it up. [528] Her brother's hands on the window bars were in there somewhere, load-bearing. [540] She told the beings what she had found, quietly, because some passions require a low voice to survive. [558] They had come, she understood now, not to judge humans but to learn how a species carries what it cannot resolve. [579] "You want our pattern," she said, "it is this: we choose, we refuse to be finished by choosing, and we leave the mark soft for whoever climbs up next." [608] The beings dimmed to the color of embers, which she decided to read as agreement. [623] In the morning the well ran clear and the blight retreated halfway up the terraces - half a mercy, exactly the size of her slack rope. [648] Vasha did not stay to be thanked. [655] She pressed her frayed rope into the soft new glaze of the grotto wall, knot, loop, and slack, and left it there for the next thumbnail to find. [683] Then she went down the goat path to the road, composing as she walked, in a meter that kept leaving room. [704] The stars, she noticed, were also layered, some of them long gone and still holding up the sky. [722]
