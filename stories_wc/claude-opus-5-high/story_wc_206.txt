Ilario mended music boxes in a town where midnight was a thief. [12] Every night the great bell swallowed an hour of lamplight, and Verrough woke a shade dimmer, its windows gray as ash. [33] The old people called it the covenant of rust. [42] Long ago, they said, the founders bargained with iron: nothing in Verrough would ever truly break, only corrode slowly, so long as no one lied inside the walls. [70] The iron kept its half of the bargain. [78] The people had not. [82] Ilario knew because of the rose. [88] It was wrought iron, red-brown, the size of a fist, and it hung over his workbench on a chain; when anyone spoke falsely near it, its petals curled shut and drooped like a flower in drought. [124] It had wilted a great deal over thirty years. [133] Customers brought him boxes that would not sing. [141] He tuned combs, straightened pins, oiled governors, and listened to the little tinkersongs bloom back into the air like sparks off a struck flint. [165] Mostly, though, he waited for the box his mother had left him to tell him what to do. [183] It was a walnut music box with a chip of the philosopher's stone set where the cylinder's axle should have been, a sliver of amber glass that hummed when light touched it. [215] The box was not broken. [220] That was the trouble. [224] His mother's last instruction had been absurd: mend what isn't torn. [235] For thirty years he had turned the phrase over like a coin he could not spend. [251] Then the bell cracked. [255] On the longest night of the year, the ringing came out wrong, and the town elders, panicking, opened the tower's lower door for the first time in a generation. [284] They sent for the only person in Verrough who understood cylinders and combs. [297] Beneath the tower lay a resonance chamber, a bronze-ribbed vault shaped like the inside of a lute, and in its center stood a mechanism the size of a haycart. [326] It was a music box. [331] Ilario laughed aloud, and the chamber gave his laugh back to him sevenfold, golden. [345] Every midnight, he saw, the great cylinder turned one notch and drew the town's lamplight down a copper tether into a sealed reservoir below, where it pooled, unspent, for centuries. [375] The founders had not bargained for durable iron alone. [384] They had pledged the town's light as collateral, a debt to be paid in honesty, and honesty had never come. [404] The elders crowded the doorway, and Ilario asked them plainly whether the covenant was as he understood it. [422] They said it was an old story, nothing more. [431] The iron rose at his belt shut like a fist. [441] So he worked alone, all that night, with the whole year hanging in the balance. [456] The mechanism was flawless. [460] No pin bent, no comb tooth snapped; it had never failed, and that was precisely the wound. [477] Mend what isn't torn, his mother had written, and now he understood her: a machine doing faithful harm needs repair more than a broken one. [502] He set his mother's little walnut box on the great cylinder's frame and pried out the chip of philosopher's stone. [522] Gold from lead, light from dark; the sliver only ever wanted to turn a thing into its truer self. [541] He fitted it into the reservoir's lock, reversed the copper tether, and retuned the comb so that the midnight note fell one step instead of rising. [567] Then, because the debt still stood, he paid it. [576] Into the resonance chamber, which returned everything sevenfold, he confessed: the clocks he had overcharged for, the widow he had let believe her husband's box still played his voice, thirty years of small, kind, corroding lies. [612] The rose at his belt opened. [618] At midnight the bell rang its new, lower note, and the tether ran backward. [632] Centuries of hoarded evenings came up through the copper, and Verrough filled with light the color of old honey, enough to see every face by. [657] The iron did not stop rusting; covenants are not so easily unmade. [669] But rust, Ilario decided, is only iron telling the truth about time. [681] In the morning the elders came down to take credit, and the rose wilted, and everyone saw it, because there was light enough now to see by. [708] Ilario went back to his bench, opened the shutters wide, and mended a child's music box that was not broken at all -- only lonely, only wanting to be wound. [737] It sang. [739]
