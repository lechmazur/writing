The cavern orchard was quietest after moonlight slid down the shaft and the pear trees stopped their whispering. [18] That was when Oskar worked. [23] He was a secretive conjurer, which in the village below meant only that nobody had ever seen him do anything except mend ladders and sell pears. [49] The trees grew by grace of the stalactites, which dripped mineral water into the roots with the patience of clocks. [69] Sixteen years ago his daughter Ilse had walked out along the mosaic stepping stones toward the upper gallery, and the cavern had not given her back, nor had it kept her. [100] No body, no note, no grave: an ambiguous loss, the kind that refuses to close and therefore refuses to finish hurting. [121] Grief, Oskar had decided, was badly engineered, and he intended to understand the mathematics of emotion the way a man understands a pulley. [144] His instrument was a pair of glass eyes he had bought from a taxidermist in the lowland market, amber irises, meant for a fox. [168] Glass remembers light, and a taxidermist's glass eye pair is ground to hold a gaze that no longer exists - which was exactly the problem Oskar was studying. [195] He set the eyes in a frame of pear wood and dovetailed the corners by lamplight himself, because a dovetail holds without glue and he wanted nothing in the whole device that could ever dry out and let go. [234] The measuring, though, happened on the path. [241] The stepping stones were mosaic, laid by some earlier tenant of the cavern, and each tile chipped a little more every year as boots and dripping water worked at them. [271] Oskar had realized, gently inventive as he was, that the chipped patterns on a mosaic stepping stone recorded every footfall that had ever crossed it - weight, hesitation, direction, the particular stagger of a man who has been told bad news. [311] Hold the glass eyes above a stone by moonlight and the amber lenses gathered the chips into a figure; read the figure, and you had a number for how much a person had loved, or feared, or lingered. [349] He had catalogued four hundred stones. [355] The fifth from the gallery arch he had saved, the way a man saves the last letter. [372] That night, after the orchard grew silent beneath the moon, he carried the frame out and knelt on the wet floor. [393] Water ticked from the stalactites onto the leaves above him, feeding the trees that had fed him for sixteen lonely years. [414] He held the eyes above the fifth stone. [422] The chips assembled. [425] Ilse's footfall was there, light, fifty-one kilos, turned three degrees toward the orchard rather than the arch - she had looked back. [446] And beneath her print, fresher by nine years, a second set: the same weight plus a child's, coming down the gallery and stopping, and turning around again. [473] She had come back once, with someone small, and had not come the last hundred steps. [489] Oskar sat down in the mud between the pear trees and laughed, which surprised him. [504] The mathematics had not closed anything. [510] It had told him only that his loss remained ambiguous, that she was alive nine years ago, that she had hesitated twice, that she had wanted to and had not. [540] But hesitation, he understood now, is not the same as refusal. [551] It is a quantity with a sign, and the sign was positive. [563] In the morning he did something he had never done in forty years of careful secrecy: he walked down to the village and told the ladder-menders and the fruit-buyers about the orchard under the rock, the sweet water, the trees, the man who lived there and the door he kept unlocked. [614] A secret, he had learned, is only a stone nobody steps on. [626] Then he went home and rebuilt the mosaic path in pale new tile, bright enough to be seen from the arch, and set the glass eyes above the gate where they could catch the moon. [661] They saw nothing, of course; they were glass, ground by a taxidermist for a fox that no longer needed them. [681] But they shone, and shining is a kind of arithmetic too. [692] Every night after the orchard grew silent he counted the new chips on the fifth stone, and every night the number was the same, and every night he went to bed believing it would change. [727] That, he decided, was the whole equation. [734]
