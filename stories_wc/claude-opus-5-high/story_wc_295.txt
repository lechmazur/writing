They hired me at the celestial mapping observatory because I could sleep on command and remember everything. [17] My title, printed on a brass plate nobody ever read, was lucid dream chronicler. [31] Doctor Amara Vey hired me for one reason: to decode temple inscriptions that no waking scholar had cracked in ninety years. [52] The inscriptions came from the drowned temple at Kavath, rubbings of them pinned along the observatory's curved north wall like a frieze of insects. [76] Amara had proven the glyphs were not words but observations, and that the ancients had timed their whole calendar through variable stars -- the slow breathing of Mira, the wink of Algol, the long sigh of Betelgeuse. [112] What she could not do was hold nine centuries of brightness in her head at once. [128] I could, if I dreamed it. [134] So we worked at midnight, the dome cracked open to the cold, and I lay on the cot beneath the great refractor while she read the star tables aloud. [163] Inside the dream I built the sky the temple had seen, let it run, and watched the glyphs arrange themselves against it. [185] My work was not to solve but to witness, and to report what I had seen before it faded. [204]

For eleven months we shared that labor, and the sharing was the only thing that made it bearable. [222] Chronicling costs something. [225] A dreamer who goes down nightly comes back thin somewhere behind the eyes, and Amara knew it, so she carried what she could of my half: she cooked, she kept my ledger, she talked me back into the room each dawn. [266] And I carried hers. [270]

Here is what I mean. [275] In the third month she showed me a telegram, daylily-pressed, the flower flattened brown across the paper so that the words beneath it had to be read like something underwater. [305] It had come from Kavath in 1911, from her father, and it said only: SURVEY HALTED. WATER RISING. COME. [324] She had been nineteen, and she had not come. [333] He drowned with his notebooks, and she pressed a daylily into the telegram because daylilies open for one day only, and she wanted a witness to the day she chose wrong. [364]

That was the real reason she wanted the inscriptions read. [374] Not scholarship. [376] She wanted to finish the sentence her father had been standing in when the water took him. [393]

I am told I heal with words, which is a generous way of saying I am good at naming things people cannot name for themselves. [418] It is the only medicine I have, and it works slowly, and it works. [432] Night after night, while the dome groaned on its rails, I told her that a nineteen-year-old cannot outrun a river, that grief is not a debt, that her father's last instruction had been a request, not a verdict. [470] That was my half of the burden: hers to hold the tables steady, mine to hold her steady. [488] There was a midnight solace in it -- two people awake in a cold room, each doing the one thing the other could not. [511]

The answer came on the last night of March. [520] In the dream I let the temple sky cycle for four hundred years, and I saw it: the glyph rows were not a calendar at all but a roster. [549] Each variable star had been assigned to a family, and each family was charged with watching its star and reporting its dimming, so that no one person had to keep the whole heaven in mind. [584] The temple had survived nine centuries by dividing the sky among its people. [597] I woke saying it aloud, and Amara wrote it down in my handwriting because my hands were shaking. [615]

Understanding, when it dawns, arrives in layers, and the second layer arrived with the actual sunrise. [631] Amara sat with the daylily-pressed telegram in her lap and said, quietly, that her father had been one man trying to hold a whole drowning sky by himself. [659] That was why he had sent for her. [666] Not to save him. [670] To share it. [673] She did not cry; she went to the north wall and began numbering the rubbings, family by family, the way the temple had. [696]

We published in the autumn, both names, and I have slept ordinary sleep ever since. [711] The telegram hangs framed in the observatory stairwell, its brown flower spread flat as a hand, and under it Amara lettered the only line of the inscriptions we could translate word for word: NO ONE WATCHES ALONE. [748]
