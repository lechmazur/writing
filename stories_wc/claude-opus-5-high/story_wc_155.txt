The fog came to the orchard district four centuries ago and never learned how to leave. [16] Mira Vance, the district's last muralist and its most vivid, had been born inside it, and she painted with light because light was the only thing the fog could not swallow whole. [48] Her brushes were lenses; her pigments were filtered lamps; her walls were the standing vapor itself, which held an image for nine or ten seconds before letting it drift. [77] Nine seconds of a quince tree in blossom, and then nothing, and then the grey again. [93] She was tired of nine seconds. [99] The fresco fragment changed that. [104] A scavenger traded it to her for two candles: a palm-sized chip of plaster from the old Harvest Hall, showing three-quarters of a laughing mouth and the crook of an arm raised mid-dance. [137] Mira sat with it for a long evening, running her thumb over the joy of strangers. [153] The district had once danced. [158] That was the fact she could not put down. [167] Her grandmother had told her a theory, half-mad and beautiful: that the fog was not weather but emulsion. [185] Droplets thick with orchard resins, hanging undisturbed for centuries, taking in every lantern and lightning stroke the way exposed film takes in a face. [209] The whole district, her grandmother said, was one enormous negative, exposed and never developed. [223] Mira could not test a theory alone, so she sent it into the knowledge network: the district's inheritance of speaking-tubes, chalk boards at every cistern, and runners who carried questions from cellar to cellar until someone answered. [260] The question she chalked was simple: what light develops a memory? [271] Answers came back over five weeks, crooked and generous. [280] A glassmaker sent formulas for resin transparency. [287] A retired chemist sent the developing baths her mother had used on plate cameras, and the wavelengths that woke silver. [307] A child sent a drawing of a quince, which Mira pinned above her workbench because accuracy is not the only kind of help. [330] And an archivist matched her fresco fragment to a mural index: Harvest Hall, north wall, the Pear-Blossom Dance, painted the year before the fog. [354] So she knew where to stand. [360] It took the winter to build the array: eleven salvaged lamp-houses, resin filters, a clockwork shutter timed to the archivist's calculations. [381] On the first mild night of spring, four hundred people came out to the ruined orchard rows to see what she would display, and Mira understood that she had promised them something she could not guarantee. [417] While the lamps warmed, she let her eyes unfocus, and inside the fold of a daydream she was already dancing. [437] Pear blossom on her shoulders, a fiddle somewhere, her grandmother young and laughing at a joke four centuries old. [456] Then the shutter cracked open, and the daydream did not end so much as widen to hold everyone. [474] The fog did not hold Mira's picture. [481] It gave up its own. [486] Depth by depth, the vapor developed: orchard rows in full white blossom, standing exactly where the dead stumps stood; a cart; a boy stealing fruit; the north wall of Harvest Hall with its painted dancers, whole, the laughing mouth complete at last. [528] People walked into it and wore blossoms of light on their coats. [540] It lasted nine seconds, then twenty, then a long slow minute as the resin gave up four hundred years of stored afternoons. [562] Nobody cheered, exactly. [565] There was laughter, but it was the pensive kind, the sort that has to sit down. [581] Mira laughed that way too, with her hand over her mouth, because developing an image spends it. [598] The fog would never hold those afternoons again. [606] Whatever joy the district recovered tonight, it would have to keep by itself, in bodies, in stories, in murals someone bothered to repaint. [629] So she chalked one more question into the knowledge network the next morning: who remembers the steps? [646] By the third day she had eleven answers, two fiddlers, and an old woman who claimed the turn came on the seventh beat. [669] Mira set the fresco fragment on a shelf where the lamps could reach it, so the laughing mouth was always lit. [690] Then she went out into the grey with her lenses, and painted the dance again, nine seconds at a time, for anyone willing to learn it. [716]
