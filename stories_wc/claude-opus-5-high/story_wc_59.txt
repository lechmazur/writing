The palace hung upside down from the throats of six giant stalactites, swaying whenever the engines below changed their minds. [20] Prince Ilar had lived in that quaking reverie all his life, and he had stopped calling the swaying an inconvenience. [40] He called it breathing. [44] He was bedraggled, and knew it: hair matted with condenser grease, coat hem frayed from crawling the chain-galleries, one boot mended with copper wire. [68] A prince of a mechanical world needs no better clothes; there is no one left to impress but the machines, and machines admire only function. [93] His father had built them all: the ore-mothers, the weather-looms, the patient beetles that polished the cavern's ceiling into false constellations. [114] His father had also confessed, dying, that the whole design had proceeded from a flawed genesis. [130] The first blueprint had omitted song. [136] Nothing in the under-kingdom could make a sound that was not a measurement. [149] So Ilar had given himself one work: to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world, even if it took the rest of his ragged life. [173] His method, lacking teachers, was hypothesizing about everything. [181] Why did the stalactites hum in thirds; why did the beetles avoid the east gallery; why did rust taste faintly of fruit left in a locked room? [208] He wrote each guess on the palace walls in chalk, and tested them one by one, and was wrong perhaps nine times in ten. [232] The tenth time paid for the others. [239] The east gallery, it turned out, was where his father had hidden the archive of the world before the engines. [259] Ilar spent eleven days there, breathing dust, and on the twelfth he found the drawer marked GENESIS, SPECIMENS. [277] Inside, packed in wool, lay two hundred shards of an extinct bird's egg. [290] A label in his father's hand: Lantern-thrush. Last clutch. Broken in transit. I could not bear to sweep it up. [310] That was the flaw, then, in its whole shape: his father had not omitted song, he had killed it, and built a world that would never remind him. [338] Through the discovery and the long months after it, Ilar worked at one table under one lamp, learning to assemble the egg. [360] He hypothesized that the shell was a machine too, a first machine, and that the archive's cold had merely paused what was inside. [383] He was wrong about that, as usual, and right enough to continue. [395] He fed the shards to the ore-mothers' finest needle and made them weep glass; he taught a polishing beetle to hold a fragment steadier than his own shaking hands. [424] Fourteen pieces were lost forever, so he filled their places with hammered copper from his mended boot. [441] When it was whole it was not whole: a pale egg veined with metal, a genesis flawed a second time, this time on purpose. [465] He set it in the warmest place he knew, the throat of the southern stalactite, where the engines' exhaust came up like a slow summer. [490] The palace swayed, and he waited, and the whole hanging house seemed to hold its six-fold breath. [507] On the ninth morning the egg rocked, and something inside knocked twice, politely, like a guest. [523] What climbed out was not the bird in the archive's drawings. [534] It had one copper wing that would never fly and one true wing of feathers the color of lamplight, and it looked at Ilar with the frank attention of the newly made. [566] Then it sang. [570] The sound was bitterly sweet, half thrush and half struck wire, grief and machinery braided into one small unrepeatable phrase. [590] Below, in the deep galleries, the ore-mothers faltered on their measure, and the weather-looms hesitated, and every beetle on the ceiling turned its blind face upward. [616] Ilar sat down on the cold floor in his ruined coat and wept without dignity, because his father had been right about the flaw and wrong about the remedy. [645] You do not repair a broken beginning by forbidding beginnings. [655] You hypothesize, and you are wrong nine times, and on the tenth you assemble something imperfect that sings anyway. [674] The thrush hopped onto his wrist, gripping with one foot of bone and one of brass. [690] He carried it out to the balcony over nothing, where the six great stones held the palace up out of habit, and the false constellations shone. [716] "Sing it again," said the bedraggled prince, "and I will write down every guess I have about you." [734] It did, and the cavern shook, and beauty came back into the world crooked, which is the only way anything comes back at all. [758]
