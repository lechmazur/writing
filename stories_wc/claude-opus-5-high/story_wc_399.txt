The dunes had been murmuring since dawn, and Sena walked the line beneath them with her half-mask turned to the wind. [21] Under the sand ran the kinetic parapet, a buried rampart of old stone that shifted a hand's width each hour, always seeking the sea it would never reach. [49] Line walkers learned its path the way other people learn a heartbeat: never by staring, only through peripheral awareness, the sole of the foot listening while the eyes drifted elsewhere. [79] Where a walker held the line true, the sand quieted and drew back into a conjured clearing, a bowl of still air in which stone could stand and voices could keep their shape. [112] In the largest of these clearings stood the murmuring pavilion. [122] Its columns held the echo of every teacher who had ever spoken there, layered and patient, a whisperfield calm that made new arrivals lower their voices without being asked. [151]

Sena had worn the half-mask for eleven years, since the day she was given the pavilion to keep. [169] One eye for the world, one eye for the line: that was the discipline, and it had cost her the habit of looking anyone full in the face. [197] She walked the line each morning to keep the clearing open, because the pavilion's echoes thinned whenever the sand crept close, and she had sworn to preserve the echo of wisdom for whoever came after her. [233]

Everything changed after the prophet arrived. [239] He came out of the west on foot, sunburned, carrying nothing, and he did not lower his voice in the pavilion. [260] The crowds gathered within three days. [266] Sena expected a fraud, and found instead a man who was strangely honest, which was worse. [282]

"I have no teaching," he told the pilgrims on the first evening. [294] "I dreamed the parapet reaches water in nine years, and the dunes will green, and every clearing you have conjured will be unnecessary." [317] Then he added, "I do not know if the dream is true." [329]

That admission was what undid her. [335] Liars she could outlast; the pavilion had outlasted dozens. [344] But the pilgrims began to drift out past the boundary of her clearing to hear him speak in the open sand, where the wind ate every word, and the old voices in the columns grew fainter as fewer people came to listen to them. [388]

On the ninth night she found the prophet sitting on the pavilion steps with sand in his beard. [406] "You are killing them," she said, to the space beside his shoulder. [418] "The echoes. They need a clearing, and the clearing needs walkers, and the walkers are all out there listening to a man who admits he might be wrong." [446]

"Walk me the line," he said. "I will keep my mouth shut." [458] He was clumsy on the parapet, stepping heel-first, staring down at his boots until she made him lift his chin and feel for the stone at the edge of his attention. [489] By the third hour a small clearing opened behind them, no wider than a granary, silent as held breath. [508]

"I felt it move," he said, astonished. "It really is going somewhere." [520] "It has always been going somewhere," Sena said. "That is not wisdom. That is only stone." [536] "Then what did your teachers say that you are so afraid of losing?" [549]

She opened her mouth and found she could not quote a single line of it. [564] She had kept the sound of wisdom for eleven years and never once its meaning. [579]

At dawn she called the pilgrims back and consecrated the new clearing, pouring water from the pavilion's old basin along its rim, as walkers had done for three hundred years. [609] But she consecrated it to a different keeping. [617] "Speak here," she told the prophet, "and the columns we raise will hold your uncertainty the way the old ones hold certainty." [639] "Wisdom does not echo unless someone is honest enough to be quoted wrongly." [652]

She taught nine pilgrims the line that season, and each of them took a half-mask, and the clearings multiplied along the parapet like beads on a slow thread. [680] The prophet died in the sixth year, still uncertain, still saying so. [692] In the ninth, the parapet struck water. [699] Grass came, and the dunes stopped murmuring, and the clearings were indeed unnecessary at last. [714]

Sena, old now, walked the green line anyway, one eye on the world, and heard both pavilions behind her: the one that had told her what was true, and the one that had taught her to ask. [751] They murmured together, calm as a whisperfield, and she could no longer tell the two sounds apart. [768]
