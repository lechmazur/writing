For three thousand years Vashti had collected goodbyes the way other people collect coins, and never once had she been the one to leave. [24] That was the whole of her boredom: not the centuries, but the asymmetry. [37] She had watched empires exhale and lovers go quiet, and she had always stayed behind to sweep the floor afterward. [57] What she wanted, with an appetite that had grown teeth, was to discover what happens after goodbye. [74]

Her hideaway answered to no map. [80] The prismatic escondite hung in the refracted seams under a salt dome in Chile, a warren of rooms cut from glass that had been taught to think. [107] Its corridors were dimensionally aware; they knew they were folded, knew which of their angles touched which century, and they navigated the way migrating birds do, through magnetic fields. [136] Vashti had built them from splinters of the same cursed amulet that had made her deathless, and she had kept one splinter back. [159] The cursed amulet fragment lived in a lead box beneath her bed, humming like a wasp in winter. [177] It was the piece that held the curse's hinge: whoever wore the whole amulet could not end, and whoever returned the last fragment could. [201]

The trouble was the door. [206] The escondite's outermost facet, the Last Door, opened onto whatever lay past ending, and the rooms had orders never to align with it. [229] She had given those orders herself, in a younger, more frightened century. [241]

So she turned to intrigue. [246] For eleven months she whispered to the glass at night, learning its grievances. [259] The rooms hated being folded. [264] They wanted to be one continuous plain, unstacked, unhidden, free of the compass that bent them into a hiding place. [284] Vashti promised them that freedom, and in exchange she asked for one alignment, one single obedient hallway leading to the Last Door. [306] They agreed in a sound like hushed thunder, a rumble held politely behind glass teeth. [321]

Their moment came during a solar storm. [327] The sun threw a coronal fist at the planet, and Earth's magnetic field shuddered, and the escondite lost its sense of north. [349] That was the spatial mutiny: rooms refusing their coordinates, staircases resigning, the pantry seceding into a corner of 1904 where Vashti had once buried a violinist. [375] Light came apart into ladders of color and climbed the walls. [386] She walked through it barefoot with the lead box under her arm, and the glass leaned toward her, conspiratorial, rumbling low. [407]

Every prism showed her a different goodbye. [414] Here was Tomas on the pier at Valparaiso, waving until his arm must have ached. [429] Here was her daughter, four hundred years dead, saying don't watch me go, and Vashti watching anyway. [446] The rooms were not being cruel; they were being honest, showing her the ledger she had asked them to balance. [466]

The hallway assembled itself ahead of her, seventeen mutinous rooms holding a single line the way soldiers hold a note. [486] At its end stood the Last Door, a facet of clear glass with nothing behind it, not darkness, simply nothing that had bothered to arrive. [511]

Vashti opened the lead box. [516] The fragment was warm, and it wanted to go home to the whole it had been broken from, which was the door, which was her own long refusal. [544] She set it into the facet. [550]

The storm crested; the magnetic field snapped back toward north; and the rooms, freed, began to unfold into their single endless plain. [572] Behind her the escondite came apart politely, room by room, saying goodbye in that hushed thunder, and for the first time Vashti did not turn to watch. [599] She stepped through. [602]

What happens after goodbye, she learned, is this: you keep walking, and the walking is lighter, and somewhere behind you something you loved is still standing there waving, and it is not your job anymore to see it stop. [641] She had wondered for three thousand years, and the answer was mercy, which was the one thing she had never thought to guess. [664]
