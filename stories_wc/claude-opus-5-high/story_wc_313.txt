For eleven years the wind chime on Midnight Ridgeway had sung whenever the reservoir breathed. [15] Ada Fenn, levee warden, timed her rounds by it. [24] That evening, at twilight, it fell silent. [31] She stopped walking, because silence in her trade was never restful; it meant the water had dropped below the chime's tuning, below the old spillway lip, below the line that engineers had promised would never be crossed. [68] Below the ridgeway, Hollowmere was resurfacing. [74] Roof ridges broke the shallows like the spines of sleeping animals, and the mud held a mirrored hush, as if the drowned village and the dusk sky were studying one another. [105]

Ada had walked this embankment for twenty years, vigilant in the way the district liked: gauges read twice, seepage logged, every crack reported before it could widen. [132] Her superiors called her precisely humane, which meant she filed her reports on time and still knocked on doors before the sirens. [154]

Ten paces on, a photograph was pinned to a fence post with a nail. [168] It showed a stone bridge, a distillery yard, and a woman laughing with a bottle raised. [184] Forty paces further, another: children on the bridge parapet, feet dangling above water that did not yet exist. [202] Then a third, a fourth - a trail of photographs leading down the ridgeway toward the exposed mud. [219] She followed, telling herself it was an inspection. [227]

At the last picture she found a man kneeling in the silt with a box of prints on his knees. [247] Cael Roe, seventy-one, who had run the drowned distillery's last summer and had been suing the water board ever since. [267] "They tried to expunge us," he said, without looking up. [277] "Struck the name off the maps, burned the parish rolls, paid everybody to move and call it progress." [295] He lifted something from the mud and wiped it with his thumb: a sapphire-hued liqueur label, the gilt lettering still legible, HOLLOWMERE SLOE, 1961. [319]

Ada's hands went cold around her gauge book. [327] She knew that blue. [331] It had stood on a shelf in her grandmother's kitchen, a bottle nobody drank from, and when Ada asked about it she was told to go outside. [358] The forgotten memory came up through her like seepage through a levee: she was four years old, carried through a yard at dawn, a chime clanging on a doorframe, her grandmother weeping into a coat that smelled of sloes. [397] She had not been sent to guard the reservoir by accident; she had been born under it. [414]

"Fenn," Cael said, reading her face, "Liss Fenn's girl - I wondered how long you would go on not remembering." [433] The chime on the ridgeway, he told her, had been salvaged from her grandmother's door and hung there by him, tuned so that it sang only while the water stood above the rooftops. [466] Its silence was an invitation he had waited eleven dry years to send. [479]

Her duty was plain: log the level, report the trespasser, recommend fencing before sightseers drowned in the silt. [497] She did log the level, because the levee did not care who was right. [511] Then she asked Cael for the box. [518]

For nine evenings, while the reservoir kept shrinking, they worked at the warden's hut under a lamp, matching faces to houses and houses to the map the board had scrubbed clean. [549] Ada wrote captions in her engineer's hand, careful and unsentimental, because that was the only voice she trusted not to lie. [570] She was not doing it for the lawsuit, nor for the board, nor even for Cael. [586] She was doing it to spin ninety scattered stories into something a valley could hold in common - a shared meaning strong enough to survive the next flood. [613]

On the last Sunday of the drought they pinned four hundred photographs along the fence posts of Midnight Ridgeway and let people walk it at twilight. [639] Two hundred came, then more, descendants and strangers and one embarrassed young man from the water board. [656] Nobody spoke much; the hush moved along the line, mirrored in the still water below, and it felt less like mourning than like a name being restored. [683] At the end of the line Ada had hung the sapphire label in a glass frame, with a caption naming the distillery, the year, and the forty-one families paid to disappear. [714]

Rain came in October. [718] The water rose over the bridge, the roofs, the distillery yard, and one evening on her rounds Ada heard the chime start up again, thin and unhurried. [745] She stood a while in the dark, hand on the cold rail, and found she could listen to it now without flinching. [767] Hollowmere was under water again, but it was no longer expunged. [778]
