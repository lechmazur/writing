The crater held the orchard the way a cupped hand holds water it does not intend to drink. [18] Ninety withered apple trees stood in rings on the frozen floor, black and fingerless, subarctic wind moving through them without finding anything left to take. [43] Ilka knelt at the center, where the oldest tree had died first, and did not move. [59] She had knelt there since the moon set. [67] Frost had welded the hem of her coat to the ground, and her knees had gone past pain into a kind of distant weather, and still her hands moved only when they had to, one placement at a time. [106]

In front of her, spiraling out through the dead rings, lay the pattern. [119] It was made of what the evacuation had left behind: bottle caps, bent nails, a child's bone buttons, tin lids, shards of green glass, apple pits by the hundred. [148] Each object marked a beat. [153] Teo's beat. [155] Her son lay in the sledge behind her, wrapped to the chin, breathing in the shallow way of a boy whose fever had finally banked. [180] For six hours she had listened to his pulse through a copper tube laid against his ribs and set down one discarded thing for every beat, spacing them by the length of the silence between. [215] Fast stretches crowded; slow stretches opened into long, patient arcs. [225]

That was the whole of her art, and the whole of her heresy: to translate heartbeats into maps. [243] The Institute had called her a flash-paper alchemist, half a compliment. [254] She made paper that burned in a single white instant and left no ash, no smoke, nothing but the print of its own light. [278] They had wanted it for signals and for war. [287] She had wanted it for this. [293] Bodies know the road. [297] A sick child's heart, mapped, will show the shape of the country that made him sick -- where the water turned, where the ash fell, which valley to run toward when the crater fills with weather. [332] Nobody at the Institute believed it. [338]

The orchard-keepers had, which was why they had given her the plate before they walked out of the crater with their bundles. [360] It lay beside her now, face up on a square of felt: a storybook illustration plate, engraved copper, the old picture of the girl who carried the winter in a jar. [391] Every child in the northern settlements had fallen asleep to that image. [403] If a map had to survive a hundred years of hands, it should be a map people wanted to keep. [423] She would print the heart-map into the storybook's own engraving, and nobody would burn a storybook. [439]

From her breast pocket she took the vial of safekept starlight. [450] Nine years of clear nights, drawn down through the orchard's last good lens and held in a suspension of silver and apple spirit, cold and slow, a light that did not want to be hurried. [485] It was the only light patient enough to read a pattern this large in one exposure. [501]

She waited for the turn of the hour, when the wind in a subarctic crater dies completely and the air goes so still that snow falls straight as thread. [530] Four minutes, at most, while the world holds its breath. [540] It came. [542] The trees stopped their small creaking. [548] Ilka lifted the plate, unstoppered the vial, and poured the starlight over the flash paper stretched across the copper. [567] The paper took nine years of night in one white breath. [578] For an instant the whole crater stood up out of the dark -- ninety dead trees, the sledge, the boy's face, and the spiral of caps and nails and pits blazing like a coastline seen from very high up. [616] Then nothing, no ash, no smoke. [622]

On the plate, under the girl with her jar, a new line had bitten itself into the copper: a river of beats running south-southwest, out of the crater by the low notch, toward water that had not turned. [660] Ilka warmed a stick of orchard wax between her palms until it gave, and pressed her thumb into it, and set the seal on the plate's back corner: her mark, the date, the boy's name. [695] A sealed plate could be printed by anyone, anywhere, forever. [705]

She tried to stand and could not. [712] The frost had kept her; she tore the coat hem free and rose in pieces, joint by joint, the way the orchard had once come into leaf. [739] Teo had opened his eyes. [744] 'Did it work?' he asked. [749] She put the plate in his hands, which were warm, which was the whole answer, and pointed at the low notch in the crater wall where the light was beginning. [779]
