In the year after luck became measurable, the Bureau of Fortune sent a boy with a brass meter into the garden maze at Hollowell. [24] His name was Teodor, and his orders were simple: audit the grounds, identify the lucky, dismiss the rest. [42] At the maze's center he found Mirela, the vine dresser, withered as the stock she tended, kneeling in gravel with a jar of marbles in her lap. [69] Every marble was cracked through the middle, and she was polishing them one by one with a rag and spit. [89] His meter, held toward her, climbed until the needle trembled at the pin. [102] "You are the luckiest thing in this county," Teodor said. [112] "Luck," she said, "is what the ignorant call a woman who knows when to cut." [127] The meter, she understood, did not measure fortune at all; it measured how far a harvest strayed from what the Bureau predicted. [149] Forty years of grafting had made her strays exceed every table in Teodor's satchel. [163] So when he asked what the marbles were for, she lied, wisely and pragmatically, the way a gardener tells a sapling it is a tree. [188] "Luck-stones," she told him. [192] "Each one drinks a misfortune and keeps the crack. [201] Take them from me and the maze dies by August." [211] It was a good lie, and it bought her the summer. [222] Teodor stayed to catalogue the stones, and stayed to watch her work, and stayed because the estate's kitchen liked him. [242] He learned that the maze was a thing of blooming and withering at once: the south wall in white rose while the east arbor went brown and brittle, all in the same hour of the same wind. [279] He learned that Mirela celebrated absurdly. [285] When a single Muscat cane took to its new rootstock, she wore her dead husband's coat, poured two thimbles of wine, read aloud the cane's number from her ledger, rang the orangery bell nine times, and cracked a marble against the sundial before adding it to the jar. [333] "Small victories," she said, "must be commemorated elaborately, because the large ones never come." [347] Teodor wrote it down as ritual behavior, superstition, category four. [357] In August the Bureau's inspector general arrived with a cart, intending to confiscate the luck-stones for the national reserve. [376] Mirela could have let the lie stand and lost the jar; instead she mended it. [391] "They are not luck-stones," she said. [397] "They are receipts. [400] One for every cane I saved, and I cracked each one myself." [412] The inspector general laughed and asked why she would confess away her only protection. [426] "Because a lie that rots takes the vine with it," she said, "and I have a better wager." [444] Her wager was this: send her away for six weeks, give the maze to the boy with the meter, and measure the grounds again on her return. [471] She wanted to prove a thing no instrument in the capital had thought to test: that gardens know their gardeners. [491] Teodor was given her shears, her ledger, and her jar. [501] He read the ledger honestly, and he was not lazy, and still the maze turned on him. [518] He watered at noon and scorched the roots; he cut the Muscat in leaf and bled it white; mildew walked the hornbeam hedges like a rumor. [544] By the fifth week the brass needle over Hollowell had fallen below the county mean, and the inspector general's tables called it bad luck. [568] Teodor knew better, because he had held the shears. [577] When Mirela came back up the gravel, the east arbor was dead and the south wall was still trying to flower, which was the maze's whole character and, she thought, her own. [609] She worked eleven days without a ceremony of any kind. [619] Then, on the twelfth, a grafted cane pushed one green inch, and she sent for the coat, the wine, the bell. [640] Teodor rang it nine times himself, and she let him. [650] Afterward she took one cracked marble, worked a bead of pine resin into the split, and held it up until the sun caught the mended seam. [676] "Polish it every morning," she said, "and by the time it is clear, you will be able to read the maze without a meter." [700] He filed his report as a correction: fortune at Hollowell was not measurable, only attributable. [715] The Bureau kept its meters, of course, and the vine dresser kept her jar; but the estate kept her too, which had been the point of the lie and was now the point of the truth. [751] She died in March, in the coat, and Teodor cracked a marble against the sundial for her and rang the bell nine times, because a large loss deserves at least the honors of a small victory. [787]
</story>
