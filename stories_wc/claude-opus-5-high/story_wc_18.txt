The mirror maker's name was Odile, and she had never dreamed a night in her life. [16] This was why the guild sent her into the orchard district, where the fog had stood for four hundred years and swallowed anyone with a soft imagination. [43] She walked with her toolbox banging her knee, firmly grounded, counting her steps aloud so the mist could not steal them. [64] The orchards here had been pear and quince once. [73] Now the trees were glass mountains in miniature, every branch sheathed in centuries of frozen breath, rising in cold ranges no higher than a man yet somehow vast, so that walking among them felt like crossing a continent of ice. [113] At the district's heart, in the ruin of a cider house, the old astronomer was waiting. [129] He had been waiting, by his own reckoning, since he was nineteen. [141] "You are the one who mends glass," he said. [150] He put a broken sextant into her hands. [158] Brass, green with age, its arc pitted; both mirrors shattered, the index mirror to powder, the horizon glass into three clean wedges. [180] "I measured a dream with this," he said. [188] "I was young and I dreamed a sky better than the real one, and when I woke I wanted the coordinates, so I shot the dream's altitude the way you shoot a star." [221] "The sextant's whole trick is erasing boundaries -- the horizon glass lays sky against ground until they touch and you can read the angle between them." [246] "I erased too much. [250] The dream ran out through the gap and took the orchard's weather with it. [264] This fog is its wake." [269] Odile turned the wedges in the grey light. [277] She did not believe in dreams, but she believed in glass, and glass remembered everything it had ever reflected. [296] "I can recreate the horizon glass," she said. [304] "Same sand, same silver, same flaw. [310] A mirror is only a decision about where a boundary goes. [321] But a mirror needs still air to cool true, and your fog moves." [334] "Then we will hold it," the astronomer said. [342] They worked eleven days. [346] She built a furnace from cider-press stones and melted the orchard's own frost-glazed sand. [360] He calculated the hour: the dream had fled upon the shimmer of twilight frost, and only at that same shimmer could it be angled back into the instrument. [388] Their remaining problem was the air. [394] Fog is nothing but a thousand small breaths disagreeing. [403] So on the last evening they sat facing each other over the cooling glass and quieted it through synchronized breathing, in four counts, out six, matching until Odile could not tell which lungs were hers. [438] The fog leaned in, listened, and lay down. [446] For the first time in four centuries the orchard district had a horizon. [459] Above the glass mountains of the branches, the frost took the last light and shimmered. [474] And there, low in the west, hung the runaway dream: a sky with too many moons in it, patient as an animal that has been gone a long time and is curious whether it is still wanted. [511] The astronomer raised the mended sextant. [517] His hands shook so badly that the moons smeared. [526] "Take it," he said. [530] "You are the only thing here heavy enough to hold still." [541] Odile set her boots in the cold mud, sighted along the arc, and brought the false sky down until it kissed the real ground. [565] The boundary went out like a candle. [572] She felt the dream come into the glass the way heat comes into a hand, and she understood, too late to refuse it, that a captured dream has to be kept somewhere. [604] The astronomer was already dwindling -- not dying, only becoming the ordinary evening he had abandoned at nineteen, a thin man breathing out at last with nothing left to hold. [633] The fog did not return. [638] Frost slid off the branches; the quince trees, waking, cracked like ice on a pond. [653] Odile walked out of the orchard district at dawn with a sextant in her toolbox, and that night, for the first time in her life, she dreamed. [680] Too many moons, and mountains of glass, and a voice counting in four and out six. [696] She woke firmly grounded, as always, boots already on -- and she got up and began grinding a new mirror, wide as a door, because a dream that has run once will run again, and someone has to stand at the edge of it.
