The temple library had no date, only a hush that resounded like a struck bell left ringing in stone. [19] Ileth had been assigned there as the end of history witness, the one required to see the last event and say what it was. [43] Her only tool was a softcover dictionary, its spine broken white, its pages soft as moth wings from use. [62] The dictionary was not sacred; it was merely the last object in creation that insisted a word could mean one thing. [83] She needed that, because everything else in the library lied beautifully. [94] The shelves rearranged themselves when unwatched, and the scrolls rewrote their own histories to flatter whoever read them. [112] Ileth had come to unravel cosmic illusions, not to be comforted by them. [125]

The end arrived, as promised, across a universe's hiccup. [134] Existence caught in its throat, skipped a beat, and in that skipped beat two accounts of the final moment both became true. [156] In the first, the universe ended because it was finished, complete, a sentence with its period placed. [173] In the second, it ended because it had never begun, and the whole of it was a rumor told by no one. [195] A witness may not report two endings. [202]

So the library offered its oldest remedy: spiral arbitration. [211] The floor of the reading hall was inlaid with a single spiral, nine turns wide, worn shallow by centuries of disputing feet. [233] Two claims entered it; one truth left. [240] The method was old and strict: journeying inward while walking out. [251] The arbiter's body must travel the spiral outward, turn by turn, while attention travels the same spiral inward, turn by turn, until body and attention meet at the place that is both rim and center. [286] There, contradictions cannot stand apart. [291]

Ileth stepped onto the innermost coil and began. [299] She walked wanderingly exact, her feet drifting the curve like a wanderer's, her count of steps never off by one. [319] That was the discipline: to seem lost and never be. [329] At the first turn outward she went inward to the word finished, and looked it up. [345] The softcover dictionary said: brought to an end; having nothing left to do. [358] At the fourth turn she looked up rumor: a story passed along without a source. [373] Between entries she kept walking, because the arbitration fails the moment either journey stops. [387] The hush in the hall deepened until it rang, a silence with a bell inside it. [403]

By the seventh turn, she understood what the illusion had been doing. [415] Both endings depended on a teller standing outside the universe: one to place the period, one to pass the rumor along. [436] There was no outside. [440] There was only her, walking, counting, inside the thing she was asked to describe. [454] The two accounts were not rivals; they were one account seen from a place that did not exist. [472]

On the ninth turn her body reached the rim and her attention reached the center, and the spiral did what spirals do: it unified them. [497] The library stopped rearranging itself. [502] The scrolls held still, ashamed, honest. [508] The hiccup ended. [511]

Time resumed, and Ileth gave her testimony aloud to nobody, which is the only audience a last witness ever has. [531] She said: it did not end because it was complete, and it did not end because it was a rumor. [551] It ended the way a walker ends a spiral, by arriving where walking is no longer different from standing. [570] She set the softcover dictionary on the arbitration stone, open, so that whatever came next would inherit at least one honest book. [592] Then the temple library, lost in time as it had always been, finally agreed to be found. [609] Its doors opened onto the next universe, and the hush went with her, resounding, like a bell that had learned her name. [631]
