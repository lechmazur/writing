The Arganum was a bookstore that had eaten a mountain, or perhaps the reverse, and nobody living knew which. [19] I shelved on the ninth spiral, where the stairs doubled back on themselves and the lamps burned low. [37] Under every floor, the thunder lived. [43] It never broke; it only turned over in its sleep, shaking dust from the spines of unsold atlases. [61] I had come here after remembering became painful. [69] My valley was gone under a reservoir, and each time I reached for its shape my chest filled with gravel. [89] I wanted to understand the shape of home without bleeding for it, and the Arganum sold everything, supposedly. [107] On the twelfth spiral lived the mercury-glass seer. [115] Her face was silvered from the inside, like the old ornaments merchants hung to frighten spirits, and her eyes gave you back to yourself, curved and small. [142] She read futures via the texture of a feather. [151] You brought her one, and she drew it slowly between finger and thumb, against the grain, listening to the barbs unzip and reknit. [174] Everything true, she said, has a nap to it, a direction in which it lies flat. [190] I brought her a crow feather and the lost language stone. [201] The stone was palm-sized, grey, cut with a script nobody had spoken in nine hundred years; it was the only thing my grandmother carried out of the valley. [229] The seer turned the feather once and went very still. [239] "You can read it," she said. [245] "No one can read it." [250] "No one has tried while standing on the mountain that remembers it." [262] Then she told me what the staff whispered and the customers refused to believe: that she substitutes reality nightly. [281] Each midnight she chose the least painful of the possible days and laid it over the world like a clean sheet over a stained mattress. [306] That was why the stairs moved, why the thunder stayed buried, why nobody in the Arganum could recall arriving. [325] "I keep you all comfortable," she said, "and comfort buries talents the way snow buries seed." [341] "Mine has been buried thirty years," I said. [349] "Then stop letting me sleep for you." [356] She gave me back the crow feather and told me to stay awake through one true midnight. [373] I sat on the ninth spiral with the stone in my lap and stroked the feather backward, again and again, until my fingertips learned its ladder of hooks. [401] At midnight the substitution came, soft as a librarian's cough, and I refused it. [415] The pain arrived immediately: the reservoir, the drowned orchard, my grandmother's hands wrapping the stone in a dishcloth. [433] Under it, the thunder rose, no longer hidden, turning the shelves into a single struck drum. [449] And with my nerves scraped raw by the feather's grain, I looked at the stone and read it. [467] It was not a monument or a prayer. [475] It was an instruction written by farmers to their descendants: do not carry us, plant us. [491] The script was a seed-list, a language of sowing, and every character named a depth and a season. [509] My buried talent, it turned out, was nothing grand; I could hear what soil wanted. [524] Grandmother had it, and her mother, and the valley had made us useless the day it went under water. [543] At dawn I climbed past the twelfth spiral to the summit terrace, where the Arganum kept a garden of ink-plants and nobody came. [566] The seer followed, her silver face dulled; refusing her had cost her a night's work. [581] I knelt and pushed the lost language stone into the cold ground, four knuckles deep, as the script instructed. [600] "That was your inheritance," the seer said. [607] "It was a seed," I said, "and carrying it was the mistake." [619] Home is not a place you remember; it is a shape you make again, with your hands, in dirt that agrees to it. [642] I said that out loud, and the thunder under the mountain answered, close and low, the way a dog answers its name. [664] The seer held out her hand for the crow feather and I kept it. [678] "Then you'll have to feel the days as they truly come," she said, and something like envy moved across her mercury face. [700] By the next spring, a green thing was standing in the ink-garden with leaves shaped like the letters of a language I was slowly, painfully relearning. [726] Customers climbed twelve spirals to see it. [733] They arrived hurting, because the seer had stopped substituting the world for them, and they left carrying seed. [751] The Arganum's stairs stopped moving, the thunder came up out of the floors and became weather, and I learned the shape of home the only honest way, one furrow at a time. [783]
