The rain came back on a Tuesday, three years late, and by Thursday the subway maintenance tunnel under Ward Street had taken on eleven inches of water. [27] By Friday, Desmond Ruiz was dead in it. [35] Imogen Vashti arrived while the pumps still coughed, carrying a clipboard, a laser rangefinder, and the watchfully calm manner the city paid her for. [59] Preventable death investigator: the title always sounded like an accusation, which was accurate. [72] Her job was to decide whether a person had to die, or had merely been allowed to. [89] Director Kwan met her at the grate with an umbrella and a theory already assembled. [104] "Freak weather," he said. "Nobody could have modeled it. Write it clean, Imogen." [117] He had said write it clean four times in two years, and four times she had, and four families had gone home with nothing. [141] She climbed down anyway. [145] The tunnel smelled of wet iron and old creosote, and her headlamp found the thing before it found the body's chalk outline: a tin lantern, pierced with star-holes, hanging from a conduit hook. [178] Desmond's sister identified it later without hesitation. [185] It had come down through her family from a station on the Underground Railroad, a signal lamp set in a Delaware farmhouse window, and Desmond had carried it into the tunnels every shift for nineteen years. [221] He said it kept him honest about darkness. [229] Imogen lifted it, and her memory came apart. [237] She was standing in the flooded tunnel and she was also crouched in wet grass under a window, counting the holes in the tin, and she was also nine years old in her grandmother's kitchen, and she could not tell whose fear was whose. [281] Entangled memories, she thought, steadying herself against the wall; grief does that, and so does an object that has been held by frightened people for two centuries. [308] The visions were vivid and useless. [313] They could not be entered into a record; they could not be cross-examined; they would only let Kwan call her unwell and write it clean himself. [339] So she did what she had been trained to do, and worked through empirical observation. [354] She measured the silt line on the tunnel wall: forty-one inches. [365] She photographed the sump pump's float switch, seized with a collar of mineral scale that had taken years of neglect to grow. [387] She pulled the maintenance logs and found nine inspection entries signed in the same handwriting, all during hours when the man who signed them had been clocked into a depot four miles north. [420] She timed the drainage herself, twice, with a stopwatch and a marked stake. [433] Nothing about the water was a freak. [440] Three dry years had let the department defer the pump program, because nothing had flooded, and because a drought is a persuasive argument for doing nothing. [466] After the drought ended, the argument ended too, and the bill came due in one man's lungs. [483] Kwan read the draft in his office with the rain drumming the window. [496] "You understand what this does to me," he said, quietly, almost kindly. "And to you." [511] For two years his kindness had been the leash, and she had told herself that staying close to him was how she did any good at all. [538] That was the part she needed to break. [546] Not him, exactly, but the habit of borrowing his nerve instead of growing her own. [561] "I'm filing it as preventable," she said. [568] Her hands shook and she let them. [575] She had learned, holding a dead man's lantern in cold water, that being frightened and being steady were not opposites; you could build a wall around a soft thing and still let the soft thing breathe. [611] The findings went public on a Monday. [617] Nine tunnels were rescheduled for pump replacement; two supervisors resigned; Kwan kept his job and stopped speaking to her, which felt less like punishment than weather. [643] At the hearing, when she read the silt measurements aloud, her voice reverberated down the marble hall and came back to her sounding like someone she wanted to be. [672] Afterward she gave the lantern back to Desmond's sister on a porch that smelled of wet earth. [689] The woman set it in the window and lit it, the old signal, the one that meant: keep coming, it is safe here tonight. [713] Imogen stood in the rain and watched the star-holes throw their small constellation onto the glass, and she did not need to be told whose memory that was. [741] It was all of theirs. [746]
