The beekeeper came every thaw for forty years, and the spring he did not come, I knew the grotto city was dying. [22] I am Antol Verrey, guide to the Museum of the Drowned Valley, and I am historically oriented the way other men are religious. [45] Our city sits behind Hesper Falls, in a grotto the old engineers hollowed when the reservoir swallowed nine towns and left us, eleven thousand displaced persons, with nowhere higher to go. [76] Water is our ceiling and our clock. [83] In winter the falls glaze over and the noise stops, and we live in a silence that smells of wet lime; at the thaw the roar returns, and with it the pedlars, the mail, and once the beekeeper with his wax and his rosewater honey. [128] That honey mattered more than sweetness. [134] His wax sealed our manuscripts; his honey glazed the bread. [144] You must understand the bread. [149] When the valley was flooded, the registries went under with the churches, and my grandmother, who could not read, invented an archive she could hold. [174] Each Sunday she baked loaves shaped like the initials of everyone her family had lost - V for Vidor, K for Katrine, a long-armed S for the town of Solt - and we ate them, and in eating learned the names. [213] Every family in the grotto keeps the custom now, though fewer each year, and the rosewater glaze gives our whole quarter a perfume that visitors call lovely and I call dread, because I can smell the arithmetic in it: less wax, thinner dough, shorter alphabets. [258] My museum has four rooms and one true treasure. [267] Not the mayor's chain, not the drowned altar bell - but a single emerald-tinted opera glass lens, all that came up from the Solt Opera House when the divers went down in the second year. [301] I hold it up for schoolchildren and let them look through it at the waterfall, and the falling water turns green as a stage curtain, and I say: this is what your great-grandparents saw when they watched the last performance of The Beekeeper's Daughter. [345] Then I sing four bars of it, badly, because the last woman who could sing it properly died in February. [365] This is my work, and my grief: I am the curator of dying arts, and I keep failing to rescue them. [386] So when the thaw came and no beekeeper came with it, I did something a cultured man should be ashamed of. [407] I closed the museum. [411] I carried the lens, three jars of last year's honey, and my grandmother's dough-board up the wet stairs to the Hall of Cisterns, and I sent word through every quarter that the museum would now be taught instead of shown. [451] Nineteen people came the first evening. [457] A cooper's widow who remembered the opera's second act. [466] Two boys who wanted the honey. [472] A girl named Mira, thirteen, who had never eaten a letter loaf because her family came late, from a different flood, and had no custom to carry. [499] I gave her the board. [504] We baked her mother's initials first - an R and a D, lopsided, glazed with the last of the rosewater - and while they rose I taught the boys to hold the lens steady and describe, in order, everything the green light showed them. [546] The cooper's widow sang. [550] Mira wrote the notes down in charcoal, since none of us could read music. [564] It was not preservation. [568] It was closer to grafting. [573] In summer a tinker brought news: the beekeeper had died in his own bed above the reservoir, and his hives had been sold down-country, and no one on the high road kept bees anymore. [607] Mira asked whether that meant the bread would stop. [616] I told her the truth, which is that honey was never the point, only the sweetness we could afford; that her grandchildren might glaze their letters with beet syrup or nothing at all and the names would still be there. [656] She has the lens now, on loan, with a receipt I wrote in my most cultured hand. [673] She shows it to the younger children on the ledge where the spray comes in, and tells them the water is green because a city looked at itself through an opera glass and refused to forget. [709] That is not history, strictly speaking. [715] But I am historically oriented, not historically imprisoned, and next thaw we will bake the letter H for Hesper, and eat our own address, and begin. [741]
