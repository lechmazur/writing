The lamp at Quill Rock flickered out on a Tuesday, and Nesta Vane, who spoke perhaps two hundred words a month, said none of them. [25] She turned the supply boat north into a chop the color of old nickel and let the engine do her talking. [46] Nine keepers on her route, nine stone cairns on nine landings, and every stack said something if you knew the grammar. [67] Flat stone crowning the pile meant fuel; a round one meant flour and lamp wick; a green-veined stone laid beside the stack, not on it, meant a keeper was sick and would not write it down. [103] Nesta had built the grammar herself, twenty years back, for a man too proud to ask. [119] Quill Rock's cairn had been knocked to gravel by the same gale that killed the light. [135] That told her nothing, which told her everything. [143]

She tied off at the iron ladder and climbed through the fog to the door, and the new keeper opened it before she knocked: a girl of nineteen named Odile, eyes red, coat buttoned wrong. [178] "It won't stay lit," Odile said. [184] "Something up there doesn't want it lit." [191] Nesta nodded and went up. [196]

The lantern room at Quill Rock was haunted; every skipper on the coast agreed, and Nesta, who did not believe in ghosts, believed in that room. [222] It held cold in its brass the way a well holds cold, and the glass panes hummed a note no wind explained, and the floorboards kept the shape of a man who had paced them for thirty-one years. [260] Aldous Reeve, dead in March, who had never once asked for help. [272] Nesta found the wick fouled with salt crust and the oil reservoir cracked fine as a hair. [289] No ghost; just a machine grieving in the only way machines can. [301] She worked two hours without speaking while Odile held the lamp and watched her hands. [316]

Under the service bench, wedged behind a tin of graphite, was the bottle. [329] Nesta drew it out and set it in the girl's lap. [340] Inside were forty-odd letters, folded small, none of them signed. [350] "It goes around," Nesta said, and that was a speech for her. [362] The rule was simple: whoever received the bottle read every letter, wrote one of their own, kept nothing back and put no name to it, then passed it up the coast with the supply boat. [397] Anonymous, so a keeper could confess terror to eight strangers who were not strangers. [411]

Odile read until dawn. [415] Nesta watched her read, warmly, the way she watched weather: noting the shoulders come down, the jaw unclench, the buttons finally done right. [438] "This one's him," Odile said at last. [445] "I can tell. He says he's afraid of the dark, at his age." [458]

Nesta took the piece of sea glass from her coat pocket and turned it over: green, thumb-sized, ground soft by forty years of surf. [482] It had been the neck of the first bottle, broken on the rocks at Sable Head the winter she started the chain. [504] She had wanted, then, to bottle the feeling of safety and ship it to people who had none, and she had been laughed at by a harbormaster and had done it anyway. [536] She set the sea glass in Odile's palm. [544] "Anchor for the letters," she said. [550] "So the bottle sits right." [555] It was not what she meant. [561] What she meant was that the room would go on humming and the brass would go on holding cold, and a girl alone here in February needed something in her hand that the sea had spent forty years making gentle. [601]

At six the lamp caught and held, and the light went out across the water in its slow wheel, and Nesta felt the ache of it, that luminescent ache, the beam touching everything it could not stay with. [639] Going down the ladder she stopped and rebuilt the cairn on the landing. [652] Flat stone, round stone, and on top a fourth she had never used: a small white one, meaning nothing yet, meaning a keeper here is new and the boat will come early. [684] The other eight would see it within the month and understand; that was how you protected someone without ever saying the word. [706] Odile shouted something from the gallery, thanks probably, lost in the gulls. [718] Nesta lifted one hand, did not turn around, and went to start the engine. [732]
