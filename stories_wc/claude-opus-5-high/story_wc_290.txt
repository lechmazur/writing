Mira Vance had surveyed eleven ruins and hated every one of them. [12] Narrow places pressed on her lungs like a closing fist, yet she kept going down into them, prudently adventurous, always with three exits mapped before she took one step. [41] The fallow period was the only season the village of Aldenmoor allowed visitors below the church. [57] With the fields unplanted and the plows oiled and hung, the elders had nothing to guard but their secret. [76] Her grandfather had drawn that secret once, and had been laughed out of the guild for it. [93] His blueprint survived; the building it described did not stay still. [104] In his margins he had written two words in a shaking hand: spatial mutiny. [118] The crypt of Saint Enda refused its own plan. [127] Corridors traded places. [130] Doors migrated. [132] Stairs decided, overnight, to descend from a different wall. [141]

Mira carried the only instrument she trusted: the steel point from her grandfather's drafting compass, worn to a needle by forty years of arcs. [165] She used it to scratch her own marks into the mortar, since the crypt erased chalk and ate string. [184] What she wanted lay at the center: a vital key lost to time, the iron key to the reliquary that held Aldenmoor's charter. [207] Without the charter, the landlords could enclose the commons, and the fallow fields would never be the village's again. [226] The elders had stopped believing anyone could reach it. [234] Mira believed, because her grandfather had left instructions, and they were absurd. [246] 'The stone obeys the organ,' he wrote. [253] 'Hold a low note and the walls hold still.' [262]

So she paid the blind organist, Teodor, four silver coins and a promise, and at dusk he climbed to the loft of the empty church above her. [289] She descended while the first pedal tone began. [297] The reverberation of that lonely organ came down through the pillars like water finding roots, and the crypt, astonishingly, settled. [317] Every doorway locked into the shape her grandfather had drawn. [327] She moved fast, one hand dragging the compass point along the wall, breathing in counts of four so the ceiling would not seem to lower. [352] Twice the note faltered as Teodor changed his hands, and twice the passage narrowed around her shoulders until she tasted iron. [373] She did not run. [377] She counted. [379] She waited for the drone to return, and it did. [389]

At the heart of the labyrinth she found what the blueprint called the nexus, and it was not what she expected. [410] Eight corridors met beneath a collapsed dome, and the shaft above was open, so the shadowed nexus lay under raw starlight, cold and enormous. [434] For the first time in her life, underground, Mira felt her chest open. [447] The sight transfixed her, and being transfixed nearly killed her. [457] The organ above had shifted into a hymn, sweet and wandering, and the corridors began to swing like compass needles. [477]

She understood then why the crypt rebelled: it had been built by masons who were losing their land, and they had hidden the charter inside a building that would never again take orders. [510] It was a wistful rebellion, stone grieving and refusing at once, and it had outlived everyone it was meant to protect. [531]

The reliquary stood at the center on a plinth, sealed, its lid seamed with rust. [546] There was no keyhole. [550] Instead there was a small round socket, the diameter of a drafting point, drilled precisely where her grandfather's plan bore his signature mark. [573] He had not merely drawn the building. [580] He had been given its key, and had drawn it as a mark so no landlord would recognize it. [599]

Mira set the worn steel into the socket and turned. [609] The lid lifted; vellum lay inside, dry as a moth. [619] Above her the hymn ended, and the eight corridors, released, began to rearrange themselves in the dark. [636] Mira did not panic. [640] She had mapped three exits, and one of them was straight up, through starlight, where no wall could move. [659]

She climbed the rubble of the fallen dome with the charter in her shirt, and came out among the bare fields at midnight. [682] Teodor was waiting at the churchyard gate, hands in his sleeves. [693] 'Did the stone obey?' he asked. [699] 'No,' she said, laughing until her eyes stung. [707] 'It agreed.' [709] In spring the commons were plowed by the people who owned them, and Mira went home to draw a map of a place that had finally chosen to stay. [738]
