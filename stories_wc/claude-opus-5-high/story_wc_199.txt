Vessa sorted seeds the way other people prayed. [8] Up in the ashen skyborne arbor, where the great tree hung rootless over the valley and shed grey flakes like slow snow, her trays clicked in rows of forty. [37] Beanlike, winged, hooked, dust-fine: each kind had a drawer, and each drawer had a label, and the labels were her comfort. [58] The village below called her the Orderly, and sent up baskets by rope, and did not climb. [75] They were frightened of the folklore. [81] Every grandmother in the Cinder Valley told it the same way: once a year, in the hush where past meets prophecy - that thin minute between the last bell of remembering and the first bell of foretelling - the arbor drops a message in falling leaves. [125] Whoever reads it emerges transformed, always - and the grandmothers never agreed on whether that was a blessing. [142] Vessa wanted to read it. [147] Not for prophecy; she had no interest in futures. [156] She wanted the past half - the remembering - because the tree was clearly trying to say something, and nobody in three hundred years had bothered to learn its alphabet. [184] Her method was monuments. [188] In the crooks of the branches she built tiny monuments to forgotten things, each no taller than a thumb: a cairn of ash for the drowned orchard at Lowmire, a spiral of husks for the dialect her mother spoke, a splinter of blue tile for the bathhouse that burned before she was born. [241] The arbor liked this. [245] She could tell because leaves fell where she worked, one at a time, politely, like someone clearing a throat. [264] Moss crept over her finished monuments within a day, and she found she loved that - the green swallowing her small griefs with such unbothered appetite. [289] On the morning of the hush she carried up her last piece: a moonstone cabochon her grandfather had cut and never sold. [311] He had been a lapidary in a valley that no longer needed one; the trade was forgotten, so it deserved a monument. [333] She set it on a plinth of bark, and as she polished the stone a single dust speck lifted from it and settled in her eye. [359] She blinked, prodded at the corner of her eyelid with one sorting-calloused finger, and stopped. [374] Through that speck of moonstone, the world had gone silver and legible. [386] The bells rang below - the last of remembering, then the hush. [397] The arbor let go of everything at once. [405] Ten thousand grey leaves came down, and through the moonstone dust they were not grey; they were ordered. [423] Vessa did the only thing she knew how to do. [433] She sorted them. [436] Hooked leaves to the left, winged to the right, notched in the middle, by weight, by fall-rate, by the angle at which they entered the light. [462] Her hands knew forty rows; the message had thirty-nine. [471] It was not prophecy at all, or not only. [480] It was an inventory. [484] The arbor had been keeping a list - every name the valley had let fall, every tool, every drowned orchard, every dialect, every lapidary - and it had been dropping that list annually for three hundred years, waiting for someone patient enough to file it. [527] The prophecy was simply the last row: what is remembered comes back. [539] Under her fingers the sorted leaves were already going green. [549] Moss ran along the veins like rumor. [556] The drowned orchard's row put out a bud that smelled of wet apples; her mother's dialect row made a soft burred noise, a word Vessa had not heard since she was six. [588] She laughed out loud, which startled her; she had not known that grief, properly sorted, came out the other side as delight. [610] When the first bell of foretelling rang, Vessa climbed down. [620] The villagers saw what the grandmothers had promised: she emerged transformed, always the way readers do - though not into a bird or a ghost or a prophet. [647] She came down green-handed, moss in the seams of her knuckles, carrying thirty-nine labels. [661] "I need help," she said. [666] "There are three hundred years of things to put back, and the tree keeps writing." [681] They prodded each other, uncertain, the way valley people do. [691] Then an old man stepped forward and said the burred word back to her, correctly. [706] By spring the ashen arbor was hung with a thousand tiny monuments, and the leaves that fell on them fell slower, as though a thing being read is in no hurry to finish. [739]
