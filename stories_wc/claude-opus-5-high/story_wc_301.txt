Ilse Marek had chronicled the river Varn for eleven years, and in all that time she had never written down the one true thing. [24] Her ledgers held everything else: flood crests, drowned willows, the migration of gravel bars, each entry keyed to map references she copied from the old survey sheets. [51] Grid and date, grid and date; the discipline had kept her upright. [63] Three weeks after stopping the medication, the discipline began to speak back. [75] She had not quit carelessly. [80] She had tapered by the doctor's schedule, logged her sleep, warned two friends, and set out on foot along the towpath with a rucksack, a tin of pencils, and her brother's accordion, its bellows patched with silver tape. [118] Daringly methodical, her friend Ruth called it, which was kinder than reckless. [130] The flatness of the pills had been mercy for four years, but mercy is not the same as truth, and Ilse was a chronicler. [154] She wanted to feel the thing she had done and still find a path to self-forgiveness on the other side of it. [176] Her plan was a survey: one site a day, upstream, ending at the place she had avoided for four years. [196]

On the fourth day she reached Ashlock Halt, a disused railway station overgrown with ivy so thick the platform canopy had become a green animal, breathing in the wind. [225] The ferry crowds had come here once; Tobin had busked on that platform, working those taped bellows for coins and applause. [246] Ilse sheltered from the rain in the ladies' washroom, where the mirror was gone and the walls were a century of pencil and knife and marker. [272] She read it the way she read the river: methodically, left to right, top to bottom. [288] Lovers, soldiers, schoolgirls, drunks. [292] "IF YOU ARE READING THIS YOU SURVIVED THE WAITING ROOM." [302] "DON'T GO BACK FOR HIM, HE WILL ONLY ASK AGAIN." [312] "THE RIVER KEEPS NOTHING IT WASN'T GIVEN." [319] Bathroom graffiti wisdom, Tobin used to call it, the only honest literature in the country. [334] He never confessed anything directly; he would only allude, sideways, in chalk and in songs. [349]

And there, low on the wall beside the cistern pipe, in the blocky capitals she had known since childhood, was his hand. [371] "T.M., grid 47.8 by 112.3, ASK HER TO COME ANYWAY." [381] It was a map reference in the old survey system, the same one she had used for eleven years without ever guessing why the numbers soothed her. [408] He had taught her to read them, summers on the gravel bars, and he had left her a coordinate on a bathroom wall because he could not say the sentence out loud. [440] He had phoned her that November night and asked, in his sideways way, whether she was busy. [457] She had said she was. [462] They found him below the weir at first light, and the accordion on the bank, dry, its bellows split. [481] She had mended it with silver tape because she could not bear to have it properly repaired. [498]

Grid 47.8 by 112.3 was the shingle spit two miles above the weir, the site she had left for last. [518] She walked it the next morning with the rain gone and her hands shaking in a way that was, she decided, only weather in the body. [544] The spit was where they had camped as children; he had marked it for her not as a grave but as a place they had both been happy. [572] That was the truth she had been unable to write: that he had asked, and she had refused, and that she had also loved him for twenty-nine years without pause. [602] Both facts held; neither cancelled the other; the river took only what was given to it, and she had not given him. [624]

She sat on the shingle and worked the accordion, badly, the tape wheezing at every pull, and the sound was ugly and alive. [647] It was not peace. [651] It was raw solace, the kind you get from a stranger's handwriting in a ruined washroom: someone stood here, someone survived the waiting. [674] Afterwards she opened the ledger and wrote the entry properly, with the date, and the map reference, and the name of the person who had asked her to come. [703] Then she walked back to Ashlock Halt, pushed the ivy aside, and added a line of her own beneath his, in pencil, for whoever came next: TELL THEM YES IF YOU CAN, AND IF YOU COULDN'T, COME ANYWAY. [741] She was still shaking, and she went on upstream. [750]
