My father, a compulsive gambler, could recite the house edge on any game in the city, and he played them all anyway. [22] He called that a lucid contradiction, and he taught me the phrase before he taught me to ride a bicycle. [42] I grew up to be a professor of probability, which he considered the family's one genuine miracle. [59] After the heart attack, the hospital let me keep three things: his wallet, a pressed clover gone brown as tea, and the candle lantern with the cracked mica pane. [88] He had carried that lantern on night shifts underground, back when he ran numbers for men who preferred not to be photographed. [110] In the ICU he squeezed my wrist and whispered, "Seat codes, Nell. Ride the whole line." [126] Then the monitors went flat and the nurses moved like weather. [137]

I am theoretically rebellious. [141] In seminars I argue that every model is a polite lie, that certainty is the true vice, that my students must learn to widen perspectives responsibly rather than shrink them into comfort. [173] Then I go home, eat the same soup, and have never once bought a lottery ticket. [189] My father bet on horses named after saints; I bet on nothing, which is also a bet. [206]

So on the first humid morning of midsummer I took the lantern down into the subway to traverse the line he meant, all nineteen stops, reading the seats. [234] His old trade worked by interpreting cryptic scrawls on subway seats: a courier would mark the vinyl in ballpoint, and the next man would read it and move money. [263] He had shown me the grammar once, as a joke, on a Sunday when my mother was still alive. [282] Now the joke was a will. [288]

At Parkside, under the third seat by the door, someone had drawn a clover with three leaves and a crossed stem. [309] Three leaves meant "not luck," he used to say, because luck is the four-leaf lie people tell to avoid arithmetic. [329] The crossed stem meant north, so I rode north. [338] At Halloway a tally of seven strokes, one struck through, sent me up to the transfer. [354] At Ember Street a tiny box with a dot inside meant a room with one window. [370] Rain had come through the ventilation grates all week, and the whole system smelled like wet pennies and cut grass from the parks above. [394]

By afternoon the scrawls had spelled out a place instead of a person, and I understood that he had not been sending me to a bookie. [420] The last mark, gouged deep into seat vinyl at Cantwell Avenue, was a lantern. [434] Above that station stands the branch library where he had waited out winters between jobs, and where the reading room has one window. [457] The rain had washed the stone facade so clean it looked apologetic. [469] Inside, the rainwashed light came through in long green bars, and the librarian said the power had been out since noon. [490] I lit the candle in the lantern, and the mica pane turned the flame into something patient and amber, the way it must have looked in tunnels forty years ago. [520]

Shelf 519, probability and statistics, third volume from the left: my own dissertation, which I had never known he owned. [540] Inside the back cover he had taped a bank envelope and a note. [553] Forty-one thousand dollars, saved a hundred at a time from the only thing he was ever disciplined about, which was hiding money from himself. [577] The note said: "You tell them the odds. Now teach somebody who never got shown the board." [594]

I sat a long while with the clover on the table and the flame behind mica, feeling the particular sadness of being understood by a man I had spent thirty years correcting. [626] He had been reckless with his life and scrupulous with mine; I had been rigorous on paper and timid everywhere else. [647] Two lucid contradictions, riding the same line in opposite directions. [657]

In September I endowed a seminar for students from the neighborhoods along that route, sixteen seats, no tuition. [675] We begin with dice and end with insurance, mortgages, plea bargains, and the arithmetic of hope, because a perspective widened carelessly just becomes another way to lose. [702] The lantern sits on the desk at the front of the room, unlit, its mica pane clouded like a small overcast sky. [724] When a student asks about it, I tell them my father knew every edge in the city and never once beat it, and that he still managed to place one bet that paid. [757] Then we open the books, and I let the clover stay pressed where he left it. [773]
