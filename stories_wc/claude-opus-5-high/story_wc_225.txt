The village of Ilesk had been holding its breath for forty years, and Kadir Vance walked into the middle of that breath carrying a platinum labyrinth in his coat pocket. [30] Nothing moved. [32] A baker stood at his door with flour suspended around his hands like a small weather system; a girl hung two inches above the cobbles, mid-jump, her braid frozen in a comma. [64] Only in the lull of distant thunder, when the storm on the western ridge drew its next breath, did the hush loosen enough for a stranger to enter. [92]

Kadir had been exiled for nine of those forty years. [102] He had taught chess to the children of the Ossar court, and to one boy in particular he had taught a sacrifice so elegant that the boy used it in life instead of on a board, and died of it. [142] The court had sent Kadir away; the sending away was the easy part. [155] The labyrinth had been the boy's gift, a palm-sized platinum maze with a single steel bead inside, and Kadir's hands worked at it constantly, tilting, listening, rolling the bead through dead ends. [187] He fidgeted productively; the bead's small clicking was how he thought, the way other men paced. [203]

In the weaving-hall at the village's heart hung the tapestry that had done all this. [218] Forty years ago the weavers of Ilesk, hearing that an army was coming up the valley, had woven a hush: a silence so complete it stopped the hour itself, so that the soldiers passed through a village that seemed made of painted stone. [261] The army passed. [264] The hush stayed. [267]

Kadir had come because a hush is a problem with a solution, and he needed, badly, to solve something he had not ruined. [290]

The tapestry's center showed the valley in green and ash. [300] Its border was where the work lived: a band of knotwork in which every seventh thread had been left frayed, deliberately, the way a composer leaves a rest. [328] The frayed threads were instructions. [332] Kadir sat on the floor with the labyrinth clicking in his left hand and began to decode them, winnowing the true threads from the decorative ones, discarding a hundred flourishes to find the eleven knots that carried meaning. [370] It was chess, essentially: winnow the ninety moves that lose, keep the one that does not. [386]

Work only advanced during the lulls. [392] When thunder rolled somewhere beyond the ridge, the hush thickened and his fingers went slow as cold honey; in the silences between, the threads gave. [417] He worked for three days in that rhythm, and he did not hurry, and he was not calm. [435] It was a fierce repose, the stillness of a man holding a blade steady over a wound. [452]

The eleven knots, read in order, gave a sentence in the old valley dialect: THE HUSH ENDS WHEN ONE INSIDE IT IS WILLING TO BE HEARD. [478]

Kadir understood then that the weavers had not been trapped by accident. [490] They had hidden so well that none of them would consent to be found; each of them, in the moment of freezing, had believed the army was still out there. [520] He was inside it now. [525]

He set the platinum labyrinth on the flagstones, and the bead stopped, and his hands had nothing to do, which frightened him more than the silence did. [552] Then, in the next lull of the distant thunder, he spoke aloud into the weaving-hall. [567] He said the boy's name, Teodar. [573] He said that he had taught him that a sacrifice is beautiful, and had never taught him that it is also a loss, and that this was his fault and would remain his fault, and that he intended to go on living anyway, teaching openings to farmers' children in towns that had never heard of the Ossar court. [631] It was not an apology; apologies go outward, and there was no one left to send one to. [649] It was the other thing, the harder thing, done out loud so that a room could witness it. [667]

The border of the tapestry unraveled from both ends at once, quietly, like a held note released. [684] Outside, flour fell. [687] The girl landed. [690] Somewhere a dog, mid-bark for forty years, finished the bark. [700] The baker looked up the valley for an army four decades gone and saw only rain moving in, and the thunder came close at last, unmuffled, enormous. [727]

Kadir picked up the labyrinth, turned it once, and let the bead roll clean through to the center for the first time in nine years. [752] Then he put it away and went out to help them remember what year it was. [768]
