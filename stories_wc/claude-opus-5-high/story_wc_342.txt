The demolition crew had given Ilsa one hour inside the Bole, and she spent the first four minutes simply listening. [20] Nothing in the hollowed trunk moved. [26] Three hundred years ago the Scholars' Guild had burned and scraped the heartwood out of a redwood nine men could not embrace, then racked the walls with shelves that spiraled up like the inside of a shell. [63] Now the roots were rotten, the city had condemned it, and at six o'clock the charges would fold the whole cathedral of paper into the ground. [89]

Ilsa was a portrait artist who had never once recognized a face. [101] She painted them beautifully; she simply could not hold them. [111] Her clients arrived as weather and voice and the particular slope of a shoulder, and she rendered them by measurement, by shadow, by the arithmetic of skin. [138] Because she could not trust her eyes, she had trained her hands. [150] That was why the guild's last archivist, dying, had told her about the book safe. [165]

On the ninth turn of the spiral, in a niche of shelving where the bark still showed through, stood a volume bound in oxblood leather: Meteorologica, spine uncracked, pages fused into a block. [198] It was not a book at all but a steel box wearing a book's skin, and its lock had no keyhole. [219] It had a dial of twelve blank positions. [227]

From her coat Ilsa took the archivist's gift, a centuries-old coin worn smooth as soap, and turned it under the work lamp. [249] Nothing showed. [251] So she closed her eyes and read it the way she read a stranger's cheekbone: with the pads of her fingers, slowly, twice. [274] Through faint indentations on the coin's face she found them: twelve tiny punch marks, some doubled, arranged around the rim like hours. [296] She set the dial to match and the lid sighed open. [307]

Inside lay no treasure, only forty folded sheets, and on them the guild's last commission, drawn in ink so fine it looked like frost. [331] They had wanted to mirror the sky inside walls. [340] Not a painted ceiling of cherubs, but the true sky: a vault of hand-cut glass tesserae that would hold dawn, noon, and the blue of six o'clock at once, each tile a smaller copy of the whole vault, fractally precise, pattern inside pattern inside pattern. [385]

The margins carried the scholars' calculations, and Ilsa, reading them in the hush of the doomed trunk, understood why the work had never been built. [410] The pattern did not stop. [415] It could not stop. [419] Every tile demanded nine smaller tiles, and those nine demanded eighty-one, and the guild had gone on cutting glass into dust until the last of them died old and blind, having finished, by their own count, eleven percent of one quadrant. [460]

Ilsa sat down on the lowest step of the spiral with the sheets on her knees. [476] Outside, the crew's radios had gone quiet; the neighborhood had been cleared; even the gulls had left the roofline. [495] The stillness was so complete she could hear the dry shelves ticking as the building settled toward its last minute. [515]

She had spent her life proving that a limit could be worked around. [527] She could not keep a face, so she had learned to craft one from angles and light, and no one had ever guessed. [550] But here, in a room built by people who refused to accept any edge at all, she saw the other kind of limit: not the one you outwit, but the one you bow to, or it eats your whole life and calls it devotion. [594]

A horn sounded twice across the street. [601] Ilsa took only four sheets, the first four, the scale a human hand can actually cut, and left the other thirty-six in the steel book. [626] She closed the lid, spun the dial to nothing, and set the coin on top of it like a payment. [646] Then she walked out of the trunk into the cold morning, and behind her the redwood came down in one long exhalation, three hundred years of shelving folding inward with almost no sound at all. [681]

Eight years later, in a small chapel of no importance, there is a ceiling of blue and white glass that visitors describe, wrongly, as infinite. [706] It stops at the fourth scale. [712] The woman who made it comes on clear afternoons and sits beneath it, and she cannot tell you the faces of the people sitting beside her, but she knows the sky is in the room, finished, and that she chose where it ended. [755]
