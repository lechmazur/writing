The ice cavern breathed the way a sleeper breathes, in and out, a slow oscillating repose that Vesna had learned to ring against. [23] She was the last prism-song bell ringer in the northern reach, and she had until dawn to save her craft. [43] The Bureau of Essential Labor had sent its letter in autumn: bells were decorative, therefore surplus; the foundry wagons would arrive at first thaw. [67] Vesna intended to certify wonder as essential, and she intended to do it with evidence no clerk could dispute. [86] The evidence was a sculpted hush. [91]

Her grandmother had described it once: a silence given shape by sound, a held form of quiet that lingers in a room after the right chord dies, measurable, weighable, real. [121] Ring the cavern correctly and the hush would stand in the air like a carved thing, and even an inspector would have to write it down. [147]

Vesna was synesthetically blessed, as all her line had been; she tasted flats as cold iron, saw the note A as a wheel of green. [172] It was not a gift for comfort. [179] It was a gift for reading the amber music sheet. [189] The sheet hung at the cavern's throat, a slab of fossil resin as broad as a door, threaded with dark inclusions that meant nothing to ordinary eyes. [216] The notation only appeared through the colors of the aurora. [226] When green light poured down the shaft above, one voice of the score surfaced; when the red curtain shivered across, a second voice answered; violet gave the rests. [254] Vesna read it the way other people read weather, and tonight the sky was generous. [269]

There remained the problem of the downbeat. [276] A sculpted hush requires a first stroke it did not make itself; the ringer must answer the world, not start it. [297] So her grandmother had bargained, decades ago, with the stonecutters' guild in the capital. [311] The score could be performed at one instant only: at the moment a sculptor's chisel first strikes marble, when the block is still whole and the figure inside it is still every figure at once. [346] That was the definition of wonder, her grandmother said: the held breath before the first cut. [362] The guild had agreed to begin a commission at dawn, and Vesna, four hundred miles north, would hear it. [381] She would hear it because the synesthetically blessed hear stone: a chisel's first strike arrives in the mind as a white spike, unmistakable, no matter the distance. [408]

Now she climbed the ice stair to the bell. [417] It was called Prism, and it was chained. [425] Her grandmother had fettered it herself after the last failed attempt, when a mistimed peal had cracked the cavern's east gallery and buried two apprentices. [450] To unfetter Prism was to accept that risk again, and Vesna stood a long moment with the cold key in her palm, breathing with the cavern, in and out. [479] Then she turned the key, and the chains slid off the yoke like water. [493]

Above, the aurora came in wide slow bands, green then red then violet, and the amber sheet lit from within. [513] Vesna took the rope. [517] The white spike came at 5:41, small and absolute, a sculptor four hundred miles away setting steel to stone. [536] She pulled. [538]

Prism swung, and its voice split as it always did, a fan of colored tones spraying across the ice walls, green and copper and a low bruised indigo that she felt in her teeth. [572] She rang the aurora's score, voice against voice, rests where the violet said rest, and the cavern rang back in its slow oscillating repose, pushing and returning, never breaking. [601] On the last stroke she caught the rope and stopped it dead. [613]

The hush arrived. [616] It stood in the cavern with edges, a shape you could walk around, cold and enormous and calm, and it did not fade for eleven minutes. [642] The Bureau's inspector, who had come to count bells for scrap and had spent the night wrapped in furs by the cavern mouth, stood inside that silence with his pen not moving. [674] When it finally thinned, he wrote one line in his ledger and showed it to her. [690] Observed: a sculpted hush, duration eleven minutes. [697] Classification: essential. [699]

Vesna rehung the chains loosely, not to bind Prism but to keep it from swinging in the wind, and went out into the green morning. [724] Somewhere south, a sculptor was making a second cut, and a third, and the block was becoming one thing instead of all things. [747] That was fine. [750] Wonder had been written down, and a thing written down is hard to melt. [764]
