Ysolde had been the summer-quill archivist for eleven years, and she had never once written in winter. [17] Her quills were cut from the wing feathers of birds that migrated only in July, and they held ink no colder season could thaw. [41] This mattered, because the fog had become solid on a February morning nine years ago, and everything after that date was winter. [63] The stormlit observatory above the clouds had once studied stars; now it studied people who could not remember being people. [83] When the fog hardened, it sealed the lowland city under a lid of pale glass, and the sealed generation woke without a past. [106] They had hands that knew trades and tongues that knew songs, but no names for their own childhoods. [124] Ysolde's work was to give them back a collective memory, not as a list of facts, but as something they could stand inside. [147] The observatory's crystal forecourt helped, though it only spoke after rain. [158] Storms broke against the mountain most evenings, and when the water withdrew across the faceted stone, the forecourt whispered. [177] It gave back the voices of everyone who had ever crossed it: pilgrims, surveyors, children, her own mother counting steps. [197] The whispers arrived layered and unlabeled, a thousand years of weather-worn talk, and dating them was the whole difficulty. [216] So Ysolde dated them by examining shadow lengths at monuments. [226] Every whisper carried the light of the hour it was spoken, and the forecourt threw that light against the observatory's old markers. [248] A shadow that reached the third ring of the Surveyor's Obelisk meant late autumn; one that barely touched the plinth meant midsummer noon. [271] From that she could place a voice in a season, a season in a year, a year in a life. [291] The rest was conjecture, and she wrote her conjecture plainly, in warm summer ink, so that no one would mistake guess for gospel. [314] That autumn a delegate climbed from the lowlands, a young woman named Tolla, chosen by the sealed city to receive its history. [336] She arrived preemptively nostalgic, mourning the climb while still climbing, touching the stair rails as though they were already ruins. [356] "We have nothing to miss," Tolla said, "so we have learned to miss things in advance." [372] Ysolde understood that better than she wanted to. [380] They waited together for rain in the deepening hush of the observatory, where the storm's light came through the dome without its sound. [403] Lightning crossed the cloudfield below them like something swimming. [411] When the rain finally ended, the forecourt began, and Ysolde took Tolla out among the monuments to listen. [429] A man argued about the price of copper; his shadow lay long across the obelisk, so: an October afternoon, a market year, forgettable and real. [454] A woman hummed to an infant at a shadow no longer than a knuckle, so: high summer, the hottest hour, a mother seeking shade. [478] Tolla wept at the second one, and could not say why, which Ysolde recorded as evidence. [494] "Whose memory is that?" Tolla asked. [500] "Nobody's alone," Ysolde said. "That is what makes it usable." [510] "A private memory belongs to one skull and dies there; a collective memory is a quarry, and a people can cut stone from it." [534] That was the point, Ysolde explained: not to restore what was lost, but to sculpt identity from experience, even borrowed experience, even guessed. [557] The sealed city could not be given its childhood back; it could be given a childhood, chiseled honestly from a thousand overheard hours. [580] Tolla stayed the winter, learning the markers, learning the rings of the obelisk, learning to say "probably" without shame. [599] In spring she carried down nine volumes of conjecture, and the lowlands read them aloud in the halls under the glass sky. [621] They argued over the copper merchant for a decade, and the humming woman became a holiday. [637] Ysolde heard about it in letters, and each summer she cut new quills and wrote until the light thinned. [656] She had grown preemptively nostalgic herself by then, missing the forecourt while standing on it, and she decided this was not a flaw. [679] It was only the ordinary grief of an archivist, who knows that every hour is someone's whisper later. [697] So she crossed the stone slowly, deliberately, speaking her own name into the rain-bright facets each evening. [714] Someday a stranger would measure her shadow against the obelisk and place her in a summer, and conjecture the rest, kindly, she hoped. [737] Above the clouds the storm gathered again, and the hush deepened, and the quarry grew by one more voice. [756]
