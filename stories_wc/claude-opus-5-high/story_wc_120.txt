The fortunes came to Ilse Marek in the hour before dawn, and she wrote them by hand, three hundred a night, for a cookie factory in a city she had not visited in eleven years. [35] Her employers wanted luck; she gave them instructions. [43] YOU WILL HEAR IT WHEN THE WATER STOPS MOVING. [52] THE SECOND SKY IS THE TRUE ONE. [59] LISTEN AT THE SEAM. [63] No one at the factory complained, because no one read them twice. [75] She lived at the top of the glass spire above the frozen lake, a failed weather observatory the state had sold for the price of its debts. [102] In January the ice went black and flawless, and at first light the lake took the sky whole, so that the horizon doubled and then vanished into itself: a mirrored horizon, seam-thin, holding its breath. [137] That was the only place the music lived. [145]

Ilse was chronologically homesick, a condition no doctor would name. [155] She did not miss a country. [161] She missed a year - the summer she was nine, when her mother still hummed while washing dishes and the radio in the kitchen played something nobody has since been able to identify. [193] On the windowsill sat a single segment of nautilus shell, cut lengthwise, salvaged from that summer's beach. [210] Nine chambers, each a perfect widening of the last, each sealed and empty and still shaped like the animal that had outgrown it. [233] Ilse had worked out, over eleven years, that this was how memory was built too. [248]

So she composed by mapping childhood memories onto the chambers. [258] The first and smallest held one sound only: a screen door. [269] The fourth held her grandfather's cough and the bell of a bicycle. [281] The ninth, the widest, held the unidentifiable radio song, and that chamber she left for last, because she was afraid of it. [303] Measured along the spiral, the intervals came out as ratios, and the ratios came out as a score. [321]

She wanted no orchestra. [325] She wanted to become the conductor of unheard symphonies - the ones that exist in the world already, unplayed, waiting for a room shaped correctly enough to release them. [353] The spire was the room. [358] The ice was the sounding board. [364] The mirrored horizon was the downbeat. [370]

She had been awake three nights, and she knew the arithmetic of her own body: she had perhaps forty minutes before exhaustion hit, and after that her hands would not be trustworthy. [402] The ice would fog by eight. [408] It had to be now. [413]

She opened all nine windows of the spire's crown. [422] Cold came in like a held chord. [429] Below, the lake lay black and the sky lay black upon it, and the gulls that should have been crying were not, and the hush over the lake was not empty but upturned, the way a bowl is upturned to keep something from escaping. [473] Ilse set the shell segment on the sill, spiral facing east, and raised her hands. [488]

Dawn arrived along the seam. [493] Not as light at first but as tension, the two skies pressing their palms together, and Ilse brought her right hand down on the instant they touched. [520]

The lake answered. [523] Ice sings when it is thin and stretched and warming: a long rising note, then a knock, then a run of tones like something enormous learning the piano. [551] It had done this every January of her eleven years here. [561] The difference was that this time she was ahead of it. [571]

She called the screen door and the ice gave her a screen door. [584] She called her grandfather's cough and got it, hollow, four hundred meters out. [597] Her arms burned. [600] At the ninth chamber she did not hesitate. [608] The whole lake took the radio song and played it back to her at the size of a county, and it was nothing she recognized, and it was exactly right, and her mother was not in it. [645] Ilse understood then what the year had been: not a place to return to, only a shape she had outgrown and could finally sound. [669]

The sun cleared the seam, the two skies parted, the ice went silver and dumb. [684] Exhaustion arrived exactly on schedule. [689] She slept eleven hours, and when she woke she wrote three hundred fortunes, and every one of them said the same thing, which the factory never noticed: IT HAS ALREADY BEEN PLAYED. YOU WERE THERE. [724]
