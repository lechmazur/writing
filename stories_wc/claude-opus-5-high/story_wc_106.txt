They called her the golden-hour portrait thief, though she never took canvas or frame. [14] Mira took the light itself, the last honeyed minute that lands on a stranger's face and makes it worth remembering. [34] She did it with a brass lens, a held breath, and a patience most thieves lacked. [50] Her clients paid for illuminated moments: a widower's wife at twenty-three, a boy's first fearless laugh, whole lives distilled into a single lit second. [74] Mira was quietly savvy about the trade, which is why she was still free and her competitors were not. [93]

Her instructions always arrived the same way: codes in flower arrangements, delivered to the florist's stall at Emerald Station. [112] Three white stock for a departure time, ranunculus for the mark's age, a single bruised aster when the job was dangerous. [133] She had read hundreds of them and had never once been wrong. [145]

On the last Thursday of October, the arrangement made no sense at all. [158] Moonflower, closed tight; nightshade; and beneath them, wrapped in wet paper, a jar. [171] The jar held moonbeams. [175] Mira knew this the way you know a voice through a wall, immediately and without evidence: pale ribbons turning slowly in the glass, cold as well water, silvering her palms. [205] She stood in the station's green-tiled arcade holding the impossible, and the feeling that rose in her was not fear but luminous disbelief. [228]

The florist, old Bettine, would not look up from her shears. [239] 'I only arrange what I'm sent,' she said. 'Read it again.' [250]

So Mira sat on a bench beneath the departure board and tried to reinterpret the flowers. [266] Moonflower did not mean a person; it meant an hour, the hour that opens only after dark. [283] Nightshade did not mean poison; in Bettine's older grammar, it meant a thing kept secret from its own owner. [302] And the jar was not payment. [308] It was a tool. [312] The commission, when she finally understood it, was for herself. [322]

Twenty years ago someone had stolen a golden hour from Mira's own childhood, a summer evening she could describe only as a gap, a warmth with no picture in it. [352] It was why she had taken up the lens in the first place: to press her ear against destiny's door, to hear what her life had been about to say before someone closed it. [386] Stolen light, Bettine had once told her, can be given back, but only under the same kind of sky it was taken from, and only if you have moonlight to pay the difference. [419]

The next evening, Mira waited on the westbound platform under a fleeting golden sunset, the jar cold against her ribs. [439] Emerald Station turned the light to lacquer; the green tiles drank it, the iron beams held it, and for six minutes the whole hall was the inside of a struck bell. [470] She opened the brass lens backward. [476] Then the sun went, and the platform lamps came up, and Mira unscrewed the jar. [491]

The moonbeams came out slow, unspooling like cold thread, and they did not rise; they settled over the empty rails and made a second station there, the same arcade seen by moonlight, quiet and enormous. [526] In it stood a woman in a summer dress, holding a child by the wrist, laughing at something outside the frame. [547] Mira's mother, at the exact minute she had lost. [556] The thief who had taken it had been careless, or perhaps merciful, and had kept it safe in glass for twenty years. [578]

Bettine stood at the arcade's mouth with her shears still in her apron, watching. [592] 'You,' Mira said, and could manage nothing else. [600] 'I was young, and I was good at it, and I stopped the day I saw what I'd done to you,' the old woman said. [625] 'I have been arranging your jobs ever since, so you would learn the grammar well enough to take it back yourself.' [646]

The moonlit station held for the length of a held breath. [657] Mira listened to her mother's laugh, thin, ordinary, unbearably warm, and heard the door she had spent her life leaning against swing quietly open onto nothing but a summer evening. [687] It was enough. [690]

Then the light thinned, the rails came back, and the 7:40 to Carrow pulled in on time. [707] Mira capped the empty jar, went to the stall, and began, carefully, to build an arrangement of her own. [726] Stock, ranunculus, one unbruised aster: a commission for the next thief, and instructions for giving things back. [743]
