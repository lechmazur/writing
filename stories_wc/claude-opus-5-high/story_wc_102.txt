The viaduct bloomed only at twilight, when the wisteria let go of the day's heat and the fog came up the valley like a held breath released. [27] Mira had built her camera obscura inside the old signal hut on its highest arch, and for nine years she had made portraits there by darkness and patience. [55] Her method was simple and nobody believed it: she did not watch the sitter's face, she watched the sitter's breathing change. [76] A widow would speak calmly about her husband's boat and her ribs would stall on the word harbor, and the plate, exposed for the length of that stall, would come out carrying something true. [110] Mira called these portraits honest; her clients called them healing and paid her in jam and firewood. [127]

Then, in March, her own past life surfaced. [135] It came while she ground a lens: a temple floor, cold under her knees, a bronze bell above her, and a child she had loved so completely that the love had never once been spent. [170] She woke to the memory the way one wakes into fog: everything blurred, everything lucid. [185] After that she could not work. [191] She had one question and it ate the others: where is unused love stored? [205] It could not simply evaporate; nothing else in the world did. [216]

The pilgrim came in May, walking the viaduct at the hour the blossoms opened their smell. [232] He introduced himself as a pilgrim of sparks, and explained without embarrassment: for eleven years he had traveled from forge to foundry to shipbreaking yard, because sparks were the only thing he had ever found that behaved like souls -- brief, bright, and impossible to own. [277] He was spiritually curious in the patient, unfashionable way of a man who expects to be answered eventually. [295] From his coat he took a bead of glass the size of a thumbnail. [309] "Kwan Yin's Tear," he said. [314] "A bellmaker's widow gave it to me in the north, where their temple bell was melted for cannon, and one drop cooled wrong and came out clear instead of bronze." [344] "She told me the bell had carried too much mercy to spend it all on sound." [360] Mira held it up and saw the viaduct upside down inside it, blossoms hanging like lamps. [376]

The pilgrim wanted a portrait, though not for vanity. [385] He said a past life had surfaced in him too, at a rolling mill in winter, and it had left a wound he could not locate: he knew he had loved someone enormously and could not remember her name, her face, or whether he had ever told her. [433] "You photograph breath," he said. [438] "Then photograph mine until it tells me where that love went." [449]

She took out her brass pinhole and set Kwan Yin's Tear in its place. [463] Then she opened the shutter and asked him to talk about anything except love. [477] He talked about slag, about the kindness of night watchmen, about a dog in Kaunas. [492] For twenty minutes his breathing was a workman's breathing, even and unremarkable. [504] Then the wisteria shifted in the fog, and he stopped mid-sentence, and his breath went high and shallow and stayed there, the way breath does when a body recognizes something the mind has agreed to forget. [540] Mira held the shutter open for exactly that long. [549]

The plate, developed by lamplight, showed no man at all. [559] It showed the viaduct in bloom, every arch furred with flowers, and the flowers were made of small struck lights, thousands of them, banked and waiting. [585] They looked at it for a long time in the foggy lucidity of the hut. [600] "It is stored in whatever will still bloom," Mira said at last, and found she believed it. [617] "Unused love does not rot." [622] "It gets banked, like sparks in ash, and it waits for a body willing to spend it." [639]

The pilgrim wept without changing expression, and his breathing came down slow and deep and level, and Mira understood that this was what it looked like to heal: not remembering the name, but letting the unspent thing move again. [678] He left Kwan Yin's Tear in her palm and walked west along the rails. [692] Mira kept it in her camera after that. [700] Every twilight she opened the shutter on the blooming arches and breathed until her own chest changed, until the temple floor and the bronze bell and the child rose up and settled, and the plate took them in. [738] She sent the prints to strangers, unsigned, one a month, spending what she had carried for centuries. [755] The fog never lifted, and she never needed it to. [765]
