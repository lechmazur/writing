After the first snowmelt, when the river ran brown and loud, I carried my brother's lamp burner down to the bronze grotto. [22] Only the burner, brass and threaded, its wick-slot black with old kerosene; the rest of the lamp had gone down with him. [44] The grotto opens in the cliff like a held breath. [54] Its walls are true bronze, poured by nobody, green at the tidelines and warm where starlight touches. [71] Inside there is a shingled hush: silence laid over silence, each layer overlapping the one beneath, so that dropping a pebble makes no sound at all but a slight loosening of the roof of quiet. [106] My grandmother called the place a whalebone reverie and would not go near it. [120] She said the grotto dreams the way old bone dreams, slowly, and of water. [134]

The fractal weaver was awake, as she is only after the first snowmelt. [147] She hung at the center of her own work, small as a hand, grey as ash, and her web went out from her in threads that branched into threads that branched again, each branch the same shape as the whole. [187] Look close and you found the grotto again inside a single knot; look closer and there was the weaver, and her web, and the knot. [212]

"You have brought me a weight," she said. [220] Her voice arrived in the layers of the hush like a shingle slipped into place. [235] I had come to have my grief weighed, because that is what she does. [249] She collects the weights of different burdens, not the burdens themselves, only their heft, the way a miller collects numbers and not grain. [272] Widows come, and thieves, and men who have lost boats. [282] She hangs each sorrow on a thread and reads how far the thread bends. [296] Then she lets it go, and the visitor walks out lighter by exactly nothing, but knowing the number. [314]

I gave her the burner. [319] She took it in her forelegs and the thread holding it dipped a long way down, past the tidemark, and kept dipping. [341] "Eleven years of a brother," she said, "and heavy for its size, as they always are." [357]

I asked her why she wanted the numbers. [365] She was quiet through several layers of the hush before she answered. [377] "Because I am boundary-lessly trapped," she said. [384] There was no wall anywhere in her web; it simply repeated, outward and inward, forever, so that every direction she traveled produced only more of the same branching, and she had no edge to push against and therefore no way out. [425] A weight, she explained, was the one thing that fell. [435] Weights pointed somewhere. [438] Collect enough of them and she might learn which way down was, and down is a direction, and a direction is almost a door. [462]

"How many do you need?" [467] "One that points true." [471]

I looked at the burner in her forelegs, and at the mouth of the grotto, where the spring stars stood over the water in their old arrangement. [498] My brother had taught me the one useful thing about a lamp: it will not burn steady unless you set the burner by orienting properly, slot crosswise to the draft, so the flame leans into the wind instead of away. [538] I took it back from her and turned it until its slot lay crosswise to the sea wind coming through the grotto mouth, and I set it on the bronze ledge where the starlight fell. [573]

The burner threw a shadow. [578] It was a small, hard, single-shaped shadow, not a fractal one, and it lay across her web like a beam laid over lace. [601] An edge, at last, with a somewhere on each side of it. [613]

She ran along the shadow's line the way water finds a gutter. [625] Where her feet touched the bronze, the threads behind her began to calcify, going white and brittle and still, a ladder of bone stiffening out of the dark. [653] All the weights she had ever collected hardened with them. [663] I heard them settle through the shingled hush, layer after layer, each one a small dry click. [680] By the time she reached the grotto mouth she was pale as scrimshaw. [693] "Your brother's weight pointed out," she said, and went into the starlight, and was gone over the snowmelt water. [712]

I left the burner on the ledge, oriented, unlit. [721] It is still there, I think, holding its one true direction in a room made entirely of repetitions. [739] I carry the number she gave me, and it is heavy, and heavy things at least tell you which way the ground is. [762]
