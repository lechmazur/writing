The greenhouse sank with the old harbor, and the apple trees went on fruiting underwater anyway. [16] Ilse Vantorre rowed out to it every low tide, past the ring of shipwrecks that had grounded themselves on the shoal like patient animals. [40] She was a hearing aid designer, and this was her workshop: green glass, drowned orchard rows, and a forge she had built on the tilted deck of a collier above. [70] The forge burned coal-ash gray under the aurora most nights, which suited her, because she could hear the aurora. [89] Ilse heard colors faintly, all her life -- green as a low sustained cello, rust as a cough, the aurora's pale rose like a bowed wineglass. [114] It made her good at her trade and useless at arguments. [125] Her aids were famous along the coast: battered little marvels of wreck-brass, tuned by ear, worn by fishermen who had lost their hearing to engines. [150] Her method was strange and she never explained it to clients: she built by collecting absences. [166] A diaphragm hammered thin in the ashen forge would imprint whatever silence she pressed into it -- the hush inside a cracked bell, the gap where a name used to be spoken at a table, the missing beat in a widow's breathing. [207] Absence, she had learned, carries more shape than noise does. [217] Memory works the same way; what is taken out of a life presses harder than what stays. [234] Her brother Ansel had taught her that, though not on purpose. [245] He had sold her tuning method to a mainland firm eleven years ago, along with the wax impressions of forty of her patients' ears, and she had learned of it from a catalog. [278] She remembered the exact moment she turned the page: no sound at all, a color like scorched paper, and something in her chest going quiet the way a hull goes quiet before it splits. [312] Ever since, she had been trying to find the sound of trust breaking. [325] Not the shouting afterward -- the break itself, the half-second hinge. [335] She believed that if she could imprint it onto a diaphragm, anyone wearing it would hear the hinge swing in time to step back. [359] That was the whole of her ambition, and it was not a kind one. [373]

The culture arrived in March, packed in seawater ice: a flask of cochlear cells grown from a deaf child's own tissue, alive and dividing. [397] The surgeon's note was blunt: seat the housing while cells divide, or they will imprint on glass instead of on an ear. [419] The child's name was Mira Vantorre, and she was nine. [429] Ansel had written on the back of the shipping label, in handwriting she still knew: I have nothing to trade you. [450]

So Ilse worked nine days in the sunken orchard greenhouse, wading the flooded rows where the apple trees hung their fruit below the waterline, listening. [475] She had come there for the absences: a greenhouse holds the missing sound of wind, and an orchard underwater holds the missing sound of bees. [500] Between the wrecks, the water was as green as a held cello note, and she could not hear the hinge anywhere in it. [523]

On the eighth night she carried the flask up to the forge and stood in the ash light with the aurora ringing overhead like a wet rim. [550] She had one blank diaphragm left, and two absences to choose between. [562] She could imprint the one she had hunted for eleven years -- the quiet of a page turning, a hull going still -- and give her niece the gift of never being fooled. [593] Or she could imprint the absence she had been standing in all week without naming it: the hollow where bees should have been, which is the sound of a place still trying to bear fruit. [628] Memories press; that is all they do, and the shape they leave is the shape you grow into. [646]

Ilse hammered the orchard into the brass. [653] The surgeon seated it on the ninth day, while the cells were still dividing, and they took the imprint and grew toward it. [676] Mira Vantorre hears in an odd, warm, humming way, and she says the color green is loud. [693] Ilse never did find the sound of trust breaking, which she has decided was the point: you cannot build an ear that only listens for betrayal and expect anything to fruit in it. [726] The forge still burns gray under the aurora, and Ansel rows the flask-ice out from the mainland now, and neither of them has said the eleven years out loud. [755] There is an absence there too, and Ilse has decided not to collect it. [769]
