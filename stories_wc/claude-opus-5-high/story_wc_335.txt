The forgotten library sat under the playground, and nobody visited it but Vesna and the dust. [16] She was the shard-mirror archivist, keeper of four thousand broken glasses, each holding one afternoon it had refused to forget. [36] Her work was simple and endless: annotate. [43] Every shard needed a card, and every card needed the truth of what the glass had seen, written small. [62] Ilya had broken that rule, which is why Ilya was exiled. [73] Exile in the archive was reversible, the way a tide is reversible, and just as indifferent to begging. [91] The boy could come back only at the hush, when the empty playground swing drifted in the breeze above the stacks, and only if someone below had done the arithmetic right. [122] Vesna did the arithmetic by analyzing the geometry of tea leaves. [133] It sounds like superstition until you have watched leaves settle into a cup and seen angles that match the archive's cracked ceiling exactly. [156] Angles told her hours. [160] That afternoon the leaves fell into a narrow isosceles pointing west, which meant the door would soften at four-twenty and stay soft for the length of one swing's arc. [189] She had nine minutes and a temper. [196] Vesna began to walk backwards between the shelves, heels finding the aisle by memory, which is what she did when she was nervous and would not admit it. [224] Backwards, the library looked like a life being unlived: lamps relighting, dust rising to the shelf it fell from. [243] In the eighth aisle she found what she needed: an afternoon shadow caught in glass, the last shard Ilya had ever filed. [265] The shadow inside it was a boy's, thrown long across gravel, hands gripping chains that were not in the picture. [285] He had annotated the card with one word: MINE. [294] That was the crime. [298] An archivist does not own an afternoon; she carries it, and the difference had cost him a year outside. [317] Vesna had signed the exile herself, pressing the pen so hard the paper tore. [331] She had also, every month since, brewed the leaves and read the angles, and never once told him she was doing it. [353] Mentoring, she believed, should be quiet, or it becomes a debt. [364] At four-nineteen the hush arrived. [369] Above, through the grate, the swing began its slow unpushed drift, and the air in the stacks went thick and warm. [390] Ilya came through the shadow in the glass the way water comes through a sieve, all at once and complaining. [410] "You let me rot out there," he said. [418] "I let you learn," Vesna said, "which smells similar and lasts longer." [430] He was taller, and his coat had been mended badly by his own hand, which pleased her more than any apology could have. [453] "You brewed for me," he said, looking at the cup. [463] "I brewed for the archive." [468] "You brewed for me, and you are walking backwards right now." [479] She stopped, caught, and hated him fondly for a full second. [490] Then she put the shard in his palm and closed his fingers over it, hard enough to hurt. [508] "Fix the card," she said. [513] "The exile reverses when the annotation is true, not when I forgive you, because I am not the door." [532] He knelt at the low desk, dipped the pen, and crossed out MINE. [545] Underneath, he wrote: Afternoon, borrowed; returned in full; the swing kept moving after I left it. [561] The glass warmed and dulled, the shadow inside relaxing into ordinary gray. [573] Above them the swing slowed, its arc shortening toward stillness. [583] "Stay," Vesna said, "or go; the door is soft either way for eleven more seconds." [598] Ilya looked at the four thousand shards, each an afternoon someone had refused to own, and sat down at the second desk. [620] "Give me the unfiled ones," he said. [627] She gave him the worst box, which was how she said welcome home, and went to boil more water, forwards this time. [649]
