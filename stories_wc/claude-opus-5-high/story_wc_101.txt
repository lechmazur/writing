The terra-cotta arbor on the ridge had been a wine shelter once, before the border moved and made us foreigners in the valley we could still see from its steps. [30] Sanctions had closed the banks to our names, and the markets below sold us flour at three times the posted price. [51] Amineh Bardas, who led us, said hunger was arithmetic and could therefore be solved. [65] She was sixty-one, gently persistent, and she owned one thing of value: an antique silver bell her mother had carried out of the old capital in a coat lining. [94] The bell hung from a beam in the arbor, and its sound carried farther than it had any right to, thin and bright across the terraces. [120] Amineh never rang it for alarms. [125] She rang it for arrivals. [130]

The arrivals began in October, when a crate of lentils appeared at the arbor's steps with no note, no name, no hand to thank. [154] Then oil, then powdered milk, then a sack of walnuts still damp from somebody's yard. [169] Anonymous donations are a strange mercy; they feed you and refuse to let you become anyone's debtor, which is its own kind of insult in a place where debt is how people stay attached. [203] The young men wanted to stake out the road and catch the giver. [216] Amineh forbade it. [219] "If we learn the name, the name gets punished," she said, and that was true: helping us was a sanctions offense in the valley, and the customs officers were bored men with quotas. [252]

Instead, she did something stranger with the gift. [260] She declared, one evening under the arbor with forty of us packed between the pillars, that nothing arriving anonymously would be distributed by the council. [285] It would move only through midnight food runs, and every run would pair one person over sixty with one person under twenty. [307] People argued. [309] Midnight was when the patrol changed shifts, yes, but the pairing seemed sentimental, inefficient, the sort of thing a tired woman invents to feel useful. [334] Amineh did not raise her voice; she simply asked again the next night, and the next, until asking became the schedule. [355]

I drew Mr. Halim, who had been a hydrologist and was now a man with a cane and eleven opinions about soil. [377] We carried flour to the lower houses with the moon behind cloud. [389] Nobody spoke above a murmur, and yet I have never heard so much. [402] That is what the runs did to sound: they amplified the quiet. [414] A cough two terraces down became news. [421] Halim's whisper about which cistern still held winter water became a map I would use for the rest of my life. [442] He told me his mother's recipe for lentils with burnt onion because there was nothing else to do with three kilometers of darkness. [465]

By January there were nineteen pairs. [471] The old taught the young the footpaths and the funeral songs; the young carried the sacks and learned who in the valley could be trusted with a question. [499] That was Amineh's whole design, I understood later: the food was the excuse, and intergenerational support was the thing being smuggled. [520] A sanctions period ends, eventually; a community that has forgotten how to hand itself down does not recover so easily. [540]

In March the crates stopped. [545] We learned why in the usual way, which is to say a rumor arrived before the fact: a cheesemaker in the valley, a woman named Sava, had been fined into ruin for undeclared exports. [579] She had never once left a note. [586]

Amineh took the silver bell down from its beam that night. [597] She did not sell it, though it would have brought a month of flour. [611] She walked it down herself, with Halim's granddaughter at her elbow, and left it on Sava's windowsill with no name attached. [632] "Let her be the one who is not thanked," Amineh said on the way up, smiling, gently persistent even in mischief. [653]

The runs continued without the crates, because by then we were feeding each other out of our own thin stores, which had been the point all along. [680] The beam in the terra-cotta arbor stayed empty for eleven years. [691] Then a girl who had been born on a midnight run brought it back up the ridge, bought at a market stall in the reopened valley, and we rang it at her wedding. [724] It still carried farther than it had any right to, and everyone under the arbor went silent to let it. [744]
