The trade always happened within the glow of the half-lit corridors, where the lamps were kept low so no one could read a face too clearly. [26] Mira came down the stairs with her burned tarot card in her fist, the edges still smelling of candle smoke. [46] She was nineteen and reckless in the way idealists are reckless, certain that a wrong could be unmade if someone simply refused to accept it. [71] Below the corridors lay the orchard. [77] The city buried its dead in the old catacombs, and then, three generations back, someone had planted pear and quince and fig among them, so that now the roots snaked between femurs and skulls like patient fingers sorting coins. [116] Fruit grew there in the lamplight, small and sweet and faintly wrong. [128] The Grafter tended it. [132] He was an old man with soil under his nails, and he was fiercely gentle, the way a surgeon is gentle: every touch soft, every cut certain. [159] He bartered destinies. [162] That was the trade: you brought him a life you did not want, and he grafted it onto another branch, and someone else carried it instead. [188]

"Show me," he said. [192]

Mira opened her hand. [196] The card had been the Tower, and now it was mostly ash-brown ruin, one scorched turret and a falling figure whose face had survived the fire. [222]

"My brother burned it the night he took the soldier's oath," she said. [235] "He said he wanted no reading, no warning, nothing to trammel him." [247] "He is dead now." [251]

The Grafter turned the card over, inlaying meaning everywhere, as was his habit; he read the grain of the ash, the angle of the tear, the way her thumb had worn the corner smooth. [285] To him nothing was only itself; a burned card was a boy's whole argument with heaven. [301]

"You want his death undone," he said. [308]

"I want the hole patched," Mira said. [315] "There is a hole in me where he was, and one in my mother, and one in the girl he was going to marry." [339] "Three holes in three souls, from one stupid oath." [348] "Give me his destiny and let me carry it, and give him mine." [361]

The Grafter laughed, not unkindly, and led her deeper, past the shelves of ribs where a quince root had wound itself through a stranger's hand. [386]

"Look," he said. [389] "Every tree here feeds on a bartered life." [397] "A woman gave away her blindness and a fig took root in the man who accepted it; he grew rich in sound and never saw his children." [424] "Nothing is patched here, girl." [429] "It is only moved." [434]

"Then move it to me," she said, and the dusk in her voice was not despair but resolve, the low steady kind that outlasts lamps. [459]

He studied her a long while. [465]

"There is a price you have not asked about." [474]

"Name it." [476]

"To take his destiny is to take his ending too." [486] "You would die in a border ditch at twenty-three, and he would live your life, and he would not remember you had given it." [510] "The holes would still be there, only in him." [519]

Mira stood among the bones and the patient roots and understood, finally, what the Tower had been trying to say before her brother burned it. [544] Not that the fall could be stopped. [551] Only that something had to be built after it. [560]

She knelt and pressed the burned card into the soil between two ribs, and covered it. [576]

"Graft nothing," she said. [580] "Let him keep his ending, since it was his to choose." [591] "I will go up and tell my mother what the card meant, and the girl too, and we will say his name until the saying is a kind of orchard." [621]

The Grafter's hands, which had unmade a hundred fates, rested on her shoulders with terrible tenderness. [637]

"You are the first to climb back out," he said. [647]

She climbed, and the half-lit corridors held her, and below, the roots went on trammeling the bones, holding what was left in place so the trees could stand. [675] It was, she thought, a kind of mending. [683]
