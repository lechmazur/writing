In the mirror world, dawn had to be made by hand. [11] Anwe had been its keeper for so long that her memories no longer lived inside her; they had scattered into the orchard, one to each tree. [37] The greenhouse stood under endless night, its glass ribs sweating alchemical dew, its rows of grafted trees breathing slow silver. [57] Nothing here bore fruit; the trees bore dawns, unripe and colorless, waiting to be woven. [72] A dawn could not be forced. [78] It had to be coaxed out on the loom of a song. [90] Not new songs, which the realm no longer had breath for, but forgotten ones. [104] Anwe walked the rows with her lantern of cold fire and pressed her palm to each trunk, and each trunk gave her back a piece of herself. [131] The pear-graft remembered a lullaby her mother hummed while hanging nets. [142] The blackthorn remembered a harvest round that forty voices used to shout across the terraces. [157] Each time she took a memory back, she had to sing it aloud to keep it, and each time she sang, the tree that had held it went bare. [186] That was the bargain of her office: bound to remember, released only by giving away. [201] At the greenhouse's east end hung the mica window, a pane of layered stone-glass no thicker than a fingernail. [220] Through it, faintly, one could see the true world's morning, split into a hundred bronze leaves. [236] The mirror world had been cast from that morning once, long ago, and had been cooling ever since. [254] Anwe's work was to rekindle it: to weave enough dawns to warm the dying realm back into living. [271] She had woven eleven. [275] Eleven had guttered out before noon, because a dawn woven by one voice cannot hold. [290] So on the twelfth attempt she opened the greenhouse doors and let the village in. [305] They came shuffling, lamp-blind, suspicious: fishers with no sea, weavers with no wool, children who had never seen a shadow. [325] Anwe stood among them, scattered and shaking, and began to hand her memories out. [339] "This one is yours," she told the old netmaker, and sang him the lullaby until his mouth found the words before hers. [361] "This one is the terraces," she said, and the harvest round went up from forty throats that had forgotten they knew it. [383] That was the secret nobody had written down: a dawn is not lit, it is knitted, and only song knits communities together tightly enough to hold light. [410] The bare trees put out threads. [416] Pale filaments drifted from the branches toward the singers, gathering, braiding, taking on the grain of forty different voices. [435] By the fourth song the woven dawns hung above the rows like sheets of warm cloth, one for every remembered melody. [456] Anwe cut them free and showed the mothers how to swaddle them, close and careful, the way you carry anything newborn through cold. [479] Wrapped, they did not gutter. [484] Carried out into the streets by people who knew the songs that made them, they kept. [500] One last tree remained, the oldest, an apple grafted onto quince at the greenhouse's dark heart. [516] It held the memory Anwe had never dared reclaim: her own name, and the voice that first taught her the keeper's song. [538] To weave the dawn that would reach the mica window, she would have to sing that too, and give it away. [559] She would not remember being the memory keeper. [567] She would only be a woman in a bright orchard, holding nothing. [579] She pressed her palm to the trunk and sang. [587] The village caught the tune on its second turn, as villages do, and lifted it away from her. [605] The thread that rose from the old apple was gold, and thick, and it went to the mica window and threaded itself through every layer of stone. [632] The pane did not break. [637] It opened, one bronze leaf at a time, and the true morning came in sideways across the rows. [655] Endless night thinned to grey, then to something almost blue. [665] The woman in the orchard did not know why she was weeping, or what the glass had cost. [683] But the children came and took her hands and taught her the song about the keeper who scattered herself so a realm could warm, and she learned it quickly, the way you learn a thing you once knew. [721] They sang it every morning after, because in the mirror world mornings still had to be made, and no one makes them alone. [744]
