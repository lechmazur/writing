The braided ravine ran in seven channels that autumn, and Ines Varga knew every one of them by sound. [19] She was a polymer chemist by trade and symphonically minded by nature, and the water gave her an orchestra: the first channel a cello, the fourth a rill of flutes, the seventh a low timpani where it struck the shale. [59] Her brother Andor had taught her to listen like that, twenty summers ago, crouched on this same gravel bar. [78] He had died in March, and she had come here to atone. [90] In her coat pocket she carried an empty chrysalis, brittle as a seed husk, which Andor had pinned to a card the year he decided to become a river ecologist instead of a chemist like her. [126] A divergent path, he had called it, laughing, as if the two of them were channels of one water. [145]

For eleven years Ines had refined a fiber called Lumaflex, soft, cheap, and beloved by every clothing manufacturer on three continents. [166] It shed microfibers with every wash, and Andor's last paper had counted them in the gut of every fish he netted in this ravine. [190] They had argued about it in February, badly, in the kitchen of their mother's house. [205] Three weeks later his car left a wet road in the Bakony hills, and the argument stood where it was, unfinished, a chord held down forever. [231] So she had taken leave, borrowed a field notebook, and come to the ravine to solve the thing he had accused her of making. [255]

For six weeks she failed. [259] Filtration screens clogged; surfactant traps collected sand and nothing else. [269] The fibers were too small, too scattered, too separate; that was their whole cruelty. [283]

Then, on a cold bright morning in October, she sat on the gravel bar tracing river foam patterns with her finger in her notebook. [307] The seven channels split and rejoined, split and rejoined, and wherever two rejoined the foam gathered in long white braids, and in the braids the foam held everything: pollen, dust, seed fluff, her brother's microfibers. [342] Confluence concentrated what divergence had spread. [348] She did not need a finer screen; she needed a surface that behaved like a confluence. [364]

Her mind, which had never been able to hold an idea without scoring it, heard the answer as counterpoint: two polymer strands with opposite charge, voiced apart, written to converge. [394] Lumaflex itself could be the first voice. [401] Give the fiber a faint cationic tail at manufacture, seed the wash water with an anionic counter-strand, and the shed fibers would find each other and braid into flocs too large to pass a household filter. [437] Not a trap, but a reunion. [443]

She wrote for four hours on the gravel bar, and as the pieces fell into place she understood what she had actually been trying to do since March: to translate separation into connection, molecule by molecule, because she could not do it with him. [487] The grief did not leave. [492] It loosened, the way a knot loosens when you stop pulling against it, and she sat in the loosened place and let the timpani channel keep its time. [520]

The laboratory work took nineteen months, and the licensing fight took longer, because a cationic tail cost four cents a kilogram and four cents is a mountain. [547] She named the process Andor, and let the manufacturers assume it was an acronym. [561]

In the spring after the first licensed run, she went back to the braided ravine with a sampling net and the chrysalis still in her pocket. [587] The counts were down by a third; it was not absolution, and she had stopped expecting any. [604] She set the empty chrysalis on the gravel at the seventh confluence, where the foam braided thickest, and watched the water take the little hollow shell. [630] It had been made to be left behind; that was the whole point of it. [645] Two channels became one below her, carrying pollen and dust and seed fluff, and she listened to the cello enter beneath the flutes, and she did not need to tell him. [676]
