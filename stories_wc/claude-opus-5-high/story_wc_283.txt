The amber warehouse lay beneath the cliffs like a held breath, and Mira, who had been its hourglass turner for nine years, worked in the unearthed silence of resin and stone. [31] Her duty was simple: three hundred hourglasses in their cradles, each to be inverted before its last grain fell. [50] If one ran empty, the amber forgot what it was holding. [61] What the amber held, in its deepest cell, was a jar. [72] Inside the jar was a canyon echo: the Oracle Sennet's final prophecy, spoken once into the Threadless Gorge and caught on its return by a woman with a lid. [101] The gorge had done what gorges do. [108] It gave the words back out of order, syllables folded over syllables, a hundred voices of one voice. [126] For four generations the turners had tried to unscramble it, and the method had never changed: by cataloging lost buttons. [146] Pilgrims came down the cliff stairs to listen at the jar, and each left the same way, poorer by one button. [167] Nobody knew why; the amber simply took them, drawing bone and shell and horn into its floor like seeds. [186] But a listener left with one syllable lodged in memory, and the button marked which. [201] So the turners dug, logged, and wrote it all down: brass button, fisherman's coat, the sound vor. [218] Wisdom in the warehouse was never one person's; it was ten thousand strangers, each carrying home a single grain of a sentence they could not read alone. [245] Mira's predecessor, old Hesk, had told her the ledger was the true oracle. [258]

On the first cold morning of her tenth year, Mira unearthed a button she had already catalogued. [275] Horn, six-sided, chipped at the rim, entered in Hesk's hand forty years earlier with the syllable kel. [292] The same button could not be lost twice. [300] Unless the amber had been giving them back. [308] She went to the jar that night and, against every rule, lifted the lid a finger's width. [325] The echo came out thin and dry, and it did not say a prophecy. [339] It said her own voice, from an hour before, asking whether nine years of turning glass had made her anything at all. [361] The jar held no message; it held listening. [369] Sennet had shouted nothing into the gorge. [376] He had shouted a question, and the canyon had answered with whatever was brought to it. [392] The four generations of ledger were not a translation, then, but an authorship. [405]

Mira sat down among the cradles as the truth unraveled, and waited for the fear to arrive. [422] It did not. [425] What arrived instead was something quieter: she was quietly inspired, the way a person is when a locked door turns out to be a curtain. [450] The prophecy said the cliffs would fall in her lifetime and bury the coast towns; the ledger had spent four hundred pages proving it. [474] Half the coast had already stopped mending its roofs. [483] But if the words were only the sum of what frightened listeners carried down the stairs, then the doom was a mirror, and mirrors could be turned. [510] Mira's task was no longer to finish the sentence. [519] It was to spin the prophecy back into raw possibility. [529]

She worked through winter, turning glass with one hand and rewriting the ledger with the other. [545] She did not forge entries; she simply added the questions, restoring beside each syllable the fear or hope that had chosen it. [567] Brass button, fisherman's coat, vor -- and beside it: he was afraid for his sons. [581] Horn, six-sided, kel -- Hesk, who wanted the world to mean something before he died. [595]

In spring she opened the warehouse to anyone who would come, and put the ledger on the floor where the buttons were. [617] They read it together, masons and fishwives and a girl with a surveyor's chain, and the reading took three days. [637] At the end of it the surveyor's girl said the cliffs would indeed fall, but slowly, and along a seam that could be drained. [661] Nobody clapped. [663] The warehouse only held its old unearthed silence, and in it Mira heard the shape of work rather than the shape of fate. [686] She set the jar open on a shelf where the sea wind could reach it, so the echo would keep collecting voices. [708] Then she went back to the cradles, and turned the hourglasses, one by one, before the last grain fell. [727]
