The final warning arrived stitched in lace, a veil left on the queen's pillow with frost still crawling its edges. [20] Isolde had stopped fearing such things the night her enchantment broke. [31] The court called her the disenchanted queen and meant it two ways. [43] Twelve years she had ruled inside a spell of borrowed wonder, and when it lifted she saw her kingdom plainly: good soil, tired people, a sky that had begun to leak. [74] The dream came every night regardless: bleeding starlight running down the dark like wax, pooling in a hollow she had never seen awake. [97] She meant to unravel the mystery of it before the lace-sender did. [109] Her guide was Wren, a goat-girl of sixteen who knew the ridge trails and did not curtsy. [126] "We climb at dusk," Wren said, softly commanding, "or the ember grotto will be cold when we need it." [145] The ember grotto was a fist of hollow stone halfway up the ridge, packed with coals that shepherds had kept alive for two hundred years, passed hand to hand like a secret. [177] Whoever climbed past it took a coal and owed one back. [188] They started up as the valley went violet. [196] Halfway to the coals, both of them stopped at once, and in a shared exhale spotted the first fireflies of summer rising out of the barley. [222] Isolde had not breathed with anyone in years. [230] "They come up when the ground's warm enough to forgive winter," Wren said. [243] "Why climb?" Wren asked, and Isolde told her about the dreams, which had come nightly for a year and always ended with her hands full of light and wet. [272] "Then I am later than the fireflies," said the queen, and took the first coal from the grotto in an iron spoon. [294] Above the ember grotto the trail turned to teeth, and by midnight they had climbed out of the weather entirely, the clouds beneath them like a floor of ash. [323] The cobalt grotto stood open at the summit, blue as a bruise lit from inside, and Isolde knew it the way one knows a room from a dream. [351] It was the hollow. [355] Starlight came down through a crack in the roof and did not fall clean; it dripped, thick and slow, and where it struck the floor it left red. [383] In the pool of it sat a woman weaving on a frame of bone, her work a lace so fine it seemed to be made of the same slow light. [413] "You sent three veils," Isolde said. [419] "I sent three mercies," the weaver answered. [426] "Your dream was the summons; the veils were the chance to refuse it." [439] She had woven the kingdom's enchantment twelve years ago out of this light, she said, and for twelve years she had sat under the leak, patching. [465] The wound was in the sky itself, and it would not close; her lace only slowed the bleeding, and her hands were done. [488] "Take the frame," she said, and the invitation had a lacy menace to it, beautiful and full of holes. [507] "Sit here, and your people keep their wonder." [515] Isolde looked at the lace and understood the trade: a queen for a spell, forever, one woman swapped for another under a bleeding sky. [539] She had lived twelve years inside that wonder and had not known her own barley until the spell broke; the fireflies had not needed it. [564] She set the coal down instead. [570] "I owe the ridge a fire," she said, with Wren's soft command in her voice, and touched the ember to the lace. [592] It went up like a moth's wing, and the starlight fell through unslowed, all at once, silver and red across the cobalt walls. [615] The weaver wept, or laughed; above the clouds the two sound the same. [628] "Now nothing is enchanted," she said. [634] "Now nothing is owed," said Isolde. [640] They came down at dawn through a plain gray rain, and Wren left a new coal in the ember grotto, and the queen carried the empty bone frame home to hang in the hall where the crown had been. [679] The dreams stopped that night. [684] In their place she dreamed of nothing at all, which is how she knew the sky had finished falling and the rest was hers to build. [710]
