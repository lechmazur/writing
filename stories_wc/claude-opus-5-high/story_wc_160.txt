The amphitheater breathed twice a day, and Mirena had learned to work between its breaths. [15] Warm air rose through the caldera's cracked stone tiers at dawn, and sank back at dusk, so the old seats seemed to swell and settle like a sleeping chest. [44] As the floodgate machinist of Vhal Caldera, a crumbling amphitheater inside a dormant volcano, she alone decided when the mountain drank. [65] That was the trouble. [69] The terrace farmers wanted the amphitheater flooded all summer, a cistern deep enough to keep their barley alive. [87] The Choir of Ash wanted it dry, because the amphitheater's stone rings sang only when empty, and the autumn Remembrance had been sung there for three hundred years. [115] Both delegations came to her workshop daily, praising her ingenious gates and demanding opposite things, and both were right, which was the cruelest arithmetic Mirena knew. [141]

Her mentor, old Bahr, had left her two things: a coil of antique surveyor's chain, and a letter she was forbidden to open until she needed it. [168] She had opened it on the day he died, and found it blank. [181] For nine years she had taken that blankness as a joke at her expense. [195] Now, with the flood season eight days off and the Choir threatening to sing in the streets, she unfolded the empty page again under the fissure-light. [221] Dappled sun fell through the cracks in the caldera roof, moving across the paper in slow coins. [238] Bahr had been slyly heartfelt in everything: he praised by insulting, mourned by joking, taught by hiding. [255] If the page said nothing, then the message was not on the page. [268]

So Mirena studied the envelope instead, and there she found her answer, by analyzing the leftover adhesive from the once-sealed flap. [289] The glue was not workshop paste. [295] Under her lens it showed gray grit, fine ash, and threads of the pale blue clay that appeared nowhere in Vhal except at the base of the eastern tiers, below the old drainage ring. [329] Bahr had sealed the letter with mud from the place he wanted her to dig. [344] It was, she thought, the most Bahr-like thing imaginable: encrypted compassion, a kindness locked in a puzzle so that she would only receive it when she was clever enough and desperate enough to need it. [379]

She took the surveyor's chain and went down into the bowl as the building exhaled its evening breath. [397] The chain was older than the republic: sixty-six brass links, worn smooth, each one a measure Bahr had trusted more than any instrument. [420] She laid it out along the eastern tiers, link by link, until the blue clay appeared under her heel. [439] Four chains from the wall, she struck stone that rang hollow. [450] Beneath a slab of fitted basalt lay a sluice mouth no survey had ever recorded, its throat curving away beneath the seats. [472] Her hands began to tremble, and she let them. [481]

The builders of the amphitheater had solved this problem once already. [492] The lower galleries were a reservoir, sealed and invisible, holding water under the singing floor instead of over it. [511] Bahr had found it in his last summer and told nobody, because a gift given too early is only an order. [532] He had wanted the machinist who saved Vhal to be her, not his ghost. [546]

It took her six days to clear the sluice and rebuild the gate that fed it. [562] She worked between the breaths, when the pressure was even and the mortar would set. [577] Farmers hauled rubble; two Choir women, suspicious and curious, held her lamps. [589] She had not set out to please them; she had set out to harmonize what they wanted, which was a harder and better thing. [613]

On the first night of flood season she opened the gate. [624] Water went down instead of up, filling the dark galleries with a long swallowing sigh. [639] The floor of the amphitheater stayed dry. [646] At the autumn Remembrance the Choir of Ash sang, and the stone rings answered strangely, deeper than memory, because there was now a sea beneath the stage. [673] The barley lived. [676]

Mirena sat high in the dappled tiers with the surveyor's chain coiled in her lap, feeling the building draw its slow breath around her. [700] She had kept the envelope. [705] Someday she would seal a blank letter with blue clay and leave it for whoever came after her, and they would curse her, and then they would understand. [733] That was how the mountain taught: dappled, patient, resolved. [742]
