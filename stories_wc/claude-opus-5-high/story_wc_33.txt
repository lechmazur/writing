The arcade turned cobalt at twilight, the way the treaty painters had promised it would, and Idris Harun arrived with his bag at the hour of the day's lone mail drop. [31] He was the mailman of a strip of land that two governments claimed and neither swept for mines. [49] His uniform belonged to both postal services and to neither; he had sewn the patches back to back so he could turn his sleeve at a checkpoint and be whatever was needed. [81] The clerks called this respectfully rebellious, which was their way of saying they would not stop him and would not defend him either. [104] Neyla waited under the seventh arch, counting drones. [112] He had fostered her for three winters, since the census trucks took her parents to be clarified, and clarity had never come back. [135] "Nine tonight," she said, "two low, seven high." [143] The smuggling drones ran the border after sunset, fat with insulin and cigarettes and seed stock, and the soldiers on both towers had agreed, without ever agreeing, to look at the ground. [175] Idris had learned their corridors the only way a postman could: by linking absences. [189] A house that stopped receiving letters, a name struck from a ration roll, a field whose owner no longer wrote to his brother -- string those blanks together on a map and a flight path appeared, drawn in people who were not there. [231] The hush in the arcade was brittle, the kind that cracks if a boot scrapes wrong. [247] He set the bag down and sorted by lamplight: four letters, one summons, and a parcel with no stamp at all. [268] Inside the parcel, wrapped in a sock, was a thunderstorm in a bottle. [281] He had heard of them and never held one: a hand-length glass flask of grey seed-vapor, illegal on both sides, that would unspool into a three-day rain over a hundred hectares. [312] Lightning the size of a thread turned over inside it, patient as a sleeping wasp. [327] The address label had been chewed by weather, but he could read the last line: the Orchard of the Signing, north slope. [349] Everyone in the strip knew that orchard. [356] Both delegations had planted apple whips there on the day of the ceasefire and promised water rights, and then the wells were re-surveyed, and the saplings died standing, and the word promise went quiet in the valley for eleven years. [396] Neyla looked at the bottle and then at him with the terrible practicality of a child who has been moved too often. [418] "It fell off a drone," she said; "somebody's waiting for it." [429] That was the whole of postal law, as far as Idris had ever needed it. [444] He could hand the parcel to the garrison and be thanked in a language that thanked no one. [462] Instead he did the thing that had kept him alive: he obeyed every rule he could, so that the one he broke would look like an error of enthusiasm. [491] He entered the parcel in the ledger, weighed it, stamped it with both cancellations, wrote undeliverable by vehicle in the margin, and signed his number. [516] Then he walked. [519] They went out through the arcade's blue shadow and up the spine of the hill, following the seam of absences he had mapped -- the empty barn, the unlit well-house, the cousin who had stopped answering -- because where nobody lived, nobody watched. [560] Drones passed over them twice, low enough to stir Neyla's hair, and did not care. [575] The orchard was forty dead whips in rows, silver in the last light, each with its ceremonial plaque. [593] A woman waited there with a shovel and a crate of rootstock, unsurprised by the uniform. [609] "You're late," she said; "the drop was supposed to land here." [620] "It landed with me," Idris said, and held out the bottle, receipt first, because a delivery without a signature is only a rumor. [643] She signed, and he tore the carbon and gave her the copy, and the paperwork of two hostile states briefly agreed that something had been received. [669] Neyla broke the wax. [673] The storm came out slowly, a grey thread climbing, spreading, folding the twilight into cloud, and the first drops hit the plaques and ran the dust off the old promises until the letters could be read again. [710] They planted in the rain, all three of them, forty new whips in forty old holes. [726] Idris had walked this strip for nineteen years looking for the place where broken promises heal, and it turned out to be a hole in the ground with water in it. [757] In the morning both garrisons would report unauthorized weather, and neither would name him. [771] Neyla asked if they could come back in spring, and he said yes, and meant it, which is how a promise starts. [793]
