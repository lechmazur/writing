My mother mapped corridors by their echoes, and when she drowned the guild sent me her calipers, her chalk, and her unpaid debts. [23] It also sent me her last unfinished commission: the Halvard house, sealed since the water came inland. [40] After the climate shifts, nobody enters old buildings anymore; the damp has eaten their beams, and a cartographer who trusts her feet instead of her ears does not come home. [70] So we listen instead. [74] We stand at a threshold, strike a wooden block, and read the returning sound the way a sailor reads a coastline. [95] Corridor echo cartographers, the guild calls us, and there are eleven of us left. [109] I was always the sensitively perceptive one, my mother said, which in our family was less a compliment than a diagnosis. [130]

The Halvard passage ran from the kitchen wing to the drowned orangery, an ivy-veiled throat of green that from outside looked like a tunnel breathing. [155] I cut a mouth in the leaves, set my block down, and listened for an hour. [171] The echo was wrong. [175] Thirty-one feet of corridor returned thirty-eight feet of sound, and the extra seven arrived late and soft, as though from a room that had politely waited its turn. [203] My mother had recorded the same figure in her fieldbook and written beneath it: the wall is dreaming. [221] That phrase cost her the guild. [227] She said it aloud at assembly, and afterward they gave her sheds to survey, and then nothing. [244] I took her commission to find out what walls dream about. [255]

I slept in the Halvard kitchen to stay out of the rain, and found the fireplace packed with half-burned diaries. [275] Someone had tried to lose them and lost their nerve halfway: the covers were charred, the middles intact and swollen with damp. [297] They were my grandmother's. [301] Over three nights I learned what my mother must have learned and never said plainly: the guild's founding survey of this house was a forgery, and my grandmother had drawn it. [332] She had been paid to omit seven feet. [340]

Between two pages, stiff as a dried leaf, lay a film negative strip. [353] I held it to the lamp and glimpsed the passage as it had been: four frames of corridor with a door in it, the door standing exactly where I had measured smooth plaster, and in the last frame a woman in that doorway with her hand raised, not waving, warning. [403] My grandmother's hand in the margin: Miss Ryse asked me not to draw her room. [418]

In the morning I found the seam, a hairline under the ivy where the plaster met older plaster, and I opened it with my mother's chisel. [444] Seven feet of shelving, dry as a held breath. [453] Glass plates, tide ledgers, parish surveys - the whole shoreline as it stood before the water moved, kept by an archivist who understood that a company which owns the old maps owns the new coast. [487] Miss Ryse had asked to be left off the map so that her room would not be cleared, and my grandmother had agreed, and had also taken the company's money, and both things are true, and that is the shape of what I inherited. [531]

I sat in the ivy light for a long time with the calipers in my lap. [547] The guild would have filed the correction and quietly lost it, because a forgery that old has become a foundation. [567] So I drew the passage as it truly is, seven feet and all, and sent it to the Coast Commission with the negative strip, my grandmother's marginal note, and my mother's fieldbook page. [600] They will take my license for it; the debt passes to me either way, and I would rather carry the honest one. [622]

My mother was not mad. [627] A wall keeps the shape of every room taken from it, and if you strike a block and stand still long enough, sensitive to the late soft part of the sound, you can hear the rooms it is still holding. [667] That is what walls dream about. [673] I intend to spend the rest of my life writing them down. [685]
