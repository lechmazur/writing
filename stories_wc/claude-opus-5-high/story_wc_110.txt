The rain quit at moonrise, and the crystal forecourt began to whisper. [12] Water in the seams of the pavement set the old quartz ringing, and every sound the square had swallowed that day came back thinned, patient, resonant. [38] Mira, the quiet night registrar, came out with her ledger, as she had every wet night since words became unnecessary. [58] Three winters ago the town had agreed, in its last spoken vote, that speech had done enough damage; since then people traded meaning on the backs of concert tickets, the only paper left in bulk after the hall closed. [97] Mira's work was to receive those tickets at the registry window, copy their cryptic doodles into the ledger, and keep the town's memory honest. [121] For that she used the lens from the cinema projector, the last thing salvaged before the hall was shuttered. [140] It was a strange piece of glass, ambiguously clear: it enlarged a doodle until every tremor of the pen showed, yet wrapped each line in a faint halo, so that a fisher's hook could be read as a question mark, and a question mark as a hook. [187] Interpretation, therefore, belonged to Mira, and the town had learned to mistrust anyone who held that much of it alone. [207]

That was why she came out on wet nights to eavesdrop. [218] The quartz gave back the day's leftover whispers - people still whispered, out of habit, when the doodles failed them - and in the resonant hush Mira could hear what the town would not write down. [252] Tonight the pavement returned the baker's voice, low and grieved: She keeps a second ledger. She decides who we are. [272] Then the smith's answer, colder: Then she is the only one still speaking. [285]

They were not wrong. [289] There was a second ledger, a thin one, and in it Mira had recorded the entries she had softened. [308] When the widow Cadhan had drawn a spiral collapsing into a dot, Mira had logged grief instead of leaving, and the search parties had never gone out. [335] When the miller's boy had scratched a jagged crown over a stick figure, Mira had logged a joke, and the boy had kept his hands. [360] She had told herself she was protecting them. [368] Standing in the whispering forecourt, hearing herself described as a lone mouth in a mute town, she understood she had only been protecting the comfort of being necessary. [396] Mistrust had frozen around her like the rime on the registry shutters, and she had built it herself, one merciful lie at a time. [420]

At dawn she did the only thing that might thaw it. [431] She carried the projector lens out to the forecourt and set it in the iron frame where the cinema's marquee bracket still stood, aimed at the white wall of the granary. [462] Behind the lens she laid the tickets themselves, in order, every one she had ever received, and let the low sun push their doodles up onto the wall, ten feet tall. [493] Beside each she pinned her ledger page, and beside that, the page from the thin second book. [510]

By full light the town had gathered in the wet forecourt, and the quartz under their feet murmured with their breathing. [531] They saw the widow's spiral, and what Mira had written, and what Mira had meant. [546] They saw the halo the glass put around every stroke, and understood, perhaps for the first time, that reading another person is never clean work. [571] Nobody spoke; that agreement held. [576] The widow Cadhan came forward, took a blank ticket, and drew a spiral opening outward, and pressed it into Mira's hand. [597] The miller's boy drew a crown and, under it, a small even line. [610] The baker drew nothing at all, only set his palm flat on the projector lens, which was the plainest thing anyone had said in three years. [636]

That night Mira kept one ledger. [642] She left the lens standing in the forecourt so that anyone could come and read what she had made of them, and correct her. [666] People did, at first in crowds, then in twos, then hardly at all. [679] Rain came again in the fourth week, and Mira stood out in the crystal whispering forecourt out of habit, waiting to eavesdrop on what the town withheld. [706] The quartz rang and gave her back nothing but rain. [716] There had been no whispering that day, because there had been nothing left to hide, and she found she was lighter than she had been in three years - freed, absurdly, by the one thing she had spent her whole tenure withholding. [757] She went in, and slept, and the forecourt hushed behind her. [768]
