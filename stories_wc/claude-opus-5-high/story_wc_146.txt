The bell tuner came to Sallow Ford on the last dry day before the collapse. [15] I was the only one left to see him, and I had stopped counting years. [30] He walked under the ancient archway at the town's mouth, where the keystone had begun to sag like a held breath. [51] He did not hurry. [55] Nobody had hurried here in eleven years, not since the mill closed and the families went east, leaving doors ajar and kettles on cold stoves. [80] The town had become a wild hush: grass splitting the cobblestones, swallows nesting in the belfry, the whole valley holding a silence that felt less like peace than like a held note nobody would release. [115]

"You still ring the bell," he said. [122]

"Out of habit," I said. [127]

"Habit is a kind of faith." [133]

He drew from his coat an ebony tuning fork, black as a well at midnight, its tines worn smooth by decades of thumbs. [156] He struck it against the archway stone, and the note that came out was not a note so much as a direction. [178] I felt it in my heels. [184]

He called himself a bell tuner, though he answered questions the way weather answers questions. [199] When I asked where he had come from, he said, "From wherever the balance slipped." [214] When I asked what he wanted, he said, "To hear this place agree with itself again." [230]

The belfry was dying. [234] The tower's mortar had turned to flour, and the surveyor from the county had already marked it for demolition; the archway too, since one leaned on the other. [262] Six weeks, the notice said. [267] The tuner read it, folded it, and handed it back like a receipt for something I had not bought. [286]

"Then we rebuild before the collapse," he said. [294]

"We," I said. "There's one of me and one of you." [305]

"There's also the road." [309]

His method made no sense to me until the third morning. [320] He would strike the ebony fork, hold it to the cobblestones, and then walk, a slow, deliberate pace, heel and toe, matching the stone's own pitch. [346] He said a town is tuned by feet, that the cobbles remember every market day, every funeral, every child running late. [367] Stop walking, he said, and the stones forget; the mortar loosens; the bell goes flat; the arch forgets which way to lean. [389]

So we walked. [392] Dawn to dusk, the two of us, up Mill Street and down Chandler's Row, under the archway and back, the rhythm of footsteps on cobblestones knocking a pulse into a body that had none. [426] He was infinitely patient. [430] When I limped, he slowed; when I cursed him for a madman, he nodded as though madness were simply a slower tempo, and waited until I fell back into step. [460]

On the ninth day, Marta Kell came back for her mother's headstone and stayed to walk with us. [478] On the fourteenth, the Ostrow brothers arrived with a truck of lime mortar, claiming they had heard the bell from the ridge road, though I had not rung it. [507] People hear footsteps farther than they think. [514] By the fifth week there were thirty of us, and the sound of the town was no longer a hush but a weave: boots, trowels, laughter, a hammer keeping time against the archway scaffold. [548]

We repointed the tower. [552] We reset the keystone. [556] We rebuilt the bell's frame from oak the tuner chose by tapping trees with the fork, listening for the ones that answered. [578] The surveyor came, looked at the scaffolds and the fresh mortar and the people, and tore up his own notice. [598]

On the last night the tuner climbed the belfry and tuned the bell against the ebony fork, shaving the bronze until the two tones lay down together. [625] I asked him, finally, why. [630]

"Every place holds a share of the world's weight," he said. "When one goes quiet, the others carry more, and then they crack too." [654] "I walk to restore the shared balance." [661]

In the morning he was gone through the archway, and the cobbles held the print of his last steps in dew. [682] He left the ebony tuning fork on my windowsill with no note, which was, I understood, the note. [700]

Sallow Ford has a school again, and a bakery, and a Sunday market loud enough to startle the swallows. [719] Still, every dawn, before the ovens light, I strike the fork against the keystone and walk the old round, heel and toe, keeping us in tune. [745] Somewhere west there is another silent town, and a man walking toward it, infinitely patient, waiting for it to answer. [765]
