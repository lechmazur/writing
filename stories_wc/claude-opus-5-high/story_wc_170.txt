They called her the mirage painter because no one could hold her in a doorway long enough to ask her name. [21] I met her on the echoing terrace in spring, the one above the reservoir, where every cough came back to you twice. [43] Rain had just quit, and the prism parapet was doing its work, splitting the last light into ladders of color along the wet stone. [67] I was fourteen and apprenticed to the Mirror Office, which meant my job was to make sure glass told the truth. [88] Truth, in our city, meant only what already was: your face, your coat, the wall behind you, and never a version of you that had not happened yet. [116]

She was painting nothing at all, that first evening, only moving a dry brush over air while she hummed. [135] The hum was a nursery tune I knew, "Two Moons for the Miller's Girl," and under her breath the notes came out wrong in a deliberate way. [162] Down the terrace, the parapet's colors bent toward her, gathering, and in a puddle I saw myself standing taller, in a coat I did not own. [188]

"That is illegal," I said. [193] "That is a possibility," she said, perceptively calm, as if she had already heard the rest of my life and found it survivable. [216]

Over the following weeks I learned her method, because she did not hide it from me: she worked through coded lullabies in children's songs. [240] A flattened third here, a held rest there; children carried the changes home without knowing, and sang them at washbasins and window glass. [263] Mirrors, she told me, are not liars but cowards, and a song sung by someone with no fear of the future can persuade them to show possibilities. [290] That was her whole ambition, and it was why the Office wanted her: a city of citizens who can see other versions of themselves is a city that stops obeying. [320]

She could only work in the hush before the bells rang, the quarter hour when the tower crews changed and the sky went to that dusky harmony of copper and violet. [351] And she could only work with a borrowed heartbeat. [360] Mirage painters give up their own pulse when they learn the craft -- that is the price of being elusive, of never quite being where a hand can close -- so to make an image hold, she had to beat on someone else's. [401] I gave her mine. [405] Her palm on my wrist, my heart running double time while she painted, and afterward a tiredness like having climbed the tower stairs twice. [429] In those borrowed minutes she filled the terrace with things that did not exist: orchards on the reservoir, a bridge made of held breath, my mother alive and mending a net. [460]

The Office found the pattern before spring ended, though not through me. [472] A censor's daughter sang the wrong lullaby to a hallway mirror and showed her father a girl who had run away to sea. [495] They gave me the arrest order because I knew the terrace, and because they believed an apprentice with no family would want the promotion. [519]

I climbed up in the rain and waited by the prism parapet until the water beaded and the light broke open along it. [542] She came at the usual hour, into the hush before the bells, and held out her hand for my wrist. [562] I gave her the order instead, and then my wrist, and told her to paint the harbor road while she still had a heartbeat to spend. [588] It was not courage; it was grace, the only kind an apprentice can grant -- twenty minutes, freely given, and no report filed. [610]

She painted, calm as ever, the road out of the city, and it hung on the wet air, doubled in every colored facet of the parapet. [636] Then the bells rang, and she was gone into them the way a mirage goes, and my pulse came back to me slow and entirely my own. [663]

I am the Mirror Office now, or the last clerk of it, and the children on the terrace still sing the crooked songs. [686] I teach them the flattened third myself, in the hush before the bells, and I do not correct the mirrors anymore. [707] When rain comes and the parapet opens its colors, I sometimes see a tall man in a coat he does not own yet, and I let him stay. [735]
