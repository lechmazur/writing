The velvet observatory had been sewn shut for a century, and at midsummer it breathed like a lung. [18] Ilse Varga climbed the spiral under panels of wine-dark cloth, carrying her notebook, her thermos, and a dread she could not file. [40] She was a provenance researcher, and the Hadrian Trust paid her to prove that beautiful things belonged to it. [59] Tonight the beautiful thing lay on a bed of packed snow beneath the dome: a violin of transparent ice crystal, strung with gut, glowing faintly from within. [86] The old records called that light the promise, and said it lasted only as long as the maker's intention stayed unbroken. [107] She had until dawn to write her report, before the promise lost its glow and the instrument became merely water. [127] Ilse poured coffee into the tin lid and let it slosh, because she had learned years ago that her future arrived in stains. [150] The ring she tipped onto the marble spread into a map she recognized: her own life, perforated. [167] That was the word for it - a perforated destiny, a fate riddled with holes where choices had been offered and declined. [188] Every stain for eleven years had shown the same lace of absences, and every year the lace grew thinner. [207] Below the dome, in the courtyard, the Trust's mulberry trees were fruiting, and they would not stop talking. [225] The plant-voices had arrived after a lightning strike in a Tbilisi greenhouse, an accident she had never reported to anyone. [245] Accidental telepathy with vegetation was not a credential; it was a symptom, and it made her the best researcher the Trust had ever ruined. [269] Trees remember hands. [273] The mulberries remembered the girl who had grafted them, and they told Ilse her name: Anezka Doll, luthier, dead sixty years, whose orchard once stood where the observatory now sat. [303] The violin was hers, made in a single winter night from river ice and patience, and it had never been sold. [324] It had been taken, along with the land, along with the trees, along with every document that might have said so. [345] There was no paper anywhere to prove it - only fruit, and fruit is not evidence. [360] Anezka's granddaughter kept a workshop two streets away, teaching eleven students to carve, and she had written to the Trust nine times asking only to hold the violin once before it thawed. [392] Ilse's job tonight was simple: weigh a dream against an empire, and report which one weighed more. [409] She drank the rest of the coffee and stared at the perforations in the stain until she understood what the holes were. [431] Each one was a night like this: an object, a true owner, a form she had signed anyway. [449] A destiny does not tear all at once; it is punched out slowly, one convenient omission at a time. [468] Under the velvet the violin's glow had gone the color of crushed mulberries, that particular ache, sweet and staining and impossible to wash out. [492] Ilse did two things before dawn. [498] First, she wrote the report she was paid for, and in the provenance field she typed the name the trees had given her, with the mulberry grove listed as the witness. [529] They would laugh, and then they would check the tax rolls, and the rolls would agree, because an empire keeps receipts for everything it takes. [554] Second, she went down into the courtyard at the hour when midsummer light is already deciding to leave, and she cut twelve green shoots from the oldest mulberry. [582] The tree consented, loudly, the way plants do when asked to propagate: yes, yes, more of me, everywhere. [600] She wrapped the cuttings in wet cloth and carried them two streets, to a workshop where eleven students were learning to carve, and left them with a note: plant these; they remember your grandmother; come at once. [637] The granddaughter arrived while the glow was still in the ice, and the velvet was drawn back, and she played four notes before the strings went slack with melting. [666] The sound was thin and enormous. [671] Ilse listened with her hands flat on the marble, and felt the stain of her old coffee under her palm, and felt, for the first time, no new hole opening. [701] The Trust dismissed her by the following Tuesday. [709] The water went into the mulberry roots, since nobody could think what else to do with a melted masterpiece. [728] That autumn the grove fruited so heavily that the pickers' hands stayed purple till November, and Ilse, jobless, reading her coffee on a borrowed table, saw a future with fewer holes in it than she deserved. [764] The trees insisted she keep it anyway. [771]
