The grotto coiled into the headland like a sleeping serpent, and Maren followed its turns by feel, because the mist swallowed even her own hands. [25] She had been the village's star watcher for nineteen years, and in all that time she had never once left the ground. [47] Earthbound, the elders called her, half a blessing and half a joke: the woman who knew every constellation by name but could not climb the cliff stair without her knees complaining. [78] Tonight she carried a satchel of sea glass, each piece gathered from the tideline over four seasons of walking. [97] The mist here never lifted; that was the grotto's gift and its curse. [110] Fog held light the way a held breath holds a word. [121]

Twelve winters ago, a beacon fire on this headland had thrown its glare into the fog until the whole cliff seemed to burn, and three boats had steered straight for it and split on the rocks. [157] Her brother Idris had been in the second boat. [166] Since then the council forbade flame above the tideline, and the coast went dark, and Maren had spent nineteen years trying to learn how to share light without using fire. [196] Stars, she had decided, were the answer, if only she could gather them. [209]

At the grotto's innermost coil she lit nothing, and instead set out her tools by touch: a leather strop, a dish of wet sand, a lump of tin oxide, and a small brass balance. [243] Gem polishing was a jeweler's craft, and she had learned it badly, from a book, and then well, from ten thousand failures. [265] Sea glass was the worst material a polisher could choose: cloudy, pitted, scarred soft by the sea. [282] But sea glass had already been weathered into roundness, and roundness was what held light. [297]

She began to grind, and as always the work drew memories up from the dark the way a bucket draws water. [318] Idris at nine, pressing a green shard into her palm and calling it a mermaid's coin. [334] Idris at twenty, laughing at her charts, saying the stars were fine but a man needed something closer to steer by. [355] Idris not at all, and the fog burning orange over the water. [367] Her hands kept moving. [371] Grief, she had found, was best spent as motion. [380]

By the third hour the shard had lost its scars. [390] She rinsed it, dried it on her sleeve, and set it on the brass balance to weigh it against the counterweight she had carried for nine years: Idris's old net-sinker. [420] The arms hung level. [424] Exactly right; too heavy and the glass would sag in its mounting, too light and it would not bend the starlight far enough. [447] She had never managed level before. [453]

Maren climbed out of the serpent's mouth into open air, where the mist lay on the headland like a sleeping animal, and she fitted the polished bead into the iron cradle she had bolted to the cliff two summers ago. [493] Then she waited for the zephyr. [499] It came near midnight, the soft landward breeze that bloomed every clear night in spring, and it opened the fog the way a hand opens a flower. [526] The stars came through. [530] And the bead took them. [535]

It did not burn, it did not flare, it did not throw glare into the fog to lie to sailors. [555] It simply held what the sky gave it, reflectively vibrant, a small round green-white gleam that could be seen from the water but never mistaken for a harbor. [583] Fire shouts; this only answered. [588]

Below her, a late boat adjusted its course a half-point north, and Maren felt something in her chest go quiet -- a pearl of stillness, round and cool, settling where nineteen years of grinding had hollowed her out. [625] She would need forty more beads to line the whole headland, and she was fifty-one, and her knees complained on the stair. [647] It did not matter. [651] An earthbound woman cannot reach the stars, but she had learned, at last, how to make them come down and stand watch. [673] In her pocket, the green shard Idris had given her waited its turn on the wheel. [689]
