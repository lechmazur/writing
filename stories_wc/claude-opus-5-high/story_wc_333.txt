The locksmith kept his shop where five alleys met, a shadowed nexus the city had forgotten to name. [18] Ines had walked past it for eleven years without seeing the door. [30] She saw it now because she had finally admitted what she wanted back. [43] Inside, the walls were lined not with keys but with envelopes, thousands of them, each sealed with wax and labeled in a hand too small to read from standing height. [73] The locksmith, small and gray, was unassumingly bold in the way of men who have never needed to raise their voices. [94]

"You left something," he said. [99]

"An idea," Ines said. [103]

She had been nineteen, and the idea had been enormous - a way of building bridges that curved instead of spanned, cheaper, stranger, beautiful - and she had been so frightened of being wrong in public that she had brought it here to be enveloped, sealed away with all the other secrets nobody could bear to carry. [158] The locksmith had taken his fee then: a lock of her hair, tied with a green ribbon, pressed into the wax as the seal. [182]

"Thirty years," he said. "You want it back." [190]

"I want to build it before I die." [198]

He climbed a ladder and drew down an envelope thick as a brick, and set it on the counter, and Ines saw her own hair on the front, the ribbon faded to the color of dishwater. [234] It disoriented her more than anything had in decades, that young brown hair, still young. [249]

"I can't open it," he said. "Only the sealer can. And the seal answers to the sky." [266]

He explained it the way other men explain bus routes: the envelope would open at the nexus, at twilight, and the first star she saw would tell her which of the three wax seals to break. [302]

"And if I choose wrong?" [307]

"Then you will read a different secret," he said, "and it will not be yours, and you will be responsible for it." [329]

So she stood in the middle of the five alleys as the light went out of the west. [347] The city hushed around her, or perhaps she only stopped hearing it; the gap of sky above the nexus went from apricot to slate to something deeper, and in that interval she felt the whole enormous hush of cosmic possibility open overhead, every future still unassigned. [393] Thirty years of improbable longing had brought her here, an old woman with arthritic hands and a stolen evening, hoping to be told she had been right at nineteen. [422]

The first star came out low and west, small, green-white, exactly between two chimneys. [436] Three seals: one at the top of the envelope, one at the left edge, one at the bottom right. [455] Low and west. [458] She turned the envelope so its edges matched the compass of the alleys and broke the lower seal, the wax cracking like a knuckle. [482]

Inside, in her own round handwriting, were forty pages of drawings. [493] They were wrong. [496] The curve was wrong; the load would have walked itself off the abutment in the first hard wind; the girl who drew them had not known enough. [523]

Ines laughed out loud in the empty nexus, and the laugh shook something loose in her chest that had been clenched since 1971. [546] She had spent thirty years afraid of a mistake she could have fixed in an afternoon. [562] The fear had not been guarding the idea; it had been guarding her from finding out how small the flaw was. [583]

She went back into the shop with the pages under her arm. [595]

"Keep the hair," she said. "I'd like the ribbon." [604]

The locksmith cut it free and handed it over without comment, and if he smiled, it was in some room behind his face. [627] Outside, the second star had arrived, and the third, and Ines tied the faded green ribbon around her wrist and walked home the long way, doing arithmetic in her head. [657] She had eleven years of hands left, maybe fewer, and one bridge to draw correctly. [672] Behind her, in the shadowed nexus, the shop's window went dark, and the thousands of enveloped secrets waited in it for their owners to grow brave, or to die, or simply to look up and see a star and guess. [712] Ines never learned his name, and never needed it; he had only ever been the lock, and she had been the key all along. [736]
