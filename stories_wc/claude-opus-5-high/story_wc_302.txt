The first letter came on a Tuesday, and it said only that my brother was dead in the fog country beyond Kelling. [22] The second letter I had not yet written, and until I did, nothing in my life could move forward. [41] Between those two letters I lived like a held breath. [51]

His effects arrived in a tin box: a comb, a pawn ticket, and the confidence man's watch he had won, he claimed, from a swindler on the Kelling road. [80] It ran backward. [83] Not broken-backward, but cyclically reversed: twelve hours widdershins, then twelve hours true, over and over, so that it told the correct time exactly twice a day and lied beautifully the rest. [114] I was a telegraph clerk, and I understood instruments that lie on a schedule. [128] So I began to document it. [134]

Three nights after the tin box, my key began to chatter on a dead circuit, and the message spelled nonsense that rearranged itself the longer I stared. [161] It was an unfolding cipher: each solved line contained the key to a longer line beneath it, the way a seed contains a ladder. [185] The first line said: THE VEILED DREAMER SPEAKS ONLY WHAT WAS NEVER SAID. [198] The second said: SHE KEEPS THE LIBRARY OF UNSPOKEN WORDS. [207] The third told me my brother had gone looking for it and had not come back, and that I would want to, and that wanting was permitted but hurrying was not. [238]

I should have been frightened. [242] Instead I felt a cautious exuberance, the way a man feels when a locked door finally rattles. [259]

If the messages were arriving on my wires, they were arriving from somewhere, and somewhere could be measured. [277] I found the place through propagation delays. [284] Every night I sent a pulse down each of the six lines out of the station and timed the echo against the watch, which was useless for hours but exquisite for fractions, its reversed escapement ticking in a rhythm I could subtract from itself. [328] Five lines answered honestly. [332] The sixth answered eleven milliseconds too late, always, and only in fog. [344] Eleven milliseconds is a little over two thousand miles of copper, and the sixth line ran nine miles to a dead orchard. [366] The difference was not distance. [371] It was reluctance. [374]

I walked the sixth line on a night thick as wool, the watch in my palm running the wrong way, and at the orchard's end the mist began to glow. [404] The library stood in it like a lamp inside a cloud: shelves without walls, aisles that went soft at the edges, every book breathing a pale light. [431] The veiled dreamer sat at the center in a chair of grey linen, asleep, her face behind layers of gauze that stirred when she exhaled. [456] Each breath became a sentence somebody in the world had swallowed. [466]

I did not lift the veil. [472] My brother had lifted it, I think; there was a book on the floor with his handwriting inside. [490] It held everything he had meant to tell me and never had: that he was afraid, that he had gambled our mother's house and lost it, that he loved me and could not say so sober. [526] The last page was blank, and warm. [532]

I understood then what the cipher had been unfolding toward, and why hurrying was forbidden. [547] A book here is finished only when the thing is finally said aloud somewhere out in the ordinary world. [566] The library is not a treasury. [572] It is a debt ledger. [577]

So I sat on the damp floor beneath the dreamer's slow breathing and wrote the second letter there, by booklight, to his widow. [600] I told her the truth about the house, and the fear, and the love, and I said it out loud as I wrote, which felt foolish and necessary. [628] Around me, shelf by shelf, a few books dimmed and closed. [639] The watch in my pocket turned and turned, wrong then right, wrong then right, and I let it. [657]

I keep the station now, and I document the delays every night in a ledger nobody audits. [674] Eleven milliseconds, most evenings. [677] Ten, on the good ones, when someone in Kelling has finally said a hard thing to a person's face. [696] I have not gone back. [701] But I am cheerful about the wires, cautiously, the way a man is cheerful about a door he knows is not locked. [723] Somewhere in the mist, a blank page waits for each of us, and mine is getting shorter. [740]
