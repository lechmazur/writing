The orchard planet Cormel wore its floating rock arches like a broken crown, and beneath them, Tobin Ashe hammered. [19] He was a meek blacksmith, the kind who apologized to the anvil. [31] When the gravity ore gave out, the mines closed, the arches stopped rising, and the orchards came down in a slow avalanche of apple wood and shale. [58] Most families chose sleep. [62] Sleep was cheap; waking was not. [68] The magician came every third season in a wagon hung with lanterns, selling bartered awakenings at whatever price the desperate could name. [90] His instrument was a length of azure silk, thin as breath, that could be drawn across a sleeper's face only in the narrow hour between understanding and confusion. [118] Too early and the sleeper woke knowing nothing; too late and the knowing curdled into nonsense. [134]

Tobin had been keeping the hour for nine years. [143] He kept it in the abandoned mine shafts, where the cold ran even and dreams ran slow. [160] Four hundred sleepers lay in the old ore galleries, and Tobin walked them nightly with a lamp and a slate, marking whose breath had begun to quicken. [187] That quickening was the signal: the sleeper had climbed as far as understanding and would soon slide toward confusion. [206]

The magician's price that winter was Tobin's forge. [214] "You have nothing else," the magician said, folding the silk into a square the size of a plum. [232] "Nobody buys horseshoes on a dying world." [239] Tobin, who had never once raised his voice, did not raise it then. [252] He offered instead the thing he had been making in secret: grafting irons. [265] Slim, hooked, hollow-handled, shaped to drive a cutting of apple wood down through shale into whatever soil survived below. [284] He had tested them on the rubble slopes under the westward arch, and eleven of forty cuttings had taken. [303] Eleven was not many, but eleven was not zero, and coaxing resilience from rubble was the only ambition Tobin had ever allowed himself. [326] "Rubble is not ruin," he said. [332] "It is only stone that has not been asked for anything yet." [344] The magician laughed, but he took the irons, because a magician who cannot mend his own wagon axle is a magician on foot. [367] Twelve awakenings, he agreed. [371] "Choose your twelve." [374]

Tobin had chosen them years ago, deliberate as a man setting rivets: four orchardists, three ditch-diggers, two masons, a grafter, a cooper, and old Hessa Vane, who remembered how the arches had been quarried and therefore knew which mine shafts still ran wet. [417] Not the kindest twelve, not his friends; the twelve who together could make water walk uphill. [433] They woke over four nights, the azure silk passing over each face like a slow blue tide, and each of them opened their eyes in the same narrow hour, holding their own names. [466] Hessa sat up in the ore gallery, looked at the lamp, and said, "The lower drifts." [482] "We flood the lower drifts and let the roots find them." [493] Tobin nodded, because that was exactly what he had woken her to say. [506]

They worked through two seasons using abandoned mine shafts as aqueducts, cracking sumps, threading meltwater from the shaded undersides of the floating arches down into the dark, so that the rubble slopes drank from below. [541] Tobin made the irons, hundreds of them, and taught the children to graft. [554] He never once gave a speech. [560] But when the first blossom came out of a shale heap that everyone had agreed was dead, the whole hillside turned a stubborn pale blue-white under the arch shadow, and the orchardists called that color azure defiance and planted it everywhere they could reach. [604]

The magician returned in the third season and found no desperate buyers. [616] Tobin bought the silk from him outright, paying in cider, iron, and passage off-world. [630] He hung it above the forge door by day, where the draft moved it like water. [646] Every night after, quietly determined, he took it down and went into the shafts with his lamp and his slate, waking three hundred and eighty-eight sleepers by hand, one narrow hour at a time, free of charge, because an awakening should never have been something to barter for. [694] It took him eleven years. [699] He apologized to each of them for the wait, and each of them, blinking in the blue lamplight between understanding and confusion, heard a meek man's voice and woke up anyway. [730]
