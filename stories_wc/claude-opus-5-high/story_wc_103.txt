The living bridge garden hung over the gorge on roots that had been braided by hand for two hundred years. [20] Mira Okonkwo had come to plant a hedge maze on it, which every engineer called madness and every gardener called prayer. [41] She was the last hedge maze designer of her school, and the only one who had ever been retrocognitively trapped. [61] The condition had a clinical name and no cure: her mind could look backward with perfect, merciless clarity, and forward not at all. [84] She remembered her brother Teo's laugh at eleven in the morning, the exact humidity of it, but she could not imagine tomorrow's breakfast. [107] Memory, for Mira, was not a room she visited; it was a room she never left. [123] Teo had drowned in the gorge below, and the photographs were fading. [135] That was the part she could not forgive. [143] Twelve prints, kept in a tin, and each year the silver lifted off them a little more, until his face was a weather pattern and his hands were fog. [172] Her memory held him; the paper let him disintegrate. [181] This offended her sense of justice, and it taught her the thing she came to want most: to discover the texture of time. [204] Not its length, which everyone knew, but its grain, its weave, whether it could be touched the way you touch cloth in the dark. [228]

The halo fragment came out of the bridge itself. [237] While she was measuring root-spans, a fig root split and gave up a curved plate of gilded copper, torn from some icon the flood had taken a century ago. [266] It was a saint's halo, or a third of one, and it did not behave. [281] Held up, it bent the garden behind it outward, as if the air had been pulled into a saddle shape, the hedges leaning away into more room than the gorge contained. [312] Mira had spent thirty years learning that a maze is a machine for changing what a person is willing to believe about distance. [335] She understood the fragment at once, and understood it through hyperbolic geometry, the only language she had ever loved. [354] On a saddle surface, parallel paths flee each other; area swells faster than any wall can enclose it. [372] If she planted the hedges along the fragment's curvature, the maze would hold corridors longer than the bridge, and a walker could travel farther than she moved. [399] Farther, she thought, or earlier. [404]

She worked for four seasons in a quaking reverie, half awake, the bridge breathing under her boots, her hands planting box and yew while her mind stood in 1998 watching Teo lace his shoes. [438] The roots accepted the pattern; living things like curvature better than rulers do. [451] She set the halo fragment at the center, upright in a socket of braided root, where it caught the gorge light and threw it outward in slow, widening rings. [480]

On the first clear night she walked in. [488] The corridors did what she had calculated: they gave more than they held. [501] Twenty paces took an hour; a turn took a year. [511] She passed her own girlhood standing in a hedge gap, and did not stop. [525] At the center, under the fragment's rings, she found the thing she had come for. [540] Time had a texture, and it was the texture of a photograph going. [553] Not cloth, not stone: a nap of silver dust, loose, already lifting, the same grain that had been leaving Teo's face in the tin for twenty years. [580] She put her palm against the air and felt it come away on her skin. [595] Everything was made of that, including the bridge, including her. [605] The grief she had kept sharp for twenty years was not an error in the world; it was the world's material. [626] She stood in the quaking hedges and let it go through her, shaking, awake for the first time in the only direction she could not see. [652]

In the morning the maze had begun to disintegrate. [661] Curvature that steep cannot be held by anything alive for long, and the yew had gone grey, the roots slack, the socket empty. [684] The halo fragment lay in the gorge, in the water that had taken her brother, shining and ordinary. [702] Mira left it there. [706] She walked out along the living bridge, and she took the tin of fading photographs out of her coat and looked at them properly, the way you look at weather. [736] Teo was almost gone from them. [742] That was correct. [745] She put the tin in the roots, where the figs would grow through it, and went to design something that would not last, and knew, for once, that it was enough. [776]
