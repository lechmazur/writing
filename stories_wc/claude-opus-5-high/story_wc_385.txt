Yarrow sang at the serpentine nexus under starlight, where nine canyons braided into one throat of stone. [17] She was the last echo singer, and her work was simple accounting: sing a name, wait, and write down whether the rock gave it back. [42] Most names returned, thinned and reed-blown, like a flute played in another room. [55] Some did not return at all. [60] Her brother Teal was one of those. [67] Eleven years ago she had sung him into the dark and the dark had kept him, and since then she had wanted only one thing: to learn why some people echo. [98]

The couriers came up the Snake Road at odd hours, and Yarrow, richly perceptive in the way of people who listen for a living, could tell a dying man by his footsteps. [130] This one arrived with his ribs broken and a cloth bundle pressed to his chest. [145] Inside was a whispering seashell half, its broken edge bright as a new tooth. [159] "Hold it up while the universe hums its secret song," the courier said, "and it will tell you everything, a little at a time." [183] Then he died, and the canyon did not give his name back. [195]

Yarrow had heard the hum all her life, the third hour after midnight, when the stars stood still enough to sound. [216] That night she climbed to the nexus and pressed the shell to her ear. [230] It gave her one sentence, and no more: "The echo is not the stone's doing." [245] The second night: "It is a ledger, and the ledger has keepers." [257] The third night she learned their name, the Registrars, and understood at last why the nexus was watched by men who claimed to be salt merchants. [283] The shell fed her through progressive disclosure, one truth per hum, and Yarrow came to suspect this was mercy rather than cruelty; the whole thing at once would have broken her. [314]

For what the shell described were webs of intrigue older than the road: nine canyon houses, three rival guilds, and a treaty that let the Registrars decide whose voices the world would keep. [347] An echo was a vote, a debt, a bribe. [356] Names were bought back from silence and sold into it. [366] Teal had not been swallowed by the dark; he had been erased by a clerk with a clean pen. [385]

On the ninth night the shell told her the last thing, the thing the courier had died carrying. [403] Teal was alive. [406] He held the other half of the shell, in a copy room beneath the Registrars' tower, where the unechoed were kept to do the writing, since a man without an echo cannot be quoted, cannot testify, cannot be missed. [445] The shell had been whispering with his voice the whole time, worn thin by distance, which was why every sentence had sounded like someone she loved. [471]

There was one way to undo an erasure. [478] The nexus would return any name sung by an echo singer, and the ledger, bound by the treaty it had written itself, was obliged to record what the stone said. [508] But the stone kept a balance. [513] To put a name back into the world, the singer had to trade her own. [528]

Yarrow sat with that under the cold spill of stars, listening to the hum, thinking that the universe sounded less like a hymn than like a man breathing through a reed, patient and unconsoled. [562] At the third hour she stood at the mouth of the ninth canyon and sang her brother's name. [580] It went out along the braided gorges, and the stone turned it over, and gave it back: Teal, Teal, Teal, thinning into the dark. [604] She sang her own name after it, to ensure the balance was paid in the right direction, and heard nothing at all. [626]

The salt merchants came up the road at dawn and walked past her without seeing. [641] In the copy room a young man with ink on his knuckles felt the shell in his pocket warm, and heard, for the first time and the last, his sister telling him to go home. [676]

Yarrow stayed at the nexus, unrecorded, singing for the strangers who climbed the Snake Road. [691] They never remembered her afterward, though some of them wept while she worked and could not say why. [709] She had her answer, which was the only thing she had asked for: people echo because someone, somewhere, decided they should, and the deciding can be taken away from them. [739] Every night the hum came, and she held the broken half to her ear, and the shell, having nothing left to disclose, hummed back. [763] It was company. [766]
