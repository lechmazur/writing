The cobalt observatory kept autumn the way other houses keep silverware: carefully, and in drawers. [15] Leaves came in under the dome's copper seam and lay there, unswept, because Maret the galaxy-chart embroiderer said sweeping frightened the quiet. [37] She was eighty-one, and everyone in the capital agreed she was a fool. [50] That was the point. [54] Maret embroidered star charts for the Ministry of Navigation, thread by thread, and she hummed nonsense while she worked, and she asked the guards questions about their mothers. [82] Fools are given access. [86] Fools are handed the sealed dispatch to hold while an officer ties his boot. [100] Her only light was the flame from the eternal candle, a thumb of blue fire that had burned in the observatory since the first astronomers cut the dome, and which, by old law, no one could extinguish or move. [139] Maret had discovered, forty years ago, that the flame leaned. [149] It leaned toward doors before they opened; it shivered when a man was about to lie. [165] It read, she believed, through adjacent possibilities -- the near-futures pressing against this one like rooms sharing a wall. [184] So she learned to synchronize her needle to its flicker. [194] One stitch per lean. [198] The charts she delivered to the Ministry were accurate; the charts she kept were something else, a second cloth on which the constellations were the names of men, and the threads between them were the things they had agreed to do in October. [241] Unraveling a conspiracy, she had found, is only embroidery run backward. [252] The Ministry inspectors came twice a year, looked at her nonsense humming, and wrote 'harmless' in the margin. [270] Autumn was the season of the plot's ripening, and Maret knew the date before the plotters did, because the flame had leaned eleven times toward the eastern barracks on the same evening hour. [303] Eleven days before the coup, a girl came up the stairs with a broken sextant and an unbroken expression. [322] 'They sent me to check whether you are senile,' she said. [333] 'Am I?' Maret asked, and offered her tea in a cup with no bottom. [347] The girl -- Ilva, a colonel's clerk -- laughed, and then stopped, because the old woman's eyes were doing arithmetic. [367] Maret did not report her, and did not warn her. [377] She only worked, and let the echoed silence of the dome do what it always did to visitors: hollow them out until something true fell loose. [403] In that silence every footstep came back to you twice, once as sound and once as judgment. [420] By the fourth visit Ilva was talking about her brother, conscripted and dead at Semel, whose name appeared in no ledger. [441] This was why Maret stayed in the cold dome at eighty-one: not to save the state, which had never asked, but to listen for unheard stories, the ones the archives were built to forget. [475] She stitched the brother into the cloth as a small unnamed star and told Ilva where to find him there. [495] Ilva wept, and then asked the dangerous question: what else was on the cloth? [509] Three nights before the coup, they spread it under the candle. [520] Nine names, thirteen threads, one knot at the Ministry of Navigation, where a man had been redrawing the coastal charts so that loyal ships would run aground while the barracks marched. [551] 'You could stop it,' Ilva said. [557] 'I am a fool,' Maret said. [563] 'Fools are not believed -- that is the price of being allowed to see.' [577] 'But you are a clerk, and clerks are believed, and the flame leans toward the harbor tonight.' [594] So Ilva carried the corrected coastal charts to the harbormaster in the dark, and the loyal ships did not run aground, and the coup, when it came, lasted six hours instead of six years. [628] No history records the embroiderer. [632] Histories are written by the believed. [638] But in the cobalt observatory the leaves still lie unswept, and the small blue flame still leans, and a woman with a needle sits beneath it, synchronizing, waiting for the next voice the archives have no room for.
