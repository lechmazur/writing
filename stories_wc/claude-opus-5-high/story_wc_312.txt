The last fish in the world was caught on a Tuesday, by a boy with a bent pin, and nobody cheered. [21] After that, the Tidewardens closed their temple doors and let their devotion go brittle, the way glaze goes brittle on a kiln-cooled pot. [44] Only Orren stayed, because Orren had nowhere else to be, and because he still believed the sea could be rebuilt. [64] They called him the lukewarm mage. [70] It was not an insult, exactly; it was a measurement. [80] Orren could heat water, but only to the temperature of a forgotten bath -- never to steam, never to boiling, never to anything useful for a siege or a supper. [109] His masters had told him, kindly, that a talent like that was a joke the universe told at his expense. [129] He had thanked them, earnestly, and written the lesson down. [139]

Every dawn he walked the sapphire viaduct, the last aqueduct of the old kingdom, whose blue-glass arches carried mountain melt across a basin that had once been an ocean. [168] At sunrise the glass drank the light and threw it down in long trembling bars, so that the dry seabed below looked, for an hour, like water. [195] Orren carried a broken ship's bell on his back. [204] It came from the Meridian, the last vessel to sail the basin, and it had cracked when she settled into the mud. [226] A cracked bell cannot ring; it can only cough. [235] But a cracked bell, hung upside down beneath an arch, makes an excellent basin for catching drips. [252]

This was the whole of Orren's theory, and he had spent four years proving it with a notebook and a candle-clock. [273] The viaduct leaked. [276] Every joint in its two hundred miles wept at a rate he had learned to read the way a physician reads a pulse: eleven drops a minute at the seventh arch, forty at the ninth, three at the drought-tight span above the salt flats. [320] Drops evaporate before they land. [325] That was the imbalance -- not a curse, not a wrathful god, just water leaving the basin faster than it returned, a ledger running one column short for nine hundred years. [355] Cosmic things, Orren had decided, are mostly arithmetic wearing robes. [365]

Here was his plan, which every surviving Tidewarden had laughed at. [376] Cold water falls straight and shatters and is lost to the air. [388] Lukewarm water, held at exactly the temperature of a forgotten bath, beads instead of shattering; it falls heavy, it falls whole, and it survives the drop. [414] He could not boil an ocean. [420] He could warm one seam of leaking glass at a time, and he could count. [435] Four years of counting had given him a number: seventeen hundred joints, warmed in sequence, would deliver enough surviving water to the seabed to start a brine pool. [463] A brine pool cools the air above it. [471] Cool air over warm rock makes fog, and fog makes rain, and rain, given two centuries, makes a sea. [490] He would not live to see it, which he found perfectly reasonable. [502]

On the morning he reached the seventeen-hundredth joint, Sister Vell came to stop him. [516] She had been the most devout of them all, and her faith had snapped so cleanly that she now hated anyone still holding a piece of theirs. [543] "The sea is dead," she said. [549] "The last fish was caught years ago." [556] "Warming puddles is not devotion; it is delusion." [564]

Orren showed her the notebook, because he had never learned that some questions are not requests for information. [582] He showed her the columns of drops, the candle-clock margins, the four years. [595] He showed her the bell, and the film of water standing in its cracked throat, which had not been there on any morning before this one. [621] Below them, on the seabed, under the blue bars of dawn, something glittered that was not glass. [638] It was small. [641] It was perhaps forty feet across, and it was wet. [651]

Sister Vell sat down on the parapet and did not speak for a long time. [666] Then she took the bell off his back, turned it upright, and struck it once with the heel of her hand. [687] It coughed, as cracked bells do. [693] "We can rebuild it," Orren said, meaning the bell, meaning the sea, meaning both. [707] "Bronze is only arithmetic too." [712] She laughed, and it was an ugly, cracked sound, and then she asked him what the drip rate was at the eighteen-hundredth joint. [735] Orren told her: nine drops a minute, and rising. [744] They walked north together into the light. [751]
