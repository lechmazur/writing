The last lore keeper of Vesper Station was eighty-one years old, and she still packed her own rope. [18] Ines Marroquin had inherited three things from her teacher: a memory palace of dead dynasties, a habit of humming pre-collapse ballads, and a ruby flake the size of a thumbnail, cut from the laser lathe that once carved the station's hull. [59] The flake was the sharpest edge left in a soft century, and she meant to use it tonight. [77]

For three hundred years the throne of Vesper had stood unclaimed, a chair of grown sapphire in a hall nobody swept, because the old law said only one who climbed the Stairway of Ascension could sit in it, and nobody remembered where the stairway was. [122] Councils had governed by committee ever since, politely, badly, with the tired courtesy of people minding someone else's house. [141] Ines had found the reference in a maintenance log: paint over paint over paint, in the chapel behind the falls. [161] So at dusk she made her sally out of the archive with her apprentice Teodor behind her, two lamps, and until sunrise to do it. [186] At dawn the station's mirrors swung, the waterfall took the sunlight, and the whole orchard behind it drowned in light too bright to work by; the passage behind the water sealed itself against the heat. [221]

The falls were the last of the old terraforming art, a curtain of engineered water threaded with prism-dust, and they threw slow kaleidoscopes across the rocks: green, plum, a gold that made Teodor stop walking. [256] Behind them lay the hidden orchard, sixty apple trees in eleven terraces, planted by hands that had also written the law about the throne. [280] The air smelled of cider and coolant. [286]

"People used to come here," Teodor said, in the voice the young use for things they never had. [304] "They will again," Ines said, "if we're wisely adventurous and not merely adventurous." [317] She had buried two students who had been only the second thing. [329]

The chapel at the orchard's top was a drum of poured stone, its interior painted the flat administrative beige of the Council years. [352] Ines set her lamp on the floor, knelt like a much younger woman, and began to work the ruby flake under the beige at the baseboard. [378] Beige curled away into white, white into a municipal blue, blue into a devotional green, and under the green, the murals. [399] They peeled all night, working the flake flat and patient, layer after layer, until the wall gave up its old faces. [420] Here was the first governor with her sleeves rolled; here the water engineers; here a stairway, painted in eleven ascending bands of ochre, each band a terrace, each terrace hung with fruit. [452]

Teodor turned slowly toward the door, toward the orchard falling away beneath them in eleven terraces. [468] "We climbed it," he said. [473] "We climbed it carrying lamps." [478]

The last panel, lowest and most stubborn, took an hour and cost Ines the corner of the ruby flake. [497] It showed the sapphire throne, empty, with the orchard growing through its arms, and a line of old script beneath: THE SEAT IS FOR THE ONE WHO TENDS THE STAIR. [527] Ines sat back on her heels and laughed until she had to wipe her eyes. [542] Three centuries of lawyers, and the answer had been pruning shears. [553]

"Then you can claim it," Teodor said. [560] "You climbed, and you found the stairway." [567] "I'm eighty-one," she said, "and a lore keeper who takes the throne becomes a throne that keeps no lore." [586] "Besides, the wall doesn't say climb; it says tend." [595]

Outside, the mirrors were turning; the kaleidoscope was going white at its edges. [608] They had minutes. [611] Ines pressed the chipped ruby flake into Teodor's palm and told him what to do with it: photograph the murals, file them under public record, and then go down and prune the eleventh terrace, which had been choking for forty years. [652] "And when the Council asks who claims the throne?" he said. [663] "Tell them it is still unclaimed," she said, "and that the stairway is open, and that it is a long climb with good apples on the way." [690]

They came out through the falls at sunrise, soaked, prism-dust glittering on their sleeves, into a light so bright it erased the orchard for a moment and then gave it back, terrace by terrace, exactly as the mural had promised. [730] Ines hummed a ballad older than the station's power grid, and Teodor, who had never heard it, hummed along one beat behind, learning it as they descended. [757]
