Vashti made maps that no eye could read. [7] Hers were charts of smell and echo and the grain of rust under a fingertip, drawn for pilots who had gone blind in the long dark and still needed to find the water valve. [41] The abandoned repair bay at Kestrel Spaceport was her masterwork. [51] She had mapped every meter of it: the cold seam where the gantry met the floor, the smell of old coolant like crushed mint, the place where a dropped bolt rang for four seconds instead of two. [88] She had mapped it because Oris had died there, and because he had died mid-sentence. [103] "Vashti, there's something I never told you about the shard--" and then the scaffold, and then nothing. [120] The green glass shard had been his, pried from the hull of a ship that had come back from somewhere it was never sent. [144] It was the size of a thumbnail and warm always, and it worked only in the murmur of half-remembered dreams. [164] That was the trouble with it. [170] Vashti could not will herself across; she could only sleep in the bay with the shard against her sternum and wait for the shallow hour before waking, when the mind loosens and the world goes thin. [206] Then she wove. [209] The weaving was cyclically spontaneous: it seized her without warning, but always on the ninth night, so she had learned to be ready for an ambush she could schedule. [238] She traveled through parallel universes the only way a sensory map maker could: by fidelity of detail. [255] In each neighboring world there was a Kestrel repair bay, and in each, some small thing differed -- the bolt rang for three seconds, the coolant smelled of iron, the cold seam ran north instead of east. [291] Her maps were her compass. [296] She catalogued the differences and used them to redirect each crossing, steering by deviation, the way a sailor steers by the drift of a current she cannot see. [324] She was looking for an Oris who had finished his sentence. [334] Not to have him back -- she was not a fool, and grief had made her precise rather than soft -- but to discover the weight of a withheld truth. [362] Whatever he had kept from her, he had kept it for eleven years, and a thing carried that long has mass. [383] She wanted to hold it and know what it had cost him. [395]

On the ninth night of the forty-first cycle, she crossed into a bay that smelled of crushed mint and nothing else. [416] No rust. [418] The gantry was whole, repainted, and a man was sitting on the bottom rung with his boots in a puddle of lamplight. [440] He was grey at the temples, which she had never gotten to see. [453] "You're the ninth," Oris said. [458] "They all come on the ninth night." [465] Vashti sat down in the puddle beside him because her legs had stopped consulting her. [480] "Finish the sentence," she said. [485] He looked at the shard in her palm, green as a bottle bottom, and did not reach for it. [504] "I found two of them," he said. [511] "I gave you one and kept one, and I used mine for six years before I ever told you the glass did anything at all." [536] "I went looking for a world where you were happier." [545] "I found forty-one of them, and in every one you had never met me." [559] He turned his hands over, empty. [565] "That was the thing I never told you." [573] "That I checked." [576]

Vashti held the shard and found that it weighed exactly what it had always weighed, and that she had been wrong about mass all along; a withheld truth does not grow heavier with the years, it grows heavier with the telling. [617] She woke in her own bay, in the rust and the four-second echo. [630] The ninth night would come again; the cycle did not care what she had learned. [645] But she got up, and instead of lying down with the glass she took out her stylus and began a new map -- not of the bay, but of the crossings. [675] Forty-one worlds, each keyed to a smell, a sound, a temperature at the seam, so that anyone coming after could redirect themselves somewhere kinder than a man's private arithmetic. [704] She left the shard in the coolant sump with the map beside it, wrapped in oilcloth, labeled in raised dots for fingers she would never meet. [730] Then she walked out of the spaceport and slept, that night, without dreaming at all. [745]
