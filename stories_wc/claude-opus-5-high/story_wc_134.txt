For forty years, Mireth knew every visitor before their feet touched the mountain road. [14] She knew their names, their griefs, the exact hour they would begin to weep. [28] So when the pilgrim arrived at dusk, unannounced by any vision, Mireth dropped her lamp. [43] The lamp did not break; nothing broke in the Grotto of Bells. [55] It only rang, a long copper note that climbed the crystal columns and set them singing. [71] The cave answered every sound with music; that was its nature and its warning. [85] Each column held a different pitch, and the columns grew steadily unpredictable as they rose, so the ceiling chords never repeated twice in a lifetime. [110] Mireth had learned to read the future in those chords. [120] Tonight they told her nothing at all. [126] The pilgrim was a young woman with road-dust in her eyebrows and a satchel of bread. [142] "I came for the cave pearl," she said. [150] Of course she had. [154] They all came for it eventually, that milky sphere sleeping in its shallow pool, grown one mineral breath at a time for nine thousand years. [179] Kings wanted it for war; merchants wanted it for gold. [189] Mireth refused them all, because the pearl did not do what they imagined. [202] "It does not grant wishes," she warned. [209] "It only magnifies what is already there," Mireth said. [218] "Bread rises higher, a kind word lands truer, a candle burns half a night longer." [233] "That is all I want," the pilgrim said. [241] "My valley is poor and tired, and I want to magnify its ordinary magic until it can feed itself." [260] Mireth reached for the woman's future and found a blank wall. [271] In forty years, that had never happened. [278] She should have been afraid. [283] Instead she felt something she had forgotten the shape of: curiosity. [294] She led the pilgrim down through the singing halls, past columns that glowed with their own cold light, so the deeper they went the brighter the darkness became. [322] That was the first thing every visitor noticed, and the last thing they understood. [336] Because light from a hundred glowing columns throws a hundred shadows from every body. [350] And where those shadows overlap, the unseen forces of the mountain find a door. [364] Mireth had felt them all her life, brushing past her, listening. [375] They were not cruel; they were patient, which is worse and better at once. [389] They had made the pearl, drop by drop, the way a person makes a habit. [404] At the pool, the pilgrim knelt and did not reach for it. [416] "May I?" she asked. [420] That, Mireth thought, was the whole difference between her and the kings. [432] She nodded, and as the pilgrim bent, their shadows slid across each other on the wet stone. [449] The cave changed key. [453] Every column struck the same note at once, a sound so total it felt like silence. [469] Mireth understood, far too late, why she had seen nothing coming. [480] A clairvoyant cannot see the hour she stops being one. [490] The forces had been waiting nine thousand years for two things: a keeper willing to ask permission, and a keeper willing to let go. [514] In the overlapping dark they crossed from the old woman into the young one, the way water crosses from cloud to leaf. [536] They would inhabit her now, and she would carry them home in her ribs to that poor valley. [554] The pilgrim lifted the cave pearl and gasped, not with power but with hearing. [568] "Everything ordinary is so loud," she whispered. [575] Mireth laughed, and her laugh made no prophecy, and that was a relief she had not predicted. [592] She walked the pilgrim to the mouth of the cave, where the columns thinned and the true night waited, ordinary and enormous. [614] "What will you do first?" Mireth asked. [621] The pilgrim thought about it, then held up the bread from her satchel. [634] "Breakfast," she said, "for both of us." [641] Behind them the crystal cave went on composing itself, note by unrepeatable note, in the bright darkness that had never needed anyone to watch it. [666]
