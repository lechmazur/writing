The amber grotto held the last of the light the way a tooth holds a nerve. [16] At dusk the walls went from honey to bruise, and Ovid Sarn opened her case of feelings. [33] Each one lay flat in its slot: a fragrance chemist's blotter petal, cut from card, tipped with a single dried drop. [54] She was professionally thorough, and so every petal was labeled in her own small hand. [69] Pity, with a note of cedar. [75] Longing, mostly bergamot and lie. [80] Her customers came down the ladder one at a time, because the grotto only forgave one liar at a time. [100]

Ovid sold half truths, which is not the same as selling nothing. [112] Each petal carried a real emotion thinned to a tenth of itself, then propped up with synthetics that smelled like conviction. [133] A widower could inhale one and mourn convincingly for an afternoon without the inconvenience of actually having loved. [151] She thought of her clients with affectionate detachment, the way a locksmith thinks of doors. [166]

She could not feel what they felt; she could only read it. [178] Her gift was sensing through static electricity. [185] In the dry amber air the fine hairs of her forearms lifted toward a person's grief, or leaned away from their boredom, and the charge told her the shape of whatever they were hiding. [219] Insight without empathy, her teacher had called it, and had not meant it kindly. [233]

That night's customer was the chemist herself, Wren Halloway, who had supplied the blotters for eleven years and had never once bought. [255] The hairs on Ovid's arms rose before Wren reached the bottom rung. [267]

"You're dying," Ovid said, pleasantly, because the charge was unmistakable and there was no point wasting either of their evenings. [287]

"Half true," Wren said. [291] "I am leaving." [294]

She set a petal on the stone between them, unlabeled, and Ovid's whole body turned toward it like a compass needle. [315]

"What is it?" [318]

"Thirty years of my life, undiluted," Wren said. [326] "Everything I ever felt about you, distilled while I still had the nose for it." [341]

Ovid understood the offer, and understood also that it was a trap built with love, which is the only kind that works. [363] A full emotion, taken in whole, does not sit beside you like a coat you can shrug off. [381] It subsumes you. [384] It takes the reader and makes her the read. [393]

"You want me to stop selling," Ovid said. [401]

"I want you to stop being half of yourself," Wren said, sitting down on the cold floor with the ease of someone who had already given up her knees. [430] "You have the finest instrument for reading people I have ever met, and you use it to sell forgeries. [449] Fuse the two. [452] Insight with empathy. [455] Then be honest with me for one minute before I go." [466]

Ovid, professionally thorough to the last, inspected the petal first: the cut of the card, the drop's even spread, the absence of any stabilizer. [490] It was the cleanest thing she had ever handled. [499] Then she lifted it to her face and breathed in. [509]

The grotto's amber went black at the edges. [517] Thirty years arrived at once: the first delivery of blotters in rain, a laugh withheld to keep a professional distance, eleven winters of quiet, deliberate patience. [543] Ovid felt her own static die, the charge grounding out through her palms, because she was no longer outside the field measuring it. [566] She was in it. [570] As the transformation completed she found she could still read Wren perfectly, every shape of her, and that now each shape cost her something. [594]

"You were not lying about leaving," Ovid said, and her voice came out wrecked and warm, a voice she had never sold. [616]

"Half truths," Wren agreed, taking her hand. [623] "I was also dying." [627]

They sat in the dark honey light until it was gone, and Ovid did not once reach for her case. [647] In the morning she burned the petals one by one, thorough as ever, and kept only the blank ones. [666] She had a new trade to learn, and no forgeries left to hide behind. [680]
