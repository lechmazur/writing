After the happiness mandate, the turbines did our singing for us. [11] Three of them stood over the farmhouse like tall grey uncles, and their shadows swept the kitchen floor in slow strokes, morning to night. [35] My wife Ilse had loved them; I had learned to hate the noise less than the law. [52] The mandate said grief was a contagion, and that any household could be audited for gloom. [68] So the Bureau sent performers to teach cheer, and mine arrived in November with a blank face. [85] Not blank like a mask, blank like a wall that had been painted over many times. [101] He called himself Petrov, produced a license, and asked where I kept the potatoes. [115] I told him I had nothing to feel, and he said that was fine, feeling was not on the schedule. [135]

Then he began to narrate. [140] "Behold," he announced to the empty kitchen, "the tuber, dug from the cold, bearing seven eyes and no opinions." [159] He peeled it like a man delivering a verdict. [167] He made an epic of the washing up, a tragedy of a stuck drawer, a coronation of the kettle. [186] I did not laugh. [190] Laughing was compulsory at market and at school assemblies, and I had used up my supply years ago. [208] But I listened, and listening was something. [215]

Evenings, he sat by the stove and whittled. [223] Ash wood, mostly, from the windbreak Ilse planted before the turbines ate the wind she planted it against. [241] He carved small hollow things, bells without clappers, whistles with no holes. [253] "Instruments for the hush," he said, when I asked. [262] "The old silence is illegal, so we will have to build a new one, by hand." [278] That was the first true thing anyone had said to me since the funeral, which had been held as a picnic, by order. [301]

I kept her dried flower bouquet under the floorboard beneath the churn. [313] Cornflowers, yarrow, a few brittle stems of lavender, the whole thing the color of old paper and smelling of nothing. [333] Keeping mourning tokens carried three years of labor. [341] On the ninth night I showed it to him, because the void in me wanted a witness more than it wanted safety. [363]

Petrov looked at the bouquet a long while. [371] Then he lifted it and narrated it: the summer field, the bent back, the hands that chose these and not others. [392] He made it no larger than it was, and no smaller. [403] Something in my chest moved sideways, like a drawer coming unstuck. [414] It was not happiness. [418] It was lightness, which is what grief needs if it is going to be carried any distance at all. [437]

In December I found his ledger in the stable. [446] Forty households, each with a column for infractions: hidden photographs, unsanctioned weeping, one man who had painted his shutters black. [466] My name was there, and beside it, in the same neat hand: compliant, cheerful, no findings. [482] He had been reporting on all of us, and lying about all of us, and drawing a salary for both. [502]

When I confronted him he did not apologize. [510] "I am the reason forty houses still have their floorboards," he said, and shaved a curl of ash from the bell he was making. [534] "I am also the reason the Bureau trusts the list." [544] I could have turned him in and been rewarded. [553] Instead I put the kettle on and told him, in my flattest voice, the epic of the water and the fire and the leaves. [577] He listened with his blank face, and it was the most attention anyone had paid me in two years. [596]

He left in spring for the next district, leaving nine wooden bells on the windowsill. [611] They make no sound. [615] That is the point; the turbines turn and hum above the roof, and under that noise I have my own hush now, remade, hand-cut, mine. [640] I keep the bouquet on the table in plain sight, and when the auditors come I will narrate it for them: cornflower, yarrow, lavender, a woman's hands in a summer field. [671] I will smile the way he taught me, which is not at all, and let them write what they like. [691]
