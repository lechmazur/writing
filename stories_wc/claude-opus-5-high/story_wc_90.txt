The Mercy Salvage was stitched together from eleven dead ships, and Bede had sewn most of the seams himself. [19] He was ninety-one, a steadfast pacifist who had never once raised his hand, not even during the Scarcity Wars that emptied the cradles of the species. [45] Nkiru slept in the greenhouse bay, seven years old, the last human ever born. [59] After her, the wombs of the world had simply closed, like doors in a corridor nobody could name. [77] Bede intended to push one of those doors open again. [87]

The archive held nine thousand photographs, paper ones, rescued from flooded houses and salvaged lockers. [102] Each had been wept on, once, by somebody who was now dust. [114] Under the right lamp the long-dried tears still shimmered, faint salt maps, and where the shimmer of one photograph aligned with the shimmer of another, a seam showed. [142] That was how he had found it: a locked dimension folded into the ship's recycled steel, a room behind the room where, he believed, the unborn waited. [169]

The door would not open for force. [176] It opened for weight. [180]

This was Bede's private curse and the ship's oldest joke: he grew gravitationally lighter when happy. [196] A good meal lifted him an inch off the deck; Nkiru's laughter once floated him into the ductwork, where he hung grinning among the filters until she stopped. [224] To bear down on the seam he would need to be heavier than he had ever been. [241] The old counsellors had a term for the only cargo dense enough: shadow work. [255] Everything he had refused to be for ninety-one years was still aboard him, unpacked, unclaimed, weighing nothing because he had never once looked at it. [280]

So he sat in the archive with Kwan Yin's Tear in his palm and began. [295] The Tear was a bead of grey glass from a drowned temple, cold as a coin, and it worked like a lens that would only show a man his own averted face. [327] Through it he saw the soldier he might have been. [337] He saw himself at nineteen, holding a wrench above a looter's skull in a ration queue, wanting it, wanting it so badly his teeth ached. [362] He had set the wrench down and called it principle. [372] Now, with ghostly resolve, he picked the moment back up and carried it. [385] The deck plates groaned. [389]

Night after night he went further down into himself, and the scales in the galley read fractions more each morning. [409] He owned the cruelty he had felt toward his dying wife, the relief when she finally stopped breathing, the contempt he had hidden under forty years of gentleness. [437] He did not act on any of it; he only agreed that it was his. [452] That was the difference, and it was the whole of his pacifism, at last honestly earned. [468]

By the ninth week he could not climb a ladder. [478] Nkiru found him in the archive, three hundred kilos of remorse in a thin old body, sorting photographs by the shimmer of dead strangers' tears. [503] "Are you sad?" she asked. [508] "No," he said. "I'm full." [513]

They went together to the seam, which lay in the aft hold where two salvaged hulls had been welded badly, leaving a hairline of somewhere else. [539] Bede knelt, and the floor bowed under him like ice. [549] He pressed the Tear into the hairline and leaned his whole gathered self against the door of the locked dimension. [569] It gave, grudgingly, the way a grave gives. [577] Light came through, warm and unhurried, and with it a sound like many small lungs learning air. [594]

Nkiru laughed, once, in pure astonishment. [600] It was the worst possible moment; joy struck Bede like a solar wind, and he felt himself begin to rise. [620] The door began to close with him. [627] So he did the last practical thing a shipwright can do. [638] He took the sail-needle from his belt and began to stitch the seam open, wide loops through the lip of one world and the lip of the other, his hands steady while his body lifted like a lantern. [676] The last stitch he tied around his own wrist. [685]

Nkiru says he is still there, floating in the aft hold, grey and shining and perfectly happy, holding the door of the world open with his weightlessness and his thread. [715] She is the last human ever born, but she is no longer the last one living, and every child who comes through learns the same first lesson from the old ghost at the seam. [749] Carry all of yourself, he tells them, and never use it. [760]
