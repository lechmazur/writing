Mira stepped off the viaduct's parapet and did not fall. [10] Rain had just stopped, and the old aqueduct glowed from within, its limestone veined with the cold blue lichen that fed on storms. [33] She walked along the underside of its arches, her boots printing wet light, because gravity had long since stopped arguing with her. [55] In her coat she carried the tablet. [62] It was clay, older than the viaduct, older perhaps than the language of its runes, and it was warm the way a sleeping animal is warm. [88] The runes never stayed the same. [94] They rearranged themselves through procedural generation, a grammar of possibility grinding out variations, so that each time she read the tablet it described a different version of her life. [123] That was the trouble with fractured timelines: they did not branch politely, one from another, but shattered, each shard reflecting a Mira who had chosen otherwise. [149] She had come to the viaduct to retrace the crack. [159] Eleven years ago she had stood on the parapet in a storm, a girl of nineteen with nothing left to weigh her down, and she had let go. [187] Between the drop and the ripple, something had caught her. [197] Not hands. [199] A pause, a held breath in the machinery of the world, and in that pause she had become periodically eternal: undying for stretches of forty days, then mortal for one, then undying again, her existence stuttering like a lamp with a loose filament. [242] The tablet had been in the puddle when she landed, or rather when she did not. [258] Tonight was a mortal day. [263] She had counted. [266] Tonight, if she fell, she would ripple. [273] The tablet wanted her to read, so she read. [282] The runes assembled a corridor: Mira at twenty-three, a bakery in a hill town, flour in the creases of her knuckles, a man who whistled badly. [308] She stepped into it and walked until the walls turned to other runes, other rooms. [323] Mira the soldier; Mira who drowned at nineteen and stayed drowned; Mira who never climbed the parapet and grew slowly, ordinarily old. [345] For eleven years she had wandered this generated maze, believing that if she mapped every version of herself she would finally conquer the labyrinth of self and know which Mira was real. [377] The maze, being procedural, was infinite. [383] It had no center, only more corridors, each brighter than the last and each darker at its edges, a light that grew while going out. [408] Standing upside down beneath the glowing arch, water falling up past her face, she understood what she had been doing. [428] A labyrinth is not conquered by walking it. [436] It is conquered by choosing a wall and calling it home. [447] She held the tablet out over the drop. [455] The runes shuffled frantically, offering her a thousand more lives, a bakery, a war, a long quiet marriage, anything but the single narrow one that had her standing on a wet viaduct at thirty with cold hands. [492] 'I was never the girl who fell,' she said. [501] 'I am the girl who was caught, and I have spent eleven years refusing to land.' [517] She let the tablet go. [522] It fell, and gravity, which had been waiting patiently for something to argue with, took it gladly. [539] Between the drop and the ripple there was a pause, that same held breath, the world offering its bargain again. [559] She declined it. [562] The clay struck the puddle and broke into pieces too small to read, and the ripple went out across the water in perfect widening rings, the way a ripple should. [592] Mira felt her weight return, all of it, eleven years' worth. [603] She dropped from the underside of the arch, landed hard in six inches of rainwater, and stood there laughing with a bleeding knee. [626] The lichen-light was already fading as the stone dried, the brightness darkening toward an ordinary night. [642] She would be mortal tomorrow too, she suspected, and the day after, and every day after that. [659] Walking home along the top of the viaduct, on the proper side of it, she did not retrace her steps once. [680]
