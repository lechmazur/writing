Cloudrest sat nine thousand feet up, where the valley's fog lay below the windows like a second, softer earth. [19] The sun found it every morning, so the staff called the place a radiant haven, half joking, half not. [38] Mira arrived at seven with her guitar and her cart of small instruments, and she moved through the halls the way water moves through stone: slowly, and with total confidence. [68] Room 4 belonged to Abel Vance, who had spent forty years operating a record press in Dayton. [85] He kept a shard of black vinyl on his bedside table, a curved wedge with three inches of groove still in it, and he would not let anyone move it to dust. [117]

The blood work came back on a Tuesday. [123] Dr. Oyelaran said the numbers had stopped arguing with them; there would be no more transfusions, and the time left was measured in days. [147] Abel took the news like a man receiving a delivery he had already signed for. [162] Then he asked Mira to help him rehearse. [169]

"Rehearse what?" she said. [173]

"My goodbye," he said. [177] "I want it flawless." [181]

He had a plan, and the plan was a performance: the right words, the right lighting, no stumbling, no snot. [201] He wanted to leave his daughter Delia with one clean, perfect memory to file away, since he had given her nothing else clean in thirty years. [227] The vinyl shard was from a record he had pressed himself in 1989: Delia at eleven singing a nonsense song about laundry, his own voice underneath naming the socks. [256] He had broken it in an argument the year she left, and she had taken half with her. [274]

Mira did not argue with the rehearsal idea. [281] She was decisively kind, which is different from gentle; she chose the kindest thing and then did it without negotiating. [301] So she sat, tuned, and asked what the pressing plant had smelled like. [314]

Abel closed his eyes. [318] "Hot shellac," he said, "and orange peel, because we ate oranges on the night shift." [333] He said that was the scent of hope to him: something warm being made, and something bright being eaten while you waited for it to cool. [359]

Mira brought oranges the next day, and she began hymning the mundane. [371] This was her method and her whole quiet theology. [380] She made a small tune out of the morphine schedule. [390] She made another from the squeak of the laundry cart, and another from the nurse's greeting, and another from the sound of the oxygen line, until the room had a liturgy of its ordinary hours. [425] Abel, who had wanted a monument, found himself singing about socks. [436] The room held a pulsing stillness then, the hush of a needle riding a groove with no music on it yet. [457]

On the fourth day Mira called Delia in Toledo, having asked permission twice and gotten it once. [474] Delia said she still had her half in a shoebox. [484] She said she was not sure she could come. [493] Mira told her about the socks song, and hummed eight bars of it over the phone. [509]

Delia arrived the next morning, coming up through the fog and out into that impossible light. [525] Abel had his speech ready, three drafts of it, and he got four words in before his voice failed. [544] The flawless goodbye collapsed immediately, as Mira had known it would. [555] So she did the kindest available thing: she put the two vinyl halves together on the blanket, edge to edge, and began to play the laundry song. [582]

Delia knew the words. [586] She had never forgotten them, and that was the thing she had been most afraid of. [602] They sang the socks, and then the towels, and then the one about the dryer that ate quarters, and the record lay in two pieces that were, for the length of a song, one record again. [638]

Nobody said the word forgive. [643] Nobody had to; the hymning of the mundane had already done the work, because the mundane was what they had lost and the only thing large enough to reunify them. [673]

Abel died two nights later, at four in the morning, with the fog gold-lit beneath the window and an orange peel drying on the sill. [698] Delia was humming about socks. [703] It was not flawless. [707] It was better than flawless, Mira thought, packing her cart in the radiant hallway: it was accurate. [724]

Delia kept both halves. [728] At the door she asked Mira how she did this work every day, and Mira said she mostly just listened for what the room was already singing, and then joined in. [759] Downstairs the fog was burning off, and the whole valley smelled faintly of oranges. [773]
