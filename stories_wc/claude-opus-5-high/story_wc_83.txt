The observatory hung nine kilometers above the salt pan, swinging on its tether like a lantern nobody had claimed. [19] Naila climbed to the instrument deck while the circuit breakers cooled, because that was the only time the machine would let her think. [42] Eleven minutes, every hour. [46] Eleven minutes when the filters dropped, the shielding went slack, and the sky came in raw. [62] She had learned to love those minutes the way a starving woman loves a thunderstorm: nothing to eat in it, but proof the world still moved. [88]

On the bench sat the geode, split clean, its violet teeth still wet-looking after four years. [104] Her brother had given it to her the morning of the wedding, unopened, saying every marriage should start with a stone you have to break to see inside. [132] The drone opened it for them. [138] It opened a great many things that afternoon: the canopy, the long table, her brother, the man she was to marry, and forty seconds of her hearing that never came back. [169] She found the geode afterward in the dust, halved by blast pressure, unscratched. [182]

Intangibly tangible, the physicists upstairs called it later, when she showed them what the crystal did to a gravimeter. [201] A thing you could hold and still not touch. [210] They gave her a contract and a bunk on the floating observatory, and she took it because she wanted to find where unused potential pools. [235] The hunt they assigned her had a bureaucratic name and a simple shape. [247] Everywhere in the world, potential pooled where nothing had spent it: in stalled engines, in unfired munitions, in unlived lives. [267] The observatory existed to find those pools and tap them, and Naila's geode, for reasons nobody could model, leaned toward them like a dowsing rod. [292]

She worked through perturbation theory, the way her supervisor had taught her: assume a quiet universe, then add the smallest possible insult to it and watch how the answer bends. [322] First order, second order, third. [327] Each correction was smaller than the last, and each one told her where the sky was holding its breath. [346]

Tonight the series refused to converge. [352] She ran it again with the breakers still ticking as they cooled, metal contracting somewhere under the floor like knuckles unclenching. [373] The corrections grew instead of shrinking. [379] Something enormous and unspent sat close enough to the observatory that it was not a perturbation at all; it was the main term, and she was the small insult added to it. [411]

Naila set the geode in the cradle and put both palms on the housing. [425] With the filters down, it came in unfiltered, and she woke inside her own life for the first time in four years. [447] The pool was her. [451] Not her body, exactly: the shape of the afternoon that had been cut off mid-sentence, forty guests' worth of unspent years, a marriage that had never been permitted to begin and so had never been permitted to be spent. [490] She had been carrying it up and down the tether every day, and the instruments had been reading around her the way you read around a stain. [517]

The hunt had been a circle all along, and the laughter that came out of her was not a good sound. [538] It was bleak and it was exhilarated and it rang off the dome like hail. [553]

Four minutes of cooling left. [558] She could report it, and they would take her apart gently, with excellent funding, and tap what she carried until the pool ran dry and she was merely a woman with bad hearing and a broken rock. [595] Or she could spend it herself. [601] She thought of her brother's rule: a stone you have to break to see inside. [616]

Naila keyed the tap array manual, aimed the draw at her own chest, and set the release for the second the breakers closed. [639] Nine kilometers below, the salt pan waited, white as a laid table. [651] The geode's violet teeth brightened until they were something she could see and never hold. [666] She said the vow she had not been allowed to finish, out loud, to nobody, in a room full of instruments that would record it as a spike in the third-order term. [698]

Then the breakers closed, the filters slammed up, and the observatory lit from the inside like a lamp finally claimed. [718] The salt pan, for nine full seconds, was bright as an afternoon in spring. [732] Somewhere in the telemetry, forty people finished their dance. [741]
