They called the hour of inversion the Upfall, and Sera Vance flew into it on purpose. [16] Her skiff was a stripped pollination barge, wings patched with orchard tin, and it climbed through the floating rock arches of Thessaly-9 like a moth in a cathedral. [44] Below, the orchards hung in terraces, and above them, for fifty-nine more minutes, the rivers would fall upward. [62] That was how you navigated during the Upfall: not by stars, which the arches sliced into confetti, but by water flow. [83] Every stream on the planet remembered where it came from, and when gravity inverted, it went home. [100] Sera followed a silver thread of creekwater as it lifted off the cliffs and braided itself into the sky. [119]

The blight had come in spring. [124] It ate the root-vines of the low orchards, then the mid-terraces, then the great gravity-pear groves that fed four colonies. [144] Everything died except one thing: a ring of wild apple trees on an arch called the Anvil, which not only survived but thrived, doubling in a season. [171] Sera had not come to admire it. [177] She had come to find the source of that resilience, bottle it, and carry it down to the dying terraces before the next harvest failed. [202]

The Anvil floated eleven kilometers up, and only during the Upfall could a skiff reach it, riding the inverted waterfalls like rails. [224] She cut the engine at the rim and let momentum carry her into the strangest quiet she had ever known. [244] It was a whirling silence: leaves spinning upward, petals turning in slow galaxies, water beading off the grass and rising, and no sound at all except her own blood. [273]

The apple trees stood rooted in a soil so dark it looked wet with ink. [288] Sera knelt and dug. [292] Twenty centimeters down, her glove struck metal, and she pulled up a broken arrow tip. [307] It was old alloy, colony-forged, the kind the first settlers had used for hunting before the game died out. [326] The moment her skin touched it, the silence filled. [335] Not with noise, exactly, but with presence, the way a room feels occupied before you turn on the light. [354]

A gathering of echoes stood among the trees. [362] They were not ghosts; they were recordings, she realized, held in the strange soil the way iron holds a magnetic field. [383] A woman in a first-wave survival suit knelt exactly where Sera knelt, planting seeds. [397] A boy chased something with a bow and lost the arrow in the dirt. [411] An old man argued with an agronomist about whether the soil should be shared or hoarded. [427] Their voices layered over each other, ninety years of one small orchard, all playing at once and none of them louder than a heartbeat. [451]

Sera understood then what she had come to discover, and it was not a chemical. [466] The soil of the Anvil accelerates plant growth because it stores the living record of everything that happens above it, and the trees read that record like instructions. [494] Ninety years of people refusing to leave a hard place had taught the roots what refusal looked like. [512] Resilience was not in the dirt; it was in the attention paid to the dirt, and the dirt had been listening. [533]

She had forty minutes. [537] She filled six sample drums with Anvil soil, then hesitated, because six drums could not cover four colonies' worth of terraces. [558] The echoes went on arguing around her, shared or hoarded, shared or hoarded, and she laughed once into her helmet at how little had changed. [583] Then she did the thing the soil would remember. [592]

She carried the drums to the arch's edge and poured them into the column of rising creekwater, seeding the whole suspended river with Anvil grain. [617] When the Upfall ended, all that water would fall back where it had come from, and it would rain black soil across every dying terrace on the planet. [645] She kept nothing for herself except the broken arrow tip, which she pocketed as proof, and as a promise that someone had once been careless here and the ground had forgiven it. [677]

The gathering of echoes turned toward her, all ninety years at once, and though she heard no words, she felt the soil take her measure and write her down. [706] Sera Vance, intrepid orchard pilot, sixty seconds to spare, gunned the barge off the Anvil as the arch shuddered and the world remembered which way was down. [733] She fell with the rain, laughing, through a whirling silence full of seeds. [746]
