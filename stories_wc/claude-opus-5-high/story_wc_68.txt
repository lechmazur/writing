The plague ended on a Tuesday, and by Thursday the survivors were already arguing about what it had meant. [19] Vosk heard the arguing through the grate above his head, which was as close to society as he cared to get. [40] He lived in the old overflow sewer beneath the sunken quarter of Halvesea, where the tide came in politely twice a day and left behind fish bones and opinions. [69] He called himself a philosopher; the drowned city called him nothing at all, which he considered a fair exchange. [88]

Above, two stories were competing for the city's memory. [97] The Chorus of Ash said the plague had been a cleansing, and that everything older than the plague was contaminated and should be burned, including books, including instruments, including the habit of measuring things. [131] The Salvage Guild said the plague had been an accident of bad water, and that the past was a warehouse to be stripped for parts and sold to the mainland. [161] Vosk thought both were lying, and he thought so calmly, and he said so rudely. [176] "You are both selling the same thing," he told a Chorister who had come down the ladder looking for heretics, "which is permission to stop knowing how anything works." [205] The Chorister called him filth and left; Vosk agreed with the noun and resented the tone. [221]

His rebellion was a rational one: he kept no weapons, joined no riot, and simply refused to forget procedures that other people found inconvenient. [245] Every low tide he went digging in the sunken quarter, down through the sedimentary layers the floods had laid over the drowned streets: silt, then plague-lime, then the ash of the harbor fire, then the packed grey clay of the century before that. [288] He read the layers the way other men read scripture, and with fewer arguments. [302] In the clay, under the roof-slates of a house that had once belonged to a navigator, he found a cog. [322] It was brass, thumbnail-wide, cut with teeth so fine they caught the light like a saw of dust: the escape wheel from a seafarer's chronometer, the kind that let a ship know its longitude and therefore its life. [360] Nobody in Halvesea could cut such teeth anymore. [368] That was the whole of his politics, in one small wheel. [379]

He sat in the sewer for three days and agonized over it, which was the only luxury he owned. [398] If he gave the cog to the Chorus, they would melt it as an idol of the old measuring sickness. [418] If he gave it to the Salvage Guild, they would sell it to a mainland collector, and the method would go into a glass case where methods die. [446] If he kept it, he was only a rat with a relic, and the teeth would rot with him. [465] What he wanted was not the object but the ancient method behind it -- the dividing plate, the index wheel, the patience -- preserved in living hands. [490]

On the fourth day a girl came down the grate to steal his lamp oil. [505] She was ten, plague-orphaned, and unimpressed by him. [513] "You stink," she said. [517] "I do," Vosk agreed, without heat. "Sit down." [525] He put the cog in her palm and told her what it was, and what it had done, and how long a ship's crew could live or die on the accuracy of forty-two teeth. [559] "The Chorus says the old work made the sickness," she said. "The Guild says it's worth money." [576] "Both of them want you ignorant, for different prices," he said, in the flat, unbothered voice that had gotten him thrown out of three parishes. [601] "Now count the teeth." [605]

She counted them twice, because she distrusted her first answer, and Vosk decided that was the beginning of a mind. [625] By winter he had her filing blanks from Guild scrap, ruining eleven before the twelfth held its shape. [643] He never praised her; he only said which tooth was wrong, and why, and waited. [658]

Above them the two stories hardened into two churches, and neither church noticed the sewer. [673] That suited Vosk. [676] History, he told the girl, is only the layer on top; the trick is to keep digging until you reach something that still works. [700] It was, he admitted to himself, a fairly good line for a man who lived in a drain. [718]

Three years later a mainland ship put into Halvesea with a dead chronometer, and the Guild, having nothing else to sell, sent for the rat in the sewer -- who sent, instead, a thirteen-year-old girl with a file, a dividing plate, and a cog she had cut herself. [765] She fixed it in a morning, and told the captain he stank. [777]
