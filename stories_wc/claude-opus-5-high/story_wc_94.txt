The last bus to Kelso Ridge stopped running the year they redacted my mother. [14] I found her instructions where she promised: a sealed envelope wedged in an old bus timetable, folded into the column of departures that no longer departed. [40] Inside, one line in her hand: Climb on the night when masks slip, and bring the timer. [57] The timer was hers, a darkroom timer with a cracked bakelite dial, the one she had used to develop photographs of the quarry crews before the ministry decided she was a poet and therefore dangerous. [92] Carnival night is the only night the checkpoints thin; when masks slip at midnight, censor and censored stand face to face and pretend not to recognize each other. [120] So I climbed. [123] Above the treeline the air began to rarefy, and my lungs learned a new arithmetic, two breaths for every step. [143] At four thousand meters the cloud deck lay beneath me like a second country, and the observatory stood on its shore: a white dome gone gray, its shutter jammed half open, lightning walking the cloudtops below in slow white legs. [183] She was inside, sitting under the dead telescope with a lamp at her feet. [197] Eleven years of official silence, and she looked up at me exactly as she had across the kitchen table, furiously tranquil, the way a kettle looks a second before it sings. [228] "You brought it," she said. [233] I set the darkroom timer on the mounting block between us, and its tick filled the dome like a heartbeat in a cave. [256] For eleven years I had kept a vigil I could not finish. [268] They had never given us a body, only a folder of her poems printed with the censored lines blacked out, forty-one bars of pure ink across three books. [296] I had spent those years trying to understand the cartography of scars: the black bars as landmass, the white page as sea, and somewhere in that geography a woman who might still be breathing. [330] Now she pushed back her sleeves and showed me the real map. [342] Burn scars up both forearms, pale and branching, from the night she fed the print run into the stove rather than name her printers. [366] "Every redaction has a shape," she said, "and so does every hand that survives it." [381] She had spent the hidden years rewriting the blacked-out lines from memory, then photographing the pages, forty-one frames on a single roll no larger than a thumb. [408] "Develop them slowly," she told me, "three minutes, agitate at thirty seconds; you know the rhythm." [424] Outside, the storm rose to meet the dome, and the light came up through the floor, lightning underfoot, thunder arriving like furniture moved in an upstairs room. [451] I had climbed expecting grief and found instead a kind of storm-kissed wonder, the giddiness of standing above weather with my mother's pulse in the room. [477] Then she asked me to stop the timer. [485] She would not come down. [490] "A vigil is only bearable in pause," she said, thumbing the lever until the tick stopped mid-breath. [507] "Not ended, paused," she said. [512] "You go down, you print the pages, and while they are wet, I am not dead and I am not caught; I am simply the count you have not restarted." [542] I understood then why she had chosen a timer and not a clock: a clock insists, but a timer waits to be told. [565] At midnight the carnival below shed its faces, and up here the clouds broke into channels of silver. [583] I put the film in my mouth, under my tongue, the way she had taught me when I was nine and thought it was a game. [609] I did not embrace her; she disliked ceremony, and the air was too thin to spend on speeches. [627] Going down, I passed through the cloud and the world coarsened again, wet grass, dog barks, a checkpoint lamp swinging in the rain. [650] The guard's mask was off, as the law allowed that one hour; he had a plain, tired face, and he waved me through without a word. [676] In the cellar I built her back one bath at a time, forty-one frames, the blacked-out lines rising out of the paper like land out of fog. [703] The timer sits on the shelf above the trays, still holding its unspent seconds. [717] People ask whether she is alive, and I tell them the truth: the count is paused, and I am not the one who restarts it. [742]
