The wandering fireworks designer arrived at the blooming fortress at dusk, when the wisteria on its ramparts looked like smoke that had decided to become flowers. [26] Her name was Oduya, and she chants while walking, a habit, not a prayer, which the gatekeeper found suspicious. [45] "Numbers," she explained, "and old syllables that keep my feet honest." [56] The fortress had been under siege for eleven months, and the siege had ended, which was somehow worse. [74] The enemy had simply left, taking the river's course with them, and the townspeople had learned to sit very still. [94] A council of three hired her for a single show, though they could not say what they wanted it to do. [115] Oduya knew. [117] She had spent nine years walking from ruin to ruin with a satchel of paper tubes, and she had one purpose only: to defy the gravity of hopelessness, which she considered a real force, measurable, with a downward pull of its own. [159] You beat gravity the way children do, she believed: briefly, loudly, and often enough that the body remembers the trick. [179] In the lower courtyard she found the mosaic. [187] It was old work, a floor of chipped tile laid by some forgotten garrison, and someone recent had been rearranging it. [208] Blue tiles pulled from the border, white ones prised out of a faded sun, all of it set into letters the size of a man. [233] A note in mosaic, half finished: LOOK UP ON THE NIGHT THEY SAY THERE IS NOTHING. [249] Beside the unfinished last word lay a fire spinner's kerosene rag knot, stiff and black, still smelling faintly of the fuel it had drunk. [273] The gatekeeper told her about the girl, Sefi, who had spun fire in the market square through all eleven months of the siege. [296] When the walls held, she had spun. [303] When the river turned away and the wells went brackish, she had gone out to the courtyard with a chisel instead, and then she had stopped coming at all. [332] "Dead?" Oduya asked. [335] "Sitting," the gatekeeper said. [339] "In her mother's house, facing the wall." [346] So Oduya worked for three days, and she chose the night with care. [359] Any showman could fill a clear sky; she wanted the one no one would compete with her for. [377] She launched during the hush of a night with no stars, a low overcast pressing down like a hand on the fortress. [399] Design, for Oduya, was less arithmetic than listening. [407] She could not measure wind with instruments she did not own, so she learned to filter the evening through ambient awareness: the damp weight on her tongue, the way the jasmine smell hung low instead of drifting, the flat sound her chant made against the wall, the loose way the kerosene rag knot swung from her wrist when she walked. [467] Heavy air meant low shells, fat and slow; she wanted them bursting just above the wisteria, close enough to feel in the teeth. [490] She had carried the knot since the courtyard, partly as a pendulum and partly as an argument. [507] The first shell went up with a sound like a door opening. [519] It opened into a wheel of white sparks that turned once, slowly, the way a fire spinner turns, and the crowd on the ramparts made a noise Oduya had heard in eleven other towns and never grown bored of. [558] Then a second wheel, then a third, low and insolent, each one a joke told with perfect timing and complete seriousness. [579] Oduya chanted the counts under her breath, walking the parapet, filthy hands, absurdly happy. [593] Below, in a street where a woman had been facing a wall for forty days, a shutter opened. [611] Oduya did not see it, but she had planned for it, which is a different kind of faith. [629] In the morning she left the kerosene rag knot on the mosaic, on top of a small pile of blue tile she had prised from the fortress steps herself; enough, she judged, to finish the last word. [666] She was two ridges away by dusk, chanting, when she heard the town behind her ringing a bell for no reason at all. [689] Someone, she guessed, had found the tiles and decided that NOTHING was a poor place for a sentence to end. [709] She added a syllable to her chant and kept walking. [719]
