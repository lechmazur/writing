The heat wave broke at three in the morning, and the lighthouse began to cool like a held breath let go. [21] Mira felt it through the iron stairs, the cobalt tower shedding the day's fever in tiny ticks and groans. [40] For eleven days the sea had lain flat and brassy, and no boat had answered the light. [57] Now the wind came in cold off the water, and somewhere out past the shoal, her brother's trawler was still missing. [78] Keeper Oduya was in the lamp room, shouting. [86] That was the method he trusted: the great brass horn beneath the lens took a human voice and pressed it into the beam, so that the light carried not only brightness but the vibrations of intent - come home, come here, this way, live. [129] He had a voice like a foghorn and forty years of proof, and every night he bellowed until his throat bled thin. [151] Mira was the assistant lighthouse keeper, and she had a theory he hated. [164] Shouting, she believed, muddied the signal. [170] A shout carried fear, or command, or the speaker's own terror of being unheard, and the sea sorted all that noise into nothing. [193] A whisper had no room for anything but the thing itself. [204] She wanted to prove that whispers travel farther than shouts, and she had spent three summers building the instrument that might do it. [227] It sat in her coat pocket: a glass vial the length of her thumb, stoppered with a cork she had turned herself from a root of the seawall oak. [256] Inside was the distillate of her mother's garden taken at night - moonflower, tuberose, wet stone, the green bitterness of tomato leaves. [277] Her mother had taught her the old trick of the coast: a message purifies intentions when it passes through the scent of a garden at night, because nothing grown in the dark is grown for show. [313] The flowers open for moths nobody watches; they beckon without wanting applause. [325] Pour that scent across a whisper and the whisper comes out clean, stripped of pride, stripped of panic - only the wanting left, and wanting travels. [350] Oduya had forbidden it. [354] Superstition, he said, from a woman who died believing the sea listened. [366] Mira climbed the last turn of the stairs at half past four. [378] Oduya had fallen asleep in the keeper's chair with his mouth open, his voice finally spent, and the horn stood empty above him like a throat. [404] Outside, the storm-washed sky was going the color of skin over a bruise. [417] Dawn on the cobalt lighthouse: the tower turned from black to that impossible blue, and the beam thinned against the morning. [438] She had one turn of the lens left before the light went out for the day. [454] She took out the vial. [459] Her hands were shaking so badly that the cork squeaked when she worked it free, and the sound was the loudest thing in the room. [484] Then the garden came out of the glass, full night and full summer, and the lamp room filled with a smell that had no business at sea. [511] She set the open vial in the horn's mouth, leaned in, and whispered her brother's name. [527] Not a command. [530] Not come home, which was what she wanted and therefore a kind of grabbing. [544] Just his name, the way their mother had said it through the kitchen screen while the moonflowers opened: Tomas. [563] She said it once, so softly that her own ears barely caught it, and the glass of the lens hummed. [583] It was screaming tenderness, that sound - a whisper amplified by love until the whole tower rang with it. [601] Oduya woke. [603] He did not shout. [607] He stood beside her with his ruined throat and listened to the note going out flat across the water, low and unhurried, carrying the smell of a dark garden nine miles into a cooling sea. [642] At six-forty, the trawler came around the shoal with its engine dead and its sail rigged from a tarp. [661] Tomas said later that he had steered by nothing he could name. [673] He said the air after the heat broke had smelled, absurdly, of their mother's tomato rows, and that he had turned toward it because a man will follow a thing that asks for nothing. [707] Mira corked the empty vial and put it back in her pocket. [720] There was a little scent left in the wood, enough for one more night, and after that she would have to plant a garden of her own. [747] Oduya, watching the blue tower brighten, cleared his throat and said, very quietly, that he would help her dig. [766]
