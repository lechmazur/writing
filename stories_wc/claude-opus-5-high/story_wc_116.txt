The chronicle keeper had forgotten her own name across the drift of untold seasons, which she considered a mercy. [19] Names were claims, and she had come here to witness change without judgment, not to claim anything. [36] The library had been an archive once, before the city above collapsed into orchards and then into scrub. [54] Now it was an overgrown library hidden in catacombs of lost knowledge, its shelves furred with pale moss, its aisles pried apart by roots that had learned to burrow through limestone in search of paper. [89] She did not stop them. [94] Her charge was to record, and roots were part of the record. [106]

Word of the world came to her the same way it always had: via cryptic folds in a well-worn origami swan. [127] It arrived by the underground stream, riding a plank, delivered by hands she never saw. [142] Unfolded, the paper said nothing outright; the creases themselves were the message, a script of angles she had spent forty years learning to read. [166] A sharp valley fold meant famine; a soft mountain fold meant a birth; a crease repeated until it grew soft and gray meant a thing that had happened so often no one bothered to name it anymore. [203] She would read the swan, copy its news into the ledgers, and fold it back into a bird, always the same sheet, thinning toward transparency. [228]

The catacombs kept a crumbling peace. [233] Stones loosened overhead in their own slow time; the quiet between their falling was not empty but working, a regenerative silence in which mold became soil and soil became seed and seed became the green vandalism of the stacks. [272] She had learned to trust it. [278] Speaking too much, she had found, tore the healing tissue of the place. [291] So she worked without words, and the library mended itself around her ruin by ruin. [306]

In the ninth year of the drought the swan arrived dry, unwet, carried in a girl's fist. [323] The girl was thin and calm and gripped reality loosely; she spoke of her grandmother's funeral and her grandmother's wedding in the same breath, in the same tense, and did not seem troubled by the collision. [359] "I fold them now," she said, "the way Grandmother taught me the angles before she stopped." [375] The keeper read the creases by lamplight and felt the old discipline strain. [388] The river had gone brackish, and the village was walking east toward the obsidian refuge along the river's dead upper channel, a shelter of black glass left by some older catastrophe. [419] Half of them wanted to go; half wanted to stay and die in their gardens. [434] The creases were smudged, folded and unfolded a dozen times, arguing with themselves. [447] "Tell me which," the girl said. [453] "You have read a hundred years," she added. [461]

This was the wound at the center of the keeper's vow. [472] She existed to witness change without judgment, because judgment had burned the city above them once already, when archivists decided which knowledge deserved air. [496] To advise was to choose, and to choose was to begin the burning again. [510] So she did what she could without speaking. [518] She took the girl's hand and led her down the collapsed north aisle, where roots had burrowed through the floor and split it open, and where, in that raw seam, seedlings stood in the ruin of a vellum treatise on irrigation. [559] She pointed at the break, then at the green. [568] She said nothing, which was the only honest thing she had. [579]

The girl looked a long time. [585] "Both, then," she said at last, "some burrow in, some walk out." [597] The keeper only bowed her head, and did not record it as advice. [610] In the ledger she wrote: the village divided; the glass shelter held nineteen; the gardens held eleven; both endured a season. [631]

The swan came back thinner each year, softer at the folds, until the paper was more crease than sheet and could be read by touch alone. [657] When the keeper's hands failed, she taught the girl the angles, and the girl folded new news into the old bird without asking again to be told what it meant. [687] Stones kept loosening overhead in their own slow time. [696] The peace kept crumbling, and kept being peace. [704] Somewhere east, black glass held its shape along a river learning a new course, and in the catacombs the silence went on making soil out of everything anyone had ever been sure of. [737]
