The sanctum sat under the aurora like a bell under snow, and Vesna Ilic came to it with wax in her pockets and a scrap of punched card sewn into her glove. [32] She was a lost wax caster by trade, which meant she understood how a thing must be destroyed to be kept. [53] The card was all that remained of the street organ that had stood in Drevno square, and its perforations, she believed, still held the names. [78] Forty-one of them, sung once a year on the flood-day, each name a chord the crank pulled out of the winter air. [100] Then the archive burned while she slept in the next room, and the organ with it, and for nine years she had refused to hear her own footsteps. [128] She had come north only after forgiving herself, because the keepers would not open the ice door to anyone still carrying a debt. [151] Grief, they said, wants; forgiveness gives. [157]

Inside, the sanctum was a vault of blue ice roofed in green light, and from its ceiling hung the Rope. [177] Four centuries of ceremonial braiding, walrus sinew and horsehair and copper thread, thick as a man's thigh at the crown and thinning to a hundred fine tails at the floor. [207] The keepers called it a library; every braid was a transcribed memory, its sequence of overs and unders a syntax older than any alphabet on the coast. [234] Pull the tails in the right order and the copper caught the aurora's current; the Rope would illuminate from within, and the ice would resonate, and whatever had been braided into it would be heard. [269]

Vesna had not come to listen. [275] She had come to write. [280]

The old keeper, Ansu, watched her lay the card scrap on a slab of ice and read it with a needle, hole by hole. [304] 'You want to put a song into the Rope,' he said. [315] 'Nobody has added a braid since my grandmother's time.' [324]

'Then the grammar is still warm,' Vesna said, and began, by reconfiguring the braids of the centuries-old ceremonial rope, to make room for forty-one names. [349]

It took eleven days. [353] She was technically gentle, the way a caster must be with a wax model that a warm thumb can ruin: she loosened each ancient strand without breaking one fiber, memorized where it had lain, and rewove it a half-turn over so the old memory survived beside the new. [401] Her hands cracked and bled and she wrapped them and kept going, wanting this with the unreasonable heat of snowmelt, which is cold water that moves anyway. [428] The punched holes became overs; the paper's blanks became unders; the organ's pipe-lengths became the pitch of the copper in the strand. [450]

On the twelfth night the aurora came down low and green, and Ansu pulled the tails in the order she taught him. [472] Light ran up the Rope like sap. [479] The sanctum hummed, and then it sang, and the voice that came out of the ice was the wheezing, patient, slightly flat voice of a street organ in a square that no longer existed, naming Anka, Pero, Milena, Dusan, forty-one names in the order they had drowned. [526]

Vesna did not weep; she listened with a pencil, checking the intervals. [538] Rope rots, she had explained on the first day, and keepers die, and grandchildren forget which tail to pull. [557] So in the sanctum's antechamber she built a wax cylinder, cut the braid-pattern into it as a spiral of ridges, sheathed it in clay, and burned the wax out over a seal-oil flame until nothing of her model remained but the hollow it had left. [602] Into that hollow she poured bronze. [608] The cast came out rough and true, a drum of metal you could read with a fingertip, instructions for a rope and a song both. [633] She hung it from the lowest tail, where the youngest keepers could reach. [646]

'You could have kept the paper,' Ansu said. [654]

'Paper burns,' she said, and put the scrap in the flame herself, and watched forty-one names go up as a single bright breath, because they were safe now in ice and bronze and in the hands of people who would pull the tails each flood-day. [699] Outside, the aurora thinned toward morning, and Vesna Ilic walked south with empty pockets, humming, at last, in her own voice. [720]
