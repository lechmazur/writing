The market under the pier opened only when the tide went out, and it closed at sunrise, and in between nobody raised a voice above a whisper. [27] They called it the truce of hush. [34] Wrenna Cobb had grown up on its rules the way other children grew up on hymns. [50] Her father ran moonshine down the coast in a flat-bottomed skiff, and she ran the quiet errands he could not. [70] Tonight the errand was her own. [76]

Lanterns hung from barnacled pilings, swaying, and the rotting planks overhead dripped brine onto the buyers' hats. [93] It smelled of tar, oysters, and lamp oil, and the sound of it was the sound of a hundred people agreeing not to shout. [117] Wrenna had always found it comforting, the way a held breath is comforting, which is to say not entirely. [136]

At the far end, past the pirate stalls of stolen compasses and salvaged rum, a man called the Archivist kept a table with a brass horn and a rack of wax cylinders. [168] He bought sounds. [171] Gull cries, dying fires, the last word of a drowning man. [182] What he wanted most, he said, was to capture the sound of trust breaking, and he was willing to pay in gold for it. [206] He had made the offer to three runners' children that season. [217] Two had taken it. [221]

Wrenna's father had pawned his father's antique watch fob at that table in March -- a braided gold chain with an enameled sparrow -- and had not slept well since. [249] The Archivist wore it now, looped on his waistcoat, where every runner's child could see it. [265]

Wrenna sat down across from him and said she had something to sell. [278] "The landing coves," she whispered. "My father's whole season of them." [289] The Archivist smiled and set the cylinder turning. [297]

Here is what she knew that he did not: the truce of hush ended at sunrise, and no cylinder cut under the pier could leave before then, and a wax cylinder holds four minutes, and the Archivist had only eleven left in his rack. [341]

So she began, and she stopped. [348] "The first cove is --" and then a cough, a long one, and an apology. [362] She started again and lost the name. [369] She started a third time and gave him the name of a cove that had silted shut before she was born, then corrected herself, then wept a little, convincingly, and asked for water. [402] Each false start cost him a cylinder, because a betrayal, he insisted, had to be whole to be worth anything. [422] He could not shout at her; nobody could shout down here. [433] That was the beauty of it. [439] The truce that kept the market safe for smugglers kept him mild, and Wrenna, who had never been brave in daylight, found herself curiously steadfast in the dark, wasting his wax one soft ruined sentence at a time. [477]

By the fourth hour there were two cylinders left. [486] By the fifth, one. [490] "You are thwarting me," he said, pleasantly, whispering. [498] "I'm nervous," she whispered back. [503] "Everybody's nervous their first time." [508]

Outside, the water began to go grey. [515] The lanterns were guttering and the crowd was thinning, and somewhere a fiddler packed away an unplayed fiddle. [533] The Archivist put the last cylinder on the spindle and said, very gently, that she should understand he only wanted the truth of the thing, that trust breaking was the most honest noise a person could make, and that everyone made it eventually. [576]

Wrenna leaned toward the brass horn. [582] She said a cove's name, true and clear -- a cove the revenue men had already burned in April -- and then, before she could name a second, she unhooked the watch fob from his waistcoat. [616] The little enameled sparrow swung, chiming faintly against the horn. [626]

Light came through the planks in long pale bars. [635] "Sunrise," said the woman at the next stall, and the market began to fold itself away. [651] He could not take it back from her, not here, not without breaking the hush, and the hush was the only law that had ever protected him. [678] He looked at his last cylinder, which held nothing but a dead cove, a girl's cough, and the small bright chime of a sparrow. [702]

Wrenna climbed the ladder into the cold pink morning with her grandfather's fob warm in her fist. [719] Her father was waiting at the skiff, not asking. [728] She gave him the fob and said nothing at all, and he understood, because they were people of the hush, and that was how they loved each other: quietly, uneasily, and without ever once handing anyone the sound of it.
