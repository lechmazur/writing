The dunes swallowed the house sixty years ago, and the house, being stubborn, kept its loft. [16] Rye Colquhoun came to it at dawn's earliest whistle, when wind first found the throat of the marram grass and made a reed of it. [41] He was a penny poet, a vagrant with ink on his knuckles, and he sold quatrains at a penny each to fishermen who could not spell their wives' names. [70] His coat pockets clinked. [74] Glass jars, a dozen of them, each stoppered with wax and labeled in his cramped hand: GULLS ARGUING 1961; TIDE UNDER PILINGS; A WOMAN HUMMING IN A LANGUAGE NOBODY ORDERED. [104] Capturing sounds in glass jars was the only trade he had ever been good at, and it had never once paid. [125] He had learned it too late. [130] His grandmother had sung to him for eleven years in a tongue he refused to carry, and when she died the songs went with her, and the family stood at her grave like people at a locked door. [168] Since then he had gleaned: scraps of other people's voices, leftovers from other people's kitchens and wakes, gathering what the young had let fall so he could hand it back to them. [200] He wanted, before his knees gave out, to become the translator between generations. [213]

The loft beneath the dunes had gone feral. [221] Sand had come in through the gable and lay in soft ridges; beach roses had rooted in the floorboards; a colony of swifts had made the rafters into a tenement. [251] Light came through the sand-scoured window the color of weak tea, and the whole ruin held a lonesome splendor, like a cathedral that had agreed to become a dune. [280] The girl was already there, sitting cross-legged in the sand with a shoebox of cassettes she could not play. [299] Nesta Ivarsdottir, sixteen, orphaned twice over, had written to him at the harbor post office because someone said the penny poet kept voices. [322] "It's Amma's," she said, pointing at the loft. [330] "All of it's Amma's, and I can't understand a word." [340]

He knelt among the beach roses and began to glean. [350] Under a floorboard, wrapped in oilcloth against the salt, he found the forgotten music box. [365] Its lacquer had blistered; its key turned with a sound like a cricket clearing its throat. [381] Three notes came out, then a fourth, warped and slow. [391] Rye sat down hard in the sand. [398] He knew the tune. [402] It was the one his grandmother had hummed over the stove, the one he had been too proud at fifteen to ask the name of, and here it was, sixty years and four hundred miles away, coming out of a stranger's box. [444]

"The old people all came from the same three islands," he said, when he could speak. [460] "They carried one song between them and spent their lives pretending it was private." [474] He unstoppered the jar marked A WOMAN HUMMING IN A LANGUAGE NOBODY ORDERED. [487] The sound came up out of the glass the way heat comes off a stone at evening, and it met the music box halfway, and the two of them agreed. [517] Nesta's face went positively wistful, which is a thing he had only ever seen on the very old. [535] "What do the words mean?" she asked. [542] He could have lied, and the lie would have been kinder. [553] Instead he gave her the true, plain sense of it: a verse about boiling seawater down to salt, and one line, repeated, asking the sea to sweeten. [580] Then he did what he was actually for: he made it a poem in her language, four lines, a penny's worth, and she paid him a penny because he insisted. [610]

They worked all that summer in the feral loft, jar by jar, tape by tape. [625] She learned the vowels; he learned the machines. [633] The swifts objected and stayed anyway. [639] He never got his grandmother back. [645] That was not on offer, and he had stopped asking for it. [657] But redemption, he found, is mostly gleaning: you go along the harvested rows of other people's lives, stooping, picking up what was dropped, and you carry it to somebody who is hungry. [689] On the last morning, at dawn's earliest whistle, Nesta wound the music box herself and sang the verse through, all of it, in Amma's tongue. [714] Rye held an empty jar up to the light, then thought better of it and let the sound go out over the dunes, unbottled, where anyone at all might glean it. [745]
