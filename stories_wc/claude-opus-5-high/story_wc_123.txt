The courtyard at dusk held three kinds of quiet, and Idris had learned to hear each one separately. [18] There was the hush of the fig tree, the hush of the shuttered arcade, and beneath both the low hush of a district that had sealed itself off from its own city. [50] Halvet Ward had raised its barricades without permission, eleven months ago, when the fever came up the river and the governors did nothing. [73] A rogue quarantine, the broadsheets called it; treason with a mop and a bell. [87]

Idris recorded storms. [90] He had done it for the Meteorological Office for nineteen years, and he did it still, though no salary had crossed the barricade since spring. [115] Every evening he climbed to the roof, read the barometer, noted wind, cloud base, the color of the eastern light, and copied the figures twice in a small hand. [144] One copy stayed in the ledger. [150] The second went into a carrier pigeon's message tube, a brass thimble no longer than a finger joint, and out over the wire fences to the observatory at Cale. [179]

No one had ever written back. [185] He did not need them to. [191] The work was a way to nurture integrity in solitude: a man alone with a barometer can lie to the whole world and no one will catch him, which is exactly why he must not. [226]

That evening he was not alone. [232] He had noticed her at the cistern queue, and again at the corner of Rope Street, and now, after being followed home through four turnings he had deliberately made unnecessary, she stood at the courtyard gate with her hands open. [272]

"You keep the storm book," she said. [278]

She was perhaps twenty-five, ink on her cuff, and credibly curious in a way that troubled him, because informers were usually either too bored or too eager, and she was neither. [309] The Ward's council had warned them all: the city wanted a pretext, some proof of conspiracy, something to justify soldiers. [329] A ledger of daily messages flying out of a sealed district would serve nicely. [342]

He did not ask her who sent her. [350] Direct questions invite prepared answers; he had learned to intuit a person's shape the way he intuited a front coming in, by progressing laterally, reading pressure and light rather than staring at the horizon. [384]

"Sit," he said, and poured her tea from a pot already cooling. [396] "Tell me what the sky did on the night of the ninth." [408]

She blinked, then answered: hail before midnight, small, then that green stillness, then rain until dawn. [424] It was right. [427] A person sent from outside would have had the ninth from a report, and the reports had the hail an hour late. [449] He asked what she did with her hands all day; she said she copied burial registers for the council, and he watched her not enjoy saying it. [476] He asked which month had been worst, and she named March, not February, which was true and which no outsider believed. [497] Sideways, sideways, and then the shape stood clear. [505]

"You want to know if the pigeons are carrying more than weather," he said at last. [521]

"The council thinks they are," she said, "and they mean to wring your neck for it." [537]

Idris took the brass tube from his coat and set it on the table between the cups. [553] "Open it." [555]

Inside was a strip of onionskin: date, pressure, wind, cloud, a column of numbers, and at the bottom, in the same small hand, the words no casualties reported today. [584] She read it three times. [589]

"That last line is not weather," she said. [597]

"No," he agreed. [600] "It is the only honest census leaving this ward." [609] The city said the Ward was dying by hundreds; the council said nobody died at all; both were lying, in opposite directions, and someone had to keep the middle. [638]

The light in the courtyard had gone the color of weak tea, and the fig tree let down one leaf, unhurried. [659] Somewhere above the roofs the evening pigeons turned in their slow, layered circles. [671]

"They will search your rooms," she said. [678]

"Then you had better learn the barometer," Idris said, "because the record should outlive the recorder." [694]

She stayed until full dark. [699] He taught her the mercury, the trick of the wind vane that stuck in damp, the knot that held the tube to the bird's leg without chafing. [726] She wrote it all down in a hand nearly as small as his. [739] When she left, she went by the back lane, and he did not watch to see who followed her. [758] He climbed to the roof instead, in the quiet, and read the pressure falling, and wrote down what was true. [778]
