Halvard Renn shook so badly on the ridgeway that the couriers' guild had stopped trusting him with glass. [18] They still trusted him with the pendant, because the pendant did not break; it only waited. [34] It hung in a felt pouch against his sternum, a single dragonfly wing sealed in old resin, veined like a map of rivers nobody had walked. [60] The aurora came down green over the spine of the mountains, and Halvard walked beneath it the way he had walked beneath it for nineteen years: fast, twitching, counting his steps to keep his hands from shaking. [97] Sabra Oyer had taught him the counting. [104] She had taught him everything else too, then walked north past the last waystation forty winters ago and never come back. [125] In the villages below they had begun to tell her as a story: Sabra of the Long Minute, who could hold a goodbye open like a door. [152] That was the trouble with a good mentor gone missing; you watched her cross, year by year, from memory into myth, and the myth answered fewer questions every season. [181]

The pendant was hers, and it worked by vibrating sympathetically. [191] Put it between two people who were about to part, and the wing found the pitch of their attention and hummed along with it, and the encounter extended -- a handshake unspooling into an hour, a stranger's directions becoming a whole biography. [232] Nothing supernatural, Sabra insisted; only that most people ended a meeting before it had properly begun, and the wing made them reluctant. [254] Halvard had used it exactly twice in nineteen years, both times by accident. [267]

His order slip said Cold Harrow, third shelf, permanent catalogue, which meant a drawer, a label, and darkness until the mountains wore down. [290] He had carried four hundred relics into that darkness and had never once objected, because objecting was not in the contract and because his hands shook. [316]

At the Sorrel waystation, three days out, a girl was waiting with a lantern and a bad map. [334] Her name was Teo and she wanted to know the way to the pass where Sabra of the Long Minute had gone up. [357] She had the details wrong: a silver staff, a cloak of moths, a promise to return when the sky went red instead of green. [381] Halvard opened his mouth to correct her and found, to his horror, that he could no longer prove the true version. [402] Sabra had worn wool, chewed pine resin, laughed like a door hinge; those things lived nowhere but in him, and he was fifty-one and jittery and mortal. [429]

Teo thanked him, shouldered her pack, and turned to go, and the whole exchange had taken ninety seconds. [447] That was the thing Sabra had hated most, he remembered suddenly: not cruelty, not even forgetting, but the speed at which people let each other pass -- the routine that let no wonder in. [480]

He took out the pouch. [485] The wing woke against his palm, found the frequency of two people standing on an aurora-lit ridgeway with somewhere else to be, and began to hum. [511] Teo stopped. [513] The ninety seconds opened like a seed case. [521]

Halvard talked until the green light rolled over to grey: the wool, the resin, the hinge-laugh, the way Sabra had made him count steps, the four hundred drawers, the two accidents. [552] Teo asked questions a myth cannot answer and a memory can. [563] Somewhere in the fourth hour he understood what the pendant had always been for: not to stop partings, but to let a true thing germinate in somebody young enough to carry it further than he could. [599]

At dawn he filled out the consignment slip differently. [608] Relic lost on the ridgeway, he wrote, hand steady for once, and he put the wing around Teo's neck and told her the rule: never in a drawer, only ever between two people about to walk away from each other. [648] She went north toward the pass anyway; he did not expect otherwise. [660] Halvard walked south with an empty pouch and arrived at Cold Harrow four days late, shaking, unemployable, and unreasonably happy. [680]

Years later, couriers on that route would tell of a jittery old man who gave away a wing of green fire, and they would get the details wrong -- a staff, a cloak, a promise -- and somewhere below the aurora a young woman would stop a stranger, hold out a pendant, and take her time correcting them.
