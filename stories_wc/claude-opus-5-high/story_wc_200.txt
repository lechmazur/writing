Echo Gorge kept its sunrise hidden. [5] The cliffs leaned so close that light arrived only as a pale seam, one held breath long, before the shadows sealed again. [27] Mira climbed down in that hush, harp strapped to her back, because the dead spoke only while the seam was open. [48]

She was a lost soul collector, licensed by no one, paid in gratitude and turnips. [63] Villagers called her modestly fearless, which she thought was fair: she feared plenty, and went anyway. [79]

The harp was deliberately misaligned. [84] Her brother Ost had built it that way, in the last year of his coughing, filing the bridge crooked so that every chord came out bent. [110] Straight notes soothed the living; distorted notes, he swore, pried open the dead. [123] He had been right, and Mira had spent four years proving it. [135]

In the gorge she braced her heels on cold shale and played. [147] The sour, sliding notes went out and came back wrong, and in the wrongness the spectral confessions began. [165] A drover admitted he had watered the milk for eleven years. [176] A midwife confessed she had loved the wrong husband and delivered his children anyway, gently. [191] They spoke, and then they could go. [198] That was the whole craft: listen, remember, carry the words home to whoever needed them. [213]

But Mira had not come down for strangers. [221] For four years she had pursued one soul through the gorge's echoes, and four years he had slipped her. [240]

Ost had died laughing at his own terrible joke, and the village had decided that was indecent. [257] Their healers taught that grief must be kept solemn, wept clean, sealed like a jar. [272] Mira wanted the opposite, and wanted it badly enough to keep climbing into a crack in the world before dawn: she wanted to integrate laughter into healing, to prove that a joke could close a wound the way stitches do. [312] For that she needed Ost's confession, and Ost would not give it. [324]

This morning she changed her tuning. [330] From her collar she drew the crocodile's tooth he had sent her from the delta the year he left home, wrapped in a note that read: Bite something. [358] She had laughed for an hour at that. [366] Now she used the tooth as a tuning key, cranking the third string until it whined past any honest pitch. [386]

The seam of light opened above her, thin and gold. [396] She played the ugliest chord of her life. [404]

The gorge answered with his voice, closer than it had ever come. [416]

"You tuned it with the tooth," Ost said. [424] "I was hiding in the notes you kept playing sad." [434]

Her hands shook; she kept them on the strings. [443] "Confess," she said. [446] "Then you can go." [450]

"That's the trouble," he said. [455] "I'm not sorry." [458] "I'm not sorry I died laughing, and you've been chasing me to hear me repent of it." [475]

The light on the wall slid a finger's width. [484] She had maybe a hundred heartbeats left of sunrise. [493]

"Then confess something true," she said. [499]

So he did. [502] He confessed that the joke had been bad, genuinely bad, the worst of his career, something about a crocodile and a dentist. [524] He confessed he had wanted her to be the one laughing, not him. [537] He confessed that he had stayed four years in the cold rock, unwilling to leave her alone with a village that measured love in tears. [562]

Then he told her the joke. [568] It was, in fact, awful. [573] Mira laughed until the gorge filled with copies of her laughing, layer on layer, a luminous ache climbing the walls toward the seam. [596] When she stopped, he was gone, and the strings had gone quiet and true. [610]

She climbed out into ordinary morning and went to the healer's house, where a widow had been sitting solemnly for six weeks. [632] Mira set the crocodile's tooth on the table as proof of nothing, and told the widow the worst joke ever made. [653] The widow stared. [656] Then she made a sound like a door unsticking, and wept, and laughed, and wept, and something in her chest let go. [678]

Mira collected her confession, carried it home, and wrote it down beside her brother's. [692] After that, the villagers began sending for her when the solemn medicine failed. [705] She always came before dawn, harp crooked, tooth in her collar, modestly afraid and going anyway. [721]
