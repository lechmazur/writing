Mirren kept the cemetery the way other people keep birdcages: cheerfully, and with a great deal of singing. [18] She pruned the yews, tenderly organized the fallen headstones into rows she called the waiting choir, and told the dead the weather every morning. [42] The mourners found her blithe manner indecent; the dead, she suspected, did not mind. [56] Thursdays she played chess with Hesper Vane, who had bought a plot in 1961 and visited it the way some men visit a summer house. [81] They used his chess clock, a brass thing with two faces, and he always insisted the game ended when the flag fell, whatever the position. [106] On the last Thursday of October his time ran out mid-move, and Hesper laughed, and then stopped. [123] He was buried eleven paces from the yew he had liked, by her own spade, in a velveteen gloom of low cloud and lantern light. [148] His solicitor delivered two things: an antique sovereign case, worn smooth as a river stone, and a tapestry with a frayed border. [170] The case held no coins. [175] Inside, pressed into the velvet, was a scrap of paper: Untangle it, gardener, you were always the patient one. [194] She had known Hesper thirty years and never known what he wanted from her; that was the ache. [212] He had paid for her mother's funeral, then vanished for a season; he had willed her nothing but riddles. [231] She wanted, more than inheritance or apology, to discover the shape of his intentions. [245] So she worked the tapestry by lamplight through November. [254] The picture was ordinary, hounds, a hill, a lady with a lute, but the border was wrong, its threads knotted and re-dyed, some frayed to fuzz. [280] Decoding the frayed threads in that border took her six weeks: each broken strand a letter, each knot a space, the whole thing a sentence stitched backward. [307] It read: COBALT SILO, NORTH FROM THE WAITING CHOIR, AFTER MOONRISE, LISTEN. [319] The silo stood four miles out in the beet fields, painted cobalt by some optimist in the fifties, and she went to it under stars so thick they seemed to hum. [350] Inside, the air was cold and round. [356] She said her own name and the silo gave it back to her nine times, each return softer and stranger, the last one hardly hers at all. [383] Hesper had called such places uncharted echoes, chambers nobody had measured, where a voice could outlive the mouth that made it. [404] Against the curved wall stood a wind-up phonograph and a crate of wax cylinders, each labeled in his small, apologetic hand. [425] He had been recording himself here for forty years, he explained on the first cylinder, because the silo held a voice longer than a church did, and because he could not say certain things to a living face. [463] She listened until dawn. [467] He had loved her mother; he had loved her mother badly, and late, and had gone away the season after the funeral because staying would have looked like a claim he had not earned. [501] He had bought his plot eleven paces from the yew because it was eleven paces from her mother's. [519] He had kept the sovereign case empty for sixty years because his father had filled it with gold and nothing else, and Hesper wanted one thing in his life that held only a message. [553] The last cylinder was recorded the week before he died, and it was short. [567] "If you got this far," he said, "you untangled me, which is more than I managed." [583] "Don't be solemn about it, you were never solemn, which is why I chose you." [598] Mirren walked home through the beet fields with the case in her pocket, and the shape of his intentions sat in her chest, awkward and warm, like a held bird. [628] That spring she planted snowdrops in the eleven paces between two graves, and sang the whole time, because the dead have heard worse. [651] The chess clock she wound and left running on his headstone, until the flag fell, and then she let it be. [672] She hung the tapestry in the potting shed, border outward, where the light could finish fraying it. [689] Some messages, she had learned, are only worth the patience they cost. [701]
