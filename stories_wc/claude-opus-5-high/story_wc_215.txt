The ghosts became visible in the third summer of the burning, and they arrived without voices. [16] Ovid Marn learned this the way he learned everything now: alone, through contextual clues. [30] The first one stood at the edge of his oasis, a woman in a beekeeper's veil, and she did not answer his greeting. [53] She only raised one hand and pointed toward the cracked cistern, where the last hive still hummed. [70] A recluse for nine years, he had not spoken to a living person in all that time. [87] The wasteland had taken the almond groves, the road, and the town of Sorrel Bend; the beekeeping cooperative that owned these twelve acres had abandoned them to him, along with four wells, tamarisk shade, and bees that made a bitter, medicinal honey nobody remained to buy. [133] He had grown tranquilly perceptive out there, the way a man does who must read weather from the slant of a wing. [155] So he was astonished, and he distrusted his astonishment, when he recognized the veil's mended tear. [171] Junia had sewn that tear herself, badly, the night before she walked into the fire lines. [187] His sister. [189] His partner in the apiary. [194] The other half of a promise he had spent nine years not saying aloud. [208]

The promise was a covenant they had tangled on purpose, so that neither could slip free of it. [226] Each had written the unsayable thing on paper -- his jealousy, her fear, the name of the man who set the first fire -- and sealed both letters in a stained glass box their mother had brought from the coast. [264] Neither would open it while the other lived; if both died, the desert could have it. [280] The clause had seemed airtight to two children who had never imagined the dead standing in daylight. [297] Now the terms were knotted past untying. [304] She was dead, and she was here, and the covenant held both facts in its teeth. [320]

He brought the box out to the cistern the next morning and set it on the stone between them. [339] Sun came through its panels and laid red and green across her forearms, and did not stop there; it went through her and puddled on the rock. [366] He waited for her gaze to fall on the latch. [376] It did not. [379] She looked instead at the hive, then at his hands, then at the smoker hanging empty on its nail, and then back at the hive. [404] That was her whole argument, and it was mute eloquence: the bees needed feeding, the season was turning, and there were nine years of work he had done alone that she wished to see done properly one more time. [443]

He worked. [445] She watched. [447] Over the following weeks he assembled her meaning the only way available, through contextual clues: the frame she hovered beside was the one with chalkbrood; the well she stood over was the one going brackish; when he reached for the box, she drifted to the far side of the tamarisk and waited there, patient as stone, until he put it down. [508] He understood, finally, that she was not waiting for the seal to break. [521]

Words spoken are spent. [525] The letters in that box had held their pressure for nine years precisely because no one had read them, and everything he had built out here -- the hives kept, the wells dug deeper, the grief carried without commentary -- had been built on that pressure. [569] To open it would be to spend her. [577] So he chose to preserve the weight of unspoken words, and told her so without speaking, which seemed the correct grammar. [598]

He carried the stained glass box to the oldest hive, cut the comb away, and set it inside the hollow super where the bees would seal it in wax. [627] Then he closed the lid and did not look at her, because looking would have been a question, and he no longer had one. [651] When he did lift his gaze, she was standing in the propolis-colored light with the veil pushed back, and her face held the astonishment his own had worn all summer -- as if she had expected, of the two of them, to be the one who understood. [697]

She stayed through the almond-less autumn. [703] Travelers began to cross the wasteland again, following the rumor of green, and they found a man who spoke little and a ghost who spoke not at all, and between the two of them an oasis that made sense. [742] The covenant was never resolved. [747] It was only, at last, honored. [753]
