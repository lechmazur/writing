The lunar-eclipse locksmith climbed the last stair under the soft hush of a distant morning. [15] Below her, the clouds lay flat and pink, a sea she had sailed once as a child, on a raft of her father's driftwood. [39] The frozen library stood above them all, its shelves furred with frost, its windows hauntingly bright with a light that had no source she could name. [65] Her name was Ise, and she could only open locks during an eclipse. [78] It was not superstition but arithmetic: her hands remembered nothing except in shadow, the way tide pools remember the sea. [98] The eclipse would arrive in four hours, and she meant to be ready. [111] In her satchel she carried her mother's clockmaker's gear assortment, forty-one brass wheels in a felt roll, each one toothed for a machine that no longer existed. [138] Her mother had died mid-repair, hands inside a mantel clock, and Ise had grown up believing that a half-fixed thing was the loneliest object in the world. [165] She had come to learn the rhythm of repair. [174] The librarians were gone; only their mittens remained, paired on the radiators that had not run in eighty years. [193] Ise found what she needed the way the old stories promised: through scraps of prophecy hidden in a library's corner, tucked behind the atlas case where the floor sloped. [222] They were not scrolls. [226] They were torn margins, bookmarks, a laundry list with three lines of verse on its back, frozen together into a brittle brick. [248] She breathed on them until they peeled apart, and read them in the order the cold released them. [266] One scrap said: the door beyond the reading room opens only to a shadowed moon. [281] Another: within lies a hall of mirrors, and within the mirrors, the clock that keeps the world's small hours. [300] A third, in a child's handwriting: bring teeth. [308] A fourth, in the same hand: do not mend what only wants witnessing. [321] She touched the felt roll of gears and understood that someone had foreseen her mother's wheels. [337] The last scrap was blank except for a smear of oil, which smelled of her childhood workshop and made her sit down on the floor for a while. [365] When the eclipse came, it came slowly, a rust-colored shade drawn across the high window. [380] Ise knelt at the door and felt her hands wake, remembering every tumbler they had ever turned. [397] The lock gave with a sound like ice letting go of a pier. [410] Inside, the hall of mirrors went on farther than the building could possibly allow. [424] Every pane held her at a different age, and in each reflection her hands were busy: splicing rope, winding a watch, holding her mother's wrist. [449] At the hall's end stood the clock, taller than a mast, its case open, its escapement missing three teeth. [468] It ticked anyway, limping, and the mirrors limped with it. [478] Ise knelt, unrolled the felt, and began trying wheels against the empty shaft. [491] Nineteen were wrong. [494] The twentieth seated with a click she felt in her molars. [505] She had to uncap her mother's oil flask with her teeth, both hands occupied, and the smell filled the hall like weather. [527] One drop, two. [530] The tick steadied, then steadied again, and Ise understood at last what her mother had been listening for all those quiet evenings. [552] Repair had a rhythm, and the rhythm was patience, oil, and the willingness to try nineteen wrong things first. [571] In the mirrors, all the versions of her worked in time, and none of them were alone. [588] She remembered the last scrap then and did not touch the mantel clock's reflection, the one still open, her mother's hands still inside it. [612] She only looked, the way you look at driftwood and see the whole ship. [626] The eclipse passed; her hands went ordinary again. [634] The great clock kept its new, even rhythm, and the frozen library hummed along above the clouds. [651] Ise packed the remaining gears, left one on the reading-room table for whoever came next, and went down into the morning. [672] Behind her, in a corner, a new scrap was already curling into being, and it said only: she learned. [691]
