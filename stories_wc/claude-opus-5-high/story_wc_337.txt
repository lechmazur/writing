The mask came to Iva in nine pieces, wrapped in oilcloth, smelling of brine. [14] She was the island's last restorer of broken masks, and she knew this one by its chipped cheekbone before she turned it over. [37] It had belonged to her brother Sanno, who had drowned with four others when the Kesper went down twelve years ago. [58] Since that winter, the dead of the Kesper had not settled. [69] They moved in the shallows like lamplight, turned the compasses of living men, and stood at the ends of beds without speaking. [91] The village council brought her the pieces and asked her, at last, to ground them. [106] Iva said yes too quickly, and everyone pretended not to notice. [117]

Restoring a mask was never only glue. [124] A mask holds a face, and a face holds what the mouth refused to say; to mend one you must first find the silence it was buried with. [152]

So on the first clear night she went up to the prism sanctuary, the old glass chapel on the headland, where the roof is cut into a thousand angled panes. [182] Under starlight the panes split the sky into colored ribbons that lay across the floor like a loom half-strung. [201] Fishermen had prayed there for six centuries, and no one prayed aloud. [213] That was the sanctuary's nature: it accumulates silence, the way a cellar accumulates cold. [227] Every unspoken thing carried through its door stayed there, layered and interlaced, an interlaced hush you could feel pressing on your eardrums. [249]

And beneath the altar stone, the old records said, the fishermen had buried their oldest net. [265] Each generation had tied a knot in it for a grief they would not name. [280] The knots held the hush; the hush held the drowned; the drowned could not go down. [296]

Iva levered up the altar stone with a bar and began to exhume the net, and it came out of the dry sand grey and heavy as a sleeping animal. [326] It was centuries old and it was full of knots, hundreds of them, hard as hazelnuts. [342] She took out the only tool fine enough for such work: a tapestry needle, blunt-tipped and long, inherited from her grandmother, who had been a restorer too. [369]

Her grandmother had told her the rule, and Iva had spent twelve years avoiding it. [384] The knots do not come out for hands. [392] They come out for speech. [397] A silence can only be paid for with a secret, and the unraveling lasts exactly as long as the telling. [417]

So she knelt in the ribbons of starlight, slid the needle into the first knot, and spoke to the empty chapel. [438]

"I was aboard the Kesper," she said, and the knot loosened a quarter turn. [452] She had been seventeen, hidden under the tarp because Sanno had let her come. [466] When the squall took the mast, the net went over and filled, and the boat began to heel. [484] Sanno put his knife in her hand and told her to cut the net free while the men hauled. [503] She cut it. [506] The net went, and the ballast of it went, and the Kesper came upright so fast that five men went over the rail and she did not. [533]

She swam ashore and let the village believe the sea had chosen her. [546] She had restored four hundred masks since, and never once told the truth, and the truth had gone somewhere; she understood now that it had come here, knot by knot, night by night. [579]

The needle moved and the knots opened, one after another, and with each one the pressure in the room eased a little, like a held breath let out in stages. [609] She told it all, plainly, without weeping, in the lucid unrest of someone who has been awake for twelve years. [629] Near dawn the last knot fell open and the net lay flat, a plain grey mesh, ordinary. [646] Five shapes stood in the colored light, and she made herself look at each of them until they thinned and were only light. [669] Sanno went last. [672] He did not speak, because the chapel takes speech, but he touched the place on his cheek where the mask was chipped. [694]

At home she set the nine pieces on the bench and mended them with fish glue and gold, leaving the chip unfilled. [716] Then she walked down to the council house, still salt-stiff, and told them what she had told the dark. [735] A secret paid once will not stay paid; she knew that much about knots. [749] It has to be told where it can be heard, or it only ties itself again somewhere quiet. [767]
