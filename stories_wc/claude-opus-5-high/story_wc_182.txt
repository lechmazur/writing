The bookstore filled the mountain the way music fills a held breath. [12] Sela had worked its switchback galleries for nine years, and still the shelves rearranged themselves behind her like a score no one had agreed to play. [38] She was an arcane linguist, which meant she was paid to listen for the grammar under things. [55] Everyone else heard silence in the deep stacks; Sela heard an unheard symphony, tuning. [69]

After the time traveler arrived, the tuning stopped. [77] He came up the Eighth Spiral in a wet coat, carrying nothing but a botanical diary bound in waxed linen. [97] His name was Orrin, he said, and he had been running rescue missions for eleven years. [113] Not of people. [116] Of lessons. [118]

"Every civilization learns something too late," he told her, thumbing the diary open. [131] Inside, pressed flat, were leaves and seed husks from plants that had never existed in her century: a grass that grew only in soil poisoned by a particular reactor, a vine that fed on smoke. [166] Each specimen was annotated in six scripts, three of them dead, one not yet invented. [181] "I collect the evidence," Orrin said. [187] "But evidence rots without a shelf." [193]

He wanted to find the library of unlearned lessons. [202] Sela had heard the rumor the way all shelvers had: a wing of the mountain that held every book a world had needed and failed to write, catalogued and waiting, unreadable because unwritten. [235] Management called it a shipping error. [241] Sela called it the missing movement of her symphony. [250]

She did not believe in maps of the place; the mountain renegotiated its own floor plan nightly. [267] But she believed in gaps. [272] Sela was carefully resourceful, the kind of scholar who budgeted candle stubs and favors. [286] She could not afford a diviner, so she worked by researching gaps: not what the catalogue held, but the shapes it refused. [308]

For three weeks they read call numbers aloud in the cold galleries, Orrin naming plants, Sela naming absences. [326] Shelf 4-J skipped from flood defense to flood poetry. [335] The agriculture spiral had no entry for the grass in Orrin's diary, though it indexed the reactor that had poisoned the soil. [357] Every absence had a grammar. [362] Every absence pointed downhill. [366]

The door was in the mountain's root, behind a barrow of returns nobody had processed since the store's founding. [385] It had no handle, only a sentence carved across it in a script Sela had studied for six years and never once spoken aloud. [409] It was a contract clause. [414] The library of unlearned lessons, it said, opened only to those who paid its price: a lesson of their own, surrendered, so the shelves could stay balanced. [441]

Orrin offered eleven years of rescue work without hesitating. [450] Sela put a hand over his mouth. [457] "Don't accept terms you can renegotiate," she said. [465]

She read the clause again, slowly, the way she read anything expensive, and found the gap in it: it demanded a lesson surrendered, not a lesson lost. [492] Nothing in the carving forbade a copy. [499] So she spoke the sentence back to the door in its own script, amended. [513] They would surrender the botanical diary, every pressed leaf and dead-script annotation, on the condition that the library issue a duplicate, stamped, circulating, borrowable. [537] A lesson shelved and a lesson loose in the world. [547]

The mountain thought about it for a long minute. [556] Then the door swung inward onto a hall of unwritten spines going up into dark, and Sela finally heard the symphony resolve - not loud, still unheard by anyone but her, but complete. [588]

Orrin laid the diary on the intake desk and took the duplicate, warm, in both hands. [604] "Where next?" Sela asked. [608] "Backward," he said. [611] "Somewhere they still have time to read it." [619] She walked him up nine spirals to the mouth of the mountain, then went back down to the cold galleries, where the absences were still singing, and began again to find what no one had thought to write.
