The orchard would come down at six, and until then the whole valley held its breath. [16] I have exorcised forty-one ghosts with a gossip column, and every single one of them was an imperfect miracle. [35] Print what the dead never got to say, and they go quiet; print it wrong, and they go quiet anyway, which is the part nobody thanks me for. [63] Hollis Vane found me in the sheet music archive under the old cannery, where the town keeps six thousand folders of dead men's waltzes on steel shelving that rusts in a slow orange tide. [97] He carried an antique perfume bottle in both hands like a man carrying soup to a sickbed. [114] Cut glass, a silver atomizer bulb gone green, and inside it, according to him, his sister Junie. [131] She died in the orchard thirty years back, he said, because he had told her the ladder was sound. [150] Tomorrow the county saws would take the trees, and with them the last address she had. [166] He wanted to reconnect long enough to deliver an overdue apology, and he wanted it printed nowhere. [183] That is not how my trade works, I told him. [193] A column is a drain; a bottle is a bathtub. [203] You can love the water all you want, but it will sour if it never moves. [219] He blinked at that, the way everyone does; metaphor is the only language I have left for this work, because plain speech about the dead is either a lie or a lawsuit. [251] Still, I set up, because the hush before a felling is the only hour the dead can hear you over the living. [273] Ghosts do not travel on prayer; they travel on induced currents. [284] I wound sixty feet of copper around the archive's steel uprights, clamped the ends to a hand-cranked dynamo, and let the shelves become one long coil. [310] Spin the crank and the metal breathes a field; whatever is folded into glass unfolds into the field, the way a hymn unfolds out of ink when somebody finally sings it. [341] I told Hollis to uncork the bottle on the third minute, not before. [354] He uncorked it on the first. [360] Orange blossom and old alcohol came up out of the glass, and the archive went cold in a way that had nothing to do with weather. [386] The dynamo whined. [389] Paper lifted off the shelves in slow spirals, a hundred waltzes turning over like leaves deciding whether to fall. [408] Junie arrived in the current, and she arrived wrong. [417] She had no voice of her own, so she borrowed the shelves, and the steel sang her in a hum that landed a half step under every note it tried for. [448] She had no face either, only a shape like heat above a road. [461] Hollis started his apology and got three words in before the hum swallowed them. [475] She cannot hear words, I said, only the weather of them. [486] So sing it. [489] He looked at the flying paper, and he understood, because grief makes fast students of slow men. [506] He caught a folder out of the air, a bad polka copied by a bad hand in 1911, and he sang the top line in a cracked baritone while I cranked. [537] He sang the ladder, and the lie, and the thirty years of not saying it. [552] None of it was in the notes, and all of it was in the room. [567] The hum came up a half step and met him, just once, and then the bottle cracked along an old flaw and she was gone before he reached the last bar. [598] He wanted her to say she forgave him. [606] She said nothing; she only matched his pitch for one second in a basement that smelled of orchards. [624] Outside, the saws started at six, right on schedule, and neither of us went up to watch. [641] I printed it that Sunday, the way I print all of them, under the headline about a local man and a broken bottle, and I did not use her name. [671] Hollis was angry for a week and then he wasn't. [681] A miracle, I told him, is a window somebody else washed: you still see the street through the streaks. [700] He keeps the cracked bottle on a shelf, empty, and he says the whole house still smells faintly of blossom in April, which is either chemistry or mercy. [728] I have stopped needing to know the difference. [736]
