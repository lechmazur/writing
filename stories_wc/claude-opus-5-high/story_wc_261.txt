The anesthesia wore off at the hour when the tower held its breath, that meteor hush between the last star and the first bird. [24] Mira woke knowing two things: her side had been opened, and the ivy on the window was worried about her. [44] Not worried in words. [48] Worried the way a plant worries, a slow green pressure behind the eyes, like someone leaning on a door you had forgotten was yours. [72] The surgeon, Idris, had warned her that the graft might have side effects. [85] He had not warned her about the ivy. [93]

The tower was a quiet observatory the village had emptied nine years ago, after the meteor came down in the beet fields and refused to be ordinary. [120] Whatever fell there healed things. [125] It healed the wrong things. [130] Mira's brother Tomas had been dying in the house nearest the crater; the miracle skipped him and settled instead in a hedge of hawthorn, which has bloomed every month since, even under snow. [163] A displaced miracle, the papers called it, before the papers stopped coming. [175]

Mira had come back to the tower to finish grieving. [185] That was the story she told people, and it was a lie shaped like the truth. [201] She had come to open the wound wider. [209] Idris had cut a strip of hawthorn heartwood into her scar, the old one under her ribs from the night she pulled Tomas out of the flooded root cellar and failed anyway. [241] She wanted to learn to read the stories in scars. [251] Wood keeps its history honestly; every drought, every fire, every year of plenty is written in rings you can count. [271] Flesh keeps its history too, she believed, but in a language nobody had bothered to learn. [287]

She had learned the grammar of it, absurdly, by examining coffee rings. [299] Idris kept his surgical ledger on the same desk where he drank, and the cups had stamped their faint brown circles across nine years of entries. [325] Overlapping rings, darker where he had sat long, pale where he had left quickly. [339] Mira had spent whole nights tracing them, and they told her what his handwriting would not: which nights he had stayed awake, which pages he had reread until the paper wore soft. [371] The page for Tomas had eleven rings on it. [380]

On the ledger's corner stood the frosted hourglass, blown from meteor glass the year the crater cooled, its sand grey and its bulbs clouded like breathed-on windows. [407] It ran for exactly as long as a person can bleed and still be saved. [422] Idris turned it during every operation. [428]

Now, at dawn, with the anesthesia letting go of her one nerve at a time, Mira sat up and heard the tower. [450] Not the stone: the roots. [455] Beneath the floor, the hawthorn had spent nine years tunneling a root under the beet fields, under the road, up through the tower's foundation toward the room where the hourglass stood. [486] It had been trying to give the miracle back. [495] It had no hands and no mouth, only nine years of patience and a direction. [510]

Mira pressed her palm to the bandage and asked, without words, what the hawthorn remembered of the night the sky opened. [531] The answer arrived as scar-language, which she could suddenly read: a hot weight, a boy's voice two fields off, a reaching that came up short. [556] The hedge had not stolen anything. [562] It had caught what fell and had been carrying it ever since, the way you carry a package for a neighbor who never comes home. [587]

Idris came up the stairs with two cups and set one down, adding a ring to the ledger without thinking. [607] "You reopened it yourself, didn't you," he said. [615] "Twice, before you agreed to the graft." [622] "I needed it to be new enough to listen," Mira said. [633]

He turned the frosted hourglass over out of habit, and the grey sand began its small fall. [650] Through the floorboards, the root pushed up a white thread, found the glass, and stopped there, satisfied. [667] Delivered. [668]

Mira did not feel Tomas return, because that is not how any of this works. [683] She felt only the hush again, the enormous quiet a meteor leaves behind it, and inside that quiet the ordinary fact of her own healing wound beginning, at last, to close. [714] She drank the coffee. [718] When she lifted the cup, she left a ring on the ledger's last page, beside Idris's, overlapping it. [736] Someone, someday, would read it and know they had both sat there a long time, at dawn, while the sand ran out. [758]
