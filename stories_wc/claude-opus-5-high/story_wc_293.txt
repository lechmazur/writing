After the hitchhiker vanished from my passenger seat, I sat at the mountain scenic pullout and counted things. [18] Counting is what I do; the county calls me the bridge-song fiddler, but my truer trade is being dynamically observant. [38] I count wind shifts, gravel ticks, the seconds between a lie and a swallow. [52] He had been solid enough to fog the window, and then he was a smell of iodine and nothing. [71] On the seat lay a flake of wet-plate collodion, a curl of silvered skin peeled from some old glass negative. [91] Held against the valley light it showed a face, and the face was my brother Ives. [107] Ives had been granted pixelated amnesty eleven years ago, which is the kindest cruelty our state ever invented. [125] The program forgave the bridge strikers by scrubbing them out of every archive, every frame, every ledger, until their faces were only soft gray squares. [150] Amnesty meant unpunished; it also meant unproven, unfound, and officially never. [161] Mother died still saying that word, never, as if it were a stone she could not spit out. [179] I have spent every summer since trying to sculpt possibility from the ashes of that never. [195] That is why I play the new bridge each dusk, standing where the old span fell, sawing the songs Ives taught me. [217] Collodion cannot be pixelated. [221] Glass predates the machine that forgives by forgetting, and so a flake of it can hold a man the law has unmade. [243] I drove to the bridge with the flake in my shirt pocket, laughing in a way that frightened me a little. [264] The bridge wore its repair scaffolding like a bride, sheathed in orange scaffold wrap that breathed with the canyon draft. [284] I had watched that wrap flutter for three weeks and assumed it was only weather. [299] But observing is a verb, and it must keep revising itself while the evidence is still moving. [316] That night the flutters came in sets: three short snaps, a long belly-swell, two snaps, a pause. [333] It was Ives's old picket rhythm, the one the strikers drummed on the girders, and no wind invents that. [352] Someone had tied the wrap in patterned slack, loose here, taut there, so the draft would play it like a card reader. [374] A secret mapped by scaffold wrap flutters, readable only by someone who stood in one place long enough to be bored. [395] I copied the sets into my rosin notebook and translated them with the fingering chart Ives used for his cipher tunes. [416] They gave a mile marker, an hour, and a single word: recount. [428] The mile marker was the scenic pullout, and the hour was the one I had already wasted there, staring at empty upholstery. [450] So I went back the next evening, and this time I understood the instruction. [464] They did not want a rescue; they wanted a witness willing to recount them aloud. [479] The amnestied had no names left in any file, so a living voice was the only ledger available. [497] I set my case on the guardrail, propped the collodion flake against the mile marker, and began to play. [516] Between tunes I recounted everything: Ives on the cable, the strike, the eleven names, the fire, my mother's stone-hard word. [536] The pullout filled the way a held breath fills a chest. [547] Hitchhikers, one after another, thumbs out toward no traffic, faces still faintly squared at the edges like bad printing. [566] As I said each name, the squares softened into cheekbones, and I kept playing because I did not trust my hands to stop. [589] Ives came last, walking up from the drop where the old span went down, looking exactly eleven years unchanged. [608] I do not ask anyone to believe this, since I can hardly manage belief myself, and I was the one glowing in it. [631] He picked up the flake, turned it in the last light, and said the amnesty could be given back. [650] Refused, rather, the way a gift can be refused by whoever it was meant to erase. [666] It only required someone outside the archive to hold a record the machine could not blur. [682] Now I play the bridge each dusk with eleven witnesses on the rail, and the wrap answers in its patterned slack. [703] Tomorrow I recount them at the county hearing, and after that the ashes of never will have to make room. [723] Come to the pullout at dusk if you want it proven. [734] Bring glass. [736]
