The workshop stood on the ridge where topaz had grown out of the ground like frozen honey, and the walls held starlight the way a jar holds fireflies. [28] Inside, Vesna Mork set type between vanishing stars. [36] That was how she measured time now: not hours, but the gaps between one constellation losing a member and the next going dark. [59] I had climbed three days to reach her, carrying my sister's name in my mouth like a stone. [77] "You want the transposition," she said, and her voice was my father's voice, down to the dry click before consonants. [97] She borrowed personalities the way other people borrowed umbrellas, habitually and without apology, and she had clearly read him somewhere in the archive of everyone who had come up the ridge before me. [130] "I want to feel what Ilya felt," I said. [139] "Not to remember her -- to feel her." [146] Vesna crossed to the press, an iron animal squatting under the topaz roof, and lifted a slug of metal that was not metal. [169] It was a halo fragment, curved, thin, warm as a held hand, cut from the corona of the first star that ever went out. [193] "It prints the only ink that crosses," she said, in a voice that was now a child's. [210] "Crosses what?" [212] "The gap between the inside of you and the inside of anyone." [224] Her broadsides were famous in the valleys below for being unreadable in a specific way. [239] Every line could be parsed two ways, three ways, and the reader's mind, hunting for the true one, would slip its moorings. [261] Through deliberate ambiguity she would disorient you just long enough for someone else's feeling to enter the vacancy. [279] That was transposed empathy: not sympathy, not pity, but another person's grief installed in your chest at full pressure, wearing your ribs. [301] She did it for arithmetic. [306] "Everyone who comes here wants a ghost," she said, borrowing a miner's flat cadence. [320] "I want the equation -- sorrow has a rate, love has a slope." [332] "I have been trying to understand the mathematics of emotion for forty years, and every borrowed self is a data point, and every stranger's dead sister is a term I have not yet solved for." [419] The topaz walls went the color of a struck match. [429] She handed me the sheet, and I read: "What she carried, you never had to." [444] Accusation, or absolution, or simple fact -- the sentence would not hold still, and in the wobble of it I lost my footing in myself. [468] Then Ilya came in. [472] Not her face; her weather. [477] I felt the long flat patience of her last winter, the way she had loved me without expecting me to notice, the small arithmetic she did each morning deciding whether to be alive. [510] It was lucid, and it was turbulence, and both at once: I could see every edge of the feeling while it threw me around the room. [536] I understood, with terrible clarity, that she had never once considered me a burden, and that I had spent four years computing the opposite. [576] Vesna was writing fast, and for the first time she spoke in no one's voice but a raw, unpracticed one, thin as a wire. [600] "Her constant," she said. [604] "I have it, same as the others -- every grief resolves to the same number." [618] "Which is?" [620] She laughed, and it was almost her own laugh. [629] "One." [630] "It always comes out one." [659] Going down the mountain at dawn I carried nothing but the broadside, and I could no longer tell whether the sentence on it accused me or forgave me. [687] That, I understood at last, was the mercy in her ambiguity: she gave you the feeling and let you keep the meaning. [709] Behind me the topaz workshop burned quietly with the last of the light, and Vesna Mork set type for the next climber, borrowing my voice already, practicing my grief until it fit. [741]
