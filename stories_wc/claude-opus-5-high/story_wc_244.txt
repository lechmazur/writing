The station at Halloway Halt died when the last timetable did, and the ivy took the rest. [17] We who use it now keep no clocks; we measure time in the only unit that still runs on schedule, which is when the story changes. [43] A rumor leaves the Halt as one thing and comes back as another, and in that gap the work gets done. [64] Odile Brasa arrived on the ninth change, wearing a courier's coat and carrying a canvas bag that clinked like a collection plate. [86] She was the logistician everyone had heard of and nobody had met: the woman who moved ten thousand blankets into the flood zone without signing a single form. [114] "Please don't stand," she said, though nobody had, "I'm allergic to ceremony and mildly to ivy." [130] That was how she talked, desperately jocular, a joke cracked like a knuckle every time silence threatened. [147] Under the platform canopy, where the ivy has pried the tiles apart and made a green ceiling of its own, she laid out her problem. [172] The disaster-relief convoys had been impounded at the border by men with clipboards and excellent manners. [188] Fourteen villages in the ash belt needed insulin, water tablets, and roof felt before the rains. [204] Paperwork could not move them; a rumor could. [212] "Official channels are a lovely idea," she said, "like unicorns, or punctual trains." [225] So we did what the Halt does, which is polite anarchy: no leaders, no ledgers, and everybody says thank you. [245] The one law we keep is that we are bound by reciprocity. [257] Take a favor, owe a favor; give a favor, and somewhere down the line it comes back wearing a stranger's face. [278] Odile understood the law better than any of us, because she had built her whole career inside it. [296] She worked through improbable rumor chains: a lie told to a taxi dispatcher, repeated by a hairdresser, overheard by a customs clerk's cousin, until the truck gates simply opened because everyone believed they already had. [331] To catalyze such a chain you need a seed absurd enough to be repeated and true enough to survive retelling. [351] She reached into the canvas bag and set the seed on the ticket counter. [365] It was a fragment of moon rock, grey as wet cement, pitted, no bigger than a walnut, sealed in a cracked acrylic case stamped with a mission number. [393] A retired flight surgeon in Tashkent had given it to her after she trucked oxygen to his clinic, and she had never found a debt large enough to repay it with. [424] "Tell them the border commander's brother wants to buy it," she said, "and that the sale happens Thursday, and that nobody is to mention it." [449] Secrecy, she explained, is the best fertilizer a rumor ever had. [460] We seeded it in nine directions before dusk. [468] By the second change, the moon rock was a meteorite; by the fourth, a stolen relic; by the seventh, proof that the impounded convoys were carrying something the commander did not want inspected. [501] On the eighth change the gates opened, and fourteen villages got their roof felt eleven days before the rains. [520] I found Odile that night on platform two, sitting among the ivy with the empty acrylic case in her lap, not laughing. [542] The rock had gone down the chain as collateral and would not come back. [556] I asked why she never stayed anywhere, and she gave me the joke first, something about being allergic to furniture. [576] Then she told me the truth: she had spent twenty years trying to find the cure for loneliness, and had decided it could not be a person, because people leave, and it could not be a place, because places flood. [616] It had to be a debt, she said: a web of obligations so wide that no matter where she woke, somebody owed her a cup of tea and she owed somebody a favor. [649] That was why she was elusive and never signed her name; a signature makes you a person to be thanked, and a thank-you closes the account. [675] She left before the next change, and the Halt's ivy closed over her footprints by morning. [691] But eleven changes later a parcel came up from the ash belt, addressed to nobody, containing a fragment of moon rock in a new case and a note that said only: your turn. [724] So I am telling you, and you will tell someone else, and the story will change. [740] That is how she stays, and how none of us are alone. [752]
