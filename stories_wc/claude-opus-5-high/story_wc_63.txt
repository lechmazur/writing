The ridge was obsidian, and twice a day the sea took it back. [13] Mirren Talvas wound the clock at the ridge's crown at four minutes past five, as he had for thirty-one years, because the town below set its nets and its funerals by him. [46] He was precise the way other men were devout. [55] One winter he had been precise about the wrong thing, and nine people had drowned. [70] The tide table had been correct; his warning bell had been two minutes late; the flats had filled with black water while the crab-pickers argued about whose turn it was to carry the baskets. [104] Two minutes, he had learned, was a distance you could not walk back. [117]

Afterward, they did not hang him or exile him, which was worse. [129] They let him keep winding. [134] So he made his own sentence, and he made it out of a broken rake handle he pulled from the mud where Sela Drun had dropped it. [161] Along its split length he carved tiny inscriptions, no taller than a grain of barley: nine names, and beside each name a ledger of debts he intended to pay. [190] Firewood for Sela's widower. [194] A roof patched for the Onnas. [200] School fees, one term at a time, for the boy who had lost a mother and a name for his grief. [221] He scratched a notch when a debt was met, and the handle filled slowly, the way a shoreline fills. [240] He did not call it forgiveness. [246] He called it arithmetic. [250]

The tower's pendulum bob was a disc of polished brass the size of a dinner plate, and Mirren had learned its language the way a shepherd learns weather. [278] Salt air thickened it; cold shortened its stride; on spring tides the ridge itself flexed under the hammering sea and the beat went shy by a hair. [305] A hair was two minutes, given enough days. [313] So he was ethically diligent about the hair. [321]

What no one knew was what happened to him at the top of the swing. [336] There is a pause there, an instant when the bob has stopped rising and has not yet consented to fall, and in that pause Mirren went somewhere. [363] It had begun the night of the drowning and never stopped. [374] In that suspended sliver the tower would go white-hot behind his eyes, and the flats would emerge from the dark exactly as they had been: the black water rising, the baskets floating, Sela Drun turning toward the tower with her hand raised, waiting for a bell that was still two minutes away. [426] A searing reverie, over in less than a heartbeat, and he endured it four times an hour while he worked. [446] He had decided, long ago, not to look away from it. [457] That was part of the arithmetic too. [464]

On the night of the highest tide in thirty years, the wind came off the water like a thrown door, and the ridge hummed. [488] Mirren climbed with the rake handle under his arm, as always, and found the suspension spring split down its centre, the brass bob swinging crooked, shaving seconds off every arc. [518] The warning bell was rigged to the hour train. [527] If the clock fell behind, the bell would ring late, and below him ninety people were still on the flats gathering storm wrack. [550] He had no brace, no spare steel, nothing in the loft but rope and rust and one seasoned length of ash covered in nine names. [575] He did not deliberate long. [580] He split the handle down its grain, lashed the halves either side of the broken spring, and cinched them until the bob hung true again. [605] The lashing bit into the carvings; the swinging rubbed them; by morning the names would be smooth wood. [623] The bell rang at the right minute. [630] The flats emptied. [633] Nobody drowned. [635]

At the top of the next swing, in the pause, the flats came up out of the dark again -- but the water was going out, and the baskets were on shoulders, and Sela Drun's raised hand was only a hand, waving. [676] Then the bob consented to fall, and the tower was only a tower. [689]

He kept the worn handle on the wall beside the winding key, unreadable now, which struck him as correct. [708] Absolution, he had always suspected, was not granted in a single stroke; it came the way a clock keeps time -- one small, faithful increment at a time, a debt at a time, a tide at a time, until the shared balance a man had broken was quietly, unremarkably restored. [757] He wound the clock at four minutes past five. [766]
