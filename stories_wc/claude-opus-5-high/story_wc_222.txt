They said the spire could only be robbed when the sky was wrong, so Verity Sable waited for the night the stars slid sideways. [24] It came in midwinter, when the aurora unbraided itself and hung wrong-way over the cloud sea, green where green had no business being. [47] On such nights the wards of the icebound spire grew confused, unable to tell hour from hour, and a patient climber could pass. [70] Verity climbed with her old master's pocket sundial lid between her teeth, its brass warm as a coal. [88] He had been the one to call her an apprentice of wonder, back when she was a starving pickpocket who stole a telescope instead of a purse. [115] The lid was all that remained of him, a hinged brass disc with no dial beneath, engraved inside: when the sky is wrong, trust your hands. [141] A sundial is a useless instrument above the clouds, which was exactly his kind of joke: sacredly playful, he called it, the habit of treating holy things as toys and toys as holy. [174] The Hourwrights of the spire had no such habit. [183] They kept their certainties in a chess set of hand-carved whalebone, each piece hollow, each hollow holding a folded secret that the Order had decided was true. [210] What the pieces said, the world below believed; what the pieces omitted, the world below forgot. [226] Twelve years ago a white bishop had been carved to say that Corin Sable died a heretic, alone, raving, having invented his discoveries out of fever. [252] Verity had come to amend it. [258] Not to steal, exactly. [262] She was a thief of a particular sort, the chivalrous kind: she never took without leaving, and what she left was always worth more than what she carried away. [291] In her coat she had three bishops of her own, carved through a winter of numb fingers, hollowed and filled. [311] The archive room sat at the spire's crown, open to the air on four sides, frost growing in ferns across the board. [333] Below her the cloud sea took the wrong-colored light and passed it around like a rumor. [349] Verity worked with cautious abandon, which was the only way she knew how to work: gloves off in killing cold, heart wide open, hands precise. [374] She lifted the white bishop and set her own in its square, matching the frost pattern, matching the weight to the grain of a hair. [399] Inside hers she had folded the truth: her father's real notes, his real death, his real last sentence. [417] Then she hesitated over the black queen. [424] The Hourwrights' greatest secret was said to live there, and it was said to be the reason the sky went wrong. [445] A cautious thief would leave it alone. [452] An apprentice of wonder would not. [458] She opened the queen and read three lines by aurora light, and understood that the Order had been quietly stopping the world's clocks for four hundred years, buying calm with borrowed hours. [490] The debt was coming due, and nobody below had been told, because nobody below had asked. [506] Footsteps rang on the stair, unhurried, certain. [513] Verity had perhaps forty seconds and two choices. [521] She could flee with the secret and be hunted, or she could do what her master had taught her, which was to hand the unknown back to the world with open arms. [553] She copied the three lines onto the inside of the sundial lid with her needle, scratching over the engraving, ruining the only thing she loved. [578] Then she put the empty queen back in her place, because a chivalrous thief leaves the board playable. [596] The Archivist reached the crown as Verity climbed onto the parapet. [607] He was old, and he looked at the board, and he saw nothing wrong, and that was the trouble with certainties: they do not check themselves. [633] 'You've taken something,' he said. [638] 'I've left something,' she said, and stepped off into the cloud, silk unfurling from her pack. [654] She fell a long time through white that tasted of iron. [665] In the valley towns they would find the lid in a miller's letterbox, then in a magistrate's ledger, then in a schoolroom, passed hand to hand, unhinged and unhidden. [694] It told no hours; it only told what was coming, and asked what people meant to do about it. [713] Verity went back up twice more that winter, whenever the sky went wrong, swapping bishops and rooks and pawns, amending the record one hollow at a time. [740] She was frightened every time, and she laughed every time, and she never once considered stopping. [756]
