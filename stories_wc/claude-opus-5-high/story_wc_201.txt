The anesthesia let go of me at 4:11 in the morning, and my body arrived before I did. [18] That is what the surgeons had promised: I would be proprioceptively enhanced, a lattice of sensors threaded along my tendons, so that I would always know exactly where my limbs were in space. [51] They meant it as a kindness for a woman who had spent nine months unable to tell up from down. [71] I wanted it as an instrument. [77] Enhanced interrogation is the official phrase; I survived it, which is the only credential anyone asks about now. [95] Before the cells I was a mathematician, and I have gone back to being one, because I am trying to find the mathematics of mercy. [120] Not the poetry of it. [125] The function. [127] A curve with a hard ceiling, a proof that a state can present in court: beyond this value, no question is worth its answer. [151] The problem is that I cannot remember the nine months. [161] Not in pictures. [164] My memory of that building lives in my spine, and it speaks only when something startles it. [181] So I have become an archivist of my own flinches, remembering through trauma responses, letting the body testify where the mind has been redacted. [205] The sensors give me numbers for what used to be only dread: forty-one degrees of shoulder rotation, eleven millimeters of jaw retreat, the exact angle at which I brace. [234] Bracing is the honest word. [239] My body braces, and in the shape of the bracing is a record of what it is bracing against. [258] I carry one trigger with me deliberately, in a paper envelope: a pewter mask strap, cold, scuffed, stamped with an inventory number. [280] It held the hood to my face. [287] The tribunal returned it to me as property, which is the funniest sentence in my file. [303] When I touch the strap, my left hand rises to my sternum along a path so consistent that I can plot it, and each plot is a coordinate in the dataset. [334] Six days after the anesthesia wore off, I took the dataset into the ultraviolet forest. [349] It grows outside the capital where the old detention annex stood, engineered glasswood that drinks ultraviolet light and gives it back at night as a low violet fog in the canopy. [380] The state calls the place ultraviolet penance. [387] Men and women who worked the annex come here by sentence, not by choice, and stand barefoot in the groves while the trees read their skin. [413] Ultraviolet writes on a body: freckles, burns, the slow dark bloom of everything they did, surfacing where anyone can see it. [434] It is elegant, and it is revenge dressed as botany, and I came anyway, because the last variable I needed was not in me. [458] I found him in the fourth grove, a narrow man with a sunburn like a map, and my hand went to my sternum before I had looked at his face. [488] Thirty-nine degrees. [490] Eleven millimeters. [492] My body had identified him from a dataset my mind could not open, and I stood in the violet fog with my own testimony ticking through my tendons. [520] I had the strap in my pocket and the arithmetic in my head: how much light he owed, by their formula, which was mine reflected and reversed. [547] That was the thing I understood standing there, rooted and displaced at once, a woman with sensors instead of pictures, home in a forest built on the slab of the room that unmade her. [581] Their ceiling was infinite. [585] Mine could not be. [589] A function with no upper bound is not justice; it is the same machine wearing a leaf. [606] I gave him the strap. [611] He held it the way you hold a tool you have used, and he began to shake, and his shaking had an angle too, and I measured it, because that was what I had come for. [647] Mercy, I wrote that night, is the smallest number that stops the shaking in both bodies. [663] It is not zero. [667] It is finite, and it is provable, and I have the coordinates. [679] I petitioned to close the groves. [685] They gave me a hearing in the spring. [693] I will stand when they call my name, and my body will brace, and this time I will know precisely what the bracing means. [717]
