The arbor stood above the clouds, forty grafted pines wired together on the ridge, and Ilse climbed it every night at eleven. [22] Below her the cloud sea lay flat and silver, and above it nothing moved but aircraft and stars. [40] She had been deaf for nine years, since the avalanche took the old station and her brother Tobias with it, and so the whole world arrived to her as a soundless echo: pressure, tremor, the cold shiver of wire under a fingertip. [82] The valley called her the midnight pattern seeker, half joking, because she counted lights. [96] It was not a joke to her. [102] Weather balloons, mail planes, the long freight flights out of Kastel, each had a rhythm, and rhythm was the only language the mountain still spoke to her. [129] She was attentively inventive, and her instrument proved it: a copper diaphragm in the crown of the tallest pine, geared to a rod that ran down the trunk to a brass plate she pressed with her palms. [166] Static came through it as sand; speech came as pebbles; and when the static cleared, the plate went smooth and she knew the channel was open. [192] On her wrist she wore a parachute buckle, scorched on one edge, the only piece of Tobias the mountain had given back. [214] He had jumped into the whiteout to reach the village with a warning, and the village had trusted his word and lived. [236] That was the burden he had handed her along with the buckle: somebody must watch, and whatever the watcher says, three hundred people will do. [261] At 11:40 that night the sand stopped. [268] After the radio static cleared, the pebbles came fast, and her palms read them the way her feet read the ladder rungs, without deciding anything: MEASURES CONFIRMED. SNOWPACK CRITICAL SECTOR NINE. EVACUATE EAST TERRACES BEFORE DAWN. [304] Then, eleven seconds later, a correction: SECTOR NINE READING SUSPECT. INSTRUMENT FAULT POSSIBLE. HOLD. [318] Then sand again, and the channel was gone for the night. [329] Ilse sat in the crown of the pine with the cold pressing her collar, and did the arithmetic of trust. [349] If she rang the bell line and the slope held, the terraces would come back in the morning cold, angry, and next time slower. [373] If she stayed silent and the slope moved, there would be nobody left to be angry. [389] Both mistakes spent the same coin, and she had only the one. [401] So she went looking for the pattern instead. [409] She had watched sector nine for nine years. [417] Warm fog rising off the east terraces always bent the cloud sea into a long comb; tonight the comb was missing, and the silver below sector nine sagged inward like cloth over an open drawer. [452] Warm air was escaping somewhere it should not. [460] The instrument at the station was not faulty; the snow was. [471] She reached for the bell line, and stopped, because the words mattered as much as the warning. [488] The standing code she and the valley had agreed on had three calls: ALL CLEAR, WATCH, and GO. [506] GO had been rung twice in nine years, and both times the mountain had obliged, which was the only reason anyone still believed her. [530] Tonight she had a suspect reading and a shape in the clouds, and if she spent GO on a guess and the slope held, she would have nothing left to spend the next time. [564] So she revised the code. [569] She rang WATCH, then, on the lamp mast, she keyed the cause in plain letters, her hands doing it from muscle memory, the way Tobias had drilled her on the kitchen table years before her ears went: NO COMB OVER NINE. WARM AIR LEAKING. TERRACES DECIDE. [615] It cost her forty minutes and a torn glove, and it gave them her eyes instead of her authority. [634] Lanterns woke along the east terraces, one, then nine, then too many to count, moving downhill. [650] At 4:20 the slope let go above the empty houses, and Ilse felt it through the pine, through the rod, through the brass plate: a long soundless echo climbing the trunk into her palms. [684] She held the parachute buckle against the plate until the tremor stopped. [696] In the morning the valley sent up a boy with bread and a new pair of gloves and a slate that said: WE READ YOU. SEND THE REASON NEXT TIME TOO. [727] Ilse climbed back up the arbor that night at eleven, as she would the night after, and began revising the code again, adding a fourth call for the things she could see but could not yet prove. [764] Somebody had to watch; the least she could do was let them see what she saw. [780]
