The opal library stood above the clouds, and no one had climbed to it in ninety years. [17] Ilva kept it anyway, gently systematic, the way some people keep promises. [29] She was the glass-orchid caretaker, the last one, and her charge was simple: to preserve the sound of forgotten names. [49] Her rounds were not measured in hours but across the museum of extinct feelings, gallery by gallery, from the hall of Awe-Before-Machines to the little alcove of Homesickness for Places One Has Never Seen. [83] By the time she reached the glass orchids, a full museum had passed beneath her lantern, and that was how she told time. [106]

The orchids were the last of the deserted marvels, and the most demanding. [119] They were grown, centuries ago, to hold names. [127] Each petal was a lens of spun silica, each throat a hollow of resonant crystal, and inside every one slept the sound of a person whose descendants had stopped saying it aloud. [159] Ilva could not read them; she could only light them. [169] And the orchids refused fire, refused oil, refused the cold electric filaments the last librarians had tried. [186] They kindled only by the echo of a long-forgotten song. [196] Sing the tune into the vault of the ceiling, wait for it to come back thinned and strange, and the returning sound would set the petals glowing from within, like frost catching dawn. [229] Then, for an hour, the names spoke themselves. [237]

Ilva had learned the song from her predecessor, Emeru, who had learned it from his, and so on back to whoever first decided that forgetting was a kind of avoidable weather. [268] But the song had eleven notes, and Ilva could carry only ten. [280] The eleventh had never lived in a human throat. [289] It lived in a wind chime that hung at the library's eastern arch: ten bamboo tubes, tuned by hand, and the longest cracked straight down its grain. [316] Ilva had cracked it herself, at nineteen, slamming the shutter against a storm she had been warned about. [334] Emeru had said nothing, only touched the split with one finger, and that silence had been growing in her for forty years like foxglove in a wall - tall, purple, lovely, and bad for the heart. [369]

Since the crack, one orchid had never lit. [377] Nine hundred and six bloomed each night; the nine hundred and seventh stayed dark, and the name inside it stayed unsaid. [398] That was the whole of her regret, and she carried it politely. [410]

On the night the clouds came up through the floor, Ilva took down the chime. [425] She had spent four decades trying to replace the tube: new bamboo, seasoned bamboo, copper, bone. [441] Nothing matched, because the note had never been the point. [451] She understood it at last the way one understands weather - all at once, too late to dress for it. [470] The orchid did not want the eleventh note. [478] It wanted the eleventh sound. [483]

So she hung the cracked tube back up, alone, and struck it. [495] It gave the flawed, buzzing, split-throated tone it had given for forty years - the sound of a girl's carelessness, preserved. [515] Then she sang her ten notes into the opal dome and let the crack's ugly eleventh ride home on the echo with them. [538]

The nine hundred and seventh orchid filled with light like a lamp remembering it was a lamp. [555] It said: Emeru. [558] He had put it there himself, she realized, on the day he touched the split and said nothing: the one name in the collection that could only be freed by someone who had stopped apologizing for the sound she made. [598]

Ilva sat down among the deserted marvels while nine hundred and seven names spoke themselves into an empty room above the clouds. [620] No one heard them but her, which had always been enough, and had never once been enough. [637]

In the morning she began her rounds again, gallery by gallery, gently systematic, dusting the cases of feelings no living person could still perform. [661] She stopped at the alcove reserved for foxglove regret, opened the case, and found she had nothing left to put in it. [683] She left it open anyway, in case some climber ever came, and wrote a card for the one beside it: This crack is part of the instrument. [710] Then she climbed to the eastern arch, where the chime hung buzzing in the high thin wind, and she taught herself, note by broken note, to call it music. [739]
