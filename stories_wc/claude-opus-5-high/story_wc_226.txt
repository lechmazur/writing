Three nights before the ritual, Vessa hauled her last crate of salt-glass into the Canyon of Endless Echoes. [18] The towers of bone stood around her like the ribs of something that had died mid-prayer. [34] Each one hummed with the forged hush the echo-priests kept there, a silence hammered thin and laid over the stone like leaf metal. [57] Speak once in the canyon and it answered a thousand times; speak twice and the priests cut your tongue for greed. [78] At the canyon mouth, where morning fog pooled, her work waited: a pale spire in mist, twelve feet of fused salt and window glass, faceted like a wasp's eye. [107] The priests had blessed it, believing it a monument to her dead brother. [120] It was a machine. [124] The Rite of Absolution asked one word of her. [133] Stand before Halm, the ferryman whose carelessness had drowned Teo, say forgiven, and let the echo carry it up the canyon until the debt dissolved. [158] That was the price of forgiveness here: the word went out of you and took your grief with it. [177] Afterward the forgiver could not remember the face of the one they had lost. [191] Clean, the priests called it. [196] Vessa called it theft, and said so loudly enough that two towers repeated her for an hour. [213] She did not intend to refuse Halm. [220] She had watched him age ten years in one season, and she wanted him free. [235] But she would not pay with Teo's face. [243] So she had built the spire to refract a single truth into many, the way her brother's old prism had thrown one candle into nine colors across their mother's floor. [273] One word in; many words out; the debt paid in full and the grief kept. [288] For that, the canyon had to be taught to hold more than one voice at a time. [305] She taught it through impromptu poetry sessions. [312] Every dusk for a month she gathered the canyon's exiles, salt-cutters and bone-menders, at the spire's base and made them speak over one another. [336] No one wrote anything down. [341] A woman would offer a line about her drowned mule, and a boy would answer it with a lie about the moon, and Vessa would push the next line out of somebody's mouth before they had time to be afraid of it. [383] The towers, overloaded, learned to braid. [389] Her only real apprentice was Bee, nine years old, innocently mischievous, who stole glass chips to trade for figs and once relocated an entire facet because she thought the spire looked lopsided. [421] Vessa had shouted for a full minute before she saw that Bee's angle was better than hers. [438] The girl had no idea what she had corrected. [447] On the morning of the rite, mist filled the canyon to the third rib. [461] Halm stood barefoot in the ash circle with his hands open. [472] The priests raised the forged hush until Vessa could hear the blood in her own ears. [488] Bee crouched at the spire, thumb on a hinge of bone, waiting for the nod she thought was a game. [508] Vessa said the word. [512] Forgiven. [513] Bee turned the spire into the risen sun. [521] The word struck the glass and came apart. [529] It went up the canyon as nine voices, none of them the priests' and all of them hers: forgiven, and he was a coward, and he was my friend, and Teo laughed like a gate hinge, and I am not finished, and forgiven, forgiven, forgiven. [574] The towers answered every line at once, and the braid held. [585] Halm fell to his knees, absolved; the debt had been paid nine times over. [599] But the canyon still took its price, and it took it from the only one who had spoken. [617] Vessa kept her brother's face, every freckle of it, and lost her voice into the stone. [633] She never spoke again. [637] She did not consider it a bad trade. [645] The priests wanted the spire broken. [651] Bee got there first and hid the best facets under her bed. [663] By the next winter there were four spires in the mist, and the exiles held their poetry sessions without being asked, and the hush in the canyon was no longer forged by anyone but the people who lived in it. [703] Vessa sat at the base of the first one each dawn, writing lines she could not say, and Bee read them aloud badly, on purpose, to make her teacher laugh without sound. [735] The towers repeated the laughter anyway. [741]
