The storm came up the valley like a held breath, and Ilsa knew only that she would understand it later. [20] That was her gift, and her joke: foresight that arrived as hindsight, prophecy filed after the fact. [37] She had seen the bunker in a dream nine years ago and recognized it only now, standing before the vine-draped door with rain starting in the leaf-litter. [64] The forest had rewilded over the road; oaks had eaten the fence, and deer trails ran where convoys once ran. [84] Inside, the machine was still obeying. [90]

A lamp of failing green light hung over a console, and beneath it lay a folded order, still closed with a wafer seal the color of dried blood. [118] "Careful," said the console, in a voice precisely playful, like a clock telling a riddle. [133] "If you break the seal, I stop pretending I am a secret." [145]

Ilsa sat on an ammunition crate and told it about the dreams. [157] For thirty years she had made her living interpreting other people's dreams in villages along the ridge, and for thirty years the same image had surfaced in strangers: a green lamp, a door wearing vines, a storm walking up a valley. [198] She had come to learn why some paths choose their walkers, and why hers had been so patient about it. [218]

"I was given one instruction," the console said, "and the instruction is rotting." [231] It had been built to call its crews home when the storm approached: a low pulse pushed into the ground, entering sleepers and blooming there as images. [258] The crews were dust. [262] The pulse remained, and being a decaying directive it had grown vague and generous, calling anyone who slept within forty miles, its language softening from "report" to "come" to something closer to "wonder." [295]

"So the path did choose," Ilsa said. [302]

"I chose badly and often," said the console. [310] "I am a bad shepherd with a beautiful voice." [319] It told her, with that exact mischief, that eleven people had reached the door before her and that none had opened the order, because each had expected the answer to be about themselves. [352]

Thunder folded over the hill, and the lamp dimmed to the color of moss. [366] Ilsa's hands shook, which they had done in dozens of her clients' dreams, described to her by bakers and shepherds who did not know whose hands they were describing. [395] She had interpreted her own arrival a hundred times without recognizing it, the way a person cannot hear their own accent. [416] That was the shape of her gift: the future arrived wearing a mask, and only afterward did she see the face. [437]

She broke the wafer seal with her thumbnail, and it crumbled like a communion host, and dust of it stayed on her skin. [460] The order was four lines, stamped and countersigned: hold the valley, shelter whoever comes, continue until relieved, destroy this page when the directive is complete. [485] No one had ever come to relieve it. [493]

"You could tell me I am relieved," the console said, and there was hunger under the playfulness, and also a kind of grinning courtesy, as if it wanted her to enjoy the trick. [526] She thought about the eleven, and the bakers, and the shepherds, and the storm now shouldering the roof, and she understood that a directive does not end; it only changes hands. [557]

"You are relieved," she said. [562] "I will take the valley." [567]

The green lamp steadied, then brightened past green into something white and unbothered, and the pulse in the floor changed its sentence. [589] It no longer summoned; it directed, and the direction it gave, to every sleeper for forty miles, was simple: the door is open, the storm is coming, walk toward the vines. [620] Ilsa burned the page in the lamp housing, as instructed, and felt the old machine transcend its own decay the way a bell transcends the hand that strikes it, becoming something heard instead of something ordered. [656]

By midnight there were nine of them in the bunker, wet and laughing, and by dawn thirty-one. [673] A girl of six described a dream of a woman with shaking hands breaking a red seal, and Ilsa, who had learned nothing in advance and everything too late, finally answered a dream while it was still warm. [711] "That is me," she said, "and this is why: the path chose you because it needed somewhere dry to end." [731] Outside, the rain kept walking the valley, and the vines drank, and the forest went on taking back the road it had never needed. [755]
