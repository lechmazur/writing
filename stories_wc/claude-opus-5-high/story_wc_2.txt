The clocks Mira mended never kept time, and that was the point. [12] She was the city's broken-clock tinkerer, hired by people who wanted to be surprised by an hour. [29] Her own workshop held forty of them, each stuttering a different wrongness, because Mira was uncertainly certain that the future could be practiced. [52] Ten years ago the harbor district had burned, and she had survived only because a stopped clock made her late to the lecture hall that collapsed. [78] Since then she had built her life out of schedules, and hated herself for it. [93] A plan, she had learned, is a promise that the world has not agreed to. [108] So she went digging beneath the ashes. [115]

The old Admiralty archive still lay under the burn scar, and in its cellar, under a crust of soot and slumped glass, Mira found the admiral's lost compass rose. [144] It was a brass disc the width of a dinner plate, thirty-two points, each engraved not with a direction but with an instruction, and the instruction at true north was Improvise. [175] The needle had rusted away. [180]

Admiral Verre had sailed by it for forty years, and the Guild of Chartwrights had called him a lunatic until every one of his voyages came home. [207] His logbooks said the rose only spoke when coincidence becomes pattern, that is, when the same small accident happens three times and the world tips its hand. [234]

Mira, who wanted above all things to befriend uncertainty in planning, began keeping a ledger of accidents. [251] Three times that autumn, a dry laurel leaf blew into her workshop and settled on the compass rose. [269] Three times she traced the draft to the same cracked pane, three streets away, in the neglected greenhouse behind the burned botanical school. [292] That was pattern enough. [296]

The greenhouse was a ruin of laurel and fig gone feral, and the wind came up through a gap in the floor tiles, so that the whole room breathed. [325] Mira set the rose on the tiles and watched, because Verre's method had been exactly this: read the instruction through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse, where the air had nothing to do but tell the truth. [363] Leaves gathered, spun, and held; and they lay banked against one engraved word, Descend. [377]

Beneath the tiles was the gap, and beneath the gap, a stair. [389] She had not planned for a stair, and so she went down it with a lantern and no schedule at all. [410]

The sound arrived before the sight: a subterranean waterfall, falling sixty feet out of the city's old aqueduct into a black pool that turned slowly, like a gear with all its teeth filed off. [444] On a ledge above the spray sat Admiral Verre's last instrument, an orrery of the harbor currents, and beside it his skeleton, and beside that a slate on which he had written the sentence Mira had come looking for. [483] The slate said: The rose has no needle because you are the needle. [496] Mira laughed until the waterfall drowned it. [503]

Then the lantern showed her what Verre had come down here to watch: the aqueduct wall was seamed with cracks, weeping, bulging where the old fire had baked the mortar to ash. [535] The harbor district had burned once; when that wall let go, it would drown. [549] She had four hours, no engineers, no plan, and a satchel of clock parts. [563] So she improvised. [566] She wedged escapement springs into the weeping seams to gauge which cracks were moving, built a float from her lantern tin and a mainspring so it would ring a bell when the pool rose an inch, and ran the bell wire up the stair and tied it to the greenhouse door. [617]

By dawn the Guild of Chartwrights was standing in the laurel, listening to a clock-bell ring from under the city, believing a woman they had called a lunatic. [645] They shored the wall in nine days. [651]

Mira kept the compass rose on her bench, needleless, among forty wrong clocks. [664] People asked her how she had known, and she told them the truth, which was that she hadn't. [682] She had only learned to keep a ledger of accidents, to walk toward the third one, and to carry enough spare parts to be wrong gracefully. [708] Uncertainty, it turned out, was not an enemy of planning but the only honest material to plan with. [726] And every hour a broken clock on her bench struck a time that did not exist, and Mira looked up, ready to be surprised. [750]
