The door in the clock tower mechanism room existed only for the eleven seconds between 3:13 and 3:14, which was why the university's archivists had, for two centuries, politely agreed it did not. [33] Mira Halloway disagreed, warmly and methodically, with a clipboard. [42] She had been an adaptable archivist for eleven years, which in practice meant she could catalogue anything: seed ledgers, confiscated puppets, the grievances of retired deans. [68] Her specialty, though, was what the department called fables of absence: the stories that grow in the shape of a missing document, the way ivy keeps the outline of a fallen wall. [100]

The tower held the oldest of them. [107] In 1691 the Concord of Vessel Bay had been signed here, and then unsigned, and then argued about for three hundred years by people who had never read it, because no copy survived. [140] What survived were two incompatible traditions: the coastal one, which held the treaty had granted the fisheries; the inland one, which held it had taken them. [166] Both were true in the mouths that carried them, and both had fed a low, patient grudge that flared every generation into something worse. [190] Mira wanted, quite simply, to translate confusion into clarity. [199]

She had climbed the tower every night that month with a thermos and a notebook, mapping the mechanism room's contents while the great escapement ticked above her like a patient animal. [230] Brass gears the size of millwheels, dust thick as felt, and a coil of rope on an iron hook. [249] The rope was the ceremonial cord of the old Concord, three centuries of hemp gone the color of weak tea, braided in nine strands because the nine signatory towns had each contributed one. [282] Knotted into its throat, under a collar of tarnished wire, was a diplomat's wax seal impression: Alder Venn's crane-and-anchor, pressed into a disc of red wax no bigger than a plum. [313]

That was the first clarity: the treaty had not vanished, it had been encoded. [327] Venn had been a careful man in a burning season; he had written the terms into the braid itself, over-and-under, each crossing a clause, and sealed the pattern so no one could quietly re-braid it. [362] Someone had, anyway. [365] The wire collar was eighteenth-century work; the seal had been lifted and reset, badly, by a thumb in a hurry. [385] Two towns, Mira guessed, had each pulled the braid toward their own reading, and the rope had been left a compromise that said nothing at all. [411]

Which brought her to the door. [417] It appeared in the north wall at 3:13 on the ninth night, a low oak door with no hinges she could account for, and behind it a cupboard holding Venn's working papers: nine strand-diagrams, keyed to the crane-and-anchor. [455] She did not waste the eleven seconds on wonder; she took the papers and let the wall close. [473] Wonder could be catalogued later, in daylight, with citations. [482]

The work took six weeks. [487] She restored the treaty by reconfiguring the braids of the centuries-old ceremonial rope, undoing the 1740s compromise crossing by crossing and returning each strand to the position Venn's diagrams demanded. [517] It was slow, warm, exacting work, her hands learning what her eyes could not hold: nine strands, forty-one crossings, one grammar. [538]

The terms, when they emerged, were neither coastal nor inland. [548] The fisheries were to be held in common, and the nine towns were to merge their harbor courts into a single bench that sat at Vessel Bay each spring. [577] Neither tradition had been lying; each had remembered half of a sentence and spent three centuries shouting it. [595] Mira wrote it up plainly, with photographs of every crossing, so that the complexity stayed complex and became, at last, legible. [616]

She hung the rope back on its hook with the seal reset true. [629] On the ninth night after, she waited at 3:13 with her thermos, half hoping. [643] The north wall stayed a wall. [649] That, she decided, was correct: a door that shouldn't exist is only a fable of absence with hinges, and it opens exactly as long as a question does. [677] She finished her tea, noted the time, and went down to file. [689]
