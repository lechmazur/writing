The cement factory ran for ninety years, and for ninety years the rain over Calder Cliffs smelled of ash and lime. [21] After the factory shut down, Mirren Oake waited three seasons for the air to forget it. [37] She was a petrichor collector, which is a sensible profession practiced almost exclusively by eccentrics. [52] Sensibly eccentric was how she described herself on forms: galoshes to the knee, a ledger ruled in four colors, forty-one stoppered vials labeled by date, humidity, and stone. [80] Buyers in the cities paid well for bottled first rain, but Mirren had stopped collecting for buyers. [97] She collected because smell was the last sense nobody had managed to explain to her satisfaction, and she wanted to find the place where explanations fail. [123]

The compass came to her from a junk dealer in Ferrow, who sold it as scrap brass. [140] It was the lost expedition compass from the Halloway survey, the one that went into the cliffs in 1908 with eleven men and came back as a rumor. [168] Mirren knew this because she read it with her hands. [178] Psychometry reading was the other half of her trade, the half she never wrote on forms. [194] Held in a dark room with her thumbs on the hinge of the lid, an object would open. [212] She called these hinged awakenings: not visions, exactly, but a door swinging wide in a house she had never entered, letting out weather. [235]

The compass opened onto water. [240] Cold water under stone, a smell of rain that had never touched sky, and a man's voice saying, gently, that the needle was useless there. [265]

So she went to delve. [270] The limestone cliffs above the dead factory are riddled like bread, and the fissure she chose breathed cool air that fogged her lamp glass. [294] She went down sensibly: rope, spare carbide, three days of biscuit, a whistle, and a note on her kitchen table listing her likely routes. [318] She went down eccentrically too, with forty-one empty vials. [327]

For six hours the passage was only rubble and the sound of her own breathing. [342] Then the walls widened, and the noise of her boots stopped coming back to her. [357] The subterranean lagoon lay below her in a resonant hush, black and absolutely level, holding the lamp's reflection like a held breath. [379] Every drip from the ceiling arrived as a chord. [387] And the smell -- she stood on the shingle and simply wept, briefly, efficiently, then got out a vial. [405] It was petrichor with no rain in it, the scent of water meeting dust in a room where no storm had ever been announced. [429] Nothing in her ledger's four colors could account for it. [439]

She waded the shallow rim for an hour, and on the far ledge she found them. [455] Eleven sets of boots, arranged in a row, and eleven neatly folded coats. [468] No bones, no bodies, no sign of struggle or hunger: only the tidy leavings of people who had decided to stop needing things. [491]

She sat down among the boots and took the compass in both hands and let it open one last time. [511] Halloway came through the hinge so easily that she understood he had been waiting on the other side of it for a century, the way rain waits in a cloud. [541] The lagoon, he told her -- not in words, in the smell of it -- does not answer questions. [558] It dissolves the part of you that asks. [566] His men had walked into the water one by one, each one hinging open, each one staying open. [584] This is the place where explanations fail, he said, and she felt him lean toward the lagoon with terrible affection. [604] Come and see. [608]

Mirren was eccentric, but she was sensibly so. [616] She had come to find the place where explanations fail, and she had found it, and finding a thing is not the same as drowning in it. [643] She stoppered forty-one vials, wrote the date, the humidity, the stone. [654] She left the compass standing upright in a boot, where the next delver would find it. [670] Then she climbed, for two days, toward a world that had shut its factory and did not yet know what its rain smelled like. [694]

She sells the vials now, one a year, to whoever can stand it. [707] They open in the buyer's hand like a hinge, and afterward those people never ask her what it means. [726] They only stand very still, the way she does, listening for a hush that is still down there being resonant, with or without anyone to hear it. [753]
