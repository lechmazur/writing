Six weeks after receiving the donor heart, I climbed the rope stair to the floating rock tea house. [18] The valley below held its mist like a bowl holds soup, and the rock drifted slow above it, turning on some current I could not feel. [44] My new heart knocked at my ribs, a stranger asking to be let further in. [59] Inside, the watchful dye master was pinning skeins to a line, and she did not look up. [76] "You brought someone else's pulse into my shop," she said. [86] "I brought a question," I said. [92] The donor had been a woman named Tova, a bus driver on the switchback road, and in the three weeks since I learned her name I had met eleven people who told me stories about her. [128] Eleven people, unprompted, as if she had left deposits of herself all over the valley. [143] Nobody had ever told a story about me. [151] "I want to learn why some people magnetize stories," I said, "and others just take up space." [168] Then she looked up. [172] The dye master's name was Ferris, and she had dyed cloth on that rock for forty years, because the rock, she claimed, was the only place in the valley where light could be trusted. [206] She worked by tracking sun trails, the bright seams that slid across her floorboards as the rock revolved, and she had scratched marks into the wood for each hour and season. [237] By those marks she knew when to lift a skein from the vat, when a blue would hold and when it would break. [260] "Sit in the trail," she said, and I sat in the moving light while she poured tea the color of wet straw. [282] From a box of cedar she took a blue morpho wing, no bigger than her thumb, and held it in the seam of sun. [306] The blue came up so hard it seemed to illuminate her knuckles from beneath the skin. [322] "Grind it," she said, "and you get brown dust. [331] There is no blue in it at all. [339] The color is architecture, ridges stacked to throw light back at whoever looks." [352] She turned it, and the blue went black. [360] "In the forest that flicker is a warning," she said; "here, now gone, and no bird can hold the shape long enough to strike." [384] She called it iridescent caution, and said she had spent forty years trying to put it into cloth and had failed every time, because dye is a promise and iridescence is a refusal. [417] "What does that have to do with Tova?" I asked. [427] "She rode up here twice a month for nine years," Ferris said. [439] "I watched her the way I watch the light." [448] Tova had been quietly explosive, Ferris said, a woman who spoke rarely and then said the one thing that rearranged the room. [470] She never told stories about herself; she asked questions, and then she changed her angle, and people saw something new flash off her and had to tell someone. [498] "She was structure, not pigment," Ferris said. [505] "Stories do not stick to a color. [512] They stick to a turning." [517] The sun trail had crossed the floor and was climbing the wall toward the door. [532] Ferris rose to lift a skein from the vat at the mark, and when she pulled it out the yarn was the green of drowned leaves, and then, drying, it turned slowly blue. [565] I laughed out loud in the mist-light, and the borrowed heart in me hammered, and for the first time I did not apologize to it. [590] "You are watching yourself to see if you are worth a story," Ferris said. [604] "That is the wrong direction. [609] Turn outward and let the light do the work." [618] She gave me the morpho wing in a fold of paper and told me to hold it up whenever I forgot. [639] Going down the rope stair, I passed a boy coming up who asked what the tea house was like, and I stopped, and instead of answering I asked him what he was hoping for. [673] He talked for ten minutes. [678] Below us the valley breathed its white breath, and the rock turned, and the light moved on, and somewhere in it I was a flicker that a stranger might later try to describe. [711]
