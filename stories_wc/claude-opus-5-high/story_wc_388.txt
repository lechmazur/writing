After the liquidation, the only thing left standing in Sector Nine was the orchard. [14] Vesna Kadar arrived on foot, goggles fogged, boots gray with the dust that the storms kept lifting from the tailings. [34] She had spent eleven years debunking conspiracies for the Commission, and she had learned to distrust comfortable lies most of all, including her employer's. [58] Her job now was to excavate truth from them, even when the shovel struck her own foundations. [75] The official story was simple: the orchard survived because of engineered rootstock, patented and sold, and the sixty families who had farmed here were compensated and relocated. [102] The unofficial story involved a diamond. [108] Vesna had debunked cursed-stone rumors a dozen times. [116] This one, though, kept appearing in the margins of the liquidation records: the Kesler stone, forty carats, flawed by a feather-shaped fracture, rumored to poison whoever held title. [144] Three owners, three deaths, one bankrupt trust. [151]

The caretaker met her at the windbreak, an old woman named Maret with a respirator slung at her throat like a necklace. [173] "You are the one who proves nothing is wrong," Maret said. [184] "I prove what is wrong is ordinary," Vesna said. [193] "It is usually worse." [197]

The trees astonished her. [201] Apricots, pears, quince, heavy and unblemished, while outside the windbreak the radioactive dust storms scoured the plain to bone. [220] Vesna was sharply observant by training and by temper, and within an hour she had counted what did not fit. [240] The dust fell everywhere except a shallow bowl of land at the orchard's heart, perhaps forty meters across, where the air hung motionless and the leaves did not stir. [269] A hidden stillpoint, she thought, and hated the poetry of it. [280] Geology would explain it: a convergence of ridge and thermal, a pocket where crosswinds cancelled and dropped their load beyond the trees. [302] That was the debunking, clean and true, and it did not explain the shredder. [316]

In the trust's abandoned office she found a bin of ribbons: correspondence destroyed the week of the liquidation. [334] She worked four days by lamplight, by reassembling shredded letters strip by strip on the packing table, taping edges, matching typefaces and coffee rings. [358] Maret brought her quince and said nothing, which Vesna understood as permission. [370]

The letters told a plainer and crueler story than any curse. [381] The Kesler stone had never been cursed; it had been collateral. [392] Its flaw made it nearly worthless on the open market, so three successive directors had pledged the same forty carats against three separate loans, inflating its appraisal each time, and used the money to buy the orchard's water rights out from under the families. [436] When the fraud came due, the trust collapsed, the families were liquidated along with the machinery, and the curse story was seeded deliberately to keep auditors away from a stone nobody dared reappraise. [469] The three dead directors had killed themselves, one by one, as the numbers closed in. [484] The last letter was unsigned, typed on Commission stationery, authorizing the appraisal Vesna's own department had certified. [501]

She sat with the taped pages until the lamp guttered. [511] She had come to kill a superstition and had instead found the machinery the superstition protected. [527]

In the morning she walked to the stillpoint with Maret. [537] Under the oldest apricot, Maret dug two handspans down and lifted out an oilcloth bundle. [552] The stone caught the flat light and split it badly, the feather-fracture running through it like a crack in ice. [572] "They came for it and I told them the curse," Maret said. [584] "Sixty families, and all I could keep was the lie." [594]

Vesna did not need testimony to intuit the rest; she could feel the shape of it the way she felt the dead air on her face, the absence of wind that let one small place bear fruit. [631] She photographed the diamond, wrote its true weight and its true flaw, and filed the report that named her own certification as fraudulent. [654] The Commission suspended her within a month, which she had expected and which changed nothing about the arithmetic. [672] She went back to the orchard to help Maret prove title, because the water rights had been bought with a lie and a lie is a thing you can unwind if you have the strips of paper. [709]

Some evenings, standing in the stillpoint while the storms howled a hundred meters off, she understood why people preferred curses. [729] A curse asks nothing of you. [735] A fraud asks everything, and the fruit keeps ripening either way. [746]
