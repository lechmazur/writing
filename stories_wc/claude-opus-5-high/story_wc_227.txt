The workshop rode the acid sea on a raft of turning gears, and every gear was rusting. [17] Iselde, who called herself an alchemist only when no one was listening, counted them each dawn. [33] Nine hundred and six cogs, four fewer than the week before. [44] When the count reached zero, the floor would open its yellow mouth and swallow her. [59]

In the center of the workshop stood the statue, a caryatid hauled up from the drowned city of Vess six centuries ago. [81] The vapors had eaten her face into a constellation of pockmarks, thousands of them, each a different depth and shape. [101] Iselde had learned, slowly and against her own doubt, that the pits were an index. [116] Every soul lost to Vess had left one mark. [125] The sea kept their memories as shards of bitter glass, and the shards carried the same corrosion, in miniature, as the pock that belonged to them. [151]

That was her method: fish a shard from the vat, hold it against the stone, and match the pockmarks until the shape agreed. [174] Matching was the easy part. [179] The binding was gold, and gold required intention, and intention required her to narrate. [193] She had to speak the memory aloud as she laid the seam, or the metal would not hold. [211] Her master had called it kintsugi: you do not hide the break, you gild it, you make the wound the brightest part of the vessel. [236]

Her master had also walked off the eastern deck eleven months ago, leaving a key ring on the workbench. [255] The keys had dissolved; only the charm survived, a small brass fish, pitted all over from the spray. [273]

The vat was the only water on the raft that would not kill her, and the shards would not rise to a hand that breathed. [298] So she worked in the interval between breaths held underwater: down, eyes open, the shards drifting toward her fingers like slow fish; up, gasping, the gold ladle steaming. [326] Each dive bought her one memory, and each memory, quilted into the statue, bought the raft another turning cog. [345] That was the bargain of Vess: the city would hold up the world so long as someone remembered it out loud. [366]

Iselde was not sure she believed any of it. [375] She was sure of the cog count, and that was enough to keep her diving. [390]

That morning she surfaced with a shard the color of weak tea and knew, before she even raised it, whose it was. [412] Its pitting matched the brass fish exactly, pock for pock, the way a face matches a face. [429] Her master had gone into the sea and the sea had kept him, and now the sea was giving him back in pieces. [452] She held the shard against the statue's cheek and found the pock that agreed with it: a shallow crescent under the left eye. [475]

The gold would not go in without words. [483] She had never been good at words; she had been good at doubting them. [497] She set the ladle down and began anyway. [505]

"He was called Oriel," she said to the empty gears and the hissing sea. [519] "He taught me that a mended thing is worth more than a whole one, and then he stopped believing it." [539] "He walked east at low tide because he could not conjure meaning out of a dying raft, and he left me his keys so I would have to try." [568]

The gold went in. [572] It ran into the crescent and spread, hairline by hairline, into a hundred neighboring pocks, seaming them, quilting them, stitching six centuries of strangers to one drowned man and one living woman who had spoken his name. [609] Beneath her feet the floor shuddered and began, cog by cog, to grow. [622] Nine hundred and seven, then nine hundred and twenty, then more than she could count. [637] The statue's face, gilded now in a lightning of seams, was not beautiful; it was legible. [653]

Iselde hung the brass fish from the statue's wrist, where the next alchemist would find it. [669] Then she filled her lungs, held the breath, and went down for the next shard, and the next, narrating each one to the fumes. [693] She was still uncertain of the bargain, of Vess, of whether any of the drowned could hear. [710] But the gears were turning under her, and the acid sea, for the first time in eleven months, was losing. [730]
