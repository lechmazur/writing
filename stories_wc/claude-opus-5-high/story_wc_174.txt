The radar dome had stopped listening for the sky in 1994, and Idris Vane had been writing on its inner wall ever since. [23] He was an urbane man in a place with no streets, his cuffs clean, his ink ground fresh each morning, his vowels shaped like a city he no longer visited. [53] The dome's curve took his brushstrokes the way a bell takes a hum. [66] Nell had left the argument still standing in the room. [76] It had been a heated one, the kind that empties a building of its air, and now the hush it left behind was so complete that Idris could hear the kettle cooling. [108] She had shouted about Booth Kerrigan. [114] They had made a puppet of him, a dockhand with a stammer and a grievance, and performed it for three nights in the Blue Ditch, the seediest bar on the harbor road, where the beer is warm and the truth is cheap. [156] That was their method and always had been: subversive puppet shows in bars nobody policed, little wooden insurrections staged where the harbor bosses drank. [180] By the fourth day Kerrigan had lost his berth, and the stammer the audience had laughed at was the only part of him anyone remembered. [205] You borrowed a man and returned him damaged, Nell had said, and slammed the hatch hard enough to ring the dome. [226] Idris was uncommonly serene, which people mistook for coldness; in truth it was the serenity of a man who had decided long ago that panic solves nothing and had never once been tested. [259] He was being tested now. [264]

He climbed the maintenance ladder to the dome's cracked crown and sat with his legs through the gap, because outside there was a meteor shower coming and the whole ridge had gone quiet for it. [299] That was the hush he liked best: not absence of sound but the held breath of things falling that will not land. [321] He had brought the story stick with him. [329] It was a length of alder his grandfather had cut for campfires, notched for every teller who had ever held it, and the rule at those fires had been simple: you may speak only while you hold it, and you must hand it on. [373] Idris had drilled it, strung it, and made it the spine of his best puppet. [388] He had never once handed it on. [395]

The first meteor went over, then a second, and in that half-light the epiphany arrived the way they always did for him, partially, lit down one side only, the rest left in the dark for later. [431] He had spent a decade telling himself he was there to explore ethical storytelling, as though ethics were a country you could tour without moving in. [457] The half-lit part said: reverse it. [463] Not the show, the direction. [468] Stop taking stories down from the harbor and carrying them up to the stage. [482] Carry the stage down and give the stick away. [491]

Three weeks later the Blue Ditch was fuller than it had ever been for a puppet show, because word had gone around that Vane had lost his nerve or his mind. [522] He stood in front of the little curtain instead of behind it, in his clean cuffs, uncommonly calm, and said that tonight the puppets would not speak first. [550] Then he walked to the back table and put the alder stick in Booth Kerrigan's hands. [566] The rule, he explained to the room, was the old campfire rule. [578] Whoever holds it tells it, and no one may tell it for him. [591] Kerrigan stammered for a full minute. [597] Nobody laughed, which surprised Idris more than anything in his life had surprised him, and afterward he understood that the laughter had never belonged to the audience. [624] It had belonged to the puppeteer, and he had been lending it out. [637]

Nell came back to the dome in the spring. [646] She found the inner wall covered, floor to crown, in names in his immaculate hand, every person they had ever staged, and beneath each name a blank space the exact width of a line. [680] He was keeping them empty, he said, until the owners came up the ridge to fill them. [697] Some already had. [700] Outside, the sky was quiet in that falling way, and the dome, which had once only listened, listened still. [719]
