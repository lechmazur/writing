The accession ledger lay open to its first page when the cameo blank arrived, and Ilse Varro, curator of the Orchard Reliquary, had until the last page to decide what it was. [32] Outside the dome, the astral orchard was blossoming again on its lonely asteroid, white petals lifting in the low gravity and hanging there like held breath. [58] The trees grew pistachios, and the pistachios grew shells, and the shells, carved, held the faces of the dead. [77] That was the Reliquary's whole business: one cameo per life, filed, catalogued, and returned to the roots so the orchard could remember. [99] The blank was the problem. [103] It had come up in the Michaelmas harvest, smooth as a thumbnail, uncarved, and it was addressed. [120] Shells did not arrive addressed; shells were carved afterward, by survivors, with a needle and a steady grief. [138] This one bore three letters etched into the hull of the nut, tiny and exact: ILS. [154] Ilse took it to the washroom, which was where she did her thinking. [167] She was not proud of this, but she had learned in forty years of stewardship that the mind unlocks over a sink, in the small honest privacy where nobody expects you to be a curator. [202] Under the mirror light she turned the blank over and had the first of the day's breakthroughs. [219] The orchard's inheritance had never run in a line. [228] Graft a scion from a young tree onto an old one and the old one began producing the young one's faces, memories moving backward along the wood. [255] The founders had called it nonlinear inheritance and had written exactly one rule about it in the charter: do not inquire of a blank. [279] Bewildered, she read the rule again on her cuff-screen, and it was, she thought, simply complicated - four plain words fencing off everything the orchard actually knew. [305] A blank is a face that has not happened yet. [315] To inquire of one - to press it to the root-port and ask - was to receive the answer from downstream, from whoever would carve it. [339] Forbidden knowledge, the charter meant, though it had been too polite to say so. [353] Ilse washed her hands for a long time. [361] She was seventy-one and had no apprentice, and the orchard, which had outlived every curator, would outlive her too. [380] The wanting in her was not morbid; it was bright, almost joyful, and that was what disquieted her most. [399] She wanted to know who would come after, whether anyone would. [409] She wanted, for once, to be inherited from. [417] Back in the archive she wrote the accession number on page one, then walked the blank out into the orchard rows. [438] Petals brushed her helmet. [442] The root-port on the oldest tree was a brass cup worn smooth by two centuries of thumbs. [459] She set the shell in it and asked her question aloud, because a rule broken quietly is broken all the same. [480] The tree answered the way trees do, slowly, in the only currency it had. [494] Over six hours the blank carved itself from the inside, the shell thinning here, thickening there, until a face stood up out of it in low relief. [521] It was a girl of perhaps nineteen, wide-jawed, unsmiling, with a mole beneath her left eye. [537] Ilse did not know her. [542] She would not know her for eleven years, when a supply pilot's daughter came up the ladder with a mole beneath her left eye and asked, bewildered, what the orchard was for. [574] But that was later. [578] She went back to the washroom before she wrote anything, and there, drying her hands, had the second breakthrough: inheritance was not a river but a ring, and she had simply been standing at the wrong point on it. [617] Now there was only the ledger, and the last page, and the entry to be written. [633] Ilse wrote: 'Cameo, pistachio shell, carved by the hand of my successor, received before it was given.' [650] Then she added, because the charter deserved an honest opponent: 'The rule is sound, and I broke it, and I am glad.' [672] She closed the book on both pages at once, first and last touching, which is how the orchard had always understood time. [694] Outside, the blossoms went on hanging in the dark, waiting for a wind that would have to be invented. [713]
