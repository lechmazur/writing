In the third year after violence became impossible, the port of Kell had nothing left to do but love badly. [20] Fists slid off jaws like water off oilskin; knives turned to warm butter an inch from skin. [37] The whalers hung up their harpoons, the press gangs dissolved, and the harbor filled with men who had forgotten every language except argument. [60] Maren unloaded the salt barges through all of it, moving down the plank with the grace of someone born to a pitching deck. [83] Nobody in Kell could lift a crate the way she could, and nobody could stand her for long. [101] Nobody except Ines, the harbormaster, who fought with her the way tide fights shore: daily, tenderly, without hope of victory. [121] "You'll drown yourself out there," Ines said, that last evening, and meant: stay. [134] "You'd like that," Maren said, and meant: I am already gone. [145] Between them on the bollard lay the scrimshaw. [153] It was whalebone, yellow as an old tooth, scratched two centuries ago by a pirate named Absalom Rue who had died laughing at his own gallows. [179] Its face showed an island with three hills and a whale beneath it, mouth open, singing. [195] The bone had a way of unlocking guarded hearts; whoever held it could not keep a true thing behind their teeth. [216] That was how Maren had learned, six months back, that Ines loved her. [229] It was also how Maren had learned she was not a woman who could be kept in a house with a stove and a chair. [254] Both truths had come out of her mouth in the same breath, and they had been fighting about it ever since. [275] "Rue's island isn't on any chart," Ines said. [283] "Because it doesn't stay," Maren said. [289] Sailors had called it the Vanishing, a green back that surfaced for a day and sank for a decade. [308] Rue had gone there once, when his crew still had hands willing to hurt, and had come back with nothing but this bone and a reputation for honesty that ruined him. [339] He had left instructions scratched along the bone's spine, and Maren had read them until the letters wore into her thumbprint: the island rises only by the call of a whale from the deep, and only for the hand willing to detach the bone from its chain and give it back to the water. [393] The chain was Maren's grandmother's, and the bone had hung from it since before Kell had a name for peace. [413] She went down at slack water to the sublittoral fringe below the ruined whaling station, that green half-drowned country where the kelp never quite breaks the surface and the tide never quite leaves. [446] Ines came with her, arms crossed, furious the whole way, which was her way of saying she would not let Maren go alone. [469] "If the island takes you," Ines said, "I'm going to be insufferable about it forever." [484] "You're insufferable now," Maren said, and kissed her hard enough to bruise, if bruising had still been allowed. [502] Then she waded in to her waist among the kelp stipes, holding the bone in both hands. [519] Holding it, she could not lie, so she said the whole thing aloud where Ines could hear it. [537] "I don't want a kitchen, I want the water, and I have wanted it since I was nine years old, and I love you, and both of those are true, and I am tired of pretending one of them isn't." [577] "I know," Ines said, which was the tenderest fighting she had ever done. [590] Maren worked the little iron ring until it opened and let the chain detach, and the bone dropped through the green water and settled among the anemones. [617] For a long minute nothing happened, and Maren thought: so I am only a dock worker after all. [635] Then the sound came up through the soles of her feet. [646] It was a whale calling from the deep water beyond the shelf, a note so low it was less heard than agreed to, and the sea answered by lifting. [675] Three hills came out of the swell a mile offshore, streaming water, black and green and impossible. [692] Maren laughed once, the way Absalom Rue must have laughed. [702] "Come or don't," she said, "but decide fast." [710] Ines swore at her, beautifully, and started unlacing her boots. [720] They swam out together over the fringe where the kelp gave way to blue, and behind them Kell went on arguing with itself about what men were for now that they could not hurt each other. [756] Maren already knew what she was for. [763] She had been for this all along, and the island stayed up until she reached it. [779]
