I never needed the sugar. [5] I needed an excuse to knock on 4C, where the dead language tutor lived among stacked dictionaries and a smell like dry cedar. [28] Mr. Anselm answered in slippers, took my empty cup, filled it, and said, 'Thursday, then, for tea.' [45] That was our first agreed upon lie, and it held for a year. [58]

I wanted to learn the language of old buildings. [67] I surveyed condemned tenements for the city, and every one of them was talking. [81] Not in creaks, though they creaked; in carved lintels, in the tiny incised marks masons left under stair treads, in cornerstones cut with an alphabet no archive would name. [110] Anselm taught Hittite and Old Prussian to graduate students who never came back, and he was, I was certain, the only person alive who could read those stones. [138] So I borrowed sugar. [142]

Thursdays became a habit with the weight of a liturgy: two cups, one lemon, and a lesson I pretended to stumble through. [164] He was old and patiently nimble, his fingers quick over paradigm charts, his patience slow and deep as a well. [184] On his desk sat an antique desk seal, brass, its handle worn to the shape of a thumb that was not his. [206] The matrix showed a glyph I had photographed that April under the water table of a demolished orphanage on Rell Street. [227] I said nothing for eleven weeks. [233]

Instead I asked him about dead languages, and he told me the truth sideways, which is how the truth prefers to travel. [255] 'A language is a set of agreed upon lies,' he said, stirring. [267] 'We agree that this sound means water, and the lie holds, and we can drink.' [282] 'When the last two speakers stop agreeing, the language dies, and everything it promised comes unglued.' [298] 'Buildings,' I said. [301] 'Buildings are promises, then.' [305] He looked at me the way you look at a stranger who knows your middle name. [321]

In August he invited me up to the rooftop garden, where he grew rosemary in cracked amphorae and the Perseids were due. [343] He brought the tea; I brought the seal, which I had taken from his desk that afternoon while he searched for honey. [365] Meteors came down in long white scratches, and I set the brass thing on the table between the cups and began, at last, to interrogate him. [391]

Who cut the Rell Street cornerstone. [397] Why the same glyph sat under the customs house, the asylum, the three tenements the city was pulling down this winter. [418] Why a man who taught Hittite owned a mason's seal worn smooth by a hand that died, by any honest arithmetic, two centuries ago. [442]

He did not flinch; he refilled my cup with those nimble old hands and watched the sky burn its small holes. [463] 'You have known since May,' he said. [470] 'You kept coming anyway, and I kept pretending the sugar was the reason.' [483] 'That is what we are: two speakers keeping a lie warm so it stays useful.' [498]

Then he told me the rest. [504] The masons had a guild tongue, spoken nowhere and written only in foundations, and its verbs were load and span and endure. [526] A building stands because its stones agree on what they have been told to be. [541] He was the last one who could still say those words correctly, and he had been saying them, quietly, block by block, for longer than the city had records. [570] When he stopped, the tenements came down easily, and the city called it demolition. [584] 'I am tired,' he said, 'and a language needs two.' [594]

He pushed the seal across the table, and under the falling light it looked less like brass than like a key. [615] I had rehearsed a dozen questions and asked none of them. [626] I picked it up; the handle fit my thumb as though the wear had been waiting. [642] That, too, was mysterious and entirely familiar, like a house you have never entered and can still walk through in the dark. [664]

'Repeat after me,' Anselm said, and spoke a word that the rosemary leaned toward. [678] I repeated it badly, then better. [684] Below us the old tenements held their breath through the whole meteor shower, and by morning I had stopped pretending I came for sugar. [708] We still say Thursday, though. [713] Some lies you keep because they hold weight. [721]
