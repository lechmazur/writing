The laboratory had been sunk on purpose, and everyone in the valley knew it. [13] I went down anyway, in a borrowed diving bell, with a lamp and a notebook and my mother's hangman's dice rattling in my coat. [37] They were three, bone-yellow, and the overseers of the Tessaly Hydrological Institute had rolled them every Friday to decide which researcher's work would be burned. [62] My mother stole them the night the water came. [71] I am a lyrical code breaker, a poor one by the standards of the bureaus, because I do not solve ciphers by arithmetic. [94] I hear them as meter. [99] A cipher has a limp or a waltz in it; a liar's key stumbles; an honest key breathes. [117] When the bureau asked me to justify a solution, I could never write the proof, so I stood up and showed them: three steps, a turn, a held breath on the fourth beat. [150] They called it unprofessional. [154] My mother had called it dancing, and taught it to me on a kitchen floor before I could read. [173] The bell settled into the sunken laboratory's east wing at forty feet, where an air pocket still held under the vaulted ceiling, sour and cold. [198] Glassware floated. [200] Tables stood in silt like patient animals. [207] And in the corner of the lamp's reach, a spider had begun a web between a retort stand and a hanging chain. [229] I gave myself that long: as the spider spun, I would read. [241] My mother's journals were where her last letter said they would be, in a tin trunk on the high shelf, dry as bread. [264] Twelve volumes, and every page a wall of numbers. [273] The Institute had read them for years and found only hydrology, and that was the point. [289] She had spoken to me only via coded journal entries, because anything she said plainly could be used to break her. [310] Fear, she once wrote in the margin of a letter, is a key; give it away and you are opened. [330] So she encrypted her vulnerability and it became her strength: every terror she felt went into the key itself, unreadable, load-bearing. [351] I rolled the hangman's dice on the dry tabletop, once, twice, and got nothing but numbers. [367] Then I understood that she would never have rolled them. [377] Her whole life was a calm defiance -- not shouting, not fleeing, simply declining to play the game as offered. [396] I set the three dice down deliberately, in the order of the kitchen dance: three, then the turn, then the held breath. [418] Three, one, four. [421] The stanza key. [424] The numbers on the first page began to scan, and I laughed out loud in that stale bubble of air, because the cadence was mine. [449] It was the strangest kind of mysterious familiarity -- reading a stranger's mathematics in my own voice, hearing a woman dead eleven years speak with the rhythm she had planted in my feet. [481] She had not been teaching me to dance. [489] She had been teaching me the key. [496] The entries named names. [500] Nine researchers, held in the Institute's northern annex under sealed contracts that the flood had conveniently erased from every public register -- their patents, their pensions, their citizenship. [527] Volume nine held the contract numbers; volume eleven held the signatures of the men who had rolled the dice. [546] Enough, read together, to liberate all nine of them, and to sink the Institute in a court instead of a lake. [567] The spider finished. [570] Its web hung complete in the lamplight, load-bearing, beautiful, a structure made entirely of tension held on purpose. [588] I packed the twelve volumes into the tin trunk and left the dice on the table, face down, unrolled. [607] In the hearing room, a month later, the lawyers asked me to explain how the cipher worked. [624] I said I could not write it down. [632] I said I could only show them. [639] So I stood in front of nine freed researchers and eleven embarrassed judges, and I danced my mother's kitchen floor: three steps, a turn, a held breath on the fourth beat. [670] Nobody laughed this time. [674] They wrote it down as testimony, three, one, four, and called it evidence. [687] I called it my mother, still talking. [694]
