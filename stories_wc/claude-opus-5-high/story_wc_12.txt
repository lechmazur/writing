The chapel on the salt flats was a dimensional anchor, and Father Ansel tuned it the way other priests tuned bells. [21] His loop pedal sat at the foot of the altar, scuffed, taped, holy. [34] He would sing one line, stamp, and let the room answer itself until the walls of the world stopped drifting. [54] Tonight the drift had a name, and the name was wearing Mira. [66] She sat in the third pew with her hands open, and the air around her had gone into unraveled stillness: not quiet, but the loose, fraying calm of a rope one thread from parting. [100] Everything Ansel heard arrived muffled, as though the chapel had been packed in wool, and yet each sound was appallingly clear. [121]

Mira had painted the chapel's icons for eleven years, and she decorated with silence. [135] Her saints were mostly absence: a shoulder, a hem, and then acres of unpainted board where the face should be. [155] Her artist's palette held one color, a grey the shade of a held breath, because she believed a single pigment could not lie about what it was. [182] The thing inside her had come through one of those empty faces. [194]

It spoke first, and it spoke well. [201] "You have until the third hour," it said, using Mira's mouth carefully, like a borrowed coat. [217] "Then the possession completes, and she will not be evicted, because there will be no she to evict." [235]

Ansel did not argue with its conclusion; he had learned that demons are never defeated at the conclusion. [253] They are defeated at the premise they never say aloud. [263] So he stepped on the pedal and began to plumb. [273]

"You say she will not be evicted," he sang, low, and the loop caught it and gave it back. [292] "Evicted from where?" [295] Layer by layer he built the question into a choir, and between the layers he left gaps of silence, the way Mira left gaps of board. [321] The gaps mattered; a thing that lives in assumptions cannot survive a pause in which it must supply its own. [341]

He worked through rhetorical devices as a diver works through weights. [352] Antithesis: "You enter what is empty; she painted emptiness on purpose." [363] Chiasmus: "A body is not a house because a house is not a body." [377] Aporia, which is doubt performed until it becomes a lantern: "I do not know what you are standing on." [396]

The loops thickened into a wall of Ansel, forty Ansels, a congregation of one man interrogating the air. [414] He did not want to exorcise it yet; he wanted to illuminate the hidden assumptions it had smuggled in with itself, because the anchor could only hold what had been named. [445] The thing laughed, and the laugh came back to it in forty voices, slightly late. [460]

"You assume," Ansel said, "that a face is a door." [470] "You assume that an unfinished thing is an open thing." [480] "You assume she was empty and not merely quiet." [489]

The stillness in the pew began to tighten, thread pulling back into rope. [502] Ansel lifted the palette from the rail and turned it toward the thing so it could see the one grey, mixed to nothing else, admitting nothing else. [529] "She kept one color," he said, "so that nothing in her work could pretend to be something it wasn't." [548] "You are pretending to be her interior, a guest calling himself a floor." [561]

The loops fell away one by one, stamped off, until only the last silence remained, and into that silence Mira breathed in. [583] It was the third hour minus a minute. [591] The thing had nothing to stand on and no name it could give that the chapel would not immediately hold, and so it went out of her the way water goes out of cupped hands: fast, and with a sound like nothing. [633] The anchor closed around the premise it had never spoken aloud, and outside, the salt flats stopped their slow drift. [653]

Mira wept without noise, which was how she did everything. [663] Ansel sat beside her and did not comfort her with words, since words had just been weapons and needed to cool. [684] Later she asked what it had actually been. [692] "Something that could only live where a question hadn't been asked," he said. [705] She nodded, and the next morning she began the face, in grey, in one color, slowly, with the loop pedal humming a single sustained chord behind her so the room would never again be too quiet to notice.
