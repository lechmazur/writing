Mira Vance had been called an errant legend seeker so often that she had stopped correcting the errant part. [19] She had wandered after twelve myths and been wrong about eleven. [30] The twelfth had brought her here, to the twilight barge canal at Hallam Reach, kneeling on a rented skiff with a grappling line in her fists. [56] Above the lock, the sky went the color of wet brass, and the water lay so still it seemed to be thinking. [78] She had until the silence of morning radio. [86] Every dawn the canal authority read the weather and the shipping claims at a quarter to six, and when the broadcast cut out the lock gates opened, and forty years of silt would rise in a slow brown storm. [125] After that, nothing down there could be found again. [134] The wreck of the barge Corabel lay eleven feet under her keel, and inside it, if the legend held, an antique pocket sundial that told the hour of a man's death. [165] Her little radio muttered on the gunwale, listing barges, listing rain. [176]

Oswin Teale had taught her the trade before he died: not how to find legends, but how to salvage them, how to lift a true thing out of the mud of rumor. [208] He had taught her by training instincts rather than rules -- eleven wrong myths, each one dissected at his kitchen table until she could smell a forgery the way a sailor smells weather. [240] A lie needs three beams, he said: an object, a witness, and a gap in the record where the truth should be. [262] She had come to Hallam Reach to understand the architecture of lies, because for three weeks she had suspected the Corabel legend was built on those exact three beams, and that Oswin had raised it himself. [298]

She went into the water at five-twenty. [305] The canal was cold and unexpectedly lucid, the way certain waters are before they are disturbed, and she could see the Corabel's ribs stitched with weed. [331] Her hands moved without asking her: the strongbox would be aft, high, where a man would keep it dry. [350] It was. [353] She surfaced with it at five-thirty-eight, and the radio was still reading rain. [366]

The sundial was inside, wrapped in oilcloth: bronze, hinged, its gnomon a folded needle, its face engraved with hours and a motto in Latin about shadows. [392] It was beautiful, and it was wrong. [399] Her instincts told her before her eyes did: the latitude scale was cut for fifty-one degrees, and Hallam Reach sits at fifty-three. [421] No bargeman's heirloom would be calibrated for a workshop two hundred miles south -- for Oswin's workshop, in fact, where she had learned to solder. [445] Folded under the dial was a letter in his handwriting, thoughtfully clear, as though he had known that whoever pried this box open would be cold and short of time. [475]

Mira, he wrote, I built this legend for you the year you turned nineteen and could not tell a true story from a well-told one. [500] I sank the object, I paid the witness, I tore the page from the lock register that made the gap. [520] If you are reading this, you found the seams; that is the whole lesson, and I am sorry it cost you eleven years of wandering. [545]

She sat back on her heels while the sky turned from brass to pearl, and the eleven failures rearranged themselves behind her. [567] Every one of them had been his: seeded, staged, sprung -- an education disguised as a career. [583] A sundial cannot tell you the hour ahead; it can only show where the light has already been, and that, she thought, was the joke he had been building toward all along. [615] Hindsight had made her clear-eyed, the way it always did, one lie too late to be useful and exactly in time to be true. [639]

At six the radio stopped mid-sentence. [645] The gates opened somewhere below, and the canal began to move -- not violently, but entirely, the whole still surface lifting and turning brown from the bottom up, a serene upheaval that took the wreck, the weed, the ribs, everything but what she had already raised. [690]

Mira closed the sundial and put it in her coat. [700] She would keep it, not as a relic but as a diagram: object, witness, gap. [715] There were real legends out there, buried under worse architecture than his, and for the first time in eleven years she trusted the hands that would go down after them. [745] She started the skiff's engine and went out through the churning water, into the morning, no longer errant. [763]
