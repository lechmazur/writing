The mist came up off the tide flats every morning and settled into the rail depot like something that had paid rent. [22] Maren Oyelu opened her repair shop under the platform arches at four bells, as she had for nineteen years, and listened to the water. [46] The depot was full of it: condensation ladders, drip basins, the long copper trough where the mist collectors emptied themselves in thin, patient threads. [70] She had not seen any of it since she was thirty-one. [81] She oversaw the shop anyway, three apprentices and a bench of broken things, by a method the depot called witchcraft and she called arithmetic. [105] That morning the harbor gun fired at half past five, which meant the Cordwain was raising anchor for her maiden voyage, and Maren allowed herself a breath of pride before the trouble arrived. [138] The trouble arrived through the north arch, ticking. [146] Tobin caught it out of the air with both hands and set it on her bench. [162] "The survey bird," he said. "It's chewed up." [170] Maren's fingers found the artificial hummingbird drone and read it the way she read everything, from the beak back. [189] Right wing membrane torn along two ribs; gull strike, probably, or hail off the shoals. [204] She had built the bird herself eleven years ago, before the dark came, and she still knew its weight the way a mother knows a sleeping child. [231] The little machine had flown the new channel all night, measuring silt and current, and everything it had learned was still inside it as feedback - a shiver, a stutter, unreadable noise. [263] The Cordwain would be over the bar in forty minutes with three hundred people aboard and a chart that was three years old. [286] "Patch," Maren said, and the word went out across the shop the way a hand goes out across a spooked horse. [307] Nobody hurried; nobody ever hurried around her; her calm was a magnet and the room leaned into it. [325] Tobin brought mica-silk and the hot iron. [332] Maren laid the torn membrane over the rib, breathed on it, and set the patch with three passes, feeling the seam cool under her thumb until it was smooth as a fingernail. [364] "Flight is the easy half," she said. "Now we make it talk." [376] The drone reported by light. [381] Its eye threw a narrow beam coded in colors, which was no use at all to a blind woman - unless the beam was made to argue with water. [410] Maren carried the drone to the copper trough and held it above the falling threads. [425] Tobin cranked the lamp behind her. [431] The beam struck the water and split, and each color bent at its own angle and came down on the black slate in a fan, and every band of that fan carried a different measure of heat. [468] Maren laid her palm flat and walked her fingers through the spectrum. [480] Refraction had done for her what eyes once did: it spread the drone's single stubborn answer out into places she could touch, one degree per finger-width. [506] "Red at the trough lip," she said. "Green two hands out. Tobin, tell me where the blue lands." [524] "Past the slate. On the rail." [530] She was quiet for the length of one breath, decoding, turning noise into a bearing the way a miller turns grain. [551] The silt had moved. "The channel has walked east," she said. [562] "Eleven degrees, and shoaling on the old line." [570] If the Cordwain held her chart she would put her new keel into a sandbar at eight knots. [588] Maren turned the hummingbird in her hands, found the pilot ring, and thumbed the new heading into it - eleven degrees east, hold until the bell buoy. [615] "Go on, then," she told it, and opened her hands. [625] The patched wing caught, stuttered once, and held. [633] It went out through the mist over the platform, a hum going thin, then gone. [648] The shop stood still around her. [654] Maren wiped her hands and said, "Bench two, the signal lantern, somebody has been oiling the wrong pivot." [672] Work resumed, because she had told it to. [680] At twenty past six the harbor horn sounded twice - course corrected, pilot aboard, all well - and the sound came up the tide flats and into the arches where the water kept falling. [714] Tobin said the mist had gone gold. [721] Maren said she could feel that much on her face, and went back to the bench, where something else was waiting to be made whole. [746]
