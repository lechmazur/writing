After the gate rusted shut, Numa kept making maps anyway. [10] She carved them into spiral shells, one whorl per league, so a traveler could turn the map in her hand and feel the coastline narrowing toward its center. [38] There were no travelers now. [42] Ostragal, the city, rode the backs of nine gargantuan turtles yoked together by centuries of bridgework, and for eleven years the turtles had not moved. [67] Sun came down white and flat on the lime-washed roofs, and the sea beyond the shell-rim lay so bright it hurt to look at, and on the horizon stood the watchers. [98] Six turtles, motionless, their dead cities bleached to bone, facing Ostragal the way a person faces someone they have already decided not to forgive. [122]

Numa's brother Hask had gone to ask them why. [131] The expedition took four skiffs, two years of water, and Hask's reed whistle, which he played badly and constantly. [150] Nothing came back except the whistle, washed up against the rim gate, and after that the council let the gate rust shut and called the matter closed. [177]

Numa did not argue with them. [182] She was quietly driven, which in Ostragal was the only safe way to be driven at all: she simply kept working, every morning, in a room full of shells. [211]

Her work was pattern languages. [216] Turtles do not speak; they ridge. [222] Over decades their shells grow bands and pits and combs, and Numa had spent her life proving that the bands were not weather but grammar. [247] She learned to read it the way she read her own maps, from the outer whorl inward, and what she found under Ostragal was a sentence repeating without end. [276]

We are listening. [279]

Eleven years of it, scored into the living shell beneath the granaries: we are listening, we are listening, we are listening. [300] Listening to what, she wrote in her ledger, and then, because she was honest: to whom. [316] The answer was on the horizon, and the answer had swallowed her brother. [329]

So she went back to the whistle. [336] It was a common reed pipe with four holes, but Hask had burned little notches along its length -- a traveler's habit, marking days. [359] Numa laid the whistle against a shell map and saw that the notches were not days. [375] They were ridge-marks, copied in miniature, in the grammar she had taught him over a kitchen table. [392]

Hask had written back. [396]

It took her nine days to translate, and she did it alone, at noon, when the light through the window bleached everything to the color of dread. [423] The message was six words long: THEY STOPPED TO HEAR A CRACK. [435]

She understood it the way you understand a thing you have always known. [448] The watchers had not stopped to threaten anyone. [456] They had stopped because something in the deep water had made a sound -- one enormous crack, a shelf of the world giving way -- and turtles, being patient, had turned to listen for the second one. [491] Their cities had died waiting. [496] Ostragal had joined them eleven years ago, and Ostragal was dying the same slow, sunlit way, its gate rusted, its people calling stillness safety. [520]

Numa carried the whistle to the rim at dawn. [529] The council would call it treason; she thought of it as a question, finally asked out loud. [546] She played the pattern Hask had sent her, blowing it into the shell beneath her feet, four holes and a steady rhythm, ridge-grammar rendered in breath. [572] Not we are listening. [576] Something older, something she had found in the innermost whorl of the oldest map in her collection: the word for keep walking. [598]

For a long while nothing happened, and the sun came up white, and the six watchers stood exactly where they had always stood. [621] Then, under Ostragal, something vast shifted its weight. [629] Mortar dust sifted from the bridgework. [635] A bell fell somewhere and rang once on the way down. [646] The rusted gate split from its frame with a sound like the world's smallest crack, and the city began, very slowly, to turn away from the horizon. [673]

Numa sat down on the warm shell with the whistle in her lap. [686] She did not know if Hask was alive out there, riding one of those bleached hulks, notching a reed. [705] But she was moving toward him now, at the speed of a turtle, which is to say the speed of an answer. [727] She took a blank shell and began the new map at the center, working outward. [742]
