The sanctuary ate sound. [4] Not muffled it, not absorbed it: ate. [11] You could scream inside the white stone and feel your throat tear and hear nothing, which was why the Council had built it here, at the hinge of the world, where reality shifted every ninth day and grace moved from one body to another like weather. [57] Sena arrived on the eighth day with a music box in her coat and no weapon at all. [75] She had never carried one. [80] She had also never, in forty years of brokering truces, told a whole truth when a shaped one would land better. [101]

Corvin was waiting where they said he would be, sitting cross-legged under the dead bell, sharpening nothing, simply holding a knife because holding it was the only prayer he had left. [132] When the shift came at dawn, grace would settle on him. [143] For one day he would be unkillable, unstoppable, luminous, and he intended to spend that day walking into the Ninth House and ending the family that had ordered his sister drowned. [174]

Sena sat down across from him and set the music box on the floor between them. [190] In the soundless sanctuary it was a joke, an object made only of noise, offering none. [206] He looked at it anyway. [211] That was the whole scheme: get him to look. [220]

She opened the lid and turned the crank, and he watched the brass cylinder rotate, watched the comb teeth lift and fall, and saw the gap where one pin had broken off at the root. [255] Sena took his hand, pressed his palm flat to the vibrating case, and let the mechanism speak into his bones, because in a place that ate sound, music could only be delivered through cellular memory. [290] Flesh remembers what ears cannot receive. [296] His mother had owned this box; his sister had wound it; the tune had run through the tissue of his hands ten thousand times before he was old enough to name it. [328]

Sena had not stolen it. [333] She never had to; the box reappears when needed, and it had turned up in her coat pocket three nights ago, damp, smelling of river, as it had turned up for her twice before in her life, each time on the eve of something irreversible. [378] She did not tell him that part. [385] She told him instead about the pin. [392]

"Listen with your hand," she wrote in the dust, since words were useless here. [406] "There is a hole in the song," she wrote. [415] "Your mother heard the missing note every night for thirty years and kept winding the box anyway." [432] He wrote back, savagely, scraping the dust: "Then she was a coward." [444]

Sena shook her head and turned the crank again, harder, and held his hand down when he tried to pull away. [465] The gap came around, that small nothing, and his body knew it the way it knew his own pulse. [484] Twelve times, twenty, forty, until his shoulders dropped and his eyes went wet and useless. [499] That was the scheme's second half, and the honest half: grace shifts, and so does ruin, and a body that remembers tenderness can be reminded of it faster than it can be argued with. [533] She had come to convince him of one thing only: that resentment is a note that never arrives, and that the winding can continue without it. [559]

Dawn came without a sound, as everything did there. [568] Reality shifted; the white stone rippled like water standing on end; the dead bell swung and gave nothing. [586] Grace landed on Corvin, and Sena watched it fill him, and she understood that she had lost or won in the next four heartbeats. [610]

He stood, looked a long while at the knife, and laid it in the dust beside the box. [628] Then, unkillable, luminous, wasting the whole gift, he walked out to the river where his sister had gone under and spent his day of grace dragging silt for her bones. [658] He found them. [661]

Sena stayed in the sanctuary and wound the box until the spring gave out, feeling the hole come around and around in her palm. [685] She had been a scheming pacifist for forty years, and she knew exactly what she was: a woman who manipulated people into mercy because she could not bear to force them into it. [718] When she left, the box was gone from her pocket again. [729] She was glad; someone downriver would need it, and reality would shift again in nine days, and grace would land on someone else with a knife and a reason. [758] She would be there, empty-handed, lying beautifully, teaching the note that never comes. [771]
