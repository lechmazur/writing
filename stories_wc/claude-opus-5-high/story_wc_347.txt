Mirel never asked for the orchard, only for a winter indoors, and the orchard did not care. [17] It grew sideways out of the cliff in terraces and switchbacks, a labyrinth of pear and quince planted by her grandmother to break the wind, which had ended up breaking travelers instead. [49] They came up the coast road, saw blossom in October, ate one pear, and forgot which turn had brought them in. [70] That was the curse: small, patient, and only lethal once the night frost formed. [84] The birds were supposed to stop it. [91] Her grandmother had assembled them from tin, clock-spring, and salvage, and they still patrolled the hedge-walls, ticking, cocking their heads, calling out in voices like scraped slate. [118] They were graceless, and beautiful the way a crutch is beautiful, and for nine years they had been leading people deeper in instead of out. [143] Mirel had buried four travelers since spring and had stopped pretending the birds were a kindness. [159] She could not repair them, because she could not touch them. [170] The orchard's curse had come into her hands with the deed: anything that came within a finger's width of her palms softened and took her shape, like wax near a flame. [201] She pruned by hovering, weeded by hovering, signed her name in the dust of the shed shelves by leaving impressions without touching, and never once shook a hand. [229] On the last warm afternoon of the year, looking for wire, she pulled down a roll of oilcloth from the rafters and found the forgotten blueprint. [255] It was not a bird. [260] It was a bell-tower of scrap the height of a man, and in her grandmother's hand, in the margin, the instructions ran offhandedly exact: a thumb and a whisper of tension on the mainspring, no more; three pins, two of brass; the drum to sit a hand's-breadth above the stone so the sound travels down the cliff. [317] The birds, the notes explained, were only ever the scouts; this was the thing that was meant to guide lost travelers to safety, ringing a slow four-beat that a confused mind could follow uphill in the dark. [354] Her grandmother had died with it unbuilt. [361] At the bottom of the roll, wrapped in a scrap of flannel, was a small nut. [377] The blueprint called it the clapper: a hazelnut from the first tree, dried nine years, hard as a knuckle and the only material in the orchard the curse had never touched. [408] Mirel stood in the shed doorway and watched the light go amber over the sea and hated, genuinely hated, that it was going to be her. [434] Then she carried the oilcloth out to the highest terrace, because two voices had been calling to each other somewhere down in the quince rows since noon, and the wind had the smell that comes before the night frost forms. [474] To assemble a tower without touching it, she learned, you work in the air a hair above the metal. [493] Her palms pressed the seams of the drum closed like a baker's thumbs and left no fingerprints, only her shape, softened and re-hardened. [516] Brass pins settled into holes she had never gripped. [525] It was hideous work, lopsided, streaked with her handprints, and by the time the sun went under, the thing standing on the terrace looked like a scarecrow made of stovepipe. [555] She hovered her hand over the mainspring, a thumb and a whisper, and dropped the small nut into the drum. [575] The first note was awful, a flat clang, ugly as a dropped pot. [588] The fourth was worse. [592] But it was slow, and it repeated, and it went down the cliff in a way the birds' scraped-slate cries never had. [614] The mechanical birds came in from their circuits and perched all along the frame, ticking, listening, as though something they had been waiting nine years for had finally been said. [644] Frost was starting on the north rail when two figures came up out of the quince rows, a man and a girl, holding each other's sleeves, walking toward the ugly sound. [675] Mirel put her hands behind her back where they belonged and told them the way to the road, and the tower kept beating its four graceless notes out over the water, in case there were others. [711] She stayed out all night, listening, and did not once call it beautiful. [725]
