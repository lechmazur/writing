The blackout had lasted nine days, and on the tenth the lamps came back yellow and shy, like bees testing a new flower. [23] Mira Okonwe kept the toll at the foot of the lightning collection tower, where the road climbed to the orchard terraces. [44] She had kept it for eleven years, ever since the tower's ground-strike had run up through her boots and left her tongue stamped with copper. [69] After that she could not lie. [75] The city called it an affliction; the tower wardens called it a qualification, and gave her the ledger. [93]

The blackout had killed the drone hives. [100] Nine days without charge, and the pollination swarms had gone down in the orchards like grey hail, ten thousand little machines with their wings folded wrong. [126] The apple terraces above the tower would set no fruit this year, and the honey stalls on Candle Street were selling syrup dyed amber. [150] Mira found the dusk unbearable because it still looked the same: that thick nectar light pouring down the copper flanks of the tower, sweet as ever, promising nothing. [178]

The woman came up the road at that hour, carrying a lacquer box against her hip the way one carries a sleeping child. [201]

'Toll,' Mira said. [204]

'I have no coin,' the woman said, 'but I have the other kind.' [217]

She set a matchbox on the counter. [224]

Mira had read the founding charter so often she could recite it in her sleep: the toll of the lightning tower may be paid in coin, in labour, or in wonder, so that those who climb here daily do not forget what they climb. [268] In eleven years nobody had paid in wonder. [276]

Inside the matchbox, on a strip of paper folded four times, someone had written: I labour in the dark and am paid in gold; I speak in dances; I die of being told. [309]

Mira turned it over twice. [314]

'You know the answer,' the woman said. [321]

'Bees,' Mira said, because she could not do otherwise, and then, because she could not do otherwise either: 'Real ones. In the box. Living.' [345]

The woman's face did something complicated. [351]

'They told me the toll keeper here was candid,' she said. 'I thought that meant honest. I did not know it meant helpless.' [374]

'It means both,' Mira said. 'Ask me anything and I will answer. That is the trouble.' [390]

Her name was Osele, and she had kept the last unlicensed hive in the city for nineteen years, in a rooftop shed behind three false walls, because the drone contracts had made living bees illegal within the wall. [428] Now the drones were dead in the furrows and the terraces needed pollinating by the eighth day of bloom, and Osele wanted to walk her queen up the orchard road in daylight, with the toll paid and written down. [467]

'Legally,' she said. 'Once, before I die of a chest full of smoke.' [480]

The tower auditor came down the stair while they were still talking, boots ringing on the iron, clipboard under his arm; he audited the ledger every dusk, and he had never once found an error in it. [517]

'Anything to declare, keeper?' [521]

Mira felt the copper stir under her tongue. [529] She could not lie, but eleven years had taught her the shape of the gap: the tongue answered what was asked, and nothing that was not. [555]

'One traveller,' she said. 'Toll paid in full.' [563]

'In what?' [566]

'In wonder,' Mira said, and turned the ledger so he could read the line, with the riddle pinned beside it as the charter required. [590]

The auditor read it twice, the way she had. [599] Something moved behind his face, slow as sap. [607] He had climbed these stairs eleven thousand times, and Mira understood, watching him, that the clause about wonder had been written by someone who knew exactly what routine does to a person. [639]

'Bees,' he said quietly. 'It's bees.' [645]

'Ask me,' Mira said, 'and I'll have to tell you.' [655]

He looked at the lacquer box a long moment, at the nectar dusk lying gold along the copper, and did not ask. [677] He signed the page. 'Wonder received,' he said. 'Tower's charged. Road's open.' [689]

Osele went up the orchard road with the box against her hip. [701] Near the first terrace she stopped, lifted the lid, and let the hive reveal itself to the light: a low golden roar rising out of the lacquer, spilling over the dead drones in the furrows, going up into the blossom. [741]

Mira stood at her window with the ledger open and the matchbox in her palm, and told the truth to nobody in particular. [764] 'That,' she said, 'was worth the climb.' [771]
