The Archive of Extinct Tongues closed at dawn, and Miren had until then to undo seven years of silence. [19] Tomorrow the collection would be split between two universities, and the scholars with it. [33] Before their paths diverged, she meant to open the Vault of Chords. [45]

Teodor found her in the lamplit stacks, sleeves inked to the elbow. [57] "You're still carefully reckless," he said. [63] "Careful enough to want you here," Miren answered. [71]

Between them on the reading table sat the glass ink well, its facets throwing green light across a hundred dead alphabets. [92] It held the last dram of Vessarine ink, drawn from a language that had no living speakers and no word for lying. [114] As an arcane linguist, Miren knew what that absence cost. [124] Vessarine worked via interconnected promises: every sentence bound itself to a vow the speaker had already kept, and to one they still owed. [147] Speak truly and the vows resonated; the room filled with symphonies of truth, chords audible to anyone standing near. [166] Speak falsely and the vows snarled. [171]

Seven years ago they had snarled spectacularly. [178] Teodor had promised the trustees that their translation was complete. [188] It had not been, and Miren had known, and neither of them had said so. [203] The Vault of Chords had sealed itself that night, its lock a tangle of broken oaths, and the two of them had stopped speaking entirely. [228]

"You want to unsnarl it," Teodor said, staring at the ink well. [240] "I want to unsnarl us," she said. [247] "The vault is only where the knot shows." [255] He laughed once, a startled sound, and she heard in it the same cautious exuberance she felt: the fear of hoping too loudly. [278]

They worked until the lamps guttered. [284] Miren dipped the last pen in the glass ink well and wrote the opening clause in Vessarine, and the air hummed a low, honest fifth. [309] "I knew the translation was unfinished," she wrote, "and I let you say otherwise, because I wanted the funding more than I wanted you honest." [334] The chord held. [337]

Teodor took the pen. [341] His hand shook, which in Vessarine was irrelevant; only the vow mattered. [353] "I lied to the trustees," he wrote, "and I blamed your silence for mine." [367] A second note joined the first, rough at the edges, then clean. [379]

That was the grammar of the thing: a confession only resonated if it was tied to a promise forward. [398] So Miren wrote what she would do, and Teodor wrote what he would do, and each clause hooked into the other like fingers. [421] She promised to send him the Karsh fragments, every month, unasked. [432] He promised to publish her name first, and to write when the work went badly, not only when it went well. [453]

The ink well emptied drop by drop. [460] It was carelessness, spending the last Vessarine ink on two scholars instead of on scholarship, and Miren had weighed it for a month before deciding. [485] Recklessness, carefully. [487]

By the last line the sound in the archive was no longer a chord but a chorus, the vault's tangled oaths loosening thread by thread as the new promises took their weight. [519] The door of the Vault of Chords swung open onto shelves of wax cylinders, the recorded voices of eleven extinct tongues singing their last true sentences. [545]

Miren did not go in. [550] She stood in the doorway with Teodor, listening, aware that in four hours the carts would come and the collection would be halved. [573] "We should catalogue it," he said, already grinning. [581] "Four hours," she agreed. [585] "We will do it badly." [590] "We will do it together," he said, "and then we will do it again, properly, from two cities." [608]

The empty glass ink well caught the first grey light. [618] Miren set it on the vault shelf, where it would stay: proof that the archive's last living language had been spent, gladly, on trust. [642] Behind them, the cylinders kept singing, and neither of them turned the sound off. [656]
