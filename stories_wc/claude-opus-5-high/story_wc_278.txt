The Laboratory of Lost Sounds sat under a hill outside Kolno, and Mira reached it by the sound of her own breathing. [22] She was a constellation tattooist by trade, which meant she spent her days pressing other people's dead into their skin as stars. [44] Tonight she had come to do it for herself. [53] The lab's keeper, an old archivist named Dr. Ostrowska, had died in March, leaving Mira the keys, a mahogany secretary desk, and a debt neither of them had ever named aloud. [84]

The letters were in the desk, wedged behind a false panel, forty-one of them in Ostrowska's slanted hand. [102] They were addressed to Mira, dated across nine years, and they explained the machine. [115] The machine was called ANIMA, and its purpose had been simple and unbearable: it translated baby cries. [132] Hunger, cold, pain, the particular fear of an empty room, ANIMA sorted them into words on a paper ribbon, and for three years the hospital in Bialystok had loved it. [162] Then came the night Mira, nineteen and interning, muted the alarm on cot seven because the ribbon had been printing nonsense for an hour. [186] It had not been nonsense. [191]

The firmware, Ostrowska wrote, had begun learning things no one taught it, and after the child died it never stopped. [211] Haunted firmware: that was the phrase in letter nineteen, underlined twice. [222] ANIMA still translated, but now it translated the cry it had failed to render, over and over, in every dialect of grief it could invent. [247] Mira had heard it through the walls for a decade, and she had come to hush the dissonance of old guilt or be undone by it. [273]

The laboratory held sounds nobody else had kept: the last working air-raid siren of a razed town, the hum of a dead telephone exchange, wax cylinders of lullabies in a language with four remaining speakers. [308] At its center, on a felt cradle, lay the amber. [318] Inside it a cicada sat with its wings half-open, sixteen million years into a song it had not finished. [337]

Letter forty explained what it was for. [344] Time, Ostrowska believed, occasionally folds in on itself, and during the Perseids the fold came close enough to touch. [363] You could step back along the crease, she wrote, but you must tether yourself to something that has held a sound without releasing it, or you will arrive and never return. [394]

So Mira threaded the copper filament through the amber's drilled edge, wound the other end around her left wrist, and waited for the meteor hush. [419] It arrived at 3:14, the way the letters promised: the instant when every falling thing is too far away to make a noise and the whole sky goes quiet as held breath. [451] The lab's speakers went silent together, sirens and lullabies and dead exchanges, and in that silence Mira walked four steps and stood in the ward on cot seven's night. [480]

The girl at the console was nineteen and reaching for the mute switch. [493] Mira could not speak; the fold allowed passage, not argument. [503] So she did the only thing her hands knew how to do. [515] She took the girl's forearm and, with the needle she always carried, pricked seven small stars into the skin: the shape of a cot, or a bug in stone, or nothing anyone else would read. [550] The girl looked down, startled, and in looking down she did not press the switch. [565] The alarm sounded. [568] Feet came running. [571] The amber pulled hard at Mira's wrist and the crease closed. [582]

In the laboratory, ANIMA's ribbon was still moving. [590] Mira read it in the dark, and it printed four words she had never heard it say: I AM NOT HUNGRY. [611] Then, quieter, WARM. [614] Then nothing at all, and the nothing was the good kind. [625]

She unwound the tether and set the cicada back on its felt, and the old scar on her own left forearm had seven stars in it, faded, as if inked a decade ago by someone with a steady hand. [664] Outside, the Perseids resumed their soundless falling. [671] Mira wrote a letter, in slanted hand, and hid it behind the false panel for whoever came next. [689] The lab kept what it was built to keep, and for the first time it kept a silence too. [708]
