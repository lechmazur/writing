Every morning for forty years, Ilsa set her easel at the end of the quay and painted the same harbor. [20] The tourists called her charming; the fishermen called her intimately aloof, a woman who knew everyone's grandmother's name and let no one past the doorstep of her attention. [48] Both were right. [51] She painted the harbor as it had been in 1974, with the sardine boats and the blue crane, because nostalgia was cheaper than paint and never ran out. [79]

In her coat pocket she carried a seashell, painted on its inner curve with a thumbnail-sized picture of a cave mouth. [100] She had made it at nineteen and promised herself, silently, that she would carry it back to the place it showed before she died. [124] She had told no one, which was the whole point; a promise spoken aloud belongs to the listener. [142]

The caves under the headland could only be entered after the mirror cracks. [155] That was the local phrase for the moment when the dawn water, flat as glass from the harbor wall to the point, finally shivered under the first land breeze, because the shiver meant the swell had died and the low entrances stood open. [198] It happened perhaps nine mornings a year. [205] For four decades Ilsa had found reasons: a commission, a bad hip, weather, cowardice. [219] On the morning the mirror cracked in September, she was seventy-one, and she put the easel in the skiff instead of on the quay. [243]

The coastal cave network ran three kilometers through the headland, a wet honeycomb the smugglers had used and the sea had edited. [265] She rowed in under the low arch, lamp on the bow thwart, counting turns the way her brother Anton had taught her the summer before he drowned: left, left, right at the chimney. [298] Fifty years of erosion had removed the chimney. [306] She took what she believed was the right passage and it narrowed, then bent, then opened into a chamber she had never seen. [329]

The wrong turn had delivered her to an oasis. [338] A freshwater spring came through the limestone and pooled in a basin of pale sand; ferns grew in the crevices, fed by a shaft of light that fell from somewhere above the gorse. [371] The water threw moving rings of gold onto the roof. [381] It was not the cave on the shell. [389] It was better, and it was nowhere, and no chart or grandmother's story had ever mentioned it. [406]

Ilsa sat on the sand for a long time with the painted seashell in her fist. [422] Then she understood what she had actually promised herself at nineteen, and the understanding arrived like a curtain pulled back: it had never been about finding the old cave. [451] It had been about painting one thing that no one would ever buy, correct, or hang above a sofa in Hamburg. [472]

She set up the easel in the sand. [480] She painted the oasis for three hours, the gold rings, the ferns, the black water at the passage mouth, and she painted it better than she had painted anything since Anton died, because there was no quay behind her and no 1974 in front of her. [526] When the light shifted off the pool she knew the tide was turning and she would have to go. [545]

She left the canvas leaning against the limestone, wet, where the next spring tide would take it. [562] Beside it she set the painted seashell, cave mouth upward. [572] A conservator would have called it vandalism against her own work. [583] Ilsa called it a temporary eternity, which is the only kind there is: a thing perfect and complete for the length of one tide, owned by nobody, remembered by one person. [614]

She rowed out by guess and came into daylight two hundred meters east of where she expected, which no longer troubled her. [636] Next morning she was on the quay again with the sardine boats and the blue crane, charming, unreachable, taking commissions. [656] A German couple asked whether she had ever painted anything from life. [668] Ilsa said once, and did not reveal anything further, and the couple bought a harbor scene and went away happy. [688] The sea kept the rest, for as long as the sea keeps anything, which is exactly long enough. [706]
