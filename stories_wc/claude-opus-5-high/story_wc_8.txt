The last petal of the old rosebush fell at dusk, and Ysolde Marn began walking. [15] She had planted the bush herself, twenty years earlier, on the platform at Hollowmere, and she had chosen it because roses keep an honest calendar. [40] No clock in the Republic did. [46] Hollowmere Station had carried no trains since the Concord War; ivy had taken the iron canopy, the ticket windows, the benches, until the whole ruin looked like a green animal sleeping on its side. [80] That was why the Ministry of Concord had put its Pit beneath it. [93] Ysolde was the Republic's fictional historian, and she said the title without flinching, because it was accurate. [110] For two decades she had written the histories the Republic needed: the martyr Alric at the Hollowmere bridge, refusing surrender, dying with the Concord banner in his teeth. [138] Children recited it. [141] Six provinces stopped killing each other over it. [149] Noble lies, her old teacher had called them, and Ysolde had believed in them the way a surgeon believes in anesthetic. [170] Then last spring she had found Alric's illuminated letter. [179] Gold leaf on the capital A, vermilion vines crawling down the margin, and in the founder's own cramped hand a plea to the enemy general: take the bridge, take my name, only spare the town. [214] Alric had surrendered, and the surrender had saved nine thousand lives, and that was a better story than the one she had invented. [237] The Ministry disagreed. [240] Her successor, Deputy Archivist Rell, filed the letter into the Pit. [251] The Pit was the Ministry's cleverest invention: a vault where a dangerous document was not burned but buried among four hundred forgeries of itself, each with its own gold leaf, each contradicting the others, so that any scholar who hauled one up could be laughed out of the lecture hall. [301] You could not destroy truth that way. [308] You only drowned it. [312] Ysolde had come to recapture it. [318] Rell was waiting under the ivy with a lamp, as she had asked him to be, on the night the last petal fell. [341] "You said you would tell me why," he said. "Not beg." [352] "I don't beg," Ysolde said. [357] "I am emotionally rational. [361] I grieve on schedule and I reason while I grieve. [371] Tonight is the schedule." [375] She did not argue about ethics; she argued by drawing parallels. [386] Kesse the Tyrant, she reminded him, had also kept a ministry of useful stories; he too had taught children a martyr who never existed; he too had called it healing. [416] The Concord had been founded to end Kesse, and now the Concord's own archive had a Pit, and Rell's title was the same word Kesse's man had used. [444] "A lie that must be defended forever is not medicine," she said. [456] "It is a diet." [460] Rell looked at the ivy, which had spent thirty years quietly taking a railway station apart. [476] "How would you tell which letter is his?" he asked, and she heard the question underneath: he had already decided. [496] "The forgeries were copied from my history," she said. [505] "Every one of them has Alric refusing. [512] The only letter in that Pit where he surrenders is the one nobody thought to invent." [528] They went down the maintenance stair together, past rails gone soft with rust, into the cold room where four hundred illuminated lies lay in numbered drawers. [554] It took until morning. [558] She found him in drawer three hundred and twelve: the cramped hand, the vermilion vines, the plea to spare the town. [579] Ysolde wept, briefly and without apology, and then wrapped the letter in oilcloth. [592] Publishing it would cost her every honor she had, and probably the peace of two provinces for a decade. [611] She had weighed that, coldly, for a year, and the arithmetic still came out the same: a republic that cannot survive a true sentence is not being protected, it is being embalmed. [643] Upstairs, light came through the ivy in green coins. [652] "They will say you destroyed the founder," Rell said. [661] "I will say I recaptured him," Ysolde answered. [669] She planted a cutting from the dead rosebush beside the platform steps before she left, because the next deadline would need a calendar too, and because Alric had asked for a town to be spared and somebody ought to grow something in it. [712] Then she walked the dead rails back toward the city, carrying one true page up out of a pit of lies. [733]
