The pub emptied at eleven, and after last orders Nell Ferrier walked the shingle to her rowboat like a woman going to work. [23] Everyone in Cranmouth knew where she went. [30] That was the trouble with the village: it kept only open secrets, worn smooth by handling, and the lighthouse on Sarn Rock was the smoothest of them all. [58] Unmanned since 1974, the fishermen said, and yet its lamp still stuttered on nights with no fog. [75] Nell wrote fortune cookie slips for the Lu Brothers factory in Perth, four hundred a week, paid by the hundred. [95] It was honest work and it had ruined her life. [105] Twenty years ago a man named Bax had shown her how a slip of paper could be a channel: not a message, but a carrier for one. [132] Print a fortune vague enough and any reader can hear their own orders inside it. [147] Bax had taught her to orchestrate the ambiguity, and she had been good at it, better than good. [165] Her slips had a habit of coming true. [173] A Glasgow accountant opened BEWARE THE SECOND STAIR and broke his ankle that night. [187] A trawler crew in Peterhead got YOUR LUCK IS UNDER THE WESTERN SHELF and hauled up a record catch. [206] Accidentally prophetic, she called it, and the accident had made her famous among people she never met and could not refuse. [227] Tonight she rowed out to end it. [234] The lighthouse door had no lock, only a jammed hinge and a smell of cold iron. [250] Inside, beneath the third tread, lay the last of Bax's legacy: two brittle pages of spycraft microdot film specs, emulsion grain sizes, developer ratios, the exact reduction to hide a page of text in a full stop. [287] The specs were obsolete; that was the joke of them. [297] Anyone could read them in a library now. [305] But the people who still used the channel believed in them the way parishioners believe in Latin, and so the pages remained the key, the thing that proved a fortune was a fortune and not just paper. [342] Nell had turned fifty-one in March and wanted, plainly and desperately, to transpose her life into a new key. [361] Same melody, she thought, different signature: still the cottage, still the gulls, still the slips, but no longer a courier for men in other countries who thought a woman on a rock was a switchboard. [396] You could not resign from an open secret. [404] You could only make it boring. [410] So she had spent the autumn doing the one thing she knew how to do: directing flow. [427] Not information, exactly, freight. [431] The Lu Brothers packed by region, and Nell chose which slips went into which crates. [446] For six weeks she had routed every genuine carrier-fortune into cookies bound for a single bankrupt buffet in Ostend, and every harmless platitude to the addresses her readers watched. [475] The channel had filled with nothing, politely, for a month and a half. [488] Now she climbed the ninety-two steps with the specs in her coat and a box of kitchen matches. [506] At the top, the lamp room smelled of paraffin and salt, and the old rotating mechanism sat under its canvas like a sleeping animal. [530] She lit the lamp, set the lens turning, and let it flash the sequence Bax had drilled into her in 1979, the signal that a channel was burned. [558] Half of Cranmouth would see it and say the lighthouse was playing up again. [572] The one man who mattered would see it too, from the ferry or the cliff road, and understand that the oracle had gone quiet. [596] Then she burned the microdot specs on the iron floor, one page at a time, and the smoke went up the vent like an answered question. [622] Rowing back, she found she had already written tomorrow's four hundred in her head. [636] They were all the same fortune, in different clothes: THE DOOR YOU THINK IS WATCHED IS ONLY OLD. [654] In the morning the pub would ask her, as it always did, what she had been doing out on Sarn Rock, and she would say, as she always did, writing. [684] They would laugh, satisfied, having confirmed the familiar enigma they kept the way other villages keep a war memorial. [703] And in eleven days a fisherman in Ostend would crack a stale cookie and read HE IS NOT COMING BACK, and weep, and be right. [728] Nell could not help that part. [734] The accident, she had come to believe, was simply the world agreeing to be read. [749]
