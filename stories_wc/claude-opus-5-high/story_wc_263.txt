Ilse was moonstruck, the village agreed, and they said it the way people name a weather. [16] She dreamed with her eyes open, and she heard the words nobody spoke aloud. [30] The baker's unsung lullaby, the smith's unspoken apology, the whole town's swallowed sentences pressed against her ears like snowfall. [49] Most of that pressure came from the north, from the arch. [60] It stood at the edge of the glacier, a span of black ice lit from within by the aurora, green and violet crawling through it like veins of thought. [89] Her master Bern had built it in the year of the great grief, after the sea took the fishing fleet and every artist in Kell stopped mid-work. [116] He gathered the unfinished things, half-carved prows and songs with no second verse and a mural of drowned men, and sealed them inside the ice so that failure could never touch them. [148] He died on the ice with the key still unwritten, and the arch kept humming. [163] Only Ilse could hear what it hummed. [169] Trapped work speaks constantly, and it speaks in the voice of whoever abandoned it. [183] Let me out, said the mural. [189] Finish me, said the song. [194] Ilse meant to free them, and Bern's own notes told her how. [206] The lock was astronomical: the arch would open to anyone who could trace the path of falling stars across the sky above it and write those paths into the ice. [236] For this she had his astrolabe, brass gone green, its alidade worn smooth by his thumb. [252] Each meteor she caught she measured, then scratched its arc onto the black span with a nail. [269] The work took years, though she could not have said how many. [281] Meteors do not keep to almanacs, so she kept no almanac either. [293] She lived across an unwritten calendar, counting not months but showers, the long drought of autumn, the sudden riot of the Leonids. [315] Her hair went grey at the temples in a season she never named. [328] The villagers brought her bread and left it at the ice, and she heard them not saying that she was mad. [349] She did not mind. [353] Under the aurora, with the instrument cold against her cheek, she felt something better than happiness and worse: a shadowed rapture, the joy of standing very close to a door that might open onto anything. [388] Bern had written one line she did not understand until the end. [400] The ice remembers stardust; so do you. [407] On the night the last arc closed the pattern, she understood it. [419] Every falling star she had traced was old matter coming home, and the calcium in her knuckles and the iron in her blood had been forged in the same furnaces. [449] Remembered stardust, calling to remembered stardust. [455] She was not reading the sky; she was being recognized by it. [467] The arch split with a sound like a held breath released. [478] Out came the voices, not as ghosts but as work: the mural's drowned men went up the glacier wall in green light, the second verse arrived whole in the throat of a girl asleep in the village. [515] Half-carved prows found hands willing to be clumsy. [523] Kell woke up talking, and for the first time in her life Ilse heard no unspoken words at all, because nothing was being withheld. [547] The silence was the most beautiful thing she had ever heard. [558] She sat down in the archway, under the ribbons of light, and set the astrolabe on her knees. [576] She had not rested since Bern died. [583] She had believed rest was a kind of abandonment, the thing that had trapped all that work behind ice in the first place. [606] Now she saw the difference. [611] Bern had stopped and held on; she could stop and let go. [623] So she rested, which was the last and hardest thing the pattern required of her. [638] The aurora went on tracing its own slow paths above the broken arch. [651] In the morning the villagers found her sleeping with her palms open, and they did not wake her, and none of them said aloud that she had been right. [680] She heard it anyway. [684]
