The telepathy pills were recalled on a Tuesday, three weeks after the static shock that emptied us. [17] Dov kept the recall notice on the refrigerator like a birth announcement, because he had never swallowed one and loved being right almost as much as he loved me. [46] "Affection first," he would say, kissing my temple, "skepticism second," and I never doubted the order. [62] Before the shock, I had been fluent in eleven minds. [72] Afterward I could not reliably find my own. [80] The pill had done to my inner speech what a river does to a canyon: carved it wide, then left. [100] Words arrived late, in the wrong sleeves. [107]

My light painting studio became the only room where I still made sense. [120] It was a black-walled shed behind the house, hung with LED wands, fiber whips, and a camera that held its shutter open like a held breath. [146] In the dark I moved, and the film remembered what I could no longer say. [161]

The sliver of muscovite from my mother's geode was my best brush. [173] Held over a penlight, the mica split the beam into leaves of light, layer within layer, fractally precise, each edge budding smaller edges until the wall wore a tree of glare. [204] Dov called it my willow. [209] Through glass, he said, hope looks like that: thin, bending, not yet broken. [222]

We needed a way to talk. [228] The clinic offered nothing; the manufacturer offered a coupon. [237] So we sat on the studio floor with a notebook and decided to devise a language that did not require me to hold a sentence still. [263] Dov's idea, and I credit him because he mistrusted it most, was to build it through somatic markers. [281] Not words, but sensations. [285] A thumb pressed to the inside of my wrist meant I am here and not leaving. [301] Two knuckles at my shoulder blade meant you are looping; come back. [313] Cold mica laid flat on my sternum meant remember the studio, remember the willow, the shock is over. [331]

He tested every marker like a man checking ice. [340] "This is just conditioning," he warned me. [347] "Pavlov with a wedding ring." [352] "Fine," I said, when I could find the word, "condition me." [363]

It took ninety-one days. [367] The markers worked the way a handrail works in a dark stairwell: they did not give me sight, but they let me move. [390] My motive was never to be fluent again. [398] It was to illuminate hidden pathways: the detours my ruined speech had started taking, the back roads from feeling to meaning that I had walked all my life without a map. [429]

In the studio we photographed them. [435] Dov would press a marker into my skin in the dark, and I would answer with the mica and the penlight, drawing the shape the sensation made inside me. [464] Wrist-thumb came out as a low steady arc, a footbridge. [474] Shoulder-knuckles branched like the willow, splitting and splitting, every fork a smaller fork. [487] Sternum-mica always bloomed outward from one bright seed. [495] Ninety-one exposures, and none of them lied. [502]

The gallery in town hung them in a spiral and called the show Recall. [516] Other pill-emptied people came, stood too long in front of the branching one, and cried without knowing why. [534] I taught the markers to eleven of them, which felt like an accounting settled. [548] Dov stood at the door taking names, still skeptical, still saying it was only touch and habit and a woman with a rock. [571] But on the last night, when the lights went down and my sentences scattered like startled birds, I felt cold mica on my sternum and his thumb at my wrist, and the words came back in order. [608] "Here," I said. [611] "Not leaving." [613] He laughed, wet-eyed, unconvinced, and entirely mine. [620]

The muscovite sits in a dish by the door now, in case anyone else arrives needing a handrail. [638] It is only a flake of rock, thin enough to bend light and not break it. [654]
