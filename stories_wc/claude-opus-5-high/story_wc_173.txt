They called her the chaste courtesan of the Glass Quarter, and they meant it as an insult until they saw her dance. [22] Sera sold nothing but attention. [27] She poured wine, she recited, she moved; she never let a patron's hand close on hers. [43] This was not virtue, she would have told you, but accounting. [54] In Varenne, feeling was currency, and the Conservatory took its tithe. [65] Every night the collectors walked the silver courtyard under snow with their brass spindles, gathering the echoes that rose off human bodies in the instant between sigh and smile. [94] That interval was the only harvestable moment; before it, longing was too raw, and after, joy went stale. [112] The harvest of echoes fed the city's great comfort: the lullaby. [123] Each dawn it drifted from the towers, spun from a thousand strangers' pleasures, and it told Varenne what to want. [143] Under it, no one argued, no one wept in the market, no one asked why the bakers all baked the same bread. [165] Sera called this the alarming serenity, and she was the only person she knew who could still hear it as a threat. [187]

Chastity was her insulation. [191] The spindles could catch an echo of touch, but hers was a bodily poetic thing - a grammar of shoulder and wrist, of breath held on a caesura - and it refracted instead of spooling. [224] So the Conservatory kept her, studied her, and left her unspooled. [235] They should have burned her. [240]

At the heart of the silver courtyard under snow stood the prism sanctuary, a low dome of fitted glass that drank starlight and split it into the threads on which every harvested echo was strung. [275] It had no door and no lock, which was why no one had ever thought to unlock it. [293] Sera had spent nine winters watching the collectors feed it, and she had learned the one truth the Conservatory guarded: the sanctuary was not a vault but a loom. [322] A lock made of knots opens by untangling threads. [331]

She went out on the longest night, barefoot, because the cold sharpened her counting. [345] Snow lay over the courtyard like combed wool, and the dome burned pale blue with a year of other people's joy. [366] The lone night-collector, a boy named Tiber with frost in his lashes, watched her kneel. [381]

"You cannot go in," he said, serene as a hymn. [391]

"I am not going in," she said. [398] "I am taking it apart." [403]

She found the first thread where the glass met the ground, a filament of someone's laughter tied to someone else's guilt, and she worked it loose with her fingernails. [432] Each knot she opened gave back a voice. [440] A fishwife swearing at the tide. [446] A father's ugly, honest sob. [451] A girl refusing a marriage in three plain words. [460] These were the echoes the lullaby had smoothed, the dissonances filed off to make the city sleep. [477] Her hands cracked and bled in the cold, and she did not hurry, because the work had a meter and haste would break it. [501]

Tiber could have called the wardens. [507] Instead he crouched and held the loosened strands off the snow so they would not freeze, and when she asked him why, he said he had a sister who used to argue. [539]

By the hour before dawn the dome stood as a skeleton of bare prisms, and the starlight came through unwoven, white and undivided. [562] Sera stood in the middle of it with a thousand freed threads in her raw hands and opened her arms in the old gesture she used to end a recitation. [592] The echoes went up over Varenne all at once. [601] There was no lullaby that morning; there was quarrelling in the market, and weeping, and a baker who tried rye. [621]

Sera did not see it. [626] She stayed in the courtyard until the wardens came, standing in the unstrung light, defying the lullaby of conformity in the only way a body can - by being, briefly, a poem no one could copy. [661] Tiber said she was smiling, but he had heard her sigh first, and he knew the exact and terrible width of what lies between. [685]
