They call me the Wick, because I keep the lamps burning in the stacks. [14] Officially I am a labyrinth maintenance worker, grade two, Harbor Authority, which means I oil the hinges and repaint the numbers on forty thousand shipping containers stacked nine high above the water. [46] Unofficially, I know the Labyrinth's moods, and I know its people by their walks. [60] Dockmaster Ilva lands on her heels like a stamp on a form; the pickpocket twins scuff in double time; Oren the Crier walks the way he talks, all forward weight and noise. [92]

Oren runs the stacks' message trade with lungs and megaphones. [102] I run it with folded maps. [108] The Labyrinth changes nightly, cranes rearranging the corridors like a puzzle nobody solves, so I fold paper: each crease a shift, each pocket of the fold a route that opens when the stack settles. [142] Unfold one way, you get Tuesday's Labyrinth; unfold another, you get tonight's. [154]

The challenge came on a Thursday, when the aurora warnings went out. [166] A geomagnetic storm was rolling over the port, and every radio, transponder, and satellite fix in the harbor was about to turn to soup. [190] Oren stood on a reefer's roof and shouted that the night belonged to volume. [204] "Wick," he called, "you and your paper. [211] Whoever gets a word from Gate Nine to the pilot boat at Berth Forty before midnight owns the trade." [230] I accepted before he finished, which looked like recklessness and was not. [242] I had been folding this night for three years. [251]

Here is the thing about shouting: it needs air, and air in the stacks is a bad courier. [269] Steel corridors swallow a voice in thirty meters and hand back nothing but its own echo, mocking. [286] A whisper, though, laid flat against a container wall, travels down the weld seams like water in a gutter. [305] I had spent three years mapping which seams touched which, welding little copper washers at the junctions, tuning the Labyrinth into an instrument. [328] Nobody noticed, because maintenance workers are furniture. [335]

At eleven, the storm arrived, and the harbor went blind. [345] Green light crawled over the cranes. [351] Compasses swung; the gantry loudspeakers hissed; Oren's megaphone worked fine and that was his mistake, because he had to shout into the same steel that eats shouts. [378]

I went to Gate Nine and took the message: a pilot's daughter had gone into labor, and the boat had to know to wait for a replacement. [405] Then I unfolded my map. [410] Tonight's crease showed the seam-line running from Gate Nine's corner post through Row C, under the walkway, across the reefer bank, to the bollard at Berth Forty - unbroken, if the stack had settled as the cranes promised. [447] One gap remained, at Row C, where a locked container had sat unmoved for nine years. [463]

Which is why I carried the key with no apparent lock. [474] My father, who held this job before me, left it to me with a shrug and no explanation, and on its ring hung a zodiac coin, the Ram, worn smooth. [504] I had tried that key in nine hundred padlocks. [513] Standing in the green dark, I finally read the coin instead of the key: the Ram, Aries, and stamped on the reverse the numerals 0321. [538] Container ARS 0321, Row C, unmoved for nine years. [547]

Its door had no padlock at all - just a flush brass plate my father had fitted, and a slot that took the key like a hand taking a hand. [576] Inside: a listening bench, a copper horn bolted to the wall, and folded maps stacked to the ceiling, forty years of them, my father's creases inside my own. [604] He had been building the instrument too. [611]

I put my mouth to the horn and whispered the pilot's message, once, slowly. [625] Then I walked out and waited, listening to Oren's voice break itself on the stacks a hundred meters away. [644] At 11:41 the pilot boat's horn answered from Berth Forty: two short, one long - the old acknowledgment, the one my father used. [666]

Oren came down off the reefer at midnight, and I knew him by his walk before I saw him - forward weight, but slower. [689] "How," he said. [692] I handed him the coin. [697] "Whispers travel farther than shouts," I said, "if you build the road first." [710] He laughed, and his laugh died in thirty meters, which was the whole argument. [724]

Now he learns folding, badly, and the trade runs on paper. [735] I still oil the hinges. [740] Nobody notices, which is the point. [746]
