Isolde Vane thought in facets. [5] Every question she touched split into seven angles, and she read them all at once, which was why the Order called her prism-minded and why they never let her near people. [36] They let her near paper instead. [41] For nine years she had catalogued the drowned library of Coil Harbor, and for nine years she had been looking for one thing: the place where forgotten combinations hide. [70] The Order had lost them when the old wardens drowned: the sequences that opened the deep vaults, the sums that unlatched the sealed archives, the counted turns that kept the harbor lamps burning. [103] Without them the city was slowly going dark, one sealed door at a time. [117]

She found her answer in a doodle. [124] It sat in the margin of a warden's inventory manuscript, a spiral of small round marks like scattered grain, drawn by a bored hand three centuries ago. [151] Under ordinary lamplight it was nothing. [157] But Isolde read documents the way she read everything, from all angles at once, and when she tilted the page into the narrow band between lamp and shadow, the doodle turned clandestinely luminous, each seed brightening with a number burning inside it. [199] The manuscript's last line named them: seeds of vigilance, sown at every threshold, to be gathered by whoever still watches. [219]

The wardens had not written their combinations down. [227] They had planted them in threshold effects: the flicker at the edge of a flame, the hum where water meets stone, the half-second when a door is neither open nor shut. [258] Numbers that only existed on a boundary, invisible to anyone who looked straight at them. [272] And the greatest threshold in Coil Harbor was the serpentine grotto, a coiling sea cave that filled with mist and turned itself inside out once a month, during the hour when gravity inverts. [305]

Isolde had been inside it once. [311] Her mentor Bram had gone in with her and had not come out, because when the Upturn ended he was still forty feet above the ceiling he had been standing on. [342] She had promised herself she would never reenter. [350] She reentered. [352]

The mist met her at the mouth like warm breath, and the walls curved away in slow serpent turns, glossy and ribbed. [374] She carried no lantern; lanterns lit things too plainly. [383] She carried a shuttered candle that threw more edge than glow. [394] At the eleventh coil the sea sighed, and the world let go. [406] Isolde fell upward and caught the ceiling. [413] Mist poured toward the roof in a slow inverted rain, and she walked the stone vault on her hands and knees with one hour of borrowed sky beneath her. [442]

Here the seeds were everywhere. [447] Where mist thinned against rock, where drip met pool, where her candle's edge grazed a crack, faint numerals surfaced and sank, each a fragment of a combination, none of them whole. [478] A single-minded reader would have gone mad chasing them one by one. [490] Isolde held seven at once, then twelve, then thirty, letting the facets of her mind turn until the fragments aligned into the sequences the Order had mourned for a century. [520] She wrote them on her forearms because paper would have floated away. [532]

In the forty-third minute she found Bram. [539] He hung in the roof mist where he had drifted and stayed, dry and light as a husk, and in his fist was a slate with four marks scratched on it. [570] He had found the lamp sequence. [576] He had simply run out of hour. [583] Something went hot behind Isolde's ribs, a slow blaze she had kept banked for nine years, and she did not let it burn her yet. [608]

She copied his four marks, took the slate, and started back along the ceiling, counting coils. [623] Eleven coils, ten, nine, and the mist began to hesitate. [633] At the mouth of the grotto she stood on the ceiling with the sea hanging above her like a grey lid, and she waited for the boundary, because the return was a threshold too. [667] When gravity turned over she was already falling, and she landed hard in wet sand under an ordinary sky. [686]

That night the harbor lamps came up one street at a time, and the city thought a miracle had happened. [706] Isolde sat in the reopened deep vault with Bram's slate on the desk and a fresh ledger before her. [725] In the margin she drew a spiral of small round marks, tilted the page to the edge of the lamplight until they woke and glowed, and sowed the next century's combinations where only a watcher would think to look. [764] Then she let the blaze come up, quiet and steady, and kept watch. [777]
