After the rainforest burned, the world caught a hush, and the hush did not leave. [15] Doctors called it chronic; sailors called it the thief. [24] Mira Vasek, last violinist of Echo Harbor, called it a room she had to enter. [39] She was acoustically perceptive in the way other people were superstitious: she heard the shape of a doorway before she touched it, heard rot inside a beam, heard grief two streets away. [71] Echo Harbor had earned its name honestly; a shout there came back to you three times, politely, like a neighbor returning borrowed tools. [94] Now a shout there died at the lip of the water, and the fishermen had stopped shouting at all. [113]

Mira packed her violin, a coil of dry cedar shavings, and her grandmother's mendicant's bowl. [128] The bowl was old brass, dented, worth nothing, and it had held everything: rice, rainwater, coins from strangers, and, once a year, the first coals of the family bonfire. [157] The Vaseks had no wealth and no land, only a tradition: every autumn they lit a fire and told the story of how they had arrived, each speaker stopping mid-sentence so the next could continue. [192] No one ever finished it. [197] That was the point. [201]

Three days south, past the ash-fall and the burnt smell that had followed the smoke across the sea, lay the sunken orchard greenhouse. [224] It had been built on a sandbar by optimists: glass walls, pear trees, fig trees, a dome like a held breath. [245] The sandbar had settled; the sea had come halfway in; and the current had gathered nine dead ships around it like listeners around a storyteller. [270]

Mira's grandmother had told her the hush was not a sickness but a knot. [284] Sound is bound into silence, she said, the way a fist binds a hand, and someone has to unbind it. [304] Mira had laughed then. [308] She did not laugh when she rowed between the shipwrecks and heard nothing: not her oars, not the gulls, not the glass. [330]

Inside the dome, the water stood knee-deep and clear. [339] Half-grown figs floated in it. [344] The pear trees were alive, stubborn, flowering out of season, and the ash had come in through the broken panes and settled on the blossoms like grey pollen. [372] She understood immediately, with her ear rather than her mind: the dome was the knot. [387] Curved glass, curved water, curved hulls; every sound that entered here had been folded inward and kept. [404] The hush was not absence. [409] It was a hoard. [413]

She found the old stone bench her family had used, dragged driftwood onto it above the waterline, and tipped the cedar shavings from the mendicant's bowl. [439] The flame caught without a sound, which was the loneliest thing she had ever seen. [454] So she began to speak, because that was what her family did around a fire. [469] She told the story of the crossing, the drowned uncle, the borrowed violin, the winter of the mendicant's bowl when her grandmother begged in three cities and never once sold it. [500] She could not hear her own voice. [506] She kept going anyway, and at the place where a Vasek always stopped, she stopped. [521]

Then she raised the violin and played the sonata she had never finished, the one with no ending because she had never found an ending worth the middle. [549] She played it to the last written bar and lifted the bow. [561] The dome answered. [564] Everything it had hoarded came unbound at once - forty years of gull cries, rain on glass, her grandmother's laugh, the creak of nine ships dying slowly - and it poured out through the broken panes toward the sea. [601] It was not music. [605] It was better: it was unfinished, all of it, every sound a beginning someone else had to answer. [623] Mira sat in the ash and the pear blossoms and heard the fire crackle. [637]

By morning, Echo Harbor was returning shouts again, three times, politely. [648] Mira rowed home with an empty bowl on her knees and no ending in her pocket. [664] She had gone looking for beauty in the unfinished, and had found that unfinished things are simply the ones still generous enough to need us. [689]

That autumn she lit the fire herself, in the dented brass bowl, and told the story to the harbor children up to the place where she always stopped. [717] A boy asked what happened next. [723] Mira handed him the bowl. [728] "You tell me," she said, and the harbor said it back to her, three times. [743]
