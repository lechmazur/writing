The grotto had been polished by ten thousand tides, and Mira used its walls as a chalkboard. [17] She was a logical dreamer, which meant she believed in miracles only after she had measured them twice. [35] Her instrument was her grandfather's balloonist's altitude gauge, a brass dial the size of a saucer, its needle still faintly trembling with the memory of high cold air. [63] The village above the grotto was dying quietly. [71] The fishery had collapsed, the young had gone inland, and the harbor filled each evening with a silence that felt like held breath. [94] Mira had come home to revitalize it, which everyone agreed was impossible, and which she therefore treated as an unsolved problem. [115]

Her method was trial balloons. [120] She built them from rice paper and candle stubs, each no bigger than a lantern, and each carrying a question written on its skin. [144] Balloon fourteen asked: what if the boats carried tourists instead of nets? [156] Balloon twenty-two asked: what if the grotto itself were the industry, and the village sold wonder? [172]

The gauge was the strange part. [177] When a balloon rose out of the grotto mouth, the needle did not climb the way altitude should; it swung, settled, and reported a number that had nothing to do with height. [209] Mira had tested it for a year before admitting what the data said: the gauge was reading outcomes across parallel timelines. [230] Each balloon drifted into a version of the village where its question had been answered yes, and the needle came back carrying that village's weight: its prosperity, its grief, its ten-year hum. [262] Tourists scored forty-one; selling wonder scored thirty-three. [269] A cannery revival scored nine, and Mira wept a little, because she had loved that one. [285]

She recorded everything by creating patterns with decorations on the grotto walls. [297] Mussel shells for low numbers, scallop fans for middling, and cracked abalone, iridescent as oil, for anything above fifty. [316] Over months the walls became a spiral mosaic, and the spiral became a graph, and the graph began to speak. [336] This was what she had wanted all along: not a plan handed down, but to puzzle out new equations for hope, derived honestly, testable by anyone with shells and patience. [365]

The pattern's message was not the one she expected. [374] Every high-scoring timeline shared one feature, and it was not an industry at all. [388] In each of them, the villagers had gathered somewhere together, often here, in the tide-polished grotto, and had argued and decided things themselves. [411] The number the gauge measured, Mira realized, was not luck; it was participation. [424]

So she did the illogical thing that her logic demanded. [434] She invited the village down at low tide. [442] Nineteen came, most of them skeptical, several of them her cousins, one of them the old harbormaster who had told her to stop wasting candle wax. [468] They stood in water to their ankles, lucidly watchful, while she explained the shells. [482] She expected mockery. [485] Instead the harbormaster picked up a mussel shell, turned it over, and asked what question had earned it. [503]

By midnight they had launched eleven balloons, each inscribed by a different hand, and the grotto filled with a serene upheaval: no shouting, only the small papery roar of things rising, and the needle swinging, and people leaning over a brass dial to read their own possible lives. [551] Her aunt's question scored sixty-eight: what if we teach the children to repair the boats we still have? [569] They set abalone in the wall for it, and the iridescence caught the lamplight and threw it across the ceiling like weather. [591]

Mira went up the cliff path near dawn, carrying the gauge, and did not look at it. [608] She did not need to. [613] Below her, in a hundred parallel villages and in this one, lamps were moving on the water, and someone was hammering a hull, and the equation had solved itself the only way equations of hope ever do: by being worked in company. [655] The needle, when she finally checked it at the top, read higher than it ever had, though she had not risen at all. [678]
