The delegates were four hours late, which suited Ilse Varga, because waiting was where she did her best thinking. [19] The glass atrium had been built for a botanist prince and abandoned to ferns, and beyond its panes a quiet brook rehearsed the same three notes. [45] Ilse unbuckled her coat, flexed the brass fingers of her mechanical arm, and considered the sundial on the terrace. [64] It was half painted. [68] Someone had gilded the morning hours and left the afternoon in bare grey stone, as though the artist had died at noon. [90] Her second, a nervous attache named Petru, set down the case of practice blades. [104] "They say you fence to avoid conversation," he said. [113] "I fence to continue it," she said, and saluted him. [123] For twenty minutes they filled the atrium with dignified chaos: ferns beheaded, a watering can rolling, Petru apologizing in three languages while Ilse drove him backward past the orange trees. [153] She had come to the Vessen border talks as the Republic's philosopher-second, the woman hired to argue and, if arguing failed, to bleed politely. [177] Her reputation was simple: diplomatically fierce. [183] She never insulted a man she intended to kill, and never killed a man she could instead persuade. [201] The arm had been the price of learning that order. [211] While they waited, Petru asked what she wanted from the treaty. [222] "To turn questions into destinations," she said. [229] "A question that stays a question becomes a war. [238] Ask it properly and it becomes a road, a bridge, a market town where two languages get married." [256] Then the steward brought her a package from the Duchess of Vessen's household: a danish tin sewing needle card, twelve needles in graduated sizes, no letter. [282] Petru called it an insult, an invitation to go home and mend socks. [295] Ilse turned the tin card in her brass fingers and thought of the Duchess, who had smuggled three peace offers past her own generals and lost three couriers to them. [325] A woman watched by her own army cannot write. [334] She can only send objects. [339] Ilse carried the needles out to the terrace, where the brook ran loud enough to hide a conversation. [357] The Duchess had spent last summer in this atrium, painting. [367] The Duchess had painted the sundial. [373] Twelve pinholes had been punched through the gilding, one in each finished hour, sized like a seamstress's insult. [391] She set each needle in its matching hole and watched the shadows lie down across the bare grey stone. [410] The unpainted half was not unfinished at all; it was ruled, faintly, into a protractor. [425] Each shadow crossed it at a coded angle, and the angles gave numbers, and the numbers gave a place and an hour: the millers' ford below Vessen, two nights hence. [455] A hidden overture, offered by a woman who could not be seen offering anything. [469] Petru went white. [472] "If her generals learn of this, they will call it treason and open the border with cannon." [489] "Then we must give them a louder thing to watch," Ilse said. [501] When the Vessen delegation finally arrived at dusk, she met them in the atrium and quarreled magnificently. [518] She insulted General Ordt's map, his moustache, and his grasp of the word sovereignty, then challenged him to first blood on the terrace, which is exactly what a garrison wants from a philosopher. [551] They fenced among the lanterns for eleven minutes. [559] She let him cut her sleeve, took his blade with the brass hand, and kissed his knuckles when it was done, so that every officer present rode home telling the same story: the Republic's madwoman fights, the Duchess sews, nothing is happening. [601] Two nights later, at the millers' ford, Ilse waited in the reeds with the tin card in her pocket. [620] The Duchess came alone, on foot, with a lantern and a folded map of a road that did not exist yet. [641] "You read a sundial in the dark," she said. [650] "I read the half you left unpainted," Ilse answered. [659] "That was the question," she said. [665] "This is the destination." [669] They talked until the water went pale, and by morning the brook had a bridge on paper, and the ford had a name in two languages. [695] Ilse kept the needle card for the rest of her life, and never once mended a sock. [712]
