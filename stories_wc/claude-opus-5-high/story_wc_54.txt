The carnival left on a Tuesday, and by Wednesday the town of Calder Flats had nothing loud left in it but wind. [22] Noor Achebi, nineteen and three weeks into an unpaid internship with the county's water authority, walked out to the old spaceport to see what the crowds had trampled. [50] The passenger terminal had been shut for twenty-two years, ever since the orbital line moved east, and the carnival had rented its cracked apron for a week of rides. [79] Now the tents were gone, and what remained was litter, tire ruts, and a silence with a ceiling ninety feet high. [100]

She loved the building the way some people love a difficult grandparent. [111] Her supervisor called it a liability; Noor called it a maze of intentions. [124] Four architects, three funding eras, and one panicked retrofit had layered corridors over corridors, drain lines under drain lines, each one built to serve a purpose nobody had written down. [154] Reading those old blueprints felt like listening to a room full of dead men argue. [168]

In the departures hall she found the carnival's leavings: a lost glove, a snapped ticket spool, and, under the dry fountain, something brass. [191] It was a piece of a sextant, the index arm with its little mirror, engraved on the back: J. TOVAR, SURVEY SHIP HALCYON, 2189. [215] Someone had won it, or lost it, at the carnival's ring toss; before that it had belonged to an explorer who measured angles between a horizon and a star to know where she stood. [249] Noor put it in her pocket and felt, distinctly, that she had been handed a tool. [264]

The fountain was why she had come. [271] Every traveler's account of the terminal mentioned it: a forty-foot water column in the atrium, lit from below, running day and night while ships burned upward outside. [298] The county had cut power to the pumps in the first year of the shutdown, and nobody had ever budgeted to resurrect it. [321] Noor had no budget either, which was, she had decided, a design constraint rather than an ending. [338] She was not brilliant; she was functionally creative, which meant she could look at a wrong thing and see the right thing it was almost shaped like. [365]

For six weeks she had traced the terminal's veins with chalk and a flashlight. [379] The mesa above the spaceport still shed its snowmelt into a stormwater culvert that dumped, uselessly, into a settling pond behind the freight sheds. [403] Thirty vertical feet of head, wasted every spring. [411] The old coolant loop for the departure gates ran within nine feet of that culvert, and its four-inch line rose straight into the atrium's pump gallery. [437] No electricity required, no new pipe worth mentioning: the fountain could be brought back through redirected flows alone, if the valves held and the math was honest. [464]

The problem was the maze. [469] Three of the junctions on the 2170 plans did not exist, and two that existed were on no plan at all. [490] She spent the day after the carnival crawling the service trench with the sextant piece in her pocket, and used its mirror, in the end, to see around a corner she could not fit her head into. [527] The lost junction was there, a bronze gate valve, stiff but whole. [539] It took her four hours and a length of scaffold pipe to turn it. [553]

She opened the culvert diversion at dusk and climbed to the atrium to wait, sitting on the cold terrazzo where ten thousand passengers had once queued. [579] The pipes began to knock, a slow arrhythmic knuckling that traveled up the building like something remembering how to breathe. [599] Then the fountain coughed, spat rust, and rose. [607]

It did not reach forty feet. [613] It reached perhaps eleven, a thick unsteady column that leaned, corrected, and held, catching the last daylight through the atrium glass. [634] Noor did not cheer. [638] She took out her notebook and wrote down the time, the estimated flow, the head loss she had guessed wrong by a factor she could now measure. [665] This was the work she had come for: to puzzle out new equations for hope, the kind with units and tolerances, the kind a county could be shown. [693] Hope that could be reviewed and stamped was hope that lasted past the season. [706]

She held up the sextant piece and sighted the top of the water against the dark rib of the roof truss, out of habit more than need, and named the angle aloud to the empty hall. [742] Somewhere, J. Tovar had done the same thing to find a coast. [754] The carnival would come back next autumn, and the town would be loud again for a week, and then it would not be. [777] The water, she calculated, would keep running until June. [786]
