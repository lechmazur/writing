I came to Vesper Hollow with a false name, a forged seed catalogue, and a flint arrowhead my grandfather pried out of the ridge in 1961. [26] Cryptid research pays nothing and trusts no one, so I told Odile Vance I was an apprentice who wanted to learn winter propagation. [49] She looked at my hands, saw no calluses, and hired me anyway. [61] Her garden lay eleven feet under the frost line, a cellar vault of grow-lamps and mirrored ducts where rosemary grew shoulder-high and the air smelled like August arguing with January. [91] I had come because of what happened after the missing return changed. [103] Eleven people walked off Kestrel Ridge in November; nine walked back in March, and they were not arranged the same way inside. [125] They hummed. [127] Not tunes - a harmony untamed, chords that refused to resolve, that slid sideways whenever you tried to write them down. [147] And they were gravitationally playful. [152] Spoons rose off their tables. [157] Their coats hung an inch above the hook. [165] One returned man, Odile's son Emmett, had to keep gravel in his pockets to stay seated at church. [183] My grandfather's arrowhead had been doing the same thing since I crossed the county line, tugging upward in my shirt pocket like a trout on a line, and that was the only evidence I trusted. [218]

Odile kept an old cordless phone on a shelf between the comfrey and the belladonna, its base unit wired to nothing I could find. [242] At 3 a.m. it rang, and the returned spoke through it. [253] That was the method, she told me: the changed could not explain themselves in daylight, but static-laden confessions came easy, the way grief comes easy to strangers on a bus. [283] I recorded forty hours. [287] Emmett's voice, threaded with hiss: "It isn't a place up there, Ma. It's a joke, and we're the punchline, and the punchline is kindness." [311] Another returned woman confessed she had stopped being able to hate her ex-husband, and wept about it for ten minutes. [331] That was when I understood I was not studying a cryptid. [342] I was studying a contagion of perspective. [349]

Odile had her own reason for tending a garden under the ice, and she gave it to me on the ninth night, with dirt to her elbows. [376] Her husband had died owing the mine money; her son had come back laughing at everything she had spent thirty years resenting. [398] "I want to turn my bitterness into a cosmic joke," she said. [410] "I have carried it so long it deserves a better career." [421] She had been breeding a hybrid - ridge moss crossed with something Emmett brought back in his boot tread - and it was the moss, she believed, that let a person metamorphose without going up the ridge at all. [458]

I should have reported it. [463] My institute wanted a specimen, a photograph, a skeleton in a freezer. [475] Instead I laid my grandfather's flint arrowhead in the moss bed, because he had walked off that same ridge in 1961 and come back wrong, and my family had spent sixty years calling it drink. [510] The arrowhead lifted an inch and hung there, turning, patient as a question. [523]

At 3 a.m. the cordless phone rang, and for the first time it was for me. [539] Under the static my grandfather said my real name, the one I had not used since I entered the Hollow, and apologized for sixty years of being unexplainable. [567] Then he laughed, and the laugh had that same unresolved chord in it, and I found I was laughing too, in a freezing cellar, with frost on the ductwork and my feet no longer quite touching the floor. [605] Odile came down the stairs with tea and did not seem surprised. [617] "Well," she said, "that's the funniest thing that's happened to me in thirty years." [631]

I filed no report. [635] In spring I planted the hybrid along the north road where the missing had walked, and the town began to hum, off-key and ungovernable, and spoons rose in kitchens. [664] It is not salvation; it is frostbitten hope, the kind that grows underground, in the dark, where nobody sensible plants anything. [685] But the arrowhead still floats above the moss, turning, and when the phone rings now I answer it on the first ring. [707]
