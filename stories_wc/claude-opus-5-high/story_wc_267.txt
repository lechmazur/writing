They sent me to the cobalt fortress to heal, and the first thing I learned was that nobody there would tell me anything aloud. [24] Snow had swallowed the lower windows, so the blue stone glowed underwater-dim in every corridor. [39] My hands were still waking up. [45] The surgeons had grafted new nerve into my palms after the frostbite took the old, and during nerve regeneration, they warned, everything would feel like insects and lightning. [73] A pianist with insect hands is a patient, nothing more. [83] The bellhop came for my trunk and did not greet me. [94] He belonged to the silent choir, the order that had sung the fortress through the siege without opening a single mouth. [115] They breathed hymns; they shaped vowels; no air left them as sound. [127] He wore a brass-buttoned uniform and carried my luggage with the seraphically mundane patience of an angel assigned to furniture. [147] I asked why the guests whispered. [153] He wrote on a slate: Sound is how they find us. [164] Outside, in the white, the listeners still walked their circuits, and the fortress had survived nine winters by giving them nothing. [185] I had come to be safe; I stayed to understand what safety had cost. [199] The answer was in the tower nobody used. [207] Its spiral staircase lay under nine years of dust, and I would have believed the whole story of disuse except for the slightest discoloration along the inner rail. [235] A pale ribbon, hand-width, where a palm had thinned the grey again and again. [249] Someone climbed here nightly, and took care to leave the treads untouched. [261] So I climbed too, on the edges, my raw fingers screaming at the cold iron. [276] The room at the top held a grand piano with every string cut. [289] The bellhop sat at it in his shirtsleeves, pressing keys that made no sound, his mouth moving through a hymn no one would ever hear. [314] When he saw me he did not startle. [322] He held up a bolt, old and rust-bloomed, and I understood the discoloration on the rail was iron leaving his hand. [343] It was the piano bench bolt. [349] On his slate, in small letters, he gave me the siege. [360] Fourteen villagers at the gate, begging in the snow; the listeners three fields away; the choir at the windows with the whole fortress in their lungs. [386] One shout would have opened the gate and ended us all. [397] They chose to remain silent, and they remained, and the villagers did not. [410] He had been nineteen, standing on that bench to reach the high window, and the loose bolt had squealed under his weight. [432] A single sound, thin as a bird, and a woman below had lifted her face to it, waiting for the gate. [453] The gate did not open. [458] That, he wrote, was the unpardonable silence: not the vow, but the hope he had accidentally given and then refused. [478] He had cut the strings himself, unscrewed the bolt, and carried it up these stairs every night since, practicing music that could harm nobody. [502] I sat beside him on the wobbling bench, and my new nerves burned, and I thought about the arithmetic of walls. [523] Nine winters of safety, bought once, then paid for nightly by one man with a rusted screw. [540] In spring, the reports said, the listeners had begun drifting south; nobody in the fortress had tested it. [558] I took the bolt from him, set it in its socket, and tightened it with my shaking, half-born fingers until the bench stood firm. [582] Then I fished a cut wire from the case, wound it back onto its pin, and played a single note into the cobalt dark. [606] It went out through the tower slits, over the snow, small and unforgivable and true. [621] We waited at the window all night, listening for hooves, for anything. [633] Nothing came, and by morning the sky over the fields was the raw pink of a healing wound. [651] Beside me, the bellhop opened his mouth, and let out a sound so hoarse and small it could hardly be called singing. [673] I told him it was a start, and that I would remain until we were both loud. [690]
