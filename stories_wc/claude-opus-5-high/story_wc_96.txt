In March he was Ash, and Ash slept in doorways and wrote poems for a penny apiece on the steps of the rusted library. [24] By April he would be someone else; he molted personalities the way the ruined city molted plaster, monthly, without apology. [44] The library had lost its roof in the Rupture, so the stacks stood open to the stars, iron shelving gone orange with rust. [67] Rain had welded the encyclopedias into bricks. [74] Ash liked that: books becoming masonry, a building rebuilding itself out of its own wrecked language. [90] Odile arrived as the moon cleared the skyline, carrying the coffee tin under her coat. [105] They had met eleven years earlier in the prison library at Marrow Hill, sorting donated paperbacks into crates that nobody would ever unpack. [128] She was serving four years for sabotage; he was serving nine months for being nobody in particular during a sweep. [148] Between the M's and the N's they discovered they both hated the same thing: not the wardens, but the ugliness. [168] Gray on gray, the deliberate drabness of a place built to make the soul agree with its walls. [186] That was the friendship: two people deciding that beauty was the only argument worth making. [201] Then the Rupture came, the government dissolved into feuding blocks, and ugliness got worse, because anarchy has no eye for proportion. [222] Odile set the tin on a shelf and pried the false bottom with her thumbnail. [237] Above it, ground chicory, in case the block militias searched her. [248] Beneath it, seeds. [251] Morning glory, foxglove, sunflower, wild mustard, bearded iris, all of it stolen from the botanical vaults the season the fences fell. [272] "Nineteen roofs and twelve courtyards this month," she said, "and the Halvers tore out everything we planted on Spinner Street." [292] "Then we rebuild Spinner Street," Ash said. [299] He always said we, though next month the mouth saying it would belong to a stranger. [315] Odile had known him as Ferris, who spoke only in questions; as Quill, who wept at weather; as Sparrow, who could not stop stealing spoons. [340] The prison doctors had a long word for it; Odile simply learned to love a person in installments. [358] Each self forgot the names of streets and the faces of friends, but none of them ever forgot the seeds. [378] That, she thought, was the proof of something: whatever he molted, the wanting stayed. [392] By midnight the customers came, as they did on every full moon, up the rusted stairs with their pennies. [411] Pennies were worthless now, which was the point; the price was a ritual, not a fee. [427] A penny bought four lines, composed on the spot, scratched onto the flyleaf torn from a water-swollen novel. [445] And folded inside each poem, three seeds. [452] That was the whole conspiracy. [457] No manifestos, no bombs, only a hundred strangers a month carrying flowers into the cracks of a broken city. [476] The militias could not shoot a morning glory, and could not explain why they wanted to. [492] A girl of maybe nine held out her coin and asked for a poem about her mother's roof. [510] Ash looked at the sky through the absent ceiling and wrote: The roof fell in so the light could get down here; mind the glass, it is only the window learning to shine everywhere at once. [546] The girl read it twice, pocketed her three seeds, and ran. [557] Odile watched Ash's hands and thought about Marrow Hill, about the crate of paperbacks, about how everything they had built began with alphabetizing somebody else's discarded words. [584] Near dawn she refilled the tin's false bottom from the pouch in her boot and set it on the shelf where the next self would find it. [611] "What should I call you in April?" she asked. [620] "I never know until it happens," he said, "so ask me then, and be patient with whoever answers." [638] She always was. [641] In April he was Wren, who stammered and hummed, who had forgotten Spinner Street entirely; Odile walked him there anyway, tin in hand. [664] The Halvers had scraped the beds down to rubble, and Wren knelt in it and began to rebuild the soil with his fingers, humming. [688] He did not remember the girl, or the poem, or the eleven years. [701] But by June the whole street was foxglove and sunflower, a luminous fracture running up through the concrete, and people came to stand in it the way they used to stand in churches. [734] Anarchy had given them a city with no shape; they were giving it one, petal by petal, penny by penny. [754] At the rusted library, under those indifferent stars, the tin stayed full and the moon kept rising. [771]
