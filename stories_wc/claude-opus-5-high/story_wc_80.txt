The drawbridge rose at nine, and Halvard Bay went quiet the way a held breath goes quiet. [17] Only after that did Mira Vance push her skiff off the ramp. [29] She was a shore surveyor for Ostrand and Bell, and the firm paid her to measure erosion, settlement, and the slow arithmetic of water taking land. [55] She did that honestly. [59] What she did after hours belonged to no client. [68]

Two hundred yards out, where the old resort lawn sloped under, stood the submerged pavilion. [83] Its cupola broke the surface like a knuckle; below, eight iron columns held a dance floor no one had danced on since the reservoir was raised in 1961. [111] Mira tied off, stripped to her wetsuit, and went down with a slate and a lamp. [127] The lamp made the water a hushed blaze, gold pressed flat between green walls. [141] She had been mapping the pavilion for three summers, not for Ostrand and Bell, but for a private catalogue she kept in a ledger at home, titled simply: Unfinished. [170] She wanted to be the archeologist of abandoned dreams. [179] Somebody ought to be. [183]

That night the silt near the northeast footing had shifted. [193] Something square glinted under a mat of root and rust. [203] She had to wrest it free with both hands, bracing a knee against the column, and when it came it came suddenly, trailing a slow brown cloud. [230] An antique traveling inkwell: brass case, hinged lid, a glass well inside still sealed, the ink dried to a black lens. [251] Engraved on the lid: T. ABERNATHY, ARCHT. [258] She knew the name from the county archive. [266] Thomas Abernathy had drawn the pavilion, the bathhouse, and a lakeside library that was never built, and had died in 1959 with the library still only lines on vellum. [295] He had carried his ink to the site, then, and lost it where his best building would drown. [313]

Mira surfaced into a night so still she could hear the drawbridge counterweights ticking as they cooled. [330] Her method was simple and slightly criminal. [337] Whenever the firm sent her to inspect a foundation repair, she wrote a note and set it in the work before the grout went in. [362] One line each, the plainest facts she could find: the name of a dream and the reason it stopped. [381] Under the new seawall at Kessler's dock: a fisherman's plan for a co-op cannery, ended by a bad winter. [400] Under the culvert on Reed Street: her mother's nursing degree, traded for a second job the year Mira turned nine. [420] The notes were not memorials. [425] Memorials faced outward. [428] These faced down, into rock, where the town's weight would sit on them and nothing would be asked of anybody. [448] She had come to think of the town as a structure of interlaced absence: every foundation resting on some abandoned thing, the missing pieces braided so tightly that they carried load. [479] Take them out and the whole shore would sag. [488]

People at the firm called her cold. [495] She let them. [498] It was easier than explaining that she felt the shoreline the way some people feel weather in an old break, that every survey stake she drove went through somebody's plan for a porch. [531] Stoicism was just the lid on the well. [539]

There was one line she had never written down. [548] Her own. [550] At twenty-three she had been accepted to study conservation in Trieste and had stayed instead, because her father was dying slowly and somebody had to hold the house. [578] Fourteen years on, she was still holding it, and she had never been able to make that sentence small enough to fit on a slip of paper. [605]

In the morning she took the inkwell to her kitchen table and worked the lid with a scalpel until the hinge gave. [627] Folded against the glass, dry as a wasp's wing, was a scrap of paper in Abernathy's hand: Library, south light, if I am not given the money I will give the drawings to the county. [662] He had made his own note and buried it in ink, sixty years before she invented the practice. [680] She sat with that a long time, the kettle going unheard. [691] Then she wrote two slips. [696] The first: Thomas Abernathy, lakeside library, 1959, unfunded. [704] The second: Mira Vance, Trieste, 1998, deferred. [711]

That Thursday the firm had her sign off on the underpinning at the ferry terminal, and while the crew broke for lunch she pressed both slips into the wet grout and smoothed it flat. [745] Then she mailed Abernathy's note and his drawings' file number to the county historical board, because a dream you have catalogued is no longer only yours to carry. [773] That night the drawbridge rose at nine, and she rowed out to tell the pavilion. [788]
