Emory Vance had sold maps for forty years and traveled almost nowhere. [12] His shop above the creek smelled of linen paper and old glue, and in the evenings it smelled of one person eating dinner. [35] He was empirically intuitive, a phrase Ilse had coined for him: he guessed, then he measured, and he was honest about which came first. [59] Ilse had run the last type foundry in the valley, casting letters from brass matrices, and she had loved caves the way he loved coastlines he would never see. [88] Thirty years ago, the night she left for Trieste, she pressed a type foundry matrix chip into his palm: a square of brass punched with a manicule, the little pointing hand printers use to say look here. [125] "Midsummer," she said, "at the doorway of decisions." [133] He had laughed, and she had not, and that difference had lingered in him for three decades like a splinter under the nail. [156]

In April a lawyer in Trieste mailed back the letters he had written her, unopened except the first, and with them a sheet of foundry proof paper printed with a map he had never drawn. [191] It showed the creek, the limestone ridge behind his shop, and no route at all - only a manicule inked at the top, pointing down the page at nothing. [219] A dealer of antique maps knows that a map without a route is a dare. [234]

He tried surveys, dowsers, and the county cave registry, wanting only one thing now - to find the doorway of decisions before midsummer - and found nothing; then, on the hottest afternoon in June, he watched ants. [269] Ilse had taught him the trick decades earlier - following ants home finds what maps omit, because the industrious ones nest where the rock exhales cold air. [295] For nine days he crawled the ridge on his knees with a jeweler's loupe, following a black thread of ants up through the sumac to a seam behind a curtain of wild hydrangea. [328] Cold air poured out of it, smelling of wet stone and, faintly, of printer's ink. [343]

He went in on the twenty-first of June, as the creek rose with three days of rain, which was stupid and which he did anyway. [368] The passage dropped, narrowed, and opened. [374] The chamber beyond was blooming. [379] Gypsum flowers curled from the walls in white petals, aragonite sprays opened like frost roses, and a shaft somewhere above let in a single midsummer blade of light that struck the crystals and set the whole room to a slow glimmer. [420]

At the far end the passage forked in two, and between the forks someone had long ago set a lintel of quarried stone. [443] Saltpeter miners, he guessed, then measured the tool marks with his thumb and believed it. [458] Across the lintel, stamped into soft lead, ran a line of type: THE DOORWAY OF DECISIONS. [474] She had cast it herself, at nineteen, the summer they met. [485] The line ended in a blank space the width of one sort, because the matrix for the manicule had left the valley in his coat pocket in 1994. [513]

He took the brass chip from his pocket and fitted it into the gap; it sat as though the gap had been waiting, which it had. [539] The promise had never been a rendezvous; it had been an instruction, pointing, look here, choose. [555]

Beyond the lintel, the left fork went down toward the water table, and the right fork climbed toward daylight he could smell rather than see. [580] Below, the creek was rising into the system; he could hear it arriving, a low patient sound, filling the passage he had come in by. [605] For thirty years he had been the man who waited in a shop for other people's journeys to be worth money. [626] He guessed right, and then he measured: airflow, the slope of the floor, the direction the crystal blooms leaned, all of it arguing for the right-hand fork. [653] He left the matrix in the lead where it belonged, finishing her sentence, and went up. [669]

He came out at dusk on the far side of the ridge, muddy, bleeding at the knees, into a field of midsummer light. [692] Below him the creek had taken the footbridge and was working on the road. [706] In the morning he put a sign in the shop window: CLOSED, GONE TO SEE THE COAST. [723] He drew the route himself, and for once he was not selling it. [736]
