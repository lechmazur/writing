Mireth called herself a stray philosopher, which meant she had no school, no stipend, and no home but the mechanical rainforest. [21] She had come here after the sixth sense awakened in her, three winters ago, uninvited as weather. [38] It was not prophecy and not telepathy. [45] It was the hum of insight: a low vibration behind the sternum that arrived whenever a true thought stood nearby, the way a tuning fork answers its twin. [73] The trouble was that the hum had gone quiet in the cities, where every thought was already thought. [91] So she walked into the brass jungle to rediscover the wild frontier of her own mind. [107]

The rainforest grew itself. [111] Copper ferns unrolled on ratchets; vines of chain climbed pylons of iron bark; canopies of tin leaves turned to catch the rain that oiled them. [136] Nobody had built it, or everybody had, long ago, and it had kept going. [150] Mireth learned to read it by the sound of wind through different trees. [163] Steel palms hissed like rumor. [168] Brass bamboo made a fluting, patient sound, the sound of a question waiting. [181] Wire willows gave a sound like a mind changing itself. [191] Where three tree-voices braided together, the hum in her chest rose, and she stopped and thought and usually found something worth thinking. [213]

This was how she found the gear. [220] She woke with it in her palm. [227] That was the other thing about her, the inconvenient thing: she translocated during sleep. [241] She would lie down beneath a ticking fig and wake nine miles east, boots dewed with condensation, dreams stitched to places her waking feet had never chosen. [268] The philosophers of the academy had called this a symptom. [278] Mireth had come to call it a method. [286] Her sleeping self went where her waking self was too reasonable to go. [299]

The small gear was no bigger than a thumbnail, bright as a new coin, with eleven teeth. [316] Everything in the rainforest ran on twelve. [323] The hum came so hard she sat down. [331] She had woken in a clearing she did not know, at the foot of a great trunk of riveted iron, and high in that trunk was a socket where an eleven-toothed gear belonged. [364] Her sleeping self had taken it out. [371]

She understood, sitting there, that the forest was a single argument, and that this gear was its flaw: the odd tooth that kept the whole machine from closing into a perfect, repeating loop. [404] Remove it, and the forest would unravel. [411] Leave it in, and the forest would go on grinding the same green thought forever, a city of metal thinking one sentence. [433]

She stayed three days in the clearing. [440] She listened to the wind. [445] The steel palms hissed their rumors; the brass bamboo held its patient question; the wire willows said, over and over, that a mind that cannot change is only a very elaborate stone. [477] On the second day, she felt the academy's tidy proofs unravel in her, thread by thread, and did not mourn them. [498]

On the third night she did not fight sleep. [507] She woke at the top of the iron trunk, two hundred feet up, straddling a branch like a lunatic, with the gear still in her fist and dawn coming up copper through the tin leaves. [542] This was the country she had come for: the place where probable miracles lived, where the impossible was merely the unlikely with better manners. [566]

She did not put the gear back. [573] She turned it over, filed one tooth thinner with the edge of her knife, and set it into the socket at a new angle, so that it caught and slipped, caught and slipped. [606]

The forest did not unravel. [611] It stuttered. [613] Down the whole valley, gears took a half-beat of hesitation, and in that hesitation the wind found new gaps to move through, and the trees made sounds no one had ever heard them make. [647] The hum in Mireth's chest rose until her teeth ached with it. [659]

She laughed, and climbed down, and walked on, a stray philosopher with nothing but a knife and a listening chest. [679] That night she lay down and let her sleeping self choose the road again. [692] She had stopped asking where it would take her. [701] The frontier, she had learned, was not a place on any map; it was the half-beat of hesitation inside a thing that had forgotten it could hesitate. [728] Behind her, all night, the strange new music of the trees went on unwinding itself, and did not repeat. [747]
</story>
