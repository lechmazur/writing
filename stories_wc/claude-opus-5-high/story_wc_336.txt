The orchard grew on the parapet, a green ribbon of trees rooted in mortar above the falls. [17] Mira had climbed to it every spring since she was six, and every spring Odris the pruner let her carry the basket and nothing else. [42] This spring he handed her the shears. [49] "Upon the hush of budding hope," he said, "the wood remembers both its worlds, and a careless cut can strand a country." [71] She knew the story. [75] Behind the orchard, where the waterfall threw its perpetual mist, the branches of the oldest quince crossed and recrossed until the eye lost them, and through that weaving a person could step sideways out of the world. [112] Odris had done it twice in fifty years. [120] Mira wanted to do it daily. [126] She was insatiably creative, and it made her dangerous to orchards. [137] She had grafted pear to hawthorn to see what would happen; she had painted the beehives in nine colors; she had built a loom out of pruned switches and woven a blanket nobody could look at without smiling. [175]

"Cut here," Odris said, tapping a water sprout. [183] "And tell me what you see through the gap." [192] Mira cut, and the lattice opened a hand's width, and the radiant hush of the other place came through like breath on a mirror. [216] She saw a valley in the exact silver of an overcast noon: hills, rivers, terraces, people; all of it perfectly shaped and utterly gray. [240] "They lost their color four generations ago," Odris said. [249] "A blight of the eye, they call it." [257] "Children there are born unable to be surprised." [265]

Mira's hands were shaking. [269] "Then we bring them succor," she said. [276] "We carry the whole orchard through, root balls and hives and my blanket, everything bright we own." [293] "And the gap closes in three days, and the trees die in their alien soil, and you have spent a world's wonder on a week of it," Odris said. [322] He reached into his coat and set something in her palm: a seed, wrinkled and dull, the size of a thumbnail. [343] "Heirloom," he said. [347] "Older than the wall." [351] "My teacher's teacher pruned the mother tree, and the mother tree is gone, and this is what remains of her." [371]

"One seed," Mira said. [375] "Against a whole gray country." [380] "Pruning is not subtraction," he said. [386] "It is how we keep a thing by capturing essence, one bud that holds the argument of the entire tree." [407] "That is what you must learn before you go weaving between worlds, girl." [419] "The color of wonder is not a quantity to spend; it is a seed to plant and leave." [437]

She went at dawn on the second day, alone, because he was too old for the sideways step and they both knew it. [460] She carried the seed, a trowel, and nothing else, not the blanket, not the hives, not one bright thing to make herself feel generous. [485] The gray valley smelled of wet stone and patience. [492] The people who gathered were kind and incurious; they helped her find a slope above their own falls, a terrace of good soil that no one had thought to plant. [522] She dug, set the heirloom seed in, and pressed the earth with both thumbs. [536] Nothing bloomed. [538] A child asked what it would look like, and Mira found she could not say, and the child's face did something it had never done: it waited. [565] That was the whole harvest of the journey, and it was enough to carry her home before the lattice knit shut. [586]

Odris was waiting on the verdant parapet beside the waterfall, shears hung on his belt, mist beading in his eyebrows. [606] "Well?" he said. [609] "I planted it and I left," Mira said, "and I want to go back so badly I can taste it." [629] "Good," he said. [632] "Wanting is the pruner's trade." [637] "You will go back in fifty years, or your apprentice will, and there will be a mother tree on that terrace, and somebody will be arguing with her neighbor about the color of the fruit." [672] Mira laughed, and the quince buds glowed above the water, and the hush between the two worlds held them both like cupped hands. [695] Then she took up her shears and began, very gently, to close the door. [709]
