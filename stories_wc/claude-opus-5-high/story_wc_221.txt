After the shutdown, the station made a sound I had never heard: nothing. [13] The dilation engines had hummed for sixty-one years, and their silence echoed down the corridors like a voice calling its own name. [35] I walked to the time dilation observation deck because that was where I had been born, and where I had been ruined. [57] My mother was a lens-keeper, and she carried me in her body onto the deck when the shielding failed. [76] For eleven minutes of station time she stood in the unbuffered field, watching the accretion disk burn, and in that window I became temporally stretched. [101] My heartbeat runs slow; my words arrive late; a conversation with me costs a listener their afternoon. [118] The colony decided I was a blessing, since I had survived what should have unmade me, and then decided a blessing is best kept at a distance. [145] Children touched my sleeve for luck and ran. [153] The council gave me a stipend, a private berth, and no work. [165] Blessed pariah: that is the whole of my title, and I wore it for thirty years without arguing. [183]

What I had instead of work was my mother's coat. [193] On its back she had sewn a gilded compass rose applique, cut from the ceremonial banner of the ship that first surveyed this black hole. [218] Eight points of tarnishing gold, and the north point longer than the rest. [231] She told me the old ships carried it as a joke and a prayer, because out here there is no true north, only the direction you agree to keep. [260]

Every anniversary of the accident, she took me to the deck and we sketched the night sky. [277] It was a family custom older than the station, handed down from her grandmother on a colony barge: on the day a thing happened to you, you draw the stars above it. [309] Sixty-one sheets accumulated in a folio, and because the deck's field bent light, no two sketches ever matched. [327] The same stars, smeared into different arcs, year after year. [337] My mother died confused, asking which of the drawings was correct. [348] I could not answer her in time. [355]

After the shutdown, the engineers said the field would decay slowly, over about nine hours, and then the deck would be ordinary space. [378] Nine hours to them; something like four days to me. [388] So I brought the folio, the coat, and charcoal, and I sat under the great window while the station emptied itself into the evacuation ships. [413] Footsteps came and went above me, doubled by the empty halls, then stopped. [426] In that echoed silence I did what my mother taught me, and I drew. [440]

As the field decayed, the smear of stars began to unwind. [451] Arcs shortened into commas, commas into points. [458] For one interval near the eighth hour, I got a clear glimpse of the sky as everyone else had always seen it: hard, still, unbent. [483] I sketched it fast, and when I laid the new sheet over the folio and held both up to the window, the sixty-one wrong skies aligned around it. [511] Each distortion was a measurement. [515] Sixty-one years of my family's grief, accidentally recording how deeply the field bent light at every distance from the hole, a map no instrument on the station had been permitted to make, because the engines were always running. [553] My confusion had been an instrument all along. [561]

I turned the coat inside out and pinned the sheets to its lining, arranging them around the gilded compass rose so that the north point ran through the clear sky. [591] A direction I had agreed to keep. [598]

The last shuttle pilot found me there and shouted from the hatch that I had six minutes. [615] To me it was most of a day, and I used it to finish the annotations. [631] On the ship, they kept their distance as always, the blessed pariah in her strange slow voice, until I unrolled the lining across the mess table. [657] The survey chief looked at it for a long time. [667] Then she asked, slowly, patiently, in words spaced for me to catch, whether I would chart the next hole. [686] It would cost me decades that the crew would not spend, and I said yes before she finished. [704]

My mother had asked which drawing was correct, and the answer, which I keep pinned inside my coat, is that none of them are. [728] They are all directions, and I am going to draw every one of them. [742]
