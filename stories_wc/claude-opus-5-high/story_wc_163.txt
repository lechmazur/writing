The lake swallowed Vellhaven eighty years ago, and Mirin came down to read what the water had rearranged. [18] She was a landscape astrologer, trained to chart fate not in stars but in the slopes and seams of ground, and the drowned city was the strangest chart she had ever been handed. [51] Her diving bell settled on the roof of the old research facility at dusk, and a hatch opened from beneath like a held breath released. [76] Oren was waiting at the bottom of the ladder with a lamp made of a glass float and forty living jellyfish. [97] "You're the astrologer," he said. [102] "Nobody has come down in eleven years." [109] He was the last caretaker of a place the mainland had forgotten to remember, and he was softly ingenious in the way of men who have only their hands and a great deal of time. [144] Pumps ran on pedal power; the air smelled of moss because he had planted the corridors with it; a bellows organ in the stairwell played when the current shifted. [173]

Mirin unrolled her charts on the flooded-cold floor of the observation dome and weighted them with bolts. [190] She traced the city's buried ridgelines with a burnt feather, the only instrument she trusted. [205] It had been a heron's, pulled from the ash of her mother's observatory the night the hill fire took it, and its charred spine drew a line finer than any pen. [236] Her mother had taught her to read the ground and then to leave it; Mirin had never stayed anywhere past a single season. [259]

"The seams here converge once a year," she told Oren. [269] "Tonight the lake gives back light instead of taking it." [279] He nodded as though she had confirmed a private theory. [289] "The bloom. [291] It comes with the warm water, and it lasts until sunrise, and then it is gone for another year." [310]

At the tenth hour the dome began to glow. [319] Plankton rose through the streets of Vellhaven in slow blue rivers, pouring over balconies, filling the bell tower, outlining a cathedral nobody had seen in eighty years. [346] It was a luminous respite, one night of light in a place built for permanent dark, and it did what light does: it captivated. [370] Mirin forgot her instruments for a full hour. [378] Oren did not look at the water at all; he watched her watching, and adjusted his jellyfish lamp so the glass would not glare. [402]

They worked together after that, in the shadowseam hours when the glow pressed against the seams of the dome and everything had two edges, one bright and one black. [431] He knew where every drowned street ran, because he had swum them; she knew what the pattern meant, because she had spent her life learning the grammar of ground. [460] With the feather she marked the convergence and found, at its center, the facility itself. [475] "Your chart says this room is the heart of it," Oren said, almost apologizing. [489] "Mine says it's only a room where I eat my supper." [500] "Both can be true," she said, and understood then what she had actually come down for. [516] Not the bloom, not the charts: she wanted to learn the rhythm of belonging, which is the one pattern her mother had never drawn for her. [542]

The glow thinned near the fifth hour, as if the lake were exhaling. [555] Oren opened the last hatch and they climbed up through the tower shaft into open air, standing on the tip of a drowned steeple with the reeds around them. [584] The east was gray; the bloom below was almost spent; and then the reeds lit. [599] The first fireflies of summer came up out of the marsh in twos and threes, and Mirin and Oren breathed out at the same moment, a shared exhale that fogged and vanished between them. [633] It was the smallest thing, and it was the answer. [643] Belonging had a rhythm after all, and it was two lungs keeping time over the same patch of water. [662]

When the sun came up she did not reach for her charts. [674] She gave Oren the burnt feather instead, and told him to keep it dry, because she would need it next summer, and the summer after, and she intended to come back down for every bloom she had left. [712] He turned it over in his palm, this thing saved from one fire and carried to the bottom of a lake, and said he would build it a case. [741] Below them, Vellhaven went dark and patient, holding its one night of light in reserve. [756] Above them the fireflies kept rising, and neither of them went inside. [768]
