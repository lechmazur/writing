Locks taught Mira patience, and patience taught her to look sideways at the world. [14] She was peripherally focused by trade; a tumbler's flaw never showed itself to a direct stare, only to the edge of attention, the way faint stars do. [41] That habit was why the quarry masters sent for her after the collapse. [53] The blast had opened a marble cavern under the eastern ridge, a room the color of skimmed milk, veined like the inside of an eyelid. [78] Three men had died opening it, and the season went quiet afterward, a wounded season of shuttered kilns and unsown terraces. [99] Across the lull of that season, Mira went down into the cavern nearly every day. [113]

The cavern did something no one could explain: it kept sounds. [124] Not as ordinary echoes, which die in a breath, but stored and folded and released later in thin bright ribbons: a hammer strike from an hour ago, a name called from last week. [157] The old quarry records called it the harvest of echoes, and claimed the cavern gave up its hoard only within a narrow window, once each cycle. [183]

Whoever had harvested before her left behind a broken timer mechanism bolted to a plinth of raw stone. [201] Its brass case was split, its escapement gone to green crumbs, its dial marked in symbols she did not recognize. [221] A locksmith cannot help herself; she set out to reverse-engineer it. [232] The trouble was that it was not a clock at all. [243] It had no mainspring, no weight, nothing to drive hands around a face; it had only a mirrored vane, a slotted aperture, and a pointer meant to be read, not turned. [274]

So Mira did what the device required, and spent the wounded months studying reflection angles at different times. [292] Dawn light came through the shaft and struck the polished marble wall, and the wall threw it to the floor, and the floor threw it up into the vane. [321] She chalked the bright patch's position each morning and evening, and by the fortieth day she had a lattice of marks crawling across the stone like a vine. [349] The marks bent as the sun sank lower with the dying season, and their bend was the answer: the mechanism was a lock, and the light was its key. [378] When the reflected beam finally entered the slotted aperture head-on, the window would be open. [393] She could not see the angle by staring at it, because the glare erased everything. [408] So she watched from the side, letting the edge of her eye do the measuring, as she always had with tumblers. [429]

Her reason for wanting the harvest was not profit, and she knew the masters would not understand it. [447] Mira had been a reflective woman all her life, which is a polite way of saying she doubted things until they proved themselves twice. [471] Doubt had served her well and left her poor in astonishment, and she had come to understand that astonishment, like cider, must be laid down while it exists. [499] She wanted to store wonder in the cellar of reason, for later: jars of it, stoppered, shelved beneath the floorboards of a sensible mind, to be opened in some winter when nothing seemed worth believing. [534]

The window opened on the last grey afternoon of the season, eleven minutes wide. [548] The beam slid into the aperture, the vane turned itself, and the cavern began to give back everything it had been keeping. [570] Chisels rang, men laughed, rain drummed on a tarpaulin that had rotted years ago. [584] And then, near the end, three voices she had been dreading and hoping for, arguing amiably about a wedge and a fault line, one of them her brother's. [612]

She did not turn toward the sound. [619] She listened the way she worked, off to the side, and let it pass through her without breaking. [637] When the eleven minutes closed, the cavern was only stone again. [648]

She carried the broken timer home in her apron, not to mend it, but because a lock you have opened honestly is worth keeping. [672] In her cellar she wrote the whole of it down, the angles, the chalk marks, the eleven minutes, the voices, and set the pages on the shelf where she kept her cider. [704] Next year the kilns would smoke again and the terraces would be sown, and she would be too busy and too sane to believe any of it. [731] That was fine. [734] It was stored. [737] She had built the lock, and she had left herself the key. [749]
