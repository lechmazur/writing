They brought Ilsa to the sanctuary at pearl twilight, when the aurora lay across the marble like spilled milk. [19] She was the last calligraphy forger in the province, and the Chancellor needed a hand that could lie beautifully. [38] Water fell from the stalactites in slow beads, counting out the hours the way her old master used to count breaths before a stroke. [62] The Chancellor unrolled the Accord of Nine Rivers across the altar of the marble sanctuary. [77] One signature was missing: that of Marek Vel, her master, who had died mid-letter with ink still wet on his fingers. [98] The Accord bound all nine clans to the crown's war levy; Marek had refused it, and refusal in a dead man's hand was worthless. [122] So forge the consent, the Chancellor said, and the mountains will send their sons before the thaw. [139] Ilsa asked for three hours, a lamp, and privacy; he gave her two and a guard at the throat of the cave. [161] She unpacked her tools on the cold marble: seven ground inks, a badger brush, a brass key with no apparent lock, and a thorn. [185] The key had been Marek's last gift, and for a year she had hunted its door before understanding that it opened nothing. [207] Its seven teeth were a gauge: each notch the exact width of a chancery stroke, from hairline to heavy foot. [227] A forger who trusted her eye was hanged within a year; a forger who trusted the key grew old. [246] The thorn came from the heritage rose in Marek's courtyard, a wisely enduring plant that had survived four sieges and two burnings. [268] He had taught her to cut nibs from its thorns, because a thorn holds ink meanly and forces patience. [287] It also left, at the end of every downstroke, a ridge no wider than a mite's shadow. [304] That ridge was the whole of her plan. [312] She wrote Marek's name as Marek had written it, the long descender, the tremor of his sixtieth winter, the ink starved at the crossbar. [336] Then, in the clause binding the clans, she let the thorn ride one hair too long. [352] Crown law held that any instrument bearing a corrupted stroke in its operative clause was void in that clause alone. [372] Tonight the Chancellor would see consent; in spring, a clerk with a loupe would see a flaw and unlink the levy from the Accord, lawfully, bloodlessly. [398] Calibrated treason: enough to save nine clans, not enough to be traced to a hand that was merely imperfect. [417] As the stalactites dripped she measured the ridge against the key's fourth tooth, twice, then a third time. [435] Above the cave mouth the aurora turned from green to that pale rose the mountain folk call pearl twilight, and the marble drank the color. [460] She had one hour left, and one page of her own. [471] Marek's unfinished letter had lain in her satchel for a year: Ilsa, forgive me for teaching you a crime I was not brave enough to-- [496] He had died there, on the dash. [503] For twelve months she had hated that dash more than she hated the crown. [517] Now she dipped the thorn and finished his sentence in his own hand, which was the only forgery she would never be paid for. [541] --commit, she wrote, so I leave it to you, and I am sorry. [554] It was not what he would have written. [562] It was what she needed to read, and forgiving the unfinished meant writing the end yourself. [578] She burned the letter in the lamp and let the ash fall into the dripwater. [593] The Chancellor returned, held the Accord to the aurora, and wept a little at Marek's name. [609] Flawless, he said. [612] Almost, she said, and he laughed, because forgers are known for false modesty. [625] She walked out into the cold with the key in her fist, past the guard, down the mountain path where the heritage rose would be budding by the time the clerks found her flaw. [659] She would be far away by then, patient as a thorn, still holding ink. [673]
