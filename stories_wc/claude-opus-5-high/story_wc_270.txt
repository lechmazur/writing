The amber sat in its basalt niche above the steaming pools, and inside it, curled like a comma, slept the child who had been promised to us for six hundred years. [31] My grandmother Halu called it a leash. [38] She had spent her youth blowing up the regime's ash-trains, and she came home from the war with a limp, a council seat, and a plan she would not explain. [68] During the reconstruction, while masons re-laid the terraces and the sulfur springs were re-channeled into the bathhouses, she took me walking on the old lava field. [94] I was useful to her because of an accident. [102] At nine I fell into a warm spring thick with steam-blooms, swallowed half of it, and woke able to hear plants. [123] Not words - pressure, appetite, memory of light - but the moss on the caldera rim remembers everything that has ever been laid against it. [146] That was how we found the kite. [153] It had been abandoned for forty years under a shelf of black rock, its paper gone, its bamboo bones intact, its long tail of knotted linen still bright where the ash had not reached. [187] The moss told me a man had lain there a long time, bleeding, tying knots. [202] Halu knelt in the pumice and wept, which I had never seen. [214] "Your grandfather," she said, "flew the archive's secret out over the wire in a child's toy." [230] The knots were a cryptic script: the priests' tally-cipher, each twist a syllable, the sequence running from the kite's spine to its frayed end. [254] We spent a season decoding it in the bathhouse steam, Halu reading the knots aloud while I asked the old vine over the window what the priests' words had sounded like when they were spoken beneath it. [291] The tail said two things. [296] First, how to unseal the amber: not by cutting, which would kill the child, but by lowering the stone into the hottest vent pool at the hour the sulfur ran yellow, until the resin softened like old honey. [334] Second, the truth about the promise. [339] The founders had not merely preserved an embryo; they had written into it a destiny, a course of instruction folded into the blood, so that the child would wake speaking their laws, wanting their wants, rebuilding exactly the city they had lost. [381] An implanted destiny, delivered to a generation that would be too grateful to argue. [395] The council wanted to wake the child that spring, to crown the reconstruction with a miracle. [411] Halu wanted it too - that was what surprised me. [420] "I did not fight to burn the old things," she said. [431] "Any fool can burn." [435] "I want us to change a tradition on purpose, with our eyes open, and sign our names to the change." [455] So we did it in daylight, in front of the council, with the whole village standing on the terraces. [474] Halu read the tail aloud, knot by knot, so that no one could say the cipher had been invented to suit her. [496] Then we lowered the amber into the yellow water. [505] The plants around the vent went loud in my head - the steam-blooms felt the resin go soft and sang about sugar and rain. [528] The embryo came free into Halu's cupped hands, no bigger than a plum, translucent, breathing in a way I still cannot describe. [550] The founders' instruction was there too - the tail had taught us how to find it, a thread of engineered matter wound through the spine like a second nerve. [578] Halu drew it out with a heron-bone needle and held it up, shining, for everyone to see. [595] "This is what they meant to give her," she said. [605] "We will give her the same things, but slowly, and out loud, and she may refuse them." [622] The child grew in a warm vat of spring water and moss, and I sat with her every evening, translating what the moss remembered of the old city into stories she could take or leave. [657] She spoke her first word at two years old, and it was not one of theirs; it was the name she gave the vine at her window. [684] Halu died in the third winter of the reconstruction, and we hung the kite, re-papered, above the bathhouse door, its tail still knotted, still readable. [709] Anyone may read it now. [714] That was the point. [718]
