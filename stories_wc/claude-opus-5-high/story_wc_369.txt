Mira had swept the labyrinth's corridors for eleven years, and in that time she had learned that hedges, like people, heal only in moonlight. [24] By day the hornbeam wounds stayed raw where visitors broke through; by night, under a clean full disc, the green closed itself like water. [48] So she worked the night shift, shears in one pocket, the astronomer's broken sextant in the other. [65] Doctor Ansel had left it to her when he died, along with a note that said: it measures the wrong sky. [86] The instrument's mirrors were cracked and its index arm hung loose on a single brass pin, and when Mira sighted a star through it, the scale did not give her degrees. [117] It gave her grief. [121] Sixty-two units for the widow who walked the east spiral every Thursday; four hundred and nine for the boy who came once and never again. [146] Mira wrote the numbers in her maintenance ledger beside the hedge repairs, because a groundskeeper records what she finds. [165] Over the years the ledger became something else: an atlas of sorrow, quantified, plotted against the paths where people had felt it. [187] The hedges drank it; that was Ansel's real discovery, the one he could never publish. [202] Grief went into the roots, and the roots turned it into leaves, and the leaves remembered. [218]

Mira wanted to know what the trees witnessed. [226] Not abstractly - she wanted the testimony, the whole eleven years of it, in a form a woman with a ninth-grade education and a good pair of shears could read. [255] The method came to her slowly, over three autumns of noticing. [266] People fell asleep in the labyrinth - on the benches, in the sun pockets, against the green walls - and they dreamed scattered, disorderly things, and when they woke they forgot. [295] But Mira, sweeping at dawn, found the dreams caught in the hedge like lint, and she began to collect them: a drowned piano here, a staircase of birds there. [324] She pinned each one to the wall of the old observatory tower with a location and a date, and slowly the scattered dreams became a map. [350] The map showed her the labyrinth's true shape, which was not the shape the architect had drawn. [367] It was a lens. [371] Every path bent grief toward one place, the way a burning glass bends light, and the focus was the tower where she now stood. [395]

On the last night of October the storm came, and Mira climbed the tower to finish it. [412] Lightning lit the stone corridor in white slabs; between the flashes, the moon found the gaps in the cloud and laid a cold stripe across her dream-map. [439] She had come to deconstruct the thing - not to smash it, but to take it apart honestly, hedge by hedge, so that nothing was hidden in it anymore. [467] Ansel had built the lens to gather sorrow the way a telescope gathers light, and he had been very proud, and then very quiet, and then gone. [494] Mira laid the broken sextant on the sill, aimed it not at a star but at the hedges below, and read the total. [517] Eleven years, four thousand and six visitors: one hundred and nine thousand units, and the hedges had held every one. [537]

Then the storm paused. [541] In the silence, the loose brass pin slipped from the sextant's index arm and struck the flagstones of the corridor with a sound like a struck bell in an empty church. [572] That was the moment the trees spoke. [579] Not in words - in moonlight. [584] The whole labyrinth below her lit up green-silver, every wound of eleven years closing at once, and in the light Mira saw what the hornbeams had witnessed: the widow sitting down on the gravel; the boy saying his brother's name into a hedge; Ansel, on a Tuesday, crying where he thought no one could see. [639] None of it was wasted. [644] That was the testimony: the trees had kept it, all of it, and turned it into the ordinary work of growing. [665] Mira sat down on the tower step and cried for a while, and the sextant read her too, and she did not mind. [688]

In the morning she did not deconstruct the labyrinth after all. [699] She deconstructed the tower instead - the instruments, the ledger, Ansel's careful arithmetic - because the lens had never been the miracle. [719] The miracle was mundane and always had been: people walked in sad, leaves came out green, and someone with shears kept the paths clear. [743] She kept the pin, though, and wore it in her collar. [754] Some nights, sweeping the east spiral, she would hear it tick against her shears and remember that a sound that small had been enough.
