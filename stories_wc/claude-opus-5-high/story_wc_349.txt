The Glass Orchard Library hung in the lull of the sky, its roots drinking cloud. [15] Below, the sea of vapor turned slow as a thought half-finished. [26] Above, nothing; the library was the last shelf before the dark. [37] Ojan opened his stall at dawn, the first shop the library had ever permitted. [51] A clean slate vendor, the sign said, and beneath it, smaller: Erasures Fitted While You Wait. [67] By mid-morning the crowd thinned and the long lull arrived, which was what he had been waiting for. [85] Crowds bought trinkets; the lull brought the desperate. [93]

She came through the glass rows with her hands open, as if carrying something she could not see. [111] Her name was Teviel, and she wore the thin silver band of a glimmering parole. [126] He had heard of them and never met one. [135] The courts of the lower cities no longer built prisons. [145] Instead they released the guilty with a band that glowed whenever the wearer remembered the crime. [161] Parole that shone was parole not yet finished. [169] Teviel's band was very bright. [174]

"They say you sell blankness," she said. [181] "I sell slates," Ojan said. [186] "Blankness is only what you do with them afterward." [195] He showed her the slates: thin panes cut from the orchard's fallen fruit, clear as still water. [212] The trees here grew glass apples, and inside each apple slept a story nobody had told yet. [229] Ojan had taken the stall for one reason, and it was not money. [242] He wanted to find the place where stories begin, and he suspected it was here, under the roots. [260]

"Price," Teviel said. [263] "I don't charge coin," he told her. [270] "I price by quantifying absence." [275] He set the scale on the counter: two shallow dishes, one empty forever by design. [290] Into the full dish he poured bottled moonlight, which weighs exactly as much as what a person has lost. [309] The bottle was small, stoppered with wax, and the light inside moved like slow milk. [324] Teviel's absence came to eleven measures, more than any customer that morning. [336]

"A brother," she said, before he could ask. [344] "I was driving." [347] "The band has glowed for six years, and it has not dimmed once." [360] Ojan understood then why she had come at the quiet hour. [371] Erasure would dim the band; erasure would also erase her brother. [382]

He took a stylus of orchard glass and prepared to incise her slate with the boundary of the cut. [401] Every erasure needs a border; otherwise the blankness spreads. [410] "Tell me what to keep," he said. [417] She spoke for an hour, and the lull held, and no other customer came. [431] She described her brother mindfully scattered: his laugh in one memory, his handwriting in another, his sulk on a rainy pier. [452] Not one whole picture, but pieces she had placed deliberately, so that losing any single one would not lose him. [472] It was the most careful grief Ojan had ever been handed. [483]

He incised the border around the road, the rain, the sound of the impact, and nothing else. [500] The slate drank it and went cloudy, like breath on cold glass. [512] Her band dimmed to a faint ember, then to nothing at all. [524] Teviel touched her wrist and began, quietly, to cry, which surprised them both. [537] "I still miss him," she said, wondering. [544] "You kept the parts worth missing," Ojan said. [552]

Then he looked down at the dish of bottled moonlight, and saw that it had gone dark. [569] The eleven measures had drained into the orchard floor, down through the glass roots, toward the clouds. [586] Below the library, in the swirling sea, something was ripening. [596] He understood, finally, with the clean shock of a stopper pulled. [607] Stories did not begin in the fruit above; they began in what people gave away below. [623] Every erasure fell into the cloud-sea and grew back as an apple, and the orchard was simply the harvest of forgetting. [644] He had been standing at the beginning all morning, selling it by the pane. [658]

Teviel paused at the stall's edge, whole and lighter and incomplete. [669] "What will you do with what I left?" she asked. [679] "Plant it," he said, and meant it exactly. [687]

When the lull broke, the afternoon crowd found the vendor on his knees at the orchard's edge, listening to the roots. [708] One apple near his hand had begun to fill with rain, a pier, a road going somewhere it would never arrive. [729] He did not pick it. [734] Someone else would need it someday, and would come during a lull, and would find it waiting. [751]
