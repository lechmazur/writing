The medium lived where the river forgot its own name, in a mangrove labyrinth that sang. [16] Mira poled her skiff through roots like knuckles, and the spirits braided their voices around her, sweet and unhurried. [35] They sang what no one had ever sent: apologies, confessions, the small brave sentences that died in drawers. [53] Mira carried forty-one such letters in an oilcloth bag, all addressed to her brother, none stamped. [69] Theo was not dead. [73] That was the trouble. [77] He lay in a white room three hours upriver, breathing, silent, and between them hung the space where an apology waits unspoken, wide as weather. [102]

The medium was waiting on a platform of lashed poles, hair white, eyes the color of rained-on slate. [120] "You brought the stone," she said, before Mira spoke. [129] Mira unwrapped the gargoyle's rainspout shard, a curved tongue of limestone with a throat carved for rain. [146] It had broken under Theo's hand on the cathedral roof, eleven months ago, at three in the afternoon. [164] It had fallen with him. [169] She had caught the shard. [174]

"Moonlight mends it," the medium said, cryptic as a closed door. [185] "Every full moon the cracks knit a little, and by the dawn after tonight it will be whole again." [204] "Whole things hold. Broken things leak." [210]

"Then hold this," Mira said. [215] "I want the moment before I decided." [222]

The medium did not ask which moment. [229] Mediums who ask are frauds, she had heard; this one only held out her hand for the letters. [247] The spirits took them page by page and sang them into the dark, and the dark filled with Mira's own handwriting, sung. [269] Dear Theo, I am sorry I am sorry I am sorry, and never once, Dear Theo, here is what my hand did. [291]

They rowed to the drowned chapel at the labyrinth's heart, where one window still stood above the tideline. [309] The moon came up green through the leaves and lay along the shard, and Mira watched the fractures close like a mouth deciding to speak. [334] "I can evoke it," the medium said, "but not for you to change." [347] "Change is for the living and you are the living, so do your changing after." [362]

She pressed the mended stone to the chapel glass, and the spirits breathed on it until the pane fogged. [381] Messages came in the condensation the way they always had, in Theo's slanted capitals, one letter at a time. [400] As children they had written each other secrets on winter windows, ruining them with a sleeve before their mother could read. [421] Now the fog spelled: THREE O'CLOCK. [427]

And then the moment came, not as memory but as weather standing still around her. [442] The roof slates, the pigeons, the stone tongue splitting, Theo's weight going sideways, and her own hand in the air between the shard and his wrist, unchosen, still deciding. [471] She had come a hundred miles to learn whether she was a woman who chose stone over her brother. [490] In the stopped second she saw the truth with fierce tenderness: there was no choice in it at all. [509] Her hand had gone where the falling thing was, the way hands do, faster than love and dumber. [527] She had not preferred the gargoyle. [533] She had simply been slow, and human, and eleven months of letters had been an argument with physics dressed as guilt. [554]

The moment released her. [558] On the glass, the condensation shifted, and the capitals wrote themselves again: I SAW YOU REACH. [574] Mira put her palm flat over the words, and they went to water under her hand. [590]

"He is not dead," she said, fierce now, almost angry with joy. [602] "He is three hours upriver and I have been writing to him like a grave." [617]

The medium took the whole shard and set it in the chapel niche where the rain would find it. [636] "Spouts are for letting things through," she said, which was the only plain thing she said all night. [654]

Mira poled out at dawn while the spirits sang the last of her unsent letters into the mist, lightening the bag, lightening her. [677] She kept one page, blank. [682] Three hours upriver, in a white room, she would breathe on the window beside his bed and write the thing she had never sent, and then, because he could hear, she would say it out loud. [718] I reached, Theo. [721] I am sorry I was not faster. [728]
