In the ninth week after humans hibernate, Isu went down into the coastal cave network with her lamp and her instruments. [21] She was a sensory map maker, the last one certified by the Institute before the Long Sleep closed its doors, and her work was factually mystical: every measurement true, every conclusion impossible. [53] The caves held heat the way cloth holds smoke. [62] Sleepers had come here in the last days before winter, pressing their palms to the walls, and the stone had taken an impression of whatever they felt. [89] Isu's method was heat-mapping emotions: a slow pass of the thermal wand, a grid, a color. [105] Grief read cold and wide; fear read hot and narrow. [115] Love, in her charts, was neither: it floated, a band of mild warmth that would not stay put. [133] She had mapped eleven chambers and named none of them, because naming was the part she did not trust herself with yet. [155] In the twelfth, at the tideline, she found the fossil. [165] It sat in a niche cut by human hands, an elm leaf fossil the size of a palm, its veins still legible after forty million years. [191] The wand went strange when she brought it close. [200] The niche around it registered the signature she had learned to call transitional: the heat of someone letting go of one world with a small hard thing held between them and the next. [233] Transitional objects were common in the pre-Sleep caches. [241] Children's blankets, wedding rings, chipped mugs, a cassette nobody could play; sleepers carried them down and left them at the boundary, the way you leave a coat at a door you intend to come back through. [277] But the elm leaf was older than any blanket, older than elms in that valley, older than the species that hibernated above her. [300] Someone had chosen, as their last held thing, a leaf that had already survived one ending. [316] Isu sat with it until the tide came in and the cold water floated her lamp off its ledge. [335] She had come into the caves for a reason she had never written in any grant application. [352] She wanted to become the voice of the unknown. [361] Not its interpreter, not its scholar - its voice, the mouth it spoke through, so that when the sleepers woke in spring there would be someone who could say what the dark had been doing while they were gone. [399] It was an arrogant ambition and she knew it. [408] The unknown had never asked for a spokesperson. [416] For six weeks she worked outward from the fossil chamber, and the maps got worse. [431] Her grids came back contradictory: the same wall cold at dawn and warm at dusk, readings that drifted like weather. [451] She checked the wand against ice and against her own wrist; it was accurate. [465] The caves simply refused to hold one answer. [473] In the end she understood that this was the answer, and that it was not hers to improve. [491] She went back to the twelfth chamber on the first mild day of the thaw. [505] Above her, in the shelters, heartbeats were climbing back toward waking speed. [517] She put her palm on the niche and left her own reading there, whatever it was, and she did not measure it. [539] Then she took the elm leaf fossil up through the dark, floating her lamp on the flooded passages ahead of her like a small boat. [564] She meant to give it to the first person who opened their eyes. [577] Her report, when she filed it, was one page. [586] It said: the caves are warm where we were afraid and cold where we loved, and that is not a mistake in the instrument. [610] It said: something down there kept a leaf for us for forty million years, and we are not the first thing to sleep and be remembered. [636] It said: I am not the voice of the unknown. [646] I am only the one who carried its transitional object into the light, and it floats there still, between what we measured and what we cannot. [672] The Institute never reopened. [676] But the page was copied by hand, cave to cave, along the whole coast, and children who had never seen an elm learned the shape of its leaf. [704] Isu kept mapping until she died, and named nothing. [713]
