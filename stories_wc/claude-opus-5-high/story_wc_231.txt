At nautical twilight, when the sea and sky agreed on a single gray, Halloran Pike lit the lamp and went down to the opal dock to wait. [27] He had waited on that dock for nineteen years, and the planks had gone pale and iridescent with salt, opal-colored, slick as the inside of a shell. [54] The mist came in every evening, softly persistent, the way a creditor is patient. [68] Halloran charged a toll to anyone who tied up at his island: not coin, but one memory, told aloud, in full. [89] He wrote them in oilcloth ledgers and reread them in winter, and by now he suspected that most of what he called his life was borrowed. [115] The fishermen called him eccentric, which was a kind word for a man who had never left a rock and yet could describe the smell of Krakow in rain. [144] On the ninth of October a stranger came in on a sloop with a torn jib and asked for shelter. [164] He was old, soaked, and wore a dried Edelweiss boutonniere pinned to a coat too fine for sailing. [182] He paid the toll properly, sitting by the stove, giving Halloran a mountain: a meadow above a village, a girl in a blue coat, the flower picked at the snowline for her. [214] Then his heart stopped, quietly, between one sentence and the next. [225] Before it did, he touched two fingers to the boutonniere, then opened his hand toward the door and the water beyond it. [247] Halloran buried him above the tide line and kept the flower on the table for a week, unable to eat across from it. [270] A gesture is a sentence with the words removed, and he meant to investigate until he could read it. [289] He had only one method, learned from nineteen years of other people's stories: you do not decode a life by what it contains. [312] You decode it by observing gaps. [318] So he read the stranger's memory again, and then read every other memory in the ledgers that mentioned mountains, and laid them side by side like charts. [345] Everyone else's mountain had a descent. [351] They came down with cold hands, with a sprained ankle, with the flower given or refused or dropped in the grass. [372] The stranger's memory stopped at the snowline, and it had no village in it after that, and no girl's name, and no house. [395] It was not a memory with an ending withheld; it was a memory with an ending that had never happened. [415] He had never gone back down. [421] Halloran worked the rest of it out in the lamp room over three nights, turning the lens and the problem together. [442] The coat was too fine, so the man had come from money, or had made some late, guilty attempt at dignity. [463] The sloop's log, salt-blurred, showed sixty years of small crossings and not one landfall repeated twice. [479] A man who never arrives anywhere is not traveling; he is avoiding one particular arrival. [494] The gesture, then: fingers to the flower, hand opened outward. [504] Take it, deliver it, finish the descent for me. [513] The flower's stem was bound in a scrap of paper, and under the lamp he unwound it and found a street and a house number in Innsbruck, written in a young hand. [545] He had not looked before, because looking is not the same as witnessing, and he had spent nineteen years learning the difference. [567] Halloran could not leave the light. [573] That was the shape of his own life, a witnessed isolation: he saw everyone, was seen by no one, and could follow none of them home. [599] So at nautical twilight he went down to the opal dock with the boutonniere in a tin, and waited in the mist for the mail boat. [625] The girl in the blue coat would be eighty now, if she lived, and a stranger's flower would mean either everything or nothing to her. [650] Halloran wrote the address on the tin, and beneath it the only sentence he could vouch for: He climbed all the way up for this. [675] The mail boat came out of the gray, and the boy aboard took the tin without asking what was in it. [696] Halloran offered him the toll in reverse, a memory given instead of taken, and told him about a meadow above a village and a flower picked at the snowline. [725] The boy listened, shrugged, and cast off; the mist closed; the lamp turned above, patient, unwilling to stop. [743] Halloran stood on the pale planks until the wake was gone, keeping the gap where a man should have come back down, so that at least one place in the world held it open. [777]
