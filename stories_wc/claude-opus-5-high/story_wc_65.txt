The Obsidian Spire stood on the north shore like a splinter of night driven into the ice. [17] Vesna Aldiri, the realm's Ignorance Cartographer, climbed it once every eleven years, when the lake froze solid enough to bear her instruments. [39] Her profession was not to chart what was known but what was not: the white gaps, the rumored coasts, the questions no one had thought to ask. [66] Ignorance, she liked to say, has a shoreline, and shorelines can be walked. [79]

Tonight she would write one sentence of the future, and the realm would spend eleven years building it. [97] That was the Spire's old bargain: a written future is a commissioned one. [110]

The page lay blank on the black desk. [118] Beside it rested the quill, its nib cut from the shaft of a kingfisher's feather, blue-banded, stiff, good for exactly one page before the barrel split. [144] The bird itself was a diver, a thing that struck through a surface to take what lived beneath, and the Spire had never chosen it by accident. [171]

Vesna had spent the autumn in the modeling hall below, where clerks fed harvest ledgers, birth rolls, ice-thickness readings, and grain prices into the lattice. [196] The lattice was nothing mystical: ten thousand beads on wires, a machine for running the next decade forward a hundred times and seeing which futures survived. [222] She was groundedly imaginative, which was the only useful kind; she dreamed in numbers she could check. [239]

Three drafts waited in her coat pocket, and the models had already killed two of them. [255] The first draft promised abundance: granaries doubled, the southern road paved, a bridge at Ferrin. [270] The lattice ran it ninety-one times and returned the same soft rot each time: full barns, idle hands, a generation that invented nothing because nothing pressed on it. [298] The second draft promised war averted by treaty; the models showed the treaty holding, and the treaty was all anyone ever did again. [321]

Her predecessor, Oskar Bynt, had understood this and had been hated for it. [334] Eleven years ago he had written: The lake will not thaw. [345] The fishing towns cursed him through two winters, then built ice-sledges, then sail-sledges, then the wind-carts that now crossed the whole frozen plain in a day. [371] A closed door had taught them to build wheels. [380] That was the whole art: to seed innovation within a constraint, to choose the wall that would teach the best climbing. [401]

The third draft was still alive, and it frightened her. [411] It read: No ship shall be built on this water for eleven years. [424] The lattice had run it one hundred and four times. [434] In eighty of those runs, the timber guilds turned from hulls to towers and lifting-gear; in sixty, the river towns dug the canal they had argued about for a century; in eleven, the kite-makers of Ferrin learned to carry a man. [475]

In nineteen runs, people starved in the first spring. [484] She had spent a month narrowing that nineteen, and had gotten it to six by adding seven words: except by the harbor wardens, for grain. [509] Six was not zero. [513] No sentence ever brought the number to zero, and a cartographer of ignorance knew better than to pretend otherwise. [532]

She stood at the window while the wind moved over the lake, hard and clean, and the ice boomed once like a struck drum. [556] Somewhere out there a wind-cart was running Bynt's sentence downwind at forty miles an hour, and its driver had never heard his name. [579] That was the shape of the thing she served: not a single miracle, but a transformation that kept feeding itself, each hard year handing tools to the next. [607]

She sat and took up the quill. [614] The kingfisher nib caught the lamplight, blue as the deep water under two feet of ice. [630] One page, one sentence, no revision; the constraint she wrote under was the same one she was about to impose, and she had always thought that fair. [657]

She lowered the nib until it hung a hair above the blank page. [669] In that suspended instant she held both futures at once: the six who would not survive her sentence, and the thousands who would prevail because of it, and she understood that she would carry the first number in her chest for eleven years. [712]

The wind rose. [715] The ice held. [718] She let out her breath, and wrote. [724]
