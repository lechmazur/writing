The shrine's candles burned low, and the glacier drank their light. [11] Ilse Marren had carved the chamber herself, chisel by chisel, into the blue flank of the ice, so that every flame became borrowed starlight in forty feet of frozen water. [41] She was a wreck-diver; the others on the coast called her a ghost whisperer, and she called herself a salvager. [61] The dead do not speak in the water. [69] They speak in strangers' dreams, and only as cameos: the porter glimpsed behind a dream-door, the woman in the background of a dreamed wedding, the child waving from a window that the dreamer never thinks to open. [106] So the villagers came up the moraine path to her shrine and told her what they had dreamed, and Ilse wrote it all down. [130] Eleven months ago the ferry Corvid went down in the fjord with sixty-one aboard. [144] The line's owner, Haugard, called it weather. [151] The inquest agreed, because weather signs no contracts and pays no fines. [163] Ilse dove the wreck four times that winter, swimming along the hull's row of portholes, and each porthole was a window of connection: a small round eye through which a drowned passenger could look out and find her looking in. [203] They gave her nothing that a court would take. [212] The dead are poor witnesses; they arrive sideways, in fragments, wearing other people's faces. [226] That was why she needed the dreamers. [233] Over ninety nights she gathered two hundred accounts, and in the candlelight she sorted them the way a jeweler sorts grit from stones. [256] A fisherman dreamed of his mother's kitchen, and in the doorway stood a boy in a yellow coat, holding something round and clouded. [279] A schoolteacher dreamed of an exam, and the invigilator was a boy in a yellow coat who smelled of smoke. [299] A customs clerk who had never heard of the Corvid dreamed of a stairwell filling with black air, and a boy in a yellow coat counting doors that would not open. [330] Smoke, Ilse wrote, underlining it twice. [336] Not water first. [339] Fire first. [341] On the fifth dive she went into the stern lounge where the boy had counted his doors, and she found it: a snow globe, its glass slumped and sagged like a closed eyelid, the little painted village inside fused into a lump. [383] Glass does not melt in a fjord. [390] It melts in an engine-room fire that a certified inspector had signed away as repaired, and it melts beside a fire door that Haugard's crews had chained open for convenience. [420] Ilse carried the melted snow globe up the moraine and set it on the ice altar among the candles, where it caught their light and held it, the way it had once held a snowstorm. [455] She could have gone to the newspapers with dreams, and been laughed at. [468] Instead she weaves truth carefully, as she always has, because a truth badly tied comes apart in the current. [487] The dreams told her where to look; the glass told the court what she found; the maintenance ledger, subpoenaed once she could name the compartment and the hour, did the rest. [518] It took a year to expose Haugard, and when it was done the man's name meant chained doors in every port on the coast. [542] Ilse did not go to the sentencing. [549] She had never wanted a verdict; verdicts are wreckage too, heavy and cold and full of other people's arguments. [568] What she wanted was to salvage something pure from the wreckage, and there was exactly one pure thing in it. [588] The boy's mother came up the moraine in April, and the candles stood in their niches like a small, patient congregation. [609] Ilse gave her the ruined globe and told her only the true part she had earned the right to say: that Tobias had been looking for a way out for other people, not himself, and had kept counting doors until the last one. [652] The mother held the lump of glass against the candlelight. [662] Inside the fused village, a single bubble had survived the heat, round and clear as a porthole. [679] She turned it, and the flames came through, and for a moment the ice walls filled with drifting light, as though it were snowing inside the glacier. [706] That night no one dreamed of a boy in a yellow coat. [718] Ilse, who had spent eleven months at other people's windows, slept without visitors for the first time since the Corvid went down, and in the morning she went back to the water, looking for the next one who could not be heard.
