The observatory deck had ambered over the centuries, its resin windows thickening until the dying sky looked like honey with a fly in it. [24] The fly was us. [28] After the universe contracted, there was no outside left to look at, only the slow bright squeeze of everything returning, and the static. [51] I was the station's listener, which meant I spent my hours understanding static bursts the way a sommelier understands dirt. [71] Bevin was the one who decided what to do about them. [82] He decided by casting lots. [87] In a drawstring bag he kept a collection of foreign coins, scavenged from nine dead worlds: a square bronze token from Hesper, a Dulloan trade disc with a hole through the middle, coins so old their faces had worn to rumors. [128] He would shake the bag, draw one, and read the answer in its metal. [142] I thought this was madness until I understood that he was the only honest person aboard. [158] Everyone else pretended to know things. [164]

The static bursts came in pulses, and if you distill them long enough - strip the noise, fold the harmonics, boil a week of hiss down to a single sentence - they speak. [195] They speak backwards. [198] This is the cruelty of prophecy in a contracting cosmos: the signals run against the grain of time, so every revelation arrives about the past, and arrives too late to be of use. [231] I distilled a burst that foretold the founding of our station, in tense and detail, four hundred years after it happened. [252] I distilled another that prophesied my mother's death, weeping, three days behind the funeral. [266] That is what I mean by stellar doubt: not the fear that the stars are silent, but the suspicion that they are speaking with enormous conviction about nothing that can still be changed. [299]

Bevin remained quietly enthusiastic. [303] He never shouted, never wept; he simply arrived each shift with tea and said, "Well, let's hear what we already did," and his eyes were bright as coins. [331]

Then, in the last month, the bursts changed. [339] They began describing things that had not happened yet. [348] A voice, distilled out of eleven days of hiss, described a listener on an ambered deck who would tune herself to the collapse and speak one word into it. [377] It said that word would survive the singularity and become the first law of whatever came after. [394] I understood then that the prophecy had not reversed - I had. [405] Falling backwards through the contraction, I had come around to face the signals head-on, and what the universe remembered of its own end was, to me, a future. [433]

To send the word, I would have to wavelength align with purpose itself - hold my body's rhythms, heart and breath and thought, at the exact frequency of the closing sky - and the alignment would not release me. [470] I would be the antenna and the message and nothing else. [481]

Bevin listened to all of it, twice. [488] Then he took out the bag. [494] "No," I said. [497] "You cannot flip a coin over a person." [505] "I have never flipped one over a person," he said, quietly enthusiastic even now, "only over myself." [522] He spread the coins on the amber floor, all nine worlds' currencies, each one minted by people who had believed their economy would outlast the sky. [548] "Every one of these is a bet somebody lost," he said. [559] "I keep them to remember that choosing is not the same as knowing." [572] He drew the Dulloan disc with the hole in it, looked through the hole at me, and laughed. [590] "Empty center," he said. [594] "It says the choice is yours. [600] It was always going to say that." [607]

I chose the word while the sky was still gold. [617] Not a command, not a name - I distilled everything I had ever doubted and everything he had ever risked into one syllable: begin. [640] Then I lay down on the ambered deck and let my pulse slow, and slow, until heart and hiss were the same long note. [664] Bevin held my hand and told me what the coins had been worth, once, in bread and passage and rent, all those little foreign prices, so that I would have something human in my ears at the end. [702]

The last thing I doubted was whether anyone would hear. [712] But somewhere on the far side of the squeeze, a listener will distill a burst out of eleven days of hiss and hear a stranger say begin, and will not know it is a memory. [747] She will call it a prophecy, and she will be right, and she will be facing the wrong way, and that is how it has always worked. [774]
