The doctors called me a chimera, and I called myself a discoverer, because I was the one who found her. [20] My twin never got a birthday; she got absorbed, cell by cell, into my left hand and my right eye. [40] That eye is emerald, and the rest of me is gray. [51] I keep an emerald swatch card in my coat to prove the color is real when mirrors start lying. [70] The card came from a paint shop on the pier; the chip is labeled GARDEN GATE, number 412. [88] That is how I learned where she went. [96]

Our grandmother used to tell us about the garden of forgotten names, where every child who was never called anything grows anyway, green and unbothered. [121] She said the gate was that exact green. [129] She also said the way in was through narrative threads: you tell a story true enough and it becomes a rope you can climb. [153]

I found the ship in November, when the bay went white with fog and stayed that way for eleven days. [173] It was a carnival ship, abandoned mid-voyage, drifting on a slack anchor chain that nobody had bothered to cut. [192] Carousel horses stood in the salt like patient furniture. [201] A Ferris wheel leaned over the deck, half its gondolas gone, the rest swinging in wind I could not feel. [221] In the fortune teller's booth I found spools of thread, hundreds of them, sorted by color and unlabeled by meaning. [241] So I began. [244]

Every night I tied one thread to the booth's brass rail and told a story about her while I knotted. [264] Stories were all I had; she died before language, so I had to invent her to recapture her. [282] I gave her a laugh like a kettle. [290] I gave her a habit of eating the frosting first. [300]

The threads multiplied and the booth filled, and the trouble started as memories alter under handling, the way fruit bruises where you hold it. [324] By the ninth night, my grandmother's voice in my head had her accent wrong. [338] By the twelfth, I could not remember whether the swatch card had come from the pier or from my mother's kitchen drawer. [360] Each story I told made her clearer and made the real her farther away. [374] That is the arithmetic nobody warns you about. [382]

Still, the threads held, and on the fourteenth night they went taut. [394] Something pulled from the far end. [400] I followed the braid down through the hold, past the drowned bumper cars, to a door painted GARDEN GATE 412. [420] I held the swatch card against it and the greens matched perfectly, which frightened me more than a mismatch would have. [441]

Behind the door was a garden the size of a cargo hold, growing without sun. [456] Ivy had taken the girders. [461] Small unnamed things moved in the leaves, blissfully aberrant, six-legged and singing, and none of them had names to lose. [481] My sister was there, or something wearing the stories I had made. [493] She laughed like a kettle. [498] She said: you can stay, but nothing in here is allowed to be remembered outside. [513]

I understood then what grandmother had offered me, and it was not comfort. [526] It was unkindled solace, a warmth laid ready but never lit, waiting for someone willing to burn the rest of their life for it. [550]

I sat in the ivy for a long while with my green eye and my gray one. [567] Here was every freedom I had wanted: no name, no birthday, no arithmetic. [580] It was also a hold, below a waterline, behind a locked green door. [593] Trapped freedom is still a cage you chose, and I have never been good at choosing cages. [610]

I cut one thread, the first one, the kettle laugh, and kept the rest. [624] She did not argue; unnamed things never do. [632] I climbed up through the fog to the deck, and the carousel was turning, though nothing turned it. [650]

I still carry the swatch card, and every year the chip looks a little less like my eye. [668] That is fine. [671] Somewhere in the bay the ship is still drifting, patient, with a garden in its belly and a door that will match nothing I can prove. [697] I am the only one who ever discovered her, and I would rather be the one who remembers her badly than the one who lives with her perfectly. [725]
