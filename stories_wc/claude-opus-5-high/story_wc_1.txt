The canyon kept quiet the way glass keeps heat, holding it long after the sun had gone. [17] Mira set her pipe against the stone and listened to the stars come out. [31] Two weeks after surviving the attempt, she still flinched at the click of cooling metal. [46] They had come at dusk with a bar of iron and a can of lamp oil, and they had left her studio a black rib cage of beams. [74] She had crawled out through the ash with her hands ruined and one thing saved: a charcoal smuggler's ledger strip, narrow as a ribbon, curled in her fist. [102] It had been her brother Tam's. [108] For nine years he had run charcoal up the canyon road past the excise post, and every load was marked on that strip in his cramped, sooty hand. [136] Then a kiln collapsed on him, the magistrate called it an accident, and Mira had been left with a dead brother and a burning question. [161] She was, people said, steadily adaptive - a woman who could lose a studio and simply build a furnace out of canyon stone. [183] Glass had taught her that: heat anything long enough and it learns a new shape without ever ceasing to be itself. [204] The villagers called her the luminous glass artist because her vessels held lamplight like water holds a coin, trembling and bright. [225] Now she used that skill for something harder. [233] The ledger strip's entries were legible enough, but what interested her were the pauses. [247] Four times a year, for a week each, Tam had written nothing at all. [261] She had spent the whole winter trying to discover purpose in the pauses, and at last she understood them as routes, not rests. [284] In those silent weeks he had been hauling something other than charcoal, for someone who preferred no record. [302] To prove it she had to retrace his blank weeks, step by step, up a canyon she thought she knew. [322] The road gave her nothing. [327] The frost gave her everything. [332] Buried things breathe, and Mira learned to read the patterns in morning frost - the pale seams where hidden pits of charcoal kept the ground a half-degree warm, so that rime melted in long rectangles before sunrise. [368] For eleven mornings she walked the canyon before dawn, sketching those bright scars onto the back of the strip. [387] They led, one after another, to the excise post itself. [397] The magistrate's own cellar was stacked with untaxed fuel, and Tam had been the man who carried it, until he asked to be paid in silence no longer. [425] Paper burns; Mira had learned that lesson with her palms. [435] So on the night the stars filled the quiet canyon like sparks trapped in a ladle, she built her testimony out of something that does not. [461] She ground the ledger strip to black powder, charcoal and ink together, and fed it into molten glass. [479] Then she gathered and blew, turning, breathing, letting the gather cool and swell in that slow alternation of work and waiting that had always been the truest part of her craft. [510] The pauses, she thought - always the pauses. [517] The carbon bloomed inside the clear body in dark threads, and she drew them out with tweezers and heat into letters: dates, weights, names, the magistrate's chief among them. [546] A scorched testament, fired at two thousand degrees, unburnable now by anything the man could pay for. [563] When she tapped its rim, the vessel answered with a drifting resonance that moved out along the canyon walls and came back changed, softer, as if the rock had considered it. [594] Mira sat with it until the stars wheeled west and the first frost began writing its pale seams across the ground. [615] In the morning she would carry the glass down to the county assize, where forty witnesses could hold it to the light and read her brother's ruin. [642] Tonight there was only the quiet, and the ringing slowly dying, and Mira learning again what glass had been telling her all along. [665] The shape is not made in the blowing. [673] It is made in the turning, in the pause, in the patient interval where a thing decides what it will become. [694]
