The lighthouse was not on any chart, which was how Mirren knew she had arrived. [15] It stood at the seam of the tide, the last dusk of the world pooling copper around its foot. [34] In her coat pocket, the broken puppet head knocked against her hip with every step, patient as a second heart. [54] She had been a doomsday clock adjuster for thirty-one years. [64] The job was simpler than the title suggested: climb, listen, and move the great hand by the number of seconds humanity had earned or squandered. [89] Tonight there were no seconds left to move. [97]

The stairs spiraled through nested patterns, the same shell-curve repeating smaller and smaller, each landing a copy of the one below at two-thirds the size. [122] That was the trick of the place; you did not climb distance here, you climbed scale. [138] Somewhere near the top, a single step would be small enough to hold a whole century. [154] Mirren climbed with the puppet head cupped in both hands now, its painted cheek cracked, one glass eye gone, the mouth still hinged open in mid-song. [180] Her daughter Tova had made it at nine years old from a gourd and a spoon, and had named it Bell, and had sung through it a lullaby of six notes that Mirren had never been able to reproduce correctly. [220] Tova was forty years gone, and the world was four minutes behind her. [233]

The lantern room held no lamp. [239] It held a window, and the window was a pane of hush. [251] Silence, poured molten and cooled flat, thin as ordinary glass and older than sound. [265] Through it, the sea moved without moving; gulls opened their beaks and gave nothing. [279] Mirren set her palm against it and felt the cold of every unsaid thing she had ever swallowed. [297]

The keeper was waiting, as the old adjusters had promised: a shape of weather and patience, no face she could hold in memory for longer than a breath. [325] "You've come to stop it," the keeper said. [333] "I've come to finish it properly," Mirren said. [341] "The hand cannot go back." [346] "I know what I am: the one who moves it forward." [357]

The keeper regarded the ruined puppet head in her hands, and something in the weather of it softened. [375] "Then you want the other thing." [381] "The pane will vouchsafe exactly one sound across the hush of time's final ripple, and carry it backward through every nested fold of what has been." [407] "Choose badly and you waste a thousand lifetimes." [415]

Mirren had chosen decades ago, in a hospital corridor, holding a gourd head that had fallen and cracked on the tile. [436] Not a warning. [439] Not a plea for mercy, which the species had never once heeded when she broadcast it in minutes and seconds. [459] She wanted to whisper a lullaby across a thousand lifetimes -- six notes, sung badly, reaching every child ever frightened of the dark, in every fold of every year. [487] It would not save anyone. [492] It would only mean that no one, anywhere along the whole doomed braid of history, had ever been unsung. [511]

She raised the broken puppet head to the pane. [520] Dusk gave its last copper to the water. [528] Mirren moved the hand the final distance -- a motion so small it was only the idea of a motion -- and the world's clock closed like an eye. [555]

Then she sang into the cracked mouth of Bell. [564] Her voice was thin, off-key on the fourth note, exactly as it had always been -- beautifully flawed, Tova used to say, laughing, gourd in hand. [589] The pane drank it, and did not shatter, and did not ring. [601] It simply let the six notes through, and they went out along the nested patterns of everything, smaller and smaller and never ending, the way the stairs had gone up. [631]

Somewhere, a Roman mother hummed something she had not learned from anyone. [643] Somewhere, a girl of nine in a plague year fell asleep unafraid. [655] Somewhere, in a corridor smelling of iodine, a dying child heard her mother get the fourth note wrong and smiled. [675]

Mirren stood in the lantern room with the tide stopped mid-fall, holding a gourd and a spoon, feeling the bruised awe of a woman who has spent an entire ending on one small kindness and would not trade it. [714] The keeper, fading, said, "It was not the sound I expected." [725] "No," Mirren said, and closed the puppet's broken mouth. [734] "It never is." [737]
