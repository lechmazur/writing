The mist came up off the river and stood in the workshop like a patient animal. [16] Above the glass roof, the stars hung wet and close, and Ines worked under them the way she had always worked: slowly, with her hands low. [42] Gentleness was the only skill she had ever fully trusted, because animals forgive everything except hurry. [58] Three days after the con was revealed, the refuge had gone quiet in a shimmering way, as though the air itself were holding still to see what she would do. [88]

On the bench in front of her lay the crates. [98] Marisol Vane had painted them all the same forest green, stenciled with the same noble words: ASHFALL REFUGE - CONFISCATED FROM TRAFFICKERS. [119] Ines had believed those words for four years. [127] Now she reconstructed the truth the only way the crates allowed: by shedding layers. [141] Under the green was a coat of gray; under the gray, the original shipping stencils, port codes and dealer numbers from Lome and Rotterdam and a quarantine broker in Miami. [171] Not rescues. [173] Purchases, dressed up as rescues, then photographed for donors who wept and gave. [186]

The chameleon watched her from his branch beside the lamp. [196] He was a veiled male, old for his kind, quietly vivid - not the tourist-brochure blaze of a threat display but a low, steady turquoise that moved through him like weather. [226] She had named him Ledger, though never out loud. [235] Three times he had arrived at Ashfall in a green crate, and three times Ines had known him by the chameleon's tail tip, which was crooked and blunted where an old injury had healed wrong. [270]

The first time, she had told herself that smugglers work the same routes and animals circle back. [287] The second time, she had told herself nothing, which was worse. [298] The third time she had gone to the sheriff's office with her notebook, and that was how the con was revealed, not by any auditor, but by the handler who fed the crickets. [331] Convictions, Ines had learned, are fragile in a particular way: they do not shatter, they flake. [347]

By dawn she had to hand the county her written statement, and the shape of it was the whole trouble. [367] Marisol had taught her how to hold a gravid boa without frightening it. [379] Marisol had sat up with her through a night of tube-feeding an orphaned kit fox and had cried when it died at four in the morning. [405] Marisol had also borrowed against the refuge, and then borrowed against the story of the refuge, until the story had to keep eating animals to stay alive. [432]

Ines wanted two things that did not sit easily together: to keep her own self-respect, which required that she write down every date and every crate, and to keep her compassion, which required that she not pretend she had never loved the woman. [475] Balance, not mercy; balance, not vengeance. [481]

So she worked the crates down to bare wood and copied each code into the ledger, and beside the codes she wrote the other true things, too. [508] That the animals had been fed, and the enclosures kept clean, and that no creature at Ashfall had ever been struck. [529] That Marisol Vane had lied to people and told the truth to animals, and that a court should know the difference even if it could not forgive it. [557] And at the end, the request: that the collection not be auctioned and scattered, that the old ones stay. [576]

The mist thinned toward three o'clock. [582] Ledger shifted along his branch, one eye rolling toward the lamp, and his colors came up slow and green-gold, the way they did when he was warm and unafraid. [611] Ines offered a cricket on her open palm and waited, because waiting was the whole of her craft. [629] The tongue came and went, quicker than the eye, and then the stillness returned, shimmering with dew and starlight and the small sounds of sleeping animals. [655]

She looked at his crooked tail and understood that she was not reconstructing a case. [670] She was reconstructing herself, one stripped layer at a time, down to whatever would hold. [685] At dawn she signed the statement, put on her gloves, and went to open the doors. [701]
