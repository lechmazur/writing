The stairs to the glade were cloud all the way up, and Ilse climbed them the way penitents do, counting. [20] Everyone called it cloudbound repentance, that climb, though nobody agreed on what it absolved. [34] At the top the air thinned into a midnight glade, nine birches standing in grass that had never known soil, and the moon so close it looked breathed on. [63] It was beautiful in the way that closes a throat. [73] The cartomancer was already dealing. [78] She worked in onyx ink, every card hand-drawn in a black so deep it drank the moonlight, and she did not look up. [101] 'You want to know where lost time accumulates,' she said. [111] 'Everyone who climbs does.' [115] Ilse knelt. [117] 'Three years,' she said. [121] 'After the birth.' [124] 'I was there and I was not there.' [132] 'The doctors had names for it; my daughter had only the absence.' [144] The cartomancer turned a card: a woman drawn in onyx lines, holding a child, both faces left blank. [162] 'Payment first,' she said. [166] 'Bring what proves the years existed.' [172] Ilse had carried a bundle up the whole stair, and now she began to unfasten it, the cord stiff with cold, her fingers clumsy. [196] Inside was a tablecloth, and inside the tablecloth, a saucer from a dolls' tea set, cracked clean across. [214] She laid the cloth flat on the impossible grass. [223] It was a family custom, older than her grandmother: on every birthday you traced your hand on the birthday tablecloth in ink, and dated it, so the linen filled year by year with hands growing larger, hands going still, hands stopping. [264] Her daughter's hands marched across it: a starfish at one, a maple leaf at two, a small confident paw at three. [285] Beside each of them, where Ilse's hand should have been, there was nothing. [298] 'And the saucer?' the cartomancer asked. [304] 'She broke it at the second birthday, and cried, and I heard her crying from the next room and could not stand up,' Ilse said. [329] 'That crack is the only thing I remember clearly from three years.' [341] The cartomancer took the saucer and held it up, and through the crack the moonlight came apart into two thin moons. [362] 'This is the door,' she said. [368] 'Lost time gathers where something broke and nobody swept it up.' [379] 'It comes here, all of it.' [385] 'Look.' [386] She pointed down, past the birches, to the cloud floor, and Ilse saw that it was not cloud. [404] It was every unlived hour of everyone below, pale and slowly turning, an ocean of Tuesday afternoons and missed first words, bright as milk, and so vast that looking at it hurt like held breath. [439] 'Can I take mine back?' Ilse asked. [446] The cartomancer laid down her last card, and it was blank, and she studied it for a long time with the expression of someone willingly perplexed, a woman who had chosen, years ago, to stop demanding answers from her own deck. [487] 'No,' she said at last. [492] 'Nobody does; that is the whole point of the climb: you see where it went, and you go back down lighter by exactly the weight of pretending it might return.' [522] Ilse looked down at the shining floor of her own missing years and understood that she had climbed a very long way to be told no. [548] It was, she thought, the kindest no she had ever received. [559] She gathered the tablecloth. [563] 'Keep the saucer,' she said. [568] The cartomancer inked her brush and offered it, handle first. [578] So Ilse spread the linen one more time in that choking, beautiful glade and traced her own hand three times in onyx, in the three blank places, and dated them, and did not pretend she had been there. [616] Under each she wrote four words: I was lost here. [626] Her daughter's fourth birthday was in two days, and there would be a new row to fill, side by side. [646] Going down, she did not look back at the cards, or the moons in the crack, or the bright drowned country beneath the stairs. [670] She had only three years of her own down there, and below her, in a house where a dolls' tea set was now missing one saucer, someone was waiting to trace her hand. [703]
