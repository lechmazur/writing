The last star hung over the ridge fort like a nail holding up the sky. [15] Vessa counted it twice, because counting was how she kept from weeping. [27] Below the walls, the sea chewed the pebble shore with a sound like slow applause. [42] The storm had been at the fort for nine days, flinging rain sideways through the arrow slits, and the garrison had thinned to four soldiers, two goats, and one cartographer who could not stop working. [77] Vessa was the cartographer, and she was mildly obsessed with the problem of the dimming. [92] One by one the stars had gone out over the last three winters, and no one in the capital would say why. [114] Her commission was absurd and she loved it: to draw a map of an endless night, so that when the final star failed, sailors might still find their way by memory. [145] She had charts of every constellation that no longer existed. [155] What she lacked was the oldest sky, the one before the dimming began, and only one person remembered it. [174]

At dawn she went down the cliff stair to the pebble shore, where the oracle sat in her usual place with the surf breaking around her knees. [201] The pebble-shore oracle did not speak. [207] She had not spoken in forty years. [214] She listened to the stones turning in the water, and when a question was worth answering she opened a flaking gold leaf booklet and pressed her thumb to a page until the gilt came away and clung to the questioner's skin. [255] The answer arrived afterward, in the shape of what she refused to say. [268]

"I need the old sky," Vessa said. [275] "You saw it as a girl. I have three nights of oil left, maybe four." [290]

The oracle turned her face toward the ridge fort, where the banners cracked in the wind, and then toward the sea. [311] She opened the booklet. [315] Gold flaked onto Vessa's wrist, bright as a coin, and then the world went quiet. [330] Not silence exactly: an ambered hush, the storm held still inside it like an insect in resin, every wave stopped mid-fall. [351] Vessa stood in that marbled hush for what felt like a season, and she understood that the oracle was answering by summarizing silence. [374] Every gap in the frozen storm was a place where a star had been. [388] The hush had a shape, and the shape was a sky. [399] She had no paper that would survive the spray, so she did what cartographers did before paper: she memorized. [418] Nine hundred absences, arranged in the old order, hanging in the amber like seeds in honey. [434]

When the hush broke, the wave that had been waiting knocked her flat and filled her mouth with salt. [453] The oracle was already climbing the stair, booklet tucked under her arm, thinner by one page. [469]

Vessa worked through the night in the fort's map room while the storm hammered the shutters. [485] The soldiers brought her bread and did not ask questions; they had seen her face when she came up from the shore. [507] By the second night the chart was done, and it was unlike any chart she had made. [524] It marked nothing that shone. [529] It marked only the places where light had been, and the angles between them, so that a helmsman in total dark could hold his course by absence alone. [557]

On the third night the last star went out. [566] The garrison gathered on the wall and nobody spoke; there was nothing to say that the sky had not already said better. [588] Vessa unrolled the chart against the parapet, and by lantern light she began to retell the vanished heavens aloud, constellation by constellation, naming each dead thing in the order the oracle had shown her. [622] The oracle stood at the back of the group with gold flaking from her fingers, and she did not speak, because she did not need to. [648]

Copies went out with the spring ships. [655] Sailors learned to steer by holes in the dark, and the dark, being fully mapped, stopped being endless in the way that frightens people. [679] It was still endless. [683] It was simply known. [687] Vessa kept one flake of gold in a locket, and on quiet evenings she would hold it and listen to the pebbles turning, and hear, very faintly, the whole sky she had been given in a single held breath.
