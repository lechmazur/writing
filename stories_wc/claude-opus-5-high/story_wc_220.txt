After the last resource war, the scrapyard settlement called Nail spread across the cracked wasteland like rust creeping through a wound. [22] Mireille lived in the hollow of an overturned tanker, and every morning she let the old bloodhound out to hunt. [42] Sable had been a body dog once, trained to find the dead under collapsed silos, and she had found too many. [63] Now she was retired from corpses and worked only wax. [73] Phonograph cylinders smelled of beeswax and paraffin, sweet as a memory of orchards, and Sable could scent one through six feet of scrap. [96] The settlement kept a wild hush in the mornings, wind moving through stacked car doors like breath through a broken flute. [117] Mireille listened to that hush the way other people read weather. [128] She was intuitively descriptive; she could not help naming what she touched. [140] A bolt was never a bolt but a stubborn little saint; a groove was never a groove but a furrow plowed by a very small, very patient ox. [168] That habit was why the runners brought her cylinders instead of the machinists. [181] Nobody in Nail had a working phonograph, but Mireille did not need one. [194] She read the grooves directly, sliding a sewing needle along them under an amber lens salvaged from a signal lamp, and translated the tremors in her fingertip into words. [223] Wartime couriers had hidden secret texts in the silent bands between songs, scratched shallow, meant for hands like hers. [242] Everything she learned arrived tinted by that amber lens, honey-colored, warmer than the truth probably was, and she had taught herself to subtract the sweetness. [267] Tinted revelations were still revelations, she said, as long as you remembered the color came from the glass. [285] For three years, one cylinder a season had come east with the salt caravans, each carrying a message from Ancel. [305] He was her husband, conscripted to the brine pans nine hundred miles west, and forbidden plain letters by the treaty censors. [326] So he cut riddles into the quiet grooves, and she spent each season translating distance into closeness, one furrow at a time. [348] That was the whole architecture of her life: needle, lens, dog, and a man's voice arriving as scratches. [366] In the fourth autumn, Sable came out of the northern heap dragging a cylinder case in her jaws and would not set it down until Mireille sat. [393] The dog's tail did not wag; it hung, the way it used to hang over the silos. [410] Mireille felt the wild hush come down over the scrapyard like a hand over a mouth. [426] She set the needle in the silent band and read. [436] The riddle said: I am the year's last word, spoken in red, kept from rotting by the same soft skin that keeps your husband's voice. [461] Where I lie, the ground remembers how to be ground. [471] Under the amber lens the words glowed kind, and Mireille made herself subtract the sweetness twice before she trusted them. [491] The answer was in the case itself, packed beneath the cylinder: an autumn leaf preserved in wax, maple red, veined like a hand held up to a lamp. [519] Nothing had grown a leaf like that in the wasteland for eleven years. [532] Sable pressed her nose to the wax and whined, and Mireille understood the second half of the riddle, the half Ancel had not dared scratch plainly. [558] He was not coming east; he was telling her where to walk. [570] There was living ground out west, past the brine pans, and a tree standing in it, and a man waiting under the tree with a knife and a pot of wax. [601] She packed the needle, three cylinders, water, and the leaf. [611] At the settlement gate, the salvage-elders asked how she could be sure, and she told them she had spent four years learning a man's handwriting in the dark, and you do not mistake that. [645] Then she took the amber lens from her pocket and threw it into the scrap, because she would not need anything tinted where she was going. [671] Sable went first, nose down, reading the cracked earth the way Mireille read wax, and for the first time in years the old dog was hunting something alive. [699] Behind them the scrapyard held its wild hush, and the wasteland, mile by mile, began to fold itself shorter. [718]
