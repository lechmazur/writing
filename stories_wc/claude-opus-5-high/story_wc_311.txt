The reaper came to the observatory at dusk, as he had every dusk since the recordings. [16] He was called Mors by the living, though the name was only a convenience. [30] Reapers do not need names; that was precisely the trouble. [40] The observatory had been abandoned for two centuries, its brass telescope frozen mid-swing toward a star that no longer existed. [60] Dust hung in the dome like suspended snow, and the cracked oculus broke the last sunlight into ribbons that crawled across the floor. [83] It was scattered light, and scattered light was the only medium through which the dead could still be gathered. [102] Mors had learned this from Ilva, who had taught him everything he knew about mercy. [117]

Ilva had been a cartographer of fears. [124] Her work was simple in principle: people died badly when they died of things they could not name, so she gave the nameless things names. [149] She drew them the way the old mapmakers drew the blank edges of the sea. [164] In her satchel Mors still carried a single atlas page, torn from a sixteenth-century folio, its margins crowded with sea monsters: a serpent with a bishop's face, a whale swallowing a fleet, a many-armed thing labeled in faded ink. [203] Sailors had not been mad to draw them, she said. [213] A monster with a name has edges, and anything with edges can be sailed around. [228] The nameless is the whole ocean. [234]

Eleven years ago, a grief had come ashore in the town below the observatory, a thing without shape that emptied houses one by one. [258] Ilva went down to name it. [264] To name a fear, she had explained once, you must let it take you first, so it will speak its name into you, and then you must say it aloud where others can hear. [298] She said it aloud. [302] The town lived; she did not; and Mors, who had come that night to carry her, arrived to find only the sound of her still ringing in the dome. [331] That was the echo of sacrifice: not a ghost, not a soul, only the shape a voice leaves in a room it saved. [354] He had nothing to reap. [359]

For eleven years he came at dusk and heard nothing. [369] Then, last month, beneath a collapsed cabinet, he found the old recordings: wax cylinders Ilva had made of her fieldwork, labeled in her cramped hand. [394] Since then the dome had begun to speak. [402] It echoed forgotten phrases, fragments with the meaning worn off them, the way a stair worn smooth forgets the feet. [422] Bring the lamp closer; the current runs north here; say it with me. [435] They arrived out of order and addressed no one. [444] This was the cruelty he had not expected: to be spoken to intimately by someone standing at an unbridgeable distance, a voice in the next room of a house that had burned. [476]

Tonight he set the atlas page on the floor where the ribbons of light would cross it. [493] Reapers cannot raise the dead, but they can crystallize an echo, if it is given something to gather around. [512] Salt needs a thread; sound needs a name. [520] He read the monsters aloud, one by one, the serpent, the whale, the many-armed thing, giving the dome its old work back. [542] The light broke on the page and the phrases began to come in order. [556] Bring the lamp closer, she said, and he did. [565] Say it with me, she said, and in the scattered light something thickened above the page, not a woman, but a clear hard shape like ice forming on a rope. [595]

He understood then what she had left him, and it was not herself. [608] It was the method. [612] The echo held the last fear she had named, and she had meant it to be passed on, the way a lamp is passed. [636] Mors spoke the name with her, and it crystallized, falling onto the page as a small clear stone. [654] The dome went quiet in a way that felt, for the first time, like rest rather than absence. [672]

He folded the page around the stone and went down into the town, where a mother was sitting up with a fear she could not name. [698] He would give it edges. [703] He would be, from now on, the cartographer of the blank places, and he would draw monsters in the margins with a steady hand. [727] Behind him, the observatory darkened, and the last ribbon of light withdrew from the floor like a hand let go gently. [748]
