The sanctuary had been a forest once, and now it was charcoal, mile on mile of black trunks standing under a sky so crowded with stars that the ash glittered. [30] Mirren kept the shadows there, because shadows need somewhere with nothing left to burn. [44] She was tender with them, as a nurse is tender, and as unsentimental. [57] At the center of the burn stood a door frame with no door, and bolted to the frame was a doorknocker ring of old iron, the last threshold in the country. [88] Thresholds are what keep the dead's shadows from wandering into kitchens and cradles, and this one was fading. [106] Mirren could see the iron thinning, the way frost thins at noon. [118] When a threshold fades, shadows encroach. [124] They come in slow and polite, a handspan a night, and then one morning a village wakes up unable to breathe. [145] Mirren knew the feeling intimately; it was why she had taken the post. [158]

Her work was mostly burning bridges, and she burned them carefully. [169] Every footbridge, cart bridge, and rope span that touched the sanctuary had to go, because shadows travel by crossings, and a careless fire only makes a charred crossing that still holds weight. [201] So she burned from the far bank inward, slow, measuring, leaving nothing a shadow could mistake for a road. [220] Eleven years of that had made the black forest, and the black forest had made her the quietest woman alive. [240]

Her method was linking through scars. [244] A shadow cannot read a face, but it remembers injury; press an old burn to an old burn and the shadow understands it is being spoken to. [271] Mirren's forearms were mapped with seams from the bridges; she had a scar for every crossing she had unmade, and through them she could hold a shadow still and teach it the thing it had forgotten. [307] That was the whole of the office: to teach suffocating to breathe. [319] Shadows suffocate because they are still trying to live in a chest that stopped. [333] You hold them, you breathe, and they copy you until they can let the habit go. [349]

On the night the ring began to go translucent, a boy came out of the black trunks with no bridge behind him, which meant he had waded. [376] He was fourteen, ash to the knees, and he had Ase's jaw. [388] "My mother said you were her sister," he said, "before you stopped answering." [401] Eleven years after cutting off all contact, Mirren found she still had a voice for family, and it came out rusted. [422] "I stopped because a keeper who is loved makes a bridge of herself," she said. [437]

The boy held out his hand; in his palm lay a second doorknocker ring, cold, exact, the twin of the one on the frame. [461] "She died in the spring," he said. "She left me this and said the threshold was hers to renew, and that she would not let it fade on you alone." [491] Then Mirren understood the emptiness she had been carrying for eleven years was not empty at all; it had been loaded the whole time, holding weight, like a bridge she had never quite burned. [525]

Ase's shadow came out of the trees behind him, gasping the way they all gasp. [540] It had waded too. [544] Mirren pushed back her sleeve and found the seam on her wrist from the first bridge, the one at the mill, the one Ase had been standing on when the beam fell and marked her the same. [581] Sisters with matching scars: the oldest link there is. [590] She took the shadow's wrist to hers and breathed, slow, under the crowded stars, until the gasping steadied into something that did not need a chest. [616]

Then she gave the boy the hammer and let him drive the new ring into the frame, because the office passes through the family, and the threshold held. [644] He stayed till morning, and she did not send word to anyone, and she did not burn the way he had come. [666] Some crossings, she decided, you keep, and you learn to breathe across them. [679]
