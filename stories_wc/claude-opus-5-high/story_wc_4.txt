Every August, when the vines climbed the factory's rusted ribs, Mirela opened her dove gray map case in the lavender field and waited. [23] She was the town's puzzle archivist, a subtle profession nobody had invented on purpose. [37] For thirty years she had catalogued the jigsaws, cryptograms, and homemade riddles that Kessel's families left behind when they moved away, and she had learned that a missing piece tells you more than a finished picture. [73] The town's missing piece was the day the dye works burned. [84] Eleven people gone, a whistle that never blew again, and afterward a silence so polite it became a second kind of injury. [106] Nobody spoke of it; everybody carried it. [113] Mirela, who was perceptively calm in the way of people who listen for a living, decided that grief was simply a puzzle laid facedown. [137] Her method was arcs in black-and-white photographs. [144] It had begun with an accident of filing. [151] Two old prints slipped together in the map case: a crane boom curving over the loading yard in 1961, and a girl's jump rope frozen mid-loop in 1978. [179] The two curves matched exactly, and together they made a single unbroken circle. [192] Mirela sat down on the archive floor and laughed until she cried, which she had not done since the funerals. [212] So she put a notice in the grocery window: BRING ME YOUR PHOTOGRAPHS, ESPECIALLY THE ONES WITH CURVES. [230] They came slowly, then all at once. [237] Dora Vasic brought a smokestack's shadow bending across snow. [246] Old Peter Lund brought the arc of water from a fire hose, photographed by a newspaperman on the worst morning of his life, and his hands shook when he let it go. [278] A welder's daughter brought sparks fountaining in a perfect parabola. [288] Mirela slid each print into the dove gray map case, which had once held the factory's floor plans and now held something better: the shape of what the town had done with its hours. [322] Her intention, which she stated plainly to anyone who asked, was to transmute pain into a masterpiece. [339] People smiled at the word, and then helped anyway. [348] The work took four summers. [353] Mirela traced every arc onto glass, then cut and fired the pieces, until the curves interlocked like a jigsaw with no straight edges. [376] The fire hose met the jump rope; the sparks met the smokestack's shadow; eleven small clear panes stood open where no photograph existed, because absence deserves a frame too. [405] They mounted it in the lavender field tucked behind the ruins, where Dora had planted rows years ago simply because the soil was warm and nobody was watching. [433] The window rose on a steel frame salvaged from the dye works, facing west, so that the evening sun poured through the arcs and scattered them across the lavender. [462] On the first evening, ninety-one people came. [469] Nobody had planned speeches, which was fortunate, because nobody could manage one. [481] Peter Lund found the water from his photograph lying in a pale curve across his own shoes and made a sound that was half sob, half delight. [508] Children ran through the eleven clear panes' light, which fell as plain white rectangles, and shouted the names printed beneath them. [529] It was the first time the names had been said aloud in public for nineteen years. [545] After that, people came to linger. [551] That was the true design, Mirela admitted later: not the glass, but the lingering. [565] Grief hurried out of town is grief that returns; grief that sits in a field until dusk, among strangers who share it, becomes something a person can carry with two hands. [596] The healing was collective or it was nothing; one woman with a map case could not have done it, and she never claimed she had. [621] Each August the vines climb higher on the factory's ribs, and the ruins grow greener and less frightening. [639] Mirela, eighty now, still opens the dove gray map case in the field, because people keep finding curves in their attics. [660] There is a plan, drawn in her tidy hand, for a second window; there are already forty arcs waiting, and two of them belong to weddings. [686] She files them patiently, one curve at a time, the way you solve anything worth solving. [702] The lavender does the rest. [707]
