The staircase had no top, and Vashti had long since stopped pretending otherwise. [13] She climbed anyway, because a woman who expects nothing can still enjoy the view. [27] Grim, her old colleagues had called her; optimistic, said everyone who watched her keep climbing. [42] Both were correct, and the contradiction did not trouble her, since she had built a career out of contradictions that resolved. [63]

The tower breathed a cold mineral air. [70] Landings repeated every thirteen steps, each with the same iron sconce, the same chalk marks she herself had left, the same faint smell of a candle burned an hour ago. [100] Infinity here was not vast; it was cramped, a boundless room with the walls always at her elbows. [118]

In her pocket rolled a pair of dice, one chipped at the corner where her brother Emil had thrown it against a hearthstone. [141] He had asked her, once, to stake everything on a machine he had drawn on the back of a tax receipt: a wheel of weighted arms that would, he swore, turn forever. [173] Perpetual motion, he called it. [178] Impossible, she called it, and proved it in four lines, and watched his face go quiet, and did not go with him to the foundry the next morning. [206] He died three winters later, and the proof stayed correct, and that was the worst of it. [223]

She had come to the tower on purpose, which was more than most of its prisoners could say. [241] The academy had achieved perpetual motion, in the end, not with weighted arms but with geometry: a stair that returned the climber to her own beginning while insisting she had risen. [272] Her labor turned some engine she would never see, and the tower drank her steps and returned only more steps. [292] Emil had been right about the wanting and wrong about the wheel; she had been right about the wheel and wrong about everything else. [316]

She had not come to escape. [322] She had come to capture the texture of a missed opportunity -- to hold in her hands the exact grain of the morning she did not walk to the foundry -- and for that she needed the tower to break. [360] A sealed system reveals its seam only at the moment of rupture. [372]

So she worked through mathematical proofs, the only tools she had ever trusted. [385] On landing after landing she chalked the recursion, mapped the stair's return map, hunted the fixed point where the geometry had to lie to itself. [410] Every closed loop conserves something; find the quantity it fails to conserve, and the loop is not closed. [428]

The dice were her randomizer. [433] She threw them at each landing and recorded the pair, and because one die was chipped it fell short of fair by a measurable bias. [458] A perfect tower would swallow the bias, averaging her throws into flatness across infinitely many landings. [474] A tower that cheated would not. [480]

After eleven thousand throws the chipped die's bias came back doubled, and Vashti sat down on the cold step and laughed until she coughed. [504] Doubled meant the tower was counting her landings twice: the stair was not infinite but folded, two turns of a loop wearing one face. [528] Fold a thing twice and there is a crease. [537]

The crease, by her arithmetic, lay at the twenty-sixth landing above her, and she went up to it balancedly daring, neither rushing nor hesitating, the way one approaches a proof one is almost sure of. [572] There she wrote the contradiction out in full on the wall, every line earned, ending in the flat statement that the space could not exist. [597] Then she threw the dice one last time. [605] The chipped corner struck stone, and the stone answered. [614]

The rupture was not loud. [619] It was a seam of grey light opening along the crease like a page turning, and through it came the smell of hot iron and coal smoke, and morning. [648] She did not step through; she knew better than to believe a tower would hand her the past. [666] But she stood in the opening with her eyes shut and let the foundry air go over her face, and there it was, the whole missed morning, its exact grain: cold cobbles, Emil waiting, the particular weight of a yes she had not said. [710] She held it as long as the seam lasted, which was eleven seconds. [723]

Then the light closed, and the landings resumed, thirteen steps apart, sconce after sconce. [737] Vashti picked up the dice, both of them, and put them in her pocket. [751] The crease would be somewhere else now; folds move. [760] She had a wall of chalk, an unfair die, and forever. [771] She began to climb. [775]
