I have never kept a face in my head, not my mother's, not my own. [15] What I keep instead are measurements, and the county keeps me for it. [28] For eleven years I have drawn the dead and the missing out of cold cases, working from index cards in a very methodical order: bone, then breath, then the thing nobody says out loud. [62] Detective Okonjo brought me the Ardley file in March, and the office went into that furrowed hush that means everyone has already decided the case is a tomb. [90] Nineteen years ago a girl named Hanne Ardley left a masquerade dance at the grange hall and was found at the bottom of the shadowed quarry in mist so thick the searchers had to hold the rope to find each other. [131] One piece of evidence survived: a two-inch fragment of ribbon from a mask, oxblood on one side, cream on the other, wound in her fist. [156] Two witnesses had described the man she danced with, and their descriptions were opposites. [170] One swore he was soft-faced, boyish, kind-eyed; the other swore he was hollow, cruel, hard as a hatchet. [188] The old investigators had chosen a side, which is why they failed. [200] I cannot choose sides, because I cannot see a whole face; I only build them, by unifying opposites, the way a jeweler solders two halves into one ring. [228] So I drew both men on one sheet, left half boy, right half hatchet, and the seam between them ran exactly where a half-mask's edge would lie. [255] Both witnesses had seen the same stranger; one had seen him masked, one unmasked, an hour apart. [272] I asked for the ribbon so I could match the dye lot, and that was the week the evidence disappeared. [292] Locker 14 held an empty envelope with an intact seal and a signature nobody could read. [308] After the evidence disappears, a cold case usually dies twice, and the office hush deepened toward relief. [325] That relief is what I hate most about this work: not cruelty, but the comfortable sleep of people who have stopped imagining other people. [349] I keep drawing to awaken dormant empathy, mine included; a face I build by hand is the only way I can love someone I would not recognize on the street. [379] Because I am methodical, I had photographed the ribbon fragment from nine angles before it vanished, including the frayed end under raking light. [402] And because I am methodical, I had also catalogued every painting I ever restored in this county, which is how I remembered the grange hall's mural. [428] It showed the 1994 masquerade committee, painted from a photograph by a local man, and one blistered patch had been swelling under the varnish for years. [454] With Okonjo watching, I set the tip of my palette knife against it and lanced the blister. [471] Under the bloom of ruined varnish lay the thing the painter had covered: a dancer in a half-mask, its ribbon oxblood on one face and cream on the reverse. [500] The painter had signed the mural in the same illegible hand as the evidence envelope. [515] He was seventy-one now, a retired property clerk who had spent nineteen years walking past his own confession. [533] We met him at the quarry, because he asked to go there, and the mist came up off the water like something exhaling. [556] I could not tell his face from the rock, so I did what I always do: I asked him to describe himself on the night of the dance, and then as he stood before me, and I drew both halves on one page. [599] The dancer he described was gentle; the man before me was a stone. [612] When I turned the drawing toward him, he looked at the seam and began, at last, to shake. [630] He told us he had taken the fragment back because Hanne's fist in his memory had finally started to feel like a hand. [653] He had kept it in his coat pocket for eight days, and the sleeping thing in him had woken up and would not let him sleep. [679] He handed Okonjo the ribbon, two inches of oxblood and cream, the two opposite faces of one stolen evening. [698] Okonjo's shoulders came down, and the mist held its breath around us. [710] I still cannot tell you what Hanne looked like, and I never will. [723] But I have her measurements, her mask, her two contradictory dance partners reconciled into one man, and I have the file closed in my own careful hand. [750] That is how I recognize people: not by their faces, but by the shape of what they refuse to remember. [770]
