The campfire on the dune spat its last spark, and that was Mira's signal to climb. [16] She had built the fire herself, as she always did, so that a client could watch certainty burn down to ash before asking her anything. [41] Tonight's client was Wren Halloran, harbormaster, who had come to have a man condemned. [55] The lighthouse at Cobb's Reach was breezy in every weather, but at dawn the wind came clean off the water and made the tower sing. [80] Sixteen wind chimes hung from the gallery rail, and Mira's whole profession lived in which ones she chose to leave up. [101] Bone, brass, bottle glass, driftwood, a string of old key blanks: each had a voice, and the voices together made an answer. [123]

She was a conscience for hire. [129] People with hard decisions paid her to carry the doubt they could not afford to carry themselves. [146] Her fee was honest; her manner, she admitted, was sincerely insincere. [157] She performed solemnity she did not always feel, because people needed ceremony before they could hear anything true. [175]

Wren arrived with salt in her hair and a folded warrant in her coat. [189] "Ferris stole the relief grain," Wren said. [196] "I saw the crates on his boat, and I am certain." [207]

"Then you do not need me," Mira said, which she told everyone and never meant. [222] Wren stayed, as they all stayed. [228]

Mira led her up the spiral stairs to the gallery, where the chimes were still shrouded in sailcloth. [246] The ritual was simple and, Mira believed, not entirely a lie. [257] She asked the client to describe what she knew, and while she listened she unhooked chimes, one by one, from the rail. [279] Every chime she unhooked was a certainty she had heard and found sound. [292] Whatever still hung at sunrise was what the wind would question. [303]

Wren talked for an hour: the crates, the tide table, the ledger, the way Ferris would not meet her eyes. [323] Down came bone, down came brass, down came the bottle glass. [334] But the driftwood chime stayed, because the tide table she quoted belonged to the wrong month. [350] And the string of key blanks stayed, because Ferris had never held a key to the grain store. [368]

At dawn the wind came up and the two remaining chimes spoke together, wood knocking under a thin metal rain. [388] Deciphering the selection was not magic; it was only arithmetic performed out loud by weather. [403] Still, Wren went pale, because a person will believe a sound before she believes an argument. [419]

"Someone with a key moved those crates onto his boat," Mira said. [431] "Maybe your deputy." [434] "Maybe you are still right." [439]

Then Mira did the thing she was actually paid for. [449] She took off the cloak of doubt. [456] It was a plain grey oilcloth thing, heavy at the shoulders, clasped at the throat by a hollow mirror shard set in lead. [479] The shard was hollow because its silvering had been scraped away from the back, so it held no reflection at all. [500] You looked into it and saw the room behind you instead of your own certain face. [516]

Mira hung the cloak on Wren's shoulders and fastened the clasp. [527] "Wear it until the hearing," she said. [534] "It will not stop you." [539] "It will only slow you down by one honest question." [549]

That was the whole of her trade: not to replace anyone's certainty, but to welcome nuance into it, the way a harbor welcomes a difficult ship. [575] Wren stood in the doorway, wrapped and weighted, looking smaller and somehow safer. [588] That was the point of the cloak; doubt, worn openly, is armor against the shame of being wrong. [606]

"You will want it back," Wren said. [613] "I always do," said Mira, who owned three. [621]

She watched the harbormaster go down the cliff path, then climbed back up and rehung the chimes she had unhooked, every one, for the next person who arrived sure. [650] Alone on the gallery, she touched her bare throat where the clasp had been, unguarded, and let the wind ask her its own questions. [674] Ferris might still be guilty. [678] Mira hoped she had not just delayed justice by a week to save her own sleep. [694] Then she lit the campfire fresh for the evening, and waited. [705]
