They called Vela the luminous skeptic, and both halves were true. [11] Twelve years of grinding phosphor for the lamp guild had left her skin faintly alight, so that she moved through the city of forgotten fountains like a lantern nobody had asked for. [43] The skeptic half was louder. [48] She did not believe the fountains remembered anything. [56] Still, she was realistically imaginative, which is a rarer thing than dreaming: she could only picture what might actually be built, and she pictured it relentlessly. [82]

So when old Mirek the fountain-keeper died in the spring, leaving her his ledger and a key of no known shape, she read the ledger twice and then went looking for the lock. [115] The ledger said: the memories are not gone, only stuck, and what is stuck can be unstuck. [132] It also said: follow the crumbs. [138] That part she had laughed at, until she found them. [148]

Mirek had baked his trail into anise bread, which birds refuse, and pressed the crumbs into the mortar seams of the dry basins all across the lower quarter. [176] Nine years of rain had not taken them. [184] By following breadcrumbs, she walked a map no council could confiscate: from the Fountain of Widows to the cracked lion mouth on Sallow Street, from the lion to the basin where the glassblowers once washed their hands. [221] The council had drained the fountains a generation ago, calling it a drought measure. [235] Vela, who measured water for the guild, knew the aqueducts ran full. [247]

Under the glassblowers' basin, behind a plug of tallow, the crumbs ended at the stained glass box. [264] It was the size of a bread loaf, leaded in nine colors, and it hummed against her palms like a hive in winter. [287] Through the red pane she saw a wedding; through the green, a boy learning to swim in water that no longer existed. [309] Her skepticism did not break; it simply enlarged to include the box. [321] Something real was in there, and real things have hinges. [331]

Mirek's last pages explained the price, and the price was sidereal patience. [343] The box had been sealed by glassblowers under a particular star, and it would only consent to open when that star stood again in the same place above the same cracked basin. [375] Not a season, not an anniversary; a star's own reckoning, indifferent to councils and to the impatience of women who glow. [396] Mirek had marked the hour the only way a keeper could: from the pause of migrating geese. [413] Every autumn the flocks settled three nights on the emptied basins, mistaking pale stone for water, and on the third night the star arrived. [437]

She waited, counting forward from the previous autumn's pause, which Mirek had circled in the ledger in his shaking hand. [458] Waiting taught her something she would not have invented: that patience is not surrender but a slower kind of trespass. [478] Word moved anyway, in the way of such cities, a defiant hush passing basin to basin. [494] Nobody shouted; people simply began leaving cups on the dry rims, a hundred small refusals arranged in a row. [513]

When the geese came down that October, the lower quarter was crowded and almost silent. [528] On the third night Vela knelt in the glassblowers' basin, and the star came over the rim exactly as promised, and the lead seams of the box loosened like old glue. [559] She had come to liberate trapped memories, and she had expected, being herself, to find a mechanism. [576] She found one. [579] The panes unstuck one by one and each released its held thing as vapor: the wedding, the swimming boy, a grandmother's name, the smell of the fountains running. [607]

The vapor went into the crowd and people began, quietly, to remember what the water had been for. [625] By dawn someone had broken the seal on the aqueduct gate, and the geese, waking to find the basins genuinely wet, refused to leave for a further day. [653]

Vela sat on the rim with the empty stained glass box in her lap, still faintly shining, still doubting. [672] She doubted it would hold. [677] She doubted the council would let the water stay a month. [688] But she had learned the star's trick, and stars are not in a hurry, and neither, now, was she. [707] She began baking anise bread that evening, and in the ledger's last blank page she wrote the date, and the name of the next star. [732]
