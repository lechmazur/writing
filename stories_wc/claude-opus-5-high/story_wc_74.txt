Ilmira had not slept properly in eleven years, so she had learned to make use of the dark. [18] Her studio was a greenhouse bolted to the roof of a dye factory, nine floors above a city that coughed. [38] Smog pooled in the streets like spilled ink, but up here the air was thin enough to see through, and the glass panes held the smell of marigolds and turmeric from the vats below. [72] She called that saffron quiet: the hour when the factory slept and her hands finally steadied. [88] On the workbench lay her grandfather's brush, its handle tipped with a sliver of pale shell he had called moon-bone. [108] He had been a calligrapher of Sivvat, a valley tongue with forty-one letters, thirty of which nobody alive could pronounce. [128] When the valley emptied, its words went with the people, scattering into other cities, other mouths, until only the shapes survived. [149] Ilmira painted the shapes. [153]

She believed a language was never truly lost, only sleeping in the bodies of the people who had once been loved in it. [176] To rouse it, she had built a method; she was methodically hopeful, the way a gardener is hopeful in February. [196] Each month, as the waxing moon climbed over the smokestacks, she painted one Sivvat character onto a pane of glass and hung it where the light would pass through. [225] Then she invited a stranger up to sit beneath it. [235] Moonlight through wet paint does something to a tired mind: it loosens it, sets it drifting, and the drifting always goes backward. [257] Her visitors daydreamed, and in the daydreams they found words they did not know they carried. [273]

The tram conductor with the burned hand murmured ishren, then covered her mouth. [286] In Sivvat, ishren meant thank you for the water, and the conductor could not say where she had learned it, only that a man had once carried buckets up to her family's stalled train. [320] That was the ninth entry in Ilmira's ledger, and every entry was a word for gratitude, shelter, or bread. [339] This was the thing she was trying to prove, and it mattered more to her than sleep: that of everything a dying tongue leaves behind, what strangers hold longest is kindness. [370]

The tenth visitor came with a clipboard. [376] His name was Ganev, and he had been sent by the consortium buying the dye factory to measure the roof for removal; the greenhouse had four days left. [404] Ilmira did not argue. [408] She poured him tea the color of the vats and pointed at the crate she used as a chair, under the newest pane, where the waxing moon was three nights from full. [440] He sat because he was tired, and because the greenhouse smelled like his grandmother's kitchen. [455] Ilmira took up the moon-tipped brush and began the next character anyway, slowly, letting him drift. [471] The light came through the painted glass and lay across his face in a shape like a bird with one long leg. [493] His clipboard slid to the floor. [498] Somewhere between the smog and the glass he was six years old again, on a station platform in a coat that did not belong to him, and a woman he never saw again was saying a word over him like a blessing. [540]

"Haveth," Ganev said, out loud, to nobody. [547] Ilmira set down the brush with both hands, the way you set down something that is still warm. [565] It meant stay, you are not a burden here. [574] She wrote it in the ledger as the tenth entry, and under it, the date and the phase of the moon. [595]

Ganev did not promise her the roof; he was only a man with a clipboard. [610] But he asked for the word again, wrote it on his wrist in her ink, and said he would put the greenhouse down as a structure of record, which would cost the consortium a year of paperwork. [647] A year was eleven more moons. [653] She would need eleven more strangers. [659]

Ilmira walked him to the stairwell, then came back and stood among the geraniums while the moon finished its slow climb, waxing, patient, unhurried as her method. [686] She did not sleep. [690] She washed the shell tip clean, mixed a new ink, and began the eleventh letter of a language that was, she could now write down as evidence, remembering itself through the kindness of strangers.
