The orchard grew in the rafters, roots laced through stone ribs where the roof of Saint Almet's had fallen away. [20] Vesna climbed it every morning, her quartz eyes catching the light like two chips of frozen river. [37] She was the mender, the last one, and the trees were her ledger. [50] Every vow the valley made was written on a leaf and grown into the canopy: marriages, debts, truces, apprenticeships. [69] Deciduous vows, the old word for them, because every autumn they yellowed and fell and had to be spoken again. [89] A promise that could not survive being repeated, Vesna liked to say, was only a mood. [105] She had said it once to her daughter Mira, and Mira had laughed, and then Mira had left. [123] That was the breach of contract: the mender's line was bound to tend the orchard, and Mira had walked out of the cathedral in the middle of a grafting season and never renewed a single leaf. [159] Eleven autumns later the valley still called the orchard cursed, and the townsfolk swore their oaths on paper instead, which tore. [180] Then the girl came up the bell-tower stair with mud on her knees. [193] "You're my grandmother," she announced, "and your eyes are rocks, and I'm not leaving." [207] Her name was Juna, she was nine, and she was endearingly feisty in the exact way Mira had been. [226] "Your mother sent you?" [230] "My mother died in the spring," Juna said, chin up, daring the old woman to soften. [246] "She said you'd know what to do with a broken promise." [257] Vesna did not know. [261] She knew grafting, pruning, the trick of reading a leaf's veins for a lie; she did not know how to mend eleven fallen years. [285] So she did what menders do, which is persist. [294] She put the girl to work. [300] All summer they climbed the rafters together, harvesting quince from the nave's high beams, thinning pears above the altar where a shaft of noon light fell like a dropped coin. [330] Juna argued with everything: the ladder, the wasps, the theory of vows. [342] "If they fall off every year, they're not promises, they're just chores," she said, and Vesna, who had spent sixty years defending the tradition, found she had no argument left, only habit. [374] "Then help me invent a better one," Vesna said, and that was the first honest thing she had said aloud in a decade. [397] Wisdom, she was learning, mostly arrived by accident, the way fruit arrives: you tend, you wait, you are surprised. [416] In autumn the leaves went gold and the canopy emptied itself over the flagstones, eleven years of unrenewed vows crackling underfoot. [437] Juna wanted to swear a new vow, a permanent one: an evergreen graft, she called it, grandmother and granddaughter, nobody leaving. [458] They needed grafting wax, and there was none in the cathedral. [469] Vesna remembered the satchel she had hung on a rafter nail the day Mira left and never touched again. [488] Inside, dried to sweetness: a cake of wax, a pruning knife orange with rust, a packet of seeds, and, coiled at the bottom, forgotten, her daughter's hair ribbon. [516] Green, frayed, tied by Vesna's own hands the morning of the grafting season Mira had abandoned. [532] They used all of it. [537] The knife took the scion; the wax sealed it; the ribbon bound the joint, green cloth over green wood, high in the rafters above the altar. [563] "Say it fresh every autumn anyway," Vesna told her, "not because the old words rot, but because you'll be a different girl each time, and she deserves to be asked too." [594] Juna thought about it, scowling, then nodded, because it was a bargain and not a rule. [610] That was the bridge, Vesna understood: not handing down the old law intact, but letting the child cut into it and letting the cut heal. [635] By spring the graft had taken. [641] By the autumn after, the valley came back, one family at a time, climbing the bell-tower stair to have their leaves written, because a girl with mud on her knees stood in the doorway telling everyone the orchard was open and the terms were negotiable. [686] Vesna sat under the grafted branch with the ribbon weathering to grey above her and quartz light on the stones. [706] She had mended nothing, exactly. [711] She had only kept climbing, and something had grown across the gap. [723]
