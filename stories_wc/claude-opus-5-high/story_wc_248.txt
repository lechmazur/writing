Two years after going off the grid, Ilke came down from the ridge because the wind smelled of burning paper. [20] She had no phone, no address, no name that any registry still held, yet the fires found her anyway, the way he always had. [44] The news in the valley towns called him the lending library arsonist: nine little roadside book boxes burned in nine weeks, each emptied of books first, the volumes stacked neatly in the grass like mourners. [79] Ilke knew Corvin had never destroyed a book in his life. [90] He was building a sentence out of fire, and she was the only reader it had. [106] In the ash of the first box she found a tile he had painted: a salt flat under a black sun. [127] The second held a clay bird with numbers pressed into its wing. [139] By the fifth she was working the way she always had, analytically intuitive, letting the numbers settle into her hands until they turned into a feeling, and the feeling into a map. [171] A trail of artistic creations leading somewhere was an old game between them, begun when they were students smuggling love notes through the university's print shop. [197] The sixth fire gave her a faded tapestry fragment, no bigger than a hand, its threads the color of old tea. [218] She recognized it before she breathed: Sereen work, eighth century, stolen from the archive they had opened together beneath the salt. [239] They had found that lost civilization's archive when they were twenty-six and certain of everything: nine basalt rooms, shelves of woven records, air that had not moved in twelve hundred years. [270] They had promised that room two things: that nothing would leave it, and that they would come back to seal it before the brine rose. [295] Then the grant collapsed, the university sold the coordinates to a mining consortium, and Ilke walked into the hills with a tent and a grudge the size of a mountain. [325] Corvin had stayed to argue. [330] Now, apparently, he had stopped arguing. [336] The ninth box stood at a crossroads outside Drenov, still warm. [347] Inside the blackened shell, propped upright, was his self-portrait on a copper plate, and someone had taken a blade to it. [368] The face was gouged through, eye to jaw, the copper showing raw beneath the paint. [383] Ilke sat down in the wet grass and held it and did not cry, because the thing in her chest was hotter than grief and gentler than rage. [411] Then she turned it to the low sun. [419] The vandalism was his own hand. [425] The gouges were not random but Sereen tally marks, the same notation they had spent a winter decoding, and laid over the tile, the bird, the tapestry threads, they resolved into a hidden message: BRINE AT ROOM SEVEN. COME. I KEPT IT OPEN. [468] He had destroyed his own face to say it, because he knew she would not look twice at anything undamaged. [488] She drove all night. [492] The mining road ended at a fence with a hole cut in it and a library book wedged in the wire as a marker. [516] Below, in the dark of the seventh room, she found him waist-deep in rising water, passing bundles of woven records up to a shelf he had built from scaffold pipe. [546] He had been at it for eleven days. [554] "You came," he said, and his voice cracked like something volcanic finally finding air. [568] "You burned nine libraries," she said, shaking, furious, already rolling up her sleeves. [581] "I emptied them first," he said, "and they are lending libraries; I intend to return everything." [597] They worked until dawn and past it, two people who had wasted two years rescuing a forgotten promise from the water. [618] When the last bundle was up, they sealed the door with the clay and the salt, as the Sereen had done. [639] Ilke put the tapestry fragment back last, laying it against the basalt like a hand on a forehead. [657] Outside, in the white glare, Corvin gave her the ruined copper plate. [669] "Fix it," he said. [673] "No," she said. [676] "I'll portray you properly instead, scars and arson and all, and hang it where the consortium's lawyers have to walk past it every morning." [700] He laughed, and she understood that she had never gone off the grid at all; she had only been standing very still, waiting for him to light something she could follow home. [732]
