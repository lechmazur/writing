My brother confessed at 1:40 in the morning, then said nothing else, and I went out into that silence with a headlamp. [22] The hush after a sudden confession has a texture; it is the drowsy alertness of a room that has heard too much and refuses to hear more. [49] I am Dell Rourke, and I appraise scrap for the county salvage office, which is a polite way of saying I climb into abandoned mills that other people have the sense to avoid. [82] Rotten catwalks, floors thin as crackers: I have gone through two of them and learned nothing. [98] Ovid told me he opened the steam line on the night of the Halloran Mill accident. [114] Nine men on third shift, and he was seventeen, and he turned the wrong wheel because a foreman shouted at him. [135] Then he stopped talking, the way a man does when he has set down something he carried for thirty years and cannot bear to look at where it landed. [164] I wanted the weight of it. [170] Not absolution - I have never believed in the machinery of forgiveness, no priest, no saints, no tunnel of light - just the weight, in pounds, of a swallowed truth. [198] So I went to the train tunnel under the Halloran works, where the coal spur used to run. [216] The mill above is condemned; the tunnel below is merely forgotten, which is worse and better. [232] Water beads on the brick arch and drips in a rhythm like a slow clock. [247] At three in the morning the tunnel begins to breathe. [257] Salvage crews call it the ghost shift: a shudder of belts and shafting overhead, a whistle, boots on iron. [276] I have measured it and found it to be wind in the elevator shaft, pigeons, and a loose door. [295] I believe in wind. [299] What I do not have is a witness, because every man who could tell me whether Ovid's story is true died in 1971 or drank himself quiet afterward. [327] So I use the radio. [332] Down in the brick, only one station comes through clean, a thousand-watt AM signal that runs psychics all night, and the callers are frantic in a way that nobody is frantic in daylight. [365] I do not believe the psychics. [371] I believe the callers. [375] Mill towns keep their secrets in the mouths of widows and grandchildren, and at two in the morning those mouths open to a stranger on the telephone who charges nothing. [405] So I sit with a notebook and appraise their grief the way I appraise brass: for content, for adulteration, for weight. [426] A woman from Shelby asked whether her father was at peace, and mentioned, incidentally, that he came home from Halloran that night with his hands cooked and would not say who sent him to the manifold. [462] A man called three weeks later to ask if his uncle forgave him, and named the uncle: Kolar, the foreman. [482] The psychic said white light; the caller said Kolar had the boys running the bypass with the interlock chained open, for production, for six months. [507] Eleven callers over two years, and the pattern held. [516] Ovid's hand was on the wheel. [522] Kolar's hand was on the mill. [528] That night, in the hush, I walked the tunnel to the loading bay and climbed up through the coal chute into the pump room. [552] The ghost shift was running: belts sighing, iron ticking, that whistle that is only a broken vent. [569] I found the bypass manifold and the beaded valve wheel still on it, brass, knurled bead around the rim, the kind you can turn with wet hands. [596] The chain was still there too, rusted through the interlock lug, exactly as a frightened caller had described it to a woman who reads auras. [621] I cut the wheel off with a hacksaw and weighed it on my scale at the truck. [638] Eleven pounds, four ounces; scrap value, nineteen dollars. [646] I brought it to Ovid's kitchen and set it on the table between us, where it made a sound like a bell that had been told to be quiet. [675] He looked at the beads on the rim and started to cry, and then he started to talk, and this time he did not stop. [700] Kolar's name came out of him like a nail out of a board. [713] I do not think the dead are working that third shift. [724] I think a building keeps its habits, and a man keeps his, and both can be surveyed if you are willing to stay awake and go inside. [751] The wheel sits on my desk now, holding down a stack of appraisals. [764] Eleven pounds, four ounces. [768] Now we both know exactly what it weighs. [776]
