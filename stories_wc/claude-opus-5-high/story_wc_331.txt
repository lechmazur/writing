The Kepler Annex opened at eleven at night and closed at four, which meant Nesrin had made a life out of hours other people slept through. [26] She was the odd-hour librarian of the only scientific poetry library in the state, a narrow three-story house where sonnets about entropy shared shelves with verse translations of lab notebooks. [56] The regulars called her sanely eccentric, which she accepted as the highest compliment available to a woman who crocheted mathematics at the circulation desk. [80] She worked through hyperbolic geometry the way other people worked through grief: stitch by stitch, adding one extra loop every third row until the fabric rebelled into ruffles. [108] Infinite surface, finite room. [112] That was the whole trick of it, and she believed it was also the trick of memory. [129]

While patrons browsed the stacks between midnight and two, insomniac grad students and one retired seismologist who read Lucretius aloud under his breath, Nesrin worked on the scarf. [157] The scarf had been her mother's: green silk, tattered now, thin as a moth's wing at the folds. [175] Her mother had been an acoustician who recorded poets on quarter-inch tape in the sixties, and who had worn that scarf in every photograph Nesrin owned. [201] The Annex basement held her mother's bequest: four hundred reels of analog memories, oxide flaking off the mylar a little more each year. [224]

Digitizing them would have been sensible. [230] Nesrin had tried, once, and found that the transferred files were accurate and dead, the way a pressed flower is accurate and dead. [253] What she wanted was not a copy. [260] She wanted to find the place where stories live, the actual room of it, and she had come to suspect the room was not shaped like any room she knew. [290] A Euclidean shelf runs out. [295] A hyperbolic one does not. [300]

So she was rebuilding the scarf. [306] Not repairing it; repair was Euclidean. [312] She unpicked the surviving silk into thread and crocheted it back with the extra loops, forcing the flat old rectangle to buckle and flare into a frilled, palm-sized cosmos. [341] Thirty square feet of surface folded into the space of a teacup. [353]

The seismologist looked up from his reading one night and asked what she was making. [368] "Storage," she said. [371] He nodded as though that were an ordinary answer, and went back to Lucretius, and that was the shape of her companionship: observed solitude, two people alone in parallel under the same green lamps. [405] She liked being witnessed without being interrupted. [412] It was, she thought, exactly how the tapes felt: sealed, and yet requiring someone in the room. [429]

On the last Thursday of March she finished the scarf at one-forty in the morning. [444] Six patrons were browsing: three grad students, the seismologist, a woman comparing two editions of a poem about tides, and a boy asleep upright in the reference chair. [472] Nesrin went down to the basement, threaded reel 112 onto the Ampex, and draped the ruffled silk over the playback head like a hood. [496] Silk is a poor conductor and an excellent memory; it keeps every crease you ever gave it. [513] She pressed play. [516]

The tape hissed, and the hiss was not nothing; it was a room in 1967 with a radiator knocking. [535] Then her mother's voice, twenty-four years old, saying, "Rolling," and laughing at something outside the frame. [551] The ruffles moved. [554] Not much, not magically; the speaker's air pushed them, and the folds took the sound and gave it back with a delay, each frill a slightly different length, so that one voice became a hundred overlapping voices arriving out of order. [595] It was not a copy and not a recording. [604] It was a place, and her mother was standing in it, and Nesrin stood there too, in a basement that had become briefly infinite. [628]

Upstairs, she knew, the patrons browsed on, unaware, which was the correct arrangement. [641] A thing can only reawaken where someone is alone enough to notice and not alone enough to vanish. [659]

At four she shut the Annex, the scarf folded in her coat pocket, thirty square feet of it, weighing nothing. [679] There were three hundred ninety-nine reels left. [686] The seismologist held the door for her and said, "Tomorrow, then," and she said yes, tomorrow, at the hour when the world was quiet enough to be heard. [714]
