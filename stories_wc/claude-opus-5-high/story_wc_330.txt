Odalis had not spoken in thirty-one years, and he had never once called it a loss. [16] He carried a satchel of chisels, a folding rule, and a string of nine wind bells that he hung from whatever eave or branch he slept beneath. [43] Carousel carpenters were rare by then, and silent ones rarer, but the fairs kept hiring him because his horses looked as if they were listening. [68]

The salt road out of Naccara ran straight for forty miles, and he took the wrong fork anyway. [86] It was not carelessness; it was his habit of following the quieter of two directions, and the left fork was quieter. [107] By dusk the gravel gave way to reeds, and the reeds to a hidden oasis no map had bothered with: a green eye of water ringed by date palms, steam lifting off it in the cooling air. [144] Rusting along the far bank, half swallowed by bougainvillea, lay an abandoned circus train. [158] Eleven cars, the locomotive nose-down in silt, the lettering flaked to a whispering gleam of gold leaf that caught his lamp and gave it back softer than it came. [187]

He walked the length of it twice before he went inside. [198] The menagerie car held nothing but straw and the smell of old hay-fire. [211] The last car had been a chapel. [218] Circus people, he had been told, carried their own saints, and here was hers: a painted woman on a cracked panel, arms open, her gilt halo shattered into a jigsaw of missing pieces. [251] One halo fragment lay on the floorboards, a curved shard of gessoed wood no longer than his thumb. [269] He picked it up and knew, the way he knew grain direction by touch, exactly where it belonged. [287] Not on the saint: on the brow of the lead horse of the carousel he had been building for nine years. [308]

He hung the wind bells from a hook in the chapel doorway, because he always did, and because the oasis air was moving. [331] They rang once. [334] Inside that one chime, everything slowed and widened, the way a held note widens a room. [350] A misting hush came off the water and filled the car, cool and white, and the sound of the bells did not end; it hung there, unspooling, and he stood inside it. [382]

He had lost his voice at eleven, in a tent fire, to smoke; the doctors called it permanent, and his mother called it God's silence, and meant it as a sentence. [413] Odalis had spent thirty-one quietly persistent years arguing the other way: that silence is not a verdict but an opening, a blank panel waiting for a figure, and that a thing unsaid is a thing still possible. [450] That was why he built carousels: machines whose only argument was that going around again was not the same as going nowhere. [472]

In the misting hush, with the chime still open around him, he found he could hear the train as it had been. [494] Not ghosts, nothing so cheap - only the shape the noise had left behind: calliope, hooves, a woman counting in Romanian, the small grief of a tent rope tightening. [522] He opened his mouth into it. [528] What came out was not a voice, not really - a dry reed of air, a two-note scrape, the sound of a hinge deciding. [551] But he held it, and bent it, and let it rise, and the bells rose with him, and for the length of that one chime the silent carousel carpenter did sing. [582] Nobody heard. [584] That was not the point. [589] The chime closed, the way chimes do, a last soft consonant on the dark. [603]

He slept in the menagerie car and left at first light with the halo fragment in his breast pocket and two good lengths of chapel oak strapped to his pack. [633] He did not think of it as theft; the train had been abandoned, and the saint's arms were open. [652]

Three years later, in the market square at Naccara, children rode a carousel whose lead horse wore a small curved shard of gold above its eyes. [678] The carpenter had rigged nine wind bells under the canopy instead of an organ, and people complained that they could barely hear it. [701] He would nod, agreeable, and say nothing, and wait. [710] Sooner or later a child would lean in close to the bells, and go still, and listen to all that room where the music wasn't - and then, hearing it, begin to hum. [742]
