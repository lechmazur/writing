<story>
The tide pools emptied at dusk, and that was when Neris climbed. [12] She was the wharf weaver of Lowmouth, the one who knotted nets with verses spoken into every crossing of twine, and the fishermen swore her lines held because the words inside them refused to drown. [47] Above the harbor rose the red canyon, and inside it, the labyrinth: centuries-old corridors carved into the sandstone by people whose names had flaked away like old paint. [75] Only the Cartographers were permitted to name a route through it, and they had named the same one for four hundred years. [97] That route ended at the Ruin Gate, where the old city had collapsed into rubble, and pilgrims turned back with their offerings still in their hands. [123]

Neris wanted more than a turning back. [130] She wanted a path from ruin to reverence, a way for Lowmouth to walk its own grief into something worth kneeling for. [152] Her only inheritance was a storybook illustration plate, a copper engraving her mother had pried from a water-ruined volume: a girl in a canyon holding an orchid, with corridors branching behind her like veins in a leaf. [189] The branches on the plate matched no map the Cartographers kept. [200] Neris had studied it since she was nine, and she had come to believe it was not invention but memory. [220] The trouble was proving a route no living person had walked. [231]

So she listened to the sleepwalkers. [237] Lowmouth had always had them, the salt-drowsy ones who rose after the tide pools emptied and wandered the canyon mouth with their eyes half-lidded, humming. [262] Old Dellis the netmender walked, and so did the twins from the smokehouse, and the widow who had lost three sons to the reef. [286] Each of them took a short, senseless walk into the labyrinth and came back before dawn, remembering nothing. [304] Neris followed them with chalk and a lantern hooded to a thread of light. [318] She marked where each sleeper turned, and over two seasons she copied the marks onto sailcloth. [334] Alone, no walk went anywhere. [339] But laid over one another, the overlapping sleepwalking paths made a single continuous line, each dreamer carrying one segment of a route the city had forgotten it knew. [367] The line ran past the Ruin Gate, through a collapse the Cartographers had declared final. [382]

Neris began to sew. [386] She stitched the sailcloth segments together with waxed net-thread, one dreamer's wandering to the next, and along each seam she embroidered the words she had heard them hum. [414] The finished cloth was twelve feet long and, held to lamplight, looked exactly like the branching veins behind the girl on the copper plate. [438]

She brought it to the Cartographers' hall, and they told her what she expected: precedent. [453] Four centuries of it, unbroken, and no sleeper's stumble would break it now. [466]

So she broke it herself. [471] On the first night of the low tides, when the pools lay empty and the canyon breathed cool, Neris hung her cloth from the Ruin Gate and walked into the collapse with a pick. [505] Dellis came, half-asleep, and lifted a stone; then the twins came, then the widow, then forty others who had never dreamed a step of it. [530] They dug for nine nights. [535]

Behind the rubble was not more rubble but a corridor, and at its end a chamber where water seeped down red stone and wild orchids grew in the dark, pale as lamp-moths. [567] The walls were carved with the names the canyon had lost, thousands of them, and beneath the names, a line of script: WALK ON. [591] It was the caption engraved under the girl with the orchid, which Neris had read a thousand times without understanding it was a direction. [615]

The Cartographers came, of course, and stood a long while in the orchid light saying nothing. [631] Afterward they asked Neris to name the route, since by their own oldest law the one who walks first holds the naming. [653] She called it the Sleepers' Seam. [658]

Pilgrims walk it now after the tide pools empty, carrying no offerings, because there is nothing there to buy back the dead. [680] They go only to read the names and to sit in that hauntingly optimistic hush, the kind of quiet that grieves and still insists on tomorrow. [706]

Neris is old now, back on the wharf, knotting nets and muttering verses into the twine. [722] When children ask how she knew, she hands them the copper plate and tells them the truth: that she did not know, that she only trusted the sleeping feet of frightened people, and that a precedent is just a path nobody has bothered to leave. [767] Then she sends them up the canyon, where the orchids keep their solace in the dark. [783]
