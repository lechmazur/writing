Every evening, after the last echo of laughter fades from the crystal cave, Mira stays behind to sweep the charcoal dust. [21] The tourists come for the geodes, for the walls of pale quartz that hold lamplight like a frozen aurora, and they stay for her hands. [46] Ten minutes, three coins, one face: she is the cave's charcoal-fingered portrait sketch artist, and she is very fast. [65] She is fast because she does not need them to sit still; she only needs to look once. [83] Mnemonically gifted, the doctors called it when she was nine, reciting the faces of a whole train carriage. [101] A curse dressed as a talent, her mother said, and her mother was right. [115] Fourteen years ago Mira sat in a courtroom in the city above and drew the face of a man from a witness's description. [138] The drawing was good enough to hang a stranger named Ilya Petrov, and she knew, even as the pencil moved, that the witness was wrong. [163] She knew because she had been on that street herself that night, and she remembered the real face, every pore of it. [185] She drew it later, alone, and then she was too frightened to carry it anywhere. [200] Her mentor Halloran, who ran the cave tours and painted badly and loved her anyway, built her a hiding place. [220] He hung one of his own canvases, a muddy sunrise over the ridge, across a niche in the quartz, and fitted the niche with a combination lock. [247] Inside went the true sketch, and the rest of it too: her charcoal studies, her half-finished cathedral of a mural, everything she had made before she decided that making things was dangerous. [279] Halloran died in March, and in April, sweeping, Mira noticed that the lock had rusted through at the shackle. [298] A broken combination lock hung there, dangling open, and had perhaps hung open for years. [313] Nothing had kept her out except her own hand, and that was the worst thing she had ever learned about herself. [334] Tonight the last tour laughs its way up the stairs, a family from the coast, the children shrieking at their own echoes. [356] Mira waits until the shrieking thins into silence, until even the cave stops repeating them. [371] Then she takes the lamp down the side passage to Halloran's ugly sunrise. [384] The crystals catch the light and throw it back in bands of rose and green, a dawn that has never once seen the sun. [408] She lifts the painting off its nail. [414] The niche smells of paper and cold stone. [422] Her drawings are still there, curled, the charcoal gone silver-grey with age, and on top lies the face of the man who actually did it. [447] Ilya Petrov served nine years and died of pneumonia in the eleventh. [459] She cannot give him back the years; she can only rectify the record, which is a small, stupid, necessary thing. [479] There is a sister in Vrana who still writes letters to the prosecutor's office. [493] Mira will post the sketch to her in the morning, with her name and her address and no excuses. [512] But she sits a while first with the other drawings spread on the cave floor. [527] This is the part she has dreaded more than the confession: the unstoppering, the uncorking of a suppressed thing that has been building pressure in the dark for fourteen years. [557] Her hands are already black to the second knuckle; they have never really stopped. [571] Three coins a face, ten minutes, a talent kept on a short leash and fed scraps. [587] She understands now what Halloran meant when he said that some callings are inescapable, that you do not get to choose whether you answer, only whether you answer well. [616] The mural is still unfinished on its roll of paper. [626] It was going to be every face she had ever kept, hundreds of them, ranked like a congregation. [644] She has room for it on the north wall of the long gallery, where the quartz runs smooth and white. [664] Mira hangs Halloran's sunrise back on its nail, because it is his and because it is terrible and because she loves it. [686] Then she carries the drawings out into the bright cold bands of stone light, grieving and enormous and finally at work. [707] Tomorrow the tourists will come back and laugh at their echoes, and she will draw them, and after the last echo fades she will stay. [732] There is a wall to fill, and a dead man's face to set right, and no lock anywhere that ever held her. [754]
