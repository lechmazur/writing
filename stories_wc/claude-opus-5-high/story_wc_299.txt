The quarry office trailer had not been opened in eleven years, and it smelled like dust and old coffee. [19] Dale Merrick set his appraisal kit on the desk and told himself this was a normal job. [36] Six weeks earlier, on a broadcast watched by two million people, he had called a Gibson Hummingbird genuine when it was a Japanese copy with a sanded serial plate. [65] The clip had a name now, and the name had his face on it. [79] So he had taken the only work offered: inventory the contents of the Echo Valley Aggregate office for the estate of Hal Ondek, foreman, deceased. [104] Hal's daughter Nita waited outside on the step, arms crossed against the wind coming up off the pit. [122] "You were here that night," she said, "in ninety-eight." [131] He had been twenty-three and hauling cable, a roadie for the Sawtooth Kings, who had convinced her father to let them play on the quarry floor because the rock walls gave them a natural reverb no hall could sell. [170] Four thousand people stood in the gravel, and when the last chord went up into the valley it came back three times before it died. [195] Two months after that, their singer Ray drowned in a hotel bathtub in Utica, and there was no band to load out for ever again. [220] Dale had gone into antiques because objects held still. [229] Inside the trailer he worked the way he always worked, out loud, narrating facts like a man laying sandbags. [248] "Underwood portable, 1936, common, forty dollars," he said, "blast log, no market value, archival interest only, oak chair, refinished badly." [268] Nita listened from the doorway with the patience of someone who had heard men insulate themselves before. [285] The bottom drawer of the filing cabinet had swollen shut, and he had to wrest it open with a pry bar and his shoulder. [309] Inside were permits, a dead flashlight, and a square of black speaker grille cloth, folded once, pinned to an index card. [330] The card said, in carpenter's pencil: EV QUARRY, AUG 8 98, THE LOUD NIGHT. [344] Dale knew that cloth. [348] He had cut it himself with a utility knife when a cymbal stand punched through the left stack, and he had taped the hole so the crowd would not see it. [379] Hal Ondek had picked the scrap out of the gravel at dawn, after the applause, after the trucks left, and kept it in a locked drawer for twenty-six years. [408] "Value it," Nita said. [462] "My father did the same thing with tonnage reports." [471] Outside, the pit lay flooded and green, and the far wall still threw back every sound the valley gave it. [491] Dale clapped once, hard, just to hear the echo come home to him twice. [505] He had spent his whole life serving other people's sound and then trying to price what was left over. [524] "I can't put a number on it," he said, and found he was steady saying so, confidently uncertain for the first time since the broadcast. [549] "But I can tell you where it hung, who cut it, and why your father kept it, and nobody else alive can do all three." [574] Nita nodded slowly. [577] "Then that's the job," she said. [624] Dale kept the scrap out on the desk where the window light hit it, and he did not appraise it again.
