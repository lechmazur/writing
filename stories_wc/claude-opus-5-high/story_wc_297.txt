The future arrived eleven years early, on a Tuesday, and nobody had time to move the furniture. [17] By the next dusk I was running toward the cobalt glade with a maze topiary clipping wrapped in wet linen against my ribs. [40] The glade earns its name at that hour: the air there turns the blue of ink under water, and every leaf holds a little of that blue like a held breath. [71] Marisol stood in the middle of it, as she had for nine years: the district's visionary, root-bound to the hour she first saw too far. [96] Her ankles had gone to willow-wood; her toes had married the loam. [108] She could still turn at the waist, and she turned now, and her face did something I can only call anxious elation. [130] "You cut it," she said. [135] "You actually cut it." [139] I had. [141] I'd gone into the Ambrose hedge maze at noon while the sky was still doing its impossible early thing -- the sun swallowed, the corona out like a rumor -- and I had clipped a hand's length of the innermost yew, the one that had never been cut since the covenant was signed beneath it. [194] The covenant was an eclipsed covenant: sealed in shadow, valid only while the shadow held, renewed every generation by two dreamers who agreed to dream the same thing on the same night. [226] Eleven years early, the shadow came and went before anyone could be woken to renew it. [242] So the dreams came loose. [247] All afternoon people had been dreaming while awake, in the streets, badly, other people's dreams. [262] Marisol took the clipping and pressed it to the seam where her bark met her skin, and she made a sound like someone tasting water after a week. [290] "A maze remembers every wrong turn," she said. [298] "That's all a maze is. [303] A record of places where people turned back." [311] She needed it because she was trying to find the intersection of dreams -- the one place where every sleeping mind overlaps, the hub of the labyrinth, the point at which my dream of falling and your dream of a door are the same dream. [355] If she could stand there, she could hold the covenant open by herself until the next shadow, early or late. [375] Her method was the only one she had ever trusted: linking through scars. [388] Smooth skin forgets, she told me once, but scar tissue is the body's written record, and anything written can be read by something else. [415] The clipping had a scar where I had cut it. [425] Her trunk had a hundred, one for every year of growth she had not consented to. [441] And I had the long white line across my palm from the day I first climbed into the maze as a child, purposefully curious, wanting to know what was in the middle badly enough to bleed for it. [479] "Give me your hand," she said, "and heed me -- whatever you see in there, do not follow it inward past me." [500] I gave her my hand, and I heeded her, which is the hardest thing I have ever done. [518] Scar to scar to scar, the three of us linked -- girl, tree-woman, cutting -- and the glade went from cobalt to black-blue to nothing. [541] I saw the hedge from above, and it was not a maze; it was a diagram of every path anyone had taken and abandoned, and all the abandoned paths leaned toward one clearing at the heart. [577] Marisol walked into it on roots that moved for the first time in nine years, and she laughed, and the laugh was the whole tone of the day: terror and joy and no room between them. [613] I wanted to follow so badly that my scar ached. [623] I did not follow. [627] When I opened my eyes, the cutting in my hand had put out a white root, and Marisol's willow-ankles had gone soft as ordinary flesh, and she was sitting in the loam like a woman who had just been let out of a room. [671] "It isn't in the hedge," she said, hoarse. [679] "The center moves. [682] It's wherever that cutting takes root." [688] So we planted it there, in the cobalt glade, at the last blue minute of dusk. [704] Tonight the whole city will sleep facing this hill, and every wrong turn anyone ever took will lean toward a small yew with a scar on its stem, and the shadow will be held open from the inside. [742] Marisol is learning to walk again, and I am the one who cannot stop checking the sky. [759] The future came early once; it can do it again, and now I know it will find me curious, and awake, and rooted right here. [784]
