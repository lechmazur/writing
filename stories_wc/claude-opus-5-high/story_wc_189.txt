Mei asked the question at slack water, and then the grotto held its breath. [14] Grandmother Ouyang did not answer right away. [21] She had spent sixty years learning that the important things are never found by looking at them directly. [39] The grotto was older than any village on the coast, a mouth of black basalt that the sea filled twice a day and abandoned twice a day, and above its broken ceiling the stars came through in a ragged seam. [79] Ouyang was the tidal-pool chronicler of Fenghai, which meant she kept a ledger of the shallow water: which anemone opened, which limpet moved, how long the blennies tolerated the heat before the tide returned. [113] Fishermen thought her work useless. [118] She thought it the only honest calendar anyone had ever kept, because the pools were nothing but transition periods: neither sea nor shore, water always becoming something else. [146] That was why she had taken the work in the first place: to discover the texture of time, which she suspected was rough in places and smooth in others, like the rim of a pool worn by sixty years of her own thumb. [189] Mei's question was this: would Ouyang come inland, to the town with the clinic, and stop climbing down the ladder at night? [211] Under it lay a second question, unspoken, about how many seasons the old woman had left, and beneath that a third, about whether Mei would inherit the ledger or a burden. [242] In the pause, Ouyang did what she always did with a hard thing. [255] She approached it tangentially. [259] She lowered her lamp toward the nearest pool and became, as her granddaughter had once written in a school essay, warmly observant: not a judge of the water but a guest in it. [292] A shore crab was halfway out of its old shell. [302] It had wedged itself into a crevice, and the split carapace hung behind it like a discarded coat, and the new body was pale and soft and terribly unfinished. [331] For eleven minutes it would be neither one crab nor another, and anything could eat it. [347] Ouyang had recorded four hundred such moltings, and she had learned to foresee which crabs would survive them by never watching the crab itself. [371] You watched the edges instead: the angle of the crevice, the height of the water, whether a wrasse was patrolling the seam of weed three feet away. [398] The future never showed itself head on; it arrived sideways, in the arrangement of everything around the thing you cared about. [419] So Ouyang looked at the edges of Mei. [427] The girl had come down the ladder in good shoes. [437] She had brought no notebook. [442] Her hands were empty, opening and closing, and she had asked her question facing the sea rather than her grandmother. [462] All of it said: I am afraid I am asking you to stop being yourself, and I do not know how else to keep you. [487] Ouyang reached into her collar and lifted out the jade cicada. [498] It had been her mother's, and her mother's mother's, a thumb-length insect the color of shallow seawater, its wings folded, its eyes two blind beads. [523] The old people carved cicadas because the creature spends seventeen years underground and one summer singing, and because they used to place such carvings in the mouths of the dead, to promise a molt rather than an ending. [561] Ouyang had worn it so long that the carved ridges had gone smooth under her collarbone, and there, she thought, was her answer about the texture of time: it wears down where you hold it, and stays rough everywhere you do not. [603] The pause had lasted perhaps forty seconds. [610] The crab flexed, and came free, and slid deeper into the crevice, alive. [623] Ouyang put the cicada into Mei's open hand and closed the fingers around it. [637] 'I will come inland in the spring,' she said, 'and you will come down the ladder tonight, and I will show you how to watch the edges.' [664] 'The ledger is not a burden, it is only a habit of attention, and habits can be handed over in the gap between two tides.' [689] Mei sat down on the wet rock in her good shoes. [700] Overhead the ragged seam of stars turned a little, and the water began, very quietly, to come back in, and the grotto that had been a cave for an hour prepared to be a sea again. [736] Ouyang wrote the time of the molt in her ledger and, in the margin, the time of the question, because both were transitions and both deserved a record. [764]
