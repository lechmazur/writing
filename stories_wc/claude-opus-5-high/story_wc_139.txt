My father could sell a man his own shadow, and twice if it rained. [14] I sell nothing. [17] I wash the dead, and I keep a ledger, and the ledger balances. [30] The prep room at Halvard and Sons hums like a hive in August: the aspirator, the fan, the fluorescent tubes, the pink fluid climbing into a carotid until the cheeks flush and the dead look better than the living who come to claim them. [74] It is the most alive place I know. [82] Tomas Rene had been hiding in our cold room for eleven days, alive among the drawers, because inspectors never open the drawers. [104] He had crossed the river during the strike, carrying a list of names the Ministry wanted burned, and the only door left open to him was political asylum, which in our country is a door with no handle on the outside. [145] My father's solution would have been a breach: forge the consul's seal, shove Tomas through, take his mother's savings, vanish. [165] I wanted a fair trade, and a fair trade has mathematics, and I intended to find them. [182] So I did the only thing my father never learned to do: I waited, actively. [197] Waiting actively means you scrub, and read, and listen to the death notices on the radio while you set features and sew jaws. [220] It means knowing that in a city of nine hundred thousand, every powerful man eventually loses a mother. [238] Twice that week the Ministry's men breached the prep room without knocking, boots in the drain gutters, lifting sheets off strangers' faces. [260] Both times I kept working, and both times they left queasy, and Tomas lay eight inches under their hands in drawer four. [282] On the twelfth day, Consul Aurel Danesh's mother arrived on my table, ninety-one years old, hands like folded paper. [301] I gave her the best work of my life. [310] I did not do it to soften him; I did it because she deserved it, and because the ledger balances only if the work is real. [336] Then I set my father's vintage ink pot on the instrument tray, beside the trocar and the eye caps. [355] It is a squat hexagon of green glass, ground at the lip, brass-collared, older than the Ministry itself. [373] Inside is iron-gall ink he mixed himself, and he swore the stuff liquefies rigid thinking. [388] He meant it as a con man's joke, but I have seen it work. [402] Old ink smells like a schoolroom and a will and a marriage register all at once; a man who smells it stops thinking in rules and starts thinking in years. [432] Danesh came at nine at night, alone, coat still on. [442] He looked at his mother's face for a long time, and the fan hummed, and the tubes buzzed, and the room was as loud with life as it always is. [472] "You are Ferreux's daughter," he said. [478] "I am," I said, "and I am not selling you anything." [489] I told him about Tomas in the cold room, and about the list of names, and then I opened the ink pot. [511] Here is the arithmetic I had worked out over eleven nights. [522] He could sign a genuine asylum certificate, not a forgery, dated and lawful, and he could do it tonight because the night registrar was his cousin. [548] In exchange, the list of names would go into his mother's casket with her, sealed under six feet of consecrated clay, where the Ministry could not have it and neither could the newspapers who would have used it to hang forty clerks who only ever typed. [594] Tomas kept his life; Danesh kept his clerks; the dead kept the secret, which is the only thing the dead are good at keeping. [618] Nobody was robbed, and that is the whole difference between my father and me. [632] Danesh stood rigid for a full minute, a man made entirely of statutes. [645] Then he leaned over the tray, breathed the ink, and something in him ran like wax. [661] He asked for a pen. [666] He wrote with my father's ink, in his own hand, the first honest document either of us had ever put our names to. [689] Tomas walked out at dawn through the loading door, blinking, stinking of formaldehyde, ridiculously alive. [704] I closed the casket myself. [709] I wrote the entry in my ledger: one life received, one betrayal declined, one secret buried, and the columns came out even. [731] My father wrote me once from prison and asked what the trick had been. [745] I wrote back: there was no trick, only a breach I refused to make, and a room full of humming machines that taught me the dead are patient, and patience is not the same thing as stillness. [782] I signed it with his ink, and I hope it liquefied something. [794]
