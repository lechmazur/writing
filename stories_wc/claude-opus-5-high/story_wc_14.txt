After the town went silent, Miron Kell kept his shutters open, because a gilded-leaf stationer must sell to somebody. [19] The mill had closed in March, and by August the last families had carried their voices down the valley road. [39] What remained was a street of shut doors and one shop that smelled of hide glue, agate burnishers, and gold. [59] Miron had built his trade on invented histories. [67] For thirty years he had gilded the edges of ledgers and stitched in frontispieces that gave merchants ancestors they never had: a Genoese navigator, a countess with a stammer, a grandmother who smuggled seed. [101] He kept every fabrication in a battered notepad, the spine repaired with linen tape, so that no two clients would inherit the same great-uncle. [125] The notepad was his only honest document, being a complete record of his lies. [139]

His apprentice, Sofi, was earnestly studious in a way that unsettled him. [151] She stayed after her parents left, alphabetizing his pigments, asking which of the ancestors were real. [167] None, he told her, and she wrote that down too. [177]

It was Sofi who noticed the lamp. [184] The fluorescent tube above the gilding bench had hummed for decades, but with the town quiet, the hum acquired structure -- a rise and a settling, like breath. [211] At dawn it was strongest, and strangest. [217] They began charting the faint hum in the fluorescent lamp at dawn, Sofi ruling columns, Miron holding a square of gold leaf in the light. [242]

Gold, hammered thin enough, does not merely shine; it refracts. [252] When the chart matched the hum exactly, the leaf threw a band of color across the wall, and inside that band Miron felt weather. [276] Not saw: felt. [279] The grain of an afternoon in 1911, dust and hot slate. [290]

He understood then what he had wanted his whole working life, which was not wealth or even craft, but to collect the textures of different times. [316] The invented ancestors had always been a way of touching centuries he could not visit. [331]

The lamp's band was narrow, though, and faded by full morning. [342] Sofi's columns suggested why: the town's old silence let the hum run clean, but the valley air was thin of resonance. [363] They needed a place that held sound the way a cupped hand holds water. [377] So they carried the lamp, a car battery, and an inverter up the mountain to the monastery gardens, abandoned since the last brother died. [401]

On that summit sprawled a blooming labyrinth of bonsai forests, hedges of dwarf pine and flowering quince grown into corridors no taller than a child. [426] Walking it, you looked down on canopies two hundred years old and four inches tall. [441] The monks had pruned time itself into shape, and the stone walls gave back every noise, patient as a bell. [461]

They set the lamp at the labyrinth's heart and waited for dawn. [473] The hum came up like water finding a channel. [482] Miron raised the leaf, and the gold refracted the hum into a wide, trembling arch of light that filled the corridors. [503] He walked into it, and the textures came. [510] Wet wool and wood smoke; the chalk of a schoolroom; a March morning when the mill was new and the whole valley smelled of cut timber and money. [538]

He did not weep, and he did not hurry. [547] He simply stood with his hands open, taking each one the way a man accepts change from a stranger. [566]

Sofi called from the outer ring, notepad ready, asking what century he was in. [580] And Miron, who had sold three hundred families pasts they never lived, found he could not lie inside that light. [600]

Ours, he said. [603] The mill, the road, the last summer. [610] Write it as it was. [615]

She did, in the battered notepad, on the pages after the countess and the navigator -- the first true history it had ever held. [638] They came back every dawn until the battery died. [646] Then Miron gilded the notepad's edges, so that whoever climbed up next, in whatever silence, would see it catch the light and know where to stand. [672]
