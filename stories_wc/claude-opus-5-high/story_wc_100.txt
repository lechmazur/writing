The package arrived on a Tuesday, unaddressed, smelling of kelp and cold iron. [12] Merrin Vale, undertaker of Cairnmouth for thirty-one years, opened it on the embalming table because that was the only clean surface he owned. [35] Inside lay a wedge of the old lighthouse lens, broken, its prisms fogged with salt. [50] He had not seen it since the night the sea took the light, the keeper, and half the harbor road. [70] Since that night, the dead of Cairnmouth had refused to settle. [81] They knocked inside coffin lids in threes, tapped gutters, guttered candles in code. [94] Merrin had learned to read the distress signals years ago, and reading them had worn him thin as a shroud. [114] Three short, three long, three short, always the same plea: we are not anywhere. [128] A grave, he had always believed, was an address. [137] But the sea kept moving the plots, dragging headstones off the cliff shelf, and no name stayed put. [155] The lens came with one line of writing, scratched on the wrapping: BRING IT HOME AND IT WILL SHOW YOU THE SHELVES. [177] Merrin was seventy-four and tired in a way sleep did not touch, but he had a fault his wife had named long ago: he was inadvertently stubborn, never arguing, never refusing, simply continuing until the world gave up first. [216] So he took his boat out at slack tide with the lens in a coffin-maker's sack. [232] Where the lighthouse had stood, the water was strangely still, a chased stillness, the kind that follows something rather than rests. [253] He dropped the lens overboard and went down after it on the old keeper's line. [268] Forty feet down, Cairnmouth had a second self. [276] The drowned city's library stood where no building had ever been recorded, its doors open, its lamps burning green behind glass. [297] Merrin stepped through and found he could breathe, which he accepted the way he accepted most impossible things, by not stopping. [318] The library was forgotten by everyone but the water. [327] Its shelves went up past any ceiling and sideways past any wall, occupying impossible spaces, rooms folded into rooms, a reading alcove tucked inside the spine of a single book. [357] That, Merrin understood at once, was the point. [365] A city could run out of ground for its dead. [375] A library that held more inside than out could not. [385] Every volume he touched was a life: dates, debts, the smell of a kitchen, the name of a dog. [404] But the shelves nearest the door were empty and the catalogue drawers hung open, gutted. [419] At the center of the floor sat a brass cradle shaped exactly like a lighthouse lamp, missing one wedge of glass. [440] The keeper had not been guarding ships. [447] He had been running the light that filed the dead, and when the lens broke, the filing stopped, and the names had nowhere to go. [472] Merrin fitted his broken wedge into the cradle. [480] It did not fit; the fracture was wrong, the edges ground down by thirty years of surf. [497] He tried again. [500] He tried for what the surface would later call two days, turning the glass a quarter-inch at a time, never once considering that he might leave. [526] That was the whole of his stubbornness: he simply had not finished. [537] On the last turn the wedge seated with a sound like a coffin lid closing kindly, and the lamp woke. [557] Green light swept the stacks and began to revitalize the empty shelves, growing spines out of the dark the way coral grows. [579] One by one the knocking names arrived and were shelved: Halloran, Pyke, Orrin Vale who was nine when the fever came, Ada Vale who had named her husband's fault and then gone quiet in a chair. [615] Each book had a number, a shelf, a room: an address, permanent, above the reach of any tide. [633] The signals stopped. [636] The stillness that came after was not chased by anything. [646] Merrin sat down in an alcove that had not existed a moment earlier and found it exactly his size. [665] Above him the lamp turned, patient, casting its slow green sweep across the water where his empty boat rocked. [684] In Cairnmouth they say the light came back and nobody drowns badly anymore. [697] They say the new undertaker received a package, unaddressed, smelling of kelp and cold iron, and that she is reading it still. [719]
