The search party disbanded on the ninth day, when the rain finally softened the ash and no one could pretend the foundry held anything but water. [26] Vesna stayed. [28] They called her the balanced alchemist, and it was not flattery; she had spent thirty years proving that every miracle has a mass, and that mass can be written down. [58] Magic in the valley was factually mystical: wonders arrived with decimal places, and the guild kept ledgers of them the way millers kept ledgers of grain. [84] Her brother Tomas had laughed at that once, standing at the crucible with soot on his teeth. [101] Now the crucible was cold, the roof gutters sang, and the rain-washed foundry smelled of iron going sweet with rust. [121] On the workbench lay the zodiac coin, broken in three. [131] It was the Ram's face, struck the winter Tomas was born, the kind of token a family buys to hang a name on. [154] Names, the old texts insisted, have weight; Vesna had always filed that under poetry. [168] She intended to file it under arithmetic before the week was out. [180]

The method was old and disreputable: exploring limits. [188] Every measuring instrument lies at its edges, and the lie it tells is shaped exactly like the thing it cannot hold. [209] So Vesna loaded her assay scales past tolerance, night after night, until the beams shrieked and the needle swung into the region the guild had painted red. [236] She weighed a spoken word against a gram of gold leaf and watched the gold rise. [252] She weighed silence, which registered nothing until she thought of the ninth day, and then it nearly cracked the pan. [272] She weighed her own name and found it light, a feather of a thing, worn smooth by use. [290] She weighed his, and the scale broke. [297] That was the first real datum. [303] A name unspoken by its owner grows heavy in whoever carries it. [315]

She had carried Tomas since the night of the pour, when the mold cracked and she had shouted for everyone to stand back, everyone, and he had not. [343] He had stepped toward the light instead, and the light had taken him somewhere the search party could not follow, somewhere between the slag and the rain. [370]

On the twelfth night she began the repair. [378] Kintsugi was a foreign word she had learned from a trader: to mend a broken vessel with gold so the break becomes the most beautiful part. [404] The guild taught the opposite: hide the seam, restore the original, pretend no damage occurred. [419] But intentions, she had measured, weigh more than materials, and kintsugi intentions weigh most of all, because they refuse to lie about what happened. [443] She melted the gold from her own guild medal, the one awarded for never exceeding tolerance. [459] She laid the three pieces of the zodiac coin in their cradle and spoke his name into the seam as she poured. [481]

The foundry brightened. [484] Not with fire; with something that had the honesty of arithmetic and the manners of dawn, rain-light running gold along every cold rail and chain. [509] Tomas stood in the doorway, wet through, thinner than the ledger of him she had kept. [525] "You shouted," he said. [529] "I heard it for days, but I could not find my way back to the sound." [545] "I know," Vesna said. [549] "I was measuring when I should have been calling." [558]

She put the mended coin into his hand, gold seam warm, the Ram's horn crossing the crack like a bridge. [578] It did not undo the pour, or the twelve days, or the neighbors who had gone home believing him drowned. [598] Nothing redeems by erasure; you redeem by paying the true weight, in public, where the seam can be seen. [617]

In the morning she filed her findings with the guild: the mass of a name, the tolerance of grief, the exact cost of a medal melted. [643] They fined her for exceeding limits, and she paid gladly. [653] Afterward, whenever rain washed the foundry and the gutters sang, she felt the old regret rise in her, luminous now rather than black: a light with a shape, the shape of a brother she had almost weighed instead of loved. [693] Tomas wore the coin on a cord and let strangers ask about the gold. [707] Vesna always answered first, because she had learned what it cost to keep a name in her mouth unspoken. [726] "That is where it broke," she would say, and then she would say his name. [741]
