They told me the procedure would leave me clean, and it did. [12] It also left me hollow, which nobody had promised and nobody apologized for. [25] I woke in a clinic in Bellwether with no debts, no dreams, and no capacity to care whether the man in the next bed lived or died. [52] On the bedside table sat a baseball, scuffed and yellowed, and a stack of newspapers dating back nine years. [71] The ball was signed in fading blue ink: T. Ferro, 2088, for the man you will have to become again. [91] A nurse said a courier had delivered both, prepaid, with instructions to read between the lines. [107] I had no memory of ever holding a newspaper, but my hands knew the trick before my head did. [126] In the classifieds of each paper, the first letters of the personal ads spelled directions, written by a man with my handwriting and my caution. [151] MEET YOURSELF AT THE GLASS WATER, the oldest one read. [161] So began a journey I felt no enthusiasm for and could not abandon. [174]

The papers themselves taught me who I had been. [183] Buried in the local sections were the fires, the foreclosures, the vanished tenants of Marrow Street, and in each story a detail only a collector for the Ossuary lenders would recognize. [214] I read about my own work the way a tourist reads a plaque. [226] That flatness, the doctors would call a success. [234]

The directions took me nine days north by bus and then on foot, through a mining town that had been dying so long it had gotten good at it. [263] Below the town, past a gate my key still fit, the tunnels opened into a labyrinth of choices: forty-one branchings, each marked with a chalk symbol I had drawn before the surgeons washed the meaning out of me. [301] I chose by instinct, which is another word for the parts of a man that no procedure can reach. [320] Twice I chose wrong and met dead ends packed with rubble; once I chose wrong and met a drop that would have killed me, and I noted my own indifference to that with mild academic interest. [356]

On the third day underground, the walls began to glow. [366] The lake lay in a cavern the size of a cathedral, lit from beneath by blue-green fire: bioluminescent algae, a delicately robust light that had survived a hundred million years of darkness and would outlast me. [402] I stood at the shore and felt wonder arrive, jaded and secondhand, like a letter forwarded from an address I no longer lived at. [426]

On a stone table above the waterline my other self had left a tin box, a hand-cranked player, and ninety-three recordings. [447] Each one was a voice from Marrow Street. [455] He had spent two years finding them and asking them, gently, to describe what I had taken. [472] A note in the box explained his reasoning in the clipped shorthand of a man who hated explaining anything. [491] The procedure erases guilt along with memory, it said; guilt is the only thing that ever made me stop. [510] So I am sending you here to kindle empathy where none remains, because a clean slate in my hands is a weapon. [532]

I cranked the player and listened for eleven hours while the algae pulsed with the slow patience of tides. [551] Around hour six, a woman named Delia Okonkwo described a boy on Marrow Street who had owned one good thing, a baseball, and how a strange old man in a bad suit had signed it and told him he came from 2088, and that the boy would need proof, someday, that he had once been worth visiting. [608] The boy was me. [612] I turned the ball over in the blue light, and something in my chest moved that had no business moving, small and unwilling, like a coal found alive under ash. [642]

I do not know whether the old man was a traveler or a liar. [656] I know that he chose to visit, and that my other self counted on the arithmetic of it: if a stranger crossed decades for me, then ninety-three voices deserved three days of my walking. [690] When the last recording ended, I filled the tin with the newspapers' coded pages and left the ball on the stone table, signed side up, for whoever the next clean slate turns out to be. [725] Then I climbed back toward the dying town with ninety-three addresses in my pocket and no illusion that any of them wanted to see me. [750] It was, I understood, only the first turn of a long corridor, and I chose it anyway. [767]
