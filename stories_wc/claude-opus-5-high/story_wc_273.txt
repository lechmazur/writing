Nuo had been called ghost, salt-boy, and moon-skin, but the encampment called him tracker, and that was the only name that fit his hands. [24] He worked after the eclipse ended, when the wind-carved encampment lay in its long blue exhale and the rock spires above the tents whistled like emptied bottles. [51] His sister Ijeri had disappeared during the darkness. [59] Not run, not taken: disappeared, the way a word leaves a page. [71] Nuo's eyes, pale as watered milk, were useless in daylight, which was why the elders had given him the artificial hummingbird drone: a palm-sized thing of brass ribs and mica wings that hovered ahead of him and hummed differently over blood, over ash, over ink. [116] Over ink it sang highest. [121] It was singing now, above the flat stone outside Ijeri's tent, where a clay jar had tipped and spilled a black tongue across the grit. [146] Disappearing ink: her ink, the kind she brewed from gall and iron and the sap of the thornbush that grew only in the wind shadow. [171] Ijeri had been a mathematician before the sky took her interest. [182] She wanted to discover why time slows in crisis: why a falling child takes an hour to reach the ground, why the heart of a hunted animal seems to beat in a wider room. [216] The elders said it was fear stretching. [223] She said fear could not stretch anything; something in the world must give. [236] So she spilled ink and watched. [242] Nuo knelt now and watched what she had watched. [251] The spill was not drying so much as thinking. [260] Fern shapes bloomed at its edges and folded away; a spiral opened, held for three breaths, and vanished into the stone. [281] The drone's hum climbed and broke. [287] He understood, with the clean cold of understanding, that these were the ephemeral patterns through which she had gone, and that they were a door only while they lasted. [316] Anger came up in him, but it arrived serene, the way the encampment's stone arrived serene after a thousand years of wind: shaped, patient, without any softness left. [344] She had gone without him. [349] She had done the one thing he could not track. [359] Or perhaps she had left a trail, and he was standing on it. [372] He gathered her jars from the tent and her ledger, whose pages were blank because she wrote in the ink that eats itself. [395] Only the last page still held a sentence, fading: THE WORLD IS CONSCIOUSLY WILD; IT ATTENDS US WHEN WE ARE ABOUT TO BREAK. [418] That was her answer, then. [423] Time did not slow for the falling child; the world leaned in, awake and unbroken by any rule, and granted the extra room out of a wildness that chose. [452] And if it chose, it could be asked. [460] Nuo poured the jar out onto the flat stone and did not wait for courage. [475] The black spread, threw out its ferns, and began to build a spiral. [488] He sent the hummingbird in first. [494] Its wings caught the pattern's turning; it shrank without shrinking and was gone, and the hum came back to him thinned, as though through water, from somewhere that was not the encampment. [526] Behind him the camp was waking; a woman called his name; the wind moved through the carved spires and made its bottle-sound. [548] The spiral held for three breaths. [554] In the first, Nuo felt the world lean toward him, curious, consciously wild, the way it leans toward anyone about to be lost. [577] In the second, the ink's edges reached up past his wrists, and time widened into that broad quiet room his sister had spent her life measuring. [603] In the third he said her name, and it was not a question. [616] Then the pattern closed, and the flat stone was dry, and the wind erased the last print of his knees. [636] The woman who came looking found nothing but a clay jar, still warm on one side where a pale hand had held it. [659] High above, out of the shadow of the spires, something small and brass hummed once, faintly, and was not heard again. [680]
