The foghorn fell silent at 4:12 that morning, and Ismet Varo, venerable oath scribe of the Halberd Tower, felt the quiet enter his bones like water into chalk. [28] For sixty years the harbor had breathed that low note every ninety seconds, and for sixty years he had cut oaths into wax beneath it. [53] The orchard garden on the roof was older than the tower under it. [66] The quince and medlar trees had been dug from a hillside monastery two centuries ago, hauled up in their own soil, and the skyscraper had been built to hold them the way a hand holds a bird. [103] Ismet worked among them at a lacquered stand, one ear always turned to the sea. [118] His office was not to judge oaths but to archive emotional patterns: the tremor in a merchant's vow, the flatness in a widow's, the hurry in a boy's. [146] The city believed that promises had weather, and that weather could be predicted if someone kept honest records. [164] He spoke nine dead languages, which was the requirement; oaths sworn in a living tongue can be argued away, but a vow in Sogdian or Old Phrygian stays where you put it. [196] That morning, with the foghorn gone, he found the shaving. [206] It lay curled on the stand like a pared fingernail: a wax cylinder phonograph shaving, thrown off by the cutting stylus during the last night's recording. [232] Ordinarily he swept such curls into the compost at the roots of the oldest quince. [247] But this one held a groove, fine as a hair, and grooves hold voices. [261] He knew how to read it because of a third-grade field trip he had never understood the point of. [280] The class had been taken not to a museum but to a cannery's sound room, where a docent with ink-stained cuffs showed them how to immerse a wax curl in warm almond oil until it softened and lay flat, then drag a sewing pin across it with a paper cone taped to the pin's head. [335] Seven-year-old Ismet had heard a woman's laugh come out of garbage, and had gone home frightened for reasons he could not name. [357] Now, sixty-odd years later, he warmed oil in a copper cup over a candle and did what the docent had done. [378] The pin hissed; the cone whispered. [384] A voice said, in Hittite, 'When the sea stops speaking, the orchard will be asked for its last oath, and the scribe will answer.' [408] It was his own voice. [413] He remembered no such sentence. [418] He had dozed near dawn, as old men do, and the stylus had gone on turning, and his sleeping mouth had wandered into the driest language he owned. [446] An accidental prophecy, then: not sent, not received, merely shaved off a cylinder and nearly buried in compost. [464] The velvet quiet of the garden pressed against him, warm and wrong, the way a hand can be too gentle. [484] At seven the harbor authority's clerk came up the stairs with a folded document and a face arranged for bad news. [505] The foghorn had been decommissioned; radar had made it sentimental; the tower's roof, being the highest calm ground in the city, was wanted for an antenna. [531] The orchard would be removed. [536] They required one oath from the custodian of the garden, sworn in a dead tongue so it could not be argued away: that he release it. [562] So the prophecy had not been a vision but a pattern, the very thing he had spent his life archiving. [582] His sleeping mind had read the silence of a decommissioning notice in a dozen small tremors: the clerk's overly kind visits, the surveyors' laughter, the way the foghorn's keeper had stopped waving. [614] Ismet set a fresh cylinder on the mandrel. [622] He did not swear the oath they wanted. [630] In Hittite, into the wax, he said: 'I record that this garden was loved, and that the city chose the antenna, and that both of these are true.' [658] The clerk waited, not understanding a word, reassured by the sound of ceremony. [671] Ismet pocketed the shaving, the prophecy he had made by accident and fulfilled on purpose, and it stayed warm against his hip all morning like something alive. [698] Below, the harbor said nothing at all. [705] He ate a medlar, bitter and soft, and listened to the archive of himself: a man's voice, telling the future by finally hearing the present. [730]
