The Ministry ground my guild cabochon to powder on the customs table, and I watched with what my old mistress called obsidian grace: dark, polished, giving nothing back. [28] One speck survived. [31] It caught in the callus of my thumb, a moonstone dust speck no larger than a thought, and I did not brush it away. [55] After the nostalgia ban, that was theft; remembering was theft; I had become a thief in a single afternoon. [74]

I was a tide dancer, the best on the Bracket Coast, which is a boast and also a diagnosis. [93] We dance the flats at the turning hour so the fishing fleets know where the channels have wandered overnight. [112] The dance is memory: forty generations of water, kept in hips and ankles rather than in books the inspectors could burn. [133] So they came for the dancers. [139]

No one arrested me. [143] I chose elective exile, which the ban permitted, because a Ministry prefers a silence it can file. [160] I signed the paper, surrendered the sea, and climbed to the cliff terrace where an astronomer's widow grew flowers that only open at dusk. [184] She was dead by the time I arrived, but the garden kept its habits: a twilight garden under stars, blooming in the hour the law had no word for. [213]

On my ninth night there, standing among the night-stock, I became spontaneously anchored. [226] Every dancer fears it and no one can explain it. [236] The body simply decides it is finished moving; the tide in the blood goes slack; the feet set like poured stone. [257] For three days I could not lift my heels from the gravel. [269] I wept, then I stopped weeping, then I looked up. [279]

Stillness, it turns out, is a kind of instrument. [288] Anchored, I began reading the sky the way I had once read water: by observing gaps. [304] Not the stars, which anyone may love, but the black lanes between them, the places where the pattern holds its breath. [325] Gaps had always been my craft. [331] A channel is only the absence of sandbar; a censored almanac says everything in the width of its redactions. [350] Night after night I copied the intervals into my palm with a wet fingertip, and a verse assembled itself, forty-one lines long. [372] It described the turning of the coast's water in the grammar of the turning sky, the same sentence spoken at two volumes. [394]

Paper burns and stone can be confiscated. [401] My ambition narrowed to one hard point: to pen a cosmic verse on mortal skin, where the Ministry could not seize it without committing murder in public. [428]

The girl came up the cliff path in autumn, carrying her mother's confiscation receipt and asking to be taught. [447] Wren, thirteen, all elbows, a fledgling in the exact sense. [457] I could not dance for her, so I taught her by observing gaps too: I told her where my body would have gone, and she filled the shapes I left empty. [488] She learned faster than I had, because absence is a stricter teacher than example. [502]

In midwinter I asked her permission twice, and twice she gave it. [514] Then I mixed lampblack with dusk-flower resin, and last of all I pressed the moonstone dust speck from my thumb into the ink. [537] One speck, one word: the verse's last, which is the name of the hour we were forbidden to miss. [556] It took nine nights, needle and stillness, my anchored feet an advantage at last. [570] Forty-one lines down her spine and ribs, under the dusk-flowers, under the gaps. [583]

In spring she fledged. [587] She went down the cliff at the turning hour, and I stood in the gate, rooted, watching the small dark shape of her cross the flats in the pattern I had given away. [620] The fleets that night found the channels where she said they were. [632]

Nobody remembered anything, officially. [636] But a girl was walking the coast with the sky written on her, and the ink had a grain of my ruined ring in it, and that is not nostalgia. [666] Nostalgia looks backward. [669] I keep my face toward the flowers that open when the light fails, and I give nothing back. [687]
