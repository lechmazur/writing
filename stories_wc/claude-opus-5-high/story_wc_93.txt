The aurora came up green over the tidal bluff, and we called it dawn because we had no other. [19] Borrowed light, my mother used to say, before friction disappeared and took her hands from the rope. [36] Nothing has gripped anything since. [41] Knots slither loose, stairs are suicide, and the sea slides up the bluff without resistance, a silk sheet pulled over stone. [62] We walk in a shuffle, on gravel, praying to weight. [72] I came for the eel herder because only she still moves like she means it. [87] They say she herds the black eels through the frictionless shallows the way a shepherd walks a hill, and that she does it with painted rocks. [113] I had seen the rocks before I ever saw her, which is the curse: I am precognitively cursed, and the future arrives in me secondhand, already used. [140] I see a thing, then I live it, and I cannot bend it by a hair. [156] So I climbed the bluff knowing I would find her line of stones: one ochre, one blue, two greens, three whites, five reds, eight blacks, curving toward the water. [185] The eels followed it. [189] Without friction they have nothing to push against, no purchase in water or sand, so they read gradients instead, and a sequence of weights laid on a slope makes a gradient they can trust. [223] She was at the end of the line, kneeling, elusive even while standing still, the kind of woman your eye slides off, the way everything slides now. [250] "You're the seer," she said, not looking up. [258] "You're late by four minutes, and you already know why." [268] I did. [271] In her cupped palm she held collapsed star dust, a pinch of it, grey and heavier than the hill. [290] It was the last substance in the world that still pulled, that made other things lean toward it, and she meant to harness it. [314] Not to save the coast, she told me; the coast was already a rumor. [328] She wanted to understand the sacred patterns, the ones the eels had been swimming since before anyone painted a rock, the spirals in shells and storms and the long sums of the tides. [361] "Friction hid them," she said. [366] "Everything was noise, drag, excuses." [371] "Now the world is honest." [376] "Now a number moves a living thing." [383] I told her what I had seen, because the curse is worse if I hold it. [399] The dust would slip. [403] Of course it would; nothing holds. [409] It would slide from her palm at the ninth stone, and she would go after it down the frictionless face of the bluff into water that no longer knew how to push a body back up. [445] She listened the way farmers listen to weather. [453] Then she asked me to hold out my hands. [462] "You can't change it," she said. [468] "But I can choose what it's for." [475] She laid the pinch of collapsed star dust in the trough of the ninth stone, a white one, painted with a spiral, and the eels turned as one, thirteen of them, drawn up the slope toward that small terrible weight. [515] The aurora stood over us like a green cathedral, and for a moment the pattern was complete, sea and stone and animal and number, and I understood it the way you understand a language in a dream. [552] Then the dust slipped, as I had seen, and she went after it. [565] But she had made me a promise first, and promises are the only things that still hold. [582] "Learn the sequence," she said. [587] "Teach it." [589] "I'll be the weight at the end of it." [598] So I herd them now, badly, elusive in my turn, painting rocks in ones and twos and fives along every bluff from here to the cape, and the eels come, and the children follow the eels, and something in the sea leans toward us that did not lean before. [647] I have seen how it ends, and I will not say. [658] I will only say that every dawn is borrowed, and hers is still out there, held down by a promise, pulling. [679]
