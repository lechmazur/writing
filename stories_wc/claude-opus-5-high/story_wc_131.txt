They called him nothing, because no one knew he was there. [11] Orrin had lived in the bones of the Marchetti Public Library for nine years, in the crawl space under the reading room and the narrow pipe chases that climbed behind its walls like hollow spines. [46] He was kindly and methodical, which is a strange thing to be when you are invisible, but he had decided long ago that habits were the only company that stayed. [76] Each night he swept the chases with a rag on a dowel, oiled the hinges of doors that squeaked, and left a cache of small repairs behind him: tightened bolts, a shimmed shelf, a note in block letters warning of a cracked riser on the east stair. [123] The staff believed in a ghost, or in luck. [132] Orrin preferred that they believed in luck. [139]

The night the grid failed, the city went dark in staggered breaths, whole avenues blinking out and shuddering back, and the library filled with a sapphire hush he could feel in his teeth. [172] With the ventilation stilled, he could hear everything: the settling of paper, the distant hum of a generator two blocks off, and a woman crying in the periodicals room. [201] That was Mrs. Adeyemi, the archivist, who worked late and talked to herself in a low, patient voice, as though soothing a child. [224] For three weeks she had been searching the building for a reel of tape. [238] Orrin knew, because he had heard her explain it to a bored contractor: a 1974 oral-history recording of her father, a bus mechanic, the only surviving sound of his voice, misfiled during the renovation and gone. [274]

He had also known, for eleven days, exactly where it was. [285] He had found it while uncovering a blockage in the third-floor pipe chase, where the renovators had stuffed insulation and trash into the void behind the plaster. [312] A ferromagnetic tape reel fragment, half a plastic hub with maybe nine feet of oxide ribbon still wound on it, label crumbled down to four letters of a name. [341] He had held it and felt the whole of his old life arrive at once: sense memory, sharp as a splinter. [362] The smell of warm acetate and machine oil was his father's repair shop in Lisbon; it was his own hands at fourteen, splicing broadcast reels with a razor and a steady thumb. [394] He had wept then, silently, in the dust, because he had not thought of that shop in a decade and had not wanted to. [418] And then he had done what he always did: cleaned the fragment, wound it properly, and added it to his cache under the reading room floor, with the lost spectacles and the pocket watches and the forty-three orphaned keys. [457]

Keeping things was easy. [461] Giving them back meant being seen. [467]

Now the lights stuttered blue and the woman wept, and Orrin lay in the crawl space with his cheek against the joist, doing arithmetic on his own cowardice. [495] He had told himself for nine years that a man could be good and hidden at the same time. [514] But a repaired hinge could not say her father's name. [524]

So he worked through the dark hours with a methodical hand, as if the work were a kind of prayer. [544] He threaded the fragment onto a salvaged hub, spliced the torn leader, and set it on the archive's old playback deck, which still held charge in its battery backup. [573]

At dawn, the shadowed library turned the color of deep water, sapphire light sliding across the long tables. [591] Mrs. Adeyemi came out of the periodicals room with her coat over her arm, and stopped. [607] A thin man stood beside the deck, gray-bearded, dusty, holding a rag on a dowel like a staff. [625] He pressed the key. [629]

Nine feet of tape is eleven seconds. [636] A man's voice said, in a warm rumble: 'ask her, she remembers the route better than me' - and then leader hiss, and then nothing. [660]

Mrs. Adeyemi's hand went to her mouth. [667] She did not ask who he was, or how long, or why. [679] She asked him to play it again, and he did, six times, while outside the streetlights gave up one final flicker and surrendered to morning. [704]

"There's more," Orrin said, his voice rusty in his own ears. [715] "Under the reading room there is a cache of things people lost, and I kept all of it safe." [734]

"Then let's start giving it back," she said, and held out her hand, and he took it, and the smell of tape and oil hung between them like a door standing open. [766] He had wanted, all along, only this: to become visible to those who needed him. [781]
