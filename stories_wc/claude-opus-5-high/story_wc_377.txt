Mireth tuned bells into harmony on the whispering bluff above the clouds, which lay below her like a second, slower sea. [21] She had come up in the last year of speech, carrying a hammer, a tuning fork, and a grievance. [40] Below, in the valleys, people had finally learned to share meaning directly, mind to mind, and words became unnecessary. [59] Nobody lied anymore; nobody needed to. [65] But Mireth had noticed something the councils had not: when everything could be known instantly, nothing could be wondered at. [85] The color drained out of the world in a way no instrument could measure, and only she seemed to mind. [105] So she climbed. [108] The bluff earned its name from wind that threaded the old belfry ruins and came out sounding like patient conversation. [128] Mireth rebuilt the ruins into something stranger: a kinetic sculpture forty feet tall, all counterweights, vanes, and hanging bronze. [147] Wind drove the vanes; the vanes drove the arms; the arms swung the hammers; the bells answered. [164] She called what it played symphonies of truth, though she never explained the name to anyone, and by then explaining was a dead art. [188] The name was literal. [192] A bell cannot flatter. [196] Struck badly, it sours; struck well, it tells you exactly what it is made of, and how, and by whom. [216] Truth you could hear but not summarize was the only kind the valleys had lost. [231] There was one difficulty, and it was hers alone. [240] Mireth was seasonally mute. [244] Every autumn, when the cold came over the bluff, her voice thinned and then vanished entirely until thaw. [262] A tuner works by singing a pitch and listening for the beat against the bell's own; without a voice, she was blind in the ears. [287] For three winters her sculpture stood silent and honest and useless. [298] In the fourth autumn, a trader's balloon left her a gift she had not asked for: an iceberg fragment the size of a sleeping dog, hauled up from the polar shelves in an insulated crate. [333] The trader's meaning arrived wordlessly, as all meaning did now: pity, mostly, and a little awe. [349] Mireth accepted both, and kept the awe. [356] The ice was ancient enough to hold air from before cities. [367] When she set it on the tuning bench, it began to glisten in the thin high sunlight, and as it warmed it spoke: tiny pops, each bubble releasing a breath ten thousand years old. [401] Mireth put her ear against it and understood that she had been given a voice. [416] She rigged the fragment into the sculpture's highest cradle, where wind and sun could work it. [432] Melt ran down a copper spine into a tipping cup; the cup filled, dropped, released a counterweight, and the great arm came around. [455] The lowest bell sounded. [459] She could not sing the reference pitch, so she let the ice do it: the popping bubbles gave her a steady, unarguable grain of sound to beat against, and her hammer found the flat spots one by one. [497] She worked all winter with muted vigor, mouth closed, arms aching, snow crusting her collar. [512] By first thaw the sculpture could run itself, drinking wind in fair weather and meltwater in foul. [529] The valley people came up because they could not translate what they were hearing, and that was novel. [547] Thought-speech moves fact perfectly and carries nothing else; a bell carries only the else. [561] Children stood at the cliff edge with their heads tipped back, feeling something rise in them that had no name to be transmitted by. [585] Mireth watched their faces and saw the thing she had climbed up to preserve: the color of wonder, returning, faint as a blush and just as involuntary. [612] They asked her, in the silent way, how it worked. [622] It was autumn again; her voice was already gone, and she found she did not miss it. [639] She pointed at the cradle, where the last of the old ice glistened and dripped and kept time. [657] The smallest child understood the arithmetic before the adults did and pushed a question at her: what happens when the ice runs out? [680] Mireth handed her the tuning hammer instead of an answer. [690] The wind came up the bluff whispering, the vanes turned, and the bronze said what could not be said. [709]
