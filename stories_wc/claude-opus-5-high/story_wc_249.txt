They called me Subject Twelve, but the nurses called me the drummer. [12] I had been the experimental treatment's guinea pig for nine months, which is long enough to learn that every surface has a note in it. [37] Bed rails hum; gurney wheels click in fives. [45] The window glass takes a knuckle like a struck bell. [55] Drumming on everything was how I stayed myself while they braided my memories into someone else's. [71]

Her name was Ilka, Subject Eleven, and I never met her body, only her mornings. [86] That is what entangled memories are: you wake with two childhoods and have to sort the laundry. [103] The doctors wanted it for the dying. [110] If a mind could be copied into a willing partner, then nothing true would be lost, only relocated. [128] They tested it on me because I had no one left to confuse me with. [143]

Therapy happened in the sound wave garden on the clinic's north terrace. [155] Nothing grew there from seed. [160] Tones grew it: low frequencies pushed silver dust into stalks, high ones split the stalks into leaves, and a sustained chord could hold a whole hedge upright until someone stopped singing. [191] Patients were given tuning forks and a protocol. [199] I was quietly rebellious about the protocol. [206] I never argued, never refused, never raised my voice at Dr. Abara; I simply played the wrong rhythms when the recorder light went amber. [230] Because Ilka's memories had given me a place, and the protocol had no frequency for it. [246]

An auric lagoon under starlight. [251] Gold water, so still it doubled the sky, ringed by black rocks warm from a sun that had set hours ago. [272] Neither of us had ever been there. [279] We had dreamed it separately as children, and the entanglement had welded the two dreams into one shoreline with two sets of footprints. [302]

Ilka was dying in Ward C, four floors below me, of something the charts spelled out and no one said aloud. [323] She could not walk to the terrace. [330] So the bargain we made, tapped in fives through the ventilation duct that connected her room to mine, was this: I would build the lagoon in the garden before she went. [361] Not a picture of it, not a recording. [369] The thing itself, standing in silver dust, so that the shared dream would have somewhere to keep breathing after one of its dreamers stopped. [393]

To breathe life into a shared dream, you need the right hour. [405] The garden's resonance ran in tides, and once a night everything went slack between them. [420] The night gardeners called it the hush of the universe inhaling. [431] Eleven seconds when the dust held any shape you gave it. [442]

I went up at three in the morning with a stolen key and no tuning fork. [458] I had my hands, which had been in training for nine months. [470] I climbed the terrace wall to perch on the cold stone lip above the dust bed, where the acoustics gathered. [490] Below, the garden's old tone-hedges sagged, waiting. [497] Then the tide went out, and the world took its breath in. [509]

I drummed the lagoon. [513] Heel of my palm on the stone for the deep water, fingertips skittering the railing for starlight, knuckles on the iron post for the warm black rocks. [540] Fives, always fives, the rhythm she had answered with through the duct. [552] The dust rose gold. [556] Not silver, gold, because the memory was hers and mine together and neither of us had ever agreed the water was any other color. [580] It spread flat and shining across the dust bed, doubling the real stars, and the hedges bent over it like black rock. [602]

Far below, in a duct four floors down, something tapped five times. [614] Then four. [616] Then nothing. [618]

I kept drumming anyway, holding the shape while the tide came back in, and the lagoon held, and held, and then thinned into an auroral shimmer, green and rose, sorrowing upward into the dark. [652]

Dr. Abara found me at dawn, still perched, hands ruined. [662] She did not scold me. [667] She had seen the recording: eleven seconds of gold where the protocol predicted nothing. [681] Whose memory made that, she asked, and I could not answer, which was the whole point of the experiment. [700]

They discharged me in spring. [705] I still drum on everything, bus windows, my own sternum, the rail of a stranger's staircase, and some nights, in fives, the air goes faintly gold. [731] She is not in me the way the doctors hoped, filed and retrievable. [744] She is in me the way a rhythm is in a hand: only there when I play it, and gone the moment I stop. [768] So I do not stop. [773]
