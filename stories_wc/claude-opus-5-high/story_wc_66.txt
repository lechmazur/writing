The story famine had ended the way droughts end: not with rain, but with everyone forgetting the taste of water. [20] Mirem copied music for the State Orchestra, and she was diligent, which was the only virtue the censors rewarded. [39] Forty parts a night, each bar ruled straight, each rest placed where the conductor's baton expected it. [56] Nobody examined the work of a woman who never made mistakes. [67] That was how she smuggled. [72]

Her ink was secretly luminous, brewed from the foxfire that grew on the rotting piano crates behind the conservatory. [91] By lamplight it read as ordinary lyrics beneath the vocal line. [102] In darkness, the words she had layered underneath rose up pale green, and the page told a story. [120]

Stories were not illegal, exactly. [125] They had simply been declared finished, all of them, catalogued and shelved and left to dust in the Depository. [144] Mirem had worked there once, as a girl, and she remembered the dust most of all: grey, patient, thick with the powder of pages nobody would ever open. [172] She had made herself a promise then, absurd and unshakeable: she would teach the dust to tell stories again. [191]

On the nights she was not copying, she climbed the switchback path to the ember alcove. [207] Charcoal burners had used the hollow for two centuries, and the stone still held a low red heat, breathing it out into the mist that pooled along the ridge. [236] The mist cloaked her, and the warmth kept her fingers loose on the key. [250] There, on a crate, sat the transmitter: a scavenged set with a cracked housing and one intact shortwave dial, its glow the amber of a held match. [277] That dial glow was the only light she permitted herself. [287]

The pirate broadcast went out at two in the morning, forty minutes long, always beginning with the same three chords so listeners could find her by ear. [314] She never spoke a narrative aloud. [320] Speech was traceable, and prose was what the censors listened for. [331] Instead she sang, and she told everything via elusive song lyrics, the tale broken across verses that meant nothing alone. [351] A brother who became a heron, a debt paid in weather, a door that opened only for the second-born. [370] The listener who kept all four broadcasts of a month, and wrote the lines out, and sat with them, would find the story assembling itself in their own handwriting. [399] That was the point. [403] A story you reassemble is a story you cannot forget. [413]

Her tone, on air, was what her mother would have called thistled calm: level, unhurried, with something sharp folded into it that caught you if you reached too casually. [442] She had not chosen it; the famine had filed her down to it. [455]

In the eleventh month, a cellist named Oshe found the glow. [466] He had taken his part home, fallen asleep over it, and woken in the dark to green letters crawling under the words of a marching hymn. [492] He came to the copyists' room the next morning, laid the page on her desk, and said only, "Verse three is missing." [514] Mirem did not look up from her ruling pen. [523] "Verse three goes out Thursday," she said. [530]

He became her second pair of hands. [537] Oshe smuggled the luminous parts into seventeen cities inside the orchestra's touring trunks, and the orchestra, all unknowing, carried her stories under its own official seal. [563]

The censors came for the transmitter in the spring. [572] They triangulated the ridge and climbed it in daylight, when the mist had lifted and could not cloak anything. [591] They found the alcove swept, the crate empty, the coals cold, and one thing left deliberately behind: a sheet of staff paper with no notes on it. [618]

They filed it, of course. [623] It went to the Depository, into the grey drifts, where after a year the powder of it had worked into everything on the shelf. [647] The ink had been in the fibers. [654] Clerks began reporting that the dust in the west stacks shone faintly when the lamps went out, and that if you looked long enough you could read it: a line about a heron, a line about weather, a line about a door. [696] They dusted, and it spread. [701]

Nobody ever found the copyist. [706] But in a hundred kitchens at two in the morning, people still turned a dial until an amber sliver of light steadied, and waited for three chords, and wrote down whatever she gave them. [740] The famine was over. [744] It had ended not with rain but with everyone remembering, one stubborn verse at a time, the taste of water. [764]
