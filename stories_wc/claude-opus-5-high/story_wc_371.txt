After the birds flew south, the city grew quieter by one degree, and only Mira noticed. [16] That was her affliction and her office: she was the one who noticed creeping normality. [31] Streetlamps dimmed a lumen a month; the word for "bakery" slipped out of the newspapers; the queue lines were repainted two steps closer to the wall. [57] Everyone adjusted, then forgot they had adjusted. [64] Mira kept a ledger of subtractions, and she kept something heavier: the last loaf from Yerev's bakery, taken the morning before the shutters came down for good. [91] Eleven years had turned it to a grey stone that smelled faintly of caraway. [105] She had discovered, that first winter, that Yerev had baked whole rye kernels into his crusts, and that some of them were still alive. [129]

So she went to work through guerrilla gardening. [137] Wherever the city erased something, Mira pried a kernel from the loaf with a pin and pushed it into the crack: rye in the tram-stop gutter, rye where the puppet theatre had stood, rye along the seam of the requisitioned orphanage. [178] Rye is a vulgar, patient grain; it grows in rubble and asks nothing. [191] By the fourth year the citizens had a new normality to fail to notice: green blades standing at attention over every absence. [213]

The thickest stand grew in the old palace courtyard, where Mira had planted a deliberate maze of rye and thistle around a gardener's shed. [237] Inside the shed was the tea house, three low tables, a brazier, and a rule: you came after the birds flew south, and you came once a year. [265] People came to drink smoke-colored tea and say the vanished names aloud, because Mira believed that if anything holy survived in the city, it survived in ordinary things - a name, a loaf, a kettle - and someone had to keep looking. [305]

This autumn a stranger came through the maze without scratching himself, which meant he had watched someone else walk it first. [326] He sat, accepted tea, and asked her to let him uncloak. [337] "Say it, then," Mira said. [342] He gave his name - Dovrin - and his old post: assessor, Section for Municipal Simplification. [356] He had signed the order that closed Yerev's bakery. [365]

The room went still in the way a room goes still when everyone decides at once not to move. [384] Dovrin explained himself badly, which Mira took as a good sign. [395] He had been heuristically gentle: a man with no courage and a private rule of thumb, who, when a file was ambiguous, always chose the lesser column. [422] Relocation instead of arrest; closure instead of confiscation; a fine where another assessor would have written a sentence. [440] He had saved, he estimated, two hundred people by half a degree each, and he had also, with the same pen, unmade a bakery, a theatre, and a street of names. [471] "I want to be told whether that arithmetic comes out," he said. [483]

Mira thought about the eleven-year-old loaf in her coat, three-quarters eaten by the city's cracks. [498] She had no authority to forgive him; she had not been the baker, and the baker was somewhere past the mountains or past everything. [522] What she could offer was thinner than forgiveness and more useful. [533] She took out the loaf, worked a kernel loose, and set it in his palm. [548]

"The arithmetic does not come out," she said, "and you will carry it." [561] "But the bakery is not finished, because this is still seed." [572] "Plant it where you signed, every year, until someone asks what the grain is doing there." [588] That was the absolution: tenuous, conditional, renewable each autumn, and revocable if he stopped. [602] Dovrin closed his hand and wept, unattractively, into good tea. [612]

In spring, rye came up on the steps of the Section for Municipal Simplification, and a clerk asked what the grain was doing there. [636] Someone had to answer, and the answer was a bakery's name. [647] Mira wrote it in her ledger under a new heading - additions - and found she had to start a second page. [667] The birds, when they came back, found the courtyard taller than they had left it. [682] Mira noticed, of course; she always did; and this time the noticing tasted of caraway and felt like grace. [701]
