Rain had just stopped over Ivory Harbor, and the bleached quays steamed like fresh bread. [15] Sela climbed them anyway, boots squeaking, because spray after rain left the clearest record she would get all season. [34] She was the harbor's ocean-spray archivist, the last of them, which meant she copied the salt patterns drying on the seawall into ledgers no one had requested in eleven years. [64] Eleven years was also the length of the exile, which had ended at dawn. [78] The Assembly that banished the harbor's fisher-clans had dissolved, and the ships were coming home. [93] Not all at once, though. [98] They returned through staggered intervals, three hulls at moonrise, one at noon, none for two days, then seven. [116] Sela had learned to read the gaps rather than the arrivals. [127] Each missing interval was a family that had died inland, or chosen the mountains, or simply stopped. [144] Her ledgers, she realized, were no longer about water. [153]

In her coat pocket she carried a brass valve, the only surviving piece of the old herald's trumpet. [171] Soldiers had melted the instrument the week the banishment was read aloud, so that no one could sound a gathering. [191] The valve had rolled into a drain, and Sela had fished it out as a girl and kept it because it clicked. [213] That click became her clock. [218] When a ship appeared, she pressed the valve once; when an expected ship did not, she pressed it twice and wrote the absence down. [242]

Colleagues had called her reactively visionary, which was a polite way of saying she could not imagine anything until something struck her first. [265] It was true. [268] She had never invented a plan in her life, but hand her a broken thing and she would see, instantly and completely, what it wanted to become. [295]

So on the ninth morning, when old Mera Tolvey walked up the quay and asked where her brother's boat had moored, Sela did not have an answer, and the question struck her. [327] The vision came the way it always did, as a reaction. [338] She saw her eleven years of gap-notes not as failure but as a chart: every absence timed, placed, and named. [358] Nobody else in the world had navigated absences for a decade with a herald's valve and a salt ledger. [377] She took Mera's hand and told her to come back at dusk, and to bring anyone who was asking the same thing. [399]

By dusk there were sixty of them on the wet white stones, smelling of tar and long roads. [417] Sela did not make a speech, because speeches were invention and invention was not her gift. [433] Instead she opened the ledgers and began to read the gaps aloud, pressing the valve twice after each one. [452] Click-click: the Tolvey boat, absent from the third spring tide, last seen turning east. [466] Click-click: the Auden twins, absent four winters, their mother's name still chalked on the seawall where the spray had preserved it. [487]

People began to correct her. [492] The Tolvey boat had not turned east; it had sunk off the shoals, and here was the man who had watched it. [514] The Auden twins were alive in a wool town nine days inland, and a cousin had their address. [532] This was exactly what Sela had foreseen when Mera's question hit her: her gaps were not holes to mourn but slots to fill, and only the returned could fill them. [562]

They worked until the lamps guttered, and then the next night, and the next. [576] Her motive had never been grand; she only wanted to compile fragments into meaning, the way salt makes a pattern if you watch it long enough. [602] But the harbor took it as something grander. [610] What heartened them was not that their dead were returned to them, since most were not. [626] It was that someone had kept counting while they were gone, that the harbor had held their shapes open in ink. [647]

On the last night Mera asked her to sound the valve once, for arrival, and Sela pressed it and pressed it and pressed it, sixty times, until her thumb ached. [677] Rain came again near morning, washing the seawall clean. [686] Sela watched the old names dissolve without grief. [694] She had them elsewhere now, in sixty mouths, and the spray would write something new by evening. [711]
