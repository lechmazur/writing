The last ship sailed at dusk on the ninth of October, and I stood on the mole and let it go without me. [23] I am a traveler who writes obituaries for the living, and my work requires winter. [38] Summer people cannot sit still long enough to be remembered properly. [49]

The palace of Oravia had been empty of its court for eleven years, and kept only Dame Halvic, who kept only the carpets. [72] She met me at the water gate with a lamp and a question she had clearly rehearsed for months. [91] "Can you write my children," she said, "though they are all three alive?" [104]

This is what I do. [109] The living leave without explanation; that is the wound. [118] I write explained absences - long, vividly descriptive accounts of who a person was, where they went, and what the leaving cost them - so that the ones left behind may hold something with weight in it. [153] Dame Halvic's three had gone out on three different ships in three different years, and none had written. [171]

We worked in the garden, because she could not bear to do it indoors. [185] It was a shadowed garden even in high summer, sunk below the seawall, and in autumn it became a kind of lonesome splendor: black medlar branches, copper beech litter, the smell of salt in the rotting apples, and above it all the palace windows holding the last light like coins held in a closed hand. [240] She talked; I listened; the year went down around us. [250]

My method is not invention. [255] I work by projecting presence - by gathering a thousand small true things until the absent one occupies the chair across from you and cannot easily get up again. [283] So I asked Dame Halvic for details no mother would think to give. [296] How did Anno hold a spoon. [302] Which stair did Beata skip. [307] What did the youngest, Lior, say the sea smelled like when he was six? [321] ("Wet iron," she said, and then she wept for the first time in front of me, which was the first honest thing either of us had done.) [348]

For the binding she gave me something intimate beyond any confession. [359] In the throne hall lay the old coronation carpet, and where the border had frayed she had spent eleven winters mending it, thread by thread, from the reverse. [387] She drew out one long palace carpet weft thread - saffron silk, three hundred years old, the color of a lamp seen through a door left ajar - and tied it around the pages of Anno's obituary. [422] "A weft goes crosswise," she said. "It is the thread that holds the ones running away." [438]

I read the three of them aloud in the garden, on three evenings, in November. [453] Anno, who left because he could not be gentle in a house that required it. [468] Beata, who left because she was the one who could. [478] Lior, who left because his brother and sister had made leaving look like the family trade. [494] None of it excused them, and that was the point; an explanation is not a pardon, only a map. [513]

That is why I travel: to understand the geography of healing, which is real geography, with distances and weather and roads that wash out. [537] I have a sister in Verrin who has not spoken to me since our mother died, and I have written her obituary nine times and burned eight. [564]
