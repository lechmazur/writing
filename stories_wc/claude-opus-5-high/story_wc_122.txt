The cavern where the drum maker worked lay under a hill that shed its leaves all at once, and Mira came to it during the faint rustle of turning seasons. [30] She could not hear the rustle. [36] She had been deaf for eleven years, which was not the problem. [48] The problem was that she had stopped feeling anything at all, and a music teacher who feels nothing is a fraud with a metronome. [72] For two years her chest had been a closed room. [82] Her students played, she watched their throats and hands and corrected them, and nothing arrived. [97]

The drum maker's cavern was lit by tallow and hung with hides in every stage of becoming. [114] Old Teodor did not look up when she entered; he only pointed to the floor, where a hundred finished drums stood like a congregation. [138] Mira had come because of a letter he had written to her school, three lines long, offering to teach her the filament of hush. [162] Every sound, he wrote, is spun around a thread of silence, the way a rope is spun around its core; pull the thread and the sound unravels into what it actually is. [194] She had thrown the letter away twice and retrieved it twice. [205]

Teodor wrote his answers on a slate, in a hand as careful as stitching. [219] "Sound goes forward, hush goes backward," the slate said, "and to find the filament you must play songs backward." [238] He kept a phonograph in the cavern's driest alcove, a hand-cranked thing with a bell like a trumpet flower, and he had cranked it in reverse for forty years. [267] Backward recorded songs, he explained, strip a melody of its intentions: no rising, no resolving, only the breath underneath. [286]

The trouble was the needle. [291] He showed her the broken phonograph needle in his palm, snapped a week ago, its steel tip missing. [309] Mira, who had spent eleven years rebuilding a life out of vibration, understood the joke immediately: the deaf woman had come for a sound she could not hear, and the sound was gone too. [343] Something in her moved -- not feeling, exactly, but the memory of its shape. [356]

She took the broken needle, filed its stump against a whetstone until a new point rose from it, and set it in the arm herself. [381] Then she lay down on the stone floor among the drums, pressed her cheek and both palms flat, and nodded. [401]

Teodor cranked backward. [404] The cavern was audibly muted -- Teodor said the hides drank every echo -- and so the drumskins took the reversed song and gave it to the rock, and the rock gave it to her bones. [438] She felt a hymn walk in from its own ending. [448] She felt a waltz she had played at her wedding arrive backward, the last chord first, so that it never resolved and never lied. [472] And between the notes, where the forward world puts nothing, she felt it: a fine continuous thread, thin as spider-silk, running through every sound in the room. [499]

A filament of hush. [503] It was not absence. [507] It was the thing she had been standing inside of for two years, mistaking it for death. [524] Grief had gone quiet in her and she had assumed quiet meant gone. [537]

She wept on the floor of the drum maker's cavern until the tallow guttered, and the weeping was the first warm thing in two years. [562] Teodor did not comfort her; he sat in the mirrored hush of that room -- his silence answering hers, hers answering his -- and waited, the way a maker waits for glue to set. [594]

She went home with a drum he would not let her pay for. [607] In November her students sat in a circle with their palms on its head while she played their own recital pieces backward from a cheap machine. [633] She watched their faces to see the moment each of them found the thread. [647] It was easy to enthrall children with noise; anyone with cymbals could do that. [661] To enthrall them with the hush between notes took a broken needle, a hill of falling leaves, and a teacher who had come back from numbness the long way. [690]

Every autumn after, when the leaves began their faint rustle, she took the bus to the hill and lay down among the drums again. [714] Teodor cranked; she listened with her hands. [721] Nothing in the cavern made a sound she could hear, and she had never heard so much. [738]
