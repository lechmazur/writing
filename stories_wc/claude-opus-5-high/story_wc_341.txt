The tether cable parted at 4,000 meters with a sound like a harp string giving up. [16] Ines Mabuya, aviation meteorologist, third grade, did not scream. [25] She wrote the time in her logbook: 18:42, after the string breaks. [37] The sky gondola, a wicker-and-aluminum cabin slung beneath a weather balloon the color of old teeth, began to drift east over the salt flats. [61] Below, the winch drum kept spinning, paying out nothing. [70] Ines had spent eleven years at the Kuro Station tether, sending up instruments and bringing them down, and in all that time she had never once been aloft when the line let go. [103] Her supervisors had called her quietly explosive in three separate performance reviews, which she considered fair. [119] She never raised her voice; she simply refused to stop. [129]

The thing she refused to stop doing was categorizing clouds by emotion. [141] The World Meteorological Organization had a perfectly serviceable taxonomy, and she used it in her official reports: cumulus, altostratus, cirrus uncinus. [162] But in the back pages, in pencil, she kept the other ledger. [174] Grieving stratus, the flat gray sheet that will not admit it is raining. [187] Boastful cumulus congestus. [190] Cirrus of small regret, which arrives eight hours before the front and apologizes for it. [205]

Teodor had started the ledger. [210] He had been her observer for nine of those eleven years, a man who carried a brass telescope up the gondola ladder every shift because he distrusted anything with a battery. [241] The telescope was older than the station: dented tube, dusty starlight in the eyepiece even at noon, a hairline crack in the objective that split every bright thing into twins. [271] Two years ago a downdraft slammed the gondola into the mast and Teodor went over the rail. [288] The inquiry ruled it clear air, unforecastable, nobody's fault. [297] Ines had disagreed, quietly, for twenty-four months. [304]

Her disagreement had a thesis: empty spaces remember. [312] Where a cloud has been, the air keeps the shape of it - a scar of humidity, a cooled column, a wake that persists long after the visible thing has gone. [342] The downdraft that killed him had been the ghost of a cloud that dissolved an hour earlier over the ridge, and if she could prove that, the sky would stop being blameless. [374] Nobody funded that. [377] So she rode up alone, with his telescope, twice a week. [388]

Now, untethered, she understood that the broken line was the only instrument she had ever needed. [404] A tethered gondola can only measure the column it hangs in. [415] A free one goes where the air goes. [423] She trimmed ballast, held altitude, and let the wind carry her toward the ridge where the cloud had died. [442] Radio crackled: recovery team, twenty minutes, stay calm. [450] She was calm. [453] She logged the thermistor every ten seconds. [460]

At 19:08, over the ridge, the air went cold and the gondola sank four meters without a cloud anywhere in the sky. [482] The trace on her chart drew the exact profile of a cumulus that had evaporated ninety minutes before - base, core, anvil - a whole cloud written in temperature where nothing stood. [512]

Ines put her eye to the brass telescope and looked at the empty place. [526] The cracked lens doubled the first star into two, and she thought of the phrase Teodor used for what they were doing up here, the phrase she had never let herself write in any report: spooled wholeness. [563] He meant it about the winch drum: eight kilometers of cable coiled into something you could put your arms around, entire and small, nothing lost in the coiling. [591] He meant it about people too. [597] A life is not shortened by being wound up; it is all still there, every meter of it, waiting on the drum. [619] She wrote the final entry by the light of a failing lamp: 19:08, ridge sector four, invisible cumulus, emotional category vindication. [640]

The recovery balloon found her at midnight over the flats. [650] The board read her chart for three weeks and then amended the finding on Teodor's death from unforecastable to residual wake, ridge-generated, now modeled. [674] They named the correction after him, which is how a bureaucracy memorializes a man: a paragraph, a coefficient, a warning that will save somebody it will never meet. [702]

Ines memorialized him differently. [706] She bolted the brass telescope to the rail of the new gondola, cracked lens and all, so that every observer after her would see each star twice and understand why. [736] And she left the pencil ledger in the cabin, open, for whoever came next. [750] The first new entry, in a stranger's handwriting, appeared that autumn: altocumulus, patient, waiting to be asked. [767]
