Three years after the future arrived early, when the long-awaited era of shared collective dreaming launched without warning, bypassing generations of negotiated community safeguards to flood global consciousness with unmoored, unregulated visions, Lira, the root-bound visionary who had tended the cobalt glade since she was a teen, knelt in the deepening hush of dusk, her calves laced tight with the fine, silver-edged roots of the glade’s cobalt sedge that had anchored her to the soil for a decade. 
Purposefully curious even when the thrum of unfiltered dream noise made her teeth ache, she had spent those three years mapping warps in the glade’s central topiary maze, the living threshold that had once marked the formal entry point to collective dream space, before the eclipsed covenant, the sacred agreement to enter shared dreams slowly, gently, with care for the most vulnerable sleepers, had been blotted out by the premature launch. 
Clutched in her scarred left palm was a single maze topiary clipping, a sprig of glossy yew she had snapped from the maze’s warped southern wall the day the first unregulated dream spilled over to wake a toddler in Nairobi with a vision of a flood that never came, a tangible anchor cut from the only structure that still held a faint resonance of the old, agreed-upon dream paths. 
Her motivation, the quiet fire that had kept her rooted even when she ached to walk out of the glade and find the engineers who had broken the covenant, was to find the intersection of dreams, the thin, glowing seam where the chaotic, unmoored dreams of the early future folded into the gentle, guided dream spaces the community had spent centuries designing, a point where the two realities could be stitched back together instead of tearing each other apart. 
Dusk deepened to bruised cobalt, painting the sedge fronds the color of old starlight, and she closed her eyes, forcing her breath to slow so she could heed the faint, thrumming signal that had tugged at her for three hours, a vibration that traveled not through air or soil, but along the thin, silvery scars crisscrossing her palms, marks shared by every dream steward who had sworn to uphold the original covenant. 
Linking through scars was the oldest steward practice, a quiet magic that required no wires or servers, only the memory of a promise pressed into skin, allowing scattered stewards across the globe to send word when they found a thread of the old dream paths, even when corporate firewalls blocked every other form of communication. 
The signal hummed louder, warm against her palm where the yew clipping rested, and she felt a jolt of that sharp, tangled anxious elation she had not let herself feel since the launch: the tight, breathless fear that she would fumble the only chance to mend what was broken, tangled up with the bright, fizzy certainty that she was finally, finally close. 
She pressed the yew clipping into the soft soil at the exact spot the scar-tug pulled her toward, right where a cluster of cobalt sedge grew in a perfect circle, and watched as the sprig’s tiny roots unfurled in seconds, weaving down to meet the glade’s own root network, sending a pulse of soft, gold light up through her ankles where the sedge held her tight. 
For a heartbeat, she could see it: the intersection of dreams, glowing just below the soil, where a child’s chaotic, giddy dream of flying on a dragon’s back curved gently to meet an elder’s quiet dream of teaching children to identify wild berries, where the messy, unplanned energy of the early future did not eclipse the old covenant but braided into it, making something stronger than either vision could have been alone. 
The scar-link hummed, and she felt the quiet, relieved greetings of stewards in Lagos, Toronto, and Seoul, all of them holding their own pieces of topiary clipping, all feeling the same braided light spread out from the glade to stitch the rift closed. 
Her hands shook as the gold light softened to wrap around the glade, and she laughed through the tightness in her chest, realizing the covenant had never been a fixed set of rules to be broken when the future came too soon, but a living promise to meet each dream, planned or unplanned, with the same care she had given the glade for all those rooted years.
