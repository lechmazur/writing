The cryptic medium waded through knee-deep briny tidal water in the mangrove labyrinth inhabited by singing spirits, moving through the thin, liminal space where an apology waits unspoken. 
Her boots caught on tangled root knots as she clutched the gargoyle’s rainspout shard, a jagged chip of weathered limestone she’d pried from the gutter of a burned-out cathedral three years prior, its rough edge worn smooth from years of being pressed to her palm when grief felt too sharp to bear. 
The shard held old, quiet magic: it healed in moonlight, its fine cracks knitting closed each time silver light spilled over its surface, just as it wove closed the rawest edges of the hurt people carried to her for readings. 
She was not here for a client this time; her core motivation was sharp, personal, unshakable: to find the moment before deciding, the split second 12 years prior when she had chosen pride over reaching for her younger sister, letting a petty, stupid fight fester until a late-night car crash had stolen any chance to make it right. 
The singing spirits swirled closer, their harmonies tangling in mangrove leaves, as she reached a weathered stilt cottage half-hidden in the prop roots, its smudged glass windows stacked high with unsent letters, each envelope sealed with wax that glowed the soft blue of forgotten, unvoiced breath. 
This was the threshold where unspoken words collected, seeping through the cypress planks to bead as condensation on windows, the method she had spent 15 years refining to carry messages between the living and the lost, no loud, performative seance required. 
She climbed the splintered porch steps, pressing the gargoyle shard to the coldest pane of wavy old glass, and began to evoke the thin, gossamer thread of memory she had chased across three states and half a dozen liminal spaces, her voice low and rough with a fierce tenderness she rarely let clients or strangers see. 
The spirits’ harmonies softened, wrapping around her like salt-kissed linen as the condensation swirled into shifting, silvery shapes, replaying the fight that had severed her from her sister: the sharp, careless words, the slamming front door, the split second where she had almost reached out, almost apologized, almost let her pride soften before she turned hard and walked away. 
A sliver of bright moonlight slipped through the mangrove canopy then, falling directly on the gargoyle shard in her hand, and she felt its familiar warmth seep into her palm as its tiny, age-worn cracks knitted closed, the stone’s magic mending not just its own fractured surface, but the frayed edge of that long-ago moment frozen in unspoken time. 
She had brought her own tattered stack of unsent letters to the cottage, each one filled with the apologies she had never gotten to say, the silly inside jokes she had wanted to share, the small updates about the jasmine plant her sister had given her that still bloomed on her fire escape every warm spring. 
The condensation shifted again, forming messy, looping handwriting she would have known even in the dark: I was just about to knock on your door, the water droplets spelled out, I was sorry too. 
For years she had thought the moment before deciding had belonged only to her, a single, terrible choice she had made alone in her anger, but the spirits’ song swelled warm around her to let her know her sister had stood on the other side of that same door, hand raised to knock, caught in the very same liminal space of unspoken apology. 
She pressed her forehead to the cool glass, hot tears mixing with the condensation as she let the weight of 12 years of sharp guilt soften, no longer needing to fix the irreparable past because the love between them had never been broken, just stuck in that quiet, waiting space between pride and forgiveness. 
As she turned to wade back through the twisting mangrove roots, the mended gargoyle shard warm in her jacket pocket and the spirits’ soft song following her out of the labyrinth, she did not carry the leaden regret she had brought with her, only the quiet, gentle knowledge that unspoken apologies do not vanish forever--they wait, held softly in the salt tides, until we are brave enough to meet them halfway.
