Beneath a sky where stars winked out one by one in slow, stuttering succession, the cryptic letterpress operator leaned over their workbench in the topaz workshop, walls lined with honey-hued crystal slabs that held the faint, warm glow of sunlight trapped centuries prior. 
They habitually borrowed personalities the way other printers borrowed lead sorts: a grieving lighthouse keeper’s gruff tenderness one week, a teenage astronomer’s skittish wonder the next, never holding a self long enough to let it stick to their bones. 
Their driving pursuit, across all the borrowed skins, was to understand the mathematics of emotion: the unquantifiable weight of a held hand, the sharp angle of unspoken regret, the way joy curved light just enough to bend a shadow at the edge of a room. 
Tucked in the brass chase of their press, tucked between slugs of cast type and ink-stained rags, sat a halo fragment, cool as forgotten moonstone, fallen from the sky three star-vanishings prior, when they had worn the personality of a nun who spent her days tracing cracks in cathedral stained glass. 
Tonight, between vanishing stars, they had locked the workshop’s retractable roof open, feeding sheets of cotton paper through the press at a steady, rhythmic pace, each impression inked with a mix of lampblack and powdered topaz dust that carried a faint, thrumming charge. 
They did not print legible sentences, not tonight, choosing instead to set type in random, half-familiar clusters that read like half-remembered dreams: fragments of lullabies, unsent apology lines, snippets of street vendor calls layered so no single voice dominated, the product of deliberate ambiguity calibrated to disorient anyone who stopped to read the sheets pinned to the workshop’s outer door. 
The halo fragment, pressed tight against the type as the press rolled over each sheet, wove transposed empathy into every fiber of the paper: a traveler who paused to squint at the text might suddenly feel the quiet homesickness of the barmaid who had walked past an hour prior, a shepherd counting the dimming stars might briefly know the sharp, bright thrill of the child who had chased a firefly past the gate at dusk. 
The air hummed with that lucid turbulence: the operator moved with precise, unhurried motions, feeding paper, inking plates, jotting tiny, numbered notes in a leather journal as the disorientation rippled outward, no confusion touching their own borrowed, temporary persona, even as the emotions of every passerby wove into a tangled, shimmering web they could trace as clearly as equations on a chalkboard. 
For months, they had borrowed personalities to avoid the bias of a single, fixed self, convinced that emotional truth could only be measured from a shifting vantage point, that standing too long in one set of memories would skew the sums they sought to tally. 
Another star winked out then, its faint golden light trailing down to brush the edge of the press, and the halo fragment hummed so loudly the lead type clattered in its chase, sending a jolt of transposed empathy straight into the operator’s chest rather than into the half-printed sheet beneath the roller. 
For a disorienting heartbeat, they did not feel the quiet wonder of the borrowed astronomer persona they had worn that evening, or the gruff patience of the lighthouse keeper from the month prior, but every emotion they had watched ripple across the broadsides: homesickness, joy, regret, the quiet softness of two strangers sharing a loaf of bread at the workshop gate, the sharp grief of a woman mourning a cat that had vanished with the first dimming star. 
They had thought the equations of feeling required distance, a neutral observer to tally angles and weights without being pulled into the current, but as the hum of the halo fragment faded, they scribbled a final line in their journal, their handwriting wobbly for the first time all night: the constant, they wrote, is not distance, but the way every feeling bleeds into the next, no single self separate enough to measure without being measured in turn. 
Outside, another star faded, and the topaz walls glowed a little warmer, holding the new equation tight, as the operator leaned against their press, for the first time in years not reaching for a new personality to borrow, content to sit in the tangled, luminous math of feeling as the sky dimmed slow above the open roof.
