The counterfeit emotion dealer knelt on fossilized resin ledges in the amber grotto at dusk, her cuffs smudged with the dried residue of fabricated joy, borrowed grief, and carefully calibrated nostalgia she had sold to clients too numb to conjure feeling on their own for three decades. 
She was professionally thorough, a trait that had earned her a loyal clientele but left her perpetually perched at a distance, observing the cravings of others without ever letting a genuine, unscripted feeling stick to her own ribs. 
For years, her trade had run on half truths: she never told clients their purchased bursts of contentment were stitched from snippets of her own long-buried memories, never admitted the sharp, bright spark of courage she sold to anxious public speakers was pulled from the split second she’d first run away from home at 17, never confessed she had not felt a full, unbroken emotion of her own since she began trading counterfeits. 
Her motivation, the quiet drive she had never voiced to even her most regular patron, was to fuse insight with empathy: to understand exactly what each client ached for without becoming so lost in their feelings that she could no longer craft the precise emotional dose they needed to get through the week. 
Her primary method of reading unspoken client need required no lab equipment, no probing questions, only the fine, innate skill of sensing through static electricity: the faint, fizzing charge hovering above a client’s wrist told her if they carried unspoken grief, the tiny snap when their fingers brushed hers signaled repressed joy, the slow, low hum clinging to their coat cuffs warned of burnout too deep to name. 
That dusk, she had come to the grotto not to meet a client, but to test a new blend she had been tinkering with for months, anchored to the fragrance chemist’s blotter petal she had traded a full vial of uncomplicated first love to acquire. 
The petal, pressed thin and treated to hold emotional essence the way ordinary blotter held perfume, was supposed to let her pour her decades of collected client insights into a single, shareable feeling that would not strip her of the careful detachment that made her work possible. 
She had layered every half truth she had ever told onto the petal first: the gentle lie that purchased joy felt identical to the real thing, the soft evasion that she never carried pieces of her clients home with her, the quiet untruth that her detachment meant she did not care deeply for every person who walked through the grotto’s moss-fringed entrance. 
As she held the petal between her thumb and forefinger, she felt the familiar static fizz rise from its porous surface, first tickling her fingertips, then winding up her wrist, then spreading in warm, honeyed waves across her chest as the transformation completes. 
She had expected the blend to remain a separate, transferable dose, something she could bottle and sell, but instead she felt it subsume the last of her carefully guarded emotional distance, folding every snippet of client grief, joy, fear, and hope she had collected over decades into the quiet, steady care she had always held just out of her own reach. 
She did not panic, did not fumble the petal as the last of the static hum softened into a quiet, even pulse, because her professionally thorough nature had already built a fail-safe into the blend: she would never be so flooded by empathy that she could not show up for the people who relied on her. 
The half truths she had hidden behind for years did not dissolve entirely, because they still held useful, gentle care for the clients who needed soft illusions to get through hard days, but they no longer formed a wall between her insight and the quiet empathy she had always been afraid to let touch her own heart. 
She slipped the blotter petal into the inner pocket of her jacket as the last amber light of dusk seeped into the grotto’s resin walls, feeling, for the first time, both perfectly removed from the weight of other people’s pain and perfectly connected to the soft, fragile wants that made every one of her clients worth showing up for.
