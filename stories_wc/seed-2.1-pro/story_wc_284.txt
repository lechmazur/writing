At the tilt of the earth toward spring, when thaw nibbles the edges of drifts but frost still lingers in shadowed hollows, Elara climbed the switchbacks to Zephyr Bluff, the mist wrapping around her oiled canvas coat like spun silver, muting every crack of twig and distant birdcall into the quiet silver hush she had not known she ached for. 
She was a burned spy, cut loose by her agency three months prior after a botched extraction that left her cover blown, her name on every watchlist between the border and the coast, and her confidence tattered so thin she could barely look her own reflection in the eye without flinching from the ghost of the choices that had gotten two civilian contacts killed. 
She had made the climb on a tip passed through a dead drop from her old mentor, a note scrawled in faded ink that claimed the bluff held the answer to what she had been searching for since the burn notice dropped: a way to find where unused courage waits, the reserve she had abandoned when she froze mid-extraction, too paralyzed by fear of making the wrong call to act until it was too late. 
Prudently daring, as she had been trained to be, she had approached the bluff in stages, checking for surveillance, scanning tree lines for tripwires, and keeping one hand hovering near the compact pepper spray she carried in her coat pocket even though she knew no agency hit team would bother tracking a disgraced asset all the way out to this wind-scoured overlook. 
The mist thinned just enough as she reached the flat stone lip of the bluff to reveal the drift bank piled against the weathered trail marker, its crusted surface scored with faint, deliberate marks she recognized instantly as codes in accumulated snow, a dead-drop language she and her mentor had invented as rookies, when they were both convinced they would change the world one quiet operation at a time. 
She knelt, brushing a fine layer of fresh frost from the marks to parse their meaning, her breath catching when she realized the code did not lead to a cache of weapons or a new identity or proof that the agency had set her up to take the fall for the botched op, but to a small brass object half-buried at the base of the marker: an engraved pillbox, its lid etched with the constellation she had grown up tracing from her childhood bedroom window, the same marking she had thought was lost forever when she fled the extraction site months prior. 
She pried the lid open to find no pills inside, only a folded scrap of paper written in her mentor’s tight, slanted script, words that would vouchsafe the truth she had been too afraid to speak even to herself: courage was never a finite stockpile to burn through on high-stakes calls, no matter how many mistakes she had made, no matter how thoroughly her old life had shattered. 
Fixing what is broken, the note continued, does not require erasing the cracks or pretending the harm never happened; it requires showing up, one small, careful choice at a time, even when the mist blurs the path ahead, even when the last thaw has not yet washed away the last of the winter’s snow. 
A soft zephyr curled over the bluff, tugging a strand of hair loose from her braid and carrying the sharp, sweet scent of budding crocuses pushing up through the crusted snow at her feet, and for the first time in three months, Elara did not feel the cold weight of failure pressing down on her chest. 
She slipped the engraved pillbox into the inner pocket of her coat, where it rested warm against her sternum, and traced the faint indentations of the snow codes once more, committing the reminder to memory before the rising sun softened the drifts and washed the marks away. 
She had thought unused courage waited in some hidden cache, locked away behind layers of tradecraft and regret, but she knew now it had been with her all along, waiting for her to stop running long enough to reach out and take it, to stop treating her broken parts as something to discard and start tending to them instead. 
As the mist began to lift, gilded by the first slanted light of the equinox sun, she turned to make her way down the switchback, her steps slower, surer, ready to begin mending what the fire of her old life had scorched, one quiet, brave choice at a time.
