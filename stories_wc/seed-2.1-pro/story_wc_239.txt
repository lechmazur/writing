The end of history witness stepped over a threshold worn smooth by eons of footprints that no longer belonged to any fixed timeline, her boots sinking half an inch into dust flecked with crushed star mica. 
She had arrived at the ancient temple library lost in time, a structure folded into the crease between cosmic expiration and rebirth where no clock had ever ticked straight, across a universe’s hiccup that had swallowed every empire, every poem, every unspoken regret in the span of a single held breath. 
Her motivation, carved into the bone of her wrist in a script that shifted when she looked directly at it, was to unravel cosmic illusions that had tangled sentient beings into loops of fear and greed for as long as stars had burned. 
The air hung in a resounding hush, thick enough to hold the echo of every unuttered truth, so quiet she could hear the slow drift of a loose page turning three stacks over without any breeze to push it. 
Beneath a shelf carved with interlocking spirals that curled outward and inward in equal measure, set at the library’s quiet core, she found the only object that did not blur when she tilted her head: a tattered softcover dictionary, its spine held together with dried reed glue and strands of her own childhood hair, a memento she had lost when she was seven years old on a planet that had ceased to exist two universal cycles prior. 
Its definitions were wanderingly exact, each entry tracing a word’s meaning across every timeline it had ever touched, never settling on a single fixed interpretation but never straying so far from the core of a concept that it lost its shape. 
She knew immediately this was the text required for spiral arbitration, the quiet, unending process of mediating between every split version of every soul, every fractured timeline, every forgotten truth that had been cast aside to make the illusion of linear history feel solid. 
She did not sit cross-legged on the dust to read, for the work of spiral arbitration demanded the method of journeying inward while walking out, her feet tracing the spiral paths of the library’s stacked aisles as her mind sifted through the wanderingly exact definitions to pick apart the threads of illusion that had stitched the universe into its false, rigid shape. 
Each step carried her past a version of the life she might have lived: the farmer who never left her home planet, the revolutionary who burned empires to ash, the child who still cried for a lost dictionary under a foreign sky, none of them separate, none of them more real than the next. 
When she stepped back onto the arrival threshold, the dictionary fell open to a single entry that glowed soft gold: "separation," it read, the definition winding through every lie that had told beings they were apart from each other, apart from the stars, apart from the parts of themselves they had disowned. 
She did not need to speak a spell or shatter a relic to unify the frayed edges of the cosmos, for the illusion of separation dissolved the moment she stopped fighting the truth of her own multiplicity, the truth that every version of every being was a single word written in the wanderingly exact script of the universe. 
The resounding hush of the library softened into a hum, the dust motes arranging themselves into new constellations as the universe exhaled, its hiccup fading into the quiet thrum of a reality stitched together not by rigid lines, but by the spiraling connections she had named in her arbitration. 
She tucked the softcover dictionary into the inner pocket of her coat, where it sat warm against her chest, a permanent anchor for the truth that the work of unravelling illusion never ended; it only spiraled gently outward, one wanderingly exact step at a time.
