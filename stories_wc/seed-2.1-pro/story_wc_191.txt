Elara, the last formally trained lore keeper of the fallen Solar Commonwealth, cut through tangles of bioluminescent fern at the edge of the old growth reserve, her wisely adventurous spirit tempered by a decade of cross-referencing corrupted data slates and elder oral accounts rather than reckless, unplanned leaps into myth. 
She had trekked 120 kilometers on foot for one singular purpose: to find the stairway of ascension that led to the unclaimed throne, the seat of interstellar governance left empty for 276 years after the last sovereign died without an heir, leaving scattered colonies to fend for themselves through generations of scarcity and conflict. 
She knew from fragmented archive records that the entrance lay past a kaleidoscopic waterfall, its cascading waters shot through with prismatic mineral deposits that shifted hue like a half-broken holo-display, and that she would have until sunrise to locate the stairway before the angled dawn light scrambled the access lock that hid the path from casual travelers. 
Tucking the chipped ruby flake -- a shard she had recovered from the ruins of the old royal palace, passed down through her family line for three generations -- into the groove of her utility belt, she took a steadying breath and chose to sally straight through the thundering curtain of water, rather than wasting precious minutes scouting for a shallower crossing. 
The shock of cold water stole her breath for three long seconds before she stumbled out the other side, landing softly on mossy turf dotted with glowing gold fruit, in the fabled hidden orchard behind the fall that the archives had described only in half-remembered verse. 
Rows of gnarled, genetically modified apple trees, bred during the Commonwealth's golden age to thrive in low light and carry a full day's nutrition in a single bite, stretched toward a domed stone pavilion at the orchard's center, its plaster walls slathered in decades of flaking, muted grey paint applied by isolated homesteaders who had stumbled on the spot during the long collapse and sought to erase all traces of the old regime. 
Remembering an archive line about "truth under layers of fear," Elara knelt at the pavilion's base and worked methodically through the peel of paint layers revealing hidden murals, picking at curled edges with the tip of her utility knife until vibrant, pigment-bright scenes unfurled under the dim, refracted light from the fall. 
The murals showed the last sovereign, not as the iron-fisted ruler history books had painted her, but as a young woman leaving the ruby scepter of office broken, scattering its shards across the colonies so no single tyrant could ever claim power again, marking the true location of the unclaimed throne not as a seat of domination, but as a threshold of collective care. 
When she reached the final section of mural, she saw a small, ruby-shaped indentation carved into the stone at the base of the pavilion, positioned exactly where the mural showed the first step of the fabled stairway, which would only activate when one of the scattered shards was pressed into its slot by someone who sought connection rather than control. 
Elara pulled the ruby flake from her belt, her fingers trembling not with greed for a crown, but with the quiet hope that she could help stitch the fractured colonies back together, and pressed the shard firmly into the indentation as the first pale streaks of pink began to bleed over the distant ridgeline, minutes before the sunrise lock would have sealed the path for another century. 
A soft, warm hum rose from the stone as the stairway of ascension materialized before her, its steps made of the same prismatic mineral as the waterfall, glowing with the same soft gold light as the orchard fruit, no gilded throne waiting at its top but a shared communications array built to reconnect every scattered colony to a network of mutual aid. 
She stepped onto the first step, tasting the crisp, sweet scent of the genetically modified apples on the air and smiling at the quiet realization that the "unclaimed throne" was never meant for a single ruler -- it was a birthright waiting for all the scattered peoples of the Commonwealth to claim together, the future tangled up perfectly with the best parts of the world they had lost.
