Over the hush of winter-cracked fields, where frost split clods of turned soil into shards that glinted like crushed bone, a faint, cold glow seeped through the warped glass panes of the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night. 
Its caretaker, Kael, had knelt for an hour brushing rime from the gnarled boughs of memory-quince, his fingers numb as he reached for a glint half-buried in sphagnum moss at the base of the oldest tree, never noticing the faint shift of ash-gray tulle at the edge of his peripheral vision. 
The glint was a carved stone bead, pocked with tiny alchemical runes that warmed the second his frost-chapped skin brushed it, thrumming with the quiet, persistent pull of resonant awakenings, the slow, thrumming unspooling of memories not anchored to his 24 years of life tending the orchard. 
He did not see the ashen-veil ghost bride linger just out of direct sight, for she had chosen to ghost through the walled space not as a full, towering specter, but through ephemeral cameo appearances: a flash of embroidered red wedding slipper on the flagstone path, the faint scent of jasmine tangled in frost, a cold brush of knuckle against his wrist when he leaned too close to a bloom of sleep-lotus, whose pollen could drop a grown person into a century of unbroken, dreamless slumber. 
She was lovingly venomous, her very essence steeped in the wolfsbane tincture her cruel would-be groom had slipped into her wedding wine three lifetimes prior, a toxin she now wove into tiny, deliberate nudges that stung just enough to jolt Kael away from mortal harm, never enough to leave so much as a faint pink mark on his skin. 
Her purpose had not wavered across the long stretch of starless dark that clung to the greenhouse glass: to repay a debt across lifetimes, for the young alchemist Kael had been in that first life had given her his only dose of wolfsbane antidote when she stumbled, gasping, through his door, trading his own chance to survive the groom's raiding party for a few extra minutes of her ragged breath. 
The carved stone bead was the last thing he had been whittling from river soapstone for her in the moments before the raiders broke down the oak door, strung on a tanned leather cord he'd meant to tie around her wrist as a promise that the orchard would always be a shelter, no matter what hunted her. 
As Kael curled his fingers tight around the bead, the resonant awakenings bloomed sharper, not as disorienting, jagged flashes, but as soft, certain knowledge: he knew the gentle slope of the orchard's winding paths, the exact ripening time of the sun-plums that grew only under heated glass, the way jasmine smelled when tangled in the folds of a bride's ashen veil. 
When he leaned too far over the raised sleep-lotus bed, drawn by the faint, tugging memory of a dark-eyed face he could almost place, he felt a sharp, tiny sting on his cheek, cold as rime and sharp as wolfsbane, that lovingly venomous caress that pulled him back a full step before he could inhale a lungful of the numbing, sleep-inducing pollen. 
For a split, breath-held second, he saw her not as a wisp or a peripheral flash, but as she had been that terrible, tender day: veil the color of cold wood ash, hands outstretched, a smile held equal parts abiding sorrow and bright, unshakable joy, the very core of that mournful elation that had clung to the greenhouse walls for three hundred years. 
He did not lunge to reach for her, for he understood in that quiet flood of returned memory that she could not stay, that her long vigil of ephemeral visits had been the only tether holding her to the mortal world long enough to keep him safe, to let him claim the quiet, fruitful life he had lost to raiders' blades centuries before. 
As the first pale streak of dawn crept over the winter-cracked fields, piercing the endless night that had wrapped the greenhouse for centuries, she faded entirely, leaving only the warm weight of the carved stone bead in his palm and the faint, sweet scent of jasmine, her cross-lifetime debt repaid, his resonant awakening complete, the mournful elation of their long-delayed reunion softening the rime on the glass.
