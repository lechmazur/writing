The floating rock tea ceremony platform bobbed half a thousand feet above the mist-choked pine valleys, its sanded cedar edges strung with dried juniper that swayed without wind in the thick, electric air of a breath between storms. 
Perched cross-legged on a woven rush mat at its center sat Elowen, a budding rune scholar whose left forearm bore the silvery, ridged scar of a rune-forging misfire that had seared through her apprentice robes two winters prior. 
For months, the scar had ached whenever she leaned over her rune tablets, and she had grown familiar with the untaught, innate attribute that marked her quiet connection to rune magic: she crystallizes when stressed, the thin, salt-kissed moisture that beaded along the scar’s raised ridges hardening into flecks of pale, frosty quartz that caught the light like scattered star shards. 
She had climbed the winding cliff path to the platform at dawn not to practice the rigid, formal rune forms her master drilled into her, but to pursue a quieter, more personal goal: to transform scars into stories instead of letting them remain markers of failure that locked her throat tight every time she picked up an etching tool. 
The entire world seemed to hang during a held breath in that lull, no bird call, no rustle of pine, no distant rumble of thunder to break the saturated hush that wrapped around the platform like a well-loved wool shawl. 
Resting on the low cedar tea table before her, beside a steeping cup of pine needle tea, sat her most precious childhood possession: a crow quill trimmed tip, plucked from the wing of a dead crow she had found at the edge of her grandmother’s orchard when she was twelve, which she had long believed could draw hidden truths out of even the most stubborn surfaces. 
She had dismissed that old belief as childish nonsense when she entered the rune academy, trading folk charms for precise calipers and formal etching needles, but today she was attempting her work by revisiting youthful superstitions, setting aside her academic training to let old wisdom guide her hand. 
She picked up the quill, its trimmed tip sharp enough to catch the faint, silver light seeping through the storm clouds, and pressed it gently to the ridged edge of her scar, letting out a slow, shaking exhale as she began to shed the tight, guarded fear that had clamped around her chest since the misfire. 
The quartz flecks that had crusted along her scar for months loosened as her shoulders dropped, each tiny crystalline grain catching on the crow quill’s barbs instead of digging into her skin, no longer a painful physical echo of her mistake. 
She dipped the quill not in academy-mixed iron gall ink, but in the clear, cool tea steeping at her elbow, and drew thin, looping lines across a square of handmade rice paper, tracing not the rigid geometric runes of her textbooks but the messy, tender sequence of the day she was hurt: rushing to finish a healing rune for her grandmother’s arthritic knees, knocking over a vial of charged resin, flinching as the flame licked her forearm, her master’s hand on her shoulder as he wrapped the wound. 
The quartz flecks caught in the quill’s barbs left a faint, shimmering trail across the paper, turning each wobbly line into something luminous, no longer a record of fumbling failure but a testament to the care that had driven her to rush in the first place. 
When the last line curved into the shape of her grandmother’s smile, she realized she had not tensed up once while drawing, and the last of the hard, sharp crystal flecks had shed from her scar to settle into the paper’s fibers, leaving the skin soft and smooth beneath her fingertips for the first time in months. 
A single, fat raindrop splattered on the edge of the paper a heartbeat later, breaking the long held breath of the lull as the second storm rolled over the peaks, and Elowen smiled, lifting her tea cup to the sky as the first rumble of thunder rolled soft and distant across the valleys, her scar no longer a weight but a story she was ready to tell.
