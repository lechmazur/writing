Maren, the reluctant caretaker of a cursed orchard wedged into the cliffside labyrinth’s upper switchbacks, scraped mud from her scuffed work boots as the sun dipped low enough to gild the brass wings of the mechanical birds patrolling the maze’s narrow paths. 
She had inherited the post three years prior when her grandmother vanished mid-prune, and her only unshakable motivation was to guide lost travelers to safety before the night frost forms, when the orchard’s gnarled boughs exuded a sweet, paralyzing mist that trapped wanderers until spring thaw. 
The brass birds, their clockwork innards humming just loud enough to track through the coastal wind, had no interest in aiding that work; they pecked at loose stone and dropped jagged pebbles to startle wanderers off the cliff edge, their movements offhandedly exact as they mapped every twist of the path without a single stray, wasted motion. 
For months she had hunted for the tool her grandmother had promised would cut through the birds’ disruptive noise, until that afternoon when she’d knelt to yank a thorny bramble from the orchard’s border and unearthed a crumpled, water-stained forgotten blueprint tucked beneath a root, its lines detailing a set of path markers that could redirect even the most disoriented traveler to the lower, sheltered trailhead. 
The blueprint called for no nails, no twine, no carved wood, only the hard-shelled fruit of the orchard’s twisted trees: each small nut, when aligned just so, would interact with the faint magnetic hum of the patrolling birds to assemble a glowing, low-lying trail that worked by leaving impressions without touching, pressing faint, warm light into the stone underfoot even when the wind whipped hard enough to tear a paper map to shreds. 
She’d fumbled the first three nuts as she moved along the path, her chapped fingers slipping on the smooth shells, knocking one into a crack in the cliff face where it spun in place before locking into alignment, no grace in her hasty, wind-chilled work but a rough, unpolished warmth bleeding from each marker as the light pooled in dented, imperfect splotches across the rock. 
She could hear the distant, shaky cough of two travelers--likely the teen siblings she’d spotted at the maze entrance at midday, who’d ignored her shouted warning to stay on the lower trail--caught in a dead end where the orchard mist was already curling thin over the stone. 
The patrolling birds tilted their brass heads as the trail of light spread, but they did not attack; their programming mapped static features of the maze, and the glowing impressions were as unobtrusive to their sensors as moonlight, so they simply returned to their endless patrol, dropping pebbles that skittered right over the lit path without dimming a single glow. 
She watched from the shadow of a gnarled plum tree as the two teens stumbled toward the first patch of light, their scraped knuckles and tear-streaked faces softening as they realized the glow marked a clear route down, the path winding past sharp drops and tangled roots with the same unpolished, lopsided rhythm as her own fumbling work. 
The first crisp edge of frost was just starting to crust the top of the grass tufts at her feet when the last nut clicked into alignment at the trailhead, the final glow spreading to meet the wooden park sign at the maze’s lower edge where a park ranger waited with a thermos of hot cider. 
She did not wave or call out to the siblings as they reached safety, because the orchard’s curse meant anyone who spoke to her while trapped in the mist would be bound to take her place as caretaker, and she had no desire to pass that burden to anyone else before she found a way to break it for good. 
The glowing trail, lopsided and dotted with splotches where her shaking hands had set the nuts askew, hummed softly in the fading light, a thing of graceless beauty that asked no thanks, offered no fanfare, and simply led whoever was lost home before the cold could settle in to stay.
