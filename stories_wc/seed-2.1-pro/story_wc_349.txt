The glass orchard library floated upon a swirling sea of clouds, its fruit-heavy boughs of blown glass chiming soft as breath as the lull settled on the clean slate vendor’s very first opening day. 
He had carted his wares three thousand cloud-miles to this perch, driven by a quiet, unshakable motivation: to find the place where stories begin. 
His stock was mindfully scattered across the low oak table at his stall edge: smooth, unmarked slate tiles waiting to catch fresh memory, vials of condensed star-thread for mending frayed plotlines, and a single, frosted vessel holding bottled moonlight, its glow pulsing like a held heartbeat. 
The first rush of opening-day visitors--ink-stained archivists, sprites who dusted glass leaves with dandelion down, travelers tucking folded map fragments into their coat pockets--had drifted up to the upper canopy stacks, leaving only the soft chime of glass fruit and the low hum of cloud current to keep him company. 
He had heard the archivists murmur of glimmering parole, the unwritten rule that let half-born stories slip their glass bindings for one hour each opening day, if only someone could leave a space for them to land. 
Most seekers hunted for story beginnings in crowded pages, in bold opening lines inked in gold, but he had learned long ago that the only reliable path was by quantifying absence: mapping the silences between paragraphs, the pauses between spoken lines, the empty spaces where a tale had not yet chosen to unfurl. 
He uncorked the bottled moonlight, letting its cool silver spill over the nearest blank slate, and used a shard of fallen glass fruit to incise a narrow, precise channel down the tile’s center, a groove calibrated to hold exactly the weight of what was not there. 
The silver liquid settled, and for a long moment nothing moved; a glass plum tapped against its branch, a wisp of cloud curled through the library’s open arch, a half-remembered lullaby drifted from a locked book on a high shelf, each fragment clicking into place like shards of a mirror no one knew had been broken. 
He had expected a roaring font of golden words, a stage where characters stepped fully formed into the light, but what pooled in the incised groove was softer: a child’s gasp before they pointed out the first star of evening, the split second of quiet before a laugh, the empty palm waiting to be held, the hush between a question and its answer. 
The half-born stories out on glimmering parole drifted to the table then, thin as spider silk, each curling into the empty spaces he had mapped, no longer trapped between glass pages because someone had stopped looking only for what was already written. 
He had carried his clean slates across hundreds of cloud currents, half-convinced he would spend a lifetime chasing a point just over the horizon, but the answer had been waiting in the scattered gaps, the quiet absences no one else bothered to count. 
A small sprite darted to the table first, her wings dusted with glass pollen, and pointed at the groove where the silver moonlight held the glimmer of unstarted tales, her mouth forming a silent o of wonder. 
He did not launch into a grand speech about his long journey, or lay out a fancy sign marking his discovery; he simply pushed a clean slate across the table, and poured a thin trickle of moonlight into its center, so she could incise her own channels, map her own absences, meet the stories waiting to be born. 
The lull was ending now; he could hear the archivists’ footsteps on the glass staircases, the rustle of turning pages, the soft murmur of voices building as more visitors filtered through the orchard arches. 
He had not found a single, fixed spot where all stories began, because there was no such place; every beginning was a glimmer, a gap, a quiet invitation held in a scattered array of small, waiting spaces, if only someone took the time to notice what was missing. 
As the sprite leaned in, her tiny fingers tracing the edge of the slate, he smiled, knowing his very first day of business had already given him exactly what he had traveled so far to find.
