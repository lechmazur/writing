Lira, a grim optimist who had logged uncountable turns climbing the infinite staircase, kept a pair of dice--one chipped at the corner marking its three face, its mate worn smooth from endless handling--tucked in the inner pocket of her scuffed canvas coat. 
The structure hummed with the quiet, unceasing thrum of perpetual motion, its sandstone steps warmed by a fixed, unblinking glow that never shifted to dusk or dawn, stretching up and down without rail, landing, or endpoint to mark progress or defeat. 
This was the texture of boundless confinement: infinite room to climb, to stretch, to scrawl notes on step risers with a nub of charcoal, but no wall to push against, no door to open, no final step to signal arrival. 
Balancedly daring by nature, Lira never risked a blind leap off the stair edge into the glowing void--she had seen too many climbers do that, vanishing without a sound chasing shortcuts to a finish that did not exist--but she also never clung to the imaginary center line other travelers wore into the stone with their timid, identical steps. 
Her quest was not for escape, or for the fabled end of the stair, but to capture the texture of a missed opportunity: the split second, tucked somewhere in the stair's endless loop, where she had frozen mid-roll of those very dice, let the chipped one clatter to a sidewalk far removed from this place, and turned away from the quiet, unnameable life she had ached to build. 
She knew, with the grim acceptance that anchored her persistent optimism, that she could never rewrite that choice, never step back into that sidewalk moment and take the risk fear had stolen from her; she only wanted to hold it for a breath, to feel the rough concrete under her palms, the weight of the dice in her hand, the warm hush of the voice that had been waiting for her answer. 
For uncounted cycles, she worked through mathematical proofs, scrawling calculus, topology, and non-Euclidean geometry across every step she passed, mapping the subtle shifts in the stair's hum, the tiny, almost imperceptible variations in step height that marked the weak points in its otherwise unbroken perpetual motion. 
Other climbers called her work foolish, asking why she wasted charcoal and energy chasing a memory when there was infinite upward progress to make, but Lira only smiled, because she knew infinite progress without anchor was just another cage. 
On the turn when her calculations aligned, when the hum of the stair rose to a thin, resonant whine, she pulled the dice from her pocket, set the chipped one exactly on the coordinate her proofs had identified, and pressed down with the steady, unshaking weight of someone who had already accepted every possible outcome. 
The rupture came soft, not as a thunderous crack or a collapsing void, but as a thin, warm split in the air above the dice, through which she could smell roasted chestnuts, hear the distant trill of a street musician, feel the faint, cool brush of a hand that had once reached for hers across a rickety cafe table. 
She did not reach through to pull that lost moment to her, or to step through and rewrite the years that had unfolded after she turned away; she only leaned close, letting the texture of that missed chance wrap around her--the rough nap of the wool coat she had worn that day, the sticky residue of lollipop on the sidewalk, the shaky catch in her own breath as she had almost spoken the words that would have changed everything. 
When the rupture sealed shut a few breaths later, leaving only the faint, warm scent of chestnuts clinging to her coat collar, she picked up the chipped dice, tucked both pieces back in her pocket, and turned to keep climbing, no longer chasing an illusory finish line but carrying the soft, tangible weight of the moment she had once let slip, turning the hum of perpetual motion from a prison song into a quiet, steady companion.
