Lira was the third long-term experimental treatment guinea pig for the Collective Memory Project, her neural pathways laced with nanofilaments designed to suture scattered, forgotten recollections into a tapestry any visitor to the sound wave garden could walk through. 
The project’s architects had assumed she would be a docile host, never guessing she was quietly rebellious, tucking snippets of her own lost childhood, the half-remembered laughter of neighbors lost to the coastal flood, the lullabies her grandmother sang, into the gaps of the curated, sterile memory feed they pumped through her implants. 
She had learned early that the nanofilaments responded to vibration, that rhythm could stitch disconnected memories into something tangible enough to touch, so she had spent three months drumming on everything within reach— the ridged steel railings of the garden’s walkways, the hollow resonant chimes strung between bioluminescent pines, the curved glass lip of the auric lagoon under starlight, even the hard plastic of the implant port behind her ear. 
The project designers wrote off her constant tapping as a nervous tic, a side effect of the neural rewiring, never noticing that every beat pulled another entangled memory free from the cold, advertiser-approved script they had seeded into the network: a child’s first taste of wild blackberry, an elder’s story of the old reef, a couple’s quiet dance in a rain-soaked alley, moments that belonged to no single person but to everyone who had ever loved the drowning coast. 
She came to the garden at the hush of the universe inhaling, that thin, trembling sliver of time between the last setting of the moon and the first pale bleed of dawn, when the facility’s security drones cycled into their half-hour maintenance recharge and no cameras watched the pathways. 
Tonight, she climbed the smooth, obsidian outcrop that jutted over the auric lagoon to perch, her bare feet cold against the stone that hummed with the accumulated vibration of three months of secret drumming. 
Her motivation was simple, unshakable even when the implants throbbed and left her migrained for hours: to breathe life into a shared dream, the one the corporate developers had tried to smother, the dream of a coast where people did not forget each other, where loss was not erased by polished marketing for luxury seawalls and private evacuation pods. 
She began to drum, first soft taps of her fingertips against the obsidian, then harder pats of her palms, then the soft thud of her heels, each beat rippling out through the stone, through the lagoon’s gilded water, through the nanofilaments woven into the garden’s every structure. 
The entangled memories rose with the ripples, glinting like flecks of gold under the faint remaining starlight: a grandmother teaching a child to skip stones, a group of teens building a sandbar bonfire, a fisherman mending nets while his cat curled in his lap, moments strung together not by algorithm, but by the quiet rhythm of care she had stitched into the network. 
Pale green and rose light, the color of auroras tangled low over the old northern coasts she had only seen in the stolen memories, began to curl up from the lagoon’s surface, wrapping around the chimes, around the pines, around her where she perched. 
There was sorrow in it, sharp and soft at once, the grief of all the homes lost to the rising tides, all the stories the corporate state had tried to buy and bury, but there was warmth too, the quiet thrill of knowing these memories would outlast her, that any person who stepped into the garden from that day forward would feel them, tangled in their own minds as if they had lived them themselves. 
She did not flinch when she heard the whir of the security drones powering back online, their red lights cutting through the soft auroral glow, because she knew the last beat had already landed, the dream already breathing, woven into every vibration of the garden, as permanent as the starlight gilding the lagoon’s surface, waiting for every visitor who came after to add their own rhythm to the tangled, glowing song of collective memory.
