After words become unnecessary, when valley communities traded tangled, half-true sentences for shared gestures and the weight of a hand on a shoulder, the harmonious bell tuner began their slow climb to the whispering bluff above the clouds. 
They were seasonally mute, their vocal cords thickened and quiet through the long frost months that locked the high peaks in glass, so they had no use for the discarded words littering valley trails anyway. 
Slung across their back in a wool-padded cradle was a jagged iceberg fragment, calved from the ancient glacier that curled around the bluff's base, its core holding trapped air bubbles from a time before words ever existed to muddle what was real. 
Their motivation was simple, carved into the small wooden mallet they carried at their hip: to preserve the color of wonder, the soft, unnameable glow that had started leaching out of valley life once people stopped pausing to listen to what lay beyond speech. 
They did not build their work with cast bronze bells, as they had in lower elevations, but through kinetic sculpture, strung between the bluff's wind-scoured pines so every gust, every shift of temperature, every slow drift of cloud below would play a note no spoken word could distort. 
They strung thin titanium wires between pine trunks, fastening small shards of the iceberg fragment at staggered intervals, weighting some with smooth river stone, balancing others on delicate copper pivots that spun with the lightest breeze. 
As the sun climbed high enough to gild the top of the cloud sea, the largest shard of ice began to glisten, catching refracted light that spilled across the bluff in shifting streaks of pale sapphire, rose, and the faint, unplaceable green of the first spring shoots pushing through snow. 
The first wind off the cloud sea stirred the sculpture, and notes spilled down the bluff in cascading, unhurried waves, the symphonies of truth that no lie, no half-meant compliment, no fear-driven exaggeration could ever replicate. 
The tuner pressed a palm to the nearest pine trunk, feeling the low thrum of the wires travel up their arm and settle in their chest, a quiet, thrumming energy that defined the high alpine: no fanfare, no shouted declaration, just the steady, alive pulse of something made to outlast frost and fading memory. 
Down in the valleys, people paused mid-gesture, mid-chore, mid-step as the first notes curled over the cloud line, soft enough that they might have mistaken them for wind in their own eaves if not for the strange, clear feeling that settled over them when the tones landed. 
A child pointed up toward the bluff, where the glistening ice shards caught the sun so bright it looked like a scatter of fallen stars hanging between the pines, and for a second, everyone remembered the wonder they had thought was lost when words stopped working: the warmth of a shared meal, the relief of a burden lifted, the quiet joy of standing next to someone you love without needing to say a thing. 
The tuner settled on a flat, lichen-crusted stone at the bluff's edge, not needing to speak to know the sculpture was working, because they could see the faint washes of refracted color painting the top of the cloud sea, shifting in time with each clear, ringing note. 
They knew the ice would slowly melt as the season turned, feeding the mountain streams that ran down to the valleys, but the resonance of the notes would linger, tangled in pine boughs and cloud mist, waiting to stir that old, bright wonder every time the wind blew just right. 
A gust spun the smallest ice shard on its copper pivot, sending a high, clear note arcing out over the clouds, and the tuner smiled, their breath fogging in the crisp air as they leaned back to let the vibration hum through their bones. 
There were no speeches to give, no crowds to cheer, no grand plaques to mark what they had built, because truth and wonder did not need labels or fanfare to take root; they only needed a clear, uncluttered space to ring, carried on wind that knew no words. 
They stayed there until the sun dipped low, painting the cloud sea in tangerine and lavender, listening as their sculpture sang the quiet, unshakable truths no language had ever been able to hold, the glint of ice holding fast to the color of wonder for as long as the frost held the bluff.
