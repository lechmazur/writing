In the murmur of half-remembered dreams, the sensory map maker knelt on the scuffed steel grating of an abandoned spaceport repair bay, where cracked fuel lines wept iridescent residue and the faint echo of launch countdowns thrummed in the rust-furred bulkheads. 
She had returned to this spot every seven years for two decades, driven by a single unshakable motivation: to discover the weight of a withheld truth, the quiet lie her family had told about the older sister who vanished mid-repair on a cargo frigate the day the port was decommissioned. 
Her craft as a sensory map maker relied on tracing the faint, overlapping imprints of sound, touch, and scent that bled across dimensional boundaries, a skill tied to a cyclically spontaneous ability that flared without warning on rigid seven-year cycles, pulling her awareness adrift, weaving between worlds even when she fought to stay grounded. 
On the floor half-buried under a collapsed workbench, her fingers closed around a jagged green glass shard, the same shard she had watched her sister tuck into the breast pocket of her repair coverall the morning she disappeared, its edge still sharp enough to catch the faint, otherworldly glow seeping through a hairline crack in reality a foot from her boots. 
She had spent years mapping the dimensional threads that twisted through the repair bay, learning to navigate not by star charts or coordinate grids but by the echo of her sister's laugh, the sharp smell of her favorite citrus welding flux, tracing paths through parallel universes where the port never closed, where the fuel line explosion that erased her never happened, where the truth of that day was not buried under redacted official reports and tight-lipped family silence. 
As the familiar tingle of the coming flare crept up her wrists, she expected the crack in reality to yawn wide and pull her to the universe where her sister waited, alive and whole at 22, holding a matching shard of the same green soda bottle they had snuck and shared on the roof of the port control tower for her 16th birthday. 
Instead, the green glass shard warmed against her palm, pulsing in time with her quickening heartbeat to redirect the surge of dimensional energy, turning the expected path on its axis so she was not staring at the frozen, 22-year-old ghost of her memory, but at a woman in a grease-streaked repair coverall with the same sharp jaw and hazel eyes flecked with green, holding a matching shard, her own hands crisscrossed with decades of tiny scars from mapping the same dimensional crossroads. 
The withheld truth landed soft as floating launch pad dust, not sharp as the explosion her family had described: her sister had not died, nor been trapped against her will, but had stepped through the first rift the map maker had accidentally opened as a scared 10-year-old, choosing to guard the fragile dimensional crossroads rather than seal it shut and erase her little sister's burgeoning, untested gift. 
For 20 years, her sister had been the one gently nudging the cyclically spontaneous flares to guide her back to the repair bay, leaving faint sensory breadcrumbs of citrus flux and distant radio static, waiting until she was skilled enough to hold a rift open without being torn apart by the strain of weaving between unmoored, overlapping timelines. 
The weight of the truth was not the crushing, lifelong guilt she had carried for decades, but a warm, unspooling relief: the sister she had grieved had never been lost, only working to build a stable path they could walk together, mapping the fragile thresholds between worlds so no other family would have to lie to protect the people they loved. 
She stepped across the thin, glowing threshold of the rift, the jagged edge of her green glass shard fitting perfectly against the matching piece in her sister's outstretched hand to form a tiny, glowing compass that hummed with the shared rhythm of their linked ability, no longer a random, unguided force but a deliberate tool they could wield side by side. 
As the repair bay's rusted bulkheads faded behind her, the hum of half-remembered dreams softened into a clear, bright purpose, and she knew the years of searching had finally led her exactly where she was meant to be.
