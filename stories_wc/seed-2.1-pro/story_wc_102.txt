The camera obscura artist leaned against the weathered stone rail of the twilight viaduct in bloom, fingers still curled around the smooth glass vial strung at her throat that held Kwan Yin's Tear, in the quiet breath after her final unspooling past life memory surfaced. 
She was spiritually curious by nature, a pilgrim of sparks who had walked 300 miles tracing faint, glimmering trails of half-felt affection to solve the quiet question that had gnawed at her across three lifetimes: where unused love is stored. 
Pale wisteria tendrils draped the viaduct's arched stone supports, their sweet scent tangled in thin silver fog that softened the edges of the valley below, wrapping her in the foggy lucidity that had clung to her since she first began chasing those faint, glinting sparks of unoffered care. 
The memory that had just broken through showed her, in a life 400 years prior, standing on this same viaduct, tucking a letter of apology she'd never sent to the weaver she'd loved into a crack in the stone, too afraid of rejection to speak the words that had burned in her chest until her dying day. 
For years, she had hauled her hand-built portable camera obscura from ridge to valley, convinced the stored love would reveal itself as a projected image if she could only find the right vantage point, but every view had only offered soft, out-of-focus landscapes empty of the glow she sought. 
The vial at her throat held Kwan Yin's Tear, a milky opalescent stone given to her by a mountain temple nun who told her it did not grant wishes, but only showed what was already there, when the seeker's breath slowed enough to stop chasing. 
She had been so focused on scanning the horizon for sparks that she had not noticed her breath coming in short, desperate gasps for months, so she closed her eyes and let her exhales lengthen, letting each out slow enough to curl through the fog like wisteria vines, letting the rhythm of her breathing shift until her shoulders dropped and the tight knot in her chest loosened. 
As her breathing steadied into a soft, even rhythm, the world sharpened through the fog, and the reveal unfolded without fanfare: tiny, gold sparks swarmed every crack in the viaduct stone, tucked between wisteria roots and primrose petals, glowing softly where every unspoken word, every unsent letter, every held-back caress had settled, not lost, just resting, waiting to be noticed. 
She did not need to unpack her camera obscura to capture the sight, for the Kwan Yin's Tear at her throat warmed against her skin, and she felt the old, sharp ache of three lifetimes of regret begin to heal as she realized unused love was never locked away in some distant, unreachable vault, but stored right in the soft, blooming edges of the world, visible the moment she stopped running long enough to breathe with it. 
One tiny spark drifted from a crack where she had tucked that unsent letter centuries before, landing light as a moth on the back of her outstretched wrist, and it did not burn, but hummed with the exact tenderness she had thought was lost forever when she turned away from the weaver all those years ago. 
She thought of the camera obscura installations she had spent years building in small mountain towns, how she had always chased sharp, perfect views of distant peaks, never noticing the soft, glowing light tucked in the cracked, blooming spaces closest to her. 
She sat on the stone ledge, letting twilight deepen to indigo around her, and did not rush to move on, for her pilgrimage of sparks had not ended at a grand, luminous destination, but at the quiet, obvious truth she had been too busy searching to see: love is never wasted, even when it is never spoken, never offered, never received. 
The Kwan Yin's Tear glowed soft as starlight against her chest, and she knew the long work to heal was complete when she breathed in the scent of wisteria and felt no pull to chase anything at all, only to sit and watch the sparks dance among the blooms, holding the quiet weight of all that unoffered love like something gentle, something precious, something finally home.
