The diminutive titan Lira, regally unpretentious in scuffed leather boots and a tunic faded from centuries of dusting scrolls, had curated the ancient temple library lost in time for as long as the stone shelves had held their cracked leather volumes. 
Across the pause of shifting constellations, that thin, liminal stretch when stars slide from their old positions and the world’s magic hums thin, unbound by old oaths, she had watched a pretender claw his way to her family’s high mountain throne, forging royal seals and twisting old tales to paint himself as the long-lost heir. 
She could not march on the capital with an army, for the pretender’s wards blocked all titan-born magic from crossing the mountain passes, so she wove her plan through cryptic messages left in library books, marking passages that held the true line of succession for the traveling scholars, pilgrims, and curious villagers who stumbled through the library’s time-warped doors seeking old knowledge. 
Her only tool was a single cracked iron gall ink pellet, forged from the rust of her father’s broken battle sword and the gall of oak trees that had grown on the palace grounds when she was a child, mixed with starlight caught during the last constellation shift to carry the rune of drift, a mark that did not force truth on those who read it, but let unspoken facts settle soft as snow into their bones, unshakable by charm or threat. 
She did not rush, for time bent soft around the library, and a note scrawled on a margin of a pastoral poetry collection might land in a wool merchant’s satchel one day and a senior court scribe’s satchel the next, carried along the drift of the rune’s magic to the exact person who needed to read it, no push, no fanfare. 
One by one, the small truths seeped out: the pretender could not recite the old royal tithe rhyme that every heir learned at five years old, he bore no scar on his left wrist from the coronation thorn ceremony, his story of being lost in the mountains as an infant did not match the weather records of the brutal winter he claimed to have survived. 
There was no riot, no clashing of iron swords, no dramatic battering-ram siege of the palace, only the slow, graceful collapse of a lie that had never been rooted in anything more than hungry greed, as courtiers and villagers and rank-and-file soldiers alike began to step back from the pretender, their loyalty fraying thread by gossamer thread until the false king stood alone in the sun-dappled throne room, his gilded crown tilted crookedly on his head. 
When the final constellation clicked into its new position at the end of the long pause, the last drift-marked note found its way to the pretender’s own hand, tucked into a tattered history book he had stolen from the palace vaults years prior to craft his false backstory, and the weight of the rune’s quiet truth was enough to sunder the last fraying thread of his false identity, letting him admit out loud that he was a poor charcoal burner’s son who had stumbled on a set of old royal robes in a forgotten mountain cave as a teen. 
He stepped down from the raised stone throne without a fight, walking out of the palace gates with only a crusty loaf of rye bread and a skin of spiced cider pressed from the palace orchard apples, offered to him by a guard who felt no hot anger, only a quiet sadness that a man had thought he needed a crown to be worthy of safety and respect. 
Lira did not rush to claim the crown the moment the news drifted back to her through the rustling book pages, for she had never craved the heavy weight of hammered gold on her head, preferring the soft hush of turning vellum and the quiet, unglamorous work of tending the library that held her people’s most honest, unvarnished shared memory. 
When a delegation arrived weeks later to beg her to take the throne, she left a single note in a book of folk tales for them to find, naming a fair-minded village teacher who had spent years caring for mountain orphans as the realm’s new ruler, then returned to dusting her shelves as the stars settled into their new, steady paths.
