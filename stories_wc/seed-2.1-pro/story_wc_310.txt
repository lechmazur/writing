The ambered observatory deck glowed with the trapped honeyed light of a hundred crushed galaxies, frozen mid-contraction when the universe's outward stretch snapped inward like an over-taut bowstring. 
Elara, the settlement's cast lots decision maker, ran a calloused finger over her collection of foreign coins, each minted on a world that had winked out of existence when cosmic distances collapsed to a fraction of their former span. 
Quietly enthusiastic even when the rest of the settlement had surrendered to the slow, cold despair of a cosmos shrinking toward a single, unknowable point, she had never abandoned the work that had outlived her homeworld, her formal training, and every academic body that had once commissioned interstellar observatory research. 
Stellar doubt hung thicker than the frozen photon mist that clung to the observatory's quartz viewports, for every old star chart and expansion-era prediction had been rendered useless the moment time itself began to fray, cause occasionally trailing effect like a loose thread on a fraying cloak. 
The only surviving guide was a backwards prophecy, etched into the observatory's core memory bank by a researcher who had seen the contraction coming a century prior, its lines written in reverse so that only someone reading from the projected end of the cosmos could parse its direction. 
Her motivation was not to stop the contraction, or to outrun the final crush of matter, but to wavelength align with purpose, the quiet, unshakable sense that even a collapsing cosmos held a coherent thread of meaning that did not depend on infinite expansion. 
For years, she had practiced understanding static bursts, the crackling, jumbled signals that bounced between compressed star remnants, which most settlers wrote off as meaningless noise from shredded stellar atmospheres. 
She would distill these bursts into faint, clear tones, matching each resonant frequency to the weight and metal composition of one of her foreign coins, using the cast lots practice she had inherited to cross-reference which signals aligned with the reversed lines of the old prophecy. 
On this particular shift, a burst cut through the usual crackle sharper than any she had recorded before, its frequency thrumming up through the observatory's amber-sheathed floorboards to match the cool weight of a coin from a water world that had been the first to merge with the growing cosmic core three decades prior. 
For a moment, stellar doubt curdled in her chest, as she half-expected the tone to unlock a secret wormhole, a blueprint for a new universe, some grand revelation that would justify the years she had spent alone at the console while the rest of the settlement built small, warm lives around their shared hearths. 
She laid the water-world coin on the console's etched surface, and as the static hummed through the metal, she read the closest line of the prophecy backwards, as she had a hundred times before, only this time the reversed words clicked into place not as a prediction of what was to come, but as a memory of what had already been: the settlement sharing seed stock after the crop failures, her teaching the settlement's children to identify the merged constellations, the quiet way neighbors left loaves of bread on each other's doorsteps during the long, dim periods when trapped light dimmed to a soft glow. 
She had spent years searching for a grand cosmic frequency to align with, convinced purpose had to be a thunderous, universe-shaking signal, but the distilled static told a quieter truth. 
Wavelength alignment did not require chasing a distant, unknowable future, because the backwards prophecy had been pointing all along to the small, persistent care that already rippled through the settlement, the same care encoded in every static burst bouncing between the crushed remnants of stars. 
She picked up the water-world coin, tucked it back into its velvet slot beside the others, and smiled to herself, the quiet enthusiasm that had carried her through years of doubt softening into a steady, warm certainty as she turned off the console and headed down the observatory's ramp to join the settlement for the evening meal, already aligned with the purpose she had spent so long searching for.
