Kaelin, a gravity defier who had spent three years outrunning the splinters of her own bad choices, stood barefoot on the glowing viaduct after rain, bioluminescent silver moss squelching softly between her toes as a single fat raindrop hung suspended half an inch above a cracked granite puddle, held fast in the thin, unspooling sliver of time between the drop and the ripple. 
This liminal pocket was periodically eternal, flaring into accessible existence only for the stretched microsecond before a falling raindrop broke the puddle’s surface, gone again before any ordinary observer could register its presence. 
She had come here carrying the ancient clay tablet with runes, pocked and worn from 12 prior failed attempts to pierce the veil of fractured timelines that had tangled around her since the day she first lifted off the ground and left pieces of her younger, braver, crueler selves scattered across every possible choice she never made. 
Her singular motivation was to conquer the labyrinth of self, the winding, shifting maze of those discarded timeline shards that had begun to yank her off mid-flight, twist her gravity into sudden, nauseating drops, and leave her waking screaming with memories of lives she had never lived. 
On prior visits, she had tried to map the labyrinth with fixed routes, scribbling turns on scrap paper and carving markers into the viaduct stone, only to find the paths warped beneath her feet the second she looked away, a trick of the fractured timelines that no static chart could ever outthink. 
This time, she would not force a path, but retrace the half-remembered echoes of her own discarded choices, letting the tablet’s runes light up one small step at a time through procedural generation, each turn pulled directly from the tangled weight of her own regrets and unspoken hopes rather than a pre-plotted route that the timelines could shift to evade. 
The first glow of the runes was the same soft silver as the viaduct moss, gilding the edge of the suspended raindrop so brightly it cast tiny prisms across the stone, but as she took her first step off the viaduct’s solid ledge, letting her gravity-defying gift hold her weight above the void where the labyrinth waited, the light began to dim, bleeding from silver to ash-gray to bruised, deep indigo as the first of her timeline shards came into view. 
She saw the 17-year-old version of herself who had abandoned her little sister to chase a life of airborne freedom, the 24-year-old who had turned down a quiet, loving partnership to keep flying solo, the 29-year-old who had run from a chance to apologize for a cruel, thoughtless comment that had ended a decade-long friendship, each shard glowing faintly like a dying ember as the tablet generated the next narrow, winding path to carry her closer to the labyrinth’s core. 
She did not fight the shards, as she had in past attempts, leaning instead into the weight of each old grief and missed chance, letting the procedural pull of the tablet weave those memories into the path beneath her feet rather than trying to outrun them, and with every step the darkening brightness around her softened, no longer a threatening shadow but a quiet, dim warmth that felt like coming home. 
When she reached the core, she found no monster to slay, no grand puzzle to solve, only the quiet realization that conquering the labyrinth of self did not mean erasing the fractured timelines or stitching her scattered shards back into a single, perfect version of herself, but accepting that every discarded choice was still part of the weight that let her defy gravity in the first place, pulling her not off course, but grounding her enough to fly steady. 
She opened her eyes back on the viaduct stone, the tablet’s runes gone dark and smooth as ordinary clay, the suspended raindrop finally close enough to the puddle that she could see its own tiny reflection in the curved surface, the once-blazing moss glow dimmed to a soft, ember-like warmth that did not strain her eyes. 
As the raindrop finally touched the puddle and the first ripple spread outward, closing the periodically eternal pocket behind her, she pushed off the stone, gravity falling away beneath her, and flew steadier than she had in three years, no longer running from the pieces of herself she had once left behind.
