Under the slow exhale of low tide, when the North Sea pulled back to bare jagged basalt outcrops strung with bladderwrack, the counter clockwise clock maker picked his way across slick rock to the mysterious lighthouse on a rocky island, its rusted lens hood creaking like a held sob. 
For forty-two years, he had crafted timepieces that spun against the accepted march of hours, every brass gear filed and calibrated by his neatly inventive hands, driven by a single, unshakable motivation: to step beyond fate's design, which had dictated a catastrophic, generation-scouring flood would crash over his cliffside village in three days’ time, as reliably as any clock’s forward tick. 
Wedged in the lighthouse’s rotting oak doorjamb, left by a lighthouse keeper who had vanished the day of the last great flood, was the artifact he had tracked for decades: an ancient yoyo, carved from whalebone, its string twisted from seal sinew, its etched surface a maze of interlocking grooves no scholar had ever been able to decipher. 
Half-buried in silt ten feet from the threshold, exposed only when the tide pulled far enough out to kiss the edge of the continental shelf, sat the hunger stone, its pocked face carved with the date of every recorded flood, the blank space below the last 1953 marker already dark with seeping salt, as if waiting to etch the coming disaster on its own. 
Local lore held the lighthouse held the power to reweave the flow of events, but every person who had tried to unlock it had been pulled out by the tide, their bones tangled in kelp before they could even place a hand on the lens mechanism. 
The clock maker knew better than to reach for the lens first, because he had spent three years cross-referencing village journals and keeper logs to understand the lock was not in the lighthouse at all, but in the yoyo, a puzzle built to turn time against itself. 
He sat on a flat, dry rock, the hunger stone glinting at his periphery, and began to work through reverse-engineering the puzzle of an ancient yoyo, his calloused fingers tracing every groove, testing how each carved ridge caught the sinew string when he spun the toy counter to its expected loop. 
For an hour, the only sounds were the gurgle of retreating water, the creak of the lighthouse, and the soft whir of the whalebone discs as he tested every permutation, his counter-clockwise trained intuition letting him spot the pattern no forward-thinking scholar had seen: the grooves mapped to the exact gear ratios of a clock running backward, calibrated to trigger the lighthouse’s long-dormant core. 
When he looped the string around the final, smallest groove and gave the yoyo a firm, counterclockwise flick, he felt a low thrum travel up his arm, and knew he was ready to initiate the sequence no villager had dared attempt in seven centuries. 
The lighthouse door swung open on its own, and he stepped inside, climbing the spiral stone stairs to the lens room, where the old Fresnel lens sat dark, coated in decades of salt and guano, at its center the core no one had ever been able to activate: a shard of the same hunger stone below, holding a latent radiance that had waited for a hand unshackled to fate’s forward march to wake it. 
He set the spinning yoyo in the notch cut into the stone shard, and for a heartbeat, the air went still, the distant crash of the waves pausing as if the whole world held its breath, waiting to see if he had broken the pattern or merely earned himself a quick, cold death in the rising swell. 
The latent radiance burst forth then, not searing gold, but the soft, silver glow of moonlight on reversed clock faces, streaming through the lens to paint the incoming storm tide in light that bent the current away from the village, carving a new path for the water to flow out to deep sea instead of scouring the cliffs. 
He leaned against the lighthouse wall, salt stinging his cheeks, the bitter knowledge that he would be labeled a crackpot by fellow villagers, that he could never prove he had diverted the flood, that the hunger stone would never bear the mark of the 2023 disaster warring with the sharp, singing thrill of knowing he had unspun fate’s fixed thread, one counter-clockwise turn at a time.
