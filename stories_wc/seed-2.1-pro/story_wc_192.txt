Three months after cutting off all contact, the tender shadow keeper climbed the splintered stone steps to the ashen charcoal sanctuary under starlight, the hem of her soot-streaked wool cloak brushing crumbled cinders that had not shifted in seven years. 
She was the sort who burned bridges carefully, never leaving a stray ember that might pull a grieving traveler back to a threshold that no longer held safe passage, never scorching the memories that made the crossing worth the pain. 
The sanctuary door loomed ahead, its oak grain turned to soft, powdery charcoal by decades of unwept grief, its doorknocker ring pitted with the faint indentations of every knuckle that had ever begged for entry before turning away for good. 
She had come because the fading threshold between the sanctuary’s hollow quiet and the world of gasping, overclung attachment had begun to fray, its edges softening until shadow seeped through to trap travelers half-in, half-out, unable to stay and unable to leave. 
Thin, wispy tendrils of that trapped, airless longing had started to encroach on the nearest village in the weeks prior, slipping through cracked windowpanes to wrap around the chests of people who had not spoken to an estranged friend or lost lover in years, squeezing until they forgot how to draw a full, unburdened breath. 
The shadow keeper did not carry spells or iron keys for this work, linking through scars instead, tracing the thin, silvery lines on her own palms that marked every time she had chosen to let go instead of clinging until both she and the other person choked on shared, stagnant air. 
Her motivation was never to punish those who clung too tight, only to teach the suffocating to breathe, to show them that air did not vanish when a door closed, that it only shifted to fill the space they had forgotten how to hold for themselves. 
She lifted her hand to the doorknocker ring, its cold metal catching the faint silver of starlight, and did not knock, because there was no one inside to answer, no resident keeper of the sanctuary, only the hollow echo of every goodbye that had ever been spoken too softly to land. 
She pressed one scarred palm flat to the charcoal door, and the thin lines on her skin lit with soft, warm gold, threading through the pitted metal of the knocker to stitch the fraying threshold closed without slamming it shut, without locking anyone out who might truly need to seek shelter there later. 
This was how she burned bridges carefully, after all: not with wildfire that consumed every trace of what had been, but with slow, intentional heat that sealed the cracks so longing could not leak out to choke the living, so no one would mistake a closed door for a prison. 
For a long moment, she stood there in the hush, the starlight casting no shadow of her own on the cinder-strewn steps, because shadow keepers carry no fixed shadow, only the collected weight of all the goodbyes they have helped other people carry gently. 
She had cut off all contact with her own most beloved person three months prior, not out of anger, but because she had watched both of them gasping for air in a relationship that had shrunk to a locked, airless room neither of them had the courage to leave first. 
The scar across her left palm, the one she had earned pressing her hand to that apartment door as she walked out, hummed against the sanctuary’s charcoal surface, and she felt the fading threshold settle, firm again, no longer leaking the suffocating fog of half-meant goodbyes and unspoken regret. 
Down in the village, a young woman who had spent three months replaying old voice messages instead of leaving her apartment drew a sudden, deep breath, her chest lightening as the fog retreated, and she reached for her jacket to walk out into the starlight instead of scrolling through old texts. 
The shadow keeper did not stay to watch, stepping back from the door as the doorknocker ring settled with a soft, final clink, because the work of mending thresholds did not require gratitude, only the quiet knowledge that empty space was not a void, but a place to breathe again. 
She turned and walked down the cinder steps, her cloak brushing the same undisturbed cinders, carrying the quiet weight of her own goodbye like a lit, gentle candle, neither rushing nor lingering, as the starlight spilled soft over the ashen sanctuary and the world beyond, where people were just learning to draw breath again.
