Three months after receiving the donor heart, the watchful dye master climbed the swaying rope ladder to the floating rock tea house, where wispy valley mist curled around chipped ceramic teacups and old storytellers traded tales that stuck to travelers’ coats like pine resin. 
He had made the journey by tracking sun trails, the thin, gilded slants of mid-morning light that curl through mountain passes to mark where unclaimed narratives hum just beyond the edge of ordinary vision, his core drive fixed tight in his chest since surgery: to learn why some people magnetize stories, drawing stray joys and griefs and quiet miracles to them the way his dye vats draw rich color from crushed bark and foraged berries. 
The tea house keeper, a woman with silver streaks braided through her black hair, pressed a cup of warm coca tea into his hands and nodded toward the corner booth, where a single iridescent blue morpho wing lay flat on a scrap of undyed cotton, its surface shifting from deep cobalt to shimmering sky blue as slanted sunlight caught its delicate scales. 
“That belonged to the man whose heart you carry,” she said softly, leaning against the booth’s rough cedar edge as cool mist seeped through the cracked window to brush the dye master’s sun-chapped cheek. 
The dye master leaned forward, his breath catching, because he had spent 20 years chasing the exact kind of iridescent caution the wing embodied: a sheen that never stayed fixed, that warned onlookers not to grasp too tight for the beauty that shifted the moment you tried to pin it down for permanent keeping. 
He knew that dynamic well as a craftsperson who mixed quietly explosive pigments—crushed jewel beetle shell, fermented indigo, mineral dust that flared bright when exposed to open air—that never settled into a single flat shade, no matter how carefully he measured ingredients or adjusted his vat temperatures. 
He reached out one calloused, dye-stained finger, not to touch the fragile wing, but to tilt its cotton mount just enough so a slant of sun hit its full surface, watching as it seemed to illuminate every woven thread beneath it, casting tiny, dancing blue flecks across the tabletop, his half-empty teacup, his own scarred wrist where IV lines had sat in the weeks after his transplant. 
The tea keeper sat across from him, pouring a second steeping of tea as the mist outside thinned just enough to show the valley floor a thousand feet below, carpeted in cloud forest that glowed the same shifting blue as the wing when high mountain winds rippled its unbroken canopy. 
“He was a wanderer,” she said of the donor, who had spent 30 years walking the same mountain trails the dye master had just traveled, never carrying a notebook or recorder, only a small vial of blue pigment he mixed from shed morpho wing dust to mark the places where stories felt thick enough to dip a finger into. 
The dye master pressed a hand to his chest, where the new heart beat steady and strong, and suddenly understood the answer he had traveled so far to find: people magnetize stories not by clinging tight to them, but by holding themselves the way the morpho wing holds light—shifting, open, ungraspable, letting every tale that passes through leave a faint, iridescent sheen instead of trying to trap it under glass for display. 
He thought of his quietly explosive pigments, which always disappointed wealthy patrons who demanded a flat, unchanging blue for their expensive tapestries, and smiled, realizing those pigments held the same iridescent caution the wing did, a quiet reminder that nothing worth holding stays still long enough to be owned or fixed in place. 
When he climbed back down the rope ladder as the sun dipped low to gild the mist, he carried the morpho wing tucked into the breast pocket of his wool coat, right over his new heart, knowing he did not need to chase sun trails anymore to find stories, because the heart in his chest already hummed with the quiet, magnetic pull of every tale the donor had ever let pass through him, and every tale he would go on to meet with open, watchful eyes.
