The cool-headed fisher knelt at the moss-cracked edge of the sound wave garden as the hour tilted, when patterns break themselves and stitched order frays at the seams. 
He had come not for silver trout or glass-finned minnows, but to catch the echo of a goddess, the faint, thrumming tone she had left tangled in the garden’s resonance when she walked the mortal realm three generations prior. 
His only tool, hammered flat on one end to cradle vibration, was a pitted old rail spike salvaged from the abandoned coastal line where the goddess had last stepped off the iron road to wander the tides. 
Unlike the village soothsayers who shouted incantations over the waves, he transliterated emotions, reading the unspoken weight in every hum and tremor the garden breathed out without forcing meaning onto the noise. 
His method required no hook, no net, no wriggling bait: by voicing silence, he held his breath until the world’s chatter faded, matching the hollow, resonant quiet that let echoes loosen from their moorings instead of snapping tight against loud, grasping calls. 
Above him, the first fraying of pattern showed as starlight seeping from the sky, the sleepwalking constellations drifting down from their fixed tracks to wander the garden’s ridges, their footsteps carving ripples that hummed half-remembered lullabies into the air. 
He spotted the faint gold thread of the goddess’s echo winding between constellation footprints, and he began to zigzag across the soft, resonant moss, bare feet skittering to avoid stepping on the stars’ trailing hems lest the ripple of contact scare the echo into dissolving back into static. 
One sleepwalking constellation, the great bear, huffed a low, rumbling note as it passed, its fur matted with stardust that drifted down to settle on his outstretched wrist, and he did not flinch, cool as the deep coastal tides he had fished his whole life, because flinching would shatter the silence he had nurtured. 
He could feel the echo’s emotion thrumming up through the moss as he closed the distance, transliterating its soft, wild grief, its longing for the salt wind it had not felt since the goddess left, its slow, sleepy drift as it tangled in the sound waves the same way sea grass tangles around a piling. 
When he was close enough to feel the echo’s gold warmth on his cheek, he lifted the pitted rail spike, holding its flattened end out like a cup to catch the tone, not trapping it, but offering it a hard, steady place to rest the same way he offered silence to the shyest fish that darted near his boat. 
The sleepwalking constellations paused in their wandering as the echo curled around the spike, their low, thrumming notes weaving into a feral lullaby that tangled with the garden’s sound waves, no pattern, no fixed rhythm, just the raw, soft sound of stars and ghost and fisher breathing in the same quiet space. 
He did not rush to leave, knowing the broken pattern hour would last only as long as the constellations felt safe wandering away from their sky tracks, so he sat cross-legged on the moss, the rail spike warm in his palm, and let the echo’s emotions wash through him without trying to pin them to words or wishes. 
Villagers had told him the echo would grant a wish if caught, that it would fill nets with fish, heal sick children, turn tides to carry lost loved ones home, but he had not come to demand anything of the goddess, only to listen. 
When the first pale edge of dawn touched the garden’s ridges, the constellations began to drift back upward, their lullaby softening to a murmur as they settled back into their fixed tracks, stitching the broken patterns closed once more for another hundred years. 
He stood slowly, his legs stiff from sitting, and tucked the rail spike into the inner pocket of his oilskin coat, where the echo hummed soft and steady against his chest, not trapped, but content to travel for a while with someone who knew how to listen without shouting. 
He walked the path back down to the coast without looking back, the faint hum of the echo seeping into his bones to mix with the sound of the tides, a quiet, feral lullaby he would carry with him every time he cast his line, proof that the softest, most patient hands always catch the things worth holding.
