When the roiling, ozone-thick storm approaches, tangled ivy and wild grapevine twist tighter across the pocked concrete facade of the vine-draped bunker hidden in a rewilded forest, their thorns catching on flaking Cold War warning stencils as wind whips pine needles against the rust-caked entry hatch. 
Inside, bioluminescent moss glows pale green along cracks where vine roots have pried through concrete, and the space’s only two occupants--Foresight, whose fingers are perpetually smudged with charcoal from mapping possible future threads, and Hindsight, whose frayed canvas pockets brim with smoothed shards of broken pottery and faded ticket stubs collected from abandoned past sites--lean over a dented steel worktable, bound by a quiet, burning motivation: to discover why some paths choose their walkers. 
Between them rests a crimped steel canister pried from a wall safe an hour prior, its lid crusted with decades of rust, holding a single yellowed sheet of paper: the bunker’s long-decaying directive, once meant to assign every resident of the surrounding valley a fixed, state-approved life path, sealed shut with a cracked, crumbing wafer seal the color of dried blood. 
Most of the directive’s typed text has bled into illegible smudges from decades of damp, its rigid mandates eaten away by mold and the slow, insistent creep of the forest that has reclaimed the land above, so Foresight and Hindsight know they cannot glean their answer from ink alone. 
Their chosen method, honed over years of wandering the rewilded valleys together, works through interpreted dreams, a practice that rejects rigid ritual in favor of a precisely playful rhythm: they measure exact pinches of dried pine resin and crushed blackberry for the small clay burner between them, arrange collected trinkets in mathematically perfect circles on the tabletop, then tuck silly, unplanned flourishes into the pattern--an acorn cap perched on the teeth of a rusted key, a moth wing tucked under a shard of old road sign, a dandelion fluff balanced on the wafer seal’s cracked edge--to keep the dreamspace from growing stiff and unyielding. 
As the first fat raindrops hammer the bunker’s exposed concrete roof and thunder rumbles so deep it vibrates in their bones, they lean forward, breathing in the sweet, resinous smoke, and let the dream pull them under. 
In the dreamspace, the decaying directive looms first as a tall, iron wall, its surface plastered with the names of people the old bureaucracy tried to chain to fixed paths, but the wall is crumbing, vines pushing through its cracks, and they watch as glowing, thread-like paths slip free of the concrete, winding out into the rewilded forest to seek out the walkers they are meant to carry. 
They see a teen who once thought they would be a bureaucrat, following a glowing thread up a mountainside to tend to neglected bee hives; they see a former soldier, assigned to guard this very bunker, following a thread to a small cottage where they would grow tomatoes and teach village children to carve wooden toys; they see the wafer seal, once meant to lock the directive’s mandates in place, dissolving into soft red dust that feeds the moss growing along the path edges. 
In that moment, they transcend the narrow limits that once defined them: Foresight stops scanning only for the branching futures that lay ahead, and Hindsight stops tracing only the worn ruts of what has already passed, because they finally understand that paths are not inert lines to be assigned or chosen--they are living, growing things, reaching for the walkers whose quiet joys and hidden strengths match their own winding shape, even when those walkers do not yet know they are being called. 
When they surface from the dream, the storm is breaking, golden sunlight slanting through the bunker’s cracked vent to gild the crumbled remains of the wafer seal and the decaying directive, and they smile, knowing they will walk out soon to follow the first path that tugs at their sleeves, no map or mandate required.
