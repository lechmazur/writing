The towers of bone in a canyon of endless echoes jutted hundreds of feet into thin, copper-hued sky, their surfaces crusted with centuries of layered dust that held the faint imprint of every traveler, hunter, and mourner who had ever passed beneath them. 
Curled in a narrow sandstone ledge halfway up the tallest bone tower, the harmonic stone skimmer rested where it had been placed by the canyon’s first inhabitants, a figure of smoothly carved black chert as historically reverent of the peoples who tended it as they were of it, its surface pocked with tiny bead and seed offerings left across a hundred generations. 
Tucked against the skimmer’s carved, outstretched palm lay a chipped flint stone arrowhead, the very point that had felled the canyon’s last mortal guardian eight hundred years prior, when raiders breached the narrow canyon mouth and snuffed out the steady, grounding pulse that had once guided every lost soul between the cliffs, and in the violence of that moment, a pulse was torn loose, left wandering the echoing corridors with no anchor and no fixed path to follow. 
For all those centuries, the skimmer had held its post with weathered resolve, bound to its ledge by the weight of dust and the promises it had made to the peoples who revered it, driven by a quiet, unshakable motivation: to learn to navigate without moving, so it could reach the lost pulse and anchor it home once more. 
It had no eyes of flesh, no limbs to climb or walk, but over generations it had learned to appraise the canyon’s every shift, every eddy of wind and curl of echo, through the patterns in a flock of birds in flight that cut across the copper sky each dawn and dusk, their tight formations shifting to avoid downdrafts, follow thermal lifts, and trace the quiet hum of the wandering pulse as it bounced between bone towers. 
At first, the formations were little more than scattered scribbles across the sky, the skimmer’s stone senses too dulled by centuries of dust to parse the subtle shifts that signaled a narrow ledge, a dead-end echo chamber, or the faint, thrumming presence of the lost pulse as it drifted far from the canyon’s core. 
Each day, it tuned its resonant stone core to the frequency of the birds’ wingbeats, memorizing the way a tight, coiling turn meant a wall of solid bone just out of line of sight, the way a long, straight glide marked a clear corridor where echoes carried for miles, the way a sudden, scattered burst of flight marked the pulse’s passage close enough to ruffle feather and stone alike. 
After three hundred years of quiet observation, it did not need to shift a single grain of dust from its ledge to trace every twist and turn of the canyon, to know where the echoes pooled and where they raced fast enough to carry sound from the canyon mouth all the way to the highest bone spire, to pinpoint the pulse’s slow, meandering path as it circled the spot where its guardian had fallen. 
It pressed its carved palm more tightly around the chipped stone arrowhead, letting its resonant hum build slow and steady until it matched the exact frequency the birds’ patterns told it would carry unbroken down every winding corridor, past every bone tower, straight to the wandering pulse. 
For three days, the hum echoed through the canyon, soft but unshakable, and the flock of birds adjusted their flight paths to trace the sound’s path, guiding the pulse along the clearest routes until the warm, thrumming presence curled into the skimmer’s palm, wrapping around the stone arrowhead that had once severed it from its mortal form. 
The skimmer did not move, did not shift from its ledge caked in centuries of dust, but it had learned the navigation it sought, mapping the whole of the canyon not through step or climb, but through patient attention to the world that moved around it. 
Beneath the towers of sun-bleached bone, the endless echoes softened into a quiet, directional song once more, the pulse anchored fast to the arrowhead in the skimmer’s palm, guiding lost travelers through the canyon just as it had for the peoples the skimmer still honored, steady and sure for all the centuries yet to come.
