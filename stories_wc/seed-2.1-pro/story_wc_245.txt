The blind mapmaker Lira pressed her palm to the bark of a resonant pine in the ultrasonic forest, where tree trunks hummed frequencies too high for most human ears to parse. 
Her rare gift let her absorb languages through skin, so the pine’s trilling, vibration-dense dialect seeped into her wrist to tell her the woods guarded a hidden stillpoint, a place where unprocessed feeling pooled instead of slipping away with turning tides and turning years. 
She had not come to chart trade routes or survey border lines, but to navigate uncharted emotional depths: the jagged, unspoken grief of losing her older sister Kaelin, a composer who had died mid-phrase of the lullaby they had written together as children. 
On her index finger glinted the platinum compass ring Kaelin had forged for her, its needle spinning uselessly everywhere but this forest, where it tugged north-northwest with a gentle, insistent pull she felt through the pad of her finger. 
She had timed her journey to fall across the pause of shifting constellations, that thin, breath-held window between the old star maps and the new, when the world’s usual magnetic holds loosened enough to let seekers cross into spaces sealed off from ordinary time. 
Lira did not carry a dowsing rod of carved hazel, as ordinary water-witches did; instead, she let the compass ring’s tug guide her outstretched hand, leaning into the hum of the trees to dowse for the frequency that matched the half-remembered lullaby thrumming in her own chest. 
The path unfolded not as blazes on bark or packed dirt underfoot, but as a trail of musical notes leading to a hidden melody: first a high, thin C brushed her cheek as she passed a fern, then a warm G curled around her ankle near a moss-covered stone, each tone seeping into her skin to add to the half-song she carried. 
For three hours she walked, her boots sinking into loam that hummed in harmony with the notes, her focus narrowed to the quiet blaze of her purpose: not to "fix" her grief, or erase the hollow left by Kaelin’s absence, but to meet that grief head-on, to collect the final notes Kaelin had never gotten to speak aloud before her heart stopped. 
When the compass ring stilled, pointing straight down at a small clearing ringed by bioluminescent mushrooms that pulsed in time with a slow, steady beat, Lira knelt, pressing both palms flat to the cool, vibrating earth. 
The hidden melody rose up through her skin at once, no longer scattered notes but a complete, tender phrase that matched the lullaby’s cadence perfectly, carrying the unspoken words Kaelin had meant to say the last time they saw each other: I am always with you, even when the stars move, even when you cannot see the path ahead. 
This was the hidden stillpoint, she realized: not a place free of pain, or a fantasy where loss never happened, but the quiet center where all the love and grief tangled together without being pulled apart by the rush of days, where unfinished songs did not have to stay unfinished. 
She sat there for the full length of the constellation pause, letting the melody loop through her bones, memorizing every rise and fall of every note until the first faint shift of the new star patterns tugged at the edge of her awareness, telling her the window between worlds was closing. 
When she stood to leave, the platinum compass ring’s needle spun once in a slow, smooth circle, then settled to point toward home, no longer tugging restlessly for a lost destination because she had found what she was looking for, tucked in the forest’s humming core. 
She did not rush down the trail, no need for sight to guide her when every vibration hummed clear through her boot soles, but let each step carry the melody with her, the notes now a permanent part of her, absorbed through skin the same way she absorbed the languages of stone and tree and wind over decades of making maps for places most people dared not travel. 
When she finally returned to her cottage, she sat at her desk and drew the first map of uncharted emotional territory she had ever made, marking the stillpoint with a tiny, raised musical note she could trace with her finger, the compass ring warm against her skin as she worked.
