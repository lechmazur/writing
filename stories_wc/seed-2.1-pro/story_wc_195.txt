The withdrawn knight, his dented pauldron slung loose over a tunic frayed from three years of self-imposed exile, stepped across the cracked stone threshold of the alchemical orchard greenhouse shrouded in endless night. 
He had followed the tug in his sternum for weeks, leaving the tourney grounds and hollow court praise behind, chasing a hum he had first heard the day he laid down his lance, convinced he had lost all capacity to feel anything but the dull thud of unmet duty. 
The space was factually mystical, its glass panes filtering starlight that turned falling petal dust to flecks of raw, unformed potential, where grafted apple boughs bore fruit that held the faint echo of every choice never taken, every road not walked by the souls who had stumbled there before him. 
He had come to capture the shape of possibility, the thing he had watched die a little every time he was ordered to charge a foe he did not hate, to bow to a king he did not respect, to wear armor that felt more like a cage than protection. 
As reality shifts, the edges of the stone paths softening into moss, the faint sound of string music curling between the trees, he spotted a crumpled paper half-buried under a bush of moon-roses, its ink smudged but still legible as an abandoned marionette theater program, printed for a show that had no record of ever being staged in any kingdom he knew. 
He knelt to lift it, his gauntleted fingers brushing the paper, and realized the lines of cast lists and stage directions were not printed text at all, but a map through forgotten patterns, the old, unspoken rhythms of joy and choice he had been taught to stamp out as a boy training for the knightly order. 
When he reached the center of the greenhouse, where a circular clearing held a stage barely bigger than a cart, strung with thin silver wires that glinted like spider silk in the starlight, he began to unsnap the buckles of his armor, letting each dented piece clatter to the moss at his feet until he stood only in his thin linen tunic, no crest, no oaths weighing on his shoulders. 
The air hummed louder, and he felt the wires brush his wrists, his ankles, not holding him tight but offering a light connection, and he spoke a murmured allegiance not to crown or code, but to the soft, unscripted versions of himself he had buried under decades of knightly expectation: the boy who loved to carve wooden puppets, who sang off-key to forest birds, who had never wanted to fight at all. 
The feeling that wrapped around him then was shivering bliss, warm enough to cut through the perpetual cool of the greenhouse night, sharp enough to make his fingers tingle as he realized the marionette show the program advertised was never meant to have wooden dolls on its strings. 
It was built for people like him, people who had let others pull their strings for so long they forgot how to move on their own, and as he let the forgotten patterns of movement flow through him, falling into small, silly dances, turns that had no martial purpose, gestures that held no duty or demand, he felt the shape of possibility solidify around him, no longer a distant hum but a tangible, living thing he could hold in his palms. 
As reality shifts again, the greenhouse's edges blurring to show glimpses of the roads he might now travel: a cottage in the woods with a small carving bench, a traveling puppet show for village children, a life unbound by oaths he never chose, he tucked the crumpled program into the neck of his tunic, close to his heart, and stepped off the small stage to walk among the glowing fruit, no longer a withdrawn knight hiding from the world, but a man who had finally found the thread of his own story.
