Long after the last ship sails for the frost-crusted southern archipelagos, its final horn bleeding thin over the salt-kissed marsh grass, the traveler steps through the lichgate into the shadowed garden in autumn. 
They are a wanderer who writes obituaries for the living, drafting tributes not to those laid in graves but to the versions of people that outlive their physical presence when they vanish without farewell, their years of slow, deliberate travel rooted in a quiet, unshakable motivation to understand the geography of healing. 
Copper beech leaves skitter across cracked flagstones, their edges burnt sienna where the low slanted sun catches them, and the air smells of fermented crabapples, woodsmoke drifting from a cottage half a mile off, and the faint, briny tang of the now-empty harbor. 
Beneath the gnarled boughs of an old pear tree sits a weathered sandstone bench, where three years prior the royal palace’s master weaver had left a single spool of indigo-dyed palace carpet weft thread, the same strand she had woven into every coronation carpet for forty years, before walking down to the harbor and never returning. 
The traveler had crossed three mountain ranges to reach this spot, chasing stories of explained absences: those rare, gentle departures where the one who leaves leaves no confusion, no unspoken argument, no unfinished fight, only small, intentional markers of the life they chose to step away from. 
Most grief, the traveler had learned, clung to the gaps where explanation failed, to the empty spaces people filled with their own worst guesses, but here, there was no gap to poison, only a clear line from the weaver’s bench to the harbor, marked by the thread spool, a half-peeled pear left for the foxes, a half-finished letter stating she had gone to weave carpets for the island villages that had lost all their weavers to the war. 
The traveler settles cross-legged on the bench, lifts the spool of indigo thread in an intimate, unrushed gesture, and begins the quiet work of projecting presence: they do not summon ghosts or speak to empty air, but trace the tight twist of the weft with a calloused thumb, letting the thread carry the weight of the weaver’s decades of skill, her choice to leave, the quiet joy of the work she was sailing toward, until the garden feels less empty, less shadowed, as if she is sitting just beside them peeling a pear. 
The indigo dye leaves a faint, cool blue smudge on their skin, the same rich hue as the twilight spilling over the garden wall, catching on the cobwebs strung between pear branches that glint like spun silver, holding the last of the day’s light even as the shadows stretch long enough to brush the harbor’s stone docks. 
For years, the traveler had mapped healing as a path through sharp, thorny thickets of grief, a long trudge toward a place where absence stopped hurting, but sitting here in the hush, holding the thread, they realize the geography is softer than they thought: it is the choice to let a person’s presence linger in the small, intentional things they leave behind, rather than chaining yourself to the empty space where their body used to be. 
They pull their leather journal from their coat, flip to a blank page, and draft the weaver’s living obituary, noting not the loss of a palace servant who vanished one autumn evening, but the arrival of a weaver on distant island shores, bringing indigo thread and decades of skill to villages that had forgotten the feel of a soft, tightly woven carpet under bare feet. 
They tuck a single loose strand of the palace weft thread between the journal’s pages as a marker, letting the indigo stain bleed softly into the paper, a permanent blue mark of an absence that does not ache, because it carries no unanswered questions, no bitter what-ifs. 
As the first star pricks through the darkening sky above the empty harbor, the traveler closes the journal, sits for a long minute breathing the crisp autumn air, and feels the quiet, unshakable splendor of being alone in a space that holds no ghosts, only the warm, lingering echo of someone who chose to live fully, even when it meant leaving a familiar life behind.
