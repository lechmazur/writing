In the silver courtyard under snow, where every drifting flake hung suspended between sigh and smile--the thin, frozen interval when the court’s annual conformity lullaby crested, catching villagers mid-resignation before locking their faces into placid, unthinking grins--Lira, the realm’s last chaste courtesan, moved with the bodily poetic grace of someone who had spent a decade holding space for truths no one else dared name. 
She had come to unlock the prism sanctuary under starlight, the faceted glass vault half-buried in a snowdrift at the courtyard’s center, where the court stored the annual harvest of echoes: every jagged, unruly memory, quiet rebellion, awkward longing, and unpolished joy the lullaby siphoned from its subjects to smooth them into compliant, identical cogs. 
The air hummed with the lullaby’s soft, honeyed tone, warm against her frozen cheeks even as it nipped at the edges of her own memories, trying to convince her to kneel, to smile, to let the court take the weight of her odd, unshareable loves so she would never feel grief or longing again. 
It was a tempting lie, and she nearly surrendered, until she remembered the weaver’s daughter who had once painted constellations on her wrist, the old baker who snuck her honey cakes when the court guards were not looking, the way their eyes had gone flat and empty the year before, their laughter replaced by polite, uniform chuckles on cue. 
The lock sealing the prism was not made of iron or key, but of a dense, glistening web of fine ice threads, each strand twisted tight around a stolen echo, so that a single snip or hard tug would shatter the memories held within into unretrievable stardust. 
She knew there was no key, no brute force that would free the harvest of echoes; she would have to unlock the sanctuary by untangling threads, one slow, careful motion at a time, even as the lullaby swelled and the suspended snowflakes inched closer to the ground, marking the shrinking window between sigh and smile. 
Her fingers, bare to the frost, moved with that same bodily poetic grace she had honed for years as a chaste courtesan, when she had sat for hours with visitors who came to her not for touch, but for the quiet safety of being seen, her hands never rushing, never pressing, only holding space for the messy, unruly parts of them the court demanded they hide. 
As she worked, the serenity of the snow-muffled courtyard wrapped around her, soft as a down blanket, but there was an alarming edge to the quiet: no bird called, no branch creaked, no distant voice rose in protest, because every other soul in the realm was already halfway lost to the lullaby, their sighs fading as the empty, conformist smiles began to set. 
Thread by thread, she teased apart the ice strands, recognizing each echo as she freed it: the baker’s secret recipe for sour cherry bread, the weaver’s daughter’s dream of sailing across the northern sea, a child’s giddy memory of chasing fireflies long after curfew, her own forgotten memory of singing off-key in the rain as a small girl. 
When the last thread slipped free, the prism sanctuary clicked open, and starlight spilled through its faceted walls to carry the harvest of echoes outward, each memory drifting like a snowflake to find its way back to the person it had been stolen from. 
For a heartbeat, the lullaby faltered, and Lira watched as the snowflakes finally settled on the courtyard’s silver stone, the suspended moment between sigh and smile breaking as villagers across the realm blinked, their eyes clearing as old joys and old sorrows rushed back to fill the hollow space the lullaby had carved. 
She sank back on her heels in the snow, her fingers numb and glowing with faint starlight, and let out a soft, shaking breath as the first distant laugh--raw, unscripted, entirely contrary to the court’s mandated uniformity--drifted over the courtyard walls, sharp and warm against the cold air. 
The serenity of the snowfall remained, but its alarming edge had softened, replaced by the quiet thrill of a world unshackled, where no one would be lulled into giving up the messy, beautiful threads of their own selves for the empty comfort of sameness ever again.
