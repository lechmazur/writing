<story>In the margin of daylight before curfew ended, Vey crossed the abandoned spaceport repair bay on bare feet, her boots tied around her neck and her eyes turned toward the roof. [31]
Beyond its torn panels, the first stars were surrendering, but she was a stargazer by stubborn habit, and stubbornness was nearly all the rebellion had left. [57]
For six days, both armies had waited while their dead commanders were buried, and the lull felt less like peace than a held breath. [81]
At full light, the guns would speak again unless the medical shuttle circling overhead landed safely, carrying wounded from both sides and the only officers still willing to negotiate. [110]
Its crew could not see the landing lamps, and the bay's reactor had been cold since the workers fled. [129]
Vey had remained because her father built this place, because her brother died trying to defend it, and because she hoped to discover meaning in the mess before someone tidied it with another victory. [163]
The bay was a wreck of hanging cranes, split coolant drums, and tool drawers spilling sockets like teeth. [181]
Beneath them stood an ancient stone circle, older than the colony, its twelve basalt pillars embedded in the floor as if the spaceport had been built around a secret. [210]
Vey's father had used its hum to tune engines, though he never explained why the note rose when certain stars touched the horizon. [233]
Now the stones were mute. [238]
She set her boots beneath a console, feeling ridiculous and resolute, then announced to the empty bay, "I can wake you." [259]
The boast sounded awkwardly confident, which was fair, since she had repaired nothing larger than a radio. [276]
From her pocket she took her mother's vintage opera glasses, their brass worn smooth and one pearl button missing. [295]
They had once watched singers from the cheapest seats; lately, they watched stars through the broken roof. [312]
Vey lifted them to the pale east and found the shuttle, a black moth above the clouds, then lowered them to a single blue spark sinking into daylight. [340]
Her mother called that spark the Tuner, and suddenly Vey understood her father's last diagram, which showed twin lenses at the circle's heart. [363]
She placed the glasses on a stone cradle, aligning their cracked left lens with the star, and stepped barefoot between the pillars. [385]
Cold struck her soles; then a hum entered her bones, so low that the cranes stopped rattling. [402]
The note traveled through copper veins in the floor, and the dead reactor answered with a blue pulse. [420]
It was working until the star thinned, the glasses slipped, and silence slapped back. [434]
Vey lunged, caught them against her chest, and cut her palm on the cradle. [448]
For one cowardly instant she imagined leaving, letting history choose people who owned boots and knew what they were doing. [468]
Instead she laughed at herself, wedged the glasses into place with a strip torn from her sleeve, and used her blood to mark the alignment. [493]
"Not a victory," she told the reactor, awkwardly confident again, "but a door." [506]
She gripped the ignition lever. [511]
To reignite the old machine, she did not fight the circle's hum; she breathed with it, pressing down on the fourth beat, as her father had once pressed her small hand to a warm engine. [546]
Thunder filled the bay. [550]
Yet beneath it, the stones held their serene note, and Vey stood perfectly still while fire ran through cables and landing lamps opened one by one like golden eyes. [579]
Outside, soldiers on both sides of the fence looked up as the abandoned spaceport blazed against the sunrise. [597]
The shuttle descended between their silent guns, wide and white, its red lamps washing the cracked concrete. [614]
When its doors opened, rebels and loyalists carried stretchers together because there was no time left to invent separate entrances. [634]
Vey retrieved the opera glasses and finally put on her boots, not because the stones had fallen silent, but because other people were coming. [658]
She had not found a reason for the deaths, or forgiven the men who caused them, or made the rebellion's lull into peace. [681]
The meaning was smaller and harder: a mess was not an instruction to burn everything, but a place to begin sorting, hand by bleeding hand. [706]
Above her, the last star vanished, and the bay thundered on with a serenity that no army had ordered. [725]</story>
