<story>During the Unbinding, when the canyon settlements were dissolving their shared mind so each survivor could cross the desert alone, Ione returned to the moonlit canyon with a broken periscope mirror wrapped against her heart. [35] She was a helix-minded engineer, unable to solve anything straight, and the mirror's spiral fracture seemed to her both a map and a summons. [59] Above her, silver rims trembled along the basalt, while below, the archive breathed through buried cables like a sleeper fighting a dream. [81] Its entangled memories had once let neighbors share courage, language, and grief, but now a single recollection of rain could drown six minds in another woman's childhood. [108] The council had ordered Ione to sever every connection before dawn. [119] She accepted because severing would also erase the unwanted futures people had surrendered to the archive, and she meant to become the archeologist of abandoned dreams, not their thief. [148] Her own abandoned dream waited among them: a green house beside a sea she had never seen, shared with a brother who had chosen the shared mind over escape. [177] At the archive mouth, she found the periscope that had once let sentries watch sandships without exposing their heads. [196] Its lower mirror was gone, its upper mirror was the fractured disk in her hands, and its empty shaft pointed straight into the moon. [220] Ione laughed once, a quaking sound that startled the dust from her sleeves. [233] To separate memories without killing them, she needed a viewpoint no single mind could occupy. [248] She would modify the archive through synaptic plasticity, using her own changing brain as a temporary grammar in which each memory could name its owner. [273] The method was chaotically precise: every new connection had to follow the archive's pulse, yet her thoughts had to remain wild enough that no memory could mistake her for home. [303] She mounted the broken periscope mirror above the archive's lens and turned it until moonlight struck the fracture. [321] Instead of one beam, the crack made seven, each bent along a different helix of wire. [337] Ione pressed the contacts to her neck and let the first beam teach her synapses its rhythm. [354] The canyon vanished. [357] She was nine and milking a goat beneath electric bells; she was an old man burning letters; she was her brother, Tomas, promising the sea to a child made entirely of other people's yearning. [391] The child wore her face. [396] For one terrifying instant, Ione wanted to keep every life, to let precision collapse into possession and call it love. [416] Then the mirror shook in its bracket, splitting the moon again, and she remembered that an archeologist brushes away dirt without pretending to have built the city. [443] She loosened her grip on the green house. [451] Around that small surrender, her brain grew new pathways, looping each stranger's memory back toward its own name while preserving the dream it had sheltered. [476] Names rose through her like bubbles through black water. [485] The archive convulsed; cables struck the walls; somewhere above, the council shouted that dawn was coming. [501] Ione could not answer, because Tomas was finally there, not as a ghost but as the memory of a door closing gently between them. [525] He had left no message, only the dream's coordinates and the hope that she would stop following him. [543] With a sob that felt like worship and terror, Ione turned the last helix. [557] Seven beams narrowed into seven silver threads, and the shared mind came apart without a scream. [573] Across the canyon, sleepers woke speaking their own names, then weeping at dreams they recognized as gifts rather than wounds. [593] At sunrise, Ione climbed out carrying only the mirror and a notebook of coordinates, her body shaking as if the moon still moved inside it. [618] She did not rebuild the green house. [625] She marked its place among a thousand other unfinished worlds, then began the slower work of listening, cataloguing, and leaving each dream free to astonish whoever chose it next. [654]</story>
