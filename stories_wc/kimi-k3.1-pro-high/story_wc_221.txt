<story>After the shutdown, the observation deck kept its own hours, stretching every second until Mira could hear the cooling metal remember thunder. [22] No engines answered, no crew spoke, and the stars beyond the glass hung motionless, as if silence had learned to echo. [43] Mira was the station's blessed pariah, anointed at birth by navigators who believed her survival of a solar storm marked her for safe passage, then exiled when every ship she boarded lost its way. [77] On the anniversaries of those disasters, her family had always sketched the night sky, not to worship it, but to argue kindly with its confusion. [102] They spread paper across kitchen tables, plotted each uncertain star, and asked one another where home might be if home refused to answer. [125] Tonight, alone above the sleeping engines, Mira followed that custom with a charcoal stub and the back of a decommission order. [146] Her mother's gilded compass rose applique, unpicked from their last shared jacket, weighted the paper's corner. [162] Its gold threads caught the emergency light, eight bright points surrounding an empty center. [176] As a child, Mira had thought true north was a place sewn into that emblem; as an outcast, she had thought it a verdict. [200] Now the deck's temporal field made her pencil move slowly through one moment while her pulse hurried through another, and the distinction felt newly important. [225] She drew the station, then the dead relay buoys, then the black interval where rescue should have appeared. [243] The shutdown had frozen the relays mid-calculation, leaving thousands of colonists with contradictory routes home. [258] Command called those routes errors; Mira, studying their patient arcs, saw questions that no one had stayed still enough to hear. [279] A chime should have marked the next hour, but it arrived as a low breath, stretched so thin that it never quite ended. [302] Within that breath, Mira caught a glimpse through the glass: not her reflection, but her mother's hands turning the applique toward an unseen star. [326] She did not mistake the vision for a message from the dead. [338] The deck recorded light from every departure, and time had loosened enough for one old kindness to return. [356] Her mother had worn that jacket while teaching Mira the custom, on the anniversary of the first lost ship. [375] Mira remembered now how the compass rose had never been aligned with the station's north. [390] Its longest point had faced the empty corner of their chart, the space reserved for whatever they had failed to imagine. [411] Turning the applique on her own drawing, Mira aligned that point with the black interval. [426] The gilded threads threw eight faint lines across the charcoal routes, and seven converged on nothing. [442] The eighth crossed a smudge she had made when the chime distorted her hand. [456] She almost erased it, then understood: the smudge repeated whenever her pencil crossed the relay symbols, a tiny drift caused by the deck itself. [480] If time stretched differently around each buoy, their routes were not wrong; they were maps drawn in mismatched durations. [499] Her curse had never been misdirection, but perception: ships trusted a single north while she felt every delay pull toward another. [520] Mira worked until her charcoal became dust, converting each route into a path the colonists could test rather than trust. [540] She marked uncertainties instead of hiding them, leaving blank spaces where exploration must begin. [554] When the chime finally faded, its silence echoed through the deck, and she transmitted the chart on the emergency band. [574] No answer came, yet the old ache of waiting no longer felt like banishment. [588] She pinned the applique beside the airlock, not as a charm guaranteeing passage, but as a promise to keep orienting herself. [609] At dawn, if dawn still meant anything here, she would wake whoever remained and teach them to sketch. [627] Together they would make an anniversary of the shutdown, filling its silence with questions until confusion became a country they could enter. [649] True north, Mira wrote beneath the chart, was not where every line ended, but the honest direction from which she agreed to begin. [672]</story>
