<story>After the water guild breached its contract and sealed the cathedral cistern, the orchard above Nessa's city began to drop fruit in July. [23] Its apple and pear trees grew in soil pockets along the rafters, so lush that their branches wove a green roof through the abandoned nave. [48] Below, the guild demanded timber rights, calling the trees doomed without its pumps. [61]

Bram, the quartz-eye mender, intended to disagree, but first he had to find his spectacles, which were already on his forehead. [82] His daughter, Lio, an endearingly feisty twelve-year-old with flour on her chin, pointed at them and then at the cracking cistern. [103] "Perhaps mend the big leak before arguing with the little ones," she said. [116]

Bram's quartz eye, a lens set where fever had taken his sight, revealed fractures as silver lines, a gift that had made him the cathedral's last repairer. [143] He climbed into the boughs, meaning to persist until he found a path around the guild's sealed valve. [161] Lio followed with his old satchel, which he had forgotten beneath a workbench after his mother's funeral. [178]

"Your grandmother packed it for the spring grafting," Lio said. [188] "She made me promise we'd open it when you stopped pretending grief was a schedule." [203] Bram, who had missed three springs by keeping busy, chose not to ask how a child could sound so much like his mother. [225]

Inside lay grafting wax, a curved knife, a copper wire coil, chalk, and his mother's pruning book. [242] There was also Lio's red hair ribbon, lost last autumn and blamed, with considerable ceremony, on a magpie. [260] "Evidence at last," said Lio, snatching it back, though she let him borrow it when the cistern gave another groan. [280]

The book showed no miracle pump, only an old gravity channel running from a rain basin, across the rafters, and into the roots. [303] The guild had sealed that too, but a fracture in the basin offered a narrow route around its lock. [322] Bram could widen it, if he accepted that mending sometimes meant making a careful crack. [337] He disliked this wisdom, mainly because Lio said it sounded exactly like something he would deny having taught her. [356]

They worked through the night. [361] With the knife, Bram cleaned the fracture; with wax and wire, he lined a trough from split shingles; with the ribbon, he tied its weakest join around a living branch. [391] The red strip would hold only through the season, but by then new shoots should bind the channel themselves. [410]

When dawn rain came, the basin shuddered, the trough lifted, and a thread of water crossed the orchard. [428] Then the ribbon knot slipped. [433] Lio swung onto the rafter, caught the trough against her shoulder, and shouted that if Bram planned to be noble, he could be noble with the wire. [460] He did, laughing so hard that his eye lens fogged. [470]

By noon, every root pocket was drinking, and the guild's timber contract looked considerably less profitable than the orchard's future pears. [491] That evening, neighbors gathered beneath the boughs, expecting Bram to demand an eternal promise from the guild. [508] Instead, he read the last page of his mother's book, where she had written about deciduous vows. [525]

A vow, she argued, should leaf out in service, fall when it stopped feeding anyone, and leave honest wood for the next season's growth. [549] So the neighbors offered the guild a yearly covenant: maintain the public basin, share the harvest, and return each spring to renegotiate before buds opened. [574] If either side refused, the water would still belong to no one, and the orchard would choose other caretakers. [593]

The guildmaster signed, mostly because the villagers had brought ladders and Lio had brought his account book. [610] No one asked where she had found it. [618]

Later, Bram returned the ribbon, now stained green at one end. [629] Lio tied it around the pruning book instead and announced that she would add her own notes, since dead people could not be allowed the last word merely by dying. [659] Bram winced, then opened to a blank page. [667]

He had wanted to preserve his mother's wisdom, but Lio showed him that a bridge between generations was not a monument; it was something each traveler repaired, crossed, and occasionally used to carry apples. [701] "Especially apples," Lio said, taking one. [707] Above them, leaves loosened in the warm rain, and Bram let the first one fall without trying to glue it back. [728]</story>
