<story>Above the clouds, where dawn made the moss blush and no bird disturbed the stillness, the verdant grotto waited for the last monarch butterfly to migrate through it. [28] Mara stood at its lip, transiently rooted as her grandmother had taught her: stay long enough to be useful, then leave no ownership behind. [52] She called herself an esoteric realist, meaning that she trusted mysteries only after testing whether they could carry water, turn wind, or guide a living thing home. [79] Today the mystery was a migration route omitted from every imperial map, along with the knotters who had maintained it. [99] Those women had built temporary structures as signals, raising reed frames whose leaf sails flashed in sequence and showed butterflies the safe current between cliffs. [124] Officials had praised the monarchs' endurance while dismissing the signals as decorative shrines, and Mara had come to honor overlooked histories by making the old guidance work once more. [153] In her pocket lay a moth wing, brown as weak tea, the only thing her grandmother had saved from their demolished workshop. [175] Its torn edge matched the pattern cut into the first signal sail, proof that the erased builders had learned their geometry from insects rather than kings. [201] Mara rebuilt that frame beside the spring, then four more along the grotto, lashing green reeds with flax that would soften by nightfall. [224] At each junction she would knot the cord twice, breathe the name of a knotter recovered from payroll scraps, and leave the third turn loose. [249] The loose turns let the sails pivot, flashing light in a five beat cadence that opened a path through the cloud wind. [271] By noon, four frames answered the sun, but the fifth remained dark, and far below the cloud sea a single orange speck struggled upward. [295] A storm was closing the western pass, its gray wall silent in the rosy hush, and without the final signal the monarch would drift toward stone. [321] Mara consulted her grandmother's notebook, where the fifth page held only a drawing of the moth wing and the words, Do not complete the cadence. [346] She had always read that warning as grief, another interrupted cadence in a life cut short by eviction. [364] Now, with the butterfly faltering, reading it as instruction felt like betrayal. [376] She tried a bright sail anyway; the frame shuddered, its reflections ran together, and the monarch wheeled away from the continuous glare. [398] Then Mara understood that a signal was not music made for human pleasure but a conversation with wind. [416] Four flashes invited ascent; the missing fifth made a pocket of shadow where tired wings could rest before choosing the sheltered eastern current. [439] The interrupted cadence was the design, preserved by builders whom history had mistaken for decorators. [454] She stripped the fifth sail away and built instead an open hoop, small enough to turn on one finger. [473] Into its loose fastening she worked the moth wing, not as an offering to magic, but as a dark vane shaped by the same patient evolution as the monarch's own. [503] When sunlight struck the first four frames, their pink flashes crossed the grotto, paused, and in that pause the wing angled itself toward the spring. [528] The monarch settled on the wet moss beside it. [537] For three breaths nothing moved except water threading downward, and the blushing stillness seemed to include Mara, the storm, and every unrecorded hand that had ever tightened flax. [565] Then the butterfly rose into the eastern current, clearing the pass before rain erased it. [580] Mara did not preserve the structures. [586] She spoke the knotters' names while the softened cords released, and the frames folded into the ferns, returning their reeds to the grotto. [609] Only the moth wing remained in her palm, lighter than the absence it had helped to shape. [626] At dusk she tied its stem into the notebook's broken binding, using the unfinished third turn. [642] It would hold until someone else needed to loosen it, add a name, and begin again. [658] Below, the clouds closed over the empty pass, but above them the moss kept the color of a signal just after light has gone. [682]</story>
