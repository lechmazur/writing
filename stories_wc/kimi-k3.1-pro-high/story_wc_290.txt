<story>During the fallow period, when every field rested and the irrigation gates stood dry, Mara Venn returned to uncover the vital key lost to time beneath Saint Orra's church. [29] Without it, spring would find the valley barren, whatever the almanacs promised. [41] She was a claustrophobic explorer of cliffs, deserts, and roofless cities because enclosed rooms made her lungs forget their work. [61] Yet she was prudently adventurous, and so she entered the ruined nave with three ropes, chalk, water, and a plan for retreat. [83] Above its broken dome, starlight silvered the dust; below, a stair descended toward the shadowed nexus where five buried waterways met. [104] From her mother, the church's last architect, Mara kept a blueprint and the steel point of her drafting compass. [123] Its tiny puncture marked the plan's center, beside a warning: geometry here owes no allegiance. [138] Mara tied her first rope to a pillar and descended, testing each step before trusting it. [154] At the bottom, arches circled a dry basin, and a lonely church organ stood beneath the open sky, its pipes black with verdigris. [177] The starlight made its keyboard gleam like teeth. [185] She crossed twenty paces toward it, but the organ receded, and the stair behind her became a sealed wall. [204] Then passages traded places with soft, grinding sighs, bridges folded into balconies, and her rope slid back to coil at her boots. [226] This was no collapse but spatial mutiny, the nexus refusing every route its makers had commanded. [242] Mara's chest tightened until the stars seemed nailed to a ceiling. [253] She nearly ran, which would have been death among those changing thresholds. [265] Instead she sat on her pack, loosened her collar, and named five things she could see until panic yielded to attention. [286] The mutiny had rhythm: arches moved only after faint tremors rose from the organ's empty bellows. [302] On the blueprint, waterways doubled as musical staves, and five small circles indicated notes. [316] Her mother had not merely drawn the nexus; she had scored its obedience. [329] Mara felt a wistful anger at that discovery, remembering a gentle woman who nevertheless believed every wild thing needed a ruler. [350] Following the score, she anchored the blueprint to the console with the compass point and pressed the first key. [369] A low note shivered through stone, transfixing one arch in mid-turn. [380] She played the second and third, and their reverberation transfixed bridges, stairs, and doors into a single trembling chamber. [399] Hope urged her forward, but prudence kept her at the keys until she had marked a safe path with chalk. [419] The fourth note required a pipe stop missing from its socket. [430] Only the compass point was narrow enough to reach the inner valve. [442] Mara pulled it free, sacrificing her fixed plan, and slid it into the mechanism. [456] The chord that followed passed through her bones and held the whole nexus still. [470] In that silence between heartbeats, she crossed the chalked bridge and opened the basin's central pedestal. [486] Inside lay no jeweled relic, only an iron sluice key wrapped in a second drawing. [501] It showed the original stream winding beyond the valley, not the five straight canals that had starved the hills to feed church lands. [524] Her mother had hidden the key after understanding what her obedient geometry had done. [538] The nexus shuddered; the chord was fading. [545] Mara could restore the old design and be carried safely upward, or follow the winding line and trust a route no living explorer had tested. [570] She checked her remaining rope, measured the distance to the stars, and chose the unknown with open eyes. [588] Jamming the organ's pedal with her pack, she sustained the reverberation, then wedged the iron key into the basin lock. [608] Instead of turning it toward the canals, she turned it along her mother's winding line. [623] Water thundered below, not as a slave recalled to duty, but as a traveler forgiven. [638] The nexus opened around its course, and Mara climbed a new stair into the nave as the organ fell silent. [658] By dawn, water had found the fallow terraces and the long-dry hill springs. [671] Mara kept the compass point, though its steel had bent. [681] Sometimes, touching its crooked tip, she mourned the mother who had mistaken obedience for love; sometimes she thanked her for leaving one rebellion unfinished. [705] When spring came, she would explore the reopened stream, walking beside it rather than drawing it straight. [722]</story>
