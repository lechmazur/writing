<story>After planting the wrong seeds, Ilyra Venn found her observatory growing wheat instead of the mirror flowers meant to shield it from the gravitational anomaly. [25] As the station's celestial signal interpreter, she had expected flowers to clarify the transmissions; instead, each stalk bowed toward a different century, filling her receivers with contradictory versions of home. [55] The anomaly had bent local time for years, but only since the wheat headed out had history begun arriving edited. [75] One broadcast mourned a city that still stood; another praised Ilyra for preventing the war she remembered losing, while her own voice ordered listeners to trust neither account. [103] Her superiors demanded the original sequence, confident that chronology was a bone the anomaly had merely bent and she could unbend. [124] Ilyra was less certain, yet she answered, "Give me until harvest," with the calmly assured manner that had kept her alive among collapsing stars. [148] She carried her instruments into the wheat, along with a dried-up fountain pen whose exotic nib, cut from a fallen satellite, was the only tool that could mark paper inside the anomaly. [180] Its former owner, her brother Tam, had used it to annotate signals before vanishing into one. [196] Though inkless, the pen scratched silver furrows wherever two events touched, revealing that the transmissions were not records but relationships. [216] The city burned because its bells had answered the observatory's first test; the war ended because a soldier heard that burning and spared an enemy engineer. [242] Remove either kindness or catastrophe, and every later account changed. [252] This was editing history through contingent relationships, not choosing which comforting lie survived, and the realization made Ilyra grip the pen until its crooked nib bit her glove. [280] At noon the wheat pulled sideways, roots holding while the sky seemed to pour downhill. [295] A new signal arrived in Tam's voice: "Sister, stop restoring the past; you are threshing away everyone who grew from it." [316] She had always wanted to thresh meaning from experience, separating nourishment from husks, but Tam made the metaphor sound like a warning. [338] If she unbent time to save the city, the spared engineer would never build the refuge where thousands now slept; if she preserved the refuge, her parents would remain ash beneath the bells. [371] There was no innocent original, only a field of consequences crossing like roots. [384] Confidently uncertain, Ilyra walked to the oldest stalk and began cutting, not to select a history but to gather what each version could teach. [408] With every handful threshed against her signal drum, the receivers released voices: enemies sharing bread, children naming constellations, Tam laughing at her solemn face. [432] She wrote these dependencies on seed packets, using the dry pen as a stylus, until a map covered the observatory floor. [453] Then she understood the pen's final trick: its nib was bent around an absence, a tiny curve of gravity like the anomaly itself. [476] She set it in the soil, where the wheat's pull might unbend it, and felt Tam's signal tighten around her wrist like a hand. [500] "If I straighten this," she said, "you may disappear again." [510] "Or arrive," he answered, "but do not make my return the price of their world." [525] His uncertainty matched hers, and somehow that made the choice bearable. [536] Ilyra lifted the pen before the nib could fully unbend, preserving its small crooked gate, then transmitted her map instead of a corrected timeline. [560] Across the colony, people began reading how their mercies depended on wounds and their joys on strangers' mistakes. [578] Some wept; some planted the wheat deliberately, mixing it with mirror flowers so no future garden would promise purity. [597] At harvest's end, the anomaly eased, not defeated but listened to, and the city remained both ruined and saved in signals that no longer competed. [622] Tam did not return, though once, beneath the static, Ilyra heard him humming while someone else cooked supper. [640] She put the pen beside her empty bowl, strangely comforted by the work still unreadable in her hands. [658] History was not a line to repair but a crop to tend, and tomorrow she would wake early, unsure of the weather and certain of the tending. [685]</story>
