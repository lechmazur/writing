<story>After time became visible, storms on the baroque asteroid Oriflamme fell as bronze rain, and every gilded cornice wore tomorrow like tarnish. [22] Cassian Vey, the defunct revolutionary who had once unseated Oriflamme's Astral Diet, now catalogued its ruins with gloved hands and verses he never spoke aloud. [47] His revolution had gone defunct before he did, its councils hardened into custodians, its songs reduced to dates on museum doors. [68] He remained poetically inclined because meter made a merciful cage for regret. [80]

The archaeological dispute consuming the dig concerned the Hall of Vases, buried beneath cherubs, twisted columns, and mirrors tall as airlocks. [101] Doctor Sen argued that its splendor proved an ancient slave court; her students therefore wanted the hall preserved as a warning. [122] The custodians called it a republican theater, since their shaky rule descended from the revolution that interpretation had inspired. [141] Cassian had supplied that interpretation from a broken inscription, and his old certainty now seemed another gilt mask. [159]

During excavation, his brush uncovered a crystal vase shard no longer than a finger, cloudy at its rim and impossibly warm. [180] Through it he saw not the trench but many hands passing one vessel beneath a roof open to the stars. [200] Then the vision closed, leaving an etched line: I am made plentiful by being emptied. [215] Sen accused him of mistaking sorrow for evidence. [223] He almost agreed, until the storm sirens began their soft, funereal chord. [235]

Visible time struck the outer mirrors first, filling them with alternate Halls where Cassian had died young or grown old as a tyrant. [258] On Oriflamme, covering mirrors during storms was no superstition but conservation law, since reflected centuries could settle over artifacts like false dust. [280] As crews hurried with velvet cloths, Cassian noticed that the shard brightened whenever a nearby reflection vanished. [297] He devised a plan before fear could make him prudent. [307] If every mirror were covered, the storm's time would have no human images to imitate and might pass through crystal as plain light. [330] The hall could then remember itself, rather than the witnesses. [340]

Sen called the theory sentimental, yet she helped him seal the doors, because refuting him cleanly mattered more than leaving him wrong. [362] Together they covered mirrors during the storm's ascent, working from boudoirs painted with nymphs to the great ballroom, where their own faces disappeared under black velvet one by one. [391] Darkness completed what revolution could not: no ruler, no rebel, no spectator, only the shard in Cassian's palm. [409]

Bronze rain entered the crystal and unfolded into a silent assembly, hundreds of miners, gardeners, cooks, and children drinking from the restored vase. [432] Each drinker added a drop of water before passing it on, so no one left thirstier than they came. [451] The hall's gold, Cassian understood, had not celebrated a throne; it had made common faces briefly luminous. [468] But the vision also showed masked officials confiscating the vase when famine made sharing inconvenient. [483] The people had built the theater, and their descendants had staged the tyranny. [496]

When the vision dimmed, Sen stopped her recorder and wept without abandoning her notes. [510] "Neither of us was right," she said. [517] "No," Cassian answered, "but the dead were more generous than our arguments." [529]

He could have used the recording to crown his old rebellion with innocence. [542] Instead, he surrendered it to both camps and asked that the museum be emptied of verdicts. [558] His motive was no longer to justify what he had broken, but to reimagine an entire civilization spacious enough to contain its kindness, cowardice, splendor, and repair. [585] The custodians resisted, Sen's students threatened resignation, and the dispute continued, but now every label had to face the crystal's evidence. [606]

Cassian spent his remaining years guiding visitors through the darkened hall during storms, inviting each to cover one mirror and imagine the face beneath it changed. [632] He never recovered the man who had believed one inscription could redeem history. [645] Yet when children asked whether the old world was beautiful or cruel, he answered with the tenderness of an epitaph: "It was thirsty, and sometimes it passed the cup." [674]</story>
