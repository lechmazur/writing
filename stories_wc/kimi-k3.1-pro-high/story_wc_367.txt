<story>After the last print newspaper folded its final gray wing, the sun failed to rise. [15]

Then she laughed until clouds answered. [89]
It was honest work, if you accepted that laughter could have teeth. [101]
In its final edition, the newspaper had quoted her careless toast: "No dawn is so old that it cannot be replaced." [122]

Now her accidentally prophetic words walked below as headlines, black paper boats battering the door while the stars watched without blinking. [152]

"You promised replacement." [201]

Her second laugh was deliberate. [233]
It called the rain into spirals and gave her time to think. [245]
The old routine could restore yesterday's dawn, she knew, but only by imprisoning the shadows again. [261]

She wanted neither surrender nor another comfortable cruelty. [322]
So she offered them work. [327]
"Help me shape a new dawn using ancient shadows," she said, "and never be swept away again." [344]

"Edges," said Vesper. [356]
She lifted the glass lantern wick, no thicker than a reed, and remembered her keeper's lessons. [372]

The new dawn did not erase the night. [510]
